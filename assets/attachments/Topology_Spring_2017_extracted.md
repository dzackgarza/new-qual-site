2. Let $Y$  be the annulus with identifications as shown in Figure 2.

1. Explain why $Y$  is a surface.
2. Is $Y$  orientable?
3. What surface is $Y$ ?

3. Show that $S^{1}\times S^{1}$  is not the union of two disks (where there is no assumption that the disks intersect along their boundaries).
4. Suppose that a continuous map $f:S^{3}\times S^{3}\mathbb{\rightarrow R}P^{3}$  is not surjective. Prove that it is homotopic to a constant function.

1. Show that any finite index subgroup of a finitely generated free group is free. State clearly any facts you use about fundamental groups of graphs.
2. Prove that if $N$  is a nontrivial normal subgroup of infinite index in a finitely generated free group $F$ , then $N$  is not finitely generated.

5. Find all three-fold covers of the wedge of two copies of $\mathbb{R}P^{2}$ . Justify your answer.
6. Let $X=S_{1}\cup S_{2}\subset \mathbb{R}^{3}$  be the union of two spheres of radius 2, one about $(1,0,0)$  and the other about $(-1,0,0)$ , i.e. $S_{1}=\{(x,y,z)\mid (x-1)^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=4\}$  and $S_{2}=\{(x,y,z)\mid (x+1)^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=4\}$ .

1. Give a description of $X$  as a CW complex.
2. Write out the cellular chain complex of $X$ .
3. Calculate $H_{*}(X\mathbb{,Z)}$ .

7. Use the circle along which the connected sum is performed and the Mayer-Vietoris long exact sequence to compute the homology of $\mathbb{R}P^{2}\mathbb{\#R}P^{2}$ .
8. Prove or disprove. Every map from $\mathbb{R}P^{2}\mathbb{\vee R}P^{2}$  to itself has a fixed point.
