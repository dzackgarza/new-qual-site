# Fall 2016 Topology Qualifying Exam

<a id="fall-2016-topology-qualifying-exam"></a>
## Instructions

Work all problems, justify your calculations, and explicitly state which theorems you are using. Each problem is worth 10 points.

<a id="problems"></a>
## Problems

1. Let $\mathscr{S,T}$  be topologies on a set $X$ . Show that $\mathscr{S\cap T}$  is a topology on $X$ . Give an example to show that $\mathscr{S\cup T}$  need not be a topology.
2. Prove that a metric space $X$  is normal, i.e. if $A,B\subset X$  are closed and disjoint then there exist open sets $A\subset U\subset X$ , $B\subset V\subset X$ , such that $U\cap V=\varnothing$ .
3. Let $S_{k}$  be the space obtained by removing $k$  disjoint open discs from the sphere $S^{2}$ , to leave a surface whose boundary is $k$  circles. Form $X_{k}$  by gluing $k$  Möbius bands onto $S_{k}$ , one for each circle boundary component of $S_{k}$  (by identifying the boundary circle of a Möbius band homeomorphically with a given boundary component circle). Use Van Kampen's theorem to calculate $\pi _{1}(X_{k})$  for each $k>0$  and identify $X_{k}$  in terms of the classification of surfaces.
4. Show that if $p:X\mathbb{\rightarrow C}P^{n}$  is a covering space map, then $X$  is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{C}P^{n}$ .
5. Let $A$  be the union of the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$  and the interval $\{(t,0,0):-1\leq t\leq 1\}\subset \mathbb{R}^{3}$ . Compute $\pi _{1}(A)$  and give an explicit description of the universal cover of $A$ .
6. Let $\Sigma$  be a closed orientable surface of genus $g$ . Compute $H_{i}(S^{1}\times \Sigma ;\mathbb{Z)}$  for $i=0,1,2,3$ .
7. Let $X$  be the topological space obtained as the quotient of the sphere $S^{2}=\{x\in \mathbb{R}^{3}:\parallel x\parallel =1\}$  under the equivalence relation $x\sim -x$  for $x$  in the equatorial circle, i.e. for $x=(x_{1},x_{2},0)$ . Calculate $H_{*}(X\mathbb{,Z)}$  from a CW complex description of $X$ .
8. Prove the Brouwer fixed point theorem. In other words, prove that every continuous map from $D^{2}$  to itself has a fixed point (without using either the Brouwer or Lefschetz fixed point theorems).
