# Algebra Qualifying Exam, Fall 2019

<a id="algebra-qualifying-exam-fall-2019"></a>
## Instructions

Complete all 8 problems. In multi-part problems, you may assume the result of any part (even if you have not been able to do it) in working on subsequent parts.

<a id="problems"></a>
## Problems

1. Let $G$  be a finite group with $n$  distinct conjugacy classes. Let $g_{1}\cdots g_{n}$  be representatives of the conjugacy classes of $G$ . Prove that if $g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i}$  for all $i,j$  then $G$  is abelian.
2. Let $G$  be a group of order $105$  and let $P,Q,R$  be Sylow $3$ , $5$ , $7$  subgroups respectively.

1. Prove that at least one of $Q$  and $R$  is normal in $G$ .
2. Prove that $G$  has a cyclic subgroup of order $35$ .
3. Prove that both $Q$  and $R$  are normal in $G$ .
4. Prove that if $P$  is normal in $G$  then $G$  is cyclic.

3. Let $R$  be a ring with the property that for every $a\in R$ , $a^{2}=a$ .

1. Prove that $R$  has characteristic $2$ .
2. Prove that $R$  is commutative.

4. Let $F$  be a finite field with $q$  elements. Let $n$  be a positive integer relatively prime to $q$  and let $\omega$  be a primitive $n$ th root of unity in an extension field of $F$ . Let $E=F[\omega ]$  and let $k=[E:F]$ .

1. Prove that $n$  divides $q^{k}-1$ .
2. Let $m$  be the order of $q$  in $\mathbb{Z/}n\mathbb{Z}$ . Prove that $m$  divides $k$ .
3. Prove that $m=k$ .

5. Let $R$  be a ring and $M$  an $R$ -module. Recall that the set of torsion elements in $M$  is defined by $Tor(M)=\{m\in M\mid \exists r\in R, \hspace{0.25em} r\ne 0, \hspace{0.25em} rm=0\}$ .

1. Prove that if $R$  is an integral domain, then $Tor(M)$  is a submodule of $M$ .
2. Give an example where $Tor(M)$  is not a submodule of $M$ .
3. If $R$  has 0-divisors, prove that every non-zero $R$ -module has non-zero torsion elements.

6. Let $R$  be a commutative ring with multiplicative identity. Assume Zorn's Lemma.

1. Show that

$$
N=\{r\in R\mid r^{n}=0 for some n>0\}
$$

- is an ideal which is contained in any prime ideal.

2. Let $r$  be an element of $R$  not in $N$ . Let $S$  be the collection of all proper ideals of $R$  not containing any positive power of $r$ . Use Zorn's Lemma to prove that there is a prime ideal in $S$ .
3. Suppose that $R$  has exactly one prime ideal $P$ . Prove that every element $r$  of $R$  is either nilpotent or a unit.

7. Let $\zeta _{n}$  denote a primitive $n$ th root of $1$ . You may assume the roots of the minimal polynomial $p_{n}(x)$  of $\zeta _{n}$  are exactly the primitive $n$ th roots of $1$ . Show that the field extension $\mathbb{Q(}\zeta _{n})$  over $\mathbb{Q}$  is Galois and prove its Galois group is $\mathbb{(Z/}n\mathbb{Z})^{*}$ . How many subfields are there of $\mathbb{Q(}\zeta _{20})$ ?
8. Let $\{e_{1},\ldots ,e_{n}\}$  be a basis of a real vector space $V$  and let $\Lambda :=\left\{\sum r_{i}e_{i}\mid r_{i}\mathbb{\in Z}\right\}$ . Let $\cdot$  be a non-degenerate ( $v\cdot w=0$  for all $w\in V\Rightarrow v=0$ ) symmetric bilinear form on $V$  such that the Gram matrix $M=(e_{i}\cdot e_{j})$  has integer entries. Define the dual of $\Lambda$  to be

$$
\Lambda ^{\vee }:=\{v\in V\mid v\cdot x\mathbb{\in Z} for all x\in \Lambda \}
$$

1. Show that $\Lambda \subset \Lambda ^{\vee }$ .
2. Prove that $detM\ne 0$  and that the rows of $M^{-1}$  span $\Lambda ^{\vee }$ .
3. Prove that $detM=\left|\Lambda ^{\vee }/\Lambda \right|$ .
