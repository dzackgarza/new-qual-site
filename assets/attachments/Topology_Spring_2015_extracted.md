# Topology Qualifying Exam, January 2015

<a id="topology-qualifying-exam"></a>
## Instructions

Work all eight problems; justify your calculations and state the theorems you use. The problems are weighted equally.

<a id="problems"></a>
## Problems

1. Prove that the product of two connected topological spaces is connected.
2. Let $X$  be the topological space obtained as the quotient space of a regular $2n$ -gon ( $n\geq 2$ ) in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$  by identifying opposite edges via translations in the plane. First show that $X$  is a compact, orientable surface without boundary and then identify its genus as a function of $n$ .
3. Let $S^{1}$  denote the unit circle in $\mathbb{C}$ , $X$  be any topological space, $x_{0}\in X$ , and $\gamma _{0},\gamma _{1}:S^{1}\rightarrow X$  two continuous maps such that $\gamma _{0}(1)=\gamma _{1}(1)=x_{0}$ . Prove that $\gamma _{0}$  is homotopic to $\gamma _{1}$  if and only if the elements represented by $\gamma _{0}$  and $\gamma _{1}$  in $\pi _{1}(X,x_{0})$  are conjugate.

1. Prove that a topological space which has a countable base for its topology also contains a countable dense subset.
2. Prove that the converse to (a) holds if the space is a metric space.

4. Let $X$  be the topological space constructed by attaching a closed 2-disk $D^{2}$  to the circle $S^{1}$  by a continuous map $\partial D^{2}\rightarrow S^{1}$  of degree $d>0$  on the boundary circle.

1. Show that every continuous map $X\rightarrow X$  has a fixed point.
2. Explain how to obtain all the connected covering spaces of $X$ .

5. Let $X$  denote the quotient space formed from the sphere $S^{2}$  by identifying two distinct points. Compute the fundamental group and the homology groups of $X$ .
6. Define a family $\mathscr{T}$  of subsets of $\mathbb{R}$  by saying that $A\mathscr{\in T}$  if and only if either $A=⌀$  or $\mathbb{R \textbackslash}A$  is a finite set. Prove that $\mathscr{T}$  is a topology on $\mathbb{R}$ , and that $\mathbb{R}$  is compact with respect to this topology.
7. Let $X$  be a topological space and let $U,V\subset X$  be open subsets with $X=U\cup V$ . Prove that the Euler characteristics of $U$ , $V$ , $U\cap V$ , and $X$  obey the relation

$$
\chi (X)=\chi (U)+\chi (V)-\chi (U\cap V)
$$

- (You may assume that the homologies of $U$ , $V$ , $U\cap V$ , $X$  are finite-dimensional so that their Euler characteristics are well-defined.)
