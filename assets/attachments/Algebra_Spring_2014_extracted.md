# Algebra Qualifying Exam, Spring 2014

<a id="algebra-qualifying-exam-spring-2014"></a>
## Instructions

In this exam, all rings are assumed to have a multiplicative identity different from zero, and homomorphisms of rings are assumed to map the multiplicative identity to the multiplicative identity.

<a id="problems"></a>
## Problems

1. Let $p$  and $n$  be integers such that $p$  is prime and $p$  does not divide $n$ . Find a real number $k=k(p,n)$  such that for every integer $m\geq k$ , every group of order $p^{m}n$  is not simple.
2. Let $G\subset S_{9}$  be a Sylow $3$ -subgroup of the symmetric group on $9$  letters.

1. Show that $G$  contains a subgroup $H$  isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times \mathbb{Z}_{3}\times \mathbb{Z}_{3}$  by exhibiting an appropriate set of cycles.
2. Show that $H$  is normal in $G$ .
3. Give generators and relations for $G$  as an abstract group, i.e. $G=\langle x,y,\ldots \mid relations\rangle$ , such that all your generators have order 3. Also exhibit elements of $S_{9}$  in cycle notation, corresponding to your abstract generators.
4. Without appealing to the previous parts of the problem, prove that $G$  contains an element of order 9.

3. Let $F\subset C$  be a field extension, with $C$  algebraically closed.

1. Prove that the intermediate field $C_{alg}\subset C$  consisting of elements algebraic over $F$  is algebraically closed.
2. Prove that if $F\rightarrow E$  is an algebraic extension, there exists a homomorphism $E\rightarrow C$  that is the identity map on $F$ .

4. Let $E\subset C$  denote the splitting field over $\mathbb{Q}$  of the polynomial $x^{3}-11$ .

1. Prove that if $n$  is a square free positive integer, then $\sqrt{n}\notin E$ . (Hint: You can describe all quadratic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$  contained in $E$ .)
2. Find the Galois group of $\left(x^{3}-11\right)\left(x^{2}-2\right)$  over $\mathbb{Q}$ .
3. Prove that the minimal polynomial of $11^{1/3}+2^{1/2}$  over $\mathbb{Q}$  has degree 6.

5. Let $R$  be a commutative ring and let $a\in R$ . Prove that $a$  is not nilpotent if and only if there exists a commutative ring $S$  and ring homomorphism $\phi :R\rightarrow S$  such that $\phi (a)$  is a unit. (Note: By definition, $a$  is nilpotent if and only if there is a natural number $n$  such that $a^{n}=0$ .)
6. Let $R$  be a commutative ring with identity and let $n$  be a positive integer.

1. Prove that every surjective $R$ -linear endomorphism of $R^{n}\overset{T}{\rightarrow }R^{n}$  is injective.
2. Show that an injective $R$ -linear endomorphism of $R^{n}$  need not be surjective.

7. Let $G=GL_{3}\mathbb{(Q[}x])$  be the group of invertible $3\times 3$  matrices over $\mathbb{Q[}x]$ . For each $f\mathbb{\in Q[}x]$ , let $S_{f}$  be the set of $3\times 3$  matrices $A$  over $\mathbb{Q[}x]$  such that $det(A)=cf(x)$  for some nonzero constant $c\mathbb{\in Q}$ .

1. Show that for $(P,Q)\in G\times G$  and $A\in S_{f}$ , the formula

$$
(P,Q)\cdot A=PAQ^{-1}
$$

- gives a well-defined map $G\times G\times S_{f}\rightarrow S_{f}$  and show that this map is an action of $G\times G$  on $S_{f}$ .

2. For $f(x)=x^{3}(x^{2}+1)^{2}$ , give one representative from each orbit of the group action given in part i. Justify your assertion.
