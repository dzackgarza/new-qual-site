## Problems

1. Show that any group of order 2020 is solvable.
2. Give (without proof) a classification of all abelian groups of order 2020.
3. Describe one nonabelian group of order 2020.

1. Let $H$  be a normal subgroup of a finite group $G$ , where the order of $H$  and the index of $H$  in $G$  are relatively prime. Prove that no other subgroup of $G$  has the same order as $H$ .
2. Let $E$  be an extension field of $F$  and $\alpha \in E$  be algebraic of odd degree over $F$ .

1. Show that $F(\alpha )=F​\left(\alpha ^{2}\right)$ .
2. Prove that $\alpha ^{2020}$  is algebraic of odd degree over $F$ .

3. Let $f(x)=x^{4}-2\mathbb{\in Q[}x]$ .

1. Define what it means for a finite extension field $E$  of a field $F$  to be a Galois extension.
2. Determine the Galois group $Gal(E\mathbb{/Q)}$  for the polynomial $f(x)$ . [Justify your answer carefully.]
3. Exhibit a subfield $K$  in (b) such that $\mathbb{Q\leq}K\leq E$  with $K$  not a Galois extension over $\mathbb{Q}$ . Explain.

4. Let $R$  be a ring and $f:M\rightarrow N$  and $g:N\rightarrow M$  be $R$ -module homomorphisms such that $g\circ f=id_{M}$ . Show that $N\cong Imf\oplus Kerg$ .
5. Let $R$  be a ring with unity.

1. Give a definition for a free module over $R$ .
2. Define what it means for an $R$ -module to be torsion free.
3. Prove that if $F$  is a free module, then any short exact sequence of $R$ -modules $0\rightarrow N\rightarrow M\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0$  splits.
4. Let $R$  be a PID. Show that any finitely generated $R$ -module $M$  can be expressed as a direct sum of a torsion module and a free module. [You may assume that a finitely generated torsion free module over a PID is free.]

6. Let

$$
A=\left[\begin{matrix}2&0&0\\4&6&1\\-16&-16&-2\end{matrix}\right]\in M_{3}\mathbb{(C)}
$$

1. Find the Jordan canonical form $J$  of $A$ .
2. Find an invertible matrix $P$  such that $P^{-1}AP=J$ . [You should not need to compute $P^{-1}$ .]
3. Write down the minimal polynomial of $A$ .

7. Let $T:V\rightarrow V$  be a linear transformation where $V$  is a finite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{C}$ . Prove the Cayley-Hamilton Theorem, that is $p(T)=0$  where $p(x)$  is the characteristic polynomial of $T$ . [You may use canonical forms.]
