## Analysis Qualifying Exam: real analysis part

January, 2005

<a id="instructions"></a>
### Instructions

Do all 5 problems.

<a id="problems"></a>
### Problems

1. Suppose $A$  is an uncountable subset of $\mathbb{R}$  and $A^{'}$  is the set of limit points of $A$ . Prove $A\cap A^{'}$  is uncountable.
2. If $0<p\leq 1$ , prove that $\frac{sinx}{x^{p}}$  is not integrable on $(0,\infty )$ , but

$$
\int_{0}^{\infty }\frac{sinx}{x^{p}} \, dx=\lim_{r\to \infty }\int_{0}^{r}\frac{sinx}{x^{p}} \, dx
$$

- exists.

3. Suppose $\left\{f_{n}:n\geq 1\right\}$  is a sequence of continuous functions on $\mathbb{R}^{m}$  with the property that for every $x\in \mathbb{R}^{m}$ ,

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty }\left|f_{n}(x)-f_{n-1}(x)\right|\leq \frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}.
$$

- Prove the sequence $\left\{f_{n}:n\geq 1\right\}$  converges to a continuous function.

4. If $\mu$  is Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$  and $f\in L\mathbb{(R,}d\mu )$ , prove that $F$  is absolutely continuous where

$$
F(x)=\int_{(-\infty ,x]}^{​}f \, d\mu .
$$

5. Let $f$  be continuously differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ , and suppose that $f$  and $f^{'}$  are both integrable. Prove

$$
\lim_{k\to \infty }\int_{-\infty }^{\infty }sin(kx)f(x) \, dx=0.
$$

## Analysis Qualifying Exam: complex analysis part

January, 2005

<a id="instructions-1"></a>
### Instructions

5 problems, counted 10 points apiece.

<a id="problems-1"></a>
### Problems

1. State the most general version that you know of Cauchy's Integral Formula and then sketch a proof of the simplest version of Cauchy's Integral Formula.
2. Evaluate the following integral by the method of residues (with justification).

$$
\int_{-\infty }^{\infty }\frac{1+x^{2}}{1+x^{4}} \, dx
$$

3. Prove that if a complex power series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty }a_{n}z^{n}$  converges for some $z_{0}\ne 0$ , then it converges absolutely in the open disk $\left\{z\mathbb{\in C:|}z|<|z_{0}|\right\}$  and uniformly on any closed subdisk.
4. State the general theorem on existence of a Laurent series and determine the Laurent series for

$$
\frac{z}{1-z^{2}}
$$

- on the annulus $0<|z-1|<1$  and on the annulus $1<|z|<\infty$ .

5. Construct a conformal mapping from $H=\{z\mathbb{\in C:}Im(z)>0\}$  in $\mathbb{C}$  onto $G=\{w\mathbb{\in C:}Re(w)>0 and |w|<1\}$ . Include a definition of conformal mapping, and indicate why your mapping is conformal.
