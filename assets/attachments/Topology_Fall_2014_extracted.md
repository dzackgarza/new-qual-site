# Topology Qualifying Examination, Fall 2014

<a id="topology-qualifying-examination"></a>
## Instructions

Justify all the calculations and state the theorems you use in your answers. The problems have equal weight.

<a id="problems"></a>
## Problems

1. Define what it means for a topological space to be:

1. connected
2. locally connected

2. Give, with proof, an example of a space that is connected but not locally connected.

1. Is every product (finite or infinite) of Hausdorff spaces Hausdorff? If yes, prove it. If no, give a counterexample.
2. Let $X$  and $Y$  be topological spaces and let $f:X\rightarrow Y$  be a function. Suppose that $X=A\cup B$  where $A$  and $B$  are closed subsets, and that the restrictions $\left.f\right|_{A}$  and $\left.f\right|_{B}$  are continuous (where $A$  and $B$  have the subspace topology). Prove that $f$  is continuous.
3. Prove that $\mathbb{R}^{2}$  is not homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{n}$  for $n>2$ .
4. Prove that every continuous map $f\mathbb{:R}P^{2}\rightarrow S^{1}$  is homotopic to a constant. (Hint: think about covering spaces.)
5. Compute the integral homology groups of the space $X=Y\cup Z\subset \mathbb{R}^{3}$  which is the union of the sphere $Y=\{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=1\}$  and the ellipsoid $Z=\left\{x^{2}+y^{2}+\frac{z^{2}}{4}=1\right\}.$
6. Identify (with proof, but of course you can appeal to the classification of surfaces) all of the compact surfaces without boundary that have a cell decomposition having exactly one 0-cell and exactly two 1-cells (with no restriction on the number of cells of dimension larger than 1).
7. Prove that, for every continuous map $f:B^{2}\rightarrow B^{2}$  there is a point $x$  such that $f(x)=x$ .
