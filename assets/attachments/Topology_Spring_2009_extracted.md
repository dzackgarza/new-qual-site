# Topology Qualifying Exam: Spring 2009

<a id="topology-qualifying-exam-spring-2009"></a>
## Problems

1. [10 points] Let $(X,d)$  be a compact metric space, and let $f:X\rightarrow X$  be an isometry: for all $x,y\in X,d(x,y)=d(f(x),f(y))$ . Show that $f$  is a bijection.
2. [10 points].

1. Show that a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism.
2. Give an example which shows that the "Hausdorff" hypothesis in part a) is necessary.

3. [10 points]. Show that a connected, normal topological space with more than a single point is uncountable.
4. [10 points]. Let $X$  be the one-point union of $S^{1}\times S^{1}$  and $S^{1}$ .

1. Compute the fundamental group of $X$ .
2. Compute the homology groups of $X$ .

5. [15 points].

1. What is the degree of the antipodal map on the $n$ -sphere? (no justification required)
2. Define a CW-complex homeomorphic to real projective $n$ -space $\mathbb{RP}^{n}$ .
3. Use parts a) and b) to compute the integral homology groups of $\mathbb{RP}^{n}$ .

6. [15 points]. Let $X$  be a CW-complex and $\pi :Y\rightarrow X$  be a covering space.

1. Show that $Y$  is compact iff $X$  is compact and $\pi$  has finite degree.
2. Assume that $\pi$  has finite degree $d$ . show that $\chi (Y)=d\chi (X)$ .
3. Let $\pi :\mathbb{RP}^{N}\rightarrow X$  be a covering map. Show that if $N$  is even, $\pi$  is a homeomorphism.

7. [10 points]. How many surfaces are there, up to homeomorphism which are: connected, compact, possibly with boundary, possibly nonorientable and with Euler characteristic $-3$ ? Describe one representative from each class.
8. [20 points] View the torus $T$  as the quotient space $\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}$ . Let $A$  be a $2\times 2$  matrix with $\mathbb{Z}$ -coefficients.

1. Show that the linear map $A:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{2}$  descends to a continuous map $\mathscr{A:}T\rightarrow T$ .
2. Show that, with respect to a suitable basis for $H_{1}(T\mathbb{,Z)}$ , the matrix $A$  represents the map induced on $H_{1}$  by $\mathscr{A}$ .
3. Find a necessary and sufficient condition on $A$  for $\mathscr{A}$  to be homotopic to the identity.
4. Assume additionally that $\mathscr{A}$  is a homeomorphism, that $detA=1$ , and that all entries of $A$  are nonnegative. Find a necessary and sufficient condition on $A$  for $\mathscr{A}$  to be homotopic to a map with no fixed points.
