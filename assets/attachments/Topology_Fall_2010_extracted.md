## Problems

1. What does it mean to say a topological space is compact?
2. Show that a product space $X\times Y$  is compact if and only if both $X$  and $Y$  are compact.

1. What does it mean to say a topological space is connected?
2. What does it mean to say a topological space is path-connected?
3. Prove that if $X$  is path-connected, then $X$  is connected.
4. Either prove or give a counterexample to the converse of part (c).

1. Fix an integer $n$ . Let $X$  be the surface obtained from the cylinder $S^{1}\times [0,1]$  by identifying the point $(z,0)$  with the point $(z^{n},1)$  for all $z\in S^{1}$ . Find the fundamental group of $X$ .
2. Calculate the homology groups of $S^{2}\times S^{2}$ .

1. What is meant by the degree of a continuous map $f:S^{n}\rightarrow S^{n}$ ?
2. Let $f:S^{n}\rightarrow S^{n}$  be a continuous map with no fixed points. Prove that $f$  has degree $(-1)^{n+1}$ .

3. Suppose that $X$  has universal cover $p:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X$  and let $A$  be a subspace of $X$  with $p(\tilde{a})=a\in A$ . Show that there is a group isomorphism

$$
ker\left(\pi _{1}(A,a)\overset{i_{*}}{\rightarrow }\pi _{1}(X,a)\right)\cong \pi _{1}\left(p^{-1}(A),\tilde{a}\right)
$$

- where $i:A\rightarrow X$  is the inclusion map.

1. State the classification theorem for compact connected surfaces (with or without boundary).
2. Show that any compact connected surface with a nonempty boundary is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of circles. (Hint: you may assume that any compact connected surface without boundary is given by identifying edges of a polygon in pairs.)
3. For each surface in your classification, say how many circles are needed in the wedge from part (b). (Hint: you should be able to do this even if you have not done part (b).)

4. For which compact connected surfaces $\Sigma$  (with or without boundary) does there exist a continuous map $f:\Sigma \rightarrow \Sigma$  that is homotopic to the identity map and has no fixed points? Explain your answer fully.
