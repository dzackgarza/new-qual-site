# Algebra Qualifying Exam, Spring 2013

<a id="algebra-qualifying-exam-spring-2013"></a>
## Instructions

Complete all 8 problems. In multi-part problems, you may assume the result of any part (even if you have not been able to do it) in working on subsequent parts.

<a id="problems-heading"></a>
## Problems

1. Let $R$  be a commutative ring.

1. Define a maximal ideal, and prove that $R$  has a maximal ideal.
2. Show that an element $r\in R$  is not invertible if and only if it is contained in a maximal ideal.
3. Let $M$  be an $R$ -module. Recall that for $\mu \in M$ , $\mu \ne 0$ , the annihilator of $\mu$  is the set $Ann(\mu )=\{r\in R:r\mu =0\}$ . Suppose that $I$  is an ideal in $R$  which is maximal with respect to the property that there exists an element $\mu \in M$  such that $I=Ann(\mu )$ , for some $\mu \in M$ . (In other words, $I=Ann(\mu )$  but there does not exist $\nu \in M$  with $J=Ann(\nu )\subsetneq R$  such that $I\subsetneq J$ .) Prove that $I$  is a prime ideal in $R$ .

1. Define a Euclidean Domain.
2. Define a Unique Factorization Domain.
3. Is a Euclidean Domain also a Unique Factorization Domain? Give either a proof or a counter example (with justification).
4. Is a Unique Factorization Domain also a Euclidean Domain? Give either a proof or a counter example (with justification).

2. Let $P$  be a finite $p$ -group. Prove that every nontrivial normal subgroup of $P$  intersects the center of $P$  nontrivially.
3. Define simple group. Prove that a group of order $56$  can not be simple.
4. Let $T:V\rightarrow V$  be a linear map from a $5$ -dimensional complex vector space to itself, and suppose that $f(T)=0$  where $f$  is the polynomial $f(x)=x^{2}+2x+1$ .

1. Show that there does not exist any vector $v\in V$  such that $Tv=v$ , but that there does exist a vector $w\in V$  such that $T^{2}w=w$ .
2. Give all the possible Jordan canonical forms of a linear transformation $T$  which satisfies the above relation $f(T)=0$ .

5. Let $V$  be a finite dimensional vector space over the field $F$  and let $T:V\rightarrow V$  be a linear operator with characteristic polynomial $f(x)\in F[x]$ .

1. Show that $f(x)$  is irreducible in $F[x]\Leftrightarrow$  there are no proper nonzero subspaces $W$  of $V$  with $T(W)\subseteq W$ .
2. If $f(x)$  is irreducible in $F[x]$  and the characteristic of $F$  is $0$ , show that $T$  is diagonalizable when we extend the field to its algebraic closure.

6. Let $f(x)=g(x)h(x\mathbb{)\in Q[}x]$ . Let $E\mathbb{/Q}$ , $B\mathbb{/Q}$ , and $C\mathbb{/Q}$  be splitting fields of $f(x)$ , $g(x)$  and $h(x)$ , respectively.

1. Prove that $Gal(E/B)$  and $Gal(E/C)$  are normal subgroups of $Gal(E\mathbb{/Q)}$ .
2. Prove that $Gal(E/B)\cap Gal(E/C)=\{1\}$ .
3. If $B\cap C\mathbb{=Q}$  show that $Gal(E/B)Gal(E/C)=Gal(E\mathbb{/Q)}$ .
4. Under the hypothesis of (c) show that $Gal(E\mathbb{/Q)\cong}Gal(E/B)\times Gal(E/C)$ .
5. Use (d) to describe $Gal(\mathbb{Q[}\alpha \mathbb{]/Q)}$  where $\alpha =\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$ .

7. Let $F$  be the field of $2$  elements and $K$  a splitting field of $f(x)=x^{6}+x^{3}+1$  over $F$ . This polynomial is known to be irreducible (you may assume this).

1. Show that if $r$  is a root of $f$  in $K$ , then $r^{9}=1$  but $r^{3}\ne 1$ .
2. Find $Gal(K/F)$  and express each intermediate field between $F$  and $K$  as $F(b)$  for appropriate $b$  in $K$ .
