# Gröbner bases and varieties

Throughout, $k$ is a field, $R = k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and $J\subseteq R$ is an ideal.

## Buchberger's algorithm

1. Fix a monomial order, such as lexicographic order or graded reverse lexicographic order.

2. For each pair of elements of the current basis, form their $S$-polynomial, reduce it by division against the current basis, and adjoin any nonzero remainder to the basis.

3. Stop when every $S$-polynomial of a pair of basis elements reduces to zero.

By Buchberger's criterion, a finite generating set of $J$ is a Gröbner basis if and only if every $S$-polynomial of a pair of its elements reduces to zero.
The algorithm terminates because the ideals generated by the leading terms of the successive bases form a strictly ascending chain of monomial ideals, which stabilizes by Dickson's lemma.

## Applications of Gröbner bases

Let $G$ be a Gröbner basis of $J$.

- **Ideal membership.** $f\in J$ if and only if the remainder of $f$ on division by $G$ is zero.

- **Elimination.** For lexicographic order with $x_1>\cdots>x_n$, $G\intersect k[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_n]$ is a Gröbner basis of the elimination ideal $J\intersect k[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_n]$; this solves polynomial systems by back-substitution and computes implicitizations.

- **Dimension and degree.** The dimension and degree of $V(J)$ are determined by the leading term ideal of $J$.

- **Equality of ideals.** For a fixed monomial order, each ideal has a unique reduced Gröbner basis, so two ideals are equal if and only if their reduced Gröbner bases are equal.

## Ideals and varieties

Let $k$ be algebraically closed.
By the Nullstellensatz, $I(V(J)) = \sqrt J$.
By the Rabinowitsch trick, $f\in\sqrt J$ if and only if $1\in J + \gens{1-yf}\subseteq R[y]$, which is an ideal membership test.
In particular $V(J) = \emptyset$ if and only if $1\in J$, if and only if the reduced Gröbner basis of $J$ is $\ts 1$.
