# Invariant theory

Let $G$ be a group acting linearly on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ over a field $k$, so that $G$ acts on the polynomial ring $k[V]$ by $k$-algebra automorphisms.
The [[D-INVRING|invariant ring]] $k[V]^G$ is the graded subring of the polynomials fixed by every element of $G$, and $(k[V]^G)_d$ is its degree-$d$ part.

## Finite generation

Suppose $G$ is finite and $\size G$ is invertible in $k$.
Then
$$
\rho\colon k[V]\too k[V]^G,\qquad \rho(f)\coloneqq\frac1{\size G}\sum_{g\in G}g\cdot f
$$
is a projection onto $k[V]^G$ that preserves degree, and it is $k[V]^G$-linear: $\rho(hf) = h\rho(f)$ for $h\in k[V]^G$.
This is the average used in the proof of [[algebra/representations/maschke-and-schur|Maschke's theorem]].

The invariant ring $k[V]^G$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra.
Let $I\subseteq k[V]$ be the ideal generated by the invariants of positive degree.
The ring $k[V]$ is Noetherian by the [[T-YYLPH|Hilbert basis theorem]], so finitely many homogeneous invariants $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ of positive degree generate $I$.
Then $k[V]^G = k[f_1,\ldots,f_r]$.
Otherwise a homogeneous invariant $f$ of least degree outside $k[f_1,\ldots,f_r]$ lies in $I$, and the homogeneous component of degree $\deg f$ of an expression of $f$ in the generators writes $f = \sum_i a_if_i$ with $a_i$ homogeneous of degree $\deg f - \deg f_i < \deg f$; applying the $k[V]^G$-linear projection $\rho$ gives $f = \sum_i \rho(a_i)f_i$, where $\rho(a_i)\in k[V]^G$ has degree at most $\deg a_i$, hence lies in $k[f_1,\ldots,f_r]$ by the minimality of $f$, which puts $f$ there as well.

The projection $\rho$ alone does not give this conclusion.
It exhibits $k[V]^G$ as a direct summand of $k[V]$ as a $k[V]^G$-module, and a direct summand of a Noetherian module is Noetherian; but $k[V]$ is Noetherian over $k$, not over $k[V]^G$, and it is Noetherian over $k[V]^G$ only if $k[V]^G$ is Noetherian, which is what finite generation supplies.
The argument above uses Noetherianity of $k[V]$ over $k$ instead, and applies $\rho$ to the coefficients of an expression in the ideal $I$.

If $G$ is finite and $\size G!$ is invertible in $k$, then [[T-NOETHD|Noether's bound]] makes $k[V]^G$ generated by its invariants of degree at most $\size G$, and [[P-APA17H|the cyclic group of order $4$ in $\GL_2(\CC)$]] works the bound out for one group.

## Computations

For $G = S_n$ permuting the variables, the invariants are the symmetric polynomials: $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]^{S_n} = k[e_1,\ldots,e_n]$, with $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ algebraically independent, which is the fundamental theorem of [[applied-algebra/symmetric-functions/index|symmetric functions]].

For $G = \ZZ/n$ acting by $x_i\mapsto\zeta x_i$ on every $i$, with $\zeta$ a primitive $n$th root of unity, a monomial $x^\alpha$ is fixed by a generator exactly when $\zeta^{\abs\alpha} = 1$, so the invariants are spanned by the monomials whose total degree is divisible by $n$.

For a finite $G\subseteq\GL(V)$ over $\CC$, [[T-MOLIEN|Molien's formula]] computes the Hilbert series of the invariant ring from the conjugacy classes alone, so the number of linearly independent invariants in each degree is known without finding generators.

## The Reynolds operator

If $\size G$ is invertible in $k$, the map $\rho$ above is the Reynolds operator: a $k[V]^G$-linear projection of $k[V]$ onto $k[V]^G$.
It is the invariant-theoretic case of the averaging projection onto the fixed points of a finite group acting on a vector space ([[P-APA24G|the averaging operator is the orthogonal projection onto the $G$-invariants]]), and its rank is computed for one permutation representation on [[P-APAS18D|the $D_8$-orbits on the diagonals of a regular octagon]].
When $\operatorname{char}k$ divides $\size G$, this operator is not defined, as in the failure of [[algebra/representations/maschke-and-schur|Maschke's theorem]] in that case.
