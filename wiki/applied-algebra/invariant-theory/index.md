# Invariant theory

For a group $G$ acting linearly on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ over a field $k$, $G$ acts on the polynomial ring $k[V]$, and the invariant ring $k[V]^G$ is the subring of polynomials fixed by $G$.

## Finiteness theorems

- **Noether.** If $G$ is finite and $\operatorname{char} k \notdivides \size G$, then $k[V]^G$ is finitely generated, by invariants of degree at most $\size G$.
  The proof uses the Reynolds operator $f\mapsto {1\over\size G}\sum_{g\in G}g\cdot f$, the averaging used in the proof of Maschke's theorem.

- **Hilbert.** If $G$ is linearly reductive, then $k[V]^G$ is finitely generated; the proof uses the Hilbert basis theorem applied to the ideal of $k[V]$ generated by the invariants of positive degree.

## Computations

- **$S_n$ permuting the variables.** The invariants are the symmetric polynomials, and $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]^{S_n} = k[e_1, \ldots, e_n]$ with $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ algebraically independent; this is the fundamental theorem of [[applied-algebra/symmetric-functions/index|symmetric functions]].

- **$\ZZ/n$ acting by a root of unity.** If a generator acts by $x_i\mapsto\zeta x_i$ for every $i$, with $\zeta$ a primitive $n$th root of unity, the invariants are spanned by the monomials of total degree divisible by $n$.

- **Molien's formula.** For a finite group $G\subseteq\GL(V)$ over $\CC$,
  $$
  \sum_d \dim(\CC[V]^G_d)\, t^d = {1\over \size G}\sum_{g\in G} {1\over \det(1 - tg)},
  $$
  which computes the Hilbert series of the invariant ring, and hence the number of linearly independent invariants in each degree, without finding generators.

## The Reynolds operator

If $\size G$ is invertible in $k$, the Reynolds operator $\rho\colon k[V]\to k[V]^G$ is a projection onto $k[V]^G$ that is $k[V]^G$-linear: $\rho(fh) = f\rho(h)$ for $f\in k[V]^G$.
Hence $k[V]^G$ is a direct summand of $k[V]$ as a $k[V]^G$-module, which gives finite generation.
When $\operatorname{char}k$ divides $\size G$, the Reynolds operator is not defined, as in the failure of [[algebra/representations/maschke-and-schur|Maschke's theorem]] in that case.
