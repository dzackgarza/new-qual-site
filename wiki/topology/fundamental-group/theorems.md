# Theorems: algebraic topology

## General homotopies

::: {.fact title="Contractible factors of products"}
For any space $X$ and $n\geq 0$, the projection is a [[D-HFR32|homotopy equivalence]]
$$
X\cross \RR^n \homotopic X \cross \pt \cong X.
$$

:::

::: {.fact title="$\pi_0$ and $H_0$ detect path components"}
$\pi_0(X)$ is the set of path components of $X$, and $H_0(X;\ZZ)$ is the free abelian group on $\pi_0(X)$.
If $X$ has finitely many path components, then $\operatorname{rank}H_0(X;\ZZ)=\abs{\pi_0(X)}$.

:::

[[T-2YTCZ]]

::: {.proof}
Let $X\subseteq\RR^n$ be convex and $f, g\colon Z \to X$ continuous.
Define $H\colon Z \cross I \to X$ by $H(z,t) \coloneqq tf(z) + (1-t)g(z)$.
By convexity $H(z,t)\in X$ for all $z$ and $t$, and $H$ is continuous with $H(\wait,0)=g$ and $H(\wait,1)=f$.

:::

## Fundamental group

### Definition

[[D-EBNUE]]

::: {.remark}
Elements of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ are homotopy classes of loops at $x_0$ rel endpoints, and a continuous map $f\colon (X,x_0)\to(Y,y_0)$ induces a homomorphism $f_*\colon\pi_1(X,x_0)\to\pi_1(Y,y_0)$, $[\gamma]\mapsto[f\circ\gamma]$.

:::

### Change of basepoint

::: {.proposition title="Change of basepoint"}
Let $h$ be a path in $X$ from $x_0$ to $x_1$, with reverse path $\bar h$.
Then $\beta_h\colon \pi_1(X,x_1)\to\pi_1(X,x_0)$, $[f]\mapsto[h\cdot f\cdot\bar h]$, is an isomorphism with inverse $\beta_{\bar h}$, and it depends only on the homotopy class of $h$ rel endpoints.
If $h'$ is another such path, then $\beta_{h'} = c\circ\beta_h$, where $c$ is conjugation by $[h'\cdot\bar h]\in\pi_1(X,x_0)$.
In particular, for $x_1=x_0$, $\beta_h$ is conjugation by $[h]$, and the isomorphism $\pi_1(X,x_1)\cong\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is canonical when $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is abelian.

:::

See [@Hat02, Prop. 1.5] for the proof.

### Calculating $\pi_1$

[[PR-HQE2T]]

[[PR-FG4DK]]

[[T-BTPU4]]

::: {.proof title="Sketch"}
Let $\Phi\colon \pi_1(U_1,x_0)*\pi_1(U_2,x_0)\to\pi_1(X,x_0)$ be induced by the inclusions.

- $\Phi$ is surjective: for a loop $\gamma$ at $x_0$, a Lebesgue number for the cover $\ts{\gamma^{-1}(U_1),\gamma^{-1}(U_2)}$ of $I$ divides $\gamma$ into paths each lying in $U_1$ or $U_2$ with endpoints in $U_1\intersect U_2$. Joining each endpoint to $x_0$ by a path in $U_1\intersect U_2$ and inserting the path and its reverse writes $[\gamma]$ as a product of classes of loops in $U_1$ or $U_2$.
- The kernel of $\Phi$ is the normal subgroup generated by $i_1^*(\omega)\,i_2^*(\omega)^{-1}$ for $\omega\in\pi_1(U_1\intersect U_2,x_0)$: given a homotopy $I\times I\to X$ between two products of such loops, subdividing $I\times I$ into a grid of squares each mapped into $U_1$ or $U_2$ shows that the two words differ by these relations.

:::

::: {.example title="An amalgamated product"}
Let $A = \ZZ/4 = \gens{x \suchthat x^4}$, $B = \ZZ/6 = \gens{y \suchthat y^6}$, and $Z = \ZZ/2 = \gens{z \suchthat z^2}$, with injections $\iota_{A}(z) = x^2$ and $\iota_{B}(z) = y^3$.
Then
$$
A\ast_{Z} B = \gens{x, y \suchthat x^4, y^6, x^2y^{-3}}.
$$

:::

[[PR-3APOT]]

::: {.proof}
Assume the basepoints $x_0\in X$ and $y_0\in Y$ have open neighborhoods $U\ni x_0$ and $V\ni y_0$ that deformation retract onto them.
Apply van Kampen's theorem to the open sets $X\vee V$ and $U\vee Y$, which deformation retract onto $X$ and $Y$ and whose intersection $U\vee V$ deformation retracts onto the wedge point.
Since $\pi_1(U\vee V)=1$, the amalgamated product is the free product.

### Facts

::: {.fact}
For path-connected $X$, the Hurewicz map induces an isomorphism $\pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong H_1(X)$.

:::

[[PR-OO3DH]]

::: {.proof title="Sketch"}
\envlist

- A loop in $X \cross Y$ at $(x_0,y_0)$ is a map $\gamma\colon I \to X \cross Y$, $\gamma(t) = (f(t), g(t))$, and $\gamma$ is continuous in the product topology if and only if the loops $f$ and $g$ are continuous.
- Likewise a homotopy $F\colon I^2 \to X \cross Y$ of loops is a pair of homotopies of the component loops.
- So $[\gamma] \mapsto ([f], [g])$ is a well-defined bijection $\pi_1(X\cross Y)\to\pi_1(X)\cross\pi_1(Y)$, and it is a homomorphism because concatenation is computed componentwise.

:::

[[PR-EWJMJ]]

::: {.proof}
For a path-connected space $X$ with basepoint $x_0$, $X$ is simply connected when every loop at $x_0$ is homotopic rel endpoints to the constant loop, that is, when every element of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ equals the identity.
By change of basepoint, this does not depend on $x_0$.

:::

::: {.fact}
For a finite connected graph $G$ and a maximal tree $T\subseteq G$,
$$
\pi_1(G)\cong F_n,
\qquad
n=\abs{E(G)\sm E(T)}=1-\chi(G),
$$
where $F_n$ is the free group of rank $n$ on the edges not in $T$.
Collapsing $T$ gives a homotopy equivalence $G\homotopic\bigvee^n S^1$, so $H_1(G;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^n$.

:::

## General homotopy theory

[[T-SDEOY]]

::: {.example title="Isomorphic homotopy groups without a weak equivalence"}
$S^2 \cross \RP^3$ and $S^3 \cross \RP^2$ have universal cover $S^2\cross S^3$ and fundamental group $\ZZ/2$, so their homotopy groups are isomorphic in every degree.
They are not homotopy equivalent, since by the Künneth theorem $H_2(S^2 \cross \RP^3)\cong\ZZ$ while $H_2(S^3 \cross \RP^2)=0$.
Whitehead's theorem requires a single map inducing the isomorphisms.

:::

[[T-GL7E6]]

[[T-I2X3M]]

::: {.example title="Applications of cellular approximation"}
\envlist

- $\pi_k(S^n)=0$ for $k<n$: with the CW structure $e^0\union e^n$, a map $S^k\to S^n$ is homotopic to a cellular map, whose image lies in the $k$-skeleton, a point.
- For a CW complex $X$, every element of $\pi_n(X)$ has a representative with image in the $n$-skeleton $X^{(n)}$, and the inclusion $X^{(n+1)}\injects X$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_n$.

:::

[[T-HZUM7]]

::: {.fact title="Higher homotopy groups"}
\envlist

- For $i\geq 2$, $\pi_{i}(X)$ is abelian.
- If $X$ is simply connected and $n\geq 2$ is the least integer with $H_n(X)\neq 0$, then $\pi_k(X)=0$ for $k<n$ and $\pi_n(X)\cong H_n(X)$.
- Homotopy groups of a wedge are not in general the products of the homotopy groups: $\pi_2(S^1\vee S^2)\cong\ZZ[t,t\inv]$, the homology $H_2$ of the universal cover, a line with a sphere attached at each integer, while $\pi_2(S^1)\times\pi_2(S^2)\cong\ZZ$.
- For a covering map $\hat X \to X$, the induced maps $\pi_{i}(\hat X) \to \pi_{i}(X)$ are isomorphisms for $i\geq 2$.
- $\pi_{i}(S^n) = 0$ for $i < n$, and $\pi_{n}(S^n) \cong \ZZ$; higher groups need not vanish, for example $\pi_3(S^2)\cong\ZZ$.
- For $0\leq k<n$, $S^n / S^k \homotopic S^n \vee \Sigma S^{k}$, and $\Sigma S^k \cong S^{k+1}$.
- For path-connected spaces $X_\alpha$, the projections induce $\pi_k\qty{\prod_\alpha X_\alpha}\cong\prod_\alpha\pi_k(X_\alpha)$.
- A fibration $F\to E\to B$ with $B$ path connected induces a long exact sequence $\cdots\to\pi_k(F)\to\pi_k(E)\to\pi_k(B)\to\pi_{k-1}(F)\to\cdots$.
- For a presentation $\pi=\gens{S\suchthat R}$, the presentation complex obtained from $\bigvee_{s\in S}S^1$ by attaching one $2$-cell along each relation has fundamental group $\pi$.
  It need not be a $K(\pi,1)$: attaching cells of dimension at least $3$ to kill its higher homotopy groups produces a $K(\pi,1)$.

:::
