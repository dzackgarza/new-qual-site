On a smooth curve the local ring at every closed point is a discrete valuation ring, and the local ring at the generic point of a prime divisor $Y$ is a discrete valuation ring with normalized valuation $v_Y$, so every prime divisor is locally principal with local equation a uniformizer raised to the order of the divisor at the point ([[D-5PQ5W]], [[PR-DIVZEROPOLE]]).

## Computing a Picard group

[[FE-ADOKK]]

For an integral projective curve $X$ over an algebraically closed field with normalization $\tilde X$, the comparison with $\tilde X$ is the exact sequence
$$
0\to\bigoplus_{p\in X}\tilde\OO_p^\times/\OO_{X,p}^\times\to\Pic(X)\to\Pic(\tilde X)\to0 ,
$$
where $\tilde\OO_p$ is the integral closure of $\OO_{X,p}$ ([[FE-ADOKK]], [@Har77, Exercise II.6.9(a)]).
For an open subset $U = X \sm Z$ of a smooth variety, the comparison is the excision sequence
$$
\ZZ^{\theset{\text{components of } Z \text{ of codimension } 1}} \to \Cl(X) \to \Cl(U) \to 0 ,
$$
[@Har77, Proposition II.6.5]; for $X=\PP^n_k$ and $Z=V(f)$ an irreducible hypersurface of degree $d$, with $\Cl(\PP^n_k)\cong\ZZ$ generated by a hyperplane [@Har77, Proposition II.6.4], it gives $\Cl(\PP^n_k\sm V(f))\cong\ZZ/d$.
