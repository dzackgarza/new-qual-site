# Tangent, normal, and canonical sheaves

For a smooth variety $X$ of dimension $n$ over a field $k$ the sheaf of differentials $\Omega_{X/k}$ is locally free of rank $n$ [@Har77, Theorem II.8.15]; its dual is the tangent sheaf, and its determinant is the canonical sheaf $\omega_X=\bigwedge^n\Omega_{X/k}$.
For a closed immersion $i\colon Z\into X$ with ideal sheaf $\mci$ the conormal sheaf is $\mci/\mci^2$ and the normal sheaf is its dual; the conormal sequence relates $\mci/\mci^2$ to $i^*\Omega_X$ and $\Omega_Z$.

## Tangent and cotangent

[[D-4GCH6]]

The fibre of $\Omega_X$ at a closed point is the Zariski cotangent space there ([[D-0SYCY]]).
A derivation is $k$-linear and satisfies the Leibniz rule, so it is not $\OO_X$-linear; the same failure makes the de Rham differential a derivation rather than an $\OO_X$-linear map ([[FE-DERHAMNONLIN]]).

## The canonical sheaf

[[D-MODCONORM]]

On $\PP^n_k$ the Euler sequence
$$
0\to\Omega_{\PP^n}\to\OO_{\PP^n}(-1)^{\oplus(n+1)}\to\OO_{\PP^n}\to0
$$
has determinant $\OO_{\PP^n}(-n-1)$, so $K_{\PP^n}=\OO(-n-1)$ ([[T-MODEULER]]).

## Normal and conormal sheaves

For a closed immersion $i\colon Z\into X$ with ideal sheaf $\mci$ the conormal sequence
$$
\mci/\mci^2\longrightarrow i^*\Omega_X\longrightarrow\Omega_Z\longrightarrow0
$$
is exact [@Har77, Proposition II.8.12], and its first map is injective when $Z$ is nonsingular [@Har77, Theorem II.8.17].
In that case $\mci/\mci^2$ is locally free and is the conormal sheaf of [[D-MODCONORM]].

## Cohen--Macaulay, Gorenstein, and local complete intersections

[[D-SCHCMGOR]]

A Gorenstein scheme has an invertible dualizing sheaf, so a dualizing sheaf is a line bundle on a singular variety exactly in the Gorenstein case, and adjunction holds for divisors in a Gorenstein scheme ([[D-SCHCMGOR]]).
An effective divisor $D$ in a smooth variety $X$ has ideal sheaf locally generated by one element, a regular sequence of length $\operatorname{codim}(D,X)$ at every point, so $D$ is a local complete intersection [@Har77, Definition II.8.22], hence Gorenstein, and has an invertible canonical sheaf even when $D$ is singular.

## Adjunction

[[T-SRFADJ]]

For a nonsingular subvariety $Y$ of codimension $r$ in a nonsingular variety $X$,
$$
\omega_Y\cong\omega_X\otimes\bigwedge^r\mcn_{Y/X},
$$
and for $r=1$, with $Y$ a divisor and $\mcl$ its associated invertible sheaf,
$$
\omega_Y\cong\omega_X\otimes\mcl\otimes\OO_Y
$$
[@Har77, Proposition II.8.20].
Smooth divisors therefore satisfy $K_Y=\restrictionof{(K_X+Y)}{Y}$, which computes the canonical class of a smooth hypersurface of degree $d$ in $\PP^n$ as $\OO(d-n-1)$ ([[FE-VARADJ]]).
