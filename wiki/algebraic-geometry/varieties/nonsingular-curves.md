# Nonsingular curves

Dimension one is the case where every question about a variety becomes a question about a discrete valuation ring: the local ring at a point of a nonsingular curve is a DVR, its maximal ideal is generated by a local parameter, and a rational function has an integer order of vanishing there.
That description is complete enough to build the curve back out of its function field — the points are the DVRs — and it is what makes a rational map from a curve extend to a morphism, which in turn is what makes the smooth projective model of a function field unique.

## Discrete valuation rings

[[D-VK2KZ]]

[[D-HWLVG]]

A DVR is the coordinate ring of a curve at a smooth point, read algebraically: it is a local PID, its maximal ideal is principal, and the valuation that measures the order of vanishing is the valuation attached to it.
The valuation rings in general are what remains when the fraction field is not required to give values in $\ZZ$: a valuation ring of $K$ is a subring that contains $x$ or $x^{-1}$ for every $x\in K^\times$, and the DVRs are the ones whose value group is $\ZZ$ [@AM18, Chapter 5].
That containment property is the algebraic form of the valuative criterion, and it is why a point of an abstract curve can be described without ever exhibiting the curve.

## Local rings of a nonsingular curve

[[D-DEFREGLR]]

[[PR-DIVZEROPOLE]]

For a curve over a field, the local ring at a point $p$ is a noetherian local domain of dimension one, and it is regular, normal, and a DVR exactly on the nonsingular locus — so on a curve the three conditions coincide, and a normal curve is a nonsingular curve ([[algebraic-geometry/schemes/normalization|normalization]]).
The DVR gives a rational function an order at each point, the order is zero except at finitely many points, and the divisor of a rational function is the sum of its zeros minus its poles.
On a normal curve the converse holds too: a rational function with no poles is regular, so $\OO_X(X)$ can be read off the valuations, which is the statement that a normal noetherian scheme is determined in codimension one.

## The abstract nonsingular curve of a function field

[[T-CRVMINMOD]]

Given a finitely generated field extension $K/k$ of transcendence degree one, the curve is rebuilt rather than found: its points are the DVRs of $K$ containing $k$, its open sets are the complements of finite sets of points, and its structure sheaf assigns to an open set the intersection of the corresponding valuation rings inside $K$.
That is the abstract nonsingular curve of $K$, and it is a nonsingular curve whose function field is $K$; every projective model of $K$ has it as its normalization, so this construction is another description of the same object.
The equivalence in the card is the precise statement that no information is lost: smooth projective curves, dominant morphisms, and function fields with their $k$-algebra maps are three descriptions of one category.

## Extension of rational maps

A rational map from a nonsingular curve to a projective variety is a morphism, which is the single fact that makes the theory of curves behave: it is what forces birational smooth projective curves to be isomorphic, and it is what lets a rational function on a curve be evaluated at every point once its poles are known.
The proof is the valuative criterion at each point: the local ring $\OO_{C,p}$ is a DVR and therefore a valuation ring, so the map is defined wherever the criterion asks for a lift, and completeness of the target supplies it.
No analogue holds in higher dimension: the rational map $\AA^2\dashrightarrow\PP^1$, $(x_1,x_2)\mapsto\thevector{x_1:x_2}$, is undefined at the origin, and blowing up the origin is the standard repair.

## Genus and the canonical model

The invariants of a nonsingular projective curve are computable from this local data: the divisor $K_C$ of a differential has degree $2g-2$, and Riemann–Roch turns the orders of vanishing of sections into the dimension of a space of functions ([[algebraic-geometry/curves-and-surfaces/riemann-roch-for-curves|Riemann–Roch for curves]]).
An embedded curve is recovered from its sheaf of differentials: $\omega_C$ is very ample once $g\ge3$ away from the hyperelliptic case, and the canonical map is where the genus and the gonality become visible at once ([[T-D8TUX]]).
