# Examples

Every toric variety is a fan, and the way to learn the dictionary is to carry a few fans through it by hand: the torus itself, affine space, projective space, the Hirzebruch surfaces, the quadric cone, the cuspidal cubic, weighted projective space.
These are the examples the rest of the chapter tests statements against, and each of them was met earlier in the guide without its fan — this page is where the two descriptions are put side by side.

## The torus and affine space

[[D-Q7Q2N]]

The fan consisting of the single cone $\theset{0}$ is the torus $T=(\CC^\times)^n$ itself, and the fan of one maximal cone $\sigma$ is the affine variety $\Spec\CC[\dualof{\sigma}\cap M]$.
For $\sigma=\Cone(e_1,\ldots,e_n)\subseteq\RR^n$ the dual cone is the positive orthant, so the semigroup is generated by the coordinate functions and $X_\sigma=\CC^n$; replacing that one cone by the $n+1$ cones spanned by all but one of $e_1,\ldots,e_n,-e_1-\cdots-e_n$ produces $\PP^n$.
The two extremes are worth stating as a slogan: an affine toric variety is one cone, and a projective one is a complete fan whose cones are the maximal ones of a polytope's normal fan.

## Projective space

[[FE-TORP2]]

The three rays $e_1,e_2,-e_1-e_2$ close up into a complete fan, and the duals of the three maximal cones are the three standard affine charts with coordinates $(x,y)$, $(x^{-1},x^{-1}y)$ and $(y^{-1},xy^{-1})$ — the usual gluing of $\PP^2$ written as a fan.
Every consecutive pair has determinant $\pm1$, which is the smoothness criterion, and the support is all of $N_\RR$, which is the completeness criterion; both are checked by a determinant and a cone count rather than by patching.

## Hirzebruch surfaces

[[FE-TORHIRZ]]

For $a\ge0$ the four rays $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(-1,a)$, $(0,-1)$ give the Hirzebruch surface $\FF_a$, smooth and complete for every $a$ by the same two determinant checks.
The relations among the ray generators compute the intersection numbers: $D_2^2=-a$ and $D_4^2=a$, so $a=0$ gives $\PP^1\times\PP^1$ and $a=1$ gives $\PP^2$ blown up at a point ([[FE-TORBLOW]]).
Every $a$ gives four rays and four maximal cones, so the combinatorics of the fan does not determine the surface: it is the lattice embedding that does, and $\FF_a\cong\FF_b$ exactly when $a=b$.

## The quadric cone

[[FE-TORDUAL]]

The cone on $(2,1)$ and $(1,4)$ is a two-dimensional cone whose dual is generated by the facets' primitive normal vectors, and the semigroup computation produces an explicit equation for $X_\sigma$ in $\AA^3$ — the cone on a conic.
It is one cone and it is not smooth, because the determinant of its two ray generators is $2\cdot4-1\cdot1=7$, so the variety is a cyclic quotient singularity of index $7$; its minimal resolution is a star subdivision by rays at lattice distance one ([[FE-TORMINRES]]).

## The cuspidal cubic

The cuspidal cubic $V(x^3-y^2)$ contains the torus $\theset{(t^2,t^3)}$, so it is a toric variety for the semigroup $\generators{2,3}\subseteq\NN$ — except that this semigroup is not saturated, and a toric variety in the sense of the dictionary is normal.
What one gets is a non-normal variety with a torus action whose normalization is the affine line: the fan of the semigroup $\generators{2,3}$ is the cone $\Cone(2,3)$, its saturation is the cone $\Cone(1)$, and the normalization $\AA^1\to V(x^3-y^2)$ is the inclusion of the saturated semigroup ([[D-FULSAT]]).
So the dictionary "fans give normal varieties" has content, and this is the example that shows what dropping normality costs.

## Weighted projective space

[[FE-TORWPS]]

Weighted projective space $\PP(q_0,\ldots,q_n)$ is the quotient lattice construction: $N=\ZZ^{n+1}/\ZZ(q_0,\ldots,q_n)$ and the fan has rays the images of the standard basis vectors, which makes the weighted scaling relation part of the lattice rather than part of the grading.
Its singularities are cyclic quotient singularities at the points where a ray is not primitive, which is the same computation as the cone over the rational normal curve ([[FE-FULQUOT]]), and $\PP(1,\ldots,1)$ recovers $\PP^n$.

## Products and blowups

[[FE-TORBLOW]]

A product of toric varieties has the product fan: the cones are products of cones, in the lattice $N_1\oplus N_2$, which is why $\PP^1\times\PP^1$ is the fan with four rays.
A blowup at a torus-fixed point is a star subdivision of the fan at that cone's ray, and the exceptional divisor is the new ray's orbit closure; iterating gives every toric surface from $\PP^2$ by blowing up fixed points, and the intersection numbers follow from the ray relations alone.
The general statement is that toric morphisms are maps of fans, so the entire geometry of these examples is combinatorial ([[algebraic-geometry/toric/surfaces-and-morphisms|toric surfaces and toric morphisms]]).
