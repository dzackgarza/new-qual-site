# Special divisors and the canonical map

A divisor $D$ on a smooth projective curve $C$ of genus $g$ is special when $h^1(C,\OO_C(D))>0$, and Riemann--Roch reads that condition as $\ell(D)>\deg D+1-g$: special divisors carry more sections than their degree forces ([[D-CRVSPEC]]).

## The canonical map

For a nonhyperelliptic curve $C$ of genus $g\ge3$ the canonical linear system $\linearsystem{K_C}$ embeds $C$ in $\PP^{g-1}$, and the image is the canonical model of $C$, of degree $2g-2$ [@Har77, Proposition IV.5.2].
A hyperelliptic curve of genus $g\ge2$ has a unique $g^1_2$, and its pairs are exactly the ones that the canonical system fails to separate: the canonical morphism is the degree-two map to $\PP^1$ followed by the $(g-1)$-uple embedding ([@Har77, Proposition IV.5.3], [[D-CRVHYP]]).

The gonality $\operatorname{gon}(C)$ is the least degree of a morphism $C\to\PP^1$, so a $g^1_d$ exists exactly when $\operatorname{gon}(C)\le d$; a hyperelliptic curve is the case $\operatorname{gon}(C)=2$ ([[D-CRVGON]], [[D-CRVHYP]]).

## Low-genus canonical models

[[FE-CRVLOWG]]

For a nonhyperelliptic curve of genus $3$ the canonical model is a plane quartic, and for genus $4$ it is a curve of degree $6$ in $\PP^3$ lying on a unique irreducible quadric surface [@Har77, Examples IV.5.2.1 and IV.5.2.2] ([[FE-CRVLOWG]]).
