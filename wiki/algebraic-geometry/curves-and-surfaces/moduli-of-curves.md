# Moduli of curves

The coarse moduli space in genus $0$ is a point, while in genus $1$ the $j$-invariant identifies the coarse moduli space with $\AA^1$.
From genus $2$ on, classification is naturally expressed through moduli spaces rather than a finite list.

[[D-CRVMOD]]

Coarse asks only that a family of curves produce a map; fine asks that a map *be* a family.
Automorphisms obstruct a coarse moduli space from carrying a universal family; the twisted family gives an explicit example of that obstruction.

Genus $1$ is the case where the space is small enough to see: on [[algebraic-geometry/curves-and-surfaces/elliptic-curves|Elliptic curves]] the $j$-invariant is the coordinate, $\AA^1$ is the coarse space, and the failure to be fine is already visible there.

## The dimension count

[[T-CRVMG]]

The dimension is $3g-3$, visible from the deformation count $h^1(C, T_C) = h^0(\omega_C^{\otimes 2}) = 3g-3$, from hyperelliptic branch points in genus $2$, and from plane quartics in genus $3$.
In the latter two descriptions the relevant coordinate changes are $\PGL_2$ on the branch points and $\PGL_3$ on the plane.

## Why automorphisms keep appearing

[[PR-CRVAUT]]

This is the card both halves of the page lean on.
Finiteness of $\Aut C$ is what makes the orbits in the quartic count $8$-dimensional, so the dimension comes out; nontriviality of $\Aut C$ on the hyperelliptic locus is what keeps any genus from having a fine moduli space.
