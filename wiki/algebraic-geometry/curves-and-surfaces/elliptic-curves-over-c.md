# Elliptic curves over $\CC$

Over $\CC$ an elliptic curve is a complex torus: the quotient of $\CC$ by a lattice, with the group law read off from addition of complex numbers ([[T-CRVUNIF]]).
The passage between the two descriptions is the Weierstrass $\wp$-function, which maps the torus isomorphically onto the plane cubic $y^2z=4x^3-g_2xz^2-g_3z^3$ and carries the group structure with it.
Two consequences organise the theory over $\CC$: isomorphism classes of elliptic curves correspond to lattices up to homothety, hence to the quotient $\HH/\SL_2(\ZZ)$, whose coordinate is the modular function $J$; and the endomorphism ring of $\CC/\Lambda$ is the set of complex numbers preserving $\Lambda$, so it is larger than $\ZZ$ exactly for the imaginary quadratic lattices ([[T-CRVMODJ]], [[T-CRVCM]]).

## Weierstrass functions

[[D-CRVWEIER]]

An elliptic function for $\Lambda$ is a meromorphic function on $\CC/\Lambda$, and the field of such functions is generated by $\wp$ and $\wp'$, where
$$
\wp(z)=\frac{1}{z^2}+\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda\smz}\left(\frac{1}{(z-\omega)^2}-\frac{1}{\omega^2}\right)
$$
converges locally uniformly and is elliptic with a double pole at each lattice point and no other poles ([[D-CRVWEIER]]).
$\wp$ is even and $\wp'$ is odd, the two satisfy the differential equation $\wp'^2=4\wp^3-g_2\wp-g_3$ with the Eisenstein series
$$
g_2=60\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda\smz}\frac{1}{\omega^4},\qquad g_3=140\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda\smz}\frac{1}{\omega^6},
$$
and $\Delta=g_2^3-27g_3^2$ is nonzero for every lattice ([[D-CRVWEIER]]).
The $\Delta\neq0$ is what makes the associated plane cubic nonsingular.

## Uniformisation

[[T-CRVUNIF]]

For a lattice $\Lambda$ put $E_\Lambda=V(y^2z=4x^3-g_2xz^2-g_3z^3)\subseteq\PP^2_\CC$.
The map $\phi\colon\CC/\Lambda\to E_\Lambda$ sending $z$ to $(\wp(z):\wp'(z):1)$ and $0$ to $(0:1:0)$ is an isomorphism of Riemann surfaces and a group isomorphism, and conversely every elliptic curve over $\CC$ is isomorphic to some $E_\Lambda$ ([[T-CRVUNIF]]).
Injectivity is the pair of facts that $\wp$ has degree $2$ and is even, so $\wp(z)=\wp(z')$ forces $z'=\pm z$, while $\wp'$ distinguishes the two signs away from the branch points; compactness then makes the bijection an isomorphism of Riemann surfaces, and GAGA makes it algebraic ([[T-CRVUNIF]], [[T-GAGA]]).

Abel's theorem on the torus is the statement that a divisor $\sum n_i(a_i)$ is the divisor of an elliptic function exactly when $\sum n_i=0$ and $\sum n_ia_i=0$ in $\CC/\Lambda$ ([[T-CRVUNIF]]).
The second condition is genus-one Abel--Jacobi, and applying the criterion to the divisor cut by a line gives the chord-and-tangent law: three points of $E_\Lambda$ are collinear exactly when their sum is zero ([[T-CRVUNIF]]).
A further immediate consequence is that the $n$-torsion is $E[n]\cong\frac1n\Lambda/\Lambda\cong(\ZZ/n)^2$, so over $\CC$ the full torsion subgroup is $(\QQ/\ZZ)^2$.

## Lattices up to homothety

[[T-CRVMODJ]]

Every lattice is homothetic to one of the form $\Lambda_\tau=\generators{1,\tau}_\ZZ$ with $\tau\in\HH$, and scaling $\Lambda\mapsto\alpha\Lambda$ sends $(g_2,g_3)$ to $(\alpha^{-4}g_2,\alpha^{-6}g_3)$, so the two Eisenstein coefficients scale by weights $4$ and $6$ and $J=g_2^3/\Delta$ is invariant ([[T-CRVMODJ]]).
With the normalization of [[T-CRVJINV]] the modular function is $j(E_{\Lambda_\tau})=1728\,J(\tau)$, and
$$
J(\tau)=J(\tau')\iff \tau'=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\ \text{ for some } \begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\SL_2(\ZZ),
$$
with every orbit meeting the fundamental region $\mathcal F=\theset{\tau\in\HH\st-\tfrac12\le\Re\tau<\tfrac12,\ \abs\tau\ge1}$, uniquely ([[T-CRVMODJ]]).
So $J\colon\HH/\SL_2(\ZZ)\to\CC$ is a bijection, and combined with the classification by $j$ it says that elliptic curves over $\CC$ up to isomorphism are the points of $\HH/\SL_2(\ZZ)$: the analytic and the algebraic moduli agree ([[T-CRVJINV]], [[D-CRVMOD]]).

## Complex multiplication

[[T-CRVCM]]

The endomorphism ring of $E=\CC/\Lambda$ fixing the origin is the ring of complex numbers preserving the lattice,
$$
\Endo(E,p_0)\cong R_\Lambda=\theset{\alpha\in\CC\st\alpha\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda},
$$
with multiplication by $\alpha$ corresponding to $\alpha$ and $[n]$ to $n\in\ZZ$ ([[T-CRVCM]]).
So $E$ has complex multiplication exactly when $R_\Lambda\supsetneq\ZZ$, which happens exactly when $\tau$ is imaginary quadratic; in that case $R_{\Lambda_\tau}\subseteq\OO_K$ is an order in the ring of integers of $K=\QQ(\tau)$, of index $f$ with discriminant $f^2d_K$ ([[T-CRVCM]]).

The three standard lattices separate the phenomena.
For $\tau=i$ the ring is $\ZZ[i]=\OO_K$ with unit group of order $4$, and the curve is $y^2=x^3-ax$ with $j=1728$.
For $\tau=\rho=e^{2\pi i/3}$ the ring is $\ZZ[\rho]=\OO_K$ with unit group of order $6$, and the curve is $y^2=x^3-b$ with $j=0$.
For $\tau=2i$ the ring is $\ZZ+2\ZZ[i]$, an order of index $2$ in $\ZZ[i]$, whose unit group is $\theset{\pm1}$, so this curve has only the two automorphisms every elliptic curve has and $j(2i)\neq0,1728$ ([[T-CRVCM]]).

The general curve therefore has $\Endo(E,p_0)=\ZZ$: the set of imaginary quadratic $\tau$ is countable inside $\HH$, while the isomorphism classes over $\CC$ are the points of $\CC$ ([[T-CRVMODJ]], [[T-CRVCM]], [[D-CRVMOD]]).
