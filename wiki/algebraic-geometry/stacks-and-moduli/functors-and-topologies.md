# Functors and topologies

A scheme is studied through the functor it represents on test schemes, and once the objects are functors the topology can be changed: a covering family need not consist of open immersions, and the sheaf condition is stated for a Grothendieck topology rather than for a topological space (Stacks Project, Definition 7.47.6, tag 00Z4).
Changing the topology changes which functors are sheaves, and that is the whole content of the étale and fppf topologies: for $n$ invertible on $S$ the Kummer sequence is exact on the étale site and not on the Zariski site, and a quasicoherent sheaf is recovered from its restriction to a faithfully flat cover by descent data.

## Functors of points

[[PR-SCHFOP]]

The functor of points of $X$ over $S$ sends a test scheme $T$ to the set $X(T)=\Hom_S(T,X)$ of $T$-valued points, and Yoneda's lemma makes $X\mapsto X(-)$ a fully faithful embedding [@Vak25, §1.2; @Vak25, Definition 7.3.10] ([[PR-SCHFOP]]).
Full faithfulness is what lets a scheme be proved isomorphic by producing a natural isomorphism of functors, and it is used in two ways: to represent a functor is to exhibit a scheme whose functor is the given one, and to identify two constructions of a scheme it suffices to identify their points on every test scheme.

Two test schemes carry most of the information in practice.
A morphism $\Spec L\to X$ for a field $L$ is a point $x\in X$ together with a field embedding $\kappa(x)\injects L$, so for $X$ a variety over an algebraically closed $k$ the $k$-points are the closed points.
A $k$-morphism $\Spec k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\to X$ is a $k$-rational point together with a tangent vector in $T_xX\cong\Hom_k(\mfm_x/\mfm_x^2,k)$, and replacing the dual numbers by $k[t]/(t^{n+1})$ gives the $n$-jets at $x$ ([[PR-SCHFOP]]).

## Representing the standard functors

[[FE-SCHREPFUNCTORS]]

Affine space represents the simplest functor: $\AA^n_R$ represents $X\mapsto\Gamma(X,\OO_X)^n$, with universal element the $n$-tuple of coordinates, and punctured affine space $\AA^n_R\sm\theset{0}$ represents the unimodular $n$-tuples, those generating the unit ideal sheaf, a condition that is checked at every point and so is not the condition that no entry be zero ([[FE-SCHREPFUNCTORS]]).

Projective space represents the functor of tuples $(\mcl,s_0,\ldots,s_n)$ consisting of an invertible sheaf $\mcl$ on $X$ and generating sections $s_0,\ldots,s_n\in\Gamma(X,\mcl)$, taken up to isomorphism of $\mcl$ carrying each $s_i$ to $s_i'$ [@Har77, Theorem II.7.1] ([[FE-SCHREPFUNCTORS]]).
Over an affine test scheme with trivial Picard group the tuple reduces to a unimodular $(n+1)$-tuple of sections modulo units, which is the classical description of $\PP^n(k)$, and the universal tuple is $(\OO(1),x_0,\ldots,x_n)$.

## Open subfunctors and gluing

[[D-OPENSUBFUNCTOR]]

A natural transformation $G\to F$ is an open subfunctor when every map $h_X\to F$ pulls it back to an open immersion of schemes, and a family of open subfunctors covering $F$ in that sense glues: a Zariski sheaf with an open cover by representable open subfunctors is itself represented by a scheme ([[D-OPENSUBFUNCTOR]]).
This is the mechanism behind the standard construction of $\PP^n$, whose open subfunctors $G_i$ of tuples in which $s_i$ generates are each represented by $\AA^n$ and cover because generating sections have no common zero; the resulting scheme is $n+1$ copies of $\AA^n$ glued along the standard tori ([[D-OPENSUBFUNCTOR]], [[FE-SCHREPFUNCTORS]]).

## Grothendieck topologies

[[D-GROTHTOP]]

A Grothendieck topology assigns to each object a collection of covering sieves, closed under pullback, containing the maximal sieve, and satisfying the transitivity condition that a sieve becomes covering when it is pulled back to a cover of a cover; a sheaf is a presheaf whose sections over $U$ are the natural transformations from the covering sieve, which for a topology generated by a pretopology is the equalizer condition $\mcf(U)\to\prod\mcf(U_i)\rightrightarrows\prod\mcf(U_i\times_UU_j)$ ([[D-GROTHTOP]]).
The open subsets of a topological space with the usual coverings form a site whose sheaves are the sheaves in the usual sense, so the definition is a strict generalization rather than an analogy ([[D-GROTHTOP]]).

## The étale and fppf topologies

[[D-ETFPPF]]

On $\Sch_{/S}$ the étale coverings are the jointly surjective families of étale morphisms, and the fppf coverings are the jointly surjective families that are flat and locally of finite presentation (Stacks Project, Definition 34.7.1, tag 021M) ([[D-ETFPPF]]).
Every Zariski covering is an étale covering, since an open immersion is étale (Stacks Project, Lemma 29.37.9, tag 02GP), and every étale covering is an fppf covering, because étale morphisms are flat and locally of finite presentation; the étale case is the one Stacks states, jointly with the smooth and Zariski cases, as the lemma that any smooth, étale or Zariski covering is an fppf covering (Stacks Project, Lemma 34.7.2, tag 021N).
Hence the implications run one way only: an fppf sheaf is an étale sheaf, and an étale sheaf restricts to a Zariski sheaf on every $S$-scheme ([[D-ETFPPF]]).

Every representable functor is an fppf sheaf, so every scheme defines sheaves on all three sites, and so does every quasicoherent module via $U\mapsto\Gamma(U,f^*\mcl)$ ([[D-ETFPPF]]).
The difference between the topologies is visible on a group scheme: for $n$ invertible on $S$ the Kummer sequence $1\to\mu_n\to\GG_m\to\GG_m\to1$ is exact on $S_{\mathrm{et}}$, because a unit acquires an $n$-th root after the étale localization $\OO_U[z]/(z^n-u)$, and it is not exact for the Zariski topology, since $t$ is not a square on any nonempty Zariski open subset of $\Spec\CC[t,t^{-1}]$ ([[D-ETFPPF]]).

## Descent

[[T-FFDESCENT]]

For a faithfully flat ring map $A\to B$ the Amitsur complex of every $A$-module is exact, so $M\mapsto M\tensor_AB$ loses nothing, and the extra structure needed to recover $M$ is exactly a descent datum: an isomorphism of $B\tensor_AB$-modules
$$
\theta\colon N\tensor_AB\longrightarrow B\tensor_AN .
$$
The two tensor orders are the two pullbacks of the $B$-module $N$ to $B\tensor_AB$, under the two structure maps $p_1(b)=b\tensor1$ and $p_2(b)=1\tensor b$: $p_1^*N=N\tensor_AB$ is a $B\tensor_AB$-module with $(b_1\tensor b_2)(n\tensor b)=b_1n\tensor b_2b$, and $p_2^*N=B\tensor_AN$ with $(b_1\tensor b_2)(b\tensor n)=b_1b\tensor b_2n$, so $\theta$ goes from $p_1^*N$ to $p_2^*N$ and not the other way.
Its pullbacks to $B\tensor_AB\tensor_AB$ are
$$
\theta_{01}=\theta\tensor\id_B\colon N\tensor_AB\tensor_AB\longrightarrow B\tensor_AN\tensor_AB,
\qquad
\theta_{12}=\id_B\tensor\theta\colon B\tensor_AN\tensor_AB\longrightarrow B\tensor_AB\tensor_AN,
$$
the first along $b\tensor b'\mapsto b\tensor b'\tensor1$ and the second along $b\tensor b'\mapsto1\tensor b\tensor b'$, so that $\theta_{12}\circ\theta_{01}$ is defined and runs from $N\tensor_AB\tensor_AB$ to $B\tensor_AB\tensor_AN$; the cocycle identity is $\theta_{02}=\theta_{12}\circ\theta_{01}$, where $\theta_{02}$ is the third pullback, along the middle insertion $b\tensor b'\mapsto b\tensor1\tensor b'$, determined by $\theta_{02}(n\tensor1\tensor1)=\sum_ia_i\tensor1\tensor n_i$ when $\theta(n\tensor1)=\sum_ia_i\tensor n_i$ (Stacks Project, Definition 35.3.1, tag 023G).
A datum written in the opposite tensor order is the inverse, $\psi=\theta^{-1}\colon p_2^*N=B\tensor_AN\to N\tensor_AB=p_1^*N$.
The two conventions are compared through two explicit maps: the flip $\tau\colon B\tensor_AB\to B\tensor_AB$, $b\tensor b'\mapsto b'\tensor b$, which is a ring isomorphism with $\tau p_1=p_2$ and $\tau p_2=p_1$, and the swap $s\colon N\tensor_AB\to B\tensor_AN$, $n\tensor b\mapsto b\tensor n$.
The swap satisfies $s(c\cdot x)=\tau(c)\cdot s(x)$ for $c\in B\tensor_AB$ and $x\in N\tensor_AB$, so it is an isomorphism $\tau^*(N\tensor_AB)\to B\tensor_AN$ of $B\tensor_AB$-modules; for the untwisted actions it is only $B$-linear, and the twist by $\tau$ is what makes it compare the two conventions.
The pullbacks of $\psi$ are the inverses of those of $\theta$ along the same three insertions: $\psi_{01}=\theta_{01}^{-1}\colon B\tensor_AN\tensor_AB\to N\tensor_AB\tensor_AB$, $\psi_{12}=\theta_{12}^{-1}\colon B\tensor_AB\tensor_AN\to B\tensor_AN\tensor_AB$, and $\psi_{02}=\theta_{02}^{-1}\colon B\tensor_AB\tensor_AN\to N\tensor_AB\tensor_AB$.
Inverting $\theta_{02}=\theta_{12}\circ\theta_{01}$ gives $\psi_{02}=\psi_{01}\circ\psi_{12}$, and this is the only order in which the composite is defined, since $\psi_{12}$ ends in $B\tensor_AN\tensor_AB$, where $\psi_{01}$ begins, while $\psi_{12}\circ\psi_{01}$ has no common intermediate object.
The canonical datum on $M\tensor_AB$ is
$$
\operatorname{can}\colon (M\tensor_AB)\tensor_AB\longrightarrow B\tensor_A(M\tensor_AB),
\qquad
(m\tensor b)\tensor b'\longmapsto b\tensor(m\tensor b'),
$$
which is $B\tensor_AB$-linear for those two actions: $(b_1\tensor b_2)$ sends $(m\tensor b)\tensor b'$ to $(m\tensor b_1b)\tensor(b_2b')$, and the right-hand side $b\tensor(m\tensor b')$ to $b_1b\tensor(m\tensor b_2b')$, the same as the image of $(m\tensor b_1b)\tensor(b_2b')$ (Stacks Project, Section 35.3, tag 023F).
The modules with descent data are equivalent to the $A$-modules, a quasi-inverse sending $(N,\theta)$ to the submodule $\theset{n\in N:\theta(n\tensor1)=1\tensor n}=\ker(n\mapsto1\tensor n-\theta(n\tensor1))$ of $N$, both sides lying in $B\tensor_AN$ with the $1$ in the first factor (Stacks Project, Proposition 35.3.9, tag 023N, and Lemma 35.3.7, tag 039W).
Replacing modules by quasicoherent sheaves and $\Spec B\to\Spec A$ by a faithfully flat quasicompact morphism gives fpqc descent of quasicoherent sheaves, and representable functors are fpqc sheaves, hence in particular fppf sheaves ([[T-FFDESCENT]], [[D-ETFPPF]]).

## Torsors

[[D-TORSOR]]

A torsor under a sheaf of groups $\mathcal G$ is a sheaf on which $\mathcal G$ acts freely and transitively after a covering, and the action being free and transitive is expressed by the map $\mathcal P\times\mathcal G\to\mathcal P\times\mathcal P$, $(p,g)\mapsto(p,pg)$, being an isomorphism ([[D-TORSOR]]).
A torsor is trivial exactly when it has a section over the base, every morphism of torsors is an isomorphism, and isomorphism classes of torsors are the Čech cohomology set $\check H^1$, which for abelian $\mathcal G$ is $H^1(X,\mathcal G)$ ([[D-TORSOR]]).

The two standard computations follow.
On the Zariski site a $\GG_m$-torsor is the sheaf of trivializations of an invertible sheaf, so $\Pic X\cong H^1(X,\OO_X^\times)$, and a $\GL_n$-torsor is the sheaf of local frames of a locally free sheaf of rank $n$ ([[D-TORSOR]]).
On the étale site of $\Spec k$ for $n$ invertible in $k$, the Kummer sequence together with Hilbert's theorem 90, $H^1((\Spec k)_{\mathrm{et}},\GG_m)=0$, gives $H^1((\Spec k)_{\mathrm{et}},\mu_n)\cong k^\times/(k^\times)^n$, the torsor of $a$ being $\Spec k[z]/(z^n-a)$ with $\mu_n$ acting by $z\mapsto\zeta z$ ([[D-TORSOR]], [[D-ETFPPF]]).
