# Hilbert schemes, algebraic spaces and stacks

A moduli problem is a functor, and the first question about it is whether a scheme represents it; when none does, the representability is weakened in three steps — a scheme, then an algebraic space, then a stack — and each step keeps more of the functor's data than the last.

## A representable moduli functor

[[D-HILBSCHEME]]

The Hilbert functor of a projective scheme is representable by a scheme, the Hilbert scheme, so flat families of closed subschemes with fixed Hilbert polynomial form a scheme up to canonical isomorphism ([[D-HILBSCHEME]]).

## Quotients by étale equivalence relations

[[D-ALGSPACE]]

An algebraic space is a sheaf for the étale topology that admits an étale surjection from a scheme whose two projections give an étale equivalence relation, and the category of algebraic spaces is closed under the fibre-product constructions the category of schemes has ([[D-ALGSPACE]]).

## Stacks

Objects with nontrivial automorphisms, such as hyperelliptic curves of genus $g\geq2$, obstruct the representability of a moduli functor by a scheme or algebraic space ([[D-CRVMOD]]); a stack records the automorphisms as part of the data ([[D-ALGSTACK]]).

[[D-ALGSTACK]]

A stack is a category fibred in groupoids over the site satisfying descent, and an algebraic stack is one admitting a smooth surjection from a scheme; the automorphism groups of the objects are then part of the structure rather than an obstruction to it ([[D-ALGSTACK]]).
