# Cohomology in families

Relative cohomology: one sheaf on the base recording the cohomology of every fibre at once.

[[D-COHRIF]]

[[T-COHLERAY]]

The spectral sequence is stated more often than it is used, and what is actually wanted is the degenerate case: for an affine morphism the higher direct images vanish and cohomology can be computed downstairs.
Pushing a coherent sheaf forward along a closed immersion into $\PP^n$ is that case, and it is why every projective computation reduces to projective space.

Formal functions is the technical engine and semicontinuity is what gets used.
The shape to memorise is a hierarchy of stability: $\chi$ is locally constant, each $h^i$ can jump up on closed subsets, and cohomology commutes with restriction to a fibre exactly when the relevant $h^i$ does not jump.

Flatness is load-bearing in all of it, and reducedness of the base is what Grauert's form needs.
Asked for an example where cohomology fails to commute with base change, give a family of degree-zero line bundles on a fixed curve: $h^0$ jumps from $0$ to $1$ at the trivial bundle, and $\chi = 1-g$ throughout.
