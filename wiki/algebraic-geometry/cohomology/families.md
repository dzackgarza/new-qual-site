# Cohomology in families

Relative cohomology: one sheaf on the base recording the cohomology of every fibre at once.

[[D-COHRIF]]

[[T-COHLERAY]]

For an affine morphism the higher direct images vanish, so the Leray spectral sequence degenerates and cohomology can be computed downstairs.
Pushing a coherent sheaf forward along a closed immersion into $\PP^n$ is that case, and it is why every projective computation reduces to projective space.

## What the fibres determine

Formal functions and semicontinuity give a hierarchy of stability: $\chi$ is locally constant, each $h^i$ can jump up on closed subsets, and cohomology commutes with restriction to a fibre exactly when the relevant $h^i$ does not jump.

Flatness is essential in these statements, and Grauert's form also uses reducedness of the base.
A family of degree-zero line bundles on a fixed curve gives a standard failure of cohomology commuting with base change: $h^0$ jumps from $0$ to $1$ at the trivial bundle, while $\chi = 1-g$ remains constant.
