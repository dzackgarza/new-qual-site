# Resources

## Hartshorne

[@Har77] covers classical varieties, schemes, cohomology, curves, and surfaces, with exercises throughout.
Chapter I treats affine and projective varieties, morphisms, rational maps, nonsingular varieties, nonsingular curves, and intersections in projective space.
II.1--II.8 treat sheaves, schemes and their first properties, separated and proper morphisms, sheaves of modules, divisors, projective morphisms, and differentials.
III.1--III.6 treat derived functors, sheaf cohomology, cohomology of noetherian affine schemes, Čech cohomology, the cohomology of projective space, and Ext; III.7 proves Serre duality and III.9 treats flat morphisms.
IV.1--IV.3 treat the Riemann--Roch theorem, Hurwitz's theorem, and embeddings of curves in projective space.

## Introductory and supplementary texts

[@Gat21] develops affine and projective varieties, schemes, sheaves and their cohomology in one set of lecture notes; Chapter 11 treats the 27 lines on a smooth cubic surface.

[@Vak25] treats sheaves, $\Proj$, and affine-local arguments in more detail than [@Har77], with exercises and hints throughout.

[@Mil08a] treats dimension, tangent spaces, smoothness, and finiteness results in the classical setting, without the scheme apparatus.

## Classical varieties and geometric examples

[@Har92] collects concrete projective examples including rational normal curves, Veronese and Segre embeddings, determinantal varieties, secant and tangent varieties, and Grassmannians.

[@Sha13] treats varieties in projective space: basic notions, local properties such as tangent spaces and singular points, divisors and differential forms, and intersection numbers.

[@Mum94] develops the geometry of $\Spec$, including generic points, examples such as $\Spec \ZZ[x]$, and nilpotent structure.
[@EH00] includes worked $\Spec$ and $\Proj$ computations and further geometric examples.

## Commutative algebra

[@AM18] covers localization, integral dependence, the Nullstellensatz, dimension theory, and completion.

[@Eis95] covers regular sequences, depth, Cohen--Macaulay and regular rings, flatness, and the Koszul complex.

## By topic

For toric varieties, [@Ful93] develops divisors, cohomology, and resolution of singularities from fan combinatorics.

For divisors and positivity, [@Laz04] treats ample, very ample, nef, and big divisors and the standard examples separating these notions.

For birational geometry --- minimal models, the cone theorem, and singularities of pairs --- [@KM98] treats these topics in Chapters 1 and 2.

The minimal model programme is introduced in [Kawamata, Matsuda and Matsuki, *Introduction to the Minimal Model Problem*](assets/algebraic-geometry/resources/Kawamata%20Intro%20MMP.pdf).

The coherent-sheaf formalism used in Chapter III of [@Har77] is developed in [@Ser55].

## Problems and solutions

[@Har07] collects sample questions from past qualifying examinations, arranged approximately by section, with the examiner named where the document records one; follow-up questions appear as separate entries.

[@Vak07] collects the Math 216 problem sets of 2005–2006, arranged in the order the course reached the material rather than by difficulty, with the problems marked "+" possibly harder or more fundamental.
[@Bor12] lists Hartshorne exercises week by week as suggested homework (the course page notes that there is no grader) and links TeX solutions to exercises in Chapter I, §§1–7, and in Chapter IV, §§1 and 3.
[@PA12] is the exercise-solution set for the BMS basic course in algebraic geometry.

### Foundational texts

- [Grothendieck, *Fondements de la géométrie algébrique* (FGA)](https://web.archive.org/web/20121109150626/http://www.math.jussieu.fr/~leila/grothendieckcircle/FGA.pdf): the collected Bourbaki exposés.

- [Grothendieck and Dieudonné, *Éléments de géométrie algébrique* (EGA), II](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1961__8__5_0/): Définition 5.5.2 states projectivity, Proposition 5.5.1 its equivalent form as a closed immersion into a projective bundle, and Théorème 5.6.1 is Chow's lemma.

- [*Séminaire de géométrie algébrique du Bois Marie* (SGA)](https://web.archive.org/web/20100909013636/http://modular.fas.harvard.edu:80/sga/sga/): the seminar volumes, listed by number.

- [Serre, *Géométrie algébrique et géométrie analytique* (GAGA)](https://www.numdam.org/item?id=AIF_1956__6__1_0): the comparison of coherent algebraic and analytic sheaves on a projective scheme over $\CC$, with the isomorphisms on cohomology, which Hartshorne states as Theorem 2.1 of Appendix B [@Har77, Appendix B, Theorem 2.1].

### Stacks and moduli

- [Edidin, "What is a stack?"; Internet Archive copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20250320052835/https://www.ams.org/notices/200304/what-is.pdf): an introduction to stacks written for the Notices of the AMS.

- [Edidin, "Equivariant geometry and the cohomology of the moduli space of curves"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.2364): the integral cohomology of a stack, its equivariant description for quotient stacks, and the rational comparison with the cohomology of the coarse moduli space, for smooth and stable curves.

- [Edidin, "Notes on the construction of the moduli space of curves"](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9805101): the moduli problem for curves, the stack of stable curves as a smooth Deligne--Mumford stack proper over $\Spec \ZZ$, and the use of geometric invariants in constructing the moduli space.

- [Fantechi, "Stacks for everybody"](http://www.mathematik.uni-bielefeld.de/~rehmann/ECM/cdrom/3ecm/pdfs/pant3/fantechi.pdf): a survey of algebraic stacks written for the third European Congress of Mathematics.

- [Mumford, "Picard groups of moduli problems"; Internet Archive copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20211129230457/http://www.mathcs.emory.edu/~brussel/Scans/mumfordpicard.pdf), with Litt's exposition in two parts: [part I](https://www.daniellitt.com/s/picardI.pdf) constructs quotient stacks and computes the line bundles on a classifying stack, and [part II](https://www.daniellitt.com/s/picardII.pdf) computes the Picard group of the moduli stack of elliptic curves.

- [Vistoli, "Notes on Grothendieck topologies, fibered categories and descent theory"](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412512): an introduction to descent theory, from Grothendieck topologies through fibered categories to stacks.

- [Olsson, Berkeley Math 274 notes on stacks, written up by Geraschenko](https://stacky.net/files/written/Stacks/Stacks.pdf): Grothendieck topologies, descent, algebraic spaces and fibered categories.
