# Riemann surfaces and uniformization

Recall that a [[D-8WMFK|Riemann surface]] is a connected one-dimensional complex manifold, and that every connected Riemann surface is a quotient of a simply connected one by its group of [[D-4VGAW|deck transformations]] ([[T-ZAEWG]]).
The proper simply connected case is the Riemann mapping theorem: a nonempty open simply connected $\Omega\subsetneq\CC$ is conformally the disc (C. McMullen, *Advanced Complex Analysis*, Theorem 4.1; [@SS03, ch. 8, Theorem 3.1]), while $\CC$ is not, since a holomorphic map $\CC\to\DD$ would be a bounded entire function ([[T-QHIHJ|Liouville's theorem]]).

## Uniformization

[[T-ZAEWG]]

[[D-4VGAW]]

The three simply connected models carry the three geometries: the [[D-3QTVR|Riemann sphere]] carries the spherical metric, invariant under the isometries $\SU(2)/\theset{\pm I}$ (C. McMullen, *Advanced Complex Analysis*, Theorem 2.14); $\CC$ carries the Euclidean metric, invariant under the maps $z\mapsto az+b$ with $\abs a=1$; and $\DD$ carries [[D-2HXLN|the hyperbolic metric]], invariant under all of its automorphisms (C. McMullen, *Advanced Complex Analysis*, Corollary 2.19). Each symmetry group is a proper subgroup of the biholomorphisms of the model.
So a Riemann surface inherits one of the three geometries from its universal cover.
