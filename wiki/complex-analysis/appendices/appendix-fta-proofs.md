# Routes to the fundamental theorem of algebra

The statement is the card below.
Each section states the step on which its route turns and links the card that carries the tool; the routes are collected for comparison, the theorem and its tools being standard course material.

[[T-6XDRA]]

## By the argument principle

The integral $\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint P'/P$ counts the zeros with multiplicity, and $zP'(z)/P(z)\to n$ as $z\to\infty$, so the count is $n$: [[T-JXDQT|the argument principle]].

## By Rouché's theorem

On a circle $\abs z=R$ with $R>1$ and $R\abs{a_n}>\abs{a_{n-1}}+\cdots+\abs{a_0}$, the lower terms of $P$ are dominated by the leading term $a_nz^n$, which has $n$ zeros in the disc: [[T-CJCKL|Rouché's theorem]].

## By Liouville's theorem

The step is the boundedness of $1/P$: for a polynomial $P$ without zeros, $\abs{P(z)}\geq\frac12\abs{a_n}\abs z^n$ for large $\abs z$ makes $1/P$ a bounded entire function: [[T-QHIHJ|Liouville's theorem]].

## By the open mapping theorem

A nonconstant polynomial extends to a holomorphic map $\CP^1\to\CP^1$ sending $\infty$ to $\infty$, whose image is open and compact, hence closed, and therefore all of $\CP^1$: [[C-FRF33|the open mapping theorem]].

## By the lemma on maps from compact Riemann surfaces

The same extension is nonconstant and $\CP^1$ is compact, so the extension is surjective: [[L-ZXBBI|the lemma on maps from compact Riemann surfaces]].

## Zero divisors

::: {.proposition}
The ring of holomorphic functions on a domain in $\CC$ has no zero divisors.

This is [[T-SVF2W|the identity principle]].
:::
