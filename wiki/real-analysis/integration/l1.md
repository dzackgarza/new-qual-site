# $L^1$

Recall that $L^1(\mu)$ consists of the [[D-R5DL3|integrable functions]] modulo [[D-ALMOSTEVERY|equality almost everywhere]], with the norm $\norm{f}_1\coloneqq\int \abs f\,d\mu$.

## Modes of convergence

[[D-IYDZU|Pointwise]], [[D-ALMOSTEVERY|almost-everywhere]], [[D-YZC3C|uniform]], and $L^1$ convergence are distinct; [[PR-TC76W]] separates them.

- If $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere and $\abs{f_n}\leq g$ for some $g\in L^1$, then $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, by the [[T-IJQQG|dominated convergence theorem]].

- If $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, then a subsequence converges to $f$ almost everywhere [@Fol99, Proposition 2.29 and Theorem 2.30]: Proposition 2.29 carries $L^1$ convergence to convergence in measure, and the second part of Theorem 2.30 extracts the subsequence convergent almost everywhere from that.

- If $\mu(X)<\infty$ and measurable $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere with $f$ finite almost everywhere, then $f_n\to f$ almost uniformly, by [[FT-OGS76|Egorov's theorem]].

- If $\mu(X)<\infty$, $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere, and $\theset{f_n}$ is uniformly integrable, then $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, by the Vitali convergence theorem [@Fol99, Exercise 6.15].
