# $L^1$

$L^1(\mu)$ is the space of integrable functions modulo equality almost everywhere, with norm $\norm{f}_1\coloneqq\int \abs f\,d\mu$.

## Modes of convergence

Pointwise, almost-everywhere, uniform, and $L^1$ convergence are distinct; [[PR-TC76W]] separates them.

- If $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere and $\abs{f_n}\leq g$ for some $g\in L^1$, then $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, by the dominated convergence theorem.

- If $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, then a subsequence converges to $f$ almost everywhere.

- If $\mu(X)<\infty$ and measurable $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere with $f$ finite almost everywhere, then $f_n\to f$ almost uniformly, by Egorov's theorem.

- If $\mu(X)<\infty$, $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere, and $\theset{f_n}$ is uniformly integrable, then $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, by the Vitali convergence theorem.
