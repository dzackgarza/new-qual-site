# $L^1$

$L^1(\mu)$ is the space of integrable functions modulo equality almost everywhere, with norm $\norm{f}_1\coloneqq\int \abs f\,d\mu$.

## Properties of integrable functions

For $f\in L^1(\mu)$: $\norm f_1=0$ if and only if $f=0$ almost everywhere; $f$ is finite almost everywhere; and for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ with $\int_E\abs f<\varepsilon$ whenever $\mu(E)<\delta$.
For Lebesgue measure on $\RR^d$, $\int f(x+h)\dx = \int f(x)\dx$, and $\int_{\abs x>R}\abs f\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$.

::: {.remark}
For $f\in L^1(\RR^d)$, $\norm{f(\wait+h)-f}_1\to0$ as $h\to0$.
The proof approximates $f$ in $L^1$ by a continuous function with compact support, for which the statement follows from uniform continuity.
This continuity of translation is used to prove that $f*\phi_t\to f$ in $L^1$ for an approximate identity $\phi_t$.

:::

## Modes of convergence

Pointwise, almost-everywhere, uniform, and $L^1$ convergence are distinct.
On $\RR$, the sequences $n\chi_{(0,1/n)}$, whose mass concentrates near $0$, $\chi_{[n,n+1]}$, whose mass moves to infinity, and $\frac1n\chi_{[0,n]}$, whose mass spreads out, converge to $0$ pointwise and have $\norm{f_n}_1=1$; the last converges uniformly.

- If $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere and $\abs{f_n}\leq g$ for some $g\in L^1$, then $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, by the dominated convergence theorem.

- If $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, then a subsequence converges to $f$ almost everywhere.

- If $\mu(X)<\infty$ and measurable $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere with $f$ finite almost everywhere, then $f_n\to f$ almost uniformly, by Egorov's theorem.

- If $\mu(X)<\infty$, $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere, and $\theset{f_n}$ is uniformly integrable, then $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$, by the Vitali convergence theorem.
