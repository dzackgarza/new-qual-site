# Workshops

The workshop reviews the real-analysis qualifying material in two week-long sessions.
The first takes up the undergraduate prerequisites; the second develops Lebesgue measure and the integration theorems that the qualifying problems use.

## Week 1: preliminaries

The first week is organized around the implications that a qualifying problem usually asks for.
A continuous function on a compact metric space is uniformly continuous ([[T-O4UD3]]), and a uniformly Cauchy sequence in $C([0,1])$ has a continuous limit ([[PR-RWROV]]); these two facts turn compactness and completeness into statements about uniform convergence.
The Weierstrass $M$-test converts a summable numerical bound into uniform convergence of a series of functions, and the same estimates give the continuity of the sum.
The Baire category theorem supplies the existence statements in the functional-analysis part of the review.
The week's topics and review problems are on [[real-analysis/workshops/real-week-1-preliminaries|Week 1: preliminaries]].

## Week 2: measure theory and Fubini--Tonelli

The second week constructs Lebesgue measure from an outer measure and Carathéodory's criterion, then passes to measurable functions and the almost-everywhere quantifier ([[D-ALMOSTEVERY]]).
The convergence theorems compare the hypotheses under which limits pass through integrals: monotone convergence handles increasing nonnegative sequences, Fatou's lemma gives the inequality that survives without a dominating function, and dominated convergence gives equality under one integrable majorant ([[T-5K3IO]], [[T-LDJNS]], [[T-IJQQG]]).
Product measures introduce slices ([[D-SLICES]]); Tonelli's theorem integrates nonnegative functions in either order ([[FT-4JRQX]]), and Fubini's theorem extends the interchange to integrable functions ([[T-4GPEF]]).
Egorov's and Lusin's theorems are the two regularity statements behind Littlewood's principles ([[FT-OGS76]], [[T-CGFCU]]).
The week's topics and review problems are on [[real-analysis/workshops/real-week-2-measure-theory|Week 2: measure theory and Fubini--Tonelli]].
