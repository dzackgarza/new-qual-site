# Field extensions

## Basics

For $K\subseteq L$, adjoining an element $\alpha\in L$ gives the smallest intermediate
field $K(\alpha)$.  If $\alpha$ is algebraic, then $K[\alpha]=K(\alpha)$ and its degree
is the degree of the minimal polynomial; if it is transcendental, $K[\alpha]$ is only a
polynomial ring inside the rational function field $K(\alpha)$.  Finite extensions are
therefore algebraic, while the primitive element theorem gives hypotheses under which
a finite extension can be generated by one element.

:::{.remark title="Tower law and degree constraints"}
The tower law is
\[
[M:K]=[M:L][L:K].
\]
Two standard consequences: an extension of prime degree has no proper intermediate fields, and $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]$ divides $[\SF(f):\QQ]$ for every root $\alpha$ of $f$, giving a lower bound on the order of the Galois group.

:::

## Finding a minimal polynomial

:::{.remark title="The standard techniques"}
\envlist

- Given $x\da \sqrt a + \sqrt b$, compute $x^2, x^3, \dots$ and look for a rational linear combination.
  The general move is to isolate the radicals on one side and raise both sides to that power.

- Better, since it always terminates: find $n \da [\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]$, so that $1, \alpha, \dots, \alpha^n$ must be $\QQ\dash$linearly dependent, then compute those powers and solve for the coefficients.

- For $x\da \sqrt a + \sqrt b$ specifically, write $x, x^2, x^3, x^4$ in the basis $\ts{1, \sqrt a, \sqrt b, \sqrt{ab}}$, so that
 \[
   A\vector v = \vector c \da A \tv{1, \sqrt a, \sqrt b, \sqrt{ab} } = \tv{x, x^2, x^3, x^4}
 ,\]
 and invert: reading the first row of $A\inv$ against $\vector b$ gives a polynomial in $x$.

- If $\alpha\beta \in \QQ$ with $\beta \neq 0$, then $\alpha = (\alpha\beta)/\beta \in \QQ(\beta)$, and symmetrically.

:::

## Algebraic extensions

Algebraicity is stable under towers: if the elements of $L/K$ are algebraic and the
elements of $M/L$ are algebraic, then the elements of $M/K$ are algebraic.  In finite
towers the degree formula above gives the stronger numerical control, so the first
question in an extension problem is usually whether algebraicity can be upgraded to a
finite degree computation.

## Quadratic extensions

Degree two is the first case with essentially no room: there are no proper intermediate
fields, and the extension is generated by any element outside the base field.  Over
fields of characteristic different from two this becomes the familiar square-root
description; over $\QQ$ it is classified by square classes, while over a finite field
there is only one quadratic extension up to isomorphism.

## Distinguished classes

The adjectives introduced next—normal, separable, and their combinations—control how
embeddings of an extension behave.  They are not degree conditions.  Normality is a
splitting condition and has strong upward behavior in a tower, but it is not transitive
in the naive sense; the next page isolates splitting fields and normal extensions before
separability is added.
