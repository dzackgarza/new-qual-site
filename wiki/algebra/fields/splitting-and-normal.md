# Splitting fields and normal extensions

A finite extension is normal exactly when it is a splitting field, so a question about normality is a question about the roots of one polynomial; the price is that normality is not transitive through a tower, which is what the counterexample below shows.

## The normal closure

For a finite extension $L = K(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)$ inside an algebraic closure $\overline K$, the normal closure is obtained by adjoining every root of each minimal polynomial, which is the splitting field of their product.

[[P-0T149]]

[[D-99IUP]]

::: {.example title="The normal closure of $\QQ(2^{1/4})/\QQ$"}
The normal closure of $\QQ(2^{1/4})$ in $\CC$ is $\QQ(2^{1/4}, i)$, the splitting field of $x^4-2$: the other roots are $\pm i\,2^{1/4}$, and $\QQ(2^{1/4},i)$ is generated by the real root and $i$.
:::

## Normality is not transitive

::: {.example title="Normality is not transitive"}
In the tower $\QQ\subseteq\QQ(\sqrt 2)\subseteq\QQ(2^{1/4})$ both steps have degree $2$ and are therefore normal, and $\QQ(2^{1/4})/\QQ$ is not: $x^4-2$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ by Eisenstein at $2$, has the root $2^{1/4}$ in $\QQ(2^{1/4})\subseteq\RR$, and its other roots $\pm i\,2^{1/4}$ are not real.
:::

## Normal subgroups and normal intermediate fields

In a finite Galois extension, an intermediate field is normal over the base exactly when its Galois group is normal in the whole group, so the two senses of normal are read off each other.
