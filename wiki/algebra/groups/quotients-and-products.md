# Quotients, products, and automorphisms

## The isomorphism theorems

The three isomorphism theorems are the whole of the quotient calculus this chapter uses: a homomorphism is read through the quotient by its kernel, and a subgroup statement is carried through a quotient by the correspondence theorem.

## Direct products

A direct product decomposition is exhibited by subgroups rather than by a map, and the recognition theorem turns the subgroup hypotheses into the isomorphism; classifying a group up to isomorphism becomes the search for normal subgroups of prescribed orders and intersections.

## Semidirect products

With one normal factor only, the same construction produces a semidirect product whose multiplication is twisted by the conjugation map $H\to\Aut(N)$, and two twisting maps give isomorphic groups when they differ by an automorphism of $H$.
A normal Sylow subgroup $N$ of $G$ with a complement $H$ is the case this arises in: $N\intersect H=1$ and $NH=G$, so the recognition theorem applies to the pair.

## Automorphism groups

An automorphism of a cyclic group is multiplication by a unit of $\ZZ/n$, so the automorphism group is a unit group, and the twisting maps $H\to\Aut(N)$ of the semidirect products are counted there.

## Finitely generated abelian groups

The two standard decompositions of a finitely generated abelian group differ only in the grouping of the same cyclic factors: the invariant factors are ordered by divisibility and the elementary divisors are the prime powers, and the count of groups of a given order is a product of partition numbers.
