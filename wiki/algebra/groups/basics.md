# Basics

Throughout, $G$ is a group with identity $e$, and $H$ and $K$ are subgroups of $G$.

## Subgroups

Membership in a subgroup is decided by the one-step criterion [[PR-U5CHT]], which replaces the three axioms of [[D-IQ4OX]] by the single condition $xy^{-1}\in H$.
Since $\generators S$ is the smallest subgroup containing $S$, the inclusion $\generators S\leq H$ follows from $S\subseteq H$, so a subgroup is shown to contain a generated subgroup by checking the generators.

## Order and presentations

The order of an element is the order of the cyclic subgroup it generates, so it divides $\size{G}$ when $G$ is finite, by Lagrange's theorem applied to that subgroup.

## Commutators and the derived series

$[x,y]=e$ exactly when $x$ and $y$ commute, and the subgroup generated by the commutators is the smallest normal subgroup whose quotient is abelian.
Iterating it gives the [[D-W2QAA|derived series]], which reaches the trivial subgroup exactly for the [[D-DFIDP|solvable]] groups.

## Conjugacy

Conjugation by $g$ is an automorphism of $G$, so conjugate elements have the same order and [[D-ES7MP|conjugate subgroups]] have the same cardinality.
Conjugation is the action of $G$ on itself, and its orbits are the conjugacy classes while the stabilizer of $x$ is the centralizer $C_G(x)$; hence the size of a class is the index of a centralizer, and the class of $x$ is a singleton exactly when $x$ is central.

## Normal subgroups

A subgroup is normal exactly when left and right multiplication by every $g$ agree on it, and exactly when it is a union of conjugacy classes.
Normality is what makes the cosets of $H$ a group, and it is weaker than being characteristic: see [[algebra/groups/characteristic-subgroups|Characteristic subgroups]].

## Centralizers, normalizers, and the center

The centralizer of a subset fixes its elements under conjugation and the normalizer permutes them, so $C_G(H)\leq N_G(H)$; the center is the centralizer of $G$, and also the intersection of the centralizers of its elements.

## Cosets and the index

A map of sets is injective exactly when it has a left inverse and surjective exactly when it has a right inverse; the coset $gH$ is the image of $H$ under the bijection $h\mapsto gh$, so all cosets of $H$ have $\size{H}$ elements and the index counts them.
