# Characteristic subgroups, normal closures, and cores

Normality is not transitive; the standard counterexample, a subgroup of $V_4$ in $A_4$, is on [[algebra/counterexamples|Counterexamples]].
A [[D-53LTN|characteristic subgroup]] is invariant under every automorphism, and a characteristic subgroup of a normal subgroup of $G$ is normal in $G$.

## Characteristic subgroups

[[D-53LTN]]

::: {.remark title="Characteristic and normal subgroups"}
Every characteristic subgroup of $G$ is [[D-EKE4Q|normal]], because conjugation $\psi_g(x) = gxg^{-1}$ by $g \in G$ is an automorphism of $G$.
A normal subgroup need not be characteristic: in the Klein four-group $V \cong C_2 \times C_2$, the subgroup $H = C_2 \times \{e\}$ is normal because $V$ is abelian, and the automorphism exchanging the two factors maps $H$ to $\{e\} \times C_2 \neq H$.
:::
