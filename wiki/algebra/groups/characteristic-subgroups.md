# Characteristic subgroups, normal closures, and cores

Normality is not transitive: in $A_4$, $\langle (1\,2)(3\,4)\rangle \normal V_4$ and $V_4 \normal A_4$, but $\langle (1\,2)(3\,4)\rangle$ is not normal in $A_4$.
A [[D-53LTN|characteristic subgroup]] is invariant under every automorphism, and a characteristic subgroup of a normal subgroup of $G$ is normal in $G$.

## Characteristic subgroups

[[D-53LTN]]

::: {.remark title="Characteristic and normal subgroups"}
Every characteristic subgroup of $G$ is [[D-EKE4Q|normal]], because conjugation $\psi_g(x) = gxg^{-1}$ by $g \in G$ is an automorphism of $G$.
A normal subgroup need not be characteristic: in the Klein four-group $V \cong C_2 \times C_2$, the subgroup $H = C_2 \times \{e\}$ is normal because $V$ is abelian, and the automorphism exchanging the two factors maps $H$ to $\{e\} \times C_2 \neq H$.
:::

[[PR-IOMVN]]

::: {.proof}
Let $A \operatorname{char} B \normal C$, and let $c \in C$.
Because $B \normal C$, conjugation $\psi_c(b) = cbc^{-1}$ restricts to an automorphism of $B$.
Because $A$ is characteristic in $B$, $\psi_c(A) = A$.
Hence $cAc^{-1} = A$ for every $c \in C$, and $A \normal C$.
:::

[[PR-GJLQP]]

::: {.proof}
Let $\psi \in \Aut(H)$ and $y\in Z(H)$.
For every $h\in H$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\psi(y)h
&= \psi(y)\,\psi\big(\psi\inv(h)\big) \\
&= \psi\big( y \cdot \psi\inv(h) \big) \\
&= \psi\big( \psi\inv(h) \cdot y \big) && \text{since } \psi\inv(h)\in H \text{ and } y\in Z(H) \\
&= h\,\psi(y).
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence $\psi(Z(H))\subseteq Z(H)$ for every $\psi\in\Aut(H)$.
:::

## Normal closures and cores

[[D-BPRD3]]

[[D-QMVEB]]

For $H\le G$, the [[D-BPRD3|normal closure]] of $H$ is the subgroup generated by the conjugates $gHg\inv$ for $g\in G$, and the [[D-QMVEB|normal core]] of $H$ is $\Intersect_{g\in G} gHg\inv$.
The normal core of $H$ is the kernel of the action of $G$ on the left cosets $G/H$ by left multiplication, so it is trivial if and only if this action is faithful.
