# Modules over a PID

## Simple, indecomposable, and cyclic

A simple module is generated by each of its nonzero elements, hence cyclic, and it has no proper nonzero direct summand, hence is indecomposable; the two notions differ over $\ZZ$, where $\ZZ$ is indecomposable and not simple.

## The structure theorem

A finitely generated module over a principal ideal domain splits as a free part and a torsion part, and the torsion part splits over the primes, each summand a direct sum of modules $R/\generators{p^e}$; the invariant factor and elementary divisor forms are two groupings of one list.

The split of the free part from the torsion part is the case in which the quotient by the torsion submodule is free, which is what a finitely generated torsion-free module over a principal ideal domain is.
