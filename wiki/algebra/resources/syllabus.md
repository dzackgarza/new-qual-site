# Topics and remarks

::: {.remark}
Adapted from a remark written by Roy Smith, August 2006:

"As a general rule, students are responsible for knowing both the theory (proofs) and practical applications (e.g. how to find the Jordan or rational canonical form of a given matrix, or the Galois group of a given polynomial) of the topics mentioned."
:::

## Rings

> References: [@DF04]; [@Hun74]; [@Smi96].

- [@DF04, chaps. 7-9, 13-14]

- [@Smi96]:

  - 843-2, sections 11, 12, and 16-21 (39 pages)

### Topics

- Ideals and quotient rings

- The isomorphism theorems for rings

- An ideal $I$ of a commutative ring $R$ is maximal if and only if $R/I$ is a field

- Zorn's lemma

  - Every vector space has a basis

  - Maximal ideals exist

  - Algebraic closures of fields exist

  - Existence of maximal ideals in countable or Noetherian rings without Zorn's lemma

  > See [@Smi96, sec. 844-1].

- The Chinese remainder theorem

- The Euclidean algorithm

- Prime and irreducible elements

- The Gaussian integers and factorization in $\ZZ[i]$

- Localization of a domain and the field of fractions

- Factorization in domains

- Characterizations and properties of classes of rings:

  - Euclidean domain $\implies$ PID $\implies$ UFD

  - Integral domains: prime elements are irreducible

  - UFDs: greatest common divisors exist, and a UFD need not be a PID

  - PIDs

    - PIDs are Noetherian

    - Irreducible elements are prime

    - PIDs are UFDs, and greatest common divisors exist

    - Results about PIDs [@DF04, sec. 8.2]

      - A PID that is not a Euclidean domain [@DF04, p. 277]

      - Proof that a Euclidean domain is a PID and hence a UFD

      - Proof that $\ZZ$ and $k[x]$ are UFDs [@Smi96, p. 289; @DF04, p. 300]

      - A polynomial ring in infinitely many variables over a UFD is a UFD [@DF04, p. 305]

  - Euclidean domains are PIDs

- Gauss's theorem on unique factorization of polynomials:

  - $\ZZ[x]$ is a UFD

  - $R[x]$ is a UFD when $R$ is a UFD

- Polynomial rings

- Polynomials

  - Gauss's lemma

  - The remainder and factor theorems

  - Eisenstein's criterion [@DF04, p. 309]

  > [@DF04, p. 309] states Eisenstein's criterion for monic polynomials; the proof of the general case is the same, and [@Smi96] states the general case.

  - Reducibility

  - The rational root test

- The structure of $(\ZZ/n\ZZ)\units$ as a product of cyclic groups

  > See [@DF04] and [@Smi96, sec. 844-2.18].

- Gröbner bases and division algorithms for polynomials in several variables [@DF04, sec. 9.6]
