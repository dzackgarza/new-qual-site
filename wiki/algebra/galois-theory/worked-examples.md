# Worked examples

Each degree has its own decision procedure, and the procedures are the ones on [[algebra/galois-theory/compute-this-galois-group|Compute this Galois group]]: for a quadratic the discriminant decides, for a cubic the discriminant decides between the two possibilities, and for a quartic the resolvent cubic and the discriminant leave one ambiguity that needs one more test.

## Quadratics

An irreducible quadratic has Galois group $C_2$, its splitting field is generated by the square root of its discriminant, and the two roots are rational exactly when that square root is.

## Cubics

An irreducible separable cubic has Galois group $A_3$ when its discriminant is a square in the base field and $S_3$ otherwise, so the whole computation is one square test.

## Quartics

The transitive subgroups of $S_4$ are $S_4$, $A_4$, $D_4$, $C_4$ and $C_2^2$; the resolvent cubic and the discriminant separate all but one pair, and the remaining pair is separated by whether the quartic stays irreducible over the field obtained by adjoining the square root of the discriminant.

## Binomials $x^n-a$

The splitting field of $x^n-a$ is generated by an $n$th root and a primitive $n$th root of unity, so the group sits inside $\ZZ/n\semidirect(\ZZ/n)^\times$ and is dihedral only in the three cases where the two generators interact that way.

## Cyclotomic and finite fields

Two families are settled without a resolvent: the cyclotomic fields, whose group is the unit group modulo $n$, and the finite fields, whose group is cyclic and generated by Frobenius.
