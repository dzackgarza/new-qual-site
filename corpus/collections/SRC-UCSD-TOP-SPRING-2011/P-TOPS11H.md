::: problem
(1) What are the integer cohomology rings of $S^2 \times S^2$ and $\mathbb{CP}^2$?
(2) Show that there is no continuous map $f: S^2 \times S^2 \to \mathbb{CP}^2$ having **odd degree**.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Compute the cohomology rings $H^*(S^2 \times S^2; \mathbb{Z})$ and $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^2; \mathbb{Z})$, and use the cup product structure to show $\deg(f) = 2ab$ is always even.

<1>1. Cohomology Rings of $S^2 \times S^2$ and $\mathbb{CP}^2$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Cohomology Ring of $\mathbb{CP}^2$:**
        - $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is a 4-dimensional compact orientable manifold with cell structure $e^0 \cup e^2 \cup e^4$.
        - Its integer cohomology ring is generated by a single element $\alpha \in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$:
          $$H^*(\mathbb{CP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[\alpha] / (\alpha^3), \qquad \deg(\alpha) = 2, \quad \alpha^2 \ne 0 \text{ generates } H^4(\mathbb{CP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$
    <2>2. **Cohomology Ring of $S^2 \times S^2$:**
        - By the **Künneth Theorem** in cohomology (since $H^*(S^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2)$ with $\deg(x) = 2$ is free):
          $$H^*(S^2 \times S^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H^*(S^2; \mathbb{Z}) \otimes_\mathbb{Z} H^*(S^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[u, v] / (u^2, v^2), \qquad \deg(u) = \deg(v) = 2$$
          where $u = x \otimes 1$ and $v = 1 \otimes x$.
        - In particular:
          $$u^2 = 0, \qquad v^2 = 0, \qquad u \cup v \ne 0 \text{ generates } H^4(S^2 \times S^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

<1>2. Induced Map on Second Cohomology:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $f: S^2 \times S^2 \to \mathbb{CP}^2$ be an arbitrary continuous map.
    <2>2. The generator $\alpha \in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2; \mathbb{Z})$ pulls back under $f^*$ to an element in $H^2(S^2 \times S^2; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}u \oplus \mathbb{Z}v$:
        $$f^*(\alpha) = a u + b v \quad \text{for some integers } a, b \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

<1>3. Computation of Degree via Cup Product:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $f^*$ is a graded ring homomorphism, it preserves cup products:
        $$f^*(\alpha^2) = (f^*(\alpha))^2 = (a u + b v)^2.$$
    <2>2. Expanding the square in the ring $H^*(S^2 \times S^2; \mathbb{Z})$:
        $$(a u + b v)^2 = a^2 u^2 + 2 a b (u \cup v) + b^2 v^2.$$
    <2>3. Using the relations $u^2 = 0$ and $v^2 = 0$:
        $$f^*(\alpha^2) = a^2(0) + 2 a b (u \cup v) + b^2(0) = 2 a b (u \cup v).$$
    <2>4. On the other hand, by definition of the topological degree $\deg(f)$ (with fundamental classes normalized so that $[\mathbb{CP}^2]$ is dual to $\alpha^2$ and $[S^2 \times S^2]$ is dual to $u \cup v$):
        $$f^*(\alpha^2) = \deg(f) \cdot (u \cup v).$$
    <2>5. Comparing coefficients:
        $$\deg(f) = 2 a b.$$

<1>4. Parity of Degree:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$, the degree $\deg(f) = 2 a b$ is a multiple of 2.
    <2>2. Therefore, $\deg(f)$ is **always an even integer**.
    <2>3. In particular, there cannot exist any map $f: S^2 \times S^2 \to \mathbb{CP}^2$ with **odd degree** ($\deg(f) \not\equiv 1 \pmod 2$).

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $H^*(S^2 \times S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}[u,v]/(u^2, v^2)$ and $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]/(\alpha^3)$; pulling back $\alpha \mapsto au+bv$ yields $\deg(f) = 2ab$, which is always even. Q.E.D.
:::
