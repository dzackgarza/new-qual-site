::: {.problem}
(1) What are the integer cohomology rings of $S^2 \times S^2$ and $\CP^2$?
(2) Show that there is no continuous map $f: S^2 \times S^2 \to \CP^2$ having **odd degree**.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
Let $u,v\in H^2(S^2\times S^2;\ZZ)$ be the pullbacks of the positive generator of $H^2(S^2;\ZZ)$ under the two projections. Then
$$
H^*(S^2\times S^2;\ZZ)
\cong \ZZ[u,v]/(u^2,v^2),
\qquad \gradeddegree{u}=\gradeddegree{v}=2,
$$
and $uv$ generates $H^4(S^2\times S^2;\ZZ)$ after choosing the product orientation.


::: pf-proof
These classes generate $H^2$ of the product, by the Künneth formula applied to the product of the two spheres.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $x\in H^2(\CP^2;\ZZ)$ is the standard generator, then
$$
H^*(\CP^2;\ZZ)
\cong \ZZ[x]/(x^3),
\qquad \gradeddegree{x}=2,
$$
with $x^2$ generating $H^4(\CP^2;\ZZ)$ after choosing the usual orientation.


::: pf-proof
The cohomology ring of $\CP^2$ is $\ZZ[x]/(x^3)$ with $x$ in degree $2$, so $x^2$ is a generator of $H^4\cong\ZZ$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Let $f:S^2\times S^2\to\CP^2$ be continuous.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Since $H^2(S^2\times S^2;\ZZ)=\ZZ u\oplus\ZZ v$, write
$$
f^*(x)=au+bv
$$
for integers $a,b$.

::: pf-proof
$f^*$ is a homomorphism on $H^2$, and that group is free abelian with basis $u,v$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Naturality of cup products gives
$$
f^*(x^2)=(au+bv)^2
=a^2u^2+2ab\,uv+b^2v^2
=2ab\,uv.
$$

::: pf-proof
$f^*$ is a homomorphism of rings, so it carries $x^2$ to the square of $f^*(x)$; $u$ and $v$ have even degree so the cross terms add rather than cancel, and $u^2=v^2=0$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
By the definition of degree,
$$
f^*(x^2)=\deg(f)\,uv.
$$
Thus $\deg(f)=2ab$, which is even.

::: pf-proof
The group $H^4(S^2\times S^2)$ is generated by $uv$, and the degree is the integer by which $f^*$ multiplies that generator; comparing with the preceding computation gives $2ab$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore no map $S^2\times S^2\to\CP^2$ has odd degree.


::: pf-proof
The integer $2ab$ is even for all choices of $a$ and $b$, so no odd value occurs.
:::
