::: problem
Let $n$ be an integer.
Let $G_n$ be the group given by generators and relations as follows.
\[
G_n = \langle x, y \mid x^3 = 1,\ xyx^{-1} = y^n \rangle.
\]

(a) Provide (with proof) necessary and sufficient conditions on the integer $n$ for the group $G_n$ to be finite.

(b) Assuming that $G_n$ is finite, compute its order as a function of $n$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Describe the finite cases of $G_n$ and compute $|G_n|$.

<1>1. Rewrite the presentation:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. From $xyx^{-1}=y^n$, conjugation by $x$ sends $y\mapsto y^n$.
    <2>2. Repeated conjugation gives
        $$x^3 y x^{-3}=y^{n^3}.$$
    <2>3. Using $x^3=e$, we get
        $$y^{n^3-1}=e.$$

<1>2. Case $n=1$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. If $n=1$, the relation becomes $xyx^{-1}=y$, so $x$ commutes with $y$.
    <2>2. Therefore
        $$G_1 \cong \langle x\mid x^3=1\rangle \times \langle y\rangle \cong C_3\times\mathbb Z,$$
        which is infinite.

<1>3. Case $n\ne 1$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $m=|n^3-1|\,(\in\mathbb N)$.
    <2>2. Relation $y^{n^3-1}=e$ gives $y^m=e$, so $H:=\langle y\rangle$ is finite of order dividing $m$.
    <2>3. Using $xyx^{-1}=y^n$, every word in $x,y$ can be rewritten uniquely in the form $x^i y^j$ with $i\in\{0,1,2\}$, $j\in\mathbb Z$.
    <2>4. Since $y^m=e$, reduce $j$ modulo $m$ and get at most $3m$ candidates:
        $$x^i y^j,\qquad 0\le i\le 2,\;0\le j\le m-1.$$

<1>4. A faithful action on a finite set:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let
        $$\phi:G_n\to\operatorname{Sym}(\{0,1,2\}\times\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)$$
        be generated by:
        $$\phi(x)(i,k)=(i+1,\, nk),\qquad \phi(y)(i,k)=(i,\,k+1),$$
        where indices are mod $3$ and mod $m$.
    <2>2. Since $\gcd(n,m)=1$, $n$ is invertible mod $m$.
    <2>3. One checks directly that $\phi(x)^3=\mathrm{id}$ and $\phi(x)\phi(y)\phi(x)^{-1}=\phi(y)^n$, so $\phi$ is a homomorphism.
    *Hence:* $G_n$ is finite, because its image is finite.

<1>5. Exact order when $n\ne 1$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For the normal form $x^i y^j$ with $0\le i<3$, $0\le j<m$, compute
        $$\phi(x^i y^j)(0,0)=(i,n^ij).$$
    <2>2. If $\phi(x^i y^j)=\mathrm{id}$ then $(i,n^ij)=(0,0)$, so $i=0$ and $j=0$.
    <2>3. Therefore these $3m$ normal-form words map to distinct points and are distinct group elements.
    <2>4. So
        $$|G_n|=3m=3|n^3-1|,\qquad n\ne1.$$ 

<1>6. Conclusion:
    
    $$G_n \text{ is finite iff } n\ne1,$$
    and for this case
    $$|G_n|=3|n^3-1|.$$
    In particular, $G_1\cong C_3\times\mathbb Z$ is infinite. Q.E.D.
:::
