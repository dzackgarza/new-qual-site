::: {.problem}
Let $n$ be an integer.
Let $G_n$ be the group given by generators and relations as follows.
\[
G_n = \langle x, y \mid x^3 = 1,\ xyx^{-1} = y^n \rangle.
\]

(a) Provide (with proof) necessary and sufficient conditions on the integer $n$ for the group $G_n$ to be finite.

(b) Assuming that $G_n$ is finite, compute its order as a function of $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The relation forces
\[
y^{n^3-1}=1.
\]

::: pf-proof

Conjugating three times gives
\[
x^3yx^{-3}=y^{n^3}.
\]
Since $x^3=1$, the left side is $y$, so $y^{n^3}=y$ and hence $y^{n^3-1}=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $n=1$, then
\[
G_1\cong C_3\times\mathbb Z,
\]
so $G_1$ is infinite.

::: pf-proof

For $n=1$ the relation says $xyx^{-1}=y$, so $x$ and $y$ commute. The presentation is therefore the direct product of the cyclic group generated by $x$ of order $3$ and the infinite cyclic group generated by $y$.

Assume henceforth that $n\ne1$ and put
\[
m=|n^3-1|>0.
\]
Then step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives $y^m=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every element of $G_n$ can be represented by one of the $3m$ words
\[
y^j x^i,
\qquad 0\le j<m,\quad 0\le i<3.
\]

::: pf-proof

Using $x^3=1$, replace every power of $x$ by $x^i$ with $0\le i<3$. The relation
\[
xy=y^n x
\]
moves each occurrence of $x$ to the right across a power of $y$. Repeating this rewrites every word as $y^j x^i$. Finally reduce $j$ modulo $m$ using $y^m=1$.
Thus $|G_n|\le3m$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There is a permutation representation of $G_n$ on
\[
\Omega=(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)\times(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)
\]
defined by
\[
X(a,b)=(a+1,nb),
\qquad
Y(a,b)=(a,b+1),
\]
with $x\mapsto X$ and $y\mapsto Y$.

::: pf-proof

Since
\[
\gcd(n,m)=\gcd(n,n^3-1)=1,
\]
multiplication by $n$ is invertible modulo $m$, so $X$ is a permutation.
Moreover
\[
X^3(a,b)=(a,n^3b)=(a,b)
\]
because $n^3\equiv1\pmod m$.
Also
\[
XYX^{-1}(a,b)=(a,b+n)=Y^n(a,b).
\]
Thus $X^3=1$ and $XYX^{-1}=Y^n$, so the defining relations of $G_n$ are satisfied and the universal property of the presentation gives a homomorphism
\[
\rho:G_n\to\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega).
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The $3m$ words $y^j x^i$ from step [](#s3){.pf-ref} are pairwise distinct.

::: pf-proof

For $0\le i<3$ and $0\le j<m$,
\[
\rho(y^j x^i)(0,0)=Y^jX^i(0,0)=(i,j).
\]
The points $(i,j)$ are all distinct in $\Omega$. Hence the permutations $\rho(y^j x^i)$ are pairwise distinct, and therefore the corresponding elements $y^j x^i$ of $G_n$ are pairwise distinct.
Thus $|G_n|\ge3m$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently
\[
G_n\text{ is finite}\iff n\ne1,
\]
and in the finite cases
\[
\boxed{|G_n|=3|n^3-1|}.
\]

::: pf-proof

The case $n=1$ is infinite by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}. If $n\ne1$, step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives $|G_n|\le3m$ and step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives $|G_n|\ge3m$, hence equality.

:::
