::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite group of order $n$. Suppose that for every divisor $d$ of $n$, there is at most one subgroup of $G$ of order $d$. Prove that $G$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #p-group-has-subgroup-each-order}
If $P$ is a finite $p$-group of order $p^a$, then for every
$k=0,1,\ldots,a$ there is a subgroup of $P$ of order $p^k$.

::: pf-proof
Proceed by induction on $a$. The assertion is immediate for $a=0$.
Assume $a\ge1$. The class equation gives $Z(P)\ne1$, so by Cauchy's
theorem there is a central subgroup $C\subseteq Z(P)$ of order $p$.
Then $P/C$ has order $p^{a-1}$. For $k=0$, take the trivial subgroup.
For $1\le k\le a$, the induction hypothesis gives a subgroup
$\overline H\le P/C$ of order $p^{k-1}$. Its inverse image under
$P\to P/C$ has order
$$
\abs{C}\abs{\overline H}=p^k.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sylow-unique-normal}
For every prime power $p^a$ exactly dividing $n$, the Sylow
$p$-subgroup $P_p$ of $G$ is unique and therefore normal.

::: pf-proof
Sylow's theorem gives at least one subgroup of order $p^a$. The
hypothesis gives at most one subgroup of that order. Hence there is
exactly one Sylow $p$-subgroup, and uniqueness makes it normal.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sylow-cyclic}
Every Sylow subgroup $P_p$ is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
Write $\abs{P_p}=p^a$. If $a=1$, then any nonidentity element has
order $p$ and generates $P_p$. Assume $a\ge2$.

By step [](#p-group-has-subgroup-each-order){.pf-ref}, $P_p$ contains a subgroup $M$ of order $p^{a-1}$.
Choose $x\in P_p\sm M$. Since $P_p$ is a $p$-group, the order of
$x$ is $p^r$ for some $1\le r\le a$. Suppose $r<a$. Applying step
[](#p-group-has-subgroup-each-order){.pf-ref} to the group $M$ gives a subgroup $K\le M$ of order $p^r$.
But $\langle x\rangle$ also has order $p^r$, and the hypothesis on
$G$ permits at most one subgroup of that order. Thus
$$
\langle x\rangle=K\subseteq M,
$$
contradicting $x\notin M$. Therefore $r=a$, so $x$ has order
$\abs{P_p}$ and $P_p=\langle x\rangle$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sylow-commute-distinct-primes}
Sylow subgroups for distinct primes commute elementwise.

::: pf-proof
Let $p\ne q$, with $x\in P_p$ and $y\in P_q$. By step [](#sylow-unique-normal){.pf-ref}, both
$P_p$ and $P_q$ are normal in $G$. Hence their commutator
$[x,y]$ lies in both subgroups:
$$
[x,y]\in P_p\cap P_q.
$$
The order of this intersection divides both a power of $p$ and a power
of $q$, so $P_p\cap P_q=1$. Therefore $[x,y]=1$, and $xy=yx$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product-of-sylows-is-G}
The product of all Sylow subgroups is $G$.

::: pf-proof
Write
$$
n=\prod_{j=1}^r p_j^{a_j}
$$
with distinct primes $p_j$. By steps [](#sylow-unique-normal){.pf-ref} and [](#sylow-commute-distinct-primes){.pf-ref}, successive products
$$ 
H=P_{p_1}P_{p_2}\cdots P_{p_r}
$$
are subgroups of $G$. Inductively, the order of
$P_{p_1}\cdots P_{p_{j-1}}$ is divisible only by
$p_1,\ldots,p_{j-1}$, so its intersection with $P_{p_j}$ is trivial.
Thus the product formula for finite subgroups gives
$$
\abs{H}
=\prod_{j=1}^r\abs{P_{p_j}}
=\prod_{j=1}^r p_j^{a_j}
=n
=\abs{G}.
$$
Thus $H=G$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #G-cyclic}
The group $G$ is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#sylow-cyclic){.pf-ref}, choose a generator $g_j$ of each $P_{p_j}$. By step
[](#sylow-commute-distinct-primes){.pf-ref}, the elements $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ commute, and their orders
$p_1^{a_1},\ldots,p_r^{a_r}$ are pairwise coprime. Therefore
$$
g\coloneqq g_1g_2\cdots g_r
$$
has order
$$
\prod_{j=1}^r p_j^{a_j}=n.
$$
Indeed, for commuting elements of coprime orders, if
$(xy)^m=1$, then $x^m=y^{-m}$ lies in
$\langle x\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle=1$, so both orders divide
$m$; induction gives the displayed product order for all the factors.
Hence
$$
\boxed{G=\langle g\rangle}.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#G-cyclic){.pf-ref} proves that $G$ is cyclic.
:::
