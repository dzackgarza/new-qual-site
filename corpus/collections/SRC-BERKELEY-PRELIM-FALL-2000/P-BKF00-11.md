::: {.problem}
Let $f_n:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R^m$ be continuous, let $K\subset\mathbb R^k$ be compact, and suppose $f_n\to f$ uniformly on $K$. Prove that
\[
S=f(K)\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}f_n(K)
\]
is compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $K=\varnothing$, then $S=\varnothing$ and there is nothing to prove.
Assume henceforth that $K\neq\varnothing$.

<1>1. The restriction $f|_K$ is continuous, and therefore $f(K)$ is
compact.

::: {.proof}
Each restriction $f_n|_K$ is continuous, and $f_n\to f$ uniformly on $K$.
The uniform limit theorem gives continuity of $f|_K$. Since $K$ is compact,
its continuous image $f(K)$ is compact.
:::

<1>2. Let $\mathcal U$ be an open cover of $S$. There are
$U_1,\ldots,U_r\in\mathcal U$ and a number $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$
f(K)\subseteq O\coloneqq U_1\cup\cdots\cup U_r
$$
and every point of $\RR^m$ at distance less than $\varepsilon$ from
$f(K)$ lies in $O$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, compactness of $f(K)$ gives a finite subcover
$U_1,\ldots,U_r$. For each $y\in f(K)$, because $O$ is open, choose
$\rho_y>0$ such that $B(y,2\rho_y)\subseteq O$. Compactness gives points
$y_1,\ldots,y_s\in f(K)$ for which
$$
f(K)\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^s B(y_j,\rho_{y_j}).
$$
Set
$$
\varepsilon=\min_{1\le j\le s}\rho_{y_j}>0.
$$
If $z$ has distance less than $\varepsilon$ from $f(K)$, choose
$y\in f(K)$ with $\norm{z-y}<\varepsilon$. For some $j$,
$\norm{y-y_j}<\rho_{y_j}$, and hence
$$
\norm{z-y_j}
<\varepsilon+\rho_{y_j}
\le2\rho_{y_j}.
$$
Thus $z\in B(y_j,2\rho_{y_j})\subseteq O$.
:::

<1>3. There is $N\ge1$ such that
$$
f_n(K)\subseteq O
$$
for every $n\ge N$.

::: {.proof}
Uniform convergence on $K$ gives $N$ such that
$$
\norm{f_n(x)-f(x)}<\varepsilon
$$
for every $x\in K$ and every $n\ge N$. Hence every point of $f_n(K)$ is
at distance less than $\varepsilon$ from the corresponding point of
$f(K)$. Step <1>2 then gives $f_n(K)\subseteq O$.
:::

<1>4. The cover $\mathcal U$ has a finite subcover of $S$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, the sets $U_1,\ldots,U_r$ cover $f(K)$ and every $f_n(K)$
with $n\ge N$. For each $1\le n<N$, the set $f_n(K)$ is compact because
$f_n$ is continuous and $K$ is compact, so finitely many members of
$\mathcal U$ cover $f_n(K)$. There are only finitely many indices
$n<N$; combining those finite subcovers with $U_1,\ldots,U_r$ gives a
finite subcover of all of $S$.
:::

<1>5. $S$ is compact.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 shows that every open cover of $S$ has a finite subcover, which
is the definition of compactness.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
