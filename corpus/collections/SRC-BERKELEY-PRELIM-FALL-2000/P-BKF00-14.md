::: {.problem}
Let $T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ be linear, where $n>1$. Prove that there exists a two-dimensional subspace $M\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ such that
\[
T(M)\subseteq M.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $V=\RR^n$, and let $T_{\CC}:V\otimes_{\RR}\CC\to
V\otimes_{\RR}\CC$ denote the complexification of $T$. We prove the
claim by induction on $n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #base-case-n2}
The claim holds when $n=2$.

::: pf-proof
Take $M=V$. Then $\dim_{\RR}M=2$ and $T(M)\subseteq M$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nonreal-eigenvalue-case}
Assume $n\ge3$ and the claim is known in dimension $n-1$. If
$T_{\CC}$ has a nonreal eigenvalue, then $T$ has a two-dimensional
invariant real subspace.

::: pf-proof
Let $\lambda=a+ib$ with $b\ne0$ be an eigenvalue of $T_{\CC}$, and
choose a corresponding eigenvector
$$
v=x+iy,
\qquad
x,y\in V.
$$
The vectors $x$ and $y$ are linearly independent over $\RR$. Indeed, if
they were dependent, then $v$ would be a nonzero complex scalar multiple
of a real vector $u\in V$. The equation $T_{\CC}v=\lambda v$ would
then imply $Tu=\lambda u$, impossible because both $Tu$ and $u$ are
real while $\lambda\notin\RR$.

Equating real and imaginary parts in
$$
T_{\CC}(x+iy)=(a+ib)(x+iy)
$$
gives
$$
Tx=ax-by,
\qquad
Ty=bx+ay.
$$
Hence $M=\operatorname{span}_{\RR}\{x,y\}$ is two-dimensional and
$T(M)\subseteq M$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #real-eigenvalues-hyperplane}
Under the same induction hypothesis, if every eigenvalue of
$T_{\CC}$ is real, then $T$ has an invariant hyperplane
$H\subseteq V$.

::: pf-proof
The dual operator $T^*:V^*\to V^*$ has the same characteristic
polynomial as $T$. Since every root of that polynomial is real, $T^*$
has a real eigenvalue $\lambda$ and a nonzero eigenvector
$\varphi\in V^*$:
$$
T^*\varphi=\lambda\varphi.
$$
Set $H=\ker\varphi$. Since $\varphi\ne0$, the space $H$ is a
hyperplane. For every $x\in H$,
$$
\varphi(Tx)=(T^*\varphi)(x)=\lambda\varphi(x)=0,
$$
so $Tx\in H$. Thus $T(H)\subseteq H$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #real-eigenvalues-case}
If every eigenvalue of $T_{\CC}$ is real, then $T$ has a
two-dimensional invariant real subspace.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#real-eigenvalues-hyperplane){.pf-ref}, there is a $T$-invariant hyperplane $H$. Its dimension is
$n-1\ge2$, so the induction hypothesis applied to the restriction
$T|_H:H\to H$ gives a two-dimensional subspace $M\subseteq H$ such
that
$$
T(M)=T|_H(M)\subseteq M.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #invariant-plane-exists}
For every $n>1$, there exists a two-dimensional subspace
$M\subseteq\RR^n$ such that
$$
\boxed{T(M)\subseteq M}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#base-case-n2){.pf-ref} is the induction base. For the induction step, either
$T_{\CC}$ has a nonreal eigenvalue, in which case step [](#nonreal-eigenvalue-case){.pf-ref} gives the
required plane, or all its eigenvalues are real, in which case step
[](#real-eigenvalues-case){.pf-ref} gives the required plane. This completes the induction.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#invariant-plane-exists){.pf-ref} is the desired conclusion.
:::
