::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a ring with identity and fewer than eight elements. Prove that $R$ is commutative.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $N=\abs{R}$ and let $m$ be the characteristic of $R$, equivalently
the additive order of $1_R$ when $R$ is nonzero.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #N-equals-one-or-m-divides-N}
If $N=1$, then $R$ is commutative. Hence assume $1<N<8$; then
$m$ is a divisor of $N$ with $m>1$.

::: pf-proof
The one-element ring is commutative. If $N>1$, then $1_R\ne0$, and its
additive order $m$ divides the order $N$ of the finite additive group
$(R,+)$ by Lagrange's theorem.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #m-equals-N-case}
If $m=N$, then $R$ is commutative.

::: pf-proof
The element $1_R$ then has additive order $N$, so it generates
$(R,+)$. Thus every element of $R$ is an integer multiple of $1_R$.
For integers $r,s$,
$$
(r1_R)(s1_R)=rs\,1_R=sr\,1_R=(s1_R)(r1_R),
$$
so all elements commute.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #possible-pairs}
If $m<N$, then the only possible pairs $(N,m)$ are
$$
(4,2),\qquad (6,2),\qquad (6,3).
$$

::: pf-proof
The integers strictly between $1$ and $8$ are
$2,3,4,5,6,7$. If $N$ is prime, the only divisor $m>1$ of $N$ is
$N$ itself. Thus a proper divisor $m<N$ can occur only for
$N=4$ or $N=6$. Their proper divisors greater than $1$ are exactly
those displayed.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order-six-impossible}
The pairs $(N,m)=(6,2)$ and $(6,3)$ cannot occur.

::: pf-proof
If the characteristic is a prime $p$, the central copy of $\FF_p$
generated by $1_R$ makes $R$ a vector space over $\FF_p$. Since $R$ is
finite, its cardinality must therefore be $p^d$ for some integer
$d\ge1$. The integer $6$ is neither a power of $2$ nor a power of $3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order-four-case}
If $(N,m)=(4,2)$, then $R$ is commutative.

::: pf-proof
Now $R$ is a two-dimensional vector space over the central subfield
$\FF_2\cdot1_R$. Choose
$$
x\in R\sm(\FF_2\cdot1_R).
$$
Then $\{1_R,x\}$ is an $\FF_2$-basis of $R$, so arbitrary elements have
the form $a1_R+bx$ and $c1_R+dx$, with $a,b,c,d\in\FF_2$. Since scalar
multiples of $1_R$ are central,
$$
(a1_R+bx)(c1_R+dx)
=ac\,1_R+(ad+bc)x+bd\,x^2,
$$
which is unchanged when the two factors are reversed. Hence every pair
of elements commutes.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #every-ring-commutative}
Every ring with identity and fewer than eight elements is
commutative.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#N-equals-one-or-m-divides-N){.pf-ref} handles $N=1$. For $1<N<8$, either $m=N$, in which case
step [](#m-equals-N-case){.pf-ref} applies, or $m<N$. In the latter case, step [](#possible-pairs){.pf-ref} lists all
possibilities, step [](#order-six-impossible){.pf-ref} excludes the two cases of order $6$, and step
[](#order-four-case){.pf-ref} proves commutativity in the remaining case of order $4$.
Therefore
$$
\boxed{xy=yx\quad\text{for all }x,y\in R}.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#every-ring-commutative){.pf-ref} proves the assertion in every possible cardinality.
:::
