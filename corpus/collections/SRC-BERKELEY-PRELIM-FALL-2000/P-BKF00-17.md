::: {.problem}
Let $U\subset\mathbb C$ be connected, simply connected, and open, and let $f$ be holomorphic on $U$. Suppose $a\in U$ and the Taylor series of $f$ at $a$ converges on an open disk $D$ that intersects $\mathbb C\setminus U$.

Must $f$ extend holomorphically to $U\cup D$? Give a proof or a counterexample.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #setup-U-and-f}
Let
$$
U=\CC\sm[0,\infty),
\qquad
a=1+i,
$$
and let
$$
f(z)=\log\abs{z}+i\arg_U(z),
\qquad
0<\arg_U(z)<2\pi,
$$
be the holomorphic branch of the logarithm on $U$. Then $U$ is connected,
simply connected, and open.

::: pf-proof
The complement of the closed ray $[0,\infty)$ is open and connected.
Choosing the square root with argument in $(0,\pi)$ gives a
biholomorphism
$$
U\longrightarrow\{w\in\CC:\operatorname{Im}w>0\},
\qquad
z\longmapsto\sqrt z,
$$
whose inverse is $w\mapsto w^2$. Hence $U$ is simply connected. The
displayed function $f$ is the corresponding holomorphic logarithm on
the slit plane.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #disk-D-meets-complement}
For
$$
D=\{z\in\CC:\abs{z-a}<\sqrt2\},
$$
the disk $D$ meets $\CC\setminus U$, and the Taylor series of $f$ at
$a$ converges throughout $D$.

::: pf-proof
Since
$$
\abs{1-a}=1<\sqrt2,
$$
the point $1$ belongs to $D\cap[0,\infty)$, so
$D\cap(\CC\setminus U)\ne\varnothing$.

Let $F$ denote the principal logarithm, with argument in
$(-\pi,\pi)$. For every real $x\le0$,
$$
\abs{x-a}^2=(x-1)^2+1\ge2,
$$
with equality only at $x=0$. Thus the open disk $D$ does not meet the
principal branch cut $(-\infty,0]$, so $F$ is holomorphic on $D$.

Near $a=1+i$, both $f$ and $F$ have argument close to $\pi/4$, and
therefore $f=F$ on a neighborhood of $a$. Hence they have the same
Taylor series at $a$. Since $F$ is holomorphic on the disk $D$, its
Taylor series at the center $a$ converges to $F$ throughout $D$.
Consequently the Taylor series of $f$ at $a$ converges throughout $D$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #logarithms-disagree}
The two holomorphic logarithms disagree on another part of
$U\cap D$.

::: pf-proof
Take
$$
b=1-\frac{i}{4}.
$$
Then
$$
\abs{b-a}=\frac54<\sqrt2,
$$
so $b\in D$, and clearly $b\in U$. The principal argument of $b$ is
$-\arctan(1/4)$, whereas its argument in $(0,2\pi)$ is
$2\pi-\arctan(1/4)$. Therefore
$$
f(b)=F(b)+2\pi i\ne F(b).
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #no-holomorphic-extension}
The function $f$ has no holomorphic extension to $U\cup D$.

::: pf-proof
Suppose that $h$ were holomorphic on $U\cup D$ and satisfied
$h|_U=f$. By step [](#disk-D-meets-complement){.pf-ref}, $f=F$ on a neighborhood of $a$, so $h$ and
$F$ agree on a nonempty open subset of the connected disk $D$. The
identity theorem gives
$$
h|_D=F.
$$
At the point $b$ from step [](#logarithms-disagree){.pf-ref}, however, membership in $U$ gives
$h(b)=f(b)$ while membership in $D$ gives $h(b)=F(b)$, contradicting
$f(b)\ne F(b)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer-is-no}
The answer is
$$
\boxed{\text{no}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#setup-U-and-f){.pf-ref}, [](#disk-D-meets-complement){.pf-ref} and [](#logarithms-disagree){.pf-ref} satisfy all hypotheses of the problem, while step
[](#no-holomorphic-extension){.pf-ref} shows that the required extension does not exist.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#answer-is-no){.pf-ref} provides the requested counterexample.
:::
