::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be a field and let $R$ be a nonzero $K$-algebra generated by two elements $a,b$ satisfying
\[
a^2=b^2=0,
\qquad
(a+b)^2=1.
\]
Show that
\[
R\cong M_2(K)
\]
as $K$-algebras.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ab-plus-ba-equals-one}
The defining relations imply
$$
ab+ba=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
Expanding $(a+b)^2=1$ and using $a^2=b^2=0$ gives
$$
a^2+ab+ba+b^2=ab+ba=1.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix-unit-relations}
Define
$$
e_{11}=ab,
\qquad
e_{12}=a,
\qquad
e_{21}=b,
\qquad
e_{22}=ba.
$$
Then
$$
e_{ij}e_{k\ell}=\delta_{jk}e_{i\ell}
$$
for $i,j,k,\ell\in\{1,2\}$, and
$$
e_{11}+e_{22}=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
The last equality is step [](#ab-plus-ba-equals-one){.pf-ref}. For the diagonal products,
$$
\begin{aligned}
e_{11}^2
&=a(ba)b
=a(1-ab)b
=ab=e_{11},\\
e_{22}^2
&=b(ab)a
=b(1-ba)a
=ba=e_{22},
\end{aligned}
$$
while
$$
e_{11}e_{22}=ab^2a=0,
\qquad
e_{22}e_{11}=ba^2b=0
$$
because $a^2=b^2=0$.

The mixed products with matching middle indices are
$$
\begin{aligned}
e_{11}e_{12}
&=(ab)a=a(ba)=a(1-ab)=a=e_{12},\\
e_{12}e_{22}
&=a(ba)=a=e_{12},\\
e_{21}e_{11}
&=b(ab)=(ba)b=(1-ab)b=b=e_{21},\\
e_{22}e_{21}
&=(ba)b=b=e_{21},\\
e_{12}e_{21}
&=ab=e_{11},\\
e_{21}e_{12}
&=ba=e_{22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The remaining products contain either $a^2$ or $b^2$ and are therefore
zero. These are exactly the matrix-unit relations.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #Phi-surjective}
There is a surjective $K$-algebra homomorphism
$$
\Phi:M_2(K)\longrightarrow R
$$
such that
$$
\Phi(E_{ij})=e_{ij}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#matrix-unit-relations){.pf-ref}, the four elements $e_{ij}$ satisfy the multiplication rules
for the standard matrix units and $e_{11}+e_{22}=1$. Therefore the
$K$-linear map determined by
$$
E_{ij}\longmapsto e_{ij}
$$
is a unital $K$-algebra homomorphism.

Its image contains
$$
e_{12}=a
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
e_{21}=b.
$$
Since $R$ is generated as a $K$-algebra by $a$ and $b$, the map is
surjective.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #M2K-simple}
Every nonzero two-sided ideal of $M_2(K)$ is all of $M_2(K)$.

::: pf-proof
Let $I\subseteq M_2(K)$ be a nonzero two-sided ideal and choose a nonzero
matrix
$$
X=(x_{pq})\in I.
$$
Choose indices $p,q$ with $x_{pq}\neq0$. For arbitrary
$i,j\in\{1,2\}$,
$$
E_{ip}XE_{qj}=x_{pq}E_{ij}\in I.
$$
Since $x_{pq}$ is invertible in the field $K$, it follows that
$E_{ij}\in I$ for every $i,j$. The four matrix units span $M_2(K)$ over
$K$, so
$$
I=M_2(K).
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #Phi-injective}
The homomorphism $\Phi$ in step [](#Phi-surjective){.pf-ref} is injective.

::: pf-proof
Its kernel is a two-sided ideal of $M_2(K)$. By step [](#M2K-simple){.pf-ref}, the kernel is
either $0$ or all of $M_2(K)$. But
$$
\Phi(I_2)=1_R,
$$
and $1_R\neq0$ because $R$ is nonzero. Hence $\Phi$ is not the zero
homomorphism, so its kernel cannot be all of $M_2(K)$. Therefore
$$
\ker\Phi=0.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #R-isomorphic-to-M2K}
There is a $K$-algebra isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
R\cong M_2(K).
}
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#Phi-surjective){.pf-ref}, $\Phi$ is surjective, and by step [](#Phi-injective){.pf-ref} it is injective.
Thus $\Phi:M_2(K)\to R$ is an isomorphism. Reversing its direction gives
the displayed isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#R-isomorphic-to-M2K){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
:::
