::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=\frac{p(z)}{q(z)},
\]
where $p,q$ are nonzero complex polynomials, $\deg p<\deg q$, and $q$ has no zeros with nonnegative imaginary part. Prove that for every $z_0$ with $\operatorname{Im}z_0>0$,
\[
f(z_0)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(t)}{t-z_0}\,dt.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

For $R>0$, let $C_R$ be the positively oriented boundary of the closed
upper half-disk $\{z:\abs{z}\le R,\ \operatorname{Im}z\ge0\}$, and let
$A_R$ denote its semicircular arc.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #decay-bound}
There are constants $C>0$ and $R_0>0$ such that
$$
\abs{f(z)}\le \frac{C}{\abs{z}}
$$
whenever $\abs{z}\ge R_0$.

::: pf-proof
Write $m=\deg p$ and $n=\deg q$. Since $m<n$, polynomial growth at
infinity gives $\abs{p(z)}=O(\abs{z}^m)$ and
$\abs{q(z)}^{-1}=O(\abs{z}^{-n})$. Hence
$$
\abs{f(z)}=O(\abs{z}^{m-n})=O(\abs{z}^{-1}),
$$
which gives the stated constants.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #integral-converges-absolutely}
The improper integral
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(t)}{t-z_0}\,dt
$$
converges absolutely.

::: pf-proof
Because $q$ has no zeros on the real axis and $z_0\notin\RR$, the
integrand is continuous on every bounded real interval. For
$\abs{t}\ge\max(R_0,2\abs{z_0})$, step [](#decay-bound){.pf-ref} and
$\abs{t-z_0}\ge\abs{t}-\abs{z_0}\ge\abs{t}/2$ give
$$
\abs{\frac{f(t)}{t-z_0}}
\le \frac{2C}{\abs{t}^2}.
$$
The comparison integral over both tails is finite.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cauchy-formula-on-CR}
If $R>\abs{z_0}$, then
$$
\int_{-R}^{R}\frac{f(t)}{t-z_0}\,dt
+
\int_{A_R}\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}\,dz
=2\pi i f(z_0).
$$

::: pf-proof
The polynomial $q$ has no zero with nonnegative imaginary part, so $f$
is holomorphic on an open neighborhood of the closed upper half-disk
bounded by $C_R$. Since $\operatorname{Im}z_0>0$ and
$\abs{z_0}<R$, the point $z_0$ lies inside $C_R$. The Cauchy integral
formula therefore gives
$$
2\pi i f(z_0)=\int_{C_R}\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}\,dz.
$$
Splitting $C_R$ into the real segment from $-R$ to $R$ and the arc
$A_R$ gives the displayed equality.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #arc-integral-vanishes}
The arc integral tends to zero:
$$
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\int_{A_R}\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}\,dz=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
For $R\ge\max(R_0,2\abs{z_0})$ and $z\in A_R$, step [](#decay-bound){.pf-ref} gives
$\abs{f(z)}\le C/R$, while
$$
\abs{z-z_0}\ge R-\abs{z_0}\ge R/2.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}}\le \frac{2C}{R^2}.
$$
Since $A_R$ has length $\pi R$, the estimation lemma yields
$$
\abs{\int_{A_R}\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}\,dz}
\le \frac{2\pi C}{R}\longrightarrow0.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #final-formula}
For every $z_0$ with $\operatorname{Im}z_0>0$,
$$
\boxed{
f(z_0)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(t)}{t-z_0}\,dt
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $R\to\infty$ in the identity from step [](#cauchy-formula-on-CR){.pf-ref}. By step [](#integral-converges-absolutely){.pf-ref}, the
real-segment integrals converge to the stated improper integral, and
step [](#arc-integral-vanishes){.pf-ref} shows that the arc integral tends to zero. Dividing the
resulting identity by $2\pi i$ gives the formula.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#final-formula){.pf-ref} is exactly the asserted identity.
:::
