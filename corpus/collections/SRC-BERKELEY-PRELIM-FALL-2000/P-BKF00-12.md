::: {.problem}
Prove that for every positive integer $k$ there exists a positive integer $N$ such that there are at least $k$ pairwise nonisomorphic groups of order $N$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix $k\ge1$. Write $C_m$ for the cyclic group of order $m$, and let
$C_2^0$ denote the trivial group.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #Gj-order-2k}
For each $j=0,\ldots,k-1$, define
$$
G_j=C_{2^{k-j}}\times C_2^j.
$$
Then every $G_j$ has order $2^k$.

::: pf-proof
Since $\abs{C_{2^{k-j}}}=2^{k-j}$ and $\abs{C_2^j}=2^j$,
$$
\abs{G_j}=2^{k-j}2^j=2^k.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #Gj-exponent}
The exponent of $G_j$ is $2^{k-j}$.

::: pf-proof
Every element of $C_{2^{k-j}}\times C_2^j$ has order dividing
$2^{k-j}$, while a generator of the first cyclic factor has order exactly
$2^{k-j}$. Hence the least common multiple of all element orders is
$2^{k-j}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #Gj-pairwise-nonisomorphic}
The groups $G_0,\ldots,G_{k-1}$ are pairwise nonisomorphic.

::: pf-proof
Group isomorphisms preserve element orders and therefore preserve the
exponent. By step [](#Gj-exponent){.pf-ref}, the exponents
$$
2^k,2^{k-1},\ldots,2
$$
are pairwise distinct, so no two of the groups $G_j$ are isomorphic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #N-equals-2k}
Taking
$$
\boxed{N=2^k}
$$
gives at least $k$ pairwise nonisomorphic groups of order $N$.

::: pf-proof
There are exactly $k$ groups in the list $G_0,\ldots,G_{k-1}$. Step [](#Gj-order-2k){.pf-ref}
gives their common order $2^k$, and step [](#Gj-pairwise-nonisomorphic){.pf-ref} gives pairwise
nonisomorphism.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#N-equals-2k){.pf-ref} supplies the required positive integer $N$ for every positive
integer $k$.
:::
