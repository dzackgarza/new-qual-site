::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be nonconstant and nondecreasing. Prove that there exist $a\in\mathbb R$ and $c>0$ such that
\[
f(a+x)-f(a-x)\ge cx
\]
for every $x\in[0,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. There exists $u\in\RR$ such that
$$
\delta\coloneqq f(u+1)-f(u)>0.
$$

::: {.proof}
If no such $u$ existed, monotonicity would give
$f(t+1)=f(t)$ for every $t\in\RR$. For arbitrary $s<t$, choose a
positive integer $N$ with $t\le s+N$. Then
$$
f(s)\le f(t)\le f(s+N)=f(s),
$$
so $f(s)=f(t)$. This would make $f$ constant, contrary to the
hypothesis.
:::

<1>2. There is a nested sequence of closed intervals
$$
I_n=[u_n,v_n],
\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,
$$
such that
$$
I_0=[u,u+1],
\qquad
v_n-u_n=2^{-n},
\qquad
f(v_n)-f(u_n)\ge \delta 2^{-n}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Start with $I_0=[u,u+1]$, for which the assertions follow from step
<1>1. Suppose $I_n=[u_n,v_n]$ has been chosen, and let
$m_n=(u_n+v_n)/2$. Since
$$
f(v_n)-f(u_n)
=
\bigl(f(m_n)-f(u_n)\bigr)
+
\bigl(f(v_n)-f(m_n)\bigr),
$$
at least one of the two increments on the right is at least
$(f(v_n)-f(u_n))/2$. Choose $I_{n+1}$ to be the corresponding half of
$I_n$. Its length is $2^{-(n+1)}$, and
$$
f(v_{n+1})-f(u_{n+1})
\ge \frac{f(v_n)-f(u_n)}{2}
\ge \delta 2^{-(n+1)}.
$$
This constructs the sequence inductively.
:::

<1>3. There is a unique point $a\in\RR$ belonging to every $I_n$.

::: {.proof}
The intervals are nonempty, closed, and nested, and their lengths
$2^{-n}$ tend to zero. The nested interval theorem therefore gives a
unique point
$$
a\in\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} I_n.
$$
:::

<1>4. For every $x\in(0,1]$,
$$
f(a+x)-f(a-x)\ge \frac{\delta}{2}x.
$$

::: {.proof}
Choose $n\ge0$ such that
$$
2^{-n}\le x<2^{1-n}.
$$
By step <1>3, $a\in I_n$, and by step <1>2 the interval $I_n$ has
length $2^{-n}\le x$. Hence every point of $I_n$ lies within distance
$x$ of $a$, so
$$
I_n\subseteq[a-x,a+x].
$$
Monotonicity and step <1>2 now give
$$
f(a+x)-f(a-x)
\ge f(v_n)-f(u_n)
\ge \delta 2^{-n}
>\frac{\delta}{2}x.
$$
:::

<1>5. With the point $a$ from step <1>3 and
$$
\boxed{c=\frac{\delta}{2}}>0,
$$
we have
$$
f(a+x)-f(a-x)\ge cx
$$
for every $x\in[0,1]$.

::: {.proof}
For $x\in(0,1]$, this is step <1>4. For $x=0$, both sides are zero.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 supplies the required $a\in\RR$ and $c>0$.
:::
