::: problem
Let $F$ be a field. Let $f(x) \in F[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree $n$, and let $g(x) \in F[x]$. Let $p(x) \in F[x]$ be an irreducible factor of degree $m = \deg p(x)$ of the composition $f(g(x))$.

(a) Prove that $n$ divides $m$.

(b) Use this to prove that if $r \in \mathbb{Z}$ is an integer that is not a perfect square, and $n$ is a positive integer, then every irreducible factor of $x^{2n} - r$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ has even degree.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that the degree of any irreducible factor of $f(g(x))$ is a multiple of $\deg f$ using a tower of field extensions in (a), and apply this to $x^{2n} - r$ in (b).

<1>1. Part (a): $n$ divides $m$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\overline{F}$ be an algebraic closure of $F$.
    <2>2. Choose a root $\alpha \in \overline{F}$ of the irreducible factor $p(x)$, so $p(\alpha) = 0$.
    <2>3. Since $p(x) \in F[x]$ is irreducible, $p(x)$ is a scalar multiple of the minimal polynomial $\operatorname{irr}(\alpha, F)$, which implies
    $$[F(\alpha) : F] = \deg p(x) = m.$$
    <2>4. Since $p(x)$ divides $f(g(x))$, $f(g(\alpha)) = 0$.
    <2>5. Define the element $\beta = g(\alpha) \in F(\alpha)$.
    <2>6. Then $f(\beta) = f(g(\alpha)) = 0$.
    <2>7. Because $f(x) \in F[x]$ is irreducible, $f(x)$ is a scalar multiple of the minimal polynomial $\operatorname{irr}(\beta, F)$, which implies
    $$[F(\beta) : F] = \deg f(x) = n.$$
    <2>8. Since $\beta = g(\alpha) \in F(\alpha)$, the subfield $F(\beta)$ is contained in $F(\alpha)$, giving the tower of finite extensions:
    $$F \subseteq F(\beta) \subseteq F(\alpha).$$
    <2>9. By the Tower Law for extension degrees:
    $$[F(\alpha) : F] = [F(\alpha) : F(\beta)] \cdot [F(\beta) : F].$$
    <2>10. Substituting the degrees gives:
    $$m = [F(\alpha) : F(\beta)] \cdot n.$$
    <2>11. Since $[F(\alpha) : F(\beta)]$ is a positive integer, $n$ divides $m$.

<1>2. Part (b): Every irreducible factor of $x^{2n} - r$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ has even degree.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define $f(y) = y^2 - r \in \mathbb{Q}[y]$ and $g(x) = x^n \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$.
    <2>2. The composition is
    $$f(g(x)) = (x^n)^2 - r = x^{2n} - r.$$
    <2>3. Irreducibility of $f(y)$:
        - $\deg f = 2$.
        - A quadratic polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}[y]$ is reducible if and only if it has a root in $\mathbb{Q}$.
        - If $y^2 - r = 0$ has a rational root $y = a/b \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $\gcd(a, b) = 1$, then $a^2 = r b^2$.
        - By the Rational Root Theorem (or unique prime factorization in $\mathbb{Z}$), $b = 1$, so $r = a^2$ is a perfect square in $\mathbb{Z}$.
        - By hypothesis, $r$ is not a perfect square, so $f(y)$ has no rational roots.
        - Therefore $f(y)$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[y]$, with degree $\deg f = 2$.
    <2>4. Application of Part (a):
        - Let $p(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be any irreducible factor of $x^{2n} - r = f(g(x))$.
        - By Part (a), $\deg f(y) = 2$ divides $\deg p(x)$.
        - Therefore $\deg p(x)$ is even.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    *Proof:*
    $[F(\alpha) : F(\beta)] \cdot [F(\beta) : F] = m \implies n \mid m$, and since $y^2 - r$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, every irreducible factor of $x^{2n} - r$ has degree divisible by 2.
:::
