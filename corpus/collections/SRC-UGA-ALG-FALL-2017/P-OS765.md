::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field. Let $f(x) \in F[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree $n$, and let $g(x) \in F[x]$. Let $p(x) \in F[x]$ be an irreducible factor of degree $m = \deg p(x)$ of the composition $f(g(x))$.

(a) Prove that $n$ divides $m$.

(b) Use this to prove that if $r \in \ZZ$ is an integer that is not a perfect square, and $n$ is a positive integer, then every irreducible factor of $x^{2n} - r$ in $\QQ[x]$ has even degree.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $p\in F[x]$ is an irreducible factor of $f(g(x))$, then $\deg f$ divides $\deg p$.

::: pf-proof

Choose a root $\alpha$ of $p$ in an algebraic closure of $F$, and put $\beta=g(\alpha)$. Since $p$ is irreducible, $[F(\alpha):F]=\deg p=m$. Because $p$ divides $f(g(x))$, one has $f(\beta)=0$. The irreducibility of $f$ therefore gives $[F(\beta):F]=\deg f=n$. The containment $F(\beta)\subseteq F(\alpha)$ and the tower law yield
\[
m=[F(\alpha):F(\beta)]\,[F(\beta):F]
=[F(\alpha):F(\beta)]\,n.
\]
The first factor is a positive integer, so $n\mid m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $r\in\ZZ$ is not a perfect square, then $y^2-r$ is irreducible over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

A reducible quadratic over a field has a root in that field. Suppose $a/b\in\QQ$ is a root of $y^2-r$, with coprime integers $a,b$ and $b>0$. Then $a^2=rb^2$, so $b^2$ divides $a^2$. Coprimality forces $b=1$, whence $r=a^2$, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus no rational root exists and the quadratic is irreducible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every irreducible factor of $x^{2n}-r$ in $\QQ[x]$ has even degree.

::: pf-proof

Set $f(y)=y^2-r$ and $g(x)=x^n$. Then $f(g(x))=x^{2n}-r$, and step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives that $f$ is irreducible of degree two over $\QQ$. Applying step [](#s1){.pf-ref} to any irreducible factor $p$ of this composition gives $2\mid\deg p$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
