::: {.problem}
Suppose $f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ is an irreducible polynomial of degree $p$ where $p$ is prime.
Let $E$ be the splitting field of $f(x)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
Let $\alpha \in E$ be a zero of $f$, $G := \mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})$, and $H := \mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}[\alpha])$.
Suppose $H$ is not trivial.

(a) Prove that $[G : H] = p$ and $\gcd(|H|, p) = 1$.

(b) Prove that $H$ is not a normal subgroup of $G$.

(c) Let $P$ be a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$.
Prove that $N_G(P) \neq P$.
(Hint: assuming $N_G(P) = P$, deduce that $H = \{ g \in G \mid o(g) \neq p \}$ where $o(g)$ is the order of $g$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
\Omega:=\{\text{roots of }f\text{ in }E\}.
\]
Because $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, the natural action of $G$ on $\Omega$ is transitive.
It is also faithful, since an automorphism of the splitting field $E$ that fixes every root of $f$ fixes the field generated by those roots and hence is the identity.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

One has
\[
[G:H]=p.
\]

::: pf-proof

By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory,
\[
[G:H]=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q].
\]
Since $f$ is irreducible of degree $p$ and $f(\alpha)=0$,
\[
[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=p.
\]
Therefore
\[
[G:H]=p.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The order of $H$ is relatively prime to $p$.

::: pf-proof

The faithful action on the $p$ roots embeds
\[
G\hookrightarrow S_p.
\]
Hence
\[
|G|\mid p!.
\]
The prime $p$ occurs exactly once in the factorization of $p!$, so
\[
p^2\nmid |G|.
\]
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
\[
|G|=p|H|.
\]
Therefore $p\nmid|H|$, i.e.
\[
\gcd(|H|,p)=1.
\]
This completes part (a).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The subgroup $H$ is the stabilizer in $G$ of the root $\alpha$.

::: pf-proof

By definition,
\[
H=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q(\alpha)).
\]
Thus an element of $G$ lies in $H$ exactly when it fixes every element of $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$, equivalently when it fixes $\alpha$.
Hence
\[
H=G_\alpha.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The subgroup $H$ is not normal in $G$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose instead that
\[
H\triangleleft G.
\]
Because the action on $\Omega$ is transitive, every point stabilizer has the form
\[
gHg^{-1}
\]
for some $g\in G$.
Normality would make all point stabilizers equal to $H$.
Therefore the kernel of the action, which is the intersection of all point stabilizers, would be
\[
H.
\]
But the action is faithful, so its kernel is trivial.
Thus $H=1$, contradicting the hypothesis that $H$ is nontrivial.
Hence
\[
H\not\triangleleft G.
\]
This proves part (b).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Every Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of $G$ has order $p$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $p$ divides $|G|$, while step [](#s2){.pf-ref} and
\[
|G|=p|H|
\]
show that $p^2$ does not divide $|G|$.
Therefore a Sylow $p$-subgroup has order exactly $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Assume for contradiction that
\[
N_G(P)=P.
\]
Then the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups is $|H|$.

::: pf-proof

The conjugation action of $G$ on its Sylow $p$-subgroups has stabilizer $N_G(P)$ at $P$.
Hence
\[
n_p=[G:N_G(P)].
\]
Under the assumption $N_G(P)=P$, steps [](#s5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1){.pf-ref} give
\[
n_p=\frac{|G|}{|P|}
=\frac{p|H|}{p}
=|H|.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

Under the assumption in step [](#s6){.pf-ref},
\[
H=\{g\in G:o(g)\neq p\}.
\]

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, distinct Sylow $p$-subgroups have order $p$ and intersect only in the identity.
Every nonidentity element of a Sylow $p$-subgroup has order $p$.
Conversely, every element of order $p$ generates a subgroup of order $p$, which is Sylow by step [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

Therefore step [](#s6){.pf-ref} implies that the total number of elements of order $p$ is
\[
n_p(p-1)=|H|(p-1).
\]
Since
\[
|G|=p|H|,
\]
the number of elements whose order is not $p$ is
\[
|G|-|H|(p-1)
=p|H|-|H|(p-1)
=|H|.
\]

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $p\nmid|H|$.
Lagrange's theorem therefore shows that no element of $H$ has order $p$.
Thus
\[
H\subseteq\{g\in G:o(g)\neq p\}.
\]
Both sets have cardinality $|H|$, so they are equal.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The equality in step [](#s7){.pf-ref} contradicts step [](#s4){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof

Element order is invariant under conjugation.
Hence the set
\[
\{g\in G:o(g)\neq p\}
\]
is stable under conjugation by $G$.
If it equals the subgroup $H$, as in step [](#s7){.pf-ref}, then
\[
H\triangleleft G,
\]
contradicting step [](#s4){.pf-ref}.
Therefore the assumption in step [](#s6){.pf-ref} is impossible.
Consequently
\[
N_G(P)\neq P,
\]
which proves part (c).

:::
