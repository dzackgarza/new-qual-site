::: {.problem}
Consider the splitting field $E$ of the polynomial $f(x) = x^4 - 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

(a) Find the degree $[E : \mathbb{Q}]$.

(b) Determine the Galois group of $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ as a subgroup of the symmetric group $S_4$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
\alpha:=\sqrt[4]{5}>0.
\]
The roots of $x^4-5$ are
\[
\alpha,
\qquad
i\alpha,
\qquad
-\alpha,
\qquad
-i\alpha.
\]

<1>1. The splitting field is
\[
E=\mathbb Q(\alpha,i).
\]
::: {.proof}
The field $\mathbb Q(\alpha,i)$ contains all four roots displayed above, so it contains the splitting field.
Conversely, any splitting field contains both $\alpha$ and $i\alpha$, hence also their quotient
\[
\frac{i\alpha}{\alpha}=i.
\]
Therefore it contains $\mathbb Q(\alpha,i)$.
Thus the two fields are equal.
:::

<1>2. One has
\[
[E:\mathbb Q]=8.
\]
::: {.proof}
The polynomial
\[
x^4-5
\]
is Eisenstein at $5$, so it is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
Hence
\[
[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=4.
\]
Since $\alpha$ is real,
\[
\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subseteq\mathbb R.
\]
Therefore
\[
i\notin\mathbb Q(\alpha).
\]
The element $i$ has degree $2$ over $\mathbb Q$, and its polynomial $x^2+1$ remains irreducible over the real field $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$.
Thus
\[
[E:\mathbb Q(\alpha)]=2.
\]
By the tower law,
\[
[E:\mathbb Q]
=[E:\mathbb Q(\alpha)]\,[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]
=2\cdot4=8.
\]
:::

<1>3. There is an automorphism $r\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$ satisfying
\[
r(\alpha)=i\alpha,
\qquad
r(i)=i,
\]
and it has order $4$.
::: {.proof}
The fields $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ and $\mathbb Q(i)$ have intersection $\mathbb Q$: their intersection has degree dividing $2$, and a nontrivial intersection would equal the imaginary quadratic field $\mathbb Q(i)$, impossible because $\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subseteq\mathbb R$.
Therefore
\[
[E:\mathbb Q(i)]=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=4.
\]
Hence the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb Q(i)$ still has degree $4$ and is $x^4-5$.
Sending the root $\alpha$ to the root $i\alpha$ while fixing $\mathbb Q(i)$ therefore defines a $\mathbb Q(i)$-automorphism $r$ of $E$.
Iterating gives
\[
\alpha\mapsto i\alpha\mapsto-\alpha\mapsto-i\alpha\mapsto\alpha,
\]
so $r$ has order $4$.
:::

<1>4. Complex conjugation gives an automorphism $s$ satisfying
\[
s(\alpha)=\alpha,
\qquad
s(i)=-i,
\]
and
\[
srs=r^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $E=\mathbb Q(\alpha,i)$ is stable under complex conjugation, conjugation restricts to a $\mathbb Q$-automorphism $s$ of $E$.
Clearly $s^2=1$.
On the generators,
\[
srs(\alpha)
=sr(\alpha)
=s(i\alpha)
=-i\alpha
=r^{-1}(\alpha),
\]
and
\[
srs(i)=i=r^{-1}(i).
\]
Thus
\[
srs=r^{-1}.
\]
:::

<1>5. With the roots labeled
\[
1:\alpha,
\qquad
2:i\alpha,
\qquad
3:-\alpha,
\qquad
4:-i\alpha,
\]
one has
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)
=\langle(1234),(24)\rangle
\cong D_4.
\]
::: {.proof}
Under the chosen labeling, $r$ acts by
\[
(1234),
\]
while complex conjugation $s$ fixes $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$ and exchanges $i\alpha$ with $-i\alpha$, so it acts by
\[
(24).
\]
The subgroup they generate is the dihedral group
\[
\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle
\]
of order $8$.
By <1>2, the Galois group of the splitting field has order
\[
[E:\mathbb Q]=8.
\]
Hence the subgroup generated by $r$ and $s$ is the entire Galois group.
Therefore, as a subgroup of $S_4$,
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)
=\langle(1234),(24)\rangle.
\]
:::
