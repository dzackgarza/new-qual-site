::: {.problem}
Consider the splitting field $E$ of the polynomial $f(x) = x^4 - 5$ over $\QQ$.

(a) Find the degree $[E : \QQ]$.

(b) Determine the Galois group of $E$ over $\QQ$ as a subgroup of the symmetric group $S_4$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\alpha:=\sqrt[4]{5}>0.
$$
The roots of $x^4-5$ are
$$
\alpha,
\qquad
i\alpha,
\qquad
-\alpha,
\qquad
-i\alpha.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step
The splitting field is
$$
E=\QQ(\alpha,i).
$$

::: pf-proof
The field $\QQ(\alpha,i)$ contains all four roots displayed above, so it contains the splitting field.
Conversely, any splitting field contains both $\alpha$ and $i\alpha$, hence also their quotient
$$
\frac{i\alpha}{\alpha}=i.
$$
Therefore it contains $\QQ(\alpha,i)$.
Thus the two fields are equal.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #degree-eight}
One has
$$
[E:\QQ]=8.
$$

::: pf-proof
The polynomial
$$
x^4-5
$$
is Eisenstein at $5$, so it is irreducible over $\QQ$.
Hence
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4.
$$
Since $\alpha$ is real,
$$
\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq\RR.
$$
Therefore
$$
i\notin\QQ(\alpha).
$$
The element $i$ has degree $2$ over $\QQ$, and its polynomial $x^2+1$ remains irreducible over the real field $\QQ(\alpha)$.
Thus
$$
[E:\QQ(\alpha)]=2.
$$
By the tower law,
$$
[E:\QQ]
=[E:\QQ(\alpha)]\,[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]
=2\cdot4=8.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
There is an automorphism $r\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/\QQ)$ satisfying
$$
r(\alpha)=i\alpha,
\qquad
r(i)=i,
$$
and it has order $4$.

::: pf-proof
The fields $\QQ(\alpha)$ and $\QQ(i)$ have intersection $\QQ$: their intersection has degree dividing $2$, and a nontrivial intersection would equal the imaginary quadratic field $\QQ(i)$, impossible because $\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq\RR$.
Therefore
$$
[E:\QQ(i)]=[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4.
$$
Hence the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ(i)$ still has degree $4$ and is $x^4-5$.
Sending the root $\alpha$ to the root $i\alpha$ while fixing $\QQ(i)$ therefore defines a $\QQ(i)$-automorphism $r$ of $E$.
Iterating gives
$$
\alpha\mapsto i\alpha\mapsto-\alpha\mapsto-i\alpha\mapsto\alpha,
$$
so $r$ has order $4$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Complex conjugation gives an automorphism $s$ satisfying
$$
s(\alpha)=\alpha,
\qquad
s(i)=-i,
$$
and
$$
srs=r^{-1}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Because $E=\QQ(\alpha,i)$ is stable under complex conjugation, conjugation restricts to a $\QQ$-automorphism $s$ of $E$.
Clearly $s^2=1$.
On the generators,
$$
srs(\alpha)
=sr(\alpha)
=s(i\alpha)
=-i\alpha
=r^{-1}(\alpha),
$$
and
$$
srs(i)=i=r^{-1}(i).
$$
Thus
$$
srs=r^{-1}.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
With the roots labeled
$$
1:\alpha,
\qquad
2:i\alpha,
\qquad
3:-\alpha,
\qquad
4:-i\alpha,
$$
one has
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(E/\QQ)
=\langle(1234),(24)\rangle
\cong D_4.
$$

::: pf-proof
Under the chosen labeling, $r$ acts by
$$
(1234),
$$
while complex conjugation $s$ fixes $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$ and exchanges $i\alpha$ with $-i\alpha$, so it acts by
$$
(24).
$$
The subgroup they generate is the dihedral group
$$
\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle
$$
of order $8$.
By step [](#degree-eight){.pf-ref}, the Galois group of the splitting field has order
$$
[E:\QQ]=8.
$$
Hence the subgroup generated by $r$ and $s$ is the entire Galois group.
Therefore, as a subgroup of $S_4$,
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(E/\QQ)
=\langle(1234),(24)\rangle.
$$
:::
