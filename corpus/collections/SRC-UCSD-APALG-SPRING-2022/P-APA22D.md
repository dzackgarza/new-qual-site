::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the group of order $16$ which is generated by $2$ elements $a, b$ that satisfy the relations ($e$ is the identity)
$$
a^8 = e,\quad b^2 = e,\quad b a b^{-1} = a^5.
$$
(Any other relation is a consequence of these relations, i.e., we have described a presentation of $G$.)

(a) Describe all of the $1$-dimensional irreducible complex representations of $G$.

(b) How many irreducible complex representations does $G$ have and what are their dimensions?
:::


::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #linear}

(a) The one-dimensional representations of $G$ are the eight maps $\rho_{\alpha,\beta}$ with $\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(a)=\alpha\in\{1,-1,i,-i\}$ and $\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(b)=\beta\in\{1,-1\}$.

::: pf-proof

Let $\rho\colon G\to\CC^\times$ be a homomorphism and put $\alpha=\rho(a)$, $\beta=\rho(b)$. The relations give $\beta^2=1$ and, since $\CC^\times$ is abelian, $\alpha=\rho(bab^{-1})=\alpha^5$, so $\alpha^4=1$. Conversely, each such pair satisfies $\alpha^8=1$, $\beta^2=1$ and $\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}=\alpha=\alpha^5$, so it defines a homomorphism on the presented group. Distinct pairs give distinct, hence nonisomorphic, one-dimensional representations.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) $G$ has $\boxed{10}$ irreducible complex representations: eight of dimension $1$ and two of dimension $2$.

::: pf-proof

The squares of the irreducible dimensions add up to $\abs G=16$. Step [](#linear){.pf-ref} gives eight of dimension $1$, contributing $8$. Each remaining dimension $d$ divides $16$ and is at least $2$, so the remaining $8$ is a sum of squares from $\{4,16\}$; the only possibility is $4+4$.

:::
