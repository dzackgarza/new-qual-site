::: problem
Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring.
Prove that $R[x]$ and $R[[x]]$ are both Noetherian.
(The first part of the question is asking you to prove the Hilbert Basis Theorem, not to use it!)
:::


::: solution
We prove the two assertions separately.

**1. The polynomial ring \(R[x]\).** Let \(I\subseteq R[x]\) be an ideal. For \(d\ge0\), let
\[
L_d=\{a\in R:\text{there is }f\in I\text{ of degree at most }d\text{ whose }x^d\text{-coefficient is }a\}.
\]
Then \(L_d\) is an ideal of \(R\), and \(L_d\subseteq L_{d+1}\), since multiplying a polynomial by \(x\) preserves its leading coefficient. Because \(R\) is Noetherian, the ascending chain
\[
L_0\subseteq L_1\subseteq L_2\subseteq\cdots
\]
stabilizes, say at \(L_N\).

For each \(0\le d\le N\), choose finitely many polynomials
\[
f_{d1},\ldots,f_{dr_d}\in I
\]
of degree at most \(d\) whose \(x^d\)-coefficients generate \(L_d\). We claim these finitely many polynomials generate \(I\).

Take \(f\in I\), \(f\ne0\), of degree \(m\). If \(m\le N\), its leading coefficient lies in \(L_m\), so subtracting a suitable \(R\)-linear combination of the \(f_{mj}\) lowers the degree. If \(m>N\), stabilization gives \(L_m=L_N\), so the leading coefficient of \(f\) is an \(R\)-linear combination of the \(x^N\)-coefficients of the \(f_{Nj}\); subtracting the corresponding combination of \(x^{m-N}f_{Nj}\) lowers the degree. Induction on degree expresses \(f\) in the ideal generated by the chosen finite set. Hence every ideal of \(R[x]\) is finitely generated, so \(R[x]\) is Noetherian.

**2. The formal power-series ring \(R[[x]]\).** Let \(I\subseteq R[[x]]\) be an ideal. For each \(n\ge0\), define
\[
J_n=\{a\in R:\text{there is }f\in I\cap x^nR[[x]]\text{ whose coefficient of }x^n\text{ is }a\}.
\]
Each \(J_n\) is an ideal, and \(J_n\subseteq J_{n+1}\), because multiplication by \(x\) sends a witness for \(a\in J_n\) to one for \(a\in J_{n+1}\). Since \(R\) is Noetherian, choose \(N\) with
\[
J_N=J_{N+1}=J_{N+2}=\cdots.
\]
For every \(0\le n\le N\), choose finitely many series
\[
g_{n1},\ldots,g_{nr_n}\in I\cap x^nR[[x]]
\]
whose coefficients of \(x^n\) generate \(J_n\). Let \(I_0\) be the ideal generated by all these finitely many \(g_{nj}\). We show \(I=I_0\).

Take \(f\in I\). We cancel its coefficients successively. At stage \(m<N\), after the coefficients of degrees \(<m\) have been killed, the current remainder lies in \(I\cap x^mR[[x]]\); its \(x^m\)-coefficient belongs to \(J_m\), so an \(R\)-linear combination of the \(g_{mj}\) cancels it without changing lower coefficients. At stage \(m\ge N\), the current \(x^m\)-coefficient lies in \(J_m=J_N\), so an \(R\)-linear combination of the shifted series \(x^{m-N}g_{Nj}\) cancels it.

Thus for each degree \(m\) we obtain correction coefficients. For a fixed generator \(g_{nj}\), collect the corrections multiplying its shifts into a formal series \(h_{nj}(x)\in R[[x]]\). Coefficientwise, the recursive cancellation says
\[
f=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{r_n}h_{nj}(x)g_{nj}(x).
\]
(The coefficient of any fixed power of \(x\) involves only finitely many terms, so the identity is well-defined in \(R[[x]]\).) Hence \(f\in I_0\). Therefore \(I=I_0\) is finitely generated.

So both \(R[x]\) and \(R[[x]]\) are Noetherian.
:::
