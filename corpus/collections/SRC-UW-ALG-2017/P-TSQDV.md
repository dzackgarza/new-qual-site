::: {.problem}
Let $H_n$ denote the Heisenberg group of order $n^3$, i.e.,
$$
H_n=\langle a,b,c\mid a^n=b^n=c^n=1,\ ba=cab,\ c\text{ is central}\rangle.
$$

(a) Show that
$$
H_n\cong\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&u&w\\0&1&v\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix} \st u,v,w\in\ZZ/n\ZZ\right\}.
$$

(b) Classify the irreducible complex representations of $H_p$ when $p$ is prime.
:::

::: {.solution}

For $n\ge1$ let
$$
\Gamma_n=\Bigl\{\begin{pmatrix}1&u&w\\0&1&v\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}:u,v,w\in\ZZ/n\ZZ\Bigr\}\subseteq GL_3(\ZZ/n\ZZ).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Part (a): $\Gamma_n$ is a group of order $n^3$ isomorphic to $H_n$.

::: pf-proof

$\Gamma_n$ is closed under multiplication and inversion: for two such matrices $A(u,v,w)$ and $A(u',v',w')$,
$$
A(u,v,w)A(u',v',w')=\begin{pmatrix}1&u+u'&w+w'+uv'\\0&1&v+v'\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}=A(u+u',v+v',w+w'+uv'),
$$
and $A(u,v,w)^{-1}=A(-u,-v,-w+uv)$. So $\Gamma_n$ is a subgroup of $GL_3(\ZZ/n\ZZ)$, and the map $(u,v,w)\mapsto A(u,v,w)$ is a bijection $\Gamma_n\cong(\ZZ/n)^3$, so $\abs{\Gamma_n}=n^3$.

Let $a=A(1,0,0)$, $b=A(0,1,0)$, $c=A(0,0,1)$. Then $a^n=b^n=c^n=1$ since $n=0$ in $\ZZ/n\ZZ$, $c$ is central (adding to $w$ commutes with everything), and
$$
ba=A(0,1,0)A(1,0,0)=A(1,1,1)=A(0,0,1)A(1,0,0)A(0,1,0)=cab .
$$
So there is a surjection $H_n\to\Gamma_n$ from the presented group. Conversely, in $H_n$ the relations show that every element can be written in normal form $c^ka^ib^j$ with $0\le i,j,k<n$: $ba=cab$ moves $b$'s to the right of $a$'s at the cost of powers of the central $c$. Hence $\abs{H_n}\le n^3=\abs{\Gamma_n}$, and a surjection between groups of equal finite order is an isomorphism. So $H_n\cong\Gamma_n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$H_p=[\cdot,\cdot]$-structure: the commutator subgroup is $\langle c\rangle$ and $H_p^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong(\ZZ/p)^2$.

::: pf-proof

From the normal form and the relation $[b,a]=b^{-1}a^{-1}ba=c^{-1}$ (or $c$, up to the inverse convention, which does not matter for the subgroup generated), the commutator subgroup of $H_p$ is generated by $c$, so $H_p/[\cdot,\cdot]=\langle\bar a,\bar b\rangle\cong(\ZZ/p)^2$, of order $p^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Part (b): up to isomorphism, the irreducible complex representations of $H_p$ are $p^2$ characters and $p-1$ representations of dimension $p$; $H_p$ is therefore the complete list, and nothing else occurs.

::: pf-proof

Let $d_1,\dots,d_k$ be the dimensions of the irreducible complex representations, $k$ the number of them ($=$ number of conjugacy classes). By the standard facts for a finite group, $\sum_id_i^2=\abs{H_p}=p^3$ and $k$ equals the number of conjugacy classes.

A one-dimensional representation factors through the abelianisation, which by step [](#s2){.pf-ref} is $(\ZZ/p)^2$, so there are exactly $p^2$ of them.

Let $\rho$ be an irreducible that is not one-dimensional. Then $\rho$ does not factor through the abelianisation, so $\rho(c)\ne\mathrm{Id}$. Since $c$ is central, Schur's lemma gives $\rho(c)=\zeta\,\mathrm{Id}$ with $\zeta$ a $p$-th root of unity, $\zeta\ne1$.

Let $A=\langle a,c\rangle\subseteq H_p$, an abelian subgroup of order $p^2$, hence of index $p$, and let $\chi$ be an irreducible constituent of $\rho|_A$. Then $\chi(c)=\zeta\ne1$, and $\chi$ is one-dimensional because $A$ is abelian. Since $A$ is the stabiliser of $\chi$ under the conjugation action of the abelian group $A$ on its own characters, the representation $\operatorname{Ind}_A^{H_p}\chi$ is irreducible of dimension $[H_p:A]=p$; its character vanishes off $A$ and equals $p\chi$ on $A$, and by Frobenius reciprocity $\rho$ is a constituent of it, so $\rho\cong\operatorname{Ind}_A^{H_p}\chi$ and $\dim\rho=p$.

Dimension count, $\sum_id_i^2=\abs{H_p}$:
$$
p^3=p^2\cdot1^2+\sum_{\dim\rho>1}\dim(\rho)^2=p^2+p^2\cdot\#\{\rho:\dim\rho>1\},
$$
so $\#\{\rho:\dim\rho>1\}=p-1$, each of dimension $p$. Nothing else occurs, and the total number of irreducibles is $p^2+(p-1)=p^2+p-1$, which equals the number of conjugacy classes of $H_p$ (namely $p^2$ classes in the abelianisation directions plus $p-1$ classes of nontrivial central elements), as it must.

Dimension count:
$$
p^3=\sum_i d_i^2=p^2\cdot1^2+\sum_{d_i>1}d_i^2=p^2+p^2\sum_{d_i>1}(d_i/p)^2 .
$$
So $\sum_{d_i>1}(d_i/p)^2=p-1$, and since each term is a positive integer this forces exactly $p-1$ terms each equal to $1$, that is, exactly $p-1$ irreducibles of dimension $p$. Hence no other dimension occurs, and $p^2+(p-1)=p^2+p-1$ irreducibles in all.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Explicit description of the $p-1$ representations of dimension $p$.

::: pf-proof

Let $\zeta\ne1$ be a $p$-th root of unity and let $V=\CC^p$ with basis $e_0,\dots,e_{p-1}$. Define operators
$$
C\,e_j=\zeta e_j,\qquad A\,e_j=e_{j+1\ (\!\!\!\mod p)},\qquad B\,e_j=\zeta^{j}e_j .
$$
Then $A^p=B^p=C^p=1$, $C$ is central, and
$$
BA\,e_j=\zeta^{j+1}e_{j+1}=\zeta\,AB\,e_j=C\,AB\,e_j,
$$
so $BA=CAB$: this is a representation of $H_p$ by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.

It is irreducible: $B$ has $p$ distinct eigenvalues $\zeta^j$, so any invariant subspace is a direct sum of eigenspaces of $B$, each one-dimensional; and $A$ cyclically permutes those eigenspaces (since $AB=\zeta^{-1}BA$ up to the sign convention, $A$ maps the $\zeta^j$-eigenspace to the $\zeta^{j+1}$-eigenspace), so a nonzero invariant subspace is everything.

Distinct $\zeta$ give non-isomorphic representations, since $C$ acts by $\zeta$. This gives the $p-1$ required representations, and by step [](#s3){.pf-ref} the list is complete.

:::
