::: {.problem}
Suppose $A$ is a commutative ring and $M$ is a finitely presented module.
Given any surjection $\phi:A^n\rightarrow M$ from a finite free $A$-module, show that $\ker\phi$ is finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}
Throughout $A$ is a commutative ring, all modules are left $A$-modules, and $M$ is finitely presented: there are a finite free $A^{r}$ and a finite free $A^{m}$ with an exact sequence $A^{m} \xrightarrow{\psi} A^{r} \xrightarrow{\pi} M \to 0$. $\phi\colon A^{n} \to M$ is a given surjection with $n$ finite. The problem is Problem 3 of the Algebra Preliminary Examination 2017 (University of Washington), retained at `assets/attachments/UW-Algebra-Qual-2017.pdf`; no solutions sheet accompanies it, so the argument below is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #presentation}
There are a finite free $A^{r}$ and a surjection $\pi\colon A^{r} \to M$ with $\ker\pi$ finitely generated.

::: pf-proof
This is the definition of finite presentation: $M$ is the cokernel of a map between finite free modules, so there are $m, r < \infty$ and an exact sequence $A^{m} \xrightarrow{\psi} A^{r} \xrightarrow{\pi} M \to 0$. Exactness says $\ker\pi = \operatorname{im}\psi$, which is the image of the finitely generated module $A^{m}$ under the homomorphism $\psi$, hence is generated by the images of the standard basis, a finite set.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #schanuel}
$\ker\phi \oplus A^{r} \cong \ker\pi \oplus A^{n}$.

::: pf-proof
This is Schanuel's lemma, whose proof we recall: let $S \coloneqq \theset{(x,y) \in A^{n}\oplus A^{r} \mid \phi(x) = \pi(y)}$ be the fibre product. The projection $S \to A^{n}$ is surjective — given $x$, choose $y$ with $\pi(y) = \phi(x)$ — with kernel $\theset{0}\oplus\ker\pi \cong \ker\pi$, so $0 \to \ker\pi \to S \to A^{n} \to 0$ is exact and splits because $A^{n}$ is free and hence projective; this gives $S \cong \ker\pi \oplus A^{n}$. Symmetrically the projection $S \to A^{r}$ is surjective with kernel $\ker\phi$, and the sequence $0 \to \ker\phi \to S \to A^{r} \to 0$ splits because $A^{r}$ is projective, giving $S \cong \ker\phi \oplus A^{r}$. Comparing the two descriptions of $S$ gives the isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #both-fg}
$\ker\pi \oplus A^{n}$ is finitely generated.

::: pf-proof
$\ker\pi$ is finitely generated by step [](#presentation){.pf-ref} and $A^{n}$ is finite free; a direct sum of two finitely generated modules is generated by the union of two finite generating sets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #summand}
$\ker\phi$ is finitely generated.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#schanuel){.pf-ref} and [](#both-fg){.pf-ref} the module $\ker\phi \oplus A^{r}$ is finitely generated; let $S$ be a finite generating set. The projection $p\colon \ker\phi \oplus A^{r} \to \ker\phi$ onto the first summand is surjective, so $p(S)$ is a finite set generating $\ker\phi$: every element of $\ker\phi$ is $p$ of an element of $\ker\phi\oplus A^{r}$, which is a finite $A$-combination of the elements of $S$, and $p$ is $A$-linear.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
For every surjection $\phi\colon A^{n} \to M$ from a finite free module, $\ker\phi$ is finitely generated.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#presentation){.pf-ref} to [](#summand){.pf-ref} prove it for the given $\phi$; no hypothesis on $\phi$ beyond surjectivity and the finiteness of $n$ was used, so the statement holds for every such surjection.

:::
