::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a topological space and let $U_1\subseteq U_2\subseteq U_3\subseteq\cdots$ be an increasing open cover of $X$.
Prove that for $n\ge 0$ the singular chain groups of $X$ satisfy $$C_n(X)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}C_n(U_j).$$ To be completely explicit, on the right we really mean $(i_j)_\#C_n(U_j)$ where $i_j:U_j\to X$ is the inclusion map.
:::

::: {.solution}
For each $j$, let
\[
i_j:U_j\hookrightarrow X
\]
denote inclusion.

<1>1. For every singular $n$-simplex
\[
\sigma:\Delta^n\longrightarrow X,
\]
there is an index $j$ such that
\[
\sigma(\Delta^n)\subseteq U_j.
\]
::: {.proof}
The standard simplex $\Delta^n$ is compact, so its continuous image
\[
K=\sigma(\Delta^n)
\]
is compact in $X$.

Because the sets $U_1,U_2,\ldots$ form an open cover of $X$, they also form an open cover of $K$.
Compactness gives indices
\[
j_1,\ldots,j_r
\]
such that
\[
K\subseteq U_{j_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{j_r}.
\]
Let
\[
J=\max\{j_1,\ldots,j_r\}.
\]
Since the cover is increasing,
\[
U_{j_s}\subseteq U_J
\]
for every $s$, and hence
\[
K\subseteq U_J.
\]
:::

<1>2. Every chain in $C_n(X)$ lies in the image of $C_n(U_J)$ for some $J$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
c=\sum_{r=1}^N a_r\sigma_r\in C_n(X),
\qquad a_r\in\mathbb Z,
\]
be a singular $n$-chain.
By definition, this sum is finite.

For each singular simplex $\sigma_r$, <1>1 gives an index $j_r$ with
\[
\sigma_r(\Delta^n)\subseteq U_{j_r}.
\]
Set
\[
J=\max\{j_1,\ldots,j_N\}.
\]
Then every $\sigma_r$ has image in $U_J$.
Thus each $\sigma_r$ factors uniquely as
\[
\Delta^n\xrightarrow{\bar\sigma_r}U_J\xrightarrow{i_J}X,
\]
and
\[
\bar c=\sum_{r=1}^N a_r\bar\sigma_r
\]
is an element of $C_n(U_J)$ satisfying
\[
(i_J)_\#(\bar c)=c.
\]
Therefore
\[
C_n(X)
\subseteq
\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}(i_j)_\#C_n(U_j).
\]
:::

<1>3. Conversely,
\[
\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}(i_j)_\#C_n(U_j)
\subseteq
C_n(X).
\]
::: {.proof}
For every $j$, the inclusion
\[
i_j:U_j\hookrightarrow X
\]
induces a homomorphism
\[
(i_j)_\#:C_n(U_j)\longrightarrow C_n(X).
\]
Hence
\[
(i_j)_\#C_n(U_j)\subseteq C_n(X)
\]
for every $j$.
Taking the union gives the asserted inclusion.
:::

<1>4. Therefore, for every $n\ge 0$,
\[
C_n(X)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}(i_j)_\#C_n(U_j).
\]
::: {.proof}
The two inclusions are <1>2 and <1>3.
