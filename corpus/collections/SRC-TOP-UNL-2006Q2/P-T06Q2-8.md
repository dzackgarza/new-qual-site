::: {.problem}
Use covering space theory to prove that every map $\RP^2\to S^1\times S^1$ is homotopic to a constant map.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $f\colon\RP^2\to S^1\times S^1$ be continuous, fix $x_0\in\RP^2$, and let $p\colon\RR^2\to S^1\times S^1$ be the universal covering.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$f_*\colon\pi_1(\RP^2,x_0)\to\pi_1(S^1\times S^1,f(x_0))$ is trivial.

::: pf-proof

$\pi_1(\RP^2,x_0)\cong\ZZ/2$ and $\pi_1(S^1\times S^1)\cong\ZZ^2$.
For the nontrivial element $a$, $2f_*(a)=f_*(2a)=0$, and $\ZZ^2$ is torsion-free, so $f_*(a)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$f$ lifts to $\widetilde f\colon\RP^2\to\RR^2$ with $p\circ\widetilde f=f$.

::: pf-proof

Choose $\widetilde y_0\in p^{-1}(f(x_0))$.
$\RP^2$ is path-connected and locally path-connected, and by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $f_*\pi_1(\RP^2,x_0)=0\subseteq p_*\pi_1(\RR^2,\widetilde y_0)$, so the lifting criterion applies.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

For $c\in\RR^2$, $H(x,t)=(1-t)\widetilde f(x)+tc$ is a homotopy from $\widetilde f$ to the constant map at $c$.
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $p\circ H$ is a homotopy from $f$ to the constant map at $p(c)$.

:::
