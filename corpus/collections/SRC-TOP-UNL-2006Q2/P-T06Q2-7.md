::: {.problem}
Let $f:S^2\to S^2$ be such that $f(x)\ne f(-x)$ for all $x\in S^2$.
Prove that $f$ is surjective.
**Hint:** Degree theory will probably not be helpful.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose $f$ misses a point $p\in S^2$, and let $\sigma\colon S^2\setminus\{p\}\to\RR^2$ be stereographic projection from $p$.
Let $E=\{x\in S^2:x_3=0\}\cong S^1$ be the equator.

<1>1. $h(x)=\dfrac{g(x)-g(-x)}{\norm{g(x)-g(-x)}}$, with $g=\sigma\circ f$, defines a continuous map $h\colon S^2\to S^1$ with $h(-x)=-h(x)$.

::: {.proof}
$\sigma$ is injective and $f(x)\neq f(-x)$, so $g(x)\neq g(-x)$ and $h$ is defined and continuous.
Exchanging $x$ and $-x$ negates the numerator and fixes the denominator.
:::

<1>2. $k=h|_E\colon E\to S^1$ has odd degree.

::: {.proof}
Identify both circles with $\{e^{it}:t\in\RR\}$, and lift $t\mapsto k(e^{it})$ through $u\mapsto e^{iu}$ to $\phi\colon\RR\to\RR$ with $k(e^{it})=e^{i\phi(t)}$.
By step <1>1, $k(e^{i(t+\pi)})=-k(e^{it})$, so $\phi(t+\pi)-\phi(t)-\pi\in2\pi\ZZ$.
This is a continuous function of $t$ with values in a discrete set, hence constant: $\phi(t+\pi)=\phi(t)+(2m+1)\pi$ for some $m\in\ZZ$.
Then $\phi(t+2\pi)=\phi(t)+2\pi(2m+1)$, so $\deg k=2m+1$.
:::

<1>3. $k$ has degree $0$.

::: {.proof}
$k$ extends over the closed upper hemisphere $D_+\cong D^2$ by $h|_{D_+}$.
Composing a contraction of $D_+$ with $h$ and restricting to $E$ gives a homotopy from $k$ to a constant map, and a nullhomotopic map $S^1\to S^1$ has degree $0$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2 and <1>3 contradict each other, so $f$ misses no point of $S^2$.
:::
