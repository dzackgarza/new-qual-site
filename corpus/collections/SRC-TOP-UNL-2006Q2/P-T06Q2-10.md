::: {.problem}
The suspension $SX$ of a topological space $X$ is the space $(X\times I)/\sim$ where $\sim$ is the equivalence relation generated by $(0,x)\sim(0,x')$ and $(1,x)\sim(1,x')$ for all $x,x'\in X$.
Compute the homology groups of $SX$ in terms of those of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
q:X\times I\longrightarrow SX
\]
be the quotient map.

<1>1. If $X=\varnothing$, then $SX=\varnothing$ and
\[
H_n(SX)=0
\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Henceforth assume that $X\ne\varnothing$.
::: {.proof}
If $X=\varnothing$, then $X\times I=\varnothing$, so its quotient $SX$ is empty.
The singular chain groups of the empty space are zero in every nonnegative degree, hence so are its homology groups.
:::

Define
\[
A=q\bigl(X\times[0,2/3)\bigr),
\qquad
B=q\bigl(X\times(1/3,1]\bigr).
\]

<1>2. The sets $A$ and $B$ are open in $SX$, they cover $SX$, and each is contractible.
::: {.proof}
The subsets
\[
X\times[0,2/3)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
X\times(1/3,1]
\]
are open in $X\times I$ because $[0,2/3)$ and $(1/3,1]$ are open in the subspace topology of $I$.
They are saturated for the equivalence relation defining $SX$.
Thus the quotient topology makes $A$ and $B$ open.
Their union is all of $SX$.

The set $A$ contracts to the lower suspension point by
\[
q(x,t)\longmapsto q(x,(1-s)t),
\qquad 0\le s\le1.
\]
This is well-defined because all points with $t=0$ already represent the same point.
Similarly, $B$ contracts to the upper suspension point, for example by
\[
q(x,t)\longmapsto q\bigl(x,1-(1-s)(1-t)\bigr).
\]
Hence both $A$ and $B$ are contractible.
:::

<1>3. The intersection $A\cap B$ deformation retracts onto a copy of $X$.
::: {.proof}
No quotient identifications occur for parameters strictly between $0$ and $1$.
Consequently
\[
A\cap B
\cong
X\times(1/3,2/3).
\]
The homotopy
\[
(x,t)\longmapsto\bigl(x,(1-s)t+s/2\bigr)
\]
deformation retracts this product onto
\[
X\times\{1/2\}\cong X.
\]
Thus
\[
\widetilde H_k(A\cap B)\cong\widetilde H_k(X)
\]
for every $k\ge0$.
:::

<1>4. For every $n\ge1$ there is a natural isomorphism
\[
\widetilde H_n(SX)
\cong
\widetilde H_{n-1}(X).
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply the reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence to the open cover
\[
SX=A\cup B.
\]
Since $A$ and $B$ are contractible by <1>2,
\[
\widetilde H_k(A)=\widetilde H_k(B)=0
\]
for every $k$.
For $n\ge1$, the relevant part of the exact sequence is therefore
\[
0
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_n(SX)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
\widetilde H_{n-1}(A\cap B)
\longrightarrow
0.
\]
Hence the connecting map is an isomorphism.
Using <1>3 gives
\[
\widetilde H_n(SX)
\cong
\widetilde H_{n-1}(X).
\]
:::

<1>5. The suspension $SX$ is path-connected, so
\[
H_0(SX)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
Fix $x_0\in X$.
Every point of $SX$ is represented by some $(x,t)$.
The path
\[
s\longmapsto q(x,(1-s)t)
\]
joins $q(x,t)$ to the lower suspension point $q(x,0)$.
Thus every point is path-connected to that common suspension point, and $SX$ is path-connected.
Therefore its zeroth singular homology is $\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>6. For nonempty $X$, the ordinary homology groups of the suspension are
\[
H_n(SX)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,\\
\widetilde H_0(X), & n=1,\\
H_{n-1}(X), & n\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\widetilde H_n(SX)\cong\widetilde H_{n-1}(X)
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
::: {.proof}
The case $n=0$ is <1>5. For $n=1$, <1>4 gives
\[
H_1(SX)=\widetilde H_1(SX)
\cong
\widetilde H_0(X),
\]
since reduced and ordinary homology agree in positive degrees.

For $n\ge2$, again by <1>4,
\[
H_n(SX)=\widetilde H_n(SX)
\cong
\widetilde H_{n-1}(X)
=H_{n-1}(X),
\]
because $n-1\ge1$.
:::
