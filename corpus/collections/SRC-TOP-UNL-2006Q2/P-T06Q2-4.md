::: {.problem}
A topological space $X$ is called locally connected if for every $x\in X$ and $O$ a neighborhood of $x$ there exists a connected open set $V$ with $x\in V\subseteq O$.
Prove that a compact, locally connected space has a finite number of components.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every connected component of $X$ is open.
::: {.proof}
Let $C$ be a connected component of $X$ and let $x\in C$.
Apply local connectedness with the neighborhood $O=X$ of $x$.
There is a connected open set $V_x$ such that
\[
x\in V_x\subseteq X.
\]

The connected sets $C$ and $V_x$ meet at $x$, so their union
\[
C\cup V_x
\]
is connected.
Since $C$ is a maximal connected subset of $X$, it follows that
\[
V_x\subseteq C.
\]
Thus every $x\in C$ has an open neighborhood $V_x$ contained in $C$.
Hence
\[
C=\bigcup_{x\in C}V_x
\]
is open in $X$.
:::

<1>2. The collection of connected components of $X$ is an open cover of $X$.
::: {.proof}
Every point of a topological space lies in a unique connected component, so the components cover $X$.
By <1>1 each component is open.
:::

<1>3. The space $X$ has only finitely many connected components.
::: {.proof}
Since $X$ is compact, the open cover in <1>2 has a finite subcover
\[
C_1,\dots,C_r,
\]
where each $C_i$ is a connected component.

Distinct connected components are disjoint.
If there were another component $D$ distinct from every $C_i$, then
\[
D\cap C_i=\varnothing
\qquad (1\le i\le r),
\]
so no point of the nonempty set $D$ would lie in
\[
C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_r=X,
\]
a contradiction.
Therefore $C_1,\dots,C_r$ are all the connected components of $X$, and there are finitely many.
:::
:::
