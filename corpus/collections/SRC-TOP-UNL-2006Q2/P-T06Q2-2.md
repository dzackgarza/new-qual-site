::: {.problem}
(a) Define what it means for a topological space $X$ to be **normal**.

(b) Prove that a closed subspace $Y \subseteq X$ of a normal space $X$ is normal.

(c) Suppose that $X$ is a normal space, $C \subseteq X$ is closed, and $C \subseteq O_1 \cup O_2$ where $O_1, O_2 \subseteq X$ are open.
Prove that there exist closed sets $C_1, C_2 \subseteq X$ with $C = C_1 \cup C_2$ and $C_i \subseteq O_i$ for $i = 1, 2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. (a) A space $X$ is \dfn{normal} if for all disjoint closed $A,B\subseteq X$ there are disjoint open $U,V\subseteq X$ with $A\subseteq U$ and $B\subseteq V$.

<1>2. (b) A closed subspace $Y$ of a normal space $X$ is normal.

::: {.proof}
Let $A,B\subseteq Y$ be disjoint and closed in $Y$.
Since $Y$ is closed in $X$, $A$ and $B$ are closed in $X$, so there are disjoint open $U\supseteq A$ and $V\supseteq B$ in $X$.
Then $U\cap Y$ and $V\cap Y$ are disjoint open subsets of $Y$ containing $A$ and $B$.
:::

<1>3. (c) With $X$, $C$, $O_1$, $O_2$ as in (c), the sets $C_1=C\setminus V$ and $C_2=C\setminus U$ have the required properties, where $U\supseteq C\setminus O_2$ and $V\supseteq C\setminus O_1$ are disjoint open sets.

<2>1. $A=C\setminus O_2$ and $B=C\setminus O_1$ are disjoint closed subsets of $X$, so $U$ and $V$ exist.

::: {.proof}
$A=C\cap(X\setminus O_2)$ and $B=C\cap(X\setminus O_1)$ are closed, and $A\cap B=C\setminus(O_1\cup O_2)=\varnothing$ because $C\subseteq O_1\cup O_2$.
Normality of $X$ gives $U$ and $V$.
:::

<2>2. $C_1$ and $C_2$ are closed, $C_1\subseteq O_1$, $C_2\subseteq O_2$, and $C=C_1\cup C_2$.

::: {.proof}
$C_1=C\cap(X\setminus V)$ and $C_2=C\cap(X\setminus U)$ are closed.
Since $C\setminus O_1=B\subseteq V$, every point of $C\setminus V$ lies in $O_1$; likewise $C\setminus O_2=A\subseteq U$ gives $C_2\subseteq O_2$.
Finally $C_1\cup C_2=C\setminus(U\cap V)=C$ because $U\cap V=\varnothing$.
:::

<2>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <2>1 and <2>2 prove the claim of step <1>3.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1, <1>2, and <1>3 answer (a), (b), and (c).
:::
