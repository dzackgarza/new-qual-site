::: {.problem}
(a) Define what it means for a topological space $X$ to be **normal**.
(b) Prove that a closed subspace $Y \subseteq X$ of a normal space $X$ is normal.
(c) Suppose that $X$ is a normal space, $C \subseteq X$ is closed, and $C \subseteq O_1 \cup O_2$ where $O_1, O_2 \subseteq X$ are open.
Prove that there exist closed sets $C_1, C_2 \subseteq X$ with $C = C_1 \cup C_2$ and $C_i \subseteq O_i$ for $i = 1, 2$ (Two-Set Shrinking Lemma).
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Define normal spaces, prove closed subspaces inherit normality, and prove the two-set shrinking lemma for closed sets.

<1>1. Part (a): Definition of a Normal Space:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. A topological space $X$ is called **normal** (or $T_4$ when combined with $T_1$) if for every pair of disjoint closed subsets $A, B \subset X$ ($A \cap B = \varnothing$), there exist disjoint open subsets $U, V \subset X$ such that:
        $$A \subseteq U, \qquad B \subseteq V, \qquad U \cap V = \varnothing.$$

<1>2. Part (b): Closed Subspaces of Normal Spaces are Normal:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $X$ be a normal space, and let $Y \subseteq X$ be a **closed** subspace endowed with the subspace topology.
    <2>2. Let $A, B \subset Y$ be disjoint closed subsets of $Y$ ($A \cap B = \varnothing$).
    <2>3. Since $Y$ is closed in $X$, a subset $S \subset Y$ is closed in the subspace topology if and only if $S = F \cap Y$ for some closed set $F \subset X$.
    <2>4. Since the intersection of two closed sets in $X$ is closed in $X$, $A$ and $B$ are **closed subsets of $X$**.
    <2>5. Since $A, B \subset X$ are disjoint closed subsets of the normal space $X$, by normality of $X$ there exist disjoint open sets $U, V \subseteq X$ such that:
        $$A \subseteq U, \qquad B \subseteq V, \qquad U \cap V = \varnothing.$$
    <2>6. Define $U_Y \coloneqq U \cap Y$ and $V_Y \coloneqq V \cap Y$.
    <2>7. Then $U_Y$ and $V_Y$ are open in $Y$, $A \subseteq U_Y$, $B \subseteq V_Y$, and:
        $$U_Y \cap V_Y = (U \cap Y) \cap (V \cap Y) = (U \cap V) \cap Y = \varnothing \cap Y = \varnothing.$$
    <2>8. Thus $Y$ is a normal topological space.

<1>3. Part (c): Two-Set Shrinking Lemma:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $X$ be normal, $C \subseteq X$ closed, and $C \subseteq O_1 \cup O_2$ with $O_1, O_2$ open in $X$.
    <2>2. Consider the two disjoint closed sets in $X$:
        - $A \coloneqq C \setminus O_2 = C \cap (X \setminus O_2)$ (closed, since $C$ is closed and $X \setminus O_2$ is closed).
        - $B \coloneqq C \setminus O_1 = C \cap (X \setminus O_1)$ (closed, since $C$ is closed and $X \setminus O_1$ is closed).
    <2>3. Notice that $A \cap B = C \cap (X \setminus O_2) \cap (X \setminus O_1) = C \setminus (O_1 \cup O_2) = \varnothing$ since $C \subseteq O_1 \cup O_2$.
    <2>4. Also, $A = C \setminus O_2 \subseteq O_1$ (since if $x \in C$ and $x \notin O_2$, then $x \in O_1$).
    <2>5. Since $A$ and $B$ are disjoint closed sets in the normal space $X$, there exist disjoint open sets $U, V \subset X$ with:
        $$A \subseteq U, \qquad B \subseteq V, \qquad U \cap V = \varnothing.$$
    <2>6. Define:
        $$C_1 \coloneqq C \setminus V = C \cap (X \setminus V), \qquad C_2 \coloneqq C \setminus U = C \cap (X \setminus U).$$
    <2>7. Since $U$ and $V$ are open in $X$, $C_1$ and $C_2$ are **closed subsets of $X$**.
    <2>8. **Verification of $C_1 \subseteq O_1$:**
        - $B = C \setminus O_1 \subseteq V \implies C \setminus V \subseteq O_1 \implies C_1 \subseteq O_1$.
    <2>9. **Verification of $C_2 \subseteq O_2$:**
        - $A = C \setminus O_2 \subseteq U \implies C \setminus U \subseteq O_2 \implies C_2 \subseteq O_2$.
    <2>10. **Verification of $C = C_1 \cup C_2$:**
        - $C_1 \cup C_2 = (C \setminus V) \cup (C \setminus U) = C \setminus (U \cap V) = C \setminus \varnothing = C$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    Normal spaces separate disjoint closed sets; closed subspaces are normal because their closed sets are closed in $X$; and $C_1 = C \setminus V, C_2 = C \setminus U$ satisfies the shrinking lemma. Q.E.D.
:::
