::: problem
Let $f$ be analytic in a region $D$ and $\gamma$ a rectifiable curve in $D$ with interior in $D$.

Prove that if $f(z)$ is real for all $z\in \gamma$, then $f$ is constant.
:::

::: solution
Let $\Omega$ be an interior component bounded by $\gamma$. Since $f$ is analytic on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega$, its imaginary part
\[
v(z)=\operatorname{Im}f(z)
\]
is harmonic on $\Omega$ and continuous on $\overline\Omega$.

By hypothesis, $f$ is real-valued on $\gamma$, so
\[
v=0
\]
on $\partial\Omega$. The maximum and minimum principles for harmonic functions therefore give
\[
v\equiv0
\]
on $\Omega$. Hence
\[
f(\Omega)\subset\mathbb R.
\]

If $f$ were nonconstant on $\Omega$, the open mapping theorem would imply that $f(\Omega)$ is open in $\mathbb C$, which is impossible because $\mathbb R$ has empty interior in $\mathbb C$. Thus $f$ is constant on the nonempty open set $\Omega$.

Finally, $D$ is connected and $f$ is analytic on $D$, so the identity theorem implies that $f$ is constant on all of $D$.
:::
