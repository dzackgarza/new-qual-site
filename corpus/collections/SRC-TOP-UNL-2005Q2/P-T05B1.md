::: {.problem}
Find a cell structure for the quotient space obtained by identifying two distinct points $a,b$ in a 2-torus to a third point $c$ in a 2-sphere, and compute a presentation for the fundamental group of this space.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $Q$ denote the quotient space.

<1>1. Choose a CW structure on the torus with two $0$-cells $a,b$, three $1$-cells
\[
r:a\longrightarrow b,
\qquad
s:b\longrightarrow a,
\qquad
t:a\longrightarrow a,
\]
and one $2$-cell attached along
\[
(rs)t(rs)^{-1}t^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Start with the standard CW structure on $T^2$ having one vertex $a$, loop $1$-cells $x,t$, and one $2$-cell attached by the commutator
\[
xtx^{-1}t^{-1}.
\]
Choose this structure so that the marked point $b$ lies in the interior of the $x$-circle, and subdivide that $1$-cell at $b$ into oriented edges $r$ and $s$.
Then $x$ is the edge path $rs$, so subdivision changes the attaching word to
\[
(rs)t(rs)^{-1}t^{-1}.
\]
Subdivision does not change the underlying torus.
:::

<1>2. Give $S^2$ its standard CW structure with the marked point $c$ as its unique $0$-cell and one $2$-cell attached by the constant map.
::: {.proof}
The quotient
\[
D^2/\partial D^2
\]
is $S^2$.
Thus one $0$-cell together with one $2$-cell whose boundary is sent to that vertex is a CW structure on $S^2$, and the vertex can be chosen to be $c$.
:::

<1>3. After identifying $a,b,c$ to a single point $v$, these structures give $Q$ a CW structure with
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{dimension}&\text{cells}\\ \hline
0&v\\
1&r,s,t\\
2&e_T,e_S,
\end{array}
\]
where $e_T$ is attached by
\[
(rs)t(rs)^{-1}t^{-1}
\]
and $e_S$ is attached constantly at $v$.
::: {.proof}
The quotient only identifies the three chosen $0$-cells.
All three torus $1$-cells consequently become loops based at $v$.
The torus $2$-cell keeps the attaching edge path from <1>1, while the sphere $2$-cell keeps its constant attaching map from <1>2. Thus the displayed cells and attaching maps describe the quotient itself.
:::

<1>4. The fundamental group has presentation
\[
\pi_1(Q,v)
\cong
\left\langle r,s,t\ \middle|\ (rs)t(rs)^{-1}t^{-1}=1\right\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is a wedge of the three loops $r,s,t$, hence has free fundamental group on these generators.
Attaching $e_T$ imposes the relation represented by its attaching loop.
The constant attaching map of $e_S$ represents the identity element and therefore imposes no additional relation.
The cellular form of the Seifert--van Kampen theorem gives the stated presentation.
:::

<1>5. Equivalently,
\[
\pi_1(Q)
\cong
\left\langle x,y,z\ \middle|\ [x,y]=1\right\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z^2*\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
In the presentation of <1>4 introduce
\[
x=rs,
\qquad
y=t,
\qquad
z=r.
\]
Then $s=z^{-1}x$, so the generators $r,s,t$ can be eliminated in favor of $x,y,z$, and the sole relation becomes
\[
[x,y]=1.
\]
Hence
\[
\pi_1(Q)
\cong
\langle x,y\mid[x,y]\rangle *\langle z\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z^2*\mathbb Z.
\]
:::
