::: {.problem}
Let $A$ and $B$ be subsets of a topological space $X$.
Suppose that $p$ is a limit point of the subset $A\cup B$.
Show that $p$ is also a limit point of $A$ or $B$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Recall that $p$ is a limit point of $C\subseteq X$ when every neighborhood of $p$ meets $C\setminus\{p\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $p$ is a limit point of neither $A$ nor $B$, then some neighborhood $W$ of $p$ satisfies $(W\setminus\{p\})\cap(A\cup B)=\varnothing$.

::: pf-proof

There are neighborhoods $U$, $V$ of $p$ with $(U\setminus\{p\})\cap A=\varnothing$ and $(V\setminus\{p\})\cap B=\varnothing$.
$W=U\cap V$ is a neighborhood of $p$, and $W\setminus\{p\}$ lies in both $U\setminus\{p\}$ and $V\setminus\{p\}$, so it misses $A$ and $B$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Since $p$ is a limit point of $A\cup B$, no neighborhood $W$ as in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} exists, so $p$ is a limit point of $A$ or of $B$.

:::
