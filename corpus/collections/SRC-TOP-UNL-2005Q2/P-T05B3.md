::: {.problem}
Find a $\Delta$-complex structure on the space $X$ obtained by identifying three distinct points $a,b,c$ in the 2-sphere to a point, and compute the simplicial homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Model $S^2$ as the boundary of a tetrahedron with vertices
\[
0=a,
\qquad
1=b,
\qquad
2=c,
\qquad
3=d.
\]
After identifying $0,1,2$ to a single vertex $v$, the quotient is a $\Delta$-complex with two vertices, six edges, and four $2$-simplices.
::: {.proof}
Choose a triangulation of $S^2$ for which the three prescribed distinct points are three vertices of a tetrahedral model; equivalently, first carry the ordered triple $(a,b,c)$ by a homeomorphism of $S^2$ to three vertices of the boundary of a tetrahedron.

Quotienting the three vertices gives a valid $\Delta$-complex structure: the interiors of all edges and faces remain embedded, while their vertices are allowed to be identified in a $\Delta$-complex.
No open simplex is otherwise identified.
:::

Orient every edge $e_{ij}$ from the lower-numbered vertex to the higher-numbered vertex and write
\[
x=e_{01},\quad
y=e_{12},\quad
z=e_{02},\quad
p=e_{03},\quad
q=e_{13},\quad
r=e_{23}.
\]
Let the oriented faces be
\[
f_{012},\quad f_{013},\quad f_{023},\quad f_{123}.
\]

<1>2. The simplicial chain groups are
\[
C_2\cong\mathbb Z^4,
\qquad
C_1\cong\mathbb Z^6,
\qquad
C_0\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\]
and $C_k=0$ for $k\ge3$.
The first boundary map is
\[
\partial_1x=\partial_1y=\partial_1z=0,
\qquad
\partial_1p=\partial_1q=\partial_1r=d-v.
\]
::: {.proof}
The edges $x,y,z$ have both endpoints among $0,1,2$, so both endpoints become $v$.
The other three edges are oriented from $v$ to $d$.
The ranks of the chain groups are the numbers of open simplices in each dimension.
:::

<1>3. Consequently
\[
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
and
\[
\ker\partial_1
=
\left\langle
x,y,z,q-p,r-p
\right\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z^5.
\]
::: {.proof}
The image of $\partial_1$ is generated by $d-v$, so
\[
C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1
\cong
(\mathbb Zv\oplus\mathbb Zd)/\langle d-v\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

For a chain
\[
\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma z+\lambda p+\mu q+\nu r,
\]
its boundary is
\[
(\lambda+\mu+\nu)(d-v).
\]
Thus it is a cycle exactly when $\lambda+\mu+\nu=0$, and then its $p,q,r$ part is uniquely a linear combination of $q-p$ and $r-p$.
This gives the stated basis of $\ker\partial_1$.
:::

<1>4. The second boundary map is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_2 f_{012}&=y-z+x,\\
\partial_2 f_{013}&=q-p+x,\\
\partial_2 f_{023}&=r-p+z,\\
\partial_2 f_{123}&=r-q+y.
\end{aligned}
\]
::: {.proof}
For an oriented simplex $[i,j,k]$ with $i<j<k$,
\[
\partial[i,j,k]=[j,k]-[i,k]+[i,j].
\]
Substituting the six edge names gives the four displayed formulas.
:::

<1>5. The first homology group is
\[
H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the basis
\[
x,y,z,q-p,r-p
\]
of $\ker\partial_1$ from <1>3.
The first three boundaries in <1>4 impose the relations
\[
z=x+y,
\qquad
q-p=-x,
\qquad
r-p=-z=-(x+y).
\]
After these substitutions the fourth boundary becomes
\[
(r-p)-(q-p)+y
=-(x+y)+x+y
=0,
\]
so it imposes no further relation.
Therefore the quotient
\[
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\]
is freely generated by the classes of $x$ and $y$.
Hence $H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2$.
:::

<1>6. The second homology group is
\[
H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $C_3=0$,
\[
H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2.
\]
For integers $A,B,C,D$, apply <1>4 to
\[
A f_{012}+B f_{013}+C f_{023}+D f_{123}.
\]
Equating the coefficients of $x,y,z,p,q,r$ to zero gives
\[
B=-A,
\qquad
C=A,
\qquad
D=-A.
\]
Thus
\[
\ker\partial_2
=
\mathbb Z\bigl(f_{012}-f_{013}+f_{023}-f_{123}\bigr),
\]
which is infinite cyclic.
:::

<1>7. Altogether,
\[
H_k(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&k=1,\\
\mathbb Z,&k=2,\\
0,&k\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>3, <1>5, and <1>6; there are no simplices in dimensions at least $3$.
:::
