::: {.problem}
Find a $\Delta$-complex structure on the space $X$ obtained by identifying three distinct points $a,b,c$ in the 2-sphere to a point, and compute the simplicial homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A homeomorphism of $S^2$ carries $a,b,c$ to three vertices of the boundary of a tetrahedron; number the vertices $0=a$, $1=b$, $2=c$, $3=d$.
Orient each edge from the lower- to the higher-numbered vertex and write $x=e_{01}$, $y=e_{12}$, $z=e_{02}$, $p=e_{03}$, $q=e_{13}$, $r=e_{23}$, with faces $f_{012},f_{013},f_{023},f_{123}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$X$ is a $\Delta$-complex with vertices $v$ (the image of $0,1,2$) and $d$, the six edges $x,y,z,p,q,r$, and the four faces.

::: pf-proof

The identification affects only vertices, so each open edge and open face is still embedded, and the vertex orders are inherited from the tetrahedron.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The boundary maps are $\partial_1x=\partial_1y=\partial_1z=0$, $\partial_1p=\partial_1q=\partial_1r=d-v$, and
$$\partial_2f_{012}=y-z+x,\quad \partial_2f_{013}=q-p+x,\quad \partial_2f_{023}=r-p+z,\quad \partial_2f_{123}=r-q+y.$$

::: pf-proof

The edges $x,y,z$ have both endpoints at $v$, and $p,q,r$ run from $v$ to $d$.
For $i<j<k$, $\partial[i,j,k]=[j,k]-[i,k]+[i,j]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$H_0(X)\cong\ZZ$ and $\ker\partial_1$ is free on $x,y,z,q-p,r-p$.

::: pf-proof

$\operatorname{im}\partial_1=\ZZ(d-v)$, so $H_0(X)=(\ZZ v\oplus\ZZ d)/\langle d-v\rangle\cong\ZZ$.
The chain $\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma z+\lambda p+\mu q+\nu r$ has boundary $(\lambda+\mu+\nu)(d-v)$, so it is a cycle exactly when $\lambda+\mu+\nu=0$, and then $\lambda p+\mu q+\nu r=\mu(q-p)+\nu(r-p)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

$H_1(X)\cong\ZZ^2$.

::: pf-proof

In the basis of step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the first three boundaries of step [](#s2){.pf-ref} give the relations $z=x+y$, $q-p=-x$, and $r-p=-(x+y)$.
The fourth, $(r-p)-(q-p)+y$, then equals $0$ and adds no relation.
So $\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2$ is free on the classes of $x$ and $y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

$H_2(X)\cong\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-simplices, so $H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2$.
For $Af_{012}+Bf_{013}+Cf_{023}+Df_{123}$, the coefficients of $x$, $z$, $y$ in its boundary give $B=-A$, $C=A$, $D=-A$, and then the coefficients of $p,q,r$ vanish.
So $\ker\partial_2=\ZZ(f_{012}-f_{013}+f_{023}-f_{123})$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives the $\Delta$-complex structure, and steps [](#s3){.pf-ref}, [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} give
$$H_k(X;\ZZ)\cong\begin{cases}\ZZ,&k=0,\\\ZZ^2,&k=1,\\\ZZ,&k=2,\\0,&k\ge3.\end{cases}$$

:::
