::: {.problem}
Show, using covering spaces, that the fundamental group of the Klein bottle is not abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K$ be the Klein bottle.

<1>1. The plane $\mathbb R^2$ is a universal covering space of $K$ for which the maps
\[
A(x,y)=(x+1,y)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B(x,y)=(-x,y+1)
\]
are deck transformations.
::: {.proof}
Let $\Gamma$ be the group of homeomorphisms of $\mathbb R^2$ generated by $A$ and $B$.
The unit square is a fundamental region for this action.

The transformation $A$ identifies the vertical sides by
\[
(0,y)\sim(1,y).
\]
For the horizontal sides,
\[
B(x,0)=(-x,1),
\]
and applying $A$ once gives
\[
(-x,1)\sim(1-x,1).
\]
Thus the bottom and top sides are identified with reversed orientation.
These are exactly the usual square identifications for the Klein bottle, so
\[
K\cong\mathbb R^2/\Gamma.
\]

The relation
\[
BA=A^{-1}B
\]
puts every element of $\Gamma$ in the form $A^mB^n$ with $m,n\in\mathbb Z$.
Since
\[
B^n(x,y)=(\pm x,y+n),
\]
an element $A^mB^n$ can fix a point only if $n=0$; then it is the translation $A^m$, which can fix a point only if $m=0$.
Thus the action is free.
Moreover, a sufficiently small Euclidean ball about any point is disjoint from all its nontrivial translates: if $n\neq0$ the centers differ in vertical coordinate by a nonzero integer, while if $n=0$ and $m\neq0$ they differ horizontally by a nonzero integer.
Hence the action is properly discontinuous.

It follows that the quotient projection
\[
p:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow K
\]
is a covering map and every element of $\Gamma$, in particular $A$ and $B$, is a deck transformation.
Since $\mathbb R^2$ is simply connected, this covering is universal.
:::

<1>2. The deck transformations $A$ and $B$ do not commute.
::: {.proof}
The inverse of $B$ is
\[
B^{-1}(x,y)=(-x,y-1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
(BAB^{-1})(x,y)
&=BA(-x,y-1)\\
&=B(1-x,y-1)\\
&=(x-1,y)\\
&=A^{-1}(x,y).
\end{aligned}
\]
But $A^{-1}\neq A$; for example,
\[
A(0,0)=(1,0)
\qquad\text{whereas}\qquad
A^{-1}(0,0)=(-1,0).
\]
Hence
\[
BAB^{-1}\neq A,
\]
so $BA\neq AB$.
:::

<1>3. Consequently $\pi_1(K)$ is nonabelian.
::: {.proof}
For a universal covering $p:\widetilde K\to K$, the deck-transformation group is isomorphic to $\pi_1(K)$.
By <1>1 the present universal cover has the noncommuting deck transformations $A$ and $B$ from <1>2. Thus its deck-transformation group is nonabelian, and therefore so is $\pi_1(K)$.
:::
