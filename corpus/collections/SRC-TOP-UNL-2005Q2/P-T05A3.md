::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a connected space with connected subset $Y$, and suppose that $X\setminus Y$ is not connected, with $X\setminus Y=A\cup B$ a separation of $X\setminus Y$.
Show that if $B$ is open in $X$, then $A\cup Y$ is a connected subset of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
\[
Z=A\cup Y.
\]
Since $X\setminus Y=A\sqcup B$, we have
\[
Z=X\setminus B.
\]

<1>1. The subspace $Z$ is closed in $X$.
::: {.proof}
The hypothesis says that $B$ is open in $X$, and $Z=X\setminus B$.
:::

<1>2. Suppose, for contradiction, that $Z$ is disconnected, and write
\[
Z=C\sqcup D
\]
as a separation of $Z$.
After interchanging $C$ and $D$ if necessary, we may assume
\[
Y\subseteq C.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $Y$ is connected and $C,D$ separate $Z$, a connected subspace of $Z$ must lie entirely in one member of the separation.
Thus either $Y\subseteq C$ or $Y\subseteq D$; relabel the two sets in the second case.
:::

<1>3. The set $D$ is a nonempty proper closed subset of $X$.
::: {.proof}
Since $C,D$ form a separation of $Z$, the set $D$ is nonempty and closed in $Z$.
By <1>1, $Z$ is closed in $X$, so $D$ is closed in $X$.
Also $C$ is nonempty, so $D\neq X$.
:::

<1>4. In fact, $D$ is open in $X$.
::: {.proof}
From $Y\subseteq C$ and $C\cap D=\varnothing$ we get
\[
D\cap Y=\varnothing,
\]
so, because $D\subseteq Z=A\cup Y$,
\[
D\subseteq A.
\]

The set $D$ is open in the subspace $Z$, so there is an open set $U\subseteq X$ such that
\[
D=U\cap Z.
\]
The set $A$ is open in the subspace $X\setminus Y$, because $A,B$ form a separation there.
Hence there is an open set $V\subseteq X$ such that
\[
A=V\cap(X\setminus Y).
\]

Fix $d\in D$.
Since $d\in A$, we have $d\in U\cap V$.
We claim that
\[
U\cap V\subseteq D.
\]
Indeed, take $q\in U\cap V$.
If $q\in Y$, then $q\in Y\subseteq Z$, so
\[
q\in U\cap Z=D,
\]
contradicting $D\cap Y=\varnothing$.
Thus $q\notin Y$.
Since $q\in V$, it follows that
\[
q\in V\cap(X\setminus Y)=A\subseteq Z.
\]
Therefore
\[
q\in U\cap Z=D.
\]
So $U\cap V$ is an open neighborhood of $d$ contained in $D$.
As $d$ was arbitrary, $D$ is open in $X$.
:::

<1>5. This contradicts the connectedness of $X$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3 and <1>4, $D$ is a nonempty proper subset of $X$ that is both open and closed.
A connected space has no such subset.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $A\cup Y$ is connected.
::: {.proof}
The assumption in <1>2 that $Z=A\cup Y$ is disconnected leads to the contradiction in <1>5.
:::
:::
