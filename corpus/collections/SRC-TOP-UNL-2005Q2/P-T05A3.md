::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a connected space with connected subset $Y$, and suppose that $X\setminus Y$ is not connected, with $X\setminus Y=A\cup B$ a separation of $X\setminus Y$.
Show that if $B$ is open in $X$, then $A\cup Y$ is a connected subset of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $Z=A\cup Y$; since $X\setminus Y=A\sqcup B$, $Z=X\setminus B$, which is closed in $X$ because $B$ is open.
Suppose $Z=C\sqcup D$ with $C,D$ disjoint and open in $Z$.
Since $Y$ is connected, it lies in $C$ or in $D$; interchange them so that $Y\subseteq C$.
It suffices to show that $C$ or $D$ is empty.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$D$ is closed in $X$, and $D\subseteq A$.

::: pf-proof

$D$ is closed in $Z$, and $Z$ is closed in $X$.
$D\cap Y\subseteq D\cap C=\varnothing$ and $D\subseteq A\cup Y$, so $D\subseteq A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$D$ is open in $X$.

::: pf-proof

Choose open $U,V\subseteq X$ with $D=U\cap Z$ and $A=V\cap(X\setminus Y)$; the latter exists because $A$ is open in $X\setminus Y$.
Let $d\in D$; by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $d\in A$, so $d\in U\cap V$.
Let $q\in U\cap V$.
If $q\in Y$, then $q\in U\cap Z=D$, contradicting $D\cap Y=\varnothing$; so $q\notin Y$, hence $q\in V\cap(X\setminus Y)=A\subseteq Z$ and $q\in U\cap Z=D$.
Thus $U\cap V$ is an open neighborhood of $d$ contained in $D$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $D$ is clopen in the connected space $X$, so $D=\varnothing$ or $D=X$.
If $D=X$, then $C\subseteq Z\setminus D=\varnothing$.
So $C$ or $D$ is empty, and $A\cup Y$ is connected.

:::
