::: {.problem}
A space $X$ is called *unicoherent* if whenever $X=U\cup V$ with $U,V$ nonempty, open, and path-connected, then $U\cap V$ is path-connected.
Use the Mayer--Vietoris sequence to show that, for $n\ge 2$, $S^n$ is unicoherent.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $n\ge2$, and suppose
\[
S^n=U\cup V
\]
with $U$ and $V$ nonempty, open, and path-connected.

<1>1. The intersection $U\cap V$ is nonempty.
::: {.proof}
If $U\cap V=\varnothing$, then the two nonempty open sets $U$ and $V$ would form a separation of $S^n$.
But $S^n$ is path-connected, hence connected.
Therefore $U\cap V\neq\varnothing$.
:::

<1>2. The relevant part of the reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence is exact:
\[
\widetilde H_1(S^n)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_0(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_0(U)\oplus\widetilde H_0(V).
\]
::: {.proof}
The sets $U$ and $V$ are open and cover $S^n$, so the Mayer--Vietoris theorem applies.
By <1>1 their intersection is nonempty, so the displayed reduced-homology segment is the usual degree-$1$ to degree-$0$ part of that exact sequence.
:::

<1>3. Both outer groups in <1>2 vanish.
::: {.proof}
Since $n\ge2$, the homology of the sphere gives
\[
\widetilde H_1(S^n)=0.
\]
Since $U$ and $V$ are nonempty and path-connected,
\[
\widetilde H_0(U)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\widetilde H_0(V)=0.
\]
Thus
\[
\widetilde H_0(U)\oplus\widetilde H_0(V)=0.
\]
:::

<1>4. Hence
\[
\widetilde H_0(U\cap V)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>3, exactness gives an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\widetilde H_0(U\cap V)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The middle group must therefore be zero.
:::

<1>5. The intersection $U\cap V$ is path-connected.
::: {.proof}
For any nonempty space $Y$, $H_0(Y;\mathbb Z)$ is free abelian on the path components of $Y$; equivalently,
\[
\widetilde H_0(Y)=0
\]
if and only if $Y$ has exactly one path component.
The space $U\cap V$ is nonempty by <1>1 and has vanishing reduced $H_0$ by <1>4. Hence it is path-connected.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $S^n$ is unicoherent for every $n\ge2$ in the sense defined in the problem.
::: {.proof}
The open path-connected cover $S^n=U\cup V$ was arbitrary, and <1>5 proves that its intersection is path-connected.
:::
