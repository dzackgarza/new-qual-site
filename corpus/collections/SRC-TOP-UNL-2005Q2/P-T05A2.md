:::{.problem}
Let $X$ be the set of integers, let
$$\mathcal C_1:=\{A\subseteq X\mid X\setminus A\text{ is finite}\},\qquad
\mathcal C_2:=\{A\subseteq X\mid 0\notin A\}.$$
Show that the union $\mathcal T:=\mathcal C_1\cup\mathcal C_2$ is a topology on $X$, and show that this
topological space is compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Verification of topology axioms:
<2>1. **Contains $\emptyset$ and $X$:**
$0 \notin \emptyset \implies \emptyset \in \mathcal{C}_2 \subseteq \mathcal{T}$, and $X \setminus X = \emptyset$ is finite $\implies X \in \mathcal{C}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{T}$.
Proof: definitions of $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$.
<2>2. **Closed under arbitrary unions:**
Let $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$, and let $U = \bigcup_{\alpha \in I} U_\alpha$.
- If there exists $\alpha_0 \in I$ such that $U_{\alpha_0} \in \mathcal{C}_1$, then $X \setminus U \subseteq X \setminus U_{\alpha_0}$ is finite, so $U \in \mathcal{C}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{T}$.
- If $U_\alpha \in \mathcal{C}_2$ for all $\alpha \in I$, then $0 \notin U_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$, so $0 \notin \bigcup U_\alpha = U$, which means $U \in \mathcal{C}_2 \subseteq \mathcal{T}$.
Proof: subset inclusions of complements and non-membership in unions.
<2>3. **Closed under finite intersections:**
Let $U, V \in \mathcal{T}$.
- If $U, V \in \mathcal{C}_1$, then $X \setminus (U \cap V) = (X \setminus U) \cup (X \setminus V)$ is a union of two finite sets, hence finite, so $U \cap V \in \mathcal{C}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{T}$.
- If $U \in \mathcal{C}_2$ or $V \in \mathcal{C}_2$, say $0 \notin U$, then $0 \notin U \cap V$, so $U \cap V \in \mathcal{C}_2 \subseteq \mathcal{T}$.
Thus $U \cap V \in \mathcal{T}$.
Proof: De Morgan's laws and set intersection.
<2>4. Therefore $\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{C}_1 \cup \mathcal{C}_2$ is a topology on $X = \mathbb{Z}$.
Proof: <2>1, <2>2, and <2>3.

<1>2. Proof of compactness:
<2>1. Let $\mathcal{U} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ be an arbitrary open cover of $X = \mathbb{Z}$.
Proof: setup.
<2>2. Since $\bigcup_{U \in \mathcal{U}} U = \mathbb{Z}$, there exists an open set $U_0 \in \mathcal{U}$ containing $0$.
Proof: cover covers $0$.
<2>3. Since $0 \in U_0$, $U_0$ cannot belong to $\mathcal{C}_2 = \{A \subseteq X \mid 0 \notin A\}$.
Therefore $U_0 \in \mathcal{C}_1$, which means the complement $X \setminus U_0$ is finite:
\[
X \setminus U_0 = \{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_k\} \quad \text{for some } k \ge 0.
\]
Proof: definition of $\mathcal{C}_1$.
<2>4. For each $i \in \{1, \dots, k\}$, choose an open set $U_i \in \mathcal{U}$ such that $x_i \in U_i$.
Proof: $\mathcal{U}$ covers each point $x_i$.
<2>5. The finite collection $\{U_0, U_1, \ldots, U_k\} \subseteq \mathcal{U}$ satisfies:
\[
X = U_0 \cup (X \setminus U_0) \subseteq U_0 \cup U_1 \cup \dots \cup U_k.
\]
Thus $\{U_0, U_1, \dots, U_k\}$ is a finite subcover of $\mathcal{U}$.
Proof: finite union covers $X$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
$\mathcal{T}$ is a topology on $\mathbb{Z}$ and $(X, \mathcal{T})$ is compact. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.
