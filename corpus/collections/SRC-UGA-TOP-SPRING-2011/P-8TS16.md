::: problem
Let $X$ be the topological space obtained by identifying three distinct points on the torus $S^1\times S^1$.
Calculate the fundamental group of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let
\[
T=S^1\times S^1,
\qquad
A=\{p_0,p_1,p_2\}\subseteq T
\]
be the three points that are identified.
Then
\[
X=T/A.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is just notation for the quotient in the problem: $T/A$ denotes the space obtained by collapsing the three-point subspace $A$ to one point and leaving every other point distinct.
:::

<1>2. The quotient $T/A$ is homotopy equivalent to the mapping cone
\[
T\cup_A CA,
\]
where $CA$ is the cone on the three-point space $A$.
::: {.proof}
Choose a triangulation of the torus having $p_0,p_1,p_2$ as vertices.
Then $A\hookrightarrow T$ is the inclusion of a subcomplex, hence a cofibration.

For a cofibration $A\hookrightarrow T$, the ordinary quotient $T/A$ is homotopy equivalent to the homotopy cofiber, represented by the mapping cone
\[
T\cup_A CA.
\]
Thus the stated quotient may be replaced, for purposes of computing fundamental groups, by this mapping-cone model.
:::

<1>3. The mapping-cone model is homotopy equivalent to
\[
T\vee S^1\vee S^1.
\]
::: {.proof}
The cone $CA$ is a triod: it has a cone vertex $c$ and three edges joining $c$ to $p_0,p_1,p_2$.

First collapse the edge joining $c$ to $p_0$.
It is a contractible subcomplex, so collapsing it is a homotopy equivalence.
After this collapse, the cone vertex is identified with $p_0$, and the remaining two cone edges are $1$-cells attached to $T$ with endpoint pairs
\[
(p_0,p_1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(p_0,p_2).
\]

Since $T$ is path-connected, choose paths in $T$ from $p_1$ to $p_0$ and from $p_2$ to $p_0$.
These paths give homotopies of the two attaching maps
\[
S^0\longrightarrow T
\]
to attaching maps that send both endpoints of each $1$-cell to $p_0$.
Adjunction spaces obtained from homotopic CW attaching maps are homotopy equivalent.
Therefore the two attached intervals may, up to homotopy, be replaced by two loops based at $p_0$.
The resulting space is
\[
T\vee S^1\vee S^1.
\]
:::

<1>4. Hence
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\pi_1(T)*\ZZ*\ZZ.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>3,
\[
X\simeq T\vee S^1\vee S^1.
\]
The Seifert--van Kampen theorem gives the fundamental group of a wedge of path-connected CW complexes as the free product of their fundamental groups.
Thus
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\pi_1(T)*\pi_1(S^1)*\pi_1(S^1)
\cong
\pi_1(T)*\ZZ*\ZZ.
\]
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\ZZ^2*F_2
\cong
\left\langle a,b,c,d\ \middle|\ [a,b]=1\right\rangle .
}
\]
::: {.proof}
The torus has
\[
\pi_1(T)\cong\ZZ^2
\cong
\langle a,b\mid [a,b]=1\rangle,
\]
while the two additional circle factors generate the free group
\[
F_2=\langle c,d\rangle.
\]
Taking the free product produces exactly the displayed presentation.
:::
