::: problem
(a) What does it mean to say that $p:Y\to X$ is a covering map?

(b) Let $p:Y\to X$ be a covering map.
Prove that, for any $y\in Y$, the homomorphism
\[
p_*:\pi_1(Y,y)\to\pi_1(X,p(y))
\]
is injective.

(c) Let $p:Y\to X$ be a covering map with $Y$ and $X$ path-connected.
Suppose that $p_*$ is an isomorphism.
Prove that $p$ is a homeomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A covering map is a continuous surjection $p:Y\to X$ such that every $x\in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ for which
\[
p^{-1}(U)=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}V_\lambda,
\]
where each $V_\lambda$ is open in $Y$ and every restriction
\[
p|_{V_\lambda}:V_\lambda\longrightarrow U
\]
is a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Such a neighborhood $U$ is called evenly covered, and the sets $V_\lambda$ are its sheets.
This is the definition requested in part (a).
:::

<1>2. For every covering map $p:Y\to X$ and every $y\in Y$, the induced homomorphism
\[
p_*:\pi_1(Y,y)\longrightarrow\pi_1(X,p(y))
\]
is injective.
::: {.proof}
Let $[\gamma]\in\pi_1(Y,y)$ satisfy
\[
p_*[\gamma]=1.
\]
Then the loop $p\circ\gamma$ is nullhomotopic in $X$ relative to its basepoint $p(y)$.
Choose a homotopy
\[
H:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to X
\]
from $p\circ\gamma$ to the constant loop at $p(y)$, with the two endpoint edges fixed at $p(y)$.

By the homotopy lifting property for covering maps, $H$ has a unique lift
\[
\widetilde H:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to Y
\]
whose initial loop is $\gamma$.
Since the endpoint edges of $H$ are constant at $p(y)$ and begin at $y$, their lifts are constant at $y$.
Hence the terminal loop of $\widetilde H$ is the lift, based at $y$, of the constant loop at $p(y)$, so it is the constant loop at $y$.
Thus $\gamma$ is nullhomotopic in $Y$ relative to $y$.
Therefore $[\gamma]=1$, proving that $p_*$ is injective.
:::

<1>3. In part (c), every fiber of $p$ contains exactly one point.
::: {.proof}
Fix $x\in X$ and suppose
\[
y_0,y_1\in p^{-1}(x).
\]
Because $Y$ is path-connected, choose a path
\[
\gamma:[0,1]\to Y
\]
from $y_0$ to $y_1$.
Then $p\circ\gamma$ is a loop at $x$.

The map $p_*$ is surjective, so there is a loop $\delta$ in $Y$ based at $y_0$ such that
\[
p_*[\delta]=[p\circ\gamma]
\]
in $\pi_1(X,x)$.
Thus the loops $p\circ\delta$ and $p\circ\gamma$ are homotopic relative to the basepoint.

Lift such a homotopy through $p$, beginning with the lift $\delta$ based at $y_0$.
By uniqueness of lifted paths, the other boundary loop of the lifted homotopy is the lift $\gamma$, since both lift $p\circ\gamma$ and start at $y_0$.
The endpoints of lifts are constant during a homotopy relative to endpoints, so $\delta$ and $\gamma$ have the same terminal point.
But
\[
\delta(1)=y_0,
\qquad
\gamma(1)=y_1.
\]
Hence $y_0=y_1$.

Since $p$ is surjective, each fiber is nonempty, so every fiber is a singleton.
:::

<1>4. Therefore the covering map in part (c) is a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, $p$ is bijective.
A covering map is a local homeomorphism and therefore an open map: if $W\subseteq Y$ is open and $y\in W$, choose an evenly covered neighborhood $U$ of $p(y)$ and the sheet $V$ containing $y$; then
\[
p(W\cap V)
\]
is an open neighborhood of $p(y)$ contained in $p(W)$.
Thus $p(W)$ is open.

A bijective continuous open map has continuous inverse, so $p$ is a homeomorphism.
This proves part (c).
:::
