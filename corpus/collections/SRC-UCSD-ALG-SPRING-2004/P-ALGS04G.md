::: problem
(a) Determine the Galois group $G$ for the splitting field $K$ of $X^5 - 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

(b) Determine all the subgroups of $G$ isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_5$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let \(\alpha=\sqrt[5]{3}\) and let \(\zeta=\zeta_5\) be a primitive fifth root of unity.
Then
\[
K=\mathbf Q(\alpha,\zeta).
\]
::: {.proof}
The roots of \(X^5-3\) are \(\alpha,\zeta\alpha,\dots,\zeta^4\alpha\). Hence adjoining \(\alpha\) and \(\zeta\) gives the splitting field, and both lie in every splitting field.
:::

<1>2. One has \([\mathbf Q(\alpha):\mathbf Q]=5\) and \([\mathbf Q(\zeta):\mathbf Q]=4\).
::: {.proof}
The polynomial \(X^5-3\) is Eisenstein at \(3\), so it is irreducible over \(\mathbf Q\). Thus \([\mathbf Q(\alpha):\mathbf Q]=5\). Also \(\Phi_5(X)=X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta\), so \([\mathbf Q(\zeta):\mathbf Q]=4\).
:::

<1>3. The intersection \(\mathbf Q(\alpha)\cap\mathbf Q(\zeta)\) is \(\mathbf Q\). Therefore \([K:\mathbf Q]=20\).
::: {.proof}
The degree of the intersection over \(\mathbf Q\) divides both \([\mathbf Q(\alpha):\mathbf Q]=5\) and \([\mathbf Q(\zeta):\mathbf Q]=4\). Since \(\gcd(5,4)=1\), the intersection has degree \(1\). Hence
\[
[K:\mathbf Q]=[\mathbf Q(\alpha):\mathbf Q]\,[\mathbf Q(\zeta):\mathbf Q]=20.
\]
:::

<1>4. Define automorphisms \(\sigma,\tau\in G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)\) by
\[
\sigma(\alpha)=\zeta\alpha,\qquad \sigma(\zeta)=\zeta,
\]
and
\[
\tau(\alpha)=\alpha,\qquad \tau(\zeta)=\zeta^2.
\]
Then \(\sigma\) has order \(5\), \(\tau\) has order \(4\), and
\[
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because \[K:\mathbf Q(\zeta)]=5\), the conjugates of \(\alpha\) over \(\mathbf Q(\zeta)\) are \(\zeta^i\alpha\), so sending \(\alpha\mapsto\zeta\alpha\) defines an automorphism \(\sigma\) of order \(5\) fixing \(\mathbf Q(\zeta)\). By <1>3, \(\mathbf Q(\alpha)\) and \(\mathbf Q(\zeta)\) are linearly disjoint over \(\mathbf Q\); therefore the automorphism \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2\) of \(\mathbf Q(\zeta)\) extends uniquely to an automorphism \(\tau\) of \(K\) fixing \(\alpha\). It has order \(4\). Since \(\tau^{-1}(\alpha)=\alpha\),
\[
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}(\alpha)=\tau(\zeta\alpha)=\zeta^2\alpha=\sigma^2(\alpha),
\]
and both sides send \(\zeta\) to \(\zeta\).
:::

<1>5. The elements \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\) generate \(G\), and
\[
G\cong C_5\rtimes C_4
\]
where a generator of \(C_4\) acts on \(C_5\) by \(\sigma\mapsto\sigma^2\). Equivalently,
\[
G\cong \langle \sigma,\tau\mid \sigma^5=\tau^4=1,\ \tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^2\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The subgroup generated by \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\) contains \(5\cdot4=20\) distinct elements \(\sigma^i\tau^j\), because \(\langle\sigma\rangle\cap\langle\tau\rangle=1\). By <1>3, \(|G|=[K:\mathbf Q]=20\), so this subgroup is all of \(G\). The conjugation relation gives the semidirect-product structure.
:::

<1>6. The subgroup \(\langle\sigma\rangle\) is the unique subgroup of \(G\) isomorphic to \(\mathbf Z/5\mathbf Z\).
::: {.proof}
Any subgroup isomorphic to \(\mathbf Z/5\mathbf Z\) has order \(5\), hence is a Sylow \(5\)-subgroup.
If \(n_5\) denotes the number of Sylow \(5\)-subgroups, Sylow's theorem gives
\[
n_5\equiv1\pmod5,\qquad n_5\mid4.
\]
Thus \(n_5=1\). Since \(\langle\sigma\rangle\) has order \(5\), it is that unique subgroup.
:::
