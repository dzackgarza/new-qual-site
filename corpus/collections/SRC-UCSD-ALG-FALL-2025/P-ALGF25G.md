::: problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer, $p$ a prime, $p \nmid n$, and $\Phi_n(x)$ the $n$-th cyclotomic polynomial.
Let $E_{n,p}$ denote the splitting field of $\Phi_n(x)$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

(a) Prove that if $\zeta \in E_{n,p}$ is a zero of $\Phi_n(x)$, then the multiplicative order of $\zeta$ is $n$.

(b) Prove that $\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ can be embedded into the group of automorphisms of $\langle\zeta\rangle$, where $\zeta$ is the element given in Part (a).

(c) Prove that $\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb{F}_p) \simeq \langle p + n\mathbb{Z} \rangle \subseteq (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$.

(d) Prove that if $n$ has two distinct odd prime factors, then $\Phi_n(x)$ is reducible over $\mathbb{F}_\ell$ for every prime $\ell$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $p\nmid n$ and $\zeta$ is a zero of $\Phi_n$ in $E_{n,p}$, then $\zeta$ has multiplicative order $n$.
::: {.proof}
Reduce the integer identity
\[
x^n-1=\prod_{d\mid n}\Phi_d(x)
\]
modulo $p$.
It shows that $\zeta^n=1$, so the multiplicative order $d$ of $\zeta$ divides $n$.

The polynomial $x^n-1$ is separable over $\mathbb F_p$: its derivative is
\[
nx^{n-1},
\]
and $p\nmid n$, while every root of $x^n-1$ is nonzero.
Suppose $d<n$.
Since
\[
x^d-1=\prod_{e\mid d}\Phi_e(x),
\]
the element $\zeta$ is a zero of $\Phi_e$ for some $e\mid d$ as well as of $\Phi_n$.
Both factors occur in the displayed factorization of $x^n-1$, so $\zeta$ would be a repeated root of $x^n-1$, a contradiction.
Thus $d=n$, proving part (a).
:::

<1>2. The splitting field is $E_{n,p}=\mathbb F_p(\zeta)$, and restriction gives an embedding
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb F_p)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\langle\zeta\rangle).
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $p\nmid n$, the polynomial $x^n-1$ has exactly $n$ distinct roots in an algebraic closure.
The powers
\[
1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}
\]
are $n$ distinct roots by <1>1, so they are all the roots of $x^n-1$.
Again using the cyclotomic factorization and <1>1 for every divisor of $n$, the zeros of $\Phi_n$ are precisely the powers $\zeta^a$ with $\gcd(a,n)=1$.
Hence every zero of $\Phi_n$ already lies in $\mathbb F_p(\zeta)$, and therefore
\[
E_{n,p}=\mathbb F_p(\zeta).
\]

Every $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb F_p)$ sends $\zeta$ to another zero of $\Phi_n$, hence to another generator of the cyclic group $\langle\zeta\rangle$.
Thus restriction defines a homomorphism
\[
\rho:\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb F_p)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\langle\zeta\rangle).
\]
If $\rho(\sigma)$ is the identity, then $\sigma$ fixes $\zeta$ and also fixes $\mathbb F_p$.
Since $E_{n,p}=\mathbb F_p(\zeta)$, it follows that $\sigma$ is the identity.
Therefore $\rho$ is injective, proving part (b).
:::

<1>3. Under the standard identification
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\langle\zeta\rangle)\cong(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times,
\]
the image of the Galois group is $\langle p+n\mathbb Z\rangle$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
[E_{n,p}:\mathbb F_p]=f.
\]
Then $E_{n,p}$ is the finite field with $p^f$ elements.
The Frobenius automorphism
\[
\operatorname{Fr}_p:E_{n,p}\longrightarrow E_{n,p},
\qquad x\longmapsto x^p,
\]
has order exactly $f$.
Indeed, $\operatorname{Fr}_p^f$ is the identity because every element of $E_{n,p}$ satisfies $x^{p^f}=x$.
If $0<k<f$ and $\operatorname{Fr}_p^k$ were the identity, all $p^f$ elements of $E_{n,p}$ would be roots of the nonzero degree-$p^k$ polynomial $x^{p^k}-x$, which is impossible.

The finite extension $E_{n,p}/\mathbb F_p$ is Galois, so its Galois group has order $f$.
Consequently it is generated by $\operatorname{Fr}_p$.
Under the embedding $\rho$ of <1>2,
\[
\rho(\operatorname{Fr}_p)(\zeta)=\zeta^p.
\]
The automorphism $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^a$ corresponds to $a+n\mathbb Z\in(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times$.
Therefore
\[
\rho\bigl(\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb F_p)\bigr)
=\langle p+n\mathbb Z\rangle,
\]
and injectivity of $\rho$ gives
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb F_p)
\cong\langle p+n\mathbb Z\rangle.
\]
This proves part (c).
:::

<1>4. If $n$ has two distinct odd prime factors and $\ell\nmid n$, then $\Phi_n$ is reducible over $\mathbb F_\ell$.
::: {.proof}
Let $q$ and $r$ be two distinct odd prime divisors of $n$.
Reduction modulo $q$ and $r$, together with the Chinese remainder theorem on the prime-power factors of $n$, gives a surjective homomorphism
\[
(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times
\twoheadrightarrow
(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times\times(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times.
\]
The group on the right is not cyclic.
Indeed, the order of any of its elements divides
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(q-1,r-1)
\le \frac{(q-1)(r-1)}2,
\]
because both $q-1$ and $r-1$ are even, whereas the group itself has order $(q-1)(r-1)$.
Thus $(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times$ cannot be cyclic, since a quotient of a cyclic group is cyclic.

Suppose for contradiction that $\Phi_n$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_\ell$.
For a zero $\zeta$ of $\Phi_n$, part (a) gives $\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)=n$, and <1>2 with $p=\ell$ gives
\[
E_{n,\ell}=\mathbb F_\ell(\zeta).
\]
Hence
\[
[E_{n,\ell}:\mathbb F_\ell]
=\deg\Phi_n
=\varphi(n).
\]
Part (c) identifies the Galois group with $\langle\ell+n\mathbb Z\rangle$, so the element $\ell+n\mathbb Z$ would have order $\varphi(n)$.
Since
\[
\bigl|(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times\bigr|=\varphi(n),
\]
this would make $(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times$ cyclic, contradicting the preceding paragraph.
Therefore $\Phi_n$ is reducible whenever $\ell\nmid n$.
:::

<1>5. The same reducibility holds when $\ell\mid n$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
n=\ell^a m,
\qquad a\ge1,
\qquad \gcd(\ell,m)=1.
\]
Since $n$ has two distinct odd prime factors, $m>1$ whenever the argument below needs positive degree.
The cyclotomic identity
\[
\Phi_{\ell^a m}(x)
=\frac{\Phi_m(x^{\ell^a})}{\Phi_m(x^{\ell^{a-1}})}
\]
holds over $\mathbb Z$ because $\ell\nmid m$.
After reduction modulo $\ell$, Frobenius gives
\[
\Phi_m(x^{\ell^j})=\Phi_m(x)^{\ell^j}
\qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_\ell[x].
\]
Cancelling in the integral domain $\mathbb F_\ell[x]$ yields
\[
\Phi_n(x)
=\Phi_m(x)^{\ell^{a-1}(\ell-1)}
\qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_\ell[x].
\]

If $\ell^{a-1}(\ell-1)>1$, this is a nontrivial power of a positive-degree polynomial, so $\Phi_n$ is reducible.
The only remaining case is $\ell=2$ and $a=1$.
Then $n=2m$, where $m$ is odd and still has two distinct odd prime factors, and the displayed congruence gives
\[
\Phi_n(x)=\Phi_m(x)
\qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[x].
\]
Since $2\nmid m$, <1>4 applied to $m$ shows that $\Phi_m$ is reducible over $\mathbb F_2$.
Thus $\Phi_n$ is reducible in this final case as well.

Combining <1>4 and <1>5 proves part (d) for every prime $\ell$.
:::
