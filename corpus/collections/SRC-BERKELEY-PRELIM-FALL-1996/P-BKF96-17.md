::: {.problem}
Let $I\subset\mathbb Z[x]$ be the ideal generated by $13$ and $x-4$. Find an integer $m$ with
\[
0\le m\le12
\]
such that
\[
(x^{26}+x+1)^{73}-m\in I.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. In the quotient ring
$$
\ZZ[x]/I,
$$
one has
$$
13=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x=4.
$$

::: {.proof}
The ideal
$$
I=(13,x-4)
$$
contains both $13$ and $x-4$. Thus their residue classes vanish, which is
exactly the two displayed relations.
:::

<1>2. Modulo $I$,
$$
x^{26}+x+1
\equiv
8.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
x^{26}+x+1
\equiv
4^{26}+4+1
\pmod I.
$$
Working modulo $13$, Fermat's theorem gives
$$
4^{12}\equiv1\pmod{13}.
$$
Since
$$
26=2\cdot12+2,
$$
one gets
$$
4^{26}
\equiv
4^2
=16
\equiv3
\pmod{13}.
$$
Therefore
$$
4^{26}+4+1
\equiv
3+4+1
=8
\pmod{13},
$$
which gives the congruence modulo $I$.
:::

<1>3. Modulo $13$,
$$
8^{73}\equiv8.
$$

::: {.proof}
Again by Fermat's theorem,
$$
8^{12}\equiv1\pmod{13}.
$$
Since
$$
73=6\cdot12+1,
$$
one has
$$
8^{73}
\equiv
8
\pmod{13}.
$$
:::

<1>4. Modulo $I$,
$$
(x^{26}+x+1)^{73}
\equiv
8.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives
$$
(x^{26}+x+1)^{73}
\equiv
8^{73}
\pmod I.
$$
The integer relation from step <1>3 is also valid modulo $I$ because
$13\in I$. Thus the right side is congruent to $8$.
:::

<1>5. One may take
$$
\boxed{m=8}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The integer $8$ lies in the required range
$$
0\leq8\leq12.
$$
Step <1>4 says exactly that
$$
(x^{26}+x+1)^{73}-8\in I.
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the required integer.
:::
:::
