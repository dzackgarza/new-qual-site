::: {.problem}
Let $I\subset\mathbb Z[x]$ be the ideal generated by $13$ and $x-4$. Find an integer $m$ with
\[
0\le m\le12
\]
such that
\[
(x^{26}+x+1)^{73}-m\in I.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

In the quotient ring
$$
\ZZ[x]/I,
$$
one has
$$
13=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x=4.
$$

::: pf-proof

The ideal
$$
I=(13,x-4)
$$
contains both $13$ and $x-4$. Thus their residue classes vanish, which is
exactly the two displayed relations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Modulo $I$,
$$
x^{26}+x+1
\equiv
8.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
x^{26}+x+1
\equiv
4^{26}+4+1
\pmod I.
$$
Working modulo $13$, Fermat's theorem gives
$$
4^{12}\equiv1\pmod{13}.
$$
Since
$$
26=2\cdot12+2,
$$
one gets
$$
4^{26}
\equiv
4^2
=16
\equiv3
\pmod{13}.
$$
Therefore
$$
4^{26}+4+1
\equiv
3+4+1
=8
\pmod{13},
$$
which gives the congruence modulo $I$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Modulo $13$,
$$
8^{73}\equiv8.
$$

::: pf-proof

Again by Fermat's theorem,
$$
8^{12}\equiv1\pmod{13}.
$$
Since
$$
73=6\cdot12+1,
$$
one has
$$
8^{73}
\equiv
8
\pmod{13}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Modulo $I$,
$$
(x^{26}+x+1)^{73}
\equiv
8.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
(x^{26}+x+1)^{73}
\equiv
8^{73}
\pmod I.
$$
The integer relation from step [](#s3){.pf-ref} is also valid modulo $I$ because
$13\in I$. Thus the right side is congruent to $8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

One may take
$$
\boxed{m=8}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The integer $8$ lies in the required range
$$
0\leq8\leq12.
$$
Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} says exactly that
$$
(x^{26}+x+1)^{73}-8\in I.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives the required integer.

:::
