::: {.problem}
If $f$ is $C^2$ on an open interval, prove that
\[
\lim_{h\to0}
\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}
=f''(x).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $x$ in the open interval.

<1>1. For every sufficiently small nonzero $h$, there are points
$\xi_h^+$ between $x$ and $x+h$ and $\xi_h^-$ between $x$ and $x-h$ such
that
$$
f(x+h)
=
f(x)+hf'(x)+\frac{h^2}{2}f''(\xi_h^+)
$$
and
$$
f(x-h)
=
f(x)-hf'(x)+\frac{h^2}{2}f''(\xi_h^-).
$$

::: {.proof}
Because the interval is open, $x\pm h$ remain in it for all sufficiently
small $h$. Apply Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder to $f$ at $x$,
once at the point $x+h$ and once at the point $x-h$.
:::

<1>2. For such $h$,
$$
\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}
=
\frac{
f''(\xi_h^+)+f''(\xi_h^-)
}{2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Add the two Taylor formulas from step <1>1. The constant terms leave
$2f(x)$ and the linear terms $hf'(x)$ and $-hf'(x)$ cancel. Subtract
$2f(x)$ and divide by $h^2$.
:::

<1>3. As $h\to0$,
$$
\xi_h^+\longrightarrow x
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\xi_h^-\longrightarrow x.
$$

::: {.proof}
Each $\xi_h^\pm$ lies between $x$ and $x\pm h$, so
$$
\abs{\xi_h^\pm-x}\leq\abs h.
$$
The right side tends to zero.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\lim_{h\to0}
\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}
=
f''(x).
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $f$ is $C^2$, the function $f''$ is continuous. Step <1>3 therefore
gives
$$
f''(\xi_h^+)\longrightarrow f''(x)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f''(\xi_h^-)\longrightarrow f''(x).
$$
Apply these limits to the identity in step <1>2.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required limit.
:::
:::
