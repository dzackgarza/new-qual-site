::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be continuous and nonnegative on $[0,1]$, and suppose
\[
f(t)^2\le 1+2\int_0^t f(s)\,ds.
\]
Prove that
\[
f(t)\le1+t
\qquad(0\le t\le1).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
F(t)
\coloneqq
\sqrt{
1+2\int_0^t f(s)\,ds
}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $t\in[0,1]$,
$$
F(t)\geq1
$$
and
$$
f(t)\leq F(t).
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $f$ is nonnegative,
$$
\int_0^t f(s)\,ds\geq0,
$$
so the expression under the square root is at least $1$, giving
$F(t)\geq1$.

The hypothesis says
$$
f(t)^2
\leq
F(t)^2.
$$
Both $f(t)$ and $F(t)$ are nonnegative, hence
$$
f(t)\leq F(t).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The function $F$ is continuously differentiable and satisfies
$$
F'(t)
=
\frac{f(t)}{F(t)}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The function
$$
t\longmapsto
1+2\int_0^t f(s)\,ds
$$
is continuously differentiable with derivative $2f(t)$, and by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}
it is strictly positive. The chain rule therefore gives
$$
F'(t)
=
\frac{1}{2F(t)}\,2f(t)
=
\frac{f(t)}{F(t)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every $t\in[0,1]$,
$$
F'(t)\leq1.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
0\leq f(t)\leq F(t).
$$
Divide by the positive number $F(t)$ and use step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For every $t\in[0,1]$,
$$
F(t)\leq1+t.
$$

::: pf-proof

One has
$$
F(0)=1.
$$
Integrating the estimate from step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
F(t)-F(0)
=
\int_0^tF'(s)\,ds
\leq
\int_0^t1\,ds
=t.
$$
Thus $F(t)\leq1+t$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

For every $t\in[0,1]$,
$$
\boxed{f(t)\leq1+t}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives $f(t)\leq F(t)$, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives
$F(t)\leq1+t$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required estimate.

:::
