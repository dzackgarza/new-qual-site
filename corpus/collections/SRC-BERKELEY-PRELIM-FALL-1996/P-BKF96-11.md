::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be continuous and nonnegative on $[0,1]$, and suppose
\[
f(t)^2\le 1+2\int_0^t f(s)\,ds.
\]
Prove that
\[
f(t)\le1+t
\qquad(0\le t\le1).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
F(t)
\coloneqq
\sqrt{
1+2\int_0^t f(s)\,ds
}.
$$

<1>1. For every $t\in[0,1]$,
$$
F(t)\geq1
$$
and
$$
f(t)\leq F(t).
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $f$ is nonnegative,
$$
\int_0^t f(s)\,ds\geq0,
$$
so the expression under the square root is at least $1$, giving
$F(t)\geq1$.

The hypothesis says
$$
f(t)^2
\leq
F(t)^2.
$$
Both $f(t)$ and $F(t)$ are nonnegative, hence
$$
f(t)\leq F(t).
$$
:::

<1>2. The function $F$ is continuously differentiable and satisfies
$$
F'(t)
=
\frac{f(t)}{F(t)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The function
$$
t\longmapsto
1+2\int_0^t f(s)\,ds
$$
is continuously differentiable with derivative $2f(t)$, and by step <1>1
it is strictly positive. The chain rule therefore gives
$$
F'(t)
=
\frac{1}{2F(t)}\,2f(t)
=
\frac{f(t)}{F(t)}.
$$
:::

<1>3. For every $t\in[0,1]$,
$$
F'(t)\leq1.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
0\leq f(t)\leq F(t).
$$
Divide by the positive number $F(t)$ and use step <1>2.
:::

<1>4. For every $t\in[0,1]$,
$$
F(t)\leq1+t.
$$

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
F(0)=1.
$$
Integrating the estimate from step <1>3,
$$
F(t)-F(0)
=
\int_0^tF'(s)\,ds
\leq
\int_0^t1\,ds
=t.
$$
Thus $F(t)\leq1+t$.
:::

<1>5. For every $t\in[0,1]$,
$$
\boxed{f(t)\leq1+t}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives $f(t)\leq F(t)$, and step <1>4 gives
$F(t)\leq1+t$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required estimate.
:::
