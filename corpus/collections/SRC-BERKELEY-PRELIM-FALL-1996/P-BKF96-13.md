::: {.problem}
Define
\[
F:\mathbb C^3\to\mathbb C^3,
\qquad
F(u,v,w)=(u+v+w,uv+vw+uw,uvw).
\]
Show that $F$ is onto but not one-to-one.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Fix an arbitrary point
$$
(a,b,c)\in\CC^3.
$$
The polynomial
$$
p(t)
\coloneqq
t^3-at^2+bt-c
$$
has three complex roots $u,v,w$, counted with multiplicity.

::: {.proof}
By the fundamental theorem of algebra, the degree-$3$ polynomial $p$
factors over $\CC$ as
$$
p(t)
=(t-u)(t-v)(t-w)
$$
for some $u,v,w\in\CC$.
:::

<1>2. The roots from step <1>1 satisfy
$$
u+v+w=a,
$$
$$
uv+uw+vw=b,
$$
and
$$
uvw=c.
$$

::: {.proof}
Expanding the factorization from step <1>1 gives
$$
(t-u)(t-v)(t-w)
=
t^3-(u+v+w)t^2
+(uv+uw+vw)t
-uvw.
$$
Compare coefficients with
$$
t^3-at^2+bt-c.
$$
:::

<1>3. The map $F$ is onto.

::: {.proof}
For the arbitrary point $(a,b,c)$ chosen in step <1>1, step <1>2 gives
$$
F(u,v,w)
=
(a,b,c).
$$
Thus every point of $\CC^3$ has a preimage.
:::

<1>4. The map $F$ is invariant under permutations of its three input
coordinates.

::: {.proof}
Each component of
$$
F(u,v,w)
=
(u+v+w,\ uv+vw+uw,\ uvw)
$$
is a symmetric polynomial in $u,v,w$.
:::

<1>5. The map $F$ is not one-to-one.

::: {.proof}
The points
$$
(1,2,3)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(2,1,3)
$$
are distinct, but step <1>4 gives
$$
F(1,2,3)
=
F(2,1,3).
$$
Explicitly, both images are
$$
(6,11,6).
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves surjectivity and step <1>5 proves noninjectivity.
:::
:::
