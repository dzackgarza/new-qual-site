::: {.problem}
Exhibit a cell decomposition of the Klein bottle and use this to compute its homology.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cw-structure}
Give the Klein bottle \(K\) the standard polygonal CW structure with one \(0\)-cell \(v\), two oriented \(1\)-cells \(a,b\), and one \(2\)-cell \(e^2\) attached along the word
\[
aba^{-1}b.
\]

::: pf-proof
Start with a square.
Identify one pair of opposite edges preserving their orientations and the other pair reversing their orientations, as in the usual square model of the Klein bottle.
All four vertices become one vertex \(v\), each pair of opposite edges becomes one \(1\)-cell, and the interior is a single open \(2\)-cell.
With compatible choices of orientations for the two \(1\)-cells, traversing the boundary of the square once gives
\[
a\,b\,a^{-1}\,b.
\]
Thus
\[
K=e^0\cup e_a^1\cup e_b^1\cup e^2
\]
with the stated attaching word.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #chain-complex}
The cellular chain complex of this CW structure is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
C_2(K)\cong\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
C_1(K)\cong\mathbb Z a\oplus\mathbb Z b
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
C_0(K)\cong\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0,
\]
and
\[
\partial_1=0.
\]

::: pf-proof
There is exactly one cell in dimensions \(0\) and \(2\), exactly two cells in dimension \(1\), and no higher-dimensional cells, giving the displayed cellular chain groups.
Both endpoints of each \(1\)-cell are the single vertex \(v\), so each oriented \(1\)-cell has cellular boundary \(v-v=0\). Hence \(\partial_1=0\).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boundary-of-2-cell}
With respect to the basis \((a,b)\) of \(C_1(K)\),
\[
\partial_2(1)=(0,2),
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\partial_2(1)=2b.
\]

::: pf-proof
For a CW complex with one \(0\)-cell, the coefficient of a \(1\)-cell in the cellular boundary of a \(2\)-cell is the degree obtained by collapsing all other \(1\)-cells; for an attaching word, this is the total signed exponent of that generator.

The attaching word from step [](#cw-structure){.pf-ref} is
\[
aba^{-1}b.
\]
The total exponent of \(a\) is
\[
1+(-1)=0,
\]
while the total exponent of \(b\) is
\[
1+1=2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\partial_2(1)=0a+2b.
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #h0-computation}
The zeroth homology is
\[
H_0(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

::: pf-proof
By step [](#chain-complex){.pf-ref},
\[
H_0(K;\mathbb Z)
=
C_0(K)/\operatorname{im}\partial_1
\cong
\mathbb Z/0
\cong
\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #h1-computation}
The first homology is
\[
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

::: pf-proof
Since \(\partial_1=0\),
\[
\ker\partial_1=C_1(K)=\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb.
\]
By step [](#boundary-of-2-cell){.pf-ref},
\[
\operatorname{im}\partial_2=\mathbb Z(2b).
\]
Hence
\[
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)
=
\frac{\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb}{\mathbb Z(2b)}
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #high-degree-vanishing}
The second and all higher homology groups vanish:
\[
H_k(K;\mathbb Z)=0
\qquad(k\ge2).
\]

::: pf-proof
By step [](#boundary-of-2-cell){.pf-ref}, the homomorphism
\[
\partial_2:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
m\longmapsto(0,2m)
\]
is injective.
Therefore
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2=0.
\]
There are no cells in dimensions \(k>2\), so \(C_k(K)=0\) and hence \(H_k(K;\mathbb Z)=0\) for every \(k>2\).
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Thus the integral homology of the Klein bottle is
\[
\boxed{
H_k(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: pf-proof
Combine step [](#h0-computation){.pf-ref}, step [](#h1-computation){.pf-ref} and step [](#high-degree-vanishing){.pf-ref}.
:::
