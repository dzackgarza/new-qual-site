::: {.problem}
a. State what it means for a topological space $X$ to be

i. compact

ii.
Hausdorff

iii.
normal

b. Prove that every compact Hausdorff space is normal.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #definitions}
The three definitions are as follows.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
\(X\) is **compact** if every open cover of \(X\) has a finite subcover.

:::

::: pf-step
\(X\) is **Hausdorff** if for every pair of distinct points \(x,y\in X\), there exist disjoint open sets \(U,V\subseteq X\) with
\[
x\in U,
\qquad
y\in V.
\]

:::

::: pf-step
\(X\) is **normal** if for every pair of disjoint closed sets \(A,B\subseteq X\), there exist disjoint open sets \(U,V\subseteq X\) with
\[
A\subseteq U,
\qquad
B\subseteq V.
\]

:::

These are precisely the open-cover definition of compactness and the separation conditions required in part (a).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hausdorff-separation}
Let \(K\subseteq X\) be compact, let \(x\in X\setminus K\), and assume \(X\) is Hausdorff.
Then there are disjoint open sets \(U,V\subseteq X\) such that
\[
K\subseteq U,
\qquad
x\in V.
\]

::: pf-proof
For each \(y\in K\), Hausdorffness gives disjoint open sets
\[
U_y\ni y,
\qquad
V_y\ni x.
\]
The family \(\{U_y:y\in K\}\) covers \(K\). By compactness, choose
\[
y_1,\dots,y_m\in K
\]
with
\[
K\subseteq U_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{y_m}.
\]
Define
\[
U=U_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{y_m},
\qquad
V=V_{y_1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{y_m}.
\]
Both sets are open, \(K\subseteq U\), and \(x\in V\). If \(z\in U\), then \(z\in U_{y_i}\) for some \(i\), whereas
\[
V\subseteq V_{y_i}
\]
and
\[
U_{y_i}\cap V_{y_i}=\varnothing.
\]
Hence \(z\notin V\), so \(U\cap V=\varnothing\).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #closed-subset-compact}
Every closed subset of a compact space is compact.

::: pf-proof
Let \(A\subseteq X\) be closed and let \(\mathcal U\) be an open cover of \(A\) by sets open in \(X\). Then
\[
\mathcal U\cup\{X\setminus A\}
\]
is an open cover of \(X\). Compactness of \(X\) gives a finite subcover.
Deleting \(X\setminus A\), if it occurs, leaves a finite subfamily of \(\mathcal U\) covering \(A\). The same conclusion holds for a cover by sets open in the subspace \(A\), after writing each such set as \(A\cap O\) with \(O\) open in \(X\).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-covered-by-tubes}
Let \(A,B\subseteq X\) be disjoint closed sets, where \(X\) is compact Hausdorff.
If both are nonempty, then for each \(a\in A\) there are disjoint open sets \(U_a,W_a\subseteq X\) satisfying
\[
a\in U_a,
\qquad
B\subseteq W_a.
\]

::: pf-proof
By step [](#closed-subset-compact){.pf-ref}, \(B\) is compact.
Since \(a\notin B\), apply step [](#hausdorff-separation){.pf-ref} with
\[
K=B,
\qquad
x=a.
\]
This gives the required pair \(U_a,W_a\).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-b-disjoint-nbhds}
The sets \(A\) and \(B\) have disjoint open neighborhoods.

::: pf-proof
If \(A=\varnothing\) or \(B=\varnothing\), use \(\varnothing\) and \(X\). Assume both are nonempty.
By step [](#closed-subset-compact){.pf-ref}, \(A\) is compact.
The open sets \(U_a\) from step [](#a-covered-by-tubes){.pf-ref} cover \(A\), so choose
\[
a_1,\dots,a_r\in A
\]
such that
\[
A\subseteq U_{a_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{a_r}.
\]
Set
\[
U=U_{a_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{a_r},
\qquad
W=W_{a_1}\cap\cdots\cap W_{a_r}.
\]
Then \(U\) and \(W\) are open,
\[
A\subseteq U,
\qquad
B\subseteq W,
\]
because every \(W_{a_i}\) contains \(B\). Moreover \(U\cap W=\varnothing\): if \(z\in U\), then \(z\in U_{a_i}\) for some \(i\), while
\[
W\subseteq W_{a_i}
\]
and \(U_{a_i}\cap W_{a_i}=\varnothing\). Thus \(z\notin W\).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #compact-hausdorff-normal}
Therefore every compact Hausdorff space is normal.

::: pf-proof
For arbitrary disjoint closed subsets \(A,B\subseteq X\), step [](#a-b-disjoint-nbhds){.pf-ref} supplies disjoint open neighborhoods.
This is the definition of normality in step [](#definitions){.pf-ref}.
:::
