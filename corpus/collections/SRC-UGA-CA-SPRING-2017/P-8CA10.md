::: {.problem}
Exhibit a sequence of explicit conformal mappings, the composite of which gives a one-to-one conformal map from the region
$$
R=\{z:\ \abs{z}<2\text{ and }\abs{z-1}>1\}
$$
onto the upper half-plane.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

Let $R=\{z:\abs{z}<2\}\cap\{z:\abs{z-1}>1\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #w1}
$w_1=\dfrac1{z-2}$ is conformal (holomorphic and injective) on $R$, and
$$
\abs{z}<2\iff\operatorname{Re}w_1<-\tfrac14,
\qquad
\abs{z-1}>1\iff\operatorname{Re}w_1>-\tfrac12 .
$$

::: pf-proof
$z\mapsto1/(z-2)$ is holomorphic off $2$, and $R$ does not contain $2$ because $\abs{2}=2$ is not $<2$; it is injective with inverse $w\mapsto2+1/w$. Writing $w=w_1$, so that $z=2+1/w$,
$$
\abs{z}^2=\abs{2+\frac1w}^2=\frac{\abs{2w+1}^2}{\abs{w}^2}=\frac{4\abs{w}^2+4\operatorname{Re}w+1}{\abs{w}^2}=4+\frac{4\operatorname{Re}w+1}{\abs{w}^2},
$$
using $\abs{2w+1}^2=4\abs{w}^2+4\operatorname{Re}w+1$. Hence $\abs{z}^2<4$ exactly when $4\operatorname{Re}w+1<0$, which is the first equivalence. Similarly $z-1=1+1/w$ and
$$
\abs{z-1}^2=\frac{\abs{w+1}^2}{\abs{w}^2}=\frac{\abs{w}^2+2\operatorname{Re}w+1}{\abs{w}^2}=1+\frac{2\operatorname{Re}w+1}{\abs{w}^2},
$$
so $\abs{z-1}^2>1$ exactly when $\operatorname{Re}w>-\frac12$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #strip}
$w_1$ maps $R$ biholomorphically onto the vertical strip $S=\{w:-\frac12<\operatorname{Re}w<-\frac14\}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#w1){.pf-ref}, $z\in R$ exactly when both inequalities on $\operatorname{Re}w_1$ hold; so $w_1(R)\subseteq S$. Conversely, if $\operatorname{Re}w\in(-\frac12,-\frac14)$ then $w\ne0$ and $z=2+1/w\ne2$ satisfies both inequalities of step [](#w1){.pf-ref}, so $z\in R$ and $w_1(z)=w$. Thus $w_1:R\to S$ is a bijection, holomorphic, with holomorphic inverse $w\mapsto2+1/w$ on $S$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #w2}
$w_2=4\pi i\left(w_1+\frac12\right)$ maps $S$ biholomorphically onto the horizontal strip $\{0<\operatorname{Im}w_2<\pi\}$.

::: pf-proof
The map $w\mapsto4\pi i(w+\frac12)$ is a nonconstant affine map, hence biholomorphic onto its image. If $w=u+iv$ with $-\frac12<u<-\frac14$ then $4\pi i\left(u+\frac12+iv\right)=-4\pi v+4\pi i\left(u+\frac12\right)$, whose imaginary part is $4\pi\left(u+\frac12\right)$, and $0<u+\frac12<\frac14$ gives $0<\operatorname{Im}w_2<\pi$. The imaginary part ranges over all of $(0,\pi)$ and the real part over all of $\RR$ as $v,u$ vary, so the image is exactly that strip.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #w3}
$w_3=e^{w_2}$ maps $\{0<\operatorname{Im}w_2<\pi\}$ biholomorphically onto the upper half-plane.

::: pf-proof
Write $w_2=s+it$ with $0<t<\pi$. Then $\abs{e^{w_2}}=e^s>0$ and $\arg e^{w_2}\equiv t\pmod{2\pi}$, so $e^{w_2}$ lies in the upper half-plane; and every $W$ with $\operatorname{Im}W>0$ has exactly one argument $t\in(0,\pi)$ and modulus $e^s$, so $W=e^{s+it}$ for a unique $s+it$ in the strip. Thus $w_3$ is a bijection onto the upper half-plane, holomorphic, with holomorphic inverse $W\mapsto\log\abs{W}+i\arg W$ taken with $\arg\in(0,\pi)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\Phi(z)=\exp\!\left(4\pi i\left(\dfrac1{z-2}+\dfrac12\right)\right)$ maps $R$ one-to-one conformally onto the upper half-plane.

::: pf-proof
$\Phi$ is the composite of the three maps of steps [](#strip){.pf-ref}, [](#w2){.pf-ref} and [](#w3){.pf-ref}, each of which is a biholomorphism onto its image; a composite of biholomorphisms is a biholomorphism onto the final image, the upper half-plane.
:::
