::: {.problem}
Prove or disprove: the equation $\sin(z)=e^{\alpha z^3}$, for $\alpha>1$, has exactly three solutions inside the unit disk.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
F(z)=\sin z-e^{\alpha z^3},
\qquad \alpha>1.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The function $F$ has finitely many zeros in the open unit disk, and $F(\bar z)=\overline{F(z)}$, so its nonreal zeros there occur in conjugate pairs of equal multiplicity.

::: pf-proof

$F$ is entire and not identically zero ($F(0)=-1$), so its zeros in the compact closed disk are isolated and hence finitely many. Since $\alpha$ is real, the power series of $\sin z$ and $e^{\alpha z^3}$ have real coefficients, which gives $F(\bar z)=\overline{F(z)}$. Differentiating this identity shows that $z_0$ and $\bar z_0$ are zeros of the same multiplicity, and the open unit disk is invariant under conjugation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$F$ has no real zeros in $(-1,1)$.

::: pf-proof

If $-1<x<0$, then $\sin x<0<e^{\alpha x^3}$. If $0\le x<1$, then $x^3\ge0$, so
$$
\sin x<1\le e^{\alpha x^3}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the zeros of $F$ in the unit disk are nonreal and pair off under conjugation, so their number, counted with or without multiplicity, is even. It is therefore never three, and the claim is $\boxed{\text{false}}$.

:::
