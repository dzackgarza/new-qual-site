::: {.problem}
Show that the equation $\sin(z)=e^{\alpha}z^3$, for $\alpha>1$, has exactly three solutions inside the unit disk.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $F(z)=e^{\alpha}z^3-\sin z$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #count}
$F$ has exactly three zeros in $\abs{z}<1$, counted with multiplicity, and none on $\abs{z}=1$.

::: pf-proof
On $\abs{z}=1$, $\abs{\sin z}\le\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{\abs{z}^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}=\sinh1<e<e^{\alpha}=\abs{e^{\alpha}z^3}$. By Rouché's theorem, $F$ and $e^{\alpha}z^3$ have the same number of zeros in $\abs{z}<1$, namely three, and $F$ has no zero on the circle.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #simple}
Every zero of $F$ in $\abs{z}<1$ is simple.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $F(z_0)=F'(z_0)=0$ with $\abs{z_0}<1$, that is, $\sin z_0=e^{\alpha}z_0^3$ and $\cos z_0=3e^{\alpha}z_0^2$. Then $z_0\neq0$, since $F'(0)=-1$. From the second equation, $3e^{\alpha}\abs{z_0}^2\le\cosh1<3e$, so $\abs{z_0}<\frac12$. Eliminating $e^{\alpha}$ gives $g(z_0)=0$, where
$$
g(z)=\frac{\sin z}{z}-\frac{\cos z}{3}.
$$
For $\abs{z}\le\frac12$, the power series give
$$
\Bigl\lvert\frac{\sin z}{z}-1\Bigr\rvert\le\frac{\sinh\frac12}{\frac12}-1<0.05,
\qquad
\abs{\cos z-1}\le\cosh\tfrac12-1<0.13,
$$
so $\abs{g(z)-\frac23}<0.05+0.05<\frac23$ and $g(z)\neq0$. This contradiction shows that no zero is multiple.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
By steps [](#count){.pf-ref} and [](#simple){.pf-ref}, the equation has exactly three distinct solutions in the unit disk. One of them is $z=0$.
:::
