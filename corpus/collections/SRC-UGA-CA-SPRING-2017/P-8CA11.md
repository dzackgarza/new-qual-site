::: {.problem}
Let $U\subset\mathbb{C}$ be the punctured unit disk $0<|z|<1$, and let $V$ be the open annulus $\tfrac12<|z|<1$.

(i) Prove that there is a smooth bijection $f:U\to V$ with a smooth inverse.
(One way to do this is to write one down.)

(ii) Prove however that no such function $f$ is conformal.
:::

::: {.solution}
For (i), in polar coordinates define
\[
F(re^{i\theta})=\frac{1+r}{2}e^{i\theta},\qquad 0<r<1.
\]
This is a smooth bijection from $U$ onto $V$, with smooth inverse
\[
F^{-1}(\rho e^{i\theta})=(2\rho-1)e^{i\theta},\qquad \frac12<\rho<1.
\]

For (ii), suppose $f:U\to V$ were conformal. Since $|f|<1$, the puncture at $0$ is removable, so $f$ extends holomorphically to $\widetilde f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$. Because $|f(z)|>1/2$ on $U$, continuity gives $|\widetilde f(0)|\ge1/2$.

If $|\widetilde f(0)|>1/2$, then $\widetilde f(0)\in V$ (also $|\widetilde f(0)|\le1$), and surjectivity gives $z_0\in U$ with $f(z_0)=\widetilde f(0)$, contradicting injectivity of the holomorphic extension near $0$. If $|\widetilde f(0)|=1/2$ or $1$, the open mapping theorem forces values of $\widetilde f$ near $0$ to cross outside the closed annulus $1/2\le|w|\le1$, contradicting $f(U)\subset V$. Thus no conformal equivalence exists.
:::
