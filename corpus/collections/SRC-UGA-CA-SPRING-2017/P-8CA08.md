::: {.problem}
Let $f:\CC\to\CC$ be given by
$$
f(x+iy)=x^2-y^2+3y+i(xy-4y).
$$
Find all points in $\CC$ at which $f$ is complex differentiable.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on it.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #parts}
With $f=u+iv$ one has $u(x,y)=x^{2}-y^{2}+3y$, $v(x,y)=xy-4y$, and the partial derivatives
$$
u_{x}=2x,\quad u_{y}=-2y+3,\quad v_{x}=y,\quad v_{y}=x-4
$$
are continuous on $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
Expanding $f(x+iy)=x^{2}-y^{2}+3y+i(xy-4y)$ gives the two real-valued parts, which are polynomials, so their partial derivatives exist and are the displayed ones and are continuous.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cr}
The Cauchy--Riemann equations $u_{x}=v_{y}$ and $u_{y}=-v_{x}$ hold exactly at $(-4,3)$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting the derivatives of step [](#parts){.pf-ref}: $u_{x}=v_{y}$ reads $2x=x-4$, that is $x=-4$; and $u_{y}=-v_{x}$ reads $-2y+3=-y$, that is $y=3$. The two conditions are independent, so they hold simultaneously exactly at $(x,y)=(-4,3)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equivalence}
$f$ is complex differentiable at a point exactly where the Cauchy--Riemann equations hold there.

::: pf-proof
$f$ is real differentiable, its real and imaginary parts having continuous partial derivatives (step [](#parts){.pf-ref}). For such a map the Cauchy--Riemann equations at a point are equivalent to complex differentiability there: they say precisely that the real linear map approximating $f$ at that point is multiplication by the complex number $u_{x}+iv_{x}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$f$ is complex differentiable exactly at $\boxed{z=-4+3i}$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#cr){.pf-ref} and [](#equivalence){.pf-ref} combine: the point of $\CC$ with coordinates $(-4,3)$ is $-4+3i$, and no other point satisfies the equations.
:::
