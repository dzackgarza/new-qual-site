::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$ be given by
\[
f(x+iy)=x^2-y^2+3y+i(xy-4y).
\]
Find all points in $\mathbb{C}$ at which $f$ is complex differentiable.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $f=u+iv$ with
\[
u=x^2-y^2+3y,\qquad v=xy-4y.
\]
Since the first partial derivatives are continuous, $f$ is complex differentiable exactly where the Cauchy--Riemann equations hold:
\[
u_x=v_y,\qquad u_y=-v_x.
\]
These become
\[
2x=x-4,\qquad -2y+3=-y.
\]
Hence $x=-4$ and $y=3$. Therefore $f$ is complex differentiable exactly at
\[
\boxed{-4+3i}.
\]
:::
