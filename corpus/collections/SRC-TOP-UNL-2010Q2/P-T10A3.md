::: {.problem}
A space $(X,\mathcal T)$ is called locally path-connected if for every $p\in X$, every open neighborhood of $p$ contains a path-connected open neighborhood of $p$.
Show that the product of two locally path-connected spaces is locally path-connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be locally path-connected spaces.

<1>1. Let $(x_0,y_0)\in X\times Y$ and let $W$ be an open neighborhood of $(x_0,y_0)$.
There are open sets $U\subseteq X$ and $V\subseteq Y$ such that
\[
(x_0,y_0)\in U\times V\subseteq W.
\]
::: {.proof}
The products of open subsets of $X$ and $Y$ form a basis for the product topology.
Since $W$ is open and contains $(x_0,y_0)$, some basic product neighborhood $U\times V$ of that point is contained in $W$.
:::

<1>2. There are path-connected open sets $U_0\subseteq X$ and $V_0\subseteq Y$ satisfying
\[
x_0\in U_0\subseteq U,
\qquad
y_0\in V_0\subseteq V.
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply local path-connectedness of $X$ to the open neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$, and local path-connectedness of $Y$ to the open neighborhood $V$ of $y_0$.
:::

<1>3. The product $U_0\times V_0$ is path-connected.
::: {.proof}
Take arbitrary points
\[
(x_1,y_1),(x_2,y_2)\in U_0\times V_0.
\]
Since $U_0$ is path-connected, choose a path
\[
\alpha:[0,1]\to U_0
\]
from $x_1$ to $x_2$.
Since $V_0$ is path-connected, choose a path
\[
\beta:[0,1]\to V_0
\]
from $y_1$ to $y_2$.
Then
\[
\gamma(t)=(\alpha(t),\beta(t))
\]
is continuous by the defining property of the product topology, lies in $U_0\times V_0$, and joins $(x_1,y_1)$ to $(x_2,y_2)$.
Thus $U_0\times V_0$ is path-connected.
:::

<1>4. The product $X\times Y$ is locally path-connected.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, $U_0\times V_0$ is open and contains $(x_0,y_0)$.
By <1>1--<1>2,
\[
U_0\times V_0\subseteq U\times V\subseteq W,
\]
and by <1>3 it is path-connected.
Thus every open neighborhood of every point of $X\times Y$ contains a path-connected open neighborhood of that point, which is exactly local path-connectedness.
:::
:::
