::: {.problem}
A space $(X,\mathcal T)$ is called locally path-connected if for every $p\in X$, every open neighborhood of $p$ contains a path-connected open neighborhood of $p$.
Show that the product of two locally path-connected spaces is locally path-connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Setup neighborhood in the product topology:
<2>1. Let $X$ and $Y$ be locally path-connected spaces, and let $(x_0, y_0) \in X \times Y$.
::: {.proof}
setup.
:::
<2>2. Let $W \subseteq X \times Y$ be an arbitrary open neighborhood of $(x_0, y_0)$.
::: {.proof}
neighborhood setup.
:::
<2>3. By the definition of the product topology, there exist open sets $U \subseteq X$ and $V \subseteq Y$ such that:
\[
(x_0, y_0) \in U \times V \subseteq W.
\]
::: {.proof}
basis for the product topology.
:::

<1>2. Construct a path-connected open neighborhood $U_0 \times V_0$:
<2>1. Since $X$ is locally path-connected and $U$ is an open neighborhood of $x_0 \in X$, there exists an open, path-connected neighborhood $U_0 \subseteq X$ such that $x_0 \in U_0 \subseteq U$.
::: {.proof}
definition of local path-connectedness for $X$.
:::
<2>2. Since $Y$ is locally path-connected and $V$ is an open neighborhood of $y_0 \in Y$, there exists an open, path-connected neighborhood $V_0 \subseteq Y$ such that $y_0 \in V_0 \subseteq V$.
::: {.proof}
definition of local path-connectedness for $Y$.
:::
<2>3. Let $N = U_0 \times V_0 \subseteq X \times Y$.
$N$ is open in $X \times Y$, contains $(x_0, y_0)$, and satisfies $N \subseteq U \times V \subseteq W$.
::: {.proof}
product of open sets is open in the product topology.
:::

<1>3. Show that $U_0 \times V_0$ is path-connected:
<2>1. Let $(x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2) \in U_0 \times V_0$ be two arbitrary points.
::: {.proof}
arbitrary pair of points in $N$.
:::
<2>2. Since $U_0$ is path-connected, there exists a continuous path $\gamma_1: [0, 1] \to U_0$ such that $\gamma_1(0) = x_1$ and $\gamma_1(1) = x_2$.
::: {.proof}
definition of path-connectedness for $U_0$.
:::
<2>3. Since $V_0$ is path-connected, there exists a continuous path $\gamma_2: [0, 1] \to V_0$ such that $\gamma_2(0) = y_1$ and $\gamma_2(1) = y_2$.
::: {.proof}
definition of path-connectedness for $V_0$.
:::
<2>4. Define $\gamma: [0, 1] \to U_0 \times V_0$ by $\gamma(t) = (\gamma_1(t), \gamma_2(t))$.
$\gamma$ is continuous because its coordinate functions $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are continuous.
::: {.proof}
universal property of the product topology.
:::
<2>5. Furthermore, $\gamma(0) = (x_1, y_1)$ and $\gamma(1) = (x_2, y_2)$, so $\gamma$ is a continuous path in $U_0 \times V_0$ connecting the two points.
::: {.proof}
evaluation at $t = 0, 1$.
:::
<2>6. Thus $U_0 \times V_0$ is path-connected.
::: {.proof}
<2>1 through <2>5.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
For every $(x_0, y_0) \in X \times Y$ and every open neighborhood $W$, $N = U_0 \times V_0$ is a path-connected open neighborhood of $(x_0, y_0)$ with $N \subseteq W$.
Therefore $X \times Y$ is locally path-connected. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>3.
:::
