::: {.problem}
Attach a 2-disc $D^2$ to a torus $S^1\times S^1$ by the attaching map $e^{2\pi it}\mapsto(e^{2\pi it},e^{2\pi it})$, thinking of the boundary $\partial D^2$ and each $S^1$ factor as the unit circle in the complex plane, and let $X$ be the resulting space.
Compute a presentation for the fundamental group of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
T=S^1\times S^1
\]
and take the basepoint $(1,1)$.
Let $a$ be the loop around the first factor and $b$ the loop around the second.

<1>1. The torus has presentation
\[
\pi_1(T,(1,1))
\cong
\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the standard CW structure on $T$ with one $0$-cell, two oriented $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell whose boundary is attached along the commutator word
\[
aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]
The Seifert--van Kampen theorem for this cell attachment gives the displayed presentation.
:::

<1>2. The attaching map
\[
\gamma(t)=\bigl(e^{2\pi it},e^{2\pi it}\bigr)
\]
represents the element $ab\in\pi_1(T,(1,1))$.
::: {.proof}
Under the standard identification
\[
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1,(1,1))
\cong
\pi_1(S^1,1)\times\pi_1(S^1,1)
\cong
\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z,
\]
the loop $\gamma$ projects to the positive generator in each circle factor.
Hence its class is $(1,1)$.
The classes of $a$ and $b$ are $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ respectively, so
\[
[\gamma]=[a][b]=ab.
\]
:::

<1>3. Attaching the disk gives
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1,\ ab=1\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
Attaching a $2$-cell along a loop quotients the fundamental group by the normal closure of that loop class.
Applying the Seifert--van Kampen theorem to the attachment and using <1>1--<1>2 therefore adds the single relator
\[
ab=1
\]
to the torus presentation.
:::

<1>4. The group in <1>3 is infinite cyclic.
::: {.proof}
The relation $ab=1$ gives
\[
b=a^{-1}.
\]
After substituting this into the commutator relation, that relation becomes
\[
aa^{-1}a^{-1}a=1,
\]
which is automatic.
Thus every element is represented by a power of $a$.

More explicitly, define
\[
\Phi:\mathbb Z\longrightarrow
\langle a,b\mid [a,b]=1,ab=1\rangle,
\qquad
n\longmapsto a^n.
\]
It is surjective because $b=a^{-1}$.
Conversely the assignments
\[
a\longmapsto1,
\qquad
b\longmapsto-1
\]
satisfy both relators and therefore induce a homomorphism to $\mathbb Z$ inverse to $\Phi$.
Hence
\[
\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>5. A presentation answering the question is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid [a,b]=1,\ ab=1\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z.
}
\]
::: {.proof}
This is exactly <1>3--<1>4, with the commutator notation
\[
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]
:::
:::
