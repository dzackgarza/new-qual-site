::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained from the 2-simplex $\Delta^2$ by identifying all three vertices together.
Describe a $\Delta$-complex structure on the space $X$ and compute its homology groups.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the vertices of the original simplex as $v_0,v_1,v_2$, and let $v$ denote their common image in $X$.
Orient the three original edges by
\[
a=[v_0,v_1],
\qquad
b=[v_1,v_2],
\qquad
c=[v_0,v_2].
\]

<1>1. The quotient $X$ has a $\Delta$-complex structure with one $0$-simplex $v$, three $1$-simplices $a,b,c$, and one $2$-simplex $\sigma$.
::: {.proof}
The quotient identifies only the three vertices of the standard simplex.
The interior of each original edge is unaffected and maps injectively to $X$, so the three edge interiors give three distinct open $1$-simplices whose two endpoints are all attached to the single vertex $v$.
The interior of $\Delta^2$ is likewise unaffected and gives one open $2$-simplex $\sigma$.

The quotient map restricted to the closed standard $2$-simplex is a characteristic map for $\sigma$; each face is one of the three stated $1$-simplices, and their vertices all map to $v$.
These identifications are allowed in a $\Delta$-complex, so the stated cells form the required $\Delta$-complex structure.
:::

<1>2. Its simplicial chain groups are
\[
C_2(X)=\mathbb Z\langle\sigma\rangle,
\qquad
C_1(X)=\mathbb Z\langle a,b,c\rangle,
\qquad
C_0(X)=\mathbb Z\langle v\rangle,
\]
with
\[
\partial_1=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\partial_2(\sigma)=a+b-c.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every $1$-simplex begins and ends at the same vertex $v$, so
\[
\partial_1(a)=\partial_1(b)=\partial_1(c)=v-v=0.
\]

Orient $\sigma$ as $[v_0,v_1,v_2]$.
The simplicial boundary formula gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_2[v_0,v_1,v_2]
&=[v_1,v_2]-[v_0,v_2]+[v_0,v_1]\\
&=b-c+a\\
&=a+b-c.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>3. We have
\[
H_2(X)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The homomorphism
\[
\partial_2:\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^3
\]
sends $1$ to the nonzero vector
\[
(1,1,-1).
\]
Hence $\partial_2$ is injective, so
\[
H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2=0.
\]
There are no $3$-simplices.
:::

<1>4. We have
\[
H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2,
\[
\ker\partial_1=C_1(X)=\mathbb Z^3
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\mathbb Z\langle a+b-c\rangle.
\]
Thus
\[
H_1(X)
\cong
\mathbb Z^3/\mathbb Z(1,1,-1).
\]
The vector $(1,1,-1)$ is primitive because its coordinates have greatest common divisor $1$.
Explicitly, the elements
\[
a,
\qquad
b,
\qquad
d=a+b-c
\]
form a basis of $\mathbb Z^3$: indeed $c=a+b-d$, so they generate, and the change-of-basis matrix has determinant $-1$.
Quotienting by $\mathbb Z d$ therefore leaves the free group generated by the classes of $a$ and $b$.
Hence
\[
H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
:::

<1>5. We have
\[
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_n(X)=0\quad(n\ge3).
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton has one vertex, so $X$ is connected; equivalently, since $\partial_1=0$ and $C_0(X)=\mathbb Z$, the chain computation gives
\[
H_0(X)=C_0(X)/\operatorname{im}\partial_1\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
There are no simplices in dimensions at least $3$, so all higher chain groups and hence all homology groups $H_n$ for $n\ge3$ vanish.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
H_n(X)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2, & n=1,\\
0, & n\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>3--<1>5.
:::
