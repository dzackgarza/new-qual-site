::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a Hausdorff space, $p\in X$, and $A\subseteq X$ a compact subset of $X$ disjoint from $p$.
Show that there exist disjoint open sets $U,V\subseteq X$ with $p\in U$ and $A\subseteq V$.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $A=\varnothing$, take
\[
U=X,
\qquad
V=\varnothing.
\]
Thus assume $A\ne\varnothing$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $a\in A$, there are disjoint open sets $U_a,V_a\subseteq X$ such that
\[
p\in U_a,
\qquad
a\in V_a.
\]

::: pf-proof

Because $p\notin A$, we have $p\ne a$ for every $a\in A$.
Since $X$ is Hausdorff, any two distinct points admit disjoint open neighborhoods.
Apply this to $p$ and $a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

There are points $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in A$ such that
\[
A\subseteq V_{a_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{a_n}.
\]

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, the family
\[
\{V_a:a\in A\}
\]
is an open cover of $A$.
Since $A$ is compact, this cover has a finite subcover.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Define
\[
U=U_{a_1}\cap\cdots\cap U_{a_n},
\qquad
V=V_{a_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{a_n}.
\]
Then $U$ and $V$ are open, $p\in U$, and $A\subseteq V$.

::: pf-proof

A finite intersection of open sets and a finite union of open sets are open.
Each $U_{a_i}$ contains $p$, so their intersection contains $p$.
The inclusion $A\subseteq V$ is exactly step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The open sets $U$ and $V$ are disjoint.

::: pf-proof

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $x\in U\cap V$.
Since $x\in V$, there is an index $i$ with
\[
x\in V_{a_i}.
\]
Since $x\in U$, the definition of $U$ gives
\[
x\in U_{a_i}.
\]
This contradicts
\[
U_{a_i}\cap V_{a_i}=\varnothing
\]
from step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Hence $U\cap V=\varnothing$.
Together with step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, these are the required neighborhoods.

:::
