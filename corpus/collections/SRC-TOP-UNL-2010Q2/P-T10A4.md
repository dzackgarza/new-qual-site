::: {.problem}
Let $Z=X\cup Y$, for $X$ and $Y$ connected subspaces of $Z$ with $X\cap Y=\varnothing$.
Let $x_0\in X$ and $y_0\in Y$.
Let $\sim$ be the equivalence relation generated by the equivalence $x_0\sim y_0$.
Show that the quotient space $Z/\sim$ is connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
q:Z\longrightarrow Z/\!\sim
\]
be the quotient map.

<1>1. The subsets $q(X)$ and $q(Y)$ of $Z/\!\sim$ are connected.
::: {.proof}
The restrictions
\[
q|_X:X\longrightarrow q(X),
\qquad
q|_Y:Y\longrightarrow q(Y)
\]
are continuous and surjective.

To see directly that a continuous image of a connected space is connected, suppose for instance that $q(X)$ had a separation
\[
q(X)=U\cup V
\]
into disjoint nonempty sets open in the subspace topology.
Then
\[
(q|_X)^{-1}(U),
\qquad
(q|_X)^{-1}(V)
\]
would be disjoint nonempty open subsets of $X$ whose union is $X$, contradicting connectedness of $X$.
Thus $q(X)$ is connected, and the same argument gives connectedness of $q(Y)$.
:::

<1>2. The sets $q(X)$ and $q(Y)$ have nonempty intersection.
::: {.proof}
The equivalence relation identifies $x_0$ and $y_0$, so
\[
q(x_0)=q(y_0).
\]
Since $x_0\in X$ and $y_0\in Y$, this common point lies in
\[
q(X)\cap q(Y).
\]
:::

<1>3. If two connected subsets of a space have nonempty intersection, then their union is connected.
::: {.proof}
Let $C,D$ be connected and suppose $C\cap D\ne\varnothing$.
If $C\cup D$ admitted a separation into disjoint nonempty relatively open sets $U,V$, connectedness of $C$ would force $C$ to lie entirely in one of $U,V$, and connectedness of $D$ would force $D$ to lie entirely in one of them.
Because $C\cap D\ne\varnothing$, they cannot lie in different members of the separation.
Hence both $C$ and $D$ lie in the same member, leaving the other empty, a contradiction.
Therefore $C\cup D$ is connected.
:::

<1>4. The quotient space $Z/\!\sim$ is connected.
::: {.proof}
Because $Z=X\cup Y$ and $q$ is surjective,
\[
Z/\!\sim=q(Z)=q(X)\cup q(Y).
\]
By <1>1 these two subsets are connected, and by <1>2 they intersect.
Applying <1>3 proves that their union, hence $Z/\!\sim$, is connected.
:::
:::
