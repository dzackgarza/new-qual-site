::: {.problem}
Use covering space theory to show that if $H$ is a subgroup of index $3$ in a finitely presented group $G$, then $H$ is finitely presented.
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose a finite presentation
\[
G\cong\langle g_1,\ldots,g_n\mid r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle.
\]

<1>1. There is a connected finite $2$-dimensional CW complex $X$ with basepoint $x_0$ such that
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong G.
\]
::: {.proof}
Start with one $0$-cell and attach $n$ oriented $1$-cells, one for each generator $g_i$.
Then attach $m$ $2$-cells along loops spelling the relators $r_j$.
The Seifert--van Kampen theorem gives exactly the chosen presentation for the fundamental group.
Because the presentation has finitely many generators and relators, this presentation complex has finitely many cells.
:::

<1>2. The subgroup $H\le G\cong\pi_1(X,x_0)$ determines a connected $3$-sheeted covering
\[
p:(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\longrightarrow(X,x_0)
\]
such that
\[
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)=H.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every CW complex is path-connected componentwise, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected.
Since $X$ is connected, the covering-space classification theorem identifies subgroups of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ with connected based covering spaces of $X$.
Apply it to $H$.

For a connected covering, the cardinality of the fiber over the basepoint equals the index of the associated subgroup.
Thus
\[
|p^{-1}(x_0)|=[G:H]=3,
\]
so the covering has three sheets.
:::

<1>3. The covering space $\widetilde X$ admits a finite CW structure for which $p$ maps every open cell homeomorphically onto an open cell of $X$.
::: {.proof}
The CW structure of a base space lifts through a covering map.
For completeness, proceed inductively over the cells of $X$.

Over the unique $0$-cell there are exactly three points, giving three $0$-cells upstairs.
Suppose the lift has been constructed over the $(k-1)$-skeleton.
For each open $k$-cell $e^k\subseteq X$, choose a characteristic map
\[
\chi:D^k\to X.
\]
Its restriction to the open disk is a homeomorphism onto $e^k$.
Because the open cell is simply connected and $p$ is a three-sheeted covering, the inverse image
\[
p^{-1}(e^k)
\]
is a disjoint union of three components, each mapped homeomorphically onto $e^k$; equivalently, the characteristic map has three lifts after a choice of a point over its center.
The lifted boundary maps attach these three disks to the already lifted $(k-1)$-skeleton.

Thus every cell of $X$ has exactly three lifted cells in $\widetilde X$.
Since $X$ has finitely many cells by <1>1, $\widetilde X$ has finitely many cells as well.
:::

<1>4. The fundamental group of a connected finite CW complex is finitely presented.
::: {.proof}
Let $Y$ be a connected finite CW complex.
Its finite connected $1$-skeleton $Y^{(1)}$ contains a maximal tree $T$.
Collapse $T$ to a point.
The quotient of $Y^{(1)}$ is a wedge of finitely many circles, one for each $1$-cell not in $T$; hence these circles give finitely many generators of the fundamental group.

Each $2$-cell of $Y$ has an attaching loop in the $1$-skeleton.
After collapsing $T$, that loop gives one word in the finite set of generators.
By Seifert--van Kampen, attaching the finitely many $2$-cells imposes precisely the normal closures of these finitely many words.
Cells of dimension at least $3$ do not change the fundamental group: their attaching spheres $S^{k-1}$ are simply connected for $k\ge3$, so van Kampen adds no new generators or relations to $\pi_1$.
Consequently $\pi_1(Y)$ has a presentation with finitely many generators and finitely many relators.
:::

<1>5. The subgroup $H$ is finitely presented.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, $\widetilde X$ is a connected finite CW complex.
Hence <1>4 shows that
\[
\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)
\]
is finitely presented.
The covering homomorphism $p_*$ is injective and, by <1>2, has image $H$.
Therefore
\[
\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\cong H,
\]
so $H$ is finitely presented.
:::
