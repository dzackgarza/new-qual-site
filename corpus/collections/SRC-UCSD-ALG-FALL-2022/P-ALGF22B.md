::: problem
Let $p$ and $q$ be distinct primes.
For any $r \geq 1$ consider the group $G$ with presentation $$\langle a, b \mid a^p = 1,\; b^q = 1,\; bab^{-1} = a^r \rangle.$$ For which $r$ is $G$ a group of order $pq$?
Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The defining relations imply
\[
a=a^{r^q}.
\]
::: {.proof}
From
\[
bab^{-1}=a^r
\]
one obtains inductively
\[
b^k a b^{-k}=a^{r^k}
\]
for every $k\ge1$.
Taking $k=q$ and using $b^q=1$ gives
\[
a=b^qab^{-q}=a^{r^q}.
\]
:::

<1>2. If
\[
r^q\not\equiv1\pmod p,
\]
then $G$ does not have order $pq$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
a^{r^q-1}=1.
\]
Together with $a^p=1$, this shows that the order of $a$ divides
\[
\gcd(p,r^q-1).
\]
Since $p$ is prime and $p\nmid r^q-1$ under the stated hypothesis, this gcd is $1$.
Hence $a=1$.
The group is then generated by $b$ with $b^q=1$, so
\[
|G|\le q<pq.
\]
:::

<1>3. Assume
\[
r^q\equiv1\pmod p.
\]
Then every element of $G$ is represented by one of the $pq$ words
\[
a^i b^j,
\qquad
0\le i<p,
\quad
0\le j<q.
\]
::: {.proof}
The relation
\[
ba=a^r b
\]
allows every occurrence of $b$ to be moved to the right of every occurrence of $a$.
Thus every word is equal in $G$ to $a^i b^j$ for some integers $i,j$.
The relations $a^p=1$ and $b^q=1$ reduce the exponents modulo $p$ and $q$ respectively.
Therefore
\[
|G|\le pq.
\]
:::

<1>4. Under the same congruence, there exists a semidirect product of order $pq$ satisfying the presentation.
::: {.proof}
Because
\[
r^q\equiv1\pmod p,
\]
the residue class of $r$ modulo $p$ is nonzero and defines an automorphism
\[
\theta:C_p\longrightarrow C_p,
\qquad
x\longmapsto x^r,
\]
whose $q$-th power is the identity.
Hence the assignment sending a generator of $C_q$ to $\theta$ defines a homomorphism
\[
C_q\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(C_p).
\]
Form the semidirect product
\[
H=C_p\rtimes_\theta C_q.
\]
It has order $pq$.
If $x$ and $y$ are the standard generators of its $C_p$ and $C_q$ factors, then
\[
x^p=1,
\qquad
y^q=1,
\qquad
yxy^{-1}=x^r.
\]
Thus the defining presentation of $G$ gives a surjective homomorphism
\[
G\twoheadrightarrow H.
\]
:::

<1>5. The group $G$ has order $pq$ if and only if
\[
r^q\equiv1\pmod p.
\]
::: {.proof}
Necessity is <1>2.
For sufficiency, <1>3 gives $|G|\le pq$, while the surjection in <1>4 gives
\[
|G|\ge|H|=pq.
\]
Hence $|G|=pq$.

Equivalently, since $q$ is prime, the residue class of $r$ modulo $p$ has multiplicative order either $1$ or $q$.
Thus one may also state the answer as
\[
r\equiv1\pmod p
\]
or else $r\bmod p$ has order $q$ in $(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times$.
:::
:::
