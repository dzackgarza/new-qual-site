::: problem
Suppose $n$ is a positive integer, $p$ is a prime number, and $a \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$.
Suppose $n \mid p - 1$.
Suppose $E$ is a splitting field of $x^n - a$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

(a) Prove that $E/\mathbb{F}_p$ is a Galois extension and $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{F}_p)$ can be embedded into $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$.

(b) Prove that $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \langle a^{\frac{p-1}{n}} \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p^\times$.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Since \(n\mid p-1\), every \(n\)-th root of unity lies in \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).
::: {.proof}
The multiplicative group \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) is cyclic of order \(p-1\). Therefore it contains a unique subgroup of order \(n\), namely the full group \(\mu_n\) of \(n\)-th roots of unity.
:::

<1>2. Let \(\alpha\in E\) satisfy \(\alpha^n=a\). Then
\[
E=\mathbb F_p(\alpha).
\]
::: {.proof}
The roots of \(x^n-a\) are precisely \(\xi\alpha\) with \(\xi\in\mu_n\). By <1>1, every such \(\xi\) lies in \(\mathbb F_p\). Hence adjoining one root \(\alpha\) already adjoins all roots, so it gives the splitting field.
:::

<1>3. The polynomial \(x^n-a\) is separable over \(\mathbb F_p\).
::: {.proof}
Because \(n\mid p-1\), one has \(p\nmid n\). The derivative is \(nx^{n-1}\), which has no common root with \(x^n-a\) because \(a\ne0\). Thus all roots are distinct.
:::

<1>4. Therefore \(E/\mathbb F_p\) is Galois.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, \(E\) is the splitting field of \(x^n-a\), and by <1>3 that polynomial is separable. A splitting field of a separable polynomial is Galois.
:::

<1>5. For \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_p)\), define
\[
\chi(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma(\alpha)}{\alpha}.
\]
Then \(\chi(\sigma)\in\mu_n\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times\).
::: {.proof}
Since \(\sigma(\alpha)\) is another root of \(x^n-a\), <1>2 gives \(\sigma(\alpha)=\xi\alpha\) for some \(\xi\in\mu_n\). Thus the quotient is exactly \(\xi\).
:::

<1>6. The map
\[
\chi:\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_p)\hookrightarrow\mathbb F_p^\times
\]
is an injective group homomorphism.
::: {.proof}
For \(\sigma,\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_p)\), the element \(\chi(\tau)\) lies in the base field and is therefore fixed by \(\sigma\). Hence
\[
\chi(\sigma\tau)
=\frac{\sigma(\tau(\alpha))}{\alpha}
=\frac{\sigma(\chi(\tau)\alpha)}{\alpha}
=\chi(\tau)\chi(\sigma).
\]
The target is abelian, so this is the homomorphism law. If \(\chi(\sigma)=1\), then \(\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha\); since \(E=\mathbb F_p(\alpha)\), this forces \(\sigma=1\). Thus \(\chi\) is injective.
:::

<1>7. Let \(\operatorname{Fr}:E\to E\) be the Frobenius automorphism \(x\mapsto x^p\). Then
\[
\chi(\operatorname{Fr})=a^{(p-1)/n}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using \(\alpha^n=a\) and \(n\mid p-1\),
\[
\chi(\operatorname{Fr})
=\frac{\alpha^p}{\alpha}
=\alpha^{p-1}
=(\alpha^n)^{(p-1)/n}
=a^{(p-1)/n}.
\]
:::

<1>8. The Galois group \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_p)\) is generated by Frobenius.
::: {.proof}
The field \(E\) is a finite extension of the finite field \(\mathbb F_p\), hence itself finite. The Galois group of a finite extension of finite fields is cyclic and generated by the Frobenius automorphism.
:::

<1>9. Consequently,
\[
\chi\bigl(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_p)\bigr)
=\left\langle a^{(p-1)/n}\right\rangle
\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_p)
\cong
\left\langle a^{(p-1)/n}\right\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>8 the Galois group is generated by \(\operatorname{Fr}\), and by <1>7 its image under the injective homomorphism \(\chi\) is \(a^{(p-1)/n}\). Therefore the image is exactly the cyclic subgroup generated by that element, and injectivity gives the desired isomorphism.
:::
