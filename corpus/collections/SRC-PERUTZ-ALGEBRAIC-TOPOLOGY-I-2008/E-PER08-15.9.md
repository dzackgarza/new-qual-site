::: {.problem}
Let
\[
h:S^3\longrightarrow S^2
\]
be the Hopf map
\[
h(z_1,z_2)=z_1/z_2,
\]
where \(S^3\subset\mathbb C^2\) is the unit sphere and \(S^2\cong\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}\) is the Riemann sphere.
Let
\[
\omega\in H_3(S^3;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
be a generator.
Since \(H_3(S^2)=0\), one has \(h_*\omega=0\).

Give a direct chain-level explanation: find a singular \(4\)-chain in \(S^2\) whose boundary is a \(3\)-cycle representing \(h_*\omega\).
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose a singular fundamental \(3\)-cycle
\[
z\in C_3(S^3),
\qquad [z]=\omega,
\]
and put
\[
c=h_\#z\in C_3(S^2).
\]
Then \(\partial c=h_\#(\partial z)=0\), and \([c]=h_*\omega\).

Let
\[
U=S^2\setminus\{N\},
\qquad
V=S^2\setminus\{S\},
\]
where \(N,S\) are distinct points.
Thus \(U\) and \(V\) are contractible, while
\[
U\cap V=S^2\setminus\{N,S\}\simeq S^1.
\]

<1>1. By locality, there are chains
\[
a\in C_3(U),\qquad b\in C_3(V),\qquad P\in C_4(S^2)
\]
such that
\[
c-(a+b)=\partial P.
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply the locality theorem for singular chains to the excisive cover \(S^2=U\cup V\).  After sufficiently many barycentric subdivisions, every simplex of \(c\) is supported in \(U\) or \(V\).  The subdivision lemma supplies a chain homotopy from the original chain to this subdivided chain.
Calling its \(U\)- and \(V\)-parts \(a\) and \(b\), and the corresponding prism/subdivision \(4\)-chain \(P\), gives exactly
\[
c-(a+b)=\partial P.
\]
:::

<1>2. The common boundary of \(a\) and \(b\) can be filled inside \(U\cap V\).
::: {.proof}
Since \(c\) is a cycle and \(\partial^2P=0\),
\[
0=\partial(a+b).
\]
Hence
\[
d:=\partial a=-\partial b.
\]
Because \(a\) is supported in \(U\) and \(b\) in \(V\), the \(2\)-chain \(d\) is supported in \(U\cap V\).  It is a cycle, and
\[
H_2(U\cap V)\cong H_2(S^1)=0.
\]
Therefore there is a \(3\)-chain
\[
e\in C_3(U\cap V)
\]
with
\[
\partial e=d.
\]
Consequently
\[
\partial(a-e)=0,
\qquad
\partial(b+e)=0.
\]
:::

<1>3. Fill the corrected \(3\)-cycles in the two contractible charts.
::: {.proof}
Since \(U\) and \(V\) are contractible,
\[
H_3(U)=H_3(V)=0.
\]
Hence there are \(4\)-chains
\[
A\in C_4(U),\qquad B\in C_4(V)
\]
such that
\[
\partial A=a-e,
\qquad
\partial B=b+e.
\]
Concretely, \(A\) and \(B\) may be obtained by applying the standard cone chain homotopies associated to contractions of \(U\) and \(V\) to points.
:::

<1>4. The chain
\[
W=P+A+B\in C_4(S^2)
\]
bounds \(c=h_\#z\).
::: {.proof}
Using the three identities above,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial W
&=\partial P+\partial A+\partial B\\
&=\bigl(c-a-b\bigr)+(a-e)+(b+e)\\
&=c.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(W\) is the requested singular \(4\)-chain and \(c\) is a \(3\)-cycle representing \(h_*\omega\).
:::

This construction is genuinely chain-level; it does not require an extension of the Hopf map to \(D^4\), which would contradict the nontriviality of the Hopf map in \(\pi_3(S^2)\).
:::
