::: {.problem}
Let \(M\) be a connected manifold. Show that \(M\) is orientable if it is simply connected. More generally, show that \(M\) is orientable whenever \(\pi_1(M)\) is finite of odd order.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
p:\widetilde M\longrightarrow M
\]
be the orientation double cover.  By the covering-space discussion preceding this exercise, it is classified by the orientation character
\[
w_1(M):\pi_1(M,x)\longrightarrow \mathbb Z/2.
\]

<1>1. The manifold \(M\) is orientable if and only if \(w_1(M)\) is trivial.
::: {.proof}
An orientation of \(M\) is, by definition, a continuous section of the orientation cover.
For a connected base, a two-sheeted cover admits a section exactly when it is the trivial two-sheeted cover: the image of a section is one sheet, and the other sheet is its complement.

Under the classification of two-sheeted covers by homomorphisms
\(\pi_1(M,x)\to\mathbb Z/2\), the trivial cover corresponds to the trivial homomorphism.  Hence \(M\) is orientable exactly when \(w_1(M)=0\).
:::

<1>2. If \(M\) is simply connected, then \(M\) is orientable.
::: {.proof}
If \(M\) is simply connected, then \(\pi_1(M,x)=0\).  Therefore every homomorphism
\[
\pi_1(M,x)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2
\]
is trivial, in particular \(w_1(M)\).  The conclusion follows from <1>1.
:::

<1>3. If \(\pi_1(M,x)\) is finite of odd order, then \(M\) is orientable.
::: {.proof}
Let \(G=\pi_1(M,x)\), with \(|G|\) odd.  The image of the homomorphism
\[
w_1(M):G\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2
\]
is a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/2\), so its order is either \(1\) or \(2\).  By the first isomorphism theorem,
\[
|\operatorname{im} w_1(M)|=[G:\ker w_1(M)]
\]
divides \(|G|\).  Since \(|G|\) is odd, the image cannot have order \(2\).  Thus the image has order \(1\), so \(w_1(M)\) is trivial.  By <1>1, \(M\) is orientable.
:::
:::
