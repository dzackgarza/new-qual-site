::: {.problem}
(a) Let $X$ be a path-connected space.
Show how two-sheeted covering spaces of $X$, up to isomorphism, correspond to homomorphisms
\[
\pi_1(X,x)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2.
\]

(b) Let $M$ be an $n$-manifold.
Let $p\colon H_M\to M$ be the natural covering space whose fibre over $x$ is $H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\})$: a point of $H_M$ is a pair $(x,h_x)$ with $x\in M$ and $h_x\in H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\})$, and $p(x,h_x)=x$.
Its topology is defined as follows.
Let $U\subset M$ be an open set homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$, and let $B\subset U$ be a closed neighbourhood homeomorphic to the closed disc $D^n$; for $x,y\in B$ there are isomorphisms
\[
H_n(M,M\setminus\{y\})\longleftarrow H_n(M,M\setminus B)\longrightarrow H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\}).
\]
A component of $p^{-1}(U)$ is an equivalence class of pairs $(x,h_x)\in p^{-1}(U)$, where $(x,h_x)$ is equivalent to $(y,h_y)$ if there is such a ball $B$ containing both $x$ and $y$ and an element $z\in H_n(M,M\setminus B)$ mapped by these isomorphisms to $h_x$ and $h_y$.
The components of $p^{-1}(U)$, as $U$ varies over open sets homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$, form a basis for a topology making $p$ a covering map.

Let $\widetilde M\subset H_M$ be the subspace consisting of pairs $(x,h_x)$ with $h_x$ a generator of
\[
H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\})\cong\mathbb{Z}.
\]
Thus $p\colon\widetilde M\to M$ is a two-sheeted covering space.
The corresponding homomorphism
\[
\pi_1(M)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2
\]
is called the orientation character, or first Stiefel--Whitney class $w_1(M)$.
Compute it for the Klein bottle and for $\mathbb{RP}^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
We use the covering-space classification established earlier in the course; equivalently, we use monodromy of the fiber over a basepoint.

<1>1. A two-sheeted cover determines a homomorphism
\[
\chi_p:\pi_1(X,x)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $p:Y\to X$ be a two-sheeted covering and write
\[
p^{-1}(x)=\{y_0,y_1\}.
\]
For a loop $\gamma$ based at $x$, lift $\gamma$ starting at each point of the fiber.
Unique path lifting gives a permutation of the two endpoints, hence a permutation of $p^{-1}(x)$.
Thus monodromy gives a homomorphism
\[
\pi_1(X,x)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}(p^{-1}(x))\cong S_2\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Relabeling the two points of the fiber conjugates the monodromy representation in $S_2$, but $S_2$ is abelian, so the resulting homomorphism is unchanged.
Isomorphic covers therefore give the same $\chi_p$.
:::

<1>2. Conversely, every homomorphism
\[
\chi:\pi_1(X,x)\to\mathbb Z/2
\]
determines a two-sheeted cover, and this construction is inverse to <1>1 up to isomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Let $\pi_1(X,x)$ act on the two-element set $\mathbb Z/2$ by
\[
g\cdot\varepsilon=\varepsilon+\chi(g).
\]
By the standard correspondence between covering spaces and monodromy actions, this two-element $\pi_1(X,x)$-set determines a two-sheeted covering of $X$.

If $\chi=0$, the action is trivial and the cover is the disconnected trivial cover
\[
X\sqcup X\longrightarrow X.
\]
If $\chi$ is nonzero, its kernel has index two and the associated cover is connected; equivalently it is the connected cover corresponding to the subgroup $\ker\chi$.

Starting with a cover, forming its monodromy action, and reconstructing the cover gives an isomorphic covering.
Starting with $\chi$, the reconstructed cover has monodromy exactly $\chi$.
Hence two-sheeted covers up to isomorphism correspond to homomorphisms $\pi_1(X,x)\to\mathbb Z/2$.
:::

<1>3. For an $n$-manifold $M$, the homomorphism associated with $\widetilde M\to M$ records whether a loop preserves or reverses a local orientation.
::: {.proof}
A point of the fiber of $\widetilde M$ over $x$ is one of the two generators
\[
\pm h_x\in H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\})\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Transport around a loop based at $x$ either returns $h_x$ to itself or to $-h_x$.
Under the identification of the two-element permutation group with $\mathbb Z/2$, these two possibilities are respectively $0$ and $1$.
Therefore
\[
w_1(M)([\gamma])=
\begin{cases}
0,&\gamma\text{ preserves local orientation},\\
1,&\gamma\text{ reverses local orientation}.
\end{cases}
\]
:::

<1>4. For the Klein bottle $K$, choose generators $a,b$ so that $a$ crosses the orientation-reversing edge identification of the standard square model and $b$ crosses the orientation-preserving edge identification.
Then
\[
w_1(K)(a)=1,
\qquad
w_1(K)(b)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Represent the Klein bottle by a square with one pair of opposite sides identified in the same orientation and the other pair identified in opposite orientations.
A local orientation transported across the first type of identification is preserved, whereas transport across the reversed identification changes sign.

By definition, $a$ crosses the reversed identification once, so it interchanges the two sheets of the orientation cover and
\[
w_1(K)(a)=1.
\]
The loop $b$ crosses the orientation-preserving identification, so
\[
w_1(K)(b)=0.
\]
With the usual presentation
\[
\pi_1(K)=\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}=b^{-1}\rangle
\]
for this convention, the assignment is compatible with the relation because its image in $\mathbb Z/2$ is
\[
1+0+1=0.
\]
Its kernel is the index-two subgroup generated by $a^2$ and $b$, corresponding to the torus orientation double cover of the Klein bottle.
:::

<1>5. For $\mathbb{RP}^2$, the orientation character is the unique nonzero homomorphism
\[
w_1(\mathbb{RP}^2):\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb Z/2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2;
\]
in other words, it is the identity under the standard identification.
::: {.proof}
The standard two-sheeted cover
\[
S^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{RP}^2
\]
identifies antipodal points.
Since $S^2$ is orientable and $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is not, this is the orientation double cover.
Thus the nontrivial element of
\[
\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb Z/2
\]
interchanges the two local orientations, so it maps to $1\in\mathbb Z/2$.
Therefore $w_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)$ is the identity homomorphism.
:::
