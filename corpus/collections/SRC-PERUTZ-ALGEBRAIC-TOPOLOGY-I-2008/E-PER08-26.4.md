::: {.problem}
Show that for every map f : S2n $\\to$ CP n, the induced map ˜f$\\ast$ on reduced homology is zero.
:::

::: {.solution}
As printed, the statement needs the hypothesis $n>1$.

<1>1. The case $n=1$ is a counterexample.
::: {.proof}
$\mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2$.
Under such a homeomorphism, the identity map
\[
S^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^1
\]
induces an isomorphism on $\widetilde H_2$, not the zero map.
Thus the assertion is false for $n=1$.
:::

<1>2. For $n>1$, every $f:S^{2n}\to\mathbb{CP}^n$ induces zero on reduced homology.
::: {.proof}
The only potentially nonzero reduced homology map is in degree $2n$.
Let $u\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)$ be the hyperplane generator, so $u^n$ generates $H^{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)$.
Since $n>1$,
\[
H^2(S^{2n};\mathbb Z)=0,
\]
so $f^*u=0$ and hence
\[
f^*(u^n)=(f^*u)^n=0.
\]
Write
\[
f_*[S^{2n}]=d[\mathbb{CP}^n].
\]
Naturality of evaluation gives
\[
d
=\langle u^n,f_*[S^{2n}]\rangle
=\langle f^*(u^n),[S^{2n}]\rangle
=0.
\]
Thus $f_*$ is zero in degree $2n$, and all other reduced homology groups of the sphere vanish.
Hence $\widetilde f_*=0$.
:::
:::
