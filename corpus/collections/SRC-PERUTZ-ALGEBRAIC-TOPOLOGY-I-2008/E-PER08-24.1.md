::: {.problem}
Find a cellular approximation to the diagonal $S^n\to S^n\times S^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Give $S^n$, $n \geq 1$, the CW structure with one $0$-cell $e^0$ and one $n$-cell, and give $S^n \times S^n$ the product structure.
Let $\Delta\colon S^n \to S^n \times S^n$ be the diagonal.
Let $c\colon S^n \to S^n \vee S^n$ be the pinch map, which collapses an equator through $e^0$ to the wedge point and maps the two hemispheres onto the two summands with degree $1$.
Let $f\colon S^n \to S^n \times S^n$ be $c$ followed by the inclusion $S^n \vee S^n = S^n \times e^0 \cup e^0 \times S^n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cellular}
$f$ is cellular.

::: pf-proof
The $n$-skeleton of $S^n \times S^n$ is $S^n \vee S^n$, and $f$ maps $S^n$ into it and $e^0$ to $(e^0, e^0)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #components}
Both components $\mathrm{pr}_1 \circ f$ and $\mathrm{pr}_2 \circ f$ have degree $1$.

::: pf-proof
$\mathrm{pr}_1 \circ f$ maps the first hemisphere onto $S^n$ with degree $1$ and collapses the second hemisphere to $e^0$.
So it is the quotient map that collapses one hemisphere, which has degree $1$.
The same holds for $\mathrm{pr}_2 \circ f$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$f$ is homotopic to $\Delta$, so $f$ is a cellular approximation to $\Delta$.

::: pf-proof
A homotopy of maps into a product is a pair of homotopies of the components.
Both components of $\Delta$ are $\id_{S^n}$, of degree $1$.
By step [](#components){.pf-ref} and the Hopf theorem, which classifies maps $S^n \to S^n$ up to homotopy by degree, each component of $f$ is homotopic to $\id_{S^n}$.
So $f \simeq \Delta$, and $f$ is cellular by step [](#cellular){.pf-ref}.
:::
