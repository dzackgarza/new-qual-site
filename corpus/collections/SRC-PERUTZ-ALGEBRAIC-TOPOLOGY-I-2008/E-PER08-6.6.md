::: {.problem}
You may use the following fact: the quotient $SU(2)/\{\pm I\}$ is isomorphic, as a topological group, to $SO(3)$.

Define a regular tetrahedron as a set of four distinct, unordered, equidistant points on $S^2\subset\mathbb{R}^3$.
Let $T$ be the space of regular tetrahedra.

(a) Show that $\pi_1(T)$ has a central subgroup $Z\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ such that $\pi_1(T)/Z\cong A_4$.

(b) Identify several (at least $5$) pairwise non-isomorphic, path connected covering spaces of $T$, describing them geometrically.

(c) Show that the fundamental group of the space $P$ of regular icosahedra (unordered collections of $20$ distinct points on $S^2$ forming the vertices of a regular icosahedron) has order $120$, but that the abelianization $\pi_1(P)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ has order at most $2$.
(In fact it is trivial.)

[Recall that the icosahedral group $A_5$ is simple.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $2T\subset SU(2)$ and $2I\subset SU(2)$ denote the binary tetrahedral and binary icosahedral groups.

<1>1. $\pi_1(T)\cong2T$, with central $\mathbb Z/2$ quotienting to $A_4$.
::: {.proof}
$SO(3)$ acts transitively on unordered regular tetrahedra, and the stabilizer of one tetrahedron is its orientation-preserving rotational symmetry group $A_4$.
Thus
\[
T\cong SO(3)/A_4.
\]
The universal cover $SU(2)\to SO(3)$ has kernel $\{\pm1\}$.
Composing with $SO(3)\to T$ gives the universal cover of $T$, whose deck group is the full inverse image $2T$ of $A_4$.
Therefore
\[
1\to\{\pm1\}\to\pi_1(T)\to A_4\to1,
\]
and the kernel $\{\pm1\}\cong\mathbb Z/2$ is central.
:::

<1>2. There are at least five pairwise non-isomorphic connected covers.
::: {.proof}
Connected covers correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups of $2T$.
Taking subgroups of distinct orders already gives pairwise non-isomorphic covers.
For example
\[
\{1\},\quad C_2=\{\pm1\},\quad C_3,\quad C_4,\quad Q_8,\quad 2T
\]
give the intermediate covers
\[
SU(2)/H\longrightarrow T
\]
of degrees $24,12,8,6,3,1$, respectively.
Their distinct degrees make them non-isomorphic as covers.
Geometrically they remember progressively more of an oriented tetrahedral frame; $SU(2)/C_2=SO(3)$ is the cover of a tetrahedron by a spatial rotation carrying a fixed reference tetrahedron to it.
:::

<1>3. The icosahedral space has fundamental group of order $120$ and abelianization of order at most $2$.
::: {.proof}
Similarly, the space $P$ of regular icosahedra is
\[
P\cong SO(3)/A_5.
\]
Its universal-cover deck group is the inverse image $2I$ of $A_5$ in $SU(2)$, hence has order $2|A_5|=120$.
There is a central extension
\[
1\to C_2\to2I\to A_5\to1.
\]
Since $A_5$ is nonabelian simple, it is perfect, so $A_5^{\mathrm{ab}}=0$.
Passing to abelianizations shows that $(2I)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is generated by the image of the central $C_2$; therefore its order is at most $2$.
:::
:::
