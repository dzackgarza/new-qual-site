::: {.problem}
Theorem (dual universal coefficients).
If $X$ is a space and $R$ any commutative unital ring, there are natural (in $X$), non-naturally split short exact sequences
\[
0\to\operatorname{Ext}(H_{p-1}(X),R)\to H^p(X;R)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_p(X),R)\to0.
\]
In particular, there are non-canonical isomorphisms $H^n(X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_n(X),\mathbb{Z})\oplus H_{n-1}(X)_{\mathrm{tors}}$, where $A_{\mathrm{tors}}$ denotes the torsion subgroup of $A$.

Verify a case of the theorem by computing $H^*(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb{Z})$ in two ways.
Do the same for $H^*(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb{Z}/2)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The standard CW structure on $\mathbb{RP}^2$ has one cell in each degree $0,1,2$, with cellular chain differential
\[
0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times2}\mathbb Z\xrightarrow0\mathbb Z\to0.
\]
Thus
\[
H_0=\mathbb Z,\qquad H_1=\mathbb Z/2,\qquad H_2=0.
\]

<1>1. Integral cohomology by cellular cochains.
::: {.proof}
Dualizing gives
\[
0\leftarrow\mathbb Z\xleftarrow{\times2}\mathbb Z\xleftarrow0\mathbb Z\leftarrow0
\]
with cohomological degrees $2,1,0$.
Hence
\[
H^0(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,
\quad H^1=0,
\quad H^2=\mathbb Z/2.
\]
:::

<1>2. Integral cohomology by UCT.
::: {.proof}
UCT gives
\[
0\to\operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1},\mathbb Z)\to H^n\to\operatorname{Hom}(H_n,\mathbb Z)\to0.
\]
For $n=2$, $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2$; for $n=1$, both terms vanish.
This reproduces the cellular result.
:::

<1>3. With $\mathbb Z/2$ coefficients.
::: {.proof}
Modulo $2$, the cellular differential vanishes, so
\[
H^n(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Z/2)\cong\mathbb Z/2
\qquad(n=0,1,2),
\]
and is zero otherwise.
UCT gives the same answer: in degree $1$ the Hom term $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb Z/2)$ contributes, and in degree $2$ the Ext term $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb Z/2)$ contributes.
:::
:::
