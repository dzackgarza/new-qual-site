::: {.problem}
Formulate these properties of cohomology, and think through how you would prove them.
:::

::: {.solution}
The dual properties are the following.

<1>1. Homotopy invariance and contravariance.
::: {.proof}
A map $f:X\to Y$ induces $f^*:H^n(Y;R)\to H^n(X;R)$, with $(gf)^*=f^*g^*$ and homotopic maps inducing the same homomorphism.
This follows by dualizing the singular chain map and the standard chain homotopy between maps induced by homotopic maps.
:::

<1>2. Long exact sequence of a pair.
::: {.proof}
The short exact sequence of cochain complexes
\[
0\to S^*(X,A;R)\to S^*(X;R)\to S^*(A;R)\to0
\]
gives
\[
\cdots\to H^n(X,A;R)\to H^n(X;R)\to H^n(A;R)
\xrightarrow{\delta}H^{n+1}(X,A;R)\to\cdots.
\]
The connecting map raises degree by one because these are cochain complexes.
:::

<1>3. Excision.
::: {.proof}
For an excisive pair, the subdivision/locality argument used for singular chains gives a chain-homotopy equivalence after restricting to small simplices.
Dualizing yields the corresponding relative cochain quasi-isomorphism and hence the usual excision isomorphism in cohomology.
:::

<1>4. Mayer--Vietoris.
::: {.proof}
For an excisive cover $X=A\cup B$, the short exact sequence of cochain complexes dual to the chain-level Mayer--Vietoris sequence gives
\[
\cdots\to H^n(X)\to H^n(A)\oplus H^n(B)\to H^n(A\cap B)
\xrightarrow{\delta}H^{n+1}(X)\to\cdots.
\]
Naturality follows from functoriality of the underlying short exact sequences.
These proofs are the cochain-duals of the corresponding homology arguments.
:::
:::
