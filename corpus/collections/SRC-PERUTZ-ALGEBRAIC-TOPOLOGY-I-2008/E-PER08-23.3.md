::: {.problem}
Prove that the cohomology groups of a compact, simply connected, oriented $4$-manifold $M$ are completely determined by the second Betti number
\[
b_2(M)=\dim_{\QQ}H^2(M;\QQ).
\]
Prove that the cohomology ring is determined by the cup-product pairing $H^2\times H^2\to\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $M$ be a compact simply connected oriented $4$-manifold and let $b_2=\dim_{\QQ}H_2(M;\QQ)$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The integral cohomology groups are determined by $b_2$.

::: pf-proof

Simple connectivity gives $H_1(M;\ZZ)=0$.
Poincaré duality gives
\[
H^3(M;\ZZ)\cong H_1(M;\ZZ)=0,
\qquad
H^1(M;\ZZ)\cong H_3(M;\ZZ).
\]
Also $H^1=\operatorname{Hom}(H_1,\ZZ)=0$, so $H_3=0$.

UCT in degree $3$ gives
\[
0\to\operatorname{Ext}(H_2(M),\ZZ)\to H^3(M)\to\operatorname{Hom}(H_3(M),\ZZ)\to0.
\]
Since $H^3(M)=0$, the injection $\operatorname{Ext}(H_2(M),\ZZ)\to H^3(M)$ forces $\operatorname{Ext}(H_2(M),\ZZ)=0$.
A compact manifold has finitely generated homology, and $\operatorname{Ext}(\ZZ/m,\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/m$, so $H_2(M)$ is torsion-free. Hence
\[
H_2(M)\cong\ZZ^{b_2}.
\]
UCT and duality now give
\[
H^0\cong H^4\cong\ZZ,\qquad
H^2\cong\ZZ^{b_2},\qquad H^1=H^3=0.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The ring is determined by the degree-$2$ cup-product pairing.

::: pf-proof

The only product of positive-degree classes that can be nonzero is
\[
H^2(M)\times H^2(M)\xrightarrow{\smile}H^4(M)\cong\ZZ.
\]
After choosing the orientation generator $\omega\in H^4$, write
\[
x\smile y=Q_M(x,y)\,\omega.
\]
Poincaré duality says $Q_M$ is a unimodular symmetric bilinear form on $\ZZ^{b_2}$.
Since multiplication by the degree-$0$ unit and this pairing account for every possible product, the graded ring is completely determined by $Q_M$.

:::
