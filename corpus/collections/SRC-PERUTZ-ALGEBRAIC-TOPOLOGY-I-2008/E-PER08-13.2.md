::: {.problem}
Re-derive
\[
H_*(S^n)\cong \mathbb Z(0)\oplus \mathbb Z(n)
\]
using excision.
:::

::: {.solution}
We write $\mathbb Z(j)$ for a copy of $\mathbb Z$ concentrated in degree $j$.

<1>1. For $n=0$, the formula holds directly.
::: {.proof}
The space $S^0$ consists of two points.
Hence
\[
H_0(S^0)\cong \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_k(S^0)=0\quad(k>0),
\]
which is exactly $\mathbb Z(0)\oplus\mathbb Z(0)$.
:::

<1>2. Suppose $n\ge 1$.
Choose two slightly overlapping closed hemispherical neighborhoods $A,B\subset S^n$ such that
\[
S^n=A\cup B,
\qquad
A\simeq *,
\qquad
B\simeq *,
\qquad
A\cap B\simeq S^{n-1}.
\]
Then $(S^n;A,B)$ is an excisive triad.
::: {.proof}
For example, for a fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$, write a point of $S^n\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}$ as $(x,t)$ and take
\[
A=\{(x,t):t\ge -\varepsilon\},
\qquad
B=\{(x,t):t\le \varepsilon\}.
\]
Their interiors cover $S^n$, so they form an excisive triad.
Each is a thickened hemisphere and hence contractible.
Their intersection is the equatorial band $|t|\le\varepsilon$, which deformation-retracts onto the equator $S^{n-1}$.
:::

<1>3. For every $k>0$,
\[
H_k(S^n)\cong H_k(S^n,B).
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $B$ is contractible, $H_k(B)=0$ for $k>0$.
For $k>1$, the long exact sequence of the pair $(S^n,B)$ therefore gives the asserted isomorphism immediately.

For $k=1$, the relevant part is
\[
0=H_1(B)\longrightarrow H_1(S^n)\longrightarrow H_1(S^n,B)
\longrightarrow H_0(B)\longrightarrow H_0(S^n).
\]
Since $n\ge1$, both $B$ and $S^n$ are path connected, and the last map is an isomorphism $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$.
Exactness therefore again gives
\[
H_1(S^n)\cong H_1(S^n,B).
\]
:::

<1>4. Excision and the long exact sequence of $(A,A\cap B)$ give, for every $k>0$,
\[
H_k(S^n)\cong \widetilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1}).
\]
::: {.proof}
Perutz's excision theorem applied to the excisive triad $(S^n;A,B)$ gives
\[
H_k(A,A\cap B)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_k(S^n,B).
\]
Combining this with <1>3 yields
\[
H_k(S^n)\cong H_k(A,A\cap B).
\]
Since $A$ is contractible, the long exact sequence of the pair $(A,A\cap B)$ identifies its positive-degree relative homology with shifted reduced homology of the intersection:
\[
H_k(A,A\cap B)\cong \widetilde H_{k-1}(A\cap B).
\]
Finally $A\cap B\simeq S^{n-1}$, so
\[
H_k(S^n)\cong \widetilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1}).
\]
:::

<1>5. Induction on $n$ now gives
\[
H_k(S^n)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\
\mathbb Z,&k=n,\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
The base case is <1>1. Assume the formula for $S^{n-1}$.
Its reduced homology is therefore
\[
\widetilde H_j(S^{n-1})\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=n-1,\
0,&j\ne n-1.
\end{cases}
\]
This remains true when $n=1$, because $\widetilde H_0(S^0)\cong\mathbb Z$.
By <1>4, for every $k>0$ we obtain $H_k(S^n)\cong\mathbb Z$ exactly when $k=n$, and $0$ otherwise.
Since $S^n$ is path connected for $n\ge1$, $H_0(S^n)\cong\mathbb Z$.
Thus, as a graded group,
\[
H_*(S^n)\cong \mathbb Z(0)\oplus\mathbb Z(n).
\]
:::
:::
