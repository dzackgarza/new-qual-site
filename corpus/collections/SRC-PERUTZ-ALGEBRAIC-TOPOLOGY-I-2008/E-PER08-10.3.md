::: {.problem}
The model Dehn twist on the annulus
\[
A=[-1,1]\times(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)
\]
is the homeomorphism
\[
t:A\to A,
\qquad
(s,u)\longmapsto \left(s,u+\frac{s+1}{2}\right).
\]
A Dehn twist along an embedded circle $C$ in a surface $S$ is a homeomorphism $S\to S$ obtained by identifying a neighbourhood of $C$ with $A$ and transplanting a model Dehn twist into $S$.

Let $\Sigma$ be a genus-$2$ surface, and let $C\subset\Sigma$ be a circle dividing it into two one-holed tori.
Show that if $f$ is a Dehn twist along $C$, then
\[
f_*:H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)
\]
is the identity, but $f$ is not homotopic to the identity map.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $T_C$ denote the Dehn twist about $C$.

<1>1. The homology class $[C]\in H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$ is zero.
::: {.proof}
Because $C$ separates $\Sigma$, it is the oriented boundary of either of the two one-holed tori obtained by cutting $\Sigma$ along $C$.
Hence $C$ is a boundary as a singular $1$-cycle, so
\[
[C]=0\in H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z).
\]
:::

<1>2. The induced map $(T_C)_*$ is the identity on $H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$.
::: {.proof}
For an oriented simple closed curve $\alpha$ transverse to $C$, a Dehn twist changes its homology class by the standard transvection formula
\[
[T_C(\alpha)]
=
[\alpha]+([\alpha]\cdot[C])[C],
\]
where $\cdot$ denotes algebraic intersection number.
By <1>1, $[C]=0$, so
\[
[T_C(\alpha)]=[\alpha].
\]
Simple closed curves generate $H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$, hence $(T_C)_*$ is the identity on all of $H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$.
:::

<1>3. There is an essential simple closed curve $\gamma\subset\Sigma$ with geometric intersection number $i(\gamma,C)=2$.
::: {.proof}
After cutting $\Sigma$ along $C$, each side is a one-holed torus.
Choose in each side a properly embedded essential arc with both endpoints on its boundary circle.
Glue the two arcs along their endpoints across $C$.
The result is an essential simple closed curve $\gamma$ meeting $C$ transversely in exactly two points.
:::

<1>4. The curves $\gamma$ and $T_C(\gamma)$ are not freely homotopic.
::: {.proof}
Put $\gamma$ and $C$ in minimal position.
Inside an annular neighbourhood of $C$, the curve $\gamma$ consists of two transverse spanning arcs.
Applying one Dehn twist makes each image arc wind once around the annulus before exiting.
Comparing the two original spanning arcs with the two twisted spanning arcs gives
\[
i(\gamma,T_C(\gamma))=i(\gamma,C)^2=4.
\]
In particular their geometric intersection number is positive.

Two essential simple closed curves on an orientable surface that are freely homotopic are isotopic.
Isotopic simple closed curves have geometric intersection number $0$.
Therefore $\gamma$ and $T_C(\gamma)$ cannot be freely homotopic.
:::

<1>5. The Dehn twist $T_C$ is not homotopic to the identity.
::: {.proof}
If $T_C$ were homotopic to $\operatorname{id}_\Sigma$, then for every loop $\gamma:S^1\to\Sigma$, the maps $T_C\circ\gamma$ and $\gamma$ would be freely homotopic.
This contradicts <1>4 for the curve constructed in <1>3. Hence
\[
T_C\not\simeq \operatorname{id}_\Sigma.
\]
Together with <1>2, this gives a homologically trivial self-homeomorphism that is not homotopic to the identity.
:::
