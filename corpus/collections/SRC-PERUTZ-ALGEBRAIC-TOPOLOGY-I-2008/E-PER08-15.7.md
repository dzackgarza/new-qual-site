::: {.problem}
Let
\[
H^n=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\mathbb R^n:x_1\ge0\}.
\]

(i) Show that there is no homeomorphism \(H^n\cong\mathbb R^n\).

(ii) Let \(M\) be a space, and let \(N\) be the subspace of points \(x\in M\) for which there is a neighbourhood \(U\) and a homeomorphism
\[
\varphi:U\longrightarrow H^n
\]
with \(\varphi(x)=0\). Say that \(M\) is an \(n\)-manifold with boundary if \(M\setminus N\) is an \(n\)-manifold, and write \(\partial M=N\). Prove that \(\partial M\) is an \((n-1)\)-manifold.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The local homology at \(0\in H^n\) vanishes in every degree.
::: {.proof}
The half-space \(H^n\) is contractible.  Radial projection gives a deformation retraction
\[
H^n\setminus\{0\}\simeq
S^{n-1}\cap H^n.
\]
The latter is a closed hemisphere, hence homeomorphic to \(D^{n-1}\) and therefore contractible.  Thus the inclusion
\[
H^n\setminus\{0\}\hookrightarrow H^n
\]
induces an isomorphism on \(H_0\), while all positive-degree homology groups of both spaces vanish.  The long exact sequence of the pair therefore gives
\[
H_k(H^n,H^n\setminus\{0\})=0
\qquad\text{for every }k.
\]
(The same conclusion holds for \(n=1\), where \(H^1\setminus\{0\}=(0,\infty)\).)
:::

<1>2. The local homology at every point of \(\mathbb R^n\) is nonzero in degree \(n\).
::: {.proof}
Translation reduces to the origin.  The long exact sequence of the pair and radial deformation retraction give
\[
H_n(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})
\cong
\widetilde H_{n-1}(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})
\cong
\widetilde H_{n-1}(S^{n-1})
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>3. Therefore \(H^n\) is not homeomorphic to \(\mathbb R^n\).
::: {.proof}
Suppose \(f:H^n\to\mathbb R^n\) were a homeomorphism and put \(y=f(0)\).  It would induce an isomorphism of pairs
\[
(H^n,H^n\setminus\{0\})
\cong
(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{y\}),
\]
hence an isomorphism of their degree-\(n\) relative homology groups.  This contradicts <1>1 and <1>2, since the first group is \(0\) and the second is \(\mathbb Z\).
:::

<1>4. In a half-space chart around a boundary point, the boundary locus is exactly the coordinate boundary hyperplane.
::: {.proof}
Let \(x\in\partial M\), and choose a neighbourhood \(U\) and a homeomorphism
\[
\varphi:U\longrightarrow H^n,
\qquad \varphi(x)=0.
\]
Set
\[
\partial H^n=\{(0,x_2,\ldots,x_n)\}\cong\mathbb R^{n-1}.
\]

First let \(y\in U\) with \(\varphi(y)\in\partial H^n\). Translation parallel to the hyperplane \(x_1=0\) is a self-homeomorphism of \(H^n\) carrying \(\varphi(y)\) to \(0\).  Composing it with \(\varphi\) shows that \(y\in N=\partial M\).

Conversely, suppose \(\varphi(y)\) lies in the interior \(\{x_1>0\}\) of \(H^n\).  A sufficiently small Euclidean ball \(B\) around \(\varphi(y)\) lies entirely in that interior, so \(\varphi^{-1}(B)\) is an \(n\)-manifold neighbourhood of \(y\).  By excision its local homology in degree \(n\) is \(\mathbb Z\), as in <1>2.  If \(y\) also belonged to \(N\), a half-space chart taking \(y\) to \(0\) would instead make its local homology vanish by <1>1, a contradiction.

Hence
\[
\varphi(U\cap\partial M)=\partial H^n.
\]
:::

<1>5. The boundary \(\partial M\) is an \((n-1)\)-manifold.
::: {.proof}
For each \(x\in\partial M\), choose a half-space chart \(\varphi:U\to H^n\) as above.  By <1>4, its restriction is a homeomorphism
\[
\varphi|_{U\cap\partial M}:
U\cap\partial M
\longrightarrow
\partial H^n
\cong\mathbb R^{n-1}.
\]
Since \(U\cap\partial M\) is open in the subspace topology on \(\partial M\), these restrictions form an \((n-1)\)-dimensional manifold atlas on \(\partial M\).
:::
