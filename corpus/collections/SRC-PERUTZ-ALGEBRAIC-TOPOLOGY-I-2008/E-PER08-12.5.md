::: {.problem}
Complex projective $n$-space is
\[
\mathbb{CP}^n=(\mathbb C^{n+1}\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb C^*,
\]
where $\mathbb C^*$ acts by scalar multiplication.
Use Mayer--Vietoris to show that
\[
H_p(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,& p\text{ even and }0\le p\le 2n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Show moreover that the inclusion
\[
\mathbb{CP}^n\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{n+1},
\]
induced by the inclusion of $\mathbb C^{n+1}$ as a linear subspace of $\mathbb C^{n+2}$, induces an isomorphism on homology through degree $2n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove the homology calculation by induction on $n$ using a cover adapted to the standard hyperplane.

<1>1. There is an open cover of $\mathbb{CP}^n$ with homotopy types
\[
U\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{n-1},
\qquad
V\simeq *,
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq S^{2n-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Write homogeneous coordinates as
\[
[z_0:\cdots:z_n]
\]
and let
\[
p=[0:\cdots:0:1].
\]
Set
\[
U=\mathbb{CP}^n\setminus\{p\},
\qquad
V=\{[z_0:\cdots:z_n]:z_n\ne0\}.
\]
Then $V$ is the standard affine chart
\[
V\cong\mathbb C^n,
\qquad
[z_0:\cdots:z_{n-1}:z_n]
\longmapsto
\left(\frac{z_0}{z_n},\ldots,\frac{z_{n-1}}{z_n}\right),
\]
so $V$ is contractible.

Let
\[
H=\{z_n=0\}\cong\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}.
\]
Since a point of $U$ has $(z_0,\ldots,z_{n-1})\ne0$, the homotopy
\[
R_t[z_0:\cdots:z_{n-1}:z_n]
=
[z_0:\cdots:z_{n-1}:(1-t)z_n]
\]
is well-defined on $U$ for $0\le t\le1$ and deformation retracts $U$ onto $H$.
Thus
\[
U\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}.
\]

Finally, under the affine coordinates on $V$, the missing point $p$ is the origin.
Hence
\[
U\cap V=V\setminus\{p\}\cong\mathbb C^n\setminus\{0\}.
\]
Radial deformation retraction gives
\[
\mathbb C^n\setminus\{0\}\simeq S^{2n-1}.
\]
:::

<1>2. For every $k\le 2n-2$, inclusion induces an isomorphism
\[
H_k(\mathbb{CP}^{n-1})\xrightarrow{\sim}H_k(\mathbb{CP}^n).
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply Mayer--Vietoris to the cover $\mathbb{CP}^n=U\cup V$ from <1>1. For $k\le2n-2$,
\[
\widetilde H_k(U\cap V)
\cong
\widetilde H_k(S^{2n-1})=0
\]
and
\[
\widetilde H_{k-1}(U\cap V)=0.
\]
The relevant part of the reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence is therefore
\[
0\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_k(U)\oplus\widetilde H_k(V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_k(\mathbb{CP}^n)
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since $V$ is contractible and $U$ deformation retracts onto the standard hyperplane $H\cong\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$,
\[
\widetilde H_k(\mathbb{CP}^{n-1})
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\widetilde H_k(\mathbb{CP}^n).
\]
For $k=0$, both spaces are path-connected, so the corresponding statement for ordinary $H_0$ also holds.
:::

<1>3. One has
\[
H_{2n-1}(\mathbb{CP}^n)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H_{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
By induction, $H_j(\mathbb{CP}^{n-1})=0$ for $j>2n-2$.
Also $V$ is contractible and
\[
\widetilde H_j(U\cap V)
\cong
\widetilde H_j(S^{2n-1}).
\]

For degree $2n-1$, Mayer--Vietoris gives
\[
H_{2n-1}(S^{2n-1})
\longrightarrow
0
\longrightarrow
H_{2n-1}(\mathbb{CP}^n)
\longrightarrow
H_{2n-2}(S^{2n-1})=0,
\]
so exactness forces
\[
H_{2n-1}(\mathbb{CP}^n)=0.
\]

For degree $2n$, the relevant segment is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
0
\longrightarrow
H_{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_{2n-1}(S^{2n-1})
\longrightarrow
0.
\]
Hence $\delta$ is an isomorphism and
\[
H_{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n)
\cong
H_{2n-1}(S^{2n-1})
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>4. The homology groups of $\mathbb{CP}^n$ are
\[
H_p(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&p\text{ even and }0\le p\le2n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
For $n=0$, $\mathbb{CP}^0$ is a point, so the formula holds.
Assume it holds for $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$.

By <1>2, all groups in degrees $0\le k\le2n-2$ agree with those of $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$, hence are $\mathbb Z$ exactly in the even degrees and vanish in the odd degrees.
By <1>3,
\[
H_{2n-1}(\mathbb{CP}^n)=0,
\qquad
H_{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
For $k>2n$, the Mayer--Vietoris sequence has zero groups on both sides because $U\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$, $V\simeq *$, and $U\cap V\simeq S^{2n-1}$ have no homology in those degrees; hence
\[
H_k(\mathbb{CP}^n)=0.
\]
This completes the induction.
:::

<1>5. The standard inclusion
\[
i_n:\mathbb{CP}^n\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}
\]
induces an isomorphism on $H_k$ for every $k\le2n$.
::: {.proof}
Realize $\mathbb{CP}^n$ as the hyperplane
\[
H=\{z_{n+1}=0\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}
\]
and put
\[
p=[0:\cdots:0:1].
\]
For the Mayer--Vietoris cover of $\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}$ used in <1>1,
\[
U=\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}\setminus\{p\}
\]
deformation retracts onto $H=\mathbb{CP}^n$, while
\[
U\cap V\simeq S^{2n+1}.
\]
For every $k\le2n$,
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^{2n+1})=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\widetilde H_{k-1}(S^{2n+1})=0.
\]
Thus Mayer--Vietoris gives an isomorphism
\[
H_k(U)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_k(\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}).
\]
The inclusion $H\hookrightarrow U$ is a homotopy equivalence, and the composite
\[
H=\mathbb{CP}^n\hookrightarrow U\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}
\]
is exactly the standard inclusion $i_n$.
Therefore
\[
(i_n)_*:H_k(\mathbb{CP}^n)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_k(\mathbb{CP}^{n+1})
\]
for all $k\le2n$.
:::
