::: {.problem}
Let
\[
G_1=\langle a_1,\dots,a_n\mid r_1,\dots,r_m\rangle,
\qquad
G_2=\langle b_1,\dots,b_p\mid s_1,\dots,s_q\rangle,
\]
and suppose $H$ is generated by $h_1,\dots,h_o$, with maps $f_i:H\to G_i$.
Define
\[
G_1*_H G_2=
\langle a_1,\dots,a_n,b_1,\dots,b_p
\mid r_1,\dots,r_m,s_1,\dots,s_q,c_1,\dots,c_o\rangle,
\]
where $c_i=f_1(h_i)f_2(h_i)^{-1}$.

Check that $G_1*_H G_2$ fits into a pushout square for $f_1$ and $f_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
P=G_1*_H G_2.
\]
We verify the commutative square and the universal property.

<1>1. The presentation gives canonical homomorphisms $p_1:G_1\to P$ and $p_2:G_2\to P$.
::: {.proof}
Send each generator $a_j$ of $G_1$ to the generator with the same name in $P$. Since every relator
\[
r_1,\dots,r_m
\]
is among the defining relators of $P$, this assignment descends from the free group on the $a_j$ to a homomorphism
\[
p_1:G_1\to P.
\]
Similarly, the relators
\[
s_1,\dots,s_q
\]
give a homomorphism
\[
p_2:G_2\to P
\]
by sending each $b_j$ to the corresponding generator of $P$.
:::

<1>2. The square commutes: $p_1f_1=p_2f_2$.
::: {.proof}
For each generator $h_i$ of $H$, the defining relation
\[
c_i=f_1(h_i)f_2(h_i)^{-1}=1
\]
in $P$ says precisely that
\[
p_1(f_1(h_i))=p_2(f_2(h_i)).
\]
Since the $h_i$ generate $H$, the two homomorphisms
\[
p_1f_1,\ p_2f_2:H\to P
\]
agree on a generating set and therefore agree on all of $H$.
:::

<1>3. Every compatible pair of homomorphisms factors through $P$.
::: {.proof}
Let $K$ be a group and suppose
\[
k_1:G_1\to K,
\qquad
k_2:G_2\to K
\]
satisfy
\[
k_1f_1=k_2f_2.
\]
Define a map from the free group on
\[
a_1,\dots,a_n,b_1,\dots,b_p
\]
to $K$ by
\[
a_j\longmapsto k_1(a_j),
\qquad
b_j\longmapsto k_2(b_j).
\]

Every $r_j$ maps to $1$ because $k_1$ is a homomorphism out of $G_1$, and every $s_j$ maps to $1$ because $k_2$ is a homomorphism out of $G_2$. Finally, for each $i$,
\[
c_i=f_1(h_i)f_2(h_i)^{-1}
\]
maps to
\[
k_1(f_1(h_i))\,k_2(f_2(h_i))^{-1}=1
\]
by the compatibility hypothesis.

Thus every defining relator of $P$ lies in the kernel, so the free-group map descends to a homomorphism
\[
h:P\to K.
\]
By construction,
\[
hp_1=k_1,
\qquad
hp_2=k_2.
\]
:::

<1>4. The factorization is unique.
::: {.proof}
The group $P$ is generated by the images of
\[
a_1,\dots,a_n,b_1,\dots,b_p.
\]
Any homomorphism $h:P\to K$ satisfying
\[
hp_1=k_1,
\qquad
hp_2=k_2
\]
must therefore satisfy
\[
h(a_j)=k_1(a_j),
\qquad
h(b_j)=k_2(b_j)
\]
for every generator. These values determine a homomorphism out of $P$ uniquely. Hence the $h$ constructed in <1>3 is unique.
:::

By <1>1--<1>4, the square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
H&\xrightarrow{f_1}&G_1\\
\downarrow f_2&&\downarrow p_1\\
G_2&\xrightarrow{p_2}&P
\end{array}
\]
is a pushout square.
:::
