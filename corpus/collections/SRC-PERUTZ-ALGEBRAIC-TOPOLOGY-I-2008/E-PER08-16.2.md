::: {.problem}
Show that the splittings in the homology universal coefficient theorem cannot be chosen naturally in the space \(X\).

Let
\[
i:\mathbb R^2\hookrightarrow\mathbb{RP}^2
\]
be an embedding, let
\[
D=i(\{z\in\mathbb R^2:|z|<1\}),
\]
and let
\[
q:\mathbb{RP}^2\longrightarrow
\mathbb{RP}^2/(\mathbb{RP}^2\setminus D)
\cong S^2
\]
be the quotient map.
Show that the universal-coefficient short exact sequences with coefficients \(k=\mathbb Z/2\) for \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) and \(S^2\) cannot be split compatibly with \(q\).
:::

::: {.solution}
Write \(k=\mathbb F_2\).  In degree \(2\), the homology universal coefficient theorem gives a natural short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\otimes k
\xrightarrow{j_X}
H_2(X;k)
\xrightarrow{\rho_X}
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(H_1(X;\mathbb Z),k)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

<1>1. For \(X=\mathbb{RP}^2\), the degree-\(2\) UCT sequence is
\[
0\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow k
\xrightarrow[\cong]{\rho_{\mathbb{RP}^2}}
k\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The integral homology groups are
\[
H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Z)=0,
\qquad
H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Also \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is a closed \(2\)-manifold and hence has a mod-\(2\) fundamental class, so
\[
H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2;k)\cong k.
\]
Finally,
\[
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/2,k)\cong k.
\]
Thus the UCT sequence has the displayed form, and \(\rho_{\mathbb{RP}^2}\) is necessarily an isomorphism.
:::

<1>2. For \(X=S^2\), the degree-\(2\) UCT sequence is
\[
0\longrightarrow k
\xrightarrow[\cong]{j_{S^2}}
k
\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Here
\[
H_2(S^2;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(S^2;\mathbb Z)=0,
\]
so
\[
H_2(S^2;\mathbb Z)\otimes k\cong k,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(0,k)=0.
\]
The middle group is \(H_2(S^2;k)\cong k\), giving the claimed sequence.
:::

<1>3. The map
\[
q_*:H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2;k)\longrightarrow H_2(S^2;k)
\]
is an isomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Choose a point \(y\in S^2\) different from the collapsed point.
Then \(y\) lies in the image of \(D\), and \(q^{-1}(y)\) consists of exactly one point at which \(q\) is a local homeomorphism.
Therefore \(q\) has mod-\(2\) degree \(1\).

Equivalently, the mod-\(2\) fundamental class of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) maps to the nonzero mod-\(2\) fundamental class of \(S^2\). Hence \(q_*\) is nonzero between one-dimensional \(k\)-vector spaces, and is therefore an isomorphism.
:::

<1>4. No splitting of the UCT sequences can be natural with respect to \(q\).
::: {.proof}
Suppose, for contradiction, that the UCT short exact sequence admitted splittings natural in \(X\).  In degree \(2\), this would give sections
\[
s_X:
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(H_1(X;\mathbb Z),k)
\longrightarrow H_2(X;k)
\]
with \(\rho_Xs_X=\operatorname{id}\), and for every map \(f:X\to Y\) one would have
\[
f_*\,s_X
=
s_Y\,\operatorname{Tor}(f_*,\operatorname{id}_k).
\]

For \(X=\mathbb{RP}^2\), <1>1 shows that \(\rho_X\) is an isomorphism, so its section \(s_X\) is the inverse isomorphism.
In particular, if \(1\in k\) is the nonzero Tor class, then
\[
s_{\mathbb{RP}^2}(1)\neq0
\quad\text{in }H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2;k).
\]
By <1>3,
\[
q_*s_{\mathbb{RP}^2}(1)\neq0
\quad\text{in }H_2(S^2;k).
\]

On the other hand, the induced integral map
\[
q_*:H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2
\longrightarrow H_1(S^2;\mathbb Z)=0
\]
is zero, so the induced map on Tor is zero.
Naturality of the hypothetical splitting would therefore imply
\[
q_*s_{\mathbb{RP}^2}(1)
=s_{S^2}(0)
=0,
\]
a contradiction.

Hence the UCT splittings cannot be chosen naturally in \(X\).
:::
