::: {.problem}
Suppose that $X$ is a subspace of $\mathbb R^n$ such that there is a map
\[
r:\mathbb R^n\to X
\]
with $r|_X=\operatorname{id}_X$. Show that $X$ has the homology of a point.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $i:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^n$ denote the inclusion. The retraction hypothesis says
\[
r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_X.
\]

<1>1. For every $k>0$, one has $H_k(X)=0$.
::: {.proof}
Functoriality of singular homology gives
\[
r_*\circ i_*=(r\circ i)_*=(\operatorname{id}_X)_*=\operatorname{id}_{H_k(X)}.
\]
But $\mathbb R^n$ is contractible, so
\[
H_k(\mathbb R^n)=0\qquad(k>0).
\]
Hence
\[
i_*:H_k(X)\longrightarrow H_k(\mathbb R^n)
\]
is the zero map. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{id}_{H_k(X)}=r_*\circ i_*=0,
\]
which forces $H_k(X)=0$.
:::

<1>2. One has $H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
Because $r|_X=\operatorname{id}_X$, the map $r:\mathbb R^n\to X$ is surjective. Since $\mathbb R^n$ is path connected and the continuous image of a path-connected space is path connected, $X$ is path connected. Therefore
\[
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>3. Thus $X$ has the homology of a point.
::: {.proof}
A one-point space $*$ satisfies
\[
H_0(*)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_k(*)=0\quad(k>0).
\]
By <1>1 and <1>2, the same is true for $X$.
:::
:::
