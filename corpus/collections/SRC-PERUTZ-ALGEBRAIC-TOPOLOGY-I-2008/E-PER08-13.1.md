::: {.problem}
Show that if
\[
B\subset A\subset X,
\]
then there is a long exact sequence of the triple
\[
\cdots\longrightarrow H_i(A,B)
\longrightarrow H_i(X,B)
\longrightarrow H_i(X,A)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_{i-1}(A,B)
\longrightarrow\cdots.
\]
Describe the maps in this sequence.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
\[
S_*(Y,Z)=S_*(Y)/S_*(Z)
\]
for the relative singular chain complex of a pair $Z\subset Y$.

<1>1. There is a short exact sequence of chain complexes
\[
0\longrightarrow S_*(A,B)
\xrightarrow{\;j\;}
S_*(X,B)
\xrightarrow{\;q\;}
S_*(X,A)
\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The inclusion $A\hookrightarrow X$ induces
\[
j:S_*(A)/S_*(B)\longrightarrow S_*(X)/S_*(B),
\qquad
j(a+S_*(B))=a+S_*(B).
\]
This is injective: if $a\in S_*(A)$ maps to zero in $S_*(X,B)$, then $a\in S_*(B)$, so its class was already zero in $S_*(A,B)$.

Because $S_*(B)\subset S_*(A)$, quotienting once more gives a well-defined chain map
\[
q:S_*(X)/S_*(B)\longrightarrow S_*(X)/S_*(A),
\qquad
q(x+S_*(B))=x+S_*(A).
\]
It is surjective.
Moreover,
\[
\ker q
=
S_*(A)/S_*(B)
=
\operatorname{im}j.
\]
Thus the displayed sequence is short exact in every degree.
:::

<1>2. Passing to homology gives the long exact sequence
\[
\cdots\to H_i(A,B)
\xrightarrow{j_*}
H_i(X,B)
\xrightarrow{q_*}
H_i(X,A)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_{i-1}(A,B)
\to\cdots.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every short exact sequence of chain complexes
\[
0\to C'_*\to C_*\to C''_*\to0
\]
induces a long exact sequence in homology.
Applying that construction to the short exact sequence in <1>1 gives exactly
\[
\cdots\to H_i(S_*(A,B))
\to H_i(S_*(X,B))
\to H_i(S_*(X,A))
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_{i-1}(S_*(A,B))
\to\cdots,
\]
which is the claimed sequence after identifying these homology groups with relative homology.
:::

<1>3. The first map is induced by inclusion, and the second by passage to the coarser quotient.
::: {.proof}
At chain level,
\[
j(a+S_i(B))=a+S_i(B),
\]
so
\[
j_*:H_i(A,B)\to H_i(X,B)
\]
is the map induced by the inclusion of pairs
\[
(A,B)\hookrightarrow(X,B).
\]

Likewise,
\[
q(x+S_i(B))=x+S_i(A),
\]
so
\[
q_*:H_i(X,B)\to H_i(X,A)
\]
is induced by the quotient map
\[
S_i(X)/S_i(B)\twoheadrightarrow S_i(X)/S_i(A).
\]
Equivalently, it is the map of relative homology groups induced by the identity on $X$ together with the inclusion $B\subset A$.
:::

<1>4. The connecting homomorphism sends a relative class to the class of its boundary:
\[
\delta[x]=[\partial x]\in H_{i-1}(A,B).
\]
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
[x]\in H_i(X,A)
\]
and choose a singular chain $x\in S_i(X)$ representing it.
Being a relative cycle means precisely that
\[
\partial x\in S_{i-1}(A).
\]
Lift the class of $x$ from $S_i(X,A)$ to the class
\[
x+S_i(B)\in S_i(X,B).
\]
Its boundary in $S_{i-1}(X,B)$ is
\[
\partial x+S_{i-1}(B).
\]
Since $\partial x\in S_{i-1}(A)$, this boundary lies in the image of
\[
j:S_{i-1}(A,B)\hookrightarrow S_{i-1}(X,B).
\]
The defining construction of the connecting homomorphism therefore gives
\[
\delta[x]
=
[\partial x+S_{i-1}(B)]
=
[\partial x]
\in H_{i-1}(A,B).
\]

This also makes exactness transparent at the connecting spot: a class in $H_i(X,A)$ has zero connecting image exactly when, after changing its representative by a relative boundary if necessary, it can be represented by a cycle modulo $B$, i.e. comes from $H_i(X,B)$.
:::

<1>5. Hence all maps in the long exact sequence are canonical maps of the triple $B\subset A\subset X$.
::: {.proof}
The maps $j_*$ and $q_*$ come directly from the inclusions and quotient maps associated to
\[
B\subset A\subset X,
\]
and <1>4 identifies the only new map, the connecting homomorphism, explicitly on chains.
Thus the long exact sequence of <1>2 is the canonical long exact sequence of the triple.
:::
