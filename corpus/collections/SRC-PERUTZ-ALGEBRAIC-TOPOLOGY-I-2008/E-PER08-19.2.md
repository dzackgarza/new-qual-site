::: {.problem}
Use cellular homology to compute H$\\ast$(Σg), where Σg is a standard closed, orientable surface of genus g (defined, for instance, as a quotient of the 4ggon). Also, compute H$\\ast$(Σg#RP 2), the connected sum of Σg and RP 2.
:::

::: {.solution}
For the closed orientable genus-$g$ surface,
\[
H_k(\Sigma_g;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,2,\\
\mathbb Z^{2g},&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
For $N=\Sigma_g\#\mathbb{RP}^2$, which is the closed nonorientable surface of genus $2g+1$,
\[
H_k(N;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z^{2g}\oplus\mathbb Z/2,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]

<1>1. Orientable surface.
::: {.proof}
Use the CW structure with one $0$-cell, $2g$ $1$-cells $a_1,b_1,\dots,a_g,b_g$, and one $2$-cell attached by
\[
\prod_{i=1}^g[a_i,b_i].
\]
The cellular differential $d_1$ is zero because every $1$-cell is a loop at the unique vertex.
The exponent sum of every $a_i,b_i$ in the attaching word is zero, so $d_2=0$.
Thus the cellular complex is
\[
0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow0\mathbb Z^{2g}\xrightarrow0\mathbb Z\to0,
\]
which gives the claimed groups.
:::

<1>2. Connected sum with $\mathbb{RP}^2$.
::: {.proof}
This is the nonorientable surface $N_{2g+1}$ with one $0$-cell, $2g+1$ $1$-cells $c_1,\dots,c_{2g+1}$, and one $2$-cell attached by
\[
c_1^2c_2^2\cdots c_{2g+1}^2.
\]
Again $d_1=0$, while
\[
d_2(1)=2(c_1+\cdots+c_{2g+1}).
\]
This map is injective, so $H_2=0$, and Smith normal form of the row $(2,\dots,2)$ gives
\[
H_1\cong\mathbb Z^{2g}\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\]
:::
:::
