::: {.problem}
Convince yourself that the definition of an orientation at \(x\) is not crazy.
More specifically, take \(R=\mathbb Z\) and let \(M\) be an \(n\)-dimensional real vector space \(V\).
An orientation for \(V\) in the familiar linear-algebra sense is an equivalence class of isomorphisms
\[
\theta:V\longrightarrow \mathbb R^n,
\]
where two isomorphisms \(\theta_1,\theta_2\) are equivalent when
\[
\det(\theta_1\theta_2^{-1})>0.
\]
Show how such an orientation of the vector space \(V\) determines an orientation of \(V\) as a manifold at any chosen point \(x\in V\), i.e. an isomorphism
\[
H_n(V,V\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix the standard orientation of \(\mathbb R^n\), and let
\[
\varepsilon:H_n(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\};\mathbb Z)
\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb Z
\]
be the corresponding local-homology isomorphism.

<1>1. Any linear isomorphism \(\theta:V\to\mathbb R^n\) determines a local orientation at \(x\).
::: {.proof}
Translation by \(-x\),
\[
t_x:V\longrightarrow V,\qquad v\longmapsto v-x,
\]
is a homeomorphism of pairs
\[
(V,V\setminus\{x\})\longrightarrow(V,V\setminus\{0\}).
\]
The linear isomorphism \(\theta\) is a homeomorphism of pairs
\[
(V,V\setminus\{0\})\longrightarrow
(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}).
\]
Hence the composite induces an isomorphism
\[
(\theta\circ t_x)_*:
H_n(V,V\setminus\{x\})
\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}
H_n(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}).
\]
Define
\[
\eta_{x,\theta}:=\varepsilon\circ(\theta\circ t_x)_*.
\]
This is an isomorphism to \(\mathbb Z\), hence an orientation of the manifold \(V\) at \(x\) in Perutz's sense.
:::

<1>2. The map \(\eta_{x,\theta}\) depends only on the ordinary orientation class of \(\theta\).
::: {.proof}
Suppose \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_2\) define the same vector-space orientation.  Put
\[
g=\theta_1\theta_2^{-1}\in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb R).
\]
Then \(\det g>0\), so \(g\in\mathrm{GL}_n^+(\mathbb R)\).
The group \(\mathrm{GL}_n^+(\mathbb R)\) is path connected; choose a path
\(g_t\) of invertible linear maps from \(g_0=I\) to \(g_1=g\).
Because every \(g_t\) fixes \(0\) and maps \(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}\) to itself, this path is a homotopy of maps of pairs
\[
(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})
\longrightarrow
(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}).
\]
Therefore \(g_*\) is the identity on relative homology.  Since
\[
\theta_1=g\circ\theta_2,
\]
we obtain
\[
(\theta_1\circ t_x)_*
=g_*\,(\theta_2\circ t_x)_*
=(\theta_2\circ t_x)_*,
\]
and hence
\[
\eta_{x,\theta_1}=\eta_{x,\theta_2}.
\]
Thus the construction depends only on the chosen orientation of \(V\), not on its representative.
:::

<1>3. The two ordinary orientations give the two possible local orientations.
::: {.proof}
The long exact sequence of the pair and radial deformation retraction give
\[
H_n(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})
\cong
\widetilde H_{n-1}(S^{n-1})
\cong \mathbb Z.
\]
If \(g\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb R)\) has negative determinant, its restriction to the sphere has degree \(-1\); equivalently, \(g_*\) acts by multiplication by \(-1\) on this local homology group.  Thus replacing \(\theta\) by an oppositely oriented chart replaces \(\eta_{x,\theta}\) by \(-\eta_{x,\theta}\).

Consequently an ordinary orientation of the vector space \(V\) determines, at every \(x\in V\), exactly the corresponding local-homology orientation required by the manifold definition.
:::
