::: {.problem}
Show that the two spaces
\[
S^2\vee S^4
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathbb{CP}^2
\]
have isomorphic homology groups.
Likewise, show that
\[
S^3\vee S^5
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
S(\mathbb{CP}^2)
\]
have isomorphic homology groups.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The homology of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is
\[
H_k(\mathbb{CP}^2)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,2,4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the case $n=2$ of Exercise 12.5, which computes
\[
H_p(\mathbb{CP}^n)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&0\le p\le 2n\text{ and }p\text{ even},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
:::

<1>2. The wedge $S^2\vee S^4$ has the same homology groups.
::: {.proof}
Use the reduced Mayer–Vietoris sequence for the union of the two spheres meeting at the wedge point.
Since the intersection is a point, its reduced homology vanishes, and therefore
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^2\vee S^4)
\cong
\widetilde H_k(S^2)\oplus \widetilde H_k(S^4).
\]
The reduced homology of $S^m$ is $\mathbb Z$ in degree $m$ and zero otherwise.
Hence
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^2\vee S^4)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=2,4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The wedge is path connected, so $H_0(S^2\vee S^4)\cong\mathbb Z$.
Comparing with <1>1 proves
\[
H_*(S^2\vee S^4)\cong H_*(\mathbb{CP}^2)
\]
as graded abelian groups.
:::

<1>3. The suspension $S(\mathbb{CP}^2)$ has reduced homology $\mathbb Z$ in degrees $3$ and $5$ and zero in every other degree.
::: {.proof}
Proposition 13.3 gives the suspension isomorphisms
\[
\widetilde H_{k+1}(SX)\cong \widetilde H_k(X).
\]
By <1>1,
\[
\widetilde H_k(\mathbb{CP}^2)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=2,4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Therefore
\[
\widetilde H_j(S(\mathbb{CP}^2))\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=3,5,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Since a suspension of a non-empty space is path connected,
\[
H_0(S(\mathbb{CP}^2))\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>4. The wedge $S^3\vee S^5$ has the same homology groups as $S(\mathbb{CP}^2)$.
::: {.proof}
Exactly as in <1>2,
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^3\vee S^5)
\cong
\widetilde H_k(S^3)\oplus\widetilde H_k(S^5),
\]
so its reduced homology is $\mathbb Z$ in degrees $3$ and $5$ and zero otherwise.
It is path connected, hence also has $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$.
Comparing with <1>3 yields
\[
H_*(S^3\vee S^5)\cong H_*(S(\mathbb{CP}^2)).
\]
:::
:::
