::: {.problem}
Compute the first homology group $H_1$ of the $n$-torus
\[
T^n=(S^1)^n.
\]
Use this to construct a surjective homomorphism
\[
G\to GL_n(\mathbb Z),
\]
where $G$ is the group of homotopy equivalences $T^n\to T^n$ (with composition understood up to homotopy).
Show that when $n=1$, its kernel consists of maps homotopic to the identity.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $T^n=\mathbb R^n/\mathbb Z^n$.

<1>1. One has $H_1(T^n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^n$.
::: {.proof}
The fundamental group of a product is the product of the fundamental groups, so
\[
\pi_1(T^n)
\cong \pi_1(S^1)^n
\cong \mathbb Z^n.
\]
For every path-connected space, first singular homology is the abelianization of the fundamental group.
Since $\mathbb Z^n$ is already abelian,
\[
H_1(T^n;\mathbb Z)
\cong \pi_1(T^n)^{\mathrm{ab}}
\cong \mathbb Z^n.
\]
:::

<1>2. A homotopy equivalence of $T^n$ induces an element of $GL_n(\mathbb Z)$.
::: {.proof}
Let $[f]$ be the homotopy class of a homotopy equivalence $f:T^n\to T^n$.
Homotopic maps induce the same map on homology, and a homotopy equivalence induces an isomorphism on homology.
Thus, after the identification in <1>1,
\[
\Phi([f]):=f_*\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_1(T^n;\mathbb Z))
=GL_n(\mathbb Z).
\]
Functoriality gives
\[
\Phi([f]\circ[g])=(f\circ g)_*=f_*g_*=\Phi([f])\Phi([g]),
\]
so $\Phi:G\to GL_n(\mathbb Z)$ is a homomorphism.
:::

<1>3. The homomorphism $\Phi$ is surjective.
::: {.proof}
Let $A\in GL_n(\mathbb Z)$.
Because $A(\mathbb Z^n)=\mathbb Z^n$, the linear map
\[
A:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n
\]
descends to a map
\[
f_A:\mathbb R^n/\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb R^n/\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
[x]\longmapsto[Ax].
\]
Since $A^{-1}\in GL_n(\mathbb Z)$, the descended map $f_A$ is a homeomorphism with inverse $f_{A^{-1}}$.

The standard coordinate loops generate $\pi_1(T^n)\cong\mathbb Z^n$, and $f_A$ sends their homotopy classes according to the matrix $A$.
Hence the induced map on $H_1(T^n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^n$ is exactly $A$.
Therefore
\[
\Phi([f_A])=A,
\]
so $\Phi$ is surjective.
:::

<1>4. When $n=1$, the kernel consists exactly of maps homotopic to the identity.
::: {.proof}
Now $T^1=S^1$ and
\[
GL_1(\mathbb Z)=\{1,-1\}.
\]
For a map $f:S^1\to S^1$, the induced endomorphism of
\[
H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
is multiplication by the degree $\deg(f)$.
Hence a homotopy equivalence lies in $\ker\Phi$ exactly when $\deg(f)=1$.

Homotopy classes of maps $S^1\to S^1$ are classified by degree.
The identity map has degree $1$, so every map in the kernel is homotopic to the identity.
Conversely, every map homotopic to the identity induces the identity on $H_1$ and hence lies in the kernel.
:::
