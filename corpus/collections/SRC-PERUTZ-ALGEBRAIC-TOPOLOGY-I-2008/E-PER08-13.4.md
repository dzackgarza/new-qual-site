::: {.problem}
Show that if $(X;A,B)$ is an excisive triad and $b\in A\cap B$ is a basepoint, there is an exact Mayer–Vietoris sequence in reduced homology, formally the same as the usual sequence but with $\widetilde H_*$ in place of $H_*$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For every subspace $Y\subset X$ containing $b$, write
\[
\widetilde S_*(Y):=S_*(Y)/S_*(\{b\}).
\]
Its homology is $H_*(Y,\{b\})=\widetilde H_*(Y)$.

<1>1. There is a short exact sequence of chain complexes
\[
0\longrightarrow \widetilde S_*(A\cap B)
\overset{\alpha}{\longrightarrow}
\widetilde S_*(A)\oplus\widetilde S_*(B)
\overset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}
\widetilde S_*(A+B)
\longrightarrow0,
\]
where
\[
\alpha[c]=([c],-[c]),
\qquad
\beta([a],[d])=[a+d].
\]
::: {.proof}
The maps are well defined because the constant simplices at $b$ lie in all four chain complexes.

The map $\alpha$ is injective.
Indeed, if $[c]$ maps to zero, then $c$ represents zero modulo $S_*(\{b\})$ in both $S_*(A)$ and $S_*(B)$, hence already in $S_*(A\cap B)$.

The map $\beta$ is surjective because
\[
S_*(A+B)=S_*(A)+S_*(B).
\]
Clearly $\beta\alpha=0$.
Conversely, suppose $\beta([a],[d])=0$.
Then
\[
a+d\in S_*(\{b\})\subset S_*(A\cap B).
\]
Since $a\in S_*(A)$ and $d\in S_*(B)$, the equality $a=(a+d)-d$ shows that $a\in S_*(B)$ as well.
Hence
\[
a\in S_*(A)\cap S_*(B)=S_*(A\cap B).
\]
Modulo $S_*(\{b\})$ we have $[d]=-[a]$, so
\[
([a],[d])=\alpha[a].
\]
Thus $\ker\beta=\operatorname{im}\alpha$.
:::

<1>2. The inclusion
\[
\widetilde S_*(A+B)\longrightarrow \widetilde S_*(X)
\]
is a quasi-isomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Because $(X;A,B)$ is excisive, the locality theorem gives a quasi-isomorphism
\[
S_*(A+B)\longrightarrow S_*(X).
\]
Consider the commutative diagram of short exact sequences
\[
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
0&\to&S_*(\{b\})&\to&S_*(A+B)&\to&\widetilde S_*(A+B)&\to&0\\
 &&\Vert&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\\
0&\to&S_*(\{b\})&\to&S_*(X)&\to&\widetilde S_*(X)&\to&0.
\end{array}
\]
The first vertical map is the identity and the second is a quasi-isomorphism.
Comparing the associated long exact homology sequences and applying the five lemma shows that the third vertical map is also a quasi-isomorphism.
:::

<1>3. Taking homology in <1>1 and using <1>2 gives the reduced Mayer–Vietoris sequence
\[
\cdots\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_n(A\cap B)
\overset{(i_*,-j_*)}{\longrightarrow}
\widetilde H_n(A)\oplus\widetilde H_n(B)
\overset{k_*+\ell_*}{\longrightarrow}
\widetilde H_n(X)
\overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow}
\widetilde H_{n-1}(A\cap B)
\longrightarrow\cdots .
\]
::: {.proof}
Every short exact sequence of chain complexes induces a long exact sequence in homology.
Applying this to <1>1 yields the displayed sequence with $\widetilde H_n(A+B)$ in the middle.
By <1>2,
\[
\widetilde H_n(A+B)\xrightarrow{\sim}\widetilde H_n(X),
\]
so replacing that term by $\widetilde H_n(X)$ preserves exactness.
The maps are induced by the same inclusions and signs as in ordinary Mayer–Vietoris, because they arise from the same chain maps after quotienting by the common basepoint complex.
Therefore the reduced sequence is formally identical to the usual Mayer–Vietoris sequence with every $H_*$ replaced by $\widetilde H_*$.
:::
