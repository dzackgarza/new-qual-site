::: {.problem}
What is the relation between $H^*(X;R)$ and $H^*(X)$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $R$ be an abelian group, and write $H^*(X)$ and $H_*(X)$ for integral cohomology and homology.

::: pf

::: pf-step
For every space $X$ and every $n$, there is a natural short exact sequence
$$
0 \to \operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1}(X), R) \to H^n(X; R) \to \operatorname{Hom}(H_n(X), R) \to 0,
$$
which splits, but not naturally.

::: pf-proof
This is the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology, applied to the singular chain complex of $X$, which is a complex of free abelian groups.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fg-cochains}
If $X$ is a CW complex with finitely many cells in each dimension, the cellular cochains satisfy $C^n(X; R) \cong C^n(X) \otimes R$, compatibly with the coboundary.

::: pf-proof
Each cellular chain group $C_n(X)$ is free of finite rank, so $\Hom(C_n(X), R) \cong \Hom(C_n(X), \ZZ) \otimes R$, naturally in $C_n(X)$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $X$ is a CW complex with finitely many cells in each dimension, there is a natural short exact sequence
$$
0 \to H^n(X) \otimes R \to H^n(X; R) \to \operatorname{Tor}(H^{n+1}(X), R) \to 0,
$$
which splits, but not naturally.
This expresses $H^*(X; R)$ through $H^*(X)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#fg-cochains){.pf-ref}, $H^n(X; R)$ is the cohomology of $C^*(X) \otimes R$.
The cochain complex $C^*(X)$ consists of free abelian groups, so the algebraic universal coefficient theorem for $({-}) \otimes R$ applies to it.
The differential raises degree, so the Tor term involves $H^{n+1}(X)$.
:::
