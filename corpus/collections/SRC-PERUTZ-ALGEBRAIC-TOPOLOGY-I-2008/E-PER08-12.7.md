::: {.problem}
Use Mayer--Vietoris to compute
\[
H_*(\Sigma_g),
\]
where $\Sigma_g$ is a $2$-sphere with $g$ handles attached, equivalently the closed connected orientable surface of genus $g$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $g\ge1$; the case $g=0$ is the usual homology computation for $S^2$.
Remove the interior of a small embedded disk from $\Sigma_g$ and call the resulting surface with one boundary component $A$.
Let $B$ be a slightly larger open disk containing the removed disk.
Then
\[
\Sigma_g=A\cup B,
\qquad
A\cap B\simeq S^1,
\qquad
B\simeq *.
\]

<1>1. The punctured surface $A$ deformation retracts onto a wedge of $2g$ circles.
::: {.proof}
Use the standard $4g$-gon model for $\Sigma_g$, whose boundary word is
\[
[a_1,b_1][a_2,b_2]\cdots[a_g,b_g],
\qquad
[a_i,b_i]=a_i b_i a_i^{-1}b_i^{-1}.
\]
Removing the interior of the unique $2$-cell leaves a graph consisting of one vertex and the $2g$ edge loops
\[
a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g.
\]
Thus
\[
A\simeq\bigvee_{i=1}^g(S^1_{a_i}\vee S^1_{b_i}),
\]
so
\[
H_1(A)\cong
\bigoplus_{i=1}^g\bigl(\mathbb Z[a_i]\oplus\mathbb Z[b_i]\bigr)
\cong\mathbb Z^{2g},
\]
and $H_k(A)=0$ for $k\ge2$.
:::

<1>2. The boundary circle of $A$ maps trivially into $H_1(A)$.
::: {.proof}
Under the deformation retraction from <1>1, the oriented boundary circle is represented by the word
\[
[a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_g,b_g].
\]
Passing to first homology abelianizes this word.
Each commutator contributes
\[
[a_i]+[b_i]-[a_i]-[b_i]=0.
\]
Hence the inclusion-induced map
\[
j_*:H_1(A\cap B)\cong\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow
H_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z^{2g}
\]
is zero.
:::

<1>3. Mayer--Vietoris gives
\[
H_2(\Sigma_g)\cong\mathbb Z
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H_1(\Sigma_g)\cong\mathbb Z^{2g}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $A$, $B$, and $A\cap B$ are connected, the map
\[
H_0(A\cap B)\longrightarrow H_0(A)\oplus H_0(B)
\]
is injective.
Since $H_2(A)=H_2(B)=0$ and $H_1(B)=0$, the relevant Mayer--Vietoris segment is
\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(\Sigma_g)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_1(A\cap B)
\xrightarrow{j_*}
H_1(A)
\longrightarrow H_1(\Sigma_g)
\longrightarrow0.
\]
By <1>2, $j_*=0$.
Exactness therefore gives
\[
H_2(\Sigma_g)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_1(A\cap B)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
and
\[
H_1(\Sigma_g)\cong H_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z^{2g}.
\]
:::

<1>4. The complete integral homology is
\[
H_k(\Sigma_g;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,2,\\
\mathbb Z^{2g},&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
The surface $\Sigma_g$ is connected, so
\[
H_0(\Sigma_g)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The groups in degrees $1$ and $2$ were computed in <1>3.
For $k>2$, the Mayer--Vietoris sequence has zero terms from $A$, $B$, and $A\cap B$, so
\[
H_k(\Sigma_g)=0.
\]
For $g=0$, $\Sigma_0=S^2$, giving the same formula with $H_1=0=\mathbb Z^0$.
:::

<1>5. Explicit minimal generators are a point, the $2g$ handle loops, and a fundamental $2$-cycle.
::: {.proof}
Choose a point $p\in\Sigma_g$; its class generates $H_0$.
The loops
\[
a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g
\]
from the standard polygon generate $H_1\cong\mathbb Z^{2g}$ and are minimal because that group has rank $2g$.
Finally, orient the standard $4g$-gon, triangulate it, and take the sum of its oriented triangles after the edge identifications.
The identified boundary edges cancel in pairs, so this descends to a singular $2$-cycle $[\Sigma_g]$.
Its Mayer--Vietoris boundary is a generator of $H_1(A\cap B)$, so by the isomorphism in <1>3 it generates
\[
H_2(\Sigma_g)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus these cycles give a minimal generating collection for all nonzero homology groups.
:::
