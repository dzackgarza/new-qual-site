::: {.problem}
Degree has the following further properties:

1. When $n=1$, the homological degree defined here coincides with the degree defined earlier via $\pi_1(S^1)$.

2. $\deg s=-1$ when $s$ is the restriction to $S^n$ of a reflection in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$.

3. $\deg a=(-1)^{n+1}$ when $a$ is the antipodal map $x\mapsto -x$.

4. $\deg f=(-1)^{n+1}$ when $f:S^n\to S^n$ has no fixed points.

Use the last point to show:

(a) no group of order greater than $2$ can act freely on $S^{2m}$;

(b) $S^{2m}$ possesses no nowhere-vanishing vector field.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For $S^1$, homological degree agrees with degree defined through $\pi_1(S^1)$.
::: {.proof}
Choose the standard positive generator
\[
\gamma\in \pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The degree defined through the fundamental group is the integer $d$ determined by
\[
f_*(\gamma)=d\gamma.
\]

The Hurewicz homomorphism
\[
h:\pi_1(S^1)\longrightarrow H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)
\]
is an isomorphism, and it is natural.
Hence
\[
h(f_*(\gamma))=f_*(h(\gamma)).
\]
If $f_*(\gamma)=d\gamma$, then
\[
f_*(h(\gamma))=d\,h(\gamma).
\]
Since $h(\gamma)$ is the standard generator of $H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)$, the induced map on top homology is multiplication by the same integer $d$.
By definition, that integer is the homological degree of $f$.
:::

<1>2. A reflection of $S^n$ has degree $-1$.
::: {.proof}
Let $r:\mathbb R^{n+1}\to\mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a linear reflection and let
\[
s=r|_{S^n}.
\]
A reflection has determinant $-1$, so it reverses the orientation of the oriented ball $D^{n+1}$.
The boundary orientation on $S^n=\partial D^{n+1}$ is characterized by the outward-normal-first convention.
Therefore the boundary map $s$ also reverses the orientation of $S^n$.
Consequently its action on the fundamental class is
\[
s_*[S^n]=-[S^n],
\]
and hence
\[
\deg s=-1.
\]
:::

<1>3. The antipodal map has degree $(-1)^{n+1}$.
::: {.proof}
For $0\le j\le n$, let
\[
r_j(x_0,\ldots,x_j,\ldots,x_n)
=(x_0,\ldots,-x_j,\ldots,x_n).
\]
Each $r_j|_{S^n}$ is a reflection, so by <1>2 it has degree $-1$.
The antipodal map is the composite of these $n+1$ coordinate reflections:
\[
a=r_0\circ r_1\circ\cdots\circ r_n.
\]
Degree is multiplicative under composition, so
\[
\deg a
=\prod_{j=0}^{n}\deg(r_j|_{S^n})
=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
:::

<1>4. If $f:S^n\to S^n$ has no fixed points, then $f$ is homotopic to the antipodal map.
::: {.proof}
Define
\[
H:S^n\times[0,1]\longrightarrow S^n,
\qquad
H(x,t)=\frac{(1-t)f(x)-tx}{\|(1-t)f(x)-tx\|}.
\]
We must check that the denominator never vanishes.
If
\[
(1-t)f(x)-tx=0,
\]
then
\[
(1-t)f(x)=tx.
\]
Taking norms and using $\|f(x)\|=\|x\|=1$ gives $1-t=t$, hence $t=1/2$; the displayed equality then gives $f(x)=x$, contrary to the hypothesis that $f$ has no fixed point.
Thus $H$ is well-defined and continuous.
Moreover,
\[
H(x,0)=f(x),
\qquad
H(x,1)=-x=a(x).
\]
Hence $f\simeq a$.
Homotopy invariance of degree and <1>3 give
\[
\deg f=\deg a=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
:::

<1>5. No group of order greater than $2$ acts freely on $S^{2m}$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose a finite group $G$ acts freely on $S^{2m}$.
For each $g\in G$, let
\[
\rho_g:S^{2m}\to S^{2m},
\qquad
\rho_g(x)=g\cdot x.
\]
Because this is a group action,
\[
\rho_{gh}=\rho_g\circ\rho_h,
\]
so multiplicativity of degree makes
\[
\chi:G\longrightarrow\{\pm1\},
\qquad
\chi(g)=\deg(\rho_g),
\]
a group homomorphism.

If $g\ne e$, freeness says that $\rho_g$ has no fixed points.
Since the sphere dimension $2m$ is even, <1>4 yields
\[
\chi(g)=(-1)^{2m+1}=-1.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\chi(e)=\deg(\operatorname{id})=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\ker\chi=\{e\}.
\]
Thus $\chi$ is injective.
Since its codomain has two elements,
\[
|G|\le2.
\]
Hence no group of order greater than $2$ can act freely on $S^{2m}$.
:::

<1>6. The even-dimensional sphere has no nowhere-vanishing tangent vector field.
::: {.proof}
Assume that
\[
v:S^{2m}\to TS^{2m}
\]
is a continuous tangent vector field with $v(x)\ne0$ for every $x$.
Normalize it:
\[
u(x)=\frac{v(x)}{\|v(x)\|}.
\]
Because $u(x)$ is tangent to the unit sphere at $x$,
\[
\langle x,u(x)\rangle=0,
\qquad
\|x\|=\|u(x)\|=1.
\]
Define
\[
K:S^{2m}\times[0,1]\longrightarrow S^{2m}
\]
by
\[
K(x,t)=\cos(\pi t)x+\sin(\pi t)u(x).
\]
Orthogonality gives
\[
\|K(x,t)\|^2
=\cos^2(\pi t)+\sin^2(\pi t)
=1,
\]
so $K$ indeed takes values in the sphere.
At the endpoints,
\[
K(x,0)=x,
\qquad
K(x,1)=-x.
\]
Thus the identity map is homotopic to the antipodal map on $S^{2m}$.
Homotopy invariance of degree would then imply
\[
1=\deg(\operatorname{id})=\deg(a)=(-1)^{2m+1}=-1,
\]
a contradiction.
Therefore $S^{2m}$ admits no nowhere-vanishing continuous tangent vector field.
:::
