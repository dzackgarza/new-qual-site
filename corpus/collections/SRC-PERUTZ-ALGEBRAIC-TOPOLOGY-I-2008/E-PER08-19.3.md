::: {.problem}
Describe how the product X $\\times$ Y of finite CW complexes X and Y inherits a structure of CW complex.
(1) Compute the Euler characteristic χ(X $\\times$ Y ) in terms of χ(X) and χ(Y ). (2) Show that Ccell $\\ast$ (X $\\times$ Y ) = Ccell $\\ast$ (X) $\\otimes$ Ccell $\\ast$ (Y ) as a graded abelian group.
(You are not asked to compute the differential.)
(3) Compute H$\\ast$(Sn $\\times$ Sn).
(4) Let X = (S3)$\\times$n = S3 $\\times$ · · · $\\times$ S3. Show that H3p(X) is isomorphic to the exterior power ΛpZn, and that Hq(X) = 0 if q is not a multiple of 3.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $e^p_\alpha$ is a cell of $X$ and $e^q_\beta$ a cell of $Y$, then
\[
e^p_\alpha\times e^q_\beta
\]
is an open $(p+q)$-cell of $X\times Y$.
For finite CW complexes these product cells give a CW structure whose skeleta satisfy
\[
(X\times Y)^n=\bigcup_{p+q\le n}X^p\times Y^q.
\]

<1>1. Euler characteristic is multiplicative.
::: {.proof}
Let $c_p(X)$ and $c_q(Y)$ be the numbers of cells.
The number of $n$-cells of the product is
\[
c_n(X\times Y)=\sum_{p+q=n}c_p(X)c_q(Y).
\]
Hence
\[
\chi(X\times Y)
=\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}c_p(X)c_q(Y)
=\chi(X)\chi(Y).
\]
:::

<1>2. Cellular chains are the graded tensor product as groups.
::: {.proof}
Both sides are free abelian groups with basis indexed by pairs of cells.
In degree $n$,
\[
C_n^{\rm cell}(X\times Y)
\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=n}C_p^{\rm cell}(X)\otimes C_q^{\rm cell}(Y).
\]
Thus
\[
C_*^{\rm cell}(X\times Y)\cong C_*^{\rm cell}(X)\otimes C_*^{\rm cell}(Y)
\]
as graded abelian groups.
:::

<1>3. Homology of $S^n\times S^n$.
::: {.proof}
Using the product of the CW structures with one cell in degrees $0,n$, the product has one $0$-cell, two $n$-cells, and one $2n$-cell, with zero cellular differentials.
Therefore, for $n>0$,
\[
H_k(S^n\times S^n)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,2n,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&k=n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
:::

<1>4. Homology of $(S^3)^n$.
::: {.proof}
The product CW structure has, in degree $3p$, one cell for every $p$-element subset of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ and no cells in other positive degrees.
All cellular differentials vanish for degree reasons.
If $e_1,\dots,e_n$ denotes the degree-$3$ basis, identify the cell indexed by $\{i_1<\cdots<i_p\}$ with
\[
e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_p}.
\]
Hence
\[
H_{3p}((S^3)^n)\cong\Lambda^p\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad H_q((S^3)^n)=0\quad(3\nmid q).
\]
:::
