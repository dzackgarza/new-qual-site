::: {.problem}
Use the locality theorem for chains to derive the relative Mayer--Vietoris sequence
\[
\cdots \longrightarrow H_{p+1}\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cap B)\right)
\longrightarrow H_p\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cup B)\right)
\longrightarrow H_p\!\left(X,X\setminus A\right)\oplus H_p\!\left(X,X\setminus B\right)
\longrightarrow H_p\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cap B)\right)
\longrightarrow \cdots,
\]
in the excisive situation of the notes.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
\[
U=X\setminus A,\qquad V=X\setminus B.
\]
Then
\[
U\cap V=X\setminus(A\cup B),
\qquad
U\cup V=X\setminus(A\cap B).
\]
Write \(S=S_*(X)\), \(S_U=S_*(U)\), and \(S_V=S_*(V)\).

<1>1. There is a short exact sequence of chain complexes
\[
0\longrightarrow \frac{S}{S_U\cap S_V}
\overset{\iota}{\longrightarrow}
\frac{S}{S_U}\oplus\frac{S}{S_V}
\overset{q}{\longrightarrow}
\frac{S}{S_U+S_V}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
where
\[
\iota([c])=([c],[c]),
\qquad
q([a],[b])=[a-b].
\]

<2>1. The maps are well defined and \(q\iota=0\).
::: {.proof}
Changing \(c\) by an element of \(S_U\cap S_V\) changes neither component of
\(\iota([c])\).  Likewise, changing \(a\) by an element of \(S_U\) or \(b\) by an
element of \(S_V\) changes \(a-b\) by an element of \(S_U+S_V\).  Finally
\(q([c],[c])=[0]\).
:::

<2>2. The map \(\iota\) is injective.
::: {.proof}
If \(\iota([c])=(0,0)\), then \(c\in S_U\) and \(c\in S_V\).  Hence
\(c\in S_U\cap S_V\), so \([c]=0\) in \(S/(S_U\cap S_V)\).
:::

<2>3. The kernel of \(q\) is the image of \(\iota\), and \(q\) is surjective.
::: {.proof}
If \(q([a],[b])=0\), then \(a-b=u+v\) for some \(u\in S_U\) and \(v\in S_V\).
Set \(c=a-u=b+v\).  Then \([a]=[c]\) in \(S/S_U\) and \([b]=[c]\) in
\(S/S_V\), so \(([a],[b])=\iota([c])\).  Conversely \(q\iota=0\).
Surjectivity is immediate because every class \([c]\in S/(S_U+S_V)\) is
\(q([c],[0])\).
:::

<1>2. The three complexes in <1>1 compute the required relative groups.

<2>1. The first two identifications are literal:
\[
\frac{S}{S_U\cap S_V}
=
S_*\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cup B)\right),
\]
and
\[
\frac{S}{S_U}\oplus\frac{S}{S_V}
=
S_*\!\left(X,X\setminus A\right)
\oplus
S_*\!\left(X,X\setminus B\right).
\]
::: {.proof}
A singular chain lies in both \(S_U\) and \(S_V\) exactly when every simplex in
it lies in \(U\cap V=X\setminus(A\cup B)\).  The second equality is just the
definition of relative singular chains.
:::

<2>2. Locality identifies the last quotient on homology with
\[
S_*\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cap B)\right).
\]
::: {.proof}
The locality theorem says that, in the excisive situation, the inclusion
\[
S_U+S_V\hookrightarrow S_*(U\cup V)
\]
is a quasi-isomorphism.  Since \(U\cup V=X\setminus(A\cap B)\), compare the
short exact sequences
\[
0\to S_U+S_V\to S\to S/(S_U+S_V)\to0
\]
and
\[
0\to S_*(U\cup V)\to S\to S/S_*(U\cup V)\to0.
\]
The induced map on the first terms is a homology isomorphism and the middle map
is the identity.  The long exact sequences and the five lemma therefore give
\[
H_*\!\left(S/(S_U+S_V)\right)
\cong
H_*\!\left(S/S_*(U\cup V)\right)
=
H_*\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cap B)\right).
\]
:::

<1>3. The long exact homology sequence of <1>1 is the relative Mayer--Vietoris sequence.
::: {.proof}
Applying homology to the short exact sequence in <1>1 gives
\[
\cdots\to
H_{p+1}\!\left(S/(S_U+S_V)\right)
\to
H_p\!\left(S/(S_U\cap S_V)\right)
\to
H_p(S/S_U)\oplus H_p(S/S_V)
\to
H_p\!\left(S/(S_U+S_V)\right)
\to\cdots.
\]
Substituting the identifications of <1>2 yields exactly
\[
\cdots \to H_{p+1}\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cap B)\right)
\to H_p\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cup B)\right)
\to H_p\!\left(X,X\setminus A\right)\oplus H_p\!\left(X,X\setminus B\right)
\to H_p\!\left(X,X\setminus(A\cap B)\right)
\to\cdots.
\]
:::
