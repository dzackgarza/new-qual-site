::: {.problem}
Check the proposition.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $a\in S^p(X)$, $b\in S^q(X)$, and let $\sigma:\Delta^{p+q+1}\to X\times X$ be a singular simplex.
The Alexander--Whitney formula is
\[
\langle\zeta(a\otimes b),\tau\rangle
=
\langle a,(\operatorname{pr}_1\tau)|[v_0,\dots,v_p]\rangle
\langle b,(\operatorname{pr}_2\tau)|[v_p,\dots,v_{p+q}]\rangle.
\]

<1>1. $\zeta$ is a cochain map.
::: {.proof}
Evaluate both sides of
\[
d\zeta(a\otimes b)
=
\zeta(da\otimes b)+(-1)^p\zeta(a\otimes db)
\]
on $\sigma$.
In the alternating boundary sum, deleting a vertex among $v_0,\dots,v_p$ gives exactly the terms of $da$ paired with the unchanged back face; deleting a vertex among $v_p,\dots,v_{p+q+1}$ gives, after the index shift, $(-1)^p$ times the terms of $db$ paired with the unchanged front face.
The two occurrences of the shared vertex $v_p$ are precisely the middle terms needed for these two sums, with opposite bookkeeping signs.
Hence the displayed Leibniz identity holds on every simplex, proving that $\zeta$ is a chain map from the tensor-product cochain complex.
:::

<1>2. $\zeta$ is natural.
::: {.proof}
For $f:X\to Y$, restricting $(f\times f)\sigma$ to a front or back face is the same as first restricting $\sigma$ and then applying $f$.
Therefore evaluation gives
\[
(f\times f)^*\zeta_Y(a\otimes b)
=\zeta_X(f^*a\otimes f^*b).
\]
:::

<1>3. The unit condition holds.
::: {.proof}
The degree-$0$ cochain $1_X$ evaluates to $1$ on every vertex.
Thus for every $0$-simplex of $X\times X$,
\[
\zeta(1_X\otimes1_X)=1,
\]
so $\zeta(1_X\otimes1_X)=1_{X\times X}$.
:::
:::
