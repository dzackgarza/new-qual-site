::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a non-empty closed subspace of $X$.
There is a natural homomorphism
\[
H_*(X,A)\longrightarrow H_*(X/A,A/A)=\widetilde H_*(X/A).
\]
Use excision to show that, when $A$ has a neighbourhood $N$ that deformation-retracts to $A$, this map is an isomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $q:X\to X/A$ be the quotient map and write $*=A/A$.

<1>1. The inclusion of pairs $(X,A)\to(X,N)$ induces an isomorphism
\[
H_k(X,A)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_k(X,N)
\]
for every $k$.
::: {.proof}
Because $N$ deformation-retracts onto $A$, the inclusion $A\hookrightarrow N$ is a homotopy equivalence.
Hence
\[
H_k(N,A)=0
\]
for every $k$, by the long exact sequence of the pair $(N,A)$.
The long exact sequence of the triple $A\subset N\subset X$ contains
\[
H_k(N,A)\longrightarrow H_k(X,A)\longrightarrow H_k(X,N)
\longrightarrow H_{k-1}(N,A).
\]
The outer groups vanish, so the middle map is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>2. Likewise, the inclusion of pairs $(X/A,*)\to(X/A,N/A)$ induces an isomorphism
\[
H_k(X/A,*)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_k(X/A,N/A).
\]
::: {.proof}
A deformation retraction $N\to A$ fixes $A$ pointwise, so it descends through the quotient to a deformation retraction
\[
N/A\longrightarrow A/A=*.
\]
Thus $H_k(N/A,*)=0$ for every $k$.
Applying the long exact sequence of the triple
\[
*\subset N/A\subset X/A
\]
gives the desired isomorphism exactly as in <1>1.
:::

<1>3. Excision gives isomorphisms
\[
H_k(X,N)\cong H_k(X\setminus A,N\setminus A)
\]
and
\[
H_k(X/A,N/A)\cong
H_k\bigl((X/A)\setminus\{*\},(N/A)\setminus\{*\}\bigr).
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $A$ is closed and $N$ is a neighbourhood of $A$,
\[
\overline A=A\subset \operatorname{int}_X(N).
\]
Therefore the standard excision hypothesis is satisfied for removing $A$ from the pair $(X,N)$.

The set $N/A$ is a neighbourhood of $*=A/A$ in $X/A$: if $U$ is open with $A\subset U\subset N$, then $q(U)$ is open because $q^{-1}(q(U))=U$, and $*\in q(U)\subset N/A$.
Hence the same excision argument removes $*$ from the pair $(X/A,N/A)$.
:::

<1>4. The quotient map restricts to a homeomorphism of pairs
\[
(X\setminus A,N\setminus A)\xrightarrow{\cong}
\bigl((X/A)\setminus\{*\},(N/A)\setminus\{*\}\bigr).
\]
::: {.proof}
The only identifications made by $q$ are among points of $A$.
Consequently $q$ is one-to-one on $X\setminus A$, maps that subspace onto $(X/A)\setminus\{*\}$, and its inverse there is continuous by the defining quotient topology.
The same restriction carries $N\setminus A$ onto $(N/A)\setminus\{*\}$.
:::

<1>5. The natural map
\[
q_*:H_k(X,A)\longrightarrow H_k(X/A,*)
\]
is an isomorphism for every $k$.
::: {.proof}
By naturality of relative homology and excision, $q_*$ fits into the commutative comparison obtained from <1>1--<1>4:
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_k(X,A)
&\xrightarrow{\sim} H_k(X,N)
\xleftarrow{\sim} H_k(X\setminus A,N\setminus A)\\
&\xrightarrow{\sim}
H_k\bigl((X/A)\setminus\{*\},(N/A)\setminus\{*\}\bigr)
\xrightarrow{\sim} H_k(X/A,N/A)
\xleftarrow{\sim} H_k(X/A,*).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every comparison map is an isomorphism, and the composite is precisely the homomorphism induced by $q$.
Therefore $q_*$ is an isomorphism.
Since
\[
H_k(X/A,*)=\widetilde H_k(X/A),
\]
this is the claimed natural isomorphism
\[
H_*(X,A)\cong \widetilde H_*(X/A).
\]
:::
