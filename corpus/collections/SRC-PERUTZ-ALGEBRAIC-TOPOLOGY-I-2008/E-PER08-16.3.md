::: {.problem}
This exercise develops an alternative approach to a special case of universal coefficients.
Let \(C_*\) be a chain complex of free abelian groups.
Show that tensoring by the short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times n}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow \mathbb Z/n\longrightarrow 0
\]
gives rise to a short exact sequence of chain complexes
\[
0\longrightarrow C_*\xrightarrow{\times n}C_*\longrightarrow C_*\otimes \mathbb Z/n\longrightarrow 0.
\]
By analyzing the resulting long exact sequence of homology groups, deduce a short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow H_p(C_*)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z/n
\longrightarrow H_p(C_*\otimes \mathbb Z/n)
\longrightarrow \{x\in H_{p-1}(C_*):nx=0\}
\longrightarrow 0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Tensoring the coefficient sequence with each \(C_q\) remains exact.
::: {.proof}
For each degree \(q\), tensor
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\times n}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/n
\longrightarrow0
\]
with the free abelian group \(C_q\).  Since \(C_q\) is free, hence flat, tensoring preserves exactness.
Using \(C_q\otimes\mathbb Z\cong C_q\), we obtain
\[
0\longrightarrow C_q
\xrightarrow{\times n}
C_q
\longrightarrow C_q\otimes\mathbb Z/n
\longrightarrow0.
\]
The maps commute with the differentials because multiplication by \(n\) and coefficient reduction are natural.
Therefore these degreewise exact sequences assemble to a short exact sequence of chain complexes
\[
0\longrightarrow C_*
\xrightarrow{\times n}
C_*
\longrightarrow C_*\otimes\mathbb Z/n
\longrightarrow0.
\]
:::

<1>2. The associated homology long exact sequence contains
\[
H_p(C_*)\xrightarrow{\times n}H_p(C_*)
\longrightarrow H_p(C_*\otimes\mathbb Z/n)
\xrightarrow{\beta}
H_{p-1}(C_*)\xrightarrow{\times n}H_{p-1}(C_*),
\]
where \(\beta\) is the connecting homomorphism.
::: {.proof}
This is the standard long exact sequence in homology associated to the short exact sequence of chain complexes from <1>1.  The maps induced by the first chain map are multiplication by \(n\) on homology.
:::

<1>3. Exactness reduces the displayed segment to
\[
0\longrightarrow
\operatorname{coker}(n:H_p(C_*)\to H_p(C_*))
\longrightarrow H_p(C_*\otimes\mathbb Z/n)
\longrightarrow
\ker(n:H_{p-1}(C_*)\to H_{p-1}(C_*))
\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Exactness at the second copy of \(H_p(C_*)\) says that the kernel of the map into \(H_p(C_*\otimes\mathbb Z/n)\) is \(nH_p(C_*)\). Thus that map descends to an injection from the cokernel of multiplication by \(n\).

Exactness at \(H_{p-1}(C_*)\) says
\[
\operatorname{im}\beta
=
\ker(n:H_{p-1}(C_*)\to H_{p-1}(C_*)).
\]
Exactness at the middle group identifies the kernel of \(\beta\) with the image of the cokernel term.
This gives the short exact sequence.
:::

<1>4. The two outer terms have the forms claimed in the exercise.
::: {.proof}
For any abelian group \(A\),
\[
A\otimes\mathbb Z/n\cong A/nA.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{coker}(n:H_p(C_*)\to H_p(C_*))
=
H_p(C_*)/nH_p(C_*)
\cong
H_p(C_*)\otimes\mathbb Z/n.
\]
Also
\[
\ker(n:H_{p-1}(C_*)\to H_{p-1}(C_*))
=
\{x\in H_{p-1}(C_*):nx=0\}.
\]
Substituting these identifications into <1>3 gives
\[
0\longrightarrow H_p(C_*)\otimes\mathbb Z/n
\longrightarrow H_p(C_*\otimes\mathbb Z/n)
\xrightarrow{\beta}
\{x\in H_{p-1}(C_*):nx=0\}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
as required.
:::
