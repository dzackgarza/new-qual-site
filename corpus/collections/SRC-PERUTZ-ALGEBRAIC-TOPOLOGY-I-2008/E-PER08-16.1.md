::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be the Klein bottle.
Compute $H_*(K;\ZZ)$. Then compute
$$
H_*(K;\ZZ/(2n))
$$
in two ways:

(i) directly;

(ii) using the universal coefficient theorem for homology.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
A=\ZZ/(2n).
$$
Use the standard CW structure on the Klein bottle with one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell attached by the word
$$
aba^{-1}b.
$$
With the corresponding cellular bases, the integral cellular chain complex is
$$
0\longrightarrow \ZZ
\xrightarrow{d_2}
\ZZ a\oplus\ZZ b
\xrightarrow{d_1}
\ZZ
\longrightarrow0,
$$
where
$$
d_1=0,
\qquad
d_2(1)=2b.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The integral homology is
$$
H_0(K;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_1(K;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2,
\qquad
H_i(K;\ZZ)=0\quad(i\ge2).
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $d_1=0$,
$$
H_0(K)=\ZZ.
$$
The map $d_2:\ZZ\to\ZZ^2$ is injective and has image generated by $2b$, so
$$
H_2(K)=\ker d_2=0
$$
and
$$
H_1(K)
=\frac{\ZZ a\oplus\ZZ b}{\langle2b\rangle}
\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2.
$$
There are no cells above dimension $2$, so all higher homology vanishes.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Directly with coefficients $A=\ZZ/(2n)$, one gets
$$
H_0(K;A)\cong A,
\qquad
H_1(K;A)\cong A\oplus\ZZ/2,
\qquad
H_2(K;A)\cong\ZZ/2,
$$
and $H_i(K;A)=0$ for $i>2$.

::: pf-proof

Tensoring the cellular chain complex with $A$ gives
$$
0\longrightarrow A
\xrightarrow{\bar d_2}
A a\oplus A b
\xrightarrow{0}
A
\longrightarrow0,
$$
with
$$
\bar d_2(r)=2r\,b.
$$
Therefore
$$
H_0(K;A)=A.
$$
Moreover
$$
\ker\bar d_2
=\{r\in A:2r=0\}.
$$
In $A=\ZZ/(2n)$, this kernel is $\{0,n\}$, hence isomorphic to $\ZZ/2$. Thus
$$
H_2(K;A)\cong\ZZ/2.
$$
Finally,
$$
H_1(K;A)
=\frac{Aa\oplus Ab}{\langle2b\rangle}
\cong A\oplus A/2A.
$$
Since $2n$ is even,
$$
A/2A\cong\ZZ/2,
$$
so
$$
H_1(K;A)\cong A\oplus\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The universal coefficient theorem gives the same answer.

::: pf-proof

For every $q$, the homology universal coefficient theorem gives a split short exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow H_q(K;\ZZ)\otimes A
\longrightarrow H_q(K;A)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\ZZ}(H_{q-1}(K;\ZZ),A)
\longrightarrow0.
$$

For $q=0$,
$$
H_0(K;A)\cong\ZZ\otimes A\cong A.
$$

For $q=1$, the Tor term vanishes because $H_0(K;\ZZ)=\ZZ$ is free.
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_1(K;A)
&\cong(\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2)\otimes A\\
&\cong A\oplus((\ZZ/2)\otimes A)\\
&\cong A\oplus A/2A\\
&\cong A\oplus\ZZ/2.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $q=2$, $H_2(K;\ZZ)=0$, so
$$
H_2(K;A)
\cong
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\ZZ}(\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2,A)
\cong
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\ZZ}(\ZZ/2,A).
$$
Using the free resolution
$$
0\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{\times2}\ZZ\to\ZZ/2\to0,
$$
one gets
$$
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\ZZ}(\ZZ/2,A)
=\ker(\times2:A\to A)
\cong\ZZ/2.
$$
For $q>2$, both UCT terms vanish.

Thus UCT reproduces exactly the direct computation.
