::: {.problem}
Let \(K\) be the Klein bottle. Compute \(H_*(K;\mathbb Z)\). Then compute
\[
H_*(K;\mathbb Z/(2n))
\]
in two ways:

(i) directly;

(ii) using the universal coefficient theorem for homology.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
\[
A=\mathbb Z/(2n).
\]
Use the standard CW structure on the Klein bottle with one \(0\)-cell, two \(1\)-cells \(a,b\), and one \(2\)-cell attached by the word
\[
aba^{-1}b.
\]
With the corresponding cellular bases, the integral cellular chain complex is
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{d_2}
\mathbb Z a\oplus\mathbb Z b
\xrightarrow{d_1}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0,
\]
where
\[
d_1=0,
\qquad
d_2(1)=2b.
\]

<1>1. The integral homology is
\[
H_0(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2,
\qquad
H_i(K;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(i\ge2).
\]
::: {.proof}
Since \(d_1=0\),
\[
H_0(K)=\mathbb Z.
\]
The map \(d_2:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z^2\) is injective and has image generated by \(2b\), so
\[
H_2(K)=\ker d_2=0
\]
and
\[
H_1(K)
=\frac{\mathbb Z a\oplus\mathbb Z b}{\langle2b\rangle}
\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\]
There are no cells above dimension \(2\), so all higher homology vanishes.
:::

<1>2. Directly with coefficients \(A=\mathbb Z/(2n)\), one gets
\[
H_0(K;A)\cong A,
\qquad
H_1(K;A)\cong A\oplus\mathbb Z/2,
\qquad
H_2(K;A)\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\]
and \(H_i(K;A)=0\) for \(i>2\).
::: {.proof}
Tensoring the cellular chain complex with \(A\) gives
\[
0\longrightarrow A
\xrightarrow{\bar d_2}
A a\oplus A b
\xrightarrow{0}
A
\longrightarrow0,
\]
with
\[
\bar d_2(r)=2r\,b.
\]
Therefore
\[
H_0(K;A)=A.
\]
Moreover
\[
\ker\bar d_2
=\{r\in A:2r=0\}.
\]
In \(A=\mathbb Z/(2n)\), this kernel is \(\{0,n\}\), hence isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z/2\). Thus
\[
H_2(K;A)\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Finally,
\[
H_1(K;A)
=\frac{Aa\oplus Ab}{\langle2b\rangle}
\cong A\oplus A/2A.
\]
Since \(2n\) is even,
\[
A/2A\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\]
so
\[
H_1(K;A)\cong A\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\]
:::

<1>3. The universal coefficient theorem gives the same answer.
::: {.proof}
For every \(q\), the homology universal coefficient theorem gives a split short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow H_q(K;\mathbb Z)\otimes A
\longrightarrow H_q(K;A)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(H_{q-1}(K;\mathbb Z),A)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

For \(q=0\),
\[
H_0(K;A)\cong\mathbb Z\otimes A\cong A.
\]

For \(q=1\), the Tor term vanishes because \(H_0(K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\) is free. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_1(K;A)
&\cong(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2)\otimes A\\
&\cong A\oplus((\mathbb Z/2)\otimes A)\\
&\cong A\oplus A/2A\\
&\cong A\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(q=2\), \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)=0\), so
\[
H_2(K;A)
\cong
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2,A)
\cong
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/2,A).
\]
Using the free resolution
\[
0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times2}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2\to0,
\]
one gets
\[
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/2,A)
=\ker(\times2:A\to A)
\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
For \(q>2\), both UCT terms vanish.

Thus UCT reproduces exactly the direct computation.
:::
