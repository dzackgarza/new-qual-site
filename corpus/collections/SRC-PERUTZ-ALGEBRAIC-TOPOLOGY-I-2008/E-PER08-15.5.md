::: {.problem}
If \(p\) is odd, then a \(\mathbb Z/p\)-orientation determines, and is determined by, a \(\mathbb Z\)-orientation.
:::

::: {.solution}
Taken literally with Definitions 15.2--15.3 of the notes, the statement is false for \(p>3\).  The correct statement is that, for odd \(p\), a manifold is \(\mathbb Z/p\)-orientable if and only if it is \(\mathbb Z\)-orientable.

<1>1. The literal claimed bijection fails already for \(M=\mathbb R^n\) and \(p=5\).
::: {.proof}
Fix the standard integral orientation
\[
\eta:H_n(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb Z.
\]
Reduction modulo \(5\) gives a \(\mathbb F_5\)-orientation \(\bar\eta\).  But
\[
2\bar\eta:
H_n(\mathbb R^n,\mathbb R^n\setminus\{x\};\mathbb F_5)
\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb F_5
\]
is also an \(\mathbb F_5\)-orientation.

There are only two integral local orientations, \(\eta\) and \(-\eta\), whose reductions are \(\bar\eta\) and \(-\bar\eta=4\bar\eta\).  Thus \(2\bar\eta\) is not the reduction of either integral orientation.  More generally, for \(p>3\) there are \(p-1\) local \(\mathbb F_p\)-orientations but only two integral ones.
:::

<1>2. Every integral orientation induces a \(\mathbb Z/p\)-orientation.
::: {.proof}
For every \(x\in M\), local homology is free of rank one:
\[
H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Changing coefficients gives
\[
H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z/p)
\cong
H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb Z/p
\cong\mathbb Z/p.
\]
A coherent integral choice of generator therefore reduces to a coherent nonzero generator modulo \(p\), hence to a \(\mathbb Z/p\)-orientation.
:::

<1>3. If \(p\) is odd, every \(\mathbb Z/p\)-orientable manifold is integrally orientable.
::: {.proof}
It suffices to work on each connected component.  Fix a basepoint \(x\).  Parallel transport in the integral local-homology system
\[
H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
gives the orientation character
\[
w_1:\pi_1(M,x)\longrightarrow\{\pm1\}.
\]
After reducing coefficients modulo \(p\), the same loop acts on the one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_p\)-local homology by multiplication by the image of \(w_1(\gamma)\), namely \(+1\) or \(-1\) in \(\mathbb F_p\).

Assume a \(\mathbb Z/p\)-orientation exists.  Its value at \(x\) selects a nonzero element
\[
v\in H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb F_p),
\]
and because the orientation is a global section, every loop must fix \(v\).  If some loop had \(w_1(\gamma)=-1\), then transport would give
\[
v=-v.
\]
Since \(p\) is odd, \(2\neq0\) in \(\mathbb F_p\), so the only vector satisfying \(v=-v\) is \(v=0\), contradicting the choice of a generator.  Therefore \(w_1\) is trivial.

Triviality of the orientation character is equivalent to triviality of the integral orientation double cover, hence to integral orientability.
:::

Thus the valid conclusion for every odd prime \(p\) is
\[
M\text{ is }\mathbb Z\text{-orientable}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M\text{ is }\mathbb Z/p\text{-orientable}.
\]
For \(p=3\) the two local \(\mathbb F_3\)-orientations happen to be exactly the reductions of the two integral orientations; for \(p>3\), there is no literal bijection of orientation choices as printed.
:::
