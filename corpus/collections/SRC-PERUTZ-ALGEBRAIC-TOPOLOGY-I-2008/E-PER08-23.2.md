::: {.problem}
(a) Suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are compact, connected, orientable $n$-manifolds.
Describe the cohomology ring of their connected sum $X\#Y$.

(b) Prove that $\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ and $\mathbb{CP}^2\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}$ are not homotopy equivalent, where $\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}$ denotes the same manifold with the opposite orientation.
:::

::: {.solution}
For compact connected oriented $n$-manifolds $X,Y$, the positive-degree cohomology of $X\#Y$ below degree $n$ is the direct sum of that of the two summands, and the top classes are identified according to the connected-sum orientation.

<1>1. Additive groups and products.
::: {.proof}
Removing an open $n$-ball from each manifold and gluing the boundary spheres gives Mayer--Vietoris isomorphisms
\[
H^k(X\#Y)\cong H^k(X)\oplus H^k(Y)
\qquad(0<k<n),
\]
while $H^0\cong H^n\cong\mathbb Z$.
Classes supported on different punctured summands have zero cup product.
Products of classes coming from one summand agree with the original product until top degree; in degree $n$, the two oriented top classes map to the single fundamental cohomology class of $X\#Y$.
Equivalently, the Poincaré pairing of $X\#Y$ is the orthogonal direct sum of those of $X$ and $Y$.
:::

<1>2. $\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ and $\mathbb{CP}^2\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}$ are not homotopy equivalent.
::: {.proof}
Let $a,b$ be the two degree-$2$ generators.
For $\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2$, after choosing the connected-sum orientation,
\[
a^2=\omega,\qquad b^2=\omega,\qquad ab=0,
\]
so the intersection form is $\operatorname{diag}(1,1)$.
For $\mathbb{CP}^2\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}$,
\[
a^2=\omega,\qquad b^2=-\omega,\qquad ab=0,
\]
so the form is $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$.

These integral forms are not isomorphic: the second represents $0$ nontrivially, since $(1,1)$ has square $0$, whereas the positive definite form $x^2+y^2$ does not.
A homotopy equivalence would induce an isomorphism of cohomology rings and hence of these pairings.
Therefore no such homotopy equivalence exists.
:::
