::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a compact, connected, oriented $3$-manifold.
Determine the graded ring $H^*(M;\mathbb Z)$ when (i) $\pi_1(M)$ is finite; (ii) $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$; (iii) $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2$.
Exhibit two such $3$-manifolds with non-isomorphic cohomology rings, both of which have $H_1\cong\mathbb Z^3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $M$ be a compact connected oriented $3$-manifold and let $\omega\in H^3(M;\mathbb Z)$ denote the orientation class.
Poincaré duality and UCT determine the ring in the stated cases.

<1>1. If $\pi_1(M)$ is finite.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
A=H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=\pi_1(M)^{\rm ab},
\]
which is finite.
Then $H^1(M)=\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mathbb Z)=0$.
Poincaré duality gives $H^2(M)\cong H_1(M)\cong A$, and $H^0\cong H^3\cong\mathbb Z$.
Every product of two positive-degree classes is zero for degree reasons.
Thus the graded ring is
\[
H^*(M)=\mathbb Z\oplus A[2]\oplus\mathbb Z\omega[3]
\]
with square-zero positive-degree ideal apart from multiplication by the unit.
:::

<1>2. If $H_1(M)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
Then
\[
H^1\cong\mathbb Z\langle a\rangle,\qquad
H^2\cong\mathbb Z\langle b\rangle,\qquad
H^3\cong\mathbb Z\langle\omega\rangle.
\]
Poincaré duality lets us choose $a,b$ so that $a\smile b=\omega$.
Graded commutativity gives $2a^2=0$, and $H^2$ is torsion-free, so $a^2=0$.
All other positive-degree products vanish by dimension.
This determines the ring.
:::

<1>3. If $H_1(M)\cong\mathbb Z^2$.
::: {.proof}
Choose bases $a_1,a_2$ of $H^1\cong\mathbb Z^2$ and $b_1,b_2$ of $H^2\cong\mathbb Z^2$ dual under Poincaré pairing, so
\[
a_i\smile b_j=\delta_{ij}\omega.
\]
Every product $a_i\smile a_j$ is zero.
Indeed its pairing with any $a_k$ is a triple product in $\Lambda^3H^1$, which vanishes because $H^1$ has rank $2$; nondegeneracy of $H^2\times H^1\to H^3$ then forces $a_i a_j=0$.
Degree reasons kill the remaining positive-degree products.
Hence these relations determine the ring.
:::

<1>4. The case $H_1\cong\mathbb Z^3$ is not determined by the group alone.
::: {.proof}
Take
\[
M_1=T^3,
\qquad
M_2=\#^3(S^1\times S^2).
\]
Both have $H_1\cong\mathbb Z^3$.
For $T^3$,
\[
H^*(T^3)\cong\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}(a_1,a_2,a_3)
\]
and $a_1a_2a_3=\omega\ne0$.
In the connected sum $M_2$, the degree-$1$ classes can be represented on separate summands and all products of two degree-$1$ classes vanish.
Hence the cohomology rings are not isomorphic.
:::
:::
