::: {.problem}
Use singular cohomology to compute $H^*(S^1\times S^1)$ as a ring.
[You may find it helpful to think of $S^1\times S^1$ as a $\Delta$-complex.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Build $T = S^1 \times S^1$ from the unit square with opposite sides identified.
The $\Delta$-complex has one vertex $v$; edges $x$ (horizontal), $y$ (vertical) and $z$ (the diagonal from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$); and two triangles
$$
U = [(0,0), (1,0), (1,1)], \qquad L = [(0,0), (0,1), (1,1)].
$$
The edges of $U$ are $[v_0 v_1] = x$, $[v_1 v_2] = y$, $[v_0 v_2] = z$, and the edges of $L$ are $[v_0 v_1] = y$, $[v_1 v_2] = x$, $[v_0 v_2] = z$.
Simplicial cohomology of this $\Delta$-complex computes singular cohomology, compatibly with cup products.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #groups}
$H_0(T) = \ZZ$, $H_1(T) = \ZZ x \oplus \ZZ y$, $H_2(T) = \ZZ [U - L]$, and $H^0 = \ZZ$, $H^1 \cong \ZZ^2$, $H^2 \cong \ZZ$, with $H^2 \to \ZZ$, $\varphi \mapsto \langle \varphi, U - L \rangle$, an isomorphism.

::: pf-proof
The boundaries are $\partial x = \partial y = \partial z = 0$ and $\partial U = \partial L = x + y - z$.
So $\ker \partial_2 = \ZZ(U - L)$, and $H_1 = \ZZ\theset{x, y, z} / \ZZ(x + y - z) \cong \ZZ x \oplus \ZZ y$.
The homology groups are free, so the universal coefficient theorem gives $H^k \cong \Hom(H_k, \ZZ)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cocycles}
The cochains $\alpha$, $\beta$ with $\alpha(x) = 1$, $\alpha(y) = 0$, $\alpha(z) = 1$ and $\beta(x) = 0$, $\beta(y) = 1$, $\beta(z) = 1$ are cocycles, and their classes $a$, $b$ form a basis of $H^1(T)$.

::: pf-proof
$\delta\alpha(U) = \delta\alpha(L) = \alpha(x) + \alpha(y) - \alpha(z) = 0$, and likewise for $\beta$.
On the basis $x$, $y$ of $H_1$, the classes $a$ and $b$ are the dual basis, so by step [](#groups){.pf-ref} they form a basis of $H^1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cups}
$\langle a \smile b, U - L \rangle = 1$, $\langle b \smile a, U - L \rangle = -1$, and $a \smile a = b \smile b = 0$.

::: pf-proof
On a $2$-simplex $\sigma$, $(\varphi \smile \psi)(\sigma) = \varphi([v_0 v_1])\, \psi([v_1 v_2])$.
So $(\alpha \smile \beta)(U) = \alpha(x)\beta(y) = 1$ and $(\alpha \smile \beta)(L) = \alpha(y)\beta(x) = 0$.
Also $(\beta \smile \alpha)(U) = \beta(x)\alpha(y) = 0$ and $(\beta \smile \alpha)(L) = \beta(y)\alpha(x) = 1$.
Finally $(\alpha \smile \alpha)(U) = \alpha(x)\alpha(y) = 0$ and $(\alpha \smile \alpha)(L) = \alpha(y)\alpha(x) = 0$, so $\alpha \smile \alpha = 0$; the same computation gives $\beta \smile \beta = 0$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$H^*(S^1 \times S^1; \ZZ) \cong \boxed{\Lambda_{\ZZ}[a, b], \quad \abs{a} = \abs{b} = 1}$: the groups are $\ZZ$, $\ZZ a \oplus \ZZ b$, $\ZZ(a \smile b)$ in degrees $0, 1, 2$, with $a^2 = b^2 = 0$ and $b \smile a = -a \smile b$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#groups){.pf-ref} and [](#cups){.pf-ref}, $a \smile b$ pairs to $1$ with the generator of $H_2$, so it generates $H^2 \cong \ZZ$.
Step [](#cups){.pf-ref} also gives the relations, and $H^k = 0$ for $k > 2$.
:::
