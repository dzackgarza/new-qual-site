::: {.problem}
Take a polynomial ring $\ZZ[u]$, graded by declaring $u$ to have degree $2$, and truncate it to the graded ring $\ZZ[u]/(u^{n+1})$.

Theorem. There is an isomorphism of graded rings
$$
H^*(\CP^n)\cong\ZZ[u]/(u^{n+1}),\qquad\deg u=2.
$$

Specify a suitable cohomology class $u$.
Describe $u^k$ for all $k$.
(Part of the exercise is to work out what format the answer should sensibly take.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Orient $\CP^k$ by its complex structure, and let $\CP^k \subseteq \CP^n$ be a linear subspace for $0 \leq k \leq n$.
Cellular homology with one cell in each even dimension gives $H_{2k}(\CP^n) = \ZZ[\CP^k]$ for $0 \leq k \leq n$, and $H_j(\CP^n) = 0$ otherwise.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #choose-u}
Take $u \in H^2(\CP^n)$ to be the class with $\langle u, [\CP^1] \rangle = 1$.
Equivalently, $u$ is the Poincaré dual of a hyperplane $[\CP^{n-1}]$, or $u = c_1(\mathcal O(1))$.

::: pf-proof
By the universal coefficient theorem, $H^2(\CP^n) \cong \Hom(H_2(\CP^n), \ZZ) \cong \ZZ$, because $H_1(\CP^n) = 0$.
So the condition $\langle u, [\CP^1] \rangle = 1$ fixes a generator.
A hyperplane meets a line $\CP^1$ transversally in one point with positive sign, so its Poincaré dual satisfies the same condition.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
For $0 \leq k \leq n$, the power $u^k$ is the generator of $H^{2k}(\CP^n) \cong \ZZ$ with $\langle u^k, [\CP^k] \rangle = 1$; it is the Poincaré dual of a linear subspace $[\CP^{n-k}]$.
For $k > n$, $u^k = 0$.

::: pf-proof
The cup product of Poincaré duals of transverse oriented submanifolds is the Poincaré dual of their intersection.
By step [](#choose-u){.pf-ref}, $u^k$ is dual to the intersection of $k$ hyperplanes in general position, which is a linear $\CP^{n-k}$, transverse with positive sign because all orientations are complex.
A linear $\CP^{n-k}$ and a linear $\CP^k$ in general position meet transversally in one point with positive sign, so $\langle u^k, [\CP^k] \rangle = 1$.
Since $H^{2k}(\CP^n) \cong \Hom(H_{2k}(\CP^n), \ZZ) = \Hom(\ZZ[\CP^k], \ZZ)$, this makes $u^k$ a generator.
For $k > n$, $H^{2k}(\CP^n) = 0$.
:::
