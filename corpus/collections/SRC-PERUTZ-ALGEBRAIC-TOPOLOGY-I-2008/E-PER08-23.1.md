::: {.problem}
Take a polynomial ring $\mathbb{Z}[u]$, graded by declaring $u$ to have degree $2$, and truncate it to the graded ring $\mathbb{Z}[u]/(u^{n+1})$.

Theorem. There is an isomorphism of graded rings
\[
H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n)\cong\mathbb{Z}[u]/(u^{n+1}),\qquad\deg u=2.
\]

Specify a suitable cohomology class $u$.
Describe $u^k$ for all $k$.
(Part of the exercise is to work out what format the answer should sensibly take.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Take $u\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)$ to be the positive generator, equivalently the hyperplane class
\[
u=c_1(\mathcal O(1)).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z[u]/(u^{n+1}),\qquad |u|=2.}
\]
Thus, for $0\le k\le n$, the power
\[
u^k\in H^{2k}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)
\]
is a generator, while $u^k=0$ for $k>n$ for dimensional reasons.
With the complex orientation chosen so that
\[
\langle u^n,[\mathbb{CP}^n]\rangle=1,
\]
these generators are fixed without a sign ambiguity.

One can characterize $u$ inductively by requiring that its restriction under the standard inclusion $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^n$ be the corresponding generator.
Poincaré duality then forces $u^n$ to generate top cohomology, exactly as in Theorem 23.1.
:::
