::: {.problem}
Describe an $n$-cycle
\[
c_n\in S_n(S^n)
\]
that generates $H_n(S^n;\mathbb Z)$. Prove that your cycle generates by induction, using the explicit form of the Mayer--Vietoris connecting map.
:::

::: {.solution}
View $S^n$ as the suspension of its equatorial sphere $S^{n-1}$, with north and south suspension points $N$ and $S$.

For a singular chain $a$ on $S^{n-1}$, write $N*a$ and $S*a$ for its singular cones to the north and south poles. The cone identity is
\[
\partial(v*a)+v*(\partial a)=a,
\qquad v\in\{N,S\}.
\]

<1>1. Start in dimension zero with a generator of reduced homology.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
S^0=\{p,q\}
\]
and define
\[
c_0=[p]-[q]\in S_0(S^0).
\]
Then $c_0$ is a cycle and its class generates
\[
\widetilde H_0(S^0;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>2. Define the higher cycles recursively by
\[
c_n=N*c_{n-1}-S*c_{n-1}
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
::: {.proof}
Assume $c_{n-1}$ is a cycle. The cone identity gives
\[
\partial(N*c_{n-1})=c_{n-1},
\qquad
\partial(S*c_{n-1})=c_{n-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\partial c_n
=c_{n-1}-c_{n-1}
=0.
\]
Thus $c_n$ is an $n$-cycle on $S^n$.
:::

<1>3. Under the Mayer--Vietoris connecting map, $[c_n]$ maps to $[c_{n-1}]$.
::: {.proof}
Use the usual Mayer--Vietoris decomposition of $S^n$ into two contractible neighbourhoods of the northern and southern hemispheres, whose intersection deformation retracts onto the equator $S^{n-1}$.

In the chain-level formula for the connecting homomorphism, write
\[
c_n=x+y
\]
with
\[
x=N*c_{n-1},
\qquad
y=-S*c_{n-1}.
\]
Then
\[
\partial x=c_{n-1},
\qquad
\partial y=-c_{n-1}.
\]
Hence the explicit connecting-map formula gives
\[
\delta_n[c_n]=[c_{n-1}].
\]
Equivalently, in reduced homology,
\[
\delta_n:\widetilde H_n(S^n)\xrightarrow{\sim}\widetilde H_{n-1}(S^{n-1})
\]
sends $[c_n]$ to $[c_{n-1}]$.
:::

<1>4. The class $[c_n]$ generates $H_n(S^n;\mathbb Z)$ for every $n\ge1$.
::: {.proof}
Proceed by induction on $n$. By <1>1, $[c_0]$ generates $\widetilde H_0(S^0)$. The preceding Mayer--Vietoris computation shows that every
\[
\delta_n:\widetilde H_n(S^n)\longrightarrow\widetilde H_{n-1}(S^{n-1})
\]
is an isomorphism. By <1>3,
\[
\delta_n[c_n]=[c_{n-1}].
\]
Therefore, if $[c_{n-1}]$ generates the target, then $[c_n]$ generates the source. Induction gives that $[c_n]$ generates $\widetilde H_n(S^n)$ for all $n\ge1$.

Since reduced and ordinary homology agree in positive degrees,
\[
H_n(S^n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[c_n].
\]
:::
