::: {.problem}
Let a group \(G\) act freely on \(S^n\), with \(n\ge2\), so that
\[
S^n\longrightarrow S^n/G
\]
is the universal covering and \(\pi_1(S^n/G)\cong G\). Show that the orientation character of the quotient is
\[
w_1:\pi_1(S^n/G)\cong G\longrightarrow\{\pm1\}\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\qquad
w_1(g)=\deg(g:S^n\to S^n).
\]
For which \(n\ge1\) is \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) orientable?
:::

::: {.solution}
Give \(S^n\) its standard orientation.

<1>1. A deck transformation \(g:S^n\to S^n\) either preserves every local orientation or reverses every local orientation, and the sign is \(\deg g\).
::: {.proof}
For each \(y\in S^n\), the chosen global orientation determines a generator
\[
[o_y]\in H_n(S^n,S^n\setminus\{y\};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The deck transformation induces
\[
g_*:H_n(S^n,S^n\setminus\{y\})
\longrightarrow
H_n(S^n,S^n\setminus\{g(y)\}).
\]
Because \(S^n\) is connected, the sign with which \(g_*\) sends the chosen local generator to the chosen local generator is locally constant in \(y\), hence constant.

The same sign is the action of \(g_*\) on the global fundamental class \([S^n]\in H_n(S^n;\mathbb Z)\): its image in every local homology group is the corresponding local generator.
By definition,
\[
g_*[S^n]=(\deg g)[S^n].
\]
Thus \(g\) acts on every local orientation by the sign \(\deg g\in\{\pm1\}\).
:::

<1>2. Under \(\pi_1(S^n/G)\cong G\), the orientation character sends \(g\) to \(\deg g\).
::: {.proof}
Fix \(x\in S^n/G\) and a lift \(\widetilde x\in S^n\).  A loop \(\gamma\) based at \(x\) representing the element \(g\in G\) lifts to a path beginning at \(\widetilde x\) and ending at \(g\widetilde x\).

Transporting a local orientation of the quotient around \(\gamma\) is computed upstairs by transporting the chosen local orientation from \(\widetilde x\) to \(g\widetilde x\) and then comparing the endpoints using the deck transformation \(g\).  By <1>1 this comparison multiplies the local generator by \(\deg g\).  Hence
\[
w_1(\gamma)=\deg g.
\]
This proves the asserted formula for the orientation character.
:::

<1>3. The antipodal map on \(S^n\) has degree \((-1)^{n+1}\).
::: {.proof}
The antipodal map is the restriction to \(S^n\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}\) of the linear map
\[
-I_{n+1}:\mathbb R^{n+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{n+1}.
\]
Its determinant is
\[
\det(-I_{n+1})=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
For an invertible linear map of \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\), the induced map on the oriented boundary sphere has degree equal to the sign of its determinant.
Therefore
\[
\deg(a)=(-1)^{n+1}
\]
for the antipodal map \(a(x)=-x\).
:::

<1>4. For \(n\ge1\), \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) is orientable exactly when \(n\) is odd.
::: {.proof}
For \(n\ge2\), the covering
\[
S^n\longrightarrow\mathbb{RP}^n
\]
has deck group \(G=\{1,a\}\cong\mathbb Z/2\).  By <1>2 its orientation character is trivial exactly when
\[
\deg a=+1.
\]
By <1>3 this is equivalent to
\[
(-1)^{n+1}=1,
\]
which holds exactly when \(n\) is odd.
Triviality of the orientation character is equivalent to orientability.

For \(n=1\), \(\mathbb{RP}^1\cong S^1\), so it is orientable as well, agreeing with the same odd-dimensional criterion.
:::
