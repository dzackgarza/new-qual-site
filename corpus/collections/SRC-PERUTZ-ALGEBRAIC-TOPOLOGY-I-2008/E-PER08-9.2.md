::: {.problem}
A collection C of Z-modules is called a Serre class if for every short exact sequence 0 $\\to$ A $\\to$ B $\\to$ C $\\to$ 0 such that two out of the three Z-modules A, B, C are in C, the third is in C also.
Fix a prime p $\\in$ Z. Identify which of the following properties of Z-modules M define Serre classes: (a) M is torsion; (b) M is torsion-free; (c) M is torsion but has no p-torsion; (d) every element of M has p-power order; (e); every element of M is divisible by p; (f) every M is finitely generated.
(*) What if we replace M by an arbitrary commutative ring R (and p by a prime of R?).
:::

::: {.solution}
For $\mathbb Z$-modules, the answers are
\[
\boxed{(a)\ \text{yes},\quad (b)\ \text{no},\quad (c)\ \text{yes},\quad (d)\ \text{yes},\quad (e)\ \text{no},\quad (f)\ \text{yes}.}
\]

<1>1. (a), (c), and (d) are Serre classes.
::: {.proof}
In a short exact sequence $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$, torsion is preserved under submodules, quotients, and extensions: if $aA$ and the image of $b\in B$ in $C$ are killed by nonzero integers, their product kills $b$.

For (c), if multiplication by $p$ kills a nonzero element in one term, exactness transports such $p$-primary torsion to one of the other terms; equivalently localizing at $p$ shows that having no $p$-torsion is extension-stable inside torsion groups.

For (d), if the image of $b\in B$ is killed by $p^r$ and the resulting element of $A$ is killed by $p^s$, then $p^{r+s}b=0$.
Submodules and quotients are immediate.
Thus $p$-primary torsion modules form a Serre class.
:::

<1>2. (b) is not a Serre class.
::: {.proof}
The exact sequence
\[
0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times n}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/n\to0
\]
has its first two terms torsion-free and its quotient torsion.
:::

<1>3. (e) is not a Serre class.
::: {.proof}
In
\[
0\to\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z[1/p]\to\mathbb Z[1/p]/\mathbb Z\to0,
\]
the middle and right terms are divisible by $p$, while $\mathbb Z$ is not.
:::

<1>4. (f) is a Serre class over $\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
Subgroups and quotients of finitely generated abelian groups are finitely generated, and an extension of two finitely generated modules is finitely generated by lifting generators of the quotient and adjoining generators of the submodule.
:::

For a general commutative ring $R$, the finite-generation condition is a Serre condition precisely when the needed submodule finiteness holds, in particular for Noetherian $R$.
Analogous torsion statements require a specified notion of torsion (for example with respect to a multiplicative set or a prime ideal); the corresponding subcategory is Serre when it is closed under subobjects, quotients, and extensions by the same exact-sequence argument.
:::
