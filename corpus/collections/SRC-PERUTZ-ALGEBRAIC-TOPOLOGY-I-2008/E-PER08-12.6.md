::: {.problem}
Use Mayer--Vietoris to compute
\[
H_*(T^2),\qquad H_*(\mathbb{RP}^2),\qquad H_*(K^2),
\]
where $K^2$ is the Klein bottle.
In each case, find an explicit minimal collection of cycles that generates the homology.
:::

::: {.solution}
We use the same Mayer--Vietoris setup for all three surfaces.
Remove the interior of a small embedded disk from the surface and call the resulting surface with boundary $A$.
Let $B$ be a slightly larger open disk containing the removed disk.
Then
\[
X=A\cup B,
\qquad
A\cap B\simeq S^1,
\qquad
B\simeq *.
\]
The map
\[
H_0(A\cap B)\longrightarrow H_0(A)\oplus H_0(B)
\]
is injective because all three spaces are connected.
Hence the degree-$2$ and degree-$1$ part of Mayer--Vietoris reduces to
\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(X)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_1(S^1)
\xrightarrow{j_*}
H_1(A)
\longrightarrow H_1(X)
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus
\[
H_2(X)\cong\ker j_*,
\qquad
H_1(X)\cong\operatorname{coker}j_*.
\]
The only input that changes from surface to surface is the homology class of the boundary circle in $A$.

<1>1. For the torus,
\[
H_0(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
H_2(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
and all higher homology vanishes.
::: {.proof}
Let $A$ be a once-punctured torus.
It deformation retracts onto a wedge of two circles with oriented loops $a$ and $b$, so
\[
H_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z[a]\oplus\mathbb Z[b].
\]
In the standard square model of the torus, the oriented boundary of the punctured fundamental polygon represents the word
\[
aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]
After abelianization its homology class is
\[
[a]+[b]-[a]-[b]=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
j_*:H_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z\longrightarrow H_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z^2
\]
is the zero map.
The Mayer--Vietoris exact sequence gives
\[
H_2(T^2)\cong\ker j_*\cong\mathbb Z
\]
and
\[
H_1(T^2)\cong\operatorname{coker}j_*\cong\mathbb Z[a]\oplus\mathbb Z[b].
\]
Since $T^2$ is connected,
\[
H_0(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
and the same Mayer--Vietoris sequence gives zero in degrees greater than $2$.

A minimal set of explicit cycles is:

- one point $p$ for $H_0$;

- the two coordinate loops $a$ and $b$ for $H_1$;

- an oriented fundamental $2$-cycle $\tau$ for $H_2$, obtained for example by triangulating the standard square and passing the sum of its oriented triangles to the quotient.

Their classes generate the nonzero homology groups.
:::

<1>2. For the real projective plane,
\[
H_0(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\qquad
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^2)=0\quad(k\ge2).
\]
::: {.proof}
Removing an open disk from $\mathbb{RP}^2$ leaves a Möbius band $A$.
Let $c$ denote its core circle.
Then
\[
A\simeq S^1,
\qquad
H_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z[c].
\]
The boundary circle of a Möbius band runs twice around the core.
Consequently the inclusion of the boundary induces
\[
j_*:\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
\qquad
1\longmapsto2[c].
\]
Thus $j_*$ is injective, so
\[
H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2)=0,
\]
while
\[
H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}(\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times2}\mathbb Z)
\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Connectedness gives $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$, and Mayer--Vietoris gives zero above degree $2$.

A minimal set of explicit cycles is one point $p$ for $H_0$ and the core loop $c$ for $H_1$; its class satisfies
\[
2[c]=0
\]
and generates the unique nonzero positive-degree homology group.
:::

<1>3. For the Klein bottle,
\[
H_0(K^2)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(K^2)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2,
\qquad
H_k(K^2)=0\quad(k\ge2).
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $A$ be the Klein bottle with an open disk removed.
It deformation retracts onto a wedge of two circles, represented by loops $a$ and $b$.
Thus
\[
H_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z[a]\oplus\mathbb Z[b].
\]
Using the standard fundamental polygon with relation
\[
aba^{-1}b=1,
\]
the oriented boundary of the punctured polygon represents the word
\[
aba^{-1}b.
\]
Its homology class is therefore
\[
[a]+[b]-[a]+[b]=2[b].
\]
Hence
\[
j_*:\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[a]\oplus\mathbb Z[b],
\qquad
1\longmapsto2[b].
\]
This map is injective, so
\[
H_2(K^2)=0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
H_1(K^2)
\cong
\frac{\mathbb Z[a]\oplus\mathbb Z[b]}{\langle2[b]\rangle}
\cong
\mathbb Z[a]\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)[b].
\]
Connectedness gives $H_0(K^2)\cong\mathbb Z$, and higher homology vanishes.

A minimal set of explicit cycles is one point $p$ for $H_0$ and the two loops $a,b$ for $H_1$.
The class $[a]$ generates the free summand and $[b]$ generates the order-$2$ summand.
There is no nonzero $2$-dimensional homology class.
:::
