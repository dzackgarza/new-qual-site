::: {.problem}
What is the smallest positive integer n such that there are three non isomorphic Abelian groups of order n? Name the three groups.
:::

::: {.solution}

The smallest such $n$ is $8$, with the three groups $\ZZ_{8}$,
$\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{4}$ and $\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #count}

For $n=\prod p_{i}^{a_{i}}$ the number of Abelian groups of order $n$ up to
isomorphism is $\prod p(a_{i})$, where $p(a)$ is the number of partitions of
$a$.

::: pf-proof

By the fundamental theorem of finite Abelian groups each such group is a direct
product of cyclic groups of prime-power order, uniquely up to isomorphism and
reordering. The factors of prime-power order $p_{i}^{m}$ with $m$ ranging over a
partition of $a_{i}$ give the Sylow $p_{i}$-subgroup, and the partitions of
$a_{i}$ are exactly the choices there; distinct partitions give non-isomorphic
$p_{i}$-groups because the multiset of exponents is an invariant. The primes are
handled independently since a finite Abelian group is the direct product of its
Sylow subgroups. Hence the count is the product of the partition numbers of the
exponents.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #three-at-8}

$n=8$ has exactly three, and they are pairwise non-isomorphic.

::: pf-proof

$8=2^{3}$ and $p(3)=3$, the partitions being $3$, $2+1$ and $1+1+1$, giving
$\ZZ_{8}$, $\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$ and $\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$. To
separate them by invariants: $\ZZ_{8}$ has an element of order $8$ and the other
two do not; $\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$ has an element of order $4$ and
$\ZZ_{2}^{3}$ does not, every nonidentity element of $\ZZ_{2}^{3}$ being of
order $2$. An isomorphism preserves element orders, so the three are distinct.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #none-below}

No $n<8$ has three non-isomorphic Abelian groups.

::: pf-proof

For $n=1$ there is one group. For $n\in\{2,3,5,7\}$ prime, the only group is
$\ZZ_{n}$. For $n=4=2^{2}$ the count is $p(2)=2$, realized by $\ZZ_{4}$ and
$\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$. For $n=6=2\cdot 3$ the count is $p(1)p(1)=1$, namely
$\ZZ_{6}$. So every $n<8$ has at most two Abelian groups up to isomorphism, and
$8$ is the smallest order with three.

:::
