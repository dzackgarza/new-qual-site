::: {.problem}
Find all the Abelian groups (up to isomorphism) of order 360.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own: the collection's provenance is a set of solution outlines. The factorization is $360=2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #classification}
A finite abelian group of order $\prod_pp^{e_p}$ is the direct sum of its Sylow $p$-subgroups, and the abelian groups of order $p^{e}$ correspond to the partitions of $e$, the partition $\lambda$ giving $\bigoplus_i\ZZ_{p^{\lambda_i}}$.

::: pf-proof
This is the fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups: every finite abelian group is a direct sum of cyclic groups of prime power order, and the multiset of those prime powers is determined by the isomorphism class. Collecting the summands whose order is a power of a fixed prime $p$ gives the Sylow $p$-subgroup, a direct summand, so the group is determined by giving, for each $p$, a partition $\lambda^{(p)}$ of $e_p$ read off the exponents of its cyclic summands. Consequently the number of isomorphism classes of order $\prod p^{e_p}$ is $\prod p(e_p)$, where $p(e)$ is the number of partitions of $e$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #partitions}
$p(3)=3$, $p(2)=2$, $p(1)=1$, so the number of abelian groups of order $360$ is $3\cdot2\cdot1=6$.

::: pf-proof
The partitions of $3$ are $3$, $2+1$, $1+1+1$; those of $2$ are $2$, $1+1$; that of $1$ is $1$. So step [](#classification){.pf-ref} gives $3\cdot2\cdot1=6$ isomorphism classes, since the choice of partition for each prime is independent.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #list}
The six groups are
$$
\ZZ_2^3\oplus\ZZ_3^2\oplus\ZZ_5,\quad \ZZ_2^3\oplus\ZZ_9\oplus\ZZ_5,\quad \ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_3^2\oplus\ZZ_5,\quad \ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_9\oplus\ZZ_5,\quad \ZZ_8\oplus\ZZ_3^2\oplus\ZZ_5,\quad \ZZ_8\oplus\ZZ_9\oplus\ZZ_5 .
$$

::: pf-proof
Match the partitions of step [](#partitions){.pf-ref}: the partition $1+1+1$ of $3$ gives $\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2$, the partition $2+1$ gives $\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_2$, and $3$ gives $\ZZ_8$; the partition $1+1$ of $2$ gives $\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_3$ and $2$ gives $\ZZ_9$; the partition $1$ of $1$ gives $\ZZ_5$. Taking every combination gives exactly the six displayed groups, each of order $2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5=360$, and by step [](#partitions){.pf-ref} there are no others.
:::
