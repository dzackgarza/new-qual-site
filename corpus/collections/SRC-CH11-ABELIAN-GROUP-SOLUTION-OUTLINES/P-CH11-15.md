::: {.problem}
How many Abelian groups (up to isomorphism) are there:

(a) of order 6?

(b) of order 15?

(c) of order 42?

(d) of order $pq$ where $p$ and $q$ are distinct primes?

(e) of order $pqr$ where $p$, $q$ and $r$ are distinct primes?

(f) Generalize parts (d) and (e).
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) 1

(b) 1

(c) 1

(d) 1

(e) 1

(f) There is a unique Abelian group of order n iff n is not divisible by the square of a prime.
:::
