::: {.problem}
How many Abelian groups (up to isomorphism) are there:

(a) of order 6?

(b) of order 15?

(c) of order 42?

(d) of order $pq$ where $p$ and $q$ are distinct primes?

(e) of order $pqr$ where $p$, $q$ and $r$ are distinct primes?

(f) Generalize parts (d) and (e).
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #count}

If $n=\prod p_{i}^{a_{i}}$ then the number of Abelian groups of order $n$ up to
isomorphism is $\prod p(a_{i})$, where $p(a)$ is the number of partitions of
$a$; in particular the count is $1$ exactly when every $a_{i}=1$.

::: pf-proof

By the fundamental theorem of finite Abelian groups every such group is a direct
product of cyclic groups of prime-power order, uniquely up to isomorphism and
reordering; the factors belonging to one prime $p_{i}$ form a partition of
$a_{i}$, and distinct partitions give non-isomorphic $p_{i}$-groups. Hence the
classes correspond to tuples of partitions of the exponents and there are
$\prod p(a_{i})$ of them. Since $p(1)=1$ and $p(a)\ge 2$ for $a\ge 2$ (the
partitions $(a)$ and $(1,1,\dots,1)$ are distinct), the product is $1$ exactly
when all $a_{i}=1$, that is exactly when $n$ is squarefree.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cyclic}

When $n$ is squarefree the one group is $\ZZ_{n}$.

::: pf-proof

If $n=p_{1}\cdots p_{k}$ with distinct primes, then
$\ZZ_{p_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\ZZ_{p_{k}}\cong\ZZ_{n}$ by the Chinese remainder
theorem, and each $\ZZ_{p_{i}}$ is the only group of order $p_{i}$. So $\ZZ_{n}$
is the unique Abelian group of order $n$ up to isomorphism. When $p^{2}\mid n$,
the two partitions of the exponent $2$ give $\ZZ_{p^{2}}$ and
$\ZZ_{p}\oplus\ZZ_{p}$, which are not isomorphic since the first has an element
of order $p^{2}$ and the second does not, so there are at least two.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #parts}

Hence the answers: (a) $1$, (b) $1$, (c) $1$, (d) $1$, (e) $1$, and (f) there is
exactly one Abelian group of order $n$ if and only if $n$ is not divisible by
the square of a prime.

::: pf-proof

The orders in (a), (b), (c) are $6=2\cdot 3$, $15=3\cdot 5$ and $42=2\cdot 3\cdot
7$, all squarefree, so each has exactly the cyclic group. Parts (d) and (e)
concern $pq$ and $pqr$ with distinct primes, again squarefree, so again one
group each, namely $\ZZ_{pq}$ and $\ZZ_{pqr}$. Part (f) is the criterion proved
in step [](#count){.pf-ref}, realized concretely in step
[](#cyclic){.pf-ref}.

:::
