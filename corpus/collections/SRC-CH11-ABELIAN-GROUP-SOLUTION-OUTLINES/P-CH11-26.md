::: {.problem}
Let $G = \{1, 7, 17, 23, 49, 55, 65, 71\}$ under multiplication modulo $96$. Express $G$ as an external and an internal direct product of cyclic groups.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the elements are sorted by order modulo $96$, which identifies the abelian type, and then two cyclic subgroups of coprime order with trivial intersection are exhibited.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #orders}

Modulo $96$: $o(1)=1$; $o(17)=o(65)=o(49)=2$; $o(7)=o(23)=o(55)=o(71)=4$; no element has order $8$.

::: pf-proof

Direct computation: $17^{2}=289=3\cdot96+1$, $65^{2}=4225=44\cdot96+1$, $49^{2}=2401=25\cdot96+1$, so these three have order $2$ and are not $1$. Also $7^{2}=49$, $7^{4}=49^{2}=1$ and $7^{2}\ne1$; $23^{2}=529=5\cdot96+49$, $23^{4}=49^{2}=1$; $55^{2}=3025=31\cdot96+49$, $55^{4}=1$; $71^{2}=5041=52\cdot96+49$, $71^{4}=1$. So these four have order exactly $4$. That accounts for all eight elements, and no element has order $8$, since the order of an element divides $\abs{G}=8$ and would otherwise be $8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #external}

$G\approx\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$.

::: pf-proof

A finite abelian group of order $8$ is one of $\ZZ_{8}$, $\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$, $\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$. Step [](#orders){.pf-ref} rules out the first (no element of order $8$) and the third (which has no element of order $4$). Hence $G$ is the external direct product $\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #internal}

$G=\langle7\rangle\times\langle17\rangle$ is an internal direct product.

::: pf-proof

$\langle7\rangle=\{1,7,49,55\}$ by step [](#orders){.pf-ref} and $\langle17\rangle=\{1,17\}$; their intersection is $\{1\}$ and $4\cdot2=8=\abs{G}$, so $G$ is the internal direct product of these two cyclic subgroups of coprime orders:
$$
\boxed{G\approx\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{2}=\langle7\rangle\times\langle17\rangle.}
$$
Every element of order $4$ squares to $49$, so the order-$2$ factor may be $\langle17\rangle$ or $\langle65\rangle$ but not $\langle49\rangle$, which lies inside $\langle7\rangle$.

:::
