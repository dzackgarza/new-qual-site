::: {.problem}
$|G| < \infty$
- Given: $G$ is characteristically simple
   - Show: $\exists H~(\text{simple})~ \ni G \cong H^n$.
   - Show: Whether or not the converse holds
- Given: $N \normal G$ minimal
   - Show: $N$ is characteristically simple, $N \cong H^n$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $G$ is characteristically simple, then $G \cong H^n$ for some simple group $H$.
<2>1. Let $H$ be a minimal normal subgroup of $G$.
Proof: $G$ is finite, so a minimal normal subgroup exists.
<2>2. $H$ is simple.
Proof: if $K \trianglelefteq H$ with $K \neq 1$, then $K$ is normal in $G$ (since $H$ is normal and $K$ is characteristic in $H$... more precisely, $K$ is normal in $G$ because $H$ is a minimal normal subgroup, so any normal subgroup of $H$ is normal in $G$); minimality of $H$ forces $K = H$.
<2>3. The subgroup generated by all $G$-conjugates of $H$ is a characteristic subgroup of $G$.
Proof: the set of conjugates of $H$ is permuted by every automorphism of $G$, so the subgroup they generate is characteristic.
<2>4. Since $G$ is characteristically simple, this subgroup is all of $G$.
Proof: it is a nontrivial characteristic subgroup, hence equals $G$.
<2>5. Hence $G$ is a direct product of conjugates of $H$, i.e. $G \cong H^n$.
Proof: distinct conjugates of a minimal normal subgroup intersect trivially and commute, so their product is direct; <2>4 says this product is all of $G$.

<1>2. The converse holds: if $H$ is simple, then $H^n$ is characteristically simple.
<2>1. The minimal normal subgroups of $H^n$ are exactly the $n$ factors $H$.
Proof: each factor is simple and normal, and any normal subgroup projects to a normal subgroup of each factor.
<2>2. Any automorphism of $H^n$ permutes these factors.
Proof: automorphisms preserve minimal normal subgroups.
<2>3. Hence the only characteristic subgroups of $H^n$ are $1$ and $H^n$.
Proof: a characteristic subgroup is a product of a subset of the factors that is invariant under all permutations of the factors; the only such subsets are $\varnothing$ and all $n$ factors.
<2>4. Therefore $H^n$ is characteristically simple.
Proof: <2>3.

<1>3. If $N \trianglelefteq G$ is minimal normal, then $N$ is characteristically simple and $N \cong H^n$ for a simple group $H$.
<2>1. $N$ is characteristically simple.
Proof: a characteristic subgroup of $N$ is normal in $G$ (since $N \trianglelefteq G$), so by minimality of $N$ it is either $1$ or $N$.
<2>2. Hence $N \cong H^n$ for a simple group $H$.
Proof: <1>1 applied to the characteristically simple group $N$.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1, <1>2, and <1>3.
:::
