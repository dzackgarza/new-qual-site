::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk and suppose
$$
\abs{f(z)}<1
\qquad(\abs z=1).
$$
Show that
$$
f(z)=z^3
$$
has exactly three solutions, counted with multiplicity, in $\abs z<1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
g(z)\coloneqq z^3-f(z).
$$

<1>1. The functions $g(z)$ and $z^3$ have the same number of zeros in $\abs z<1$, counted with multiplicity.

::: {.proof}
On the unit circle,
$$
\abs{g(z)-z^3}
=\abs{f(z)}
<1
=\abs{z^3}.
$$
Both functions are holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk. Rouché's theorem therefore implies that $g$ and $z^3$ have the same number of zeros in the open unit disk, counted with multiplicity.
:::

<1>2. The equation $f(z)=z^3$ has exactly $\boxed{3}$ solutions in $\abs z<1$, counted with multiplicity.

::: {.proof}
The function $z^3$ has one zero at $0$ of multiplicity $3$. By step <1>1, $g(z)=z^3-f(z)$ therefore has exactly three zeros in the unit disk, counted with multiplicity. These are precisely the solutions of $f(z)=z^3$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 is the required count.
:::
:::
