::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(x)=\frac14+x-x^2.
$$
For $x\in\RR$, define $x_0=x$ and
$$
x_{n+1}=f(x_n).
$$
If the sequence converges, write its limit as $x_\infty$.

1. For $x=0$, show that $(x_n)$ is bounded and nondecreasing and find its limit $\lambda$.
2. Determine all $y\in\RR$ for which $y_\infty=\lambda$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For an orbit $(x_n)$, set
$$
u_n\coloneqq\frac12-x_n.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #un-recurrence}
The recurrence for $(u_n)$ is
$$
u_{n+1}=u_n^2.
$$

::: pf-proof
Completing the square gives
$$
f(x)=\frac12-\left(x-\frac12\right)^2.
$$
Hence
$$
u_{n+1}
=\frac12-x_{n+1}
=\left(x_n-\frac12\right)^2
=u_n^2.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-one-answer}
For the initial value $x_0=0$,
$$
x_n=\frac12-2^{-2^n},
$$
so $(x_n)$ is bounded and strictly increasing and
$$
\lambda=\boxed{\frac12}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Here $u_0=1/2$. Iterating step [](#un-recurrence){.pf-ref} gives
$$
u_n=u_0^{2^n}=2^{-2^n},
$$
and therefore
$$
x_n=\frac12-u_n=\frac12-2^{-2^n}.
$$
The positive sequence $2^{-2^n}$ is strictly decreasing to $0$. Thus $x_n$ is strictly increasing, is bounded above by $1/2$, and converges to $1/2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-two-answer}
For an initial value $y\in\RR$, one has $y_\infty=\lambda$ exactly when
$$
y\in\boxed{\left(-\frac12,\frac32\right)}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $(y_n)$ be the orbit with $y_0=y$, and set $v_n=1/2-y_n$. By step [](#un-recurrence){.pf-ref},
$$
v_n=v_0^{2^n}=\left(\frac12-y\right)^{2^n}.
$$
Since $y_n=1/2-v_n$, the equality $y_\infty=\lambda=1/2$ holds exactly when $v_n\to0$. This occurs exactly when
$$
\abs{v_0}=\abs{\frac12-y}<1,
$$
which is equivalent to $-1/2<y<3/2$.

At either endpoint, $\abs{v_0}=1$ and $v_n=1$ for all $n\geq1$, so the limit is $-1/2$ rather than $1/2$. If $\abs{v_0}>1$, then $v_n\to+\infty$, so the orbit does not converge to $1/2$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#part-one-answer){.pf-ref} proves part 1, and step [](#part-two-answer){.pf-ref} proves part 2.
:::
