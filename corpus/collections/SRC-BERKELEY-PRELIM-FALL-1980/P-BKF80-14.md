::: {.problem}
Exhibit a set $S$ of real $2\times2$ matrices with the following property: every invertible $2\times2$ integer matrix whose characteristic roots all lie on the unit circle is similar over $\RR$ to exactly one matrix in $S$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $A\in GL_2(\ZZ)$ have both characteristic roots on the unit circle, and write
$$
\chi_A(T)=T^2-tT+d,
\qquad
t=\operatorname{tr}A,
\qquad
d=\det A.
$$

<1>1. The only possible characteristic polynomials are
$$
T^2-1,
\quad
(T-1)^2,
\quad
(T+1)^2,
\quad
T^2+T+1,
\quad
T^2+1,
\quad
T^2-T+1.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $\alpha,\beta$ are the two roots, then
$$
\abs d=\abs{\alpha\beta}=1.
$$
Since $d$ is a nonzero integer, $d=\pm1$.

Suppose first that $d=1$. Then
$$
\abs t=\abs{\alpha+\beta}\leq2.
$$
Because $t\in\ZZ$, one has $t\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$, yielding the five determinant-$1$ polynomials in the displayed list.

If $d=-1$, the two roots cannot be nonreal: nonreal roots of a real quadratic are conjugate and would have product $1$. Thus both roots are real points of the unit circle. Their product is $-1$, so they are $1$ and $-1$, giving $\chi_A(T)=T^2-1$.
:::

<1>2. For $t\in\{-1,0,1\}$, every matrix with characteristic polynomial $T^2-tT+1$ is similar over $\RR$ to
$$
C_t\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
1&t
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and every matrix with characteristic polynomial $T^2-1$ is similar to $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$.

::: {.proof}
For $t\in\{-1,0,1\}$ the discriminant $t^2-4$ is negative, so $A$ has no real eigenvector. Choose any nonzero $v\in\RR^2$. Then $v$ and $Av$ are linearly independent and form a basis. Cayley--Hamilton gives
$$
A^2v=tAv-v,
$$
so the matrix of $A$ in the basis $(v,Av)$ is exactly $C_t$.

If $\chi_A(T)=T^2-1$, the two eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$ are distinct. Hence $A$ is diagonalizable over $\RR$ and is similar to $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$.
:::

<1>3. A matrix with characteristic polynomial $(T-1)^2$ is similar to exactly one of
$$
I_2,
\qquad
J_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$
and a matrix with characteristic polynomial $(T+1)^2$ is similar to exactly one of
$$
-I_2,
\qquad
J_-=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Suppose $\chi_A(T)=(T-1)^2$. If $A=I_2$, this is the scalar class. Otherwise $N=A-I_2$ is nonzero and satisfies $N^2=0$ by Cayley--Hamilton. Choose $v$ with $Nv\neq0$. Then $(Nv,v)$ is a basis and the matrix of $N$ in this basis is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus $A$ is similar to $J_+$. The two possibilities are not similar because one is scalar and the other is not.

The same argument applied to $N=A+I_2$ gives the two classes $-I_2$ and $J_-$ when $\chi_A(T)=(T+1)^2$.
:::

<1>4. A set with the required property is
$$
S=\boxed{\left\{
I_2,
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
-I_2,
\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
\right\}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 show that every admissible $A$ is similar to at least one matrix in $S$.

Matrices in $S$ having different characteristic polynomials cannot be similar. The only pairs in $S$ with the same characteristic polynomial are $I_2$ and $J_+$, and $-I_2$ and $J_-$. Step <1>3 shows that the two matrices in each such pair are not similar. Hence every admissible matrix is similar to exactly one member of $S$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 exhibits and verifies the required set.
:::
