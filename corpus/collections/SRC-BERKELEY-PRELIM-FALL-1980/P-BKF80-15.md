::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
x''+x'+x^3=0
$$
and
$$
F(x,x')=(x+x')^2+(x')^2+x^4.
$$

1. Show that $F$ decreases along trajectories of the differential equation.
2. Show that every solution satisfies
$$
(x(t),x'(t))\longrightarrow(0,0)
\qquad(t\to\infty).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
y(t)\coloneqq x'(t),
\qquad
E(t)\coloneqq F(x(t),y(t)).
$$
Then the differential equation is
$$
x'=y,
\qquad
y'=-y-x^3.
$$

<1>1. Along every solution,
$$
E'(t)=-2x(t)^4-2y(t)^2\leq0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Differentiating
$$
E=(x+y)^2+y^2+x^4
$$
and using $x'=y$ and $y'=-y-x^3$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
E'
&=2(x+y)(x'+y')+2yy'+4x^3x'\\
&=2(x+y)(-x^3)+2y(-y-x^3)+4x^3y\\
&=-2x^4-2y^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $E$ is nonincreasing, and it decreases strictly away from $(x,y)=(0,0)$.
:::

<1>2. Every solution is defined for all $t\geq0$, and both $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ are bounded on $[0,\infty)$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
0\leq E(t)\leq E(0).
$$
Since
$$
y(t)^2+x(t)^4\leq E(t),
$$
both $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ remain bounded as long as the solution exists. The vector field
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto (y,-y-x^3)
$$
is locally Lipschitz. A maximal solution that remains in a bounded subset of $\RR^2$ cannot terminate at a finite positive time, so the solution extends to all $t\geq0$.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\int_0^\infty x(t)^4\,dt<\infty,
\qquad
\int_0^\infty y(t)^2\,dt<\infty.
$$

::: {.proof}
The nonincreasing function $E$ is bounded below by $0$, so $E(t)$ has a finite limit $E_\infty\geq0$. Integrating step <1>1 from $0$ to $T$ gives
$$
2\int_0^T\bigl(x(t)^4+y(t)^2\bigr)\,dt
=E(0)-E(T)
\leq E(0).
$$
Letting $T\to\infty$ proves both integrability statements.
:::

<1>4. Every solution satisfies
$$
(x(t),x'(t))\longrightarrow\boxed{(0,0)}
\qquad(t\to\infty).
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $y=x'$ is bounded, so $x$ is uniformly continuous on $[0,\infty)$. Also
$$
y'=-y-x^3
$$
is bounded because both $x$ and $y$ are bounded; hence $y$ is uniformly continuous as well.

Suppose $x(t)$ did not tend to $0$. Then there would be $\varepsilon>0$ and times $t_n\to\infty$ with $\abs{x(t_n)}\geq\varepsilon$. Uniform continuity gives a fixed $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{x(t)}\geq\frac\varepsilon2
$$
whenever $\abs{t-t_n}<\delta$. Passing to a subsequence makes these intervals disjoint, and then
$$
\int_0^\infty x(t)^4\,dt=\infty,
$$
contrary to step <1>3. Therefore $x(t)\to0$.

The same argument applied to the uniformly continuous function $y$, using the finiteness of $\int_0^\infty y(t)^2\,dt$ from step <1>3, gives $y(t)\to0$. Since $y=x'$, the asserted limit follows.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves part 1, and step <1>4 proves part 2.
