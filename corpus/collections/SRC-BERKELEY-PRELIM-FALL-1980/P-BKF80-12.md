::: {.problem}
Prove that every group of order $6$ is isomorphic to either
$$
\ZZ/6\ZZ
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
S_3.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be a group of order $6$.

<1>1. There are elements $a,b\in G$ such that $a$ has order $3$, $b$ has order $2$, $P\coloneqq\langle a\rangle$ is normal in $G$, and every element of $G$ has a unique expression $a^i b^j$ with $0\leq i<3$ and $0\leq j<2$.

::: {.proof}
By Sylow's theorems, the number $n_3$ of Sylow $3$-subgroups satisfies
$$
n_3\equiv1\pmod3,
\qquad
n_3\mid2,
$$
so $n_3=1$. Thus the Sylow $3$-subgroup $P$ is normal and cyclic; write $P=\langle a\rangle$. By Cauchy's theorem there is an element $b$ of order $2$. Then $P\cap\langle b\rangle=\{1\}$, and
$$
\abs{P\langle b\rangle}
=\frac{\abs P\,\abs{\langle b\rangle}}{\abs{P\cap\langle b\rangle}}
=6.
$$
Hence $G=P\langle b\rangle$. The trivial intersection also makes the expression $a^i b^j$ unique.
:::

<1>2. Exactly one of the relations
$$
bab^{-1}=a
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
bab^{-1}=a^{-1}
$$
holds.

::: {.proof}
Because $P$ is normal, conjugation by $b$ restricts to an automorphism of the cyclic group $P$ of order $3$. An automorphism sends the generator $a$ to either $a$ or $a^2=a^{-1}$.
:::

<1>3. If $bab^{-1}=a$, then $G\cong\ZZ/6\ZZ$.

::: {.proof}
In this case $a$ and $b$ commute. If $(ab)^k=1$, then
$$
a^k=b^{-k}\in P\cap\langle b\rangle=\{1\}.
$$
Thus $3\mid k$ and $2\mid k$, so $6\mid k$. Therefore $ab$ has order $6$ and generates $G$, proving that $G$ is cyclic of order $6$.
:::

<1>4. If $bab^{-1}=a^{-1}$, then $G\cong S_3$.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
r=(123),
\qquad
s=(12)
$$
in $S_3$. They satisfy $r^3=s^2=1$ and $srs^{-1}=r^{-1}$. By step <1>1, every element of $G$ has a unique normal form $a^i b^j$, and the relation $ba=a^{-1}b$ determines multiplication of these normal forms. The same multiplication rule holds for the six elements $r^i s^j$ of $S_3$. Hence
$$
a^i b^j\longmapsto r^i s^j
$$
is a well-defined bijective homomorphism $G\to S_3$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 exhausts the possible conjugation actions, and steps <1>3 and <1>4 give the corresponding isomorphism types.
:::
:::
