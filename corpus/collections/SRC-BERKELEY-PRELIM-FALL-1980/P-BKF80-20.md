::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a ring with identity, and let $G(R)$ denote its set of units.

1. Prove that $G(R)$ is a group under multiplication.
2. Let $R=\ZZ[i]$. Prove that
$$
G(R)\cong\ZZ/4\ZZ.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #g-r-group}
$G(R)$ is a group under multiplication.

::: pf-proof
Multiplication on $G(R)$ is associative because multiplication in $R$ is associative, and the identity $1$ is a unit. If $x,y\in G(R)$, then
$$
(xy)(y^{-1}x^{-1})
=x(yy^{-1})x^{-1}
=1
$$
and
$$
(y^{-1}x^{-1})(xy)
=y^{-1}(x^{-1}x)y
=1.
$$
Thus $xy$ is a unit, with inverse $y^{-1}x^{-1}$, so $G(R)$ is closed under multiplication. Finally, every $x\in G(R)$ has its two-sided inverse $x^{-1}\in G(R)$. Hence all group axioms hold.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #gaussian-units}
The units of $\ZZ[i]$ are exactly
$$
G(\ZZ[i])=\{1,-1,i,-i\}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For $z=a+bi\in\ZZ[i]$, define
$$
N(z)\coloneqq z\overline z=a^2+b^2.
$$
The norm is multiplicative. If $z$ is a unit with inverse $w\in\ZZ[i]$, then
$$
1=N(1)=N(zw)=N(z)N(w).
$$
Both $N(z)$ and $N(w)$ are nonnegative integers, so $N(z)=1$. Therefore
$$
a^2+b^2=1,
$$
which gives $z\in\{1,-1,i,-i\}$. Conversely, each of these four elements is a unit.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cyclic-z4}
$G(\ZZ[i])\cong\boxed{\ZZ/4\ZZ}$.

::: pf-proof
The element $i$ has order $4$, and
$$
\langle i\rangle=\{1,i,-1,-i\}=G(\ZZ[i])
$$
by step [](#gaussian-units){.pf-ref}. Thus the unit group is cyclic of order $4$, hence isomorphic to $\ZZ/4\ZZ$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#g-r-group){.pf-ref} proves part 1, and steps [](#gaussian-units){.pf-ref} and [](#cyclic-z4){.pf-ref} prove part 2.
:::
