::: {.problem}
Let $A,B$ be real $n\times n$ matrices. Suppose
$$
A^{\mathsf T}=A,
\qquad
v^{\mathsf T}Av\ge0\quad(v\in\RR^n),
$$
and
$$
AB+BA=0.
$$
Prove that
$$
AB=BA=0,
$$
and give an example in which neither $A$ nor $B$ is zero.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
There is an orthogonal matrix $Q$ such that
$$
Q^{\mathsf T}AQ
=D
=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)
$$
with $\lambda_i\geq0$ for every $i$.

::: pf-proof
The matrix $A$ is real symmetric, so the spectral theorem gives an orthogonal diagonalization. The hypothesis
$$
v^{\mathsf T}Av\geq0
$$
for all $v$ says that $A$ is positive semidefinite, hence every eigenvalue $\lambda_i$ is nonnegative.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-vanishes-condition}
If $C\coloneqq Q^{\mathsf T}BQ=(c_{ij})$, then $c_{ij}=0$ whenever $\lambda_i>0$ or $\lambda_j>0$.

::: pf-proof
Conjugating the relation $AB+BA=0$ by $Q$ gives
$$
DC+CD=0.
$$
Its $(i,j)$ entry is
$$
(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)c_{ij}=0.
$$
Because $\lambda_i,\lambda_j\geq0$, the sum $\lambda_i+\lambda_j$ is positive whenever either eigenvalue is positive. Hence $c_{ij}=0$ in that case.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ab-ba-zero}
$AB=BA=0$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#c-vanishes-condition){.pf-ref}, a possibly nonzero entry $c_{ij}$ can occur only when $\lambda_i=\lambda_j=0$. Therefore every entry of both $DC$ and $CD$ is zero, so
$$
DC=CD=0.
$$
Conjugating back by $Q$ yields
$$
AB=BA=0.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #example-nonzero}
An example with neither matrix zero is
$$
\boxed{
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The matrix $A$ is symmetric and positive semidefinite, both $A$ and $B$ are nonzero, and direct multiplication gives
$$
AB=BA=0.
$$
Thus $AB+BA=0$ as required.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#ab-ba-zero){.pf-ref} proves the asserted annihilation, and step [](#example-nonzero){.pf-ref} supplies the requested example.
:::
