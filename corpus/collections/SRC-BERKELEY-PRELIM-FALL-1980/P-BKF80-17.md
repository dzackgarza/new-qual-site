    Fixed D+1 interpolation nodes in [0,1]. Lagrange interpolation

    coefficients tend to zero, which forces the uniform norm to zero.

::: {.problem}
Let $(P_n)$ be real polynomials whose degrees are bounded by a fixed integer $D$. Suppose
\[
P_n(x)\longrightarrow0
\]
for every $x\in[0,1]$. Prove that $P_n\to0$ uniformly on $[0,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
Choose $D+1$ distinct points
$$
x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_D\in[0,1]
$$
and define the Lagrange polynomials
$$
L_j(x)
\coloneqq
\prod_{\substack{0\leq k\leq D\\k\neq j}}
\frac{x-x_k}{x_j-x_k}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Such points exist because $[0,1]$ contains infinitely many points.
The displayed expression is well defined because the $x_j$ are
pairwise distinct.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #interpolation-identity}
Every polynomial $P$ of degree at most $D$ satisfies
$$
P(x)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{D}P(x_j)L_j(x).
$$

::: pf-proof
The right-hand side is a polynomial of degree at most $D$ and agrees
with $P$ at each of the $D+1$ points $x_0,\ldots,x_D$, because
$$
L_j(x_k)=
\begin{cases}
1,&j=k,\\
0,&j\neq k.
\end{cases}
$$
Their difference therefore has degree at most $D$ and at least $D+1$
distinct zeros, so it is the zero polynomial.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #norm-bound}
For every $n$,
$$
\norm{P_n}_\infty
\leq
\sum_{j=0}^{D}
\abs{P_n(x_j)}\norm{L_j}_\infty,
$$
where the sup norms are taken on $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof
Apply step [](#interpolation-identity){.pf-ref} to $P_n$. For each $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
\abs{P_n(x)}
\leq
\sum_{j=0}^{D}
\abs{P_n(x_j)}\abs{L_j(x)}.
$$
Taking the supremum over $x\in[0,1]$ gives the displayed inequality.
Each $\norm{L_j}_\infty$ is finite because $L_j$ is continuous on
the compact interval $[0,1]$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rhs-vanishes}
The right-hand side in step [](#norm-bound){.pf-ref} tends to $0$ as $n\to\infty$.

::: pf-proof
For every fixed $j$, the assumed pointwise convergence gives
$$
P_n(x_j)\longrightarrow0.
$$
There are only finitely many indices $j$, and the numbers
$\norm{L_j}_\infty$ are fixed. Hence the finite sum in step [](#norm-bound){.pf-ref}
tends to $0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #uniform-convergence}
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\norm{P_n}_\infty\longrightarrow0.
}
$$
Equivalently, $P_n\to0$ uniformly on $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof
Combine steps [](#norm-bound){.pf-ref} and [](#rhs-vanishes){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#uniform-convergence){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
:::
