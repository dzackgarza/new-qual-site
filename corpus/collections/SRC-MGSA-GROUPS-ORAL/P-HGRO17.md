::: {.problem}
Is every finite group the Galois group over $\QQ$ of the splitting field of a polynomial in $\QQ[x]$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The question is the **inverse Galois problem** over $\QQ$: whether every finite group is $\Gal(K/\QQ)$ for some finite Galois extension $K/\QQ$, equivalently — by step [](#equivalent){.pf-ref} — the Galois group of the splitting field of a polynomial in $\QQ[x]$. The collection's `provenance` is the MGSA groups oral examination compilation, and no solutions sheet accompanies it; what follows records the equivalence and the state of the problem rather than a solution, since the general assertion is open.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #splitting-galois}
If $f \in \QQ[x]$ and $K$ is the splitting field of $f$ over $\QQ$, then $K/\QQ$ is finite Galois and $\Gal(K/\QQ) \cong \Gal(f)$.

::: pf-proof
The splitting field is generated over $\QQ$ by the roots of $f$, so it is finite over $\QQ$ because $f$ has finitely many roots each algebraic. $\QQ$ has characteristic $0$, so $f$ is separable and its roots are distinct; a splitting field of a separable polynomial is normal, hence the extension is Galois, and an automorphism of $K$ over $\QQ$ permutes the roots of $f$ and is determined by that permutation, which is the identification $\Gal(K/\QQ) = \Gal(f)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #galois-splitting}
If $K/\QQ$ is finite Galois then $K$ is the splitting field over $\QQ$ of a polynomial in $\QQ[x]$, namely the minimal polynomial of a primitive element.

::: pf-proof
The primitive element theorem applies since $\QQ$ is infinite and $K/\QQ$ is finite separable: $K = \QQ(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha \in K$ with minimal polynomial $m \in \QQ[x]$. Normality of $K/\QQ$ says that every conjugate of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$, that is every root of $m$, lies in $K$; so $K$ is generated by the roots of $m$ and is its splitting field.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equivalent}
The two formulations of the question agree: every finite group is $\Gal(K/\QQ)$ for a finite Galois $K/\QQ$ exactly when every finite group is the Galois group of the splitting field of a polynomial over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
One direction is step [](#splitting-galois){.pf-ref}, the other step [](#galois-splitting){.pf-ref}; a group occurs in one sense exactly when it occurs in the other, with $K$ and $f$ matched as there.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #known}
Many classes of finite groups are realised, by theorems that are named rather than proved here: the finite abelian groups, by Kronecker--Weber together with Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions; the finite solvable groups, by Shafarevich's theorem; and the symmetric groups $S_{n}$, as $\Gal(x^{n} - x - 1)$ (Selmer).

::: pf-proof
For the abelian case: given a finite abelian $A = \ZZ/d_{1} \oplus \cdots \oplus \ZZ/d_{r}$, take distinct primes $p_{i} \equiv 1 \pmod{d_{i}}$, whose existence is Dirichlet's theorem; then $(\ZZ/p_{i})^{\times}$ is cyclic of order divisible by $d_{i}$, so $A$ is a quotient of $(\ZZ/n)^{\times}$ with $n = \prod p_{i}$, and $\Gal(\QQ(\zeta_{n})/\QQ) = (\ZZ/n)^{\times}$, so the subgroup $H$ corresponding to the kernel has $\Gal(\QQ(\zeta_{n})^{H}/\QQ) \cong A$; this uses Kronecker--Weber to know that the abelian extensions obtained are the cyclotomic ones. The other two claims are the named theorems, quoted as the standard partial results and not re-proved on this card.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
The question as posed is unknown: it is not known whether every finite group is the Galois group over $\QQ$ of a splitting field, and no counterexample is known.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#equivalent){.pf-ref} reduce the splitting-field phrasing to the standard one, step [](#known){.pf-ref} records the classes that are known, and the universal assertion — the inverse Galois problem — is open, so the honest answer to the question asked is that it is not known; the card states this rather than a decision either way.

:::
