::: {.problem}
Prove that a uniform limit of complex analytic functions is complex analytic.
Is the analogous statement true for real analytic functions?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $U\subseteq\mathbb C$ be open, let
$$
f_n:U\to\mathbb C
$$
be holomorphic, and suppose $f_n\to f$ uniformly on $U$.

<1>1. The limit function $f$ is continuous on $U$.

::: {.proof}
Each $f_n$ is continuous, and a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous.
:::

<1>2. If $T$ is any triangle whose closure is contained in $U$, then
$$
\int_{\partial T}f(z)\,dz=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
For every $n$, Cauchy's theorem gives
$$
\int_{\partial T}f_n(z)\,dz=0.
$$
Since $f_n\to f$ uniformly on $U$, the convergence is uniform on the compact curve $\partial T$. If $\ell(\partial T)$ denotes its length, then
$$
\left|
\int_{\partial T}(f-f_n)(z)\,dz
\right|
\leq
\ell(\partial T)
\sup_{z\in\partial T}|f(z)-f_n(z)|
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Hence
$$
\int_{\partial T}f(z)\,dz
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\int_{\partial T}f_n(z)\,dz
=0.
$$
:::

<1>3. The function $f$ is holomorphic on $U$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $f$ is continuous. Step <1>2 shows that its integral around the boundary of every triangle compactly contained in $U$ vanishes. Morera's theorem therefore implies that $f$ is holomorphic.
:::

<1>4. The analogous statement for real analytic functions is false.

::: {.proof}
For $n\geq1$, define
$$
g_n(x)\coloneqq
\sqrt{x^2+\frac1{n^2}},
\qquad
x\in\mathbb R.
$$
Each $g_n$ is real analytic on $\mathbb R$: the polynomial
$$
x^2+\frac1{n^2}
$$
takes values in $(0,\infty)$, and the square-root function is real analytic on $(0,\infty)$.

For every $x\in\mathbb R$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
0
\leq
g_n(x)-|x|
&=
\frac{n^{-2}}
{\sqrt{x^2+n^{-2}}+|x|}\\
&\leq
\frac1n,
\end{aligned}
$$
because
$$
\sqrt{x^2+n^{-2}}\geq\frac1n.
$$
Thus
$$
\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|g_n(x)-|x||
\leq
\frac1n
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Hence $g_n\to|x|$ uniformly on $\mathbb R$. The limit $|x|$ is not differentiable at $0$, and therefore is not real analytic.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\text{uniform limits of holomorphic functions are holomorphic,}
}
$$
whereas
$$
\boxed{
\text{uniform limits of real analytic functions need not be real analytic.}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves the complex-analytic statement, and step <1>4 gives a counterexample to the real-analytic analogue.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 answers both parts.
:::
