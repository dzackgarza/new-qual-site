::: {.problem}
1. For which real numbers $\alpha>0$ does
   $$
   x'=x^\alpha,\qquad x(0)=0,
   $$
   have a solution on some interval $[0,b]$, $b>0$?
2. For which values of $\alpha$ are there intervals on which two solutions are defined?
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #existence}
For every $\alpha>0$, the initial-value problem has a solution on every interval $[0,b]$ with $b>0$.

::: pf-proof
The function
$$
x(t)\coloneqq0
$$
satisfies $x(0)=0$ and
$$
x'(t)=0=x(t)^\alpha
$$
for every $t$. Thus a solution exists for every $\alpha>0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #delayed-solutions}
Suppose $0<\alpha<1$. For every $c\geq0$, define
$$
x_c(t)\coloneqq
\begin{cases}
0,&0\leq t\leq c,\\
\big((1-\alpha)(t-c)\big)^{1/(1-\alpha)},&t>c.
\end{cases}
$$
Then $x_c$ is a solution with $x_c(0)=0$.

::: pf-proof
The two formulas agree at $t=c$. For $t>c$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_c'(t)
&=\big((1-\alpha)(t-c)\big)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\\
&=x_c(t)^\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
Moreover, because $\alpha/(1-\alpha)>0$,
$$
\lim_{t\downarrow c}x_c'(t)=0.
$$
The left derivative at $c$ is also $0$, so $x_c$ is differentiable at $c$ and satisfies the differential equation there as well. On $0\leq t<c$ both $x_c'$ and $x_c^\alpha$ vanish. Finally $x_c(0)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nonuniqueness}
Hence for $0<\alpha<1$ there are intervals carrying two distinct solutions of the initial-value problem.

::: pf-proof
Fix $b>0$ and choose $c$ with $0<c<b$. On $[0,b]$, both $x_0$ and $x_c$ from [](#delayed-solutions){.pf-ref} solve the initial-value problem. They are distinct, since $x_0(t)>0=x_c(t)$ for $0<t\leq c$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #uniqueness}
Suppose $\alpha\geq1$. Then the zero solution is the unique nonnegative solution of the initial-value problem on every interval $[0,b]$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x$ be a solution on $[0,b]$. Since $x'=x^\alpha\geq0$ and $x(0)=0$, one has $x(t)\geq0$ for $0\leq t\leq b$. Fix $T\in(0,b]$ and put
$$
M\coloneqq\max_{0\leq t\leq T}x(t).
$$
If $\alpha=1$, then for $0\leq t\leq T$,
$$
x(t)=\int_0^t x(s)\,ds.
$$
If $\alpha>1$, then $0\leq x(s)\leq M$ gives
$$
x(s)^\alpha\leq M^{\alpha-1}x(s),
$$
and hence
$$
0\leq x(t)
=\int_0^t x(s)^\alpha\,ds
\leq M^{\alpha-1}\int_0^t x(s)\,ds.
$$
In either case Grönwall's inequality, with zero initial value, gives $x(t)=0$ on $[0,T]$. Since $T\leq b$ was arbitrary, $x\equiv0$ on $[0,b]$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore the answer to Part 1 is every $\alpha>0$, and the answer to Part 2 is exactly
$$
\boxed{0<\alpha<1}.
$$

::: pf-proof
[](#existence){.pf-ref} proves the existence assertion. [](#nonuniqueness){.pf-ref} and [](#uniqueness){.pf-ref} give respectively nonuniqueness for $0<\alpha<1$ and uniqueness for $\alpha\geq1$.
:::
