::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a matrix over a field $F$.

1. Prove that the row rank of $M$ equals its column rank.
2. Over $F=\RR$, find a maximal linearly independent set of columns of
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix}
   1&0&3&-2\\
   2&1&2&0\\
   0&1&-4&4\\
   1&1&1&2\\
   1&0&1&2
   \end{pmatrix}.
   \]
3. If $F\subset K$ is a field extension and $M$ has entries in $F$, compare the row rank of $M$ over $F$ and over $K$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose first that $M$ is an $m\times n$ matrix over $F$, and let
$$
T:F^n\to F^m
$$
be the linear map represented by $M$ in the standard bases.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #column-rank}
The column rank of $M$ is $\dim_F\operatorname{im}T$.

::: pf-proof
The columns of $M$ are precisely $T(e_1),\ldots,T(e_n)$, where $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is the standard basis of $F^n$. Since these vectors span $\operatorname{im}T$, their span has dimension $\dim_F\operatorname{im}T$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #row-rank}
The row rank of $M$ is $\dim_F\operatorname{im}T^*$.

::: pf-proof
For the dual map
$$
T^*:(F^m)^*\to(F^n)^*,
$$
the coordinate row vector of $T^*(e_i^*)$ is the $i$th row of $M$. Hence the image of $T^*$ is exactly the span of the rows of $M$, under the standard identification of $(F^n)^*$ with row vectors.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dual-rank}
One has
$$
\dim_F\operatorname{im}T^*
=
\dim_F\operatorname{im}T.
$$

::: pf-proof
A functional $\varphi\in(F^m)^*$ lies in $\ker T^*$ exactly when
$$
\varphi(Tv)=0
\qquad\text{for every }v\in F^n,
$$
that is, exactly when $\varphi$ annihilates $\operatorname{im}T$. Thus
$$
\ker T^*=(\operatorname{im}T)^\circ.
$$
If $r=\dim_F\operatorname{im}T$, then the annihilator of an $r$-dimensional subspace of the $m$-dimensional space $F^m$ has dimension $m-r$. Rank-nullity for $T^*$ therefore gives
$$
\dim_F\operatorname{im}T^*
=m-(m-r)
=r.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The row rank of every matrix over $F$ equals its column rank.

::: pf-proof
By [](#column-rank){.pf-ref} and [](#row-rank){.pf-ref}, the column and row ranks are respectively $\dim_F\operatorname{im}T$ and $\dim_F\operatorname{im}T^*$. [](#dual-rank){.pf-ref} shows these dimensions are equal.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
For the matrix in Part 2, all four columns form a maximal linearly independent set.

::: pf-proof
Take the submatrix formed by rows $1,2,4,5$:
$$
N=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&3&-2\\
2&1&2&0\\
1&1&1&2\\
1&0&1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Elementary row reduction gives
$$
\det N=8\neq0.
$$
Hence the four columns of $N$ are linearly independent in $\RR^4$. Any linear relation among the four columns of the original $5\times4$ matrix would restrict to the same relation among the columns of $N$, so all four original columns are linearly independent. Since the matrix has only four columns, this set is maximal.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #scalar-extension-independence}
Let $F\subset K$ be a field extension. Vectors in $F^n$ are linearly independent over $F$ if and only if they are linearly independent over $K$ after scalar extension.

::: pf-proof
Linear independence over $K$ plainly implies linear independence over $F$.

Conversely, let $v_1,\ldots,v_r\in F^n$ be linearly independent over $F$. Extend them to an $F$-basis of $F^n$, and let
$$
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\in(F^n)^*
$$
be the corresponding coordinate functionals, so that $\lambda_i(v_j)=\delta_{ij}$. The same coordinate formulas define $K$-linear maps
$$
\lambda_{i,K}:K^n\to K.
$$
If
$$
a_1v_1+\cdots+a_rv_r=0,
\qquad a_i\in K,
$$
then applying $\lambda_{i,K}$ gives $a_i=0$ for every $i$. Thus the $v_i$ remain linearly independent over $K$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The row rank of $M$ over $F$ equals its row rank over $K$.

::: pf-proof
The rows of $M$ lie in $F^n$. By [](#scalar-extension-independence){.pf-ref}, every subset of these rows is linearly independent over $F$ exactly when it is linearly independent over $K$. Therefore the maximal possible cardinality of a linearly independent subset of rows is the same over both fields.
:::
