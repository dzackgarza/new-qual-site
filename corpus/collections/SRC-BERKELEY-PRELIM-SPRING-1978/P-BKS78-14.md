::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group of order $10$ with a normal subgroup of order $2$.
Prove that $G$ is abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $H\trianglelefteq G$ be the given subgroup of order $2$.

::: pf

::: pf-step
The group $G$ has a unique subgroup $K$ of order $5$, and $K\trianglelefteq G$.

::: pf-proof
By Sylow's theorems, the number $n_5$ of Sylow $5$-subgroups satisfies
$$
n_5\mid2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n_5\equiv1\pmod5.
$$
Hence $n_5=1$. Denote the unique Sylow $5$-subgroup by $K$. A unique Sylow subgroup is normal, so $K\trianglelefteq G$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #trivial-intersection}
One has $H\cap K=\{e\}$.

::: pf-proof
By Lagrange's theorem,
$$
\cardinality{H\cap K}\mid \cardinality{H}=2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\cardinality{H\cap K}\mid \cardinality{K}=5.
$$
Since $\gcd(2,5)=1$, it follows that $\cardinality{H\cap K}=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #elementwise-commutation}
Every element of $H$ commutes with every element of $K$.

::: pf-proof
Let $h\in H$ and $k\in K$. Since $H$ is normal,
$$
kh^{-1}k^{-1}\in H,
$$
so the commutator
$$
[h,k]=hkh^{-1}k^{-1}
$$
lies in $H$. Since $K$ is normal,
$$
hkh^{-1}\in K,
$$
so $[h,k]\in K$ as well. Therefore
$$
[h,k]\in H\cap K=\{e\}
$$
by [](#trivial-intersection){.pf-ref}. Thus $hk=kh$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product-decomposition}
Every element of $G$ can be written as $hk$ with $h\in H$ and $k\in K$.

::: pf-proof
Because $H$ and $K$ are finite subgroups,
$$
\cardinality{HK}
=\frac{\cardinality{H}\cardinality{K}}{\cardinality{H\cap K}}
=\frac{2\cdot5}{1}
=10
=\cardinality{G}.
$$
Hence $HK=G$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The group $G$ is abelian.

::: pf-proof
Both $H$ and $K$ are cyclic, because they have prime order, and hence are abelian. By [](#elementwise-commutation){.pf-ref} they commute elementwise with each other. If
$$
g_1=h_1k_1,
\qquad
g_2=h_2k_2
$$
are arbitrary elements of $G$ as in [](#product-decomposition){.pf-ref}, then
$$
g_1g_2
=h_1h_2k_1k_2
=h_2h_1k_2k_1
=g_2g_1.
$$
Thus every two elements of $G$ commute.
:::
