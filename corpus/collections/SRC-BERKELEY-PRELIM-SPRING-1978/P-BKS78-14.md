::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group of order $10$ with a normal subgroup of order $2$.
Prove that $G$ is abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $H\trianglelefteq G$ be the given subgroup of order $2$.

<1>1. The group $G$ has a unique subgroup $K$ of order $5$, and $K\trianglelefteq G$.

::: {.proof}
By Sylow's theorems, the number $n_5$ of Sylow $5$-subgroups satisfies
$$
n_5\mid2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n_5\equiv1\pmod5.
$$
Hence $n_5=1$. Denote the unique Sylow $5$-subgroup by $K$. A unique Sylow subgroup is normal, so $K\trianglelefteq G$.
:::

<1>2. One has $H\cap K=\{e\}$.

::: {.proof}
By Lagrange's theorem,
$$
|H\cap K|\mid |H|=2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|H\cap K|\mid |K|=5.
$$
Since $\gcd(2,5)=1$, it follows that $|H\cap K|=1$.
:::

<1>3. Every element of $H$ commutes with every element of $K$.

::: {.proof}
Let $h\in H$ and $k\in K$. Since $H$ is normal,
$$
kh^{-1}k^{-1}\in H,
$$
so the commutator
$$
[h,k]=hkh^{-1}k^{-1}
$$
lies in $H$. Since $K$ is normal,
$$
hkh^{-1}\in K,
$$
so $[h,k]\in K$ as well. Therefore
$$
[h,k]\in H\cap K=\{e\}
$$
by step <1>2. Thus $hk=kh$.
:::

<1>4. Every element of $G$ can be written as $hk$ with $h\in H$ and $k\in K$.

::: {.proof}
Because $H$ and $K$ are finite subgroups,
$$
|HK|
=\frac{|H||K|}{|H\cap K|}
=\frac{2\cdot5}{1}
=10
=|G|.
$$
Hence $HK=G$.
:::

<1>5. The group $G$ is abelian.

::: {.proof}
Both $H$ and $K$ are cyclic, because they have prime order, and hence are abelian. By step <1>3 they commute elementwise with each other. If
$$
g_1=h_1k_1,
\qquad
g_2=h_2k_2
$$
are arbitrary elements of $G$ as in step <1>4, then
$$
g_1g_2
=h_1h_2k_1k_2
=h_2h_1k_2k_1
=g_2g_1.
$$
Thus every two elements of $G$ commute.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
