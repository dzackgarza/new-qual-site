::: {.problem}
Let $A$ and $B$ be commuting real symmetric $n\times n$ matrices.
Prove that they have a common eigenvector in $\mathbb R^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The matrix $A$ has a nonzero real eigenspace $E_\lambda$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb R$.

::: {.proof}
Since $A$ is real symmetric, the spectral theorem gives an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb R^n$ consisting of eigenvectors of $A$. In particular, $A$ has a real eigenvalue $\lambda$ and
$$
E_\lambda\coloneqq\ker(A-\lambda I)\neq\{0\}.
$$
:::

<1>2. The subspace $E_\lambda$ is invariant under $B$.

::: {.proof}
Let $v\in E_\lambda$. Since $AB=BA$,
$$
A(Bv)
=B(Av)
=B(\lambda v)
=\lambda Bv.
$$
Thus $Bv\in E_\lambda$.
:::

<1>3. The restriction
$$
B|_{E_\lambda}:E_\lambda\to E_\lambda
$$
is self-adjoint with respect to the Euclidean inner product on $E_\lambda$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 makes the restriction well-defined. For $u,v\in E_\lambda$, symmetry of $B$ gives
$$
\langle B|_{E_\lambda}u,v\rangle
=\langle Bu,v\rangle
=\langle u,Bv\rangle
=\langle u,B|_{E_\lambda}v\rangle.
$$
:::

<1>4. There is a nonzero vector $v\in E_\lambda$ and a real number $\mu$ such that $Bv=\mu v$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $E_\lambda$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional real inner-product space. By step <1>3, $B|_{E_\lambda}$ is self-adjoint. The spectral theorem applied to this restriction therefore gives a nonzero eigenvector $v\in E_\lambda$ with
$$
Bv=\mu v
$$
for some $\mu\in\mathbb R$.
:::

<1>5. The vector $v$ from step <1>4 is a common eigenvector of $A$ and $B$.

::: {.proof}
Since $v\in E_\lambda$,
$$
Av=\lambda v.
$$
Step <1>4 gives $Bv=\mu v$, and $v\neq0$. Hence $v$ is a common eigenvector.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
