::: {.problem}
Let $A$ and $B$ be commuting real symmetric $n\times n$ matrices.
Prove that they have a common eigenvector in $\RR^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nonzero-eigenspace}
The matrix $A$ has a nonzero real eigenspace $E_\lambda$ for some $\lambda\in\RR$.

::: pf-proof
Since $A$ is real symmetric, the spectral theorem gives an orthonormal basis of $\RR^n$ consisting of eigenvectors of $A$. In particular, $A$ has a real eigenvalue $\lambda$ and
$$
E_\lambda\coloneqq\ker(A-\lambda I)\neq\{0\}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #invariant-eigenspace}
The subspace $E_\lambda$ is invariant under $B$.

::: pf-proof
Let $v\in E_\lambda$. Since $AB=BA$,
$$
A(Bv)
=B(Av)
=B(\lambda v)
=\lambda Bv.
$$
Thus $Bv\in E_\lambda$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #self-adjoint-restriction}
The restriction
$$
B\restrict_{E_\lambda}:E_\lambda\to E_\lambda
$$
is self-adjoint with respect to the Euclidean inner product on $E_\lambda$.

::: pf-proof
[](#invariant-eigenspace){.pf-ref} makes the restriction well-defined. For $u,v\in E_\lambda$, symmetry of $B$ gives
$$
\langle B\restrict_{E_\lambda}u,v\rangle
=\langle Bu,v\rangle
=\langle u,Bv\rangle
=\langle u,B\restrict_{E_\lambda}v\rangle.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #restricted-eigenvector}
There is a nonzero vector $v\in E_\lambda$ and a real number $\mu$ such that $Bv=\mu v$.

::: pf-proof
By [](#nonzero-eigenspace){.pf-ref}, $E_\lambda$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional real inner-product space. By [](#self-adjoint-restriction){.pf-ref}, $B\restrict_{E_\lambda}$ is self-adjoint. The spectral theorem applied to this restriction therefore gives a nonzero eigenvector $v\in E_\lambda$ with
$$
Bv=\mu v
$$
for some $\mu\in\RR$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The vector $v$ from [](#restricted-eigenvector){.pf-ref} is a common eigenvector of $A$ and $B$.

::: pf-proof
Since $v\in E_\lambda$,
$$
Av=\lambda v.
$$
[](#restricted-eigenvector){.pf-ref} gives $Bv=\mu v$, and $v\neq0$. Hence $v$ is a common eigenvector.
:::
