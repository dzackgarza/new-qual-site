::: {.problem}
Evaluate
$$
\iint_A e^{-x^2-y^2}\,dx\,dy,
$$
where
$$
A=\{(x,y)\in\RR^2:x^2+y^2\le1\}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #polar-coordinates}
In polar coordinates the integral is
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1 e^{-r^2}r\,dr\,d\theta.
$$

::: pf-proof
The unit disk is parametrized by
$$
x=r\cos\theta,
\qquad
y=r\sin\theta,
\qquad
0\leq r\leq1,
\quad
0\leq\theta\leq2\pi.
$$
Under this change of variables,
$$
x^2+y^2=r^2
$$
and the Jacobian determinant has absolute value $r$. The change-of-variables theorem gives the stated iterated integral.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #radial-integral}
The radial integral equals
$$
\int_0^1 e^{-r^2}r\,dr
=\frac12(1-e^{-1}).
$$

::: pf-proof
With $u=r^2$, so that $du=2r\,dr$,
$$
\int_0^1 e^{-r^2}r\,dr
=\frac12\int_0^1 e^{-u}\,du
=\frac12(1-e^{-1}).
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\iint_A e^{-x^2-y^2}\,dx\,dy
=\pi(1-e^{-1})
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By [](#polar-coordinates){.pf-ref} and [](#radial-integral){.pf-ref},
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1 e^{-r^2}r\,dr\,d\theta
=2\pi\cdot\frac12(1-e^{-1})
=\pi(1-e^{-1}).
$$
:::
