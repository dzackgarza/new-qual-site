::: {.problem}
Is
\[
x^4+1
\]
irreducible over each of the following fields: $\mathbb R$, $\mathbb Q$, and a field with $16$ elements?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
f(x)\coloneqq x^4+1.
$$

<1>1. The polynomial $f$ is reducible over $\mathbb R$.

::: {.proof}
In $\mathbb R[x]$,
$$
x^4+1
=
(x^2+\sqrt2x+1)(x^2-\sqrt2x+1).
$$
Both factors have positive degree, so this is a nontrivial factorization.
:::

<1>2. The polynomial $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.

::: {.proof}
Translation by $1$ is an automorphism of $\mathbb Q[x]$, so $f(x)$ is irreducible if and only if $f(x+1)$ is irreducible. Now
$$
f(x+1)
=(x+1)^4+1
=x^4+4x^3+6x^2+4x+2.
$$
Every nonleading coefficient is divisible by $2$, while the constant coefficient $2$ is not divisible by $4$. Eisenstein's criterion at the prime $2$ therefore shows that $f(x+1)$, and hence $f(x)$, is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
:::

<1>3. The polynomial $f$ is reducible over every field with $16$ elements.

::: {.proof}
Every field $F$ with $16=2^4$ elements has characteristic $2$. Hence in $F[x]$,
$$
(x+1)^4
=x^4+4x^3+6x^2+4x+1
=x^4+1.
$$
Thus $f=(x+1)^4$, so $f$ is reducible.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $x^4+1$ is reducible over $\mathbb R$, irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, and reducible over a field with $16$ elements.

::: {.proof}
This is exactly the combination of steps <1>1--<1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 answers all three cases.
:::
:::
