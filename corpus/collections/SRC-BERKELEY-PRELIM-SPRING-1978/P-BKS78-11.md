::: {.problem}
Let $U=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|>4\}$.

1. Show that there is a holomorphic function on $U$ whose derivative is
   \[
   \frac{z}{(z-1)(z-2)(z-3)}.
   \]
2. Is there a holomorphic function on $U$ whose derivative is
   \[
   \frac{z^2}{(z-1)(z-2)(z-3)}?
   \]
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
r_1(z)\coloneqq
\frac{z}{(z-1)(z-2)(z-3)}.
$$

<1>1. The function
$$
H(w)\coloneqq
\frac1{(1-w)(1-2w)(1-3w)}
$$
has a Taylor expansion
$$
H(w)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_mw^m
$$
for $|w|<1/3$.

::: {.proof}
The nearest singularity of $H$ to $0$ is at $w=1/3$, so $H$ is holomorphic on the disk $|w|<1/3$. Every holomorphic function has a Taylor expansion throughout the largest disk centered at the expansion point containing no singularity.
:::

<1>2. For $|z|>3$,
$$
r_1(z)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_mz^{-m-2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Factor $z^3$ from the denominator:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1(z)
&=
\frac{z}
{z^3(1-z^{-1})(1-2z^{-1})(1-3z^{-1})}\\
&=
z^{-2}H(z^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
$$
If $|z|>3$, then $|z^{-1}|<1/3$, so step <1>1 applies and gives the displayed Laurent series.
:::

<1>3. The function
$$
\boxed{
F(z)
\coloneqq
-
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{c_m}{m+1}z^{-m-1}
}
$$
is holomorphic on $U$ and satisfies
$$
F'(z)=r_1(z).
$$

::: {.proof}
The series in step <1>2 is a Laurent series converging on $|z|>3$. Its termwise antiderivative displayed above converges normally on every compact subset of $|z|>3$, and hence defines a holomorphic function there. Termwise differentiation gives
$$
F'(z)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_mz^{-m-2}
=
r_1(z)
$$
by step <1>2. Since $U\subset\{|z|>3\}$, this proves part 1.
:::

<1>4. Put
$$
r_2(z)\coloneqq
\frac{z^2}{(z-1)(z-2)(z-3)}.
$$
The residues of $r_2$ at $1,2,3$ are respectively
$$
\frac12,\qquad -4,\qquad \frac92.
$$

::: {.proof}
All three poles are simple. Thus
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=1}r_2
=
\frac{1^2}{(1-2)(1-3)}
=
\frac12,
$$
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=2}r_2
=
\frac{2^2}{(2-1)(2-3)}
=
-4,
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=3}r_2
=
\frac{3^2}{(3-1)(3-2)}
=
\frac92.
$$
:::

<1>5. For every $R>4$,
$$
\int_{|z|=R}r_2(z)\,dz=2\pi i.
$$

::: {.proof}
The positively oriented circle $|z|=R$ encloses all three poles. By the residue theorem and step <1>4,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{|z|=R}r_2(z)\,dz
&=
2\pi i
\left(
\frac12-4+\frac92
\right)\\
&=
2\pi i.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>6. There is no holomorphic function $G$ on $U$ with
$$
G'=r_2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Suppose such a function existed. For any $R>4$, the circle
$$
\gamma(t)=Re^{it},
\qquad
0\leq t\leq2\pi,
$$
lies in $U$. By the chain rule,
$$
\int_\gamma G'(z)\,dz
=
\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{d}{dt}G(\gamma(t))\,dt
=
G(\gamma(2\pi))-G(\gamma(0))
=0.
$$
This contradicts step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Therefore the answer to part 2 is
$$
\boxed{\text{No}.}
$$

::: {.proof}
This is exactly step <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 constructs the function required in part 1, and step <1>7 answers part 2.
:::
:::
