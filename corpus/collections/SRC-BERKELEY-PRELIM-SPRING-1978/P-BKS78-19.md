::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,1]\to\RR$ be Riemann integrable on $[b,1]$ for every $0<b\le1$.

1. If $f$ is bounded, prove that $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$.
2. What can happen if $f$ is unbounded?
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: pf-step
Suppose $f$ is bounded on $[0,1]$. Choose $M\geq0$ such that
$$
\abs{f(x)}\leq M
\qquad(0\leq x\leq1).
$$

::: pf-proof
This is exactly the boundedness hypothesis.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #darboux-partition}
For every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a partition $P$ of $[0,1]$ such that
$$
U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon.
$$

::: pf-proof
If $M=0$, then $f\equiv0$ and the assertion is immediate. Assume $M>0$.

Choose $b\in(0,1]$ so small that
$$
2Mb<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$
By hypothesis, $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[b,1]$. By the Darboux criterion there is a partition $Q$ of $[b,1]$ such that
$$
U(f,Q)-L(f,Q)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$
Adjoin the interval $[0,b]$ to obtain a partition $P$ of $[0,1]$. On $[0,b]$ the difference between the supremum and infimum of $f$ is at most $2M$, so this interval contributes at most $2Mb$ to the difference of the upper and lower sums. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
U(f,P)-L(f,P)
&\leq
2Mb+U(f,Q)-L(f,Q)\\
&<
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
=\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $f$ is bounded, then $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof
[](#darboux-partition){.pf-ref} verifies the Darboux criterion for Riemann integrability.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $f$ is unbounded on $[0,1]$, then $f$ is not Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$, even though it may be Riemann integrable on every $[b,1]$ with $b>0$.

::: pf-proof
Every Riemann-integrable function on a compact interval is bounded, so an unbounded $f$ cannot be Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$.

For example, define
$$
f(0)=0,
\qquad
f(x)=\frac1x\quad(0<x\leq1).
$$
For every $b>0$, the restriction of $f$ to $[b,1]$ is continuous and hence Riemann integrable. But $f$ is unbounded on $[0,1]$, so it is not Riemann integrable there.
:::
