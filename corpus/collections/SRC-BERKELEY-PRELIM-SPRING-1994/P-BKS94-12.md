::: {.problem}
Let $f = u + i v$ and $g = p + i q$ be analytic functions defined in a neighborhood of the origin in the complex plane. Assume $| g ^ { \prime } ( 0 ) | \ < \ | f ^ { \prime } ( 0 ) |$ Prove that there is a neighborhood of the origin in which the function $h = f + \overline { { g } }$ is one-to-one.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
h
=
(u+p)+i(v-q).
$$

<1>1. The real Jacobian determinant of $h$ is
$$
\det Dh
=
\abs{f'}^2-\abs{g'}^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $f=u+iv$ and $g=p+iq$ are analytic, the Cauchy--Riemann equations give
$$
u_x=v_y,
\qquad
u_y=-v_x,
\qquad
p_x=q_y,
\qquad
p_y=-q_x.
$$
The real-coordinate form of $h$ is
$$
h(x,y)
=
\bigl(u(x,y)+p(x,y),\,v(x,y)-q(x,y)\bigr).
$$
Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det Dh
&=
(u_x+p_x)(v_y-q_y)
-(u_y+p_y)(v_x-q_x)\\
&=
(u_x+p_x)(u_x-p_x)
-(-v_x-q_x)(v_x-q_x)\\
&=
u_x^2+v_x^2-p_x^2-q_x^2\\
&=
\abs{f'}^2-\abs{g'}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. The derivative $Dh(0)$ is invertible.

::: {.proof}
By the hypothesis and step <1>1,
$$
\det Dh(0)
=
\abs{f'(0)}^2-\abs{g'(0)}^2
>0.
$$
Hence the real linear map $Dh(0):\RR^2\to\RR^2$ is invertible.
:::

<1>3. There is a neighborhood $U$ of $0$ on which $h$ is one-to-one.

::: {.proof}
The functions $f$ and $g$ are analytic, so $h$, regarded as a map
$\RR^2\to\RR^2$, is continuously differentiable. By step <1>2, its
derivative at $0$ is invertible. The real inverse function theorem therefore
gives a neighborhood $U$ of $0$ on which $h$ is a diffeomorphism onto its
image. In particular, $h|_U$ is injective.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the required local one-to-one statement.
:::
:::
