::: {.problem}
Prove that there are at least two nonisomorphic nonabelian groups of order 30.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let
$$
G\coloneqq C_{15}\rtimes C_2,
$$
where the nonidentity element of $C_2$ acts on $C_{15}$ by inversion. Then
$G$ is a nonabelian group of order $30$.

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
C_{15}=\langle a\rangle,
\qquad
C_2=\langle s\rangle,
$$
with
$$
sas^{-1}=a^{-1}.
$$
Inversion is an automorphism of $C_{15}$ of order $2$, so the semidirect
product is defined and has
$$
\abs{G}=15\cdot2=30.
$$
Since $a\neq a^{-1}$, one has $sa\neq as$, so $G$ is nonabelian.
:::

<1>2. The group $G$ has exactly $15$ elements of order $2$.

::: {.proof}
The subgroup $\langle a\rangle$ has odd order, so it contains no
involution. Every element outside it has the form $a^is$, and
$$
(a^is)^2
=
a^is a^is
=
a^ia^{-i}
=1.
$$
The $15$ elements $a^is$ are distinct and nonidentity. Thus they are
exactly the $15$ involutions of $G$.
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
H\coloneqq S_3\times C_5.
$$
Then $H$ is a nonabelian group of order $30$.

::: {.proof}
Its order is
$$
\abs{H}
=
\abs{S_3}\abs{C_5}
=
6\cdot5
=30.
$$
Since $S_3$ is nonabelian, the direct product $S_3\times C_5$ is
nonabelian.
:::

<1>4. The group $H$ has exactly $3$ elements of order $2$.

::: {.proof}
If $(\sigma,c)\in S_3\times C_5$ has order $2$, then
$$
c^2=1.
$$
Because $C_5$ has odd order, this forces $c=1$. Thus the involutions of
$H$ are exactly the pairs
$$
(\sigma,1)
$$
where $\sigma$ is an involution of $S_3$. The three transpositions are the
only involutions in $S_3$, so $H$ has exactly three.
:::

<1>5. The groups $G$ and $H$ are not isomorphic.

::: {.proof}
An isomorphism preserves element orders and therefore preserves the number
of involutions. Step <1>2 gives $15$ involutions in $G$, while step <1>4
gives $3$ in $H$. Hence
$$
G\not\cong H.
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>3 construct two nonabelian groups of order $30$, and step
<1>5 proves that they are nonisomorphic.
:::
:::
