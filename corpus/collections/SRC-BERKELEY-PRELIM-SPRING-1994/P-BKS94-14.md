::: {.problem}
Let $W$ be a real $3 \times 3$ antisymmetric matrix, i.e., $W^t = -W$. Let the function

$$
X(t) = \begin{pmatrix} x_1(t) \\ x_2(t) \\ x_3(t) \end{pmatrix}
$$

be a real solution of the vector differential equation $\frac{dX}{dt} = WX$.

1. Prove that $\|X(t)\|$, the Euclidean norm of $X(t)$, is independent of $t$.

2. Prove that if $v$ is a vector in the null space of $W$, then $X(t) \cdot v$ is independent of $t$.

3. Prove that the values $X(t)$ all lie on a fixed circle in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every vector $x\in\RR^3$,
$$
x^tWx=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

The scalar $x^tWx$ equals its transpose. Using $W^t=-W$,
$$
x^tWx
=(x^tWx)^t
=
x^tW^tx
=
-x^tWx.
$$
Hence it is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The Euclidean norm $\norm{X(t)}$ is independent of $t$.

::: pf-proof

Differentiate its square:
$$
\frac d{dt}\norm{X(t)}^2
=
\frac d{dt}\bigl(X(t)^tX(t)\bigr)
=
2X(t)^tX'(t).
$$
Since $X'=WX$, step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\frac d{dt}\norm{X(t)}^2
=
2X(t)^tWX(t)
=0.
$$
Thus $\norm{X(t)}^2$, and hence $\norm{X(t)}$, is constant.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $v\in\ker W$, then
$$
X(t)\cdot v
$$
is independent of $t$.

::: pf-proof

Using $W^t=-W$ and $Wv=0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac d{dt}\bigl(X(t)\cdot v\bigr)
&=
X'(t)^tv\\
&=
(WX(t))^tv\\
&=
X(t)^tW^tv\\
&=
-X(t)^tWv\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

If $W\neq0$, then $\ker W$ contains a nonzero vector.

::: pf-proof

Since $W$ is a $3\times3$ skew-symmetric matrix,
$$
\det W
=
\det W^t
=
\det(-W)
=
-\det W.
$$
Thus $\det W=0$, so the null space is nontrivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

If the solution $X$ is nonconstant, its values lie on a fixed
nondegenerate circle in $\RR^3$.

::: pf-proof

A nonconstant solution forces $W\neq0$. Choose
$$
0\neq v\in\ker W
$$
using step [](#s4){.pf-ref}. By steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}, there are constants $R\geq0$ and
$c\in\RR$ such that every value $X(t)$ satisfies
$$
\norm{X(t)}=R,
\qquad
X(t)\cdot v=c.
$$
Thus the trajectory is contained in the intersection of the fixed sphere
$$
\{x\in\RR^3:\norm{x}=R\}
$$
with the fixed plane
$$
\{x\in\RR^3:x\cdot v=c\}.
$$

This intersection is a circle unless the plane is tangent to the sphere. In
the tangent case, the intersection is a single point, which would force
$X(t)$ to be constant. Since the present solution is nonconstant, the
intersection is a nondegenerate fixed circle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Every solution, including a constant solution, has all its values on
some fixed circle in $\RR^3$.

::: pf-proof

For a nonconstant solution this is step [](#s5){.pf-ref}. If $X$ is constant, choose
any fixed circle passing through its single value.

:::

3.
