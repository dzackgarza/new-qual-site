::: {.problem}
Let f be a continuous real valued function on R such that the improper Riemann integral $\int _ { - \infty } ^ { \infty } | f ( x ) |$ dx converges. Define the function g on R by

$$
g ( y ) = \int _ { - \infty } ^ { \infty } f ( x ) \cos ( x y ) d x .
$$

Prove that g is continuous.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $y_0\in\RR$ and $\varepsilon>0$.

<1>1. There is $R>1$ such that
$$
\int_{\abs{x}>R}\abs{f(x)}\,dx
<
\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The improper integral
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\abs{f(x)}\,dx
$$
converges, so its two tails tend to zero.
:::

<1>2. For all real $x,y$,
$$
\abs{\cos(xy)-\cos(xy_0)}
\leq
\abs{x}\abs{y-y_0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The real function $\cos$ is $1$-Lipschitz because its derivative has
absolute value at most $1$. Thus
$$
\abs{\cos u-\cos v}\leq\abs{u-v}.
$$
Take $u=xy$ and $v=xy_0$.
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
C\coloneqq\int_{-R}^{R}\abs{f(x)}\,dx
$$
and choose
$$
\delta\coloneqq
\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+RC)}.
$$
If $\abs{y-y_0}<\delta$, then
$$
\abs{g(y)-g(y_0)}<\varepsilon.
$$

::: {.proof}
Split the difference into the central interval and the two tails. By step
<1>2,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{g(y)-g(y_0)}
&\leq
\int_{-R}^{R}
\abs{f(x)}
\abs{\cos(xy)-\cos(xy_0)}\,dx\\
&\quad+
\int_{\abs{x}>R}
\abs{f(x)}
\abs{\cos(xy)-\cos(xy_0)}\,dx\\
&\leq
R\abs{y-y_0}C
+2\int_{\abs{x}>R}\abs{f(x)}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$
By the definitions of $\delta$ and $R$,
$$
R\abs{y-y_0}C
<
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
$$
and
$$
2\int_{\abs{x}>R}\abs{f(x)}\,dx
<
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$
Adding gives the claim.
:::

<1>4. The function $g$ is continuous on $\RR$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves continuity at the arbitrary point $y_0$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
