::: {.problem}
Let α be a number in $( 0 , 1 )$ . Prove that any sequence $( x _ { n } ) _ { 0 } ^ { \infty }$ of real numbers satisfying the recurrence relation

$$
x _ { n + 1 } = \alpha x _ { n } + ( 1 - \alpha ) x _ { n - 1 }
$$

has a limit, and find an expression for the limit in terms of $\alpha$, $x_0$, and $x_1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $n\geq1$, define
$$
d_n\coloneqq x_n-x_{n-1}.
$$

<1>1. The differences satisfy
$$
d_{n+1}=(\alpha-1)d_n.
$$

::: {.proof}
Using the recurrence,
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{n+1}
&=
x_{n+1}-x_n\\
&=
\alpha x_n+(1-\alpha)x_{n-1}-x_n\\
&=
(\alpha-1)(x_n-x_{n-1})\\
&=
(\alpha-1)d_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. For every $n\geq1$,
$$
d_n=(\alpha-1)^{n-1}(x_1-x_0).
$$

::: {.proof}
This follows by iterating step <1>1 from
$$
d_1=x_1-x_0.
$$
:::

<1>3. For every $n\geq1$,
$$
x_n
=
x_0
+(x_1-x_0)
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\alpha-1)^j.
$$

::: {.proof}
The differences telescope:
$$
x_n-x_0
=
\sum_{k=1}^n(x_k-x_{k-1})
=
\sum_{k=1}^n d_k.
$$
Substitute the expression from step <1>2 and reindex.
:::

<1>4. The sequence $(x_n)$ converges.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
0<\alpha<1,
$$
one has
$$
\abs{\alpha-1}<1.
$$
Therefore the geometric sums in step <1>3 converge as $n\to\infty$.
:::

<1>5. Its limit is
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n
=
\frac{(1-\alpha)x_0+x_1}{2-\alpha}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n
&=
x_0
+(x_1-x_0)\frac1{1-(\alpha-1)}\\
&=
x_0+\frac{x_1-x_0}{2-\alpha}\\
&=
\frac{(1-\alpha)x_0+x_1}{2-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4 and <1>5 prove convergence and give the requested expression.
:::
:::
