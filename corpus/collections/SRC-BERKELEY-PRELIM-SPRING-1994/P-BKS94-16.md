::: {.problem}
For which numbers a in $( 1 , \infty )$ is it true that $x ^ { a } \leqslant a ^ { x }$ for all x in $( 1 , \infty ) \ ?$
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
\phi(t)\coloneqq\frac{\log t}{t},
\qquad
t>1.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The function $\phi$ has a unique maximum at $t=e$, with
$$
\phi(e)=\frac1e.
$$

::: pf-proof

Differentiate:
$$
\phi'(t)
=
\frac{1-\log t}{t^2}.
$$
Thus
$$
\phi'(t)>0
\quad\text{for }1<t<e,
$$
and
$$
\phi'(t)<0
\quad\text{for }t>e.
$$
Hence $\phi$ increases on $(1,e)$ and decreases on $(e,\infty)$, so its
unique maximum is attained at $e$. Since $\log e=1$,
$$
\phi(e)=\frac1e.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For $a>1$ and $x>1$, the inequality
$$
x^a\leq a^x
$$
is equivalent to
$$
\phi(x)\leq\phi(a).
$$

::: pf-proof

Both sides of the original inequality are positive, so taking logarithms
gives
$$
a\log x\leq x\log a.
$$
Since $ax>0$, division by $ax$ yields
$$
\frac{\log x}{x}
\leq
\frac{\log a}{a},
$$
which is exactly the displayed inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If
$$
x^a\leq a^x
$$
for every $x>1$, then
$$
a=e.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the hypothesis gives
$$
\phi(x)\leq\phi(a)
$$
for every $x>1$. Taking $x=e$ gives
$$
\frac1e=\phi(e)\leq\phi(a).
$$
But step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\phi(a)\leq\frac1e,
$$
with equality only when $a=e$. Therefore $a=e$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The value
$$
a=e
$$
satisfies
$$
x^a\leq a^x
$$
for every $x>1$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\phi(x)\leq\phi(e)
$$
for every $x>1$. Step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, with $a=e$, converts this inequality back to
$$
x^e\leq e^x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The complete answer is
$$
\boxed{a=e}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves necessity and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves sufficiency.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives all $a\in(1,\infty)$ with the required property.

:::
