::: {.problem}
For which numbers a in $( 1 , \infty )$ is it true that $x ^ { a } \leqslant a ^ { x }$ for all x in $( 1 , \infty ) \ ?$
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
\phi(t)\coloneqq\frac{\log t}{t},
\qquad
t>1.
$$

<1>1. The function $\phi$ has a unique maximum at $t=e$, with
$$
\phi(e)=\frac1e.
$$

::: {.proof}
Differentiate:
$$
\phi'(t)
=
\frac{1-\log t}{t^2}.
$$
Thus
$$
\phi'(t)>0
\quad\text{for }1<t<e,
$$
and
$$
\phi'(t)<0
\quad\text{for }t>e.
$$
Hence $\phi$ increases on $(1,e)$ and decreases on $(e,\infty)$, so its
unique maximum is attained at $e$. Since $\log e=1$,
$$
\phi(e)=\frac1e.
$$
:::

<1>2. For $a>1$ and $x>1$, the inequality
$$
x^a\leq a^x
$$
is equivalent to
$$
\phi(x)\leq\phi(a).
$$

::: {.proof}
Both sides of the original inequality are positive, so taking logarithms
gives
$$
a\log x\leq x\log a.
$$
Since $ax>0$, division by $ax$ yields
$$
\frac{\log x}{x}
\leq
\frac{\log a}{a},
$$
which is exactly the displayed inequality.
:::

<1>3. If
$$
x^a\leq a^x
$$
for every $x>1$, then
$$
a=e.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, the hypothesis gives
$$
\phi(x)\leq\phi(a)
$$
for every $x>1$. Taking $x=e$ gives
$$
\frac1e=\phi(e)\leq\phi(a).
$$
But step <1>1 gives
$$
\phi(a)\leq\frac1e,
$$
with equality only when $a=e$. Therefore $a=e$.
:::

<1>4. The value
$$
a=e
$$
satisfies
$$
x^a\leq a^x
$$
for every $x>1$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
\phi(x)\leq\phi(e)
$$
for every $x>1$. Step <1>2, with $a=e$, converts this inequality back to
$$
x^e\leq e^x.
$$
:::

<1>5. The complete answer is
$$
\boxed{a=e}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves necessity and step <1>4 proves sufficiency.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives all $a\in(1,\infty)$ with the required property.
:::
:::
