::: {.problem}
Let $T : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ be a diagonalizable linear transformation. Prove that there is an orthonormal basis for $\mathbb{R}^n$ with respect to which $T$ has an upper-triangular matrix.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

There is a basis
$$
v_1,\ldots,v_n
$$
of $\RR^n$ consisting of eigenvectors of $T$.

::: pf-proof

This is the meaning of diagonalizability over $\RR$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $1\leq k\leq n$, the subspace
$$
V_k\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}
$$
is $T$-invariant.

::: pf-proof

Each $v_j$ is an eigenvector, so
$$
T(v_j)\in\operatorname{span}\{v_j\}\subseteq V_k
$$
for $j\leq k$. By linearity, $T(V_k)\subseteq V_k$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Apply Gram--Schmidt to the ordered basis
$$
v_1,\ldots,v_n
$$
and let
$$
q_1,\ldots,q_n
$$
be the resulting orthonormal basis. Then for every $k$,
$$
\operatorname{span}\{q_1,\ldots,q_k\}=V_k.
$$

::: pf-proof

Gram--Schmidt preserves each successive span of the ordered input vectors.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For every $k$,
$$
T(q_k)\in\operatorname{span}\{q_1,\ldots,q_k\}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $q_k\in V_k$. Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives $T(q_k)\in V_k$, and step
[](#s3){.pf-ref} again identifies $V_k$ with the displayed span.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The matrix of $T$ in the orthonormal basis
$$
q_1,\ldots,q_n
$$
is upper triangular.

::: pf-proof

The $k$th column consists of the coordinates of $T(q_k)$. By step [](#s4){.pf-ref},
those coordinates can be nonzero only in rows $1,\ldots,k$. Hence every
entry below the diagonal is zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives the required orthonormal basis and upper-triangular matrix.

:::
