::: {.problem}
Let
$$
\mathcal A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_n\}
$$
be a family of distinct subsets of an $n$-element set $X$. Define a graph $G$ on $\mathcal A$ by joining $A_i$ and $A_j$ whenever
$$
\cardinality{A_i\triangle A_j}=1.
$$
If $A_i\triangle A_j=\{x\}$, label the edge $A_iA_j$ by $x$.

Prove that there is a forest $F\subseteq G$ whose edges use every label that occurs on an edge of $G$. Deduce that there exists $x\in X$ such that
$$
A_1\setminus\{x\},\ldots,A_n\setminus\{x\}
$$
are all distinct.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

In every cycle of $G$, each label occurs an even number of times.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
A_{i_0}A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_r}=A_{i_0}
$$
be a cycle. Traversing an edge labeled $x\in X$ toggles whether $x$ belongs to the current subset. Since the traversal returns to the starting subset, membership of $x$ must be toggled an even number of times. Hence the label $x$ occurs an even number of times on the cycle. This holds for every $x\in X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There is a forest $F\subseteq G$ whose edges use every label occurring on an edge of $G$.


::: pf-proof

Choose a forest $F\subseteq G$ that is maximal with respect to inclusion. Suppose some label $x$ occurs on an edge $e=AB$ of $G$ but on no edge of $F$. If $A$ and $B$ lay in the same component of $F$, the unique path in $F$ from $A$ to $B$, together with $e$, would form a cycle in $G$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the label $x$ would then have to occur on another edge of that cycle, necessarily on the path in $F$, contradicting the choice of $x$. Thus $A$ and $B$ lie in different components of $F$, so adjoining $e$ to $F$ still gives a forest. This contradicts maximality of $F$. Therefore every label used in $G$ already occurs in $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

At most $n-1$ distinct labels occur on edges of $G$.


::: pf-proof

The graph $F$ from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} has $n$ vertices and is a forest, so it has at most $n-1$ edges. Every label occurring in $G$ occurs on at least one edge of $F$, so the number of distinct labels used by $G$ is at most the number of edges of $F$, hence at most $n-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

There exists $x\in X$ such that
$$
\boxed{A_1\setminus\{x\},\ldots,A_n\setminus\{x\}\text{ are all distinct}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The set $X$ has $n$ elements, while step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that at most $n-1$ of them occur as edge labels. Choose $x\in X$ that is not an edge label. Suppose for contradiction that
$$
A_i\setminus\{x\}=A_j\setminus\{x\}
$$
for some $i\ne j$. Since $A_i$ and $A_j$ are distinct, they must differ exactly in membership of $x$, so
$$
A_i\triangle A_j=\{x\}.
$$
Then $A_iA_j$ is an edge of $G$ labeled $x$, contradicting the choice of $x$. Hence all the displayed sets are distinct.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves the forest assertion, and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves the deduction.

:::
