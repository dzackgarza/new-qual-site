::: {.problem}
Let
\[
\mathcal A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_n\}
\]
be a family of distinct subsets of an $n$-element set $X$. Define a graph $G$ on $\mathcal A$ by joining $A_i$ and $A_j$ whenever
\[
|A_i\triangle A_j|=1.
\]
If $A_i\triangle A_j=\{x\}$, label the edge $A_iA_j$ by $x$.

Prove that there is a forest $F\subseteq G$ whose edges use every label that occurs on an edge of $G$. Deduce that there exists $x\in X$ such that
\[
A_1\setminus\{x\},\ldots,A_n\setminus\{x\}
\]
are all distinct.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. In every cycle of $G$, each label occurs an even number of times.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
A_{i_0}A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_r}=A_{i_0}
$$
be a cycle. Traversing an edge labeled $x\in X$ toggles whether $x$ belongs to the current subset. Since the traversal returns to the starting subset, membership of $x$ must be toggled an even number of times. Hence the label $x$ occurs an even number of times on the cycle. This holds for every $x\in X$.
:::

<1>2. There is a forest $F\subseteq G$ whose edges use every label occurring on an edge of $G$.

::: {.proof}
Choose a forest $F\subseteq G$ that is maximal with respect to inclusion. Suppose some label $x$ occurs on an edge $e=AB$ of $G$ but on no edge of $F$. If $A$ and $B$ lay in the same component of $F$, the unique path in $F$ from $A$ to $B$, together with $e$, would form a cycle in $G$. By step <1>1, the label $x$ would then have to occur on another edge of that cycle, necessarily on the path in $F$, contradicting the choice of $x$. Thus $A$ and $B$ lie in different components of $F$, so adjoining $e$ to $F$ still gives a forest. This contradicts maximality of $F$. Therefore every label used in $G$ already occurs in $F$.
:::

<1>3. At most $n-1$ distinct labels occur on edges of $G$.

::: {.proof}
The graph $F$ from step <1>2 has $n$ vertices and is a forest, so it has at most $n-1$ edges. Every label occurring in $G$ occurs on at least one edge of $F$, so the number of distinct labels used by $G$ is at most the number of edges of $F$, hence at most $n-1$.
:::

<1>4. There exists $x\in X$ such that
$$
\boxed{A_1\setminus\{x\},\ldots,A_n\setminus\{x\}\text{ are all distinct}.}
$$

::: {.proof}
The set $X$ has $n$ elements, while step <1>3 shows that at most $n-1$ of them occur as edge labels. Choose $x\in X$ that is not an edge label. Suppose for contradiction that
$$
A_i\setminus\{x\}=A_j\setminus\{x\}
$$
for some $i\ne j$. Since $A_i$ and $A_j$ are distinct, they must differ exactly in membership of $x$, so
$$
A_i\triangle A_j=\{x\}.
$$
Then $A_iA_j$ is an edge of $G$ labeled $x$, contradicting the choice of $x$. Hence all the displayed sets are distinct.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves the forest assertion, and step <1>4 proves the deduction.
:::
