::: {.problem}
Prove that a tree $T$ has a perfect matching if and only if, for every vertex $v$ of $T$, the number of odd components of
$$
T-v
$$
is $1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $T$ has a perfect matching, then for every vertex $v$, the graph $T-v$ has exactly one odd component.


::: pf-proof

Let $M$ be a perfect matching and let $vw\in M$. In $T-v$, let $C$ be the component containing $w$. Every component of $T-v$ other than $C$ is matched entirely by edges of $M$, so each such component has even order. In $C$, every vertex except $w$ is still matched by an edge of $M$ contained in $C$, so $\cardinality{C}-1$ is even and $\cardinality{C}$ is odd. Thus $C$ is the unique odd component of $T-v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Conversely, assume that for every vertex $v$, the graph $T-v$ has exactly one odd component. Then $\cardinality{V(T)}$ is even.


::: pf-proof

If $T$ had one vertex, deleting it would leave no odd component, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence $T$ has a leaf $\ell$. The graph $T-\ell$ is connected, so by hypothesis its unique component has odd order. Therefore
$$
\cardinality{V(T)}=\cardinality{V(T-\ell)}+1
$$
is even.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For each vertex $v$, there is a unique neighbor $m(v)$ such that the component of $T-v$ containing $m(v)$ has odd order, and one has
$$
m(m(v))=v.
$$


::: pf-proof

The components of $T-v$ are in one-to-one correspondence with the neighbors of $v$, so the hypothesis gives a unique neighbor $m(v)$ whose component has odd order.

Let $u=m(v)$ and delete the edge $uv$. Denote by $C_u$ and $C_v$ the two resulting components containing $u$ and $v$, respectively. The component of $T-v$ containing $u$ is exactly $C_u$, so $\cardinality{C_u}$ is odd. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\cardinality{C_u}+\cardinality{C_v}=\cardinality{V(T)}
$$
is even, hence $\cardinality{C_v}$ is also odd. But $C_v$ is the component of $T-u$ containing $v$. Since $T-u$ has exactly one odd component, its unique odd component is $C_v$, so $m(u)=v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The edges
$$
\boxed{M=\{vm(v):v\in V(T)\}}
$$
form a perfect matching of $T$ after duplicate edges are identified.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, whenever $m(v)=u$, one also has $m(u)=v$. Thus the map $m$ partitions the vertex set into disjoint two-element sets $\{u,v\}$ with $uv\in E(T)$. Taking one edge from each such pair gives a matching that meets every vertex exactly once, hence a perfect matching.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves the forward implication, and steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove the converse.

:::
