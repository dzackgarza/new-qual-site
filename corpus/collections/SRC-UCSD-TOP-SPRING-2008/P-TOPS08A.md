::: problem
Calculate the fundamental group of $\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2$ is the Klein bottle.
Proof: the connected sum of two projective planes is homeomorphic to the Klein bottle (both are the non-orientable surface of Euler characteristic $0$).

<1>2. $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) = \ZZ/2$.
Proof: $\mathbb{RP}^2$ has universal cover $S^2$ (a double cover).

<1>3. By van Kampen, $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2) = \ZZ/2 * \ZZ/2$.
Proof: the connected sum is the union of two punctured projective planes (each homotopy equivalent to $S^1$, with fundamental group $\ZZ/2$ generated by the boundary circle) glued along their boundary circles; van Kampen gives the free product.

<1>4. $\ZZ/2 * \ZZ/2 = \langle a, b \mid a^2 = b^2 = 1 \rangle$.
Proof: presentation of the free product.

<1>5. This is the infinite dihedral group $D_\infty$, which is also the fundamental group of the Klein bottle $\langle a, b \mid a b a^{-1} = b^{-1} \rangle$.
Proof: the two presentations are equivalent (the Klein bottle group is $\langle a, b \mid a b a^{-1} b = 1 \rangle$, isomorphic to $\langle a, b \mid a^2 = b^2 = 1 \rangle$ via the substitution).

<1>6. Hence $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2) \cong \ZZ/2 * \ZZ/2 \cong D_\infty$.
Proof: <1>3–<1>5.

<1>7. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>6.
:::
