::: problem
Let $\mathbb{CP}^2$ be the complex projective plane.

(a) Compute $\pi_1(\mathbb{CP}^2)$.

(b) Could $\mathbb{CP}^2$ be an $n$-sheeted covering space ($n > 1$) of another space $D$?
If so, compute $H_*(D; \mathbb{Z})$.
If not, explain your reason.

(c) Compute $H_*(\mathbb{CP}^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z})$.

(d) Compute $H_*(\mathbb{CP}^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}/2)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. One has
$$\boxed{\pi_1(\mathbb{CP}^2)=0.}$$
::: {.proof}
The standard CW decomposition of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ has one cell in dimensions $0,2,4$ and no $1$-cells, so its fundamental group is trivial.
:::

<1>2. The space $\mathbb{CP}^2$ cannot be a nontrivial connected finite-sheeted covering space of another space $D$.
::: {.proof}
If it were an $n$-sheeted connected cover with $n>1$, then its deck group need not equal the full fundamental group quotient unless the cover is regular, but because $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is simply connected it would be the universal cover and the deck group would be $\pi_1(D)$ of order $n$, acting freely on $\mathbb{CP}^2$. Choose an element $g$ of prime order in this finite group. On $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$, $g^*$ is multiplication by $\pm1$; if the prime is odd it must be $+1$, while for order $2$ either sign is possible. Since $H^4$ is generated by the square of the degree-$2$ generator, $g^*$ acts as $+1$ on $H^4$. Thus the Lefschetz number is either $3$ or $1$, in either case nonzero, so $g$ has a fixed point. This contradicts freeness of deck transformations.
:::

<1>3. The integral homology of $\mathbb{CP}^2\times\mathbb{RP}^2$ is
$$\boxed{H_k(-;\mathbb Z)\cong\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,2,4,\\
\mathbb Z/2,&k=1,3,5,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}}$$
::: {.proof}
The homology of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is free, equal to $\mathbb Z$ in degrees $0,2,4$, while $H_*(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Z)$ is $\mathbb Z$ in degree $0$, $\mathbb Z/2$ in degree $1$, and zero otherwise. Hence the integral Künneth theorem has no Tor terms, and tensoring produces the displayed groups.
:::

<1>4. With $\mathbb F_2$ coefficients,
$$\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_k(\mathbb{CP}^2\times\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb F_2)=(1,1,2,1,2,1,1)}$$
for $k=0,\dots,6$.
::: {.proof}
The mod-$2$ Poincaré polynomials are
$$1+t^2+t^4\quad\text{and}\quad1+t+t^2.$$
Their product is
$$1+t+2t^2+t^3+2t^4+t^5+t^6,$$
and field-coefficient Künneth identifies these coefficients with the homology dimensions.
:::
