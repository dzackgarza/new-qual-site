::: {.problem}
(a) Show that if $I$ is an ideal in $\CC[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ and $V(I)$ is finite, then $\CC[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I$ is finite dimensional when considered as a vector space of $\CC$.

(b) Find a reduced Gröbner basis for $I=\langle x^2+xy+y,\ xy+y^2\rangle$ with respect to the graded lexicographic order where $x>y$.

(c) Show that $x^2+y^2\in\sqrt{I}\setminus I$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own; the Gröbner basis of (b) is verified twice, by the leading-term criterion and by exhibiting the three $S$-polynomials, and the standard monomials are identified so that the non-membership in (c) has a basis behind it. No retained set solves this exercise, and the sets searched are recorded in the audit trail.

Let $R=\CC[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$, $\mathfrak m=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})$, and for (b) and (c) let $R=\CC[x,y]$ with the graded lexicographic order $x>y$ and
$$
I=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle,\qquad f_{1}=x^{2}+xy+y,\quad f_{2}=xy+y^{2}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a-residue}
(a) If $V(I)=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}$ then $R/\sqrt I\cong\CC^{r}$ and $(\sqrt I)^{N}\subseteq I$ for some $N$.

::: pf-proof
The radical of $I$ is the intersection of the maximal ideals of the points of $V(I)$ (Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, in the form $\sqrt I=I(V(I))$), so $\sqrt I=\mathfrak m_{a_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak m_{a_{r}}$. Distinct maximal ideals $\mathfrak m_{a_{i}}$ are pairwise comaximal, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives $R/\sqrt I\cong\prod_{i}R/\mathfrak m_{a_{i}}\cong\CC^{r}$, which is finite dimensional over $\CC$. Each $a\in\sqrt I$ satisfies $a^{k}=0$ in $R/I$ for some $k$, because some power of $a$ lies in $I$; the ideal $\sqrt I/I$ of the Noetherian ring $R/I$ is therefore finitely generated by nilpotent elements, and a finite sum of nilpotent ideals is nilpotent, so $(\sqrt I)^{N}\subseteq I$ for some $N\ge1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-dimension}
(a) If $V(I)$ is finite then $R/I$ is finite dimensional over $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#a-residue){.pf-ref}, $R/I$ is a quotient of $R/(\sqrt I)^{N}$, so it suffices to bound the dimension of the latter. The chain
$$
R\supset\sqrt I\supset(\sqrt I)^{2}\supset\cdots\supset(\sqrt I)^{N}
$$
gives a filtration of $R/(\sqrt I)^{N}$ whose successive quotients are $(\sqrt I)^{k}/(\sqrt I)^{k+1}$, $k=0,\ldots,N-1$. Each of these is a finitely generated module over $R/\sqrt I$ — it is annihilated by $\sqrt I$ and is finitely generated over the Noetherian ring $R$ — hence finite dimensional over $\CC$, because a finitely generated module over the finite-dimensional algebra $R/\sqrt I$ is a quotient of a finite free module. A finite succession of finite-dimensional extensions is finite dimensional, so $\dim_{\CC}R/(\sqrt I)^{N}$ is finite, and so is $\dim_{\CC}R/I$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #members}
(b) $y^{2}\in I$, $xy\in I$ and $x^{2}+y\in I$.

::: pf-proof
The $S$-polynomial of $f_{1}$ and $f_{2}$ with respect to the least common multiple $x^{2}y$ of their leading monomials is
$$
S(f_{1},f_{2})=y f_{1}-x f_{2}=(x^{2}y+xy^{2}+y^{2})-(x^{2}y+xy^{2})=y^{2},
$$
so $y^{2}\in I$. Then $xy=f_{2}-y^{2}\in I$ and $x^{2}+y=f_{1}-f_{2}+y^{2}\in I$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #groebner}
(b) $G=\{y^{2},xy,x^{2}+y\}$ is a Gröbner basis for $I$.

::: pf-proof
The leading monomials of the members of $G$ are $y^{2}$, $xy$, $x^{2}$ with respect to the graded lexicographic order, so $\langle \operatorname{LT}(G)\rangle=\langle x^{2},xy,y^{2}\rangle=\mathfrak m^{2}$. Since $G\subset I$ by step [](#members){.pf-ref}, $\langle\operatorname{LT}(G)\rangle\subseteq\langle\operatorname{LT}(I)\rangle$; and $I\subseteq\mathfrak m^{2}$ because both generators are sums of monomials of degree at least $2$, so the leading monomial of any nonzero element of $I$ has degree at least $2$ and hence lies in $\mathfrak m^{2}$. So $\langle\operatorname{LT}(I)\rangle=\mathfrak m^{2}=\langle\operatorname{LT}(G)\rangle$, which is the criterion for $G$ to be a Gröbner basis. Directly, the three $S$-polynomials are $S(y^{2},xy)=xy^{2}-xy^{2}=0$, $S(xy,x^{2}+y)=x(xy)-y(x^{2}+y)=-y^{2}$, and $S(y^{2},x^{2}+y)=x^{2}y^{2}-y^{2}(x^{2}+y)=-y^{3}$, and both nonzero ones reduce to $0$ by the single step of subtracting a multiple of $y^{2}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #reduced}
(b) $G$ is reduced, so it is the reduced Gröbner basis of $I$.

::: pf-proof
Each member of $G$ is monic: $y^{2}$, $xy$ and $x^{2}+y$ all have leading coefficient $1$. A monomial of a member of $G$ must not be divisible by the leading monomial of another member: the monomials occurring are $y^{2}$, $xy$, $x^{2}$ and $y$; the last is divisible by neither $y^{2}$ nor $xy$, and each of $x^{2}$, $xy$, $y^{2}$ is divisible by none of the other two (they are distinct monomials of degree $2$). So $G$ is reduced, and the reduced Gröbner basis of a given ideal and order is unique, so $G$ is the one asked for.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #standard}
(b) The standard monomials of $G$ are $1,x,y$, and their images form a $\CC$-basis of $R/I$.

::: pf-proof
The standard monomials are those not divisible by any element of $\{x^{2},xy,y^{2}\}$, namely $1,x,y$. For a Gröbner basis $G$, the remainders on division by $G$ are the $\CC$-linear combinations of the standard monomials, and division exhibits every $f\in R$ as an element of $I$ plus such a combination (the division algorithm); uniqueness of the remainder follows because a nonzero combination of standard monomials has leading monomial among them, which is not in $\langle\operatorname{LT}(I)\rangle$ and so cannot lie in $I$. Hence the three classes are a basis and $\dim_{\CC}R/I=3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not-in}
(c) $x^{2}+y^{2}\notin I$.

::: pf-proof
Division by $G$ gives
$$
x^{2}+y^{2}=(x^{2}+y)+y^{2}-y\equiv -y \pmod I .
$$
The remainder $-y$ is a nonzero combination of the standard monomials, so its class in $R/I$ is nonzero by step [](#standard){.pf-ref}, and $x^{2}+y^{2}\notin I$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #in-radical}
(c) $x^{2}+y^{2}\in\sqrt I$, so $x^{2}+y^{2}\in\sqrt I\setminus I$.

::: pf-proof
In $R/I$ the relations of step [](#members){.pf-ref} read $y^{2}=0$ and $x^{2}=-y$. Hence
$$
(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}=x^{4}+2x^{2}y^{2}+y^{4}=(x^{2})^{2}+2x^{2}y^{2}+(y^{2})^{2}=(-y)^{2}+0+0=y^{2}=0
$$
in $R/I$, so $(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}\in I$ and $x^{2}+y^{2}$ lies in the radical of $I$. With step [](#not-in){.pf-ref} this gives
$$
\boxed{x^{2}+y^{2}\in\sqrt I\setminus I.}
$$
:::
