::: {.problem}
Consider the equations
\begin{align*}
xy+x^2&=1,\\
y^2-2x^2&=-2.
\end{align*}

(a) Let $I$ be the ideal of $\CC[x,y]$ generated by these equations.
Find the Gröbner basis for $I$ relative to lexicographic order where $y>x$.

(b) Find a Gröbner basis for $\CC[x]\cap I$.

(c) Find all solutions to these equations that lie in $\CC^2$.

(d) Find a vector space basis for $\CC[x,y]/I$.
:::

::: {.solution}
With lexicographic order and $y>x$, the leading terms of $f_1=xy+x^2-1$ and $f_2=y^2-2x^2+2$ are $xy$ and $y^2$, and the reduced Gröbner basis of $I=(f_1,f_2)$ is $G=\{y-x^3+x,\ x^4-1\}$. The two inclusions are computed below rather than asserted, and one arithmetic step of the card's earlier prose is corrected: substituting $y=x^3-x$ into $f_2$ gives $(x^4-1)(x^2-2)$, not the polynomial the prose displayed. Buchberger's criterion then applies because the two leading monomials $y$ and $x^4$ are relatively prime. The argument is this card's own: no retained solution set covers this exam.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #order}
In the lexicographic order with $y>x$, the monomials are compared first by their exponent of $y$; so $\operatorname{LT}(f_1)=xy$, $\operatorname{LT}(f_2)=y^2$, $\operatorname{LT}(y-x^3+x)=y$ and $\operatorname{LT}(x^4-1)=x^4$.

::: pf-proof
In lexicographic order the monomials are compared by the exponent vector from the largest variable down: the variable order is $y>x$, so a monomial with a larger exponent of $y$ is larger, and among monomials with equal $y$-exponent the one with larger $x$-exponent is larger. $f_1=xy+x^2-1$: its monomials are $xy$ (y-exponent $1$), $x^2$ and $1$ (y-exponent $0$), so $xy$ is the largest. $f_2=y^2-2x^2+2$: $y^2$ has y-exponent $2$, the others $0$, so $y^2$ leads. $y-x^3+x$ has $y$ with y-exponent $1$ and the rest with $0$, so $y$ leads. $x^4-1$ has both y-exponents $0$ and $x^4>x^0$, so $x^4$ leads.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #g-in-I}
$y-x^3+x\in I$ and $x^4-1\in I$.

::: pf-proof
Compute in $I$: $(x-y)f_1+xf_2=(x-y)(xy+x^2-1)+x(y^2-2x^2+2)$. Expanding the first product gives $x^2y+x^3-x-xy^2-x^2y+y$, and the second is $xy^2-2x^3+2x$; adding, the $x^2y$ and $xy^2$ terms cancel, leaving $x^3-x+y-2x^3+2x=y-x^3+x$. So $y-x^3+x\in I$. Next, $f_1-x(y-x^3+x)=(xy+x^2-1)-(xy-x^4+x^2)=x^4-1$, and both $f_1$ and $y-x^3+x$ lie in $I$, so $x^4-1\in I$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #f-in-G}
$f_1$ and $f_2$ lie in the ideal $(G)$ generated by $G=\{y-x^3+x,x^4-1\}$.

::: pf-proof
Modulo $y-x^3+x$ one may replace $y$ by $x^3-x$. Then $f_1=xy+x^2-1=x(x^3-x)+x^2-1=x^4-x^2+x^2-1=x^4-1$, which is the second generator, so $f_1=x(y-x^3+x)+(x^4-1)\in(G)$. For $f_2$: $y^2=(x^3-x)^2=x^6-2x^4+x^2$, so
$$
f_2=y^2-2x^2+2=x^6-2x^4+x^2-2x^2+2=x^6-2x^4-x^2+2=(x^4-1)(x^2-2),
$$
the last identity by expansion: $(x^4-1)(x^2-2)=x^6-2x^4-x^2+2$. Hence $f_2$ is a multiple of the second generator modulo the first, so $f_2\in(G)$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #gb}
$G$ is a Gröbner basis of $I$, and it is reduced.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#g-in-I){.pf-ref} and [](#f-in-G){.pf-ref} give $I=(G)$: the generators of $G$ lie in $I$ and the generators of $I$ lie in $(G)$. For the Gröbner property, the leading monomials of $G$ are $y$ and $x^4$, which are relatively prime, so the Buchberger criterion for such a pair says $S(y-x^3+x,x^4-1)$ reduces to $0$ with respect to $G$; with only one pair, $G$ is a Gröbner basis. Reduced: the leading coefficient of each element is $1$, and no non-leading monomial of either element is divisible by $y$ or $x^4$ — the non-leading monomials are $-x^3$ and $x$ in the first and $-1$ in the second, none of them divisible by $y$ or by $x^4$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #elimination}
(b) $I\cap\CC[x]=(x^4-1)$.

::: pf-proof
The elimination theorem says that $G\cap\CC[x]$ generates $I\cap\CC[x]$, and $G\cap\CC[x]=\{x^4-1\}$ by step [](#order){.pf-ref} since the other element involves $y$. So $I\cap\CC[x]=(x^4-1)$, and the single element is a Gröbner basis for the elimination ideal in the induced order.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #solutions}
(c) The solutions in $\CC^2$ are $(1,0)$, $(-1,0)$, $(i,-2i)$ and $(-i,2i)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#gb){.pf-ref} the common zeros of $f_1,f_2$ are the common zeros of $G$: the ideal of a Gröbner basis is the same ideal, so the zero sets agree. From $x^4-1=0$ we get $x\in\{1,-1,i,-i\}$, and from $y-x^3+x=0$ then $y=x^3-x=x(x^2-1)$. For $x=1$ and $x=-1$ this gives $y=0$; for $x=i$, $x^2=-1$ so $y=i(-2)=-2i$; for $x=-i$, $y=-i(-2)=2i$. Substituting one of them back as a check: at $(i,-2i)$, $xy+x^2-1=i(-2i)+(-1)-1=2-1-1=0$ and $y^2-2x^2+2=-4+2+2=0$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #basis}
(d) The residue classes of $1,x,x^2,x^3$ form a $\CC$-basis of $\CC[x,y]/I$, so $\dim_\CC\CC[x,y]/I=4$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#gb){.pf-ref} the leading monomials of $I$ are $y$ and $x^4$, so the standard monomials — those divisible by neither — are exactly $1,x,x^2,x^3$. The standard monomials of a Gröbner basis form a basis of the quotient as a $\CC$-vector space, so these four classes are a basis and the dimension is $4$. This agrees with the four solutions counted in step [](#solutions){.pf-ref} without multiplicity.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
Parts (a) to (d) hold with $G=\{y-x^3+x,\ x^4-1\}$ the reduced lexicographic Gröbner basis, $I\cap\CC[x]=(x^4-1)$, the four displayed solutions, and $\dim\CC[x,y]/I=4$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#gb){.pf-ref} gives (a), step [](#elimination){.pf-ref} gives (b), step [](#solutions){.pf-ref} gives (c) and step [](#basis){.pf-ref} gives (d).
:::
