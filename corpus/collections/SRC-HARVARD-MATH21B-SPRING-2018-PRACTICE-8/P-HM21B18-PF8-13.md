::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
\dot x=x^2-y^2+3,
\qquad
\dot y=-x+2y-3.
$$

(a) Find the nullclines.

(b) Find the unique equilibrium point.

(c) Find the eigenvalues of the Jacobian at the equilibrium.

(d) Decide whether the equilibrium is stable.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The nullclines are
$$
\boxed{y^2-x^2=3}
$$
for $\dot x=0$ and
$$
\boxed{x=2y-3}
$$
for $\dot y=0$.


::: pf-proof

Setting the first right-hand side equal to zero gives
$$
x^2-y^2+3=0,
$$
equivalently $y^2-x^2=3$. Setting the second right-hand side equal to zero gives
$$
-x+2y-3=0,
$$
equivalently $x=2y-3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The unique equilibrium point is
$$
\boxed{(1,2).}
$$


::: pf-proof

At an equilibrium both nullcline equations from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} hold. Substituting $x=2y-3$ into $x^2-y^2+3=0$ gives
$$
(2y-3)^2-y^2+3
=3(y-2)^2
=0.
$$
Thus $y=2$, and then $x=2(2)-3=1$. The squared factor shows that no other intersection occurs.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The eigenvalues of the Jacobian at the equilibrium are
$$
\boxed{0\text{ and }4.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The Jacobian is
$$
DF(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2x&-2y\\
-1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
At $(1,2)$ this becomes
$$
DF(1,2)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-4\\
-1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its characteristic polynomial is
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}2-\lambda&-4\\-1&2-\lambda\end{pmatrix}
=(2-\lambda)^2-4
=\lambda(\lambda-4),
$$
so the eigenvalues are $0$ and $4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The equilibrium $(1,2)$ is $\boxed{\text{unstable}}$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the linearization has an eigenvalue $4$ with positive real part. The instability part of the linearization theorem for autonomous systems therefore implies that the equilibrium is unstable. The zero eigenvalue does not affect this conclusion: a single eigenvalue with positive real part is already sufficient for instability.

:::
