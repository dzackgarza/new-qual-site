::: {.problem}
Consider
\[
\dot x=x^2-y^2+3,
\qquad
\dot y=-x+2y-3.
\]

(a) Find the nullclines.

(b) Find the unique equilibrium point.

(c) Find the eigenvalues of the Jacobian at the equilibrium.

(d) Decide whether the equilibrium is stable.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The nullclines are
\[
\boxed{y^2-x^2=3}
\]
for $\dot x=0$ and
\[
\boxed{x=2y-3}
\]
for $\dot y=0$.

::: {.proof}
Setting the first right-hand side equal to zero gives
\[
x^2-y^2+3=0,
\]
equivalently $y^2-x^2=3$. Setting the second right-hand side equal to zero gives
\[
-x+2y-3=0,
\]
equivalently $x=2y-3$.
:::

<1>2. The unique equilibrium point is
\[
\boxed{(1,2).}
\]

::: {.proof}
At an equilibrium both nullcline equations from step <1>1 hold. Substituting $x=2y-3$ into $x^2-y^2+3=0$ gives
\[
(2y-3)^2-y^2+3
=3(y-2)^2
=0.
\]
Thus $y=2$, and then $x=2(2)-3=1$. The squared factor shows that no other intersection occurs.
:::

<1>3. The eigenvalues of the Jacobian at the equilibrium are
\[
\boxed{0\text{ and }4.}
\]

::: {.proof}
The Jacobian is
\[
DF(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2x&-2y\\
-1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
At $(1,2)$ this becomes
\[
DF(1,2)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-4\\
-1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its characteristic polynomial is
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}2-\lambda&-4\\-1&2-\lambda\end{pmatrix}
=(2-\lambda)^2-4
=\lambda(\lambda-4),
\]
so the eigenvalues are $0$ and $4$.
:::

<1>4. The equilibrium $(1,2)$ is $\boxed{\text{unstable}}$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, the linearization has an eigenvalue $4$ with positive real part. The instability part of the linearization theorem for autonomous systems therefore implies that the equilibrium is unstable. The zero eigenvalue does not affect this conclusion: a single eigenvalue with positive real part is already sufficient for instability.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>4 answer parts (a)--(d), respectively.
:::
:::
