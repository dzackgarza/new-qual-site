::: {.problem}
Find a single $3\times3$ matrix $A$ such that

1. $\ker A$ is the line spanned by $(1,1,1)^T$;
2. $(1,0,1)^T$ is an eigenvector of $A$;
3. $(1,2,-1)^T\in\operatorname{im}A$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One matrix satisfying all three requirements is
$$
\boxed{
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&-2\\
2&0&-2\\
-1&1&0
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The remaining steps verify the three required properties of this matrix.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The kernel of $A$ is $\operatorname{span}\{(1,1,1)^T\}$.


::: pf-proof

The sum of the three columns of $A$ is zero, so
$$
A(1,1,1)^T=0.
$$
Thus $(1,1,1)^T\in\ker A$. The first two columns,
$$
(1,2,-1)^T,
\qquad
(1,0,1)^T,
$$
are linearly independent, so $\operatorname{rank}A\ge2$. Since $A$ has a nonzero kernel vector, its rank is at most $2$. Therefore $\operatorname{rank}A=2$, and rank--nullity gives $\dim\ker A=1$. The nonzero vector $(1,1,1)^T$ therefore spans the entire kernel.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The vector $(1,0,1)^T$ is an eigenvector of $A$ with eigenvalue $-1$.


::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives
$$
A\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}-1\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix}
=-
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The vector $(1,2,-1)^T$ lies in $\operatorname{im}A$.


::: pf-proof

It is the first column of $A$, equivalently
$$
A(1,0,0)^T=(1,2,-1)^T.
$$

:::

:::

:::
