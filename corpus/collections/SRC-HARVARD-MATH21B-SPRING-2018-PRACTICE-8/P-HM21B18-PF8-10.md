::: {.problem}
Does the continuous linear system
\[
\dot x=Bx
\]
have a stable origin when
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1&-9&-9&-8\\
0&0&0&-1&-9\\
5&0&5&0&-5\\
1&9&0&0&0\\
1&9&9&8&0
\end{pmatrix}?
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The trace of $B$ is $5$.

::: {.proof}
The diagonal entries are $0,0,5,0,0$, so
\[
\operatorname{tr}B=5.
\]
:::

<1>2. If the origin of $\dot x=Bx$ is stable, then every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $B$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re}\lambda\le0$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that $\lambda=a+ib$ is an eigenvalue with $a>0$, with complex eigenvector $v=p+iq$. Then
\[
e^{\lambda t}v
=e^{at}(\cos bt+i\sin bt)(p+iq)
\]
is a complex solution of the complexified system. Its real and imaginary parts are real solutions of $\dot x=Bx$. At least one is nonzero, and its oscillatory factor is periodic and not identically zero, while the common factor $e^{at}$ is unbounded. After scaling its initial value arbitrarily small, that solution still eventually leaves a fixed neighborhood of the origin. Hence the origin would be unstable, a contradiction.
:::

<1>3. The origin is $\boxed{\text{not stable}}$.

::: {.proof}
The eigenvalues of $B$, counted with algebraic multiplicity, satisfy
\[
\sum_j\lambda_j=\operatorname{tr}B=5
\]
by step <1>1. Taking real parts gives
\[
\sum_j\operatorname{Re}\lambda_j=5.
\]
Therefore at least one eigenvalue has positive real part, contradicting the necessary condition in step <1>2. Thus the origin is unstable.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 answers the question.
:::
:::
