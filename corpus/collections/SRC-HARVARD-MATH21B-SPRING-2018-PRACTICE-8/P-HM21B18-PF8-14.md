::: {.problem}
Solve
$$
u_t=-u_{xxxx}+u_{xx}-u
$$
on $[0,\pi]$ with initial condition
$$
u(x,0)=\abs{\sin x}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

On $[0,\pi]$ one has $\abs{\sin x}=\sin x$.


::: pf-proof

The sine function is nonnegative on $[0,\pi]$, so taking its absolute value does not change it there.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The function $\sin x$ is an eigenfunction of $T=-D^4+D^2-I$ with eigenvalue $-3$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
D^2\sin x=-\sin x,
\qquad
D^4\sin x=\sin x,
$$
one obtains
$$
T(\sin x)
=-\sin x-\sin x-\sin x
=-3\sin x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

A solution with the prescribed initial data is
$$
\boxed{u(x,t)=e^{-3t}\sin x.}
$$


::: pf-proof

For the displayed function,
$$
u_t=-3e^{-3t}\sin x,
\qquad
u_{xx}=-e^{-3t}\sin x,
\qquad
u_{xxxx}=e^{-3t}\sin x.
$$
Hence
$$
-u_{xxxx}+u_{xx}-u
=-3e^{-3t}\sin x
=u_t.
$$
At $t=0$, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
u(x,0)=\sin x=\abs{\sin x}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} verifies both the differential equation and the stated initial condition. Since no boundary conditions are specified, the problem statement by itself does not assert a uniqueness class for solutions on the bounded interval.

:::
