::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be $1$ on $[\pi/3,2\pi/3]$ and $0$ elsewhere on $[0,\pi]$.

(a) Write $f$ as a Fourier sine series.

(b) Solve $u_t=\mu u_{xx}$ with $u(x,0)=f(x)$.

(c) Solve $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$ with $u(x,0)=f(x)$ and $u_t(x,0)=0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

For $n\ge1$, define
$$
b_n=\frac{2}{\pi n}\left(\cos\frac{n\pi}{3}-\cos\frac{2n\pi}{3}\right).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Fourier sine series of $f$ is
$$
\boxed{f(x)\sim\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin(nx).}
$$


::: pf-proof

The sine coefficient on $[0,\pi]$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n
&=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac2\pi\int_{\pi/3}^{2\pi/3}\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac{2}{\pi n}
\left(\cos\frac{n\pi}{3}-\cos\frac{2n\pi}{3}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$
which is the stated coefficient. Since $f$ is piecewise smooth, the sine series converges to $f$ at every continuity point and to the midpoint value $1/2$ at the two jump points $\pi/3$ and $2\pi/3$; in particular it represents $f$ in $L^2(0,\pi)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For $\mu>0$, with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions implicit in the sine-series formulation, the heat-equation solution is
$$
\boxed{
u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty
b_n e^{-\mu n^2t}\sin(nx).}
$$


::: pf-proof

For each $n$,
$$
u_n(x,t)=e^{-\mu n^2t}\sin(nx)
$$
satisfies
$$
(u_n)_t=-\mu n^2u_n,
\qquad
(u_n)_{xx}=-n^2u_n,
$$
and therefore $(u_n)_t=\mu(u_n)_{xx}$. Each mode vanishes at $x=0$ and $x=\pi$. At $t=0$, the displayed series reduces to the Fourier sine series from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, so it has the prescribed initial data in the Fourier-series sense.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

With the same homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, the wave-equation solution with zero initial velocity is
$$
\boxed{
u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty
b_n\cos(nct)\sin(nx).}
$$


::: pf-proof

For each $n$,
$$
u_n(x,t)=\cos(nct)\sin(nx)
$$
satisfies
$$
(u_n)_{tt}=-n^2c^2u_n,
\qquad
(u_n)_{xx}=-n^2u_n,
$$
so $(u_n)_{tt}=c^2(u_n)_{xx}$. Again each mode vanishes at $x=0$ and $x=\pi$. At $t=0$, the series becomes the sine series from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, while differentiating in $t$ gives factors $-nc\sin(nct)$, which vanish at $t=0$. Thus the initial displacement is $f$ in the Fourier-series sense and the initial velocity is zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives the standard Dirichlet heat solution for part (b), and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the standard Dirichlet wave solution for part (c). Without boundary conditions, parts (b) and (c) do not determine unique solutions on $[0,\pi]$.

:::
