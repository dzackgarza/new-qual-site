::: {.problem}
Suppose $f$ has an isolated singularity at $a\in\mathbb{C}$ and $\operatorname{Re} f$ is bounded above in a deleted neighborhood of $a$.
Prove that the singularity is removable.
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose $M\in\mathbb R$ and $r>0$ such that
\[
\operatorname{Re}f(z)\le M
\qquad(0<|z-a|<r).
\]
Set
\[
g(z)=e^{f(z)-M}.
\]
Then $g$ is holomorphic on the punctured disk and
\[
|g(z)|=e^{\operatorname{Re}f(z)-M}\le1.
\]
Hence the isolated singularity of $g$ at $a$ is removable. Let
$\widetilde g$ denote the holomorphic extension.

We claim $\widetilde g(a)\ne0$. If instead $\widetilde g(a)=0$, then for some
$m\ge1$,
\[
\widetilde g(z)=(z-a)^m h(z),\qquad h(a)\ne0.
\]
On the punctured disk,
\[
\frac{g'(z)}{g(z)}=f'(z),
\]
while the factorization gives
\[
\frac{g'(z)}{g(z)}=\frac{m}{z-a}+\frac{h'(z)}{h(z)}.
\]
Thus $f'$ has residue $m$ at $a$. But the derivative of a holomorphic
function on a punctured disk has residue $0$: in the Laurent expansion of
$f$, differentiating termwise produces no $(z-a)^{-1}$ term. This is a
contradiction.

Therefore $\widetilde g(a)\ne0$. Shrinking the disk if necessary,
$\widetilde g$ is nowhere zero there, so it has a holomorphic logarithm $L$.
On the punctured disk,
\[
e^{L(z)}=\widetilde g(z)=e^{f(z)-M}.
\]
Hence $f(z)-M-L(z)$ takes values in $2\pi i\mathbb Z$ and is continuous, so
it is constant on the punctured disk. Therefore $f$ agrees there with a
holomorphic function $M+L+2\pi i k$, and the singularity at $a$ is removable.
:::
