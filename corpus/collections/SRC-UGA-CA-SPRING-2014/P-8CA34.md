::: problem
Suppose $\ell$ is a line and $C$ is a circle in the plane.
Under what conditions is there a Möbius map which simultaneously sends $\ell\cup\{\infty\}$ and $C$ to concentric circles?
Justify your conclusions.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A Möbius map sends generalized circles (circles or lines) to generalized circles.
Proof: Möbius transformations are conformal and preserve the class of generalized circles.

<1>2. Two generalized circles can be sent to concentric circles iff they are disjoint (do not intersect).
<2>1. Concentric circles are disjoint.
Proof: two distinct concentric circles have no common point.
<2>2. Conversely, two disjoint generalized circles can be mapped to concentric circles.
Proof: a Möbius map sending a point of one circle to $\infty$ turns it into a line; then a further map can be arranged so the two images are concentric circles (this is the standard fact that disjoint circles are "separated" and can be normalized to concentric circles).

<1>3. Hence the condition is that $\ell \cup \{\infty\}$ and $C$ are disjoint.
<2>1. $\ell \cup \{\infty\}$ is a generalized circle (a line together with the point at infinity).
Proof: a line is a circle through $\infty$.
<2>2. The condition is that $\ell$ does not meet $C$ (in the finite plane).
Proof: $\ell \cup \{\infty\}$ meets $C$ iff $\ell$ meets $C$ (since $\infty \notin C$ for a finite circle).

<1>4. Therefore a Möbius map sending $\ell \cup \{\infty\}$ and $C$ to concentric circles exists iff the line $\ell$ and the circle $C$ are disjoint.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>3.

<1>5. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>4.
:::
