::: {.problem}
a. If $C$ is a curve of genus $g$, show that the diagonal $\Delta \subseteq C \times C$ has self-intersection $\Delta^2=2-2 g$. (Use the definition of $\Omega_{C / k}$ in (II, §8).)

b. Let $l=C \times \mathrm{pt}$ and $m=\mathrm{pt} \times C$. If $g \geqslant 1$, show that $l, m$, and $\Delta$ are linearly independent in $\Num(C \times C)$. Thus $\Num(C \times C)$ has rank $\geqslant 3$, and in particular,
\[
\Pic(C \times C) \neq p_1^* \Pic C \oplus p_2^* \Pic C
.\]
Cf. (III, Ex. 12.6), (V, Ex. 4.10).
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X=C\times C$, let $p_1,p_2:X\to C$ be the two projections, and let
$\delta:C\hookrightarrow X$ be the diagonal embedding with image $\Delta$.

<1>1. The conormal sheaf of the diagonal is canonically
$$
\mci_\Delta/\mci_\Delta^2\cong\Omega_{C/k}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For an affine open $U=\Spec B\subseteq C$, the diagonal in
$U\times U=\Spec(B\otimes_kB)$ has ideal
$$
J=\ker\qty{B\otimes_kB\longrightarrow B},
$$
where the map is multiplication. The standard description of Kähler
differentials identifies
$$
J/J^2\cong\Omega_{B/k},
\qquad
1\otimes b-b\otimes1\longmapsto db.
$$
These affine identifications are natural under localization, so they glue to
the displayed isomorphism on $C$; see [[D-4GCH6]].
:::

<1>2. The normal bundle of the diagonal is
$$
\mcn_{\Delta/X}\cong\qty{\Omega_{C/k}}^\vee,
$$
and hence
$$
\boxed{\Delta^2=2-2g}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By definition of the normal sheaf [[D-MODCONORM]], step <1>1 gives
$$
\mcn_{\Delta/X}
=
\sheafhom_{\OO_C}(\mci_\Delta/\mci_\Delta^2,\OO_C)
\cong
\qty{\Omega_{C/k}}^\vee.
$$
Since $C$ is nonsingular, $\Omega_{C/k}$ is the canonical line bundle and has
degree $2g-2$. Therefore
$$
\deg\mcn_{\Delta/X}=-(2g-2)=2-2g.
$$
For a smooth curve on a smooth surface, its self-intersection is the degree of
its normal bundle, equivalently
$$
\Delta^2
=
\deg\OO_X(\Delta)|_\Delta
=
\deg\mcn_{\Delta/X}.
$$
This proves part (a).
:::

<1>3. With
$$
l=C\times\{Q\},
\qquad
m=\{P\}\times C,
$$
for fixed points $P,Q\in C$, the intersection numbers are
$$
l^2=m^2=0,
\qquad
l\cdot m=l\cdot\Delta=m\cdot\Delta=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Each of $l$ and $m$ is a fibre of one of the two projections. Distinct fibres
of the same projection are disjoint, and each fibre has trivial normal bundle,
so
$$
l^2=m^2=0.
$$
The curves $l$ and $m$ meet transversely at the single point $(P,Q)$, hence
$l\cdot m=1$. The diagonal meets $l$ at $(Q,Q)$ and $m$ at $(P,P)$, again
transversely in one point, so
$$
l\cdot\Delta=m\cdot\Delta=1.
$$
:::

<1>4. If $g\geq1$, the classes of $l,m,\Delta$ are linearly independent in
$\Num(C\times C)$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
a[l]+b[m]+c[\Delta]=0
$$
in $\Num(C\times C)$ for integers $a,b,c$. Intersecting with $l$, then with
$m$, and then with $\Delta$, and using steps <1>2--<1>3, gives
$$
b+c=0,
\qquad
a+c=0,
\qquad
a+b+c(2-2g)=0.
$$
The first two equations give
$$
a=b=-c.
$$
Substitution into the third gives
$$
-2gc=0.
$$
Since $g\geq1$, it follows that $c=0$, and hence $a=b=0$.
Thus the three numerical classes are linearly independent.
:::

<1>5. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\Num(C\times C)\geq3}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{\Pic(C\times C)\neq p_1^*\Pic C\oplus p_2^*\Pic C}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The rank bound follows immediately from step <1>4.

For any divisor $D$ on $C$, the numerical class of $p_1^*D$ is
$(\deg D)[m]$, and the numerical class of $p_2^*D$ is $(\deg D)[l]$. Hence the
image in $\Num(C\times C)$ of
$$
p_1^*\Pic C+p_2^*\Pic C
$$
is contained in the subgroup generated by $[l]$ and $[m]$. Step <1>4 shows
that $[\Delta]$ is not in that subgroup. Therefore the divisor class
$\OO_X(\Delta)$ cannot lie in
$p_1^*\Pic C+p_2^*\Pic C$, so the asserted equality of Picard groups is
impossible.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>2 prove part (a), and steps <1>3--<1>5 prove every assertion in
part (b).
:::
