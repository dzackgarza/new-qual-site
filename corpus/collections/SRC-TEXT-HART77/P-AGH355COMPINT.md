::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be a field, let $X=\PP_k^r$, and let $Y$ be a closed subscheme of dimension $q\ge1$, which is a complete intersection (II, Ex. 8.4).
Prove the following assertions.

(a) For all $n\in\ZZ$, the natural map
$$
H^0(X, \mco_X(n)) \to H^0(Y, \mco_Y(n))
$$
is surjective.
This gives a generalization and another proof of (II, Ex. 8.4c), where we assumed $Y$ was normal.

(b) The scheme $Y$ is connected.

(c) One has $H^i(Y,\mco_Y(n))=0$ for $0<i<q$ and all $n\in\ZZ$.

(d) One has $p_a(Y)=\dim_k H^q(Y,\mco_Y)$.
:::

::: {.hint}
Use exact sequences and induction on the codimension, starting from $Y=X$, which is covered by Theorem III.5.1.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $c=r-q$ and $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_r]$.
The global complete-intersection hypothesis means that the saturated homogeneous ideal of $Y$ is generated by $c$ homogeneous forms $F_1,\ldots,F_c$ of positive degrees $d_1,\ldots,d_c$.
For $0\le j\le c$, put $Y_j=\operatorname{Proj}(S/(F_1,\ldots,F_j))$, so $Y_0=X$ and $Y_c=Y$.
All twists on these schemes are the restrictions of the standard twists on $\PP_k^r$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The forms $F_1,\ldots,F_c$ are a regular sequence, and $\dim Y_j=r-j$ for every $j$.

::: pf-proof

Let $\mathfrak m=(x_0,\ldots,x_r)$.
The dimension of the homogeneous coordinate ring of the nonempty $Y$ is $q+1$; localization at the vertex $\mathfrak m$ has that same dimension [@Har10a, Chapter I, §§2 and 7].
The regular local ring $S_{\mathfrak m}$ is Cohen--Macaulay of dimension $r+1$.
Since quotienting by the $c$ forms drops its dimension by exactly $c$, the [dimension criterion for a regular sequence](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02JN) makes them a regular sequence in $S_{\mathfrak m}$.
That criterion also gives dimension $r+1-j$ for every prefix quotient at the vertex [@Har10a, Theorem II.8.21A].

The sequence is regular in $S$ as well.
At each stage the kernel testing injectivity is a graded module.
A nonzero homogeneous element of such a kernel cannot become zero after localization at $\mathfrak m$: multiplication by a polynomial outside $\mathfrak m$ has a nonzero constant term, which gives a nonzero component in the original degree of that element.
Thus every prefix is globally regular.
Its homogeneous cone has dimension $r+1-j$, and its Proj has dimension one less.
These Proj schemes are nonempty, since $r+1-j\ge q+1\ge2$ and a cone supported only at the vertex would have dimension zero.
Hence $\dim Y_j=r-j$.
The argument is algebraic over $k$ and does not require algebraic closedness.
For $c=0$ the assertions refer to the empty regular sequence and $Y=X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $j$ and $n\in\ZZ$, the restriction map $H^0(X,\OO_X(n))\to H^0(Y_j,\OO_{Y_j}(n))$ is surjective, and
$$
H^i(Y_j,\OO_{Y_j}(n))=0\qquad(0<i<r-j).
$$

::: pf-proof

Induct on $j$.
For $j=0$, the restriction is the identity and the vanishing is the projective-space calculation [@Har10a, Theorem III.5.1].
For $j\ge1$, regularity in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives the exact sequence on $Y_{j-1}$
$$
0\longrightarrow\OO_{Y_{j-1}}(n-d_j)
\xrightarrow{F_j}\OO_{Y_{j-1}}(n)
\longrightarrow\OO_{Y_j}(n)\longrightarrow0,
$$
where the last sheaf is extended by the closed inclusion into $Y_{j-1}$.
Sheafification of the graded regular-sequence quotient gives this sequence, and twisting by an invertible sheaf preserves exactness.
Cohomology of the last term equals its cohomology on $Y_j$ [@Har10a, Lemma III.2.10].

Write $s=r-j=\dim Y_j$, so $s\ge q\ge1$ and $\dim Y_{j-1}=s+1\ge2$.
The induction hypothesis gives $H^1(Y_{j-1},\OO(n-d_j))=0$.
Thus the long exact sequence makes restriction from $Y_{j-1}$ onto $H^0(Y_j,\OO(n))$ surjective.
Composing with the preceding restriction from $X$ proves the new surjectivity.

For $0<i<s$, the same long exact sequence has the segment
$$
H^i(Y_{j-1},\OO(n))\longrightarrow H^i(Y_j,\OO(n))
\longrightarrow H^{i+1}(Y_{j-1},\OO(n-d_j)).
$$
Both outer groups vanish by induction, since $0<i<i+1\le s<s+1$.
The middle group therefore vanishes.
This holds for every integer $n$, including negative twists, and completes the induction.
At $j=c$ it proves (a) and (c).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The constants map $k\to H^0(Y,\OO_Y)$ is an isomorphism, and $Y$ is connected.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} at $n=0$ gives a surjection
$$
k=H^0(\PP_k^r,\OO)\longrightarrow H^0(Y,\OO_Y).
$$
This is the constants map.
It is injective: $Y$ is nonempty, and a nonzero element of the field maps to a unit in every nonzero local ring of $Y$.
Thus $H^0(Y,\OO_Y)=k$.
A decomposition of $Y$ into two nonempty open-and-closed subsets would yield the nontrivial idempotent global section equal to $1$ on one subset and $0$ on the other.
A field has no such idempotent, so $Y$ is connected, proving (b).
No reducedness is used in this argument.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The arithmetic genus is $\boxed{p_a(Y)=\dim_kH^q(Y,\OO_Y)}$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives $h^0(Y,\OO_Y)=1$, and step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives $h^i(Y,\OO_Y)=0$ for $0<i<q$.
Cohomology also vanishes for $i>q$ by Grothendieck vanishing, since $\dim Y=q$ [@Har10a, Theorem III.2.7].
Coherent cohomology is finite-dimensional because $Y$ is projective [@Har10a, Theorem III.5.2].
Therefore
$$
\chi(Y,\OO_Y)=1+(-1)^q h^q(Y,\OO_Y).
$$
The definition $p_a(Y)=(-1)^q(\chi(Y,\OO_Y)-1)$ now gives the claimed value, proving (d).

:::
