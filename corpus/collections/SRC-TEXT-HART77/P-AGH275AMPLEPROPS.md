::: {.problem}
Establish the following properties of ample and very ample invertible sheaves on a noetherian scheme $X$.
Here $\mcl, \mcm$ denote invertible sheaves, and for (d), (e) we assume furthermore that $X$ is of finite type over a noetherian ring $A$.

(a) If $\mcl$ is ample and $\mcm$ is generated by global sections, then $\mcl \tensor \mcm$ is ample.

(b) If $\mcl$ is ample and $\mcm$ is arbitrary, then $\mcm \tensor \mcl^n$ is ample for sufficiently large $n$.

(c) If $\mcl, \mcm$ are both ample, so is $\mcl \tensor \mcm$.

(d) If $\mcl$ is very ample and $\mcm$ is generated by global sections, then $\mcl \tensor \mcm$ is very ample.

(e) If $\mcl$ is ample, then there is an $n_0 > 0$ such that $\mcl^n$ is very ample for all $n \geq n_0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
All tensor products of sheaves on $X$ are over $\OO_X$, and a power of an invertible sheaf means its tensor power.
Use the [[D-MODAMPLE|noetherian definition of ampleness]]: $\mathcal L$ is ample when, for every coherent $\mathcal F$, the sheaf $\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal L^q$ is globally generated for all sufficiently large integers $q$.
In (d) and (e), very ampleness is relative to $\Spec A$.
The assertions hold on the empty scheme, so assume $X\ne\varnothing$.

<1>1. Tensor products of globally generated sheaves are globally generated, and every positive power of an ample invertible sheaf is ample.

::: {.proof}
If global sections $s_i$ generate $\mathcal F$ and $t_j$ generate $\mathcal G$, the global sections $s_i\otimes t_j$ generate $\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G$.
At each stalk this follows by expanding the two factors of an elementary tensor in their respective generators; elementary tensors span the tensor product.
In particular, all nonnegative powers of a globally generated invertible sheaf are globally generated, with its zeroth power generated by $1$.

If $\mathcal L$ is ample and $a>0$, then for any coherent $\mathcal F$ the sheaves
$$
\mathcal F\otimes(\mathcal L^a)^q=\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal L^{aq}
$$
are globally generated for all sufficiently large $q$, since $aq$ eventually exceeds the bound in the ample definition for $\mathcal F$.
Thus $\mathcal L^a$ is ample.
:::

<1>2. Part (a) holds.

::: {.proof}
Fix a coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$.
For all sufficiently large $q$, ampleness of $\mcl$ makes $\mathcal F\otimes\mcl^q$ globally generated.
The sheaf $\mcm^q$ is globally generated by step <1>1.
Their tensor product
$$
\mathcal F\otimes(\mcl\otimes\mcm)^q
\cong(\mathcal F\otimes\mcl^q)\otimes\mcm^q
$$
is therefore globally generated for all those $q$.
This is the definition of ampleness of $\mcl\otimes\mcm$.
:::

<1>3. Part (b) holds for every $n\ge a+1$, where $a\ge0$ is any integer for which $\mcm\otimes\mcl^a$ is globally generated.

::: {.proof}
Such an $a$ exists because $\mcm$ is coherent and $\mcl$ is ample.
For $n\ge a+1$, the sheaf $\mcl^{n-a}$ is ample by step <1>1.
Apply part (a), proved in step <1>2, to this sheaf and the globally generated sheaf $\mcm\otimes\mcl^a$.
Their product is $\mcm\otimes\mcl^n$, which is therefore ample for every such $n$.
:::

<1>4. Part (c) holds.

::: {.proof}
For any coherent $\mathcal F$, ampleness gives an integer beyond which $\mathcal F\otimes\mcl^q$ is globally generated, and an integer beyond which $\mcm^q$ is globally generated, using $\OO_X$ in the second application.
Choose $q$ at least both bounds.
Step <1>1 then makes
$$
(\mathcal F\otimes\mcl^q)\otimes\mcm^q
\cong\mathcal F\otimes(\mcl\otimes\mcm)^q
$$
globally generated.
The same holds for every larger $q$, proving that $\mcl\otimes\mcm$ is ample.
:::

<1>5. In part (d), the graph of the morphism defined by $\mcm$, followed by a Segre immersion, realizes $\mcl\otimes\mcm$ as very ample over $\Spec A$.

::: {.proof}
Choose an $A$-immersion $i:X\to\PP_A^r$ with $i^*\OO(1)\cong\mcl$.
Because $\mcm$ is [[D-MODGG|globally generated]], the open sets where one global section generates it cover $X$.
Quasi-compactness supplies a finite subcover, so finitely many global sections $t_0,\ldots,t_s$ generate $\mcm$.
They define an $A$-morphism $g:X\to\PP_A^s$ with $g^*\OO(1)\cong\mcm$ [@Har10a, Theorem II.7.1].

The morphism $(i,g):X\to\PP_A^r\times_A\PP_A^s$ factors as
$$
X\xrightarrow{\Gamma_g}X\times_A\PP_A^s
\xrightarrow{i\times\id}\PP_A^r\times_A\PP_A^s.
$$
The graph $\Gamma_g$ is a closed immersion because $\PP_A^s$ is separated over $A$; it is a base change of its diagonal.
The second arrow is a base change of the immersion $i$ and is an immersion.
Their composite is therefore an immersion.

Compose with the Segre closed immersion
$$
\sigma:\PP_A^r\times_A\PP_A^s\longrightarrow\PP_A^{(r+1)(s+1)-1}.
$$
The Segre coordinates are the pairwise products of the coordinates of the two factors, so
$$
\sigma^*\OO(1)\cong\operatorname{pr}_1^*\OO(1)\otimes\operatorname{pr}_2^*\OO(1)
$$
[@Har10a, Exercise II.5.11].
The resulting immersion $h=\sigma\circ(i,g)$ satisfies
$$
h^*\OO(1)\cong i^*\OO(1)\otimes g^*\OO(1)\cong\mcl\otimes\mcm.
$$
This proves very ampleness over the specified base, without assuming that $X$ is proper.
:::

<1>6. Part (e) holds for $n_0=h+b$, where $h>0$ has $\mcl^h$ very ample and $b\ge0$ has $\mcl^q$ globally generated for every $q\ge b$.

::: {.proof}
The integer $h$ exists by [@Har10a, Theorem II.7.6], since $X$ is of finite type over the noetherian ring $A$.
The integer $b$ exists by applying the ample definition to $\OO_X$.
For every $n\ge h+b$, the factor $\mcl^{n-h}$ is globally generated.
Part (d), proved in step <1>5, shows that
$$
\mcl^n\cong\mcl^h\otimes\mcl^{n-h}
$$
is very ample.
This treats every sufficiently large exponent, not only multiples of $h$, and $n_0=h+b$ is positive.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2--<1>6 prove parts (a)--(e), respectively.
:::
