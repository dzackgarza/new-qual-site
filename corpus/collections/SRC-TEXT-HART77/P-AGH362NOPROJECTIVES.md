::: {.problem}
Let $X=\PP_k^1$, with $k$ an infinite field.

(a) Show that there does not exist a projective object $\mcp \in \Mod(X)$, together with a surjective map $\mcp \to \mco_X \to 0$.

(b) Show that there does not exist a projective object $\mcp$ in either $\QCoh(X)$ or $\Coh(X)$ together with a surjection $\mcp \to \mco_X \to 0$.
:::

::: {.hint}
For (a), consider surjections $\mco_V\to k(x)\to0$, where $x\in X$ is a closed point, $V$ is an open neighborhood of $x$, and $\mco_V=j_!(\mco_X|_V)$ for $j:V\hookrightarrow X$.
For (b), consider surjections $\mcl\to\mcl\otimes k(x)\to0$ with $\mcl$ invertible.
:::

::: {.solution}
For a closed point $x$, write $k(x)$ for the skyscraper $\OO_X$-module with fibre $\kappa(x)$.
An object is projective when every morphism from it to the target of an epimorphism lifts through that epimorphism.
Suppose, in each of the indicated categories, that $p:\mcp\twoheadrightarrow\OO_X$ is an epimorphism with $\mcp$ projective.

<1>1. In $\Mod(X)$, there is a nonempty open $W$ and a section $s\in\mcp(W)$ with $p(s)=1$, and $W$ contains two distinct $k$-rational points $x,y$.

::: {.proof}
Surjectivity of $p$ as a sheaf morphism gives a local lift of the section $1$ near any chosen point.
After shrinking that neighborhood, the lift is a section $s$ on $W$ with $p(s)=1$.
Every nonempty open in $\PP_k^1$ omits only finitely many closed points: a proper closed subset has dimension zero and is finite.
There are infinitely many $k$-rational points because $k$ is infinite.
Thus two distinct such points lie in $W$.
:::

<1>2. No projective object of $\Mod(X)$ can admit the epimorphism $p$.

::: {.proof}
Choose $W,s,x,y$ as in step <1>1, put $V=X\setminus\{y\}$, and let $j:V\hookrightarrow X$.
The residue map on $V$ extends by zero to an epimorphism
$$
q:j_!(\OO_X|_V)\twoheadrightarrow k(x).
$$
Its stalk at $x$ is the residue surjection, and the target has zero stalk at every other point.
Projectivity would lift the composite $\mcp\xrightarrow{p}\OO_X\to k(x)$ to $h:\mcp\to j_!(\OO_X|_V)$.

There is a natural injection from the [[D-SHFSIX|extension by zero]] $j_!(\OO_X|_V)$ into $\OO_X$: its stalk is the identity on $V$ and is the map from zero outside $V$ [@Har10a, Exercise II.1.19].
The image of $h(s)$ under this injection is a regular function on $W$ with zero germ at $y$.
It therefore vanishes on a nonempty open neighborhood of $y$ in $W$.
Since $W$ is integral, a regular function with zero generic value is zero everywhere.
Injectivity then gives $h(s)=0$.
However, its image under $q$ must be the residue of $p(s)=1$ at $x$, which is nonzero.
This contradiction proves (a).
:::

<1>3. For every coherent sheaf $F$ on $X$, there is an integer $m$ such that $\Hom_X(F,\OO_X(-m))=0$.

::: {.proof}
For sufficiently large $a$, the sheaf $F(a)$ is generated by finitely many global sections [@Har10a, Theorem II.5.17].
Thus there is an epimorphism $\OO_X(-a)^{\oplus N}\twoheadrightarrow F$.
Precomposition embeds $\Hom_X(F,\OO_X(-m))$ into
$$
\Hom_X(\OO_X(-a)^{\oplus N},\OO_X(-m))
\cong H^0(X,\OO_X(a-m))^{\oplus N}.
$$
For $m>a$ this is zero, since negative twists on $\PP^1$ have no global sections [@Har10a, Proposition II.5.13].
:::

<1>4. No projective object of $\QCoh(X)$ or $\Coh(X)$ can admit the epimorphism $p$.

::: {.proof}
First work in $\QCoh(X)$ and fix a $k$-rational point $x$.
The composite $\mcp\to k(x)$ is nonzero and surjective on its stalk at $x$.
A quasi-coherent sheaf on a noetherian scheme is the union of its coherent subsheaves, as proved in [[P-AGH2515EXTCOH]], part (e).
Choose a germ mapping to $1\in\kappa(x)$ and a coherent subsheaf $F\subseteq\mcp$ containing a representative of that germ.
The restricted map $F\to k(x)$ is nonzero.

Choose $m$ as in step <1>3 and put $L=\OO_X(-m)$.
Trivializing its one-dimensional fibre at $x$ gives an epimorphism
$$
L\twoheadrightarrow L\otimes k(x)\xrightarrow{\cong}k(x)
$$
in $\QCoh(X)$.
Projectivity of $\mcp$ lifts its fixed map to $k(x)$ through this epimorphism, giving $h:\mcp\to L$.
Its restriction to $F$ is zero by step <1>3, whereas the composite $F\to\mcp\xrightarrow{h}L\to k(x)$ was chosen nonzero.
This is a contradiction.

In $\Coh(X)$ the same proof uses $F=\mcp$ directly.
The sheaves $L$ and $k(x)$ and the epimorphism between them are coherent, so the required lifting problem lies in that category.
It yields the same contradiction and proves (b).
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>2 prove (a), and steps <1>3--<1>4 prove (b).
In particular none of the three categories has enough projectives, since enough projectives would supply an epimorphism from a projective object onto $\OO_X$.
:::
