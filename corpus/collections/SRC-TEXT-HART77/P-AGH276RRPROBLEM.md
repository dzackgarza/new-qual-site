::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a nonsingular projective variety over an algebraically closed field, and let $D$ be a divisor on $X$.
For any $n > 0$ consider the complete linear system $\abs{nD}$.
The **Riemann-Roch problem** is to determine $\dim \abs{nD}$ as a function of $n$, and in particular its behavior for large $n$.

If $\mcl$ is the corresponding invertible sheaf, then $\dim \abs{nD} = \dim \Gamma(X, \mcl^n) - 1$, so an equivalent problem is to determine $\dim \Gamma(X, \mcl^n)$ as a function of $n$.

(a) Show that if $D$ is very ample, and if $X \injects \PP^N_k$ is the corresponding embedding in projective space, then for all sufficiently large $n$ we have $\dim \abs{nD} = P_X(n) - 1$, where $P_X$ is the Hilbert polynomial of $X$ (I, §7).
Thus in this case $\dim \abs{nD}$ is a polynomial function of $n$ for $n$ large.

(b) If $D$ corresponds to a torsion element of $\Pic X$ of order $r$, then $\dim \abs{nD} = 0$ if $r \divides n$ and $-1$ otherwise.
In this case the function is periodic of period $r$.

It follows from the general Riemann-Roch theorem that $\dim \abs{nD}$ is a polynomial function for $n$ large whenever $D$ is an ample divisor; see (IV, 1.3.2), (V, 1.6), and Appendix A.
In the case of algebraic surfaces, Zariski has shown for any effective divisor $D$ that there is a finite set of polynomials $P_1, \ldots, P_r$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$, $\dim \abs{nD} = P_{i(n)}(n)$, where $i(n) \in \ts{1, \ldots, r}$ is a function of $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $\mathcal L=\OO_X(D)$ and $h^0(X,\mathcal F)=\dim_k\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)$.
The [[D-DIVLINSYS|complete linear system]] $\abs{nD}$ has dimension $h^0(X,\mathcal L^n)-1$, with the empty system assigned dimension $-1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For the embedding given by a very ample $D$, one has $h^0(X,\mathcal L^n)=P_X(n)$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

::: pf-proof

Let $i:X\hookrightarrow\PP_k^N$ be the embedding and write
$$
S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_N]/I_X
$$
for its homogeneous coordinate ring.
The isomorphism $i^*\OO(1)\cong\mathcal L$ identifies $\mathcal L^n$ with $\OO_X(n)$.
Apply the high-degree comparison of [[P-AGH259GAMMASTAR]], step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, to the finite graded $S$-module $S$.
Its hypotheses hold because $S_0=k$ and $S$ is generated by finitely many degree-one elements.
It gives
$$
S_n\xrightarrow{\cong}\Gamma(X,\OO_X(n))
$$
for every sufficiently large $n$.
The [[D-L6ERW|Hilbert polynomial]] satisfies $P_X(n)=\dim_k S_n$ for every sufficiently large $n$ [@Har10a, Chapter I, §7].
Taking $n$ beyond both bounds proves the equality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Part (a) gives the eventual polynomial
$$
\boxed{\dim\abs{nD}=P_X(n)-1\qquad(n\gg0).}
$$

::: pf-proof

Substitute the equality in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} into the dimension formula for the complete linear system.
The result is a polynomial in $n$ because $P_X$ is a polynomial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

A torsion invertible sheaf on $X$ with a nonzero global section is trivial.

::: pf-proof

First, $\Gamma(X,\OO_X)=k$.
A global regular function gives a morphism $X\to\AA_k^1\subseteq\PP_k^1$.
Since $X$ is projective, its image in $\PP^1$ is closed; it is irreducible and misses $\infty$, so it is a point.
The field $k$ is algebraically closed, and $X$ is reduced, so the function equals that point's constant value as a section.

Let $\mathcal N$ be invertible with $\mathcal N^a\cong\OO_X$ for some $a>0$, and suppose $0\ne s\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal N)$.
As $X$ is integral, $s$ is nonzero at the generic point: on each trivializing affine open, restriction of a regular function to the fraction field is injective.
Thus $s^{\otimes a}$ is also nonzero at the generic point.
Under the chosen isomorphism it becomes a nonzero global regular function, hence a nonzero constant by the preceding argument.

At any point, write $s=b e$ in a local frame for $\mathcal N$.
The image of $e^{\otimes a}$ under the isomorphism $\mathcal N^a\cong\OO_X$ is a unit, so the constancy of $s^{\otimes a}$ makes $b^a$ a unit and therefore makes $b$ a unit.
Consequently $s$ is a local frame at every point, and the morphism $\OO_X\to\mathcal N$ defined by $1\mapsto s$ is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

If $[\mathcal L]$ has order $r$ in $\Pic(X)$, then for every $n>0$,
$$
\boxed{\dim\abs{nD}=
\begin{cases}
0,&r\mid n,\\
-1,&r\nmid n.
\end{cases}}
$$
The least positive period is $r$.

::: pf-proof

If $r\mid n$, the sheaf $\mathcal L^n$ is trivial and has a one-dimensional space of global sections by step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.
If $r\nmid n$, the sheaf $\mathcal L^n$ is nontrivial and torsion, so step [](#s3){.pf-ref} forces its space of global sections to be zero.
Subtracting one gives the two dimensions.
Divisibility by $r$ is unchanged on replacing $n$ by $n+r$, so the function has period $r$.
If $p>0$ were any period, its values at $n=r$ and $n=r+p$ would both be zero, forcing $r\mid(r+p)$ and hence $r\mid p$.
This proves the least-period assertion, including $r=1$.

:::
