::: {.problem}
If $\pi: \tilde{X} \rightarrow X$ is a monoidal transformation with center $P$, show that $H^1\left(\tilde{X}, \Omega_{\tilde{X}}\right) \cong H^1\left(X, \Omega_X\right) \oplus k$.
This gives another proof of (5.8).

Hints: Use the projection formula (III, Ex.
8.3) and (III, Ex.
8.1) to show that $H^i\left(X, \Omega_X\right) \cong H^i\left(\tilde{X}, \pi^* \Omega_X\right)$ for each $i$.
Next use the exact sequence
\[
0 \rightarrow \pi^* \Omega_X \rightarrow \Omega_{\tilde{X}} \rightarrow \Omega_{\tilde{X} / X} \rightarrow 0
\]
and a local calculation with coordinates to show that there is a natural isomorphism $\Omega_{\tilde{X} / X} \cong \Omega_E$, where $E$ is the exceptional curve.
Now use the cohomology sequence of the above sequence (you will need every term) and Serre duality to get the result.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\pi:\widetilde X=\operatorname{Bl}_P X\longrightarrow X
$$
be the blowup, and let
$$
E=\pi^{-1}(P)\cong\PP^1
$$
be its exceptional curve.

Put
$$
\mathcal A=\pi^*\Omega_X,
\qquad
\mathcal B=\Omega_{\widetilde X}.
$$

<1>1. For every $i\ge0$ there is a natural isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
H^i(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
\cong
H^i(X,\Omega_X).}
$$

::: {.proof}
For a monoidal transformation of a nonsingular surface at a point,
$$
\pi_*\OO_{\widetilde X}\cong\OO_X
$$
and the higher direct images
$$
R^q\pi_*\OO_{\widetilde X}
$$
vanish for $q>0$ by the blowup-cohomology theorem V.3.4; the same input underlies [[P-AGH531PABLOWUP]].

Because $\Omega_X$ is locally free, the projection formula gives
$$
R^q\pi_*(\pi^*\Omega_X)
\cong
\Omega_X\otimes R^q\pi_*\OO_{\widetilde X}.
$$
This is the higher-direct-image projection formula [[P-AGH383PROJFORMULA|Exercise III.8.3]]. Thus
$$
\pi_*\mathcal A\cong\Omega_X
$$
and
$$
R^q\pi_*\mathcal A=0
\qquad(q>0).
$$
Exercise III.8.1 [[P-AGH381DEGENLERAY]] therefore gives the displayed cohomology isomorphisms.
:::

<1>2. There is an exact sequence
$$
\boxed{
0
\longrightarrow
\pi^*\Omega_X
\longrightarrow
\Omega_{\widetilde X}
\longrightarrow
\Omega_E
\longrightarrow
0.}
$$

::: {.proof}
The usual sequence of relative differentials gives a right-exact sequence
$$
\pi^*\Omega_X
\longrightarrow
\Omega_{\widetilde X}
\longrightarrow
\Omega_{\widetilde X/X}
\longrightarrow
0.
$$
The first map is injective because it is an isomorphism away from the exceptional divisor and $\pi^*\Omega_X$ is locally free, hence torsion-free.
It remains to identify the quotient.

Choose regular parameters $x,y$ at $P$.
On the blowup chart
$$
U_x=\Spec k[x,t],
\qquad
y=xt,
$$
the map on differentials is
$$
dx\longmapsto dx,
\qquad
dy\longmapsto t\,dx+x\,dt.
$$
Hence the cokernel is generated by $dt$ with the single relation
$$
x\,dt=0.
$$
Since $E\cap U_x$ is $x=0$, this cokernel is
$$
\OO_{E\cap U_x}\,dt
=
\Omega_E|_{E\cap U_x}.
$$

On the other chart
$$
U_y=\Spec k[s,y],
\qquad
x=sy,
$$
the cokernel is similarly
$$
\OO_{E\cap U_y}\,ds.
$$
On the overlap one has
$$
t=s^{-1},
\qquad
dt=-s^{-2}ds,
$$
which is exactly the transition rule for the cotangent sheaf of $E\cong\PP^1$.
Thus the local cokernels glue to
$$
\Omega_{\widetilde X/X}\cong\Omega_E,
$$
giving the asserted short exact sequence.
:::

<1>3. The cohomology of the quotient is
$$
\boxed{
H^0(E,\Omega_E)=0,
\qquad
H^1(E,\Omega_E)\cong k,
\qquad
H^i(E,\Omega_E)=0\ (i\ge2).}
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
E\cong\PP^1,
$$
one has
$$
\Omega_E\cong\OO_{\PP^1}(-2).
$$
Therefore there are no global sections:
$$
H^0(E,\Omega_E)=0.
$$
Serre duality on $E$ gives
$$
H^1(E,\Omega_E)
\cong
\dualof{H^0(E,\OO_E)}
\cong
k.
$$
Finally a coherent sheaf on the one-dimensional projective scheme $E$ has no cohomology in degrees at least two.
:::

<1>4. The map
$$
H^0(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
\longrightarrow
H^0(\widetilde X,\mathcal B)
$$
is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
The beginning of the long exact cohomology sequence of step <1>2 is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H^0(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
\longrightarrow
H^0(\widetilde X,\mathcal B)
\longrightarrow
H^0(E,\Omega_E).
$$
The last term is zero by step <1>3. Hence the first map is both injective and surjective.
:::

<1>5. The map
$$
\boxed{
H^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal B)}
$$
is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
The end of the long exact sequence of step <1>2 contains
$$
H^1(E,\Omega_E)
\longrightarrow
H^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
\longrightarrow
H^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal B)
\longrightarrow
H^2(E,\Omega_E)=0.
$$
Thus the displayed map on $H^2$ is surjective.
We show its source and target have the same finite dimension.

By step <1>1,
$$
h^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
=
h^2(X,\Omega_X).
$$
For a nonsingular projective surface $S$, Serre duality and the rank-two identity
$$
(\Omega_S)^\vee\otimes\omega_S
\cong
\Omega_S
$$
give
$$
h^2(S,\Omega_S)=h^0(S,\Omega_S).
$$
Therefore
$$
h^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
=
h^0(X,\Omega_X).
$$
Step <1>1 in degree zero and step <1>4 give
$$
h^0(X,\Omega_X)
=
h^0(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
=
h^0(\widetilde X,\mathcal B).
$$
Applying Serre duality on $\widetilde X$ gives
$$
h^0(\widetilde X,\mathcal B)
=
h^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal B).
$$
Hence
$$
h^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
=
h^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal B).
$$
A surjective linear map between finite-dimensional vector spaces of equal dimension is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>6. The long exact sequence of step <1>2 reduces in degree one to a short exact sequence
$$
\boxed{
0
\longrightarrow
H^1(X,\Omega_X)
\longrightarrow
H^1(\widetilde X,\Omega_{\widetilde X})
\longrightarrow
k
\longrightarrow
0.}
$$

::: {.proof}
Using step <1>3, the middle part of the long exact sequence is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H^1(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
\longrightarrow
H^1(\widetilde X,\mathcal B)
\longrightarrow
H^1(E,\Omega_E)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal A).
$$
Step <1>5 says the following map
$$
H^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
\longrightarrow
H^2(\widetilde X,\mathcal B)
$$
is injective.
Exactness therefore forces
$$
\operatorname{im}\delta=0.
$$
Thus the map
$$
H^1(\widetilde X,\mathcal B)
\longrightarrow
H^1(E,\Omega_E)
$$
is surjective.

Now apply step <1>1 to identify
$$
H^1(\widetilde X,\mathcal A)
\cong
H^1(X,\Omega_X)
$$
and step <1>3 to identify
$$
H^1(E,\Omega_E)\cong k.
$$
This gives the asserted short exact sequence.
:::

<1>7. Consequently
$$
\boxed{
H^1(\widetilde X,\Omega_{\widetilde X})
\cong
H^1(X,\Omega_X)\oplus k.}
$$

::: {.proof}
All groups in step <1>6 are vector spaces over $k$.
Every short exact sequence of $k$-vector spaces splits.
Choosing a lift in $H^1(\widetilde X,\Omega_{\widetilde X})$ of $1\in k$ therefore gives a splitting and the displayed isomorphism.

The splitting need not be canonical; the assertion of the exercise is the vector-space isomorphism.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 identifies the cohomology of the pulled-back cotangent bundle, steps <1>2--<1>3 identify the relative differential contribution with the one-dimensional group $H^1(E,\Omega_E)$, and steps <1>4--<1>6 show that its connecting map to degree two vanishes.
Step <1>7 gives the required direct sum.
:::
