::: {.problem}
Let $S$ be a scheme, let $X$ be a reduced scheme over $S$, and let $Y$ be a separated scheme over $S$.
Let $f$ and $g$ be two $S\dash$morphisms from $X$ to $Y$ which agree on an open dense subset of $X$.
Show that $f = g$.

Give examples to show that this result fails if either

a. $X$ is nonreduced, or

b. $Y$ is nonseparated.

*Hint:* consider the map $h: X \to \fiberprod{Y}{S}{Y}$ obtained from $f$ and $g$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let
\[
h=(f,g):X\longrightarrow Y\times_SY.
\]
The scheme-theoretic equalizer of $f$ and $g$ is
\[
E=X\times_{Y\times_SY}Y,
\]
where the second map is the diagonal
\[
\Delta_{Y/S}:Y\longrightarrow Y\times_SY.
\]
::: {.proof}
For any $S$-scheme $T$ with a morphism $u:T\to X$, the map $u$ factors through $E$ exactly when
\[
h\circ u
\]
factors through the diagonal.  By the defining property of the diagonal, this is equivalent to
\[
f\circ u=g\circ u.
\]
Thus $E$ represents the equalizer of $f$ and $g$.
:::

<1>2. Because $Y$ is separated over $S$, the morphism
\[
E\longrightarrow X
\]
is a closed immersion.
::: {.proof}
Separatedness means that
\[
\Delta_{Y/S}:Y\longrightarrow Y\times_SY
\]
is a closed immersion.  Closed immersions are preserved by base change, and $E\to X$ is its base change along $h$.
:::

<1>3. If $U\subseteq X$ is the dense open subset on which $f=g$, then
\[
U\subseteq |E|.
\]
Consequently
\[
|E|=|X|.
\]
::: {.proof}
The equality $f|_U=g|_U$ says exactly that the inclusion $U\hookrightarrow X$ factors through the equalizer $E$, so $U\subseteq|E|$.

By <1>2, $|E|$ is closed in $X$.  Since it contains the dense subset $U$, it contains its closure $X$.
:::

<1>4. Since $X$ is reduced, the closed immersion $E\hookrightarrow X$ with $|E|=|X|$ is an isomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_X
\]
be the ideal sheaf defining $E$.  The equality of underlying spaces means
\[
V(\mathcal I)=X.
\]
Affine-locally this says every element of the corresponding ideal lies in every prime ideal, hence is nilpotent.  Thus $\mathcal I$ is a sheaf of nilpotent ideals.

Because $X$ is reduced, its nilradical is zero, so
\[
\mathcal I=0.
\]
Therefore $E=X$ as schemes.
:::

<1>5. Hence
\[
\boxed{f=g.}
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, every morphism to $X$ factors through the equalizer $E=X$.  In particular the identity of $X$ does, and the equalizer property in <1>1 gives $f=g$.
:::

<1>6. The conclusion can fail when $X$ is nonreduced even if $Y$ is separated.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
R=k[x,\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2,x\epsilon),
\qquad
X=\Spec R,
\qquad
Y=\mathbb A^1_k=\Spec k[t].
\]
Define two morphisms
\[
f,g:X\longrightarrow Y
\]
by the ring maps
\[
f^\sharp(t)=0,
\qquad
g^\sharp(t)=\epsilon.
\]
They are distinct because
\[
\epsilon\ne0\text{ in }R.
\]

Let
\[
U=D(x)\subseteq X.
\]
Since $x$ becomes invertible on $U$ and $x\epsilon=0$, one has
\[
\epsilon|_U=0.
\]
Hence
\[
f|_U=g|_U.
\]

The nilradical of $R$ is generated by $\epsilon$, so
\[
|X|\cong\Spec k[x].
\]
Under this homeomorphism $D(x)$ is the usual punctured affine line, which is dense.  Thus $f$ and $g$ agree on a dense open but are not equal.

The target $Y=\mathbb A^1_k$ is separated, so the failure is exactly the nonreducedness of $X$.
:::

<1>7. The conclusion can fail when $Y$ is nonseparated even if $X$ is reduced.
::: {.proof}
Let $Y$ be the affine line with doubled origin, obtained by gluing two copies
\[
Y_1\cong\mathbb A^1_k,
\qquad
Y_2\cong\mathbb A^1_k
\]
along the common open
\[
\mathbb G_m=D(t)
\]
by the identity.  The morphism
\[
Y\longrightarrow S=\Spec k
\]
is not separated.

Take
\[
X=\mathbb A^1_k
\]
and let
\[
f:X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y_1\hookrightarrow Y,
\qquad
g:X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y_2\hookrightarrow Y
\]
be the two chart inclusions.  The scheme $X$ is reduced.

On the dense open
\[
D(t)=\mathbb G_m\subseteq X,
\]
the maps agree because this is precisely the open along which the two copies were glued.  But at $0$ they land at the two distinct origins of $Y$, so
\[
f\ne g.
\]
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>5 prove the theorem; <1>6 and <1>7 give the requested counterexamples.
:::
