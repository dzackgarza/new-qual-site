::: {.problem}
For any closed subscheme $X \subseteq \PP^n$, we denote by $C(X) \subseteq \PP^{n+1}$ the projective cone over $X$ (I, Ex.
2.10). If $I \subseteq k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ is the largest homogeneous ideal of $X$, then $C(X)$ is defined by the ideal generated by $I$ in $k[x_0, \ldots, x_{n+1}]$.

(a) Give an example to show that if $\theset{X_t}$ is a flat family of closed subschemes of $\PP^n$, then $\theset{C(X_t)}$ need not be a flat family in $\PP^{n+1}$.

(b) To remedy this situation, we make the following definition.
Let $X \subseteq \PP_T^n$ be a closed subscheme, where $T$ is a noetherian integral scheme.
For each $t \in T$, let $I_t \subseteq S_t = k(t)[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ be the homogeneous ideal of $X_t$ in $\PP_{k(t)}^n$.
We say that the family $\theset{X_t}$ is \dfn{very flat} if for all $d \geq 0$ the dimension
$$
\dim_{k(t)} (S_t / I_t)_d
$$
is independent of $t$.
Here $(\cdot)_d$ means the homogeneous part of degree $d$.

(c) If $\theset{X_t}$ is a very flat family in $\PP^n$, show that it is flat.
Show also that $\theset{C(X_t)}$ is a very flat family in $\PP^{n+1}$, and hence flat.

(d) If $\theset{X_{(t)}}$ is an algebraic family of projectively normal varieties in $\PP_k^n$, parametrized by a nonsingular curve $T$ over an algebraically closed field $k$, then $\theset{X_{(t)}}$ is a very flat family of schemes.
:::

::: {.solution}
For a fibre $X_t\subseteq\PP^n_{k(t)}$, put
$$
h_t(d)=\dim_{k(t)}(S_t/I_t)_d.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

There is a flat family of three reduced points in $\PP^2$ whose general fibre is noncollinear and whose special fibre is collinear.

::: pf-proof

Take $T=\AA^1$ with coordinate $t$ and define three sections of
$$
\PP^2\times T\longrightarrow T
$$
by
$$
P_1(t)=[1:0:0],\qquad
P_2(t)=[0:1:0],\qquad
P_3(t)=[1:1:t].
$$
They are pairwise disjoint for every $t$.
Let $\mathcal X$ be their union.
Then
$$
\mathcal X\cong T\amalg T\amalg T
$$
over $T$, so $\mathcal X\to T$ is flat.

For $t\ne0$, the point $P_3(t)$ does not lie on the line $z=0$ through $P_1,P_2$, so the three points are noncollinear.
For $t=0$, all three distinct points lie on $z=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The Hilbert functions of the fibres in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} are
$$
h_t(d)=
\begin{cases}
1,&d=0,\\
3,&d\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad(t\ne0),
$$
whereas
$$
h_0(d)=
\begin{cases}
1,&d=0,\\
2,&d=1,\\
3,&d\ge2.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf-proof

For three noncollinear points, linear forms already give arbitrary values at the three points, so the degree-one coordinate quotient has dimension three.
Multiplying separating linear forms by powers of a linear form nonvanishing at all three points shows that the restriction map remains surjective in every degree $d\ge1$.
Thus the first formula holds.

For the special fibre, all points lie on the line $z=0$.
The restrictions of linear forms therefore have dimension two.
Quadratic polynomials on that line interpolate arbitrary values at three distinct points, so the degree-two quotient has dimension three; multiplying by a nonvanishing linear form gives dimension three in every higher degree.
This gives the second formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The cones over the flat family of step [](#s1){.pf-ref} do not form a flat family.

::: pf-proof

If $R_t=S_t/I_t$ is the homogeneous coordinate ring of $X_t$, then the homogeneous coordinate ring of the projective cone is
$$
R_t[u],
$$
where $u$ is the new degree-one cone coordinate.
Hence its degree-$d$ Hilbert function is
$$
H_{C(X_t)}(d)=\sum_{j=0}^d h_t(j).
$$
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, for $t\ne0$ and $d\ge1$ this equals
$$
1+3d,
$$
so the cone has Hilbert polynomial $3d+1$.
For $t=0$ and $d\ge2$ it equals
$$
1+2+3(d-1)=3d,
$$
so the special cone has Hilbert polynomial $3d$.
The Hilbert polynomials are different.
By Theorem III.9.9, fibres of a flat projective family over an integral base have constant Hilbert polynomial.
Therefore the cones cannot form a flat family.
This proves part (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Every very flat family is flat.

::: pf-proof

Very flatness says that for every $d\ge0$ the integer
$$
h_t(d)=\dim_{k(t)}(S_t/I_t)_d
$$
is independent of $t$.
For $d\gg0$, this Hilbert function agrees with the Hilbert polynomial $P_t(d)$ of the fibre.
Thus all fibres have the same Hilbert polynomial.
Theorem III.9.9 then implies that the closed subscheme
$$
X\subseteq\PP_T^n
$$
is flat over the integral noetherian base $T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conefamily}

The cones assemble into a closed subscheme
$$
C\subseteq\PP^{n+1}_T
$$
whose fibre over $t\in T$ is $C(X_t)$.

::: pf-proof

Let $\mathcal I\subseteq\OO_{\PP^n_T}$ be the ideal sheaf of $X\subseteq\PP^n_T$, and put
$$
F=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}\pi_*\mathcal I(d)\subseteq\bigoplus_{d\ge0}\pi_*\OO_{\PP^n_T}(d)=\OO_T[x_0,\ldots,x_n],
$$
where $\pi:\PP^n_T\to T$ is the projection. Define
$$
C=\Proj\bigl(\OO_T[x_0,\ldots,x_{n+1}]/F[x_{n+1}]\bigr)\subseteq\PP^{n+1}_T .
$$
This is a closed subscheme, and its fibre over $t$ is $\Proj$ of the graded ring
$\bigl(\OO_T[x_0,\ldots,x_{n+1}]/F[x_{n+1}]\bigr)\tensor_Tk(t)$.
So it suffices to identify
$$
F\tensor_Tk(t)=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}(I_t)_d,
$$
where $(I_t)_d\subseteq S_t$ is the degree-$d$ part of the homogeneous ideal of $X_t$.

Since $X$ is flat over $T$ by step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, the sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal I(d)\longrightarrow\OO_{\PP^n_T}(d)\longrightarrow\OO_X(d)\longrightarrow0
$$
has $\OO_X(d)$ flat over $T$ and $\OO_{\PP^n_T}(d)$ flat over $T$, hence $\mathcal I(d)$ is flat over $T$.
Therefore
$$
\mathcal I(d)\tensor_Tk(t)=\mathcal I_t(d)\subseteq\OO_{\PP^n_{k(t)}}(d),
$$
the ideal sheaf of $X_t$.

Fix $d$, write $N_d=\binom{n+d}{n}$, and put $M_d=\pi_*\mathcal I(d)\subseteq R^{N_d}$ for an affine open $\Spec R\subseteq T$ containing $t$, with $K$ the fraction field of $R$.
The quotient $R^{N_d}/M_d$ embeds in $H^0(X\cap\Spec R,\OO_X(d))$, and that module is torsion-free over $R$: a nonzerodivisor $\pi\in R$ acts injectively on the sheaf $\OO_X(d)$, because $X$ is flat over the domain $R$, and a morphism of sheaves that is injective on every open set is injective on sections.
Hence $M_d$ is a saturated submodule of $R^{N_d}$, and
$$
M_d\tensor_Rk(t)\longrightarrow k(t)^{N_d}=H^0(\PP^n_{k(t)},\OO(d))
$$
is injective, with image contained in $H^0(\PP^n_{k(t)},\mathcal I_t(d))$ by the identification $\mathcal I(d)\tensor_Tk(t)=\mathcal I_t(d)$ above.
Flat base change to the generic point gives $\dim_K(M_d\tensor_RK)=\dim_K(I_\eta)_d=N_d-h_\eta(d)$, so
$$
\dim_{k(t)}H^0(\PP^n_{k(t)},\mathcal I_t(d))\ge N_d-h_\eta(d).
$$
Very flatness gives $h_t(d)=h_\eta(d)$, while
$\dim_{k(t)}H^0(\PP^n_{k(t)},\mathcal I_t(d))=N_d-h_t(d)$, since by definition $I_t$ is the ideal generated by the kernel of $H^0(\PP^n_{k(t)},\OO(d))\to H^0(X_t,\OO_{X_t}(d))$ and $h_t(d)$ is the dimension of the image of that map.
Hence the two dimensions agree, the displayed injection is an isomorphism, and
$F\tensor_Tk(t)=\bigoplus_d(I_t)_d$.

Therefore the fibre of $C$ over $t$ is $\Proj$ of $\bigoplus_d(S_t/I_t)_d[u]$, whose degree-$d$ part has dimension $\sum_{j=0}^d h_t(j)$; this is exactly $C(X_t)$ by the definition in part (b) of the exercise.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

If $\{X_t\}$ is very flat, then the family of cones $\{C(X_t)\}$ is very flat.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#conefamily){.pf-ref} the cones form a closed subscheme $C\subseteq\PP^{n+1}_T$ whose fibre over $t$ is $C(X_t)$.
For each fibre, the cone coordinate ring is
$$
(S_t/I_t)[u].
$$
Therefore
$$
\dim_{k(t)}\bigl((S_t/I_t)[u]\bigr)_d
=\sum_{j=0}^d h_t(j),
$$
and this is the dimension $\dim_{k(t)}(S_t'/J_t)_d$ of the definition of very flatness for the cone family, where $J_t$ is the homogeneous ideal of $C(X_t)$ in $k(t)[x_0,\ldots,x_{n+1}]$, generated by $I_t$.
Every summand on the right is independent of $t$ by very flatness.
Hence the degree-$d$ dimension of the cone coordinate ring is independent of $t$ for every $d\ge0$.
Thus the cone family is very flat.
By step [](#s4){.pf-ref} it is therefore flat as well.
This proves part (c).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Let $\eta$ be the generic point of the nonsingular parameter curve $T$ in part (d), and let $t\in T$ be closed. The algebraic family is a flat family of schemes near $t$.

::: pf-proof

Every fibre $X_{(s)}$ is projectively normal, hence normal.
Theorem III.9.11 says that an algebraic family of normal varieties parametrized by a nonsingular curve is flat as a family of schemes.
Thus, after replacing $T$ by an affine neighborhood of $t$, we have a flat closed family
$$
\mathcal X\subseteq\PP_T^n.
$$
Since the assertion is local on the parameter curve, we may further replace the base by
$$
\Spec R,
\qquad R=\OO_{T,t},
$$
a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K=k(\eta)$ and residue field $k(t)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

For each $d\ge0$, one has
$$
h_t(d)\le h_\eta(d).
$$

::: pf-proof

Let $\mathcal I$ be the ideal sheaf of
$$
\mathcal X\subseteq\PP_R^n
$$
and put
$$
M_d=H^0(\PP_R^n,\mathcal I(d)).
$$
This is a finite $R$-module, being the kernel of a map between finite modules of global sections on a projective $R$-scheme.
It sits inside
$$
H^0(\PP_R^n,\OO(d))\cong R^{N_d},
\qquad N_d=\binom{n+d}{n}.
$$

The quotient
$$
R^{N_d}/M_d
$$
injects into
$$
H^0(\mathcal X,\OO_{\mathcal X}(d)).
$$
Because $\mathcal X$ is flat over the DVR $R$, the invertible sheaf $\OO_{\mathcal X}(d)$ is $R$-flat and therefore torsion-free; its module of global sections is torsion-free as well.
Thus $R^{N_d}/M_d$ is torsion-free, so $M_d$ is a saturated submodule of the free module $R^{N_d}$.
Consequently reduction modulo the uniformizer gives an injection
$$
M_d\otimes_R k(t)
\hookrightarrow
H^0(\PP_{k(t)}^n,\OO(d)).
$$
Its image consists of equations vanishing on the special fibre, hence lies in $(I_t)_d$.

After tensoring with the fraction field $K$, cohomology commutes with this flat base change, so
$$
M_d\otimes_R K=(I_\eta)_d.
$$
Since $M_d$ is finite torsion-free over the DVR, it is free; therefore
$$
\dim_{k(t)}(M_d\otimes k(t))
=\dim_K(I_\eta)_d.
$$
We obtain
$$
\dim_K(I_\eta)_d\le\dim_{k(t)}(I_t)_d.
$$
Subtracting from $N_d$ gives
$$
h_t(d)\le h_\eta(d).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

Projective normality and upper semicontinuity give the reverse inequality
$$
h_t(d)\ge h_\eta(d).
$$

::: pf-proof

For a projectively normal variety $X_s\subseteq\PP_{k(s)}^n$ of the family, the restriction map
$$
H^0(\PP_{k(s)}^n,\OO(d))
\longrightarrow
H^0(X_s,\OO_{X_s}(d))
$$
is surjective for every $d\ge0$.
Equivalently,
$$
h_s(d)=\dim_{k(s)}H^0(X_s,\OO_{X_s}(d)).
$$
This is applied below only to the closed fibre $X_t$: the generic fibre need
not be projectively normal, and is not assumed to be.

For the proper flat family $\mathcal X\to\Spec R$ and the coherent sheaf $\OO_{\mathcal X}(d)$, upper semicontinuity of fibre cohomology gives
$$
\dim_{k(t)}H^0(X_t,\OO(d))
\ge
\dim_KH^0(X_\eta,\OO(d)).
$$
On the generic fibre the image of $H^0(\PP^n_K,\OO(d))\to H^0(X_\eta,\OO(d))$ has dimension
$$
N_d-\dim_K(I_\eta)_d=h_\eta(d),
$$
so $h^0(X_\eta,\OO(d))\ge h_\eta(d)$ with no hypothesis on $X_\eta$.
Combining the three displays with the projective normality of $X_t$ gives
$$
h_t(d)=\dim_{k(t)}H^0(X_t,\OO(d))\ge h^0(X_\eta,\OO(d))\ge h_\eta(d).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s9}

The algebraic family of projectively normal varieties in part (d) is very flat.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s7){.pf-ref} and [](#s8){.pf-ref} give
$$
h_t(d)=h_\eta(d)
$$
for every closed point $t\in T$ and every $d\ge0$.
Thus, for each fixed degree $d$, $h_t(d)$ is independent of $t$.
This is exactly very flatness.

:::
