::: {.problem}
For any closed subscheme $X \subseteq \PP^n$, we denote by $C(X) \subseteq \PP^{n+1}$ the projective cone over $X$ (I, Ex.
2.10). If $I \subseteq k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ is the largest homogeneous ideal of $X$, then $C(X)$ is defined by the ideal generated by $I$ in $k[x_0, \ldots, x_{n+1}]$.

(a) Give an example to show that if $\ts{X_t}$ is a flat family of closed subschemes of $\PP^n$, then $\ts{C(X_t)}$ need not be a flat family in $\PP^{n+1}$.

(b) To remedy this situation, we make the following definition.
Let $X \subseteq \PP_T^n$ be a closed subscheme, where $T$ is a noetherian integral scheme.
For each $t \in T$, let $I_t \subseteq S_t = k(t)[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ be the homogeneous ideal of $X_t$ in $\PP_{k(t)}^n$.
We say that the family $\ts{X_t}$ is **very flat** if for all $d \geq 0$ the dimension
\[
\dim_{k(t)} (S_t / I_t)_d
\]
is independent of $t$.
Here $(\cdot)_d$ means the homogeneous part of degree $d$.

(c) If $\ts{X_t}$ is a very flat family in $\PP^n$, show that it is flat.
Show also that $\ts{C(X_t)}$ is a very flat family in $\PP^{n+1}$, and hence flat.

(d) If $\ts{X_{(t)}}$ is an algebraic family of projectively normal varieties in $\PP_k^n$, parametrized by a nonsingular curve $T$ over an algebraically closed field $k$, then $\ts{X_{(t)}}$ is a very flat family of schemes.
:::

::: {.solution}
For a fibre $X_t\subseteq\PP^n_{k(t)}$, put
$$
h_t(d)=\dim_{k(t)}(S_t/I_t)_d.
$$

<1>1. There is a flat family of three reduced points in $\PP^2$ whose general fibre is noncollinear and whose special fibre is collinear.

::: {.proof}
Take $T=\AA^1$ with coordinate $t$ and define three sections of
$$
\PP^2\times T\longrightarrow T
$$
by
$$
P_1(t)=[1:0:0],\qquad
P_2(t)=[0:1:0],\qquad
P_3(t)=[1:1:t].
$$
They are pairwise disjoint for every $t$.
Let $\mathcal X$ be their union.
Then
$$
\mathcal X\cong T\amalg T\amalg T
$$
over $T$, so $\mathcal X\to T$ is flat.

For $t\ne0$, the point $P_3(t)$ does not lie on the line $z=0$ through $P_1,P_2$, so the three points are noncollinear.
For $t=0$, all three distinct points lie on $z=0$.
:::

<1>2. The Hilbert functions of the fibres in step <1>1 are
$$
h_t(d)=
\begin{cases}
1,&d=0,\\
3,&d\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad(t\ne0),
$$
whereas
$$
h_0(d)=
\begin{cases}
1,&d=0,\\
2,&d=1,\\
3,&d\ge2.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
For three noncollinear points, linear forms already give arbitrary values at the three points, so the degree-one coordinate quotient has dimension three.
Multiplying separating linear forms by powers of a linear form nonvanishing at all three points shows that the restriction map remains surjective in every degree $d\ge1$.
Thus the first formula holds.

For the special fibre, all points lie on the line $z=0$.
The restrictions of linear forms therefore have dimension two.
Quadratic polynomials on that line interpolate arbitrary values at three distinct points, so the degree-two quotient has dimension three; multiplying by a nonvanishing linear form gives dimension three in every higher degree.
This gives the second formula.
:::

<1>3. The cones over the flat family of step <1>1 do not form a flat family.

::: {.proof}
If $R_t=S_t/I_t$ is the homogeneous coordinate ring of $X_t$, then the homogeneous coordinate ring of the projective cone is
$$
R_t[u],
$$
where $u$ is the new degree-one cone coordinate.
Hence its degree-$d$ Hilbert function is
$$
H_{C(X_t)}(d)=\sum_{j=0}^d h_t(j).
$$
By step <1>2, for $t\ne0$ and $d\ge1$ this equals
$$
1+3d,
$$
so the cone has Hilbert polynomial $3d+1$.
For $t=0$ and $d\ge2$ it equals
$$
1+2+3(d-1)=3d,
$$
so the special cone has Hilbert polynomial $3d$.
The Hilbert polynomials are different.
By Theorem III.9.9, fibres of a flat projective family over an integral base have constant Hilbert polynomial.
Therefore the cones cannot form a flat family.
This proves part (a).
:::

<1>4. Every very flat family is flat.

::: {.proof}
Very flatness says that for every $d\ge0$ the integer
$$
h_t(d)=\dim_{k(t)}(S_t/I_t)_d
$$
is independent of $t$.
For $d\gg0$, this Hilbert function agrees with the Hilbert polynomial $P_t(d)$ of the fibre.
Thus all fibres have the same Hilbert polynomial.
Theorem III.9.9 then implies that the closed subscheme
$$
X\subseteq\PP_T^n
$$
is flat over the integral noetherian base $T$.
:::

<1>5. If $\{X_t\}$ is very flat, then the family of cones $\{C(X_t)\}$ is very flat.

::: {.proof}
For each fibre, the cone coordinate ring is
$$
(S_t/I_t)[u].
$$
Therefore
$$
\dim_{k(t)}\bigl((S_t/I_t)[u]\bigr)_d
=\sum_{j=0}^d h_t(j).
$$
Every summand on the right is independent of $t$ by very flatness.
Hence the degree-$d$ dimension of the cone coordinate ring is independent of $t$ for every $d\ge0$.
Thus the cone family is very flat.
By step <1>4 it is therefore flat as well.
This proves part (c).
:::

<1>6. Let $\eta$ be the generic point of the nonsingular parameter curve $T$ in part (d), and let $t\in T$ be closed. The algebraic family is a flat family of schemes near $t$.

::: {.proof}
Every fibre $X_{(s)}$ is projectively normal, hence normal.
Theorem III.9.11 says that an algebraic family of normal varieties parametrized by a nonsingular curve is flat as a family of schemes.
Thus, after replacing $T$ by an affine neighborhood of $t$, we have a flat closed family
$$
\mathcal X\subseteq\PP_T^n.
$$
Since the assertion is local on the parameter curve, we may further replace the base by
$$
\Spec R,
\qquad R=\OO_{T,t},
$$
a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K=k(\eta)$ and residue field $k(t)$.
:::

<1>7. For each $d\ge0$, one has
$$
h_t(d)\le h_\eta(d).
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $\mathcal I$ be the ideal sheaf of
$$
\mathcal X\subseteq\PP_R^n
$$
and put
$$
M_d=H^0(\PP_R^n,\mathcal I(d)).
$$
This is a finite $R$-module, being the kernel of a map between finite modules of global sections on a projective $R$-scheme.
It sits inside
$$
H^0(\PP_R^n,\OO(d))\cong R^{N_d},
\qquad N_d=\binom{n+d}{n}.
$$

The quotient
$$
R^{N_d}/M_d
$$
injects into
$$
H^0(\mathcal X,\OO_{\mathcal X}(d)).
$$
Because $\mathcal X$ is flat over the DVR $R$, the invertible sheaf $\OO_{\mathcal X}(d)$ is $R$-flat and therefore torsion-free; its module of global sections is torsion-free as well.
Thus $R^{N_d}/M_d$ is torsion-free, so $M_d$ is a saturated submodule of the free module $R^{N_d}$.
Consequently reduction modulo the uniformizer gives an injection
$$
M_d\otimes_R k(t)
\hookrightarrow
H^0(\PP_{k(t)}^n,\OO(d)).
$$
Its image consists of equations vanishing on the special fibre, hence lies in $(I_t)_d$.

After tensoring with the fraction field $K$, cohomology commutes with this flat base change, so
$$
M_d\otimes_R K=(I_\eta)_d.
$$
Since $M_d$ is finite torsion-free over the DVR, it is free; therefore
$$
\dim_{k(t)}(M_d\otimes k(t))
=\dim_K(I_\eta)_d.
$$
We obtain
$$
\dim_K(I_\eta)_d\le\dim_{k(t)}(I_t)_d.
$$
Subtracting from $N_d$ gives
$$
h_t(d)\le h_\eta(d).
$$
:::

<1>8. Projective normality and upper semicontinuity give the reverse inequality
$$
h_t(d)\ge h_\eta(d).
$$

::: {.proof}
For a projectively normal variety $X_s\subseteq\PP_{k(s)}^n$, the restriction map
$$
H^0(\PP_{k(s)}^n,\OO(d))
\longrightarrow
H^0(X_s,\OO_{X_s}(d))
$$
is surjective for every $d\ge0$.
Equivalently,
$$
h_s(d)=\dim_{k(s)}H^0(X_s,\OO_{X_s}(d)).
$$

For the proper flat family $\mathcal X\to\Spec R$ and the coherent sheaf $\OO_{\mathcal X}(d)$, upper semicontinuity of fibre cohomology gives
$$
\dim_{k(t)}H^0(X_t,\OO(d))
\ge
\dim_KH^0(X_\eta,\OO(d)).
$$
Using projective normality on both fibres gives
$$
h_t(d)\ge h_\eta(d).
$$
:::

<1>9. The algebraic family of projectively normal varieties in part (d) is very flat.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>7--<1>8 give
$$
h_t(d)=h_\eta(d)
$$
for every closed point $t\in T$ and every $d\ge0$.
Thus, for each fixed degree $d$, $h_t(d)$ is independent of $t$.
This is exactly very flatness.
:::

<1>10. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 prove part (a), steps <1>4--<1>5 prove part (c), and steps <1>6--<1>9 prove part (d). Part (b) is the definition used in those arguments.
:::
