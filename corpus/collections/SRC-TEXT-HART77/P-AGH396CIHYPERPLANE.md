::: {.problem}
Let $Y \subseteq \PP^n$ be a nonsingular variety of dimension $\geq 2$ over an algebraically closed field $k$.
Suppose $\PP^{n-1}$ is a hyperplane in $\PP^n$ which does not contain $Y$, and such that the scheme $Y' = Y \intersect \PP^{n-1}$ is also nonsingular.
Prove that $Y$ is a complete intersection in $\PP^n$ if and only if $Y'$ is a complete intersection in $\PP^{n-1}$.

Hint: see (II, Ex.
8.4) and use (9.12) applied to the affine cones over $Y$ and $Y'$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
c=\operatorname{codim}_{\PP^n}Y=n-\dim Y.
$$
Then
$$
\operatorname{codim}_{\PP^{n-1}}Y'=c.
$$

<1>1. If $Y$ is a complete intersection in $\PP^n$, then $Y'$ is a complete intersection in the hyperplane $\PP^{n-1}$.

::: {.proof}
Choose coordinates with $H=V_+(\ell)$ and write
$$
S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n].
$$
Since $Y$ is a complete intersection of codimension $c$, its homogeneous ideal is generated by $c$ forms
$$
I(Y)=(F_1,\ldots,F_c).
$$
The scheme-theoretic hyperplane section inside
$$
H=\Proj S/(\ell)
$$
is defined by the images
$$
\bar F_1,\ldots,\bar F_c\in S/(\ell).
$$
Its codimension in $H$ is $c$ because $H$ does not contain the $r$-dimensional variety $Y$ and $Y'$ has dimension $r-1$.
Thus $Y'$ is a codimension-$c$ closed subscheme of $H$ defined scheme-theoretically by $c$ hypersurface equations.
By II.8.4(a), it is a complete intersection in $H\cong\PP^{n-1}$.
:::

<1>2. For the converse, choose coordinates so that
$$
H=V_+(\ell),\qquad \ell=x_n.
$$
Let
$$
S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n],\qquad
I=I(Y),\qquad
A=S/I.
$$
Let $\mathfrak p\subseteq A$ be the homogeneous prime ideal defining the affine cone over $Y'$ inside the affine cone over $Y$.

::: {.proof}
The affine cone over $Y$ is
$$
C(Y)=\Spec A.
$$
The scheme-theoretic hyperplane section is cut out projectively by $\ell$, but the affine scheme $V(\ell)\subseteq C(Y)$ may a priori have extra structure at the cone vertex.
Its unique projective component is the cone over the irreducible nonsingular variety $Y'$.
The corresponding homogeneous prime in $A$ is denoted $\mathfrak p$.
It is the saturation of the principal homogeneous ideal $\ell A$ away from the irrelevant maximal ideal.
:::

<1>3. The ideal $\ell A$ has the unique minimal prime $\mathfrak p$, and
$$
\ell A_{\mathfrak p}=\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Every minimal prime over the principal ideal $\ell A$ has height at most one by the principal ideal theorem.
The cone vertex has codimension
$$
\dim C(Y)=\dim Y+1\ge3,
$$
so no component supported only at the vertex can be a minimal prime of $\ell A$.
Away from the vertex, $V(\ell)$ is the $\GG_m$-bundle over the projective hyperplane section $Y'$, which is irreducible.
Thus $\mathfrak p$ is the unique minimal prime over $\ell A$.

At the generic point of $Y'$, the scheme-theoretic intersection $Y\cap H$ is nonsingular and hence reduced.
Therefore the hyperplane equation vanishes there to order one.
On the cone this says exactly that the one-dimensional local ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ has maximal ideal generated by $\ell$:
$$
\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}=\ell A_{\mathfrak p}.
$$
:::

<1>4. The quotient $A/\mathfrak p$, the homogeneous coordinate ring of $Y'$, is a normal domain.

::: {.proof}
By assumption $Y'$ is a complete intersection and is nonsingular.
Its dimension is
$$
\dim Y'=\dim Y-1\ge1.
$$
Thus $Y'$ is normal.
Exercise II.8.4(b) says that a normal complete intersection of positive dimension is projectively normal.
By definition, its homogeneous coordinate ring is therefore integrally closed.
That homogeneous coordinate ring is precisely
$$
A/\mathfrak p.
$$
Hence $A/\mathfrak p$ is normal.
:::

<1>5. One has
$$
\boxed{\mathfrak p=\ell A},
$$
and the affine cone ring $A$ is normal.

::: {.proof}
Let $\mathfrak q\subseteq A$ be a prime containing $\ell$.
Because $\mathfrak p$ is the unique minimal prime over $\ell A$, we have
$$
\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q.
$$
Apply Proposition III.9.12 to the noetherian local domain
$$
A_{\mathfrak q}
$$
with $t=\ell$ and prime $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak q}$.
Step <1>3 gives hypotheses (1) and (2) of that proposition, and step <1>4 gives hypothesis (3), since
$$
A_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak q}
$$
is a localization of the normal domain $A/\mathfrak p$.
Therefore III.9.12 gives
$$
\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak q}=\ell A_{\mathfrak q}
$$
and also shows that $A_{\mathfrak q}$ is normal.

If $\mathfrak q$ does not contain $\ell$, both ideals localize to the unit ideal.
Hence $\mathfrak p$ and $\ell A$ have equal localizations at every prime of $A$, so
$$
\mathfrak p=\ell A.
$$

For completeness, $A$ is also normal at primes not containing $\ell$.
Indeed,
$$
A_\ell\cong B[\ell,\ell^{-1}],
$$
where
$$
B=(A_\ell)_0
$$
is the affine coordinate ring of the open subset
$$
Y\setminus Y'.
$$
This open subset is nonsingular and therefore normal, so the Laurent polynomial ring $A_\ell$ is normal.
Together with the normality supplied by III.9.12 at primes containing $\ell$, every local ring of $A$ is normal.
:::

<1>6. Let $f_1,\ldots,f_c\in k[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}]$ generate the homogeneous ideal of $Y'$ in $H\cong\PP^{n-1}$. Then there exist homogeneous elements
$$
g_1,\ldots,g_c\in I(Y)
$$
whose restrictions to $H$ are $f_1,\ldots,f_c$.

::: {.proof}
Because $Y'$ is a complete intersection of codimension $c$, choose homogeneous generators
$$
I_H(Y')=(f_1,\ldots,f_c).
$$
Viewed in $S$, the homogeneous ideal of the affine cone over $Y'$ is
$$
(f_1,\ldots,f_c,\ell).
$$
Step <1>5 says that its image in $A=S/I$ is the principal ideal $\ell A$.
Equivalently,
$$
I+(\ell)=(f_1,\ldots,f_c,\ell).
$$
Thus for each $i$ there are homogeneous $g_i\in I$ and $h_i\in S$ with
$$
f_i=g_i+\ell h_i.
$$
After choosing homogeneous components of the same degree, each $g_i$ is homogeneous and restricts to $f_i$ on $H$.
:::

<1>7. The ideal $I(Y)$ is generated by $g_1,\ldots,g_c$.

::: {.proof}
Put
$$
J=(g_1,\ldots,g_c)\subseteq I.
$$
Step <1>6 gives
$$
I+(\ell)=J+(\ell).
$$
Let $F\in I$ be homogeneous.
Then
$$
F=G+\ell H
$$
for some $G\in J$ and $H\in S$.
Since $F,G\in I$, we have
$$
\ell H\in I.
$$
The prime ideal $I$ does not contain $\ell$, because the hyperplane does not contain $Y$.
Therefore $H\in I$.
Thus
$$
I=J+\ell I.
$$

Pass to the finitely generated graded $S$-module
$$
M=I/J.
$$
The equality above says
$$
M=\ell M.
$$
If $M\ne0$, choose a nonzero homogeneous element of least degree.
Writing it as $\ell$ times another element of $M$ lowers the degree by one, a contradiction.
Hence $M=0$, so
$$
I=J=(g_1,\ldots,g_c).
$$
:::

<1>8. The variety $Y$ is a complete intersection in $\PP^n$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 shows that the homogeneous ideal of $Y$ is generated by exactly
$$
c=\operatorname{codim}_{\PP^n}Y
$$
homogeneous elements.
This is the definition of a complete intersection.
Thus the converse implication holds.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves the forward implication, and steps <1>2--<1>8 prove the converse.
:::
