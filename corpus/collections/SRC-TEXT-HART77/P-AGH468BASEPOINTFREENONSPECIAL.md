::: {.problem}
A curve $X$ of genus $g$ has a nonspecial divisor $D$ of degree $d$ such that $\abs{D}$ has no base points if and only if $d \geq g+1$.
:::

::: {.remark title="Erratum"}
As printed, there is one exceptional case.  If $g=0$, then
$X\cong\PP^1$ and the zero divisor is nonspecial and has base-point-free
complete linear system, but its degree is $0<g+1$.

Thus the stated equivalence is correct for $d>0$.  Without the positivity
assumption, the complete statement is that such a divisor exists exactly when
$$
d\ge g+1
$$
or
$$
(g,d)=(0,0).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
L=\OO_X(D).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $D$ is nonspecial and $\abs{D}$ is base-point free, then either
$$
(g,d)=(0,0)
$$
or
$$
d\ge g+1.
$$

::: pf-proof

Nonspeciality means
$$
h^1(X,L)=0.
$$
Hence Riemann--Roch gives
$$
h^0(X,L)=d+1-g.
$$

Suppose first that
$$
h^0(X,L)=1.
$$
Because $\abs{D}$ has no base points, its unique nonzero section vanishes
nowhere.  It therefore trivializes $L$, so
$$
L\cong\OO_X,
\qquad
d=0.
$$
Nonspeciality now says
$$
0=h^1(X,\OO_X)=g,
$$
so $(g,d)=(0,0)$.

Outside this exceptional case one has
$$
h^0(X,L)\ge2.
$$
Using Riemann--Roch again,
$$
d+1-g=h^0(X,L)\ge2,
$$
and therefore
$$
d\ge g+1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $g=0$ and $d\ge1$, then there is a nonspecial base-point-free
divisor of degree $d$.

::: pf-proof

For a smooth projective genus-zero curve over the algebraically closed ground
field,
$$
X\cong\PP^1.
$$
Take
$$
L=\OO_{\PP^1}(d).
$$
For $d\ge0$ this line bundle is generated by its global sections, so its
complete linear system has no base points.  Also
$$
h^1(\PP^1,\OO(d))=0
$$
for $d\ge-1$, hence in particular for $d\ge1$.  Any divisor $D$ with
$\OO_X(D)\cong L$ has the required properties.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Assume $g\ge1$ and fix an integer
$$
d\ge g+1.
$$
Then $\Pic^d(X)$ is an irreducible variety of dimension $g$.

::: pf-proof

Fix a point $P_0\in X$.  Tensoring by
$$
\OO_X(dP_0)
$$
identifies
$$
\Pic^0(X)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\Pic^d(X).
$$
By [[T-CRVJACFUN]],
$$
\Pic^0(X)=\Jac(X)
$$
is an irreducible variety of dimension $g$.  Hence the same is true of
$\Pic^d(X)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The locus
$$
S_d
=
\{L\in\Pic^d(X):h^1(X,L)>0\}
$$
of special line bundles is contained in a proper closed subset of
$\Pic^d(X)$.

::: pf-proof

By Serre duality,
$$
h^1(X,L)=h^0(X,K\otimes L^{-1}).
$$
Thus $L$ is special exactly when
$$
K\otimes L^{-1}
$$
is effective.

Put
$$
e=2g-2-d.
$$
If $e<0$, there are no such line bundles and $S_d$ is empty.

If $e\ge0$, every special $L$ has the form
$$
L\cong\OO_X(K-E)
$$
for some effective divisor $E$ of degree $e$.  Hence $S_d$ is the image of
the proper morphism
$$
\Sym^eX
\longrightarrow
\Pic^d(X),
\qquad
E\longmapsto\OO_X(K-E).
$$
It is therefore closed, and
$$
\dim S_d\le e=2g-2-d.
$$
Since $d\ge g+1$,
$$
2g-2-d\le g-3<g.
$$
Thus $S_d$ is proper in the $g$-dimensional variety $\Pic^d(X)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The locus of line bundles in $\Pic^d(X)$ having a base point is
contained in a proper closed subset.

::: pf-proof

Let $L\in\Pic^d(X)$ and suppose that a point $P\in X$ is a base point of
$\abs{L}$.  Then
$$
h^0(X,L(-P))=h^0(X,L).
$$
Riemann--Roch for $L$ and $L(-P)$ gives
$$
h^0(X,L)
=
d+1-g+h^0(X,K\otimes L^{-1})
$$
and
$$
h^0(X,L(-P))
=
d-g+h^0(X,K\otimes L^{-1}(P)).
$$
Equality therefore implies
$$
h^0(X,K\otimes L^{-1}(P))
=
h^0(X,K\otimes L^{-1})+1
\ge1.
$$
Hence
$$
K\otimes L^{-1}(P)
$$
is effective.

Put
$$
e'=2g-1-d.
$$
If $e'<0$, this is impossible, so every degree-$d$ line bundle is
base-point free.

If $e'\ge0$, every line bundle having a base point is contained in the image
of
$$
X\times\Sym^{e'}X
\longrightarrow
\Pic^d(X),
\qquad
(P,E)\longmapsto\OO_X(K+P-E).
$$
The source is proper, so its image $B_d$ is closed.  Moreover,
$$
\dim B_d
\le
1+e'
=
2g-d
\le
g-1
<g.
$$
Thus $B_d$ is a proper closed subset of $\Pic^d(X)$ containing every line
bundle with a base point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

If $g\ge1$ and $d\ge g+1$, there exists a nonspecial line bundle
$L$ of degree $d$ whose complete linear system has no base points.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $\Pic^d(X)$ is irreducible of dimension $g$.  Steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} give two proper closed subsets
$$
S_d,
\qquad
B_d.
$$
Their union is still a proper closed subset of the irreducible variety
$\Pic^d(X)$.  Choose
$$
L\in\Pic^d(X)\setminus(S_d\cup B_d).
$$
Then $L\notin S_d$ makes $L$ nonspecial, and $L\notin B_d$ implies that
$\abs{L}$ has no base points.

Choose a divisor $D$ with
$$
\OO_X(D)\cong L.
$$
Then
$$
\deg D=d,
$$
and $D$ has the required properties.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

For positive degree $d$, a nonspecial divisor of degree $d$ with
base-point-free complete linear system exists if and only if
$$
\boxed{d\ge g+1}.
$$

::: pf-proof

For the forward implication, positive degree rules out the exceptional case
in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, so that step gives $d\ge g+1$.

For the reverse implication, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves existence when $g=0$, and
step [](#s6){.pf-ref} proves it when $g\ge1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

Without the positivity assumption, the only additional case is
$$
\boxed{(g,d)=(0,0)}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The zero divisor on $\PP^1$ supplies this case, as recorded in the erratum.
Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} shows that no other case with $d<g+1$ can occur.

:::
