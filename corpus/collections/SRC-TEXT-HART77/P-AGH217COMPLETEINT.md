::: problem
A variety $Y$ of dimension $r$ in $\PP^n$ is a **strict complete intersection** if $I(Y)$ can be generated by $n-r$ elements.
It is a **set-theoretic complete intersection** if $Y$ can be written as the intersection of $n-r$ hypersurfaces.

1. Let $Y \subseteq \PP^n$ be a variety with $Y = Z(\mfa)$, and suppose $\mfa$ can be generated by $q$ elements.
   Show that $\dim Y \geq n - q$.

2. Show that a strict complete intersection is a set-theoretic complete intersection.

3. The converse of the previous part is false.
   Let $Y$ be the twisted cubic curve in $\PP^3$.
   Show that $I(Y)$ cannot be generated by two elements, and find hypersurfaces $H_1, H_2$ of degrees $2$ and $3$ with $Y = H_1 \intersect H_2$.

4. It is an unsolved problem whether every closed irreducible curve in $\PP^3$ is a set-theoretic intersection of two surfaces.
:::
