::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $n\ge0$.
A projective variety $Y \subseteq \PP^n$ has dimension $n-1$ if and only if it is the zero set of a single irreducible homogeneous polynomial $f$ of positive degree.
In this case $Y$ is called a **hypersurface** in $\PP^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ and $I=I(Y)$, so $S(Y)=S/I$ is the homogeneous coordinate ring.
The [[P-AGH24CORRESPONDENCE|projective ideal correspondence]] makes $I$ a homogeneous prime ideal and gives $Z(I)=Y$.
Use the cone dimension formula $\dim S(Y)=\dim Y+1$ [@Har10a, Exercise I.2.6] and the dimension formula $\operatorname{height}\mathfrak p+\dim(S/\mathfrak p)=n+1$ for primes in the polynomial ring [@Har10a, Chapter I, §1].

<1>1. If $\dim Y=n-1$, then $I$ is generated by one irreducible homogeneous polynomial of positive degree.

::: {.proof}
The dimension formulas give $\dim(S/I)=n$ and $\operatorname{height}I=1$.
The ring $S$ is a unique factorization domain.
Choose a nonzero element of $I$ and an irreducible factor $f$ which belongs to $I$; primality ensures that some factor does.
The ideal $(f)$ is a nonzero prime contained in $I$.
If it were strictly smaller than $I$, the chain $(0)\subsetneq(f)\subsetneq I$ would contradict $\operatorname{height}I=1$.
Thus $I=(f)$.

The generator $f$ is homogeneous.
To prove this, choose a nonzero homogeneous component $f_d$ of $f$.
Since $I$ is homogeneous, $f_d\in I=(f)$, so $f_d=fg$ for some nonzero $g\in S$.
In a nonnegatively graded domain, the least and greatest nonzero degrees of a product are the respective sums of those degrees in its factors.
The product $fg$ has just one degree, so both factors have just one degree.
Hence $f$ is homogeneous, and its degree is positive because $I$ is proper.
Finally $Y=Z(I)=Z(f)$.
:::

<1>2. If $Y=Z(f)$ for an irreducible homogeneous $f$ of positive degree, then $\dim Y=n-1$.

::: {.proof}
The irreducible element $f$ generates a prime ideal in the UFD $S$.
Since $Y$ is nonempty, [[P-AGH23ZIPROPS]], part (d), gives
$$
I(Y)=I(Z(f))=\sqrt{(f)}=(f).
$$
The principal ideal theorem and $f\ne0$ give $\operatorname{height}(f)=1$.
The polynomial-ring dimension formula yields $\dim(S/(f))=n$.
Therefore
$$
\dim Y=\dim S(Y)-1=\dim(S/(f))-1=n-1.
$$
This uses the homogeneous coordinate ring, whose dimension exceeds that of the projective variety by one.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>2 prove both implications.
When $n=0$, the only projective variety in $\PP^0$ is its single point, of dimension zero, while every nonzero positive-degree homogeneous polynomial in $k[x_0]$ has empty projective zero set.
Thus neither condition holds in that case, consistent with the equivalence.
:::
