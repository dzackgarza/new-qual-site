::: {.problem}
Describe $\Spec \ZZ$, and show that it is a final object for the category of schemes: each scheme $X$ admits a unique morphism to $\Spec \ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The prime ideals of $\mathbb Z$ are
\[
(0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(p)
\]
for prime integers $p$.
::: {.proof}
The ring $\mathbb Z$ is a principal ideal domain.  A nonzero prime ideal is therefore generated by an irreducible element, and the irreducible elements of $\mathbb Z$ are precisely the primes up to sign.  Thus every nonzero prime ideal is $(p)$ for a prime $p$.  Since $\mathbb Z$ is an integral domain, $(0)$ is prime as well.
:::

<1>2. Hence, as a set,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z
=\{(0)\}\cup\{(p):p\text{ prime}\}.
}
\]
The point $(0)$ is the generic point, and every $(p)$ is closed.
::: {.proof}
The closure of a point $\mathfrak p$ in an affine spectrum is
\[
V(\mathfrak p)
=\{\mathfrak q:\mathfrak q\supseteq\mathfrak p\}.
\]
Thus
\[
\overline{\{(0)\}}
=V(0)
=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z,
\]
so $(0)$ is generic.

For a prime $p$,
\[
V(p)=\{(p)\},
\]
because the only prime ideal containing the maximal ideal $(p)$ is itself.  Hence $(p)$ is closed.
:::

<1>3. The proper closed subsets of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ are finite sets of closed points.
More precisely, for $0\ne n\in\mathbb Z$,
\[
\boxed{
V(n)=\{(p):p\mid n\}.
}
\]
::: {.proof}
A prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ belongs to $V(n)$ exactly when
\[
n\in\mathfrak p.
\]
The generic prime $(0)$ contains $n$ only when $n=0$.  For a closed point $(p)$,
\[
n\in(p)
\iff
p\mid n.
\]
Only finitely many primes divide a nonzero integer.  Conversely, every closed subset is $V(I)$ for an ideal $I=(n)$, since every ideal of $\mathbb Z$ is principal.
:::

<1>4. The local rings are
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z,(0)}=\mathbb Q,
\qquad
\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z,(p)}=\mathbb Z_{(p)}.
}
\]
::: {.proof}
For every affine scheme,
\[
\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}A,\mathfrak p}=A_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
At $(0)$, localizing $\mathbb Z$ at all nonzero integers gives its fraction field $\mathbb Q$.  At $(p)$ one obtains the localization
\[
\mathbb Z_{(p)}
=\left\{\frac ab:p\nmid b\right\}.
\]
:::

<1>5. For every scheme $X$, morphisms
\[
X\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z
\]
are naturally in bijection with ring homomorphisms
\[
\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X).
\]
::: {.proof}
This is Hartshorne II.2.4 applied to the ring $A=\mathbb Z$:
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sch}}(X,\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}
(\mathbb Z,\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)).
\]
:::

<1>6. There is exactly one unital ring homomorphism
\[
\mathbb Z\longrightarrow R
\]
for every ring $R$.
::: {.proof}
A unital ring homomorphism must send
\[
1\longmapsto1_R.
\]
Then additivity forces
\[
n\longmapsto n\cdot1_R
\]
for every positive integer $n$, and similarly for negative integers.  Thus the homomorphism is uniquely determined and always exists.
:::

<1>7. Consequently, every scheme has a unique morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sch}}(X,\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z)
\text{ is a singleton}.
}
\]
Hence $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ is the final object in the category of schemes.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>5 and <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>4 describe the spectrum, and <1>5--<1>7 prove its terminal property.
