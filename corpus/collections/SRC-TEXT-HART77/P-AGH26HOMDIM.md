::: {.problem}
Let $Y$ be a projective variety with homogeneous coordinate ring $S(Y)$.
Show that $\dim S(Y) = \dim Y + 1$.
:::

::: {.hint}
Let $\phi_i : U_i \to \AA^n$ be the homeomorphism
$$
\thevector{a_0 : \cdots : a_n} \mapsto \qty{ \frac{a_0}{a_i}, \ldots, \widehat{\frac{a_i}{a_i}}, \ldots, \frac{a_n}{a_i} }
$$
from the open set $U_i = \PP^n \sm Z(x_i)$, let $Y_i$ be the affine variety $\phi_i(Y \intersect U_i)$, and let $A(Y_i)$ be its affine coordinate ring.
Show that $A(Y_i)$ can be identified with the subring of elements of degree $0$ of the localized ring $S(Y)_{x_i}$.
Then show that $S(Y)_{x_i} \cong A(Y_i)[x_i, \inverseof{x_i}]$.
Now use Proposition I.1.7, Theorem I.1.8A and Ex. I.1.10, and look at transcendence degrees.
Conclude also that $\dim Y = \dim Y_i$ whenever $Y_i$ is nonempty.
:::

::: {.solution}

The proof is the card's own, following the printed hint; the two published accounts are recorded in the remark below and were used as a check, not transcribed.

Let $Y\subseteq\PP^{n}$ be a projective variety, $S=k[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]$, and $\bar x_{j}$ the image of $x_{j}$ in $S(Y)=S/I(Y)$, a graded domain ([[P-AGH24CORRESPONDENCE]]). Fix $i$ with $Y_{i}=\phi_{i}(Y\cap U_{i})$ nonempty, so that $x_{i}\notin I(Y)$ and $\bar x_{i}\ne0$; write $S_{(\bar x_{i})}$ for the degree-zero part of the localization $S(Y)_{\bar x_{i}}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #chart-ring}
$A(Y_{i})\cong S(Y)_{(\bar x_{i})}$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #substitution}
The substitution $y_{j}\mapsto x_{j}/x_{i}$, $j\ne i$, is an isomorphism $k[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]\to S_{(x_{i})}$; its inverse sends a homogeneous $F$ of degree $d$ to $F/x_{i}^{d}$, read with $x_{i}=1$.

::: pf-proof
Assigning $x_{j}/x_{i}$ to $y_{j}$ defines a $k$-algebra homomorphism into the degree-zero part of the localized polynomial ring, which is generated by those $n$ elements: a degree-zero element of $S_{x_{i}}$ is $F/x_{i}^{d}$ with $F$ homogeneous of degree $d$, and substituting $x_{i}=1$ in $F$ expresses it as a polynomial in the $x_{j}/x_{i}$, $j\ne i$. The two assignments are mutually inverse, so both maps are isomorphisms.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ideal}
That isomorphism carries $I(Y_{i})$ onto $I(Y)S_{(x_{i})}$.

::: pf-proof
For homogeneous $F$ of degree $d$, the function $F/x_{i}^{d}$ vanishes on $Y_{i}$ exactly when $F$ vanishes on $Y\cap U_{i}$, that is, at every point of $Y$ with $x_{i}\ne0$; and $x_{i}F$ vanishes at every point of $Y$ where $F$ does, and vice versa on $U_{i}$, so $F$ vanishes on $Y\cap U_{i}$ exactly when $x_{i}F\in I(Y)$. Since $F/x_{i}^{d}=x_{i}F/x_{i}^{d+1}$, the ideal $I(Y_{i})$ corresponds to $I(Y)S_{(x_{i})}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
Localization is exact, and taking degree-zero parts of a graded ring is exact, so passing to quotients in step [](#ideal){.pf-ref} gives
$$
A(Y_{i})=k[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]/I(Y_{i})\cong S_{(x_{i})}\big/I(Y)S_{(x_{i})}\cong S(Y)_{(\bar x_{i})},
$$
the last identification because localizing at $x_{i}$ and then reducing modulo $I(Y)$ is localizing $S(Y)$ at $\bar x_{i}$.
:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #laurent}
$S(Y)_{\bar x_{i}}\cong A(Y_{i})[t,\inverseof{t}]$ with $t$ transcendental over $A(Y_{i})$.

::: pf-proof
A homogeneous element $g/\bar x_{i}^{m}$ of degree $e$ equals $\big(g/\bar x_{i}^{m+e}\big)\bar x_{i}^{e}$, and $g/\bar x_{i}^{m+e}$ has degree $0$; so the localization is generated over its degree-zero part by $\bar x_{i}$ and $\bar x_{i}^{-1}$, that is $S(Y)_{\bar x_{i}}=S(Y)_{(\bar x_{i})}[\bar x_{i},\bar x_{i}^{-1}]$. The element $\bar x_{i}$ is transcendental over $S(Y)_{(\bar x_{i})}$: in a relation $\sum_{j}c_{j}\bar x_{i}^{j}=0$ with each $c_{j}$ of degree $0$, the term $c_{j}\bar x_{i}^{j}$ is the homogeneous component of degree $j$, so each $c_{j}\bar x_{i}^{j}=0$ and hence $c_{j}=0$, the ring being a domain and $\bar x_{i}\ne0$. Writing $t$ for $\bar x_{i}$ and applying step [](#chart-ring){.pf-ref} gives the display.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #transcendence}
$\dim S(Y)=\dim Y_{i}+1$ for every $i$ with $Y_{i}\ne\emptyset$.

::: pf-proof
Fraction fields: $\operatorname{Frac}S(Y)=\operatorname{Frac}S(Y)_{\bar x_{i}}=K(Y_{i})(t)$ by step [](#laurent){.pf-ref}, with $t$ transcendental over $K(Y_{i})$, so
$$
\trdeg_{k}\operatorname{Frac}S(Y)=\trdeg_{k}K(Y_{i})+1 .
$$
For a finitely generated $k$-domain the Krull dimension equals the transcendence degree of its fraction field [@Har77, Theorem I.1.8A], which gives $\dim S(Y)=\trdeg_{k}\operatorname{Frac}S(Y)$; and $\dim Y_{i}=\trdeg_{k}K(Y_{i})$ by Proposition I.1.7, since $Y_{i}$ is an affine variety with coordinate ring $A(Y_{i})$ and function field $K(Y_{i})$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dimension}
$\dim S(Y)=\dim Y+1$.

::: pf-proof
The open sets $Y\cap U_{i}$ cover $Y$, and $\phi_{i}$ maps $Y\cap U_{i}$ homeomorphically onto $Y_{i}$, so $\dim Y=\sup_{i}\dim Y_{i}$ over the $i$ with $Y_{i}\ne\emptyset$ (Ex. I.1.10(b)). Every such $Y_{i}$ satisfies $\dim Y_{i}=\dim S(Y)-1$ by step [](#transcendence){.pf-ref}, so the supremum is $\dim S(Y)-1$ and
$$
\boxed{\dim S(Y)=\dim Y+1}.
$$
In particular $\dim Y_{i}=\dim Y$ for every nonempty $Y_{i}$, as the hint also asks.
:::

::: {.remark title="Published solution"}
Richard E. Borcherds, *Hartshorne, Chapter 1: Answers to exercises* (1994), Exercise 2.6, PDF p. 2, text version `tmp/sources/hartshorne-solutions-borcherds.md`, and William Troiani, *Hartshorne Exercise Solutions* (October 2020), Exercise 2.6, PDF p. 4, text version `tmp/sources/hartshorne-solutions-troiani.md`: both identify the degree-zero part of $S(Y)_{x_i}$ with $A(Y_i)$, obtain $S(Y)_{x_i}\cong A(Y_i)[x_i,x_i^{-1}]$, and conclude through transcendence degrees that $\dim S(Y)=1+\dim Y_i$, then use that the $Y_i$ cover $Y$ to get $\dim S(Y)=1+\dim Y$; Troiani isolates the two ring facts as lemmas, one of them a criterion for a polynomial extension to be injective.
The proof above writes the two isomorphisms through the substitutions $y_j\mapsto x_j/x_i$, checks that $\bar x_i$ is transcendental over the degree-zero part by the degree of $c_j\bar x_i^j$, and takes the cover step from Exercise I.1.10(b).
Agree.
:::
