::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a noetherian, integral, separated, regular scheme.
(We say a scheme is regular if all of its local rings are regular local rings.)
Recall the definition of the Grothendieck group $K(X)$ from (II, Ex.
6.10).

We define similarly another group $K_1(X)$ using locally free sheaves: it is the quotient of the free abelian group generated by all locally free (coherent) sheaves, by the subgroup generated by all expressions of the form $\mce-\mce'-\mce''$, whenever $0 \to \mce' \to \mce \to \mce'' \to 0$ is a short exact sequence of locally free sheaves.

Clearly there is a natural group homomorphism $\varepsilon: K_1(X) \to K(X)$.
Show that $\varepsilon$ is an isomorphism as follows.

a. Given a coherent sheaf $\mcf$, use (Ex.
6.8) to show that it has a locally free resolution $\mce_\bullet \to \mcf \to 0$.
Then use (6.11A) and (Ex.
6.5) to show that it has a finite locally free resolution
\[
0 \to \mce_n \to \cdots \to \mce_1 \to \mce_0 \to \mcf \to 0.
\]

b. For each $\mcf$, choose a finite locally free resolution $\mce_\bullet \to \mcf \to 0$, and let $\delta(\mcf)=\sum_i (-1)^i \gamma(\mce_i)$ in $K_1(X)$.
Show that $\delta(\mcf)$ is independent of the resolution chosen, that it defines a homomorphism of $K(X)$ to $K_1(X)$, and finally, that it is an inverse to $\varepsilon$.
:::
