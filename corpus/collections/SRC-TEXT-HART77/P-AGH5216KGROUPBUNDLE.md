::: {.problem}
Let $C$ be a nonsingular affine curve.
Show that two locally free sheaves $\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{E}^{\prime}$ of the same rank are isomorphic if and only if their classes in the Grothendieck group $K(X)$ (II, Ex.
6.10) and (II, Ex.
6.11) are the same.
This is false for a projective curve.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
C=\Spec A.
$$
Because $C$ is a nonsingular affine curve, $A$ is a noetherian integrally
closed domain of dimension one; hence $A$ is a Dedekind domain.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Locally free sheaves of finite rank on $C$ are equivalent to finitely
generated projective $A$-modules.

::: pf-proof

For an affine scheme, the functor
$$
M\longmapsto\widetilde M
$$
identifies finitely generated projective $A$-modules with locally free
coherent sheaves on $\Spec A$. Under this correspondence, direct sums,
exterior powers, ranks, and isomorphisms are preserved.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If
$$
\mathcal E\cong\mathcal E',
$$
then
$$
[\mathcal E]=[\mathcal E']
$$
in $K(C)$.

::: pf-proof

The Grothendieck group $K(C)$ is generated by isomorphism classes of coherent
sheaves subject to the short-exact-sequence relations
[[P-AGH2610KGROUP]]. Isomorphic coherent sheaves define the same generator,
so the implication is immediate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If
$$
[\mathcal E]=[\mathcal E']
$$
in $K(C)$, then
$$
\det\mathcal E\cong\det\mathcal E'.
$$

::: pf-proof

Hartshorne II.6.11, proved on [[P-AGH2611KCURVE]], constructs a homomorphism
$$
\det:K(C)\longrightarrow\Pic(C)
$$
whose value on a locally free sheaf is its usual top exterior power.
Applying this homomorphism to the assumed equality of Grothendieck classes
gives
$$
[\det\mathcal E]=[\det\mathcal E']
$$
in $\Pic(C)$, hence the two determinant line bundles are isomorphic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Let $P$ be a finitely generated projective module of rank $r\ge1$
over a Dedekind domain $A$. Then
$$
\boxed{P\cong A^{r-1}\oplus\det P.}
$$

::: pf-proof

This is the Steinitz classification of finitely generated projective modules
over a Dedekind domain. Equivalently, every such module is a direct sum of
rank-one projective modules
$$
P\cong I_1\oplus\cdots\oplus I_r,
$$
and the standard ideal identity
$$
I\oplus J\cong A\oplus IJ
$$
allows one to combine the summands successively:
$$
P
\cong
A^{r-1}\oplus(I_1\cdots I_r).
$$
The final rank-one projective module is the top exterior power
$$
I_1\cdots I_r\cong\bigwedge^rP=\det P.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

If $\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal E'$ have the same rank and the same
class in $K(C)$, then
$$
\boxed{\mathcal E\cong\mathcal E'.}
$$

::: pf-proof

Let $P$ and $P'$ be the finite projective $A$-modules corresponding to
$\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal E'$ by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, and let their common rank be
$r$.

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\det P\cong\det P'.
$$
Applying step [](#s4){.pf-ref} twice gives
$$
P
\cong
A^{r-1}\oplus\det P
\cong
A^{r-1}\oplus\det P'
\cong
P'.
$$
Sheafifying this module isomorphism gives
$$
\mathcal E\cong\mathcal E'.
$$
Together with step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, this proves the asserted equivalence on a
nonsingular affine curve.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The assertion fails on the projective curve $\PP^1$.

::: pf-proof

Fix a closed point $P\in\PP^1$. The standard exact sequences
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\OO_{\PP^1}(-1)
\longrightarrow
\OO_{\PP^1}
\longrightarrow
k(P)
\longrightarrow0
$$
and
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\OO_{\PP^1}
\longrightarrow
\OO_{\PP^1}(1)
\longrightarrow
k(P)
\longrightarrow0
$$
give in $K(\PP^1)$
$$
[\OO(-1)]
=[\OO]-[k(P)]
$$
and
$$
[\OO(1)]
=[\OO]+[k(P)].
$$
Hence
$$
[\OO(1)\oplus\OO(-1)]
=2[\OO]
=[\OO\oplus\OO].
$$
The two bundles have the same rank, namely $2$, and the same Grothendieck
class.

They are not isomorphic. After twisting by $\OO(-1)$,
$$
H^0\bigl(\PP^1,(\OO\oplus\OO)(-1)\bigr)
=0,
$$
whereas
$$
H^0\bigl(\PP^1,(\OO(1)\oplus\OO(-1))(-1)\bigr)
=
H^0(\PP^1,\OO\oplus\OO(-2))
\cong k.
$$
Thus the original bundles cannot be isomorphic. This proves the stated
projective counterexample.

:::

:::

step [](#s6){.pf-ref} proves that it fails for projective curves.
