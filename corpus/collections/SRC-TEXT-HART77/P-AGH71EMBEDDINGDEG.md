::: {.problem}
(a) Find the degree of the $d$-uple embedding of $\PP^n$ in $\PP^N$.

(b) Find the degree of the Segre embedding of $\PP^r \times \PP^s$ in $\PP^N$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Recall that if a projective variety $X$ has dimension $m$ and Hilbert polynomial $P_X(t)$, then
$$
P_X(t)=\frac{\deg X}{m!}t^m+\text{terms of lower degree}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step
The homogeneous coordinate ring of the $d$-uple embedding of $\PP^n$ is the Veronese ring
$$
S^{(d)}=\bigoplus_{q\ge0}S_{dq},
\qquad S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n].
$$

::: pf-proof
The $d$-uple embedding is defined by all monomials of degree $d$.
Its homogeneous coordinate ring is therefore the subring of $S$ generated by $S_d$, with grading in which $S_d$ has degree one.
Its degree-$q$ piece is exactly $S_{dq}$, giving the displayed Veronese ring.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #duple-degree}
The degree of the $d$-uple embedding is
$$
\boxed{d^n}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For every $q\ge0$,
$$
\dim_k S_{dq}=\binom{dq+n}{n}.
$$
Hence the Hilbert polynomial of the embedded copy of $\PP^n$ is
$$
P(q)=\binom{dq+n}{n}
=\frac{d^n}{n!}q^n+\text{terms of lower degree}.
$$
The image has dimension $n$.
Comparing the leading coefficient with the degree formula gives
$$
\deg(\nu_d(\PP^n))=n!\cdot\frac{d^n}{n!}=d^n.
$$
This proves part (a).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #segre-coordinate-ring-piece}
The homogeneous coordinate ring of the Segre embedding has degree-$q$ piece
$$
k[x_0,\ldots,x_r]_q\otimes_k k[y_0,\ldots,y_s]_q.
$$

::: pf-proof
The Segre embedding is defined by the bihomogeneous products $x_i y_j$.
A homogeneous polynomial of degree $q$ in the Segre coordinates pulls back to a bihomogeneous polynomial of bidegree $(q,q)$.
Conversely, every monomial of bidegree $(q,q)$ is a product of $q$ terms $x_i y_j$, so every such bihomogeneous monomial lies in the image of the degree-$q$ piece of the Segre coordinate ring.
Thus that piece identifies with
$$
k[x_0,\ldots,x_r]_q\otimes_k k[y_0,\ldots,y_s]_q.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #segre-degree}
The degree of the Segre embedding is
$$
\boxed{\binom{r+s}{r}=\binom{r+s}{s}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#segre-coordinate-ring-piece){.pf-ref}, its Hilbert function is
$$
H(q)
=\binom{q+r}{r}\binom{q+s}{s}.
$$
This is already a polynomial in $q$, with leading term
$$
\frac{1}{r!s!}q^{r+s}.
$$
The Segre image has dimension $r+s$.
Therefore its degree is
$$
(r+s)!\frac{1}{r!s!}
=\binom{r+s}{r}
=\binom{r+s}{s}.
$$
This proves part (b).
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#duple-degree){.pf-ref} gives the degree of the $d$-uple embedding and step [](#segre-degree){.pf-ref} gives the degree of the Segre embedding.
:::

:::
:::

::: {.remark}
The answers are $d^n$ for the $d$-uple embedding and $\binom{r+s}{s}$ for the Segre embedding.
:::

::: {.remark title="Published solution"}
Richard E. Borcherds, *Hartshorne, Chapter 1: Answers to exercises* (1994), Exercise 7.1, PDF p. 9: (a) the polynomials of degree $m$ in $N+1$ variables restricted to the image of $\PP^n$ are the polynomials of degree $md$ in $n+1$ variables, so the Hilbert polynomial is $\binom{dk+n}{n}=(dk)^n/n!+\cdots$ and the degree is $d^n$; (b) the Hilbert polynomial of the Segre embedding is the product $\binom{k+r}{r}\binom{k+s}{s}=\binom{r+s}{r}k^{r+s}/(r+s)!+\cdots$, so the degree is $\binom{r+s}{r}$.
William Troiani, *Hartshorne Exercise Solutions* (October 2020), stops at §I.5 and gives no solution to 7.1.
Andrew Egbert, *Solutions to Hartshorne's Algebraic Geometry* (October 3, 2013), Exercise I.7.1, PDF p. 55: (a) "$\binom{dk+n}{n}=(dk)^n/n!+O(d^{n-1}k^{n-1})$, this is the dimension of the space of monomials of degree $d$"; (b) the subring generated by the products $x_iy_j$ has dimension $\binom{k+s}{s}\binom{k+r}{r}=\binom{r+s}{r}k^{r+s}/(r+s)!+O(k^{r+s-1})$.
Joe Cutrone and Nick Marshburn, *Algebraic Geometry by Robin Hartshorne: Solutions*, Exercise I.7.1, PDF p. 25: (a) $\varphi_Y(l)=\binom{n+dl}{n}$, so $P_Y(z)=\binom{n+dz}{n}$ and the degree is $n!\cdot d^n/n!=d^n$; (b) $\varphi_Y(l)=\binom{r+l}{l}\binom{s+l}{l}$, so the degree is $(r+s)!/(r!s!)=\binom{r+s}{r}$.
All three compute the Hilbert function of the embedded variety directly and agree with the card. Step [](#segre-coordinate-ring-piece){.pf-ref} records the identification $S(\text{Segre})_q\cong k[x_0,\ldots,x_r]_q\otimes k[y_0,\ldots,y_s]_q$ that the three use implicitly when they multiply the two binomials. Matches.
:::
