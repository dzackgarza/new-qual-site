::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, and let $V\subseteq\PP_k^n$ be a projective variety of positive dimension which is nonsingular in codimension one.
Let $X=C(V)\subseteq\AA_k^{n+1}$ be its affine cone (I, Ex. 2.10), and let $\bar X\subseteq\PP_k^{n+1}$ be its projective closure.
Let $P \in X$ be the vertex of the cone.

(a) Let $\pi:\bar X\setminus\{P\}\to V$ be the projection map.
Show that $V$ has an open cover $(U_i)$ for which $\pi^{-1}(U_i)\cong U_i\times\AA_k^1$ over $U_i$.
Then show, as in (6.6), that $\pi^*:\Cl(V)\to\Cl(\bar X\setminus\{P\})$ is an isomorphism.
Since $\Cl(\bar X)\cong\Cl(\bar X\setminus\{P\})$, conclude that $\Cl(V)\cong\Cl(\bar X)$.

(b) Regard $V\subseteq\bar X$ as the hyperplane section at infinity.
Show that $[V]=\pi^*[V.H]$ in $\Cl(\bar X)$, where $H\subseteq\PP_k^n$ is any hyperplane not containing $V$ and the pullback uses the identification in (a).
Deduce from (6.5) the exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow\ZZ\xrightarrow{1\mapsto[V.H]}\Cl(V)\longrightarrow\Cl(X)\longrightarrow0.
$$
The second map is $\pi^*$ followed by restriction to $X\setminus\{P\}$ and the inverse of $\Cl(X)\to\Cl(X\setminus\{P\})$.
Prove injectivity of the first map using the degree map of the preceding exercise.

(c) Let $S(V)$ be the homogeneous coordinate ring of $V$, also the affine coordinate ring of $X$.
Show that $S(V)$ is a unique factorization domain if and only if $V$ is projectively normal (Ex. 5.14) and $\Cl(V)\cong\ZZ$ is generated by $[V.H]$.

(d) Let $\OO_{X,P}$ be the local ring at the vertex.
Show that restriction induces an isomorphism $\Cl(X)\to\Cl(\Spec\OO_{X,P})$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $S=S(V)=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]/I_V$, so $X=\Spec S$ and $\bar X=\Proj S[z]$.
Then $P=[0:\cdots:0:1]$, the section at infinity is $V_\infty=V(z)\cong V$, and $X=D_+(z)$.
All class groups use the [[D-5PQ5W|Weil-divisor convention]].

<1>1. If $W$ is an integral variety regular in codimension one and $p:Y\to W$ is a Zariski locally trivial $\AA^1$-bundle, then $Y$ is regular in codimension one and $p^*:\Cl(W)\to\Cl(Y)$ is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
Each trivializing chart has the form $U\times\AA^1$.
These charts are integral and regular in codimension one by [@Har10a, Proposition II.6.6].
Any two nonempty trivializing charts meet over the generic point of $W$, so $Y$ is integral.
The local prime-divisor classification in that proposition applies on every chart.
Thus a prime divisor of $Y$ is either vertical, of the form $p^{-1}(D)$ for a prime divisor $D\subseteq W$, or horizontal, dominating $W$.

At the generic point of $p^{-1}(D)$, a uniformizer of $\OO_{W,\eta_D}$ remains a uniformizer.
At a horizontal prime divisor, every nonzero element of $K(W)$ is a unit.
Consequently $p^*D=p^{-1}(D)$ extends linearly to divisors and satisfies
$$
\operatorname{div}_Y(p^*g)=p^*(\operatorname{div}_W g)
\qquad(g\in K(W)^\times).
$$
It therefore induces a homomorphism on class groups.

Choose a coordinate $t$ on the generic fiber $Y_{K(W)}\cong\AA^1_{K(W)}$.
A horizontal prime divisor $Z$ determines a prime ideal $(f)$ of $K(W)[t]$, with $f$ irreducible.
Viewed as a rational function on $Y$, $f$ has horizontal divisor exactly $Z$, with coefficient one.
Thus $Z-\operatorname{div}_Y(f)$ is vertical, proving surjectivity.

If $p^*D=\operatorname{div}_Y(g)$, then the divisor of $g$ on $\AA^1_{K(W)}$ is zero.
A quotient of relatively prime polynomials in $K(W)[t]$ has zero divisor only when both polynomials are constant.
Hence $g\in K(W)^\times$, and the vertical valuations give $D=\operatorname{div}_W(g)$.
This proves injectivity.
:::

<1>2. The projection in part (a) is locally trivial and induces $\Cl(V)\cong\Cl(\bar X)$.

::: {.proof}
For each nonempty $U_i=V\cap D_+(x_i)$, put $B_i=(S_{x_i})_0$.
Projection is $[x_0:\cdots:x_n:z]\mapsto[x_0:\cdots:x_n]$, and its inverse image of $U_i$ has coordinate ring
$$
(S[z]_{x_i})_0=B_i[z/x_i].
$$
Thus $\pi^{-1}(U_i)\cong U_i\times\AA_k^1$, with fiber coordinate $t_i=z/x_i$.
The coordinates satisfy $t_j=(x_i/x_j)t_i$ on overlaps.
Step <1>1 now gives $\pi^*:\Cl(V)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Cl(\bar X\setminus\{P\})$.

The charts show that $\dim\bar X=\dim V+1\ge2$ and that $\bar X\setminus\{P\}$ is regular in codimension one.
The closed point $P$ has codimension at least two, so $\bar X$ and its open subset $X$ are regular in codimension one as well.
Deleting $P$ changes neither their prime-divisor groups nor their valuations.
Hence restriction identifies $\Cl(\bar X)$ with $\Cl(\bar X\setminus\{P\})$, and $\Cl(X)$ with $\Cl(X\setminus\{P\})$ [@Har10a, Proposition II.6.5(b)].
This proves part (a).
:::

<1>3. The hyperplane class $h=[V.H]$ has infinite order, and part (b) gives
$$
\boxed{\Cl(X)\cong\Cl(V)/\ZZ h}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Write $H=V(\ell)$ for a linear form $\ell$ not vanishing identically on $V$.
The rational function $z/\ell$ on $\bar X$, restricted to $\pi^{-1}(U_i)$, is $t_i/(\ell/x_i)$.
The numerator vanishes to order one on $V_\infty$, and the denominator has divisor $\pi^*(V.H)$ by the vertical valuation computation in step <1>1.
These divisors have no common component.
There are no prime divisors supported at $P$, so
$$
\operatorname{div}_{\bar X}(z/\ell)=V_\infty-\pi^*(V.H).
$$
This proves $[V_\infty]=\pi^*h$ under the identification of step <1>2.

Since $X=\bar X\setminus V_\infty$, divisor restriction gives an exact sequence [@Har10a, Proposition II.6.5(c)]
$$
\ZZ\xrightarrow{1\mapsto[V_\infty]}\Cl(\bar X)\longrightarrow\Cl(X)\longrightarrow0
$$
Transport this sequence along the isomorphism in step <1>2.
The result is the sequence in part (b), with the stated maps.
The [[P-AGH262DEGDIV|degree map]] satisfies $\deg(h)=\deg V>0$.
Thus $mh=0$ implies $m\deg V=0$, hence $m=0$, proving injectivity at the left end and the displayed quotient description.
:::

<1>4. The unique-factorization criterion in part (c) holds.

::: {.proof}
The finitely generated domain $S$ is noetherian.
By the definition of [[P-AGH2514PROJNORM|projective normality]], $V$ is projectively normal precisely when $S$ is integrally closed.
A noetherian integrally closed domain is a unique factorization domain precisely when its divisor class group is zero [@Har10a, Proposition II.6.2].
Every unique factorization domain is integrally closed, so
$$
S\text{ is a unique factorization domain}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
S\text{ is integrally closed and }\Cl(X)=0.
$$
Step <1>3 identifies the last vanishing with $\Cl(V)=\ZZ h$.
Since $h$ has infinite order, this is exactly the asserted infinite cyclic group with its specified generator.
:::

<1>5. Restriction $\rho:\Cl(X)\to\Cl(\Spec\OO_{X,P})$ is surjective, and its kernel is generated by the classes of prime divisors of $X$ not containing $P$.

::: {.proof}
Let $\mathfrak m=(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\subseteq S$, so $\OO_{X,P}=S_{\mathfrak m}$.
The height-one primes of $S_{\mathfrak m}$ correspond to the height-one primes of $S$ contained in $\mathfrak m$, and their local DVRs are unchanged.
Thus restriction of divisors discards exactly the prime divisors not containing $P$, and it carries principal divisors to principal divisors.
Every local prime divisor is the restriction of its closure in $X$, proving surjectivity.

If a divisor $D$ becomes principal locally, choose $g\in K(X)^\times$ whose local divisor is its restriction.
Then $D-\operatorname{div}_X(g)$ is supported on prime divisors not containing $P$.
Conversely, every such divisor restricts to zero.
This proves the kernel description.
:::

<1>6. Every prime divisor $Z\subseteq X$ not containing $P$ has zero class in $\Cl(X)$.

::: {.proof}
Let $p:T\to V$ be the total space of the tautological line bundle $\OO_V(-1)$.
Concretely, its points are pairs $(x,[v])\in\AA_k^{n+1}\times V$ for which $x$ lies on the line represented by $[v]$.
Over $U_i$, it has coordinate ring $B_i[u_i]$, and its projection $q:T\to X$ is given by
$$
x_i=u_i,\qquad x_j=u_i(x_j/x_i)\quad(j\ne i),
$$
where $x_j/x_i$ on the right denotes the coordinate function on $U_i$.
These formulas glue and satisfy the homogeneous equations of $X$.
The zero section $E\cong V$ is $q^{-1}(P)$, and $q$ identifies $T\setminus E$ with $X\setminus\{P\}$; its inverse there is $x\mapsto(x,[x])$.

The inverse image $\widetilde Z=q^{-1}(Z)$ is therefore a prime divisor of $T$ disjoint from $E$.
Let $W\subseteq V$ be the regular locus and put $T_W=p^{-1}(W)$.
The regular locus is open for a variety [@Har10a, Theorem I.5.3], and $V\setminus W$ has codimension at least two by hypothesis.
Local triviality shows that $T\setminus T_W$ also has codimension at least two.
The variety $T_W$ is regular, since locally it is the product of the regular variety $W$ with $\AA_k^1$.

On regular varieties, [[D-5PQ5W|Cartier and Weil divisor classes]] both identify with the Picard group [@Har10a, Remark II.6.11.1A and Corollary II.6.16].
Step <1>1, applied over $W$, consequently makes
$$
p_W^*:\Pic(W)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Pic(T_W)
$$
an isomorphism, where $p_W:T_W\to W$ is the projection.
Indeed, pullback of a Cartier local equation gives the same vertical Weil divisor as in step <1>1.
For the zero section $s:W\to T_W$, the identity $p_Ws=\operatorname{id}_W$ shows that $s^*$ is the inverse isomorphism.

The divisor $\widetilde Z|_{T_W}$ is Cartier and disjoint from $s(W)$.
Its associated invertible sheaf therefore restricts to the trivial sheaf on $s(W)$: the canonical section is nowhere zero there.
Injectivity of $s^*$ gives
$$
[\OO_{T_W}(\widetilde Z|_{T_W})]=0\quad\text{in }\Pic(T_W).
$$
Hence $[\widetilde Z|_{T_W}]=0$ in $\Cl(T_W)$.
Step <1>1 ensures that $T$ is regular in codimension one, so deleting $T\setminus T_W$ preserves its class group by Proposition II.6.5(b).
It follows that $[\widetilde Z]=0$ in $\Cl(T)$.
Restrict to $T\setminus E$, identify it with $X\setminus\{P\}$, and use step <1>2 to conclude $[Z]=0$ in $\Cl(X)$.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves part (a), step <1>3 proves part (b), and step <1>4 proves part (c).
Steps <1>5 and <1>6 make $\rho$ surjective with zero kernel, proving the isomorphism in part (d).
:::
