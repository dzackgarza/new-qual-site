::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed.
A nonempty closed subscheme $Y$ of $\PP^n_k$ is called a **(strict, global) complete intersection** if its saturated homogeneous ideal $I$ in $S = k[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ can be generated by $r = \codim(Y, \PP^n)$ elements (I, Ex. 2.17).

(a) Let $Y$ be a nonempty closed subscheme of codimension $r$ in $\PP^n$.
   Then $Y$ is a complete intersection if and only if there are hypersurfaces, i.e. locally principal subschemes of codimension $1$, $H_1, \ldots, H_r$ such that $Y = H_1 \intersect \cdots \intersect H_r$ as schemes, i.e. $\mci_Y = \mci_{H_1} + \cdots + \mci_{H_r}$.

(b) If $Y$ is a complete intersection of dimension $\geq 1$ in $\PP^n$, and if $Y$ is normal, then $Y$ is projectively normal (Ex. 5.14).

(c) With the same hypotheses as (b), conclude that for all $l \geq 0$ the natural map $\Gamma(\PP^n, \OO_{\PP^n}(l)) \to \Gamma(Y, \OO_Y(l))$ is surjective.
   In particular, taking $l = 0$, show that $Y$ is connected.

(d) Now suppose given integers $d_1, \ldots, d_r \geq 1$ with $r < n$.
   Use Bertini's theorem (8.18) to show that there exist nonsingular hypersurfaces $H_1, \ldots, H_r$ in $\PP^n$ with $\deg H_i = d_i$ such that the scheme $Y = H_1 \intersect \cdots \intersect H_r$ is irreducible and nonsingular of codimension $r$ in $\PP^n$.

(e) If $Y$ is a nonsingular complete intersection as in (d), show that
$$
\omega_Y \cong \OO_Y\left(\sum_i d_i - n - 1\right)
.
$$

(f) If $Y$ is a nonsingular hypersurface of degree $d$ in $\PP^n$, with $n\ge2$, use (c) and (e) to show that $p_g(Y) = \binom{d-1}{n}$.
   Thus $p_g(Y) = p_a(Y)$ (I, Ex. 7.2).
   In particular, if $Y$ is a nonsingular plane curve of degree $d$, then $p_g(Y) = \frac{1}{2}(d-1)(d-2)$.

(g) If $Y$ is a nonsingular curve in $\PP^3$ which is a complete intersection of nonsingular surfaces of degrees $d, e$, then
$$
p_g(Y) = \frac{1}{2} de(d + e - 4) + 1
.
$$
   Again the geometric genus is the same as the arithmetic genus (I, Ex. 7.2).
:::

::: {.hint}
For (a), apply the unmixedness theorem to the homogeneous ideal generated by the hypersurface equations.
For (b), apply Theorem II.8.23 to the affine cone over $Y$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $\mathfrak m=(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\subset S$.
The [[P-AGH2510SATIDEAL|saturated homogeneous ideal]] of a closed subscheme is the ideal corresponding to its ideal sheaf, not an arbitrary ideal defining the same Proj.
For a nonnegative integer $a$, use $\binom{a}{b}=0$ when $a<b$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $J=(F_1,\ldots,F_r)$ is generated by positive-degree homogeneous polynomials and $\operatorname{Proj}(S/J)$ is nonempty of codimension $r$, then the $F_i$ form a regular sequence, $S/J$ is Cohen--Macaulay of dimension $n+1-r$, and $J$ is saturated.

::: pf-proof

The dimension formula for a homogeneous cone gives $\dim(S/J)=n+1-r$ [@Har10a, Chapter I, §2].
At the homogeneous maximal ideal this is also the local dimension: every homogeneous irreducible component of the cone contains its vertex.
The regular local ring $S_{\mathfrak m}$ has dimension $n+1$ and is Cohen--Macaulay.
The [dimension criterion for regular sequences](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02JN) makes $F_1,\ldots,F_r$ a regular sequence there.
It is also a regular sequence in $S$.
Indeed, each kernel testing regularity is graded, and a nonzero homogeneous element of a graded module cannot become zero upon localization at $\mathfrak m$: multiplying it by a polynomial with nonzero constant term has a nonzero component in its original degree.
The quotient is a [Cohen--Macaulay complete intersection](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00S8), with the stated dimension.

An element of $J^{\mathrm{sat}}/J$ is annihilated by a power of $\mathfrak m$.
To see this using the saturation definition, choose powers of all the $x_i$ annihilating it; every monomial of sufficiently large degree is divisible by one of those powers.
But $(S/J)_{\mathfrak m}$ has depth $n+1-r\ge1$.
Choose a non-zero-divisor in its maximal ideal; no nonzero element can be killed by a power of that ideal, since it would then be killed by a power of this non-zero-divisor.
Thus $(J^{\mathrm{sat}}/J)_{\mathfrak m}=0$.
The graded localization observation also applies to this quotient, so it is zero globally.
Hence $J$ is saturated.
This excludes an embedded component at the vertex, which would otherwise be invisible on Proj.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The equivalence in (a) holds.

::: pf-proof

If the homogeneous ideal $I$ is generated by $r$ elements, it can be generated by $r$ homogeneous elements.
Choose homogeneous lifts of a basis of $I/\mathfrak m I$; graded Nakayama, or induction on degree, shows that these generate $I$.
Their number is at most $r$, and the height bound for a generated ideal makes it at least $\operatorname{height}I=r$.
Write these generators as $F_i$ of degrees $d_i>0$.
Their zero schemes $H_i=V_+(F_i)$ are hypersurfaces, and sheafifying $I=(F_1,\ldots,F_r)$ gives precisely the required scheme-theoretic intersection.

Conversely, a nonempty hypersurface in $\PP^n$ is an effective Cartier divisor.
Its invertible ideal is $\OO(-d)$ for an integer $d>0$: the Picard group of projective space is generated by $\OO(1)$, and its inclusion into $\OO$ is a nonzero homogeneous form of degree $d$ [@Har10a, Proposition II.6.4, Corollary II.6.16 and Proposition II.5.13].
The degree cannot be negative, and degree zero would give the empty divisor.
Thus each given $H_i$ is defined by a form $F_i$.
Put $J=(F_1,\ldots,F_r)$.
The stated equality of ideal sheaves says $\operatorname{Proj}(S/J)=Y$ and identifies $I$ with $J^{\mathrm{sat}}$ by [[P-AGH2510SATIDEAL]].
Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives $J^{\mathrm{sat}}=J$, so $I$ has the required $r$ generators.
For $r=0$, the only nonempty codimension-zero closed subscheme of the integral scheme $\PP^n$ is $\PP^n$ itself, and the empty intersection and zero ideal give the assertion, including $n=0$.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Under the assumptions of (b), the homogeneous coordinate ring $R=S/I$ is a normal domain.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} makes $R$ Cohen--Macaulay of dimension $\dim Y+1\ge2$, so it satisfies Serre's condition $(S_2)$.
Let $o$ be the vertex, defined by $R_+$.
The complement of $o$ is covered by the affine opens $D(x_i)$, and the standard grading gives
$$
R_{x_i}\cong (R_{x_i})_0[x_i,x_i^{-1}].
$$
Here $\operatorname{Spec}(R_{x_i})_0$ is the corresponding affine open of $Y$ and is normal by assumption.
Polynomial extension and localization preserve normality, so each $R_{x_i}$ is normal.
This also holds when a chart has several components: its finitely many normal irreducible components are disjoint, so its coordinate ring is a finite product of normal domains.
Apply the polynomial normality lemma used in [[P-AGH2514PROJNORM]], step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, to each factor.

The vertex has codimension $\dim R\ge2$.
All local rings of $R$ at primes of height at most one therefore lie in this normal complement and are regular.
Thus $R$ satisfies $(R_1)$, and [Serre's normality criterion](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/031S) makes $R$ normal, including at the vertex.

In particular $R_{R_+}$ is a domain.
The map $R\to R_{R_+}$ is injective: if $a\ne0$, multiplying its lowest nonzero homogeneous component by the nonzero constant term of any element outside $R_+$ cannot give zero.
Therefore $R$ itself is a domain.
As a normal domain it is integrally closed, proving projective normality.
No connectedness or integrality of $Y$ was assumed in this argument.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Part (c) holds, and under its hypotheses $H^0(Y,\OO_Y)=k$ and $H^0(Y,\OO_Y(l))=0$ for $l<0$.

::: pf-proof

The projectively normal coordinate ring in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} has
$$
R_l\cong H^0(Y,\OO_Y(l))\qquad(l\ge0)
$$
by [[P-AGH2514PROJNORM]], part (d).
The quotient $S_l\to R_l$ is surjective, proving the requested restriction-map surjectivity.
At $l=0$ it identifies $H^0(Y,\OO_Y)$ with $k$.
A decomposition into two nonempty open-and-closed subschemes would give a nontrivial idempotent global function, impossible in a field.
Thus $Y$ is connected; it is also integral, since it is the Proj of the domain $R$.

For the extra negative-degree assertion, let $l=-q<0$ and suppose $s\ne0$ is a section of $\OO_Y(-q)$.
Choose two distinct closed points of the positive-dimensional integral projective variety $Y$ and a linear form $h$ vanishing at one but not the other.
The product $h^q s$ is a nonzero global section of $\OO_Y$: its generic value is nonzero because $Y$ is integral.
It vanishes at the first point, whereas every global function is constant.
This is a contradiction, so no such $s$ exists.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The hypersurfaces required in (d) exist.

::: pf-proof

Construct $Y_j=H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_j$ inductively, starting from $Y_0=\PP^n$.
Suppose $Y_{j-1}$ is smooth and integral of dimension $n-j+1\ge2$.
The complete degree-$d_j$ system gives the Veronese embedding of $\PP^n$ and an embedding of $Y_{j-1}$ by restriction.
By [[T-BERTINI|Bertini's hyperplane theorem]], applied to these two embedded smooth varieties, a dense open subset of the same space of degree-$d_j$ forms gives hypersurfaces smooth in $\PP^n$ and meeting $Y_{j-1}$ smoothly in codimension one [@Har10a, Theorem II.8.18].
Intersect the two dense open subsets and choose a point in their intersection; the parameter space is irreducible and $k$ is algebraically closed.
This yields a smooth hypersurface $H_j$ and a nonempty smooth $Y_j$ of codimension $j$ in $\PP^n$.
Nonemptiness follows because a hypersurface of positive degree meets every positive-dimensional projective variety not contained in it [@Har10a, Chapter I, §7].

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} makes $Y_j$ a global complete intersection.
Its dimension $n-j$ is positive, and smoothness makes it normal.
Steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} therefore make it connected and integral.
This completes the induction through $j=r$ and proves all the requirements, including smoothness of each ambient $H_j$.
For $r=0$, take $Y=\PP^n$.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

For the smooth complete intersection in (e),
$$
\boxed{\omega_Y\cong\OO_Y\left(\sum_{i=1}^r d_i-n-1\right).}
$$

::: pf-proof

The defining equations give a surjection
$$
\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\OO_Y(-d_i)\longrightarrow\mathcal I_Y/\mathcal I_Y^2.
$$
The conormal sheaf is locally free of rank $r$, since $Y$ and $\PP^n$ are smooth [@Har10a, Theorem II.8.17].
The source has the same rank, so the surjection is an isomorphism: over a local ring its square matrix is invertible after reduction modulo the maximal ideal, hence has unit determinant.
Take determinants in the exact conormal sequence
$$
0\to\mathcal I_Y/\mathcal I_Y^2\to\Omega_{\PP^n/k}|_Y\to\Omega_{Y/k}\to0.
$$
Using $\omega_{\PP^n}=\OO_{\PP^n}(-n-1)$ and the determinant identity in [[D-MODCONORM]] gives
$$
\OO_Y(-n-1)\cong\OO_Y\left(-\sum_i d_i\right)\otimes\omega_Y,
$$
which rearranges to the stated formula.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

For the hypersurface in (f),
$$
\boxed{p_g(Y)=p_a(Y)=\binom{d-1}{n}.}
$$

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} this smooth positive-dimensional hypersurface is integral and projectively normal.
Step [](#s6){.pf-ref} gives $\omega_Y=\OO_Y(a)$ with $a=d-n-1$.
If $a<0$, step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives $p_g(Y)=0$, in agreement with the binomial convention.
If $a\ge0$, then $a<d$.
The ideal generated by its degree-$d$ equation has no degree-$a$ component, so step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
p_g(Y)=\dim_k(S/(F))_a=\dim_kS_a
=\binom{a+n}{n}=\binom{d-1}{n}.
$$

The homogeneous quotient sequence has Hilbert polynomial
$$
P_Y(q)=\binom{q+n}{n}-\binom{q+n-d}{n},
$$
where both binomial expressions here are polynomials in $q$.
At zero, the second polynomial is
$$
\binom{n-d}{n}=(-1)^n\binom{d-1}{n},
$$
as follows by reversing the signs of its $n$ factors.
Consequently
$$
p_a(Y)=(-1)^{n-1}(P_Y(0)-1)=\binom{d-1}{n}
$$
[@Har10a, Chapter I, §7].
For $n=2$ this is $(d-1)(d-2)/2$, the plane-curve formula.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

For the curve in (g),
$$
\boxed{p_g(Y)=p_a(Y)=1+\frac{de(d+e-4)}2.}
$$

::: pf-proof

Let $F,G$ be the equations of the two surfaces, of degrees $d,e$, and put $R=S/(F,G)$ with $S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]$.
They form a regular sequence by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.
In this two-element case, its graded resolution is
$$
0\to S(-d-e)\xrightarrow{h\mapsto(-Gh,Fh)}
S(-d)\oplus S(-e)\xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto Fa+Gb}
S\to R\to0.
$$
For exactness at the middle term, $Fa+Gb=0$ implies $b\in(F)$ because $G$ is a non-zero-divisor modulo $F$; substituting $b=Fh$ then gives $a=-Gh$.
The first map is injective since $S$ is a domain.

Put $a=d+e-4$.
Steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s6){.pf-ref} identify $H^0(Y,\omega_Y)$ with $R_a$ when $a\ge0$, and make both zero when $a<0$.
Taking the degree-$a$ part of this resolution gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_g(Y)
&=\binom{d+e-1}{3}-\binom{e-1}{3}-\binom{d-1}{3}\\
&=1+\frac{de(d+e-4)}2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last term $S_{a-d-e}=S_{-4}$ vanishes.
The displayed binomials agree with $b(b-1)(b-2)/6$ also at $b=0,1,2$, so direct expansion proves the final equality for every $d,e\ge1$, including the line and conic cases.

The same resolution gives
$$
P_Y(q)=\binom{q+3}{3}-\binom{q-d+3}{3}
-\binom{q-e+3}{3}+\binom{q-d-e+3}{3}
=deq-\frac{de(d+e-4)}2.
$$
For a curve, $p_a(Y)=1-P_Y(0)$.
This is the same value as the computed geometric genus, proving (g).

::: {.remark title="Positive dimension in the hypersurface genus formula"}
Part (f) uses the positive-dimensional hypotheses of (c) and (e).
For $n=1$, a smooth degree-$d$ hypersurface is a disjoint union of $d$ reduced points.
Its zeroth exterior power of differentials is $\OO_Y$, so $h^0(Y,\omega_Y)=d$, whereas its arithmetic genus and $\binom{d-1}{1}$ both equal $d-1$.
Thus the displayed equality of genera does not extend to that zero-dimensional case.
:::
