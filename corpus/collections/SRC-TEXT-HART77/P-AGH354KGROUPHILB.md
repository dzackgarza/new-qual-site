::: {.problem}
Recall from (II, Ex. 6.10) the definition of the Grothendieck group $K(X)$ of a noetherian scheme $X$.

(a) Let $X$ be a projective scheme over a field $k$, and let $\mco_X(1)$ be a very ample invertible sheaf on $X$. Show that there is a unique additive homomorphism
$$
P: K(X) \to \QQ[z]
$$
such that for each coherent sheaf $\mcf$ on $X$, $P(\gamma(\mcf))$ is the Hilbert polynomial of $\mcf$ (Ex. 5.2).

(b) Now let $X=\PP_k^r$, where $r\ge0$.
For each $i=0,1,\ldots,r$, let $L_i$ be a linear space of dimension $i$ in $X$.
Show that:

(i) $K(X)$ is the free abelian group generated by $\ts{\gamma(\mco_{L_i})\st i=0,\ldots,r}$;

(ii) the map $P:K(X)\to\QQ[z]$ is injective.
:::

::: {.hint}
Show that (b)(i) implies (b)(ii), and then prove them simultaneously by induction on $r$, using (II, Ex. 6.10c).
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves defined in [[P-AGH2610KGROUP]], and write $\gamma_X(F)$ for the class of $F$.
A sheaf on a closed linear subspace is regarded on projective space by closed direct image.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Hilbert polynomials define the unique homomorphism in (a).

::: pf-proof

For any short exact sequence $0\to F'\to F\to F''\to0$, twisting by the invertible sheaf $\OO_X(n)$ is exact for every integer $n$.
Euler additivity [[P-AGH351EULERCHAR]] and the polynomial identity [[P-AGH352HILBPOLY]] give
$$
P_F(n)=P_{F'}(n)+P_{F''}(n)\qquad(n\in\ZZ).
$$
The polynomials are therefore equal as polynomials: $P_F=P_{F'}+P_{F''}$.
The map from the free abelian group on coherent sheaves sending $F$ to $P_F$ annihilates every defining relation of $K(X)$.
It descends to the required additive homomorphism $P$.
The classes of coherent sheaves generate $K(X)$, so its specified values determine it uniquely.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Every finite module over $R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ has a finite resolution by finite free $R$-modules.

::: pf-proof

Take a finite presentation $R^b\to R^a\to M\to0$ and set $S=k[x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ with its standard grading.
In each column of the presentation matrix, homogenize the entries to one common degree by inserting powers of $x_0$.
This gives a homogeneous map $\bigoplus_j S(-d_j)\to S^a$ with finite graded cokernel $N$ satisfying
$$
N/(x_0-1)N\cong M.
$$
The equality follows by setting $x_0=1$ in the presentation and using right exactness of tensor product.

The graded Hilbert syzygy argument gives a finite graded free resolution of $N$ as follows.
Construct a minimal graded free resolution $F_\bullet\to N$ by successively taking homogeneous generators lifting a basis modulo $\mathfrak m=(x_0,\ldots,x_r)$.
The graded Nakayama argument shows that these generators generate: any nonzero quotient would have a lowest nonzero degree that could not lie in its own $\mathfrak m$-multiple.
Noetherianness makes every kernel finite, so every $F_i$ is a finite sum of shifts of $S$.
Minimality makes every differential matrix have entries in $\mathfrak m$, and hence $F_\bullet\otimes_S k$ has zero differentials.

The variables form an $S$-regular sequence, so their Koszul complex is a free resolution of $k$ of length $r+1$, as in [[D-DEFREGSQ]] [@Har10a, Proposition III.7.10A].
Computing $\operatorname{Tor}^S_i(N,k)$ with this resolution gives zero for $i>r+1$.
Computing it with $F_\bullet$ gives $F_i\otimes_S k$, since the differentials there are zero.
The equality of the two computations follows by tensoring the two free resolutions and taking homology in either order.
Thus $F_i\otimes_S k=0$ for $i>r+1$, which forces the finite graded free module $F_i$ itself to be zero.
The resolution terminates.

Multiplication by $x_0-1$ is injective on every graded module, in particular on $N$: if a nonzero element has lowest nonzero homogeneous component $v_d$, the degree-$d$ component of $(x_0-1)v$ is $-v_d\ne0$.
The two-term free resolution of $S/(x_0-1)=R$ therefore gives $\operatorname{Tor}^S_i(N,R)=0$ for all $i>0$.
Tensoring the finite graded free resolution of $N$ with $R$ is consequently exact in positive degrees and has degree-zero homology $M$.
Its terms are finite free $R$-modules, proving the claim for arbitrary finite modules, not only graded ones.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Rank identifies $K(\AA_k^r)$ with $\ZZ$, generated by $\gamma(\OO_{\AA^r})$.

::: pf-proof

The affine associated-sheaf equivalence identifies coherent sheaves with finite $R$-modules and preserves exact sequences [@Har10a, Proposition II.5.4].
Let $E_\bullet\to M$ be a finite free $R$-resolution from step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.
Breaking it into short exact sequences gives
$$
\gamma(\widetilde M)=\sum_i(-1)^i\operatorname{rank}(E_i)\,\gamma(\OO_{\AA^r}).
$$
Tensoring the resolution with the function field of $R$ shows that the integer coefficient is the rank of $M$.
Rank is additive and sends $\gamma(\OO_{\AA^r})$ to $1$, by [[P-AGH2610KGROUP]], part (b).
Thus this generator has infinite order, and the rank map and $n\mapsto n\gamma(\OO_{\AA^r})$ are inverse isomorphisms.
This includes $r=0$, for which $R=k$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For any linear space $L_i\cong\PP_k^i\subseteq\PP_k^r$,
$$
P\bigl(\gamma(\OO_{L_i})\bigr)=\binom{z+i}{i}.
$$
These polynomials are linearly independent over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

The restriction of $\OO_{\PP^r}(1)$ to $L_i$ is its standard $\OO(1)$.
For sufficiently large $n$, the Euler characteristic is the dimension of its global sections, which is $\binom{n+i}{i}$ by the degree-$n$ monomial basis [@Har10a, Theorems III.5.1 and III.5.2].
Closed direct image preserves cohomology [@Har10a, Lemma III.2.10], so the same computation is the Hilbert polynomial of $\OO_{L_i}$ regarded on $\PP^r$.
Uniqueness of the polynomial proves the formula.
Its degree is $i$ with leading coefficient $1/i!$.
In a nonzero linear relation, the term with largest index having a nonzero coefficient would have strictly larger degree than all the others and could not cancel.
Thus the polynomials are independent.
In particular, once these classes generate $K(\PP^r)$, this independence proves both their freeness and injectivity of $P$, as indicated by the hint.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

For every $r$, a linear flag supplies a free basis of $K(\PP_k^r)$, and $P$ is injective.

::: pf-proof

Induct on $r$.
For $r=0$, step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives $K(\PP_k^0)=K(\Spec k)=\ZZ$, generated by its structure sheaf; its polynomial is $1$, so $P$ is injective.
For $r>0$, take a coordinate hyperplane $H\cong\PP_k^{r-1}$ and a flag of linear spaces $F_0\subset\cdots\subset F_{r-1}=H$, with $\dim F_i=i$.
Set $F_r=\PP_k^r$.
The coherent localization sequence of [[P-AGH2610KGROUP]], part (c), is
$$
K(H)\longrightarrow K(\PP_k^r)\longrightarrow K(\AA_k^r)\longrightarrow0.
$$
By induction, the first group is generated by the classes of $\OO_{F_i}$ for $i<r$.
By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the last is generated by $\OO_{\AA^r}$, and this generator lifts to $\OO_{\PP^r}=\OO_{F_r}$.
Exactness therefore shows that $\gamma(\OO_{F_0}),\ldots,\gamma(\OO_{F_r})$ generate the middle group.
Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} makes them linearly independent and makes $P$ injective.
This completes the simultaneous induction without assuming that the left map in the localization sequence is injective in advance.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The same basis assertion holds for the arbitrary linear spaces specified in (b), and
$$
\boxed{K(\PP_k^r)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^r\ZZ\,\gamma(\OO_{L_i}),
\qquad P\text{ is injective}.}
$$

::: pf-proof

Each $L_i$ and the flag space $F_i$ have the same Hilbert polynomial by step [](#s4){.pf-ref}.
Injectivity from step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives $\gamma(\OO_{L_i})=\gamma(\OO_{F_i})$ in $K(\PP^r)$.
Thus the specified classes are exactly the basis classes already obtained, regardless of the relative positions of the $L_i$.
Both assertions in (b) follow.

:::
