::: {.problem}
If $X$ is a complete intersection of surfaces of degrees $a, b$ in $\PP^3$, then $X$ does not lie on any surface of degree $<\min (a, b)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3].
$$

<1>1. The saturated homogeneous ideal of $X$ is
$$
I(X)=(F,G),
$$
where $F$ and $G$ are homogeneous forms of degrees $a$ and $b$.

::: {.proof}
By hypothesis, $X$ is the scheme-theoretic complete intersection of two
surfaces of degrees $a$ and $b$.  Under the complete-intersection convention
of [[P-AGH284COMPINT|Exercise II.8.4]], its saturated homogeneous ideal is
generated by the defining equations of those surfaces.  Thus we may choose
$$
\deg F=a,
\qquad
\deg G=b,
$$
with
$$
I(X)=(F,G).
$$
:::

<1>2. For every integer
$$
m<\min(a,b),
$$
the degree-$m$ component of $I(X)$ is zero:
$$
I(X)_m=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because $(F,G)$ is a homogeneous ideal,
$$
I(X)_m
=
F\,S_{m-a}+G\,S_{m-b}.
$$
If $m<a$ and $m<b$, then
$$
S_{m-a}=S_{m-b}=0,
$$
since a polynomial ring has no nonzero homogeneous forms of negative degree.
Hence
$$
I(X)_m=0.
$$
:::

<1>3. The curve $X$ lies on no surface of degree
$$
m<\min(a,b).
$$

::: {.proof}
Suppose a surface $T\subseteq\PP^3$ of degree $m<\min(a,b)$ contained $X$.
Let
$$
0\ne H\in S_m
$$
be a homogeneous equation for $T$.  Since $T$ contains $X$, the form $H$
lies in the saturated homogeneous ideal of $X$, so
$$
H\in I(X)_m.
$$
Step <1>2 gives $I(X)_m=0$, contradicting $H\ne0$.  Therefore no such
surface exists.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is exactly the required assertion.
:::
:::
