::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed, let $n\ge0$, and put $S\coloneqq k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ and $S_+=(x_0,\ldots,x_n)$.

(a) Show that $Y \mapsto I(Y)$ and $\mfa \mapsto Z(\mfa)$ give a one-to-one inclusion-reversing correspondence between algebraic sets in $\PP^n$ and homogeneous radical ideals of $S$ not equal to $S_+$.
Because $S_+$ does not occur in this correspondence, it is called the **irrelevant maximal ideal** of $S$.

(b) An algebraic set $Y \subseteq \PP^n$ is irreducible if and only if $I(Y)$ is a prime ideal.

(c) Show that $\PP^n$ is itself irreducible.
:::

::: {.solution}
The ideal $I(Y)$ is generated by the homogeneous polynomials vanishing on $Y$, and $Z(J)$ is the projective zero set of a homogeneous ideal $J$.
Irreducible spaces are nonempty, and prime ideals are proper.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The maps in (a) are inverse inclusion-reversing bijections, with $\varnothing$ corresponding to $S$.

::: pf-proof

For every projective algebraic set $Y$, the identity $Z(I(Y))=Y$ follows from [[P-AGH23ZIPROPS]], part (e), since $Y$ is closed.
If $Y\ne\varnothing$, part (d) of that card, applied to $I(Y)$, gives
$$
I(Y)=I(Z(I(Y)))=\sqrt{I(Y)}.
$$
Thus $I(Y)$ is homogeneous and radical.
It cannot equal $S_+$, whose projective zero set is empty.
For $Y=\varnothing$, every homogeneous polynomial vanishes vacuously, including $1$, so $I(Y)=S$; this is also a homogeneous radical ideal distinct from $S_+$.

Conversely, let $J$ be homogeneous and radical with $J\ne S_+$.
When $Z(J)$ is nonempty, the same homogeneous Nullstellensatz calculation gives $I(Z(J))=\sqrt J=J$.
When $Z(J)$ is empty, each coordinate $x_i$ vanishes on it, so [[P-AGH21HOMNSS]] gives a power of $x_i$ in $J$.
Radicality implies $x_i\in J$ for every $i$, hence $S_+\subseteq J$.
Since $S/S_+=k$ is a field, $S_+$ is maximal.
The assumption $J\ne S_+$ therefore forces $J=S$, and again $I(Z(J))=I(\varnothing)=S=J$.

Both maps reverse inclusions by [[P-AGH23ZIPROPS]], parts (a) and (b).
Their two inverse identities prove (a), including the unit ideal rather than restricting the correspondence to proper ideals.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $Y$ is irreducible, then $I(Y)$ is prime.

::: pf-proof

Nonemptiness gives $1\notin I(Y)$.
For homogeneous $f,g\in S$ with $fg\in I(Y)$, the equality of zero sets
$$
Z(fg)=Z(f)\cup Z(g)
$$
holds because the values lie in a field.
Thus $Y$ is the union of its closed subsets $Y\cap Z(f)$ and $Y\cap Z(g)$.
Irreducibility forces one of them to be all of $Y$, so $f\in I(Y)$ or $g\in I(Y)$.

This homogeneous test implies ordinary primality.
In the nonnegatively graded ring $R=S/I(Y)$ it says that a product of two nonzero homogeneous elements is nonzero.
For arbitrary nonzero $a,b\in R$, let $a_d,b_e$ be their highest nonzero homogeneous components.
The component of degree $d+e$ of $ab$ is $a_db_e$, which is nonzero by that test.
Hence $ab\ne0$, so $R$ is a domain and $I(Y)$ is prime.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $I(Y)$ is prime, then $Y$ is irreducible.

::: pf-proof

The empty set has ideal $S$, which is not prime, so $Y$ is nonempty.
Suppose $Y=Y_1\cup Y_2$ for closed proper subsets $Y_1,Y_2$ of $Y$.
Since $Y$ is projectively closed, both $Y_i$ are projective algebraic sets.
Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} makes each strict inclusion $Y_i\subsetneq Y$ correspond to a strict inclusion $I(Y)\subsetneq I(Y_i)$.
Choose homogeneous
$$
f\in I(Y_1)\setminus I(Y),\qquad
g\in I(Y_2)\setminus I(Y).
$$
Such homogeneous choices exist because all these ideals are homogeneous: an element outside the smaller ideal has a homogeneous component outside it.
The product $fg$ vanishes on $Y_1\cup Y_2=Y$, so $fg\in I(Y)$.
This contradicts primality, since neither factor belongs to $I(Y)$.
Thus no such decomposition exists, proving this implication and completing (b).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Projective space $\PP_k^n$ is irreducible.

::: pf-proof

It is nonempty, and it equals $Z((0))$.
By [[P-AGH23ZIPROPS]], part (d),
$$
I(\PP_k^n)=I(Z((0)))=\sqrt{(0)}=(0),
$$
since the polynomial ring $S$ is a domain.
The zero ideal is therefore prime, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves (c), also when $n=0$.

:::

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves (a), steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} prove (b), and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves (c).
