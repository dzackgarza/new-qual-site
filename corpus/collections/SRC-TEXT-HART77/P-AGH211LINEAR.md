::: {.problem}
A hypersurface defined by a linear polynomial is called a **hyperplane**.

1. Show that the following two conditions are equivalent for a variety $Y \subseteq \PP^n$.

   - $I(Y)$ can be generated by linear polynomials.

   - $Y$ can be written as an intersection of hyperplanes.

   In this case $Y$ is called a **linear variety** in $\PP^n$.

2. If $Y$ is a linear variety of dimension $r$ in $\PP^n$, show that $I(Y)$ is minimally generated by $n-r$ linear polynomials.

3. Let $Y, Z$ be linear varieties in $\PP^n$ with $\dim Y = r$ and $\dim Z = s$.
   If $r + s - n \geq 0$, then $Y \intersect Z \neq \emptyset$.
   Furthermore, if $Y \intersect Z \neq \emptyset$, then $Y \intersect Z$ is a linear variety of dimension $\geq r+s-n$.

*Hint:* think of $\AA^{n+1}$ as a vector space over $k$ and work with its subspaces.
:::
