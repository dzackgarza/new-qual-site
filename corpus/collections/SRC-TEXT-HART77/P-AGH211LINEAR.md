::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $n\ge0$.
A hypersurface defined by a linear polynomial is called a **hyperplane**.

(a) Show that the following two conditions are equivalent for a projective variety $Y \subseteq \PP_k^n$.

(i) $I(Y)$ can be generated by linear polynomials.

(ii) $Y$ can be written as an intersection of hyperplanes.

In this case $Y$ is called a **linear variety** in $\PP^n$.

(b) If $Y$ is a linear variety of dimension $r$ in $\PP^n$, show that $I(Y)$ is minimally generated by $n-r$ linear polynomials.

(c) Let $Y, Z$ be linear varieties in $\PP^n$ with $\dim Y = r$ and $\dim Z = s$.
If $r + s - n \geq 0$, then $Y \intersect Z \neq \emptyset$.
Furthermore, if $Y \intersect Z \neq \emptyset$, then $Y \intersect Z$ is a linear variety of dimension $\geq r+s-n$.
:::

::: {.hint}
Think of $\AA^{n+1}$ as a vector space over $k$ and work with its subspaces.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $V=k^{n+1}$ and $S=\operatorname{Sym}(V^*)=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$.
For a vector subspace $W\subseteq V$, let $\PP(W)$ be its set of one-dimensional subspaces, with $\PP(0)=\varnothing$, and let
$$
W^\perp=\{\ell\in V^*: \ell|_W=0\}
$$
be its annihilator.
The intersection of an empty family of hyperplanes is understood to be $\PP^n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For a nonzero subspace $W\subseteq V$ of dimension $r+1$, the set $\PP(W)$ is a linear projective variety of dimension $r$, and
$$
I(\PP(W))=(W^\perp).
$$

::: pf-proof

Choose a basis of $W$ and extend it to one of $V$.
In the dual coordinates $y_0,\ldots,y_n$, the subspace $W$ is given by $y_{r+1}=\cdots=y_n=0$.
The same linear coordinate change defines a projective automorphism; it and its inverse are regular on the standard affine charts by their coordinate-ratio formulas.
Consequently $\PP(W)$ is identified with the coordinate copy of $\PP^r$ and has dimension $r$ [@Har10a, Chapter I, §2].

The ideal $J=(y_{r+1},\ldots,y_n)$ is prime, since its quotient is the polynomial ring $k[y_0,\ldots,y_r]$.
Its projective zero set is nonempty and equals $\PP(W)$.
By [[P-AGH24CORRESPONDENCE]], $I(\PP(W))=J$.
Its degree-one part is precisely $W^\perp$.
Primality also proves that $\PP(W)$ is irreducible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Conditions (i) and (ii) in (a) are equivalent.

::: pf-proof

If $I(Y)$ is generated by linear forms $\ell_i$, then
$$
Y=Z(I(Y))=\bigcap_i Z(\ell_i).
$$
Zero generators can be omitted, and every remaining $Z(\ell_i)$ is a hyperplane.
Since the space $S_1=V^*$ is finite-dimensional, the span of the $\ell_i$ has a finite basis, so the same intersection is defined by finitely many hyperplanes.

Conversely, suppose $Y$ is an intersection of hyperplanes with equations $\ell_i=0$.
Set $W=\bigcap_i\ker\ell_i$ in $V$.
The projective intersection is exactly $\PP(W)$.
Since a variety is nonempty, $W\ne0$.
Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives $I(Y)=(W^\perp)$, which is generated by linear forms.
This proves (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For the variety in (b), the minimum number of ideal generators is $\boxed{n-r}$, and a basis of $W^\perp$ is a minimal generating set.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, write $Y=\PP(W)$ with $\dim W=r+1$.
Linear algebra gives $\dim W^\perp=n-r$, so a basis of this annihilator gives $n-r$ generators of $I(Y)$.

For the lower bound, put $\mathfrak m=S_+=(x_0,\ldots,x_n)$.
The ideal $I(Y)$ is generated in degree one, and $\mathfrak m I(Y)$ consists of its components in degrees at least two.
Hence the natural map gives a vector-space isomorphism
$$
I(Y)/\mathfrak m I(Y)\cong I(Y)_1=W^\perp.
$$
Any ideal generating set maps to a spanning set of this quotient over $S/\mathfrak m=k$.
It must therefore have at least $n-r$ elements, even if nonhomogeneous generators are allowed.
When $r=n$, the ideal is zero and the empty generating set gives the same conclusion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The nonemptiness and dimension assertions in (c) hold.

::: pf-proof

Write $Y=\PP(W)$ and $Z=\PP(T)$, where $\dim W=r+1$ and $\dim T=s+1$.
A projective point belongs to both precisely when its one-dimensional subspace lies in $W\cap T$.
Thus
$$
Y\cap Z=\PP(W\cap T).
$$
The vector-space dimension identity gives
$$
\dim(W\cap T)=\dim W+\dim T-\dim(W+T)
\ge r+s+2-(n+1)=r+s-n+1.
$$
If $r+s-n\ge0$, this dimension is at least one, so the projective intersection is nonempty.
Whenever $W\cap T\ne0$, step [](#s1){.pf-ref} makes its projectivization a linear variety and gives
$$
\dim(Y\cap Z)=\dim(W\cap T)-1\ge r+s-n.
$$
This proves both assertions of (c), including intersections with the whole projective space.

:::

:::

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves (a), step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves the minimal-generation assertion in (b), and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves (c).
