::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed, let $S\coloneqq k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$, and let $S^h$ denote its set of homogeneous elements.

(a) If $T_1 \subseteq T_2$ are subsets of $S^h$, then $Z(T_1) \supseteq Z(T_2)$.

(b) If $Y_1 \subseteq Y_2$ are subsets of $\PP^n$, then $I(Y_1) \supseteq I(Y_2)$.

(c) For any two subsets $Y_1, Y_2 \subseteq \PP^n$, $I(Y_1 \union Y_2) = I(Y_1) \intersect I(Y_2)$.

(d) If $\mfa \subseteq S$ is a homogeneous ideal with $Z(\mfa) \neq \emptyset$, then $I(Z(\mfa)) = \sqrt{\mfa}$.

(e) For any subset $Y \subseteq \PP^n$, $Z(I(Y)) = \bar{Y}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $T\subseteq S^h$, let $Z(T)$ be the set of projective points where every member of $T$ vanishes.
For a subset $Y\subseteq\PP_k^n$, write
$$
I(Y)_d=\{f\in S_d:f(v)=0\text{ for every nonzero representative }v\text{ of a point of }Y\},
\qquad I(Y)=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}I(Y)_d.
$$
Homogeneity makes vanishing independent of the representative.
Each $I(Y)_d$ is a vector subspace, and $S_e I(Y)_d\subseteq I(Y)_{d+e}$, so this direct sum is a homogeneous ideal.
It is exactly the ideal generated by all homogeneous polynomials vanishing on $Y$, the convention in [@Har10a, Chapter I, §2].

<1>1. The operators $Z$ and $I$ reverse inclusions, proving (a) and (b).

::: {.proof}
If every member of $T_2$ vanishes at a point, then every member of its subset $T_1$ vanishes there.
Thus $Z(T_2)\subseteq Z(T_1)$.
Likewise, a homogeneous polynomial vanishing at every point of $Y_2$ vanishes at every point of $Y_1\subseteq Y_2$.
This gives $I(Y_2)_d\subseteq I(Y_1)_d$ in every degree, and hence $I(Y_2)\subseteq I(Y_1)$.
:::

<1>2. One has $I(Y_1\cup Y_2)=I(Y_1)\cap I(Y_2)$, proving (c).

::: {.proof}
For $f\in S_d$, vanishing on $Y_1\cup Y_2$ is equivalent to vanishing on each of $Y_1$ and $Y_2$.
Therefore $I(Y_1\cup Y_2)_d=I(Y_1)_d\cap I(Y_2)_d$.
Membership in a homogeneous ideal is equivalent to membership of every homogeneous component, so these degreewise equalities imply equality of the ideals.
:::

<1>3. The radical of a homogeneous ideal is homogeneous.

::: {.proof}
Let $J$ be homogeneous and let $0\ne f\in\sqrt J$; the zero polynomial needs no argument.
Choose $m\ge1$ with $f^m\in J$ and write $f_D$ for the highest nonzero homogeneous component of $f$.
The degree-$mD$ component of $f^m$ is $f_D^m$, and belongs to $J$ because $J$ is homogeneous.
Hence $f_D\in\sqrt J$.
The radical is an ideal, so $f-f_D\in\sqrt J$.
Repeating on the strictly shorter sum of components proves that every homogeneous component of $f$ lies in $\sqrt J$.
This proves the assertion.
:::

<1>4. For the ideal in (d), $I(Z(\mfa))=\sqrt{\mfa}$.

::: {.proof}
Both ideals are homogeneous, the second by step <1>3, so it suffices to compare homogeneous elements.
If a homogeneous $f$ lies in $\sqrt{\mfa}$, some power $f^m$ belongs to $\mfa$ and vanishes on $Z(\mfa)$.
At each nonzero coordinate representative its value is a power in a field; $f^m(v)=0$ implies $f(v)=0$.
Thus $f\in I(Z(\mfa))$.

Conversely, let $f$ be homogeneous and vanish on $Z(\mfa)$.
For positive degree, the [[P-AGH21HOMNSS|homogeneous Nullstellensatz]] gives $f^m\in\mfa$ for some $m\ge1$, hence $f\in\sqrt{\mfa}$.
For degree zero, the assumed nonemptiness of $Z(\mfa)$ forces the constant $f$ to be zero, which also belongs to $\sqrt{\mfa}$.
These cases prove both inclusions.
:::

<1>5. For any $Y\subseteq\PP^n$, one has $Z(I(Y))=\overline Y$, proving (e).

::: {.proof}
By definition, every homogeneous member of $I(Y)$ vanishes on $Y$.
Consequently $Y\subseteq Z(I(Y))$.
The latter is projectively closed, so $\overline Y\subseteq Z(I(Y))$.

For the reverse inclusion, let $W$ be any closed subset containing $Y$.
Write $W=Z(T)$ for a family $T$ of homogeneous polynomials, using the [[D-CP2MH|projective Zariski topology]].
Every member of $T$ vanishes on $Y$ and therefore belongs to $I(Y)$.
Part (a), proved in step <1>1, gives $Z(I(Y))\subseteq Z(T)=W$.
Intersecting all closed sets $W$ containing $Y$ gives $Z(I(Y))\subseteq\overline Y$.
For $Y=\varnothing$, the same argument applies; explicitly $I(Y)=S$ and $Z(S)=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves (a) and (b), step <1>2 proves (c), step <1>4 proves (d), and step <1>5 proves (e).
:::
:::

::: {.remark title="The nonempty-locus hypothesis"}
For $\mfa=S_+=(x_0,\ldots,x_n)$, the projective zero set is empty.
Thus $I(Z(\mfa))=I(\varnothing)=S$, whereas $\sqrt{\mfa}=S_+$, since $S/S_+=k$ is a field.
This shows why (d) excludes the empty projective zero set; its possible radicals are computed in [[P-AGH22IRRELEVANT]].
:::
