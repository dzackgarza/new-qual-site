::: {.problem}
Let $Y$ be an affine variety of dimension $r$ in $\AA^n$, let $H$ be a hypersurface in $\AA^n$, and assume $Y \not\subseteq H$.
Show that every irreducible component of $Y \intersect H$ has dimension $r - 1$ (see (7.1) for a generalization).
:::

::: {.solution}
The published solution is **Richard E. Borcherds, *Hartshorne, Chapter 1: Answers to exercises* (1994), Exercise 1.8** (`tmp/sources/hartshorne-solutions-borcherds.md`): since $f$ is neither a unit nor a zero divisor in $A(Y)$, every minimal prime containing it has height one by (1.11A), and the component's coordinate ring is $B/P$ with $\dim(B/P)=r-1$ by (1.8A). **William Troiani, *Hartshorne Exercise Solutions* (October 2020), Exercise 1.8**, and **Jinhyun Park, *Algebraic Geometry by Robin Hartshorne: Exercises solutions*, Exercise 1.8**, run the same argument, Park through a primary decomposition of $(f)$ in $A(Y)$ and its minimal primes. The proof below states the correspondence between components of $Y\cap H$ and minimal primes over $(f)$, which all three use and none writes, and then applies (1.11A) and (1.8A) as they do.

Throughout $Y\subseteq\AA^n$ is an affine variety of dimension $r$, $H=V(f)$ a hypersurface with $f$ a nonzero polynomial, $Y\not\subseteq H$, and $A(Y)$ the coordinate ring, a finitely generated domain over the algebraically closed field with $\dim A(Y)=r$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #components}
If $Y\cap H\ne\varnothing$, the irreducible components of $Y\cap H$ are the closed sets $V(\mfp_i)$, where $\mfp_1,\dots,\mfp_s$ are the minimal primes of $A(Y)$ containing the image of $f$.

::: pf-proof
Under the correspondence between closed subsets of $Y$ and radical ideals of $A(Y)$, $Y\cap H$ corresponds to the radical of the ideal $(f)$ generated by the image of $f$, and a closed set $V(\mfp)$ is irreducible exactly when $\mfp$ is prime (printed p. 4). The irreducible components of $V(I)$ are the closed sets $V(\mfp)$ for the minimal primes $\mfp$ over $I$: every prime containing $I$ contains a minimal one, so $V(\mfp_i)\subseteq V(\mfp)$ for such $\mfp$, and the minimality gives maximality of the component. If $Y\cap H=\varnothing$ there is no such prime and the claim of the exercise is vacuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nonzero-nonunit}
The image of $f$ in $A(Y)$ is neither zero nor a unit.

::: pf-proof
$Y\cap H=Y\cap V(f)$ is the zero set of $f$ on $Y$, so $Y\subseteq H$ iff $f$ vanishes on $Y$ iff $f$ maps to $0$ in $A(Y)$. Hence $Y\not\subseteq H$ makes the image nonzero. If the image were a unit, then $f$ would be invertible as a function on $Y$, which is impossible since it takes the value $0$ at some point of $Y$ when $Y\cap H\ne\varnothing$; so in the non-vacuous case the image is not a unit. Finally $A(Y)$ is a domain, being the coordinate ring of an irreducible affine variety.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #height}
Each minimal prime $\mfp_i$ over $(f)$ has $\operatorname{ht}\mfp_i=1$.

::: pf-proof
This is the principal ideal theorem, (1.11A): in a Noetherian ring, a minimal prime over an ideal generated by a nonzerodivisor has height one. $A(Y)$ is Noetherian since it is a finitely generated algebra over a field; the image of $f$ is a nonzerodivisor because it is nonzero in the domain $A(Y)$ by step [](#nonzero-nonunit){.pf-ref}. So each $\mfp_i$ has height exactly $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dimension}
$\dim A(Y)/\mfp_i=r-1$ for every $i$.

::: pf-proof
For a finitely generated domain $B$ over a field and a prime $\mfp$, $\dim B/\mfp+\operatorname{ht}\mfp=\dim B$ — the dimension formula, (1.8A). With $B=A(Y)$, $\dim B=r$ and $\operatorname{ht}\mfp_i=1$ by step [](#height){.pf-ref}, so $\dim A(Y)/\mfp_i=r-1$. The coordinate ring of the closed subvariety $V(\mfp_i)\subseteq Y$ is $A(Y)/\mfp_i$, and the dimension of a closed subvariety equals the Krull dimension of its coordinate ring, so the component $V(\mfp_i)$ has dimension $r-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
Every irreducible component of $Y\cap H$ has dimension $r-1$.

::: pf-proof
The components are the $V(\mfp_i)$ by step [](#components){.pf-ref}, and each has dimension $r-1$ by step [](#dimension){.pf-ref}; in the vacuous case there is nothing to prove.

:::
