::: {.problem}
Again let $Y$ be a hypersurface in $X = \PP_k^N$, this time with $N \geq 2$, and let $\hat{X}$ be the formal completion of $X$ along $Y$.

a. If $\mcf$ is a locally free sheaf on $X$, show that the natural map
\[
H^0(X, \mcf) \to H^0(\hat{X}, \hat{\mcf})
\]
is an isomorphism.

b. Show that the following conditions are equivalent:

    (i) For each locally free sheaf $\mcf$ on $\hat{X}$, there exists a coherent sheaf $\mcg$ on $X$ such that $\mcf \cong \hat{\mcg}$, that is, $\mcf$ is algebraizable;

    (ii) For each locally free sheaf $\mcf$ on $\hat{X}$, there is an integer $n_0$ such that $\mcf(n)$ is generated by global sections for all $n \geq n_0$.

    Hint: for (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i), show that one can find sheaves $\mce_0, \mce_1$ on $X$, which are direct sums of sheaves of the form $\mco(-q_i)$, and an exact sequence $\hat{\mce}_1 \to \hat{\mce}_0 \to \mcf \to 0$ on $\hat{X}$. Then apply (a) to the sheaf $\sheafhom(\mce_1, \mce_0)$.

c. Show that the conditions (i) and (ii) of (b) imply that the natural map $\Pic X \to \Pic \hat{X}$ is an isomorphism.

Note: in fact (i) and (ii) always hold if $N \geq 3$. This fact, coupled with (Ex. 11.5), leads to Grothendieck's proof [SGA 2] of the Lefschetz theorem: if $Y$ is a hypersurface in $\PP_k^N$ with $N \geq 4$, then $\Pic Y \cong \ZZ$, generated by $\mco_Y(1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
d=\deg Y,
\qquad
\mci=\mci_Y\cong\mco_X(-d),
$$
and for $r\ge1$ put
$$
X_r=\bigl(Y,\mco_X/\mci^r\bigr).
$$
For a coherent sheaf $\mcg$ on $X$, write
$$
\mcg_r=\mcg\otimes_{\mco_X}\mco_{X_r}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $\mce$ is locally free on $X=\PP_k^N$, then for all sufficiently
large $r$,
$$
H^0\bigl(X,\mce(-rd)\bigr)
=
H^1\bigl(X,\mce(-rd)\bigr)
=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

The canonical sheaf of $\PP_k^N$ is
$$
\omega_X\cong\mco_X(-N-1).
$$
By [[T-COHSD|Serre duality]], for $i=0,1$,
$$
H^i\bigl(X,\mce(-rd)\bigr)^\vee
\cong
H^{N-i}\bigl(
X,\mce^\vee(rd-N-1)
\bigr).
$$
Because $N\ge2$, both
$$
N>0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
N-1>0.
$$
The twist on the right tends to $+\infty$ with $r$. Hence
[[T-COHSVAN|Serre vanishing]] makes both right-hand groups zero for
$r\gg0$. Dualizing gives the asserted vanishings.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every locally free sheaf $\mce$ on $X$, the natural map
$$
H^0(X,\mce)\longrightarrow H^0(\hat X,\hat\mce)
$$
is an isomorphism. This proves (a).

::: pf-proof

Since $\mci^r\cong\mco_X(-rd)$ and $\mce$ is locally free, tensoring the
defining sequence of $X_r$ with $\mce$ gives
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\mce(-rd)
\longrightarrow
\mce
\longrightarrow
\mce_r
\longrightarrow
0.
$$
Its cohomology begins
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H^0(X,\mce(-rd))
\longrightarrow
H^0(X,\mce)
\longrightarrow
H^0(X_r,\mce_r)
\longrightarrow
H^1(X,\mce(-rd)).
$$
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref} the two outer groups vanish for all $r\gg0$. Therefore
$$
H^0(X,\mce)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^0(X_r,\mce_r)
$$
for every sufficiently large $r$.

By definition,
$$
\hat\mce=\varprojlim_r\mce_r.
$$
Global sections commute with inverse limits of sheaves, so
$$
H^0(\hat X,\hat\mce)
=
\varprojlim_r H^0(X_r,\mce_r).
$$
The inverse system on the right is eventually canonically constant with
value $H^0(X,\mce)$ by the preceding paragraph. Its inverse limit is
therefore $H^0(X,\mce)$, and the resulting isomorphism is exactly the natural
restriction map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Condition (i) implies condition (ii).

::: pf-proof

Let $\mathfrak F$ be locally free on $\hat X$. By (i), choose a coherent
sheaf $\mcg$ on $X$ and an isomorphism
$$
\hat\mcg\cong\mathfrak F.
$$
By [[T-MODSERRE|Serre's theorem A]], there is $n_0$ such that
$$
\mcg(n)
$$
is generated by finitely many global sections for every $n\ge n_0$.
Completing the corresponding evaluation surjection gives a surjection onto
$$
\hat\mcg(n)\cong\mathfrak F(n).
$$
The completed generating sections are global sections on $\hat X$, so
$\mathfrak F(n)$ is generated by global sections for all $n\ge n_0$.
Thus (ii) holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Assume (ii). Every locally free sheaf $\mathfrak F$ on $\hat X$ admits
an exact presentation
$$
\hat\mce_1
\xrightarrow{\hat\delta}
\hat\mce_0
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak F
\longrightarrow0,
$$
where $\mce_0$ and $\mce_1$ are finite direct sums of twists
$\mco_X(-q)$.

::: pf-proof

By (ii), choose $q_0$ such that $\mathfrak F(q_0)$ is generated by global
sections. Since $\mathfrak F(q_0)$ is coherent and $|\hat X|=|Y|$ is
quasi-compact, finitely many global sections suffice. Twisting their
evaluation map back by $-q_0$ gives a surjection
$$
\hat\mce_0=\mco_{\hat X}(-q_0)^{\oplus a}
\twoheadrightarrow
\mathfrak F
$$
for some finite $a$.

Let $\mathfrak K$ be its kernel. Locally the target $\mathfrak F$ is free,
so the surjection splits locally. Hence $\mathfrak K$ is again locally free
of finite rank.

Apply (ii) to $\mathfrak K$. For some $q_1$ the sheaf
$\mathfrak K(q_1)$ is generated by finitely many global sections. Twisting
back gives a surjection
$$
\hat\mce_1=\mco_{\hat X}(-q_1)^{\oplus b}
\twoheadrightarrow
\mathfrak K.
$$
Composing with $\mathfrak K\hookrightarrow\hat\mce_0$ gives
$\hat\delta$ and the asserted exact presentation. Taking
$$
\mce_0=\mco_X(-q_0)^{\oplus a},
\qquad
\mce_1=\mco_X(-q_1)^{\oplus b}
$$
realizes both formal bundles as completions of the required bundles on $X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The presentation in step [](#s4){.pf-ref} algebraizes $\mathfrak F$. Hence
condition (ii) implies condition (i), proving (b).

::: pf-proof

The morphism
$$
\hat\delta:\hat\mce_1\longrightarrow\hat\mce_0
$$
is a global section of
$$
\sheafhom_{\mco_{\hat X}}(\hat\mce_1,\hat\mce_0).
$$
Since $\mce_1$ and $\mce_0$ are locally free, there is a canonical
identification
$$
\sheafhom_{\mco_{\hat X}}(\hat\mce_1,\hat\mce_0)
\cong
\widehat{\sheafhom_{\mco_X}(\mce_1,\mce_0)}.
$$
The sheaf
$$
\mch=\sheafhom_{\mco_X}(\mce_1,\mce_0)
$$
is locally free. By part (a), proved in step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
H^0(X,\mch)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^0(\hat X,\hat\mch).
$$
Therefore $\hat\delta$ is the completion of a unique morphism
$$
\delta:\mce_1\longrightarrow\mce_0.
$$

Set
$$
\mcg=\coker(\delta).
$$
This is coherent on the noetherian scheme $X$. Adic completion is exact on
coherent sheaves; affine-locally this is the exactness of completion on
finitely generated modules over a noetherian ring
([Stacks Project, Tag 0BNH](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BNH)).
Consequently
$$
\hat\mcg
\cong
\coker(\hat\delta)
\cong
\mathfrak F.
$$
Thus $\mathfrak F$ is algebraizable, proving (i).
Together with step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, conditions (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Under the equivalent conditions of (b), the map
$$
\Pic X\longrightarrow\Pic\hat X
$$
is injective.

::: pf-proof

Let $\mcl$ be an invertible sheaf on $X$ whose completion is trivial, and
choose an isomorphism
$$
\hat\varphi:\mco_{\hat X}\xrightarrow{\sim}\hat\mcl.
$$
Its inverse is
$$
\hat\psi:\hat\mcl\xrightarrow{\sim}\mco_{\hat X}.
$$

Apply part (a) to the locally free sheaves
$$
\sheafhom(\mco_X,\mcl)\cong\mcl
$$
and
$$
\sheafhom(\mcl,\mco_X)\cong\mcl^\vee.
$$
It lifts $\hat\varphi$ and $\hat\psi$ uniquely to morphisms
$$
\varphi:\mco_X\longrightarrow\mcl,
\qquad
\psi:\mcl\longrightarrow\mco_X.
$$
The two composites are endomorphisms of line bundles, hence global sections
of $\mco_X$:
$$
\psi\circ\varphi\in H^0(X,\mco_X),
\qquad
\varphi\circ\psi\in H^0(X,\sheafhom(\mcl,\mcl))
\cong H^0(X,\mco_X).
$$
Their completions are the identity because $\hat\varphi$ and $\hat\psi$ are
inverse. Part (a), now applied to $\mco_X$, makes the map on these global
sections injective. Hence
$$
\psi\circ\varphi=\id_{\mco_X},
\qquad
\varphi\circ\psi=\id_{\mcl}.
$$
Thus $\mcl\cong\mco_X$, so the kernel of
$\Pic X\to\Pic\hat X$ is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

Let $\mathfrak L$ be an invertible sheaf on $\hat X$. By (i), choose
a coherent sheaf $\mcg$ on $X$ with
$$
\hat\mcg\cong\mathfrak L.
$$
Then $\mcg$ is locally free of rank one on an open neighbourhood of $Y$.

::: pf-proof

Fix $y\in Y$ and put
$$
A=\mco_{X,y},
\qquad
I=\mci_y,
\qquad
M=\mcg_y.
$$
The coherent completion theorem identifies the completed module with
$$
\hat M\cong M\otimes_A\hat A,
$$
where $\hat A$ is the $I$-adic completion of $A$. The isomorphism
$\hat\mcg\cong\mathfrak L$ makes $\hat M$ a free $\hat A$-module of rank
one.

Since $A$ is noetherian local and $I\subseteq\mathfrak m_A$, the map
$$
A\longrightarrow\hat A
$$
is faithfully flat
([Stacks Project, Tag 00MC](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00MC)).
Finite projectivity and rank descend under faithfully flat maps
([Stacks Project, Tag 058S](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/058S)). Hence
$M$ is a projective $A$-module of rank one. Because $A$ is local,
$$
M\cong A.
$$
Thus $\mcg$ is locally free of rank one at every point of $Y$.

The locally-free-of-rank-one locus of a coherent sheaf of finite
presentation is open. Therefore there is an open neighbourhood
$$
V\supseteq Y
$$
on which $\mcg|_V$ is invertible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

The reflexive hull
$$
\mcl=\mcg^{\vee\vee}
$$
is an invertible sheaf on $X$, and
$$
\hat\mcl\cong\mathfrak L.
$$

::: pf-proof

The open set $V$ of step [](#s7){.pf-ref} is nonempty. Since
$X=\PP_k^N$ is integral, its generic point lies in $V$. Hence $\mcg$ has
generic rank one, so its reflexive hull $\mcl=\mcg^{\vee\vee}$ is a coherent
reflexive sheaf of rank one; see
[Stacks Project, Tag 0AVT](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AVT).

Projective space is regular, hence normal and locally factorial. On an
integral locally noetherian normal scheme, rank-one coherent reflexive
sheaves represent Weil divisor classes
([Stacks Project, Tag 0EBM](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EBM)).
On a locally factorial scheme every Weil divisor is Cartier, so
[[D-MODPIC|the Cartier--Weil comparison]] shows that every such rank-one
reflexive sheaf is invertible. Thus $\mcl$ is an invertible sheaf on $X$.

On $V$, the sheaf $\mcg$ is already locally free, so the natural map
$$
\mcg\longrightarrow\mcg^{\vee\vee}=\mcl
$$
is an isomorphism. Since $V$ is an actual open neighbourhood of $Y$, two
coherent sheaves which agree on $V$ have the same formal completion along
$Y$. Therefore
$$
\hat\mcl
\cong
\hat\mcg
\cong
\mathfrak L.
$$
Thus every class in $\Pic\hat X$ lies in the image of $\Pic X$.

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref} prove (a). Steps [](#s3){.pf-ref}, [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} prove the equivalence in (b).
Step [](#s6){.pf-ref} proves injectivity of
$$
\Pic X\longrightarrow\Pic\hat X,
$$
and steps [](#s7){.pf-ref} and [](#s8){.pf-ref} prove surjectivity under those equivalent conditions.
Hence
$$
\boxed{\Pic X\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pic\hat X}.
$$
This proves (c).
