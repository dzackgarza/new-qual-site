::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the curve $y^2=x^3-7x+10$.
This curve has at least 26 points with integer coordinates.
Find them (use a calculator), and verify that they are all contained in the subgroup (maybe equal to all of $X(\QQ)$?)
generated by $P=(1,2)$ and $Q=(2,2)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
E:y^2=x^3-7x+10,
\qquad
P=(1,2),
\qquad
Q=(2,2).
$$

<1>1. The following are $26$ integral points of $E$:
$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
(-3,\pm2),\quad
(-2,\pm4),\quad
(-1,\pm4),\quad
(1,\pm2),\quad
(2,\pm2),\\
(3,\pm4),\quad
(5,\pm10),\quad
(9,\pm26),\quad
(13,\pm46),\\
(31,\pm172),\quad
(41,\pm262),\quad
(67,\pm548),\quad
(302,\pm5248).
\end{gathered}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
For each displayed $x$, direct substitution in
$$
x^3-7x+10
$$
gives respectively
$$
2^2,4^2,4^2,2^2,2^2,4^2,10^2,26^2,46^2,
172^2,262^2,548^2,5248^2.
$$
Thus every displayed pair lies on $E$, and the two signs give $26$ distinct
points.
:::

<1>2. The addition law needed for the subgroup check is as follows. If
$$
R=(x_1,y_1),
\qquad
S=(x_2,y_2),
$$
with $R\ne\pm S$, then
$$
\lambda=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1},
\qquad
x(R+S)=\lambda^2-x_1-x_2,
$$
and
$$
y(R+S)=\lambda\bigl(x_1-x(R+S)\bigr)-y_1.
$$
For doubling,
$$
\lambda=\frac{3x_1^2-7}{2y_1}.
$$

::: {.proof}
These are the chord-and-tangent formulas for the short Weierstrass equation
$$
y^2=x^3+Ax+B
$$
with $A=-7$. They follow by intersecting the chord or tangent line with the
cubic and reflecting the third intersection point across the $x$-axis.
:::

<1>3. Successive applications of the addition law give
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P+Q&=(-3,-2),&
2P&=(-1,-4),\\
3P&=(9,-26),&
2P+Q&=(3,-4),\\
3P+Q&=(5,10),&
4P+Q&=(-2,4),\\
P-Q&=(13,46),&
2P+2Q&=(31,172),\\
5P+2Q&=(41,-262),&
6P+Q&=(67,548),\\
-4P+Q&=(302,5248).&&
\end{aligned}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Using step <1>2, the required slopes and sums are
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{sum} & \lambda & \text{result}\\ \hline
P+Q & 0 & (-3,-2)\\
P+P & -1 & (-1,-4)\\
2P+P & 3 & (9,-26)\\
2P+Q & 2 & (3,-4)\\
(2P+Q)+P & -3 & (5,10)\\
(3P+Q)+P & 2 & (-2,4)\\
P+(-Q) & -4 & (13,46)\\
(P+Q)+(P+Q) & -5 & (31,172)\\
(4P+Q)+(P+Q) & 6 & (41,-262)\\
3P+(3P+Q) & -9 & (67,548)\\
(Q-P)+(-3P) & -18 & (302,5248).
\end{array}
$$
Each row is obtained by substituting the already computed summands into the
formulas of step <1>2. For example, the last row uses
$$
Q-P=(13,-46),
\qquad
-3P=(9,26),
$$
so
$$
\lambda=\frac{26-(-46)}{9-13}=-18,
$$
and hence
$$
x=18^2-13-9=302,
\qquad
y=-18(13-302)+46=5248.
$$
This proves all the displayed identities.
:::

<1>4. Every point listed in step <1>1 belongs to the subgroup
$\langle P,Q\rangle\subseteq E(\QQ)$.

::: {.proof}
The points with first coordinates $1$ and $2$ are $\pm P$ and $\pm Q$.
For the remaining first coordinates, step <1>3 gives one of the two points
as
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
x & \text{point in }\langle P,Q\rangle\\ \hline
-3 & P+Q=(-3,-2)\\
-2 & 4P+Q=(-2,4)\\
-1 & 2P=(-1,-4)\\
3 & 2P+Q=(3,-4)\\
5 & 3P+Q=(5,10)\\
9 & 3P=(9,-26)\\
13 & P-Q=(13,46)\\
31 & 2P+2Q=(31,172)\\
41 & 5P+2Q=(41,-262)\\
67 & 6P+Q=(67,548)\\
302 & -4P+Q=(302,5248).
\end{array}
$$
On a short Weierstrass equation the inverse of $(x,y)$ is $(x,-y)$.
Therefore the point with the opposite sign of $y$ is also in
$\langle P,Q\rangle$ in every row. Together with $\pm P$ and $\pm Q$, this
accounts for all $26$ points in step <1>1.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 finds the $26$ integral points requested in the exercise, and
step <1>4 verifies that every one of them lies in the subgroup generated by
$P$ and $Q$.
:::
