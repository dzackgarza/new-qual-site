::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a noetherian separated scheme.
For nonempty $X$, define its **cohomological dimension**, denoted $\operatorname{cd}(X)$, to be the least integer $n$ such that $H^i(X,\mcf)=0$ for every quasi-coherent sheaf $\mcf$ and every $i>n$.
Set $\operatorname{cd}(\varnothing)=-\infty$.

Thus for nonempty $X$, Serre's theorem (3.7) says that $\operatorname{cd}(X)=0$ if and only if $X$ is affine.
Grothendieck's theorem (2.7) implies that $\operatorname{cd}(X) \leq \dim X$.

(a) In the definition of $\operatorname{cd}(X)$, show that it is sufficient to consider only coherent sheaves on $X$.
Use (II, Ex. 5.15) and (2.9).

(b) If $X$ is quasi-projective over a field $k$, then it is even sufficient to consider only locally free coherent sheaves on $X$.
Use (II, 5.18).

(c) Suppose $X$ has a covering by $r+1$ open affine subsets.
Use Čech cohomology to show that $\operatorname{cd}(X) \leq r$.

(d) If $X$ is a nonempty quasi-projective scheme of dimension $r$ over a field $k$, then $X$ can be covered by $r+1$ open affine subsets.
Conclude (independently of (2.7)) that $\operatorname{cd}(X) \leq \dim X$.
The empty scheme is covered by the empty family, and both dimensions are taken to be $-\infty$ there.

(e) Let $Y$ be a set-theoretic complete intersection (I, Ex. 2.17) of codimension $r$ in $X=\PP_k^n$.
Show that $\operatorname{cd}(X-Y) \leq r-1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A nonempty scheme has the nonzero global section $1$ of its structure sheaf, so any bound in the definition is at least zero.
Noetherian quasi-compactness gives a finite affine cover; step <1>3 will also show directly that a finite cohomological bound exists for a separated scheme.
The empty scheme satisfies every required vanishing and is treated by the convention in the statement.

<1>1. Testing coherent sheaves gives the same bound as testing all quasi-coherent sheaves, proving (a).

::: {.proof}
Every quasi-coherent sheaf $F$ on the noetherian scheme is the filtered union of its coherent subsheaves, by [[P-AGH2515EXTCOH]], part (d).
These subsheaves form a directed system because the sum of two is again coherent.
Cohomology of abelian sheaves on a noetherian space commutes with filtered direct limits [@Har10a, Proposition III.2.9].
Consequently
$$
H^i(X,F)\cong\varinjlim_\lambda H^i(X,F_\lambda).
$$
If the groups on the right vanish for every coherent $F_\lambda$ in every degree $i>n$, so do the groups on the left.
The converse is immediate because coherent sheaves are quasi-coherent.
Thus the same candidate bounds, and hence the same least bound, result from either class of sheaves.
:::

<1>2. On a quasi-projective scheme, testing locally free coherent sheaves suffices, proving (b).

::: {.proof}
Let $L$ be the very ample invertible sheaf of a quasi-projective immersion into $\PP_k^N$.
It is ample by [@Har10a, Theorem II.7.6].
For any coherent $F$, some twist $F\otimes L^m$ is generated by finitely many global sections: ampleness gives global generation, and quasi-compactness supplies finitely many generators.
Untwisting gives a surjection $(L^{-m})^{\oplus a}\twoheadrightarrow F$ from a locally free coherent sheaf.
Its kernel is coherent, so the construction may be repeated as many times as needed.
This is the quotient-by-twists construction of [@Har10a, II.5.18].

Suppose that $H^j(X,E)=0$ for all locally free coherent $E$ and all $j>n$.
For a coherent $F$, form exact sequences
$$
0\longrightarrow K_{a+1}\longrightarrow E_a\longrightarrow K_a\longrightarrow0,
\qquad K_0=F,
$$
with all $E_a$ locally free and all $K_a$ coherent.
Fix $i>n$.
Their long exact sequences give successive isomorphisms
$$
H^i(X,F)\cong H^{i+1}(X,K_1)\cong\cdots\cong H^{i+\ell}(X,K_\ell),
$$
because both adjacent cohomology groups of each $E_a$ vanish in these degrees.
Choose $\ell$ with $i+\ell>\dim X$, which is finite for a quasi-projective scheme.
Grothendieck vanishing [@Har10a, Theorem III.2.7] makes the last group zero.
Thus every coherent $F$ satisfies the same bound, and step <1>1 extends it to all quasi-coherent sheaves.
Only a finite initial segment of a locally free resolution was used; termination of that resolution is not assumed.
:::

<1>3. A cover by $r+1$ affine opens gives $\operatorname{cd}(X)\le r$, proving (c).

::: {.proof}
Finite intersections of affine opens of a separated scheme are affine.
The affine-cover Čech comparison therefore computes $H^i(X,F)$ for every quasi-coherent $F$ by a complex with terms only in degrees $0,\ldots,r$ [@Har10a, Theorem III.4.5].
Its higher cohomology vanishes, yielding the stated bound, as in [[D-PTIW0]].
This argument does not use Grothendieck vanishing.
:::

<1>4. Let $Z\subseteq\overline X\subseteq\PP_k^N$ be closed subsets, and let $T$ be a nonempty closed subset of $\overline X\setminus Z$.
There is a positive-degree homogeneous polynomial vanishing on $Z$ and not vanishing identically on any irreducible component of $T$.

::: {.proof}
Let $J\subseteq k[z_0,\ldots,z_N]$ be the homogeneous ideal of $Z$, and let $\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_s$ be the homogeneous primes of the closures of the irreducible components of $T$.
These primes are incomparable: inclusion between their closures would give inclusion between the corresponding components after intersection with the open set $\overline X\setminus Z$.
Each component meets that open set, so its closure is not contained in $Z$.
Thus $J$ is not contained in any $\mathfrak p_a$.

Choose homogeneous $u_a\in J\setminus\mathfrak p_a$ of positive degree.
This is possible by choosing a homogeneous component outside the homogeneous prime and, if necessary, multiplying by a coordinate not in that prime.
For every $b\ne a$, choose a homogeneous $v_{ab}\in\mathfrak p_b\setminus\mathfrak p_a$.
The polynomial
$$
w_a=u_a\prod_{b\ne a}v_{ab}
$$
belongs to $J$, avoids $\mathfrak p_a$ by primality, and belongs to every other $\mathfrak p_b$.
Its degree is positive.
Let $D$ be a common positive multiple of the degrees of all the $w_a$, and define
$$
g=\sum_{a=1}^s w_a^{D/\deg w_a}.
$$
This is homogeneous and belongs to $J$.
Modulo $\mathfrak p_a$, only its $a$th summand survives and is nonzero, so $g\notin\mathfrak p_a$ for every $a$.
This proves the assertion over any field; no choice of a scalar outside a finite union of proper vector subspaces was used.
:::

<1>5. A nonempty quasi-projective scheme of dimension $r$ has a cover by $r+1$ affine opens, proving (d).

::: {.proof}
Realize $X$ as an open subscheme of a closed projective subscheme $\overline X\subseteq\PP_k^N$ and let $Z=\overline X\setminus X$ with its reduced closed structure.
Starting from $T_0=|X|$, apply step <1>4 successively to choose positive-degree homogeneous polynomials $g_j$ vanishing on $Z$ and not vanishing identically on any component of the nonempty closed subset $T_j\subseteq X$.
Set
$$
T_{j+1}=T_j\cap V_+(g_j).
$$
This intersects each component of $T_j$ in a proper closed subset, so $\dim T_{j+1}\le\dim T_j-1$ whenever $T_{j+1}$ is nonempty.
The dimension inequality follows by appending the component to every chain of irreducible closed subsets in its proper closed subset.
After at most $r+1$ choices, the residual set is therefore empty.

For each chosen polynomial, put
$$
W_j=\overline X\cap D_+(g_j).
$$
Because $g_j$ vanishes on $Z$, this is an open subset of $X$.
It is affine: $D_+(g_j)$ is an affine distinguished open of projective space, and $\overline X\cap D_+(g_j)$ is closed in it.
The residual-set construction says that these $W_j$ cover $X$.
There are at most $r+1$ of them; repeating a member gives a cover with exactly $r+1$ indexed members when necessary.
Step <1>3 now gives $\operatorname{cd}(X)\le r=\dim X$ without using (2.7).
No reducedness or irreducibility hypothesis was imposed on $X$; only the underlying closed subsets were used in constructing the cover.
:::

<1>6. For the complement in (e), $\operatorname{cd}(\PP_k^n\setminus Y)\le r-1$.

::: {.proof}
For $r\ge1$, choose homogeneous equations $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ whose projective zero sets intersect set-theoretically in $Y$, as in the definition of a set-theoretic complete intersection.
Then
$$
\PP_k^n\setminus Y=\bigcup_{a=1}^r D_+(f_a).
$$
Each member is affine, and this open subscheme is noetherian and separated.
Step <1>3 with $r$ affine opens gives the required bound $r-1$.
If $r=0$, the empty family of defining hypersurfaces has intersection all of $\PP_k^n$, so the complement is empty and its cohomological dimension is $-\infty$ by convention.
The same inequality holds in this case.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 prove (a)--(c), steps <1>4--<1>5 construct the affine cover and prove (d), and step <1>6 proves (e).
:::
:::

::: {.remark title="The empty scheme"}
Every sheaf on the empty scheme is zero, so all of its cohomology groups vanish.
Consequently the least-integer definition alone has no value there; the convention $\operatorname{cd}(\varnothing)=-\infty$ covers the empty complement in (e).
With this convention the equivalence $\operatorname{cd}(X)=0$ and affineness is stated for nonempty schemes.
:::
