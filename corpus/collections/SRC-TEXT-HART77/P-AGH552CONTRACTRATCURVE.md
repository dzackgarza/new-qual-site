::: {.problem}
Let $Y \cong \PP^1$ be a curve in a surface $X$, with $Y^2<0$.
Show that $Y$ is contractible (5.7.2) to a point on a projective variety $X_0$ (in general singular).
:::

::: {.solution}
Under the standing hypotheses for this section, $X$ is a nonsingular
projective surface. Put
$$
m=-Y^2>0.
$$

<1>1. There is a very ample divisor $H$ on $X$ such that, with
$$
k=H\cdot Y,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{
k\ge2,
\qquad
H^1\bigl(X,\OO_X(mH)\bigr)=0.}
$$

::: {.proof}
Choose any very ample divisor $A$. Since $A\cdot Y>0$, for all sufficiently
large integers $n$ the divisor
$$
H=nA
$$
is very ample and satisfies
$$
H\cdot Y=n(A\cdot Y)\ge2.
$$
Serre vanishing applied to the ample line bundle $\OO_X(A)$ gives
$$
H^1\bigl(X,\OO_X(mnA)\bigr)=0
$$
for all sufficiently large $n$. Choosing one $n$ satisfying both conditions
gives the required $H$.
:::

Define
$$
D=mH+kY
$$
and
$$
\mathcal M=\OO_X(D).
$$

<1>2. The divisor $D$ has degree zero on $Y$, and hence
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M|_Y\cong\OO_{\PP^1}.}
$$

::: {.proof}
By the definitions of $m$ and $k$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
D\cdot Y
&=(mH+kY)\cdot Y\\
&=m(H\cdot Y)+kY^2\\
&=mk-km\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $Y\cong\PP^1$, every line bundle of degree zero on $Y$ is trivial.
Therefore
$$
\mathcal M|_Y\cong\OO_Y\cong\OO_{\PP^1}.
$$
:::

<1>3. For every integer
$$
0\le i\le k,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{
H^1\bigl(X,\OO_X(mH+iY)\bigr)=0.}
$$

::: {.proof}
We argue by induction on $i$. The case $i=0$ is step <1>1.

Assume the assertion for $i-1$, where $1\le i\le k$. The divisor sequence
for $Y$ gives
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\OO_X(mH+(i-1)Y)
\longrightarrow
\OO_X(mH+iY)
\longrightarrow
\OO_Y(mH+iY)
\longrightarrow
0.
$$
Its restriction term has degree
$$
\begin{aligned}
(mH+iY)\cdot Y
&=mk-im\\
&=m(k-i)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, because $Y\cong\PP^1$,
$$
\OO_Y(mH+iY)
\cong
\OO_{\PP^1}\bigl(m(k-i)\bigr)
$$
and
$$
H^1\!\left(
Y,\OO_Y(mH+iY)
\right)=0.
$$
The long exact cohomology sequence, together with the induction hypothesis,
therefore gives
$$
H^1\bigl(X,\OO_X(mH+iY)\bigr)=0.
$$
:::

Let
$$
s_Y\in H^0(X,\OO_X(Y))
$$
be the canonical section whose zero divisor is $Y$.

<1>4. The line bundle $\mathcal M$ is generated by global sections at every
point of
$$
X\setminus Y.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $H$ is very ample and $m\ge1$, the line bundle
$$
\OO_X(mH)
$$
is very ample, hence globally generated. Multiplication by $s_Y^k$ gives a
linear map
$$
H^0\bigl(X,\OO_X(mH)\bigr)
\longrightarrow
H^0(X,\mathcal M).
$$
On $X\setminus Y$, the section $s_Y$ is nowhere zero. Therefore at every
point $x\notin Y$, a section of $\OO_X(mH)$ nonzero at $x$ remains nonzero
after multiplication by $s_Y^k$. Hence these multiplied sections generate
$\mathcal M$ at every point outside $Y$.
:::

<1>5. The restriction map
$$
H^0(X,\mathcal M)
\longrightarrow
H^0(Y,\mathcal M|_Y)
$$
is surjective.

::: {.proof}
Tensor the divisor sequence of $Y$ by $\mathcal M$. Since
$$
\mathcal M(-Y)
=
\OO_X(mH+(k-1)Y),
$$
we obtain
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\OO_X(mH+(k-1)Y)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal M
\longrightarrow
\mathcal M|_Y
\longrightarrow
0.
$$
Step <1>3 gives
$$
H^1\bigl(X,\OO_X(mH+(k-1)Y)\bigr)=0.
$$
The associated long exact sequence therefore makes the displayed
restriction map on $H^0$ surjective.
:::

<1>6. The line bundle $\mathcal M$ is globally generated on all of $X$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 gives generation away from $Y$. By step <1>2,
$$
\mathcal M|_Y\cong\OO_Y.
$$
Choose the nowhere-zero constant section
$$
1\in H^0(Y,\mathcal M|_Y).
$$
By step <1>5 it lifts to a section
$$
\tau\in H^0(X,\mathcal M).
$$
For every $y\in Y$, the value $\tau(y)$ is nonzero in the fibre
$\mathcal M_y\otimes k(y)$, because its restriction is the constant section
$1$. Thus $\tau$ generates the fibre at every point of $Y$. Together with
step <1>4 this proves global generation.
:::

<1>7. The complete linear system $|\mathcal M|$ defines a morphism
$$
\varphi:X\longrightarrow\PP^N
$$
which maps all of $Y$ to a single point
$$
\boxed{P=\varphi(Y).}
$$

::: {.proof}
Global generation from step <1>6 gives the morphism associated with the
complete linear system.

By step <1>2,
$$
\mathcal M|_Y\cong\OO_Y,
$$
so every global section of $\mathcal M$, when restricted to $Y$, is a
constant multiple of one fixed nowhere-zero trivializing section. Therefore
all homogeneous coordinate ratios of $\varphi|_Y$ are constant. Hence
$\varphi|_Y$ is constant, with image a single point $P$.
:::

<1>8. One has
$$
\boxed{\varphi^{-1}(P)=Y.}
$$

::: {.proof}
Choose a basis
$$
\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_r
$$
of
$$
H^0\bigl(X,\OO_X(mH)\bigr).
$$
The sections
$$
s_Y^k\sigma_0,\ldots,s_Y^k\sigma_r
\in
H^0(X,\mathcal M)
$$
all restrict to zero on $Y$, so their corresponding homogeneous
coordinates vanish at $P$.

Now let $x\in X\setminus Y$. Since $\OO_X(mH)$ is globally generated,
some $\sigma_j(x)$ is nonzero. Also $s_Y(x)\ne0$, so
$$
(s_Y^k\sigma_j)(x)\ne0.
$$
Thus the image $\varphi(x)$ has at least one of these coordinates nonzero
and cannot equal $P$. Hence no point outside $Y$ maps to $P$, proving the
assertion.
:::

<1>9. The restriction
$$
\boxed{
\varphi|_{X\setminus Y}:
X\setminus Y
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\varphi(X)\setminus\{P\}}
$$
is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\iota_m:X\hookrightarrow\PP^r
$$
be the closed immersion defined by the very ample complete linear system
$|mH|$ and the basis $\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_r$ from step <1>8.

Choose homogeneous coordinates on $\PP^N$ for $\varphi$ so that the first
$r+1$ coordinates are the sections
$$
s_Y^k\sigma_0,\ldots,s_Y^k\sigma_r.
$$
Let
$$
L=V(z_0,\ldots,z_r)\subseteq\PP^N.
$$
Step <1>8 shows
$$
\varphi(X)\cap L=\{P\}.
$$
Hence the linear projection
$$
\rho:\PP^N\setminus L\longrightarrow\PP^r,
\qquad
[z_0:\ldots:z_N]\longmapsto[z_0:\ldots:z_r]
$$
is defined on $\varphi(X)\setminus\{P\}$.

For $x\in X\setminus Y$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\varphi(x))
&=
[s_Y(x)^k\sigma_0(x):\cdots:s_Y(x)^k\sigma_r(x)]\\
&=
[\sigma_0(x):\cdots:\sigma_r(x)]\\
&=
\iota_m(x).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\rho\circ\varphi|_{X\setminus Y}
=
\iota_m|_{X\setminus Y}.
$$
Because $\iota_m$ is a closed immersion, its inverse on its image is a
morphism. Therefore
$$
\bigl(\iota_m|_{X\setminus Y}\bigr)^{-1}
\circ
\rho
$$
is a morphism on $\varphi(X)\setminus\{P\}$ and is inverse to
$\varphi|_{X\setminus Y}$. Step <1>8 ensures that every point of
$\varphi(X)\setminus\{P\}$ comes from $X\setminus Y$. Hence the restriction
is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>10. Let
$$
X_0=\varphi(X)\subseteq\PP^N.
$$
Then $X_0$ is a projective variety, and
$$
\boxed{
\varphi:X\longrightarrow X_0
}
$$
contracts precisely $Y$ to the point $P$ and is an isomorphism away from
$Y$.

::: {.proof}
Since $X$ is projective, its image under the projective morphism $\varphi$
is closed in $\PP^N$. Since $X$ is irreducible, $X_0$ is irreducible.
Thus $X_0$ is a projective variety.

Step <1>7 maps $Y$ to $P$, step <1>8 says the fibre over $P$ is exactly
$Y$, and step <1>9 says the morphism is an isomorphism
$$
X\setminus Y
\xrightarrow{\sim}
X_0\setminus\{P\}.
$$
Moreover $X_0$ has dimension two because it contains the open subset
isomorphic to $X\setminus Y$. This is precisely the required contraction in
the sense of (5.7.2). The point $P$ need not be nonsingular when
$m>1$, which is why the target is allowed to be singular.
:::

<1>11. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>6 construct the globally generated line bundle
$\OO_X(mH+kY)$. Steps <1>7--<1>8 show that its morphism contracts exactly
$Y$, while step <1>9 proves that nothing else changes. Step <1>10 identifies
the image as the required projective surface $X_0$.
:::
