::: {.problem}
Let $S$ be a graded ring, generated by $S_1$ as an $S_0$-algebra, and put $A=S_0$.
For any integer $d > 0$, let $S^{(d)}$ be the graded ring $\bigoplus_{n \geq 0} S^{(d)}_n$ where $S^{(d)}_n = S_{nd}$.
Let $X = \Proj S$.
Show that $\Proj S^{(d)} \cong X$, and that the sheaf $\OO(1)$ on $\Proj S^{(d)}$ corresponds via this isomorphism to $\OO_X(d)$.

This construction is related to the $d$-uple embedding as follows.
If $x_0, \ldots, x_r$ generate $S_1$, corresponding to an embedding $X \injects \PP^r_A$, then the monomials of degree $d$ in the $x_i$ generate $S^{(d)}_1 = S_d$.
These define a projective embedding of $\Proj S^{(d)}$ which is the image of $X$ under the $d$-uple embedding of $\PP^r_A$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $R=S^{(d)}$ and $Y=\Proj R$.
For a positive-degree homogeneous element $f$ of a graded ring $B$, write $B_{(f)}=(B_f)_0$.
Use the [[D-MODGRMOD|associated-sheaf convention]] $\OO_{\Proj B}(e)=\widetilde{B(e)}$, with $B(e)_n=B_{n+e}$.

<1>1. The opens $D_X(s)$ for $s\in S_1$ cover $X$, and the opens $D_Y(s^d)$ cover $Y$.
Here $D_X$ and $D_Y$ denote the homogeneous distinguished opens in the respective Proj schemes.

::: {.proof}
Since $S$ is generated by $S_1$, a homogeneous prime containing every element of $S_1$ contains $S_+$ and does not define a point of $X$.
This proves the first covering assertion.

Every monomial of degree $nd$ in elements of $S_1$ is a product of $n$ monomials of degree $d$.
Thus $R$ is generated as an $A$-algebra by $R_1=S_d$.
If a homogeneous prime of $R$ contains $s^d$ for every $s\in S_1$, it contains every product $s_1\cdots s_d\in S_d$: the $d$th power of that product is $s_1^d\cdots s_d^d$.
These products span $S_d$ over $A$, so the prime contains $R_1$, hence $R_+$.
It does not define a point of $Y$, proving the second covering assertion.
:::

<1>2. There is a canonical isomorphism $\phi:X\xrightarrow{\cong}Y$.

::: {.proof}
For $s\in S_1$, the inclusion $R\subseteq S$ induces
$$
R_{(s^d)}\longrightarrow S_{(s)},\qquad
a/(s^d)^n\longmapsto a/s^{dn},\quad a\in S_{dn}.
$$
It is injective: if a numerator maps to zero, some power of $s$ annihilates it in $S$.
Increasing the exponent to a multiple of $d$ gives the same annihilating equality in the subring $R$.
It is surjective because, for $a\in S_e$, a fraction $a/s^e$ can be written
$$
\frac{a}{s^e}=\frac{a s^{dn-e}}{(s^d)^n}
$$
for any $n$ with $dn\ge e$.
The numerator on the right has degree $dn$ in $S$ and degree $n$ in $R$.

On the intersection with the chart for $t\in S_1$, the function $t^d/s^d$ corresponds to $(t/s)^d$.
Inverting it is equivalent to inverting $t/s$.
Thus the affine isomorphisms identify the chart intersections and their restriction maps, all of which send a fraction to the same fraction after localization.
They glue along the covers of step <1>1, as do their inverses, giving $\phi$ [@Har10a, Chapter II, §2].
The map is canonical because every chart map is induced by $R\subseteq S$.
:::

<1>3. The twisting sheaves correspond by
$$
\boxed{\phi^*\OO_Y(1)\cong\OO_X(d)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
On $D_Y(s^d)$, the unit $s^d$ in the localization has degree one for the $R$-grading and gives a frame of $\OO_Y(1)$.
On $D_X(s)$, the section $s^d$ gives a frame of $\OO_X(d)$, since $s$ has degree one in $S$ [@Har10a, Proposition II.5.12].
Send the first frame to the second.
On an overlap, the transition function for the first is $t^d/s^d$, and for the second it is $(t/s)^d$.
Step <1>2 identifies these functions, so the local module isomorphisms glue to the asserted isomorphism.
:::

<1>4. With finitely many degree-one generators $x_0,\ldots,x_r$, the resulting projective embedding is the restriction of the $d$-uple embedding.

::: {.proof}
For a multiindex $\alpha=(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_r)\in\NN^{r+1}$, put $|\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i$ and $x^\alpha=x_0^{\alpha_0}\cdots x_r^{\alpha_r}$.
Let $e_i$ be the multiindex whose $i$th entry is one and whose other entries are zero.
The monomials $x^\alpha$ with $|\alpha|=d$ span $S_d$ over $A$.
By step <1>1, they generate $R$ as an $A$-algebra.
Hence, with one degree-one variable $z_\alpha$ for each such monomial, there is a graded surjection
$$
A[z_\alpha:|\alpha|=d]\longrightarrow R,\qquad z_\alpha\longmapsto x^\alpha.
$$
It induces a closed immersion $Y\hookrightarrow\PP_A^N$, where $N=\binom{r+d}{d}-1$ [@Har10a, Exercise II.3.12].
Under step <1>3 its coordinate sections pull back to the monomials $x^\alpha$ in $\Gamma(X,\OO_X(d))$.

Applying steps <1>1--<1>3 first to the polynomial ring $P=A[X_0,\ldots,X_r]$ gives $\Proj P^{(d)}\cong\PP_A^r$.
The surjection $A[z_\alpha]\to P^{(d)}$, $z_\alpha\mapsto X^\alpha$, therefore gives the closed immersion defined by all degree-$d$ monomials, namely the $d$-uple embedding.
The graded quotient $P\to S$ induces $P^{(d)}\to R$, so the same construction restricted to $X$ is the closed immersion just obtained.
On the chart where $x_i\ne0$, its coordinate map is explicitly
$$
z_\alpha/z_{d e_i}\longmapsto x^\alpha/x_i^d.
$$
Thus the morphisms agree as scheme morphisms, including their maps on structure sheaves.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives the Proj isomorphism, step <1>3 identifies the twisting sheaf, and step <1>4 identifies the projective embedding.
:::
