::: problem
Let $Y$ be the curve $y^2 = x^3 - x$ in $\AA^2$, and assume $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$. This exercise shows that $Y$ is not a rational curve, and hence that $K(Y)$ is not a purely transcendental extension of $k$.

1. Show that $Y$ is nonsingular, and deduce that $A = A(Y) \cong k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^3 + x)$ is an integrally closed domain.
2. Let $k[x]$ be the subring of $K = K(Y)$ generated by the image of $x$ in $A$. Show that $k[x]$ is a polynomial ring, and that $A$ is the integral closure of $k[x]$ in $K$.
3. Show that there is an automorphism $\sigma: A \to A$ sending $y$ to $-y$ and fixing $x$. For $a \in A$, define the norm of $a$ to be $N(a) = a \cdot \sigma(a)$. Show that $N(a) \in k[x]$, that $N(1) = 1$, and that $N(ab) = N(a) N(b)$ for all $a, b \in A$.
4. Using the norm, show that the units in $A$ are precisely the nonzero elements of $k$. Show that $x$ and $y$ are irreducible elements of $A$, and that $A$ is not a unique factorization domain.
5. Prove that $Y$ is not a rational curve.
:::
