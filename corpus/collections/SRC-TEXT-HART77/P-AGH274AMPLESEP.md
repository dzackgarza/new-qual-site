::: {.problem}
(a) Use (7.6) to show that if $X$ is a scheme of finite type over a noetherian ring $A$, and if $X$ admits an ample invertible sheaf, then $X$ is separated.

(b) Let $X$ be the affine line over a field $k$ with the origin doubled (4.0.1).
Calculate $\Pic X$, determine which invertible sheaves are generated by global sections, and then show directly, without using (a), that there is no ample invertible sheaf on $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For part (b), write the [[FE-SCHLINE|doubled-origin line]] as
$$
X=U\cup V,\qquad U=\Spec k[t],\quad V=\Spec k[t],
\quad U\cap V=\Spec k[t,t^{-1}],
$$
with the two copies identified by the identity on the intersection.
Let $0_U$ and $0_V$ denote the two origins.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Part (a) follows from Theorem II.7.6.

::: pf-proof

Let $\mathcal L$ be ample on the finite-type $A$-scheme.
By the [[D-MODAMPLE|ample/very-ample criterion]] [@Har10a, Theorem II.7.6], some positive tensor power of $\mathcal L$ is the pullback of $\OO(1)$ under an immersion $X\hookrightarrow\PP_A^N$.
An immersion is separated, projective space is separated over $\Spec A$, and composites of separated morphisms are separated [@Har10a, Chapter II, §4].
Thus $X\to\Spec A$ is separated.
The argument only uses an immersion; it does not require $X$ to be proper or the immersion to be closed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The Picard group of the doubled-origin line is $\boxed{\Pic(X)\cong\ZZ}$.
A representative $\mathcal L_a$ of $a\in\ZZ$ is given by frames $e_U,e_V$ with transition $e_V=t^a e_U$.

::: pf-proof

Every invertible sheaf is trivial on $U$ and $V$: both have coordinate ring the PID $k[t]$, so their rank-one projective modules are free.
It is therefore determined by a gluing unit in $k[t,t^{-1}]^*$.
Every such unit is $ct^a$ for a unique $a\in\ZZ$ and $c\in k^*$, since the largest and smallest exponents in a Laurent polynomial add under multiplication.
Changing either frame multiplies the gluing unit by a unit of $k[t]$, which is a nonzero constant.
We may remove $c$, and such frame changes never change $a$.

Conversely, every transition $t^a$ glues two trivial invertible sheaves to an invertible sheaf on $X$.
Tensor products multiply transitions, so
$$
\mathcal L_a\otimes\mathcal L_b\cong\mathcal L_{a+b},
\qquad\mathcal L_0\cong\OO_X.
$$
This proves the claimed group isomorphism and exhausts all isomorphism classes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

An invertible sheaf on this $X$ is generated by global sections exactly when it is trivial.

::: pf-proof

A section of $\mathcal L_a$ is a pair $(p,q)\in k[t]^2$ satisfying
$$
p(t)e_U=q(t)e_V,
\qquad p(t)=t^a q(t)\text{ in }k[t,t^{-1}].
$$
For $a>0$, every such $p$ is divisible by $t$, so all global sections have zero image in the fibre at $0_U$.
They cannot generate the stalk there: every linear combination remains in its maximal-ideal multiple.
For $a<0$, the equivalent identity $q=t^{-a}p$ shows that all global sections vanish at $0_V$, giving the same obstruction there.
For $a=0$, the pair $(1,1)$ is a global frame and generates everywhere.
Thus
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_a\text{ is globally generated}\iff a=0.}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

No invertible sheaf on the doubled-origin line is ample.

::: pf-proof

The scheme is noetherian, as it has the finite noetherian affine cover $U,V$.
Suppose $\mathcal L_a$ is ample, using the definition that $\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal L_a^{\otimes q}$ is globally generated for all sufficiently large $q$ and every coherent $\mathcal F$ [@Har10a, Chapter II, §7].
Apply this to $\mathcal F=\OO_X$.
Since $\mathcal L_a^{\otimes q}\cong\mathcal L_{qa}$, step [](#s3){.pf-ref} forces $qa=0$ for a positive $q$, hence $a=0$.
But $\OO_X$ is not ample either: take the coherent invertible sheaf $\mathcal F=\mathcal L_1$.
For every $q$, its twist by $\OO_X^{\otimes q}$ remains $\mathcal L_1$, which is not globally generated by step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.
This contradiction proves the assertion directly, without using step [](#s1){.pf-ref} or the nonseparatedness of $X$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves (a).
Steps [](#s2){.pf-ref}, [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} compute the Picard group, determine its globally generated classes, and exclude every ample class, proving all of (b).

:::
