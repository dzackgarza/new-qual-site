::: {.problem}
A $k\dash$algebra $A$ is said to be **rigid** if it has no infinitesimal deformations, or equivalently, by (Ex.
9.8), if $T^1(A) = 0$.

Let $A = k[x,y,z,w] / (x,y) \intersect (z,w)$, and show that $A$ is rigid.
This corresponds to two planes in $\AA^4$ which meet at a point.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
S=k[x,y,z,w],
\qquad
J=(x,y)\cap(z,w),
\qquad
A=S/J.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The ideal $J$ is
$$
J=(xz,xw,yz,yw).
$$

::: pf-proof

Both $(x,y)$ and $(z,w)$ are monomial ideals, and their intersection is
generated by the least common multiples of one generator from each ideal.
Hence
$$
(x,y)\cap(z,w)
=(xz,xw,yz,yw)
=(x,y)(z,w).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4$ map respectively to
$$
xz,\quad xw,\quad yz,\quad yw,
$$
then the syzygy module of these four generators is generated by
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_1&=(w,-z,0,0),&
s_2&=(y,0,-x,0),\\
s_3&=(0,y,0,-x),&
s_4&=(0,0,w,-z).
\end{aligned}
$$

::: pf-proof

Each displayed vector is visibly a syzygy.
Conversely, suppose
$$
(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)\in S^4
$$
satisfies
$$
xz a_1+xw a_2+yz a_3+yw a_4=0.
$$
Then
$$
x(za_1+wa_2)+y(za_3+wa_4)=0.
$$
Since $x$ and $y$ are relatively prime, there is $h\in S$ such that
$$
za_1+wa_2=yh,
\qquad
za_3+wa_4=-xh.
$$
The first equality shows $yh\in(z,w)$. Since $(z,w)$ is prime and
$y\notin(z,w)$, we have $h\in(z,w)$, so write
$$
h=zu+wv.
$$
Then
$$
z(a_1-yu)+w(a_2-yv)=0,
$$
so relative primeness of $z,w$ gives some $r\in S$ with
$$
a_1-yu=wr,
\qquad
a_2-yv=-zr.
$$
Similarly,
$$
z(a_3+xu)+w(a_4+xv)=0
$$
gives some $t\in S$ with
$$
a_3+xu=wt,
\qquad
a_4+xv=-zt.
$$
Therefore
$$
(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)
=r s_1+u s_2+v s_3+t s_4,
$$
as required.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

An element of $\Hom_A(J/J^2,A)$ is therefore exactly a quadruple
$$
(a,b,c,d)\in A^4
$$
satisfying
$$
\boxed{
wa=zb,
\qquad
ya=xc,
\qquad
yb=xd,
\qquad
wc=zd.
}
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives a presentation
$$
S^4\longrightarrow S^4\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow0,
$$
where the second free module maps its basis to $xz,xw,yz,yw$ and the first
map has columns $s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4$.
Tensoring with $A=S/J$ gives the right-exact presentation
$$
A^4\longrightarrow A^4\longrightarrow J/J^2\longrightarrow0.
$$
Applying $\Hom_A(-,A)$ identifies a homomorphism $J/J^2\to A$ with the four
values
$$
a=\phi(xz),\quad b=\phi(xw),\quad c=\phi(yz),\quad d=\phi(yw)
$$
that annihilate the four syzygies.
Those four conditions are precisely the displayed equations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Let
$$
B=A/(z,w)\cong k[x,y],
\qquad
C=A/(x,y)\cong k[z,w].
$$
The natural map
$$
A\longrightarrow B\oplus C
$$
is injective.

::: pf-proof

An element of $A=S/J$ maps to zero in both quotients exactly when a lift in
$S$ lies in
$$
(z,w)\cap(x,y)=J.
$$
Hence its class in $A$ is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Every quadruple $(a,b,c,d)$ satisfying the equations in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=zp+xr,&
b&=wp+xs,\\
c&=zq+yr,&
d&=wq+ys
\end{aligned}
$$
for some $p,q,r,s\in A$.

::: pf-proof

Write $a_B,a_C$ for the images of $a$ in $B,C$, and similarly for
$b,c,d$.

Projecting $wa=zb$ to $C=k[z,w]$ gives
$$
w a_C=z b_C.
$$
Since $z$ and $w$ are relatively prime in the UFD $C$, there is
$p_C\in C$ such that
$$
a_C=zp_C,
\qquad
b_C=wp_C.
$$
Likewise, projecting $wc=zd$ to $C$ gives some $q_C\in C$ with
$$
c_C=zq_C,
\qquad
d_C=wq_C.
$$

Projecting $ya=xc$ to $B=k[x,y]$ gives some $r_B\in B$ with
$$
a_B=xr_B,
\qquad
c_B=yr_B,
$$
and projecting $yb=xd$ to $B$ gives some $s_B\in B$ with
$$
b_B=xs_B,
\qquad
d_B=ys_B.
$$

Choose lifts
$$
p,q,r,s\in A
$$
of $p_C,q_C,r_B,s_B$, respectively.
The elements $a$ and $zp+xr$ have the same images in both $B$ and $C$.
By the injectivity in step [](#s4){.pf-ref},
$$
a=zp+xr.
$$
The same argument gives the other three displayed identities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The map
$$
\Hom_A(\Omega_{S/k}\tensor_SA,A)
\longrightarrow
\Hom_A(J/J^2,A)
$$
is surjective.

::: pf-proof

Let $\phi$ correspond to a quadruple $(a,b,c,d)$ as in step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.
Choose $p,q,r,s$ as in step [](#s5){.pf-ref} and define a $k$-derivation
$$
\delta:S\longrightarrow A
$$
by
$$
\delta(x)=p,
\qquad
\delta(y)=q,
\qquad
\delta(z)=r,
\qquad
\delta(w)=s.
$$
Under the universal property of Kähler differentials, $\delta$ is an element
of
$$
\Hom_A(\Omega_{S/k}\tensor_SA,A).
$$
Its restriction to $J/J^2$ sends the four generators to
$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta(xz)&=zp+xr=a,\\
\delta(xw)&=wp+xs=b,\\
\delta(yz)&=zq+yr=c,\\
\delta(yw)&=wq+ys=d.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus it maps to $\phi$, proving surjectivity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

The algebra $A$ is rigid.

::: pf-proof

By [[P-AGH398TONEMODULE|Exercise III.9.8]],
$$
T^1(A)
=\operatorname{coker}\!\left(
\Hom_A(\Omega_{S/k}\tensor_SA,A)
\longrightarrow
\Hom_A(J/J^2,A)
\right).
$$
Step [](#s6){.pf-ref} shows that this map is surjective, hence
$$
\boxed{T^1(A)=0}.
$$
Exercise III.9.8 identifies $T^1(A)$ with the infinitesimal deformation space
of $A$, so $A$ has no nontrivial infinitesimal deformations and is rigid.

:::
