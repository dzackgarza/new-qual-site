::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that $\alpha=\sqrt3+\sqrt2$ is algebraic over $\QQ$ by writing down a polynomial $f(x)\in\QQ[x]$ of degree $4$ having $\alpha$ as a root.

(b) Show that $f(x)$ is irreducible over $\QQ$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The number $\alpha=\sqrt3+\sqrt2$ satisfies
$$
\boxed{f(x)=x^4-10x^2+1}.
$$

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
\alpha^2=(\sqrt3+\sqrt2)^2=5+2\sqrt6.
$$
Hence
$$
\alpha^2-5=2\sqrt6,
$$
so squaring gives
$$
(\alpha^2-5)^2=24.
$$
Expanding and simplifying yields
$$
\alpha^4-10\alpha^2+1=0.
$$
Thus $f(x)=x^4-10x^2+1\in\QQ[x]$ is a degree-$4$ polynomial having
$\alpha$ as a root.
:::

<1>2. The field generated by $\alpha$ contains both $\sqrt2$ and
$\sqrt3$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
(\sqrt3+\sqrt2)(\sqrt3-\sqrt2)=1,
$$
one has
$$
\alpha^{-1}=\sqrt3-\sqrt2.
$$
Therefore
$$
\sqrt3=\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{-1}}2,
\qquad
\sqrt2=\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{-1}}2.
$$
Both expressions belong to $\QQ(\alpha)$.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
[\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\QQ]=4.
$$

::: {.proof}
The extension $\QQ(\sqrt2)/\QQ$ has degree $2$. It remains to show that
$\sqrt3\notin\QQ(\sqrt2)$.

Suppose instead that
$$
\sqrt3=a+b\sqrt2
$$
for some $a,b\in\QQ$. Squaring gives
$$
3=a^2+2b^2+2ab\sqrt2.
$$
Since $1$ and $\sqrt2$ are linearly independent over $\QQ$, one has
$ab=0$.

If $b=0$, then $a^2=3$, impossible because $3$ is not a square in
$\QQ$. If $a=0$, then $b^2=3/2$, also impossible because $3/2$ is not a
square in $\QQ$. Indeed, a rational square has even valuation at every
prime, whereas $3$ has odd $3$-adic valuation and $3/2$ has odd
$2$-adic valuation.

Thus $x^2-3$ is irreducible over $\QQ(\sqrt2)$, so
$$
[\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\QQ(\sqrt2)]=2.
$$
The tower law now gives degree $2\cdot2=4$ over $\QQ$.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\QQ(\alpha)=\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3).
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $\alpha=\sqrt3+\sqrt2$, one inclusion
$$
\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)
$$
is immediate. Step <1>2 gives the reverse inclusion.
:::

<1>5. The polynomial
$$
f(x)=x^4-10x^2+1
$$
is irreducible over $\QQ$.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>3 and <1>4,
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4.
$$
Therefore the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ has degree $4$.
By step <1>1 it divides the degree-$4$ monic polynomial $f$. Hence it is
equal to $f$, so $f$ is irreducible over $\QQ$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves part (a), and step <1>5 proves part (b).
:::
