::: {.problem}
Recalling that $S^2=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}$, define the equator of $S^2$ to be $\{(x,y,z)\in S^2:z=0\}$, so that the equator is homeomorphic to $S^1$.
Let $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ be disjoint copies of the $2$-sphere $S^2$, let $f$ be a homeomorphism from the equator of $Z_1$ to the equator of $Z_2$, and let $Z$ be the quotient space obtained from $Z_1\sqcup Z_2$ by identifying the equator of $Z_1$ to the equator of $Z_2$ via $f$.
Find a $\Delta$-complex structure on the space $Z$, and compute the simplicial homology groups of $Z$.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. Triangulate the common equator by three vertices and three edges. In each of the four hemispherical disks (north and south in each sphere), add one interior vertex and cone the equatorial triangle to it.
::: {.proof}
The two sphere triangulations agree along the equator after using the given homeomorphism $f$ to identify their equatorial triangulations. Thus the quotient has a simplicial, hence $\Delta$-complex, structure with $7$ vertices, $15$ edges, and $12$ triangles.
:::

<1>2. Let $E$ be the oriented equatorial $1$-cycle, and let $C_1,C_2,C_3,C_4$ be the coherently oriented sums of the three triangles in the four disks. Choose signs so that
$$
\partial C_1=E,\quad \partial C_2=-E,\quad \partial C_3=E,\quad \partial C_4=-E.
$$
::: {.proof}
Inside each triangulated disk the radial edges cancel, leaving its oriented boundary. The two hemispheres of a given sphere induce opposite orientations on their common equator.
:::

<1>3. The group of simplicial $2$-cycles is free of rank $3$.
::: {.proof}
Cancellation on radial edges forces the three triangle coefficients in each disk to be equal, so every $2$-cycle is $r_1C_1+r_2C_2+r_3C_3+r_4C_4$. Its boundary is
$$
(r_1-r_2+r_3-r_4)E.
$$
Thus the cycle condition is one primitive linear relation on $\mathbb Z^4$, and
$$
H_2^\Delta(Z)=\ker\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z^3,
$$
since there are no $3$-simplices.
:::

<1>4. The space $Z$ is simply connected.
::: {.proof}
It is the union of the two copies of $S^2$ along their equators. Applying Seifert--van Kampen to small open neighborhoods of the two spheres, the fundamental group is the pushout of
$$
\pi_1(S^2)=0\longleftarrow\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\pi_1(S^2)=0,
$$
which is the trivial group.
:::

<1>5. Hence $H_1^\Delta(Z)=0$ and $H_0^\Delta(Z)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
Simplicial and singular homology agree for a $\Delta$-complex. Since $Z$ is simply connected, $H_1(Z)$, the abelianization of $\pi_1(Z)$, is zero. The space is connected, so $H_0(Z)\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
H_n^\Delta(Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&n=0,\\
\mathbb Z^3,&n=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>3 and <1>5; the complex has dimension $2$.
:::
