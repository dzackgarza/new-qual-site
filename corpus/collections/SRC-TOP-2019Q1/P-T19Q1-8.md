::: {.problem}
Recalling that $S^2=\{(x,y,z)\in\RR^3:x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}$, define the equator of $S^2$ to be $\{(x,y,z)\in S^2:z=0\}$, so that the equator is homeomorphic to $S^1$.
Let $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ be disjoint copies of the $2$-sphere $S^2$, let $f$ be a homeomorphism from the equator of $Z_1$ to the equator of $Z_2$, and let $Z$ be the quotient space obtained from $Z_1\sqcup Z_2$ by identifying the equator of $Z_1$ to the equator of $Z_2$ via $f$.
Find a $\Delta$-complex structure on the space $Z$, and compute the simplicial homology groups of $Z$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $Z$ has a $\Delta$-complex structure with $7$ vertices, $15$ edges, and $12$ triangles: triangulate the equator of $Z_1$ by three vertices and three edges, transport this triangulation to the equator of $Z_2$ by $f$, and cone the equatorial triangle to one interior vertex in each of the four hemispheres.

::: {.proof}
The two sphere triangulations agree on the identified equator by construction, so the quotient is a simplicial complex with $3+4=7$ vertices, $3+4\cdot3=15$ edges, and $4\cdot3=12$ triangles.
:::

<1>2. $H_2^\Delta(Z)\cong\ZZ^3$.

::: {.proof}
Let $E$ be the oriented equatorial $1$-cycle and $C_1,\ldots,C_4$ the sums of the three triangles in the four hemispheres, oriented so that $\partial C_1=E$, $\partial C_2=-E$, $\partial C_3=E$, $\partial C_4=-E$; within each hemisphere the radial edges cancel.
In a $2$-cycle, cancellation along the radial edges forces the three coefficients in each hemisphere to be equal, so every $2$-cycle is $\sum r_iC_i$, with boundary $(r_1-r_2+r_3-r_4)E$.
The kernel of this primitive linear form on $\ZZ^4$ is free of rank $3$, and there are no $3$-simplices.
:::

<1>3. $\pi_1(Z)=1$.

::: {.proof}
Apply the Seifert--van Kampen theorem to open neighborhoods of $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ in $Z$ that deformation retract onto them and whose intersection deformation retracts onto the equator.
$\pi_1(Z)$ is the pushout of $\pi_1(S^2)=1\leftarrow\pi_1(S^1)\to\pi_1(S^2)=1$, which is trivial.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Simplicial and singular homology agree for a $\Delta$-complex.
$Z$ is connected, so $H_0^\Delta(Z)\cong\ZZ$; by step <1>3, $H_1^\Delta(Z)$, the abelianization of $\pi_1(Z)$, is $0$; and with step <1>2 and no simplices above dimension $2$,
$$H_n^\Delta(Z)\cong\begin{cases}\ZZ,&n=0,\\\ZZ^3,&n=2,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$
:::
