::: {.problem}
Let $X=\RR\times\RR$ with the product topology $\mathcal T$, giving $\RR$ the usual metric topology, and let $p:(X,\mathcal T)\to(\RR,\text{usual})$ be the projection on the first coordinate, $p(x,y)=x$.
Let $A=\{(x,y)\in X:x\ge0\text{ or }y=0\}$, with the subspace topology that it inherits from $X$.
Show that $f=p|_A:A\to\RR$ is a quotient map, but that $f$ is neither an open map nor a closed map.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #f-continuous-surjective}
$f$ is continuous and surjective.

::: pf-proof
$f$ is a restriction of the continuous projection $p$, and $(x,0)\in A$ maps to $x$ for every $x\in\RR$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #preimage-open-criterion}
If $V\subseteq\RR$ and $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $A$, then $V$ is open in $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
Fix $x\in V$.
Since $(x,0)\in f^{-1}(V)$, there is an open rectangle $R=(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)\times(-\eta,\eta)$ with $R\cap A\subseteq f^{-1}(V)$.
For every $t\in(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)$, $(t,0)\in R\cap A$, so $t=f(t,0)\in V$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-not-open}
$f$ is not open.

::: pf-proof
$O=A\cap(\RR\times(1,2))=[0,\infty)\times(1,2)$ is open in $A$, but $f(O)=[0,\infty)$ is not open in $\RR$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-not-closed}
$f$ is not closed.

::: pf-proof
$C=\{(x,y)\in\RR^2:xy=1\}$ is closed in $\RR^2$ as the preimage of $\{1\}$ under $(x,y)\mapsto xy$.
No point of $C$ has $y=0$, so $C\cap A=\{(x,1/x):x>0\}$, which is closed in $A$.
But $f(C\cap A)=(0,\infty)$ is not closed in $\RR$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
By steps [](#f-continuous-surjective){.pf-ref} and [](#preimage-open-criterion){.pf-ref}, $f$ is a quotient map, and by steps [](#f-not-open){.pf-ref} and [](#f-not-closed){.pf-ref} it is neither open nor closed.
:::
