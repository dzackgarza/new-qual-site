::: {.problem}
Let $X=\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ with the product topology $\mathcal T$, giving $\mathbb R$ the usual metric topology, and let $p:(X,\mathcal T)\to(\mathbb R,\text{usual})$ be the projection on the first coordinate, $p(x,y)=x$.
Let $A=\{(x,y)\in X:x\ge0\text{ or }y=0\}$, with the subspace topology that it inherits from $X$.
Show that $f=p|_A:A\to\mathbb R$ is a quotient map, but that $f$ is neither an open map nor a closed map.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. The map $f:A\to\mathbb R$ is continuous and surjective.
::: {.proof}
It is the restriction of the continuous projection $p$. For every $x\in\mathbb R$, the point $(x,0)$ lies in $A$ and maps to $x$.
:::

<1>2. Suppose $V\subseteq\mathbb R$ and $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $A$. Then every $x\in V$ has an open interval contained in $V$.
::: {.proof}
Fix $x\in V$. Since $(x,0)\in f^{-1}(V)$ and this set is open in $A$, there is an open rectangle $R=(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)\times(-\eta,\eta)$ in $\mathbb R^2$ such that
$$
(x,0)\in R\cap A\subseteq f^{-1}(V).
$$
For every $t\in(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)$, the point $(t,0)$ belongs to $A\cap R$, hence $t=f(t,0)\in V$.
:::

<1>3. Therefore $f$ is a quotient map.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $f$ is continuous and surjective. By <1>2, whenever $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $A$, the set $V$ is open in $\mathbb R$. This is the quotient-map criterion.
:::

<1>4. The map $f$ is not open.
::: {.proof}
The set
$$
O=A\cap(\mathbb R\times(1,2))=[0,\infty)\times(1,2)
$$
is open in $A$, but $f(O)=[0,\infty)$ is not open in $\mathbb R$.
:::

<1>5. The map $f$ is not closed.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
C=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:xy=1\}.
$$
This is closed in $\mathbb R^2$ as the inverse image of $\{1\}$ under the continuous map $(x,y)\mapsto xy$, and every point of $C$ has $x\ne0$. Its positive branch
$$
C_+=C\cap A=\{(x,1/x):x>0\}
$$
is closed in $A$ because $C$ is closed in $\mathbb R^2$. But $f(C_+)=(0,\infty)$ is not closed in $\mathbb R$.
:::
:::
