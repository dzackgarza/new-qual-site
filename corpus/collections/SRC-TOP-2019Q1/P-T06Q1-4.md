::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal T,\mathcal T'$ be two topologies on $X$.
Show that if $(X,\mathcal T)$ is compact and Hausdorff, $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T'$, and $\mathcal T\ne\mathcal T'$, then $(X,\mathcal T')$ is Hausdorff but not compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The space $(X,\mathcal T')$ is Hausdorff.
::: {.proof}
Let $x,y\in X$ with $x\neq y$.
Because $(X,\mathcal T)$ is Hausdorff, there are disjoint sets $U,V\in\mathcal T$ with
\[
x\in U,
\qquad
y\in V.
\]
Since
\[
\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T',
\]
the same $U$ and $V$ are $\mathcal T'$-open.
Thus they separate $x$ and $y$ in $(X,\mathcal T')$.
:::

<1>2. The identity map
\[
i:(X,\mathcal T')\longrightarrow(X,\mathcal T),
\qquad
i(x)=x,
\]
is a continuous bijection.
::: {.proof}
It is plainly bijective.
If $U\in\mathcal T$, then
\[
i^{-1}(U)=U.
\]
The inclusion $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T'$ makes this preimage $\mathcal T'$-open.
Hence $i$ is continuous.
:::

<1>3. If $(X,\mathcal T')$ were compact, then $i$ would be a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Assume $(X,\mathcal T')$ is compact.
The codomain $(X,\mathcal T)$ is Hausdorff by hypothesis, and <1>2 gives a continuous bijection from the compact domain to this Hausdorff codomain.

For completeness, let $F$ be closed in $(X,\mathcal T')$.
Then $F$ is compact because it is closed in a compact space.
Its image
\[
i(F)=F
\]
is compact in $(X,\mathcal T)$ because continuous images of compact spaces are compact.
Compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed, so $F$ is $\mathcal T$-closed.
Therefore $i$ is a closed bijection, hence $i^{-1}$ is continuous.
Thus $i$ is a homeomorphism.
:::

<1>4. The space $(X,\mathcal T')$ is not compact.
::: {.proof}
If it were compact, <1>3 would make the identity map $i$ a homeomorphism.
Continuity of its inverse
\[
i^{-1}:(X,\mathcal T)\longrightarrow(X,\mathcal T')
\]
would imply that every $\mathcal T'$-open set is $\mathcal T$-open, so
\[
\mathcal T'\subseteq\mathcal T.
\]
Together with the assumed inclusion $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T'$, this would give
\[
\mathcal T=\mathcal T',
\]
contrary to hypothesis.
Therefore $(X,\mathcal T')$ cannot be compact.
:::

<1>5. Hence $(X,\mathcal T')$ is Hausdorff but not compact.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>1 and <1>4.
:::
:::
