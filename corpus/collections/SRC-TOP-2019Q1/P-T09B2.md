::: {.problem}
Let $p:\widetilde X\to X$ be a covering space.
Show that if $X$ is Hausdorff, then $\widetilde X$ is also Hausdorff.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\widetilde x,\widetilde y\in\widetilde X$ be distinct.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #case-different-images}
If $p(\widetilde x)\neq p(\widetilde y)$, then $\widetilde x$ and $\widetilde y$ have disjoint open neighborhoods.

::: pf-proof
Since $X$ is Hausdorff, there are disjoint open $U\ni p(\widetilde x)$ and $V\ni p(\widetilde y)$.
Then $p^{-1}(U)$ and $p^{-1}(V)$ are disjoint open neighborhoods of $\widetilde x$ and $\widetilde y$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #case-same-image}
If $p(\widetilde x)=p(\widetilde y)=x$, then $\widetilde x$ and $\widetilde y$ have disjoint open neighborhoods.

::: pf-proof
Choose an evenly covered open neighborhood $U$ of $x$, with $p^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{\alpha\in A}V_\alpha$ and each $p|_{V_\alpha}\colon V_\alpha\to U$ a homeomorphism.
Let $\widetilde x\in V_\alpha$ and $\widetilde y\in V_\beta$.
If $\alpha=\beta$, injectivity of $p|_{V_\alpha}$ would give $\widetilde x=\widetilde y$; so $\alpha\neq\beta$, and $V_\alpha$, $V_\beta$ are disjoint open neighborhoods.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Steps [](#case-different-images){.pf-ref} and [](#case-same-image){.pf-ref} cover both cases, so $\widetilde X$ is Hausdorff.
:::
