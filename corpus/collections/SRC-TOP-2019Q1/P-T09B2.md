::: {.problem}
Let $p:\widetilde X\to X$ be a covering space.
Show that if $X$ is Hausdorff, then $\widetilde X$ is also Hausdorff.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\widetilde x,\widetilde y\in\widetilde X$ be distinct.
We show that they have disjoint open neighborhoods.

<1>1. If
\[
p(\widetilde x)\ne p(\widetilde y),
\]
then $\widetilde x$ and $\widetilde y$ have disjoint open neighborhoods in $\widetilde X$.
::: {.proof}
Since $X$ is Hausdorff, there are disjoint open sets $U,V\subseteq X$ such that
\[
p(\widetilde x)\in U,
\qquad
p(\widetilde y)\in V.
\]
The sets
\[
p^{-1}(U)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p^{-1}(V)
\]
are open in $\widetilde X$ by continuity of the covering map $p$, they contain $\widetilde x$ and $\widetilde y$ respectively, and they are disjoint because $U\cap V=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>2. If
\[
p(\widetilde x)=p(\widetilde y),
\]
then $\widetilde x$ and $\widetilde y$ have disjoint open neighborhoods in $\widetilde X$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
x=p(\widetilde x)=p(\widetilde y).
\]
Choose an evenly covered open neighborhood $U$ of $x$.
Thus
\[
p^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{\alpha\in A}V_\alpha,
\]
where the $V_\alpha$ are pairwise disjoint open subsets of $\widetilde X$ and every restriction
\[
p|_{V_\alpha}:V_\alpha\longrightarrow U
\]
is a homeomorphism.

There are indices $\alpha,\beta\in A$ with
\[
\widetilde x\in V_\alpha,
\qquad
\widetilde y\in V_\beta.
\]
If $\alpha=\beta$, then injectivity of the homeomorphism $p|_{V_\alpha}$ and the equality
\[
p(\widetilde x)=p(\widetilde y)
\]
would imply $\widetilde x=\widetilde y$, contrary to our choice of the two points.
Hence $\alpha\ne\beta$.
Therefore $V_\alpha$ and $V_\beta$ are disjoint open neighborhoods of $\widetilde x$ and $\widetilde y$.
:::

<1>3. The space $\widetilde X$ is Hausdorff.
::: {.proof}
For any two distinct points of $\widetilde X$, either their images under $p$ are distinct or their images are equal.
The first case is handled by <1>1 and the second by <1>2.
Thus every pair of distinct points of $\widetilde X$ has disjoint open neighborhoods, which is exactly the Hausdorff property.
:::
