::: {.problem}
Let $U=\{(x,y,0)\in\mathbb R^3:x^2+y^2=1\}$, and let $W=\mathbb R^3-U$.
Let $V=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:x^2+y^2+z^2=4\}\cup\{(0,0,z)\in\mathbb R^3:-2\le z\le2\}$.

(a) Describe a deformation retraction of $W$ onto $V$.

(b) Use (a) and the Seifert--van Kampen Theorem to compute $\pi_1(W)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Write points of $\mathbb R^3$ in cylindrical coordinates $(r,\theta,z)$, with $r\ge0$, and let
$$
H=\{(r,z):r\ge0\},\qquad p=(1,0).
$$
Then removing the circle $U$ corresponds in the orbit half-plane to removing the single point $p$.
::: {.proof}
The rotation group about the $z$-axis preserves both $U$ and $V$. A rotation orbit is determined by $(r,z)=(\sqrt{x^2+y^2},z)$. The deleted circle is exactly the orbit with $(r,z)=(1,0)$.
:::

<1>2. Let
$$
D=\{(r,z)\in H:r^2+z^2\le4\}.
$$
Its boundary in the half-plane is
$$
\partial_H D=\{r^2+z^2=4,\ r\ge0\}\cup\{r=0,\ |z|\le2\}.
$$
::: {.proof}
This is the closed half-disk of radius $2$. Its relative boundary consists of the semicircle together with the diameter on the $z$-axis. The deleted point $p=(1,0)$ lies in the relative interior of $D$.
:::

<1>3. There is a strong deformation retraction of $H\setminus\{p\}$ onto $D\setminus\{p\}$ which preserves the $z$-axis.
::: {.proof}
For $u=(r,z)\in H$, put $\|u\|=\sqrt{r^2+z^2}$ and define
$$
q(u)=
\begin{cases}
u,&\|u\|\le2,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle \frac{2}{\|u\|}u,&\|u\|>2.
\end{cases}
$$
Then
$$
Q_s(u)=(1-s)u+s q(u)
$$
is a strong deformation retraction of $H$ onto $D$. It preserves each ray from the origin, hence preserves the $z$-axis. It also preserves $H\setminus\{p\}$: points already in $D$ are fixed, while a point outside $D$ stays on a ray at radius at least $2$, so it cannot pass through $p$, whose radius is $1$.
:::

<1>4. There is a strong deformation retraction of $D\setminus\{p\}$ onto $\partial_H D$ which fixes the diameter $\{r=0,|z|\le2\}$ pointwise.
::: {.proof}
Because $D$ is compact and convex and $p$ is an interior point, every ray starting at $p$ meets $\partial_H D$ in exactly one point. Let $\rho(u)$ denote this point. The radial projection $\rho:D\setminus\{p\}\to\partial_HD$ is continuous, and
$$
R_s(u)=(1-s)u+s\rho(u)
$$
stays on the same ray from $p$, hence never passes through $p$. If $u\in\partial_HD$, then $\rho(u)=u$, so the boundary is fixed pointwise. In particular, every point of the diameter is fixed.
:::

<1>5. The two homotopies lift rotationally to a strong deformation retraction of $W$ onto $V$.
::: {.proof}
The first homotopy $Q_s$ preserves rays from the origin in the $(r,z)$-half-plane, so it lifts by keeping the angular coordinate $\theta$ fixed; on the axis this is unambiguous because the axis remains on the axis. After the first stage, all points lie over $D\setminus\{p\}$. For the second stage, if $r>0$ keep $\theta$ fixed while applying $R_s$ to $(r,z)$. Points with $r=0$ lie on the diameter of $D$ and are fixed by <1>4, so there is again no angular ambiguity. Thus the lifted homotopy is continuous and rotation-equivariant. At the end its orbit-space image is $\partial_HD$, whose rotation is exactly
$$
\{x^2+y^2+z^2=4\}\cup\{(0,0,z):-2\le z\le2\}=V.
$$
Both stages fix $V$ pointwise.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $W$ strongly deformation-retracts onto $V$.
::: {.proof}
Concatenate the lifted homotopies from <1>3 and <1>4 as described in <1>5.
:::

<1>7. The fundamental group of $V$ is infinite cyclic.
::: {.proof}
Let $S$ be the sphere of radius $2$ and $I$ the polar segment, so $V=S\cup I$, with $S\cap I$ equal to the two poles. Take small open thickenings $A$ of $S$ and $B$ of $I$ in $V$ so that $A$ deformation-retracts onto $S$, $B$ deformation-retracts onto $I$, and $A\cap B$ has exactly two contractible path-components, one near each pole. Joining the two components by a path in $A$ and applying the usual groupoid form of Seifert--van Kampen adds one free generator to $\pi_1(S)=1$. Equivalently, collapsing a path in $S$ joining the poles gives the homotopy equivalence
$$
V\simeq S^2\vee S^1.
$$
Hence $\pi_1(V)\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>8. Consequently
$$
\pi_1(W)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
::: {.proof}
The strong deformation retraction in <1>6 induces an isomorphism $\pi_1(W)\cong\pi_1(V)$, and <1>7 identifies the latter group with $\mathbb Z$.
:::
