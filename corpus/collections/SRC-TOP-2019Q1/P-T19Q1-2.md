::: {.problem}
Let $(X,\mathcal T)$ be a path-connected space and $\mathcal C=\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in I}$ an open covering of $X$.
Show that for every $x,y\in X$ there is an $n\in\mathbb N$ and a sequence of sets $U_{\alpha_1},\ldots,U_{\alpha_n}$ in $\mathcal C$ with $x\in U_{\alpha_1}$, $y\in U_{\alpha_n}$ and $U_{\alpha_{i-1}}\cap U_{\alpha_i}\ne\varnothing$ for all $i=2,\ldots,n$.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. Choose a path $\gamma:[0,1]\to X$ with $\gamma(0)=x$ and $\gamma(1)=y$.
::: {.proof}
Such a path exists because $X$ is path-connected.
:::

<1>2. The family $\{\gamma^{-1}(U_\alpha)\}_{\alpha\in I}$ is an open cover of $[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
Each inverse image is open by continuity of $\gamma$, and the family covers because $\mathcal C$ covers $X$.
:::

<1>3. There is a Lebesgue number $\delta>0$ for this cover.
::: {.proof}
The interval $[0,1]$ is compact metric, so the Lebesgue number lemma applies to the open cover in <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Choose a partition $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=1$ with $t_i-t_{i-1}<\delta$ for every $i$.
::: {.proof}
For example, take a sufficiently fine equal subdivision.
:::

<1>5. For each $i$, choose $U_{\alpha_i}\in\mathcal C$ such that
$$
\gamma([t_{i-1},t_i])\subseteq U_{\alpha_i}.
$$
::: {.proof}
The interval $[t_{i-1},t_i]$ has diameter less than the Lebesgue number, hence it lies in some $\gamma^{-1}(U_{\alpha_i})$.
:::

<1>6. The sequence $U_{\alpha_1},\ldots,U_{\alpha_n}$ has the required properties.
::: {.proof}
We have $x=\gamma(t_0)\in U_{\alpha_1}$ and $y=\gamma(t_n)\in U_{\alpha_n}$. For $2\le i\le n$, the point $\gamma(t_{i-1})$ belongs to both $U_{\alpha_{i-1}}$ and $U_{\alpha_i}$, so their intersection is nonempty.
:::
:::
