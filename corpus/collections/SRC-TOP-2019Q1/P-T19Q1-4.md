::: {.problem}
Show that if $f,g:(X,\mathcal T)\to(Y,\mathcal T')$ and $h,k:(Y,\mathcal T')\to(Z,\mathcal T'')$ are continuous and $f\simeq g$ and $h\simeq k$, then $h\circ f\simeq k\circ g$.
Show, however, that the converse ($h\circ f\simeq k\circ g$ implies $f\simeq g$ and $h\simeq k$) need not be true.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $F:X\times I\to Y$ be a homotopy from $f$ to $g$.
::: {.proof}
This exists because $f\simeq g$.
:::

<1>2. The formula $(x,t)\mapsto h(F(x,t))$ is a homotopy from $h\circ f$ to $h\circ g$.
::: {.proof}
At $t=0$ it equals $h(f(x))$, and at $t=1$ it equals $h(g(x))$.
:::

<1>3. Let $K:Y\times I\to Z$ be a homotopy from $h$ to $k$. Then $(x,t)\mapsto K(g(x),t)$ is a homotopy from $h\circ g$ to $k\circ g$.
::: {.proof}
This is the homotopy $K$ precomposed with $g\times\operatorname{id}_I$.
:::

<1>4. Hence $h\circ f\simeq k\circ g$.
::: {.proof}
Concatenate the homotopies in <1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. The converse fails.
::: {.proof}
Take $X=Y=S^1$, let $f=\operatorname{id}_{S^1}$ and let $g$ be constant. Take $Z$ to be a one-point space and let $h=k:Y\to Z$ be the unique map. Then $h\circ f=k\circ g$ exactly, hence the composites are homotopic. But $f\not\simeq g$: the induced maps on $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z$ are respectively the identity and the zero homomorphism, and homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism up to the standard basepoint change, which here cannot turn the identity into zero.
:::
:::
