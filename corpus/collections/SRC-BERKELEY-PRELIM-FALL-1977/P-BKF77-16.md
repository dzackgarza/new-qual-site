::: {.problem}
Let $E,F$ be vector spaces and $S:E\to F$ linear.

(a) Prove $S(E)$ is a vector space.

(b) Show $\ker S=\{0\}$ if and only if $S$ is injective.

(c) If $S$ is injective, prove $S^{-1}:S(E)\to E$ is linear.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The image $S(E)$ is a vector subspace of $F$.
::: {.proof}
Because $S$ is linear,
$$
S(0)=0,
$$
so $0\in S(E)$. If $u,v\in S(E)$, write
$$
u=S(x),
\qquad
v=S(y)
$$
for some $x,y\in E$. For scalars $a,b$,
$$
au+bv
=aS(x)+bS(y)
=S(ax+by),
$$
which belongs to $S(E)$. Thus $S(E)$ is a vector subspace of $F$.
:::

<1>2. The kernel is trivial exactly when $S$ is injective.
::: {.proof}
Suppose first that $S$ is injective. If $x\in\ker S$, then
$$
S(x)=0=S(0),
$$
so injectivity gives $x=0$. Hence
$$
\ker S=\{0\}.
$$

Conversely suppose $\ker S=\{0\}$ and
$$
S(x)=S(y).
$$
Then
$$
S(x-y)=S(x)-S(y)=0,
$$
so $x-y\in\ker S$. Therefore $x-y=0$, hence $x=y$. Thus $S$ is injective.
:::

<1>3. If $S$ is injective, its inverse on the image is linear.
::: {.proof}
Assume $S$ is injective. Then every $u\in S(E)$ has a unique preimage, so
$$
S^{-1}:S(E)\to E
$$
is well-defined.

Take $u,v\in S(E)$ and write
$$
u=S(x),
\qquad
v=S(y).
$$
For scalars $a,b$,
$$
au+bv
=S(ax+by).
$$
Applying $S^{-1}$ gives
$$
S^{-1}(au+bv)
=ax+by
=aS^{-1}(u)+bS^{-1}(v).
$$
Hence $S^{-1}$ is linear.
:::
