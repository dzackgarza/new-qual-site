::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the set of real $3\times3$ upper triangular matrices with ones on the diagonal.

(a) Prove $G$ is a group under multiplication.

(b) Determine the center of $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
g(a,b,c)
=\begin{pmatrix}
1&a&b\\
0&1&c\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,b,c\in\mathbb R.
$$

<1>1. Verify that $G$ is a group.
::: {.proof}
Direct multiplication gives
$$
g(a,b,c)g(x,y,z)
=g(a+x,\ b+y+az,\ c+z).
$$
Thus $G$ is closed under multiplication. The identity is
$$
g(0,0,0)=I.
$$
Associativity is inherited from matrix multiplication.

To find the inverse, solve
$$
g(a,b,c)g(x,y,z)=I.
$$
The first and third coordinates force
$$
x=-a,
\qquad
z=-c,
$$
and the middle coordinate then gives
$$
b+y-ac=0,
$$
so
$$
y=ac-b.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{g(a,b,c)^{-1}=g(-a,ac-b,-c).}
$$
Hence $G$ is a group under multiplication.
:::

<1>2. Compute the center.
::: {.proof}
Let $g(a,b,c)$ commute with every $g(x,y,z)\in G$. From the product formula,
$$
g(a,b,c)g(x,y,z)
=g(a+x,b+y+az,c+z),
$$
while
$$
g(x,y,z)g(a,b,c)
=g(a+x,b+y+xc,c+z).
$$
Thus commutation for all $x,z\in\mathbb R$ is equivalent to
$$
az=xc
$$
for all $x,z$. Setting $x=0,z=1$ gives $a=0$, and setting $x=1,z=0$
gives $c=0$. There is no restriction on $b$.

Conversely, if $a=c=0$, the two product formulas agree for every
$g(x,y,z)$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
Z(G)=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&b\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
:b\in\mathbb R
\right\}.}
$$
:::
