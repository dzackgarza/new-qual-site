::: problem
Let $f_n:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be differentiable with $|f_n'(x)|\le1$ for all $n,x$. Assume $f_n(x)\to g(x)$ for every $x$. Prove that $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ is continuous.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Every $f_n$ is $1$-Lipschitz.
::: proof
Fix $x,y\in\mathbb R$. If $x=y$, the assertion is trivial. Otherwise, by
the mean value theorem there is a point $\xi$ between $x$ and $y$ such that
$$
f_n(x)-f_n(y)=f_n'(\xi)(x-y).
$$
Hence
$$
|f_n(x)-f_n(y)|
\le |x-y|
$$
for every $n$.
:::

<1>2. Pass the Lipschitz inequality to the pointwise limit.
::: proof
For fixed $x,y$, pointwise convergence gives
$$
f_n(x)-f_n(y)\longrightarrow g(x)-g(y).
$$
Taking limits in the inequality from step <1>1 yields
$$
|g(x)-g(y)|\le |x-y|.
$$
Thus $g$ itself is $1$-Lipschitz.
:::

<1>3. Conclude continuity.
::: proof
Given $\varepsilon>0$, if
$$
|x-y|<\varepsilon,
$$
then the Lipschitz estimate gives
$$
|g(x)-g(y)|\le |x-y|<\varepsilon.
$$
Therefore $g$ is continuous on $\mathbb R$.
:::
:::
