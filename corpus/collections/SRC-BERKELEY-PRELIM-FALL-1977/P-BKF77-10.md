::: {.problem}
Let $f_n:\RR\to\RR$ be differentiable with $|f_n'(x)|\le1$ for all $n,x$. Assume $f_n(x)\to g(x)$ for every $x$. Prove that $g:\RR\to\RR$ is continuous.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Every $f_n$ is $1$-Lipschitz.

::: pf-proof

Fix $x,y\in\RR$. If $x=y$, the assertion is trivial. Otherwise, by
the mean value theorem there is a point $\xi$ between $x$ and $y$ such that
$$
f_n(x)-f_n(y)=f_n'(\xi)(x-y).
$$
Hence
$$
|f_n(x)-f_n(y)|
\le |x-y|
$$
for every $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Pass the Lipschitz inequality to the pointwise limit.

::: pf-proof

For fixed $x,y$, pointwise convergence gives
$$
f_n(x)-f_n(y)\longrightarrow g(x)-g(y).
$$
Taking limits in the inequality from step [](#s1){.pf-ref} yields
$$
|g(x)-g(y)|\le |x-y|.
$$
Thus $g$ itself is $1$-Lipschitz.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Conclude continuity.

::: pf-proof

Given $\varepsilon>0$, if
$$
|x-y|<\varepsilon,
$$
then the Lipschitz estimate gives
$$
|g(x)-g(y)|\le |x-y|<\varepsilon.
$$
Therefore $g$ is continuous on $\RR$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves that $g$ is $1$-Lipschitz, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} converts this
estimate into continuity.

:::
