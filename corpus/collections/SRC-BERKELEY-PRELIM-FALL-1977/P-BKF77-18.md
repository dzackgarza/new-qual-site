::: {.problem}
Suppose the nonzero complex number $\alpha$ is a root of a polynomial of degree $n$ with rational coefficients. Prove that $1/\alpha$ is also a root of a polynomial of degree $n$ with rational coefficients.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
p(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_nx^n
\in\mathbb Q[x],
\qquad a_n\ne0,
$$
be a polynomial of degree $n$ with
$$
p(\alpha)=0.
$$
Since $\alpha\ne0$, its reciprocal is defined.

<1>1. Reverse the coefficients.
::: {.proof}
Define
$$
q(x)=x^np(1/x)
=a_0x^n+a_1x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_n.
$$
This is a nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients. Evaluating at
$1/\alpha$ gives
$$
q(1/\alpha)
=\alpha^{-n}p(\alpha)
=0.
$$
Thus $1/\alpha$ is algebraic over $\mathbb Q$ and is a root of a rational
polynomial of degree at most $n$.
:::

<1>2. Ensure that the resulting polynomial has degree exactly $n$.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
d=\deg q\le n.
$$
If $d=n$, there is nothing to prove. If $d<n$, define
$$
r(x)=x^{n-d}q(x).
$$
Then $r\in\mathbb Q[x]$ and
$$
\deg r=n.
$$
Moreover
$$
r(1/\alpha)
=\left(\frac1\alpha\right)^{n-d}q(1/\alpha)
=0.
$$
Therefore $1/\alpha$ is a root of a polynomial of degree exactly $n$ with
rational coefficients.
:::
:::
