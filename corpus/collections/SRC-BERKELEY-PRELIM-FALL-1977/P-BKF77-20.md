::: {.problem}
Define
\[
F(u,v,w)=(u+v+w,\ uv+vw+wu,\ uvw)
\]
from $\mathbb C^3$ to $\mathbb C^3$. Prove that $F$ is onto but not one-to-one.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Prove that $F$ is onto.
::: {.proof}
Take an arbitrary point
$$
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb C^3.
$$
Consider the monic cubic
$$
p(t)=t^3-at^2+bt-c.
$$
By the fundamental theorem of algebra, $p$ factors over $\mathbb C$ as
$$
p(t)=(t-u)(t-v)(t-w)
$$
for some $u,v,w\in\mathbb C$, counted with multiplicity. Expanding,
$$
(t-u)(t-v)(t-w)
=t^3-(u+v+w)t^2+(uv+uw+vw)t-uvw.
$$
Comparing coefficients with $p(t)$ gives
$$
u+v+w=a,
$$
$$
uv+uw+vw=b,
$$
and
$$
uvw=c.
$$
Thus
$$
F(u,v,w)=(a,b,c).
$$
Since $(a,b,c)$ was arbitrary, $F$ is onto.
:::

<1>2. Prove that $F$ is not one-to-one.
::: {.proof}
Each component of $F(u,v,w)$ is symmetric in $u,v,w$, so permuting the
three coordinates does not change the value of $F$. For example,
$$
(1,2,3)\ne(2,1,3),
$$
but
$$
F(1,2,3)=F(2,1,3).
$$
Hence $F$ is not injective.
:::
:::
