::: {.problem}
Let $f:\RR^n\to\RR$ have continuous partial derivatives satisfying
$$
\abs{\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}(x)}\le K
$$
for every $x$ and $j$. Prove that
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le\sqrt n\,K\norm{x-y}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $x,y\in\RR^n$ and define
$$
\gamma(t)=y+t(x-y),
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
$$
Set
$$
h(t)=f(\gamma(t)).
$$
Since the partial derivatives of $f$ are continuous, the chain rule gives
$$
h'(t)
=\nabla f(\gamma(t))\cdot(x-y).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Bound the gradient uniformly.

::: pf-proof

For every $z\in\RR^n$,
$$
\norm{\nabla f(z)}^2
=\sum_{j=1}^n\abs{\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}(z)}^2
\le nK^2.
$$
Hence
$$
\norm{\nabla f(z)}\le\sqrt n\,K.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Integrate along the segment.

::: pf-proof

By Cauchy--Schwarz and step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{h'(t)}
\le \norm{\nabla f(\gamma(t))}\,\norm{x-y}
\le \sqrt n\,K\norm{x-y}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}
&=\abs{h(1)-h(0)}\\
&=\abs{\int_0^1h'(t)\,dt}\\
&\le\int_0^1\abs{h'(t)}\,dt\\
&\le\sqrt n\,K\norm{x-y}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le\sqrt n\,K\norm{x-y}.}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves the required global Lipschitz estimate.
