::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ have continuous partial derivatives satisfying
\[
\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}(x)\right|\le K
\]
for every $x$ and $j$. Prove that
\[
|f(x)-f(y)|\le\sqrt n\,K\|x-y\|.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $x,y\in\mathbb R^n$ and define
$$
\gamma(t)=y+t(x-y),
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
$$
Set
$$
h(t)=f(\gamma(t)).
$$
Since the partial derivatives of $f$ are continuous, the chain rule gives
$$
h'(t)
=\nabla f(\gamma(t))\cdot(x-y).
$$

<1>1. Bound the gradient uniformly.
::: {.proof}
For every $z\in\mathbb R^n$,
$$
\|\nabla f(z)\|^2
=\sum_{j=1}^n\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}(z)\right|^2
\le nK^2.
$$
Hence
$$
\|\nabla f(z)\|\le\sqrt n\,K.
$$
:::

<1>2. Integrate along the segment.
::: {.proof}
By Cauchy--Schwarz and step <1>1,
$$
|h'(t)|
\le \|\nabla f(\gamma(t))\|\,\|x-y\|
\le \sqrt n\,K\|x-y\|.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
|f(x)-f(y)|
&=|h(1)-h(0)|\\
&=\left|\int_0^1h'(t)\,dt\right|\\
&\le\int_0^1|h'(t)|\,dt\\
&\le\sqrt n\,K\|x-y\|.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{|f(x)-f(y)|\le\sqrt n\,K\|x-y\|.}
$$
:::
:::
