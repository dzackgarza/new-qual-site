::: problem
For nonzero complex $a$, define $a^b$ as the set of complex numbers $c$ for which $c=e^{b\alpha}$ for some logarithm $\alpha$ of $a$. Describe the set $a^b$ when $a=1$ and $b=1/3+i$.
:::

::: solution
The logarithms of $1$ are exactly
$$
\alpha_k=2\pi i k,
\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
$$
Therefore every value in $1^{1/3+i}$ has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\exp\left(\left(\frac13+i\right)2\pi i k\right)
&=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i k}{3}-2\pi k\right)\\
&=e^{-2\pi k}e^{2\pi i k/3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Conversely, each integer $k$ gives a logarithm $2\pi i k$ of $1$, so every
number in this list occurs. Hence
$$
\boxed{
1^{1/3+i}
=\left\{e^{-2\pi k}e^{2\pi i k/3}:k\in\mathbb Z\right\}.}
$$

Since the angular factor cycles with period $3$, the values lie on the three
rays of arguments
$$
0,\qquad \frac{2\pi}{3},\qquad \frac{4\pi}{3},
$$
with moduli forming the geometric progression $e^{-2\pi k}$.
:::
