::: problem
Show that the differential equation
\[
x'=3x^2
\]
has no solution with $x(0)=1$ that is defined for all real $t$.
:::

::: solution
Suppose, for contradiction, that a solution $x:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ with
$x(0)=1$ is defined for all real $t$.

<1>1. The solution stays positive for $t\ge0$.
::: proof
The differential equation gives
$$
x'(t)=3x(t)^2\ge0.
$$
Thus $x$ is nondecreasing. Since $x(0)=1$,
$$
x(t)\ge1
$$
for every $t\ge0$. In particular $x(t)\ne0$ on $[0,\infty)$.
:::

<1>2. Differentiate the reciprocal.
::: proof
Because $x(t)$ is nonzero for $t\ge0$, the function $1/x(t)$ is
differentiable there, and
$$
\left(\frac1{x(t)}\right)'
=-\frac{x'(t)}{x(t)^2}
=-3.
$$
Integrating from $0$ to $t$ gives
$$
\frac1{x(t)}-\frac1{x(0)}=-3t.
$$
Since $x(0)=1$,
$$
\frac1{x(t)}=1-3t.
$$
:::

<1>3. Obtain the contradiction at finite time.
::: proof
Set
$$
t=\frac13.
$$
Step <1>2 gives
$$
\frac1{x(1/3)}=0.
$$
But $x(1/3)$ is a finite real number because the assumed solution is defined
there, and the reciprocal of a nonzero finite real number cannot be zero.
This contradiction proves that no such solution can be defined for all real
$t$.

Equivalently, the local solution is
$$
x(t)=\frac1{1-3t},
$$
which blows up at $t=1/3$.
:::
:::
