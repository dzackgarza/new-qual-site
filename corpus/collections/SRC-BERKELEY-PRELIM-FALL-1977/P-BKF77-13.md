::: {.problem}
Consider the transformations
$$
z\mapsto z+b,\qquad z\mapsto1/z,\qquad z\mapsto kz\ (k\ne0),
$$
and
$$
z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\qquad(ad-bc\ne0).
$$
Show that each sends circles to circles or straight lines.
:::

::: {.solution}
A circle or straight line in the complex plane can be written in the form
$$
A\abs{z}^2+Bz+\overline B\,\overline z+C=0,
$$
where $A,C\in\RR$, $B\in\CC$, and not all coefficients vanish.
When $A\ne0$ this is a circle; when $A=0$ it is a straight line.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Translations preserve generalized circles.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
w=z+b,
\qquad z=w-b.
$$
Substituting $w-b$ for $z$ in the displayed equation and expanding again
produces an equation
$$
A'\abs{w}^2+B'w+\overline{B'}\,\overline w+C'=0
$$
with $A',C'\in\RR$. Thus the image is again a circle or line.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Nonzero complex scalings preserve generalized circles.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
w=kz,
\qquad k\ne0,
$$
so $z=w/k$. Substitution gives
$$
\frac{A}{\abs{k}^2}\abs{w}^2+\frac{B}{k}w
+\frac{\overline B}{\overline k}\overline w+C=0,
$$
again an equation of the same type. Hence $z\mapsto kz$ sends circles and
lines to circles or lines.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Inversion preserves generalized circles.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
w=\frac1z,
\qquad z=\frac1w.
$$
Away from the point at infinity, substituting $z=1/w$ gives
$$
\frac{A}{\abs{w}^2}+\frac{B}{w}
+\frac{\overline B}{\overline w}+C=0.
$$
Multiplying by $\abs{w}^2$ yields
$$
C\abs{w}^2+\overline B\,w+B\overline w+A=0.
$$
This is again the equation of a circle or line. In particular, a circle
through $0$ becomes a line, while a circle not through $0$ remains a circle;
the same generalized-circle statement also includes lines.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Every Möbius transformation is a composition of the preceding maps.

::: pf-proof

Consider
$$
M(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},
\qquad ad-bc\ne0.
$$
If $c=0$, then $d\ne0$ and
$$
M(z)=\frac ad z+\frac bd,
$$
which is a nonzero scaling followed by a translation.

If $c\ne0$, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
M(z)
&=\frac ac-\frac{ad-bc}{c(cz+d)}\\
&=\frac ac-\frac{ad-bc}{c^2}\,
\frac1{z+d/c}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $M$ is the composition of a translation, inversion, nonzero scaling,
and another translation. By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref}, [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}, each factor sends generalized
circles to generalized circles, so $M$ does as well.

Therefore every transformation listed in the problem sends a circle to a
circle or a straight line.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref}, [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} handle the three elementary transformations, and step
[](#s4){.pf-ref} reduces the general Möbius transformation to their composition.
