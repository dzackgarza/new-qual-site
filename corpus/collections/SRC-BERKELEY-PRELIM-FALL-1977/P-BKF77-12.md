::: problem
Let $X\subset\mathbb R$ be nonempty and connected. If every element of $X$ is rational, prove that $X$ has only one element.
:::

::: solution
Suppose, for contradiction, that $X$ contains two distinct points. After
renaming them, choose
$$
a,b\in X,
\qquad
a<b.
$$
Since every point of $X$ is rational, both $a$ and $b$ are rational.

<1>1. Choose an irrational number strictly between $a$ and $b$.
::: proof
For example, set
$$
c=a+\frac{b-a}{\sqrt2}.
$$
Because
$$
0<\frac1{\sqrt2}<1,
$$
we have
$$
a<c<b.
$$
Also $b-a$ is a nonzero rational number, so
$$
\frac{b-a}{\sqrt2}
$$
is irrational, and therefore $c$ is irrational. Hence
$$
c\notin X.
$$
:::

<1>2. Use $c$ to disconnect $X$.
::: proof
Define
$$
U=X\cap(-\infty,c),
\qquad
V=X\cap(c,\infty).
$$
These sets are open in the subspace topology on $X$. They are disjoint, and
because $c\notin X$,
$$
X=U\cup V.
$$
Moreover $a\in U$ and $b\in V$, so both sets are nonempty. Thus $U$ and $V$
form a separation of $X$, contradicting the assumed connectedness.
:::

Therefore $X$ cannot contain two distinct points. Since $X$ is nonempty, it
has exactly one element.
:::
