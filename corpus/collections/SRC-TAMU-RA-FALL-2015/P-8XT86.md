::: {.problem}
Let $Y$ be a closed subspace of a Banach space $X$, with norm $\norm{\cdot}$.
Let $\norm{\cdot}$ be a norm on $Y$ which is equivalent to $\norm{\cdot}$, meaning that there is a $C\ge 1$ so that $$\frac1C\,\norm{y}\le\norm{y}\le C\norm{y}\qquad\text{for all }y\in Y.$$ Let $S$ be the set of all linear functionals $\varphi:X\to\RR$ so that $$\abs{\varphi(y)}\le\norm{y}\qquad\text{for all }y\in Y,$$ and $$\abs{\varphi(x)}\le C\norm{x}\qquad\text{for all }x\in X.$$ Prove the following statements:

(i) $\norm{x}:=\sup_{\varphi\in S}\abs{\varphi(x)}$, $x\in X$, defines a norm on $X$.

(ii) $\norm{y} = \norm{y}$ for $y\in Y$.

(iii) The norms $\norm{\cdot}$ and $\norm{\cdot}$ are equivalent on $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
N(x):=\sup_{\varphi\in S}\abs{\varphi(x)},
\qquad x\in X.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $x\in X$,
$$
N(x)\le C\norm{x}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Every $\varphi\in S$ satisfies
$$
\abs{\varphi(x)}\le C\norm{x}.
$$
Taking the supremum over $\varphi\in S$ gives the claim. In particular, $N(x)$ is finite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $x\in X$,
$$
N(x)\ge \frac1C\norm{x}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The case $x=0$ is immediate. Let $x\ne0$.
By Hahn--Banach for the original norm on $X$, there is a linear functional $\psi\in X^*$ such that
$$
\norm{\psi}=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\psi(x)=\norm{x}.
$$
Set
$$
\varphi=\frac1C\psi.
$$
For every $u\in X$,
$$
\abs{\varphi(u)}\le \frac1C\norm{u}\le C\norm{u}.
$$
For $y\in Y$, the assumed norm equivalence gives
$$
\norm{y}\le C\norm{y},
$$
so
$$
\abs{\varphi(y)}
\le \frac1C\norm{y}
\le \norm{y}.
$$
Hence $\varphi\in S$. Therefore
$$
N(x)\ge \abs{\varphi(x)}
=\frac1C\norm{x}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$N$ is a norm on $X$.

::: pf-proof

For every scalar $a$,
$$
N(ax)
=\sup_{\varphi\in S}\abs{\varphi(ax)}
=\abs{a}N(x).
$$
Also, for $x,z\in X$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
N(x+z)
&=\sup_{\varphi\in S}\abs{\varphi(x)+\varphi(z)}\\
&\le
\sup_{\varphi\in S}\bigl(\abs{\varphi(x)}+\abs{\varphi(z)}\bigr)\\
&\le N(x)+N(z).
\end{aligned}
$$
Finally, if $N(x)=0$, then step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\frac1C\norm{x}\le0,
$$
so $x=0$.
Thus $N=\norm{\cdot}$ is a norm.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For every $y\in Y$,
$$
N(y)\le \norm{y}.
$$

::: pf-proof

This is immediate from the defining condition for $S$:
$$
\abs{\varphi(y)}\le\norm{y}
\qquad(\varphi\in S).
$$
Taking the supremum gives the inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

For every $y\in Y$,
$$
N(y)\ge \norm{y}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Again the case $y=0$ is immediate. Fix $y\ne0$.
Apply Hahn--Banach in the normed space $(Y,\norm{\cdot})$ to the one-dimensional subspace $\RR y$. There is a linear functional
$$
\ell:Y\to\RR
$$
with
$$
\abs{\ell(v)}\le\norm{v}
\qquad(v\in Y)
$$
and
$$
\ell(y)=\norm{y}.
$$
Because
$$
\norm{v}\le C\norm{v}
$$
by the other half of the assumed norm equivalence, we also have
$$
\abs{\ell(v)}\le C\norm{v}
\qquad(v\in Y).
$$
Hahn--Banach, now applied using the original norm on $X$, extends $\ell$ to a linear functional
$$
\varphi:X\to\RR
$$
such that
$$
\abs{\varphi(x)}\le C\norm{x}
\qquad(x\in X).
$$
On $Y$, $\varphi=\ell$, hence
$$
\abs{\varphi(v)}\le\norm{v}
\qquad(v\in Y).
$$
Therefore $\varphi\in S$, and
$$
N(y)\ge\abs{\varphi(y)}=\abs{\ell(y)}=\norm{y}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently, the new norm restricts to $\norm{\cdot}$ on $Y$.

::: pf-proof

Combining steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} yields
$$
\norm{y}=N(y)=\norm{y}
\qquad(y\in Y).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The norms $\norm{\cdot}$ and $\norm{\cdot}$ are equivalent on $X$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\frac1C\norm{x}
\le
\norm{x}
\le
C\norm{x}
\qquad(x\in X).
$$
This is exactly equivalence of the two norms.

:::
