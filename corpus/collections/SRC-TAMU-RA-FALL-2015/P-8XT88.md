::: {.problem}
(a) State a version of the Stone--Weierstrass Theorem.

(b) Let $A$ be a uniformly dense subspace of $C[0,1]$ and let $$B=\Bigl\{F(x):F(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt,\ 0\le x\le 1,\ f\in A\Bigr\}.$$ Prove that $B$ is uniformly dense in $$C_0[0,1]:=\{g\in C[0,1]:g(0)=0\}.$$

(c) Prove that the span of $\{\sin(nx):n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ is dense in $C_0[0,1]$.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. A suitable real Stone--Weierstrass theorem is the following.
::: {.proof}
Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space and let $\mathcal A\subseteq C(X,\mathbb R)$ be a subalgebra that contains the constant functions and separates points of $X$. Then $\mathcal A$ is uniformly dense in $C(X,\mathbb R)$.
:::

<1>2. Let
\[
B'=
\left\{
F:F(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt\text{ for some }f\in C[0,1]
\right\}.
\]
Then $B$ is uniformly dense in $B'$.
::: {.proof}
Take $F\in B'$, say
\[
F(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt
\]
with $f\in C[0,1]$.
Since $A$ is uniformly dense in $C[0,1]$, for every $\varepsilon>0$ choose $a\in A$ such that
\[
\|f-a\|_\infty<\varepsilon.
\]
Set
\[
G(x)=\int_0^x a(t)\,dt\in B.
\]
Then for every $x\in[0,1]$,
\[
|F(x)-G(x)|
\le
\int_0^x|f(t)-a(t)|\,dt
\le
\|f-a\|_\infty,
\]
so
\[
\|F-G\|_\infty<\varepsilon.
\]
:::

<1>3. $B'$ is a subalgebra of $C_0[0,1]$ and separates points of $[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
Every $F\in B'$ satisfies $F(0)=0$, so $B'\subseteq C_0[0,1]$.
It is plainly a vector space.
If
\[
F(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt,
\qquad
G(x)=\int_0^x g(t)\,dt,
\]
then $F,G\in C^1[0,1]$ and the product rule gives
\[
(FG)'=Fg+Gf\in C[0,1].
\]
Since $(FG)(0)=0$,
\[
F(x)G(x)=\int_0^x\bigl(F(t)g(t)+G(t)f(t)\bigr)\,dt,
\]
so $FG\in B'$.
Thus $B'$ is a subalgebra.

Moreover,
\[
x=\int_0^x1\,dt
\]
belongs to $B'$, and the function $x\mapsto x$ separates points of $[0,1]$.
:::

<1>4. $B'$ is uniformly dense in $C_0[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
\mathcal A=B'+\mathbb R1.
\]
By <1>3, $\mathcal A$ is a subalgebra of $C[0,1]$, it contains the constants, and it separates points. Hence Stone--Weierstrass gives
\[
\overline{\mathcal A}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}=C[0,1].
\]

Fix $g\in C_0[0,1]$ and $\varepsilon>0$.
Choose $F\in B'$ and $c\in\mathbb R$ such that
\[
\|g-(F+c)\|_\infty<\frac\varepsilon2.
\]
Evaluating at $0$, using $g(0)=F(0)=0$, gives
\[
|c|<\frac\varepsilon2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|g-F\|_\infty
\le
\|g-(F+c)\|_\infty+|c|
<\varepsilon.
\]
Thus $B'$ is dense in $C_0[0,1]$.
:::

<1>5. Consequently, $B$ is uniformly dense in $C_0[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2,
\[
\overline B\supseteq B',
\]
and hence also
\[
\overline B\supseteq\overline{B'}=C_0[0,1]
\]
by <1>4. Since $B\subseteq C_0[0,1]$, equality follows.
:::

<1>6. The unital cosine algebra
\[
\mathcal C=\operatorname{span}\{1,\cos(nx):n\ge1\}
\]
is uniformly dense in $C[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
The product identity
\[
\cos(mx)\cos(nx)
=
\frac12\bigl(\cos((m+n)x)+\cos((m-n)x)\bigr)
\]
shows that $\mathcal C$ is an algebra.
It contains the constants.
Also $x\mapsto\cos x$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,1]\subset[0,\pi]$, hence separates points there.
Stone--Weierstrass therefore gives
\[
\overline{\mathcal C}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}=C[0,1].
\]
:::

<1>7. The constant function $1$ lies in the uniform closure of
\[
\mathcal C_+=\operatorname{span}\{\cos(nx):n\ge1\}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Set
\[
a=\cos1>0.
\]
On the interval $[a^2,1]$, the function
\[
v\longmapsto v^{-1/2}
\]
is continuous. By the Weierstrass polynomial approximation theorem, there are polynomials $q_k$ such that
\[
\sup_{v\in[a^2,1]}|q_k(v)-v^{-1/2}|\longrightarrow0.
\]
For $x\in[0,1]$, put $u=\cos x\in[a,1]$. Then
\[
u q_k(u^2)\longrightarrow1
\]
uniformly in $u\in[a,1]$, hence uniformly in $x\in[0,1]$.

Each function
\[
u q_k(u^2)
\]
is an odd polynomial in $u=\cos x$. Every odd polynomial in $\cos x$ is a finite linear combination of
\[
\cos x,\cos(3x),\cos(5x),\ldots
\]
because the Chebyshev polynomials satisfy
\[
T_n(\cos x)=\cos(nx)
\]
and the odd $T_{2j+1}$ form a triangular basis of the odd polynomials. Therefore every $u q_k(u^2)$ belongs to $\mathcal C_+$.
Thus $1\in\overline{\mathcal C_+}$.
:::

<1>8. $\mathcal C_+$ is uniformly dense in $C[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>7, the closure of $\mathcal C_+$ contains $1$ and of course contains every $\cos(nx)$ for $n\ge1$. Hence it contains $\mathcal C$. By <1>6, $\mathcal C$ is dense in $C[0,1]$. Therefore
\[
\overline{\mathcal C_+}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}=C[0,1].
\]
:::

<1>9. The span of $\{\sin(nx):n\in\mathbb N\}$ is dense in $C_0[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
A_0=\operatorname{span}\{n\cos(nx):n\ge1\}.
\]
Since multiplication of each generator by the nonzero scalar $n$ does not change its linear span,
\[
A_0=\mathcal C_+.
\]
By <1>8, $A_0$ is uniformly dense in $C[0,1]$.

For every $n\ge1$,
\[
\sin(nx)=\int_0^x n\cos(nt)\,dt.
\]
Thus the space obtained from $A_0$ by the construction in part (b) is exactly
\[
\operatorname{span}\{\sin(nx):n\ge1\}.
\]
Applying <1>5 with $A=A_0$ proves that this span is uniformly dense in $C_0[0,1]$.
:::
