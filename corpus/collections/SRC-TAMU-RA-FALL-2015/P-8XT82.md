::: {.problem}
Assume that $(f_n)$ is a sequence in $C[0,1]$.

(a) Show that $(f_n)$ converges weakly to $0$ if and only if $(f_n)$ is bounded in $C[0,1]$ and $\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(t)=0$ for all $t\in[0,1]$.

(b) Show that if $(f_n)$ converges weakly in $C[0,1]$, then it converges in norm in $L^p[0,1]$ for all $1\le p<\infty$.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $f_n\rightharpoonup0$ weakly in $C[0,1]$, then $f_n(t)\to0$ for every $t\in[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
For each fixed $t\in[0,1]$, evaluation
\[
\delta_t:C[0,1]\to\mathbb R,
\qquad
\delta_t(f)=f(t),
\]
is a bounded linear functional, since
\[
|f(t)|\le\|f\|_\infty.
\]
Weak convergence therefore gives
\[
f_n(t)=\delta_t(f_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
:::

<1>2. If $f_n\rightharpoonup0$ weakly in $C[0,1]$, then $(f_n)$ is bounded in the supremum norm.
::: {.proof}
Let $X=C[0,1]$ and let
\[
J:X\to X^{**}
\]
be the canonical isometric embedding.
For every $\Lambda\in X^*$,
\[
Jf_n(\Lambda)=\Lambda(f_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence the scalar sequence $\{Jf_n(\Lambda)\}_n$ is bounded for every $\Lambda\in X^*$.
Since $X^*$ is Banach, the uniform boundedness principle applied to the family $\{Jf_n\}\subset X^{**}$ yields
\[
\sup_n\|Jf_n\|<\infty.
\]
Because $J$ is isometric,
\[
\sup_n\|f_n\|_\infty<\infty.
\]
:::

<1>3. Conversely, if $(f_n)$ is bounded in $C[0,1]$ and $f_n(t)\to0$ for every $t$, then $f_n\rightharpoonup0$ weakly.
::: {.proof}
Choose $M<\infty$ such that
\[
\|f_n\|_\infty\le M
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Let $\Lambda\in C[0,1]^*$.
By the Riesz--Markov representation theorem, there is a finite signed regular Borel measure $\mu$ on $[0,1]$ such that
\[
\Lambda(f)=\int_{[0,1]}f\,d\mu
\qquad(f\in C[0,1]).
\]
The total variation measure $|\mu|$ is finite, and
\[
|f_n(t)|\le M
\]
for every $n$ and $t$.
Since $f_n(t)\to0$ pointwise, dominated convergence with respect to $|\mu|$ gives
\[
\int_{[0,1]}|f_n|\,d|\mu|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore
\[
|\Lambda(f_n)|
=
\left|\int_{[0,1]}f_n\,d\mu\right|
\le
\int_{[0,1]}|f_n|\,d|\mu|
\longrightarrow0.
\]
As $\Lambda$ was arbitrary, $f_n\rightharpoonup0$.
Together with <1>1 and <1>2, this proves part (a).
:::

<1>4. Weak convergence in $C[0,1]$ implies convergence in every $L^p[0,1]$ norm with $1\le p<\infty$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
f_n\rightharpoonup f
\qquad\text{in }C[0,1].
\]
Set
\[
g_n=f_n-f.
\]
Then $g_n\rightharpoonup0$ weakly.
By part (a), there is $M<\infty$ such that
\[
\|g_n\|_\infty\le M
\qquad(n\ge1),
\]
and
\[
g_n(t)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(t\in[0,1]).
\]
Fix $1\le p<\infty$.
Then
\[
|g_n(t)|^p\longrightarrow0
\]
pointwise and
\[
|g_n(t)|^p\le M^p
\qquad(t\in[0,1]).
\]
Since $M^p$ is integrable on $[0,1]$, dominated convergence gives
\[
\|f_n-f\|_p^p
=
\int_0^1|g_n(t)|^p\,dt
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence
\[
\|f_n-f\|_p\longrightarrow0.
\]
:::
