::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be increasing on $[0,1]$ and let $$g(x)=\limsup_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h},\qquad\text{for }0<x<1.$$ Prove that if $A=\{x\in(0,1):g(x)>1\}$ then $$f(1)-f(0)\ge m^*(A).$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $x\in A$. Since
\[
\limsup_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}>1,
\]
there are arbitrarily small $h>0$ such that $[x-h,x+h]\subset(0,1)$ and
\[
f(x+h)-f(x-h)>2h.
\]
Thus the family of all such intervals
\[
I=(x-h,x+h),\qquad x\in A,
\]
is a Vitali cover of $A$.

By the Vitali covering theorem, there is a countable pairwise disjoint subfamily $(I_j)$ whose union covers $A$ up to a null set. Write
\[
I_j=(x_j-h_j,x_j+h_j).
\]
Because the $I_j$ are disjoint subsets of $(0,1)$,
\[
\sum_j |I_j|\le1.
\]
Hence, given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ so that
\[
m^*\!\left(A\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^N I_j\right)<\varepsilon;
\]
indeed the uncovered set is contained in the Vitali-null remainder together with the tail $\bigcup_{j>N}I_j$, whose measure tends to $0$.

For each $j\le N$,
\[
|I_j|=2h_j<f(x_j+h_j)-f(x_j-h_j).
\]
Order the finitely many disjoint intervals from left to right. Since $f$ is increasing, the total increase over disjoint intervals is at most its increase over $[0,1]$:
\[
\sum_{j=1}^N\bigl(f(x_j+h_j)-f(x_j-h_j)\bigr)
\le f(1)-f(0).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
m^*(A)
&\le m^*\!\left(A\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^N I_j\right)
   +\sum_{j=1}^N|I_j|\\
&<\varepsilon+f(1)-f(0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ yields
\[
\boxed{m^*(A)\le f(1)-f(0).}
\]
:::
