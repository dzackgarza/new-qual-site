::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be increasing on $[0,1]$ and let $$g(x)=\limsup_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h},\qquad\text{for }0<x<1.$$ Prove that if $A=\{x\in(0,1):g(x)>1\}$ then $$f(1)-f(0)\ge m^*(A).$$
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this problem: the only retained file for this collection, `assets/attachments/ra15.pdf`, is the examination. The argument below is the card's own and follows the card's previous prose, with the Vitali input named and the telescoping inequality justified.

Throughout $f:[0,1]\to\RR$ is increasing (not necessarily continuous), $m^*$ is Lebesgue outer measure, and
$$
g(x)=\limsup_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h},\qquad A=\theset{x\in(0,1)\mid g(x)>1}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #intervals}
For every $x\in A$ there are arbitrarily small $h>0$ with $[x-h,x+h]\subset(0,1)$ and $f(x+h)-f(x-h)>2h$.

::: pf-proof
$g(x)>1$ means that for some sequence $h_n\to0$ with $h_n>0$ the quotients $\bigl(f(x+h_n)-f(x-h_n)\bigr)/(2h_n)$ exceed $1$ for infinitely many $n$; dropping to such a subsequence we may suppose all of them do. Since $0<x<1$, all but finitely many of the intervals satisfy $[x-h_n,x+h_n]\subset(0,1)$. Multiplying the inequality by $2h_n>0$ gives $f(x+h_n)-f(x-h_n)>2h_n$, and the $h_n$ can be taken as small as we please.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #vitali-cover}
The family $\mathcal V=\theset{(x-h,x+h)\mid x\in A,\ h\text{ as above}}$ is a Vitali cover of $A$.

::: pf-proof
Each member contains its centre $x$, and by step [](#intervals){.pf-ref} the lengths $2h$ can be made arbitrarily small at each $x\in A$; that is the definition of a Vitali cover.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #disjoint-subfamily}
There is a countable family of pairwise disjoint members $I_j=(x_j-h_j,x_j+h_j)$ of $\mathcal V$ with $m^*\bigl(A\setminus\bigcup_jI_j\bigr)=0$, and then $\sum_jm(I_j)=\sum_j2h_j\le1$.

::: pf-proof
This is the Vitali covering theorem for a set of finite outer measure covered by a Vitali family: a countable pairwise disjoint subfamily covers $A$ up to a null set. The $I_j$ are pairwise disjoint subsets of $(0,1)$ by step [](#intervals){.pf-ref}, so their total measure is at most $m((0,1))\le1$, and the series converges.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #truncation}
For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N$ with $m^*\bigl(A\setminus\bigcup_{j\le N}I_j\bigr)<\varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof
$A\setminus\bigcup_{j\le N}I_j\subseteq\bigl(A\setminus\bigcup_jI_j\bigr)\cup\bigcup_{j>N}I_j$, so its outer measure is at most $0+\sum_{j>N}2h_j$, a tail of a convergent series of positive terms, which tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$. Choose $N$ accordingly.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #increments}
For pairwise disjoint $I_1,\dots,I_N\subset(0,1)$ arising as above,
$$
\sum_{j=1}^Nm(I_j)\le\sum_{j=1}^N\bigl(f(x_j+h_j)-f(x_j-h_j)\bigr)\le f(1)-f(0).
$$

::: pf-proof
The first inequality is step [](#intervals){.pf-ref}, term by term. For the second, relabel so that the intervals are ordered by their left endpoints. Disjointness gives $x_j+h_j\le x_{j+1}-h_{j+1}$, and $f$ increasing gives $f(x_j+h_j)\le f(x_{j+1}-h_{j+1})$; hence
$$
\sum_{j=1}^N\bigl(f(x_j+h_j)-f(x_j-h_j)\bigr)\le\sum_{j=1}^N\bigl(f(x_{j+1}-h_{j+1})-f(x_j-h_j)\bigr),$$
where the right side is the telescoping sum over the subintervals $[x_j-h_j,\ x_{j+1}-h_{j+1}]$ of $[0,1]$, together with the two end pieces, so it is at most $f(1)-f(0)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
$m^*(A)\le f(1)-f(0)$.

::: pf-proof
By subadditivity of outer measure,
$$
m^*(A)\le m^*\Bigl(A\setminus\bigcup_{j\le N}I_j\Bigr)+\sum_{j\le N}m(I_j)<\varepsilon+\bigl(f(1)-f(0)\bigr)
$$
by steps [](#truncation){.pf-ref} and [](#increments){.pf-ref}. This holds for every $\varepsilon>0$, so $m^*(A)\le f(1)-f(0)$, which is the assertion.

:::
