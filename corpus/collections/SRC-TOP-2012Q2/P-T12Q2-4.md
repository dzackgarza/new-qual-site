::: {.problem}
Give a complete and careful proof of the following well-known theorem: If $X$ is a compact Hausdorff space, then $X$ is normal.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $A,B\subseteq X$ be disjoint closed subsets.
::: {.proof}
To prove that $X$ is normal, it suffices to construct disjoint open neighborhoods of arbitrary such $A$ and $B$.
:::

<1>2. The sets $A$ and $B$ are compact.
::: {.proof}
They are closed subsets of the compact space $X$.
:::

<1>3. For every $(a,b)\in A\times B$, choose disjoint open sets $U_{a,b},V_{a,b}\subseteq X$ with
$$
a\in U_{a,b},\qquad b\in V_{a,b}.
$$
::: {.proof}
Because $A\cap B=\varnothing$, we have $a\neq b$. Since $X$ is Hausdorff, distinct points admit disjoint open neighborhoods.
:::

<1>4. Fix $a\in A$. There exist $b_1,\ldots,b_n\in B$ such that
$$
B\subseteq V_{a,b_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{a,b_n}.
$$
::: {.proof}
The family $\{V_{a,b}:b\in B\}$ is an open cover of the compact set $B$, so it has a finite subcover.
:::

<1>5. Define
$$
U_a=\bigcap_{i=1}^n U_{a,b_i},
\qquad
V_a=\bigcup_{i=1}^n V_{a,b_i}.
$$
Then $U_a,V_a$ are open, $a\in U_a$, $B\subseteq V_a$, and $U_a\cap V_a=\varnothing$.
::: {.proof}
Finite intersections and unions of open sets are open. The containments follow from <1>3 and <1>4. If $x\in U_a\cap V_a$, then for some $i$ we have $x\in V_{a,b_i}$, while $x\in U_a\subseteq U_{a,b_i}$, contradicting the disjointness in <1>3.
:::

<1>6. There exist $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in A$ such that
$$
A\subseteq U_{a_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{a_m}.
$$
::: {.proof}
By <1>5, the family $\{U_a:a\in A\}$ is an open cover of the compact set $A$, so it has a finite subcover.
:::

<1>7. Set
$$
U=\bigcup_{j=1}^m U_{a_j},
\qquad
V=\bigcap_{j=1}^m V_{a_j}.
$$
Then $U,V$ are open, $A\subseteq U$, and $B\subseteq V$.
::: {.proof}
The first containment is <1>6. By <1>5, $B\subseteq V_{a_j}$ for every $j$, hence $B\subseteq V$. The sets are open because the unions and intersections are finite.
:::

<1>8. The sets $U$ and $V$ are disjoint.
::: {.proof}
If $x\in U\cap V$, then $x\in U_{a_j}$ for some $j$. Since $x\in V\subseteq V_{a_j}$, this contradicts $U_{a_j}\cap V_{a_j}=\varnothing$ from <1>5.
:::

<1>9. Therefore $X$ is normal.
::: {.proof}
The arbitrary disjoint closed sets $A$ and $B$ have disjoint open neighborhoods by <1>7 and <1>8.
:::
:::
