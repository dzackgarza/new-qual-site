::: {.problem}
Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are topological spaces and $f:X\to Y$ is a continuous function.

(a) Prove there exists a topological space $Z$, a quotient map $q:X\to Z$ and a one-to-one continuous map $h:Z\to Y$ such that $f=h\circ q$.

(b) (This part is a continuation of part (a).) If $Z_1$ is a topological space, $q_1:X\to Z_1$ is a quotient map, $h_1:Z_1\to Y$ is one-to-one and continuous, and $f=h_1\circ q_1$, prove that $Z$ and $Z_1$ are homeomorphic.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Define an equivalence relation on $X$ by
$$
x\sim x'\iff f(x)=f(x'),
$$
and let $Z=X/\!\sim$ with the quotient topology. Let $q:X\to Z$ be the quotient map.
::: {.proof}
Equality of $f$-values is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, so this is an equivalence relation. By definition of the quotient topology, the canonical surjection $q$ is a quotient map.
:::

<1>2. Define $h:Z\to Y$ by
$$
h([x])=f(x).
$$
::: {.proof}
If $[x]=[x']$, then $x\sim x'$, hence $f(x)=f(x')$. Therefore the formula is well-defined.
:::

<1>3. The map $h$ is one-to-one and satisfies $f=h\circ q$.
::: {.proof}
For $x\in X$,
$$
(h\circ q)(x)=h([x])=f(x).
$$
If $h([x])=h([x'])$, then $f(x)=f(x')$, hence $x\sim x'$ and therefore $[x]=[x']$.
:::

<1>4. The map $h$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
We have $h\circ q=f$, which is continuous by hypothesis. Since $q$ is a quotient map, the defining property of the quotient topology implies that $h$ is continuous.
:::

<1>5. Thus $f$ factors as a quotient map followed by a one-to-one continuous map.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>1--<1>4.
:::

<1>6. Now suppose $f=h_1\circ q_1$ with $q_1:X\to Z_1$ quotient and $h_1:Z_1\to Y$ one-to-one and continuous. Then
$$
q_1(x)=q_1(x')\iff f(x)=f(x').
$$
::: {.proof}
If $q_1(x)=q_1(x')$, applying $h_1$ gives $f(x)=f(x')$. Conversely, if $f(x)=f(x')$, then
$$
h_1(q_1(x))=h_1(q_1(x')),
$$
and injectivity of $h_1$ gives $q_1(x)=q_1(x')$.
:::

<1>7. There is a unique bijection $\varphi:Z\to Z_1$ satisfying
$$
\varphi\circ q=q_1,
$$
namely $\varphi([x])=q_1(x)$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>6, $q_1$ is constant exactly on the equivalence classes defining $Z$, so the formula is well-defined. Surjectivity follows from surjectivity of the quotient map $q_1$. If $\varphi([x])=\varphi([x'])$, then $q_1(x)=q_1(x')$, hence $f(x)=f(x')$ by <1>6, so $[x]=[x']$; thus $\varphi$ is injective. The equation $\varphi q=q_1$ determines $\varphi$ because $q$ is surjective.
:::

<1>8. The bijection $\varphi$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
The composite $\varphi\circ q=q_1$ is continuous. Since $q$ is a quotient map, $\varphi$ is continuous.
:::

<1>9. The inverse $\varphi^{-1}:Z_1\to Z$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
From $\varphi q=q_1$, we obtain
$$
\varphi^{-1}\circ q_1=q.
$$
The right-hand side is continuous, and $q_1$ is a quotient map. Hence $\varphi^{-1}$ is continuous.
:::

<1>10. Therefore $Z$ and $Z_1$ are homeomorphic.
::: {.proof}
By <1>7--<1>9, $\varphi$ is a bijection with continuous inverse.
:::
:::
