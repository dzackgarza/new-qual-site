::: {.problem}
Suppose $G$ is a finite group acting on a Hausdorff space $X$ and assume the action is fixed point free (i.e., if $g\in G$ is not the identity element, then $g\cdot x\ne x$ for all $x\in X$).

(a) Prove that the action is necessarily a covering space action.
(Note: Recall that this means every point $x\in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ such that $U\cap g\cdot U=\varnothing$ for all non-identity elements $g\in G$.)

(b) Assume $X$ is simply connected and let $X/G$ be the quotient space.
Prove every map from $X/G$ to $S^1$ is contractible.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Part (a).**

<1>1. Fix $x\in X$. For each $g\in G\setminus\{e\}$ there is an open neighborhood $U_g$ of $x$ such that $U_g\cap gU_g=\varnothing$.
::: {.proof}
Freeness gives $x\ne gx$. Since $X$ is Hausdorff, choose disjoint open neighborhoods $A_g\ni x$ and $B_g\ni gx$. Set
$$
U_g=A_g\cap g^{-1}B_g.
$$
Then $U_g$ is open and contains $x$, while $gU_g\subset B_g$ and $U_g\subset A_g$, so $U_g\cap gU_g=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>2. The open set
$$
U=\bigcap_{g\ne e}U_g
$$
contains $x$ and satisfies $U\cap gU=\varnothing$ for every $g\ne e$.
::: {.proof}
The intersection is finite because $G$ is finite, hence is open. For fixed $g\ne e$, one has $U\subset U_g$ and $gU\subset gU_g$, so
$$
U\cap gU\subset U_g\cap gU_g=\varnothing.
$$
:::

<1>3. Hence the action is a covering-space action, and the quotient map $X\to X/G$ is a covering map.
::: {.proof}
The condition in <1>2 is precisely the defining neighborhood condition for a covering-space action. Its translates are pairwise disjoint and map homeomorphically to their common image in $X/G$.
:::

**Part (b).**

<1>4. As printed, part (b) is false without a local path-connectedness hypothesis.
::: {.proof}
Take $G$ to be the trivial finite group. Then $X/G=X$, so the assertion would say that every map from every simply connected Hausdorff space to $S^1$ is nullhomotopic. Hatcher's standard quasi-circle example is a path-connected Hausdorff space $Y$ with $\pi_1(Y)=0$ and a quotient map $Y\to S^1$ that does not lift to $\mathbb R\to S^1$; in particular this map is not nullhomotopic. Thus simple connectivity alone is insufficient for the lifting argument used in the intended proof.
:::

<1>5. If, in addition, $X$ is locally path-connected, then every map $X/G\to S^1$ is nullhomotopic.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, $p:X\to X/G$ is a covering map and hence a local homeomorphism. Thus local path-connectedness of $X$ implies local path-connectedness of $X/G$. Since $X$ is simply connected and path-connected, it is a simply connected covering of $X/G$. The deck group is $G$, so the standard covering-space theorem gives
$$
\pi_1(X/G)\cong G,
$$
which is finite.

Let $f:X/G\to S^1$. Then
$$
f_*:\pi_1(X/G)\to\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z
$$
has finite image, hence is trivial. Because $X/G$ is path-connected and locally path-connected, the lifting criterion for the universal covering $\mathbb R\to S^1$ applies and yields a lift $\widetilde f:X/G\to\mathbb R$. Since $\mathbb R$ is contractible, $\widetilde f$ is nullhomotopic, and composing its nullhomotopy with $\mathbb R\to S^1$ gives a nullhomotopy of $f$.
:::
:::
