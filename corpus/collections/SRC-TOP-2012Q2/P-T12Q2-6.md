::: {.problem}
Suppose $G$ is a finite group acting on a Hausdorff space $X$ and assume the action is fixed point free (i.e., if $g\in G$ is not the identity element, then $g\cdot x\ne x$ for all $x\in X$).

(a) Prove that the action is necessarily a covering space action.
(Note: Recall that this means every point $x\in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ such that $U\cap g\cdot U=\varnothing$ for all non-identity elements $g\in G$.)

(b) Assume $X$ is simply connected and let $X/G$ be the quotient space.
Prove every map from $X/G$ to $S^1$ is contractible.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Part (a).**

<1>1. For each $x \in X$ there is an open neighborhood $U$ with $U \cap gU = \varnothing$ for all $g \neq e$.
<2>1. For each $g \neq e$, the fixed-point set $\operatorname{Fix}(g) = \{x : gx = x\}$ is closed.
Proof: $\operatorname{Fix}(g)$ is the preimage of the diagonal under the continuous map $x \mapsto (x, gx)$, and $X$ is Hausdorff so the diagonal is closed.
<2>2. $x \notin \operatorname{Fix}(g)$ for all $g \neq e$.
Proof: the action is fixed point free.
<2>3. Since $G$ is finite, $\bigcup_{g \neq e} \operatorname{Fix}(g)$ is a finite union of closed sets not containing $x$.
Proof: <2>1, <2>2, and finiteness of $G$.
<2>4. Hence there is an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ disjoint from $\bigcup_{g \neq e} \operatorname{Fix}(g)$.
Proof: $X$ is Hausdorff (hence regular enough to separate a point from a finite union of closed sets avoiding it); equivalently, the complement is open and contains $x$.
<2>5. For this $U$, $U \cap gU = \varnothing$ for all $g \neq e$.
Proof: if $y \in U \cap gU$, then $y = gz$ for some $z \in U$, so $g^{-1}y = z$; then $g^{-1}$ fixes $y$ only if $y = z$, forcing $g^{-1}y = y$, i.e. $y \in \operatorname{Fix}(g^{-1})$, contradicting <2>4.

<1>2. Hence the action is a covering space action.
Proof: <1>1 is exactly the defining condition.

**Part (b).**

<1>1. $X \to X/G$ is a covering map with deck group $G$.
Proof: <1>2 (part (a)) and the standard fact that a covering space action yields a covering map.

<1>2. $\pi_1(X/G) \cong G$.
Proof: $X$ is simply connected, so $X$ is the universal cover of $X/G$ and $G$ is its group of deck transformations; hence $\pi_1(X/G) \cong G$.

<1>3. Every map $f: X/G \to S^1$ is nullhomotopic.
<2>1. $f_*: \pi_1(X/G) \to \pi_1(S^1) = \ZZ$ is a homomorphism from the finite group $G$ to $\ZZ$.
Proof: <1>2 and $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \ZZ$.
<2>2. $f_* = 0$.
Proof: the image of a finite group under a homomorphism to the torsion-free group $\ZZ$ is trivial.
<2>3. Hence $f$ lifts to a map $\tilde f: X/G \to \RR$.
Proof: the lifting criterion: $f_*(\pi_1(X/G)) = 0 \subseteq \pi_1(\RR) = 0$.
<2>4. $\RR$ is contractible, so $\tilde f$ is nullhomotopic, hence $f = p \circ \tilde f$ is nullhomotopic.
Proof: $S^1$ is covered by $\RR$ via $p: \RR \to S^1$; a nullhomotopy of $\tilde f$ composes with $p$ to give a nullhomotopy of $f$.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>3 shows every map $X/G \to S^1$ is nullhomotopic (contractible).
