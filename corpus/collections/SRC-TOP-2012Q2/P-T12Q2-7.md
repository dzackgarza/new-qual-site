::: {.problem}
Let $X=\RP^2\vee\RP^2$, i.e., the result of gluing two copies of the real projective plane together along specified base points.
Recall that $\RP^2$ is the quotient of $S^2$ by the antipodal map.

(a) Find $\pi_1(X)$.

(b) Find the homology of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and two $2$-cells attached along $a^2$ and $b^2$.

::: {.proof}
$\RP^2$ is a disk with antipodal boundary points identified, so it has one $0$-cell, one $1$-cell forming $\RP^1\cong S^1$, and one $2$-cell whose boundary runs twice around that circle.
Wedging two copies identifies only the two $0$-cells.
:::

<1>2. $\pi_1(X,v)\cong\langle a,b\mid a^2,\ b^2\rangle\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)*(\ZZ/2\ZZ)$.

::: {.proof}
By the Seifert--van Kampen theorem, the $1$-skeleton $S^1\vee S^1$ has fundamental group free on $a,b$, and each $2$-cell adds the relation given by its attaching loop from step <1>1.
:::

<1>3. The cellular chain complex is $0\to\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{\partial_2}\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{\partial_1}\ZZ\to0$ with $[\partial_2]=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}$ and $\partial_1=0$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, each $2$-cell runs twice around its own $1$-cell and not around the other, and both $1$-cells are loops at $v$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 answers (a).
For (b), by step <1>3, $H_0(X)\cong\ZZ$, $H_1(X)=\ZZ^2/(2\ZZ)^2\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^2$, and $H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2=0$; there are no cells above dimension $2$, so $H_k(X)=0$ for $k\ge2$.
:::
:::
