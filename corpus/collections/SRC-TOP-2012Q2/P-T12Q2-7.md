::: {.problem}
Let $X=\mathbb{RP}^2\vee\mathbb{RP}^2$, i.e., the result of gluing two copies of the real projective plane together along specified base points.
Recall that $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is the quotient of $S^2$ by the antipodal map.

(a) Find $\pi_1(X)$.

(b) Find the homology of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Give each copy of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ its standard CW structure with one $0$-cell, one $1$-cell, and one $2$-cell attached by a map of degree $2$.
::: {.proof}
The standard model of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is obtained from a disk by identifying antipodal boundary points. Its $1$-skeleton is $\mathbb{RP}^1\cong S^1$, and the boundary of the $2$-cell maps twice around this circle.
:::

<1>2. After wedging the two base $0$-cells, $X$ has one $0$-cell $v$, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and two $2$-cells attached along $a^2$ and $b^2$.
::: {.proof}
The wedge identification affects only the chosen $0$-cells; the cells of positive dimension from the two projective planes remain distinct.
:::

<1>3. Seifert--van Kampen gives
$$
\pi_1(X,v)\cong\langle a,b\mid a^2=1,\ b^2=1\rangle
\cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)*(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z).
$$
::: {.proof}
Equivalently, van Kampen for the wedge gives the free product of the two fundamental groups, and $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. The displayed presentation is the standard presentation of that free product.
:::

<1>4. The cellular chain complex is
$$
0\longrightarrow \mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0,
$$
with
$$
[\partial_2]=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\partial_1=0.
$$
::: {.proof}
Each $2$-cell winds twice around its own $1$-cell and not around the other. Both $1$-cells are loops at the unique $0$-cell, so their cellular boundaries vanish.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2,
\qquad
H_k(X;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(k\ge2).
$$
::: {.proof}
Since $\partial_1=0$, $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$ and
$$
H_1=C_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\cong\mathbb Z^2/(2\mathbb Z)^2
\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2.
$$
The map $\partial_2$ is injective, so $H_2=0$; there are no cells above dimension $2$.
:::
:::
