::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained from the torus $T=S^1\times S^1$ by gluing in a disk $D^2$ along its boundary $\partial D^2=S^1$ using the map $\alpha:S^1\to T$ given by $z\mapsto(z,(1,0))$ for $z\in S^1$.

(a) Find $\pi_1(X)$.

(b) Give a CW complex for $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Give $T$ its standard CW structure from the square with opposite sides identified: one $0$-cell $v=(1,1)$, two $1$-cells $a=S^1\times\{1\}$ and $b=\{1\}\times S^1$, and one $2$-cell $t$ attached along $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.
Here $(1,0)\in S^1\subset\RR^2$ is the point $1\in S^1\subset\CC$.

<1>1. (b) $X$ is a CW complex with one $0$-cell $v$, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and two $2$-cells $t$ and $d$, attached along $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ and $a$ respectively.

::: {.proof}
The map $\alpha(z)=(z,1)$ runs once around the first factor with the second coordinate fixed, so it is the characteristic loop of the $1$-cell $a$, a cellular map into the $1$-skeleton.
The disk glued in along $\alpha$ is therefore a new $2$-cell $d$ attached along $a$.
:::

<1>2. $\pi_1(X,v)\cong\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1},\ a\rangle$.

::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is $S^1\vee S^1$, with fundamental group free on $a,b$.
By the Seifert--van Kampen theorem, attaching a $2$-cell quotients the fundamental group by the normal closure of its attaching loop, so by step <1>1 the relations are $aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1$ and $a=1$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 answers (b).
For (a), in the presentation of step <1>2 the relation $a=1$ makes the commutator relation redundant, leaving $\langle b\mid\ \rangle$.
Hence $\pi_1(X)\cong\boxed{\ZZ}$, generated by the class of $b$.
:::
