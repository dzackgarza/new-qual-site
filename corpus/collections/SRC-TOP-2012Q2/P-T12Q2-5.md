::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained from the torus $T=S^1\times S^1$ by gluing in a disk $D^2$ along its boundary $\partial D^2=S^1$ using the map $\alpha:S^1\to T$ given by $z\mapsto(z,(1,0))$ for $z\in S^1$.

(a) Find $\pi_1(X)$.

(b) Give a CW complex for $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Give the torus its standard CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$, two oriented $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell $t$ attached along the commutator $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.
::: {.proof}
This is the CW structure obtained from the square model of $T^2$ by identifying opposite sides. The two edge classes are the two coordinate circles.
:::

<1>2. Under this CW structure, the attaching map $\alpha$ of the new disk is the loop $a$.
::: {.proof}
The map $\alpha(z)=(z,(1,0))$ runs once around the first $S^1$ factor while the second coordinate is fixed, so it represents the first coordinate $1$-cell $a$.
:::

<1>3. Hence $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and two $2$-cells $t,d$, where
$$
\partial t\sim aba^{-1}b^{-1},
\qquad
\partial d\sim a.
$$
::: {.proof}
Start with the standard CW structure of <1>1 and regard the disk glued in by $\alpha$ as one additional $2$-cell. Its attaching map is $a$ by <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Van Kampen's theorem gives
$$
\pi_1(X,v)\cong\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1},\ a\rangle.
$$
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is $S^1\vee S^1$, so its fundamental group is the free group $F(a,b)$. Attaching a $2$-cell imposes the normal closure of its attaching loop as a relation. The two $2$-cells therefore impose $[a,b]=1$ and $a=1$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z,
$$
generated by the class of $b$.
::: {.proof}
In the presentation of <1>4, the relation $a=1$ makes the commutator relation redundant, leaving the presentation $\langle b\mid\ \rangle$, which is infinite cyclic.
:::
:::
