::: {.problem}
Assume that $(X,\tau)$ is a topological space with the property that for every open set $G\subseteq X$, the closure of $G$, $\overline G$, is open.
Such topological spaces are called *extremally disconnected*. Prove the following.

(a) If $F\subseteq X$ is a closed set, then the interior of $F$, $F^\circ$, is closed.

(b) If $G\subseteq X$ is an open set, then $G$ equipped with the subspace topology is extremally disconnected.

(c) If $X$ is Hausdorff and $f:\mathbb R\to X$ is continuous, prove that $f$ is a constant function.
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

<1>1. $F^\circ = X - \overline{X - F}$.
Proof: the interior of $F$ is the complement of the closure of the complement.

<1>2. $X - F$ is open, so $\overline{X - F}$ is open.
Proof: <1>1 and the extremal disconnectedness hypothesis.

<1>3. Hence $F^\circ = X - \overline{X - F}$ is closed.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2 (complement of an open set is closed).

**(b).**

<1>1. Let $U \subseteq G$ be open in the subspace topology.
Proof: take an arbitrary open subset of $G$.

<1>2. $U$ is open in $X$.
Proof: $U = G \cap V$ for some open $V \subseteq X$, and $G$ is open, so $U$ is open in $X$.

<1>3. The closure of $U$ in $G$ is $\overline U \cap G$, where $\overline U$ is the closure in $X$.
Proof: closure in a subspace.

<1>4. $\overline U$ is open in $X$.
Proof: <1>2 and extremal disconnectedness.

<1>5. Hence $\overline U \cap G$ is open in $G$, so $G$ is extremally disconnected.
Proof: <1>3 and <1>4.

**(c).**

<1>1. Suppose for contradiction that $f$ is not constant, so there exist $a < b$ with $f(a) \ne f(b)$.
Proof: assume the conclusion fails.

<1>2. Since $X$ is Hausdorff, there are disjoint open sets $U, V$ with $f(a) \in U$ and $f(b) \in V$.
Proof: Hausdorff separation.

<1>3. $f^{-1}(U)$ and $f^{-1}(V)$ are disjoint open subsets of $\mathbb{R}$.
Proof: <1>2 and continuity of $f$.

<1>4. $\overline{f^{-1}(U)}$ is open in $\mathbb{R}$.
Proof: <1>3 and extremal disconnectedness.

<1>5. $\overline{f^{-1}(U)}$ is clopen, and it is nonempty and not all of $\mathbb{R}$.
Proof: <1>4 (open and closed); it contains $a$ (so nonempty) and is disjoint from the nonempty open set $f^{-1}(V)$ (so not all of $\mathbb{R}$).

<1>6. But $\mathbb{R}$ is connected, so it has no nonempty proper clopen subset.
Proof: connectedness of $\mathbb{R}$.

<1>7. Contradiction, so $f$ is constant.
Proof: <1>5 and <1>6.

<1>8. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>3 (a), <1>5 (b), <1>7 (c).
:::
