::: {.problem}
Assume that $(X,\tau)$ is a topological space with the property that for every open set $G\subseteq X$, the closure of $G$, $\overline G$, is open.
Such topological spaces are called *extremally disconnected*. Prove the following.

(a) If $F\subseteq X$ is a closed set, then the interior of $F$, $F^\circ$, is closed.

(b) If $G\subseteq X$ is an open set, then $G$ equipped with the subspace topology is extremally disconnected.

(c) If $X$ is Hausdorff and $f:\mathbb R\to X$ is continuous, prove that $f$ is a constant function.
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

<1>1. $F^\circ = X - \overline{X - F}$.
::: {.proof}
the interior of $F$ is the complement of the closure of the complement.
:::

<1>2. $X - F$ is open, so $\overline{X - F}$ is open.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and the extremal disconnectedness hypothesis.
:::

<1>3. Hence $F^\circ = X - \overline{X - F}$ is closed.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2 (complement of an open set is closed).
:::

**(b).**

<1>1. Let $U \subseteq G$ be open in the subspace topology.
::: {.proof}
take an arbitrary open subset of $G$.
:::

<1>2. $U$ is open in $X$.
::: {.proof}
$U = G \cap V$ for some open $V \subseteq X$, and $G$ is open, so $U$ is open in $X$.
:::

<1>3. The closure of $U$ in $G$ is $\overline U \cap G$, where $\overline U$ is the closure in $X$.
::: {.proof}
closure in a subspace.
:::

<1>4. $\overline U$ is open in $X$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and extremal disconnectedness.
:::

<1>5. Hence $\overline U \cap G$ is open in $G$, so $G$ is extremally disconnected.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and <1>4.
:::

**(c).**

<1>1. Every connected subset of a Hausdorff extremally disconnected space consists of at most one point.
<2>1. Let $C\subseteq X$ be connected and suppose $x,y\in C$ are distinct.
::: {.proof}
Assume toward a contradiction that a connected subset contains two distinct points.
:::
<2>2. Since $X$ is Hausdorff, choose disjoint open sets $U,V\subseteq X$ with $x\in U$ and $y\in V$.
::: {.proof}
Hausdorff separation.
:::
<2>3. The closure $\overline U$ is open by extremal disconnectedness, and it is closed by definition; hence $\overline U$ is clopen.
::: {.proof}
$U$ is open, so the defining hypothesis says $\overline U$ is open. Every closure is closed.
:::
<2>4. We have $x\in\overline U$ and $y\notin\overline U$.
::: {.proof}
Certainly $x\in U\subseteq\overline U$. Since $V$ is an open neighborhood of $y$ disjoint from $U$, the point $y$ is not in the closure of $U$.
:::
<2>5. Thus $C\cap\overline U$ is a nonempty proper clopen subset of $C$, contradicting connectedness of $C$.
::: {.proof}
Because $\overline U$ is clopen in $X$, its intersection with $C$ is clopen in the subspace $C$. By <2>4 it contains $x$ and omits $y$.
:::

<1>2. The image $f(\mathbb R)$ is connected.
::: {.proof}
$\mathbb R$ is connected and the continuous image of a connected space is connected.
:::

<1>3. Hence $f(\mathbb R)$ is a singleton, so $f$ is constant.
::: {.proof}
Apply <1>1 to the connected subset $f(\mathbb R)\subseteq X$ from <1>2.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 (a), <1>5 (b), <1>7 (c).
:::
:::
