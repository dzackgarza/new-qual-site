::: {.problem}
For bounded real sequences $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$, prove
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}(a_n+b_n)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_n
+
\limsup_{n\to\infty}b_n.
\]
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

For every $n$ and every $k\geq n$,
$$
a_k+b_k
\leq
\sup_{j\geq n}a_j
+
\sup_{j\geq n}b_j.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition of supremum,
$$
a_k\leq\sup_{j\geq n}a_j
$$
and
$$
b_k\leq\sup_{j\geq n}b_j.
$$
Adding these inequalities gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore, for every $n$,
$$
\sup_{k\geq n}(a_k+b_k)
\leq
\sup_{k\geq n}a_k
+
\sup_{k\geq n}b_k.
$$


::: pf-proof

The right-hand side in step <1>1 is an upper bound for every element
$a_k+b_k$ with $k\geq n$. Hence it is an upper bound for the whole tail
$\{a_k+b_k:k\geq n\}$. The least upper bound of that tail cannot exceed it.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Passing to the limit in step <1>2 gives
$$
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}(a_n+b_n)
\leq
\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_n
+
\limsup_{n\to\infty}b_n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Since the sequences are bounded, all three tail-supremum sequences are finite
and converge:
$$
\sup_{k\geq n}(a_k+b_k)
\longrightarrow
\limsup(a_n+b_n),
$$
$$
\sup_{k\geq n}a_k
\longrightarrow
\limsup a_n,
$$
and
$$
\sup_{k\geq n}b_k
\longrightarrow
\limsup b_n.
$$
The sum of the last two sequences converges to the sum of their limits.
Passing the inequality in step <1>2 to the limit yields the result.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>3 is the required inequality.

:::

:::
