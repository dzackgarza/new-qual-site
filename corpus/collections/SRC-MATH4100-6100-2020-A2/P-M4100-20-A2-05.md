::: {.problem}
(a) Explain why there is no real sequence whose set of subsequential limits is exactly
\[
\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}.
\]

(b) Give an example of a sequence whose set of subsequential limits is
\[
\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}\cup\{0\}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Suppose a real sequence $(a_n)$ has $1/m$ as a subsequential limit for
every $m\in\NN$.


::: pf-proof

This is the hypothesis needed to show that $0$ must also be a subsequential
limit.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There exists a strictly increasing sequence of indices $(n_m)$ such that
$$
\left|a_{n_m}-\frac1m\right|<\frac1m
$$
for every $m$.


::: pf-proof

Choose $n_1$ from a subsequence converging to $1$. Having chosen $n_{m-1}$,
use the fact that $1/m$ is a subsequential limit. Along some subsequence
converging to $1/m$, all sufficiently late terms satisfy
$$
\left|a_n-\frac1m\right|<\frac1m,
$$
and the indices of that subsequence tend to infinity. Hence one can choose
$n_m>n_{m-1}$ satisfying the displayed inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The subsequence $(a_{n_m})$ converges to $0$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
|a_{n_m}|
\leq
\left|a_{n_m}-\frac1m\right|+\frac1m
<
\frac2m.
$$
Since $2/m\to0$, the squeeze theorem gives
$$
a_{n_m}\longrightarrow0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

There is no sequence whose set of subsequential limits is exactly
$$
\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If all the numbers $1/n$ were subsequential limits, step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} would force
$0$ to be a subsequential limit as well. But
$$
0\notin\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}.
$$
Thus the proposed set cannot be the complete set of subsequential limits.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Define a sequence by concatenating the finite blocks
$$
(1),\quad
\left(1,\frac12\right),\quad
\left(1,\frac12,\frac13\right),\quad
\ldots.
$$


::: pf-proof

Equivalently, the $m$th block is
$$
B_m=\left(1,\frac12,\ldots,\frac1m\right).
$$
Concatenating the blocks gives a well-defined real sequence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For every fixed $j\in\NN$, the number $1/j$ is a subsequential limit
of the sequence in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

The value $1/j$ occurs once in every block $B_m$ with $m\geq j$. Hence it
occurs infinitely often. Choosing those occurrences gives a constant
subsequence equal to $1/j$, which converges to $1/j$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The number $0$ is a subsequential limit of the sequence in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

Choose from block $B_m$ its last entry $1/m$. These chosen terms occur at
strictly increasing indices and satisfy
$$
\frac1m\longrightarrow0.
$$
Hence they form a subsequence converging to $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The sequence in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} has no other subsequential limits.


::: pf-proof

Let $(a_{n_k})$ be a convergent subsequence with limit $L$. Every term of the
original sequence has the form $1/j$. Write
$$
a_{n_k}=\frac1{j_k}.
$$

If the integers $j_k$ are bounded, then some value $j$ occurs infinitely often.
Passing to that constant-denominator subsubsequence gives limit $1/j$. Since a
subsequence of a convergent sequence has the same limit,
$$
L=\frac1j.
$$

If the integers $j_k$ are unbounded, choose a further subsequence with
$$
j_{k_\ell}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$
Then
$$
a_{n_{k_\ell}}
=
\frac1{j_{k_\ell}}
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Again, this further subsequence must have the same limit $L$, so $L=0$.

Thus every subsequential limit is either $0$ or $1/j$ for some $j$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The set of subsequential limits of the sequence in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is exactly
$$
\boxed{
\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}\cup\{0\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} show that every displayed point occurs as a subsequential
limit, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} excludes all others.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives the required example and verifies its full set of
subsequential limits.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::
