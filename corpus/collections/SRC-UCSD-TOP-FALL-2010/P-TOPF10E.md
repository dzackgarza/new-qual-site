::: problem
Show that there does not exist a map of degree $1$ from $S^2 \times S^2$ to $\mathbb{CP}^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $H^2(S^2 \times S^2; \ZZ) \cong \ZZ \oplus \ZZ$, generated by $\alpha = [S^2 \times \text{pt}]^*$ and $\beta = [\text{pt} \times S^2]^*$.
Proof: Künneth theorem.

<1>2. $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2; \ZZ) \cong \ZZ$, generated by $x$ (the hyperplane class), with $x^2$ a generator of $H^4(\mathbb{CP}^2) \cong \ZZ$.
Proof: cohomology of $\mathbb{CP}^2$.

<1>3. Suppose $f : S^2 \times S^2 \to \mathbb{CP}^2$ has degree $1$.
Proof: assume such a map exists.

<1>4. Then $f^* : H^4(\mathbb{CP}^2) \to H^4(S^2 \times S^2)$ is an isomorphism (multiplication by $\deg f = 1$).
Proof: degree $1$ means $f^*$ sends the fundamental class to the fundamental class.

<1>5. Write $f^*(x) = a\alpha + b\beta$ for integers $a, b$.
Proof: <1>1.

<1>6. Then $f^*(x^2) = f^*(x)^2 = (a\alpha + b\beta)^2 = 2ab\,(\alpha \smile \beta)$.
Proof: $\alpha^2 = \beta^2 = 0$ (each is a class of a $2$-sphere, and $H^4(S^2 \times S^2) \cong \ZZ$ is generated by $\alpha \smile \beta$).

<1>7. But $f^*(x^2)$ must be a generator of $H^4(S^2 \times S^2) \cong \ZZ$, i.e. $\pm(\alpha \smile \beta)$.
Proof: <1>4 and <1>2.

<1>8. Hence $2ab = \pm 1$, which is impossible for integers $a, b$.
Proof: <1>6 and <1>7; $2ab$ is even, $\pm 1$ is odd.

<1>9. Contradiction, so no degree-$1$ map exists.
Proof: <1>8.

<1>10. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>9.
:::
