::: {.problem}
Let $f_n(x)=\dfrac{x}{1+nx^2}$, $x\in\mathbb{R}$.
Show that

(a) $f_n$ converges uniformly to a function $f$, and

(b) $f'(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n'(x)$ is true for $x\neq 0$, and false for $x=0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Identify the pointwise limit and prove uniform convergence.
::: {.proof}
For every fixed $x\in\mathbb R$,
\[
f_n(x)=\frac{x}{1+nx^2}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so the pointwise limit is
\[
f\equiv0.
\]

To compute the uniform norm, set
\[
h_n(t):=\frac{t}{1+nt^2},
\qquad t\ge0.
\]
Then
\[
h_n'(t)=\frac{1-nt^2}{(1+nt^2)^2},
\]
so $h_n$ attains its maximum at
\[
t=\frac1{\sqrt n}.
\]
Hence
\[
\|f_n\|_\infty
=\frac{1/\sqrt n}{1+n(1/n)}
=\frac1{2\sqrt n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{f_n\to0\text{ uniformly on }\mathbb R.}
\]
:::

<1>2. Compare the derivatives.
::: {.proof}
Differentiating,
\[
f_n'(x)=\frac{1-nx^2}{(1+nx^2)^2}.
\]
If $x\neq0$, then
\[
f_n'(x)
=\frac{n^{-1}-x^2}{n^{-1}(1+nx^2)^2}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
more directly because the numerator is of order $n$ and the denominator of order $n^2$. Thus
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n'(x)=0=f'(x)
\qquad(x\neq0).
\]

At $x=0$,
\[
f_n'(0)=1
\qquad\text{for every }n,
\]
whereas
\[
f'(0)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
f'(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n'(x)
\text{ for }x\neq0,
\quad\text{but not for }x=0.}
\]
:::
:::
