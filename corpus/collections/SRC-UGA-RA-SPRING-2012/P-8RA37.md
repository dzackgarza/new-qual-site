::: {.problem}
Let $V$ be the vector space of absolutely continuous functions on $[0,1]$ endowed with the norm
$$
\norm{f}:=\int_0^1\abs{f(t)}\,dt+\int_0^1\abs{f'(t)}\,dt,\qquad f\in V.
$$
Show that $(V,\norm{\cdot})$ is a Banach space.
You do not need to prove that $\norm{\cdot}$ is a norm.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Show that the norm controls the value at the left endpoint.

::: pf-proof

Let $f\in V$. For every $x\in[0,1]$, absolute continuity gives
$$
f(0)=f(x)-\int_0^x f'(t)\,dt.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{f(0)}\le \abs{f(x)}+\int_0^1\abs{f'(t)}\,dt.
$$
Integrating this inequality in $x$ over $[0,1]$ yields
$$
\abs{f(0)}
\le
\norm{f}_{L^1}+\norm{f'}_{L^1}
=
\norm{f}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Extract limits of the initial values and derivatives.

::: pf-proof

Let $(f_n)$ be Cauchy in $V$. Then
$$
\norm{f_n'-f_m'}_1\le \norm{f_n-f_m},
$$
so $(f_n')$ is Cauchy in $L^1([0,1])$. Since $L^1$ is complete, there exists $g\in L^1([0,1])$ such that
$$
f_n'\to g
\quad\text{in }L^1.
$$

By Step 1,
$$
\abs{f_n(0)-f_m(0)}
\le
\norm{f_n-f_m},
$$
so $(f_n(0))$ is Cauchy in $\RR$. Let
$$
f_n(0)\to c.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Reconstruct the candidate limit.

::: pf-proof

Define
$$
f(x):=c+\int_0^x g(t)\,dt.
$$
Then $f$ is absolutely continuous on $[0,1]$ and
$$
f'=g
$$
almost everywhere.

Also, since
$$
f_n(x)=f_n(0)+\int_0^x f_n'(t)\,dt,
$$
we have for every $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
\abs{f_n(x)-f(x)}
\le
\abs{f_n(0)-c}+\norm{f_n'-g}_1.
$$
Therefore
$$
\norm{f_n-f}_\infty
\le
\abs{f_n(0)-c}+\norm{f_n'-g}_1
\longrightarrow0.
$$
In particular,
$$
\norm{f_n-f}_1\to0.
$$
Together with $f_n'\to g=f'$ in $L^1$, this gives
$$
\norm{f_n-f}
=
\norm{f_n-f}_1+\norm{f_n'-f'}_1
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Thus every Cauchy sequence in $V$ converges in $V$, so
$$
\boxed{(V,\norm{\cdot})\text{ is a Banach space}.}
$$

:::
