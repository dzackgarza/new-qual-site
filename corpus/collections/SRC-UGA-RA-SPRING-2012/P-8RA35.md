::: {.problem}
Let $f\colon\RR\to\RR$ be a nonnegative integrable function.

(a) Show that $\sin\circ f$ is integrable.

(b) Use Fubini's theorem to show that
$$
\int_{[0,\infty)} m(\theset{x \suchthat f(x)\ge y})\cos y\,dy=\int_{\RR}\sin\qty(f(x))\,dx.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #sin-integrable}

The function $\sin\circ f$ is integrable, with $\int_\RR\abs{\sin(f(x))}\,dx\le\int_\RR f(x)\,dx$.

::: pf-proof

The function $\sin\circ f$ is measurable as a continuous function of a measurable function.
Since $f\ge0$ and $\abs{\sin t}\le t$ for $t\ge0$, we have $\abs{\sin(f(x))}\le f(x)$ for every $x$.
Because $f\in L^1(\RR)$,
$$
\int_{\RR}\abs{\sin(f(x))}\,dx
\le
\int_{\RR}f(x)\,dx<\infty.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product-integrable}

The function $H(x,y)\coloneqq\mathbf1_{\theset{0\le y\le f(x)}}\cos y$ on $\RR\times[0,\infty)$ is integrable.

::: pf-proof

The set $\theset{(x,y) \suchthat 0\le y\le f(x)}$ is measurable, being the intersection of $\RR\times[0,\infty)$ with the preimage of $[0,\infty)$ under the measurable function $(x,y)\mapsto f(x)-y$, so $H$ is measurable.
Moreover $\abs{H(x,y)}\le \mathbf1_{\theset{0\le y\le f(x)}}$, so by Tonelli's theorem
$$
\int_{\RR}\int_0^\infty \abs{H(x,y)}\,dy\,dx
\le
\int_{\RR}\int_0^{f(x)}1\,dy\,dx
=
\int_{\RR}f(x)\,dx<\infty.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #two-orders}

The two iterated integrals of $H$ are $\int_{\RR}\sin(f(x))\,dx$ and $\int_0^\infty m(\theset{x \suchthat f(x)\ge y})\cos y\,dy$.

::: pf-proof

Integrating first in $y$ gives
$$
\int_0^\infty H(x,y)\,dy
=
\int_0^{f(x)}\cos y\,dy
=
\sin(f(x)).
$$
For fixed $y\ge0$,
$$
\int_{\RR}H(x,y)\,dx
=
\cos y\;m(\theset{x \suchthat f(x)\ge y}).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Part (a) is step [](#sin-integrable){.pf-ref}.
By step [](#product-integrable){.pf-ref}, Fubini's theorem applies to $H$, so its two iterated integrals in step [](#two-orders){.pf-ref} agree:
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty m(\theset{x \suchthat f(x)\ge y})\cos y\,dy
=
\int_{\RR}\sin(f(x))\,dx.}
$$

:::
