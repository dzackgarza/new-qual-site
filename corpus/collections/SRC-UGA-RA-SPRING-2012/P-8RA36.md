::: {.problem}
Let $p\in[1,\infty)$, and suppose that $f:\RR\to\RR$ is an absolutely continuous function such that $\norm{f'}_p<\infty$.

(a) Prove that $f$ is Hölder continuous with exponent $\dfrac{p-1}{p}$, i.e. there is a constant $C$ such that
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le C\abs{x-y}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.
$$

(b) Write down the corresponding statement when $p=\infty$ and prove your assertion.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Prove the estimate for finite $p$.

::: pf-proof

Let $x<y$. Since $f$ is absolutely continuous,
$$
f(y)-f(x)=\int_x^y f'(t)\,dt.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}
\le
\int_x^y \abs{f'(t)}\,dt.
$$
If $1<p<\infty$, let $q=p/(p-1)$. Hölder's inequality gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}
&\le
\left(\int_x^y \abs{f'(t)}^p\,dt\right)^{1/p}
\left(\int_x^y1^q\,dt\right)^{1/q}\\
&\le
\norm{f'}_p\,\abs{y-x}^{1/q}\\
&=
\norm{f'}_p\,\abs{y-x}^{(p-1)/p}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $f$ is Hölder continuous of exponent $(p-1)/p$, with $C=\norm{f'}_p$.

When $p=1$, the same fundamental-theorem estimate gives
$$
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}
\le
\int_x^y\abs{f'(t)}\,dt
\le
\norm{f'}_1,
$$
which is precisely the exponent-$0$ form of the displayed estimate.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Handle $p=\infty$.

::: pf-proof

Assume $f'\in L^\infty(\RR)$. Then for $x<y$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}
&\le \int_x^y \abs{f'(t)}\,dt\\
&\le \norm{f'}_\infty \abs{y-x}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore $f$ is Lipschitz continuous, i.e. Hölder continuous of exponent $1$, with Lipschitz constant at most $\norm{f'}_\infty$.

:::
