::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that if
$$
f,g:\mathcal P(n)\longrightarrow\RR
$$
are real-valued functions on the power set of $[n]$, then
$$
g(A)=\sum_{B\subseteq A}f(B)
\qquad\text{for every }A\subseteq[n]
$$
if and only if
$$
f(A)=\sum_{B\subseteq A}(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}g(B)
\qquad\text{for every }A\subseteq[n].
$$

(b) A *subcube* of $\mathcal P(n)$ is a subset
$$
\mathcal C_{A,B}=\{C\subseteq[n]:A\subseteq C\subseteq B\}
$$
for some $A\subseteq B\subseteq[n]$.
It is *trivial* if $A=B$, and hence $|\mathcal C|=1$.

Characterize all functions
$$
h:\mathcal P(n)\longrightarrow\RR
$$
such that for every nontrivial subcube $\mathcal C$ of $\mathcal P(n)$,
$$
\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}h(C)=0.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Assume first that
$$
g(A)
=
\sum_{B\subseteq A}f(B)
$$
for every
$$
A\subseteq[n].
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the forward hypothesis in part (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For fixed
$$
A\subseteq[n],
$$
consider
$$
S(A)
\coloneqq
\sum_{B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}g(B).
$$
Substituting the expression from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
S(A)
=
\sum_{C\subseteq A}
f(C)
\sum_{C\subseteq B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Indeed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
S(A)
&=
\sum_{B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}
\sum_{C\subseteq B}f(C)\\
&=
\sum_{C\subseteq A}
f(C)
\sum_{C\subseteq B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|},
\end{aligned}
$$
where the order of summation is exchanged over a finite set of pairs
$$
(C,B)
$$
with
$$
C\subseteq B\subseteq A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For
$$
C\subseteq A,
$$
one has
$$
\sum_{C\subseteq B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}
=
\begin{cases}
1,&C=A,\\
0,&C\ne A.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

Set
$$
E=A\setminus C.
$$
Every intermediate set
$$
B
$$
has the form
$$
B=C\cup D
$$
for a unique
$$
D\subseteq E.
$$
Then
$$
|A\setminus B|
=
|E\setminus D|
=
|E|-|D|.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{C\subseteq B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}
&=
\sum_{D\subseteq E}
(-1)^{|E|-|D|}\\
&=
(-1)^{|E|}
\sum_{D\subseteq E}
(-1)^{|D|}\\
&=
(-1)^{|E|}
(1-1)^{|E|}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is
$$
1
$$
when
$$
E=\varnothing,
$$
i.e. when
$$
C=A,
$$
and is
$$
0
$$
otherwise.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
f(A)
=
\sum_{B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}g(B)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} into the expression in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. Every term with
$$
C\ne A
$$
vanishes, while the
$$
C=A
$$
term contributes
$$
f(A).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Conversely, assume
$$
f(A)
=
\sum_{B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}g(B)
$$
for every
$$
A\subseteq[n].
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
g(A)
=
\sum_{B\subseteq A}f(B)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compute
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{B\subseteq A}f(B)
&=
\sum_{B\subseteq A}
\sum_{C\subseteq B}
(-1)^{|B\setminus C|}g(C)\\
&=
\sum_{C\subseteq A}
g(C)
\sum_{C\subseteq B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|B\setminus C|}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For fixed
$$
C,
$$
write
$$
B=C\cup D
$$
with
$$
D\subseteq A\setminus C.
$$
Then the inner sum is
$$
\sum_{D\subseteq A\setminus C}
(-1)^{|D|}
=
(1-1)^{|A\setminus C|},
$$
which is
$$
1
$$
if
$$
C=A
$$
and
$$
0
$$
otherwise.

Thus only the term
$$
C=A
$$
survives, giving
$$
\sum_{B\subseteq A}f(B)
=
g(A).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence the two formulas in part (a) are equivalent.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves one implication and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves the converse.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For part (b), define
$$
G(B)
\coloneqq
\sum_{C\subseteq B}h(C).
$$
Then the hypothesis implies
$$
\boxed{
G(B)=0
}
$$
for every nonempty
$$
B\subseteq[n].
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
B\ne\varnothing,
$$
then the subcube
$$
\mathcal C_{\varnothing,B}
=
\{
C:
\varnothing\subseteq C\subseteq B
\}
$$
is nontrivial.

The assumed zero-sum property therefore gives
$$
0
=
\sum_{C\in\mathcal C_{\varnothing,B}}h(C)
=
\sum_{C\subseteq B}h(C)
=
G(B).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Let
$$
c
\coloneqq
h(\varnothing).
$$
Then
$$
G(\varnothing)=c.
$$


::: pf-proof

The only subset of
$$
\varnothing
$$
is
$$
\varnothing
$$
itself, so
$$
G(\varnothing)
=
\sum_{C\subseteq\varnothing}h(C)
=
h(\varnothing)
=
c.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Applying part (a) to
$$
G(B)
=
\sum_{C\subseteq B}h(C)
$$
gives
$$
h(A)
=
\sum_{B\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}G(B).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the inversion formula established in steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, with
$$
f=h
$$
and
$$
g=G.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
h(A)
=
c(-1)^{|A|}}
$$
for every
$$
A\subseteq[n].
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
G(B)=0
$$
for every nonempty
$$
B.
$$
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
G(\varnothing)=c.
$$
Thus in the sum from step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} only
$$
B=\varnothing
$$
survives:
$$
h(A)
=
(-1)^{|A\setminus\varnothing|}
c
=
c(-1)^{|A|}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Conversely, every function of the form
$$
h(A)=c(-1)^{|A|}
$$
satisfies the required zero-sum condition on every nontrivial subcube.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
A\subsetneq B.
$$
Every
$$
C
$$
with
$$
A\subseteq C\subseteq B
$$
has the form
$$
C=A\cup D
$$
for a unique
$$
D\subseteq B\setminus A.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{A\subseteq C\subseteq B}h(C)
&=
c
\sum_{D\subseteq B\setminus A}
(-1)^{|A|+|D|}\\
&=
c(-1)^{|A|}
\sum_{D\subseteq B\setminus A}
(-1)^{|D|}\\
&=
c(-1)^{|A|}
(1-1)^{|B\setminus A|}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because
$$
A\subsetneq B,
$$
one has
$$
|B\setminus A|>0,
$$
so the final expression is
$$
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Hence the complete characterization in part (b) is
$$
\boxed{
h(A)=c(-1)^{|A|}
\text{ for some constant }c\in\RR.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} proves necessity and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} proves sufficiency.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
