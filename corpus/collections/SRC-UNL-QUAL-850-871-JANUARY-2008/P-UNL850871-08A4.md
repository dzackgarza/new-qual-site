::: {.problem}
(a) State and prove Burnside's lemma concerning the number of orbits of a group action.

(b) Identity cards are made by taking square cards ruled into a $7\times7$ grid and punching out two of the squares.
A card can be inserted into a scanner in any orientation.
How many different identity cards can be produced this way?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Burnside's lemma states:
$$
\boxed{
\cardinality{X/G}
=
\frac1{\cardinality{G}}
\sum_{g\in G}
\cardinality{\operatorname{Fix}(g)}}
$$
for every action of a finite group
$$
G
$$
on a finite set
$$
X,
$$
where
$$
\operatorname{Fix}(g)
\coloneqq
\{x\in X:g\cdot x=x\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
S
\coloneqq
\{
(g,x)\in G\times X:
g\cdot x=x
\}.
$$
Count
$$
S
$$
in two ways.

First, fixing
$$
g
$$
gives
$$
\cardinality{S}
=
\sum_{g\in G}
\cardinality{\operatorname{Fix}(g)}.
$$

Second, fixing
$$
x
$$
gives the stabilizer
$$
G_x,
$$
so
$$
\cardinality{S}
=
\sum_{x\in X}\cardinality{G_x}.
$$
Partition
$$
X
$$
into orbits. If
$$
\mathcal O
$$
is one orbit and
$$
x\in\mathcal O,
$$
then orbit-stabilizer gives
$$
\cardinality{G_x}
=
\frac{\cardinality{G}}{\cardinality{\mathcal O}}.
$$
Thus the contribution of all points of
$$
\mathcal O
$$
to the second sum is
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal O}
\frac{\cardinality{G}}{\cardinality{\mathcal O}}
=
\cardinality{G}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\cardinality{S}
=
\cardinality{G}\,\cardinality{X/G}.
$$
Comparing the two expressions for
$$
\cardinality{S}
$$
and dividing by
$$
\cardinality{G}
$$
proves the formula.

:::

::: pf-step

For part (b), let
$$
\Omega
$$
be the set of unordered pairs of distinct squares in the
$$
7\times7
$$
grid. Then
$$
\cardinality{\Omega}
=
\binom{49}{2}
=
1176.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are
$$
49
$$
grid squares, and an identity card is determined before quotienting by symmetry by choosing two distinct squares to punch.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
D_4
$$
act on
$$
\Omega
$$
by the eight symmetries of the square.


::: pf-proof

The full symmetry group consists of:

1. the identity;
2. rotations by
$$
90^\circ,
180^\circ,
270^\circ;
$$
3. four reflections.

Two punched cards are scanner-equivalent exactly when one hole pattern is carried to the other by such a symmetry, under the interpretation that a card may be inserted in any spatial orientation, including being turned over.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The identity fixes all
$$
1176
$$
two-hole patterns.


::: pf-proof

Immediate from the definition of the identity action.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

A rotation by
$$
90^\circ
$$
or
$$
270^\circ
$$
fixes no two-hole pattern.


::: pf-proof

Under a quarter-turn, the center square is fixed and every other square lies in an orbit of length
$$
4.
$$

An invariant two-element subset would have to be a union of whole square-orbits whose total size is
$$
2.
$$
There is only one orbit of size
$$
1
$$
and no orbit of size
$$
2.
$$
Hence no two-element invariant subset exists.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The
$$
180^\circ
$$
rotation fixes exactly
$$
\boxed{24}
$$
two-hole patterns.


::: pf-proof

The center square is fixed.
The other
$$
48
$$
squares are paired into
$$
24
$$
two-cycles under half-turn rotation.

A two-element subset is invariant exactly when it is one of those two-cycles; there are not two distinct fixed squares to choose.
Thus there are
$$
24
$$
fixed patterns.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Each reflection fixes exactly
$$
\boxed{42}
$$
two-hole patterns.


::: pf-proof

For any of the four reflection axes of a
$$
7\times7
$$
square grid, exactly
$$
7
$$
squares lie on the axis and are fixed.
The remaining
$$
42
$$
squares form
$$
21
$$
two-cycles.

An invariant two-hole pattern is of exactly one of two types:

1. choose two distinct fixed squares on the axis:
$$
\binom72
=
21
$$
choices;
2. choose both squares from one reflected pair:
$$
21
$$
choices.

Hence each reflection fixes
$$
21+21
=
42
$$
patterns.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Burnside's lemma gives
$$
\boxed{
\frac{
1176+0+24+0+4\cdot42
}{8}
=
171}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The eight group elements contribute fixed-set sizes
$$
1176,
\quad
0,
\quad
24,
\quad
0,
\quad
42,42,42,42.
$$
Their sum is
$$
1176+24+168
=
1368.
$$
Dividing by
$$
\cardinality{D_4}=8
$$
gives
$$
1368/8
=
171.
$$
Thus there are
$$
171
$$
scanner-distinct cards under the full square-symmetry convention.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If “orientation” were intended to allow rotations but not turning the card over, the corresponding count would instead be
$$
\boxed{300}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The rotation subgroup has order
$$
4.
$$
Using only the fixed counts for the identity and the three rotations,
$$
\frac{1176+0+24+0}{4}
=
300.
$$
The source does not state a face-up restriction, so step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the natural physical-card interpretation.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves Burnside's lemma, and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the identity-card count under the full symmetry convention.
