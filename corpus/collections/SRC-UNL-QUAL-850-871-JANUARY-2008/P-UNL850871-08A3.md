::: {.problem}
Suppose $0\le t<n/2$ and $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$ is an antichain such that $\cardinality{A}\le t$ for all $A\in\mathcal A$.
Define
$$
\mathcal A_t
=
\left\{
B\in\binom{[n]}{t}:
\text{there is some }A\in\mathcal A\text{ with }A\subseteq B
\right\}.
$$
Prove that
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal A}\le \cardinality{\mathcal A_t}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

A maximal chain in
$$
\mathcal P(n)
$$
has the form
$$
\varnothing
=
C_0
\subset
C_1
\subset
\cdots
\subset
C_n
=
[n],
$$
with
$$
\cardinality{C_j}=j
$$
for every
$$
j.
$$
There are exactly
$$
n!
$$
maximal chains.


::: pf-proof

Every permutation
$$
(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
$$
of
$$
[n]
$$
determines the chain
$$
C_j
=
\{x_1,\ldots,x_j\}.
$$
Conversely, a maximal chain records the unique element added at each step, hence determines a permutation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

A fixed set
$$
A\subseteq[n]
$$
of size
$$
k
$$
lies on exactly
$$
\boxed{
k!(n-k)!
}
$$
maximal chains.


::: pf-proof

In the permutation model from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the chain contains
$$
A
$$
at rank
$$
k
$$
exactly when the first
$$
k
$$
entries of the permutation are precisely the elements of
$$
A.
$$

Those
$$
k
$$
elements may be ordered in
$$
k!
$$
ways, and the remaining
$$
n-k
$$
elements may be ordered in
$$
(n-k)!
$$
ways.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

No maximal chain contains two distinct members of
$$
\mathcal A.
$$


::: pf-proof

Any two sets on the same chain are comparable by inclusion.
Since
$$
\mathcal A
$$
is an antichain, two distinct members of
$$
\mathcal A
$$
are never comparable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore the number
$$
N
$$
of maximal chains containing some member of
$$
\mathcal A
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
N
=
\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}
\cardinality{A}!(n-\cardinality{A})!}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives the number of maximal chains containing each fixed
$$
A\in\mathcal A.
$$
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that these collections of maximal chains are pairwise disjoint, so their cardinalities add.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Every maximal chain counted by
$$
N
$$
contains a member of
$$
\mathcal A_t
$$
at rank
$$
t.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let a counted maximal chain contain
$$
A\in\mathcal A.
$$
By hypothesis,
$$
\cardinality{A}\le t.
$$

The unique rank-
$$
t
$$
member
$$
T
$$
of that maximal chain contains every lower-rank member of the chain, in particular
$$
A.
$$
Thus
$$
A\subseteq T,
\qquad
\cardinality{T}=t.
$$
By the definition of
$$
\mathcal A_t,
$$
this means
$$
T\in\mathcal A_t.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The number of maximal chains whose rank-
$$
t
$$
member belongs to
$$
\mathcal A_t
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
\cardinality{\mathcal A_t}\,t!(n-t)!}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every fixed
$$
t
$$
-set belongs to
$$
t!(n-t)!
$$
maximal chains by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

Different
$$
t
$$
-sets cannot lie on the same maximal chain, because each maximal chain has exactly one set of rank
$$
t.
$$
Therefore the counts for distinct members of
$$
\mathcal A_t
$$
are disjoint and add.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Hence
$$
\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}
\cardinality{A}!(n-\cardinality{A})!
\le
\cardinality{\mathcal A_t}\,t!(n-t)!.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, every maximal chain counted on the left is among the maximal chains counted in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.
Substitute the formula for
$$
N
$$
from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

If
$$
k\le t<n/2,
$$
then
$$
\boxed{
k!(n-k)!
\ge
t!(n-t)!}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The binomial coefficients
$$
\binom{n}{j}
$$
increase with
$$
j
$$
for
$$
0\le j<n/2.
$$
Indeed,
$$
\frac{\binom n{j+1}}{\binom nj}
=
\frac{n-j}{j+1}
>1
$$
when
$$
j<n/2.
$$

Thus
$$
\binom nk
\le
\binom nt.
$$
Using
$$
k!(n-k)!
=
\frac{n!}{\binom nk}
$$
and the analogous identity for
$$
t,
$$
the inequality reverses:
$$
k!(n-k)!
\ge
t!(n-t)!.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\cardinality{\mathcal A}
\le
\cardinality{\mathcal A_t}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every
$$
A\in\mathcal A,
$$
one has
$$
\cardinality{A}\le t.
$$
Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} therefore gives
$$
\cardinality{A}!(n-\cardinality{A})!
\ge
t!(n-t)!.
$$
Summing over
$$
A\in\mathcal A
$$
gives
$$
\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}
\cardinality{A}!(n-\cardinality{A})!
\ge
\cardinality{\mathcal A}\,t!(n-t)!.
$$
Combine this with step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}:
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal A}\,t!(n-t)!
\le
\cardinality{\mathcal A_t}\,t!(n-t)!.
$$
Since
$$
t!(n-t)!>0,
$$
cancel it to obtain
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal A}
\le
\cardinality{\mathcal A_t}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is the required inequality.
