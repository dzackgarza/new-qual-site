::: {.problem}
(a) How many increasing functions are there which map
$$
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
$$
to $[m]$?
The functions are not required to be strictly increasing.

(b) Give a closed-form expression for
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{n-k}{m-k}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

A weakly increasing function
$$
f:[n]\to[m]
$$
is equivalent to a weakly increasing sequence
$$
1\le
f(1)
\le
f(2)
\le
\cdots
\le
f(n)
\le
m.
$$


::: pf-proof

The function is determined by the ordered list of its values
$$
f(1),\ldots,f(n).
$$
The condition that
$$
f
$$
is increasing in the non-strict sense is exactly the displayed chain of inequalities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Such a function is determined uniquely by the multiplicities
$$
x_j
\coloneqq
|f^{-1}(j)|,
\qquad
1\le j\le m.
$$


::: pf-proof

The integers
$$
x_j
$$
are nonnegative and satisfy
$$
x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_m=n.
$$

Conversely, given such a tuple, the unique weakly increasing function with these multiplicities is obtained by assigning the first
$$
x_1
$$
inputs the value
$$
1,
$$
the next
$$
x_2
$$
inputs the value
$$
2,
$$
and so on.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The number of weakly increasing functions
$$
[n]\to[m]
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
\binom{n+m-1}{m-1}
=
\binom{n+m-1}{n}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the problem is to count the nonnegative integer solutions of
$$
x_1+\cdots+x_m=n.
$$
By the stars-and-bars formula, the number of such solutions is
$$
\binom{n+m-1}{m-1}.
$$
The second expression follows from symmetry of binomial coefficients.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For part (b), set
$$
j=m-k.
$$
Then
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{m}
\binom{n-k}{m-k}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{m}
\binom{n-m+j}{j}.
$$


::: pf-proof

When
$$
k=0,
$$
one has
$$
j=m,
$$
and when
$$
k=m,
$$
one has
$$
j=0.
$$
Also,
$$
n-k
=
n-(m-j)
=
n-m+j,
$$
while
$$
m-k=j.
$$
Reversing the order of summation gives the displayed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The hockey-stick identity gives
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{k=0}^{m}
\binom{n-k}{m-k}
=
\binom{n+1}{m}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
r=n-m.
$$
Then step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} becomes
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{m}
\binom{r+j}{j}.
$$
Using symmetry,
$$
\binom{r+j}{j}
=
\binom{r+j}{r}.
$$
The hockey-stick identity says
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{m}
\binom{r+j}{r}
=
\binom{r+m+1}{r+1}.
$$
Since
$$
r+m=n,
$$
this is
$$
\binom{n+1}{r+1}
=
\binom{n+1}{m}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} solves part (a), and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} solves part (b).
