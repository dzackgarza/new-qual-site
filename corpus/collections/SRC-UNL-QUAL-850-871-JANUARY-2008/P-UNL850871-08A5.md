::: {.problem}
Given integers $0<d\le n$ and a prime power $q$, prove that there exists a linear code
$$
C\subseteq\FF_q^n
$$
of minimum distance at least $d$, containing at least
$$
\frac{q^n}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\binom ni(q-1)^i}
$$
codewords.
:::

::: {.solution}

For
$$
x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\FF_q^n,
$$
write
$$
\operatorname{wt}(x)
$$
for its Hamming weight, and write
$$
d_H(x,y)
=
\operatorname{wt}(x-y)
$$
for Hamming distance.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

There exists a linear subspace
$$
C\le\FF_q^n
$$
which is maximal under inclusion among linear subspaces satisfying
$$
\operatorname{wt}(c)\ge d
$$
for every nonzero
$$
c\in C.
$$


::: pf-proof

The zero subspace satisfies the condition vacuously.
Since
$$
\FF_q^n
$$
has only finitely many subspaces, there is at least one such subspace maximal under inclusion.

For a linear code, the minimum distance equals the minimum Hamming weight of a nonzero codeword, so this condition is exactly
$$
d_{min}(C)\ge d.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every
$$
x\in\FF_q^n,
$$
there exists
$$
c\in C
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{
d_H(x,c)\le d-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose not.
Then there is some
$$
x\in\FF_q^n
$$
such that
$$
d_H(x,c)
\ge
d
$$
for every
$$
c\in C.
$$

In particular
$$
x\notin C,
$$
because otherwise taking
$$
c=x
$$
would give distance
$$
0.
$$

Consider the larger subspace
$$
C'
\coloneqq
C+\FF_q x.
$$
It strictly contains
$$
C.
$$

We claim every nonzero vector of
$$
C'
$$
has Hamming weight at least
$$
d.
$$
Let
$$
y=c+\alpha x
$$
with
$$
c\in C,
\qquad
\alpha\in\FF_q.
$$

If
$$
\alpha=0,
$$
then
$$
y=c,
$$
and the desired bound follows from the defining property of
$$
C.
$$

If
$$
\alpha\ne0,
$$
then scalar multiplication by
$$
\alpha^{-1}
$$
does not change Hamming weight, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{wt}(y)
&=
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha^{-1}c+x)\\
&=
d_H(x,-\alpha^{-1}c).
\end{aligned}
$$
Because
$$
-\alpha^{-1}c\in C,
$$
the assumed distance bound gives
$$
\operatorname{wt}(y)\ge d.
$$

Thus
$$
C'
$$
is a strictly larger linear code with minimum distance at least
$$
d,
$$
contradicting maximality of
$$
C.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Hence the Hamming balls
$$
B(c,d-1)
\coloneqq
\{
x\in\FF_q^n:
d_H(x,c)\le d-1
\},
\qquad
c\in C,
$$
cover
$$
\FF_q^n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} says exactly that every vector
$$
x\in\FF_q^n
$$
belongs to at least one of these balls.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Every Hamming ball of radius
$$
d-1
$$
in
$$
\FF_q^n
$$
has cardinality
$$
\boxed{
V_q(n,d-1)
=
\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}
\binom ni(q-1)^i}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By translation invariance of Hamming distance, all such balls have the same size as the ball centered at
$$
0.
$$

A vector has Hamming weight exactly
$$
i
$$
if:

1. its
$$
i
$$
nonzero coordinate positions are chosen in
$$
\binom ni
$$
ways;
2. each chosen coordinate is assigned one of the
$$
q-1
$$
nonzero field elements.

Thus there are
$$
\binom ni(q-1)^i
$$
vectors of weight exactly
$$
i.
$$
Summing for
$$
0\le i\le d-1
$$
gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Since the balls in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} cover the ambient space,
$$
q^n
\le
|C|
\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}
\binom ni(q-1)^i.
$$


::: pf-proof

The ambient space has cardinality
$$
q^n.
$$
The union of the
$$
|C|
$$
balls has cardinality at most the sum of their cardinalities, even if the balls overlap.
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, each ball has size
$$
\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}
\binom ni(q-1)^i.
$$
Therefore
$$
q^n
=
\left|
\bigcup_{c\in C}
B(c,d-1)
\right|
\le
\sum_{c\in C}
|B(c,d-1)|
$$
which is the stated inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
|C|
\ge
\frac{
q^n
}{
\displaystyle
\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}
\binom ni(q-1)^i
}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The denominator is positive, so divide the inequality in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} by it.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} constructs a linear code with minimum distance at least
$$
d,
$$
and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives the required lower bound on its number of codewords.

:::
