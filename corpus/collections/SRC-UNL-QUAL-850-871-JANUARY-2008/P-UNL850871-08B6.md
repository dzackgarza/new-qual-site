::: {.problem}
(a) Let $A$ be a subset of a topological space $X$.
Prove that both
$$
\overline A\setminus A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A\setminus\operatorname{int}A
$$
have empty interior.

(b) A family $\mathcal A$ of subsets of a topological space $X$ is *locally finite* if for every $x\in X$ there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that $U$ meets only finitely many members of $\mathcal A$.
If $\mathcal A$ is locally finite, prove that
$$
\overline{\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}A}
=
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}\overline A.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The set
$$
\overline A\setminus A
$$
has empty interior.


::: pf-proof

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that there exists a nonempty open set
$$
U
\subseteq
\overline A\setminus A.
$$
Choose
$$
x\in U.
$$
Because
$$
x\in\overline A,
$$
every open neighborhood of
$$
x
$$
must meet
$$
A.
$$
But
$$
U
$$
is an open neighborhood of
$$
x
$$
and
$$
U\cap A
=
\varnothing,
$$
because
$$
U\subseteq\overline A\setminus A.
$$
This is a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The set
$$
A\setminus\operatorname{int}A
$$
has empty interior.


::: pf-proof

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that there is a nonempty open set
$$
U
\subseteq
A\setminus\operatorname{int}A.
$$
Then
$$
U
$$
is open and
$$
U\subseteq A.
$$
By definition of the interior, every open subset of
$$
A
$$
is contained in
$$
\operatorname{int}A.
$$
Hence
$$
U
\subseteq
\operatorname{int}A,
$$
contradicting
$$
U
\subseteq
A\setminus\operatorname{int}A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every family
$$
\mathcal A
$$
of subsets of
$$
X,
$$
one always has
$$
\boxed{
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}\overline A
\subseteq
\overline{
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}A
}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every
$$
A\in\mathcal A,
$$
one has
$$
A
\subseteq
\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal A}B.
$$
Monotonicity of closure gives
$$
\overline A
\subseteq
\overline{
\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal A}B
}.
$$
Taking the union over
$$
A\in\mathcal A
$$
gives the inclusion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Assume now that
$$
\mathcal A
$$
is locally finite, and let
$$
x
\notin
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}\overline A.
$$
Then there is a neighborhood
$$
U
$$
of
$$
x
$$
which meets only finitely many members of
$$
\mathcal A.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the definition of local finiteness.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Let
$$
A_1,\ldots,A_m
$$
be all members of
$$
\mathcal A
$$
which meet
$$
U.
$$
For each
$$
i,
$$
there is an open neighborhood
$$
V_i
$$
of
$$
x
$$
with
$$
V_i\cap A_i
=
\varnothing.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
x
\notin
\overline{A_i},
$$
the definition of closure gives an open neighborhood
$$
V_i
$$
of
$$
x
$$
which does not meet
$$
A_i.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The set
$$
W
\coloneqq
U\cap V_1\cap\cdots\cap V_m
$$
is an open neighborhood of
$$
x
$$
which misses every member of
$$
\mathcal A.
$$


::: pf-proof

The intersection is finite, so
$$
W
$$
is open and contains
$$
x.
$$

If
$$
A\in\mathcal A
$$
is one of
$$
A_1,\ldots,A_m,
$$
then
$$
W\subseteq V_i
$$
for the corresponding
$$
i,
$$
so
$$
W\cap A
=
\varnothing.
$$

If
$$
A
$$
is not among
$$
A_1,\ldots,A_m,
$$
then by definition it does not meet
$$
U.
$$
Since
$$
W\subseteq U,
$$
it also does not meet
$$
W.
$$

Thus
$$
W
$$
meets no member of
$$
\mathcal A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
x
\notin
\overline{
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}A
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives an open neighborhood
$$
W
$$
of
$$
x
$$
with
$$
W
\cap
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}A
=
\varnothing.
$$
This is exactly the criterion for
$$
x
$$
not to belong to the closure of the union.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence
$$
\overline{
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}A
}
\subseteq
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}\overline A.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves the contrapositive:
if
$$
x
$$
does not belong to the right-hand side, then it does not belong to the left-hand side.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Combining steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\boxed{
\overline{
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}A
}
=
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A}\overline A
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives one inclusion and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the reverse inclusion.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} prove part (a), and steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} prove part (b).

:::
