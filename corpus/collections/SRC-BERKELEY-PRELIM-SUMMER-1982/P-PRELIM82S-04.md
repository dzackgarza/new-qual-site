    gives a^{27}=b^{-2}a^8b^2=a^{18}, so a^9=1. A second two-step

::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be generated by $a,b$ subject to
\[
a^{-1}b^2a=b^3,
\qquad
b^{-1}a^2b=a^3.
\]
Is $G$ trivial?
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
For every integer $m$,
$$
b^{-1}a^{2m}b=a^{3m}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The second defining relation gives
$$
b^{-1}a^2b=a^3.
$$
Conjugation is an automorphism, so
$$
b^{-1}a^{2m}b
=
\left(b^{-1}a^2b\right)^m
=
a^{3m}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}
One has
$$
b^{-3}a^8b^3=a^{27}.
$$

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s2-1}
One has
$$
b^{-1}a^8b=a^{12}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Apply step [](#s1){.pf-ref} with $m=4$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-2}
One has
$$
b^{-2}a^8b^2=a^{18}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#s2-1){.pf-ref},
$$
b^{-2}a^8b^2
=
b^{-1}a^{12}b.
$$
Apply step [](#s1){.pf-ref} with $m=6$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-3}
One has
$$
b^{-3}a^8b^3=a^{27}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#s2-2){.pf-ref},
$$
b^{-3}a^8b^3
=
b^{-1}a^{18}b.
$$
Apply step [](#s1){.pf-ref} with $m=9$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#s2-3){.pf-ref} is the claim of step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}
The first defining relation implies
$$
ab^3=b^2a
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b^{-3}a^{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-2}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Multiplying
$$
a^{-1}b^2a=b^3
$$
on the left by $a$ gives
$$
b^2a=ab^3.
$$
Taking inverses gives
$$
b^{-3}a^{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-2}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}
One has
$$
a^{27}=b^{-2}a^8b^2.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{27}
&=b^{-3}a^8b^3\\
&=(b^{-3}a^{-1})a^8(ab^3)\\
&=a^{-1}b^{-2}a^8b^2a,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the last equality uses step [](#s3){.pf-ref}. Multiplying on the left by $a$
and on the right by $a^{-1}$ yields
$$
a^{27}=b^{-2}a^8b^2.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}
One has
$$
a^9=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#s2-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
b^{-2}a^8b^2=a^{18}.
$$
Together with step [](#s4){.pf-ref} this gives
$$
a^{27}=a^{18}.
$$
Cancelling $a^{18}$ yields $a^9=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}
One has
$$
a^4=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref} with $m=2$,
$$
b^{-1}a^4b=a^6.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
b^{-2}a^4b^2
&=b^{-1}a^6b\\
&=a^9,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the last equality is step [](#s1){.pf-ref} with $m=3$. Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives
$a^9=1$, so
$$
b^{-2}a^4b^2=1.
$$
Conjugating by $b^2$ gives $a^4=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}
One has
$$
a=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#s5){.pf-ref} and [](#s6){.pf-ref} give $a^9=a^4=1$. Since
$$
1=9-2\cdot4,
$$
we have
$$
a=a^9(a^4)^{-2}=1.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}
One has
$$
b=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
Substituting step [](#s7){.pf-ref} into
$$
a^{-1}b^2a=b^3
$$
gives $b^2=b^3$. Cancelling $b^2$ yields $b=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s9}
Therefore
$$
\boxed{G=\{1\}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#s7){.pf-ref} and [](#s8){.pf-ref}, both generators $a$ and $b$ are the identity.
Hence the group they generate is trivial.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#s9){.pf-ref} answers the question.
:::
