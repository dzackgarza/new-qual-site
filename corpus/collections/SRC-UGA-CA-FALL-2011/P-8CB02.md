::: {.problem}
Do either (a) or (b).

(a) Suppose $f$ is meromorphic on $\DD$, continuous on $\overline{\DD}$ except at finitely many points of $\DD$, and real on $\circlegroup$.
Prove that $f$ is a rational function.

(b) Let $\Omega\subset\CC$ be the region inside the unit circle $\abs{z}=1$ and outside the circle $\abs{z-\frac14}=\frac14$.
Find a one-to-one conformal map from $\Omega$ onto an annulus $r<\abs{z}<1$ for the appropriate value of $r$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Both parts are solved.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #poles}

In part (a), $f$ has finitely many poles $p_1,\ldots,p_k$, all in $\DD$, and $f$ is holomorphic on $\{1-\delta<\abs{z}<1\}$ for some $\delta>0$ and continuous on $\{1-\delta<\abs{z}\le1\}$.

::: pf-proof

A pole is a point of discontinuity, and $f$ is continuous on $\overline\DD$ except at finitely many points of $\DD$. Choose $\delta$ so that the annulus $1-\delta<\abs{z}\le1$ contains none of these points.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #reflect}

The function
$$
g(z)=\begin{cases}f(z),&\abs{z}\le1,\\ \overline{f(1/\bar z)},&\abs{z}>1,\end{cases}
$$
is meromorphic on $\CC$.

::: pf-proof

For $\abs{z}>1$ the map $z\mapsto\overline{f(1/\bar z)}$ is meromorphic, because $1/\bar z\in\DD$ and conjugation on both sides preserves complex differentiability. On $\TT$, $1/\bar z=z$ and $f(z)$ is real, so the two formulas agree there. By Step [](#poles){.pf-ref}, $f$ is holomorphic on an inner collar of $\TT$, continuous up to $\TT$, and real on $\TT$. The Schwarz reflection principle for the circle therefore shows that $g$ is holomorphic on the annulus $1-\delta<\abs{z}<1/(1-\delta)$. Away from this annulus, $g$ is meromorphic by its formulas.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rat}

$f$ is a rational function.

::: pf-proof

Near $\infty$, $g(z)=\overline{f(1/\bar z)}$ and $1/\bar z\to0$. Since $f$ is holomorphic or has a pole at $0$, $g$ is holomorphic or has a pole at $\infty$. So $g$ is meromorphic on the Riemann sphere, and a meromorphic function on the Riemann sphere is rational. $f$ is the restriction of $g$ to $\DD$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #annulus}

In part (b), with $\alpha=2-\sqrt3$,
$$
\boxed{T(z)=\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\alpha z}}
$$
maps $\Omega$ one-to-one and conformally onto the annulus $2-\sqrt3<\abs{w}<1$.

::: pf-proof

Since $0<\alpha<1$ is real, $T$ is a Möbius automorphism of $\DD$, so it maps $\DD$ onto $\DD$ and $\TT$ onto $\TT$. The small circle $\abs{z-\frac14}=\frac14$ is symmetric about $\RR$ and meets $\RR$ at $0$ and $\frac12$. $T$ has real coefficients, so the image circle is symmetric about $\RR$ and has the diameter with endpoints
$$
T(0)=-\alpha,\qquad T\left(\tfrac12\right)=\frac{\frac12-\alpha}{1-\frac\alpha2}=\frac{1-2\alpha}{2-\alpha}.
$$
For $\alpha=2-\sqrt3$, $1-2\alpha=2\sqrt3-3=\sqrt3(2-\sqrt3)$ and $2-\alpha=\sqrt3$, so $T(\frac12)=\alpha$. Hence the image of the small circle is $\abs{w}=\alpha$. The closed small disk is connected, contains $\frac14$, and $T(\frac14)=\frac{\frac14-\alpha}{1-\frac\alpha4}$ has modulus less than $\alpha$, so the closed small disk maps onto $\abs{w}\le\alpha$. Therefore $T(\Omega)=\{\alpha<\abs{w}<1\}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#rat){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and Step [](#annulus){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
