::: problem
Do either (a) or (b).

(a) Suppose $f$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb{D}$, continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ except at finitely many points of $\mathbb{D}$, and real on $\mathbb{T}$.
Prove that $f$ is a rational function.

(b) Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}$ be the region inside the unit circle $|z|=1$ and outside the circle $\bigl|z-\frac14\bigr|=\frac14$.
Find a one-to-one conformal map from $\Omega$ onto an annulus $r<|z|<1$ for the appropriate value of $r$.
:::

::: solution
We solve part (a). Since $f$ is meromorphic in $\mathbb D$, it has only
finitely many poles there: otherwise the poles would have an accumulation
point in the compact closed disk after excluding a thin boundary collar, while
continuity on the boundary makes $f$ bounded near $\mathbb T$.

Define on the exterior disk
\[
F(z)=\overline{f(1/\overline z)},\qquad |z|>1.
\]
This is meromorphic there. On $|z|=1$, the hypothesis that $f$ is real gives
\[
F(z)=\overline{f(z)}=f(z).
\]
Schwarz reflection across the unit circle therefore glues $f$ and $F$ to a
meromorphic function on the Riemann sphere. A meromorphic function on the
compact Riemann sphere is rational. Hence the original $f$ is the restriction
of a rational function.

For completeness, part (b) can also be made explicit. The two boundary
circles belong to a coaxial family with limiting points
\[
\alpha=\frac{1-\sqrt3}{4},\qquad
\beta=\frac{1+\sqrt3}{4}.
\]
Thus $T(z)=(z-\alpha)/(z-\beta)$ maps them to concentric circles. Multiplying
$T$ by a constant normalizes the outer image circle to radius $1$; the inner
radius is the constant ratio of the two resulting moduli. This gives the
required conformal annulus map.
:::
