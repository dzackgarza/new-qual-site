::: problem
(a) Use the Class Equation to prove that any finite $p$-group (a group of order $p^k$ for a prime $p$ and integer $k \ge 1$) has a non-trivial center.

(b) Prove that any group of order $p^2$ (where $p$ is prime) is abelian.

(c) Prove that any group of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2 = 1225$ is abelian.

(d) Write down exactly one representative from each isomorphism class of groups of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove non-triviality of the center for $p$-groups via the class equation in (a), deduce abelianness of order $p^2$ in (b), establish direct product decomposition of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2$ via Sylow theory in (c), and enumerate isomorphism classes in (d).

<1>1. Part (a): Finite $p$-groups have non-trivial centers.
::: {.proof}
    <2>1. Let $G$ be a group of order $|G| = p^k$ with $k \ge 1$.
    <2>2. $G$ acts on itself by conjugation ($g \cdot x = g x g^{-1}$).
    <2>3. The orbit of $x \in G$ is the conjugacy class $\operatorname{Cl}(x)$, and the stabilizer of $x$ is the centralizer $C_G(x)$.
    <2>4. By the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem, $|\operatorname{Cl}(x)| = [G : C_G(x)]$.
    <2>5. The conjugacy class has size 1 if and only if $g x g^{-1} = x$ for all $g \in G$, which is equivalent to $x \in Z(G)$.
    <2>6. Partitioning $G$ into disjoint conjugacy classes yields the Class Equation:
    $$|G| = |Z(G)| + \sum_{i=1}^m [G : C_G(x_i)],$$
    where $x_1, \dots, x_m$ are representatives of the conjugacy classes of size strictly greater than 1.
    <2>7. For each $i \in \{1, \dots, m\}$, $[G : C_G(x_i)] > 1$ divides $|G| = p^k$, so $p$ divides $[G : C_G(x_i)]$.
    <2>8. Rearranging the Class Equation:
    $$|Z(G)| = |G| - \sum_{i=1}^m [G : C_G(x_i)].$$
    <2>9. Since $p \mid |G|$ and $p \mid [G : C_G(x_i)]$ for each $i$, $p$ divides $|Z(G)|$.
    <2>10. Since $e \in Z(G)$, $|Z(G)| \ge 1$. Since $p \mid |Z(G)|$, $|Z(G)| \ge p > 1$.
    <2>11. Thus $Z(G)$ is non-trivial.

:::

<1>2. Part (b): Every group of order $p^2$ is abelian.
::: {.proof}
    <2>1. Let $|G| = p^2$. By Part (a), $|Z(G)| \in \{p, p^2\}$.
    <2>2. If $|Z(G)| = p^2$, then $Z(G) = G$, so $G$ is abelian.
    <2>3. If $|Z(G)| = p$, then the quotient group $G/Z(G)$ has order $|G/Z(G)| = p^2 / p = p$.
    <2>4. Every group of prime order is cyclic, so $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, generated by some coset $x Z(G)$.
    <2>5. Lemma: If $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, then $G$ is abelian:
        - Every element $g \in G$ can be written as $g = x^a z_1$ for some $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $z_1 \in Z(G)$.
        - For any two elements $g = x^a z_1$ and $h = x^b z_2$:
        $$g h = (x^a z_1)(x^b z_2) = x^a x^b z_1 z_2 = x^{a+b} z_2 z_1 = x^b x^a z_2 z_1 = (x^b z_2)(x^a z_1) = h g.$$
        - Thus $G$ is abelian.
    <2>6. But if $G$ is abelian, $Z(G) = G$, so $|Z(G)| = p^2 \ne p$, a contradiction.
    <2>7. Therefore $|Z(G)| = p^2$, which proves $G$ is abelian.

:::

<1>3. Part (c): Every group of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2$ is abelian.
::: {.proof}
    <2>1. Let $|G| = 5^2 \cdot 7^2 = 1225$.
    <2>2. By Sylow's Theorems:
        - Number of Sylow 5-subgroups: $n_5 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ and $n_5 \mid 7^2 = 49$.
          Divisors of 49 are $\{1, 7, 49\}$. Among these, only $1 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$, so $n_5 = 1$.
        - Number of Sylow 7-subgroups: $n_7 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$ and $n_7 \mid 5^2 = 25$.
          Divisors of 25 are $\{1, 5, 25\}$. Among these, only $1 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$, so $n_7 = 1$.
    <2>3. Let $P$ be the unique Sylow 5-subgroup and $Q$ be the unique Sylow 7-subgroup.
    <2>4. Both $P$ and $Q$ are normal in $G$ ($P \trianglelefteq G, Q \trianglelefteq G$).
    <2>5. Since $\gcd(|P|, |Q|) = \gcd(25, 49) = 1$, $P \cap Q = \{e\}$.
    <2>6. Since $|P Q| = |P||Q| = 25 \cdot 49 = 1225 = |G|$, $G$ is the internal direct product:
    $$G \cong P \times Q.$$
    <2>7. By Part (b), since $|P| = 5^2$ and $|Q| = 7^2$, both $P$ and $Q$ are abelian.
    <2>8. The direct product of abelian groups is abelian, so $G$ is abelian.

:::

<1>4. Part (d): Isomorphism classes of groups of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2$.
::: {.proof}
    <2>1. By Part (c), every group of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2$ is abelian of the form $P \times Q$, where $P$ has order 25 and $Q$ has order 49.
    <2>2. By the Fundamental Theorem of Finite Abelian Groups:
        - Abelian groups of order $5^2 = 25$: $\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$.
        - Abelian groups of order $7^2 = 49$: $\mathbb{Z}/49\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$.
    <2>3. Combining these yields exactly $2 \times 2 = 4$ non-isomorphic abelian groups:
    1. $\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/49\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/1225\mathbb{Z}$
    2. $(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}) \times \mathbb{Z}/49\mathbb{Z}$
    3. $\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z} \times (\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})$
    4. $(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}) \times (\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})$

:::

<1>5. Conclusion:
::: {.proof}
    $p$-groups have $|Z(G)| \ge p$, order $p^2$ groups are abelian, order $5^2 \cdot 7^2$ groups decompose into $P \times Q$ (hence abelian), with exactly 4 isomorphism classes.
:::
:::
