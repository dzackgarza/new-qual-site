::: {.problem}
(a) Use the Class Equation to prove that any finite $p$-group (a group of order $p^k$ for a prime $p$ and integer $k \ge 1$) has a non-trivial center.

(b) Prove that any group of order $p^2$ (where $p$ is prime) is abelian.

(c) Prove that any group of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2 = 1225$ is abelian.

(d) Write down exactly one representative from each isomorphism class of groups of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Every finite group of order $p^k$, with $k\geq1$, has nontrivial center.

::: pf-proof

Under conjugation, the classes of elements outside the center have cardinalities $[G:C_G(x)]>1$, each a divisor of $p^k$ and hence divisible by $p$. The class equation
$$
\cardinality{G}=\cardinality{Z(G)}+\sum_{[x]\text{ noncentral}}[G:C_G(x)]
$$
therefore gives $p\mid \cardinality{Z(G)}$. Since the identity belongs to $Z(G)$, its cardinality is positive, so $\cardinality{Z(G)}\geq p>1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Every group of order $p^2$ is abelian.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, the center of such a group $G$ has order $p$ or $p^2$. If its order is $p^2$, the group is abelian. Otherwise $G/Z(G)$ has order $p$ and is cyclic, say generated by $gZ(G)$. Any two elements of $G$ can then be written as $g^az$ and $g^bw$ with $z,w\in Z(G)$; these commute because powers of $g$ commute and $z,w$ are central. Thus $G$ is abelian in this case as well.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every group of order $1225$ is abelian.

::: pf-proof

Let $G$ have order $5^2 7^2$, and write $n_5,n_7$ for the numbers of its Sylow subgroups. Sylow's theorems give $n_5\mid49$ and $n_5\equiv1\pmod5$. Among $1,7,49$, only $1$ meets the congruence, so $n_5=1$. Likewise $n_7\mid25$ and $n_7\equiv1\pmod7$; among $1,5,25$, only $1$ meets that congruence, so $n_7=1$. Hence both Sylow subgroups $P$ and $Q$ are normal. Their orders are relatively prime, so $P\cap Q=1$; their product has order $25\cdot49=\cardinality{G}$, and $G\cong P\times Q$. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, both factors are abelian, hence so is $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

There are exactly four isomorphism classes of groups of order $1225$, represented by
$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
C_{25}\times C_{49},\qquad
(C_5\times C_5)\times C_{49},\\
C_{25}\times(C_7\times C_7),\qquad
(C_5\times C_5)\times(C_7\times C_7).
\end{gathered}}
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, every such group is the product of an abelian group of order $25$ and one of order $49$. The classification theorem for finite abelian groups gives exactly two possibilities for each prime-power factor: $C_{25}$ or $C_5\times C_5$ for the first, and $C_{49}$ or $C_7\times C_7$ for the second. Their four products are pairwise nonisomorphic because the respective Sylow subgroups are characteristic and have different invariant factors. The list is therefore exhaustive without repetitions.

:::
