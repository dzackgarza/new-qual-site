::: {.problem}
(a) Use the Class Equation to prove that any finite $p$-group (a group of order $p^k$ for a prime $p$ and integer $k \ge 1$) has a non-trivial center.

(b) Prove that any group of order $p^2$ (where $p$ is prime) is abelian.

(c) Prove that any group of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2 = 1225$ is abelian.

(d) Write down exactly one representative from each isomorphism class of groups of order $5^2 \cdot 7^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every finite group of order $p^k$, with $k\geq1$, has nontrivial center.

::: {.proof}
Under conjugation, the classes of elements outside the center have cardinalities $[G:C_G(x)]>1$, each a divisor of $p^k$ and hence divisible by $p$. The class equation
\[
|G|=|Z(G)|+\sum_{[x]\text{ noncentral}}[G:C_G(x)]
\]
therefore gives $p\mid |Z(G)|$. Since the identity belongs to $Z(G)$, its cardinality is positive, so $|Z(G)|\geq p>1$.
:::

<1>2. Every group of order $p^2$ is abelian.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the center of such a group $G$ has order $p$ or $p^2$. If its order is $p^2$, the group is abelian. Otherwise $G/Z(G)$ has order $p$ and is cyclic, say generated by $gZ(G)$. Any two elements of $G$ can then be written as $g^az$ and $g^bw$ with $z,w\in Z(G)$; these commute because powers of $g$ commute and $z,w$ are central. Thus $G$ is abelian in this case as well.
:::

<1>3. Every group of order $1225$ is abelian.

::: {.proof}
Let $G$ have order $5^2 7^2$, and write $n_5,n_7$ for the numbers of its Sylow subgroups. Sylow's theorems give $n_5\mid49$ and $n_5\equiv1\pmod5$. Among $1,7,49$, only $1$ meets the congruence, so $n_5=1$. Likewise $n_7\mid25$ and $n_7\equiv1\pmod7$; among $1,5,25$, only $1$ meets that congruence, so $n_7=1$. Hence both Sylow subgroups $P$ and $Q$ are normal. Their orders are relatively prime, so $P\cap Q=1$; their product has order $25\cdot49=|G|$, and $G\cong P\times Q$. By step <1>2, both factors are abelian, hence so is $G$.
:::

<1>4. There are exactly four isomorphism classes of groups of order $1225$, represented by
\[
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
C_{25}\times C_{49},\qquad
(C_5\times C_5)\times C_{49},\\
C_{25}\times(C_7\times C_7),\qquad
(C_5\times C_5)\times(C_7\times C_7).
\end{gathered}}
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, every such group is the product of an abelian group of order $25$ and one of order $49$. The classification theorem for finite abelian groups gives exactly two possibilities for each prime-power factor: $C_{25}$ or $C_5\times C_5$ for the first, and $C_{49}$ or $C_7\times C_7$ for the second. Their four products are pairwise nonisomorphic because the respective Sylow subgroups are characteristic and have different invariant factors. The list is therefore exhaustive without repetitions.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>4 answer parts (a)--(d), respectively.
