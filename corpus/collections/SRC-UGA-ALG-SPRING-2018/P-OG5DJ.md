::: {.problem}
Let $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^2 + 2 \in \QQ[x]$.

a.
Find the splitting field $K$ of $f$, and compute $[K: \QQ]$.

b.
Find the Galois group $G$ of $f$, both as an explicit group of automorphisms, and as a familiar abstract group to which it is isomorphic.

c.
Exhibit explicitly the correspondence between subgroups of $G$ and intermediate fields between $\QQ$ and $k$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $\alpha_1=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}$ and $\alpha_2=\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}$, taking the positive real square roots.

<1>1. The splitting field is $K=\QQ(\alpha_1)$ and has degree $4$ over $\QQ$.

::: {.proof}
Note that $g(x) = x^2 - 4x + 2$ has roots $\beta = 2 \pm \sqrt{2}$, and so $f$ has roots 
\[
\alpha_1 &= \sqrt{2 + \sqrt 2} \\
\alpha_2 &= \sqrt{2 - \sqrt 2} \\
\alpha_3 &= -\alpha_1 \\
\alpha_4 &= -\alpha_2
.\]

and splitting field $K = \QQ(\theset{\alpha_i})$.

:::

<1>2. The Galois group is cyclic of order four, generated by the automorphism $\sigma$ with $\sigma(\alpha_1)=\alpha_2$.

::: {.proof}
$K$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial and thus Galois over $\QQ$.
Moreover, Since $f$ is irreducible by Eisenstein with $p=2$, the Galois group is a transitive subgroup of $S^4$, so the possibilities are:

- $S_4$
- $A_4$
- $D_4$
- $\ZZ/(2) \cross \ZZ/(2)$
- $\ZZ/(4)$

We can note that $g$ splits over $L \definedas \QQ(\sqrt 2)$, an extension of degree 2.

The positive roots satisfy $\alpha_1\alpha_2=\sqrt2$. Since $\sqrt2=\alpha_1^2-2$, we have $\alpha_2=\sqrt2/\alpha_1\in\QQ(\alpha_1)$, hence $K=\QQ(\alpha_1)$. Eisenstein's criterion at $2$ gives $[K:\QQ]=4$, so $[K:L]=2$.

We then have
\[
[K: \QQ] = [K: L] [L : \QQ] = (2)(2) = 4
.\]

This $\abs{\Gal(K/\QQ)} = 4$, which leaves only two possibilities:

- $\ZZ/(2) \cross \ZZ/(2)$
- $\ZZ/(4)$

We can next check orders of elements.
Take
\[
\sigma &\in \Gal(K/\QQ) \\
\alpha_1 &\mapsto \alpha_2
.\]

The action on the other positive root is determined exactly:

- $\alpha_1^2 \alpha_2^2 = 2$, so $\alpha_1 \alpha_2 = \sqrt 2$
- $\alpha_1^2 = 2 + \sqrt 2 \implies \sqrt 2 = \alpha_1^2 - 2$

and thus
\[
\sigma^2(\alpha_1) 
&= \sigma(\alpha_2) \\
&= \sigma\left(\frac{\sqrt 2}{\alpha_1}\right) \\
&= \frac{\sigma(\sqrt 2)}{\sigma(\alpha_1)} \\
&= \frac{\sigma(\alpha_1^2 - 2)}{\alpha_2} \\
&= \frac{\alpha_2^2 - 2}{\alpha_2} \\
&= -\frac{\sqrt2}{\alpha_2} \\
&= -\alpha_1
,\]

Since $\sigma^2(\alpha_1)=-\alpha_1$, the automorphism $\sigma^2$ is nontrivial and $\sigma^4(\alpha_1)=\alpha_1$. As $K=\QQ(\alpha_1)$, the order of $\sigma$ is exactly four. Therefore $\Gal(K/\QQ)=\theset{\sigma^k\suchthat 0\leq k<4}\cong\ZZ/(4)$.

:::

<1>3. The intermediate fields and their corresponding subgroups are
\[
\boxed{\{1\}\leftrightarrow K,\qquad
\langle\sigma^2\rangle\leftrightarrow\QQ(\sqrt2),\qquad
G\leftrightarrow\QQ}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By part (b), $G = \Gal(K/\QQ) \cong \ZZ/4$ is cyclic of order $4$, generated by $\sigma$ (with $\sigma(\alpha_1) = \alpha_2$).
A cyclic group of order $4$ has exactly three subgroups, forming a chain:
\[
\{1\} \subset \generators{\sigma^2} \subset G,
\]
where $\generators{\sigma^2}$ has order $2$ (index $2$ in $G$).
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, the intermediate fields between $\QQ$ and $K$ form the reverse chain:
\[
K \supset K^{\generators{\sigma^2}} \supset \QQ,
\]
with $[K : K^{\generators{\sigma^2}}] = |\generators{\sigma^2}| = 2$ and $[K^{\generators{\sigma^2}} : \QQ] = 2$.

We already know one intermediate field of degree $2$ over $\QQ$, namely $\QQ(\sqrt 2)$ (from part (a), $g(x) = x^2 - 4x + 2$ splits over $\QQ(\sqrt 2)$, and $[K : \QQ(\sqrt 2)] = 2$).
Since the subgroup lattice is a chain, there is exactly one intermediate field of each degree, so $K^{\generators{\sigma^2}} = \QQ(\sqrt 2)$.

Thus the full correspondence is:
\[
\{1\} \leftrightarrow K, \qquad \generators{\sigma^2} \leftrightarrow \QQ(\sqrt 2), \qquad G \leftrightarrow \QQ.
\]
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1, <1>2, and <1>3 prove parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
:::
