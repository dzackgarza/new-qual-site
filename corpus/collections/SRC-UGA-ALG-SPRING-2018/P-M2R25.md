::: {.problem}
Let $R = C[0, 1]$ be the ring of continuous real-valued functions on the interval $[0, 1]$. Let I be an ideal of $R$.

a.
Show that if $f \in I, a \in [0, 1]$ are such that $f (a) \neq 0$, then there exists $g \in I$ such that $g(x) \geq 0$ for all $x \in [0, 1]$, and $g(x) > 0$ for all $x$ in some open neighborhood of $a$.

b.
If $I \neq R$, show that the set $Z(I) = \{x \in [0, 1] \suchthat f(x) = 0 \text{ for all } f \in I\}$ is nonempty.

c.
Show that if $I$ is maximal, then there exists $x_0 \in [0, 1]$ such that $I = \{ f \in R \suchthat f (x_0 ) = 0\}$.
:::

::: {.remark}
Cool problem, but pretty specific topological tricks needed.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $f\in I$ and $f(a)\neq0$, then $g=f^2$ is nonnegative everywhere and strictly positive on an open neighborhood of $a$.

::: {.proof}

Because $I$ is an ideal, $g=f^2$ belongs to $I$. Every square of a real number is nonnegative, and $g(a)=f(a)^2>0$. Continuity supplies a relatively open neighborhood of $a$ in $[0,1]$ on which $g>0$.
:::

<1>2. If $I\neq R$, then $Z(I)\neq\varnothing$.

::: {.proof}

Suppose instead that $Z(I)$ is empty. For every $a\in[0,1]$, choose $f_a\in I$ with $f_a(a)\neq0$. By step <1>1, the function $g_a=f_a^2\in I$ is nonnegative and strictly positive on a relatively open neighborhood $U_a$ of $a$. Compactness supplies a finite subcover $U_{a_1},\ldots,U_{a_m}$. The function $h=\sum_{j=1}^m g_{a_j}\in I$ is strictly positive at every point, because some summand is positive and the others are nonnegative. Its reciprocal $1/h$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, so $1=(1/h)h\in I$, contradicting $I\neq R$.

:::

<1>3. If $I$ is maximal, then for some $x_0\in[0,1]$,
\[
\boxed{I=\{f\in R:f(x_0)=0\}}.
\]

::: {.proof}

Since a maximal ideal is proper, step <1>2 supplies $x_0\in Z(I)$. The evaluation map $\operatorname{ev}_{x_0}:R\to\mathbb R$ is a surjective unital ring homomorphism: every real number is the image of its constant function. Its kernel $\mathfrak m_{x_0}=\{f\in R:f(x_0)=0\}$ is a proper ideal since the constant function $1$ does not belong to it. The condition $x_0\in Z(I)$ gives $I\subseteq\mathfrak m_{x_0}$, so maximality forces equality.

:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1, <1>2, and <1>3 prove parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
:::
