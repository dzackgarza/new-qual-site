::: {.problem}
Let \(m,n\) be positive integers.
Prove that the ideal generated by
\[
x^m-1\quad\text{and}\quad x^n-1
\]
in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) is principal.
:::

::: {.solution}
For nonnegative integers $r,s$, write
$$
I(r,s)=(x^r-1,x^s-1)\subseteq\ZZ[x].
$$

<1>1. If $r\ge s>0$ and
$$
r=qs+t,
\qquad
0\le t<s,
$$
then
$$
I(r,s)=I(s,t),
$$
where $x^0-1=0$ when $t=0$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
x^{qs}-1
=
(x^s-1)
\left(
1+x^s+\cdots+x^{(q-1)s}
\right),
$$
one has
$$
x^r-1
=
x^t(x^{qs}-1)+(x^t-1).
$$
Thus $x^r-1\in I(s,t)$, while $x^s-1$ is already a generator of
$I(s,t)$. Hence
$$
I(r,s)\subseteq I(s,t).
$$

Conversely,
$$
x^t-1
=
(x^r-1)-x^t(x^{qs}-1),
$$
and $x^{qs}-1$ is a multiple of $x^s-1$. Therefore
$x^t-1\in I(r,s)$, while $x^s-1\in I(r,s)$ by definition. Hence
$$
I(s,t)\subseteq I(r,s).
$$
The two ideals are equal.
:::

<1>2. If $d=\gcd(m,n)$, repeated application of step <1>1 gives
$$
I(m,n)=I(d,0).
$$

::: {.proof}
Interchange $m$ and $n$ first if necessary; this does not change
$I(m,n)$. Apply the ordinary Euclidean algorithm to the pair $(m,n)$.
At each
division step
$$
r=qs+t,
\qquad
0\le t<s,
$$
replace the pair $(r,s)$ by $(s,t)$. Step <1>1 shows that the
corresponding ideal is unchanged. The Euclidean algorithm terminates
at the pair $(d,0)$, where $d=\gcd(m,n)$. Therefore the initial and
terminal ideals are equal.
:::

<1>3. The ideal generated by $x^m-1$ and $x^n-1$ is
$$
\boxed{(x^d-1)},
\qquad
d=\gcd(m,n).
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
(x^m-1,x^n-1)
=
I(d,0)
=
(x^d-1,x^0-1).
$$
Since $x^0-1=0$,
$$
I(d,0)=(x^d-1).
$$
Thus the given ideal is principal, with the displayed generator.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves the assertion.
:::
