::: {.problem}
Let \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be an arbitrary function.
Prove that there exists a countable subfield \(K\subseteq\mathbb R\) such that
\[
f(K)\subseteq K.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #generated-subfield-countable}
If $S\subseteq\RR$ is countable, then the subfield of $\RR$
generated by $S$ is countable.

::: pf-proof
Set
$$
E_0=S\cup\QQ.
$$
Having defined $E_m$, let $E_{m+1}$ be the union of $E_m$ with all
real numbers of the forms
$$
a+b,
\qquad
a-b,
\qquad
ab,
\qquad
\frac ab
$$
where $a,b\in E_m$ and, in the last expression, $b\ne0$.

The set $E_0$ is countable. If $E_m$ is countable, then
$E_m\times E_m$ is countable, so each of the four displayed
operation-images is countable. Thus $E_{m+1}$ is countable. By
induction every $E_m$ is countable, and hence
$$
E=\bigcup_{m=0}^\infty E_m
$$
is countable.

The set $E$ contains $S\cup\QQ$ and is closed under addition,
subtraction, multiplication, and division by nonzero elements. Hence
$E$ is a subfield of $\RR$ containing $S$. Conversely, every
subfield containing $S$ and $\QQ$ contains every $E_m$ by induction.
Therefore $E$ is exactly the subfield generated by $S$, proving the
claim.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #Kn-sequence}
Define an increasing sequence of countable subfields
$$
K_0\subseteq K_1\subseteq K_2\subseteq\cdots
$$
by
$$
K_0=\QQ
$$
and, for $n\ge0$,
$$
K_{n+1}
=
\text{the subfield of $\RR$ generated by }K_n\cup f(K_n).
$$

::: pf-proof
The field $K_0=\QQ$ is countable. Suppose $K_n$ is countable. Then
$f(K_n)$ is countable because it is the image of a countable set, so
$K_n\cup f(K_n)$ is countable. Step [](#generated-subfield-countable){.pf-ref} therefore shows that
$K_{n+1}$ is countable.

By construction, $K_{n+1}$ contains $K_n$, so the sequence is
increasing.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #K-countable-subfield}
The set
$$
K=\bigcup_{n=0}^\infty K_n
$$
is a countable subfield of $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
It is countable as a countable union of countable sets by step [](#Kn-sequence){.pf-ref}.
To check the field operations, let $a,b\in K$. There are indices
$m,n$ with $a\in K_m$ and $b\in K_n$. Since the sequence is
increasing, both elements lie in
$$
K_N,
\qquad
N=\max\{m,n\}.
$$
Because $K_N$ is a field,
$$
a+b,\quad a-b,\quad ab
$$
belong to $K_N\subseteq K$, and if $b\ne0$, then
$a/b\in K_N\subseteq K$. Also $0,1\in K_0\subseteq K$. Thus $K$
is a subfield of $\RR$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-K-subseteq-K}
The field $K$ satisfies
$$
\boxed{f(K)\subseteq K}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $x\in K$. Then $x\in K_n$ for some $n$. By the definition of
$K_{n+1}$,
$$
f(x)\in f(K_n)\subseteq K_{n+1}\subseteq K.
$$
Since $x$ was arbitrary, $f(K)\subseteq K$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Steps [](#K-countable-subfield){.pf-ref} and [](#f-K-subseteq-K){.pf-ref} give the required countable subfield and its
$f$-invariance.
:::
