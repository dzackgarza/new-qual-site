::: {.problem}
Let \(p\) be prime and
\[
R=\mathbb F_p[x]/(x^3).
\]
For which primes \(p\) is the unit group \(R^\times\) cyclic?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\bar x$ denote the image of $x$ in
$$
R=\FF_p[x]/(x^3).
$$

<1>1. An element
$$
c+a\bar x+b\bar x^2
$$
of $R$ is a unit if and only if $c\ne0$. Consequently
$$
\abs{R^\times}=(p-1)p^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $c=0$, then
$$
a\bar x+b\bar x^2
$$
is nilpotent. No nilpotent element of this nonzero ring can be a unit:
if $v^k=0$ and $v$ were invertible, multiplying by $v^{-k}$ would give
$1=0$. If
$c\ne0$, factor the element as
$$
c(1+u),
\qquad
u=c^{-1}(a\bar x+b\bar x^2).
$$
Since $u^3=0$,
$$
(1+u)(1-u+u^2)=1,
$$
so the element is a unit. There are $p-1$ choices for $c$ and $p$
choices each for $a,b$, giving the stated cardinality.
:::

<1>2. Suppose $p\ge3$. Then the subset
$$
U=
\{1+a\bar x+b\bar x^2:a,b\in\FF_p\}
\subseteq R^\times
$$
has $p^2$ elements, all satisfying
$$
u^p=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 shows that every displayed element is a unit, and distinct
pairs $(a,b)$ give distinct elements, so $\abs U=p^2$.

Since $R$ is a commutative ring of characteristic $p$, the Frobenius
map is a ring homomorphism. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1+a\bar x+b\bar x^2)^p
&=
1+a^p\bar x^p+b^p\bar x^{2p}
\\
&=
1,
\end{aligned}
$$
because $p\ge3$ and $\bar x^3=0$.
:::

<1>3. If $p\ge3$, the group $R^\times$ is not cyclic.

::: {.proof}
In a finite cyclic group, the equation
$$
g^p=1
$$
has at most $p$ solutions: if the group has order $N$, the number is
$\gcd(p,N)\le p$. Step <1>2 exhibits $p^2>p$ distinct solutions in
$R^\times$. Hence $R^\times$ cannot be cyclic.
:::

<1>4. If $p=2$, then $R^\times$ has four elements and
$$
1+\bar x
$$
has order four.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives
$$
\abs{R^\times}=(2-1)2^2=4.
$$
In characteristic two,
$$
(1+\bar x)^2=1+\bar x^2\ne1
$$
because $\bar x^2\ne0$ in $\FF_2[x]/(x^3)$. Squaring once more gives
$$
(1+\bar x)^4
=(1+\bar x^2)^2
=
1+\bar x^4
=
1.
$$
Thus $1+\bar x$ has order exactly four.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{R^\times\text{ is cyclic exactly when }p=2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 excludes every prime $p\ge3$, while step <1>4 gives an
element of order $\abs{R^\times}=4$ when $p=2$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required classification.
