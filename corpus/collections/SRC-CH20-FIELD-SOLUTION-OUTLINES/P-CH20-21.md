::: {.problem}
Let $f(x) \in F[x]$ and let $a \in F$. Show that $f(x)$ and $f(x + a)$ have the same splitting field over $F$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. Let $E$ be a splitting field of $f$ over $F$, and write the zeros of $f$ in $E$ as $\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}$, so that $E=F(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factorization}
For some $c\in F$ with $c\ne0$, $f(x)=c\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x-\alpha_{i})$ in $E[x]$, and consequently $f(x+a)=c\prod_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(x-(\alpha_{i}-a)\bigr)$.

::: pf-proof
Since $E$ is a splitting field, $f$ factors into linear factors over $E$, with leading coefficient $c\in F$ and the $\alpha_{i}$ being its zeros counted with multiplicity. Substituting $x\mapsto x+a$ in the factorization gives $f(x+a)=c\prod_{i}\bigl((x+a)-\alpha_{i}\bigr)=c\prod_{i}\bigl(x-(\alpha_{i}-a)\bigr)$, and $a\in F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #zeros}
The zeros of $f(x+a)$ in $E$ are exactly the elements $\alpha_{i}-a$, each with the multiplicity of $\alpha_{i}$ as a zero of $f$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#factorization){.pf-ref} presents $f(x+a)$ as a constant times a product of the linear factors $x-(\alpha_{i}-a)$, so those are its zeros with the multiplicities shown. Directly: for $b\in E$ one has $f(b+a)=0$ exactly when $b+a$ is a zero of $f$ in $E$, that is exactly when $b=\alpha_{i}-a$ for some $i$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #generated-equal}
$F(\alpha_{1}-a,\dots,\alpha_{k}-a)=F(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})=E$.

::: pf-proof
Each $\alpha_{i}-a$ belongs to $E$, so $F(\alpha_{1}-a,\dots,\alpha_{k}-a)\subset E$; conversely each $\alpha_{i}-a$ lies in $F(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})$, because $\alpha_{i}$ does and $a\in F$, whence $F(\alpha_{1}-a,\dots,\alpha_{k}-a)\subset F(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})$. For the reverse inclusion, $\alpha_{i}=(\alpha_{i}-a)+a$ with $a\in F$, so $\alpha_{i}\in F(\alpha_{1}-a,\dots,\alpha_{k}-a)$, giving $F(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\subset F(\alpha_{1}-a,\dots,\alpha_{k}-a)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$f(x+ a)$ and $f(x)$ have the same splitting field over $F$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#factorization){.pf-ref} the polynomial $f(x+a)$ splits over $E$, and by steps [](#zeros){.pf-ref} and [](#generated-equal){.pf-ref} the field $E$ is generated over $F$ by its zeros; so $E$ is a splitting field of $f(x+a)$ over $F$. It is also a splitting field of $f(x)$, and a splitting field is determined by the polynomial, so the two splitting fields are the same subfield of $E$. The shifted polynomial in the statement is written with argument $x$, the substitution being $x\mapsto x+a$.
:::
