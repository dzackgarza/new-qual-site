::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field, and let $a$ and $b$ belong to $F$ with $a \neq 0$. If $c$ belongs to some extension of $F$, prove that $F(c) = F(ac + b)$. ($F$ “absorbs” its own elements.)
:::

::: {.solution}

The exercise is Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra* (7th ed.), Chapter 20 exercise 20,
printed p. 367, and is item 20 of the retained Chapter 20 solution outline
(`assets/attachments/Ch20Sltns.pdf`, PDF p. 2). The outline's two inclusions are kept, with
the minimality they use made explicit.

Let $E$ be an extension field of $F$ containing $c$. For $d\in E$, $F(d)$ denotes the
smallest subfield of $E$ containing $F$ and $d$ (printed p. 357): it is a subfield of $E$
contained in every subfield of $E$ that contains $F$ and $d$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #inside}

$ac+b\in F(c)$.

::: pf-proof

$c\in F(c)$ by definition, $a,b\in F\subseteq F(c)$, and $F(c)$ is a subfield of $E$, hence
closed under multiplication and addition; so $ac\in F(c)$ and $ac+b\in F(c)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #first}

$F(ac+b)\subseteq F(c)$.

::: pf-proof

$F(c)$ is a subfield of $E$ containing $F$, and by step [](#inside){.pf-ref} it also contains
$ac+b$. Since $F(ac+b)$ is the smallest subfield of $E$ containing $F$ and $ac+b$, it is
contained in $F(c)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #second}

$F(c)\subseteq F(ac+b)$.

::: pf-proof

Since $a\ne0$, $a$ has an inverse in $F$, and $a^{-1}b\in F\subseteq F(ac+b)$. Also
$ac+b\in F(ac+b)$ by definition, and $F(ac+b)$ is a subfield, hence closed under
multiplication and subtraction. Therefore
$c=a^{-1}(ac+b)-a^{-1}b\in F(ac+b)$. As $F(c)$ is the smallest subfield of $E$ containing
$F$ and $c$, it is contained in $F(ac+b)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equality}

$F(c)=F(ac+b)$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#first){.pf-ref} and [](#second){.pf-ref} give the two inclusions.

:::
