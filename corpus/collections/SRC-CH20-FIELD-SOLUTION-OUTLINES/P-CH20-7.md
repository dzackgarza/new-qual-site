::: {.problem}
Find a polynomial $p(x)$ in $\QQ[x]$ such that $\QQ\big(\sqrt{1 + \sqrt{5}}\big)$ is ring isomorphic to $\QQ[x]/\langle p(x) \rangle$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is that of the retained solution outline for Chapter 20, Exercise 7 (`assets/attachments/Ch20Sltns.pdf`), restructured into numbered steps; the outline says the polynomial is irreducible 'simply check in Z3' and the card's original text carried out that check, which is kept and verified here. Here $\alpha=\sqrt{1+\sqrt5}$ and the criterion used is Gallian's Theorem 20.3: for $p$ irreducible over $\QQ$ with $p(\alpha)=0$, the map $\QQ[x]\to\QQ(\alpha)$, $x\mapsto\alpha$, induces an isomorphism $\QQ[x]/\langle p\rangle\cong\QQ(\alpha)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #root}

$\alpha^{4}-2\alpha^{2}-4=0$.

::: pf-proof

$\alpha^{2}=1+\sqrt5$, so $\alpha^{2}-1=\sqrt5$ and squaring gives $\alpha^{4}-2\alpha^{2}+1=5$, that is $\alpha^{4}-2\alpha^{2}-4=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #noroot}

$p(x)=x^{4}-2x^{2}-4$ has no root in $\ZZ_{3}$, where $\bar p=x^{4}+x^{2}+2$.

::: pf-proof

$\bar p(0)=2$, $\bar p(1)=1+1+2=4\equiv1$ and $\bar p(2)=16+4+2=22\equiv1$, so no residue of $\ZZ_3$ is a root.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #noquad}

$\bar p$ has no factorization into two monic quadratics over $\ZZ_{3}$.

::: pf-proof

Such a factorization is $(x^{2}+ax+b)(x^{2}-ax+c)$, since the coefficient of $x^{3}$ is zero; comparing coefficients gives $a(c-b)=0$, $b+c-a^{2}=1$ and $bc=2$. If $a=0$ then $b+c=1$ and $bc=2$, so $b$ is a root of $t^{2}-t+2$, whose discriminant $1-8=-7\equiv2$ is not a square in $\ZZ_3$ (the squares are $0,1$), a contradiction. If $a\neq0$ then $b=c$ and $b^{2}=2$, which has no solution in $\ZZ_3$ since $2$ is not a square there.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #irred}

$p$ is irreducible over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

$p$ is monic of degree $4$, so its reduction modulo $3$ has the same degree; a factorization of $p$ over $\QQ$ into monic factors of positive degree would, by Gauss's lemma, give a factorization over $\ZZ$, and reducing it modulo $3$ would factor $\bar p$ into factors of the corresponding degrees. Steps [](#noroot){.pf-ref} and [](#noquad){.pf-ref} rule out a linear and a quadratic factor of $\bar p$; a factorization of $\bar p$ into factors of degree $1+3$ contains a linear factor, and one into $2+2$ is excluded, so no factorization of $p$ exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$\QQ(\sqrt{1+\sqrt5})\cong\QQ[x]/\langle x^{4}-2x^{2}-4\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

$p$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ by step [](#irred){.pf-ref} and $p(\alpha)=0$ by step [](#root){.pf-ref}, so Gallian's Theorem 20.3 applies with $p(x)=x^{4}-2x^{2}-4$.

:::
