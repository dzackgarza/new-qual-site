::: {.problem}
Show that $\QQ(\sqrt{7}, i)$ is the splitting field for $x^{4} - 6x^{2} - 7$ (over $\QQ$).
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own: the collection's provenance is a set of solution outlines. Let $f(x)=x^4-6x^2-7\in\QQ[x]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factor}
$f(x)=(x^2-7)(x^2+1)$, so the roots of $f$ in $\CC$ are $\sqrt7,-\sqrt7,i,-i$.

::: pf-proof
Expanding, $(x^2-7)(x^2+1)=x^4+x^2-7x^2-7=x^4-6x^2-7$. A product of polynomials vanishes exactly where one factor does, so the roots are the roots of $x^2-7$, namely $\pm\sqrt7$, and the roots of $x^2+1$, namely $\pm i$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #splitting}
The splitting field of $f$ over $\QQ$ is $\QQ(\sqrt7,i)$.

::: pf-proof
The splitting field is by definition $\QQ$ adjoined all the roots of $f$, that is $\QQ(\sqrt7,-\sqrt7,i,-i)$. But $\QQ(\sqrt7)$ already contains $-\sqrt7=-( \sqrt7)$ and $\QQ(i)$ contains $-i$, so adjoining the four roots is the same as adjoining $\sqrt7$ and $i$: $\QQ(\sqrt7,-\sqrt7,i,-i)=\QQ(\sqrt7,i)$. Hence the splitting field is $\QQ(\sqrt7,i)$, which answers the question.
:::
