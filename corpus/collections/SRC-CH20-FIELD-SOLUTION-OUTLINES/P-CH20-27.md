::: {.problem}
Prove or disprove that $\QQ(\sqrt{3})$ and $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ are ring isomorphic.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is that of the retained solution outline for Chapter 20, Exercise 27, PDF p. 2 of `assets/attachments/Ch20Sltns.pdf`, restructured into numbered steps. Here $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})=\QQ(i\sqrt3)$ and $\QQ(\sqrt3)$ are the subfields of $\CC$ generated by the indicated elements, and a ring isomorphism is an isomorphism of the additive groups that preserves multiplication.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #fix}

A ring isomorphism $\phi:\QQ(\sqrt{-3})\to\QQ(\sqrt3)$ fixes $\QQ$ pointwise, so $\phi(-3)=-3$.

::: pf-proof

A ring homomorphism of fields carries $1$ to $1$, hence fixes every integer and every rational, since $\phi(n)=n\phi(1)$ and $\phi(1/n)=1/\phi(n)$; so $\phi(-3)=-3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square}

$\phi(\sqrt{-3})^{2}=-3$.

::: pf-proof

$(\sqrt{-3})^{2}=-3$, and $\phi$ preserves multiplication, so $\phi(\sqrt{-3})^{2}=\phi((\sqrt{-3})^{2})=\phi(-3)=-3$ by step [](#fix){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #noreal}

No element of $\QQ(\sqrt3)$ has square $-3$.

::: pf-proof

$\QQ(\sqrt3)\subset\RR$, and the square of a real number is $\geq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$\QQ(\sqrt3)$ and $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ are not ring isomorphic.

::: pf-proof

A ring isomorphism would give an element of $\QQ(\sqrt3)$ with square $-3$ by step [](#square){.pf-ref}, which step [](#noreal){.pf-ref} excludes.

:::
