::: {.problem}
Describe the elements of $\QQ(\sqrt[3]{5})$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\alpha=\sqrt[3]{5}$. Then

$$\QQ(\alpha)=\{a+b\alpha+c\alpha^{2}\mid a,b,c\in\QQ\},$$

each element being written uniquely in that form.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #irreducible}

$x^{3}-5$ is irreducible over $\QQ$, so it is the minimal polynomial of
$\alpha$.

::: pf-proof

$x^{3}-5$ is Eisenstein at the prime $5$: it is monic, the prime $5$ divides all
non-leading coefficients (namely $-5$ and $0$), and $5^{2}=25$ does not divide
$-5$. So it is irreducible over $\QQ$. Since $\alpha^{3}=5$, the polynomial
vanishes at $\alpha$, and an irreducible polynomial with $\alpha$ as a root is
the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$. In particular
$[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #span}

Every element of $\QQ(\alpha)$ is $a+b\alpha+c\alpha^{2}$ with
$a,b,c\in\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

Consider the evaluation homomorphism $\QQ[x]\to\QQ(\alpha)$, $f\mapsto f(\alpha)$.
Its kernel is the ideal of polynomials vanishing at $\alpha$, which is
$\langle x^{3}-5\rangle$ by step [](#irreducible){.pf-ref}; so by the first
isomorphism theorem $\QQ[x]/\langle x^{3}-5\rangle\cong\QQ(\alpha)$. Every class
in $\QQ[x]/\langle x^{3}-5\rangle$ has a representative of degree at most $2$,
by division with remainder by the monic polynomial $x^{3}-5$, so every element
of $\QQ(\alpha)$ is a polynomial in $\alpha$ of degree at most $2$, that is of
the form $a+b\alpha+c\alpha^{2}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #unique}

The representation is unique: $1,\alpha,\alpha^{2}$ are linearly independent
over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $a+b\alpha+c\alpha^{2}=0$ with $a,b,c\in\QQ$ not all zero. Then
$\alpha$ is a root of the nonzero polynomial $a+bx+cx^{2}$ of degree at most
$2$, contradicting that its minimal polynomial has degree $3$. Hence
$a=b=c=0$, so the three powers are independent and distinct triples
$(a,b,c)$ give distinct field elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}

Therefore $\QQ(\sqrt[3]{5})=\{a+b\sqrt[3]{5}+c\sqrt[3]{25}\mid a,b,c\in\QQ\}$,
with $(a,b,c)\in\QQ^{3}$ in bijection with the field elements.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#span){.pf-ref} and [](#unique){.pf-ref} give the two inclusions
together with uniqueness, and $\alpha^{2}=\sqrt[3]{25}$. In particular
$\QQ(\sqrt[3]{5})$ has degree $3$ over $\QQ$ and, unlike $\QQ(\sqrt{5})$, it is
not stable under complex conjugation: all its elements are real and the two
nonreal roots of $x^{3}-5$ lie outside it.

:::
