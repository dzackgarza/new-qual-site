::: {.problem}
Find the splitting field of $x^{3} - 1$ over $\QQ$. Express your answer in the form $\QQ(a)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

No solution set for this chapter is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the factorization is the one this collection's outline records and the verification below is the card's own. The statement was checked against Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra* (7th edition), Chapter 20 Exercises, printed p. 366.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factor}
$x^{3}-1=(x-1)(x^{2}+x+1)$.

::: pf-proof
Multiplying out, $(x-1)(x^{2}+x+1)=x^{3}+x^{2}+x-x^{2}-x-1=x^{3}-1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #roots}
The roots of $x^{3}-1$ in $\CC$ are $1$, $\omega=\tfrac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}$ and $\omega^{2}=\tfrac{-1-\sqrt{-3}}{2}$.

::: pf-proof
A product of field elements is zero exactly when a factor is, so the roots are $1$ together with the roots of $x^{2}+x+1$, which the quadratic formula gives as $\tfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{1-4}}{2}=\tfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{-3}}{2}$. (These are distinct, the discriminant $-3$ being nonzero in $\CC$.)
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #splitting}
The splitting field of $x^{3}-1$ over $\QQ$ is $E=\QQ(1,\omega)$, which equals $\QQ(\omega)$.

::: pf-proof
The splitting field is the subfield of $\CC$ generated by $\QQ$ and all the roots, that is $\QQ(1,\omega,\omega^{2})$. Since $1\in\QQ$ and $\omega^{2}=-1-\omega\in\QQ(\omega)$, this field is $\QQ(\omega)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equal}
$\QQ(\omega)=\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$.

::: pf-proof
$\omega=\tfrac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}$ lies in $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ because $\sqrt{-3}\in\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ and $\QQ$ contains $\tfrac12$ and $-1$. Conversely $\sqrt{-3}=2\omega+1\in\QQ(\omega)$, so each field is contained in the other and they are equal.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The splitting field is $E=\QQ(\omega)=\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$, of degree $2$ over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#splitting){.pf-ref} and [](#equal){.pf-ref} identify the field. The degree is $2$ because $x^{2}+x+1$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ (it has no rational root, so no linear factor, and it is quadratic), and $\omega$ is a root of it, so its minimal polynomial over $\QQ$ is $x^{2}+x+1$.
:::
