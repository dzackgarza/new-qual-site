::: {.problem}
Let $p$ be prime and let $R$ be a ring with identity having $p^2$ elements. Prove that $R$ is commutative.
:::

::: {.hint}
The additive order of $1_R$ is either $p$ or $p^2$. In the
second case, every element is an integer multiple of $1_R$.
In the first case, view $R$ as a two-dimensional vector space
over its central prime field and choose a basis containing
$1_R$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $1_R$ for the multiplicative identity and let $c$ be
the [[FD-LXZIW|characteristic]] of $R$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The integer $c$ is either $p$ or $p^2$.

::: pf-proof

Since $\abs{R}=p^2>1$, the identity is nonzero: otherwise
every $r\in R$ would satisfy $r=r1_R=r0=0$. The additive
order of $1_R$ is finite and divides $p^2$ by
[[T-GJNT5|Lagrange's theorem]] applied to the additive group
of $R$. It is not $1$, since $1_R\neq0$. This order is $c$,
so $c=p$ or $c=p^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $c=p^2$, then $R$ is commutative.

::: pf-proof

The additive subgroup generated by $1_R$ has $p^2$ elements,
so it equals $R$. Given $r,s\in R$, choose integers $a,b$
with $r=a1_R$ and $s=b1_R$. Distributivity gives
$$
rs=(a1_R)(b1_R)=ab1_R=ba1_R=(b1_R)(a1_R)=sr.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $c=p$, then $R$ is commutative.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s3-1}

The map
$$
j\colon\FF_p\longrightarrow R,
\qquad\overline a\longmapsto a1_R,
$$
identifies $\FF_p=\ZZ/p\ZZ$ with a central subfield of $R$.

::: pf-proof

The equality $p1_R=0$ makes $j$ well defined. Addition and
multiplication of integer multiples of $1_R$ show that $j$
is a unital ring homomorphism. Since the additive order of
$1_R$ is exactly $p$, its kernel is zero. Thus its image
is a subfield. For every integer $a$ and every $r\in R$,
distributivity gives
$$
(a1_R)r=ar=r(a1_R).
$$
Therefore every element in the image of $j$ commutes with
every element of $R$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-2}

There exists $x\in R$ such that every element of $R$
has a unique expression $a1_R+bx$ with $a,b\in\FF_p$.

::: pf-proof

Use the embedding $j$ from step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref} to define scalar
multiplication $ar\coloneqq j(a)r$ for $a\in\FF_p$ and
$r\in R$. The ring laws make $R$ a vector space over
$\FF_p$. It has finite dimension $d$, since its underlying
set is finite, and its cardinality is $p^d$. Hence
$p^d=p^2$ gives $d=2$. The vector $1_R$ is nonzero, so it
extends to a basis $1_R,x$. Expansion in this basis gives
the claimed expressions and their uniqueness.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-3}

Every pair of elements of $R$ commutes.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3-2){.pf-ref}, given $r,s\in R$ there are
$a,b,u,v\in\FF_p$ such that $r=a1_R+bx$ and
$s=u1_R+vx$. The scalars commute with all elements of $R$
by step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref}, so distributivity gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
rs&=au1_R+(av+bu)x+bv x^2,\\
sr&=ua1_R+(ub+va)x+vb x^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The coefficients agree because $\FF_p$ is commutative.
Consequently $rs=sr$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3-3){.pf-ref} proves that $R$ is commutative, using the scalar
embedding and basis from steps [](#s3-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s3-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives all possible values of $c$. Steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} prove commutativity in each case.

:::
