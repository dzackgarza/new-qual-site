::: problem
Let $X$ be the quotient space
\[
X=\frac{S^1\times[0,1]}{(e^{i\theta},1)\sim(e^{3i\theta},0)\text{ for all }e^{i\theta}\in S^1}.
\]
Show how to give $X$ the structure of a cell complex, and use this to compute the homology (in all degrees) of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
q:S^1\times[0,1]\longrightarrow X
\]
be the quotient map, and write points of $S^1$ as $e^{2\pi i s}$ with $s\in[0,1]$.

<1>1. The space $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell $c$.
::: {.proof}
Take
\[
v=q(1,0)=q(1,1),
\]
and let
\[
a=q\bigl((S^1\setminus\{1\})\times\{0\}\bigr),
\qquad
b=q\bigl(\{1\}\times(0,1)\bigr).
\]
The bottom circle is not identified with itself by the quotient relation, so $a$ is an open $1$-cell whose two ends meet at $v$.
Likewise, the two ends of $\{1\}\times[0,1]$ are identified because $1^3=1$, so $b$ is a second open $1$-cell attached at $v$.

For the $2$-cell use the characteristic map
\[
\Phi:[0,1]^2\longrightarrow X,
\qquad
\Phi(s,t)=q(e^{2\pi i s},t).
\]
Its restriction to $(0,1)^2$ is a homeomorphism onto
\[
c=q\bigl((S^1\setminus\{1\})\times(0,1)\bigr),
\]
and every side of $[0,1]^2$ maps into $v\cup a\cup b$.
Thus $v,a,b,c$ are the cells of a CW structure on $X$.
:::

<1>2. With suitable orientations, the cellular boundary of the $2$-cell is
\[
d_2(c)=-2a,
\]
while $d_1=0$.
::: {.proof}
Orient the square counterclockwise and orient $a$ by increasing $s$ along the bottom edge and $b$ by increasing $t$ along the right edge.

The four oriented sides of the characteristic square contribute as follows.
The bottom edge traverses $a$ once, so contributes $+a$.
The right edge traverses $b$ once, so contributes $+b$.
Along the top edge the boundary orientation runs from $s=1$ to $s=0$, while the quotient relation gives
\[
\Phi(s,1)
=q(e^{2\pi i s},1)
=q(e^{6\pi i s},0).
\]
Hence the top edge traverses $a$ three times with the opposite orientation and contributes $-3a$.
Finally, the left edge traverses $b$ downward and contributes $-b$.
Therefore
\[
d_2(c)=a+b-3a-b=-2a.
\]

There is only one $0$-cell, and both $1$-cells are loops based at it, so their cellular boundaries vanish. Hence $d_1=0$.
:::

<1>3. The cellular chain complex is
\[
0\longrightarrow \ZZ
\xrightarrow{\ d_2\ }
\ZZ a\oplus\ZZ b
\xrightarrow{\ 0\ }
\ZZ
\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
d_2(1)=(-2,0).
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 there is one cell in dimensions $0$ and $2$, two cells in dimension $1$, and no cells in higher dimensions.
By <1>2 the only nonzero cellular differential is the displayed map $d_2$.
:::

<1>4. The homology groups of $X$ are
\[
\boxed{
H_n(X;\ZZ)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ, & n=0,\\[2mm]
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2\ZZ, & n=1,\\[2mm]
0, & n\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
The map
\[
d_2:\ZZ\longrightarrow\ZZ^2,
\qquad
1\longmapsto(-2,0),
\]
is injective, so
\[
H_2(X)=\ker d_2=0.
\]
Since $d_1=0$,
\[
H_1(X)
=\frac{\ZZ a\oplus\ZZ b}{\langle 2a\rangle}
\cong \ZZ/2\ZZ\oplus\ZZ.
\]
Also
\[
H_0(X)=\ZZ
\]
because the cellular differential into $C_0$ is zero and there is one $0$-cell.
There are no cells in dimensions $n\ge3$, so the homology vanishes there as well.
:::
