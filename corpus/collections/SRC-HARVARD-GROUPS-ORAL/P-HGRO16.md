::: {.problem}
Let $G=\RR/\ZZ$.
Describe the subgroup generated by an irrational element $r\in[0,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The subgroup is
$$
\langle r+\ZZ\rangle
=\{nr+\ZZ:n\in\ZZ\}.
$$
It is infinite cyclic and dense in $\RR/\ZZ$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The subgroup $\langle r+\ZZ\rangle$ is infinite cyclic.

::: pf-proof

The homomorphism
$$
\ZZ\longrightarrow \RR/\ZZ,
\qquad n\longmapsto nr+\ZZ
$$
has image $\langle r+\ZZ\rangle$. If $nr\in\ZZ$ for some
$n\ne0$, then $r$ is rational, contrary to hypothesis. Thus the homomorphism is
injective, so the generated subgroup is isomorphic to $\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The subgroup $\langle r+\ZZ\rangle$ is dense in
$\RR/\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

Let $C$ be its closure and let
$$
\pi:\RR\to\RR/\ZZ
$$
be the quotient map. Then $L=\pi^{-1}(C)$ is a closed subgroup of the additive
group $\RR$ containing $\ZZ$.

Every closed subgroup of $\RR$ is either $\RR$ or $a\ZZ$ for
some $a\ge0$: if a nonzero closed subgroup has no least positive element, it has
positive elements arbitrarily close to $0$ and is therefore dense, hence equal
to $\RR$ by closedness; otherwise its least positive element $a$ generates
the subgroup by the division algorithm.

If $L=a\ZZ$, the inclusion $\ZZ\subseteq L$ forces $a=1/m$ for
some positive integer $m$. Then
$$
C=L/\ZZ
$$
is finite. But $C$ contains the infinite subgroup from step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, a contradiction.
Hence $L=\RR$, so $C=\RR/\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the cyclic subgroup generated by an irrational class is countably
infinite and dense in the circle group.

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::
