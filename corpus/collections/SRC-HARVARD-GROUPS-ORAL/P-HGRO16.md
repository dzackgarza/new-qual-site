::: problem
Let $G=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$.
Describe the subgroup generated by an irrational element $r\in[0,1]$.
:::

::: solution
The subgroup is
\[
\langle r+\mathbb Z\rangle
=\{nr+\mathbb Z:n\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
It is infinite cyclic and dense in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$.

<1>1. The subgroup $\langle r+\mathbb Z\rangle$ is infinite cyclic.
::: proof
The homomorphism
\[
\mathbb Z\longrightarrow \mathbb R/\mathbb Z,
\qquad n\longmapsto nr+\mathbb Z
\]
has image $\langle r+\mathbb Z\rangle$. If $nr\in\mathbb Z$ for some
$n\ne0$, then $r$ is rational, contrary to hypothesis. Thus the homomorphism is
injective, so the generated subgroup is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>2. The subgroup $\langle r+\mathbb Z\rangle$ is dense in
$\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$.
::: proof
Let $C$ be its closure and let
\[
\pi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z
\]
be the quotient map. Then $L=\pi^{-1}(C)$ is a closed subgroup of the additive
group $\mathbb R$ containing $\mathbb Z$.

Every closed subgroup of $\mathbb R$ is either $\mathbb R$ or $a\mathbb Z$ for
some $a\ge0$: if a nonzero closed subgroup has no least positive element, it has
positive elements arbitrarily close to $0$ and is therefore dense, hence equal
to $\mathbb R$ by closedness; otherwise its least positive element $a$ generates
the subgroup by the division algorithm.

If $L=a\mathbb Z$, the inclusion $\mathbb Z\subseteq L$ forces $a=1/m$ for
some positive integer $m$. Then
\[
C=L/\mathbb Z
\]
is finite. But $C$ contains the infinite subgroup from <1>1, a contradiction.
Hence $L=\mathbb R$, so $C=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>3. Therefore the cyclic subgroup generated by an irrational class is countably
infinite and dense in the circle group.
::: proof
Combine <1>1 and <1>2.
:::
:::
