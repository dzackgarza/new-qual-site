::: problem
Let $f$ be Lebesgue measurable on $\RR$ and $E \subset \RR$ be measurable such that
$$
0<A=\int_{E} f(x) d x<\infty.
$$

Show that for every $0 < t < 1$, there exists a measurable set $E_t \subset E$ such that
$$
\int_{E_{t}} f(x) d x=t A.
$$
:::
::: solution
<1>1. Reduce to the positive part without changing the target.
::: proof
Since
\[
A=\int_E f\,dx
=\int_E f^+\,dx-\int_E f^-\,dx>0,
\]
we have
\[
P:=\int_E f^+\,dx\ge A>tA.
\]
Thus it is enough to find a measurable subset of $E\cap\{f>0\}$ on which the integral of $f$ equals $tA$.
:::

<1>2. Build a continuous cumulative integral.
::: proof
Define the finite positive measure
\[
\nu(B):=\int_{E\cap B} f^+(x)\,dx
\]
on Borel subsets $B\subseteq\mathbb R$, and set
\[
F(s):=\nu(({-\infty},s])
=\int_{E\cap(-\infty,s]}f^+(x)\,dx.
\]
Because $\nu\ll m$, every singleton has $\nu$-measure zero. Hence $F$ is continuous: if $s_n\downarrow s$, continuity from above gives
\[
F(s_n)\to \nu(({-\infty},s])=F(s),
\]
and if $s_n\uparrow s$, continuity from below gives
\[
F(s_n)\to \nu(({-\infty},s))=F(s)
\]
because $\nu(\{s\})=0$.

Moreover,
\[
\lim_{s\to-\infty}F(s)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{s\to\infty}F(s)=P.
\]
:::

<1>3. Apply the intermediate value theorem.
::: proof
Since
\[
0<tA<P,
\]
continuity of $F$ gives some $s_t\in\mathbb R$ such that
\[
F(s_t)=tA.
\]
Set
\[
E_t:=E\cap\{f>0\}\cap(-\infty,s_t].
\]
Then $E_t$ is measurable, $E_t\subseteq E$, and on $E_t$ we have $f=f^+$. Therefore
\[
\int_{E_t}f(x)\,dx
=\int_{E\cap(-\infty,s_t]}f^+(x)\,dx
=F(s_t)
=tA.
\]
This proves the claim.
:::
:::
