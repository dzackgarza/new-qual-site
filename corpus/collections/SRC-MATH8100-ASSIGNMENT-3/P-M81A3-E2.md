::: problem
Prove that if $f$ is Borel measurable, then $f\circ g$ is Lebesgue measurable when $g$ is Lebesgue measurable.
Prove the corresponding Borel-measurable statement.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $B\subseteq\mathbb R$ be Borel. Since $f$ is Borel measurable,
\[
f^{-1}(B)
\]
is a Borel subset of the domain of $f$.

If $g$ is Lebesgue measurable, then the preimage under $g$ of every Borel set is Lebesgue measurable. Therefore
\[
(f\circ g)^{-1}(B)
=g^{-1}(f^{-1}(B))
\]
is Lebesgue measurable. Since this holds for every Borel $B$, the composition $f\circ g$ is Lebesgue measurable.

If instead $g$ is Borel measurable, the same identity gives
\[
(f\circ g)^{-1}(B)
=g^{-1}(f^{-1}(B)),
\]
and now the right-hand side is Borel because $f^{-1}(B)$ is Borel and $g$ is Borel measurable. Hence $f\circ g$ is Borel measurable.

Thus a Borel measurable outer function may be composed on the right with either a Lebesgue measurable or Borel measurable function without losing the corresponding measurability.
:::
