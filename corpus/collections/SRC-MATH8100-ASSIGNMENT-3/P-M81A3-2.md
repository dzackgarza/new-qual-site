::: problem
Let $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be measurable with $m(E)>0$.

1. For every $\varepsilon>0$, prove that a closed cube $Q$ exists with $m(E\cap Q)\geq(1-\varepsilon)m(Q)$.

2. Prove that $E-E$ contains an open ball centered at the origin.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Find a cube on which $E$ has arbitrarily high relative density.
::: {.proof}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$. If $\varepsilon\ge1$, any cube works, so assume $0<\varepsilon<1$.

Choose a bounded measurable subset
\[
F=E\cap[-N,N]^n
\]
with
\[
0<m(F)<\infty.
\]
Write $M=m(F)$. By inner and outer regularity, choose a compact set $K\subseteq F$ and an open set $G\supseteq F$ such that
\[
m(K)>(1-\eta)M,
\qquad
m(G)<M+\eta,
\]
where $\eta>0$ is so small that
\[
(1-\eta)M>(1-\varepsilon)(M+\eta).
\]

Since $K$ is compact and $G$ is open,
\[
d:=\operatorname{dist}(K,G^c)>0.
\]
Choose a sufficiently fine axis-parallel grid of closed cubes, with side length smaller than $d/\sqrt n$, and take the finitely many grid cubes $Q_1,\dots,Q_r$ that meet $K$. Their interiors are pairwise disjoint, they cover $K$, and every $Q_j$ lies in $G$.

Suppose for contradiction that every one of these cubes satisfies
\[
m(E\cap Q_j)<(1-\varepsilon)m(Q_j).
\]
Because cube boundaries have measure zero,
\[
\begin{aligned}
m(K)
&\le m\left(E\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^rQ_j\right)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^r m(E\cap Q_j)\\
&<(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{j=1}^r m(Q_j)\\
&=(1-\varepsilon)m\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^rQ_j\right)\\
&\le(1-\varepsilon)m(G),
\end{aligned}
\]
contradicting the choice of $K$ and $G$. Therefore some closed cube $Q$ satisfies
\[
m(E\cap Q)\ge(1-\varepsilon)m(Q).
\]
:::

<1>2. The difference set contains a neighborhood of the origin.
::: {.proof}
Choose a bounded measurable subset $A\subseteq E$ with
\[
0<m(A)<\infty.
\]
For example, take $A=E\cap[-N,N]^n$ for large $N$.

Since $\mathbf 1_A\in L^1(\mathbb R^n)$, translations are continuous in $L^1$:
\[
\|\mathbf 1_A(\cdot-h)-\mathbf 1_A\|_1\longrightarrow0
\qquad(h\to0).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
m\bigl(A\triangle(A+h)\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence for all sufficiently small $h$,
\[
m\bigl(A\triangle(A+h)\bigr)<2m(A).
\]
Using
\[
m(A\cap(A+h))
=m(A)-\frac12m\bigl(A\triangle(A+h)\bigr),
\]
we obtain
\[
m(A\cap(A+h))>0.
\]
Thus there is some $z\in A\cap(A+h)$. Then
\[
z\in A\subseteq E,
\qquad
z-h\in A\subseteq E,
\]
so
\[
h=z-(z-h)\in E-E.
\]
Therefore every sufficiently small $h$ belongs to $E-E$, and hence $E-E$ contains an open ball centered at $0$.
:::
