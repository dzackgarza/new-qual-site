::: {.problem}
Find all cube roots of $8$ in the complex plane.
Write your answers in the form $a + bi$, where $a$ and $b$ are real numbers, and justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on it, and the computation is elementary.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #roots}
$z_{k}=2e^{2\pi ik/3}$ satisfies $z_{k}^{3}=8$ for $k=0,1,2$.

::: pf-proof
$z_{k}^{3}=2^{3}e^{2\pi ik}=8\cdot1=8$, since $e^{2\pi i}=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #explicit}
In the form $a+bi$, the three numbers are $\boxed{2,\ -1+\sqrt{3}\,i,\ -1-\sqrt{3}\,i}$.

::: pf-proof
For $k=0$: $2e^{0}=2$. For $k=1$: $e^{2\pi i/3}=\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}+i\sin\frac{2\pi}{3}=-\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$, so $z_{1}=-1+\sqrt3\,i$. For $k=2$: $e^{4\pi i/3}=-\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$, so $z_{2}=-1-\sqrt3\,i$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #distinct}
The three numbers are pairwise distinct.

::: pf-proof
$2$ has imaginary part $0$; the others have imaginary parts $\pm\sqrt3\ne0$; and $-1+\sqrt3 i\ne-1-\sqrt3 i$ since $\sqrt3\ne0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #complete}
There are no other cube roots of $8$.

::: pf-proof
A cube root of $8$ is a root of $z^{3}-8$, a polynomial of degree $3$ over $\CC$; a nonzero polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ roots, by the factor theorem and induction. Steps [](#roots){.pf-ref} and [](#distinct){.pf-ref} exhibit three distinct roots, so these are all of them.
:::
