::: {.problem}
Decide, without too much computation, whether a finite limit
$$
\lim_{z\to0}
\left(
(\tan z)^{-2}-z^{-2}
\right)
$$
exists, where $z$ is a complex variable, and if yes, compute the limit.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #tan-expansion}
As $z\to0$ in $\CC$,
$$
\tan z
=
z+\frac{z^3}{3}+O(z^5)
=
z\left(
1+\frac{z^2}{3}+O(z^4)
\right).
$$

::: pf-proof
The Taylor expansions at the origin are
$$
\sin z
=
z-\frac{z^3}{6}+O(z^5)
$$
and
$$
\cos z
=
1-\frac{z^2}{2}+O(z^4).
$$
Since
$$
\frac1{\cos z}
=
1+\frac{z^2}{2}+O(z^4),
$$
their quotient satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan z
&=
\left(
z-\frac{z^3}{6}+O(z^5)
\right)
\left(
1+\frac{z^2}{2}+O(z^4)
\right)\\
&=
z+\frac{z^3}{3}+O(z^5).
\end{aligned}
$$
Factoring out $z$ gives the second form.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #reciprocal-square-expansion}
As $z\to0$ through $z\ne0$,
$$
(\tan z)^{-2}
=
z^{-2}
-\frac23
+O(z^2).
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#tan-expansion){.pf-ref},
$$
(\tan z)^{-2}
=
z^{-2}
\left(
1+\frac{z^2}{3}+O(z^4)
\right)^{-2}.
$$
The Taylor expansion
$$
(1+w)^{-2}=1-2w+O(w^2)
$$
at $w=0$, applied to
$$
w=\frac{z^2}{3}+O(z^4),
$$
gives
$$
\left(
1+\frac{z^2}{3}+O(z^4)
\right)^{-2}
=
1-\frac{2z^2}{3}+O(z^4).
$$
Multiplying by $z^{-2}$ proves the claim.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #limit-value-boxed}
The requested finite limit exists and equals
$$
\boxed{-\frac23}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Subtracting $z^{-2}$ from the expansion in step [](#reciprocal-square-expansion){.pf-ref} gives
$$
(\tan z)^{-2}-z^{-2}
=
-\frac23+O(z^2).
$$
The error term tends to $0$ as $z\to0$ in $\CC$, so the limit is
$-2/3$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#limit-value-boxed){.pf-ref} establishes existence and computes the value requested by the
source.
:::
