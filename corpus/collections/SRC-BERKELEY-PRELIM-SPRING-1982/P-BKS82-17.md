::: {.problem}
Let $(f_n)$ be a sequence of continuous functions from $[0,1]$ to
$\mathbb R$. Suppose
$$
f_n(x)\to0
$$
for every $x\in[0,1]$, and suppose there is a constant $K$ such that
$$
\left|\int_0^1f_n(x)\,dx\right|\le K
$$
for every $n$.

Must
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1f_n(x)\,dx=0?
$$
Prove your answer or give a counterexample.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The answer is no. Set $f_1=f_2=0$. For $n\ge3$, define
$$
f_n(x)
=
\begin{cases}
n^2\left(x-\dfrac1n\right),
&
\dfrac1n\le x\le\dfrac2n,
\\[6pt]
n^2\left(\dfrac3n-x\right),
&
\dfrac2n\le x\le\dfrac3n,
\\[6pt]
0,
&
\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
For $n\ge3$, the support interval $[1/n,3/n]$ lies in $[0,1]$. The two
linear pieces both take the value $n$ at $x=2/n$ and vanish at their outer
endpoints. Hence they join continuously to each other and to the zero
pieces. Thus every $f_n$ is continuous on $[0,1]$.
:::

<1>2. For every fixed $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
f_n(x)\longrightarrow0.
$$

::: {.proof}
At $x=0$, one has $f_n(0)=0$ for every $n$. Fix $x>0$. Choose $N$ so
large that
$$
\frac3n<x
$$
for every $n\ge N$. Then $x$ lies outside the support
$[1/n,3/n]$ of $f_n$, so
$$
f_n(x)=0
$$
for every $n\ge N$. Hence $f_n(x)\to0$.
:::

<1>3. For every $n\ge3$,
$$
\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
The graph of $f_n$ is a triangle with base
$$
\frac3n-\frac1n=\frac2n
$$
and height
$$
f_n\left(\frac2n\right)=n.
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx
=
\frac12\frac2n\,n
=1.
$$
:::

<1>4. The integrals are uniformly bounded but do not converge to $0$.

::: {.proof}
For $n=1,2$ the integral is $0$, and for $n\ge3$ it is $1$ by step
<1>3. Hence
$$
\left|
\int_0^1f_n(x)\,dx
\right|
\le1
$$
for every $n$, so the hypothesis holds with $K=1$. But
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\int_0^1f_n(x)\,dx
=1\ne0.
$$
:::

<1>5. Therefore pointwise convergence to zero together with the stated
uniform bound on the integrals does not force convergence of the integrals
to zero.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>4 give a sequence satisfying every source hypothesis but
violating the proposed conclusion.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 answers the question negatively.
:::
