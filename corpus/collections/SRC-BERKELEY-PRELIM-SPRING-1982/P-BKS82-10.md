::: {.problem}
Prove that every group of order $77$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be a group of order
$$
77=7\cdot11.
$$

<1>1. The Sylow $11$-subgroup of $G$ is unique and therefore normal.

::: {.proof}
If $n_{11}$ denotes the number of Sylow $11$-subgroups, Sylow's theorem
gives
$$
n_{11}\mid7
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n_{11}\equiv1\pmod{11}.
$$
The divisors of $7$ are $1$ and $7$, and only $1$ is congruent to $1$
modulo $11$. Hence $n_{11}=1$.
:::

<1>2. The Sylow $7$-subgroup of $G$ is unique and therefore normal.

::: {.proof}
If $n_7$ denotes the number of Sylow $7$-subgroups, then
$$
n_7\mid11
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n_7\equiv1\pmod7.
$$
The divisors of $11$ are $1$ and $11$, while
$$
11\equiv4\pmod7.
$$
Thus $n_7=1$.
:::

<1>3. If $P$ and $Q$ are the Sylow $7$- and $11$-subgroups, respectively,
then every element of $P$ commutes with every element of $Q$.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1 and <1>2, both $P$ and $Q$ are normal. For $p\in P$ and
$q\in Q$, the commutator
$$
[p,q]=pqp^{-1}q^{-1}
$$
lies in $P$ because $P$ is normal and also lies in $Q$ because $Q$ is
normal. But
$$
P\cap Q=\{1\},
$$
since the order of the intersection divides both $7$ and $11$. Hence
$[p,q]=1$, so $pq=qp$.
:::

<1>4. The group $G$ is cyclic of order $77$.

::: {.proof}
Choose generators $p$ of $P$ and $q$ of $Q$. These subgroups are cyclic
because they have prime order. By step <1>3, $p$ and $q$ commute.

If
$$
(pq)^m=1,
$$
then
$$
p^m=q^{-m}\in P\cap Q=\{1\}.
$$
Thus $7\mid m$ and $11\mid m$, so $77\mid m$. Since
$(pq)^{77}=1$, the element $pq$ has order $77$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{G=\langle pq\rangle}
$$
is cyclic.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves the claim.
:::
:::
