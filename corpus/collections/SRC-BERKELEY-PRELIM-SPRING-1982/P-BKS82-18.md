::: {.problem}
Define
\[
F(z)=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-zt}}{1+t^4}\,dt.
\]
Prove that $F$ is continuous on the closed half-plane
\[
\operatorname{Re}z\ge0
\]
and analytic on the open half-plane
\[
\operatorname{Re}z>0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #absolute-convergence}
For every $z$ with $\operatorname{Re}z\ge0$, the defining integral
for $F(z)$ converges absolutely.

::: pf-proof
For $t\ge0$,
$$
\abs{e^{-zt}}
=
e^{-t\operatorname{Re}z}
\le1.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{
\frac{e^{-zt}}{1+t^4}
}
\le
\frac1{1+t^4}.
$$
The majorant is integrable on $[0,\infty)$, so the defining integral is
absolutely convergent.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-is-continuous}
The function $F$ is continuous on
$$
\{z\in\CC:\operatorname{Re}z\ge0\}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $z_n\to z$, with
$$
\operatorname{Re}z_n\ge0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{Re}z\ge0.
$$
For each $t\ge0$,
$$
\frac{e^{-z_nt}}{1+t^4}
\longrightarrow
\frac{e^{-zt}}{1+t^4}.
$$
By step [](#absolute-convergence){.pf-ref}, every term is dominated in absolute value by the integrable
function
$$
\frac1{1+t^4}.
$$
The dominated convergence theorem therefore gives
$$
F(z_n)
=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-z_nt}}{1+t^4}\,dt
\longrightarrow
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-zt}}{1+t^4}\,dt
=F(z).
$$
Thus $F$ is continuous on the closed half-plane.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #difference-quotient-formula}
Fix $z_0$ with $\operatorname{Re}z_0>0$. For sufficiently small
nonzero $h\in\CC$,
$$
\frac{F(z_0+h)-F(z_0)}{h}
=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-z_0t}}{1+t^4}
\frac{e^{-ht}-1}{h}
\,dt.
$$

::: pf-proof
Choose $h$ with
$$
\abs h<\frac12\operatorname{Re}z_0.
$$
Then $\operatorname{Re}(z_0+h)>0$, so both integrals are absolutely
convergent by step [](#absolute-convergence){.pf-ref}. Linearity of the integral gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{F(z_0+h)-F(z_0)}{h}
&=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-(z_0+h)t}-e^{-z_0t}}{h(1+t^4)}
\,dt\\
&=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-z_0t}}{1+t^4}
\frac{e^{-ht}-1}{h}
\,dt.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #difference-quotient-dominated}
For
$$
\abs h<\frac12\operatorname{Re}z_0,
$$
the integrand in step [](#difference-quotient-formula){.pf-ref} is dominated in absolute value by the integrable
function
$$
\frac{
t e^{-t\operatorname{Re}z_0/2}
}{
1+t^4
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For $h\ne0$,
$$
\frac{e^{-ht}-1}{h}
=
-t\int_0^1 e^{-sht}\,ds.
$$
Therefore
$$
\abs{
\frac{e^{-ht}-1}{h}
}
\le
t\int_0^1 e^{s\abs h t}\,ds
\le
t e^{\abs h t}.
$$
Multiplying by
$$
\abs{e^{-z_0t}}=e^{-t\operatorname{Re}z_0}
$$
and using
$\abs h<\operatorname{Re}z_0/2$ gives the stated majorant. It is
integrable because of its exponential decay as $t\to\infty$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #derivative-exists}
The complex derivative of $F$ at $z_0$ exists and equals
$$
F'(z_0)
=
-\int_0^\infty
\frac{t e^{-z_0t}}{1+t^4}\,dt.
$$

::: pf-proof
For each fixed $t\ge0$,
$$
\lim_{h\to0}
\frac{e^{-ht}-1}{h}
=
-t.
$$
Apply dominated convergence to the representation in step [](#difference-quotient-formula){.pf-ref}, using the
majorant from step [](#difference-quotient-dominated){.pf-ref}. This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{h\to0}
\frac{F(z_0+h)-F(z_0)}{h}
&=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-z_0t}}{1+t^4}(-t)\,dt\\
&=
-\int_0^\infty
\frac{t e^{-z_0t}}{1+t^4}\,dt.
\end{aligned}
$$
The limit is independent of the direction in which the complex number
$h$ tends to $0$, so it is the complex derivative.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-is-analytic}
The function $F$ is analytic on
$$
\{z\in\CC:\operatorname{Re}z>0\}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The point $z_0$ in steps [](#difference-quotient-formula){.pf-ref}, [](#difference-quotient-dominated){.pf-ref}, and [](#derivative-exists){.pf-ref} was arbitrary in the open right
half-plane. Thus $F$ is complex differentiable at every point of that open
set, hence analytic there.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#f-is-continuous){.pf-ref} proves continuity on the closed half-plane, and step [](#f-is-analytic){.pf-ref} proves
analyticity on the open half-plane.
:::
