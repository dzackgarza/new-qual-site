::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field with $p$ elements, where $p$ is prime. How many nonsingular $2\times2$ matrices are there over $F$?
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #first-column-count}
The first column of a nonsingular $2\times2$ matrix over $F$ can be
chosen in
$$
p^2-1
$$
ways.

::: pf-proof
There are $p^2$ vectors in $F^2$. The first column of an invertible matrix
must be nonzero, and every nonzero vector is an allowable first column.
Hence there are $p^2-1$ choices.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #second-column-count}
After the first column has been chosen, the second column can be
chosen in
$$
p^2-p
$$
ways.

::: pf-proof
Let $v\ne0$ be the chosen first column. The matrix is nonsingular exactly
when the second column does not lie in the one-dimensional subspace
$$
Fv.
$$
That subspace has $p$ elements, while $F^2$ has $p^2$ elements. Thus
there are $p^2-p$ allowable second columns.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #count-boxed}
The number of nonsingular $2\times2$ matrices over $F$ is
$$
\boxed{
(p^2-1)(p^2-p)
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#first-column-count){.pf-ref} and [](#second-column-count){.pf-ref}, each choice of a nonzero first column admits exactly
$p^2-p$ choices of a second column independent from it. The multiplication
principle gives
$$
(p^2-1)(p^2-p).
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#count-boxed){.pf-ref} is the requested count.
:::
