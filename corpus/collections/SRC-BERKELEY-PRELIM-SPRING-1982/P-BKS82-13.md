::: {.problem}
Let $T:V\to W$ be a linear transformation between finite-dimensional vector spaces. Prove that
\[
\dim V=\dim\ker T+\dim\operatorname{im}T.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
v_1,\ldots,v_k
$$
be a basis of $\ker T$, and extend it to a basis
$$
v_1,\ldots,v_k,v_{k+1},\ldots,v_n
$$
of $V$.

<1>1. The vectors
$$
T(v_{k+1}),\ldots,T(v_n)
$$
span $\operatorname{im}T$.

::: {.proof}
Let $y\in\operatorname{im}T$. Then $y=T(v)$ for some $v\in V$. Write
$$
v
=
\sum_{i=1}^n a_i v_i.
$$
Since $v_1,\ldots,v_k\in\ker T$,
$$
y
=
T(v)
=
\sum_{i=k+1}^n a_iT(v_i).
$$
Thus the displayed images span $\operatorname{im}T$.
:::

<1>2. The vectors
$$
T(v_{k+1}),\ldots,T(v_n)
$$
are linearly independent.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
\sum_{i=k+1}^n a_iT(v_i)=0.
$$
By linearity,
$$
T\left(
\sum_{i=k+1}^n a_iv_i
\right)=0,
$$
so
$$
\sum_{i=k+1}^n a_iv_i\in\ker T.
$$
Hence there are scalars $b_1,\ldots,b_k$ such that
$$
\sum_{i=k+1}^n a_iv_i
=
\sum_{i=1}^k b_iv_i.
$$
Since $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ is a basis of $V$, all coefficients in this
relation vanish. In particular,
$$
a_{k+1}=\cdots=a_n=0.
$$
Thus the displayed images are linearly independent.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\dim\ker T=k
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\dim\operatorname{im}T=n-k.
$$

::: {.proof}
The first equality holds because $v_1,\ldots,v_k$ is a basis of
$\ker T$. By steps <1>1 and <1>2,
$$
T(v_{k+1}),\ldots,T(v_n)
$$
is a basis of $\operatorname{im}T$, so its dimension is $n-k$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\dim V
=
\dim\ker T
+
\dim\operatorname{im}T
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The chosen basis of $V$ has $n$ vectors, so $\dim V=n$. Step <1>3 gives
$$
\dim\ker T+\dim\operatorname{im}T
=
k+(n-k)
=n.
$$
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the rank--nullity formula.
:::
:::
