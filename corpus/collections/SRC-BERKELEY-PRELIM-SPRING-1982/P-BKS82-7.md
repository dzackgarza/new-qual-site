::: {.problem}
Suppose a group $G$ is generated by $x,y$ and satisfies
\[
x^5y^3=x^8y^5=1.
\]
Is $G$ the trivial group?
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #relations-solved}
The two defining relations imply
$$
x^5=y^{-3}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x^8=y^{-5}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Right-multiplying $x^5y^3=1$ by $y^{-3}$ gives
$x^5=y^{-3}$, and right-multiplying $x^8y^5=1$ by $y^{-5}$ gives
$x^8=y^{-5}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #x-is-identity}
One has
$$
x=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
Since
$$
5\cdot5-8\cdot3=1,
$$
powers of the single element $x$ give
$$
x
=
(x^5)^5(x^8)^{-3}.
$$
Substituting the identities from step [](#relations-solved){.pf-ref},
$$
x
=
(y^{-3})^5(y^{-5})^{-3}
=
y^{-15}y^{15}
=1.
$$
No commutativity assumption on $G$ is used: only powers of the same element
are combined.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #y-is-identity}
One has
$$
y=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
With $x=1$, the original relations become
$$
y^3=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y^5=1.
$$
Since
$$
2\cdot3-5=1,
$$
it follows that
$$
y=(y^3)^2(y^5)^{-1}=1.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #g-is-trivial}
Therefore
$$
\boxed{G=\{1\}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The group $G$ is generated by $x$ and $y$, and steps [](#x-is-identity){.pf-ref} and [](#y-is-identity){.pf-ref} show
that both generators are the identity.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#g-is-trivial){.pf-ref} answers the question affirmatively.
:::
