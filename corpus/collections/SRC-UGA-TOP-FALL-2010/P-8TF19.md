::: problem
a. What is meant by the degree of a continuous map $f:S^n\to S^n$?

b. Let $f:S^n\to S^n$ be a continuous map with no fixed points.
Prove that $f$ has degree $(-1)^{n+1}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For a continuous map $f:S^n\to S^n$, its degree is the unique integer $d$ such that
\[
f_*:\widetilde H_n(S^n;\ZZ)\longrightarrow \widetilde H_n(S^n;\ZZ)
\]
is multiplication by $d$ after choosing the standard generator of
\[
\widetilde H_n(S^n;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
\]
We write $d=\deg(f)$.
::: {.proof}
The top reduced homology of $S^n$ is infinite cyclic, so every endomorphism of it is multiplication by a unique integer.
For $n\ge1$ this is the usual definition using $H_n(S^n;\ZZ)$; reduced homology also makes the same definition valid for $S^0$.
:::

<1>2. If $f:S^n\to S^n$ has no fixed points, then $f$ is homotopic to the antipodal map
\[
A:S^n\to S^n,
\qquad
A(x)=-x.
\]
::: {.proof}
Regard $S^n$ as the unit sphere in $\RR^{n+1}$.
For $0\le t\le1$, define
\[
H(x,t)
=
\frac{(1-t)f(x)-tx}
{\left\lVert(1-t)f(x)-tx\right\rVert}.
\]
We first verify that the denominator never vanishes.
If
\[
(1-t)f(x)-tx=0,
\]
then
\[
(1-t)f(x)=tx.
\]
Taking norms and using $\lVert f(x)\rVert=\lVert x\rVert=1$ gives
\[
1-t=t,
\]
so $t=1/2$.
The vector equality then gives $f(x)=x$, contrary to the hypothesis that $f$ has no fixed points.

Thus $H$ is a well-defined continuous map into $S^n$.
At the endpoints,
\[
H(x,0)=f(x),
\qquad
H(x,1)=-x=A(x).
\]
Hence $H$ is a homotopy from $f$ to $A$.
:::

<1>3. The antipodal map $A:S^n\to S^n$ has degree
\[
\deg(A)=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The antipodal map is the restriction to the unit sphere of the linear automorphism
\[
-I:\RR^{n+1}\to\RR^{n+1}.
\]
An invertible linear map of $\RR^{n+1}$ induces on the oriented boundary sphere a map of degree equal to the sign of its determinant.
Here
\[
\det(-I)=(-1)^{n+1},
\]
so
\[
\deg(A)=\operatorname{sgn}\det(-I)=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
:::

<1>4. Therefore every fixed-point-free continuous map $f:S^n\to S^n$ has degree $(-1)^{n+1}$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, $f\simeq A$.
Homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism on homology, so degree is homotopy invariant.
Using <1>3,
\[
\deg(f)=\deg(A)=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
This proves part (b).
:::
