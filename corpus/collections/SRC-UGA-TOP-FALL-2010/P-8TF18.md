::: problem
Fix an integer $n$.
Let $X$ be the space obtained from the cylinder $S^1\times[0,1]$ by identifying the point $(z,0)$ with the point $(z^n,1)$ for all $z\in S^1$.
Find the fundamental group of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Reversing the interval coordinate identifies $X$ with the mapping torus
\[
M_f=\frac{S^1\times[0,1]}{(z,1)\sim(f(z),0)},
\qquad
f(z)=z^n.
\]
::: {.proof}
The homeomorphism
\[
S^1\times[0,1]\longrightarrow S^1\times[0,1],
\qquad
(z,s)\longmapsto(z,1-s)
\]
sends the relation $(z,0)\sim(z^n,1)$ to
\[
(z,1)\sim(z^n,0).
\]
Thus it descends to a homeomorphism from the stated quotient to $M_f$.
:::

<1>2. The space $M_f$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $a,t$, and one $2$-cell whose attaching word is
\[
a\,t\,a^{-n}\,t^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Give $S^1$ its CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$ and one oriented $1$-cell $a$.
The image of $v\times[0,1]$ in the mapping torus is a loop; call its oriented $1$-cell $t$.
The image of the strip $a\times[0,1]$ is the single $2$-cell.

Traverse the boundary of this strip starting along $a\times\{0\}$.
The four sides contribute, in order,
\[
a,
\qquad
t,
\qquad
f(a)^{-1},
\qquad
t^{-1}.
\]
Since the degree-$n$ map $f(z)=z^n$ sends the generator of $\pi_1(S^1)$ to its $n$th power,
\[
f(a)=a^n.
\]
Hence the attaching word is $a t a^{-n}t^{-1}$.
:::

<1>3. Therefore
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\left\langle a,t\ \middle|\ t^{-1}at=a^n\right\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
By the Seifert--van Kampen theorem for a CW complex, the two $1$-cells generate the fundamental group of the $1$-skeleton, and attaching the $2$-cell imposes the relation represented by its boundary word.
Thus
\[
\pi_1(M_f)
\cong
\left\langle a,t\ \middle|\ a t a^{-n}t^{-1}=1\right\rangle.
\]
The displayed relator is equivalent to
\[
a t=t a^n,
\]
and hence to
\[
t^{-1}at=a^n.
\]
By <1>1 the same presentation is $\pi_1(X)$.
:::

<1>4. In particular, the answer specializes correctly in the basic cases:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
n&\pi_1(X)\\
\hline
0&\ZZ,\\
1&\ZZ^2,\\
-1&\langle a,t\mid t^{-1}at=a^{-1}\rangle.
\end{array}
\]
::: {.proof}
If $n=0$, the relation $t^{-1}at=1$ forces $a=1$, leaving the infinite cyclic group generated by $t$.
If $n=1$, the relation says $a$ and $t$ commute, giving $\ZZ^2$.
If $n=-1$, the displayed presentation is the standard Klein-bottle group.
For $n\ne0$, this is the Baumslag--Solitar group $BS(1,n)$ (up to the usual choice of stable-letter orientation).
:::
