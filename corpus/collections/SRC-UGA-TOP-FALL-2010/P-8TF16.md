::: problem
a. What does it mean to say a topological space is compact?

b. Assume $X$ and $Y$ are nonempty. Show that $X\times Y$ is compact if and only if both $X$ and $Y$ are compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
For (a), a topological space $Z$ is **compact** if every open cover of $Z$ has
a finite subcover.

For (b), first suppose $X\times Y$ is compact. Since $X$ and $Y$ are nonempty,
the coordinate projections
\[
\pi_X:X\times Y\to X,
\qquad
\pi_Y:X\times Y\to Y
\]
are continuous and surjective. Continuous images of compact spaces are compact,
so both $X$ and $Y$ are compact.

Conversely, suppose $X$ and $Y$ are compact, and let $\mathcal U$ be an open
cover of $X\times Y$.

Fix $x\in X$. The fiber $\{x\}\times Y$ is homeomorphic to $Y$, hence compact,
so finitely many members
\[
U_{x,1},\ldots,U_{x,m_x}\in\mathcal U
\]
cover $\{x\}\times Y$. Put
\[
W_x=U_{x,1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{x,m_x}.
\]
Then $W_x$ is an open set containing the whole fiber $\{x\}\times Y$.

We use the tube lemma in this special case. For each $y\in Y$, choose a basic
open rectangle
\[
A_y\times B_y
\]
with
\[
(x,y)\in A_y\times B_y\subseteq W_x.
\]
The sets $B_y$ cover $Y$, so compactness of $Y$ gives
$y_1,\ldots,y_r$ such that $B_{y_1},\ldots,B_{y_r}$ cover $Y$. Define
\[
V_x=A_{y_1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{y_r}.
\]
Then $V_x$ is an open neighborhood of $x$, and
\[
V_x\times Y\subseteq W_x.
\]

The family $\{V_x:x\in X\}$ is an open cover of $X$. By compactness of $X$,
choose $x_1,\ldots,x_s$ with
\[
X=V_{x_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{x_s}.
\]
For each $j$, the finitely many sets
$U_{x_j,1},\ldots,U_{x_j,m_{x_j}}$ cover $V_{x_j}\times Y$. Taking all of
these finitely many members of $\mathcal U$ therefore covers
\[
X\times Y=\bigcup_{j=1}^s(V_{x_j}\times Y).
\]
Thus $X\times Y$ is compact.

The nonempty hypothesis is necessary for the converse: if $Y=\varnothing$,
then $X\times Y=\varnothing$ is compact for every space $X$.
:::
