::: problem
a. What does it mean to say a topological space is compact?

b. Assume $X$ and $Y$ are nonempty.
Show that $X\times Y$ is compact if and only if both $X$ and $Y$ are compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A topological space $Z$ is compact if every open cover of $Z$ has a finite subcover.
::: {.proof}
This is the definition of compactness, answering part (a).
:::

<1>2. If $X\times Y$ is compact, then $X$ and $Y$ are compact.
::: {.proof}
Since $X$ and $Y$ are nonempty, the coordinate projections
\[
\pi_X:X\times Y\to X,
\qquad
\pi_Y:X\times Y\to Y
\]
are continuous and surjective.
Continuous images of compact spaces are compact, so both $X$ and $Y$ are compact.
:::

<1>3. Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are compact. For every open cover $\mathcal U$ of $X\times Y$ and every $x\in X$, there is an open neighborhood $V_x$ of $x$ such that $V_x\times Y$ is covered by finitely many members of $\mathcal U$.
::: {.proof}
Fix $x\in X$. The fiber $\{x\}\times Y$ is homeomorphic to $Y$, hence compact, so finitely many members
\[
U_{x,1},\ldots,U_{x,m_x}\in\mathcal U
\]
cover $\{x\}\times Y$.
Put
\[
W_x=U_{x,1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{x,m_x}.
\]
Then $W_x$ is an open set containing the whole fiber $\{x\}\times Y$.

For each $y\in Y$, choose a basic open rectangle
\[
A_y\times B_y
\]
with
\[
(x,y)\in A_y\times B_y\subseteq W_x.
\]
The sets $B_y$ cover $Y$, so compactness of $Y$ gives $y_1,\ldots,y_r$ such that $B_{y_1},\ldots,B_{y_r}$ cover $Y$.
Define
\[
V_x=A_{y_1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{y_r}.
\]
Then $V_x$ is an open neighborhood of $x$, and
\[
V_x\times Y\subseteq W_x.
\]
Thus the finitely many $U_{x,j}$ cover $V_x\times Y$.
:::

<1>4. If $X$ and $Y$ are compact, then $X\times Y$ is compact.
::: {.proof}
Let $\mathcal U$ be an open cover of $X\times Y$. By <1>3, for each $x\in X$ there is an open $V_x\ni x$ such that $V_x\times Y$ is covered by finitely many members of $\mathcal U$.

The family $\{V_x:x\in X\}$ covers $X$. Compactness of $X$ gives $x_1,\ldots,x_s$ with
\[
X=V_{x_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{x_s}.
\]
For each $j$, the finitely many sets $U_{x_j,1},\ldots,U_{x_j,m_{x_j}}$ cover $V_{x_j}\times Y$.
Taking all of these finitely many members of $\mathcal U$ therefore covers
\[
X\times Y=\bigcup_{j=1}^s(V_{x_j}\times Y).
\]
Thus $X\times Y$ is compact.
:::

<1>5. The nonempty-factor hypothesis is necessary for the reverse implication in <1>2.
::: {.proof}
If $Y=\varnothing$, then $X\times Y=\varnothing$ is compact for every space $X$, including noncompact $X$. Thus compactness of the product alone cannot imply compactness of both factors without the nonempty assumption.
:::

<1>6. Part (b) follows.
::: {.proof}
Under the stated nonempty hypotheses, <1>2 and <1>4 give both implications.
:::
:::
