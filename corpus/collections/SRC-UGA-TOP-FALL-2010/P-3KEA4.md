::: problem
For which compact connected surfaces $\Sigma$ (with or without boundary) does there exist a continuous map $f : \Sigma \to \Sigma$ that is homotopic to the identity and has no fixed point?

Explain your answer fully.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If a compact connected surface $\Sigma$ admits a fixed-point-free map $f:\Sigma\to\Sigma$ homotopic to the identity, then
\[
\chi(\Sigma)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
A compact surface has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex, so the Lefschetz number of $f$ is
\[
L(f)=\sum_q(-1)^q
\operatorname{tr}\left(
f_*:H_q(\Sigma;\QQ)\to H_q(\Sigma;\QQ)
\right).
\]
Since $f\simeq\operatorname{id}_\Sigma$, homotopy invariance of homology gives
\[
f_*=\operatorname{id}
\]
in every degree.
Hence
\[
L(f)=\sum_q(-1)^q\dim_{\QQ}H_q(\Sigma;\QQ)=\chi(\Sigma).
\]

The Lefschetz fixed point theorem says that $L(f)\ne0$ implies that $f$ has a fixed point.
Since $f$ is fixed-point-free, necessarily $L(f)=0$.
Therefore $\chi(\Sigma)=0$.
:::

<1>2. The compact connected surfaces with Euler characteristic zero are precisely
\[
T^2,
\qquad
S^1\times[0,1],
\qquad
K,
\qquad
M,
\]
where $K$ is the Klein bottle and $M$ is the Möbius band.
::: {.proof}
By the classification of compact connected surfaces, an orientable surface has the form $\Sigma_{g,b}$ and
\[
\chi(\Sigma_{g,b})=2-2g-b,
\]
where $g\ge0$ and $b\ge0$.
The equation $2-2g-b=0$ has exactly the solutions
\[
(g,b)=(1,0),\qquad(g,b)=(0,2),
\]
which give the torus and the annulus.

A nonorientable surface has the form $N_{k,b}$ and
\[
\chi(N_{k,b})=2-k-b,
\]
where $k\ge1$ and $b\ge0$.
The equation $2-k-b=0$ has exactly the solutions
\[
(k,b)=(2,0),\qquad(k,b)=(1,1),
\]
which give the Klein bottle and the Möbius band.
:::

<1>3. The torus admits a fixed-point-free map homotopic to the identity.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
T^2=S^1\times S^1.
\]
Define
\[
F_t(z,w)=\left(e^{\pi i t}z,w\right),
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
Then $F_0=\operatorname{id}$, while
\[
F_1(z,w)=(-z,w).
\]
The equality $F_1(z,w)=(z,w)$ would require $-z=z$, impossible for $z\in S^1$.
Thus $F_1$ is fixed-point-free and homotopic to the identity.
:::

<1>4. The annulus admits a fixed-point-free map homotopic to the identity.
::: {.proof}
Write the annulus as $S^1\times[0,1]$ and define
\[
F_t(z,s)=\left(e^{\pi i t}z,s\right).
\]
Again $F_0=\operatorname{id}$ and $F_1(z,s)=(-z,s)$ has no fixed point.
:::

<1>5. The Möbius band admits a fixed-point-free map homotopic to the identity.
::: {.proof}
Use the quotient model
\[
M=\frac{\RR\times[-1,1]}{(x,s)\sim(x+1,-s)}.
\]
For $0\le t\le1$, define
\[
F_t([x,s])=\left[x+\frac t2,s\right].
\]
Translation in the first coordinate commutes with the deck transformation
\[
(x,s)\longmapsto(x+1,-s),
\]
so every $F_t$ is well-defined and continuous.
Also $F_0=\operatorname{id}$.

If $F_1([x,s])=[x,s]$, then for some $m\in\ZZ$,
\[
\left(x+\frac12,s\right)=\left(x+m,(-1)^m s\right).
\]
The first coordinate would give $1/2=m$, impossible because $m$ is an integer.
Thus $F_1$ has no fixed point.
:::

<1>6. The Klein bottle admits a fixed-point-free map homotopic to the identity.
::: {.proof}
Use the quotient model
\[
K=\RR^2/\Gamma,
\]
where $\Gamma$ is generated by
\[
T(x,y)=(x+1,y),
\qquad
S(x,y)=(-x,y+1).
\]
For $0\le t\le1$, put
\[
F_t([x,y])=\left[x,y+\frac t2\right].
\]
Vertical translations commute with both $T$ and $S$, so $F_t$ is well-defined and continuous, and $F_0=\operatorname{id}$.

Suppose $F_1([x,y])=[x,y]$.
Every element of $\Gamma$ can be written as $T^mS^n$ with $m,n\in\ZZ$, and its action changes the second coordinate from $y$ to $y+n$.
Hence equality of the two classes would force
\[
y+\frac12=y+n
\]
for some $n\in\ZZ$, which is impossible.
Thus $F_1$ is fixed-point-free.
:::

<1>7. Therefore the required surfaces are exactly the torus, annulus, Klein bottle, and Möbius band.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and <1>2, no other compact connected surface can admit such a map.
By <1>3--<1>6, each of the four listed surfaces does admit one.
:::
:::
