::: {.problem}
Show that the polynomial ring over a commutative ring with identity has infinitely many maximal ideals.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let \(A\) be a commutative ring with \(1\neq0\).
We prove that \(A[x]\) has infinitely many maximal ideals.

<1>1. For every field \(k\), the ring \(k[x]\) has infinitely many maximal ideals.
::: {.proof}
The ring \(k[x]\) is a Euclidean domain, hence a PID and a UFD.
Its nonzero maximal ideals are exactly the ideals
\[
(f)
\]
generated by irreducible polynomials \(f\in k[x]\).
Thus it is enough to prove that \(k[x]\) has infinitely many irreducible polynomials up to associates.

Suppose instead that there were only finitely many monic irreducibles
\[
f_1,\ldots,f_r.
\]
There is at least one, for example \(x\), so \(r\ge1\).
Consider
\[
h:=f_1f_2\cdots f_r+1.
\]
This is nonconstant and therefore has an irreducible factor \(g\).
After multiplying \(g\) by a nonzero scalar, assume \(g\) is monic.
Then \(g=f_i\) for some \(i\) by completeness of the list.
But \(g\) divides both
\[
f_1\cdots f_r
\]
and
\[
h=f_1\cdots f_r+1,
\]
so \(g\) divides their difference \(1\), impossible for a nonconstant irreducible polynomial.
Hence \(k[x]\) has infinitely many monic irreducibles, and therefore infinitely many maximal ideals.
:::

<1>2. The ring \(A[x]\) has a quotient isomorphic to \(k[x]\) for some field \(k\).
::: {.proof}
Since \(A\neq0\) is a commutative ring with identity, it has a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\).
Set
\[
k:=A/\mathfrak m.
\]
Then \(k\) is a field.
The quotient map \(A\to k\) extends coefficientwise to a surjective homomorphism
\[
\pi:A[x]\longrightarrow k[x].
\]
Its kernel is
\[
\mathfrak m A[x],
\]
so
\[
A[x]/\mathfrak m A[x]\cong k[x].
\]
:::

<1>3. Distinct maximal ideals of \(k[x]\) pull back to distinct maximal ideals of \(A[x]\).
::: {.proof}
Let \(M\) be a maximal ideal of \(k[x]\).
Since \(\pi\) is surjective,
\[
A[x]/\pi^{-1}(M)\cong k[x]/M,
\]
and the latter is a field.
Thus \(\pi^{-1}(M)\) is maximal in \(A[x]\).

If \(M_1\neq M_2\), then surjectivity gives
\[
\pi(\pi^{-1}(M_i))=M_i,
\]
so
\[
\pi^{-1}(M_1)\neq\pi^{-1}(M_2).
\]
Therefore the infinitely many maximal ideals of \(k[x]\) from <1>1 give infinitely many distinct maximal ideals of \(A[x]\).
:::
:::
