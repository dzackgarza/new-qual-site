::: {.problem}
Let $L/K$ be a finite normal extension.

a. Show that if $L/K$ is cyclic and $E/K$ is normal with $L/E/K$ then $L/E$ and $E/K$ are cyclic.

b. Show that if $L/K$ is cyclic then there exists exactly one extension $E/K$ of degree $n$ with $L/E/K$ for each divisor $n$ of $[L:K]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

\begin{tikzcd}
	L &&&& 1 \\
	\\
	E &&&& {H\definedas \Gal(L/E)\hspace{4em} } \\
	\\
	K &&&& {G\definedas \Gal(L/K) \cong C_m}
	\arrow[from=1-5, to=3-5]
	\arrow["{[G:H]}", from=3-5, to=5-5]
	\arrow["{[E:K]}"', from=5-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow["{[L:E]}", from=3-1, to=1-1]
	\arrow["m", curve={height=-30pt}, from=5-1, to=1-1]
	\arrow[curve={height=-30pt}, from=1-5, to=5-5]
\end{tikzcd}

Let $m=[L:K]$. That $L/K$ is cyclic means $L/K$ is Galois and $G\definedas \Gal(L/K)\cong C_m$.

**Part (a).**

- $L/E$ is Galois because $L/K$ is, and $H\definedas\Gal(L/E)$ is a subgroup of the cyclic group $G$, hence cyclic. So $L/E$ is cyclic.

- By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, $E/K$ normal gives $H \normal G$ and $\Gal(E/K)\cong G/H$. Writing $G = \generators{x}$, one has $G/H = \generators{xH}$, so $E/K$ is cyclic.

**Part (b).**

- Fix a divisor $n$ of $m$. The cyclic group $G\cong C_m$ has exactly one subgroup of each order dividing $m$; in particular it has exactly one subgroup $H$ of index $n$, namely the subgroup of order $m/n$.

- By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, $E\mapsto\Gal(L/E)$ is a bijection from intermediate fields $K\subseteq E\subseteq L$ to subgroups of $G$, with $[E:K]=[G:\Gal(L/E)]$.

- Hence the intermediate fields of degree $n$ over $K$ correspond to the subgroups of index $n$ in $G$. There is exactly one such subgroup, so $E\definedas L^H$ is the unique intermediate field with $[E:K]=n$.
:::
