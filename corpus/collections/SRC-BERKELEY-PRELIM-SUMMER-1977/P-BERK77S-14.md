  note: >-
    Put finitely many rational generators over one common denominator B.
    Their subgroup is (d/B)Z, where d is the gcd of the resulting integer
    numerators. Modulo Z, the same common-denominator argument places every
    finitely generated subgroup inside the finite cyclic group generated by
    1/B+Z, so it is cyclic as well.
---

::: {.problem}
1. Prove that every finitely generated subgroup of the additive group $\mathbb Q$ is cyclic.
2. Does the same conclusion hold for finitely generated subgroups of $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let
$$
H=\langle q_1,\ldots,q_m\rangle
\subseteq\QQ
$$
be a finitely generated subgroup. Choose a positive integer $B$ such that
$$
Bq_j\in\ZZ
$$
for every $j$.

::: {.proof}
Write each generator in lowest terms as
$$
q_j=\frac{a_j}{b_j}.
$$
Taking, for example,
$$
B=b_1b_2\cdots b_m
$$
makes $Bq_j$ an integer for every $j$.
:::

<1>2. If
$$
c_j=Bq_j\in\ZZ
$$
and
$$
d=\gcd(c_1,\ldots,c_m),
$$
then
$$
\boxed{
H=\frac dB\ZZ.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
An arbitrary element of $H$ has the form
$$
\sum_{j=1}^m n_jq_j
=
\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^m n_jc_j,
\qquad
n_j\in\ZZ.
$$
Thus
$$
H
=
\frac1B
\langle c_1,\ldots,c_m\rangle.
$$
The subgroup of $\ZZ$ generated by $c_1,\ldots,c_m$ is exactly
$d\ZZ$. Therefore
$$
H=\frac dB\ZZ.
$$
:::

<1>3. Every finitely generated subgroup of the additive group $\QQ$ is
cyclic.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 shows that $H$ is generated by the single rational number
$$
\frac dB.
$$
:::

<1>4. Let
$$
\bar H
=
\langle
q_1+\ZZ,\ldots,q_m+\ZZ
\rangle
\subseteq
\QQ/\ZZ.
$$
For a common denominator $B$ as in step <1>1,
$$
\bar H
\subseteq
\left\langle\frac1B+\ZZ\right\rangle.
$$

::: {.proof}
For each $j$,
$$
q_j
=
\frac{c_j}{B}
$$
with $c_j\in\ZZ$. Hence
$$
q_j+\ZZ
=
c_j\left(\frac1B+\ZZ\right).
$$
Thus every generator of $\bar H$ lies in the cyclic subgroup generated by
$1/B+\ZZ$, and so does $\bar H$.
:::

<1>5. The subgroup
$$
\left\langle\frac1B+\ZZ\right\rangle
$$
is cyclic of order $B$.

::: {.proof}
The multiples
$$
0+\ZZ,\ 
\frac1B+\ZZ,\ 
\ldots,\ 
\frac{B-1}{B}+\ZZ
$$
are distinct, while
$$
B\left(\frac1B+\ZZ\right)
=
1+\ZZ
=
0+\ZZ.
$$
Thus $1/B+\ZZ$ has order $B$.
:::

<1>6. Every finitely generated subgroup of $\QQ/\ZZ$ is cyclic.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>4--<1>5, $\bar H$ is a subgroup of a finite cyclic group.
Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic. Hence $\bar H$ is cyclic.
:::

<1>7. The answer to part (2) is
$$
\boxed{\text{Yes.}}
$$

::: {.proof}
This is exactly step <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves part (1), and step <1>7 answers part (2).
:::
