::: {.problem}
In $\mathbb Z[x]$, show that the ideal
\[
I=(5,x^2+2)
\]
is maximal.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

There is a ring isomorphism
$$
\ZZ[x]/(5,x^2+2)
\cong
\FF_5[x]/(x^2+2).
$$

::: pf-proof

Reduction of coefficients modulo $5$ gives a surjective ring homomorphism
$$
\pi:\ZZ[x]\longrightarrow\FF_5[x]
$$
with kernel $(5)$. Under this map, the ideal generated by $5$ and $x^2+2$
maps to the ideal generated by $x^2+2$ in $\FF_5[x]$. The third
isomorphism theorem therefore gives
$$
\ZZ[x]/(5,x^2+2)
\cong
(\ZZ[x]/(5))/(\overline{x^2+2})
\cong
\FF_5[x]/(x^2+2).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The polynomial
$$
x^2+2
$$
has no root in $\FF_5$.

::: pf-proof

A root would satisfy
$$
x^2=-2=3
$$
in $\FF_5$. The squares modulo $5$ are
$$
0^2=0,\qquad
1^2=4^2=1,\qquad
2^2=3^2=4.
$$
Thus the only squares in $\FF_5$ are $0,1,4$, and $3$ is not one of them.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The polynomial $x^2+2$ is irreducible in $\FF_5[x]$.

::: pf-proof

A polynomial of degree two over a field is reducible if and only if it has
a root in that field. Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} shows that no such root exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The quotient
$$
\FF_5[x]/(x^2+2)
$$
is a field.

::: pf-proof

The polynomial ring $\FF_5[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, and the ideal
generated by an irreducible polynomial is maximal. Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} therefore
implies that the displayed quotient is a field.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The ideal
$$
\boxed{
(5,x^2+2)
}
$$
is maximal in $\ZZ[x]$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref}, the quotient of $\ZZ[x]$ by this ideal is a field.
An ideal is maximal exactly when its quotient ring is a field.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
