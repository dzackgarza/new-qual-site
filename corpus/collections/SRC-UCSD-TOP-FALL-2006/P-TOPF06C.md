::: problem
How many connected covering spaces does $\mathbb{RP}^3 \times \mathbb{RP}^7$ have?
Can you identify any of them?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. We have
$$
\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^3\times\mathbb{RP}^7)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2.
$$
::: {.proof}
Each projective-space factor has fundamental group $\mathbb Z/2$, and fundamental groups commute with products.
:::

<1>2. Since the group is abelian, equivalence classes of connected covers correspond exactly to its subgroups. It has five subgroups.
::: {.proof}
The subgroups are the trivial subgroup, the three one-dimensional subspaces of $(\mathbb F_2)^2$, and the whole group. Conjugacy does nothing because the group is abelian.
:::

<1>3. Thus there are
$$
\boxed{5}
$$
connected covering spaces up to equivalence.
::: {.proof}
Apply the connected-cover/subgroup correspondence to <1>2.
:::

<1>4. They may be identified as the base itself; the universal cover $S^3\times S^7$; the two product double covers
$$
S^3\times\mathbb{RP}^7,\qquad \mathbb{RP}^3\times S^7;
$$
and the double cover
$$
(S^3\times S^7)/\langle(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)\rangle.
$$
::: {.proof}
The three order-two subgroups of the deck group $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$ are generated by antipodal action in the first factor, in the second factor, and diagonally. Quotienting the universal cover by these subgroups gives the three intermediate double covers.
:::
:::
