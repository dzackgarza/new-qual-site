::: problem
Give an example of a space $X$ such that $H^*(X; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$ for all $0 \leq i < \infty$, and such that the cohomology ring $H^*(X; \mathbb{Z})$ is finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Take
$$
X=S^1\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}.
$$
::: {.proof}
We use the standard integral cohomology rings
$$
H^*(S^1;\mathbb Z)=\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}(u),\quad |u|=1,
\qquad
H^*(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty};\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[c],\quad |c|=2.
$$
:::

<1>2. The integral Künneth theorem gives
$$
H^*(X;\mathbb Z)\cong \Lambda_{\mathbb Z}(u)\otimes\mathbb Z[c].
$$
::: {.proof}
All cohomology groups of the two factors are free abelian, so there are no Tor terms, and the cross product identifies the cohomology ring with the graded tensor product.
:::

<1>3. Consequently $H^i(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ for every $i\ge0$.
::: {.proof}
For $i=2j$ a generator is $c^j$, and for $i=2j+1$ a generator is $u c^j$.
:::

<1>4. The ring is generated by the two elements $u$ and $c$:
$$
\boxed{H^*(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\Lambda(u)\otimes\mathbb Z[c].}
$$
::: {.proof}
Every basis element in <1>3 is a monomial in $u,c$, and the only additional relation is $u^2=0$.
:::
:::
