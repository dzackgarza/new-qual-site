::: {.problem}
Give an example of a space $X$ such that $H^*(X; \ZZ) = \ZZ$ for all $0 \leq i < \infty$, and such that the cohomology ring $H^*(X; \ZZ)$ is finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
Take
$$
X=S^1\times\CP^{\infty}.
$$

::: pf-proof
We use the standard integral cohomology rings
$$
H^*(S^1;\ZZ)=\Lambda_{\ZZ}(u),\quad \gradeddegree{u}=1,
\qquad
H^*(\CP^{\infty};\ZZ)=\ZZ[c],\quad \gradeddegree{c}=2.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The integral Künneth theorem gives
$$
H^*(X;\ZZ)\cong \Lambda_{\ZZ}(u)\otimes\ZZ[c].
$$

::: pf-proof
All cohomology groups of the two factors are free abelian, so there are no Tor terms, and the cross product identifies the cohomology ring with the graded tensor product.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hi-generators}
Consequently $H^i(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ$ for every $i\ge0$.

::: pf-proof
For $i=2j$ a generator is $c^j$, and for $i=2j+1$ a generator is $u c^j$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The ring is generated by the two elements $u$ and $c$:
$$
\boxed{H^*(X;\ZZ)\cong\Lambda(u)\otimes\ZZ[c].}
$$

::: pf-proof
Every basis element in step [](#hi-generators){.pf-ref} is a monomial in $u,c$, and the only additional relation is $u^2=0$.
:::
