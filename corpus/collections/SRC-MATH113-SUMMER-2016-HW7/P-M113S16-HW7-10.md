::: {.problem}
Compute the Galois group of
\[
\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2},i\sqrt3).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
\alpha
\coloneqq
\sqrt[3]{2},
\qquad
\omega
\coloneqq
\frac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2}.
$$
Then
$$
\omega^3=1,
\qquad
\omega\ne1.
$$

<1>1. The field in the problem is
$$
\boxed{
K
\coloneqq
\mathbb Q(\alpha,i\sqrt3)
=
\mathbb Q(\alpha,\omega).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
i\sqrt3
=
2\omega+1,
$$
one has
$$
i\sqrt3
\in
\mathbb Q(\omega).
$$
Conversely,
$$
\omega
=
\frac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2}
\in
\mathbb Q(i\sqrt3).
$$
Adjoining $\alpha$ to either field therefore gives the same extension.
:::

<1>2. The field
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega)
$$
has degree $2$ over $\mathbb Q$.

::: {.proof}
The element $\omega$ satisfies
$$
x^2+x+1=0.
$$
The discriminant is
$$
-3,
$$
which is not a square in $\mathbb Q$.
Hence
$$
x^2+x+1
$$
is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
:::

<1>3. The only real elements of
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega)
$$
are rational:
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega)\cap\mathbb R
=
\mathbb Q.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because
$$
\omega
=
\frac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2},
$$
the field
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega)
$$
equals
$$
\mathbb Q(i\sqrt3).
$$
Every element has the form
$$
a+b\,i\sqrt3
$$
with
$$
a,b\in\mathbb Q.
$$
Such an element is real only when
$$
b=0.
$$
:::

<1>4. The polynomial
$$
x^3-2
$$
is irreducible over
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega).
$$

::: {.proof}
If a cubic over a field is reducible, then it has a root in that field.
The roots of
$$
x^3-2
$$
are
$$
\alpha,
\qquad
\omega\alpha,
\qquad
\omega^2\alpha.
$$

If
$$
\alpha
\in
\mathbb Q(\omega),
$$
then since $\alpha$ is real, step <1>3 would imply
$$
\alpha\in\mathbb Q,
$$
contradicting irreducibility of
$$
x^3-2
$$
over $\mathbb Q$ by Eisenstein at $2$.

If
$$
\omega\alpha
\in
\mathbb Q(\omega),
$$
then multiplying by
$$
\omega^{-1}
$$
would give
$$
\alpha
\in
\mathbb Q(\omega),
$$
impossible.
The same argument applies to
$$
\omega^2\alpha.
$$
Therefore the cubic has no root in
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega)
$$
and is irreducible.
:::

<1>5. Hence
$$
\boxed{
[K:\mathbb Q]
=
6.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
K
=
\mathbb Q(\omega)(\alpha).
$$
Step <1>4 gives
$$
[K:\mathbb Q(\omega)]
=
3,
$$
and step <1>2 gives
$$
[\mathbb Q(\omega):\mathbb Q]
=
2.
$$
The tower law yields
$$
[K:\mathbb Q]
=
3\cdot2
=
6.
$$
:::

<1>6. The field $K$ is the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of
$$
x^3-2.
$$

::: {.proof}
The three roots are
$$
\alpha,
\qquad
\omega\alpha,
\qquad
\omega^2\alpha.
$$
By step <1>1,
$$
\alpha,\omega
\in
K,
$$
so all three roots lie in $K$.
Conversely, the splitting field contains $\alpha$ and the ratio
$$
\frac{\omega\alpha}{\alpha}
=
\omega,
$$
so it contains
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha,\omega)
=
K.
$$
Thus equality holds.
:::

<1>7. Therefore
$$
K/\mathbb Q
$$
is Galois and
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)}
=
6.
$$

::: {.proof}
The polynomial
$$
x^3-2
$$
is separable in characteristic $0$.
By step <1>6, $K$ is its splitting field, hence is normal and separable over $\mathbb Q$.
Thus
$$
K/\mathbb Q
$$
is Galois.
For a finite Galois extension, the order of the Galois group equals the extension degree, which is $6$ by step <1>5.
:::

<1>8. Define
$$
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\sigma(\alpha)
=
\omega\alpha,
\qquad
\sigma(\omega)
=
\omega.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The assignment sends the root $\alpha$ of
$$
x^3-2
$$
to another root and fixes the base field
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega)
$$
pointwise.
Since
$$
x^3-2
$$
is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega)
$$
by step <1>4, the assignment extends uniquely to a
$$
\mathbb Q(\omega)
\text{-automorphism}
$$
of $K$.
Hence it is a $\mathbb Q$-automorphism.
:::

<1>9. The automorphism $\sigma$ has order $3$.

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
\sigma^j(\alpha)
=
\omega^j\alpha.
$$
Thus
$$
\sigma^3(\alpha)
=
\omega^3\alpha
=
\alpha,
$$
and $\sigma$ fixes $\omega$.
Therefore
$$
\sigma^3=1.
$$
Since
$$
\sigma(\alpha)
\ne
\alpha,
$$
its order is exactly $3$.
:::

<1>10. Complex conjugation restricts to an automorphism
$$
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
$$
with
$$
\boxed{
\tau(\alpha)=\alpha,
\qquad
\tau(\omega)=\omega^2.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The number $\alpha$ is real.
Complex conjugation sends
$$
\omega
=
\frac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2}
$$
to
$$
\overline\omega
=
\frac{-1-i\sqrt3}{2}
=
\omega^2.
$$
Hence conjugation preserves
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha,\omega)
=
K
$$
and fixes $\mathbb Q$ pointwise.
:::

<1>11. The automorphism $\tau$ has order $2$.

::: {.proof}
Complex conjugation applied twice is the identity.
It is nontrivial because
$$
\omega\ne\omega^2.
$$
:::

<1>12. The generators satisfy
$$
\boxed{
\tau\sigma\tau
=
\sigma^{-1}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Evaluate both sides on the generators $\alpha,\omega$.
First,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\tau\sigma\tau)(\alpha)
&=
\tau\sigma(\alpha)\\
&=
\tau(\omega\alpha)\\
&=
\omega^2\alpha\\
&=
\sigma^{-1}(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$
Also,
$$
(\tau\sigma\tau)(\omega)
=
\tau\sigma(\omega^2)
=
\tau(\omega^2)
=
\omega
=
\sigma^{-1}(\omega).
$$
Thus the automorphisms agree on generators of $K$.
:::

<1>13. The subgroup generated by $\sigma$ and $\tau$ has at least six elements:
$$
1,
\quad
\sigma,
\quad
\sigma^2,
\quad
\tau,
\quad
\sigma\tau,
\quad
\sigma^2\tau.
$$

::: {.proof}
The first three are distinct because $\sigma$ has order $3$.
The last three are distinct because right multiplication by $\tau$ is bijective.
No element of the first three equals an element of the last three, since otherwise
$$
\tau
\in
\langle\sigma\rangle,
$$
but $\tau$ has order $2$ while
$$
\langle\sigma\rangle
$$
has order $3$.
Hence all six are distinct.
:::

<1>14. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
=
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>13 gives a subgroup with at least six elements.
Step <1>7 says the entire Galois group has exactly six elements.
Hence the subgroup is the whole group.
:::

<1>15. Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}
\left(
\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2},i\sqrt3)
/
\mathbb Q
\right)
\cong
S_3.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The symmetric group
$$
S_3
$$
has the presentation
$$
\langle
r,s
\mid
r^3=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}
\rangle.
$$
By steps <1>9, <1>11, and <1>12, the automorphisms
$$
\sigma,\tau
$$
satisfy these same relations.
By step <1>14 they generate the Galois group, which has order $6$.
Thus the resulting surjective homomorphism
$$
S_3
\to
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
$$
is a bijection.
:::

<1>16. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>15 computes the required Galois group.
:::
