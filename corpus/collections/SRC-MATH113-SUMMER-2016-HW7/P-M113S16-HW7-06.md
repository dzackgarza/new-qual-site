::: {.problem}
Define
\[
\mathcal A=\{\alpha\in\CC: \text{there exists } f\in\QQ[x]\text{ such that }f(\alpha)=0\},
\]
the set of algebraic numbers.
For example, $\sqrt2\in\mathcal A$ since it is a root of $x^2-2$, and $\sqrt{-2}+\sqrt3\in\mathcal A$ since it is a root of $x^4-2x^2+25$.

(a) Show that $\QQ\subset\mathcal A$.

(b) Let $\QQ\subset L$ be an algebraic extension.
Prove that $L\subset\mathcal A$.

(c) Prove that $\mathcal A$ is a field.
Deduce that it is the largest algebraic extension of $\QQ$ in $\CC$.

(d) Explain, using a single sentence, why $\mathcal A\ne\CC$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Every rational number is algebraic over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
q\in\QQ.
$$
Then $q$ is a root of the nonzero polynomial
$$
x-q
\in
\QQ[x].
$$
Hence
$$
q\in\mathcal A.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\QQ\subseteq\mathcal A.
}
$$
This proves part (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Let
$$
\QQ\subseteq L
$$
be an algebraic extension.
Then every element of $L$ lies in $\mathcal A$.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\alpha\in L,
$$
then by definition of an algebraic extension, $\alpha$ is algebraic over $\QQ$.
Thus some nonzero
$$
f\in\QQ[x]
$$
satisfies
$$
f(\alpha)=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\alpha\in\mathcal A.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
L\subseteq\mathcal A.
}
$$
This proves part (b).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If
$$
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A,
$$
then the field
$$
\QQ(\alpha,\beta)
$$
is finite over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
\alpha
$$
is algebraic over $\QQ$,
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]
<\infty.
$$
Because
$$
\beta
$$
is algebraic over $\QQ$, it is also algebraic over
$$
\QQ(\alpha),
$$
since the same polynomial in $\QQ[x]$ that annihilates $\beta$ also lies in
$$
\QQ(\alpha)[x].
$$
Thus
$$
[\QQ(\alpha,\beta):\QQ(\alpha)]
<\infty.
$$
The tower law gives
$$
[\QQ(\alpha,\beta):\QQ]
<\infty.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Every element of
$$
\QQ(\alpha,\beta)
$$
is algebraic over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

A finite field extension is algebraic.
Apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The set $\mathcal A$ is closed under addition and multiplication.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A,
$$
then
$$
\alpha+\beta
$$
and
$$
\alpha\beta
$$
belong to
$$
\QQ(\alpha,\beta).
$$
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, both are algebraic over $\QQ$.
Thus
$$
\alpha+\beta,\alpha\beta\in\mathcal A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The set $\mathcal A$ is closed under additive inverses.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\alpha\in\mathcal A,
$$
then
$$
-\alpha
\in
\QQ(\alpha).
$$
The simple extension
$$
\QQ(\alpha)/\QQ
$$
is finite, so every element of it is algebraic.
Hence
$$
-\alpha\in\mathcal A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The set $\mathcal A$ is closed under multiplicative inverses of nonzero elements.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
0\ne\alpha\in\mathcal A.
$$
Then
$$
\alpha^{-1}
\in
\QQ(\alpha).
$$
Again,
$$
\QQ(\alpha)/\QQ
$$
is finite and therefore algebraic.
Thus
$$
\alpha^{-1}
\in
\mathcal A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The elements
$$
0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
1
$$
belong to $\mathcal A$.


::: pf-proof

They are rational numbers.
Apply step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A
\text{ is a field}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} verify the field axioms inherited from $\CC$: closure under addition, multiplication, additive inverses, and nonzero multiplicative inverses, together with the presence of $0$ and $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The extension
$$
\QQ\subseteq\mathcal A
$$
is algebraic.


::: pf-proof

By definition, every element of
$$
\mathcal A
$$
is algebraic over $\QQ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The field $\mathcal A$ contains every algebraic extension of $\QQ$ that lies inside $\CC$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\QQ\subseteq L\subseteq\CC
$$
be algebraic.
Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
L\subseteq\mathcal A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A
\text{ is the largest algebraic extension of }\QQ
\text{ contained in }\CC.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} shows that $\mathcal A$ itself is algebraic over $\QQ$, while step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} shows that every other algebraic extension inside $\CC$ is contained in it.
This proves part (c).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

One has
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A\ne\CC.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The number
$$
\pi
$$
is transcendental over $\QQ$, so
$$
\pi\in\CC
\setminus
\mathcal A.
$$
This is the requested one-sentence explanation for part (d).

:::
