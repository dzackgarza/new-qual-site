::: {.problem}
Find a primitive element for each of the following extensions, then use it to find the minimal polynomial and degree:

(a) $\QQ(i,\sqrt3)$;

(b) $\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2},\sqrt2)$;

(c) $\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt{10})$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For
$$
K
\coloneqq
\QQ(i,\sqrt3),
$$
set
$$
\alpha
\coloneqq
\sqrt3+i.
$$
Then
$$
\QQ(\alpha)=K.
$$


::: pf-proof

Clearly
$$
\alpha\in K,
$$
so
$$
\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq K.
$$

Also
$$
\alpha(\sqrt3-i)
=(\sqrt3+i)(\sqrt3-i)
=
4.
$$
Hence
$$
\frac4\alpha
=
\sqrt3-i.
$$
Therefore
$$
\sqrt3
=
\frac12
\left(
\alpha+\frac4\alpha
\right)
\in
\QQ(\alpha),
$$
and
$$
i
=
\frac12
\left(
\alpha-\frac4\alpha
\right)
\in
\QQ(\alpha).
$$
Thus
$$
K\subseteq\QQ(\alpha).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The element $\alpha$ satisfies
$$
\alpha^4-4\alpha^2+16=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compute
$$
\alpha^2
=(\sqrt3+i)^2
=
2+2\sqrt3\,i.
$$
Thus
$$
\alpha^2-2
=
2\sqrt3\,i.
$$
Squaring gives
$$
(\alpha^2-2)^2=-12.
$$
Therefore
$$
\alpha^4-4\alpha^2+16=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
[K:\QQ]=4.
$$


::: pf-proof

The extension
$$
\QQ(\sqrt3)/\QQ
$$
has degree $2$.
Moreover,
$$
i\notin\QQ(\sqrt3)
$$
because
$$
\QQ(\sqrt3)\subset\RR.
$$
Thus adjoining $i$ gives another quadratic extension:
$$
[K:\QQ(\sqrt3)]
=
2.
$$
By the tower law,
$$
[K:\QQ]
=
2\cdot2
=
4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Hence for part (a),
$$
\boxed{
\alpha=\sqrt3+i,\qquad
m_\alpha(x)=x^4-4x^2+16,\qquad
[K:\QQ]=4.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives a monic quartic annihilating $\alpha$.
Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} show
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4.
$$
Therefore the minimal polynomial has degree $4$ and equals the quartic from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For
$$
L
\coloneqq
\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2},\sqrt2),
$$
set
$$
\beta
\coloneqq
\sqrt[4]{2}.
$$
Then
$$
\QQ(\beta)=L.
$$


::: pf-proof

Clearly
$$
\beta\in L.
$$
Conversely,
$$
\beta^2
=
\sqrt2,
$$
so the second generator already belongs to
$$
\QQ(\beta).
$$
Hence
$$
L
=
\QQ(\beta).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The minimal polynomial of $\beta$ is
$$
\boxed{
x^4-2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The element $\beta$ satisfies
$$
\beta^4-2=0.
$$
The polynomial
$$
x^4-2
$$
is irreducible over $\QQ$ by Eisenstein's criterion at the prime $2$:
the nonleading coefficients are divisible by $2$, and the constant term $-2$ is not divisible by $4$.
Hence it is the minimal polynomial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore for part (b),
$$
\boxed{
\beta=\sqrt[4]{2},\qquad
[L:\QQ]=4.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The extension is simple by step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, and the minimal polynomial of its generator has degree $4$ by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

For
$$
M
\coloneqq
\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt{10}),
$$
set
$$
\gamma
\coloneqq
\sqrt2+\sqrt{10}.
$$
Then
$$
\QQ(\gamma)=M.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\sqrt{10}
=
\sqrt2\,\sqrt5,
$$
the field $M$ equals
$$
\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt5).
$$
Clearly
$$
\gamma
\in
M,
$$
so
$$
\QQ(\gamma)\subseteq M.
$$

Now
$$
\gamma^2
=
2+10+2\sqrt{20}
=
12+4\sqrt5.
$$
Thus
$$
\sqrt5
=
\frac{\gamma^2-12}{4}
\in
\QQ(\gamma).
$$
Also
$$
\gamma
=
\sqrt2(1+\sqrt5),
$$
so
$$
\sqrt2
=
\frac{\gamma}{1+\sqrt5}
\in
\QQ(\gamma).
$$
Consequently
$$
\sqrt{10}
=
\sqrt2\,\sqrt5
\in
\QQ(\gamma).
$$
Hence
$$
M\subseteq\QQ(\gamma).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The element $\gamma$ satisfies
$$
\gamma^4-24\gamma^2+64=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
\gamma^2-12
=
4\sqrt5.
$$
Squaring gives
$$
(\gamma^2-12)^2
=
80.
$$
Thus
$$
\gamma^4-24\gamma^2+144-80=0,
$$
which is
$$
\gamma^4-24\gamma^2+64=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

One has
$$
[M:\QQ]=4.
$$


::: pf-proof

The field
$$
\QQ(\sqrt2)
$$
has degree $2$ over $\QQ$.
It does not contain $\sqrt5$.
Indeed, if
$$
\sqrt5=a+b\sqrt2
$$
with
$$
a,b\in\QQ,
$$
then squaring gives
$$
5
=
a^2+2b^2+2ab\sqrt2.
$$
Thus
$$
ab=0.
$$
If
$$
b=0,
$$
then $\sqrt5\in\QQ$, impossible.
If
$$
a=0,
$$
then
$$
b^2=5/2,
$$
which has no rational solution.
Hence
$$
[\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt5):\QQ(\sqrt2)]
=
2.
$$
The tower law gives
$$
[M:\QQ]=4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore for part (c),
$$
\boxed{
\gamma=\sqrt2+\sqrt{10},\qquad
m_\gamma(x)=x^4-24x^2+64,\qquad
[M:\QQ]=4.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives a monic quartic annihilating $\gamma$.
Steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} show
$$
[\QQ(\gamma):\QQ]=4.
$$
Thus the minimal polynomial has degree $4$ and equals the displayed quartic.

:::
