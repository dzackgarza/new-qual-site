::: {.problem}
Compute the Galois group of
\[
\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb Q(i+\sqrt2).
\]
List all intermediate subfields of the extension.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
\alpha
\coloneqq
i+\sqrt2
$$
and
$$
K
\coloneqq
\mathbb Q(\alpha).
$$

<1>1. One has
$$
\boxed{
K
=
\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Clearly
$$
\alpha=i+\sqrt2
\in
\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2),
$$
so
$$
K
\subseteq
\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2).
$$

Also
$$
(i+\sqrt2)(\sqrt2-i)
=
2-i^2
=
3.
$$
Thus
$$
\frac3\alpha
=
\sqrt2-i.
$$
Therefore
$$
\sqrt2
=
\frac12
\left(
\alpha+\frac3\alpha
\right)
\in
K,
$$
and
$$
i
=
\frac12
\left(
\alpha-\frac3\alpha
\right)
\in
K.
$$
Hence
$$
\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2)
\subseteq
K.
$$
:::

<1>2. The extension
$$
K/\mathbb Q
$$
has degree $4$.

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2):\mathbb Q]
=
2.
$$
The field
$$
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)
$$
is real, so it does not contain $i$.
Thus
$$
[\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2):
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)]
=
2.
$$
By step <1>1 and the tower law,
$$
[K:\mathbb Q]
=
2\cdot2
=
4.
$$
:::

<1>3. The field $K$ is the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of
$$
(x^2+1)(x^2-2).
$$

::: {.proof}
The roots of
$$
x^2+1
$$
are
$$
\pm i,
$$
and the roots of
$$
x^2-2
$$
are
$$
\pm\sqrt2.
$$
By step <1>1, $K$ contains all four roots.
Conversely, adjoining these roots gives
$$
\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2)
=
K.
$$
Thus $K$ is exactly the splitting field.
:::

<1>4. Hence
$$
K/\mathbb Q
$$
is Galois.

::: {.proof}
The polynomial
$$
(x^2+1)(x^2-2)
$$
is separable over $\mathbb Q$ because the characteristic is $0$.
Its splitting field is therefore a normal separable extension.
Apply step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Every $\mathbb Q$-automorphism of $K$ is determined by independent choices
$$
i\longmapsto\pm i
$$
and
$$
\sqrt2\longmapsto\pm\sqrt2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Any $\mathbb Q$-automorphism must send $i$ to a root of its minimal polynomial
$$
x^2+1,
$$
so
$$
i\mapsto\pm i.
$$
Likewise,
$$
\sqrt2
$$
must map to one of the roots
$$
\pm\sqrt2
$$
of
$$
x^2-2.
$$

Conversely, any independent choice of signs defines a $\mathbb Q$-automorphism of
$$
\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2)
$$
because it preserves the defining algebraic relations
$$
i^2=-1
$$
and
$$
(\sqrt2)^2=2.
$$
:::

<1>6. Define
$$
\sigma:
\begin{cases}
i\mapsto-i,\\
\sqrt2\mapsto\sqrt2,
\end{cases}
$$
and
$$
\tau:
\begin{cases}
i\mapsto i,\\
\sqrt2\mapsto-\sqrt2.
\end{cases}
$$
Then
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
=
\{
1,
\sigma,
\tau,
\sigma\tau
\}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives exactly four possible automorphisms.
The displayed maps realize all four sign choices:
$$
(+,+),
\quad
(-,+),
\quad
(+,-),
\quad
(-,-).
$$
Thus these are all elements of the Galois group.
:::

<1>7. The automorphisms $\sigma$ and $\tau$ commute and both have order $2$.

::: {.proof}
Each changes the sign of exactly one generator.
Applying either twice restores both generators, so
$$
\sigma^2=\tau^2=1.
$$
They act on different generators, so
$$
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma.
$$
:::

<1>8. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2
\times
\mathbb Z/2.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6 the group has four elements, and by step <1>7 every nonidentity element has order $2$.
Hence it is the Klein four group.
:::

<1>9. The subgroup
$$
\langle\sigma\rangle
$$
has fixed field
$$
\boxed{
K^{\langle\sigma\rangle}
=
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The automorphism $\sigma$ fixes
$$
\sqrt2
$$
and negates $i$, so
$$
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)
\subseteq
K^{\langle\sigma\rangle}.
$$
By Galois theory,
$$
[K:
K^{\langle\sigma\rangle}]
=
\abs{\langle\sigma\rangle}
=
2.
$$
Since
$$
[K:\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)]
=
2,
$$
the inclusion must be equality.
:::

<1>10. The subgroup
$$
\langle\tau\rangle
$$
has fixed field
$$
\boxed{
K^{\langle\tau\rangle}
=
\mathbb Q(i).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The automorphism $\tau$ fixes $i$ and negates $\sqrt2$.
The same degree argument as in step <1>9 gives
$$
K^{\langle\tau\rangle}
=
\mathbb Q(i).
$$
:::

<1>11. The subgroup
$$
\langle\sigma\tau\rangle
$$
has fixed field
$$
\boxed{
K^{\langle\sigma\tau\rangle}
=
\mathbb Q(i\sqrt2)
=
\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2}).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The automorphism
$$
\sigma\tau
$$
negates both $i$ and $\sqrt2$.
Thus
$$
(\sigma\tau)(i\sqrt2)
=
(-i)(-\sqrt2)
=
i\sqrt2.
$$
Hence
$$
\mathbb Q(i\sqrt2)
\subseteq
K^{\langle\sigma\tau\rangle}.
$$
Both fields have degree $2$ over $\mathbb Q$:
the fixed field has degree
$$
\frac{\abs{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)}}{
\abs{\langle\sigma\tau\rangle}}
=
\frac42
=
2,
$$
and
$$
i\sqrt2
$$
has minimal polynomial
$$
x^2+2.
$$
Therefore the inclusion is equality.
:::

<1>12. The complete list of intermediate fields is
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb Q,
\qquad
\mathbb Q(i),
\qquad
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2),
\qquad
\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2}),
\qquad
K=\mathbb Q(i+\sqrt2).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The subgroup lattice of the Klein four group consists of:
$$
\{1\},
$$
the three subgroups of order $2$,
and the whole group.
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory these correspond bijectively, in reverse inclusion order, to the five displayed fields.
Steps <1>9--<1>11 identify the three proper nontrivial intermediate fields.
:::

<1>13. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 computes the Galois group, and step <1>12 lists all intermediate subfields.
