::: {.problem}
Consider the extension
\[
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\subset\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2}).
\]

(a) Prove that every automorphism of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2})$ fixes $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ pointwise.

(b) Deduce that
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2}):\mathbb Q(\sqrt2))
=
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2}):\mathbb Q).
\]

(c) Show that $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\subset\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2})$ is a normal extension, but $\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2})$ is not.

(d) Using (b), or otherwise, compute $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2}):\mathbb Q)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
\alpha
\coloneqq
\sqrt[4]{2},
$$
$$
K
\coloneqq
\mathbb Q(\alpha),
$$
and
$$
F
\coloneqq
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)
=
\mathbb Q(\alpha^2).
$$

<1>1. The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb Q$ is
$$
\boxed{x^4-2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The element $\alpha$ satisfies
$$
\alpha^4=2.
$$
The polynomial
$$
x^4-2
$$
is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by Eisenstein's criterion at the prime $2$.
Hence it is the minimal polynomial.
:::

<1>2. The four roots of the minimal polynomial are
$$
\alpha,
\qquad
-\alpha,
\qquad
i\alpha,
\qquad
-i\alpha.
$$

::: {.proof}
These are exactly the four fourth roots of $2$ in $\mathbb C$.
:::

<1>3. The field $K$ is contained in $\mathbb R$.

::: {.proof}
The generator
$$
\alpha
$$
is real.
Every element of
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha)
$$
is obtained from rational numbers and $\alpha$ by field operations, so it is real.
Thus
$$
K\subseteq\mathbb R.
$$
:::

<1>4. If
$$
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(K),
$$
then
$$
\sigma(\alpha)
\in
\{\alpha,-\alpha\}.
$$

::: {.proof}
An automorphism fixing $\mathbb Q$ sends $\alpha$ to another root of its minimal polynomial
$$
x^4-2.
$$
By step <1>2, the possible roots are
$$
\pm\alpha,
\qquad
\pm i\alpha.
$$
But
$$
\sigma(\alpha)\in K\subseteq\mathbb R
$$
by step <1>3.
Therefore only the two real roots remain:
$$
\sigma(\alpha)=\pm\alpha.
$$
:::

<1>5. Every $\mathbb Q$-automorphism of $K$ fixes $\sqrt2$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
\sqrt2=\alpha^2,
$$
step <1>4 gives
$$
\sigma(\sqrt2)
=
\sigma(\alpha^2)
=
\sigma(\alpha)^2
=
(\pm\alpha)^2
=
\alpha^2
=
\sqrt2.
$$
:::

<1>6. Hence every $\mathbb Q$-automorphism of $K$ fixes $F$ pointwise.

::: {.proof}
The field
$$
F
=
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)
$$
is generated over $\mathbb Q$ by $\sqrt2$.
Step <1>5 shows that every $\mathbb Q$-automorphism fixes this generator and, by definition, fixes every rational number.
Thus it fixes every element of $F$.
This proves part (a).
:::

<1>7. One has
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K:F)
\subseteq
\operatorname{Gal}(K:\mathbb Q).
$$

::: {.proof}
Every automorphism that fixes $F$ pointwise certainly fixes the smaller field
$$
\mathbb Q
$$
pointwise.
:::

<1>8. One also has
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K:\mathbb Q)
\subseteq
\operatorname{Gal}(K:F).
$$

::: {.proof}
This is exactly step <1>6.
:::

<1>9. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(K:F)
=
\operatorname{Gal}(K:\mathbb Q).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>7--<1>8.
This proves part (b).
:::

<1>10. The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$ is
$$
\boxed{
x^2-\sqrt2.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The element $\alpha$ satisfies
$$
\alpha^2=\sqrt2,
$$
so it is a root of
$$
x^2-\sqrt2
\in
F[x].
$$
If this polynomial were reducible over $F$, it would have a root in $F$, so
$$
\alpha\in F.
$$
But
$$
[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]
=
4
$$
by step <1>1, while
$$
[F:\mathbb Q]
=
2.
$$
Thus
$$
\alpha\notin F.
$$
Hence the quadratic is irreducible and is the minimal polynomial.
:::

<1>11. The extension
$$
F\subset K
$$
is normal.

::: {.proof}
The roots of
$$
x^2-\sqrt2
$$
are
$$
\alpha
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
-\alpha.
$$
Both lie in
$$
K.
$$
Thus the minimal polynomial of the generator $\alpha$ splits completely over $K$.
Since
$$
K=F(\alpha)
$$
and the extension is separable in characteristic $0$, it is normal.
This proves the first assertion of part (c).
:::

<1>12. The extension
$$
\mathbb Q\subset K
$$
is not normal.

::: {.proof}
The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb Q$ is
$$
x^4-2
$$
by step <1>1.
Its roots include
$$
i\alpha
$$
and
$$
-i\alpha.
$$
By step <1>3,
$$
K\subseteq\mathbb R,
$$
so these nonreal roots do not belong to $K$.
Hence
$$
x^4-2
$$
does not split over $K$.
Therefore
$$
\mathbb Q\subset K
$$
is not normal.
This proves the second assertion of part (c).
:::

<1>13. The assignment
$$
\tau(\alpha)
=
-\alpha
$$
defines a nontrivial $\mathbb Q$-automorphism of $K$.

::: {.proof}
Because
$$
-\alpha
$$
is another root of the minimal polynomial
$$
x^4-2,
$$
the assignment
$$
\alpha\mapsto-\alpha
$$
extends uniquely to a $\mathbb Q$-embedding
$$
\tau:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C.
$$
Its image is
$$
\mathbb Q(-\alpha)
=
\mathbb Q(\alpha)
=
K.
$$
Hence $\tau$ is an automorphism.
It is nontrivial because
$$
\alpha\ne-\alpha.
$$
:::

<1>14. The only $\mathbb Q$-automorphisms of $K$ are
$$
\operatorname{id}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\tau.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4, any $\mathbb Q$-automorphism is determined by sending
$$
\alpha
$$
to either
$$
\alpha
$$
or
$$
-\alpha.
$$
The first choice gives the identity; step <1>13 gives the second.
No other choices are possible.
:::

<1>15. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(K:\mathbb Q)
=
\{\operatorname{id},\tau\}
\cong
\mathbb Z/2.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>14 lists the two automorphisms.
The nontrivial one satisfies
$$
\tau^2=\operatorname{id},
$$
so the group is cyclic of order $2$.
This proves part (d).
:::

<1>16. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>6, <1>9, <1>11--<1>12, and <1>15 prove parts (a), (b), (c), and (d), respectively.
:::
