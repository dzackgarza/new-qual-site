::: {.problem}
Consider the extension
$$
\QQ(\sqrt2)\subset\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2}).
$$

(a) Prove that every automorphism of $\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2})$ fixes $\QQ(\sqrt2)$ pointwise.

(b) Deduce that
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2}):\QQ(\sqrt2))
=
\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2}):\QQ).
$$

(c) Show that $\QQ(\sqrt2)\subset\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2})$ is a normal extension, but $\QQ\subset\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2})$ is not.

(d) Using (b), or otherwise, compute $\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2}):\QQ)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Set
$$
\alpha
\coloneqq
\sqrt[4]{2},
$$
$$
K
\coloneqq
\QQ(\alpha),
$$
and
$$
F
\coloneqq
\QQ(\sqrt2)
=
\QQ(\alpha^2).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ is
$$
\boxed{x^4-2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The element $\alpha$ satisfies
$$
\alpha^4=2.
$$
The polynomial
$$
x^4-2
$$
is irreducible over $\QQ$ by Eisenstein's criterion at the prime $2$.
Hence it is the minimal polynomial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The four roots of the minimal polynomial are
$$
\alpha,
\qquad
-\alpha,
\qquad
i\alpha,
\qquad
-i\alpha.
$$


::: pf-proof

These are exactly the four fourth roots of $2$ in $\CC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The field $K$ is contained in $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

The generator
$$
\alpha
$$
is real.
Every element of
$$
\QQ(\alpha)
$$
is obtained from rational numbers and $\alpha$ by field operations, so it is real.
Thus
$$
K\subseteq\RR.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If
$$
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\QQ}(K),
$$
then
$$
\sigma(\alpha)
\in
\{\alpha,-\alpha\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

An automorphism fixing $\QQ$ sends $\alpha$ to another root of its minimal polynomial
$$
x^4-2.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the possible roots are
$$
\pm\alpha,
\qquad
\pm i\alpha.
$$
But
$$
\sigma(\alpha)\in K\subseteq\RR
$$
by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.
Therefore only the two real roots remain:
$$
\sigma(\alpha)=\pm\alpha.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Every $\QQ$-automorphism of $K$ fixes $\sqrt2$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\sqrt2=\alpha^2,
$$
step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\sigma(\sqrt2)
=
\sigma(\alpha^2)
=
\sigma(\alpha)^2
=
(\pm\alpha)^2
=
\alpha^2
=
\sqrt2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence every $\QQ$-automorphism of $K$ fixes $F$ pointwise.


::: pf-proof

The field
$$
F
=
\QQ(\sqrt2)
$$
is generated over $\QQ$ by $\sqrt2$.
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} shows that every $\QQ$-automorphism fixes this generator and, by definition, fixes every rational number.
Thus it fixes every element of $F$.
This proves part (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

One has
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K:F)
\subseteq
\operatorname{Gal}(K:\QQ).
$$


::: pf-proof

Every automorphism that fixes $F$ pointwise certainly fixes the smaller field
$$
\QQ
$$
pointwise.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

One also has
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K:\QQ)
\subseteq
\operatorname{Gal}(K:F).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(K:F)
=
\operatorname{Gal}(K:\QQ).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.
This proves part (b).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$ is
$$
\boxed{
x^2-\sqrt2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The element $\alpha$ satisfies
$$
\alpha^2=\sqrt2,
$$
so it is a root of
$$
x^2-\sqrt2
\in
F[x].
$$
If this polynomial were reducible over $F$, it would have a root in $F$, so
$$
\alpha\in F.
$$
But
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]
=
4
$$
by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, while
$$
[F:\QQ]
=
2.
$$
Thus
$$
\alpha\notin F.
$$
Hence the quadratic is irreducible and is the minimal polynomial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The extension
$$
F\subset K
$$
is normal.


::: pf-proof

The roots of
$$
x^2-\sqrt2
$$
are
$$
\alpha
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
-\alpha.
$$
Both lie in
$$
K.
$$
Thus the minimal polynomial of the generator $\alpha$ splits completely over $K$.
Since
$$
K=F(\alpha)
$$
and the extension is separable in characteristic $0$, it is normal.
This proves the first assertion of part (c).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The extension
$$
\QQ\subset K
$$
is not normal.


::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ is
$$
x^4-2
$$
by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
Its roots include
$$
i\alpha
$$
and
$$
-i\alpha.
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
K\subseteq\RR,
$$
so these nonreal roots do not belong to $K$.
Hence
$$
x^4-2
$$
does not split over $K$.
Therefore
$$
\QQ\subset K
$$
is not normal.
This proves the second assertion of part (c).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The assignment
$$
\tau(\alpha)
=
-\alpha
$$
defines a nontrivial $\QQ$-automorphism of $K$.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
-\alpha
$$
is another root of the minimal polynomial
$$
x^4-2,
$$
the assignment
$$
\alpha\mapsto-\alpha
$$
extends uniquely to a $\QQ$-embedding
$$
\tau:K\hookrightarrow\CC.
$$
Its image is
$$
\QQ(-\alpha)
=
\QQ(\alpha)
=
K.
$$
Hence $\tau$ is an automorphism.
It is nontrivial because
$$
\alpha\ne-\alpha.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

The only $\QQ$-automorphisms of $K$ are
$$
\operatorname{id}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\tau.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, any $\QQ$-automorphism is determined by sending
$$
\alpha
$$
to either
$$
\alpha
$$
or
$$
-\alpha.
$$
The first choice gives the identity; step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} gives the second.
No other choices are possible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(K:\QQ)
=
\{\operatorname{id},\tau\}
\cong
\ZZ/2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} lists the two automorphisms.
The nontrivial one satisfies
$$
\tau^2=\operatorname{id},
$$
so the group is cyclic of order $2$.
This proves part (d).

:::
