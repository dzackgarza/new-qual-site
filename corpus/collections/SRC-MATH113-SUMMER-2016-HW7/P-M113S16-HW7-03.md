::: {.problem}
Prove that, up to isomorphism, there are no finite extensions of $\mathbb C$ except $\mathbb C$ itself.
In other words, if $L$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb C$, prove that $L\cong\mathbb C$.

Does there exist an algebraic extension of $\mathbb C$?
Explain your answer.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
$$
L/\mathbb C
$$
be an algebraic extension and let
$$
\alpha\in L.
$$
Then $\alpha$ has a minimal polynomial
$$
m_\alpha(x)\in\mathbb C[x].
$$


::: pf-proof

Because the extension is algebraic, every element
$$
\alpha\in L
$$
is algebraic over $\mathbb C$.
Hence there exists a nonzero polynomial in
$$
\mathbb C[x]
$$
annihilating $\alpha$, and therefore a unique monic irreducible minimal polynomial
$$
m_\alpha(x).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every irreducible polynomial in
$$
\mathbb C[x]
$$
has degree $1$.


::: pf-proof

By the fundamental theorem of algebra, every nonconstant polynomial in
$$
\mathbb C[x]
$$
has a root in $\mathbb C$.

If an irreducible polynomial had degree at least $2$, a root
$$
c\in\mathbb C
$$
would give a nontrivial factorization
$$
f(x)
=
(x-c)g(x),
$$
contradicting irreducibility.
Therefore every irreducible polynomial is linear.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ has the form
$$
m_\alpha(x)
=
x-c
$$
for some
$$
c\in\mathbb C.
$$


::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial is irreducible.
Apply step <1>2 and use the fact that it is monic.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\alpha=c\in\mathbb C.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
m_\alpha(\alpha)=0,
$$
step <1>3 gives
$$
\alpha-c=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\alpha=c\in\mathbb C.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence every algebraic extension of $\mathbb C$ is trivial:
$$
\boxed{
L=\mathbb C.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The element
$$
\alpha\in L
$$
was arbitrary.
Step <1>4 shows every element of $L$ already lies in $\mathbb C$.
Since
$$
\mathbb C\subseteq L,
$$
one has
$$
L=\mathbb C.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In particular, every finite extension of $\mathbb C$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb C$.


::: pf-proof

Every finite field extension is algebraic.
Thus step <1>5 applies to any finite extension
$$
L/\mathbb C.
$$
Indeed, after identifying the given copy of $\mathbb C$ in $L$, one gets equality
$$
L=\mathbb C,
$$
and therefore certainly
$$
L\cong\mathbb C.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There is no proper algebraic extension of $\mathbb C$.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>5 applies to arbitrary algebraic extensions, not only finite ones.
Thus any algebraic extension
$$
L/\mathbb C
$$
must satisfy
$$
L=\mathbb C.
$$
Hence no proper algebraic extension exists.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>6 proves the finite-extension statement, and step <1>7 answers the algebraic-extension question.

:::
