::: {.problem}
Prove that, up to isomorphism, there are no finite extensions of $\CC$ except $\CC$ itself.
In other words, if $L$ is a finite extension of $\CC$, prove that $L\cong\CC$.

Does there exist an algebraic extension of $\CC$?
Explain your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
$$
L/\CC
$$
be an algebraic extension and let
$$
\alpha\in L.
$$
Then $\alpha$ has a minimal polynomial
$$
m_\alpha(x)\in\CC[x].
$$


::: pf-proof

Because the extension is algebraic, every element
$$
\alpha\in L
$$
is algebraic over $\CC$.
Hence there exists a nonzero polynomial in
$$
\CC[x]
$$
annihilating $\alpha$, and therefore a unique monic irreducible minimal polynomial
$$
m_\alpha(x).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every irreducible polynomial in
$$
\CC[x]
$$
has degree $1$.


::: pf-proof

By the fundamental theorem of algebra, every nonconstant polynomial in
$$
\CC[x]
$$
has a root in $\CC$.

If an irreducible polynomial had degree at least $2$, a root
$$
c\in\CC
$$
would give a nontrivial factorization
$$
f(x)
=
(x-c)g(x),
$$
contradicting irreducibility.
Therefore every irreducible polynomial is linear.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ has the form
$$
m_\alpha(x)
=
x-c
$$
for some
$$
c\in\CC.
$$


::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial is irreducible.
Apply step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and use the fact that it is monic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\alpha=c\in\CC.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
m_\alpha(\alpha)=0,
$$
step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\alpha-c=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\alpha=c\in\CC.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence every algebraic extension of $\CC$ is trivial:
$$
\boxed{
L=\CC.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The element
$$
\alpha\in L
$$
was arbitrary.
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows every element of $L$ already lies in $\CC$.
Since
$$
\CC\subseteq L,
$$
one has
$$
L=\CC.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

In particular, every finite extension of $\CC$ is isomorphic to $\CC$.


::: pf-proof

Every finite field extension is algebraic.
Thus step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} applies to any finite extension
$$
L/\CC.
$$
Indeed, after identifying the given copy of $\CC$ in $L$, one gets equality
$$
L=\CC,
$$
and therefore certainly
$$
L\cong\CC.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

There is no proper algebraic extension of $\CC$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} applies to arbitrary algebraic extensions, not only finite ones.
Thus any algebraic extension
$$
L/\CC
$$
must satisfy
$$
L=\CC.
$$
Hence no proper algebraic extension exists.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves the finite-extension statement, and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} answers the algebraic-extension question.

:::
