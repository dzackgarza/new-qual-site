::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained by attaching a torus $T=S^1\times S^1$ to a cylinder $C=S^1\times I$ via a homeomorphism of the circle $S^1\times\{(1,0)\}$ of $T$ with the circle $S^1\times\{0\}$ of $C$.
Compute the homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
A=S^1\times\{(1,0)\}\subseteq T,
\qquad
B=S^1\times\{0\}\subseteq C,
\]
and let $h:A\to B$ be the homeomorphism used in the attachment.

<1>1. The torus $T\subseteq X$ is a strong deformation retract of $X$.
::: {.proof}
On the cylinder define
\[
H_C:C\times I\longrightarrow C,
\qquad
H_C((z,t),s)=(z,(1-s)t).
\]
At $s=0$ this is the identity, at $s=1$ its image is $B=S^1\times\{0\}$, and every point of $B$ is fixed for every $s$.

Extend this homotopy over the attached space $X$ by keeping every point of $T$ fixed.
This is well-defined on the gluing locus: if $a\in A$ is identified with $h(a)\in B$, then the torus-side homotopy fixes $a$ and the cylinder-side homotopy fixes $h(a)$, so the two representatives remain identified for all $s$.
By the quotient property this gives a continuous homotopy
\[
H:X\times I\longrightarrow X.
\]
It starts at the identity, fixes $T$ pointwise throughout, and at time $1$ sends the whole cylinder into its attached boundary $B$, hence into the copy of $A$ already lying in $T$.
Thus $H$ is a strong deformation retraction of $X$ onto $T$.
:::

<1>2. For every $n\ge0$,
\[
H_n(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_n(T;\mathbb Z).
\]
::: {.proof}
A deformation retraction is a homotopy equivalence, and singular homology is invariant under homotopy equivalence.
Apply <1>1.
:::

<1>3. The cellular chain complex of the standard CW structure on
\[
T=S^1\times S^1
\]
is
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z
\overset{0}{\longrightarrow}
\mathbb Z^2
\overset{0}{\longrightarrow}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the usual CW structure with one $0$-cell, two loop $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell attached by the word
\[
aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]
Both $1$-cells begin and end at the unique $0$-cell, so the cellular boundary
\[
\partial_1:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z
\]
is zero.

For $\partial_2$, the coefficient of each oriented $1$-cell is the total signed number of times the attaching map traverses it.
The commutator word traverses $a$ once positively and once negatively, and likewise for $b$.
Hence both coefficients vanish and
\[
\partial_2=0.
\]
There are no cells in dimensions greater than $2$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
\[
H_n(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2, & n=1,\\
\mathbb Z, & n=2,\\
0, & n\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, the homology of the torus is the homology of a chain complex whose boundary maps are zero, so
\[
H_0(T)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(T)\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
H_2(T)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
with all higher groups zero.
Transfer these groups to $X$ using <1>2.
:::
