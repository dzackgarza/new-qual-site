::: {.problem}
Let $D^2$ be the closed unit disk in $\mathbb R^2$ and let $\sim$ be the smallest equivalence relation on $D^2$ satisfying $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\sim(\cos(\theta+2\pi/3),\sin(\theta+2\pi/3))$ and $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\sim(\cos(-\theta),\sin(-\theta))$ for all $\theta\in[0,2\pi/3]$.
Let $X:=D^2/\sim$ be the corresponding quotient space.

(a) Compute a presentation for $\pi_1(X)$.

(b) Is $X$ homotopy equivalent to $S^1$?
(Prove your answer.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
\[
v_0=(1,0),
\qquad
v_1=\left(\cos\frac{2\pi}{3},\sin\frac{2\pi}{3}\right),
\qquad
v_2=\left(\cos\frac{4\pi}{3},\sin\frac{4\pi}{3}\right),
\]
and divide the boundary circle into the three counterclockwise arcs
\[
e_0:[0,2\pi/3],
\qquad
e_1:[2\pi/3,4\pi/3],
\qquad
e_2:[4\pi/3,2\pi].
\]

<1>1. In the quotient, the three boundary vertices $v_0,v_1,v_2$ have one common image, and the interiors of $e_0,e_1,e_2$ have one common image $1$-cell, which we orient by the counterclockwise orientation of $e_0$ and call $a$.
::: {.proof}
For $0\le\theta\le2\pi/3$, the first generating relation identifies the point of $e_0$ with parameter $\theta$ with the point of $e_1$ having parameter $\theta+2\pi/3$.
Thus it identifies $e_0$ with $e_1$ preserving their counterclockwise orientations.

The second generating relation identifies the same point of $e_0$ with the point of $e_2$ having angle $-\theta$ modulo $2\pi$, namely angle $2\pi-\theta$.
As $\theta$ increases from $0$ to $2\pi/3$, the angle $2\pi-\theta$ decreases from $2\pi$ to $4\pi/3$.
Thus this identification reverses the counterclockwise orientation of $e_2$.

At $\theta=0$, the first relation gives $v_0\sim v_1$.
At $\theta=2\pi/3$, it gives $v_1\sim v_2$.
Hence all three vertices have the same image.

For an interior parameter $0<\theta<2\pi/3$, the only generating pairs involving the corresponding point of $e_0$ identify it with the points at angles $\theta+2\pi/3$ and $2\pi-\theta$.
Conversely, those two points occur only in these same generating pairs.
Thus no two distinct interior points of $e_0$ are identified with one another, and the three open arcs descend to one open $1$-cell $a$.
:::

<1>2. The quotient $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, one loop $1$-cell $a$, and one $2$-cell whose attaching word is
\[
aa a^{-1}=a.
\]
::: {.proof}
The quotient relation acts only on the boundary of $D^2$, so the open disk is unchanged and descends homeomorphically to one open $2$-cell.
By <1>1, the quotient of the boundary is the single loop $a$.

Traverse $\partial D^2$ once counterclockwise.
Along $e_0$ the quotient map traverses $a$ positively.
Along $e_1$ it again traverses $a$ positively, because the $e_0$--$e_1$ identification preserves orientation.
Along $e_2$ it traverses $a$ negatively, because the $e_0$--$e_2$ identification reverses orientation.
Therefore the attaching loop represents
\[
aa a^{-1}=a.
\]
:::

<1>3. We have the presentation
\[
\pi_1(X)\cong\langle a\mid a=1\rangle\cong 1.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is a circle, so its fundamental group is the free group on the generator $a$.
Attaching the unique $2$-cell quotients this free group by the normal closure of its attaching word.
By <1>2 that word is $a$, so
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a\mid a=1\rangle,
\]
the trivial group.
This proves part (a).
:::

<1>4. The space $X$ is not homotopy equivalent to $S^1$.
::: {.proof}
Homotopy equivalent path-connected spaces have isomorphic fundamental groups.
By <1>3,
\[
\pi_1(X)=1,
\]
whereas
\[
\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
These groups are not isomorphic.
Therefore $X$ is not homotopy equivalent to $S^1$.
:::
