::: {.problem}
Let $S^1$ and $D^2$ be the unit circle and closed unit disk in the (Euclidean) space $\mathbb R^2$.
Let $\sim$ be the smallest equivalence relation on the product space $S^1\times D^2$ satisfying $(x,y)\sim(x,(0,0))$ for all $x\in S^1$ and $y\in D^2$.
Let $Z:=(S^1\times D^2)/\sim$ be the corresponding quotient space.
Prove that $Z$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
\pi:S^1\times D^2\longrightarrow S^1,
\qquad
\pi(x,y)=x,
\]
and let
\[
q:S^1\times D^2\longrightarrow Z
\]
be the quotient map.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The equivalence classes of $\sim$ are exactly the sets
\[
\{x\}\times D^2
\qquad(x\in S^1).
\]

::: pf-proof

Each generating relation has the form
\[
(x,y)\sim(x,(0,0)),
\]
so taking the equivalence relation generated by these pairs never changes the first coordinate.
Thus equivalent points have the same first coordinate.

Conversely, if $(x,y)$ and $(x,y')$ have the same first coordinate, then
\[
(x,y)\sim(x,(0,0))\sim(x,y'),
\]
where the second relation follows by symmetry of an equivalence relation.
Hence all points of $\{x\}\times D^2$ are equivalent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

There is a unique continuous map
\[
f:Z\longrightarrow S^1
\]
such that
\[
f(q(x,y))=x.
\]

::: pf-proof

The projection $\pi$ is continuous and, by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, constant on every equivalence class of $\sim$.
By the universal property of the quotient topology, it therefore factors uniquely through $q$ as
\[
\pi=f\circ q
\]
with $f$ continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The map $f:Z\to S^1$ is bijective.

::: pf-proof

For surjectivity, if $x\in S^1$, then
\[
f(q(x,(0,0)))=x.
\]

For injectivity, suppose
\[
f(q(x_1,y_1))=f(q(x_2,y_2)).
\]
Then $x_1=x_2$, so step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
\[
(x_1,y_1)\sim(x_2,y_2).
\]
Thus their quotient classes agree:
\[
q(x_1,y_1)=q(x_2,y_2).
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The quotient space $Z$ is compact.

::: pf-proof

Both $S^1$ and $D^2$ are compact.
The finite product theorem therefore gives compactness of
\[
S^1\times D^2.
\]
The quotient map $q$ is continuous and surjective, so its image
\[
Z=q(S^1\times D^2)
\]
is compact.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $f$ is a homeomorphism, and hence
\[
Z\cong S^1.
\]

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $f$ is a continuous bijection.
Its domain $Z$ is compact by step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, and its target $S^1$, being a subspace of the Hausdorff space $\mathbb R^2$, is Hausdorff.
A continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism.

:::
