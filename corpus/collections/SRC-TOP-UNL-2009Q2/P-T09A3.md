::: {.problem}
Let $S^1$ and $D^2$ be the unit circle and closed unit disk in the (Euclidean) space $\mathbb R^2$.
Let $\sim$ be the smallest equivalence relation on the product space $S^1\times D^2$ satisfying $(x,y)\sim(x,(0,0))$ for all $x\in S^1$ and $y\in D^2$.
Let $Z:=(S^1\times D^2)/\sim$ be the corresponding quotient space.
Prove that $Z$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Projection map and fibers:
<2>1. Define the projection map $\pi: S^1 \times D^2 \to S^1$ by $\pi(x, y) = x$.
$\pi$ is continuous and surjective.
Proof: coordinate projection of a product space.
<2>2. For any $x \in S^1$, the fiber $\pi^{-1}(\{x\}) = \{x\} \times D^2$.
By definition of $\sim$, $(x_1, y_1) \sim (x_2, y_2) \iff x_1 = x_2$, so the equivalence classes of $\sim$ in $S^1 \times D^2$ are precisely the fibers $\{x\} \times D^2$.
Proof: definition of $\sim$.

<1>2. Induced continuous bijection on the quotient:
<2>1. Let $q: S^1 \times D^2 \to Z = (S^1 \times D^2)/\sim$ be the canonical quotient map.
Since $\pi$ is constant on the equivalence classes of $\sim$, by the universal property of quotient topologies there exists a unique continuous map $f: Z \to S^1$ such that:
\[
f(q(x, y)) = \pi(x, y) = x.
\]
Proof: universal property of quotient spaces.
<2>2. **Injectivity:** If $f(q(x_1, y_1)) = f(q(x_2, y_2))$, then $x_1 = x_2$, so $(x_1, y_1) \sim (x_2, y_2)$, whence $q(x_1, y_1) = q(x_2, y_2)$.
**Surjectivity:** For any $x \in S^1$, $f(q(x, (0, 0))) = x$.
Thus $f: Z \to S^1$ is a continuous bijection.
Proof: fiber characterization from <1>1.

<1>3. Proof that $f$ is a homeomorphism:
<2>1. $S^1$ and $D^2$ are compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}^2$. By Tychonoff’s Theorem, the product $S^1 \times D^2$ is compact.
Proof: product of compact spaces is compact.
<2>2. Since $q: S^1 \times D^2 \to Z$ is continuous and surjective, the quotient space $Z = q(S^1 \times D^2)$ is compact.
Proof: continuous image of a compact space is compact.
<2>3. The target space $S^1 \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ is Hausdorff.
Proof: subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^2$ are Hausdorff.
<2>4. Any continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a closed map, hence a homeomorphism.
Therefore $f: Z \xrightarrow{\sim} S^1$ is a homeomorphism.
Proof: Closed Map Lemma for compact-to-Hausdorff maps.

<1>4. Conclusion:
$Z = (S^1 \times D^2)/\sim$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>3.
:::
