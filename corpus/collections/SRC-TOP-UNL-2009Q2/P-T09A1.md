::: {.problem}
Show that every compact metrizable space has a countable basis.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $d$ be a metric inducing the topology on the compact space $X$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every positive integer $n$, there is a finite set $F_n\subseteq X$ such that
\[
X=\bigcup_{c\in F_n}B(c,1/n).
\]

::: pf-proof

The family
\[
\{B(x,1/n):x\in X\}
\]
is an open cover of $X$.
Compactness gives a finite subcover.
Let $F_n$ be the finite set of centers occurring in such a subcover.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The collection
\[
\mathcal B
=
\{B(c,1/n):n\in\mathbb N_{>0},\ c\in F_n\}
\]
is countable.

::: pf-proof

For each fixed $n$, the set $F_n$ is finite by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Hence $\mathcal B$ is a countable union of finite collections and is therefore countable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The collection $\mathcal B$ is a basis for the topology of $X$.

::: pf-proof

Let $U\subseteq X$ be open and let $x\in U$.
Because $d$ induces the topology, choose $\varepsilon>0$ such that
\[
B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq U.
\]
Choose $n$ large enough that
\[
\frac{2}{n}<\varepsilon.
\]
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref} there is $c\in F_n$ with
\[
x\in B(c,1/n).
\]
If $y\in B(c,1/n)$, then
\[
d(y,x)
\le d(y,c)+d(c,x)
<\frac1n+\frac1n
=\frac2n
<\varepsilon.
\]
Thus
\[
x\in B(c,1/n)\subseteq B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq U.
\]
So every point of every open set lies in a member of $\mathcal B$ contained in that open set, which is exactly the basis criterion.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore every compact metrizable space has a countable basis.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $\mathcal B$ is countable, and by step [](#s3){.pf-ref} it is a basis.

:::
