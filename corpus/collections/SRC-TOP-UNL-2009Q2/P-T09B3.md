::: {.problem}
Use covering space theory to prove that any finitely presented group $G$ has only finitely many subgroups of index $3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose a finite presentation
\[
G\cong\langle g_1,\ldots,g_n\mid r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle.
\]

<1>1. There is a connected finite CW complex $X$ with basepoint $x_0$ such that
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong G.
\]
::: {.proof}
Take one $0$-cell, attach one oriented $1$-cell for each generator $g_i$, and attach one $2$-cell along the loop spelling each relator $r_j$.
The cellular form of the Seifert--van Kampen theorem gives
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)
\cong
\langle g_1,\ldots,g_n\mid r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle
\cong G.
\]
This is the presentation complex of the chosen finite presentation.
:::

<1>2. Every subgroup $H\le G$ of index $3$ determines a connected $3$-sheeted covering
\[
p_H:(\widetilde X_H,\widetilde x_H)\longrightarrow(X,x_0)
\]
whose associated subgroup is $H$.
::: {.proof}
A CW complex is path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected.
Hence the covering-space classification theorem applies to $X$.
Under the identification in <1>1, the subgroup $H\le\pi_1(X,x_0)$ corresponds to a connected based covering for which
\[
(p_H)_*\pi_1(\widetilde X_H,\widetilde x_H)=H.
\]
The number of sheets is the subgroup index, so
\[
|p_H^{-1}(x_0)|=[G:H]=3.
\]
:::

<1>3. The covering in <1>2 gives a homomorphism
\[
\rho_H:G\longrightarrow S_3
\]
such that, after labeling the chosen point $\widetilde x_H$ of the fiber by $1$,
\[
H=\{g\in G:\rho_H(g)(1)=1\}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The fundamental group acts by monodromy on the fiber $p_H^{-1}(x_0)$: a loop at $x_0$ sends a point of the fiber to the endpoint of its lift beginning at that point.
After labeling the three fiber points by $\{1,2,3\}$ with $\widetilde x_H$ labeled $1$, this action is a homomorphism
\[
\rho_H:\pi_1(X,x_0)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,2,3\})=S_3.
\]
Using <1>1, regard it as a homomorphism from $G$.

An element $g\in G$ fixes $1$ under this action exactly when a representing loop lifts from $\widetilde x_H$ to a loop ending again at $\widetilde x_H$.
By the lifting criterion for the associated subgroup, this happens exactly when
\[
g\in(p_H)_*\pi_1(\widetilde X_H,\widetilde x_H)=H.
\]
This proves the displayed formula for $H$.
:::

<1>4. There are only finitely many homomorphisms
\[
G\longrightarrow S_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
A homomorphism out of $G$ is determined by the images of the finite generating set
\[
g_1,\ldots,g_n.
\]
Each generator has only
\[
|S_3|=6
\]
possible images.
Thus there are at most
\[
6^n
\]
possible assignments to the generators, and only some of these satisfy the relators and extend to homomorphisms.
Hence the set $\operatorname{Hom}(G,S_3)$ is finite.
:::

<1>5. The group $G$ has only finitely many subgroups of index $3$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, every index-$3$ subgroup is the inverse image of a point stabilizer in $S_3$ under a homomorphism
\[
\rho:G\to S_3.
\]
There are only finitely many such homomorphisms by <1>4, and $S_3$ has only three point stabilizers in its natural action on $\{1,2,3\}$.
Therefore only finitely many subgroups can arise.
In fact the argument gives the crude bound
\[
\#\{H\le G:[G:H]=3\}\le 3\cdot6^n.
\]
:::
:::
