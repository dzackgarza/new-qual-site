::: {.problem}
Let $f_n(x) = nx(1-x)^n$, $n \in \NN$.

(a) Show that $f_n \to 0$ pointwise but not uniformly on $[0,1]$.

(b) Show that
    $$
    \lim_{n\to\infty} \int_0^1 n(1-x)^n \sin x\,dx = 0.
    $$
:::

::: {.hint}
For part (a): consider the maximum of $f_n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

$f_n(x)=nx(1-x)^n$ converges to $0$ pointwise but not uniformly on $[0,1]$, and $\int_0^1 n(1-x)^n\sin x\,dx\to0$.

::: pf-proof

$f_n(0)=0$, and for fixed $x\in(0,1]$, $n(1-x)^n \to 0$ because $0 \leq 1-x < 1$, so $f_n\to0$ pointwise. Since $f_n'(x) = n(1-x)^{n-1}(1-(n+1)x)$, the maximum is at $x=1/(n+1)$ and equals
$$
\frac{n}{n+1}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n\to e^{-1},
$$
so convergence is not uniform. Since $0\le\sin x\le x$ on $[0,1]$,
$$
0\le \int_0^1 n(1-x)^n\sin x\,dx
\le n\int_0^1x(1-x)^n\,dx
=\frac{n}{(n+1)(n+2)}\to0.
$$

:::
