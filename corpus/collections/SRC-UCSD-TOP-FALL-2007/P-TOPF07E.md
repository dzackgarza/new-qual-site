::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a closed (that is, compact and without boundary) path-connected orientable $3$-manifold.
Suppose that $M$ contains a $2$-dimensional orientable submanifold $\Sigma$ which is non-separating, meaning that $M \setminus \Sigma$ is still path-connected.
Show that $H_1(M; \ZZ)$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Choose a small oriented tubular neighborhood $\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ of $\Sigma$ in $M$.

::: pf-proof

An orientable codimension-one submanifold of an orientable manifold is two-sided, so its normal line bundle is trivial and such a product neighborhood exists.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Because $M\setminus\Sigma$ is path-connected, there is a loop $\gamma$ meeting $\Sigma$ transversely in exactly one point, with algebraic intersection number $\pm1$.

::: pf-proof

Take a short normal arc crossing from the negative to the positive side of the tubular neighborhood. Join its two endpoints by a path in $M\setminus\Sigma$; concatenating gives the required loop. Orientations determine a single transverse intersection of sign $\pm1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The homology class $[\gamma]\in H_1(M;\ZZ)$ has infinite order.

::: pf-proof

The oriented surface $\Sigma$ determines a cohomology class $u\in H^1(M;\ZZ)$ Poincaré dual to $[\Sigma]$. Evaluation gives
$$
\langle u,[\gamma]\rangle=\gamma\cdot\Sigma=\pm1.
$$
If $[\gamma]$ were torsion, every homomorphism $H_1(M)\to\ZZ$, including evaluation by $u$, would vanish on it, contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
\boxed{H_1(M;\ZZ)\text{ contains a subgroup isomorphic to }\ZZ.}
$$

::: pf-proof

The cyclic subgroup generated by the infinite-order element $[\gamma]$ is isomorphic to $\ZZ$.

:::
