::: problem
Let $M$ be a closed (that is, compact and without boundary) path-connected orientable $3$-manifold.
Suppose that $M$ contains a $2$-dimensional orientable submanifold $\Sigma$ which is non-separating, meaning that $M \setminus \Sigma$ is still path-connected.
Show that $H_1(M; \mathbb{Z})$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Choose a small oriented tubular neighborhood $\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ of $\Sigma$ in $M$.
::: {.proof}
An orientable codimension-one submanifold of an orientable manifold is two-sided, so its normal line bundle is trivial and such a product neighborhood exists.
:::

<1>2. Because $M\setminus\Sigma$ is path-connected, there is a loop $\gamma$ meeting $\Sigma$ transversely in exactly one point, with algebraic intersection number $\pm1$.
::: {.proof}
Take a short normal arc crossing from the negative to the positive side of the tubular neighborhood. Join its two endpoints by a path in $M\setminus\Sigma$; concatenating gives the required loop. Orientations determine a single transverse intersection of sign $\pm1$.
:::

<1>3. The homology class $[\gamma]\in H_1(M;\mathbb Z)$ has infinite order.
::: {.proof}
The oriented surface $\Sigma$ determines a cohomology class $u\in H^1(M;\mathbb Z)$ Poincaré dual to $[\Sigma]$. Evaluation gives
$$
\langle u,[\gamma]\rangle=\gamma\cdot\Sigma=\pm1.
$$
If $[\gamma]$ were torsion, every homomorphism $H_1(M)\to\mathbb Z$, including evaluation by $u$, would vanish on it, contradiction.
:::

<1>4. Hence
$$
\boxed{H_1(M;\mathbb Z)\text{ contains a subgroup isomorphic to }\mathbb Z.}
$$
::: {.proof}
The cyclic subgroup generated by the infinite-order element $[\gamma]$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$.
:::
:::
