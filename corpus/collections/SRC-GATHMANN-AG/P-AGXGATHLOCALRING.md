::: {.problem}
Let $X\subset \AA^n$ be an affine variety and $a\in X$.
Show that
\[
\OO_{X, a} = \OO_{\AA^n, a} / I(X) \OO_{\AA^n,a}
,\]
where $I(X) \OO_{\AA^n, a}$ denotes the ideal in $\OO_{\AA^n, a}$ generated by all quotients $f/1$ for $f\in I(X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
B=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n],
\qquad
I=I(X),
\qquad
A=B/I=A(X).
$$
Let
$$
\mfm=\{f\in B:f(a)=0\}
$$
be the maximal ideal corresponding to $a\in\AA^n$.

<1>1. Since $a\in X$, one has
$$
I\subseteq\mfm,
$$
and the maximal ideal of $A$ corresponding to $a\in X$ is
$$
\boxed{\overline{\mfm}=\mfm/I.}
$$

::: {.proof}
Every polynomial in $I(X)$ vanishes at every point of $X$, hence in
particular at $a$. Therefore
$$
I\subseteq\mfm.
$$
The evaluation homomorphism
$$
B\longrightarrow k,
\qquad
f\longmapsto f(a)
$$
has kernel $\mfm$ and factors through
$$
A=B/I
$$
because $I\subseteq\mfm$. Its kernel in $A$ is therefore
$$
\mfm/I.
$$
This is exactly the maximal ideal of the point $a$ in the coordinate ring
$A(X)$.
:::

<1>2. The two local rings are
$$
\OO_{\AA^n,a}=B_{\mfm}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\OO_{X,a}=A_{\overline{\mfm}}
=(B/I)_{\mfm/I}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For an affine variety $Z$ with coordinate ring $A(Z)$, the local ring at a
point is the localization of $A(Z)$ at the maximal ideal of that point
([[D-VARREG]]).

For $\AA^n$ the coordinate ring is $B$, with maximal ideal $\mfm$ at $a$.
For $X$ the coordinate ring is $A=B/I$, with maximal ideal
$\overline{\mfm}=\mfm/I$ by step <1>1. Substituting these two coordinate
rings gives the displayed formulas.
:::

<1>3. The quotient map
$$
B\longrightarrow B/I
$$
induces a surjective homomorphism
$$
\Phi:B_{\mfm}\longrightarrow(B/I)_{\mfm/I},
\qquad
\frac{f}{s}\longmapsto
\frac{f+I}{s+I}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
s\notin\mfm,
$$
then, because $I\subseteq\mfm$,
$$
s+I\notin\mfm/I.
$$
Hence the displayed fraction is defined, so $\Phi$ is a well-defined
localization homomorphism.

Conversely, every element of $(B/I)_{\mfm/I}$ has the form
$$
\frac{f+I}{s+I}
$$
with
$$
s+I\notin\mfm/I.
$$
Again using $I\subseteq\mfm$, this is equivalent to
$$
s\notin\mfm.
$$
Thus the fraction is the image under $\Phi$ of $f/s\in B_{\mfm}$.
Therefore $\Phi$ is surjective.
:::

<1>4. The kernel of $\Phi$ is
$$
\boxed{\ker\Phi=IB_{\mfm}
=I(X)\OO_{\AA^n,a}.}
$$

::: {.proof}
First, every element $h\in I$ maps to zero in $B/I$, so
$$
\frac h1\in\ker\Phi.
$$
Since $\ker\Phi$ is an ideal of $B_{\mfm}$, it contains the ideal generated
by all such $h/1$:
$$
IB_{\mfm}\subseteq\ker\Phi.
$$

Conversely, suppose
$$
\frac fs\in\ker\Phi,
\qquad
s\notin\mfm.
$$
Then
$$
\frac{f+I}{s+I}=0
$$
in $(B/I)_{\mfm/I}$. By the definition of equality to zero in a
localization, there exists
$$
t+I\notin\mfm/I
$$
such that
$$
(t+I)(f+I)=0.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
tf\in I.
$$
The condition
$$
t+I\notin\mfm/I
$$
implies $t\notin\mfm$, so $t$ is a unit in $B_{\mfm}$. Hence
$$
\frac fs
=
\frac{tf}{ts}.
$$
Because $tf\in I$, the last fraction lies in $IB_{\mfm}$. Therefore
$$
\ker\Phi\subseteq IB_{\mfm}.
$$
Combining the two inclusions proves the claim.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\OO_{X,a}
\cong
\OO_{\AA^n,a}\big/
I(X)\OO_{\AA^n,a}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, $\Phi$ is surjective, and by step <1>4 its kernel is
$$
I(X)\OO_{\AA^n,a}.
$$
The first isomorphism theorem gives
$$
B_{\mfm}/IB_{\mfm}
\cong
(B/I)_{\mfm/I}.
$$
Using the local-ring identifications from step <1>2 gives precisely
$$
\OO_{\AA^n,a}/I(X)\OO_{\AA^n,a}
\cong
\OO_{X,a}.
$$
This is the canonical identification asserted in the problem.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>2 identify the two local rings as localizations of $B$ and
$B/I$. Steps <1>3--<1>4 compute the induced localization map and its
kernel. Step <1>5 applies the first isomorphism theorem.
:::
