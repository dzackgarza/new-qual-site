::: {.problem}
Show that

a. Every isomorphism $f:\PP^1\to \PP^1$ is of the form
\[
f(x) = {ax+b \over cx+d}, \qquad a,b,c,d\in k
,\]
where $x$ is an affine coordinate on $\AA^1\subset \PP^1$.

b. Given three distinct points $a_i \in \PP^1$ and three distinct points $b_i \in \PP^1$, there is a unique isomorphism $f:\PP^1 \to \PP^1$ such that $f(a_i) = b_i$ for all $i$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Use homogeneous coordinates $[X:Y]$ on $\PP^1$ and the affine coordinate
$$
x=X/Y
$$
on $D(Y)\cong\AA^1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #pullback-preserves-o1}
Part (a): for every automorphism
$$
f:\PP^1\longrightarrow\PP^1,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{f^*\OO_{\PP^1}(1)\cong\OO_{\PP^1}(1).}
$$

::: pf-proof
The Picard group of the projective line is
$$
\Pic(\PP^1)\cong\ZZ,
$$
generated by $\OO_{\PP^1}(1)$.
Since pullback by an isomorphism preserves ampleness,
$$
f^*\OO_{\PP^1}(1)\cong\OO_{\PP^1}(m)
$$
for some $m>0$.
Likewise
$$
(f^{-1})^*\OO_{\PP^1}(1)\cong\OO_{\PP^1}(n)
$$
for some $n>0$.
Applying $f^*$ to the second isomorphism gives
$$
\OO_{\PP^1}(1)
\cong
f^*(f^{-1})^*\OO_{\PP^1}(1)
\cong
\OO_{\PP^1}(mn).
$$
Thus
$$
mn=1.
$$
Because $m,n$ are positive integers, $m=n=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #automorphism-from-matrix}
Part (a): every automorphism of $\PP^1$ is induced by an invertible
$2\times2$ matrix.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
[U:V]
$$
be homogeneous coordinates on the target.
By the linear-system description of morphisms to projective space
[[T-DIVMAPPN]], the morphism $f$ is determined by the two generating
sections
$$
f^*U,\quad f^*V
$$
of
$$
f^*\OO_{\PP^1}(1).
$$
By step [](#pullback-preserves-o1){.pf-ref} this line bundle is $\OO_{\PP^1}(1)$, whose global sections
are the linear forms in $X,Y$.
Hence there are scalars $a,b,c,d\in k$ such that
$$
f^*U=aX+bY,
\qquad
f^*V=cX+dY.
$$
Therefore
$$
f([X:Y])
=
[aX+bY:cX+dY].
$$

The two sections have no common zero because they define a morphism to
$\PP^1$ everywhere.
If the two linear forms were linearly dependent, they would vanish
simultaneously at the zero of their common nonzero linear factor.
Thus they are linearly independent, so
$$
ad-bc\ne0.
$$
Consequently the matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix}
$$
is invertible.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #mobius-form}
Part (a): in the affine coordinate $x=X/Y$, the automorphism is
$$
\boxed{f(x)=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
On the locus where
$$
cX+dY\ne0,
$$
step [](#automorphism-from-matrix){.pf-ref} gives
$$
f(x)
=
\frac{aX+bY}{cX+dY}
=
\frac{a(X/Y)+b}{c(X/Y)+d}
=
\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}.
$$
Since $ad-bc\ne0$, this linear fractional transformation has inverse
given by the inverse matrix, so it is indeed an automorphism of
$\PP^1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #triple-to-standard-position}
Part (b): every ordered triple of distinct points of $\PP^1$ can
be carried to
$$
(\infty,0,1)
$$
by a projective linear map.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
p_1,p_2,p_3\in\PP^1
$$
be distinct, and choose nonzero vectors
$$
v_i\in k^2
$$
representing them.
Since $p_1\ne p_2$, the vectors $v_1,v_2$ form a basis of $k^2$.
Write
$$
v_3=\alpha v_1+\beta v_2.
$$
Because $p_3$ differs from both $p_1$ and $p_2$,
$$
\alpha\ne0,
\qquad
\beta\ne0.
$$

Define an invertible linear map $T_p:k^2\to k^2$ by
$$
T_p(v_1)=\alpha^{-1}e_1,
\qquad
T_p(v_2)=\beta^{-1}e_2.
$$
Then
$$
T_p(v_3)=e_1+e_2.
$$
The induced projective automorphism $\tau_p$ therefore satisfies
$$
\tau_p(p_1)=[1:0]=\infty,
\qquad
\tau_p(p_2)=[0:1]=0,
\qquad
\tau_p(p_3)=[1:1]=1.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #existence-of-triple-map}
Part (b): for any two ordered triples of distinct points
$$
(a_1,a_2,a_3),
\qquad
(b_1,b_2,b_3),
$$
there exists an automorphism $f$ with
$$
\boxed{f(a_i)=b_i\quad(i=1,2,3).}
$$

::: pf-proof
Apply step [](#triple-to-standard-position){.pf-ref} to obtain automorphisms
$$
\tau_a,\tau_b:\PP^1\longrightarrow\PP^1
$$
such that
$$
\tau_a(a_1,a_2,a_3)
=
(\infty,0,1)
=
\tau_b(b_1,b_2,b_3).
$$
Then
$$
f=\tau_b^{-1}\circ\tau_a
$$
satisfies
$$
f(a_i)=b_i
$$
for all $i$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #uniqueness-of-triple-map}
Part (b): the automorphism in step [](#existence-of-triple-map){.pf-ref} is unique.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $f$ and $g$ both send $a_i$ to $b_i$.
Then
$$
h=\tau_b\circ g\circ f^{-1}\circ\tau_b^{-1}
$$
fixes the three points
$$
\infty,\quad0,\quad1.
$$
By part (a), write
$$
h([X:Y])=[aX+bY:cX+dY]
$$
with $ad-bc\ne0$.

Fixing $\infty=[1:0]$ gives
$$
c=0,
$$
and fixing $0=[0:1]$ gives
$$
b=0.
$$
Thus
$$
h([X:Y])=[aX:dY].
$$
Fixing $1=[1:1]$ implies
$$
[a:d]=[1:1],
$$
so $a=d\ne0$.
The matrix of $h$ is therefore scalar, and hence $h$ is the identity on
$\PP^1$.
Consequently
$$
g=f.
$$
:::
