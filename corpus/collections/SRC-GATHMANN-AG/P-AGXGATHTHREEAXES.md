::: {.problem}
Let $X\subset \AA^3/k$ be the union of the three coordinate axes.
Compute generators for the ideal $I(X)$ and show that it cannot be generated by fewer than 3 elements.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $k$ be the algebraically closed ground field, $R \coloneqq k[x_1,x_2,x_3]$,
$\mfm \coloneqq \generators{x_1,x_2,x_3}$, and
$J \coloneqq \generators{x_2x_3,\, x_1x_3,\, x_1x_2}$.
The $x_1$-axis is $X_1 \coloneqq V(x_2,x_3)$, the $x_2$-axis is $X_2 \coloneqq V(x_1,x_3)$,
and the $x_3$-axis is $X_3 \coloneqq V(x_1,x_2)$, so $X = X_1 \union X_2 \union X_3$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #axis-ideals}
$I(X_1) = \generators{x_2,x_3}$, $I(X_2) = \generators{x_1,x_3}$ and $I(X_3) = \generators{x_1,x_2}$.

::: pf-proof
The quotient $R/\generators{x_2,x_3} \cong k[x_1]$ is an integral domain, so $\generators{x_2,x_3}$ is prime and hence radical.
By the Nullstellensatz, $I(V(x_2,x_3)) = \sqrt{\generators{x_2,x_3}} = \generators{x_2,x_3}$.
The other two axes are the same computation with the variables permuted.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #union-intersection}
$I(X) = \generators{x_2,x_3} \intersect \generators{x_1,x_3} \intersect \generators{x_1,x_2}$.

::: pf-proof
A polynomial vanishes on $X_1 \union X_2 \union X_3$ exactly when it vanishes on each $X_i$.
Hence $I(X) = I(X_1) \intersect I(X_2) \intersect I(X_3)$, and step [](#axis-ideals){.pf-ref} gives the three ideals.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ideal-computed}
$I(X) = J$.

::: pf-proof
Each of $\generators{x_2,x_3}$, $\generators{x_1,x_3}$, $\generators{x_1,x_2}$ is a monomial ideal, so their intersection is a monomial ideal.
A polynomial lies in a monomial ideal exactly when each of its monomials does, so it suffices to compare monomials.
A monomial lies in $\generators{x_i,x_j}$ exactly when $x_i$ or $x_j$ divides it.
It therefore lies in all three ideals exactly when at most one of $x_1,x_2,x_3$ fails to divide it, that is, when at least two distinct variables $x_i,x_j$ divide it.
This holds exactly when one of $x_2x_3$, $x_1x_3$, $x_1x_2$ divides it, that is, when it lies in $J$.
With step [](#union-intersection){.pf-ref}, $I(X) = J$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fiber-dimension}
$\dim_k J/\mfm J = 3$.

::: pf-proof
Since $\mfm$ annihilates $J/\mfm J$, it is a vector space over $R/\mfm \cong k$.
The images of $x_2x_3$, $x_1x_3$, $x_1x_2$ span it, because these elements generate $J$.
The ideal $\mfm J$ is generated by the products of a variable with one of the three quadratic generators, which are monomials of degree $3$.
So every monomial of every element of $\mfm J$ has degree at least $3$.
If $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3 \in k$ are not all zero, then $\alpha_1x_2x_3 + \alpha_2x_1x_3 + \alpha_3x_1x_2$ is a nonzero polynomial whose monomials have degree $2$, so it is not in $\mfm J$.
Hence the three images are linearly independent, and they form a basis.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$I(X) = \generators{x_2x_3,\, x_1x_3,\, x_1x_2}$, and $I(X)$ is not generated by fewer than $3$ elements.

::: pf-proof
The equality is step [](#ideal-computed){.pf-ref}.
Suppose $I(X) = J = \generators{r_1,\ldots,r_m}$ with $m \leq 2$.
The images of $r_1,\ldots,r_m$ span $J/\mfm J$ over $k$, so $\dim_k J/\mfm J \leq m \leq 2$.
This contradicts step [](#fiber-dimension){.pf-ref}.
:::
