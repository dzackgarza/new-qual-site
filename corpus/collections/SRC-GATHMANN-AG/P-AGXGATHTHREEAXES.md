::: {.problem}
Let $X\subset \AA^3/k$ be the union of the three coordinate axes.
Compute generators for the ideal $I(X)$ and show that it cannot be generated by fewer than 3 elements.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Claim**:
\[
I(X) = \generators{x_2 x_3,\, x_1 x_3,\, x_1 x_2}
.\]

Write $X = X_1 \union X_2 \union X_3$, where

- the $x_1\dash$axis is $X_1 \definedas V(x_2 x_3)$, so $I(X_1) = \generators{x_2 x_3}$,
- the $x_2\dash$axis is $X_2 \definedas V(x_1 x_3)$, so $I(X_2) = \generators{x_1 x_3}$,
- the $x_3\dash$axis is $X_3 \definedas V(x_1 x_2)$, so $I(X_3) = \generators{x_1 x_2}$.

Here we have used, for example, that
\[
I(V(x_2 x_3)) = \sqrt{\generators{x_2 x_3}} = \generators{x_2 x_3}
\]
by the Nullstellensatz, noting that $\generators{x_2x_3}$ is radical since it is generated by a squarefree monomial.

Then
\[
I(X)
&= I(X_1 \union X_2 \union X_3) \\
&= I(X_1) \intersect I(X_2) \intersect I(X_3) \\
&= \sqrt{I(X_1) + I(X_2) + I(X_3)} \\
&= \sqrt{\generators{x_2 x_3} + \generators{x_1 x_3} + \generators{x_1 x_2}} \\
&= \sqrt{\generators{x_2x_3,\, x_1 x_3,\, x_1 x_2}} \\
&= {\generators{x_2x_3,\, x_1 x_3,\, x_1 x_2}}
,\]
using $\generators{a} + \generators{b} = \generators{a, b}$, and in the last equality again that an ideal generated by squarefree monomials is radical.

**Claim**: $I(X)$ cannot be generated by 2 or fewer elements.

Let $J\definedas I(X)$ and $R\definedas k[x_1, x_2, x_3]$, and toward a contradiction suppose $J = \generators{r, s}$.
Define $\mfm \definedas \generators{x, y, z}$ and a quotient map
\[
\pi: J \to J/\mfm J
\]
and consider the images $\pi(r), \pi(s)$.

Note that $J/\mfm J$ is an $R/\mfm\dash$module, and since $R/\mfm \cong k$, $J/\mfm J$ is in fact a $k\dash$vector space.
Since $\pi(r), \pi(s)$ generate $J/\mfm J$ as a $k\dash$module,
\[
\dim_k J/\mfm J \leq 2
.\]

This is a contradiction, since one can produce 3 $k\dash$linearly independent elements in $J/\mfm J$: namely $\pi(x_1 x_2), \pi(x_1 x_3), \pi(x_2 x_3)$.
Suppose there exist $\alpha_i$ such that
\[
\alpha_1 \pi(x_1 x_2) + \alpha_2 \pi(x_1 x_3) + \alpha_3 \pi(x_2 x_3) = 0 \in J/\mfm J
\iff \alpha_1 x_1 x_2 + \alpha_2 x_1 x_3 + \alpha_3 x_2 x_3 \in \mfm J
.\]
But
\[
\mfm J = \generators{x_1, x_2, x_3}\generators{x_1 x_2, x_1 x_3, x_2 x_3} = \generators{x_1^2 x_2,\, x_1^2 x_3,\, x_1x_2 x_3, \cdots}
\]
cannot contain any nonzero element of degree $d<3$, so no such $\alpha_i$ exist and these elements are $k\dash$linearly independent.
:::

::: {.remark}
Erratum: the computation of $I(X)$ in the solution is wrong at two steps, although its final ideal is correct.

- $V(x_2x_3)$ is the union of the coordinate planes $\theset{x_2=0}$ and $\theset{x_3=0}$, not the $x_1$-axis. The $x_1$-axis is $V(x_2,x_3)$, with ideal $\generators{x_2,x_3}$, and similarly for the other two axes.
- The ideal of a union is the intersection of the ideals; the radical of the sum is the ideal of the intersection $X_1\intersect X_2\intersect X_3$. The correct computation is $I(X)=\generators{x_2,x_3}\intersect\generators{x_1,x_3}\intersect\generators{x_1,x_2}$, an intersection of monomial ideals, which equals $\generators{x_2x_3,\,x_1x_3,\,x_1x_2}$.

In the second claim $\mfm$ means $\generators{x_1,x_2,x_3}$.
:::
