::: {.problem}
Let
\[
R = k[x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4] / \gens{x_1 x_4 - x_2 x_3}
\]
and show the following:

a. $R$ is an integral domain of dimension 3.

b. $x_1, \cdots, x_4$ are irreducible but not prime in $R$, and thus $R$ is not a UFD.

c. $x_1 x_4$ and $x_2 x_3$ are two decompositions of the same element in $R$ which are nonassociate.

d. $\gens{x_1, x_2}$ is a prime ideal of codimension 1 in $R$ that is not principal.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
S=k[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],
\qquad
F=x_1x_4-x_2x_3,
\qquad
R=S/(F),
$$
and write $x_i$ also for the residue class of $x_i$ in $R$.

<1>1. (a) The polynomial $F$ is irreducible in $S$, hence $(F)$ is prime and $R$ is an integral domain.

::: {.proof}
View $F$ as a polynomial of degree one in $x_4$ over
$$
A=k[x_1,x_2,x_3].
$$
Suppose
$$
F=gh.
$$
Since $\deg_{x_4}F=1$, after interchanging the factors if necessary we have
$g\in A$ and
$$
h=ux_4+v
$$
for some $u,v\in A$. Comparing the coefficient of $x_4$ and the constant
term gives
$$
gu=x_1,
\qquad
gv=-x_2x_3.
$$
Thus $g$ divides both $x_1$ and $x_2x_3$ in the UFD $A$. These two
polynomials are relatively prime, so $g$ is a unit. Hence $F$ is
irreducible.

The polynomial ring $S$ is a UFD, so every irreducible element is prime.
Therefore $(F)$ is prime and $R=S/(F)$ is an integral domain.
:::

<1>2. (a) One has
$$
\boxed{\dim R=3}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The ideals
$$
(0)
\subsetneq
\gens{x_1,x_2}
\subsetneq
\gens{x_1,x_2,x_3}
\subsetneq
\gens{x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4}
$$
form a chain of prime ideals in $R$. Indeed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R/\gens{x_1,x_2}
&\cong k[x_3,x_4],\\
R/\gens{x_1,x_2,x_3}
&\cong k[x_4],\\
R/\gens{x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4}
&\cong k,
\end{aligned}
$$
and $(0)$ is prime by step <1>1. Thus $\dim R\ge3$.

Conversely, a strict chain of prime ideals in $R=S/(F)$ lifts to a strict
chain of prime ideals of $S$ containing $(F)$. Since
$$
(0)\subsetneq(F)
$$
in the four-dimensional polynomial ring $S$, prepending this inclusion to
the lifted chain shows that the original chain has length at most $3$.
Hence $\dim R\le3$.
:::

<1>3. (b) Each of $x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4$ is irreducible in $R$.

::: {.proof}
The relation $F$ is homogeneous of degree $2$, so $R$ is a positively
graded domain with
$$
R_0=k
$$
and each $x_i$ homogeneous of degree $1$.

Let $x_i=ab$. For a nonzero element of a graded domain, the lowest-degree
homogeneous component and the highest-degree homogeneous component of a
product are respectively the products of the corresponding components of
the factors, and neither product vanishes. Since $ab=x_i$ is homogeneous
of degree $1$, both $a$ and $b$ must therefore be homogeneous and their
degrees add to $1$. One factor has degree $0$, hence lies in $k^\times$.
Thus $x_i$ is irreducible.
:::

<1>4. (b) None of $x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4$ is prime in $R$.

::: {.proof}
The defining relation gives
$$
x_1x_4=x_2x_3.
$$
For $x_1$, this shows
$$
x_2x_3\in(x_1).
$$
But
$$
R/(x_1)
\cong
k[x_2,x_3,x_4]/(x_2x_3),
$$
in which the images of both $x_2$ and $x_3$ are nonzero. Hence neither
$x_2$ nor $x_3$ lies in $(x_1)$, so $x_1$ is not prime.

Similarly,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R/(x_2)&\cong k[x_1,x_3,x_4]/(x_1x_4),\\
R/(x_3)&\cong k[x_1,x_2,x_4]/(x_1x_4),\\
R/(x_4)&\cong k[x_1,x_2,x_3]/(x_2x_3).
\end{aligned}
$$
Together with $x_1x_4=x_2x_3$, these quotients show in the same way that
$x_2,x_3,x_4$ are not prime.

By step <1>3 the four elements are irreducible. In a UFD every irreducible
element is prime, so
$$
\boxed{R\text{ is not a UFD}.}
$$
:::

<1>5. (c) The equality
$$
x_1x_4=x_2x_3
$$
gives two factorizations into irreducibles, and no factor in one is associate to a factor in the other.

::: {.proof}
The equality is the defining relation of $R$, and step <1>3 shows that all
four factors are irreducible.

Every unit of $R$ lies in $k^\times$: if $uv=1$, then comparison of the
highest nonzero homogeneous degrees in the graded domain $R$ forces both
$u$ and $v$ to have degree $0$. Moreover, the degree-one component $R_1$
has $k$-basis
$$
x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4,
$$
because the defining ideal has no degree-one element. Thus no two distinct
$x_i$ are scalar multiples, hence no two are associates. The two displayed
factorizations are therefore nonassociate.
:::

<1>6. (d) The ideal
$$
I=\gens{x_1,x_2}
$$
is a prime ideal of codimension $1$.

::: {.proof}
As in step <1>2,
$$
R/I\cong k[x_3,x_4],
$$
so $I$ is prime. The chain
$$
I
\subsetneq
\gens{x_1,x_2,x_3}
\subsetneq
\gens{x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4}
$$
consists of prime ideals. Since $\dim R=3$ by step <1>2, there cannot be a
nonzero prime strictly between $(0)$ and $I$, for otherwise adjoining the
two displayed primes would produce a prime chain of length at least $4$.
Because $I\ne(0)$ and $R$ is a domain, it follows that
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ht} I=1}.
$$
Thus $I$ has codimension $1$.
:::

<1>7. (d) The ideal $I=\gens{x_1,x_2}$ is not principal.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
I=(g).
$$
Since $I$ is proper, $g$ is not a unit. As $x_1\in I$, write
$$
x_1=gh.
$$
By the irreducibility of $x_1$ from step <1>3, the factor $h$ must be a
unit. Hence $g$ is associate to $x_1$ and
$$
I=(x_1).
$$
But step <1>4 shows, in particular, that $x_2\notin(x_1)$, contradicting
$x_2\in I$. Therefore $I$ is not principal.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>2 prove (a), steps <1>3--<1>4 prove (b), step <1>5 proves
(c), and steps <1>6--<1>7 prove (d).
:::
