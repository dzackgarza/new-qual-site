::: {.problem}
Which of the following are isomorphic as ringed spaces over $\CC$?

(a) $\AA^{1} \sm \ts{1}$

(b) $V(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}) \subset \AA^{2}$

(c) $V(x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}, x_{3}-x_{1}^{3}) \sm \ts{0} \subset \AA^{3}$

(d) $V(x_{1} x_{2}) \subset \AA^{2}$

(e) $V(x_{2}^{2}-x_{1}^{3}-x_{1}^{2}) \subset \AA^{2}$

(f) $V(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}-1) \subset \AA^{2}$
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the six spaces as $X_a,\ldots,X_f$ in the order listed.

<1>1. The spaces $X_a$ and $X_c$ are isomorphic.

::: {.proof}
The curve
$$
V(x_2-x_1^2,x_3-x_1^3)
$$
is parametrized by
$$
t\longmapsto(t,t^2,t^3),
$$
with inverse given by projection to the first coordinate.
Removing the origin therefore identifies $X_c$ with
$$
\AA^1\sm\{0\}.
$$
Translation
$$
t\longmapsto t-1
$$
identifies
$$
X_a=\AA^1\sm\{1\}
$$
with $\AA^1\sm\{0\}$.
Thus $X_a\cong X_c$.
:::

<1>2. The space $X_f$ is isomorphic to $\AA^1\sm\{0\}$, and hence
$$
X_a\cong X_c\cong X_f.
$$

::: {.proof}
On $X_f$ one has
$$
(x_1-x_2)(x_1+x_2)=1.
$$
Thus
$$
u=x_1+x_2
$$
is nowhere zero.
Define
$$
\phi:X_f\longrightarrow\AA^1\sm\{0\},
\qquad
(x_1,x_2)\longmapsto u.
$$
Its inverse is
$$
u\longmapsto
\left(
\frac{u+u^{-1}}2,
\frac{u-u^{-1}}2
\right).
$$
Both maps are regular and inverse to each other.
Step <1>1 then gives the displayed three-way isomorphism.
:::

<1>3. The spaces $X_b$ and $X_d$ are isomorphic.

::: {.proof}
Set
$$
u=x_1+ix_2,
\qquad
v=x_1-ix_2.
$$
This is an invertible complex-linear change of coordinates, with
$$
uv=x_1^2+x_2^2.
$$
Hence it carries
$$
V(x_1^2+x_2^2)
$$
isomorphically onto
$$
V(uv),
$$
which is $X_d$ after renaming $u,v$ as the two affine coordinates.
Therefore $X_b\cong X_d$.
:::

<1>4. The space $X_e$ is irreducible, whereas $X_b$ and $X_d$ are reducible.

::: {.proof}
The defining polynomial of $X_e$ is
$$
y^2-x^2(x+1).
$$
Viewed as a monic quadratic in $y$ over the UFD $\CC[x]$, it could factor only if $x^2(x+1)$ were a square in $\CC[x]$.
This is impossible because the irreducible factor $x+1$ occurs with odd multiplicity.
Hence the polynomial is irreducible, so $X_e$ is irreducible.

By contrast,
$$
x_1^2+x_2^2=(x_1+ix_2)(x_1-ix_2)
$$
shows that $X_b$ is the union of two distinct lines, and
$$
X_d=V(x_1x_2)
$$
is the union of the two coordinate axes.
Thus both are reducible.

Irreducibility is preserved by a homeomorphism, hence by an isomorphism of ringed spaces.
Therefore
$$
X_e\not\cong X_b,
\qquad
X_e\not\cong X_d.
$$
:::

<1>5. The space $X_e$ is not isomorphic to $X_a$, $X_c$, or $X_f$.

::: {.proof}
At the origin of $X_e$, let
$$
A=\CC[x,y]/(y^2-x^3-x^2),
\qquad
\mfm=(x,y)\normal A.
$$
The local ring is $A_{\mfm}$ with maximal ideal
$$
\mathfrak{n}=\mfm A_{\mfm}.
$$
The defining equation has no linear term, so the images of $x$ and $y$ form a basis of
$$
\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^2\cong\mfm/\mfm^2.
$$
Consequently
$$
\dim_{\CC}\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^2=2.
$$

On the other hand, each of $X_a,X_c,X_f$ is isomorphic by steps <1>1--<1>2 to
$$
\AA^1\sm\{0\}
=\Spec\CC[t,t^{-1}]
$$
with its usual affine-variety ringed-space structure.
At a point $t=\lambda\ne0$, the maximal ideal of the local ring is generated by $t-\lambda$, so its cotangent space has dimension
$$
1.
$$

An isomorphism of ringed spaces induces isomorphisms of local rings and therefore preserves the dimension of
$$
\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^2
$$
for the corresponding maximal ideal of each stalk.
Hence the origin of $X_e$ cannot map to any point of the punctured-line class, and $X_e$ is not isomorphic to $X_a$, $X_c$, or $X_f$.
:::

<1>6. The complete list of isomorphism classes is
$$
\boxed{
\{X_a,X_c,X_f\},
\qquad
\{X_b,X_d\},
\qquad
\{X_e\}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 give the isomorphisms within the first two displayed classes.
Step <1>4 separates $X_e$ from the reducible class $\{X_b,X_d\}$, and step <1>5 separates $X_e$ from $\{X_a,X_c,X_f\}$.

It remains to separate the first two classes.
The spaces $X_a,X_c,X_f\cong\AA^1\sm\{0\}$ are irreducible, whereas $X_b$ and $X_d$ are reducible by step <1>4. Hence no member of the first class is isomorphic to a member of the second.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 gives all and only the isomorphisms among the six ringed spaces.
:::
