::: {.problem}
Does there exist an analytic function mapping the annulus
\[
A=\{z:1\le |z|\le4\}
\]
onto
\[
B=\{z:1\le |z|\le2\}
\]
and taking the boundary circles $C_1$ to $C_1$ and $C_4$ to $C_2$, where $C_r=\{z:|z|=r\}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\Omega\coloneqq\{z\in\CC:1<\abs{z}<4\}.
$$

<1>1. If such a function $f$ existed, then on $\Omega$ one would have
$$
\log\abs{f(z)}
=
\frac12\log\abs{z}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because $f$ maps into the annulus $1\le\abs{w}\le2$, it has no zeros.
Thus
$$
u(z)\coloneqq\log\abs{f(z)}
$$
is harmonic on $\Omega$. The boundary-circle assumptions give
$$
u=0
\quad\text{on }C_1,
\qquad
u=\log2
\quad\text{on }C_4.
$$
The function
$$
h(z)
\coloneqq
u(z)-\frac12\log\abs{z}
$$
is harmonic on $\Omega$ and continuous on its closure. Since
$\frac12\log4=\log2$, the function $h$ vanishes on both boundary
components. The maximum principle applied to $h$ and $-h$ therefore gives
$h\equiv0$.
:::

<1>2. There is a constant $c\in\CC$ with $\abs{c}=1$ such that
$$
f(z)^2=cz
$$
for every $z\in\Omega$.

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
g(z)\coloneqq\frac{f(z)^2}{z}.
$$
By step <1>1,
$$
\abs{g(z)}
=
\frac{\abs{f(z)}^2}{\abs{z}}
=
1.
$$
Thus the holomorphic function $g$ has image contained in the unit circle.
By the open mapping theorem, $g$ must be constant. Writing this constant as
$c$ gives the claim, and its modulus is $1$.
:::

<1>3. The identity in step <1>2 is impossible for an analytic $f$ on
$\Omega$.

::: {.proof}
Since $f$ has no zeros, differentiating $f(z)^2=cz$ and dividing by
$f(z)^2=cz$ gives
$$
2\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}=\frac1z.
$$
Let $\gamma(t)=2e^{it}$ for $0\le t\le2\pi$. Integrating around this
circle gives
$$
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\int_\gamma\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz
=
\frac12
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\int_\gamma\frac{dz}{z}
=
\frac12.
$$
But the left side is the winding number of the closed curve
$f\circ\gamma$ about $0$, hence is an integer. This contradiction proves
the claim.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\text{no such analytic function exists}.}
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 are consequences of the assumed existence, while step
<1>3 shows those consequences are impossible.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 answers the existence question.
:::
:::
