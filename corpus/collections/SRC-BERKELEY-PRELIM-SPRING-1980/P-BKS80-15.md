::: {.problem}
Show that a vector space over an infinite field cannot be the union of finitely many proper linear subspaces.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $V$ be the given vector space and let $F$ be its scalar field.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
V=W_1\cup\cdots\cup W_m
$$
for proper linear subspaces $W_1,\ldots,W_m$, and choose such a cover with
$m$ minimal.

::: pf-proof

If any finite cover by proper subspaces existed, the positive integers
indexing the possible numbers of members would have a least element. Since a
single proper subspace cannot equal $V$, one has $m\ge2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For each $i$, there is a vector
$$
v_i\in W_i\setminus\bigcup_{j\ne i}W_j.
$$

::: pf-proof

If no such $v_i$ existed for some $i$, then
$$
W_i\subseteq\bigcup_{j\ne i}W_j.
$$
Removing $W_i$ would still leave a cover of $V$ by $m-1$ proper subspaces,
contradicting the minimality in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Put $x\coloneqq v_1$ and $y\coloneqq v_2$. The affine line
$$
L\coloneqq\{x+ty:t\in F\}
$$
has infinitely many points.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $y\notin W_1$. Since $0\in W_1$, it follows that $y\ne0$.
Hence the map
$$
F\longrightarrow L,
\qquad
t\longmapsto x+ty
$$
is injective. The field $F$ is infinite, so $L$ is infinite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Every subspace $W_i$ meets $L$ in at most one point.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s4-1}

The intersection $W_1\cap L$ has at most one point.

::: pf-proof

If $x+sy$ and $x+ty$ were distinct points of $W_1$, then
$$
(s-t)y\in W_1.
$$
Since $s-t\ne0$, this would imply $y\in W_1$, contradicting step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4-2}

The intersection $W_2\cap L$ has at most one point.

::: pf-proof

In fact $W_2\cap L$ is empty. If $x+ty\in W_2$ for some $t\in F$, then
$y\in W_2$ gives
$$
x=(x+ty)-ty\in W_2,
$$
contradicting step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4-3}

For every $i\ge3$, the intersection $W_i\cap L$ has at most one
point.

::: pf-proof

If $x+sy$ and $x+ty$ were two distinct points of $W_i$, their difference
would imply $y\in W_i$, again contradicting step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s4-1){.pf-ref}, [](#s4-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s4-3){.pf-ref} cover all indices $1\le i\le m$.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The assumed finite cover cannot contain all of $L$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, the union
$$
W_1\cup\cdots\cup W_m
$$
contains at most $m$ points of $L$. Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that $L$ has infinitely
many points. Thus $L$ is not contained in the union, contradicting the
equality assumed in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

The contradiction in step [](#s5){.pf-ref} proves that no finite union of proper linear
subspaces can equal $V$.

:::
