::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nz^n
\]
be analytic and one-to-one on the unit disk $\mathbb D$, mapping it onto a domain $G$ of area $A$.
Prove that
\[
A=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|c_n|^2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For $0<r<1$, put
$$
D_r\coloneqq\{z\in\CC:\abs{z}<r\},
\qquad
G_r\coloneqq f(D_r),
\qquad
A_r\coloneqq\operatorname{area}(G_r).
$$

<1>1. For every $0<r<1$,
$$
A_r=\int_{D_r}\abs{f'(z)}^2\,dA(z).
$$

::: {.proof}
An injective holomorphic function has nonvanishing derivative, so the
restriction
$$
f:D_r\longrightarrow G_r
$$
is a real $C^1$ diffeomorphism. For a holomorphic map, the determinant of
the real Jacobian is
$$
J_f(z)=\abs{f'(z)}^2.
$$
The change-of-variables formula therefore gives the displayed identity.
:::

<1>2. For every $0<r<1$,
$$
A_r
=
\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\abs{c_n}^2r^{2n}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Differentiating the power series gives
$$
f'(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n c_n z^{n-1}.
$$
For each fixed $0\le\rho<1$, orthogonality of the functions
$e^{i(n-1)\theta}$ on $[0,2\pi]$ gives
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}
\abs{f'(\rho e^{i\theta})}^2\,d\theta
=
2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
n^2\abs{c_n}^2\rho^{2n-2}.
$$
Hence step <1>1 and polar coordinates give
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_r
&=
\int_0^r\int_0^{2\pi}
\abs{f'(\rho e^{i\theta})}^2
\rho\,d\theta\,d\rho\\
&=
2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
n^2\abs{c_n}^2
\int_0^r\rho^{2n-1}\,d\rho\\
&=
\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
n\abs{c_n}^2r^{2n}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The interchange with the radial integral is valid because all terms in the
resulting series are nonnegative.
:::

<1>3. As $r\uparrow1$,
$$
A_r\longrightarrow A
$$
and
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\abs{c_n}^2r^{2n}
\longrightarrow
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\abs{c_n}^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
The disks $D_r$ increase to the unit disk, so the sets $G_r=f(D_r)$
increase to $G$. Continuity from below of planar area gives
$A_r\to\operatorname{area}(G)=A$.

For each $n$, the term
$$
n\abs{c_n}^2r^{2n}
$$
increases to $n\abs{c_n}^2$ as $r\uparrow1$. Monotone convergence for
the counting measure on the positive integers therefore gives the second
limit.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
A=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\abs{c_n}^2
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Take $r\uparrow1$ in the identity from step <1>2 and use both limits in
step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required area formula.
:::
