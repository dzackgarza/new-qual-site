::: {.problem}
Let
\[
S=\{(x,y,z)\in\RR^3:x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}.
\]
Evaluate
\[
\iint_S (x^2+y+z)\,dA.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The linear terms have zero surface integral:
$$
\iint_S y\,dA=0,
\qquad
\iint_S z\,dA=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Reflection across the plane $y=0$ preserves the sphere and its area
measure while replacing $y$ by $-y$. Hence the integral of $y$ is its own
negative and is therefore zero. Reflection across $z=0$ gives the same
argument for $z$.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
\iint_S x^2\,dA=\frac{4\pi}{3}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Rotations permuting the coordinate axes preserve $S$ and its area measure,
so
$$
\iint_S x^2\,dA
=
\iint_S y^2\,dA
=
\iint_S z^2\,dA.
$$
Call this common value $I$. Since
$$
x^2+y^2+z^2=1
$$
on $S$,
$$
3I
=
\iint_S(x^2+y^2+z^2)\,dA
=
\iint_S1\,dA
=
\operatorname{area}(S)
=
4\pi.
$$
Thus $I=4\pi/3$.
:::

<1>3. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\iint_S(x^2+y+z)\,dA=\frac{4\pi}{3}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>1 and <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the requested value.
