::: {.problem}
For each $t\in\mathbb R$, let $P(t)$ be a real symmetric $n\times n$ matrix whose entries depend continuously on $t$. Suppose every eigenvalue of $P(t)$ is at most $-1$ for every $t$.
If $x(t)$ solves
\[
x'(t)=P(t)x(t),
\]
prove that
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
E(t)\coloneqq\norm{x(t)}^2,
$$
where $\norm{\cdot}$ is the Euclidean norm on $\RR^n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $t\in\RR$ and every $v\in\RR^n$,
$$
v^{\mathsf T}P(t)v\le-\norm{v}^2.
$$

::: pf-proof

Because $P(t)$ is real symmetric, it has an orthonormal eigenbasis. In that
basis,
$$
v^{\mathsf T}P(t)v
=
\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_j(t)v_j^2.
$$
Every eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_j(t)\le-1$, so
$$
\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_j(t)v_j^2
\le
-\sum_{j=1}^n v_j^2
=
-\norm{v}^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The energy satisfies
$$
E'(t)\le-2E(t).
$$

::: pf-proof

Using $x'(t)=P(t)x(t)$ and step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
E'(t)
&=2x(t)^{\mathsf T}x'(t)\\
&=2x(t)^{\mathsf T}P(t)x(t)\\
&\le-2\norm{x(t)}^2\\
&=-2E(t).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every fixed $t_0$ and every $t\ge t_0$,
$$
\norm{x(t)}^2
\le
e^{-2(t-t_0)}\norm{x(t_0)}^2.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{2t}E(t)\right)
=
e^{2t}\bigl(E'(t)+2E(t)\bigr)
\le0.
$$
Hence $e^{2t}E(t)\le e^{2t_0}E(t_0)$ for $t\ge t_0$, which is exactly
the displayed estimate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=0}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The right side of the estimate in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} tends to zero as
$t\to\infty$. Thus $\norm{x(t)}^2\to0$, and hence $x(t)\to0$ in
$\RR^n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is the required limit.

:::
