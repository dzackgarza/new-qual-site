::: {.problem}
Find a real matrix $B$ such that
\[
B^4=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0&0\\
0&2&0\\
0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
a\coloneqq\sqrt[4]{2},
\qquad
C\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
a&0&0\\
0&a&0\\
0&1-a&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

One has
$$
C^2=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^2&0&0\\
0&a^2&0\\
0&1-a^2&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The only nondiagonal entry that requires computation is the $(3,2)$ entry.
It is
$$
(1-a)a+(1-a)=1-a^2.
$$
The remaining entries follow immediately from the triangular form of $C$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The fourth power of $C$ is the prescribed matrix:
$$
C^4=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0&0\\
0&2&0\\
0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Squaring the matrix in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
C^4=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^4&0&0\\
0&a^4&0\\
0&1-a^4&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Since $a^4=2$, this is exactly the displayed matrix.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Therefore one valid choice is
$$
\boxed{
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt[4]{2}&0&0\\
0&\sqrt[4]{2}&0\\
0&1-\sqrt[4]{2}&1
\end{pmatrix}.
}
$$

::: pf-proof

This matrix is $C$, and step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves that its fourth power is the
required matrix.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives the requested real matrix.

:::
