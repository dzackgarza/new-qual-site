::: {.problem}
Find a real matrix $B$ such that
\[
B^4=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0&0\\
0&2&0\\
0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
a\coloneqq\sqrt[4]{2},
\qquad
C\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
a&0&0\\
0&a&0\\
0&1-a&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

<1>1. One has
$$
C^2=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^2&0&0\\
0&a^2&0\\
0&1-a^2&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The only nondiagonal entry that requires computation is the $(3,2)$ entry.
It is
$$
(1-a)a+(1-a)=1-a^2.
$$
The remaining entries follow immediately from the triangular form of $C$.
:::

<1>2. The fourth power of $C$ is the prescribed matrix:
$$
C^4=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0&0\\
0&2&0\\
0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Squaring the matrix in step <1>1 gives
$$
C^4=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^4&0&0\\
0&a^4&0\\
0&1-a^4&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Since $a^4=2$, this is exactly the displayed matrix.
:::

<1>3. Therefore one valid choice is
$$
\boxed{
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt[4]{2}&0&0\\
0&\sqrt[4]{2}&0\\
0&1-\sqrt[4]{2}&1
\end{pmatrix}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
This matrix is $C$, and step <1>2 proves that its fourth power is the
required matrix.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives the requested real matrix.
:::
:::
