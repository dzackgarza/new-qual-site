::: {.problem}
Let $G\le S_n$ act transitively on the $n$ letters.

1. Prove that $n$ divides $|G|$.
2. Suppose $n=4$. For which integers $k\ge1$ can such a transitive subgroup have order $4k$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

One has
$$
n\mid |G|.
$$

::: pf-proof

Fix one of the $n$ letters, say $x$. Since the action is transitive, the
orbit of $x$ has cardinality $n$. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem,
$$
|G|
=
|G\cdot x|\,|G_x|
=
n|G_x|.
$$
Hence $n$ divides $|G|$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $n=4$ and $|G|=4k$, then
$$
k\in\{1,2,3,6\}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Because $G\le S_4$, Lagrange's theorem gives
$$
|G|\mid |S_4|=24.
$$
Thus $4k\mid24$, so $k\mid6$. Since $k\ge1$, this gives
$k\in\{1,2,3,6\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every value in step [](#s2){.pf-ref} occurs for a transitive subgroup of $S_4$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s3-1}

The value $k=1$ occurs.

::: pf-proof

The cyclic subgroup
$$
\langle(1234)\rangle
$$
has order $4$ and is transitive because the orbit of $1$ under the powers of
$(1234)$ is all of $\{1,2,3,4\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-2}

The value $k=2$ occurs.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
r=(1234),
\qquad
s=(13).
$$
Then $r^4=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$. Hence every element of
$\langle r,s\rangle$ can be written as $r^i$ or $sr^i$ with
$0\le i<4$, so this subgroup has at most eight elements. These eight
elements are distinct: the four powers of $r$ are distinct, and an equality
$sr^i=r^j$ would imply $s=r^{j-i}$, whereas $(13)$ is not a power of the
four-cycle $r$. Thus
$$
|\langle r,s\rangle|=8.
$$
It is transitive because it contains $r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-3}

The value $k=3$ occurs.

::: pf-proof

The alternating group $A_4$ has order $12$. Its natural action on the four
letters is transitive: for each $j\ne1$, a three-cycle containing $1$ and
$j$ sends $1$ to $j$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-4}

The value $k=6$ occurs.

::: pf-proof

The full symmetric group $S_4$ has order $24$ and acts transitively on its
four letters.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s3-1){.pf-ref}, [](#s3-2){.pf-ref}, [](#s3-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s3-4){.pf-ref} exhibit transitive subgroups of orders $4$, $8$, $12$, and
$24$, respectively.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore the possible integers are exactly
$$
\boxed{k\in\{1,2,3,6\}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} shows that no other value can occur, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that every
listed value does occur.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves part (1), and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves part (2).

:::
