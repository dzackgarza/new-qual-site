::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ be continuously differentiable. Assume its Jacobian matrix has rank $n$ everywhere and that $f$ is proper: $f^{-1}(K)$ is compact whenever $K$ is compact.
Prove that
\[
f(\mathbb R^n)=\mathbb R^n.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
U\coloneqq f(\RR^n).
$$

<1>1. The set $U$ is open in $\RR^n$.

::: {.proof}
At every $x\in\RR^n$, the Jacobian matrix $Df(x)$ has rank $n$, hence is
invertible. By the inverse function theorem, there is an open neighborhood
$V_x$ of $x$ such that
$$
f|_{V_x}:V_x\longrightarrow f(V_x)
$$
is a diffeomorphism onto an open subset $f(V_x)$ of $\RR^n$. Therefore
$$
U=\bigcup_{x\in\RR^n}f(V_x)
$$
is open.
:::

<1>2. The set $U$ is closed in $\RR^n$.

::: {.proof}
Let $(y_j)$ be a sequence in $U$ converging to $y\in\RR^n$. Choose
$x_j\in\RR^n$ with
$$
f(x_j)=y_j.
$$
The set
$$
K\coloneqq\{y\}\cup\{y_j:j\ge1\}
$$
is compact because it consists of a convergent sequence together with its
limit. Properness therefore makes $f^{-1}(K)$ compact. Since every $x_j$
belongs to $f^{-1}(K)$, some subsequence satisfies
$$
x_{j_\ell}\longrightarrow x\in f^{-1}(K).
$$
Continuity of $f$ gives
$$
f(x)
=
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}f(x_{j_\ell})
=
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}y_{j_\ell}
=
y.
$$
Thus $y\in U$. Hence $U$ contains the limit of every convergent sequence
from $U$, and therefore $U$ is closed in the metric space $\RR^n$.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\boxed{f(\RR^n)=\RR^n}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The set $U$ is nonempty, since $\RR^n$ is nonempty. By steps <1>1 and <1>2,
$U$ is both open and closed in $\RR^n$. Since $\RR^n$ is connected, its
only nonempty subset that is both open and closed is $\RR^n$ itself. Thus
$U=\RR^n$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the desired surjectivity statement.
:::
:::
