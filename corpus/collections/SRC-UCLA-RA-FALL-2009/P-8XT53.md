::: {.problem}
Construct a Borel subset $E$ of the real line $\mathbb{R}$ such that for all intervals $[a,b]$ we have $$0<m(E\cap[a,b])<|b-a|,$$ where $m$ denotes Lebesgue measure.
:::

::: {.solution}
We interpret the assertion for nondegenerate intervals $a<b$.

<1>1. Every nonempty open interval contains a compact nowhere-dense subset of positive Lebesgue measure.
::: {.proof}
It suffices to construct one in $[0,1]$, since affine maps carry such a set to any prescribed open interval.

Use the Smith--Volterra construction.
Starting with $[0,1]$, at stage $k\ge1$ remove from the middle of each of the $2^{k-1}$ surviving closed intervals an open interval of length
\[
2^{-2k}.
\]
Let $K$ be the set remaining after all stages.

The removed intervals have total length
\[
\sum_{k=1}^\infty 2^{k-1}2^{-2k}
=\sum_{k=1}^\infty2^{-k-1}
=\frac12.
\]
Hence
\[
m(K)=\frac12>0.
\]
The set $K$ is an intersection of nested finite unions of closed intervals, so it is compact.
At each stage every surviving interval is split into two proper subintervals; consequently the maximum length of a surviving component tends to $0$.
Thus no nonempty open interval is contained in $K$, so $K$ has empty interior.
Being closed with empty interior, it is nowhere dense.

An affine copy of a sufficiently small version of $K$ inside any given nonempty open interval has the same three properties: compactness, nowhere density, and positive measure.
:::

<1>2. Enumerate all bounded open intervals with rational endpoints as
\[
I_1,I_2,I_3,\ldots.
\]
There exist compact nowhere-dense sets
\[
A_n,B_n\subseteq I_n
\]
of positive measure such that all sets in the family
\[
A_1,B_1,A_2,B_2,\ldots
\]
are pairwise disjoint.
::: {.proof}
Proceed inductively.

Suppose $A_j,B_j$ have been chosen for $j<n$.
The finite union
\[
F_{n-1}=\bigcup_{j<n}(A_j\cup B_j)
\]
is compact and nowhere dense.
Therefore
\[
I_n\setminus F_{n-1}
\]
is a nonempty open subset of $I_n$.
In fact it contains two disjoint nonempty open intervals $J_n^A,J_n^B$: choose any nonempty open interval in the complement and split a smaller subinterval into two disjoint open subintervals.

By <1>1, choose a compact nowhere-dense positive-measure set
\[
A_n\subseteq J_n^A
\]
and another
\[
B_n\subseteq J_n^B.
\]
They are disjoint from each other and from all earlier sets.
This completes the induction.
:::

<1>3. Define
\[
E=\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n.
\]
Then $E$ is a Borel subset of $\mathbb R$, and
\[
B_n\subseteq\mathbb R\setminus E
\]
for every $n$.
::: {.proof}
Each $A_n$ is compact and therefore closed.
Hence $E$ is an $F_\sigma$ set, so it is Borel.

By the pairwise disjointness in <1>2, no $B_n$ meets any $A_j$.
Therefore
\[
B_n\cap E=\varnothing,
\]
which is the claimed containment.
:::

<1>4. Every nondegenerate interval $[a,b]$ satisfies
\[
m(E\cap[a,b])>0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Choose rational numbers $p,q$ with
\[
a<p<q<b.
\]
The rational interval $(p,q)$ is one of the $I_n$ in <1>2.
For its index $n$,
\[
A_n\subseteq I_n=(p,q)\subseteq[a,b].
\]
Since $A_n\subseteq E$ and $m(A_n)>0$,
\[
m(E\cap[a,b])\ge m(A_n)>0.
\]
:::

<1>5. Every nondegenerate interval $[a,b]$ also satisfies
\[
m(E\cap[a,b])<b-a.
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the same rational interval $I_n=(p,q)\subset(a,b)$ as in <1>4.
By <1>3,
\[
B_n\subseteq([a,b]\setminus E),
\]
and $m(B_n)>0$.
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
m(E\cap[a,b])
&=(b-a)-m([a,b]\setminus E)\\
&\le(b-a)-m(B_n)\\
&<b-a.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>6. Hence the Borel set $E$ constructed in <1>3 satisfies
\[
\boxed{0<m(E\cap[a,b])<|b-a|}
\]
for every $a<b$.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>4 and <1>5.
:::
:::
