::: {.problem}
Consider the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with poles removed: $$S=\bigl\{(\cos\theta\sin\phi,\,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\,\cos\phi):0<\phi<\pi\text{ and }\theta\in\mathbb{R}\bigr\}.$$ Give an explicit formula for a conformal map from the complex plane onto $S$ so that horizontal lines are mapped to circles of constant $\phi$ and vertical lines are mapped to arcs of constant $\theta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $z=x+iy$ and define
\[
F(z)=\bigl(\operatorname{sech}y\cos x,\,\operatorname{sech}y\sin x,\,-\tanh y\bigr).
\]

<1>1. If
\[
\phi(y)=2\arctan(e^y),
\]
then $0<\phi(y)<\pi$ and
\[
\sin\phi(y)=\operatorname{sech}y,
\qquad
\cos\phi(y)=-\tanh y.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $e^y>0$, one has
\[
0<\arctan(e^y)<\frac\pi2,
\]
so $0<\phi(y)<\pi$.
With $t=e^y$, the tangent half-angle identities give
\[
\sin\phi(y)=\frac{2t}{1+t^2}
=\frac{2e^y}{1+e^{2y}}
=\frac{2}{e^y+e^{-y}}
=\operatorname{sech}y
\]
and
\[
\cos\phi(y)=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}
=\frac{1-e^{2y}}{1+e^{2y}}
=-\tanh y.
\]
:::

<1>2. The map $F$ is onto $S$; horizontal lines map to circles of constant $\phi$, and vertical lines map to arcs of constant $\theta$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
F(x+iy)
=\bigl(\cos x\sin\phi(y),\,\sin x\sin\phi(y),\,\cos\phi(y)\bigr),
\]
so $F(\mathbb C)\subseteq S$.

Conversely, let
\[
p=(\cos\theta\sin\phi,\,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\,\cos\phi)\in S,
\qquad 0<\phi<\pi.
\]
Set
\[
x=\theta,
\qquad
y=\log\tan\frac\phi2.
\]
Then $e^y=\tan(\phi/2)$, hence <1>1 gives $\phi(y)=\phi$ and therefore
\[
F(x+iy)=p.
\]
Thus $F$ is onto.

On a horizontal line $y=y_0$, the value $\phi(y_0)$ is constant while $x$ varies, so the image is the latitude circle of constant $\phi(y_0)$.
On a vertical line $x=x_0$, the angular coordinate is the constant value
\[
\theta=x_0\pmod{2\pi},
\]
while $y$ varies, so the image is an arc of constant $\theta$ joining the two omitted poles in the limiting directions.
:::

<1>3. The map $F$ is conformal.
::: {.proof}
Its coordinate derivatives are
\[
F_x=
\bigl(-\operatorname{sech}y\sin x,\,
\operatorname{sech}y\cos x,\,0\bigr)
\]
and
\[
F_y=
\bigl(-\operatorname{sech}y\tanh y\cos x,\,
-\operatorname{sech}y\tanh y\sin x,\,
-\operatorname{sech}^2y\bigr).
\]
Their inner product is zero, while
\[
\lVert F_x\rVert^2=\operatorname{sech}^2y
\]
and, using $\tanh^2y+\operatorname{sech}^2y=1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert F_y\rVert^2
&=\operatorname{sech}^2y\tanh^2y+\operatorname{sech}^4y\\
&=\operatorname{sech}^2y.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because $\operatorname{sech}y>0$, the two derivatives are nonzero and orthogonal with equal length. Hence the pullback of the round metric is
\[
F^*(ds^2)=\operatorname{sech}^2y\,(dx^2+dy^2),
\]
so $F$ is conformal.
:::

Thus the displayed $F$ has all the required properties.
:::
