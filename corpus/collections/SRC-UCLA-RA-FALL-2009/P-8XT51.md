::: {.problem}
Find a nonempty closed set in the Hilbert space $L^2([0,1])$ that does not contain an element of smallest norm.
Prove your assertion.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $n\ge1$, let
\[
I_n=(2^{-n},2^{-n+1}]
\]
and define
\[
e_n=2^{n/2}\mathbf 1_{I_n}\in L^2([0,1]).
\]
Set
\[
A=\left\{\left(1+\frac1n\right)e_n:n\ge1\right\}.
\]

<1>1. The sequence $(e_n)_{n\ge1}$ is orthonormal in $L^2([0,1])$.
::: {.proof}
The intervals $I_n$ are pairwise disjoint and have measure
\[
|I_n|=2^{-n}.
\]
Hence
\[
\|e_n\|_2^2
=2^n|I_n|
=1.
\]
If $m\ne n$, then $I_m\cap I_n=\varnothing$ up to a null set, so
\[
\langle e_m,e_n\rangle=0.
\]
Thus the family is orthonormal.
:::

<1>2. Distinct points of $A$ are separated by at least $\sqrt2$.
::: {.proof}
For $m\ne n$, orthogonality from <1>1 gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|\left(1+\frac1m\right)e_m
-\left(1+\frac1n\right)e_n\right\|_2^2
&=\left(1+\frac1m\right)^2
+\left(1+\frac1n\right)^2\\
&>2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore the distance between two distinct points of $A$ is greater than $\sqrt2$.
:::

<1>3. The set $A$ is closed in $L^2([0,1])$.
::: {.proof}
Because $L^2([0,1])$ is a metric space, it suffices to show that every convergent sequence of points of $A$ has its limit in $A$.

Let $(a_k)$ be a convergent sequence in $A$.
Then $(a_k)$ is Cauchy.
Taking
\[
\varepsilon=1,
\]
there is $K$ such that
\[
\|a_k-a_\ell\|_2<1
\]
for all $k,\ell\ge K$.
By <1>2, two distinct points of $A$ have distance greater than $\sqrt2>1$.
Hence
\[
a_k=a_\ell
\]
for all $k,\ell\ge K$.
Thus $(a_k)$ is eventually constant, and its limit is that constant point of $A$.
Therefore $A$ is closed.
:::

<1>4. The set $A$ has no element of smallest norm.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
\left\|\left(1+\frac1n\right)e_n\right\|_2
=1+\frac1n.
\]
Consequently
\[
\inf_{a\in A}\|a\|_2=1.
\]
But every element of $A$ has norm strictly greater than $1$, so the infimum is not attained.
Equivalently, for each $n$,
\[
1+\frac1{n+1}<1+\frac1n,
\]
so no element can have smallest norm.
:::

<1>5. Hence $A$ is a nonempty closed subset of $L^2([0,1])$ with no element of smallest norm.
::: {.proof}
The set is nonempty by construction, closed by <1>3, and lacks a smallest-norm element by <1>4.
:::
