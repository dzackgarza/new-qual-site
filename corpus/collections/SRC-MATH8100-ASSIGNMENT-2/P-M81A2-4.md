::: {.problem}
Let $A\subseteq B$ be compact subsets of $\RR^n$, with $m(A)=a$ and $m(B)=b$.
For each $a<c<b$, prove that there is a compact set $E$ with $A\subseteq E\subseteq B$ and $m(E)=c$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is the homework sheet alone, so the argument is the card's own. Let $A\subseteq B$ be compact subsets of $\RR^n$ with $m(A)=a$ and $m(B)=b$, where $m$ is Lebesgue measure, and let $a<c<b$. Write $H_t=\{x\in\RR^n:x_1\le t\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #difference}
$D=B\setminus A$ is measurable with $m(D)=b-a$.

::: pf-proof
Compact sets are closed and hence Borel, so both are measurable, and $A\subseteq B$ gives $m(B)=m(A)+m(B\setminus A)$ by additivity of $m$ on measurable sets; all three measures are finite because compact sets are bounded. Hence $m(D)=b-a$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ends}
$F(t)=m(D\cap H_t)$ is nondecreasing, vanishes for all sufficiently negative $t$, and equals $b-a$ for all sufficiently large $t$.

::: pf-proof
$H_s\subseteq H_t$ for $s\le t$, so $D\cap H_s\subseteq D\cap H_t$ and monotonicity of $m$ gives $F(s)\le F(t)$. Since $B$ is compact it is bounded, so there are $\alpha<\beta$ with $B\subseteq\{x:\alpha\le x_1\le\beta\}$; then $D\cap H_t=\varnothing$ for $t<\alpha$ and $D\cap H_t=D$ for $t\ge\beta$. So $F(t)=0$ respectively $F(t)=m(D)=b-a$ at those ends.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #continuity}
$F$ is continuous on $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
$D$ has finite measure. If $t_k\downarrow t$ then $D\cap H_{t_k}\downarrow D\cap H_t$, and continuity of $m$ from above for a set of finite measure gives $F(t_k)\to F(t)$. If $t_k\uparrow t$ then $D\cap H_{t_k}\uparrow D\cap\{x_1<t\}$, and continuity from below gives $F(t_k)\to m(D\cap\{x_1<t\})$. The difference between $\{x_1<t\}$ and $\{x_1\le t\}$ is the hyperplane $\{x_1=t\}$, which has $n$-dimensional measure zero, so $m(D\cap\{x_1<t\})=m(D\cap\{x_1\le t\})=F(t)$; hence the limit is $F(t)$ in this case too. Sequential continuity at every point gives continuity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ivt}
There is $t_0\in\RR$ with $F(t_0)=c-a$.

::: pf-proof
By the hypotheses $0<c-a<b-a$, and by step [](#ends){.pf-ref} $F$ takes the value $0$ at some point and $b-a$ at another. Since $F$ is continuous by step [](#continuity){.pf-ref}, the intermediate value theorem applies on the interval between those two points: there is $t_0$ with $F(t_0)=c-a$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #E}
$E=A\cup\bigl(B\cap H_{t_0}\bigr)$ is compact, $A\subseteq E\subseteq B$, and $m(E)=c$.

::: pf-proof
$B\cap H_{t_0}$ is closed (intersection of the closed set $B$, compact hence closed, with the closed half-space $H_{t_0}$) and contained in the compact set $B$, hence compact; a union of two compact sets is compact, so $E$ is compact. The inclusions hold because $A\subseteq B$ and $B\cap H_{t_0}\subseteq B$. For the measure, $E=A\sqcup\bigl(D\cap H_{t_0}\bigr)$: a point of $E$ either lies in $A$ or lies in $B$ off $A$, and conversely both pieces lie in $E$; the two pieces are disjoint, so additivity gives $m(E)=m(A)+F(t_0)=a+(c-a)=c$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
For every $c$ with $a<c<b$ there is a compact $E$ with $A\subseteq E\subseteq B$ and $m(E)=c$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#E){.pf-ref} produces, for the $t_0$ of step [](#ivt){.pf-ref}, a compact set between $A$ and $B$ of measure exactly $c$. Since $c$ was arbitrary in $(a,b)$, this is the assertion.
:::
