::: problem
Prove that measurable sets $E_1,E_2\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ satisfy
$$
m(E_1\cup E_2)+m(E_1\cap E_2)=m(E_1)+m(E_2).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The sets
\[
E_1
\quad\text{and}\quad
E_2\setminus E_1
\]
are disjoint and have union $E_1\cup E_2$, so countable additivity gives
\[
m(E_1\cup E_2)
=m(E_1)+m(E_2\setminus E_1).
\]
Likewise,
\[
E_2=(E_2\setminus E_1)\sqcup(E_1\cap E_2),
\]
so
\[
m(E_2)
=m(E_2\setminus E_1)+m(E_1\cap E_2).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
m(E_1\cup E_2)+m(E_1\cap E_2)
&=m(E_1)+m(E_2\setminus E_1)+m(E_1\cap E_2)\\
&=m(E_1)+m(E_2).
\end{aligned}
\]
This argument remains valid when one or more of the measures are infinite, since it uses only addition of nonnegative extended real numbers.
:::
