::: problem
Construct a generalized Cantor set by removing the open middle $\alpha_j$ fraction from each interval at stage $j$.

1. Prove that $\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_j)>0$ if and only if $\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha_j<\infty$.

2. Given $\beta\in(0,1)$, choose $(\alpha_j)$ with $\prod_j(1-\alpha_j)=\beta$.

3. Construct an open set in $[0,1]$ whose boundary has measure $\beta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
At stage $j$, each surviving interval is replaced by two closed subintervals whose total length is a fraction $1-\alpha_j$ of the parent interval. Hence after $n$ stages the total surviving length is
\[
L_n=\prod_{j=1}^n(1-\alpha_j).
\]

<1>1. Characterize when the infinite product is positive.
::: {.proof}
Assume $0<\alpha_j<1$. If
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty \alpha_j<\infty,
\]
then $\alpha_j\to0$. Choose $J$ so that $\alpha_j\le1/2$ for $j\ge J$. For $0\le t\le1/2$,
\[
-\log(1-t)\le 2t.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{j=J}^\infty -\log(1-\alpha_j)<\infty,
\]
so
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty \log(1-\alpha_j)
\]
converges to a finite real number. Exponentiating gives
\[
\prod_{j=1}^\infty(1-\alpha_j)>0.
\]

Conversely, for $0<t<1$ one has
\[
-\log(1-t)\ge t.
\]
Thus if the product is positive, then
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty -\log(1-\alpha_j)<\infty,
\]
and hence
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty\alpha_j<\infty.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\prod_{j=1}^\infty(1-\alpha_j)>0
\iff
\sum_{j=1}^\infty\alpha_j<\infty.}
\]
:::

<1>2. Prescribe the product.
::: {.proof}
Fix $\beta\in(0,1)$ and set
\[
\alpha_j=1-\beta^{2^{-j}}.
\]
Then $0<\alpha_j<1$, and
\[
1-\alpha_j=\beta^{2^{-j}}.
\]
Since
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty2^{-j}=1,
\]
we obtain
\[
\prod_{j=1}^\infty(1-\alpha_j)
=\prod_{j=1}^\infty\beta^{2^{-j}}
=\beta.
\]
:::

<1>3. Construct the required open set.
::: {.proof}
Perform the generalized Cantor construction with the sequence from <1>2, and let $C_\beta$ be the surviving compact set. Since the stage-$n$ sets decrease to $C_\beta$ and have measures
\[
L_n=\prod_{j=1}^n(1-\alpha_j),
\]
continuity from above gives
\[
m(C_\beta)=\lim_{n\to\infty}L_n=\beta.
\]

Let
\[
U=[0,1]\setminus C_\beta,
\]
viewed as an open subset of the relative space $[0,1]$. We claim that
\[
\partial_{[0,1]}U=C_\beta.
\]
Indeed, $C_\beta$ is closed. Moreover, every component interval remaining after stage $n$ has length
\[
2^{-n}\prod_{j=1}^n(1-\alpha_j)\le2^{-n},
\]
so these lengths tend to zero. Hence $C_\beta$ contains no nonempty interval and therefore has empty interior in $[0,1]$. Thus every point of $C_\beta$ is a boundary point of its complement, while points of $U$ are interior points of $U$.

Consequently
\[
m(\partial_{[0,1]}U)=m(C_\beta)=\beta.
\]
:::
