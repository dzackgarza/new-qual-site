::: {.problem}
The connect-sum ($\#$) of two oriented $4$-manifolds is defined by removing an open $4$-ball from each, and gluing the resulting manifolds using a homeomorphism between their boundary $3$-spheres, in such a way that the orientations match to make a new oriented manifold.
Compute the cohomology ring of the connect-sum $X = \mathbb{CP}^2 \# (S^2 \times S^2)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let $x\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb Z)$ be the standard generator and let $a,b\in H^2(S^2\times S^2;\mathbb Z)$ be the two factor generators.

::: pf-proof

These groups are respectively $\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Z^2$ in degree two.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For the connected sum
$$
X=\mathbb{CP}^2\#(S^2\times S^2),
$$
one has
$$
H^0(X)=H^4(X)=\mathbb Z,\qquad H^2(X)=\mathbb Z^3,
$$
and all other cohomology groups vanish.

::: pf-proof

For connected sums of closed oriented $4$-manifolds, the degree-two cohomology is the direct sum of the degree-two cohomologies of the summands, while the two top-dimensional fundamental classes are identified to the single orientation class of the connected sum.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Let $u\in H^4(X;\mathbb Z)$ be the orientation generator. Then
$$
x^2=u,\qquad a^2=b^2=0,\qquad ab=u.
$$

::: pf-proof

The intersection form of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is $(1)$, while that of $S^2\times S^2$ in the basis $a,b$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The connected-sum intersection form is the orthogonal direct sum of these forms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Mixed products between classes coming from different connected-sum summands vanish:
$$
xa=xb=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Degree-two representatives can be chosen supported away from the connect-sum neck in their respective summands, hence disjoint from representatives supported in the other summand. Equivalently, the intersection form is the orthogonal direct sum.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus the cohomology ring is the graded ring generated by $x,a,b$ in degree $2$ and $u$ in degree $4$, with
$$
\boxed{x^2=ab=u,\qquad a^2=b^2=xa=xb=0,}
$$
and every product of total degree $>4$ equal to zero.

::: pf-proof

The additive groups are those of steps [](#s2){.pf-ref}, [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} determine every product of positive-degree generators.

:::
