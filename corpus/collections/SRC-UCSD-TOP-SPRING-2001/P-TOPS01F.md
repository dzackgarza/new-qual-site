::: problem
(1) Show that any self-homotopy equivalence $f: \mathbb{CP}^{2n} \to \mathbb{CP}^{2n}$ is orientation-preserving, i.e. has degree $+1$.
(2) Is this necessarily true for $\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}$?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Use the cohomology ring structure $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^m; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]/(\alpha^{m+1})$ and functoriality of the cup product to determine the degree of self-homotopy equivalences.

<1>1. Cohomology Ring of $\mathbb{CP}^m$: *Proof:* <2>1. The complex projective space $\mathbb{CP}^m$ is a compact, connected, orientable manifold of real dimension $2m$.
<2>2. Its graded integer cohomology ring is given by: $$H^*(\mathbb{CP}^m; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[\alpha] / (\alpha^{m+1}), \qquad \deg(\alpha) = 2.$$ <2>3. The top cohomology group $H^{2m}(\mathbb{CP}^m; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is generated by the fundamental class dual $\alpha^m$.

<1>2. Induced Map on Cohomology for a Homotopy Equivalence: *Proof:* <2>1. Let $f: \mathbb{CP}^m \to \mathbb{CP}^m$ be a homotopy equivalence.
<2>2. The induced map $f^*: H^*(\mathbb{CP}^m; \mathbb{Z}) \to H^*(\mathbb{CP}^m; \mathbb{Z})$ is a graded ring isomorphism.
<2>3. In degree 2, $f^*: H^2(\mathbb{CP}^m; \mathbb{Z}) \to H^2(\mathbb{CP}^m; \mathbb{Z})$ is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}\alpha$, which forces: $$f^*(\alpha) = d \alpha \quad \text{where } d \in \{\pm 1\}.$$ <2>4. Since $f^*$ is a ring homomorphism preserving the cup product ($f^*(\alpha^m) = (f^*(\alpha))^m$): $$f^*(\alpha^m) = (d \alpha)^m = d^m \alpha^m.$$ <2>5. By definition of the topological degree $\deg(f)$ on an orientable manifold: $$f^*(\alpha^m) = \deg(f) \cdot \alpha^m \implies \deg(f) = d^m.$$

<1>3. Part 1: Case $m = 2n$ (Even Projective Spaces $\mathbb{CP}^{2n}$): *Proof:* <2>1. When $m = 2n$ is even, for either choice $d \in \{+1, -1\}$: $$\deg(f) = d^{2n} = (\pm 1)^{2n} = +1.$$ <2>2. Therefore, every self-homotopy equivalence of $\mathbb{CP}^{2n}$ has degree $+1$, and is strictly **orientation-preserving**.

<1>4. Part 2: Case $m = 2n+1$ (Odd Projective Spaces $\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}$): *Proof:* <2>1. **No**, this is not necessarily true for $\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}$.
<2>2. Complex conjugation on homogeneous coordinates: $$c: \mathbb{CP}^{2n+1} \longrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}, \qquad [z_0 : z_1 : \dots : z_{2n+1}] \longmapsto [\bar{z}_0 : \bar{z}_1 : \dots : \bar{z}_{2n+1}]$$ is a homeomorphism (hence a homotopy equivalence) with $c^2 = \operatorname{id}$.
<2>3. Since complex conjugation reverses orientation on each complex line $\mathbb{C} \subset \mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}$, the induced map on $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}; \mathbb{Z})$ has $c^*(\alpha) = -\alpha$ (so $d = -1$). <2>4. The degree of $c$ on the top cohomology $H^{4n+2}(\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}; \mathbb{Z})$ is: $$\deg(c) = (-1)^{2n+1} = -1.$$ <2>5. Thus complex conjugation provides an explicit **orientation-reversing** self-homotopy equivalence on $\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}$ of degree $-1$.

<1>5. Conclusion: Every homotopy equivalence on $\mathbb{CP}^{2n}$ has degree $d^{2n} = (+1)^{2n} = (-1)^{2n} = +1$; on $\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}$, complex conjugation has degree $(-1)^{2n+1} = -1$.
Q.E.D.
:::
