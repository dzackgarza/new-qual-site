::: {.problem}
(1) Show that any self-homotopy equivalence $f: \CP^{2n} \to \CP^{2n}$ is orientation-preserving, i.e. has degree $+1$.
(2) Is this necessarily true for $\CP^{2n+1}$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
For every $m\ge1$,
$$
H^*(\CP^m;\ZZ)
\cong \ZZ[x]/(x^{m+1}),
\qquad \gradeddegree{x}=2,
$$
and $x^m$ generates the top cohomology $H^{2m}(\CP^m;\ZZ)$.

::: pf-proof
This is the standard computation of the cohomology of complex projective space: $H^{2k}(\CP^m;\ZZ)=\ZZ$ generated by $x^k$ for $0\le k\le m$, all odd groups vanish, and the product is truncated at $x^{m+1}=0$. The real dimension of $\CP^m$ is $2m$, so $H^{2m}$ is the top group.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Let $f:\CP^m\to\CP^m$ be a homotopy equivalence.

::: pf-proof
This is the map under consideration. A homotopy equivalence induces isomorphisms on all cohomology groups, which is what makes the next subclaim an automorphism rather than a mere endomorphism.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since $f^*:H^2\to H^2$ is an automorphism of $\ZZ$, there is an $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}$ such that
$$
f^*(x)=\varepsilon x.
$$

::: pf-proof
$H^2(\CP^m;\ZZ)$ is infinite cyclic with generator $x$, and the automorphisms of the group $\ZZ$ are multiplication by $1$ and by $-1$; hence $f^*(x)=\varepsilon x$ for one of those two signs.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore
$$
f^*(x^m)=(f^*x)^m=\varepsilon^m x^m.
$$
By the definition of degree, this means
$$
\deg(f)=\varepsilon^m.
$$

::: pf-proof
$f^*$ is a homomorphism of graded rings, so it carries a power of $x$ to the same power of $f^*(x)$, which gives the first identity. On the top group $H^{2m}$, which is generated by $x^m$, the map $f^*$ is multiplication by an integer, and that integer is by definition the degree.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
For $m=2n$, the exponent is even, so
$$
\deg(f)=\varepsilon^{2n}=1.
$$
Thus every self-homotopy equivalence of $\CP^{2n}$ preserves orientation.

::: pf-proof
$\varepsilon=\pm1$ gives $\varepsilon^{2n}=1$ for every $n$, and a self-map of degree $1$ is orientation-preserving by definition.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The analogous statement is false for $\CP^{2n+1}$.

::: pf-proof
The subclaims exhibit a self-homeomorphism of $\CP^{2n+1}$ of degree $-1$.

::: pf-step
Complex conjugation
$$
c([z_0:\cdots:z_{2n+1}])=[\overline z_0:\cdots:\overline z_{2n+1}]
$$
is a homeomorphism and satisfies $c^*(x)=-x$.

::: pf-proof
Conjugation is well defined on projective classes, continuous, and its own inverse, so it is a homeomorphism. On $H^2(\CP^{2n+1};\ZZ)$ it acts by $-1$, because that class is the first Chern class of the tautological line bundle and conjugation is conjugate-linear on the fibres, which negates the Chern class.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Hence
$$
\deg(c)=(-1)^{2n+1}=-1,
$$
so $c$ is an orientation-reversing self-homotopy equivalence.

::: pf-proof
$c^*(x^{2n+1})=(c^*x)^{2n+1}=(-1)^{2n+1}x^{2n+1}=-x^{2n+1}$, so the degree is $-1$ by the definition used above; a homeomorphism is in particular a homotopy equivalence.
:::
