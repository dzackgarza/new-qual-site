::: {.problem}
In the ring of integers, find a positive integer $a$ such that:

(a) $\langle a \rangle = \langle 2 \rangle + \langle 3 \rangle$.

(b) $\langle a \rangle = \langle 6 \rangle + \langle 8 \rangle$.

(c) $\langle a \rangle = \langle m \rangle + \langle n \rangle$.
:::

::: {.solution}

In $\ZZ$ every ideal is principal, $\langle d\rangle=d\ZZ$, and the sum of
ideals is $\langle m\rangle+\langle n\rangle=\{mx+ny:x,y\in\ZZ\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #sum}

$\langle m\rangle+\langle n\rangle=\langle\gcd(m,n)\rangle$ for all integers
$m,n$ not both zero.

::: pf-proof

An ideal of $\ZZ$ is principal, so $\langle m\rangle+\langle n\rangle=\langle
d\rangle$ for some $d\ge0$, and $d\in\langle m\rangle+\langle n\rangle$ means
$d=mx+ny$ for some integers $x,y$. Since $m,n\in\langle d\rangle=d\ZZ$, both
are divisible by $d$, so $d$ is a common divisor; and every common divisor $c$
of $m$ and $n$ divides $mx+ny=d$, so $d$ is the greatest common divisor.
Conversely $\gcd(m,n)=mx+ny$ by Bezout, so
$\langle\gcd(m,n)\rangle\subseteq\langle m\rangle+\langle n\rangle$ and the
reverse inclusion holds since the gcd divides both.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #parts}

$\langle 2\rangle+\langle 3\rangle=\langle 1\rangle$ and $\langle
6\rangle+\langle 8\rangle=\langle 2\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

$1=3-2\in\langle2\rangle+\langle3\rangle$, so the sum contains $1$ and hence
equals $\ZZ=\langle1\rangle$; the positive generator is $a=1$, which is
$\gcd(2,3)$. By step [](#sum){.pf-ref},
$\langle6\rangle+\langle8\rangle=\langle\gcd(6,8)\rangle=\langle2\rangle$,
giving $a=2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #general}

$\langle m\rangle+\langle n\rangle=\langle\gcd(m,n)\rangle$, so the answer to
(c) is $a=\gcd(m,n)$.

::: pf-proof

This is step [](#sum){.pf-ref} restated for general $m,n$; taking $m=2,n=3$
and $m=6,n=8$ recovers (a) and (b). The generator is unique up to sign, and
the exercise asks for a positive integer, which is $\gcd(m,n)$ when the two
are not both zero.

:::
