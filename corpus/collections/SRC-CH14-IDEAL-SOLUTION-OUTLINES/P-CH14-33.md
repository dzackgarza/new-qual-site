::: {.problem}
How many elements are in $\ZZ[i]/\langle 3 + i \rangle$? Give reasons for your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is that of the retained solution outline for Chapter 14, Exercise 33, PDF p. 2 of `assets/attachments/Ch14Sltns.pdf`, restructured into numbered steps, with the outline's appeal to the additive order of $1+I$ replaced by the norm computation that proves it (the card's original text had already made that replacement). Here $I=\langle 3+i\rangle$ is the ideal of $\ZZ[i]$ generated by $3+i$, and $N(a+bi)=a^{2}+b^{2}$ is multiplicative.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ten}

$10\in I$.

::: pf-proof

$(3+i)(3-i)=9-3i+3i-i^{2}=9+1=10$, and $I$ contains all multiples of $3+i$ by elements of $\ZZ[i]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #reduce}

$i\equiv-3\pmod I$, hence $a+bi\equiv a-3b\pmod I$ for all $a,b\in\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

From $3+i\in I$ we get $i\equiv-3$; multiplying by $b$ and adding $a$ preserves congruence modulo the ideal $I$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #classes}

Every coset of $I$ is $k+I$ for some $k\in\theset{0,1,\dots,9}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#reduce){.pf-ref} the class of $a+bi$ is that of the integer $a-3b$; dividing $a-3b$ by $10$ with remainder gives $a-3b=10q+k$ with $0\leq k\leq9$, and $10\in I$ by step [](#ten){.pf-ref}, so $a-3b\equiv k\pmod I$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #distinct}

The ten cosets $0+I,1+I,\dots,9+I$ are pairwise distinct.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $0<k<10$ and $k\in I$. Then $k=(3+i)(u+vi)$ for some $u,v\in\ZZ$, so $k=3u-v$ and $0=u+3v$; taking norms in $k=(3+i)(u+vi)$ gives $k^{2}=N(3+i)N(u+vi)=10(u^{2}+v^{2})$. Hence $10$ divides $k^{2}$, so $2$ divides $k$ and $5$ divides $k$ — using that $2$ and $5$ are prime — and therefore $10$ divides $k$, contradicting $0<k<10$. So no nonzero multiple of $10$ below $10$ lies in $I$, and distinct $k$ in the range give distinct cosets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$\ZZ[i]/I$ has exactly $10$ elements.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#classes){.pf-ref} and [](#distinct){.pf-ref}: the quotient is $\theset{k+I\mid k=0,\dots,9}$ and the ten listed cosets are distinct.

:::
