::: {.problem}
Let $A$ and $B$ be ideals of a ring.
Prove that $AB \subseteq A \cap B$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The exercise is Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra* (7th ed.), Chapter 14 exercise 14,
printed p. 270, with $AB$ the product defined in exercise 12 on the same page. The argument
is the retained solution outline's item 14 (`assets/attachments/Ch14Sltns.pdf`, PDF p. 1),
restated as numbered steps and run on a general element of $AB$ — the outline writes such an
element as a single product $ab$, which is not what $AB$ contains.

Throughout, $R$ is a ring, $A$ and $B$ are ideals of $R$, and
$AB=\theset{a_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}b_{n} : a_{i}\in A,\ b_{i}\in B,\ n\ge 1}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #summands}

For $a\in A$ and $b\in B$, the product $ab$ lies in $A\cap B$.

::: pf-proof

Since $b\in B\subseteq R$ and $A$ is an ideal, $ab\in A$ by the definition of ideal (printed
p. 262). Since $a\in A\subseteq R$ and $B$ is an ideal, $ab\in B$. Hence $ab\in A\cap B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #closure}

$A\cap B$ is closed under addition.

::: pf-proof

An ideal is a subring (Definition, printed p. 262), so $A$ and $B$ are each closed under
addition. If $x,y\in A\cap B$ then $x+y\in A$ and $x+y\in B$, that is $x+y\in A\cap B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product}

$AB\subseteq A\cap B$.

::: pf-proof

Let $x\in AB$. Then $x=a_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}b_{n}$ with $a_{i}\in A$ and $b_{i}\in B$, and
$n\ge 1$. By step [](#summands){.pf-ref} each summand $a_{i}b_{i}$ lies in $A\cap B$, and by
step [](#closure){.pf-ref} a sum of $n$ elements of $A\cap B$ lies in $A\cap B$. Hence
$x\in A\cap B$. (Under the convention that the empty sum is $0$, the case $n=0$ gives
$0\in A\cap B$, which holds since both ideals contain $0$.)

:::
