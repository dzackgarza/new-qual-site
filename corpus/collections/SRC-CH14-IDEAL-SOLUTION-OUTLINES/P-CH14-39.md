::: {.problem}
In $\ZZ_{5}[x]$, let $I = \langle x^{2} + x + 2 \rangle$. Find the multiplicative inverse of $2x + 3 + I$ in $\ZZ_{5}[x]/I$.
:::

::: {.solution}
In $\ZZ_5[x]$ one computes $(3x+1)(2x+3)=6x^2+11x+3\equiv x^2+x+3$, and $x^2+x+3=(x^2+x+2)+1$, so the product is congruent to $1$ modulo $I=\langle x^2+x+2\rangle$. Hence $3x+1+I$ is the inverse. The collection retains its solution outline as `assets/attachments/Ch14Sltns.pdf`, whose entry "# 39" on PDF p. 2 is this exercise: it observes $(3x+1)(2x+3)=6x^2+11x+3\equiv x^2+x+3=1+(x^2+x+2)$ and concludes that $3x+1+I$ is the inverse. The card adds that $2x+3+I$ is not the zero class and that the inverse is unique; the wording is its own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #product}
In $\ZZ_5[x]$, $(3x+1)(2x+3)=x^2+x+3$.

::: pf-proof
Multiply out: $(3x+1)(2x+3)=6x^2+9x+2x+3=6x^2+11x+3$. Reducing the coefficients modulo $5$ gives $6\equiv1$, $11\equiv1$ and $3\equiv3$, so the product is $x^2+x+3$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #reduce}
$x^2+x+3\equiv1\pmod{I}$, where $I=\langle x^2+x+2\rangle$.

::: pf-proof
$x^2+x+3-(x^2+x+2)=1$, and $x^2+x+2\in I$ since $I$ is the ideal generated by it; so $x^2+x+3=1+(x^2+x+2)\in1+I$, which is the assertion modulo $I$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #nonzero}
$2x+3+I\ne0+I$.

::: pf-proof
The element $2x+3$ has degree $1$ and is nonzero. The elements of $I$ are the multiples $(x^2+x+2)h$ with $h\in\ZZ_5[x]$; those are either $0$ or have degree at least $2$, since $\ZZ_5$ is a domain and the generator has degree $2$ and leading coefficient $1$. So $2x+3\notin I$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
The multiplicative inverse of $2x+3+I$ in $\ZZ_5[x]/I$ is $3x+1+I$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#product){.pf-ref} and [](#reduce){.pf-ref}, $(3x+1+I)(2x+3+I)=1+I$; by step [](#nonzero){.pf-ref} the class $2x+3+I$ is not the zero class, so this exhibits it as a unit with inverse $3x+1+I$. The class is unique because $I$ is an ideal and the quotient is a ring, in which multiplicative inverses are unique.
:::
