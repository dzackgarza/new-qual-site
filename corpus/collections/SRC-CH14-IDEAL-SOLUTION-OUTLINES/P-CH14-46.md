::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $A$ be any ideal of $R$. Show that the nil radical of $A$,
$$
N(A) = \{ r \in R \mid r^{n} \in A \text{ for some positive integer } n \},
$$
is an ideal of $R$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the binomial expansion of $(x+y)^{n+m}$ puts a high power of $x$ or of $y$ in every term, and commutativity handles the $R$-multiples.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #closed}

$N(A)$ is closed under addition and contains $0$.

::: pf-proof

$0^{1}=0\in A$, so $0\in N(A)$. If $x^{n}\in A$ and $y^{m}\in A$, then in the binomial expansion of $(x+y)^{n+m}=\sum_{k}\binom{n+m}{k}x^{k}y^{n+m-k}$ each term has $k\ge n$ or $n+m-k\ge m$, so each term is a product containing $x^{n}\in A$ or $y^{m}\in A$ together with an arbitrary element of $R$; since $A$ is an ideal, every term lies in $A$, and so does the sum. Hence $x+y\in N(A)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subgroup}

$x-y\in N(A)$ whenever $x,y\in N(A)$.

::: pf-proof

If $y^{m}\in A$ then $(-y)^{m}=(-1)^{m}y^{m}\in A$ because $A$ is closed under multiplication by $-1\in R$, so $-y\in N(A)$; with step [](#closed){.pf-ref}, $x-y=x+(-y)\in N(A)$. So $N(A)$ is an additive subgroup of $R$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #absorb}

$rx\in N(A)$ for $r\in R$ and $x\in N(A)$.

::: pf-proof

If $x^{n}\in A$ then $(rx)^{n}=r^{n}x^{n}\in A$, using commutativity to move the factors and the fact that $A$ is an ideal so that $r^{n}x^{n}\in A$. Hence $rx\in N(A)$; by commutativity also $xr\in N(A)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$N(A)$ is an ideal of $R$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#closed){.pf-ref} and [](#subgroup){.pf-ref} make $N(A)$ an additive subgroup and step [](#absorb){.pf-ref} makes it closed under multiplication by elements of $R$:
$$
\boxed{N(A)=\{r\in R:r^{n}\in A \text{ for some } n\ge1\}\ \text{is an ideal of } R.}
$$

:::
