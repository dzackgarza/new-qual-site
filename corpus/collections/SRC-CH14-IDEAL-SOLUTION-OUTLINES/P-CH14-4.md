::: {.problem}
Find a subring of $\ZZ \oplus \ZZ$ that is not an ideal of $\ZZ \oplus \ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The diagonal $D=\{(a,a)\mid a\in\ZZ\}$ is a subring of $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ that is not
an ideal.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #subring}

$D$ is a subring of $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

$D$ contains $(0,0)$. For $(a,a),(b,b)\in D$ we have
$(a,a)-(b,b)=(a-b,a-b)\in D$ and $(a,a)(b,b)=(ab,ab)\in D$, so $D$ is closed
under subtraction and multiplication. Addition and multiplication in
$\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ are componentwise, hence associative and distributive on $D$, and
$(1,1)\in D$ is a unity. Equivalently, $D$ is the image of the ring
homomorphism $\ZZ\to\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$, $a\mapsto(a,a)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not-ideal}

$D$ is not an ideal of $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

An ideal is closed under multiplication by every element of the ring. Take
$(a,a)=(1,1)\in D$ and $(1,2)\in\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$. Then
$(1,2)(1,1)=(1,2)$, and $(1,2)\notin D$ since its two coordinates differ. So $D$
is not closed under multiplication by ring elements and is not an ideal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #why}

Only the products by diagonal elements keep $D$ inside itself.

::: pf-proof

For $(a,a)\in D$ and $(c,d)\in\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ the product is $(ac,ad)$, which lies
in $D$ exactly when $ac=ad$, that is when $a=0$ or $c=d$. So $D$ is closed under
multiplication by its own elements and by no others, which is precisely the
distinction between a subring and an ideal.

:::
