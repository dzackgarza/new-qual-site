::: {.problem}
Let $R = \ZZ_{8} \oplus \ZZ_{30}$. Find all maximal ideals of $R$, and for each maximal ideal $I$, identify the size of the field $R/I$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: an ideal of a direct product of rings is maximal exactly when one coordinate is maximal and the other coordinate is the whole ring, and a quotient of $\ZZ_{n}$ by $\langle d\rangle$ has $d$ elements.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #quotients}

The maximal ideals of $\ZZ_{8}$ are $\langle2\rangle$ and those of $\ZZ_{30}$ are $\langle2\rangle,\langle3\rangle,\langle5\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

The ideals of $\ZZ_{n}$ are the $\langle d\rangle$ for $d\mid n$, and $\ZZ_{n}/\langle d\rangle\approx\ZZ_{d}$; the ideal $\langle d\rangle$ is maximal exactly when $\ZZ_{d}$ is a field, that is when $d$ is prime. The prime divisors of $8$ are just $2$, giving $\langle2\rangle$ in $\ZZ_{8}$ with quotient of size $2$; the prime divisors of $30=2\cdot3\cdot5$ give $\langle2\rangle,\langle3\rangle,\langle5\rangle$ in $\ZZ_{30}$ with quotients of sizes $2,3,5$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #maximal}

The maximal ideals of $R=\ZZ_{8}\oplus\ZZ_{30}$ are the four ideals $\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle p\rangle$ for $p\in\{2,3,5\}$ and $\langle2\rangle\oplus\langle1\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

For rings $R_{1},R_{2}$, an ideal of $R_{1}\times R_{2}$ is maximal exactly when it is $M_{1}\times R_{2}$ with $M_{1}$ maximal in $R_{1}$, or $R_{1}\times M_{2}$ with $M_{2}$ maximal in $R_{2}$: such an ideal gives the field $R_{1}/M_{1}$ or $R_{2}/M_{2}$ as quotient, and any ideal that is proper in both coordinates is properly contained in one of these. Taking $R_{1}=\ZZ_{8}$, $R_{2}=\ZZ_{30}$ and the lists of step [](#quotients){.pf-ref} gives the four ideals, where $\langle1\rangle$ denotes the whole ring.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sizes}

The quotients $R/I$ have sizes $2,3,5,2$ for the ideals $\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle2\rangle$, $\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle3\rangle$, $\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle5\rangle$, $\langle2\rangle\oplus\langle1\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

$\abs{R}=8\cdot30=240$ and $\abs{I_{1}\oplus I_{2}}=\abs{I_{1}}\abs{I_{2}}$, so the quotient has size $240/\abs{I}$. For $\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle2\rangle$ this is $240/(8\cdot15)=2$; for $\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle3\rangle$, $240/(8\cdot10)=3$; for $\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle5\rangle$, $240/(8\cdot6)=5$; for $\langle2\rangle\oplus\langle1\rangle$, $240/(4\cdot30)=2$. Each is prime, as a field order must be.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$R$ has exactly four maximal ideals, with residue fields of orders $2,3,5,2$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#maximal){.pf-ref} and [](#sizes){.pf-ref} list them and their residue field sizes:
$$
\boxed{\langle1\rangle\oplus\langle2\rangle\ (2),\quad \langle1\rangle\oplus\langle3\rangle\ (3),\quad \langle1\rangle\oplus\langle5\rangle\ (5),\quad \langle2\rangle\oplus\langle1\rangle\ (2).}
$$

:::
