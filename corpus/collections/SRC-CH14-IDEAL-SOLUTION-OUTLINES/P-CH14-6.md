::: {.problem}
Find all maximal ideals in:

(a) $\ZZ_8$.

(b) $\ZZ_{10}$.

(c) $\ZZ_{12}$.

(d) $\ZZ_n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ideals-of-zn}

The ideals of $\ZZ_{n}$ are the principal ideals $(d)$ with $d$ a divisor of
$n$.

::: pf-proof

Ideals of $\ZZ_{n}$ correspond to ideals $I$ of $\ZZ$ with
$n\ZZ\subseteq I\subseteq\ZZ$, and every ideal of $\ZZ$ is principal, so
$I=(d)$ with $d\mid n$; the image in $\ZZ_{n}$ is $(d)$ again. Explicitly,
$(d)=\{0,d,2d,\dots,n-d\}$ has $n/d$ elements and is the set of multiples of $d$
modulo $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #quotient}

$\ZZ_{n}/(d)\cong\ZZ_{d}$, and $(d)$ is maximal exactly when $d$ is prime.

::: pf-proof

Reduction $x\bmod n\mapsto x\bmod d$ is a surjective ring homomorphism
$\ZZ_{n}\to\ZZ_{d}$ whose kernel is precisely the multiples of $d$, that is
$(d)$; so by the first isomorphism theorem $\ZZ_{n}/(d)\cong\ZZ_{d}$. For a
commutative ring with unity an ideal is maximal exactly when the quotient ring
is a field, and $\ZZ_{d}$ is a field exactly when $d$ is prime (and is the zero
ring for $d=1$, which is not a field). Hence the maximal ideals of $\ZZ_{n}$
are the $(p)$ with $p$ a prime dividing $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #parts}

Hence: $\ZZ_{8}$ has the single maximal ideal $(2)$; $\ZZ_{10}$ has $(2)$ and
$(5)$; $\ZZ_{12}$ has $(2)$ and $(3)$.

::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#quotient){.pf-ref} to the prime factorizations $8=2^{3}$,
$10=2\cdot 5$ and $12=2^{2}\cdot 3$. The distinct primes dividing $n$ are $2$
for $8$, $2$ and $5$ for $10$, $2$ and $3$ for $12$, and each gives one maximal
ideal. For a general $n$ the number of maximal ideals of $\ZZ_{n}$ is therefore
the number of distinct prime divisors of $n$.

:::
