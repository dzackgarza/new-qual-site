::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,1]\to\RR$ be a bounded Lebesgue measurable function.
Prove that
$$
\lim_{p\to\infty}\norm{f}_p=\norm{f}_\infty.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Obtain the upper bound.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
M:=\norm{f}_\infty.
$$
Since $[0,1]$ has measure $1$ and $\abs{f}\le M$ almost everywhere,
$$
\norm{f}_p^p=\int_0^1\abs{f}^p\,dx\le M^p.
$$
Hence
$$
\norm{f}_p\le M
$$
for every finite $p$, so
$$
\limsup_{p\to\infty}\norm{f}_p\le M.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Obtain the matching lower bound from the essential supremum.

::: pf-proof

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. If $M=0$, the result is immediate, so assume $M>0$ and choose $0<\varepsilon<M$. By definition of essential supremum,
$$
E_\varepsilon:=\{x\in[0,1]:\abs{f(x)}>M-\varepsilon\}
$$
has positive measure. Therefore
$$
\norm{f}_p^p
\ge \int_{E_\varepsilon}\abs{f}^p\,dx
\ge (M-\varepsilon)^p m(E_\varepsilon).
$$
Thus
$$
\norm{f}_p
\ge (M-\varepsilon)m(E_\varepsilon)^{1/p}.
$$
Letting $p\to\infty$ gives
$$
\liminf_{p\to\infty}\norm{f}_p\ge M-\varepsilon.
$$
Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary,
$$
\liminf_{p\to\infty}\norm{f}_p\ge M.
$$
Combining with Step 1 yields
$$
\boxed{\lim_{p\to\infty}\norm{f}_p=\norm{f}_\infty.}
$$

:::
