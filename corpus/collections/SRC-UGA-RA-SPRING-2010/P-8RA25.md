::: {.problem}
Let $f(x)$ be Lebesgue measurable on $\RR$.
Suppose that $f(x)$ and $xf(x)$ are both integrable over the whole of $\RR$.
Define
\[
F(t):=\int_{\RR}f(x)\sin(tx)\,dx.
\]
Prove that $F(t)$ is differentiable at every $t$, and find a formula for $F'(t)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
First, $F(t)$ is well defined for every $t$ because
\[
|f(x)\sin(tx)|\le |f(x)|
\]
and $f\in L^1(\RR)$.

Fix $t\in\RR$. For $h\ne0$,
\[
\frac{F(t+h)-F(t)}h
=\int_{\RR}
f(x)\frac{\sin((t+h)x)-\sin(tx)}h\,dx.
\]
For each fixed $x$,
\[
\frac{\sin((t+h)x)-\sin(tx)}h
\longrightarrow x\cos(tx)
\qquad(h\to0).
\]
By the mean-value theorem applied to the sine function,
\[
\left|
\frac{\sin((t+h)x)-\sin(tx)}h
\right|
\le |x|.
\]
Hence the integrand is dominated by
\[
|x f(x)|,
\]
which is integrable by hypothesis.

The dominated convergence theorem therefore permits passage of the limit through the integral:
\[
\begin{aligned}
F'(t)
&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{F(t+h)-F(t)}h\\
&=\int_{\RR}x f(x)\cos(tx)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $F$ is differentiable for every $t\in\RR$, with
\[
\boxed{F'(t)=\int_{\RR}x f(x)\cos(tx)\,dx.}
\]
:::
