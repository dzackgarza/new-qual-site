::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be a polynomial with real coefficients.
Show that there exists $x_0\in\RR$ such that $\abs{f(x_0)}\le\abs{f(x)}$ for all $x\in\RR$.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $f$ is constant, every $x_0\in\RR$ is a minimizer. Assume that $f$ is nonconstant.

Let $d\ge1$ be the degree of $f$ and let $a_d\ne0$ be its leading coefficient. Then
$$
\frac{f(x)}{a_dx^d}\longrightarrow1
\qquad\text{as }\abs{x}\to\infty,
$$
so
$$
\abs{f(x)}\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad\text{as }\abs{x}\to\infty.
$$
Choose $R>0$ so large that
$$
\abs{x}\ge R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\abs{f(x)}>\abs{f(0)}.
$$

The function $\abs{f}$ is continuous on the compact interval $[-R,R]$. By the extreme-value theorem, there exists $x_0\in[-R,R]$ such that
$$
\abs{f(x_0)}=\min_{\abs{x}\le R}\abs{f(x)}.
$$
Since
$$
\abs{f(x_0)}\le \abs{f(0)}<\abs{f(x)}
$$
for every $\abs{x}>R$, the same $x_0$ minimizes $\abs{f}$ on all of $\RR$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\abs{f(x_0)}\le \abs{f(x)}\quad\text{for all }x\in\RR.}
$$
:::
