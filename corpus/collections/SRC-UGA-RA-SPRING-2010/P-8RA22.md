::: problem
Let $f$ be a polynomial with real coefficients.
Show that there exists $x_0\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $|f(x_0)|\le|f(x)|$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$.
:::

::: solution
If $f$ is constant, every $x_0\in\mathbb R$ is a minimizer. Assume that $f$ is nonconstant.

Let $d\ge1$ be the degree of $f$ and let $a_d\ne0$ be its leading coefficient. Then
\[
\frac{f(x)}{a_dx^d}\longrightarrow1
\qquad\text{as }|x|\to\infty,
\]
so
\[
|f(x)|\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad\text{as }|x|\to\infty.
\]
Choose $R>0$ so large that
\[
|x|\ge R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|f(x)|>|f(0)|.
\]

The function $|f|$ is continuous on the compact interval $[-R,R]$. By the extreme-value theorem, there exists $x_0\in[-R,R]$ such that
\[
|f(x_0)|=\min_{|x|\le R}|f(x)|.
\]
Since
\[
|f(x_0)|\le |f(0)|<|f(x)|
\]
for every $|x|>R$, the same $x_0$ minimizes $|f|$ on all of $\mathbb R$. Thus
\[
\boxed{|f(x_0)|\le |f(x)|\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb R.}
\]
:::
