::: problem
Let $f\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$, and for $r>0$ put
\[
f_r(x):=\frac{1}{2r}\int_{x-r}^{x+r}f\,dm.
\]

(a) Prove that $\|f_r\|_1\le\|f\|_1$ for all $r>0$.
(Hint: use Fubini's theorem.)

(b) Prove that $f_r\to f$ in $L^1$ as $r\downarrow 0$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Prove the $L^1$ contraction estimate.
::: proof
For almost every $x$,
\[
|f_r(x)|
\le \frac1{2r}\int_{x-r}^{x+r}|f(y)|\,dy.
\]
Integrating and applying Tonelli,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|f_r\|_1
&\le \frac1{2r}
\int_{\mathbb R}\int_{x-r}^{x+r}|f(y)|\,dy\,dx\\
&=\frac1{2r}
\int_{\mathbb R}|f(y)|
\left(\int_{y-r}^{y+r}dx\right)dy\\
&=\|f\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{\|f_r\|_1\le\|f\|_1.}
\]
:::

<1>2. Recall $L^1$ continuity under translations.
::: proof
For $t\in\mathbb R$, write
\[
\tau_t f(x):=f(x+t).
\]
We claim
\[
\|\tau_t f-f\|_1\longrightarrow0
\qquad(t\to0).
\]
Indeed, let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose $\varphi\in C_c(\mathbb R)$ with
\[
\|f-\varphi\|_1<\varepsilon.
\]
Translations preserve the $L^1$ norm, so
\[
\|\tau_t f-f\|_1
\le 2\varepsilon+\|\tau_t\varphi-\varphi\|_1.
\]
Since $\varphi$ is uniformly continuous and all sufficiently small translates have support in one fixed compact set,
\[
\|\tau_t\varphi-\varphi\|_1\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence the claim follows.
:::

<1>3. Average the translation differences.
::: proof
Changing variables in the definition of $f_r$ gives
\[
f_r(x)=\frac1{2r}\int_{-r}^r f(x+t)\,dt.
\]
Therefore, by Minkowski's integral inequality (or Tonelli followed by the triangle inequality),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|f_r-f\|_1
&\le \frac1{2r}\int_{-r}^r
\|\tau_t f-f\|_1\,dt\\
&\le \sup_{|t|\le r}\|\tau_t f-f\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
By Step 2, the right-hand side tends to $0$ as $r\downarrow0$. Hence
\[
\boxed{f_r\to f\text{ in }L^1(\mathbb R).}
\]
:::
:::
