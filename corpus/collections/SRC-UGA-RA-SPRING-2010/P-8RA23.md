::: {.problem}
Prove that there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$
\cos(\sin x)>\frac12\bigl(1+\cos^2 x\bigr)
$$
whenever $0<\abs{x}<\varepsilon$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Throughout $h(x) \coloneqq \cos(\sin x) - \tfrac12\bigl(1 + \cos^{2}x\bigr)$, and $O(x^{k})$ denotes a quantity bounded by $C\abs{x}^{k}$ for small $\abs{x}$ and a constant $C$. The collection's `provenance` names the Spring 2010 examination paper, and no solutions sheet accompanies it, so the argument below is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #expansion}
$h(x) = \frac{x^{4}}{24} + O(x^{6})$ as $x \to 0$; in particular $h(0) = h'(0) = h''(0) = h'''(0) = 0$ and $h^{(4)}(0) = 1$.

::: pf-proof
From $\sin x = x - \frac{x^{3}}{6} + O(x^{5})$ one gets $\sin^{2}x = x^{2} - \frac{x^{4}}{3} + O(x^{6})$ and $\sin^{4}x = x^{4} + O(x^{6})$, so with $\cos u = 1 - \frac{u^{2}}{2} + \frac{u^{4}}{24} + O(u^{6})$ at $u = \sin x$, $\cos(\sin x) = 1 - \frac{x^{2}}{2} + \Bigl(\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{24}\Bigr)x^{4} + O(x^{6}) = 1 - \frac{x^{2}}{2} + \frac{5x^{4}}{24} + O(x^{6})$. From $\cos 2x = 1 - 2x^{2} + \frac{2x^{4}}{3} + O(x^{6})$ and $\cos^{2}x = \frac{1+\cos 2x}{2}$ one gets $\cos^{2}x = 1 - x^{2} + \frac{x^{4}}{3} + O(x^{6})$, so $\frac{1+\cos^{2}x}{2} = 1 - \frac{x^{2}}{2} + \frac{x^{4}}{6} + O(x^{6})$. Subtracting gives $h(x) = \Bigl(\frac{5}{24} - \frac{4}{24}\Bigr)x^{4} + O(x^{6}) = \frac{x^{4}}{24} + O(x^{6})$. The first four derivatives of $h$ at $0$ vanish because $h(x) - h(0) = O(x^{4})$, and the fourth divided difference gives $h^{(4)}(0) = 4! \cdot \frac{1}{24} = 1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #positivity}
There is $\varepsilon > 0$ with $h^{(4)}(t) > 0$ for every $\abs{t} < \varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof
$h$ is a composition of smooth functions, hence has a continuous fourth derivative, and $h^{(4)}(0) = 1 > 0$ by step [](#expansion){.pf-ref}; continuity at $0$ gives the neighbourhood.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #taylor}
For $0 < \abs{x} < \varepsilon$ there is $\xi$ between $0$ and $x$ with $h(x) = \frac{h^{(4)}(\xi)}{4!}x^{4}$.

::: pf-proof
Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder, expanded at $0$ to order three, gives $h(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{h^{(k)}(0)}{k!}x^{k} + \frac{h^{(4)}(\xi)}{4!}x^{4}$ for some such $\xi$; step [](#expansion){.pf-ref} makes the polynomial part vanish.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\cos(\sin x) > \frac12\bigl(1 + \cos^{2}x\bigr)$ whenever $0 < \abs{x} < \varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof
For such $x$ the point $\xi$ of step [](#taylor){.pf-ref} satisfies $\abs{\xi} < \varepsilon$, so $h^{(4)}(\xi) > 0$ by step [](#positivity){.pf-ref}; also $x^{4} > 0$ and $4! > 0$. Hence $h(x) > 0$, that is $\cos(\sin x) > \frac12(1+\cos^{2}x)$, which is the assertion with $\varepsilon$ the number produced in step [](#positivity){.pf-ref}.

:::
