::: {.problem}
Show that $\int _ { 0 } ^ { \infty } { \frac { \sin { x } } { x } } d x = { \frac { \pi } { 2 } }$

[For a Hint, see textbook by Stein and Shakarchi p64, #2]
:::

Let
$$
F(z)=\frac{e^{iz}}z.
$$
For $0<\varepsilon<R$, let $C_{R,\varepsilon}$ be the positively oriented boundary of the upper half-disk $\{\abs z<R,\ \operatorname{Im}z>0\}$ with the upper half-disk $\{\abs z\leq\varepsilon,\ \operatorname{Im}z\geq0\}$ removed. Thus the small semicircle is traversed clockwise.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\int_{C_{R,\varepsilon}}F(z)\,dz=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The only singularity of $F$ is at $z=0$, which lies outside the indented region bounded by $C_{R,\varepsilon}$. Hence $F$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of that region, and Cauchy's theorem gives the stated integral.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The sum of the integrals over the two real segments is
$$
2i\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx.
$$


::: pf-proof

The real-axis contribution is
$$
\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon}\frac{e^{ix}}x\,dx
+\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{e^{ix}}x\,dx.
$$
In the first integral substitute $x=-t$. This gives
$$
-\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{e^{-it}}t\,dt
+\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{e^{it}}t\,dt
=\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{e^{it}-e^{-it}}t\,dt
=2i\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $S_\varepsilon$ is the small upper semicircle, oriented clockwise, then
$$
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\int_{S_\varepsilon}F(z)\,dz=-i\pi.
$$


::: pf-proof

Parametrize
$$
z=\varepsilon e^{i\theta},
\qquad \pi\geq\theta\geq0.
$$
Then $dz=i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\,d\theta$, so
$$
\int_{S_\varepsilon}F(z)\,dz
=i\int_\pi^0 e^{i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta.
$$
The integrand converges uniformly to $1$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Hence
$$
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\int_{S_\varepsilon}F(z)\,dz
=i\int_\pi^0 1\,d\theta
=-i\pi.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $A_R$ is the large upper semicircle, then
$$
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{A_R}F(z)\,dz=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Parametrize $z=Re^{i\theta}$ for $0\leq\theta\leq\pi$. Then
$$
\left|\int_{A_R}F(z)\,dz\right|
\leq\int_0^\pi e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta.
$$
By symmetry this is
$$
2\int_0^{\pi/2}e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta.
$$
On $[0,\pi/2]$, concavity of sine gives
$$
\sin\theta\geq\frac{2\theta}{\pi}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int_{A_R}F(z)\,dz\right|
&\leq2\int_0^{\pi/2}e^{-2R\theta/\pi}\,d\theta\\
&\leq\frac{\pi}{R},
\end{aligned}
$$
which tends to zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For every $R>0$,
$$
2i\int_0^R\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
+\int_{A_R}F(z)\,dz
-i\pi=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Decompose the contour integral in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} into the two real segments, the small semicircle, and the large semicircle. Use step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Since $\sin x/x$ extends continuously to $1$ at $x=0$, the real integral converges to the integral from $0$ to $R$. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the limiting small-arc contribution, while the large arc is independent of $\varepsilon$. This yields the displayed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The improper integral exists and satisfies
$$
\boxed{\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx=\frac\pi2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $R\to\infty$ in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}. By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the large-arc integral tends to zero. Hence
$$
2i\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_0^R\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx-i\pi=0.
$$
Solving for the limit gives $\pi/2$. In particular, this proves the existence of the improper integral as well as its value.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is the required identity.

:::

:::
