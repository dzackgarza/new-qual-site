::: {.problem}
Suppose $f'(z)$ exists in a region $\Omega$ except possibly at point $w$ in $\Omega$. Let $T \subset \Omega$ be a triangle whose interior is also contained in $\Omega$ and contains $w$. Show that
$$
\int_T f(z)\,dz = 0.
$$
:::

::: {.remark}
Erratum: the statement is false as written in the source, which puts no hypothesis on $f$ at $w$.
For $f(z) = 1/(z - w)$ on $\Omega = \mathbb{C}$, $f'$ exists except at $w$, $f$ is not even defined at $w$, and for any positively oriented triangle $T$ containing $w$ in its interior $\int_T f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \ne 0$; setting $f(w) = 0$ does not change the integral.
The corrected hypothesis is that $f$ is continuous on $\Omega$, or more generally bounded near $w$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The statement as posed is false without an additional hypothesis at $w$.


::: pf-proof

Take $\Omega=\mathbb C$ and define
$$
f(z)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac1{z-w},&z\ne w,\\
0,&z=w.
\end{cases}
$$
Then $f'(z)$ exists for every $z\ne w$. If $T$ is any positively oriented triangle whose interior contains $w$, the residue theorem gives
$$
\int_T f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\ne0.
$$
Thus the conclusion does not follow from the stated hypotheses.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Assume instead that $f$ is bounded in a neighborhood of $w$. Then there are $M,\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{f(z)}\leq M
$$
whenever $0<\abs{z-w}<\delta$.


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the meaning of boundedness near $w$, after shrinking the neighborhood if necessary. Since $w$ lies in the interior of $T$, we may also choose $\delta$ so that the closed disk $\overline{B(w,\delta)}$ lies in the interior of $T$ and in $\Omega$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every sufficiently small $r>0$, let $Q_r$ be the closed square centered at $w$ with side length $2r$. Then
$$
\int_T f(z)\,dz=\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz,
$$
where both boundaries are positively oriented relative to their interiors.


::: pf-proof

Choose $r$ so small that $Q_r\subset B(w,\delta)$. The closed polygonal region between $\partial T$ and $\partial Q_r$ can be triangulated into finitely many triangles whose interiors and boundaries avoid $w$. On a neighborhood of each such triangle, $f$ is holomorphic because $f'(z)$ exists there. Goursat's theorem applied to each triangle says that its boundary integral is zero.

Summing these finitely many identities cancels every internal edge. The outer boundary remains with the positive orientation of $T$, while the inner boundary remains with the negative orientation of $Q_r$. Therefore
$$
\int_T f(z)\,dz-\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz=0,
$$
which is the asserted equality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every sufficiently small $r>0$,
$$
\left|\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz\right|
\leq8Mr.
$$


::: pf-proof

The square $Q_r$ has perimeter $8r$. Every point of its boundary lies in the punctured neighborhood from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, where $\abs f\leq M$. The $ML$ estimate therefore gives
$$
\left|\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz\right|
\leq M\,\operatorname{length}(\partial Q_r)
=8Mr.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Under the corrected boundedness hypothesis,
$$
\boxed{\int_T f(z)\,dz=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, for every sufficiently small $r$,
$$
\left|\int_Tf(z)\,dz\right|
=\left|\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz\right|.
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} bounds the right-hand side by $8Mr$. Letting $r\downarrow0$ forces the fixed quantity on the left to be zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

In particular, the conclusion holds if $f$ is continuous at $w$.


::: pf-proof

Continuity at $w$ implies local boundedness near $w$, so step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} applies.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} refutes the statement as posed. Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} prove the corrected statement recorded in the card's erratum.

:::
