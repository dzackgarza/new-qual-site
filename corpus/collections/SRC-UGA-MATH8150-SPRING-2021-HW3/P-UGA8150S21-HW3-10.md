::: {.problem}
Suppose $f$ is continuous on a region $\Omega$ and $f'(z)$ exists in $\Omega$ except possibly at a point $w$ in $\Omega$. Let $T \subset \Omega$ be a triangle whose interior is also contained in $\Omega$ and contains $w$. Show that
$$
\int_T f(z)\,dz = 0.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There are $M,\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{f(z)}\leq M
$$
whenever $\abs{z-w}<\delta$, and such that the closed disk $\overline{B(w,\delta)}$ lies in the interior of $T$.


::: pf-proof

Since $f$ is continuous at $w$, there is $\delta>0$ with $\abs{f(z)-f(w)}\leq1$ for $\abs{z-w}<\delta$; take $M=\abs{f(w)}+1$. Since $w$ lies in the interior of $T$, which is open and contained in $\Omega$, shrinking $\delta$ puts $\overline{B(w,\delta)}$ inside the interior of $T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every sufficiently small $r>0$, let $Q_r$ be the closed square centered at $w$ with side length $2r$. Then
$$
\int_T f(z)\,dz=\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz,
$$
where both boundaries are positively oriented relative to their interiors.


::: pf-proof

Choose $r$ so small that $Q_r\subset B(w,\delta)$. The closed polygonal region between $\partial T$ and $\partial Q_r$ can be triangulated into finitely many triangles whose interiors and boundaries avoid $w$. On a neighborhood of each such triangle, $f$ is holomorphic because $f'(z)$ exists there. Goursat's theorem applied to each triangle says that its boundary integral is zero.

Summing these finitely many identities cancels every internal edge. The outer boundary remains with the positive orientation of $T$, while the inner boundary remains with the negative orientation of $Q_r$. Therefore
$$
\int_T f(z)\,dz-\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz=0,
$$
which is the asserted equality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every sufficiently small $r>0$,
$$
\abs{\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz}
\leq8Mr.
$$


::: pf-proof

The square $Q_r$ has perimeter $8r$. Every point of its boundary lies in the disk from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, where $\abs f\leq M$. The $ML$ estimate therefore gives
$$
\abs{\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz}
\leq M\,\operatorname{length}(\partial Q_r)
=8Mr.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\int_T f(z)\,dz=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, for every sufficiently small $r$,
$$
\abs{\int_Tf(z)\,dz}
=\abs{\int_{\partial Q_r}f(z)\,dz}.
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} bounds the right-hand side by $8Mr$. Letting $r\downarrow0$ forces the fixed quantity on the left to be zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} is the required identity.

:::
