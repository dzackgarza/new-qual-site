::: {.problem}
Suppose $f'(z)$ exists in a region $\Omega$ except possibly at point $w$ in $\Omega$. Let $T \subset \Omega$ be a triangle whose interior is also contained in $\Omega$ and contains $w$. Show that
$$
\int_T f(z)\,dz = 0.
$$
:::

::: {.remark}
Erratum: the statement is false as written in the source, which puts no hypothesis on $f$ at $w$.
For $f(z) = 1/(z - w)$ on $\Omega = \mathbb{C}$, $f'$ exists except at $w$, $f$ is not even defined at $w$, and for any positively oriented triangle $T$ containing $w$ in its interior $\int_T f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \ne 0$; setting $f(w) = 0$ does not change the integral.
The corrected hypothesis is that $f$ is continuous on $\Omega$, or more generally bounded near $w$.
:::
