::: {.problem}
Let $f ( z )$ be bounded and analytic in $\CC .$ . Let $a \neq b$ be any fixed complex numbers.
Show that the following limit exists

$$
\operatorname* { l i m } _ { R \to \infty } \int _ { | z | = R } { \frac { f ( z ) } { ( z - a ) ( z - b ) } } d z .
$$

Use this to show that $f ( z )$ must be a constant (Liouville’s theorem).
:::

::: {.solution}

Choose $M<\infty$ such that
$$
\abs{f(z)}\leq M
$$
for every $z\in\CC$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every
$$
R>\max\{\abs a,\abs b\},
$$
one has
$$
\left|
\int_{\abs z=R}\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)(z-b)}\,dz
\right|
\leq
\frac{2\pi RM}{(R-\abs a)(R-\abs b)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $\abs z=R$, then the reverse triangle inequality gives
$$
\abs{z-a}\geq R-\abs a,
\qquad
\abs{z-b}\geq R-\abs b.
$$
Hence on the circle
$$
\left|\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)(z-b)}\right|
\leq
\frac{M}{(R-\abs a)(R-\abs b)}.
$$
The circle has length $2\pi R$, so the $ML$ estimate gives the stated bound.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The limit in the problem exists and equals $0$:
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\int_{\abs z=R}\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)(z-b)}\,dz=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The bound in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} satisfies
$$
\frac{2\pi RM}{(R-\abs a)(R-\abs b)}\longrightarrow0
$$
as $R\to\infty$. Therefore the absolute value of the contour integral tends to zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every sufficiently large $R$,
$$
\int_{\abs z=R}\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)(z-b)}\,dz
=2\pi i\,\frac{f(a)-f(b)}{a-b}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Use the partial-fraction identity
$$
\frac1{(z-a)(z-b)}
=\frac1{a-b}\left(\frac1{z-a}-\frac1{z-b}\right).
$$
For $R>\max\{\abs a,\abs b\}$, both $a$ and $b$ lie inside the circle. Cauchy's integral formula gives
$$
\int_{\abs z=R}\frac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz=2\pi i f(a),
$$
and similarly
$$
\int_{\abs z=R}\frac{f(z)}{z-b}\,dz=2\pi i f(b).
$$
Substitution into the partial-fraction decomposition yields the claimed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
f(a)=f(b).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the contour integral is independent of $R$ once $R$ is large enough and equals
$$
2\pi i\,\frac{f(a)-f(b)}{a-b}.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the same integral tends to $0$ as $R\to\infty$. Therefore
$$
2\pi i\,\frac{f(a)-f(b)}{a-b}=0.
$$
Since $a\ne b$, this implies $f(a)=f(b)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The function $f$ is constant on $\CC$.


::: pf-proof

The points $a\ne b$ were arbitrary. Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} therefore shows that $f$ takes the same value at every pair of distinct points of $\CC$. Hence $f$ is constant.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves existence of the requested limit, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives Liouville's theorem.
