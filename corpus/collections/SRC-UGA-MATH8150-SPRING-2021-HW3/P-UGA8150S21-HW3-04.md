::: {.problem}
Suppose that $f ( z )$ is entire and lim $_ { z \to \infty } f ( z ) / z = 0$ . Show that $f ( z )$ is a constant.
:::

::: {.solution}

Define
$$
g(z)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{f(z)-f(0)}{z},&z\ne0,\\
f'(0),&z=0.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The function $g$ is entire.


::: pf-proof

For $z\ne0$, the quotient is holomorphic. Since $f$ is entire,
$$
\lim_{z\to0}\frac{f(z)-f(0)}z=f'(0).
$$
Thus the apparent singularity at $0$ is removable, and the chosen value $g(0)=f'(0)$ gives its holomorphic extension across $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\lim_{z\to\infty}g(z)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $z\ne0$,
$$
g(z)=\frac{f(z)}z-\frac{f(0)}z.
$$
By hypothesis, the first term tends to $0$ as $z\to\infty$, and the second term also tends to $0$. Hence $g(z)\to0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The entire function $g$ is bounded on $\CC$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, there exists $R>0$ such that
$$
\abs{g(z)}\leq1
$$
whenever $\abs z\geq R$. On the compact disk $\abs z\leq R$, the continuous function $\abs g$ attains a finite maximum. Combining the two regions gives a global bound.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
g\equiv0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $g$ is entire, and by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} it is bounded. Liouville's theorem therefore makes $g$ constant. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} says that this constant has limit $0$ at infinity, so the constant is $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The function $f$ is constant.


::: pf-proof

For every $z\ne0$, step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
0=g(z)=\frac{f(z)-f(0)}z,
$$
so $f(z)=f(0)$. The same equality is tautological at $z=0$. Hence
$$
\boxed{f(z)=f(0)\text{ for every }z\in\CC}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
