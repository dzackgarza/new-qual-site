::: {.problem}
Compute $\displaystyle\int_{\abs{z} = 1} \left(z + \frac{1}{z}\right)^{2n} \frac{dz}{z}$ and use it to show that
$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \cos^{2n}\theta\,d\theta = 2\pi\, \frac{1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdots (2n-1)}{2 \cdot 4 \cdot 6 \cdots (2n)}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
I_n=\int_{\abs z=1}\left(z+\frac1z\right)^{2n}\frac{dz}{z}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\boxed{I_n=2\pi i\binom{2n}{n}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand the Laurent polynomial:
$$
\left(z+\frac1z\right)^{2n}
=\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{k}z^kz^{-(2n-k)}
=\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{k}z^{2k-2n}.
$$
After multiplication by $dz/z$, the term contributing a residue at $0$ is the one for which
$$
2k-2n-1=-1,
$$
namely $k=n$. Its coefficient is $\binom{2n}{n}$. Hence the residue theorem gives
$$
I_n=2\pi i\binom{2n}{n}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Parametrizing the unit circle by $z=e^{i\theta}$ gives
$$
I_n
=i2^{2n}\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^{2n}\theta\,d\theta.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $z=e^{i\theta}$,
$$
z+\frac1z
=e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}
=2\cos\theta,
$$
and
$$
\frac{dz}{z}=i\,d\theta.
$$
Therefore
$$
I_n
=\int_0^{2\pi}(2\cos\theta)^{2n}i\,d\theta
=i2^{2n}\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^{2n}\theta\,d\theta.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^{2n}\theta\,d\theta
=2\pi\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{2^{2n}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Equate the two expressions for $I_n$ from steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and cancel the common factor $i$:
$$
2^{2n}\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^{2n}\theta\,d\theta
=2\pi\binom{2n}{n}.
$$
Division by $2^{2n}$ gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The combinatorial factor satisfies
$$
\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{2^{2n}}
=\frac{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)}{2\cdot4\cdot6\cdots(2n)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Indeed,
$$
\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{2^{2n}}
=\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2 2^{2n}}.
$$
Split $(2n)!$ into its odd and even factors:
$$
(2n)!
=\bigl(1\cdot3\cdots(2n-1)\bigr)
\bigl(2\cdot4\cdots2n\bigr).
$$
Since
$$
2\cdot4\cdots2n=2^n n!,
$$
the expression becomes
$$
\frac{1\cdot3\cdots(2n-1)}{2^n n!}
=\frac{1\cdot3\cdots(2n-1)}{2\cdot4\cdots2n}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^{2n}\theta\,d\theta
=2\pi
\frac{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)}{2\cdot4\cdot6\cdots(2n)}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} into the formula of step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} computes the contour integral, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves the required real integral identity.
