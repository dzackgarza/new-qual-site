::: {.problem}

Let $f(x) = x^3 - 7 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$.
Determine the splitting field and Galois group of $f$ over each of $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, and $\mathbb{F}_{13}$.
:::


::: {.solution}
Let \(\alpha=\sqrt[3]{7}\) and let \(\zeta_3\) be a primitive cube root of unity.

<1>1. Over \(\mathbb Q\), the splitting field is
\[
L=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta_3),
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
The polynomial \(x^3-7\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) by Eisenstein's criterion at \(7\), so \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=3\). Its roots are
\[
\alpha,\qquad \alpha\zeta_3,\qquad \alpha\zeta_3^2,
\]
so the splitting field is \(\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta_3)\). The field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) is contained in \(\mathbb R\), while \(\zeta_3\notin\mathbb R\), hence \(\zeta_3\notin\mathbb Q(\alpha)\). Therefore
\[
[L:\mathbb Q]=6.
\]
A separable irreducible cubic has Galois group acting transitively on its three roots, hence as a transitive subgroup of \(S_3\). Since the group has order \(6\), it is all of \(S_3\).
:::

<1>2. Over \(\mathbb R\), the splitting field is \(\mathbb C\), and
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb C/\mathbb R)\cong C_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
The real root \(\alpha=\sqrt[3]{7}\) already lies in \(\mathbb R\). The other two roots are \(\alpha\zeta_3\) and \(\alpha\zeta_3^2\), which are nonreal. Thus adjoining either one gives \(\mathbb C\), so the splitting field is \(\mathbb C\). Its Galois group over \(\mathbb R\) is generated by complex conjugation and has order \(2\).
:::

<1>3. Over \(\mathbb F_{13}\), the polynomial \(x^3-7\) is irreducible.
::: {.proof}
The nonzero cubes in \(\mathbb F_{13}\) are
\[
1,\ 5,\ 8,\ 12.
\]
Thus \(7\) is not a cube in \(\mathbb F_{13}\), so \(x^3-7\) has no root. A cubic over a field is reducible if and only if it has a root, hence \(x^3-7\) is irreducible.
:::

<1>4. The splitting field over \(\mathbb F_{13}\) is \(\mathbb F_{13^3}\), and its Galois group is cyclic of order \(3\).
::: {.proof}
Since \(3\mid 12=|\mathbb F_{13}^\times|\), the field \(\mathbb F_{13}\) already contains the three cube roots of unity; explicitly \(1,3,9\) satisfy \(u^3=1\). If \(\beta\) is one root of \(x^3-7\), the other roots are \(3\beta\) and \(9\beta\), so adjoining one root gives the full splitting field.

By <1>3, \([\mathbb F_{13}(\beta):\mathbb F_{13}]=3\), hence the splitting field is the unique field \(\mathbb F_{13^3}\). Every finite-field extension is Galois with cyclic Galois group generated by Frobenius \(z\mapsto z^{13}\), so
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_{13^3}/\mathbb F_{13})\cong C_3.
\]
:::
:::
