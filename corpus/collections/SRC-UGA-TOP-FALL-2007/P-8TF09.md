::: problem
Find all the connected $2$-sheeted covering spaces of the wedge product of two circles $S^1\vee S^1$.
Also, find all the index $2$ subgroups of the free group on two generators, and explain the relationship between these problems.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
X=S^1\vee S^1,
\qquad
\pi_1(X,x_0)=F(a,b),
\]
where $a$ and $b$ are the two oriented circle generators.

<1>1. A connected two-sheeted covering of $X$ is determined, up to equivalence over $X$, by a transitive monodromy homomorphism
\[
\rho:F(a,b)\longrightarrow S_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Choose labels $0,1$ for the two points in the fiber over $x_0$.
Lifting loops at $x_0$ permutes this fiber, giving the monodromy homomorphism $\rho$.
The covering is connected exactly when the monodromy action on the fiber is transitive.
Conversely, a permutation action of $F(a,b)$ on two points constructs the corresponding covering graph by lifting the two loop edges according to the permutations $\rho(a)$ and $\rho(b)$.
Changing the labeling of the fiber conjugates $\rho$ in $S_2$, which is exactly the equivalence ambiguity for the covering.
:::

<1>2. There are exactly three transitive monodromy homomorphisms $F(a,b)\to S_2$ up to conjugacy.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
S_2=\{1,\tau\},
\qquad
\tau=(01).
\]
Because $F(a,b)$ is free, a homomorphism is determined arbitrarily by the pair
\[
(\rho(a),\rho(b))\in S_2^2.
\]
The action on two points is transitive exactly when the image contains $\tau$, so the pair cannot be $(1,1)$.
Thus the three possibilities are
\[
(\tau,1),
\qquad
(1,\tau),
\qquad
(\tau,\tau).
\]
Since $S_2$ is abelian, conjugation does not identify any of these three homomorphisms.
:::

<1>3. The three connected double covers can be described as follows, with vertices $v_0,v_1$ lying over the wedge point.
::: {.proof}
For each base loop labeled $a$ or $b$, there is one outgoing lifted edge of that label at each $v_i$, and its terminal vertex is obtained by applying the corresponding monodromy permutation.

For $(\rho(a),\rho(b))=(\tau,1)$, the two lifted $a$-edges join $v_0$ to $v_1$, while there is a lifted $b$-loop at each vertex.

For $(\rho(a),\rho(b))=(1,\tau)$, there is a lifted $a$-loop at each vertex, while the two lifted $b$-edges join $v_0$ to $v_1$.

For $(\rho(a),\rho(b))=(\tau,\tau)$, both lifted $a$-edges and both lifted $b$-edges join $v_0$ to $v_1$.

Each graph is connected because at least one generator interchanges the two vertices, and <1>2 shows that there are no other connected two-sheeted covers.
:::

<1>4. Every index-$2$ subgroup $H\le F(a,b)$ is the kernel of a surjective homomorphism
\[
F(a,b)\longrightarrow\ZZ/2\ZZ.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every subgroup of index $2$ is normal: left multiplication on the two cosets gives a nontrivial homomorphism
\[
F(a,b)\longrightarrow S_2\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ
\]
whose kernel is $H$.
Equivalently,
\[
F(a,b)/H\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ.
\]
Thus $H$ is the kernel of the quotient map.
:::

<1>5. Hence $F(a,b)$ has exactly three index-$2$ subgroups:
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_a&=\ker\epsilon_a,
&\epsilon_a(a)&=1,&\epsilon_a(b)&=0,\\
H_b&=\ker\epsilon_b,
&\epsilon_b(a)&=0,&\epsilon_b(b)&=1,\\
H_{a+b}&=\ker\epsilon_{a+b},
&\epsilon_{a+b}(a)&=1,&\epsilon_{a+b}(b)&=1,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the values lie in $\ZZ/2\ZZ$.
::: {.proof}
A homomorphism from the free group $F(a,b)$ to $\ZZ/2\ZZ$ is determined by the images of $a$ and $b$.
There are four choices, and surjectivity excludes only the choice $(0,0)$.
By <1>4 every index-$2$ subgroup occurs as the kernel of one of the remaining three maps.
The three kernels are distinct because, for example,
\[
b\in H_a\setminus H_b,
\qquad
a\in H_b\setminus H_a,
\]
and
\[
a,b\notin H_{a+b}.
\]
:::

<1>6. Explicit free bases for these three subgroups are
\[
H_a=\langle b,a^2,aba^{-1}\rangle,
\]
\[
H_b=\langle a,b^2,bab^{-1}\rangle,
\]
and
\[
H_{a+b}=\langle ba^{-1},a^2,ab\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
Each subgroup has index $2$, hence by the Schreier index formula has free rank
\[
1+2(2-1)=3.
\]
For $H_a$, use the Schreier transversal $\{1,a\}$.
The nontrivial Schreier generators are
\[
b,
\qquad
a^2,
\qquad
aba^{-1}.
\]
For $H_b$, the symmetric computation with transversal $\{1,b\}$ gives
\[
a,
\qquad
b^2,
\qquad
bab^{-1}.
\]
For $H_{a+b}$, using $\{1,a\}$ gives
\[
ba^{-1},
\qquad
a^2,
\qquad
ab.
\]
Schreier's theorem says that these are free bases of the respective kernels.
:::

<1>7. Under the classification of connected covering spaces, the three covers in <1>3 correspond exactly to the three subgroups in <1>5.
::: {.proof}
For a connected cover with chosen point $\widetilde x_0$ over $x_0$, the associated subgroup is
\[
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0),
\]
equivalently the subgroup of $F(a,b)$ whose monodromy fixes the chosen sheet.
For a transitive action on two points, the image is all of $S_2$, whose point stabilizer is trivial.
Thus this subgroup is precisely the kernel of the monodromy homomorphism.

Consequently the monodromies
\[
(\tau,1),
\qquad
(1,\tau),
\qquad
(\tau,\tau)
\]
correspond respectively to
\[
H_a,
\qquad
H_b,
\qquad
H_{a+b}.
\]
Since index-$2$ subgroups are normal, each is its own conjugacy class, so this is also the unbased covering-space classification.
:::
