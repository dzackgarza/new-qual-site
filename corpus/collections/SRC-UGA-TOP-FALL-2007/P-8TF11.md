::: problem
Let $X$ be a compact orientable surface (without boundary) and let $f:X\to X$ be a continuous map.
Assume that $f$ is homotopic to the identity map but that $f$ has no fixed points.
What is the genus of $X$?
Prove your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $X$ has genus $g$, then
\[
H_k(X;\QQ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\QQ,&k=0,2,\\
\QQ^{2g},&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
A closed connected orientable surface of genus $g$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, $2g$ $1$-cells, and one $2$-cell, with both cellular differentials zero.
Thus its rational homology has the displayed form.
:::

<1>2. Since $f$ is homotopic to the identity, its Lefschetz number is
\[
L(f)=2-2g.
\]
::: {.proof}
Homotopic maps induce the same maps on homology, so
\[
f_*=\operatorname{id}
\]
on every $H_k(X;\QQ)$.
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
L(f)
&=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(f_*:H_k(X;\QQ)\to H_k(X;\QQ)\bigr)\\
&=1-2g+1\\
&=2-2g.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently, $L(f)=L(\operatorname{id}_X)=\chi(X)$.
:::

<1>3. The assumption that $f$ has no fixed points forces
\[
L(f)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem states that a continuous self-map of a compact manifold with nonzero Lefschetz number has a fixed point.
Taking the contrapositive, a fixed-point-free self-map must have Lefschetz number zero.
:::

<1>4. Hence
\[
g=1.
\]
::: {.proof}
Combining <1>2 and <1>3 gives
\[
2-2g=0,
\]
so $g=1$.
Thus $X$ is a torus.
:::

<1>5. The genus-$1$ conclusion is sharp: the torus admits fixed-point-free self-maps homotopic to the identity.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
T^2=\RR^2/\ZZ^2
\]
and choose
\[
v=(1/2,0).
\]
The translation
\[
\tau_v([x])=[x+v]
\]
has no fixed point, since a fixed point would imply $v\in\ZZ^2$.
The map
\[
H:T^2\times[0,1]\longrightarrow T^2,
\qquad
H([x],t)=[x+tv],
\]
is a homotopy from the identity to $\tau_v$.
Hence genus $1$ actually occurs under the hypotheses.
:::
:::
