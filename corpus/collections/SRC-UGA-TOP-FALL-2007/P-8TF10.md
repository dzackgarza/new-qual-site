::: problem
a. State van Kampen's theorem.

b. Calculate the fundamental group of the space obtained by taking two copies of the torus $T=S^1\times S^1$ and gluing them together along a circle $S^1\times\{p\}$, where $p$ is a point in $S^1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A based form of van Kampen's theorem is the following.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
X=U\cup V,
\]
where $U,V$, and $U\cap V$ are open and path connected, and choose a basepoint
\[
x_0\in U\cap V.
\]
Then the inclusion maps induce a surjection
\[
\pi_1(U,x_0)*\pi_1(V,x_0)
\longrightarrow
\pi_1(X,x_0),
\]
whose kernel is the normal closure of
\[
(i_U)_*(\gamma)(i_V)_*(\gamma)^{-1}
\qquad
(\gamma\in\pi_1(U\cap V,x_0)).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)
\cong
\pi_1(U,x_0)*_{\pi_1(U\cap V,x_0)}\pi_1(V,x_0).
\]
This is van Kampen's theorem in the form needed here.
:::

<1>2. Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ be the two tori and let $C$ be the circle along which they are identified.
There is a van Kampen cover with
\[
U\simeq T_1,
\qquad
V\simeq T_2,
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq C\simeq S^1.
\]
::: {.proof}
The images of $T_1$ and $T_2$ in the glued space are closed subspaces, so enlarge each slightly across a collar of the common circle.
The resulting sets $U$ and $V$ are open and path connected.
They deformation retract onto $T_1$ and $T_2$, while their intersection deformation retracts onto the common circle $C$.
Thus <1>1 applies with the displayed homotopy types.
:::

<1>3. Choose generators so that
\[
\pi_1(T_1)=\langle a,b\mid[a,b]=1\rangle,
\qquad
\pi_1(T_2)=\langle c,d\mid[c,d]=1\rangle,
\]
and the generator $z$ of $\pi_1(C)\cong\ZZ$ maps to $a$ in the first torus and to $c$ in the second.
::: {.proof}
In each torus $S^1\times S^1$, take the first-factor loop as $a$ or $c$ and the second-factor loop as $b$ or $d$.
The gluing circle is $S^1\times\{p\}$ in each copy, so its fundamental-group generator is exactly the first-factor generator in both tori.
:::

<1>4. Van Kampen gives
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b,c,d\mid[a,b]=1,\ [c,d]=1,\ a=c\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The free product of the two torus groups has presentation
\[
\langle a,b,c,d\mid[a,b]=1,\ [c,d]=1\rangle.
\]
By <1>1 and <1>3, amalgamating over $\pi_1(C)=\langle z\rangle$ imposes precisely the relation
\[
a=c.
\]
No other cross-relation is introduced.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b,d\mid[a,b]=1,\ [a,d]=1\rangle
\cong
\ZZ\times F(b,d).
\]
::: {.proof}
Eliminate $c$ from the presentation in <1>4 using $c=a$.
The remaining relations say exactly that $a$ commutes with both free generators $b$ and $d$, while there is no relation between $b$ and $d$.
But a presentation of the direct product of the infinite cyclic group $\langle a\rangle$ with the free group $F(b,d)$ is precisely
\[
\langle a,b,d\mid[a,b]=1,\ [a,d]=1\rangle.
\]
Hence
\[
\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ\times F_2.
\]
:::
:::
