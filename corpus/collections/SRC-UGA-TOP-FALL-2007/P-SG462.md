::: problem
Show that a compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $A$ be a compact subset of a Hausdorff space $X$.
To prove that $A$ is closed, it suffices to prove that $X\setminus A$ is open.
::: {.proof}
A subset of a topological space is closed exactly when its complement is open.
:::

<1>2. Fix $x\in X\setminus A$.
For every $a\in A$, choose disjoint open sets
\[
U_a\ni a,
\qquad
V_a\ni x.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $x\notin A$, one has $x\ne a$ for every $a\in A$.
Because $X$ is Hausdorff, the distinct points $a$ and $x$ admit disjoint open neighborhoods $U_a$ and $V_a$.
:::

<1>3. There are finitely many points $a_1,\dots,a_m\in A$ such that
\[
A\subseteq U_{a_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{a_m}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The family
\[
\{U_a:a\in A\}
\]
is an open cover of the compact set $A$.
Compactness therefore gives a finite subcover.
:::

<1>4. The set
\[
V=V_{a_1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{a_m}
\]
is an open neighborhood of $x$ disjoint from $A$.
::: {.proof}
It is open as a finite intersection of open sets, and it contains $x$ because every $V_{a_i}$ contains $x$.

Suppose $y\in V\cap A$.
By <1>3, $y\in U_{a_i}$ for some $i$.
But $y\in V\subseteq V_{a_i}$ as well, contradicting
\[
U_{a_i}\cap V_{a_i}=\varnothing.
\]
Thus $V\cap A=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore $A$ is closed in $X$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, every point $x\in X\setminus A$ has an open neighborhood contained in $X\setminus A$.
Hence $X\setminus A$ is open.
By <1>1, $A$ is closed.
:::
:::
