::: {.problem}
Describe a cell complex structure on the torus $T = S^1 \times S^1$ and use this to compute the homology groups of $T$.

> To justify your answer you will need to consider the attaching maps in detail.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cw-structure}
The torus has a CW structure with one $0$-cell $e^0$, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell $e^2$.

::: pf-proof
Represent
\[
T=S^1\times S^1
\]
as the quotient of the square $[0,1]^2$ obtained by identifying opposite vertical sides and opposite horizontal sides in the same direction.
All four vertices become the single $0$-cell $e^0$.
The two pairs of opposite sides become the two $1$-cells $a$ and $b$, and the interior of the square becomes the single open $2$-cell $e^2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #attaching-word}
The $1$-skeleton is
\[
T^{(1)}=a\vee b\cong S^1\vee S^1,
\]
and the attaching map of $e^2$ represents the loop
\[
aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]

::: pf-proof
Traverse the boundary of the square once counterclockwise from a chosen vertex.
The four sides project successively to
\[
a,
\qquad
b,
\qquad
a^{-1},
\qquad
b^{-1}.
\]
After the four vertices are identified, the attaching map
\[
\varphi:S^1=\partial D^2\longrightarrow T^{(1)}
\]
therefore has word $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #chain-complex-shape}
The cellular chain complex has the form
\[
0
\longrightarrow \ZZ
\xrightarrow{d_2}
\ZZ^2
\xrightarrow{d_1}
\ZZ
\longrightarrow0.
\]

::: pf-proof
By step [](#cw-structure){.pf-ref}, the cellular chain groups are free on one $2$-cell, two $1$-cells, and one $0$-cell, with no cells in any other dimension.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #d1-zero}
The first cellular differential is zero:
\[
d_1=0.
\]

::: pf-proof
Each $1$-cell has both endpoints attached to the single $0$-cell.
After orienting either $1$-cell, its cellular boundary is therefore
\[
e^0-e^0=0.
\]
Hence $d_1$ vanishes on both generators $a$ and $b$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coeff-a-zero}
The coefficient of $a$ in $d_2(e^2)$ is zero.

::: pf-proof
By the cellular boundary formula, this coefficient is the degree of the composite
\[
S^1
\xrightarrow{\varphi}
a\vee b
\longrightarrow
(a\vee b)/b
\cong a\cong S^1,
\]
where the second map collapses the $b$-circle to the wedge point.
By step [](#attaching-word){.pf-ref}, after this collapse the attaching word becomes
\[
aa^{-1}.
\]
Thus the resulting map traverses $a$ once positively and once negatively, so its degree is
\[
1+(-1)=0.
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coeff-b-zero}
The coefficient of $b$ in $d_2(e^2)$ is also zero.

::: pf-proof
Collapse the $a$-circle instead.
The cellular boundary coefficient of $b$ is the degree of
\[
S^1
\xrightarrow{\varphi}
a\vee b
\longrightarrow
(a\vee b)/a
\cong b\cong S^1.
\]
The attaching word reduces to
\[
bb^{-1},
\]
whose degree is again $1-1=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #d2-zero}
Therefore
\[
d_2=0.
\]

::: pf-proof
The target $C_1(T)$ is freely generated by $a$ and $b$.
Steps [](#coeff-a-zero){.pf-ref} and [](#coeff-b-zero){.pf-ref} show that both coordinates of $d_2(e^2)$ in this basis are zero.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The integral homology groups of the torus are
\[
H_k(T;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,2,\\
\ZZ^2,&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

::: pf-proof
By step [](#chain-complex-shape){.pf-ref}, step [](#d1-zero){.pf-ref}, and step [](#d2-zero){.pf-ref}, the cellular chain complex is
\[
0\longrightarrow\ZZ\xrightarrow{0}\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{0}\ZZ\longrightarrow0.
\]
Taking kernels modulo images gives
\[
H_2\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_1\cong\ZZ^2,
\qquad
H_0\cong\ZZ,
\]
and all other groups vanish because the corresponding cellular chain groups vanish.
:::
