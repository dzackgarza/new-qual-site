::: {.problem}
Let $D_5 = \langle r, s : s^2 = r^5 = 1,\ srs = r^{-1} \rangle$ denote the group of symmetries of a regular pentagon.
Find the character table of $D_5$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: classification of the classes, the abelianisation for the linear characters, the degree count, and two explicit two-dimensional representations. No retained set solves this exercise, and the sets searched are recorded in the audit trail.

Let $D_{5}=\langle r,s\mid s^{2}=r^{5}=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle$, of order $10$, with elements $r^{j}$ and $sr^{j}$, $0\le j\le4$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #classes}
The conjugacy classes are $\{1\}$, $\{r,r^{4}\}$, $\{r^{2},r^{3}\}$ and $\{s,sr,sr^{2},sr^{3},sr^{4}\}$.

::: pf-proof
The rotations commute with each other, so conjugating $r^{j}$ by $r^{k}$ fixes it, while $sr^{j}s=r^{-j}$, so the class of $r^{j}$ is $\{r^{j},r^{-j}\}$; for $j=1,2$ these give the two classes $\{r,r^{4}\}$ and $\{r^{2},r^{3}\}$, and $r^{0}=1$ is alone since $D_{5}$ has trivial centre ($srs^{-1}=r^{-1}\ne r$). For the reflections, $r^{k}(sr^{j})r^{-k}=sr^{j-2k}$ and $sr^{j}s=sr^{j}$, so conjugating $sr^{j}$ by rotations moves $j$ by multiples of $2$ modulo $5$, and since $\gcd(2,5)=1$ every residue is reached; the five reflections form one class.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #four}
$D_{5}$ has exactly four irreducible complex representations.

::: pf-proof
The number of inequivalent irreducible complex representations of a finite group equals the number of conjugacy classes, which is $4$ by step [](#classes){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #linear}
There are exactly two linear characters, $\mathbf 1$ and $\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon(r)=1$, $\varepsilon(s)=-1$.

::: pf-proof
A linear character of $D_{5}$ is a homomorphism to $\CC^{\times}$, that is a character of the abelianisation. In an abelian quotient the relation $srs=r^{-1}$ reads $r=r^{-1}$, so $r^{2}=1$; as $r^{5}=1$ and $\gcd(2,5)=1$, this forces $r=1$. Hence the abelianisation is generated by the image of $s$, which has order $2$, so it has order at most $2$; and it has order exactly $2$ because $\varepsilon(r)=1$, $\varepsilon(s)=-1$ defines a homomorphism — it respects $s^{2}=r^{5}=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$ since $(-1)\cdot1\cdot(-1)=1$ — which is not the trivial one. So there are exactly two linear characters, $\mathbf 1$ and $\varepsilon$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #degrees}
The two remaining irreducible degrees are both $2$.

::: pf-proof
The sum of the squares of the dimensions of the inequivalent irreducibles equals the group order, so with two linear characters of dimension $1$ and two remaining dimensions $d_{1},d_{2}$,
$$
10=1^{2}+1^{2}+d_{1}^{2}+d_{2}^{2},\qquad\text{that is}\quad d_{1}^{2}+d_{2}^{2}=8,
$$
whose positive integer solutions are $d_{1}=d_{2}=2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rho}
With $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$ and $k=1,2$, the assignments
$$
\rho_{k}(r)=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta^{k}&0\\0&\zeta^{-k}\end{pmatrix},\qquad \rho_{k}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$
define representations of $D_{5}$, with $\chi_{k}(r^{j})=\zeta^{kj}+\zeta^{-kj}$ and $\chi_{k}(sr^{j})=0$.

::: pf-proof
$\rho_{k}(s)^{2}=I$ and $\rho_{k}(r)^{5}=I$ since $\zeta^{5k}=1$. And $\rho_{k}(s)\rho_{k}(r)\rho_{k}(s)$ is obtained by interchanging the diagonal entries of $\rho_{k}(r)$, that is $\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{-k},\zeta^{k})=\rho_{k}(r)^{-1}$, which is the relation $srs=r^{-1}$. So the assignments respect a presentation of $D_{5}$ and extend to a homomorphism. The characters follow: $\rho_{k}(r^{j})=\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{kj},\zeta^{-kj})$ has trace $\zeta^{kj}+\zeta^{-kj}$, and $\rho_{k}(s)\rho_{k}(r^{j})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\zeta^{-kj}\\ \zeta^{kj}&0\end{pmatrix}$ has trace $0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #irreducible}
$\rho_{1}$ and $\rho_{2}$ are irreducible and inequivalent.

::: pf-proof
For $k=1,2$ the eigenvalues $\zeta^{k}$ and $\zeta^{-k}$ of $\rho_{k}(r)$ are distinct, so its eigenspaces are the two coordinate lines and they are the only $\rho_{k}(r)$-stable lines. A one-dimensional invariant subspace of $\rho_{k}$ would be such a line, and $\rho_{k}(s)$ interchanges the two coordinate lines, so no one-dimensional subspace is invariant and $\rho_{k}$ is irreducible. They are inequivalent because $\chi_{1}(r)=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=2\cos(2\pi/5)$ and $\chi_{2}(r)=\zeta^{2}+\zeta^{-2}=2\cos(4\pi/5)$ are different real numbers.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #table}
The character table of $D_{5}$ is
$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 & C_{1} & C_{2} & C_{3} & C_{4}\\ \hline
\mathbf 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1\\
\varepsilon & 1 & 1 & 1 & -1\\
\chi_{1} & 2 & a & b & 0\\
\chi_{2} & 2 & b & a & 0
\end{array}
$$
with class sizes $1,2,2,5$ and $a=2\cos\frac{2\pi}{5}=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$, $b=2\cos\frac{4\pi}{5}=-\frac{\sqrt5+1}{2}$.

::: pf-proof
The four rows are the four irreducibles of steps [](#linear){.pf-ref} and [](#irreducible){.pf-ref}, and they are all of them by step [](#four){.pf-ref} together with the degree count of step [](#degrees){.pf-ref}. The entries on $C_{2}=\{r,r^{4}\}$ and $C_{3}=\{r^{2},r^{3}\}$ are the values $\chi_{k}(r^{j})$ of step [](#rho){.pf-ref}, unchanged on the inverse because $\chi$ is a class function; the reflections give $0$; and $\varepsilon$ takes the value $1$ on rotations and $-1$ on reflections. The class sizes are those of step [](#classes){.pf-ref}. As a check, the columns are orthogonal: for $C_{2}$ one gets $1+1+a^{2}+b^{2}=2+3=5$, the order of the centraliser of $r$, using $a+b=-1$ and $ab=-1$, which follow from $a=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}$ and $b=-\frac{\sqrt5+1}{2}$.
:::
