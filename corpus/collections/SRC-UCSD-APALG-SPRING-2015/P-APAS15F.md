::: problem
Consider the matrix $A \in \mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{C})$ given by
\[
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & 1 \\
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let $G$ be the order $3$ cyclic group generated by $A$.

(1) Use Molien’s Theorem to find the Hilbert series of the invariant ring $\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^G$.

(2) Find two finite subgroups $H, K \leq \mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{C})$ such that $H$ and $K$ are isomorphic as abstract groups but the invariant rings $\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^H$ and $\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^K$ are not isomorphic as graded algebras.
(Hint: There is an example where $|H| = |K| = 2$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
**(1).**

<1>1. By Molien’s Theorem, the Hilbert series of the invariant ring $\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^G$ is given by:
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^G, t) = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \frac{1}{\det(I - t g)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Molien's Theorem for invariant rings of finite subgroups of $\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$.
:::

<1>2. Compute $\det(I - tg)$ for each of the 3 elements of $G = \{I, A, A^2\}$: <2>1. For $g = I$: $\det(I - t I) = (1 - t)^3$.
::: {.proof}
$I$ has eigenvalue $1$ with multiplicity $3$.
:::
<2>2. For $g = A$: the characteristic polynomial of $A$ is $\det(\lambda I - A) = \lambda^3 - 1$, so the eigenvalues of $A$ are the cube roots of unity $1, \omega, \omega^2$ (where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$).
::: {.proof}
expansion of the $3 \times 3$ cyclic permutation matrix determinant.
:::
<2>3. Thus $\det(I - t A) = (1 - t)(1 - \omega t)(1 - \omega^2 t) = 1 - t^3$.
::: {.proof}
$(1 - t)(1 - \omega t)(1 - \omega^2 t) = 1 - t^3$.
:::
<2>4. For $g = A^2$: the eigenvalues of $A^2$ are $1, \omega^2, \omega$, so $\det(I - t A^2) = (1 - t)(1 - \omega^2 t)(1 - \omega t) = 1 - t^3$.
::: {.proof}
eigenvalues of powers of diagonalizable matrices.
:::

<1>3. Combine the terms in Molien's formula: <2>1. $\operatorname{Hilb}(\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^G, t) = \frac{1}{3} \left[ \frac{1}{(1-t)^3} + \frac{2}{1-t^3} \right]$.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2. <2>2. Express over a common denominator:
:::
\[
\frac{1}{(1-t)^3} + \frac{2}{(1-t)(1+t+t^2)} = \frac{(1+t+t^2) + 2(1-t)^2}{(1-t)^3(1+t+t^2)} = \frac{1+t+t^2 + 2(1 - 2t + t^2)}{(1-t)^2(1-t^3)} = \frac{3(1 - t + t^2)}{(1-t)^2(1-t^3)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
algebraic simplification.
:::
<2>3. Dividing by $3$:
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^G, t) = \frac{1 - t + t^2}{(1-t)^2(1-t^3)} = \frac{1 + t^3}{(1-t)(1-t^2)(1-t^3)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
$(1-t+t^2)(1+t) = 1+t^3$ and $(1-t)(1+t) = 1-t^2$.
:::

**(2).**

<1>4. Construct two subgroups $H, K \leq \operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{C})$ of order 2: <2>1. Let $H = \{I, -I\} = \langle -I \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.
::: {.proof}
$(-I)^2 = I$.
:::
<2>2. Let $K = \left\{I, \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\right\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.
::: {.proof}
reflection matrix has order 2. <2>3. Both $H$ and $K$ are isomorphic to the cyclic group of order 2 as abstract groups.
:::
::: {.proof}
both are generated by an element of order 2.
:::

<1>5. Show that the invariant rings are not isomorphic as graded algebras: <2>1. For $H$: the generator $-I$ acts by $(x, y, z) \mapsto (-x, -y, -z)$.
A homogeneous polynomial $P$ of degree $d$ satisfies $(-I) \cdot P = (-1)^d P$.
::: {.proof}
homogeneous polynomials of degree $d$ are scaled by $(-1)^d$ under $-I$.
:::
<2>2. Thus $(\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^H)_d = 0$ for all odd $d$.
In particular, $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} (\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^H)_1 = 0$.
::: {.proof}
no non-zero odd degree polynomial is invariant under $-I$.
:::
<2>3. For $K$: the generator acts by $(x, y, z) \mapsto (-x, y, z)$.
The polynomials $y$ and $z$ are invariant of degree 1.
::: {.proof}
$M \cdot y = y$ and $M \cdot z = z$.
:::
<2>4. Thus $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} (\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^K)_1 = 2 \neq 0$.
::: {.proof}
$\{y, z\}$ is a linearly independent set of invariants of degree 1. <2>5. Since graded algebra isomorphisms preserve the dimensions of graded components, $\mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^H \not\cong \mathbb{C}[x, y, z]^K$ as graded algebras.
:::
::: {.proof}
$\dim (\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]^H)_1 = 0 \neq 2 = \dim (\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]^K)_1$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 (1) and <1>5 (2).
:::
