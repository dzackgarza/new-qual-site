::: {.problem}
(a) Prove directly from the definition of functional limit that
$$
\lim_{x\to2}x^3=8.
$$

(b) Prove directly from the definition of continuity that
$$
f(x)=\frac{x^2+2x-5}{x-2}
$$
is continuous at $x=1$.

(c) Using the sequential characterization of functional limits, prove that
$$
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x}{|x|}
$$
does not exist.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If
$$
0<|x-2|<1,
$$
then
$$
|x^2+2x+4|<19.
$$


::: pf-proof

The condition $|x-2|<1$ gives
$$
1<x<3.
$$
Hence
$$
|x^2+2x+4|
\leq
x^2+2x+4
<
9+6+4
=19.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\boxed{\lim_{x\to2}x^3=8}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$, and choose
$$
\delta
=
\min\left\{
1,\frac{\varepsilon}{19}
\right\}.
$$
If
$$
0<|x-2|<\delta,
$$
then by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
|x^3-8|
&=
|x-2|\,|x^2+2x+4|\\
&<
19|x-2|\\
&<
19\delta\\
&\leq
\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the definition of the limit.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For
$$
f(x)=\frac{x^2+2x-5}{x-2},
$$
one has
$$
f(1)=2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Direct substitution gives
$$
f(1)
=
\frac{1+2-5}{1-2}
=
\frac{-2}{-1}
=2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If
$$
|x-1|<\frac12,
$$
then
$$
|f(x)-2|
\leq
5|x-1|.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $x\neq2$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)-2
&=
\frac{x^2+2x-5-2(x-2)}{x-2}\\
&=
\frac{x^2-1}{x-2}\\
&=
\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If $|x-1|<1/2$, then
$$
\frac12<x<\frac32,
$$
so
$$
|x+1|<\frac52
$$
and
$$
|x-2|>\frac12.
$$
Therefore
$$
|f(x)-2|
<
|x-1|\frac{5/2}{1/2}
=
5|x-1|.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The function $f$ is continuous at $x=1$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
$$
\delta
=
\min\left\{
\frac12,\frac{\varepsilon}{5}
\right\}.
$$
If
$$
|x-1|<\delta,
$$
then by step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
|f(x)-f(1)|
=
|f(x)-2|
\leq
5|x-1|
<
5\delta
\leq
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $f$ is continuous at $1$ by definition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The sequence
$$
x_n=\frac1n
$$
converges to $0$, and
$$
\frac{x_n}{|x_n|}=1
$$
for every $n$.


::: pf-proof

Since $1/n>0$,
$$
\frac{1/n}{|1/n|}=1.
$$
Also $1/n\to0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The sequence
$$
y_n=-\frac1n
$$
converges to $0$, and
$$
\frac{y_n}{|y_n|}=-1
$$
for every $n$.


::: pf-proof

Since $-1/n<0$,
$$
\frac{-1/n}{|-1/n|}=-1.
$$
Also $-1/n\to0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The limit
$$
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x}{|x|}
$$
does not exist.


::: pf-proof

If the functional limit existed and equaled $L$, the sequential
characterization of limits would force
$$
\frac{x_n}{|x_n|}
\longrightarrow L
$$
and
$$
\frac{y_n}{|y_n|}
\longrightarrow L
$$
for every two sequences $x_n,y_n\to0$ with nonzero terms.
But steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} give limits $1$ and $-1$, respectively. Since these
are different, no such $L$ exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Part (c) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::
