::: {.problem}
(a) Prove directly from the definition of functional limit that
\[
\lim_{x\to2}x^3=8.
\]

(b) Prove directly from the definition of continuity that
\[
f(x)=\frac{x^2+2x-5}{x-2}
\]
is continuous at $x=1$.

(c) Using the sequential characterization of functional limits, prove that
\[
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x}{|x|}
\]
does not exist.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If
$$
0<|x-2|<1,
$$
then
$$
|x^2+2x+4|<19.
$$

::: {.proof}
The condition $|x-2|<1$ gives
$$
1<x<3.
$$
Hence
$$
|x^2+2x+4|
\leq
x^2+2x+4
<
9+6+4
=19.
$$
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
\boxed{\lim_{x\to2}x^3=8}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$, and choose
$$
\delta
=
\min\left\{
1,\frac{\varepsilon}{19}
\right\}.
$$
If
$$
0<|x-2|<\delta,
$$
then by step <1>1,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|x^3-8|
&=
|x-2|\,|x^2+2x+4|\\
&<
19|x-2|\\
&<
19\delta\\
&\leq
\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the definition of the limit.
:::

<1>3. Part (a) follows.

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>2.
:::

<1>4. For
$$
f(x)=\frac{x^2+2x-5}{x-2},
$$
one has
$$
f(1)=2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Direct substitution gives
$$
f(1)
=
\frac{1+2-5}{1-2}
=
\frac{-2}{-1}
=2.
$$
:::

<1>5. If
$$
|x-1|<\frac12,
$$
then
$$
|f(x)-2|
\leq
5|x-1|.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $x\neq2$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)-2
&=
\frac{x^2+2x-5-2(x-2)}{x-2}\\
&=
\frac{x^2-1}{x-2}\\
&=
\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If $|x-1|<1/2$, then
$$
\frac12<x<\frac32,
$$
so
$$
|x+1|<\frac52
$$
and
$$
|x-2|>\frac12.
$$
Therefore
$$
|f(x)-2|
<
|x-1|\frac{5/2}{1/2}
=
5|x-1|.
$$
:::

<1>6. The function $f$ is continuous at $x=1$.

::: {.proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
$$
\delta
=
\min\left\{
\frac12,\frac{\varepsilon}{5}
\right\}.
$$
If
$$
|x-1|<\delta,
$$
then by step <1>5,
$$
|f(x)-f(1)|
=
|f(x)-2|
\leq
5|x-1|
<
5\delta
\leq
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $f$ is continuous at $1$ by definition.
:::

<1>7. Part (b) follows.

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>6.
:::

<1>8. The sequence
$$
x_n=\frac1n
$$
converges to $0$, and
$$
\frac{x_n}{|x_n|}=1
$$
for every $n$.

::: {.proof}
Since $1/n>0$,
$$
\frac{1/n}{|1/n|}=1.
$$
Also $1/n\to0$.
:::

<1>9. The sequence
$$
y_n=-\frac1n
$$
converges to $0$, and
$$
\frac{y_n}{|y_n|}=-1
$$
for every $n$.

::: {.proof}
Since $-1/n<0$,
$$
\frac{-1/n}{|-1/n|}=-1.
$$
Also $-1/n\to0$.
:::

<1>10. The limit
$$
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x}{|x|}
$$
does not exist.

::: {.proof}
If the functional limit existed and equaled $L$, the sequential
characterization of limits would force
$$
\frac{x_n}{|x_n|}
\longrightarrow L
$$
and
$$
\frac{y_n}{|y_n|}
\longrightarrow L
$$
for every two sequences $x_n,y_n\to0$ with nonzero terms.
But steps <1>8 and <1>9 give limits $1$ and $-1$, respectively. Since these
are different, no such $L$ exists.
:::

<1>11. Part (c) follows.

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>10.
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3, <1>7, and <1>11 establish parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
:::
