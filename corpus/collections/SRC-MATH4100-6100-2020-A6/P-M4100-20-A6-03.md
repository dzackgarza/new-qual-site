::: {.problem}
(a) Let
\[
g(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&x\in\QQ,\\
0,&x\notin\QQ.
\end{cases}
\]
Prove that $g$ is discontinuous at every real number.

(b) Let
\[
h(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in\QQ,\\
0,&x\notin\QQ.
\end{cases}
\]
Prove that $h$ is continuous at $0$ and discontinuous at every $x\ne0$.

(c) Define Thomae's function by
\[
t(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&x=0,\\
1/n,&x=m/n\in\QQ\setminus\{0\}\text{ in lowest terms with }n>0,\\
0,&x\notin\QQ.
\end{cases}
\]
Prove that $t$ is continuous at every irrational point and has a removable discontinuity at every rational point.

Hint: show that
\[
\lim_{x\to c}t(x)=0
\]
for every $c\in\RR$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $c\in\RR$, there are sequences of rationals and irrationals
converging to $c$.


::: pf-proof

Both $\QQ$ and $\RR\setminus\QQ$ are dense in $\RR$. Hence for each
$n\in\NN$ one can choose
$$
q_n\in\QQ\cap\left(c-\frac1n,c+\frac1n\right)
$$
and
$$
r_n\in(\RR\setminus\QQ)
\cap
\left(c-\frac1n,c+\frac1n\right).
$$
Then
$$
q_n\to c,
\qquad
r_n\to c.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The Dirichlet function
$$
g(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&x\in\QQ,\\
0,&x\notin\QQ
\end{cases}
$$
is discontinuous at every $c\in\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $c$. Choose $q_n,r_n$ as in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Then
$$
g(q_n)=1
$$
for every $n$, while
$$
g(r_n)=0
$$
for every $n$. Thus the two image sequences have different limits.

If $g$ were continuous at $c$, the sequential criterion for continuity would
force both
$$
g(q_n)\to g(c)
$$
and
$$
g(r_n)\to g(c),
$$
which is impossible. Hence $g$ is discontinuous at $c$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

The point $c$ in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} was arbitrary.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every $x\in\RR$,
$$
|h(x)|\leq|x|.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $x\in\QQ$, then
$$
h(x)=x,
$$
so equality holds. If $x\notin\QQ$, then
$$
h(x)=0,
$$
so the inequality is immediate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The function $h$ is continuous at $0$.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
h(0)=0.
$$
Let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
$$
\delta=\varepsilon.
$$
If
$$
|x|<\delta,
$$
then by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
|h(x)-h(0)|
=
|h(x)|
\leq
|x|
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Hence $h$ is continuous at $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The function $h$ is discontinuous at every $c\neq0$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $c\neq0$, and choose rational and irrational sequences
$$
q_n\to c,
\qquad
r_n\to c
$$
as in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Then
$$
h(q_n)=q_n\to c,
$$
while
$$
h(r_n)=0\to0.
$$
Since $c\neq0$, these limits are different. Therefore the sequential
criterion for continuity fails at $c$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves continuity at $0$, and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves discontinuity at every
nonzero point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Fix $c\in\RR$ and $N\in\NN$. There are only finitely many rational
numbers
$$
\frac mn
$$
in lowest terms satisfying
$$
\left|\frac mn-c\right|\leq1
$$
and
$$
1\leq n\leq N.
$$


::: pf-proof

For each fixed denominator $n\leq N$, the condition
$$
c-1\leq\frac mn\leq c+1
$$
implies
$$
n(c-1)\leq m\leq n(c+1).
$$
Only finitely many integers $m$ satisfy this inequality. Since there are only
finitely many possible denominators $n=1,\ldots,N$, the total collection is
finite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For every $c\in\RR$,
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{x\to c}t(x)=0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $N\in\NN$ such that
$$
\frac1N<\varepsilon.
$$
Let $F$ be the finite set from step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}. Remove $c$ from $F$ if $c\in F$.
Since the remaining set
$$
F\setminus\{c\}
$$
is finite, there exists $\delta>0$ with
$$
0<\delta<1
$$
such that
$$
(c-\delta,c+\delta)
\cap
(F\setminus\{c\})
=
\varnothing.
$$

Now suppose
$$
0<|x-c|<\delta.
$$
If $x$ is irrational, then
$$
t(x)=0.
$$

If $x$ is rational, write
$$
x=\frac mn
$$
in lowest terms with $n>0$. Since $|x-c|<1$ but $x\notin F$, one must have
$$
n>N.
$$
Therefore
$$
0<t(x)
=
\frac1n
<
\frac1N
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus in all cases
$$
|t(x)-0|<\varepsilon
$$
whenever $0<|x-c|<\delta$, proving the limit.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Thomae's function is continuous at every irrational point.


::: pf-proof

If $c$ is irrational, then
$$
t(c)=0.
$$
By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
\lim_{x\to c}t(x)=0=t(c).
$$
Hence $t$ is continuous at $c$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Thomae's function has a removable discontinuity at every rational
point.


::: pf-proof

If $c=0$, then
$$
t(c)=1.
$$
If $c=m/n\neq0$ is rational in lowest terms, then
$$
t(c)=\frac1n>0.
$$
In either case,
$$
t(c)\neq0.
$$
But step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\lim_{x\to c}t(x)=0.
$$
Thus $t$ is discontinuous at $c$, while the finite limit exists. Redefining
$t(c)$ to be $0$ removes the discontinuity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Part (c) follows.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} give exactly the stated continuity and removable
discontinuity sets.

:::

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} establish parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
