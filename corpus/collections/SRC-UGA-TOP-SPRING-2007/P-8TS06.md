::: problem
Let $X$ be the quotient space of the disjoint union of two copies of the closed unit disk $D^2$ by the identification $x\sim h(x)$, where $h:S^1\to S^1$ is a homeomorphism of the unit circle (the boundary of the unit disk).
Prove that $X$ is homeomorphic to the unit sphere $S^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $D_1^2$ and $D_2^2$ for the two copies of the disk, and let $X_h$ denote their quotient under
\[
x\in\partial D_1^2
\sim
h(x)\in\partial D_2^2.
\]

<1>1. The boundary homeomorphism $h:S^1\to S^1$ extends to a homeomorphism
\[
H:D^2\to D^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Regard $D^2$ as
\[
\{rz:0\le r\le1,\ z\in S^1\}.
\]
Define
\[
H(0)=0,
\qquad
H(rz)=r h(z)
\quad(0<r\le1).
\]
This map is continuous away from the origin.
At the origin,
\[
\lVert H(rz)\rVert=r=\lVert rz\rVert,
\]
so it is continuous there as well.

Applying the same construction to $h^{-1}$ gives a continuous map $K:D^2\to D^2$ satisfying
\[
K(H(rz))=rz,
\qquad
H(K(rz))=rz.
\]
Thus $K=H^{-1}$ and $H$ is a homeomorphism extending $h$.
:::

<1>2. The space $X_h$ is homeomorphic to the quotient $X_{\mathrm{id}}$ obtained by gluing two disks by the identity map on their boundaries.
::: {.proof}
On the disjoint union $D_1^2\sqcup D_2^2$, define
\[
F|_{D_1^2}=\operatorname{id}_{D^2},
\qquad
F|_{D_2^2}=H^{-1}.
\]
If $x\in\partial D_1^2$, then in the identity-glued quotient the two points
\[
F(x)=x
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
F(h(x))=H^{-1}(h(x))=x
\]
are identified.
Hence $F$ is constant on the equivalence classes defining $X_h$ and induces a continuous map
\[
\overline F:X_h\to X_{\mathrm{id}}.
\]

Conversely, the map that is the identity on $D_1^2$ and $H$ on $D_2^2$ respects the identity boundary gluing and induces a continuous inverse to $\overline F$.
Therefore $\overline F$ is a homeomorphism.
:::

<1>3. The identity-glued double $X_{\mathrm{id}}$ is homeomorphic to $S^2$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
S^2=\{(u,t)\in\RR^2\times\RR:\lVert u\rVert^2+t^2=1\}.
\]
Map the first disk to the upper hemisphere and the second to the lower hemisphere by
\[
\phi_+(u)=\left(u,\sqrt{1-\lVert u\rVert^2}\right),
\qquad
\phi_-(u)=\left(u,-\sqrt{1-\lVert u\rVert^2}\right).
\]
Each map is a homeomorphism onto its hemisphere.
For $u\in S^1=\partial D^2$,
\[
\phi_+(u)=(u,0)=\phi_-(u),
\]
so the two maps descend to a continuous map
\[
\Phi:X_{\mathrm{id}}\to S^2.
\]
The upper and lower hemispheres cover $S^2$ and meet exactly along their common equator, so $\Phi$ is bijective.

The space $X_{\mathrm{id}}$ is compact because it is a quotient of the compact space $D_1^2\sqcup D_2^2$, while $S^2$ is Hausdorff.
Thus the continuous bijection $\Phi$ is a homeomorphism.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $X$ is homeomorphic to $S^2$.
::: {.proof}
The given space is $X=X_h$.
By <1>2 and <1>3,
\[
X_h\cong X_{\mathrm{id}}\cong S^2.
\]
:::
