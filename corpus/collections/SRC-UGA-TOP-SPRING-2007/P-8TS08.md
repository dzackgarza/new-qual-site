::: problem
(a) What is the definition of a regular (or Galois) covering space?

(b) Describe a non-regular $3$-sheeted covering space of the figure-eight (two circles identified at a point), and prove it is not regular.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the figure-eight as
\[
R=S^1_a\vee S^1_b
\]
with common vertex $v$.

<1>1. A connected covering
\[
p:\widetilde X\longrightarrow X
\]
is **regular** (or **Galois**, equivalently **normal**) if its deck transformation group acts transitively on each fiber: for any $x\in X$ and any
\[
\widetilde x_1,\widetilde x_2\in p^{-1}(x),
\]
there is a deck transformation $h$ with
\[
h(\widetilde x_1)=\widetilde x_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the deck-transformation definition of a regular covering.
For connected coverings under the usual covering-classification hypotheses, it is equivalent to normality of
\[
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)
\]
in $\pi_1(X,x_0)$.
:::

<1>2. Define a three-sheeted covering graph $p:\widetilde R\to R$ as follows.
Take vertices
\[
v_1,v_2,v_3
\]
over $v$, and prescribe the monodromy permutations
\[
\sigma_a=(12),
\qquad
\sigma_b=(123).
\]
For $x\in\{a,b\}$ and each $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, attach one oriented edge labeled $x$ from $v_i$ to $v_{\sigma_x(i)}$, and map it homeomorphically onto the $x$-edge of $R$.
::: {.proof}
At every $v_i$ there is exactly one outgoing and one incoming lift of each oriented base edge $a$ and $b$.
Hence a small star neighborhood of $v_i$ maps homeomorphically to a star neighborhood of $v$.
On edge interiors the map is already a homeomorphism.
Thus $p$ is a covering map.

The fiber over $v$ is precisely $\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}$, so the cover has three sheets.
:::

<1>3. The covering graph $\widetilde R$ is connected.
::: {.proof}
The lifted $b$-edges follow the cycle
\[
v_1\longrightarrow v_2\longrightarrow v_3\longrightarrow v_1.
\]
Thus the three vertices lie in one component.
Every edge has one of these vertices as an endpoint, so the whole graph is connected.
:::

<1>4. The $a$-loop lifts to a closed loop from $v_3$, but its lift from $v_1$ is not closed.
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
\sigma_a(3)=3,
\]
the lift of $a$ starting at $v_3$ ends at $v_3$.
But
\[
\sigma_a(1)=2,
\]
so the lift of $a$ starting at $v_1$ ends at $v_2\ne v_1$.
:::

<1>5. The covering $p:\widetilde R\to R$ is not regular.
::: {.proof}
Suppose it were regular.
By <1>1, some deck transformation $h$ would satisfy
\[
h(v_3)=v_1.
\]
Let $\widetilde a_3$ be the closed lift of $a$ based at $v_3$ from <1>4. Since $p\circ h=p$, the path $h\circ\widetilde a_3$ is a lift of $a$ starting at $v_1$.
Because $\widetilde a_3$ is closed, its image under $h$ is also closed.

This contradicts <1>4, which says that the unique lift of $a$ starting at $v_1$ ends at $v_2$.
Hence no deck transformation sends $v_3$ to $v_1$, the deck group is not transitive on the fiber, and the covering is not regular.
:::
