::: problem
Prove that the product of two compact topological spaces is compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be compact spaces, and let $\mathcal U$ be an arbitrary open cover of $X\times Y$.

<1>1. If either $X$ or $Y$ is empty, then $X\times Y$ is compact.
::: {.proof}
In this case $X\times Y=\varnothing$, and the empty space is compact.
Hence it remains to consider the case in which both factors are nonempty.
:::

<1>2. For each $x\in X$, there are an open neighborhood $V_x$ of $x$ and finitely many members
\[
U_{x,1},\ldots,U_{x,m_x}\in\mathcal U
\]
such that
\[
V_x\times Y
\subseteq
U_{x,1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{x,m_x}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Fix $x\in X$.
For every $y\in Y$, choose a member $U_{x,y}\in\mathcal U$ containing $(x,y)$.
Since the product topology has a basis of products of open sets, choose open sets $V_{x,y}\subseteq X$ and $W_{x,y}\subseteq Y$ such that
\[
x\in V_{x,y},
\qquad
y\in W_{x,y},
\qquad
V_{x,y}\times W_{x,y}\subseteq U_{x,y}.
\]

The sets $W_{x,y}$ cover the compact space $Y$.
Choose $y_1,\ldots,y_{m_x}\in Y$ with
\[
Y=W_{x,y_1}\cup\cdots\cup W_{x,y_{m_x}}.
\]
Set
\[
V_x=V_{x,y_1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{x,y_{m_x}}.
\]
Then $V_x$ is an open neighborhood of $x$.

If $(z,y)\in V_x\times Y$, choose $j$ with $y\in W_{x,y_j}$.
Since $z\in V_x\subseteq V_{x,y_j}$,
\[
(z,y)\in V_{x,y_j}\times W_{x,y_j}\subseteq U_{x,y_j}.
\]
Thus $V_x\times Y$ is covered by the finitely many sets $U_{x,y_1},\ldots,U_{x,y_{m_x}}$.
Rename them $U_{x,1},\ldots,U_{x,m_x}$.
:::

<1>3. Finitely many of the neighborhoods $V_x$ from <1>2 cover $X$.
::: {.proof}
The family
\[
\{V_x:x\in X\}
\]
is an open cover of $X$.
By compactness of $X$, choose $x_1,\ldots,x_r\in X$ such that
\[
X=V_{x_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{x_r}.
\]
:::

<1>4. The cover $\mathcal U$ has a finite subcover of $X\times Y$.
::: {.proof}
For each $i=1,\ldots,r$, <1>2 gives finitely many members of $\mathcal U$ covering $V_{x_i}\times Y$.
Taking the union of these finitely many finite families gives a finite subfamily of $\mathcal U$ covering
\[
\bigcup_{i=1}^r(V_{x_i}\times Y)
=
\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^rV_{x_i}\right)\times Y
=X\times Y.
\]
:::

<1>5. Therefore $X\times Y$ is compact.
::: {.proof}
The open cover $\mathcal U$ was arbitrary, and <1>4 constructs a finite subcover.
This is the definition of compactness.
:::
:::
