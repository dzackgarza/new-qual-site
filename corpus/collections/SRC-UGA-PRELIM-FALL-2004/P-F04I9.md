::: problem
Suppose $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^2$ and $g: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ are differentiable.
Prove that the composite function $f \circ g$ cannot have a differentiable inverse.
:::

::: solution
Let
\[
h=f\circ g:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2.
\]
For every $x\in\mathbb R^2$, the chain rule gives
\[
Dh_x=Df_{g(x)}\,Dg_x.
\]
Here $Dg_x:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R$ has rank at most $1$, so $Dh_x$ has rank at most $1$. In particular $Dh_x$ is never an invertible linear map on $\mathbb R^2$.

If $h$ had a differentiable inverse $k$, then
\[
k\circ h=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb R^2}.
\]
Differentiating at $x$ would give
\[
Dk_{h(x)}\,Dh_x=I_2,
\]
which would force $Dh_x$ to be invertible, a contradiction. Therefore $f\circ g$ cannot have a differentiable inverse.
:::
