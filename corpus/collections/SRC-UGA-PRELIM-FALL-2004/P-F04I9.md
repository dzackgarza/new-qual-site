::: {.problem}
Suppose $f: \RR \to \RR^2$ and $g: \RR^2 \to \RR$ are differentiable.
Prove that the composite function $f \circ g$ cannot have a differentiable inverse.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #composite}
$h\coloneqq f\circ g:\RR^2\to\RR^2$, $h(x)=f(g(x))$, is differentiable, and for every $x\in\RR^2$
$$
Dh_x=Df_{g(x)}\,Dg_x .
$$

::: pf-proof
A composite of differentiable maps is differentiable, by the chain rule, which also gives the displayed identity: $Dg_x$ is a linear map $\RR^2\to\RR$ and $Df_{g(x)}$ is a linear map $\RR\to\RR^2$, so the product is a linear map $\RR^2\to\RR^2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rank}
$\operatorname{rank}Dh_x\le1$ for every $x$.

::: pf-proof
The image of $Dg_x$ lies in the one-dimensional space $\RR$, so $\operatorname{rank}Dg_x\le1$. The rank of a product of linear maps is at most the rank of either factor, so $\operatorname{rank}Dh_x\le\operatorname{rank}Dg_x\le1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #singular}
$Dh_x$ is not an invertible linear map, for every $x\in\RR^2$.

::: pf-proof
An invertible linear endomorphism of $\RR^2$ has rank $2$, and step [](#rank){.pf-ref} bounds the rank by $1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$h=f\circ g$ has no differentiable inverse.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $k:\RR^2\to\RR^2$ were differentiable with $k\circ h=\operatorname{id}_{\RR^2}$. Differentiating this identity at $x$ by step [](#composite){.pf-ref} gives
$$
Dk_{h(x)}\,Dh_x=I_2,
$$
so $Dh_x$ has a right inverse and therefore $\operatorname{rank}Dh_x=2$, contradicting step [](#singular){.pf-ref}. Hence no such $k$ exists.
:::
