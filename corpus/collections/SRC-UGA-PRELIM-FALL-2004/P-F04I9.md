::: {.problem}
Suppose $f: \RR \to \RR^2$ and $g: \RR^2 \to \RR$ are differentiable.
Prove that the composite function $f \circ g$ cannot have a differentiable inverse.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
h=f\circ g:\RR^2\to\RR^2.
$$
For every $x\in\RR^2$, the chain rule gives
$$
Dh_x=Df_{g(x)}\,Dg_x.
$$
Here $Dg_x:\RR^2\to\RR$ has rank at most $1$, so $Dh_x$ has rank at most $1$. In particular $Dh_x$ is never an invertible linear map on $\RR^2$.

If $h$ had a differentiable inverse $k$, then
$$
k\circ h=\operatorname{id}_{\RR^2}.
$$
Differentiating at $x$ would give
$$
Dk_{h(x)}\,Dh_x=I_2,
$$
which would force $Dh_x$ to be invertible, a contradiction. Therefore $f\circ g$ cannot have a differentiable inverse.
:::
