::: {.problem}
Suppose $f, g: \RR \to \RR$ and $a \in \RR$.

(a) State the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition of what it means for $f$ to be continuous at $a$.

(b) Prove that if $f$ and $g$ are continuous at $3$, then their sum $f + g$ is also continuous at $3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A function $f:\RR\to\RR$ is continuous at $a$ if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{x-a}<\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \abs{f(x)-f(a)}<\varepsilon.
$$

Assume $f$ and $g$ are continuous at $3$. Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $\delta_f>0$ so that
$$
\abs{x-3}<\delta_f\Longrightarrow \abs{f(x)-f(3)}<\varepsilon/2,
$$
and choose $\delta_g>0$ so that
$$
\abs{x-3}<\delta_g\Longrightarrow \abs{g(x)-g(3)}<\varepsilon/2.
$$
Set $\delta=\min\{\delta_f,\delta_g\}$. Then if $\abs{x-3}<\delta$,
$$
\abs{(f+g)(x)-(f+g)(3)}
\le \abs{f(x)-f(3)}+\abs{g(x)-g(3)}
<\varepsilon.
$$
Hence $f+g$ is continuous at $3$.
:::
