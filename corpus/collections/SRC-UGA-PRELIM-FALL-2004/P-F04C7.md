::: {.problem}
Suppose $f, g: \RR \to \RR$ and $a \in \RR$.

(a) State the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition of what it means for $f$ to be continuous at $a$.

(b) Prove that if $f$ and $g$ are continuous at $3$, then their sum $f + g$ is also continuous at $3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no solution set for this examination is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed, and the card's existing solution is restructured into numbered steps. Here $f,g:\RR\to\RR$ and $a\in\RR$; part (a) asks for the definition and part (b) applies it at $a=3$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #def}

(a) $f$ is continuous at $a$ when for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ with $\abs{x-a}<\delta$ implying $\abs{f(x)-f(a)}<\varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof

This is the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition of continuity at a point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #halves}

(b) If $f$ and $g$ are continuous at $3$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there are $\delta_f,\delta_g>0$ with $\abs{x-3}<\delta_f\Rightarrow\abs{f(x)-f(3)}<\varepsilon/2$ and $\abs{x-3}<\delta_g\Rightarrow\abs{g(x)-g(3)}<\varepsilon/2$.

::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#def){.pf-ref} at $a=3$ to $f$ and to $g$ with the positive number $\varepsilon/2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sum}

(b) With $\delta=\min\theset{\delta_f,\delta_g}$, $\abs{x-3}<\delta$ implies $\abs{(f+g)(x)-(f+g)(3)}<\varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof

If $\abs{x-3}<\delta$ then $\abs{x-3}$ is below both $\delta_f$ and $\delta_g$, so $\abs{(f+g)(x)-(f+g)(3)}=\abs{(f(x)-f(3))+(g(x)-g(3))}\leq\abs{f(x)-f(3)}+\abs{g(x)-g(3)}<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon$, using the triangle inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

(b) $f+g$ is continuous at $3$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#halves){.pf-ref} and [](#sum){.pf-ref} produce for each $\varepsilon>0$ a $\delta>0$ as required by step [](#def){.pf-ref}.

:::
