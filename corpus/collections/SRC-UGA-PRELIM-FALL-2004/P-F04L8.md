::: {.problem}
Evaluate the line integral $\oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy$ where $P = y^2$, $Q = 2x - 3y$, and $C$ is the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. The printed statement fixes no orientation of $C$, so the counterclockwise value is computed and the clockwise one recorded.

Let $D=\{(x,y):x^2+y^2\le9\}$ and $P=y^2$, $Q=2x-3y$, both of class $C^1$ on an open set containing $D$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #green}
With $C$ oriented counterclockwise,
$$
\oint_CP\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dA.
$$

::: pf-proof
$D$ is the closed disk bounded by the positively oriented circle $C$, and $P,Q$ have continuous partial derivatives on the open disk $x^2+y^2<10\supseteq D$. These are the hypotheses of Green's theorem.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #integrand}
$\dfrac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}=2-2y$ on $D$.

::: pf-proof
$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x-3y)=2$ and $\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^2)=2y$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #area}
$\iint_D\,dA=9\pi$ and $\iint_Dy\,dA=0$.

::: pf-proof
The first is the area of a disk of radius $3$. For the second, $D$ is invariant under $(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)$, which reverses the sign of the integrand $y$ while preserving area, so the integral equals its own negative, hence is $0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #value}
$\oint_CP\,dx+Q\,dy=18\pi$ for the counterclockwise orientation, and $-18\pi$ for the clockwise one.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#green){.pf-ref} and [](#integrand){.pf-ref},
$$
\oint_CP\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D(2-2y)\,dA=2\iint_DdA-2\iint_Dy\,dA=2\cdot9\pi-2\cdot0=18\pi,
$$
using linearity of the double integral and step [](#area){.pf-ref}. Reversing the orientation of $C$ changes the sign of the line integral and nothing else.
:::
