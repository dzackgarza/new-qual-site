::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group containing no subgroup of index $2$.
Prove that every subgroup of index $3$ in $G$ is normal.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $H\le G$ satisfy
$$
[G:H]=3.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The action of $G$ on the three left cosets $G/H$ defines a homomorphism
$$
\varphi:G\longrightarrow S_3
$$
whose image acts transitively on $G/H$.

::: pf-proof

For $g\in G$, let $\varphi(g)$ be the permutation
$$
xH\longmapsto gxH
$$
of the three left cosets. This is the usual left coset action and is a group homomorphism. It is transitive because for any two cosets $xH,yH$, the element $yx^{-1}$ sends $xH$ to $yH$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The image $\varphi(G)$ is either $A_3$ or $S_3$.

::: pf-proof

Since $\varphi(G)$ acts transitively on a set of three points, the orbit-stabilizer theorem implies that $3$ divides $\abs{\varphi(G)}$. A subgroup of $S_3$ has order dividing $6$, so the only possibilities divisible by $3$ are $3$ and $6$. The unique subgroup of order $3$ is $A_3$, while a subgroup of order $6$ is all of $S_3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The image $\varphi(G)$ cannot be $S_3$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $\varphi(G)=S_3$. Then
$$
K\coloneqq\varphi^{-1}(A_3)
$$
is a subgroup of $G$. Since $A_3$ has index $2$ in $S_3$ and $\varphi$ is surjective onto $S_3$, one has
$$
[G:K]=2.
$$
This contradicts the hypothesis that $G$ has no subgroup of index $2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\varphi(G)=A_3.
$$

::: pf-proof

This follows from steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The stabilizer in $\varphi(G)=A_3$ of the coset $H\in G/H$ is trivial.

::: pf-proof

The group $A_3$ has order $3$ and acts transitively on the three cosets. By orbit-stabilizer,
$$
3
=
\abs{A_3\cdot H}
=
\frac{\abs{A_3}}{\abs{\operatorname{Stab}_{A_3}(H)}}.
$$
Hence the stabilizer has order $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

One has
$$
H=\ker\varphi.
$$

::: pf-proof

By definition of the coset action,
$$
H
=
\{g\in G:gH=H\}
=
\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Stab}_{\varphi(G)}(H)\bigr).
$$
Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} identifies this stabilizer with the identity subgroup, so the preimage is $\ker\varphi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

The subgroup $H$ is normal in $G$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s6){.pf-ref}, $H$ is the kernel of a group homomorphism. Therefore
$$
\boxed{H\trianglelefteq G}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s7){.pf-ref} proves the assertion for an arbitrary subgroup $H$ of index $3$.

:::
