::: {.problem}
Let $P$ be the vector space of real polynomials and define
\[
E:P\to P,
\qquad
Ef=f+f'.
\]
Prove that $E$ is invertible.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
D:P\to P,
\qquad
D(f)=f'.
$$
Then
$$
E=I+D.
$$

<1>1. For every polynomial $g\in P$, only finitely many derivatives $D^jg$ are nonzero.

::: {.proof}
If $g$ has degree $d$, then
$$
D^jg=0
$$
for every $j>d$.
:::

<1>2. Define a linear map
$$
F:P\to P
$$
by
$$
F(g)\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^jD^jg.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the displayed sum is finite for each polynomial $g$, so it defines a polynomial. Since every $D^j$ is linear and the sum is pointwise finite, $F$ is linear.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
E\circ F=I_P.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $g\in P$, choose $d$ such that $D^{d+1}g=0$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
E(F(g))
&=(I+D)\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^jD^jg\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^jD^jg
+\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^jD^{j+1}g\\
&=g+(-1)^dD^{d+1}g\\
&=g.
\end{aligned}
$$
All intermediate derivative terms cancel in pairs.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
F\circ E=I_P.
$$

::: {.proof}
The operators $D$ and $I+D$ commute. Thus for $f\in P$, using the same finite geometric-series calculation as in step <1>3,
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(E(f))
&=\sum_{j\ge0}(-D)^j(I+D)f\\
&=(I+D)\sum_{j\ge0}(-D)^jf\\
&=f.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sums are finite on $f$ by step <1>1.
:::

<1>5. Therefore $E$ is invertible, with
$$
\boxed{
E^{-1}(g)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^jg^{(j)}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3--<1>4 show that $F$ is both a left and a right inverse of $E$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 proves the assertion.
:::
:::
