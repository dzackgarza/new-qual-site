::: {.problem}
Let $f_n:[0,4]\to\mathbb R$ be continuous, twice continuously differentiable on $(0,4)$, and suppose
\[
f_n(1)=f_n'(1)=0
\]
for all $n$. Assume there is a constant $C$, independent of $n$, such that
\[
|f_n'(x)|\le C
\qquad(0<x<4).
\]
Prove that $(f_n)$ has a uniformly convergent subsequence.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For every $n$ and every $x,y\in[0,4]$,
$$
\abs{f_n(x)-f_n(y)}\le C\abs{x-y}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $x,y\in(0,4)$, the mean value theorem gives a point $\xi$ between them such that
$$
\abs{f_n(x)-f_n(y)}
=
\abs{f_n'(\xi)}\abs{x-y}
\le
C\abs{x-y}.
$$
If one of $x,y$ is an endpoint, approximate that endpoint by points in $(0,4)$ and pass to the limit using continuity of $f_n$ on $[0,4]$.
:::

<1>2. The family $(f_n)$ is equicontinuous on $[0,4]$.

::: {.proof}
Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose
$$
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{C}
$$
when $C>0$. Then step <1>1 gives
$$
\abs{x-y}<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\abs{f_n(x)-f_n(y)}<\varepsilon
$$
uniformly in $n$. If $C=0$, every $f_n$ is constant on $(0,4)$ and therefore on $[0,4]$, so equicontinuity is immediate.
:::

<1>3. The family $(f_n)$ is uniformly bounded:
$$
\abs{f_n(x)}\le3C
$$
for every $n$ and every $x\in[0,4]$.

::: {.proof}
Using $f_n(1)=0$ and step <1>1,
$$
\abs{f_n(x)}
=
\abs{f_n(x)-f_n(1)}
\le
C\abs{x-1}
\le3C.
$$
:::

<1>4. The sequence $(f_n)$ has a uniformly convergent subsequence on $[0,4]$.

::: {.proof}
The interval $[0,4]$ is compact. By steps <1>2--<1>3, the sequence is equicontinuous and uniformly bounded. The Arzelà--Ascoli theorem therefore gives a uniformly convergent subsequence.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
