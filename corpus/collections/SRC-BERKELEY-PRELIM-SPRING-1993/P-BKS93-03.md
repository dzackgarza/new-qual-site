::: {.problem}
Evaluate
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{x^3\sin x}{(1+x^2)^2}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
F(z)\coloneqq\frac{z^3e^{iz}}{(1+z^2)^2}.
$$

<1>1. The only pole of $F$ in the open upper half-plane is a double pole at $z=i$.

::: {.proof}
The denominator factors as
$$
(1+z^2)^2=(z-i)^2(z+i)^2.
$$
Thus the poles are $i$ and $-i$, each of order $2$, and only $i$ lies in the upper half-plane.
:::

<1>2. The residue of $F$ at $i$ is
$$
\operatorname{Res}(F;i)=\frac{e^{-1}}4.
$$

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
F(z)=\frac{h(z)}{(z-i)^2},
\qquad
h(z)=\frac{z^3e^{iz}}{(z+i)^2}.
$$
The residue at the double pole is $h'(i)$. First,
$$
h(i)
=
\frac{i^3e^{-1}}{(2i)^2}
=
\frac{i e^{-1}}4.
$$
Using the logarithmic derivative of $h$,
$$
\frac{h'(z)}{h(z)}
=
\frac3z+i-\frac2{z+i}.
$$
At $z=i$ the right-hand side is
$$
-3i+i+i=-i.
$$
Therefore
$$
h'(i)
=
\frac{i e^{-1}}4(-i)
=
\frac{e^{-1}}4.
$$
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{x^3e^{ix}}{(1+x^2)^2}\,dx
=
\frac{\pi i}{2e}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Integrate $F$ over the positively oriented upper semicircle of radius $R$. Since
$$
\frac{z^3}{(1+z^2)^2}=O(1/z),
$$
Jordan's lemma gives that the semicircular-arc integral tends to $0$ as $R\to\infty$. The residue theorem and step <1>2 therefore give
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{x^3e^{ix}}{(1+x^2)^2}\,dx
=
2\pi i\frac{e^{-1}}4
=
\frac{\pi i}{2e}.
$$
:::

<1>4. The real part of the integral in step <1>3 is $0$.

::: {.proof}
Its real part is
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{x^3\cos x}{(1+x^2)^2}\,dx.
$$
The integrand is odd, so the symmetric improper integral is zero.
:::

<1>5. The requested integral is
$$
\boxed{\frac{\pi}{2e}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The imaginary part of the integral in step <1>3 is exactly
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{x^3\sin x}{(1+x^2)^2}\,dx.
$$
Taking imaginary parts of
$$
\frac{\pi i}{2e}
$$
gives the stated value.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the required evaluation.
:::
