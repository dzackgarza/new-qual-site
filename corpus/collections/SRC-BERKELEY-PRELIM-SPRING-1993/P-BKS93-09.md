::: {.problem}
Let $a\in\mathbb C$ and let $\varepsilon>0$. Define
\[
f(z)=\sin z+\frac1{z-a}.
\]
Prove that $f$ has infinitely many zeros in the strip
\[
|\operatorname{Im}z|<\varepsilon.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Choose a real number $r$ such that
$$
0<r<\min\left\{\varepsilon,\frac{\pi}{3}\right\}.
$$
Then
$$
m\coloneqq\min_{|w|=r}|\sin w|
$$
is positive.

::: {.proof}
The circle $|w|=r$ is compact. Since $r<\pi$, none of the zeros $k\pi$ of $\sin w$ lies on that circle. Thus $|\sin w|$ is a positive continuous function there and has a positive minimum $m$.
:::

<1>2. For all sufficiently large positive integers $n$, the closed disk
$$
D_n\coloneqq\{z:|z-n\pi|\le r\}
$$
does not contain $a$, and on its boundary
$$
\left|\frac1{z-a}\right|<m.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $|z-n\pi|\le r$, then
$$
|z-a|
\ge
n\pi-|a|-r.
$$
The right-hand side tends to infinity with $n$. Hence for all sufficiently large $n$ it is positive and greater than $1/m$. For those $n$, $a\notin D_n$ and the displayed boundary estimate follows.
:::

<1>3. On the boundary of every such disk $D_n$,
$$
\left|\frac1{z-a}\right|<|\sin z|.
$$

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
z=n\pi+w,
\qquad
|w|=r.
$$
Then
$$
\sin z=(-1)^n\sin w,
$$
so step <1>1 gives
$$
|\sin z|=|\sin w|\ge m.
$$
Step <1>2 gives the strict inequality for the perturbation.
:::

<1>4. For every sufficiently large $n$, the function
$$
f(z)=\sin z+\frac1{z-a}
$$
has exactly one zero in the interior of $D_n$, counted with multiplicity.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $f$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $D_n$. By step <1>3, Rouché's theorem shows that $f$ and $\sin z$ have the same number of zeros in $D_n$.

The zeros of $\sin z$ are the points $k\pi$. Since $r<\pi/3$, the disk centered at $n\pi$ contains exactly the single zero $n\pi$, and that zero is simple. Hence $f$ has exactly one zero in $D_n$.
:::

<1>5. Every zero supplied by step <1>4 lies in the strip
$$
|\operatorname{Im}z|<\varepsilon.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $z\in D_n$ and $n\pi$ is real, then
$$
|\operatorname{Im}z|
=
|\operatorname{Im}(z-n\pi)|
\le
|z-n\pi|
<r
<\varepsilon.
$$
:::

<1>6. The function $f$ has infinitely many distinct zeros in that strip.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 supplies a zero in $D_n$ for every sufficiently large positive integer $n$. Since $2r<\pi$, the disks $D_n$ are pairwise disjoint for distinct integers $n$. Thus these zeros are distinct, and step <1>5 places all of them in the required strip.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is exactly the assertion.
:::
