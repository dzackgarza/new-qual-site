::: {.problem}
Classify, up to isomorphism, all groups of order $45$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be a group of order
$$
45=3^2\cdot5.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The Sylow $5$-subgroup of $G$ is unique and normal.

::: pf-proof

If $n_5$ denotes the number of Sylow $5$-subgroups, Sylow's theorems give
$$
n_5\mid9
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n_5\equiv1\pmod5.
$$
The divisors of $9$ are $1,3,9$, and only $1$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $5$. Thus $n_5=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The Sylow $3$-subgroup of $G$ is unique and normal.

::: pf-proof

If $n_3$ denotes the number of Sylow $3$-subgroups, then
$$
n_3\mid5
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n_3\equiv1\pmod3.
$$
The only divisors of $5$ are $1$ and $5$, and $5\not\equiv1\pmod3$. Hence $n_3=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $P$ and $Q$ are the Sylow $3$- and $5$-subgroups, then
$$
G\cong P\times Q.
$$

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, both $P$ and $Q$ are normal. Their orders are coprime, so
$$
P\cap Q=\{1\}.
$$
For $p\in P$ and $q\in Q$, normality of both subgroups gives
$$
[p,q]=pqp^{-1}q^{-1}\in P\cap Q=\{1\},
$$
so $P$ and $Q$ commute elementwise. Moreover,
$$
\abs{PQ}
=
\frac{\abs P\abs Q}{\abs{P\cap Q}}
=
9\cdot5
=45,
$$
so $PQ=G$. Therefore multiplication induces an isomorphism $P\times Q\cong G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One has
$$
Q\cong C_5,
$$
while
$$
P\cong C_9
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
P\cong C_3\times C_3.
$$

::: pf-proof

Every group of prime order is cyclic, so $Q\cong C_5$.

Every group of order $p^2$ is abelian. Thus the order-$9$ group $P$ is abelian. If it contains an element of order $9$, then $P\cong C_9$. Otherwise every nonidentity element has order $3$; choosing two elements that do not generate the same order-$3$ subgroup gives
$$
P\cong C_3\times C_3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Up to isomorphism, the groups of order $45$ are exactly
$$
\boxed{
C_{45}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
C_3\times C_3\times C_5}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref}, the two possibilities are
$$
C_9\times C_5
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(C_3\times C_3)\times C_5.
$$
Since $9$ and $5$ are coprime,
$$
C_9\times C_5\cong C_{45}.
$$
The two displayed groups are not isomorphic: the first contains an element of order $9$, while the second does not.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives the complete classification.
