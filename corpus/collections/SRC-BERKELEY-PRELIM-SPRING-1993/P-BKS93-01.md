::: {.problem}
Let $(a_n)$ and $(\varepsilon_n)$ be sequences of positive real numbers. Assume
\[
\varepsilon_n\to0
\]
and that there is a number $k\in(0,1)$ such that
\[
a_{n+1}\le k a_n+\varepsilon_n
\]
for every $n$. Prove that
\[
a_n\to0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $\delta>0$. There exists $N$ such that for every $n\ge N$,
$$
\varepsilon_n<\frac{1-k}{2}\delta.
$$

::: {.proof}
This follows from the hypothesis $\varepsilon_n\to0$ and the positivity of $(1-k)\delta/2$.
:::

<1>2. For every integer $m\ge1$,
$$
a_{N+m}
\le
k^m a_N
+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}k^{m-1-j}\varepsilon_{N+j}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The statement follows by induction on $m$. For $m=1$ it is exactly the given recurrence at $N$. If it holds for $m$, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{N+m+1}
&\le k a_{N+m}+\varepsilon_{N+m}\\
&\le k^{m+1}a_N
+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}k^{m-j}\varepsilon_{N+j}
+\varepsilon_{N+m},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is the required formula for $m+1$.
:::

<1>3. For every $m\ge1$,
$$
a_{N+m}
<
k^m a_N+\frac{\delta}{2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1--<1>2,
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{N+m}
&\le k^m a_N
+\frac{1-k}{2}\delta
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}k^{m-1-j}\\
&=k^m a_N
+\frac{1-k}{2}\delta
\frac{1-k^m}{1-k}\\
&<k^m a_N+\frac{\delta}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>4. There exists $M$ such that for every $m\ge M$,
$$
k^m a_N<\frac{\delta}{2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $0<k<1$, one has $k^m\to0$. The number $a_N$ is fixed and positive.
:::

<1>5. For every $m\ge M$,
$$
a_{N+m}<\delta.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>3 and <1>4:
$$
a_{N+m}
<
\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}
=
\delta.
$$
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{a_n\to0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 shows that for every $\delta>0$, all sufficiently large terms of the sequence lie in $(0,\delta)$. This is exactly convergence to $0$.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
