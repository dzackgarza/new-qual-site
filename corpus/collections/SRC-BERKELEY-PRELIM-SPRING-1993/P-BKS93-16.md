::: {.problem}
Prove that for $x,y\ge0$,
\[
\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}\le e^{x+y-2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For $x,y\ge0$,
$$
x^2+y^2\le(x+y)^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $xy\ge0$,
$$
(x+y)^2-(x^2+y^2)=2xy\ge0.
$$
:::

<1>2. For every $t>0$,
$$
t\le e^{t-1}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The standard logarithmic inequality
$$
\log t\le t-1
$$
follows, for example, from concavity of $\log$ and its tangent line at $t=1$. Exponentiating gives the displayed inequality.
:::

<1>3. If $x+y>0$ and
$$
t\coloneqq\frac{x+y}{2},
$$
then
$$
\frac{(x+y)^2}{4}\le e^{x+y-2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
t^2\le e^{2t-2}.
$$
Since
$$
t^2=\frac{(x+y)^2}{4}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2t-2=x+y-2,
$$
this is the claimed estimate.
:::

<1>4. For all $x,y\ge0$,
$$
\boxed{\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}\le e^{x+y-2}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $x+y>0$, combine steps <1>1 and <1>3. If $x+y=0$, nonnegativity forces $x=y=0$, and the inequality becomes
$$
0\le e^{-2},
$$
which is true.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required inequality.
:::
:::
