::: {.problem}
Prove that for $x,y\ge0$,
\[
\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}\le e^{x+y-2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For $x,y\ge0$,
$$
x^2+y^2\le(x+y)^2.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $xy\ge0$,
$$
(x+y)^2-(x^2+y^2)=2xy\ge0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $t>0$,
$$
t\le e^{t-1}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The standard logarithmic inequality
$$
\log t\le t-1
$$
holds because $\log$ is concave, so its graph lies below its tangent line $t\mapsto t-1$ at $t=1$. Exponentiating gives the displayed inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $x+y>0$ and
$$
t\coloneqq\frac{x+y}{2},
$$
then
$$
\frac{(x+y)^2}{4}\le e^{x+y-2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
t^2\le e^{2t-2}.
$$
Since
$$
t^2=\frac{(x+y)^2}{4}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2t-2=x+y-2,
$$
this is the claimed estimate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For all $x,y\ge0$,
$$
\boxed{\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}\le e^{x+y-2}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

If $x+y>0$, combine steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}. If $x+y=0$, nonnegativity forces $x=y=0$, and the inequality becomes
$$
0\le e^{-2},
$$
which is true.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is the required inequality.

:::
