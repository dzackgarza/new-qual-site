::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field and let $\Lambda$ be the set of $2\times2$ matrices
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-b\\
b&a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,b\in F.
\]
Prove that $\Lambda$, with the usual matrix operations, is a commutative ring with identity.

For which of the following fields is $\Lambda$ a field?
\[
F=\mathbb Q,\qquad \mathbb C,\qquad \mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z,\qquad \mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For $a,b\in F$, write
$$
M(a,b)\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-b\\
b&a
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The set $\Lambda$ is closed under addition and multiplication, with
$$
M(a,b)+M(c,d)=M(a+c,b+d)
$$
and
$$
M(a,b)M(c,d)=M(ac-bd,ad+bc).
$$

::: pf-proof

Both identities follow by direct matrix addition and multiplication. In particular, the resulting matrices again have the defining form of elements of $\Lambda$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

With the usual matrix operations, $\Lambda$ is a commutative ring with identity.

::: pf-proof

Associativity and distributivity are inherited from the full matrix ring $M_2(F)$. The zero matrix is $M(0,0)$, additive inverses are given by
$$
-M(a,b)=M(-a,-b),
$$
and the identity matrix is
$$
I=M(1,0).
$$
Finally, the multiplication formula in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} is symmetric in $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$, so multiplication in $\Lambda$ is commutative.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

A nonzero element $M(a,b)$ is invertible in $\Lambda$ if and only if
$$
a^2+b^2\ne0.
$$

::: pf-proof

One has
$$
M(a,b)M(a,-b)
=
M(a^2+b^2,0)
=
(a^2+b^2)I.
$$
Thus if $a^2+b^2\ne0$, then
$$
M(a,b)^{-1}
=
\frac1{a^2+b^2}M(a,-b)
\in\Lambda.
$$
Conversely, if $a^2+b^2=0$, then
$$
\det M(a,b)=0,
$$
so the matrix is not invertible even in $M_2(F)$ and hence not in $\Lambda$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The ring $\Lambda$ is a field if and only if the equation
$$
a^2+b^2=0
$$
has no nonzero solution $(a,b)\in F^2$.

::: pf-proof

This is exactly the condition from step [](#s3){.pf-ref} that every nonzero element of the commutative ring $\Lambda$ be invertible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

For $F=\QQ$, the ring $\Lambda$ is a field.

::: pf-proof

If $a,b\in\QQ$ satisfy $a^2+b^2=0$, then viewing the equality in $\RR$ shows that both nonnegative rational numbers $a^2$ and $b^2$ are zero. Thus $a=b=0$, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} applies.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

For $F=\CC$, the ring $\Lambda$ is not a field.

::: pf-proof

The nonzero pair
$$
(a,b)=(i,1)
$$
satisfies
$$
a^2+b^2=-1+1=0.
$$
Apply step [](#s4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

For $F=\ZZ/5\ZZ$, the ring $\Lambda$ is not a field.

::: pf-proof

In $\ZZ/5\ZZ$,
$$
2^2+1^2=4+1=0.
$$
Thus the equation in step [](#s4){.pf-ref} has a nonzero solution.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

For $F=\ZZ/7\ZZ$, the ring $\Lambda$ is a field.

::: pf-proof

The nonzero squares modulo $7$ are
$$
1,2,4.
$$
Thus $-1=6$ is not a square. If $a^2+b^2=0$ and $b\ne0$, division by $b^2$ would give
$$
(a/b)^2=-1,
$$
a contradiction. Hence $b=0$, and then $a=0$. Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} applies.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s9}

Among the four fields in the problem, the field cases are exactly
$$
\boxed{F=\QQ\quad\text{and}\quad F=\ZZ/7\ZZ}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s5){.pf-ref}, [](#s6){.pf-ref}, [](#s7){.pf-ref} and [](#s8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves the ring assertion, and step [](#s9){.pf-ref} gives the requested field classification.
