::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field and let $\Lambda$ be the set of $2\times2$ matrices
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-b\\
b&a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,b\in F.
\]
Prove that $\Lambda$, with the usual matrix operations, is a commutative ring with identity.

For which of the following fields is $\Lambda$ a field?
\[
F=\mathbb Q,\qquad \mathbb C,\qquad \mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z,\qquad \mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For $a,b\in F$, write
$$
M(a,b)\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-b\\
b&a
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

<1>1. The set $\Lambda$ is closed under addition and multiplication, with
$$
M(a,b)+M(c,d)=M(a+c,b+d)
$$
and
$$
M(a,b)M(c,d)=M(ac-bd,ad+bc).
$$

::: {.proof}
Both identities follow by direct matrix addition and multiplication. In particular, the resulting matrices again have the defining form of elements of $\Lambda$.
:::

<1>2. With the usual matrix operations, $\Lambda$ is a commutative ring with identity.

::: {.proof}
Associativity and distributivity are inherited from the full matrix ring $M_2(F)$. The zero matrix is $M(0,0)$, additive inverses are given by
$$
-M(a,b)=M(-a,-b),
$$
and the identity matrix is
$$
I=M(1,0).
$$
Finally, the multiplication formula in step <1>1 is symmetric in $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$, so multiplication in $\Lambda$ is commutative.
:::

<1>3. A nonzero element $M(a,b)$ is invertible in $\Lambda$ if and only if
$$
a^2+b^2\ne0.
$$

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
M(a,b)M(a,-b)
=
M(a^2+b^2,0)
=
(a^2+b^2)I.
$$
Thus if $a^2+b^2\ne0$, then
$$
M(a,b)^{-1}
=
\frac1{a^2+b^2}M(a,-b)
\in\Lambda.
$$
Conversely, if $a^2+b^2=0$, then
$$
\det M(a,b)=0,
$$
so the matrix is not invertible even in $M_2(F)$ and hence not in $\Lambda$.
:::

<1>4. The ring $\Lambda$ is a field if and only if the equation
$$
a^2+b^2=0
$$
has no nonzero solution $(a,b)\in F^2$.

::: {.proof}
This is exactly the condition from step <1>3 that every nonzero element of the commutative ring $\Lambda$ be invertible.
:::

<1>5. For $F=\QQ$, the ring $\Lambda$ is a field.

::: {.proof}
If $a,b\in\QQ$ satisfy $a^2+b^2=0$, then viewing the equality in $\RR$ shows that both nonnegative rational numbers $a^2$ and $b^2$ are zero. Thus $a=b=0$, and step <1>4 applies.
:::

<1>6. For $F=\CC$, the ring $\Lambda$ is not a field.

::: {.proof}
The nonzero pair
$$
(a,b)=(i,1)
$$
satisfies
$$
a^2+b^2=-1+1=0.
$$
Apply step <1>4.
:::

<1>7. For $F=\ZZ/5\ZZ$, the ring $\Lambda$ is not a field.

::: {.proof}
In $\ZZ/5\ZZ$,
$$
2^2+1^2=4+1=0.
$$
Thus the equation in step <1>4 has a nonzero solution.
:::

<1>8. For $F=\ZZ/7\ZZ$, the ring $\Lambda$ is a field.

::: {.proof}
The nonzero squares modulo $7$ are
$$
1,2,4.
$$
Thus $-1=6$ is not a square. If $a^2+b^2=0$ and $b\ne0$, division by $b^2$ would give
$$
(a/b)^2=-1,
$$
a contradiction. Hence $b=0$, and then $a=0$. Step <1>4 applies.
:::

<1>9. Among the four fields in the problem, the field cases are exactly
$$
\boxed{F=\QQ\quad\text{and}\quad F=\ZZ/7\ZZ}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>5--<1>8.
:::

<1>10. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves the ring assertion, and step <1>9 gives the requested field classification.
