::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ be $C^1$ and assume the improper integral
\[
\int_1^\infty |f'(x)|\,dx
\]
converges.
Prove that
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty f(n)
\]
converges if and only if
\[
\int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx
\]
converges.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $n\ge1$, define
$$
d_n\coloneqq
f(n)-\int_n^{n+1}f(x)\,dx.
$$

<1>1. For every $n\ge1$,
$$
\abs{d_n}
\le
\int_n^{n+1}\abs{f'(t)}\,dt.
$$

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_n
&=\int_n^{n+1}\bigl(f(n)-f(x)\bigr)\,dx\\
&=-\int_n^{n+1}\int_n^x f'(t)\,dt\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{d_n}
&\le
\int_n^{n+1}\int_n^x\abs{f'(t)}\,dt\,dx\\
&\le
\int_n^{n+1}\abs{f'(t)}\,dt,
\end{aligned}
$$
because the inner integration region has length at most $1$ in the $x$-direction.
:::

<1>2. The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_n
$$
converges absolutely.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\abs{d_n}
\le
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\int_n^{n+1}\abs{f'(t)}\,dt
=
\int_1^{\infty}\abs{f'(t)}\,dt
<\infty.
$$
:::

<1>3. For every positive integer $N$,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^N f(n)
-
\int_1^{N+1}f(x)\,dx
=
\sum_{n=1}^N d_n.
$$

::: {.proof}
By definition of $d_n$,
$$
f(n)
=
d_n+\int_n^{n+1}f(x)\,dx.
$$
Summing from $n=1$ to $N$ makes the integrals telescope over adjacent intervals to $\int_1^{N+1}f$.
:::

<1>4. If the improper integral
$$
\int_1^{\infty}f(x)\,dx
$$
converges, then
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n)
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
Convergence of the improper integral implies that
$$
\int_1^{N+1}f(x)\,dx
$$
has a finite limit as $N\to\infty$. By step <1>2, the right-hand side of step <1>3 also has a finite limit. Hence the partial sums $\sum_{n=1}^Nf(n)$ converge.
:::

<1>5. Suppose instead that
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n)
$$
converges. Then the sequence
$$
\int_1^{N+1}f(x)\,dx
$$
converges as $N\to\infty$.

::: {.proof}
By hypothesis, the first term on the left-hand side of step <1>3 has a finite limit. By step <1>2, the right-hand side has a finite limit. Rearranging step <1>3 therefore shows that the displayed sequence of integrals has a finite limit.
:::

<1>6. Under the hypothesis of step <1>5,
$$
\sup_{N\le x\le N+1}\abs{f(x)}\longrightarrow0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Convergence of the series implies $f(N)\to0$. For $x\in[N,N+1]$,
$$
\abs{f(x)}
\le
\abs{f(N)}+\int_N^{N+1}\abs{f'(t)}\,dt.
$$
The first term tends to $0$, and the second tends to $0$ because it is bounded by the tail
$$
\int_N^{\infty}\abs{f'(t)}\,dt.
$$
The bound is uniform in $x\in[N,N+1]$.
:::

<1>7. Under the hypothesis of step <1>5, the improper integral
$$
\int_1^{\infty}f(x)\,dx
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
Let $R\to\infty$ and choose the integer $N$ with
$$
N\le R<N+1.
$$
Then
$$
\int_1^R f(x)\,dx
=
\int_1^N f(x)\,dx
+\int_N^R f(x)\,dx.
$$
The first term has a finite limit by step <1>5, with the index shifted by one. The second satisfies
$$
\left|\int_N^R f(x)\,dx\right|
\le
\sup_{N\le x\le N+1}\abs{f(x)},
$$
which tends to $0$ by step <1>6. Thus the full improper integral converges.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n)\text{ converges}
\iff
\int_1^{\infty}f(x)\,dx\text{ converges}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves one implication, and step <1>7 proves the converse.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the required equivalence.
:::
