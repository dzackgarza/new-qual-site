::: {.problem}
Prove that no commutative ring with identity has additive group isomorphic to
\[
\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every element of the additive group $\QQ/\ZZ$ has finite order.

::: {.proof}
If
$$
q=\frac ab+\ZZ
$$
with $a\in\ZZ$ and $b>0$, then
$$
bq=a+\ZZ=0.
$$
:::

<1>2. Suppose, for contradiction, that a ring $R$ with identity $1_R$ has
$$
(R,+)\cong\QQ/\ZZ.
$$
Then there is a positive integer $n$ such that
$$
n1_R=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Under an additive-group isomorphism, $1_R$ corresponds to an element of $\QQ/\ZZ$. By step <1>1, that element has finite additive order.
:::

<1>3. The integer $n$ from step <1>2 annihilates every element of the additive group of $R$.

::: {.proof}
For every $r\in R$,
$$
nr=(n1_R)r=0r=0.
$$
:::

<1>4. No positive integer annihilates every element of $\QQ/\ZZ$.

::: {.proof}
For a given $n\ge1$, the element
$$
\frac1{n+1}+\ZZ
$$
has additive order $n+1$. In particular,
$$
n\left(\frac1{n+1}+\ZZ\right)\ne0.
$$
:::

<1>5. Therefore no ring with identity has additive group $\QQ/\ZZ$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 would make the additive group of $R$ have exponent dividing $n$. An additive-group isomorphism would transfer this property to $\QQ/\ZZ$, contradicting step <1>4. Hence
$$
\boxed{(R,+)\not\cong\QQ/\ZZ}
$$
for every ring $R$ with identity, in particular for every commutative such ring.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 proves the required nonexistence statement.
:::
:::
