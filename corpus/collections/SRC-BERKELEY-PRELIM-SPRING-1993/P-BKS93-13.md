::: {.problem}
Prove that no commutative ring with identity has additive group isomorphic to
\[
\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Every element of the additive group $\QQ/\ZZ$ has finite order.

::: pf-proof

If
$$
q=\frac ab+\ZZ
$$
with $a\in\ZZ$ and $b>0$, then
$$
bq=a+\ZZ=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Suppose, for contradiction, that a ring $R$ with identity $1_R$ has
$$
(R,+)\cong\QQ/\ZZ.
$$
Then there is a positive integer $n$ such that
$$
n1_R=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Under an additive-group isomorphism, $1_R$ corresponds to an element of $\QQ/\ZZ$. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, that element has finite additive order.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The integer $n$ from step [](#s2){.pf-ref} annihilates every element of the additive group of $R$.

::: pf-proof

For every $r\in R$,
$$
nr=(n1_R)r=0r=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

No positive integer annihilates every element of $\QQ/\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

For a given $n\ge1$, the element
$$
\frac1{n+1}+\ZZ
$$
has additive order $n+1$. In particular,
$$
n\left(\frac1{n+1}+\ZZ\right)\ne0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore no ring with identity has additive group $\QQ/\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} would make the additive group of $R$ have exponent dividing $n$. An additive-group isomorphism would transfer this property to $\QQ/\ZZ$, contradicting step [](#s4){.pf-ref}. Hence
$$
\boxed{(R,+)\not\cong\QQ/\ZZ}
$$
for every ring $R$ with identity, in particular for every commutative such ring.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} proves the required nonexistence statement.

:::
