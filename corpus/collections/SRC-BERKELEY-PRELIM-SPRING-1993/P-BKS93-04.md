::: {.problem}
Suppose a group $G$ is generated by elements $x$ and $y$ satisfying
\[
x^5y^3=1,
\qquad
x^8y^5=1.
\]
Does it follow that $G$ is trivial? Prove your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

The defining relations imply
$$
x^5=y^{-3},
\qquad
x^8=y^{-5}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Rearranging
$$
x^5y^3=1
$$
gives $x^5=y^{-3}$, and rearranging
$$
x^8y^5=1
$$
gives $x^8=y^{-5}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

One has
$$
x=y^{-1}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $16-15=1$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
x
&=x^{16}x^{-15}\\
&=(x^8)^2(x^5)^{-3}\\
&=(y^{-5})^2(y^{-3})^{-3}\\
&=y^{-10}y^9\\
&=y^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

One has
$$
y^2=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y^3=1.
$$

::: pf-proof

Substitute $x=y^{-1}$ from step [](#s2){.pf-ref} into the first defining relation:
$$
1=x^5y^3=y^{-5}y^3=y^{-2},
$$
so $y^2=1$. Substitution into the second relation gives
$$
1=x^8y^5=y^{-8}y^5=y^{-3},
$$
so $y^3=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One has
$$
x=y=1.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
y=y^3(y^2)^{-1}=1.
$$
Then step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives $x=y^{-1}=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{G\text{ is trivial}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The group $G$ is generated by $x$ and $y$, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} shows that both generators are the identity.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} answers the question affirmatively.

:::
