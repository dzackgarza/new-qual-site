::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic in the unit disk $\mathbb D$.

1. Prove that there is a sequence $(z_n)$ in $\mathbb D$ such that
   \[
   |z_n|\to1
   \]
   and the finite limit
   \[
   \lim_{n\to\infty}f(z_n)
   \]
   exists.
2. If $f$ is nonconstant, prove that there are sequences $(z_n)$ and $(w_n)$ in $\mathbb D$ such that
   \[
   |z_n|\to1,
   \qquad
   |w_n|\to1,
   \]
   both finite limits
   \[
   \lim_{n\to\infty}f(z_n),
   \qquad
   \lim_{n\to\infty}f(w_n)
   \]
   exist, and the two limits are different.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\CC_\infty\coloneqq\CC\cup\{\infty\}
$$
denote the Riemann sphere.

<1>1. There is a sequence $(z_n)$ in $\DD$ such that
$$
\abs{z_n}\to1
$$
and $(f(z_n))$ has a finite limit.

::: {.proof}
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that no such sequence exists. Then
$$
\abs{f(z)}\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad\text{uniformly as }\abs z\to1.
$$
Indeed, if this failed, there would be a constant $M$ and a sequence $(z_n)$ with
$$
\abs{z_n}\to1,
\qquad
\abs{f(z_n)}\le M.
$$
The bounded sequence $(f(z_n))$ would have a convergent subsequence in $\CC$, contrary to the supposition.

In particular, all zeros of $f$ lie in some smaller closed disk. Since $f$ is not identically zero under the supposition, its zeros are isolated; compactness therefore makes their total number finite, counted with multiplicity. List them as $a_1,\ldots,a_N$, repeated according to multiplicity, and put
$$
P(z)\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^N(z-a_j),
$$
with $P=1$ if $f$ has no zeros.

Then
$$
h(z)\coloneqq\frac{P(z)}{f(z)}
$$
has removable singularities at the zeros of $f$ and extends to a holomorphic, nonzero function on $\DD$. The polynomial $P$ is bounded on the closed unit disk, while $\abs f\to\infty$ uniformly at the boundary. Hence
$$
\max_{\abs{w}=r}\abs{h(w)}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(r\uparrow1).
$$
For any fixed $z\in\DD$ and any $r>\abs z$, the maximum modulus principle gives
$$
\abs{h(z)}
\le
\max_{\abs{w}=r}\abs{h(w)}.
$$
Letting $r\uparrow1$ yields $h(z)=0$. Thus $h\equiv0$, contradicting its construction. Therefore the required sequence exists.
:::

<1>2. For $0<r<1$, define
$$
A_r\coloneqq\{z\in\DD:r<\abs z<1\}
$$
and let
$$
K_r\coloneqq\overline{f(A_r)}^{\,\CC_\infty}.
$$
Then each $K_r$ is a nonempty compact connected subset of $\CC_\infty$, and
$$
r<s
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
K_s\subseteq K_r.
$$

::: {.proof}
The annulus $A_r$ is nonempty and connected. Its continuous image $f(A_r)$ is connected, and its closure in the compact Riemann sphere is compact and connected. The nesting follows from $A_s\subseteq A_r$ when $r<s$.
:::

<1>3. The set
$$
K\coloneqq\bigcap_{0<r<1}K_r
$$
is nonempty and connected.

::: {.proof}
Choose any sequence $r_n\uparrow1$. The compact connected sets $K_{r_n}$ are nested and nonempty. The nested-intersection theorem in the compact space $\CC_\infty$ gives a nonempty intersection, and the standard nested-continuum theorem gives that this intersection is connected. Since every $0<r<1$ is dominated by some $r_n$, one has
$$
\bigcap_nK_{r_n}
=
\bigcap_{0<r<1}K_r
=K.
$$
:::

<1>4. A point $L\in\CC_\infty$ belongs to $K$ if and only if there is a sequence $(z_n)$ in $\DD$ such that
$$
\abs{z_n}\to1
$$
and
$$
f(z_n)\longrightarrow L
$$
in the Riemann sphere.

::: {.proof}
First suppose $L\in K$. Choose $r_n\uparrow1$. Since $L\in K_{r_n}$, there is $z_n\in A_{r_n}$ such that the spherical distance from $f(z_n)$ to $L$ is less than $1/n$. Then
$$
\abs{z_n}>r_n\to1
$$
and $f(z_n)\to L$ spherically.

Conversely, suppose such a sequence exists. For each fixed $r<1$, all sufficiently large $z_n$ belong to $A_r$. Hence the spherical limit $L$ belongs to $K_r$. Since this holds for every $r$, one has $L\in K$.
:::

<1>5. The set $K$ contains at least one finite point.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives a sequence with $\abs{z_n}\to1$ and finite limit
$$
L=\lim_{n\to\infty}f(z_n)\in\CC.
$$
Step <1>4 then gives $L\in K$.
:::

<1>6. If $f$ is nonconstant, then $K$ contains at least two distinct finite points.

::: {.proof}
Let $L\in K\cap\CC$ be supplied by step <1>5. Suppose $L$ were the only finite point of $K$. Then
$$
K\subseteq\{L,\infty\}.
$$
By step <1>3, $K$ is connected and contains $L$. The two-point set $\{L,\infty\}$ is disconnected, so necessarily
$$
K=\{L\}.
$$

It follows that
$$
f(z)\longrightarrow L
\qquad\text{uniformly as }\abs z\to1.
$$
Otherwise there would be an $\varepsilon>0$ and a sequence $z_n$ with $\abs{z_n}\to1$ and
$$
\abs{f(z_n)-L}\ge\varepsilon.
$$
Compactness of $\CC_\infty$ would give a subsequence of $f(z_n)$ converging spherically to some point of $K=\{L\}$; convergence to the finite point $L$ in the spherical metric implies ordinary complex convergence, contradicting the displayed lower bound.

Thus
$$
\max_{\abs{w}=r}\abs{f(w)-L}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(r\uparrow1).
$$
For any fixed $z\in\DD$, the maximum modulus principle applied to $f-L$ on $\abs{w}<r$ gives
$$
\abs{f(z)-L}
\le
\max_{\abs{w}=r}\abs{f(w)-L}.
$$
Letting $r\uparrow1$ yields $f(z)=L$. Hence $f$ would be constant, contrary to hypothesis. Therefore $K$ contains a finite point different from $L$.
:::

<1>7. If $f$ is nonconstant, there are sequences $(z_n)$ and $(w_n)$ in $\DD$ such that
$$
\abs{z_n}\to1,
\qquad
\abs{w_n}\to1,
$$
and the two finite limits
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(z_n),
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(w_n)
$$
exist and are distinct.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6, choose distinct finite points
$$
L,M\in K.
$$
Apply step <1>4 separately to $L$ and $M$. It supplies sequences approaching the unit circle whose images converge to $L$ and $M$, respectively.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves part 1, and step <1>7 proves part 2.
:::
