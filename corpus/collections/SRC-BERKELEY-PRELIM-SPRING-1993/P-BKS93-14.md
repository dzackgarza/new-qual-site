::: {.problem}
Prove that every solution $x(t)$, $t\ge0$, of
\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=x^2-x^6
\]
with $x(0)>0$ satisfies
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=1.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
F(x)\coloneqq x^2-x^6=x^2(1-x^4).
$$

<1>1. A solution with $x(0)>0$ remains positive for every $t\ge0$.

::: {.proof}
The function $F$ is a polynomial, hence locally Lipschitz, so initial-value problems for $x'=F(x)$ have unique solutions. The constant function $x\equiv0$ is a solution. If a solution with positive initial value reached $0$ at some time, uniqueness for the initial condition at that time would force it to coincide locally, and hence by uniqueness throughout its common interval, with the zero solution, contradicting $x(0)>0$.
:::

<1>2. A solution that starts below $1$ remains below $1$, and a solution that starts above $1$ remains above $1$.

::: {.proof}
The constant function $x\equiv1$ is a solution. As in step <1>1, uniqueness prevents any other solution from meeting this equilibrium unless it is identically $1$.
:::

<1>3. If $0<x(0)<1$, then $x(t)$ is strictly increasing and bounded above by $1$.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1--<1>2,
$$
0<x(t)<1
$$
for all $t\ge0$. On this interval,
$$
F(x)=x^2(1-x^4)>0,
$$
so $x'(t)>0$.
:::

<1>4. If $x(0)>1$, then $x(t)$ is strictly decreasing and bounded below by $1$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $x(t)>1$ for all $t\ge0$. Hence
$$
F(x)=x^2(1-x^4)<0,
$$
so $x'(t)<0$.
:::

<1>5. If $0<x(0)<1$, then
$$
\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, the limit
$$
L\coloneqq\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)
$$
exists with $0<L\le1$. Suppose $L<1$. Then $F(L)>0$. By continuity of $F$, there are numbers $\eta>0$ and $T$ such that
$$
F(x(t))\ge\eta
$$
for every $t\ge T$. Thus $x'(t)\ge\eta$, so
$$
x(t)\ge x(T)+\eta(t-T),
$$
contradicting the upper bound $x(t)<1$. Hence $L=1$.
:::

<1>6. If $x(0)>1$, then
$$
\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4, the limit $L$ exists with $L\ge1$. If $L>1$, then $F(L)<0$. By continuity, there are $\eta>0$ and $T$ such that
$$
F(x(t))\le-\eta
$$
for every $t\ge T$. Thus
$$
x(t)\le x(T)-\eta(t-T),
$$
contradicting the lower bound $x(t)>1$. Therefore $L=1$.
:::

<1>7. Every solution in the problem satisfies
$$
\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=1}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $x(0)=1$, uniqueness gives the constant solution $x\equiv1$. The cases $0<x(0)<1$ and $x(0)>1$ are steps <1>5 and <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 is exactly the assertion.
