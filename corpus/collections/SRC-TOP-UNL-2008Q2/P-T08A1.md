::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a subset of a topological space $X$ and let $B$ be a subset of a topological space $Y$.
Let $X\times Y$ be the product space, and let $\operatorname{int}_Z(C)$ denote the interior of the set $C$ in the space $Z$.
Prove or give a counterexample to $$\operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A\times B)=\operatorname{int}_X(A)\times\operatorname{int}_Y(B).$$
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Prove $\operatorname{int}_{X \times Y}(A \times B) = \operatorname{int}_X(A) \times \operatorname{int}_Y(B)$.

<1>1. $\operatorname{int}_X(A) \times \operatorname{int}_Y(B) \subseteq \operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A \times B)$.
<2>1. $\operatorname{int}_X(A) \times \operatorname{int}_Y(B)$ is open in $X \times Y$.
Proof: a product of open sets is open in the product topology.
<2>2. It is contained in $A \times B$.
Proof: $\operatorname{int}_X(A) \subseteq A$ and $\operatorname{int}_Y(B) \subseteq B$.
<2>3. Hence it is contained in the interior of $A \times B$.
Proof: the interior is the largest open set contained in $A \times B$.

<1>2. $\operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A \times B) \subseteq \operatorname{int}_X(A) \times \operatorname{int}_Y(B)$.
<2>1. Let $(x, y) \in \operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A \times B)$.
Proof: take an arbitrary interior point.
<2>2. There is a basic open set $U \times V$ with $(x, y) \in U \times V \subseteq A \times B$.
Proof: the product topology has a basis of open rectangles, and the interior point has a basic neighborhood inside $A \times B$.
<2>3. Then $U \subseteq A$ and $V \subseteq B$.
Proof: if $U \times V \subseteq A \times B$, then projecting gives $U \subseteq A$ and $V \subseteq B$.
<2>4. Hence $x \in \operatorname{int}_X(A)$ and $y \in \operatorname{int}_Y(B)$.
Proof: $U$ is an open neighborhood of $x$ contained in $A$, and $V$ is an open neighborhood of $y$ contained in $B$.
<2>5. Hence $(x, y) \in \operatorname{int}_X(A) \times \operatorname{int}_Y(B)$.
Proof: by <1>2.4.

<1>3. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2 give both inclusions, so the equality holds.
:::
