::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a subset of a topological space $X$ and let $B$ be a subset of a topological space $Y$.
Let $X\times Y$ be the product space, and let $\operatorname{int}_Z(C)$ denote the interior of the set $C$ in the space $Z$.
Prove or give a counterexample to $$\operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A\times B)=\operatorname{int}_X(A)\times\operatorname{int}_Y(B).$$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. We have
\[
\operatorname{int}_X(A)\times\operatorname{int}_Y(B)
\subseteq
\operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A\times B).
\]
::: {.proof}
Both $\operatorname{int}_X(A)$ and $\operatorname{int}_Y(B)$ are open, so their product is open in $X\times Y$.
Also
\[
\operatorname{int}_X(A)\times\operatorname{int}_Y(B)\subseteq A\times B.
\]
The interior of $A\times B$ is the largest open subset of $A\times B$, which proves the inclusion.
:::

<1>2. We also have
\[
\operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A\times B)
\subseteq
\operatorname{int}_X(A)\times\operatorname{int}_Y(B).
\]
::: {.proof}
Take
\[
(x,y)\in\operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A\times B).
\]
Since products of open sets form a basis for the product topology, there are open sets $U\subseteq X$ and $V\subseteq Y$ such that
\[
(x,y)\in U\times V\subseteq A\times B.
\]
In particular $U$ and $V$ are nonempty.
For any $u\in U$, choose the fixed point $y\in V$; then
\[
(u,y)\in U\times V\subseteq A\times B,
\]
so $u\in A$.
Thus $U\subseteq A$.
Similarly, using the fixed point $x\in U$, we obtain $V\subseteq B$.
Therefore
\[
x\in\operatorname{int}_X(A),
\qquad
y\in\operatorname{int}_Y(B),
\]
and hence
\[
(x,y)\in\operatorname{int}_X(A)\times\operatorname{int}_Y(B).
\]
:::

<1>3. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{int}_{X\times Y}(A\times B)
=
\operatorname{int}_X(A)\times\operatorname{int}_Y(B).
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>1 and <1>2.
:::
:::
