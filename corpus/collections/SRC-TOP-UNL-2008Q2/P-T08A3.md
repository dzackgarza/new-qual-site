::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that a map $f:X\to Y$ between topological spaces is continuous if and only if $$f(\overline A)\subseteq\overline{f(A)}$$ for all $A\subseteq X$.

(b) Prove that if $f$ is continuous and $f(\overline A)$ is closed for some $A\subseteq X$ then $$f(\overline A)=\overline{f(A)}.$$
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $f:X\to Y$ is continuous, then for every $A\subseteq X$,
\[
f(\overline A)\subseteq\overline{f(A)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $x\in\overline A$.
To show $f(x)\in\overline{f(A)}$, let $V$ be any open neighborhood of $f(x)$ in $Y$.
Continuity of $f$ makes
\[
f^{-1}(V)
\]
an open neighborhood of $x$.
Since $x\in\overline A$, this neighborhood meets $A$; choose
\[
a\in f^{-1}(V)\cap A.
\]
Then
\[
f(a)\in V\cap f(A),
\]
so every open neighborhood of $f(x)$ meets $f(A)$.
Thus $f(x)\in\overline{f(A)}$.
:::

<1>2. Conversely, suppose that
\[
f(\overline A)\subseteq\overline{f(A)}
\]
for every $A\subseteq X$.
Then $f$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
It is enough to show that the inverse image of every closed subset of $Y$ is closed.
Let $C\subseteq Y$ be closed and put
\[
A=f^{-1}(C).
\]
By the assumed closure property,
\[
f(\overline A)
\subseteq
\overline{f(A)}.
\]
Since $f(A)\subseteq C$ and $C$ is closed,
\[
\overline{f(A)}\subseteq C.
\]
Hence
\[
f(\overline A)\subseteq C,
\]
which says
\[
\overline A\subseteq f^{-1}(C)=A.
\]
The reverse inclusion $A\subseteq\overline A$ always holds, so $A=\overline A$ is closed.
Therefore inverse images of closed sets under $f$ are closed, and $f$ is continuous.
:::

<1>3. This proves part (a):
\[
f\text{ is continuous}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f(\overline A)\subseteq\overline{f(A)}
\text{ for all }A\subseteq X.
\]
::: {.proof}
The forward implication is <1>1 and the converse is <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Suppose now that $f$ is continuous and that $f(\overline A)$ is closed for a fixed $A\subseteq X$.
Then
\[
f(\overline A)=\overline{f(A)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By part (a), continuity gives
\[
f(\overline A)\subseteq\overline{f(A)}.
\]
For the reverse inclusion, $A\subseteq\overline A$ implies
\[
f(A)\subseteq f(\overline A).
\]
The set $f(\overline A)$ is closed by hypothesis, so it contains the closure of its subset $f(A)$:
\[
\overline{f(A)}\subseteq f(\overline A).
\]
Combining the two inclusions yields the desired equality.
:::
