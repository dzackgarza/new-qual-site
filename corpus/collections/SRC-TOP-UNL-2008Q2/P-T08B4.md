::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a hexagon in $\mathbb R^2$.
Define an equivalence relation on $X$ corresponding to labeling the 6 edges in the boundary of $X$ in a counterclockwise fashion in order by: counterclockwise $a$, counterclockwise $b$, counterclockwise $a$, counterclockwise $b$, counterclockwise $a$, counterclockwise $b$.
Let $M$ be the corresponding quotient space.
Compute $H_n(M)$ for all $n\ge 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Label the vertices of the hexagon counterclockwise by $v_0,\ldots,v_5$, so the boundary edges are
\[
a:v_0\to v_1,
\quad
b:v_1\to v_2,
\quad
a:v_2\to v_3,
\quad
b:v_3\to v_4,
\quad
a:v_4\to v_5,
\quad
b:v_5\to v_0.
\]

<1>1. The quotient $M$ has two $0$-cells
\[
u=[v_0]=[v_2]=[v_4],
\qquad
v=[v_1]=[v_3]=[v_5],
\]
two $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell $e^2$.
The edge $a$ is oriented from $u$ to $v$, while $b$ is oriented from $v$ to $u$.
::: {.proof}
Identifying all edges carrying the same label and orientation identifies their initial vertices with one another and their terminal vertices with one another.
For the three $a$-edges this identifies $v_0,v_2,v_4$ and separately $v_1,v_3,v_5$.
The $b$-edge identifications give the same two classes, with the opposite direction.
The interior of the hexagon is unaffected and gives one open $2$-cell.
:::

<1>2. The cellular chain groups are
\[
C_2(M)=\mathbb Z\langle e^2\rangle,
\qquad
C_1(M)=\mathbb Z\langle a,b\rangle,
\qquad
C_0(M)=\mathbb Z\langle u,v\rangle,
\]
and
\[
\partial_1(a)=v-u,
\qquad
\partial_1(b)=u-v.
\]
Consequently
\[
\ker\partial_1=\mathbb Z\langle a+b\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The formulas for $\partial_1$ are terminal vertex minus initial vertex.
For a chain $ra+sb$,
\[
\partial_1(ra+sb)=(r-s)(v-u),
\]
which vanishes exactly when $r=s$.
Thus the kernel is generated by $a+b$.
:::

<1>3. The cellular boundary of the $2$-cell is
\[
\partial_2(e^2)=3a+3b=3(a+b).
\]
::: {.proof}
The attaching loop of the hexagonal $2$-cell traverses the boundary word
\[
ababab
\]
with every occurrence oriented in the positive direction of the corresponding $1$-cell.
Hence the degree of the attaching map on each of the two $1$-cells is $3$.
The cellular boundary formula therefore gives
\[
\partial_2(e^2)=3a+3b.
\]
:::

<1>4. The zeroth and first homology groups are
\[
H_0(M)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(M)\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton contains both vertices joined by edges, so $M$ is connected and hence $H_0(M)\cong\mathbb Z$.
Also, by <1>2--<1>3,
\[
H_1(M)
=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=\mathbb Z\langle a+b\rangle/3\mathbb Z\langle a+b\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>5. We have
\[
H_n(M)=0
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $C_2(M)\cong\mathbb Z$ and
\[
\partial_2(e^2)=3(a+b)\ne0,
\]
the map $\partial_2$ is injective, so
\[
H_2(M)=\ker\partial_2=0.
\]
There are no cells in dimensions greater than $2$, hence $H_n(M)=0$ for $n\ge3$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
H_n(M)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,\\
\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z, & n=1,\\
0, & n\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>4 and <1>5.
:::
:::
