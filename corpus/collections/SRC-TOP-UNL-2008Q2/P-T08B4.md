::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a hexagon in $\mathbb R^2$.
Define an equivalence relation on $X$ corresponding to labeling the 6 edges in the boundary of $X$ in a counterclockwise fashion in order by: counterclockwise $a$, counterclockwise $b$, counterclockwise $a$, counterclockwise $b$, counterclockwise $a$, counterclockwise $b$.
Let $M$ be the corresponding quotient space.
Compute $H_n(M)$ for all $n\ge 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. CW structure of the quotient space $M$:
<2>1. The interior of the hexagon gives one 2-cell $e^2$.
Proof: definition of quotient space of a polygon.
<2>2. The boundary edges are partitioned into two equivalence classes of 1-cells, labeled $a$ and $b$.
Proof: edge labeling.
<2>3. Let the vertices in counterclockwise order be $v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4, v_5$.
The edges are $e_1 = a: v_0 \to v_1$, $e_2 = b: v_1 \to v_2$, $e_3 = a: v_2 \to v_3$, $e_4 = b: v_3 \to v_4$, $e_5 = a: v_4 \to v_5$, and $e_6 = b: v_5 \to v_0$.
Proof: edge-vertex incidences.
<2>4. The identifications on vertices yield exactly two equivalence classes (two 0-cells):
$V_0 = \{v_0, v_2, v_4\}$ (the initial vertex of $a$ and terminal vertex of $b$) and $V_1 = \{v_1, v_3, v_5\}$ (the terminal vertex of $a$ and initial vertex of $b$).
Proof: tracing vertex equivalence relations.

<1>2. Cellular chain complex and boundary maps:
<2>1. The cellular chain groups are $C_2 = \mathbb{Z}\langle e^2 \rangle$, $C_1 = \mathbb{Z}\langle a \rangle \oplus \mathbb{Z}\langle b \rangle$, and $C_0 = \mathbb{Z}\langle V_0 \rangle \oplus \mathbb{Z}\langle V_1 \rangle$.
Proof: cell counts from <1>1.
<2>2. The boundary map $d_1: C_1 \to C_0$ is given by:
\[
d_1(a) = V_1 - V_0, \quad d_1(b) = V_0 - V_1 = -(V_1 - V_0).
\]
Proof: initial and terminal vertices of edges $a$ and $b$.
<2>3. Thus $\operatorname{im}(d_1) = \mathbb{Z}(V_1 - V_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $\ker(d_1) = \mathbb{Z}\langle a + b \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
Proof: $u(V_1 - V_0) + v(V_0 - V_1) = (u - v)(V_1 - V_0) = 0 \iff u = v$.
<2>4. The boundary map $d_2: C_2 \to C_1$ is obtained from the boundary word $ababab$:
\[
d_2(e^2) = a + b + a + b + a + b = 3(a + b).
\]
Proof: degree of attaching map on 1-cells.
<2>5. Since $d_2(e^2) \neq 0$, $\ker(d_2) = 0$ and $\operatorname{im}(d_2) = 3\mathbb{Z}\langle a + b \rangle \subset \ker(d_1)$.
Proof: $C_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $d_2(1) = 3(a+b)$.

<1>3. Compute the homology groups $H_n(M)$:
<2>1. $H_0(M) = C_0 / \operatorname{im}(d_1) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2 / \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
Proof: $M$ is path-connected.
<2>2. $H_1(M) = \ker(d_1) / \operatorname{im}(d_2) = \mathbb{Z}\langle a + b \rangle / 3\mathbb{Z}\langle a + b \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$.
Proof: quotient of rank 1 free abelian group by its index 3 subgroup.
<2>3. $H_2(M) = \ker(d_2) / \operatorname{im}(d_3) = 0 / 0 = 0$.
Proof: $\ker(d_2) = 0$.
<2>4. $H_n(M) = 0$ for all $n \ge 3$ since $C_n = 0$ for $n \ge 3$.
Proof: dimension of $M$ is 2.

<1>4. Conclusion:
\[
H_n(M) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & n = 0 \\ \mathbb{Z}_3 & n = 1 \\ 0 & n \ge 2. \end{cases}
\]
Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>3.
