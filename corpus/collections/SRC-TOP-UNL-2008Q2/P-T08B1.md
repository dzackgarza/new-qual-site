::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the triangular parachute formed from the standard 2-simplex $\Delta^2$ by identifying the three vertices with one another.

(a) Compute a presentation for $\pi_1(X)$.

(b) Show that $\pi_1(X)$ is isomorphic to a free group $F_n$ for some $n$; what is $n$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let the three oriented edges of the boundary of $\Delta^2$ be $a,b,c$, oriented consecutively around the triangle.
After the three vertices are identified, denote their common image by $v$.

<1>1. The quotient has a CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$, three loop $1$-cells $a,b,c$, and one $2$-cell whose attaching word is
\[
abc.
\]
::: {.proof}
Identifying the three vertices changes only the boundary of the standard $2$-simplex.
Each of its three edges becomes a loop based at the common vertex $v$, while the interior of the simplex remains an open $2$-cell.
Traversing the boundary once in the chosen orientation traverses the three edge loops in order
\[
a,b,c.
\]
Thus the attaching map of the unique $2$-cell represents the word $abc$ in the free group on the three $1$-cells.
:::

<1>2. Therefore
\[
\pi_1(X,v)
\cong
\langle a,b,c\mid abc=1\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is
\[
S^1\vee S^1\vee S^1,
\]
so its fundamental group is the free group $F(a,b,c)$.
Attaching the single $2$-cell quotients this group by the normal closure of the attaching loop from <1>1. Hence the displayed presentation follows.
:::

<1>3. The presentation in <1>2 defines the free group on two generators:
\[
\pi_1(X,v)\cong F_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
The single relation
\[
abc=1
\]
is equivalent to
\[
c=b^{-1}a^{-1}.
\]
Thus $c$ can be eliminated from the presentation, leaving
\[
\langle a,b\mid\ \rangle,
\]
the free group on $a$ and $b$.
Equivalently, define
\[
\Phi:F(a,b)\longrightarrow\langle a,b,c\mid abc=1\rangle
\]
by the generators $a\mapsto a$, $b\mapsto b$.
It is surjective because the relation gives $c=b^{-1}a^{-1}$.
Conversely the assignments
\[
a\mapsto a,
\qquad
b\mapsto b,
\qquad
c\mapsto b^{-1}a^{-1}
\]
satisfy the relation $abc=1$ and therefore define a homomorphism in the reverse direction.
The two maps are inverse on generators.
Hence the group is $F_2$, so the requested rank is
\[
n=2.
\]
:::
