::: {.problem}
Let $p_i:(\widetilde X_i,\widetilde x_i)\to(X_i,x_i)$ be covering spaces for $i=1,2$.

(a) Show that the product space $\widetilde X_1\times\widetilde X_2$ together with the map $p:\widetilde X_1\times\widetilde X_2\to X_1\times X_2$ defined by $p(y,z):=(p_1(y),p_2(z))$ is also a covering space.

(b) Find the universal covering of the space $S^1\times D^2\times S^1$.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $(x_1,x_2)\in X_1\times X_2$.
Choose evenly covered open neighborhoods $U_i$ of $x_i$ for $p_i$ such that
\[
p_i^{-1}(U_i)=\coprod_{\alpha\in A_i}V_{i,\alpha},
\]
where every restriction
\[
p_i|_{V_{i,\alpha}}:V_{i,\alpha}\longrightarrow U_i
\]
is a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the covering-space property of $p_i$ at $x_i$, for $i=1,2$.
:::

<1>2. The product neighborhood $U_1\times U_2$ is evenly covered by
\[
p:\widetilde X_1\times\widetilde X_2\longrightarrow X_1\times X_2,
\qquad
p(y,z)=(p_1(y),p_2(z)).
\]
::: {.proof}
We have
\[
\begin{aligned}
p^{-1}(U_1\times U_2)
&=p_1^{-1}(U_1)\times p_2^{-1}(U_2)\\
&=
\left(\coprod_{\alpha\in A_1}V_{1,\alpha}\right)
\times
\left(\coprod_{\beta\in A_2}V_{2,\beta}\right)\\
&=
\coprod_{(\alpha,\beta)\in A_1\times A_2}
\bigl(V_{1,\alpha}\times V_{2,\beta}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Each product $V_{1,\alpha}\times V_{2,\beta}$ is open in $\widetilde X_1\times\widetilde X_2$, and the products are pairwise disjoint.
On such a product sheet, $p$ restricts to
\[
(p_1|_{V_{1,\alpha}})\times(p_2|_{V_{2,\beta}}):
V_{1,\alpha}\times V_{2,\beta}
\longrightarrow
U_1\times U_2.
\]
This is a homeomorphism, with inverse the product of the two inverse homeomorphisms.
Thus $U_1\times U_2$ is evenly covered.
:::

<1>3. Hence $p$ is a covering map, proving part (a).
::: {.proof}
Every point $(x_1,x_2)$ has the evenly covered neighborhood produced in <1>1--<1>2.
:::

<1>4. Define
\[
P:\mathbb R\times D^2\times\mathbb R
\longrightarrow
S^1\times D^2\times S^1
\]
by
\[
P(s,z,t)=\bigl(e^{2\pi i s},z,e^{2\pi i t}\bigr).
\]
Then $P$ is a covering map.
::: {.proof}
The exponential map
\[
q:\mathbb R\longrightarrow S^1,
\qquad
q(s)=e^{2\pi i s},
\]
is the standard covering of the circle, while
\[
\operatorname{id}_{D^2}:D^2\longrightarrow D^2
\]
is a one-sheeted covering.
Applying part (a) twice to
\[
q\times\operatorname{id}_{D^2}\times q
\]
gives exactly the map $P$.
:::

<1>5. The covering in <1>4 is the universal covering of $S^1\times D^2\times S^1$.
::: {.proof}
The space $D^2$ is contractible, as is $\mathbb R$.
Therefore their product
\[
\mathbb R\times D^2\times\mathbb R
\]
is contractible, for example by the straight-line homotopy to $(0,0,0)$.
In particular it is path-connected and simply connected.
A connected covering with simply connected total space is a universal covering.
Hence $P$ is the universal covering requested in part (b).
:::
