::: {.problem}
Let $Y=(0,1)\times(0,1)$ and let $X=Y\cup\{*\}$.
Let $\mathcal T_1:=\{U\mid U\text{ is a Euclidean open subset of }Y\}$, $\mathcal T_2:=\{X-C\mid C\text{ is a compact subset of the Euclidean space }Y\}$, and let $\mathcal T:=\mathcal T_1\cup\mathcal T_2$.
Then $(X,\mathcal T)$ is a topological space called the one-point compactification of $(0,1)\times(0,1)$ (you do not need to prove this).
Show that the space $(X,\mathcal T)$ is compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\mathcal U$ be an arbitrary open cover of $X$.

<1>1. Some member $U_0\in\mathcal U$ has the form
\[
U_0=X\setminus C
\]
for a compact subset $C\subseteq Y$.
::: {.proof}
The cover $\mathcal U$ contains a member $U_0$ with $*\in U_0$.
No member of $\mathcal T_1$ contains $*$, so $U_0\in\mathcal T_2$.
By definition of $\mathcal T_2$, there is a compact set $C\subseteq Y$ such that
\[
U_0=X\setminus C.
\]
:::

<1>2. For every $U\in\mathcal U$, the intersection $U\cap Y$ is open in the Euclidean topology on $Y$.
::: {.proof}
If $U\in\mathcal T_1$, then $U\subseteq Y$ is Euclidean open, so $U\cap Y=U$ is open in $Y$.
If $U\in\mathcal T_2$, write
\[
U=X\setminus K
\]
with $K\subseteq Y$ compact.
The Euclidean space $Y$ is Hausdorff, so its compact subset $K$ is closed in $Y$.
Hence
\[
U\cap Y=Y\setminus K
\]
is open in $Y$.
:::

<1>3. Finitely many members of $\mathcal U$ cover the compact set $C$.
::: {.proof}
Since $\mathcal U$ covers $X$ and $C\subseteq Y$, the family
\[
\{U\cap Y:U\in\mathcal U\}
\]
is an open cover of $C$ in $Y$ by <1>2. Compactness of $C$ gives members
\[
U_1,\ldots,U_m\in\mathcal U
\]
such that
\[
C\subseteq (U_1\cap Y)\cup\cdots\cup(U_m\cap Y)
\subseteq U_1\cup\cdots\cup U_m.
\]
:::

<1>4. The finite family
\[
U_0,U_1,\ldots,U_m
\]
covers $X$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
X=U_0\cup C.
\]
By <1>3, $C\subseteq U_1\cup\cdots\cup U_m$.
Therefore
\[
X\subseteq U_0\cup U_1\cup\cdots\cup U_m.
\]
Thus every open cover of $X$ has a finite subcover, so $X$ is compact.
:::
:::
