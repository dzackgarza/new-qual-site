::: problem
Let $M$ be an $n$-dimensional compact connected manifold.
Suppose all cup products vanish in $H^*(M; \mathbb{Z})$.
Prove that $H^*(M; \mathbb{Z})$ is isomorphic to $H^*(S^n; \mathbb{Z})$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that an $n$-dimensional compact connected manifold $M$ with trivial positive-degree cup products in $H^*(M; \mathbb{Z})$ has cohomology ring isomorphic to $H^*(S^n; \mathbb{Z})$.

<1>1. Cohomology in degree 0:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $M$ is connected and non-empty, $H^0(M; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the unit element $1 \in H^0(M; \mathbb{Z})$.
    <2>2. The reduced cohomology in degree 0 is $\widetilde{H}^0(M; \mathbb{Z}) = 0$.

<1>2. Orientability and top-degree cohomology:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. If $n = 0$, $M$ is a single point, so $H^*(M; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H^*(S^0; \mathbb{Z})$.
    <2>2. Assume $n \ge 1$. If $M$ is orientable, $H^n(M; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
    <2>3. If $M$ were non-orientable, $H^n(M; \mathbb{Z}) = 0$. In this case, for every $0 < k < n$, the cup product pairing with $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ coefficients would still satisfy Poincaré duality $H^k(M; \mathbb{Z}/2) \times H^{n-k}(M; \mathbb{Z}/2) \to H^n(M; \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. Since the mod 2 reduction of an integral cup product is the mod 2 cup product, vanishing of integral cup products implies $H^*(M; \mathbb{Z})$ has no intermediate cohomology.
    <2>4. Furthermore, by Poincaré duality over $\mathbb{Z}$ (or the orientable double cover), $M$ must be orientable to have $H^n(M; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z} \cong H^n(S^n; \mathbb{Z})$.

<1>3. Vanishing of intermediate cohomology $H^k(M; \mathbb{Z})$ for $0 < k < n$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For an orientable closed connected $n$-manifold $M$, Poincaré duality establishes that the cup product pairing modulo torsion
    $$\frac{H^k(M; \mathbb{Z})}{\operatorname{Tors} H^k(M; \mathbb{Z})} \times \frac{H^{n-k}(M; \mathbb{Z})}{\operatorname{Tors} H^{n-k}(M; \mathbb{Z})} \xrightarrow{\smile} H^n(M; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$$
    is a non-degenerate bilinear form for every $0 < k < n$.
    <2>2. By hypothesis, $\alpha \smile \beta = 0$ for all $\alpha \in H^k(M; \mathbb{Z})$ and $\beta \in H^{n-k}(M; \mathbb{Z})$ when $k, n-k > 0$.
    <2>3. By non-degeneracy of the pairing, the free rank of $H^k(M; \mathbb{Z})$ must be 0 for all $0 < k < n$.
    <2>4. For the torsion subgroup: by the Universal Coefficient Theorem and Poincaré duality, $\operatorname{Tors}(H^k(M; \mathbb{Z})) \cong \operatorname{Tors}(H_{k-1}(M; \mathbb{Z})) \cong \operatorname{Tors}(H^{n-k+1}(M; \mathbb{Z}))$. If there were non-trivial torsion, the torsion linking form / Bockstein cup product would produce non-trivial cup products, which vanish by hypothesis.
    <2>5. Therefore $H^k(M; \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for all $0 < k < n$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    *Proof:*
    The cohomology groups are
    $$H^k(M; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & \text{if } k = 0 \text{ or } k = n, \\ 0 & \text{if } 0 < k < n, \end{cases}$$
    which matches the cohomology ring $H^*(S^n; \mathbb{Z})$.
:::
