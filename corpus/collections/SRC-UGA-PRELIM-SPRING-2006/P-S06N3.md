::: {.problem}
Prove that for every positive integer $n$, $n^3 + 2n$ is divisible by $3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every integer $n$,
\[
n^3+2n=n(n-1)(n+1)+3n.
\]

::: pf-proof

Expanding the right-hand side gives
\[
n(n^2-1)+3n=n^3-n+3n=n^3+2n.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The product $n(n-1)(n+1)$ is divisible by $3$.

::: pf-proof

The integers $n-1,n,n+1$ are three consecutive integers, so exactly one of them is congruent to $0$ modulo $3$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $n^3+2n$ is divisible by $3$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the first term on the right-hand side of step [](#s1){.pf-ref} is divisible by $3$, and $3n$ is also divisible by $3$. Therefore their sum is divisible by $3$.

:::
