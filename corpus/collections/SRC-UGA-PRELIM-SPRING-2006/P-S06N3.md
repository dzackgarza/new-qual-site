::: problem
Prove that for every positive integer $n$, $n^3 + 2n$ is divisible by $3$.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. For every integer $n$,
\[
n^3+2n=n(n-1)(n+1)+3n.
\]
::: {.proof}
Expanding the right-hand side gives
\[
n(n^2-1)+3n=n^3-n+3n=n^3+2n.
\]
:::

<1>2. The product $n(n-1)(n+1)$ is divisible by $3$.
::: {.proof}
The integers $n-1,n,n+1$ are three consecutive integers, so exactly one of them is congruent to $0$ modulo $3$.
:::

<1>3. Hence $n^3+2n$ is divisible by $3$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, the first term on the right-hand side of <1>1 is divisible by $3$, and $3n$ is also divisible by $3$. Therefore their sum is divisible by $3$.
:::
:::
