::: {.problem}
Find an invertible matrix $Q$ so that $Q^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} Q$ is diagonal.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are $-1$ and $2$.
::: {.proof}
Because $A$ is upper triangular, its eigenvalues are its diagonal entries.
:::

<1>2. An eigenvector for $\lambda=-1$ is
\[
v_{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Directly,
\[
Av_{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\0\end{pmatrix}=-v_{-1}.
\]
:::

<1>3. An eigenvector for $\lambda=2$ is
\[
v_2=\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Solving $(A-2I)v=0$ gives
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-3&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}=0,
\]
so $y=3x$; taking $x=1$ gives $v_2=(1,3)^t$.
:::

<1>4. Set
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $Q$ is invertible and
\[
\boxed{Q^{-1}AQ=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
The columns of $Q$ are the eigenvectors from <1>2 and <1>3, and $\det Q=3\ne0$. Hence
\[
AQ=Q\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},
\]
and multiplying on the left by $Q^{-1}$ gives the displayed diagonalization.
:::
:::
