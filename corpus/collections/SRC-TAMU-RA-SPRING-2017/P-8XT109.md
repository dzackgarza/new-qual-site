::: {.problem}
Find all $f$ in $L^1(1,2)$ such that for every natural number $n$ we have $$\int_1^2 x^{2n}f(x)\,dx=0.$$ Give reasons for all assertions you make.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. The span of the even powers is uniformly dense in $C[1,2]$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
\mathcal A=\{P(x^2):P\text{ is a polynomial}\}.
\]
Because $x\mapsto x^2$ is a homeomorphism from $[1,2]$ onto $[1,4]$, every $u\in C[1,2]$ can be written as
\[
u(x)=v(x^2)
\]
for some $v\in C[1,4]$.
By the Weierstrass approximation theorem, polynomials are uniformly dense in $C[1,4]$; hence $\mathcal A$ is uniformly dense in $C[1,2]$.

If the convention $\mathbb N=\{1,2,\dots\}$ is used, the hypothesis only gives the span of $x^{2n}$ for $n\ge1$. This span is still dense: for any $u\in C[1,2]$, the function $u(x)/x^2$ is continuous, so approximate it uniformly by a polynomial $P(x^2)$ and multiply by $x^2$.
:::

<1>2. The integral pairing with $f$ vanishes on all continuous functions.
::: {.proof}
Let $u\in C[1,2]$ and choose finite linear combinations $p_m$ of the functions $x^{2n}$ such that
\[
\|u-p_m\|_\infty\to0.
\]
By the hypothesis,
\[
\int_1^2 p_m(x)f(x)\,dx=0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\left|\int_1^2 (u-p_m)f\right|
\le \|u-p_m\|_\infty\|f\|_1\to0.
\]
Thus
\[
\int_1^2 u(x)f(x)\,dx=0
\qquad\text{for every }u\in C[1,2].
\]
:::

<1>3. Conclude that $f=0$ almost everywhere.
::: {.proof}
The signed measure
\[
\nu(E)=\int_E f(x)\,dx
\]
is a finite signed Borel measure on $[1,2]$. By <1>2,
\[
\int u\,d\nu=0
\]
for every $u\in C[1,2]$.
By uniqueness in the Riesz--Markov representation theorem, $\nu=0$. Therefore
\[
\int_E f=0
\]
for every measurable $E$, which implies $f=0$ almost everywhere.

Conversely, $f=0$ a.e. plainly satisfies the stated moment conditions. Hence the only solutions are the functions equal to zero almost everywhere.
:::
