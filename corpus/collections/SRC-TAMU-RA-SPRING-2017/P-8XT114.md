::: {.problem}
Prove or disprove that the unit ball of $L^7(0,1)$ is norm closed in $L^1(0,1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The unit ball of $L^7(0,1)$ **is** norm closed in $L^1(0,1)$; the claim in the title is false.

<1>1. Let $B = \{f \in L^7(0,1) : \|f\|_7 \le 1\}$ be the unit ball of $L^7(0,1)$.
Proof: definition.

<1>2. Let $(f_n)_{n \ge 1} \subseteq B$ with $f_n \to f$ in $L^1(0,1)$.
Proof: take an arbitrary $L^1$-convergent sequence in $B$.

<1>3. Some subsequence $f_{n_k} \to f$ pointwise a.e.
Proof: convergence in $L^1$ implies a subsequence converges a.e.

<1>4. Then $|f_{n_k}|^7 \to |f|^7$ pointwise a.e.
Proof: <1>3 and continuity of $t \mapsto t^7$.

<1>5. $\int_0^1 |f|^7 \le \liminf_k \int_0^1 |f_{n_k}|^7 \le 1$.
Proof: Fatou's lemma applied to the nonnegative sequence $|f_{n_k}|^7$ (using <1>4), and $\|f_{n_k}\|_7 \le 1$ since $f_{n_k} \in B$.

<1>6. Hence $\|f\|_7 \le 1$, so $f \in B$.
Proof: <1>5.

<1>7. Therefore every $L^1$-limit of a sequence in $B$ lies in $B$, so $B$ is closed in $L^1(0,1)$.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>6.

<1>8. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>7.
:::
