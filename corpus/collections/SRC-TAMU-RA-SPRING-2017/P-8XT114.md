::: {.problem}
Prove or disprove that the unit ball of $L^7(0,1)$ is norm closed in $L^1(0,1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The unit ball of $L^7(0,1)$ **is** norm closed in $L^1(0,1)$; the claim in the title is false.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let $B = \{f \in L^7(0,1) : \|f\|_7 \le 1\}$ be the unit ball of $L^7(0,1)$.

::: pf-proof

definition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Let $(f_n)_{n \ge 1} \subseteq B$ with $f_n \to f$ in $L^1(0,1)$.

::: pf-proof

take an arbitrary $L^1$-convergent sequence in $B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Some subsequence $f_{n_k} \to f$ pointwise a.e.

::: pf-proof

convergence in $L^1$ implies a subsequence converges a.e.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Then $|f_{n_k}|^7 \to |f|^7$ pointwise a.e.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} and continuity of $t \mapsto t^7$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

$\int_0^1 |f|^7 \le \liminf_k \int_0^1 |f_{n_k}|^7 \le 1$.

::: pf-proof

Fatou's lemma applied to the nonnegative sequence $|f_{n_k}|^7$ (using step [](#s4){.pf-ref}), and $\|f_{n_k}\|_7 \le 1$ since $f_{n_k} \in B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Hence $\|f\|_7 \le 1$, so $f \in B$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

Therefore every $L^1$-limit of a sequence in $B$ lies in $B$, so $B$ is closed in $L^1(0,1)$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s6){.pf-ref}.

:::
