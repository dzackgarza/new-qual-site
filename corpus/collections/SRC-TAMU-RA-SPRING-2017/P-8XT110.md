::: {.problem}
A. Prove that there exists a sequence of measurable functions $g_n$ on $[0,1]$ such that:

(a) $g_n(x)\ge 0$ for any $x\in[0,1]$;

(b) $\lim_{n\to\infty}g_n(x)=0$ a.e.;

(c) For any continuous function $f\in C[0,1]$, $$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 f(x)g_n(x)\,dx=\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx.$$

B. If $g_n$ is a sequence of measurable functions on $[0,1]$ such that (a), (b), and (c) are satisfied, what can you say about $\int_0^1\sup_n g_n(x)\,dx$?
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Construct a sequence with summably small supports.
::: {.proof}
For $n\ge1$, set
\[
\varepsilon_n=2^{-n},\qquad m_n=n,
\]
and for $0\le k<m_n$ define
\[
I_{n,k}=\left[\frac{k}{m_n},\frac{k}{m_n}+\frac{\varepsilon_n}{m_n}\right].
\]
Let
\[
g_n(x)=\frac1{\varepsilon_n}\sum_{k=0}^{m_n-1}\mathbf 1_{I_{n,k}}(x).
\]
Then $g_n\ge0$ and is measurable. Its support
\[
S_n=\bigcup_{k=0}^{m_n-1}I_{n,k}
\]
has measure
\[
m(S_n)=m_n\frac{\varepsilon_n}{m_n}=\varepsilon_n=2^{-n}.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty m(S_n)<\infty.
\]
By the Borel--Cantelli lemma,
\[
m(\limsup S_n)=0.
\]
Thus for almost every $x$, $x$ belongs to only finitely many $S_n$, so $g_n(x)=0$ eventually. Therefore
\[
g_n(x)\to0\qquad\text{a.e.}
\]
:::

<1>2. The measures $g_n(x)\,dx$ converge weakly to Lebesgue measure.
::: {.proof}
Let $f\in C[0,1]$. Since $f$ is uniformly continuous, let
\[
\omega_f(\delta)=\sup\{|f(x)-f(y)|:|x-y|\le\delta\},
\]
so $\omega_f(\delta)\to0$ as $\delta\to0$.
For each $k$,
\[
\frac1{\varepsilon_n}\int_{I_{n,k}} f(x)\,dx
\]
differs from
\[
\frac1{m_n}f\left(\frac{k}{m_n}\right)
\]
by at most
\[
\frac1{m_n}\omega_f\left(\frac{\varepsilon_n}{m_n}\right).
\]
Summing over $k$ gives
\[
\left|\int_0^1 f(x)g_n(x)\,dx-
\frac1{m_n}\sum_{k=0}^{m_n-1}f\left(\frac{k}{m_n}\right)\right|
\le \omega_f\left(\frac{\varepsilon_n}{m_n}\right)\to0.
\]
The Riemann sums converge to $\int_0^1f(x)\,dx$. Hence
\[
\int_0^1 f(x)g_n(x)\,dx\longrightarrow\int_0^1f(x)\,dx.
\]
This proves A.
:::

<1>3. For any sequence satisfying A(a)--(c), the integral of the pointwise supremum is infinite.
::: {.proof}
Take $f\equiv1$ in A(c). Then
\[
\int_0^1 g_n(x)\,dx\longrightarrow1.
\]
Let
\[
G(x)=\sup_n g_n(x).
\]
Suppose, for contradiction, that $G\in L^1[0,1]$. Since
\[
0\le g_n\le G
\]
and $g_n\to0$ a.e. by A(b), the dominated convergence theorem would imply
\[
\int_0^1g_n(x)\,dx\longrightarrow0,
\]
contradicting the preceding limit. Therefore $G$ is not integrable. Because $G\ge0$,
\[
\int_0^1\sup_n g_n(x)\,dx=\infty.
\]
:::
:::
