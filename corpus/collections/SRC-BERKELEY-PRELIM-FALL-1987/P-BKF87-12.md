::: {.problem}
Let $A,B$ be real $n\times n$ matrices. Suppose there is a nonsingular complex matrix $C$ such that
\[
CAC^{-1}=B.
\]
Show that there exists a nonsingular real matrix $D$ satisfying
\[
DAD^{-1}=B.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Write
$$
C=X+iY
$$
with $X,Y$ real $n\times n$ matrices. Then
$$
XA=BX
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
YA=BY.
$$

::: pf-proof

The relation
$$
CAC^{-1}=B
$$
is equivalent to
$$
CA=BC.
$$
Substituting $C=X+iY$ gives
$$
XA+iYA=BX+iBY.
$$
Because all four matrices $XA,YA,BX,BY$ are real, equality of real and imaginary parts gives the two displayed identities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every real number $t$, the real matrix
$$
D_t=X+tY
$$
satisfies
$$
D_tA=BD_t.
$$

::: pf-proof

Using step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_tA
&=
XA+tYA\\
&=
BX+tBY\\
&=
B(X+tY)\\
&=
BD_t.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The polynomial
$$
p(t)=\det(X+tY)\in\RR[t]
$$
is not identically zero.

::: pf-proof

Viewed as a polynomial over $\CC$, its value at $t=i$ is
$$
p(i)
=
\det(X+iY)
=
\det C
\neq0,
$$
because $C$ is nonsingular. Therefore $p$ is not the zero polynomial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

There exists a real number $t_0$ such that
$$
D=X+t_0Y
$$
is nonsingular.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $p$ is a nonzero real polynomial. Hence it has only finitely many real roots. Choose a real number $t_0$ that is not a root. Then
$$
\det D
=
p(t_0)
\neq0,
$$
so $D$ is nonsingular.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The real nonsingular matrix $D$ from step [](#s4){.pf-ref} satisfies
$$
\boxed{DAD^{-1}=B}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
DA=BD.
$$
Since $D$ is invertible by step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, right multiplication by $D^{-1}$ yields
$$
DAD^{-1}=B.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives the required real similarity matrix.

:::
