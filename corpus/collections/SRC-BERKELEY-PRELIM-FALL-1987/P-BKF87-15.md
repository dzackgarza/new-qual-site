::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic on $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ and suppose
\[
f(1/z)=f(z).
\]
Assume also that $f(z)$ is real whenever $|z|=1$. Prove that $f(z)$ is real for every nonzero real $z$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function
$$
g(z)=\overline{f(\overline z)}
$$
is analytic on $\CC\setminus\{0\}$.

::: {.proof}
Fix $z_0\neq0$. Since $f$ is analytic near $\overline{z_0}$, it has a convergent power-series expansion
$$
f(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n(w-\overline{z_0})^n
$$
there. Hence near $z_0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(z)
&=
\overline{
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
a_n(\overline z-\overline{z_0})^n
}\\
&=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
\overline{a_n}(z-z_0)^n.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $g$ is analytic near every nonzero $z_0$.
:::

<1>2. For every $z$ on the unit circle,
$$
g(z)=f(z).
$$

::: {.proof}
If $\abs z=1$, then
$$
\overline z=\frac1z.
$$
The inversion hypothesis gives
$$
f(\overline z)
=
f(1/z)
=
f(z).
$$
Since $f(z)$ is real on the unit circle,
$$
g(z)
=
\overline{f(\overline z)}
=
\overline{f(z)}
=
f(z).
$$
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
g(z)=f(z)
$$
for every $z\neq0$.

::: {.proof}
The functions $f$ and $g$ are analytic on the connected domain
$$
\CC\setminus\{0\}.
$$
By step <1>2, they agree on the unit circle, which has accumulation points in this domain. The identity theorem therefore gives
$$
f\equiv g
$$
on $\CC\setminus\{0\}$.
:::

<1>4. If $x\in\RR\setminus\{0\}$, then $f(x)$ is real.

::: {.proof}
Since $\overline x=x$, step <1>3 gives
$$
f(x)
=
g(x)
=
\overline{f(\overline x)}
=
\overline{f(x)}.
$$
A complex number equals its conjugate exactly when it is real. Hence $f(x)\in\RR$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
