::: {.problem}
How many zeros, counted with multiplicity, does
\[
2z^5-6z^3+z+1
\]
have in the annular region
\[
1\le |z|\le2?
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
P(z)=2z^5-6z^3+z+1.
$$

<1>1. The polynomial $P$ has exactly three zeros in $\abs z<1$, counted with multiplicity.

::: {.proof}
On $\abs z=1$,
$$
\abs{-6z^3}=6,
$$
while
$$
\abs{2z^5+z+1}
\leq
2+1+1
=
4.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{2z^5+z+1}
<
\abs{-6z^3}
$$
on the unit circle. By Rouché's theorem, $P$ and $-6z^3$ have the same number of zeros in $\abs z<1$, namely three.
:::

<1>2. The polynomial $P$ has exactly five zeros in $\abs z<2$, counted with multiplicity.

::: {.proof}
On $\abs z=2$,
$$
\abs{2z^5}=2\cdot2^5=64,
$$
while
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{-6z^3+z+1}
&\leq
6\cdot2^3+2+1\\
&=
51.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{-6z^3+z+1}
<
\abs{2z^5}
$$
on $\abs z=2$. Rouché's theorem gives the same number of zeros as $2z^5$, namely five.
:::

<1>3. The polynomial $P$ has no zero on either boundary circle $\abs z=1$ or $\abs z=2$.

::: {.proof}
Each strict Rouché inequality in steps <1>1 and <1>2 implies in particular that the corresponding dominant term cannot be cancelled by the remaining terms. Thus $P(z)\neq0$ on both circles.
:::

<1>4. The number of zeros of $P$ in
$$
1\leq\abs z\leq2
$$
is
$$
\boxed{2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2 there are five zeros in $\abs z<2$, and by step <1>1 three of them lie in $\abs z<1$. Step <1>3 shows that neither boundary circle contains zeros. Hence the annulus contains
$$
5-3=2
$$
zeros, counted with multiplicity.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required count.
:::
:::
