::: {.problem}
Let $u$ be a positive harmonic function on $\mathbb R^2$:
\[
u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0.
\]
Prove that $u$ is constant.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

There is a $C^1$ function $v:\RR^2\to\RR$ such that
$$
v_x=-u_y,
\qquad
v_y=u_x.
$$

::: pf-proof

Consider the one-form
$$
\omega=-u_y\,dx+u_x\,dy.
$$
Its exterior derivative is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\omega
&=
(u_{xx}+u_{yy})\,dx\wedge dy\\
&=
0
\end{aligned}
$$
because $u$ is harmonic.

The plane $\RR^2$ is simply connected, so every closed $C^1$ one-form on it is exact. Hence there is a function $v$ with
$$
dv=\omega,
$$
which is precisely the pair of displayed derivative identities.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The function
$$
F(z)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y),
\qquad
z=x+iy,
$$
is entire.

::: pf-proof

The identities in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} are the Cauchy--Riemann equations
$$
u_x=v_y,
\qquad
u_y=-v_x.
$$
Thus $F$ is holomorphic on all of $\CC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The entire function
$$
H(z)=e^{-F(z)}
$$
is bounded.

::: pf-proof

Since $u$ is positive,
$$
\abs{H(z)}
=
\abs{e^{-u(x,y)-iv(x,y)}}
=
e^{-u(x,y)}
\leq
1
$$
for every $z\in\CC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The function $F$ is constant.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref} and Liouville's theorem, $H$ is constant. Since an exponential never vanishes,
$$
H(z)\neq0
$$
for every $z$. Differentiating
$$
H=e^{-F}
$$
gives
$$
H'=-F'e^{-F}.
$$
Because $H'=0$ and $e^{-F}\neq0$, one has
$$
F'=0.
$$
Therefore $F$ is constant on the connected plane.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The harmonic function $u$ is constant.

::: pf-proof

The function $u$ is the real part of the constant function $F$ from step [](#s4){.pf-ref}. Hence $u$ is constant.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
