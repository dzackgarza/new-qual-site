::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic in $|z|<1$ and suppose
\[
|f(z)|\le\frac1{1-|z|}.
\]
Show that, for
\[
a_n=\frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!},
\]
one has
\[
|a_n|\le (n+1)\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n<e(n+1).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The displayed estimate is for integers $n\geq1$, as indicated by the occurrence of $1/n$.

<1>1. For every $0<r<1$ and every integer $n\geq0$,
$$
\abs{a_n}
\leq
\frac1{(1-r)r^n}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By Cauchy's coefficient formula on the circle $\abs z=r$,
$$
a_n
=
\frac1{2\pi i}
\int_{\abs z=r}
\frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz.
$$
The hypothesis gives
$$
\max_{\abs z=r}\abs{f(z)}
\leq
\frac1{1-r}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{a_n}
&\leq
\frac1{2\pi}
\cdot
2\pi r
\cdot
\frac1{1-r}
\cdot
\frac1{r^{n+1}}\\
&=
\frac1{(1-r)r^n}.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. For every integer $n\geq1$,
$$
\abs{a_n}
\leq
(n+1)\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n.
$$

::: {.proof}
In step <1>1 choose
$$
r=\frac{n}{n+1}.
$$
Then
$$
1-r=\frac1{n+1}
$$
and
$$
r^{-n}
=
\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^n
=
\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n.
$$
Substitution gives the asserted inequality.
:::

<1>3. For every integer $n\geq1$,
$$
\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n<e.
$$

::: {.proof}
For every $x>0$,
$$
\log(1+x)<x.
$$
Indeed, the function
$$
h(x)=x-\log(1+x)
$$
satisfies
$$
h(0)=0,
\qquad
h'(x)=\frac{x}{1+x}>0
$$
for $x>0$.

Taking $x=1/n$ gives
$$
n\log\left(1+\frac1n\right)<1.
$$
Exponentiating yields the claim.
:::

<1>4. Hence, for every integer $n\geq1$,
$$
\boxed{
\abs{a_n}
\leq
(n+1)\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n
<
e(n+1)
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required estimate.
:::
:::
