::: {.problem}
Let $V$ be finite-dimensional and let $T:V\to V$ be diagonalizable. If $W\subseteq V$ is a linear subspace with $T(W)\subseteq W$, prove that the restriction $T|_W$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r
$$
be the distinct eigenvalues of $T$, and let
$$
E_i=\ker(T-\lambda_iI).
$$
Since $T$ is diagonalizable,
$$
V=E_1\oplus\cdots\oplus E_r.
$$

<1>1. The subspace $W$ is invariant under every polynomial in $T$.

::: {.proof}
The assumption
$$
T(W)\subseteq W
$$
implies inductively that
$$
T^k(W)\subseteq W
$$
for every $k\geq0$. Hence for any polynomial
$$
p(t)=a_0+a_1t+\cdots+a_mt^m,
$$
one has
$$
p(T)(W)
\subseteq
W.
$$
:::

<1>2. For each $i$, define
$$
p_i(t)
=
\prod_{j\neq i}
\frac{t-\lambda_j}{\lambda_i-\lambda_j}.
$$
Then $p_i(T)$ is the projection of $V$ onto $E_i$ along the sum of the other eigenspaces.

::: {.proof}
The Lagrange polynomial $p_i$ satisfies
$$
p_i(\lambda_i)=1
$$
and
$$
p_i(\lambda_j)=0
\qquad
(j\neq i).
$$
If $v\in E_j$, then
$$
T(v)=\lambda_jv,
$$
so
$$
p_i(T)v=p_i(\lambda_j)v.
$$
Thus $p_i(T)$ acts as the identity on $E_i$ and as zero on every $E_j$ with $j\neq i$. Since the $E_j$ form a direct-sum decomposition of $V$, this is exactly the stated projection.
:::

<1>3. If
$$
w=w_1+\cdots+w_r
$$
is the eigenspace decomposition of a vector $w\in W$, with $w_i\in E_i$, then every $w_i$ belongs to $W$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
w_i=p_i(T)w.
$$
By step <1>1, $p_i(T)$ preserves $W$. Since $w\in W$, it follows that
$$
w_i\in W.
$$
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
W
=
\bigoplus_{i=1}^r(W\cap E_i).
$$

::: {.proof}
Every vector $w\in W$ has an eigenspace decomposition in $V$. Step <1>3 shows that each component lies in the corresponding intersection $W\cap E_i$, so
$$
W\subseteq\sum_i(W\cap E_i).
$$
The reverse inclusion is immediate. The sum is direct because the sum of the $E_i$ in $V$ is direct.
:::

<1>5. The restriction $T|_W$ is diagonalizable.

::: {.proof}
For each $i$, every vector in $W\cap E_i$ is an eigenvector of $T|_W$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_i$. By step <1>4, the direct sum of these eigenspaces is all of $W$. Choosing a basis of each nonzero $W\cap E_i$ and taking their union gives a basis of $W$ consisting of eigenvectors of $T|_W$. Hence $T|_W$ is diagonalizable.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
