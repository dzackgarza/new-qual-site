::: {.problem}
Let $S$ be the set of real $C^1$ functions $f$ on $[0,1]$ such that
\[
f(0)=0,
\qquad
\int_0^1 f'(x)^2\,dx\le1.
\]
Define
\[
J(f)=\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx.
\]
Show that $J$ is bounded on $S$ and compute its supremum. Determine whether the maximum is attained, and if so find a maximizing function.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $f\in S$,
$$
J(f)=\int_0^1(1-x)f'(x)\,dx.
$$

::: pf-proof

Integration by parts gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx
&=
\left[(x-1)f(x)\right]_0^1
-\int_0^1(x-1)f'(x)\,dx\\
&=
\int_0^1(1-x)f'(x)\,dx,
\end{aligned}
$$
because $f(0)=0$ and the factor $x-1$ vanishes at $x=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The functional $J$ is bounded on $S$, and
$$
\abs{J(f)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt3}
$$
for every $f\in S$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref} and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{J(f)}
&\leq
\left(\int_0^1(1-x)^2\,dx\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_0^1f'(x)^2\,dx\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq
\left(\frac13\right)^{1/2}
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The second factor is at most $1$ by the defining condition for $S$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Define
$$
f_0(x)=\sqrt3\left(x-\frac{x^2}{2}\right).
$$
Then $f_0\in S$ and
$$
J(f_0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}.
$$

::: pf-proof

One has $f_0(0)=0$ and
$$
f_0'(x)=\sqrt3(1-x).
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_0^1 f_0'(x)^2\,dx
=
3\int_0^1(1-x)^2\,dx
=
1,
$$
so $f_0\in S$. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
J(f_0)
=
\sqrt3\int_0^1(1-x)^2\,dx
=
\frac{\sqrt3}{3}
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt3}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The supremum of $J$ on $S$ is
$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{\sqrt3}},
$$
and this value is attained at
$$
\boxed{f_0(x)=\sqrt3\left(x-\frac{x^2}{2}\right)}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
J(f)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt3}
$$
for every $f\in S$, while step [](#s3){.pf-ref} exhibits $f_0\in S$ with equality. Hence the upper bound is the supremum and is attained by $f_0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The maximizing function is unique.

::: pf-proof

If $f\in S$ satisfies $J(f)=1/\sqrt3$, then equality holds in both inequalities of step [](#s2){.pf-ref}. Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
f'(x)=c(1-x)
$$
for some constant $c\geq0$, and equality in the norm bound gives
$$
1
=
\int_0^1f'(x)^2\,dx
=
\frac{c^2}{3}.
$$
Thus $c=\sqrt3$. Since $f(0)=0$, integration yields
$$
f(x)=\sqrt3\left(x-\frac{x^2}{2}\right)=f_0(x).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} prove boundedness, identify the supremum, and exhibit a maximizing function; step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives uniqueness.
