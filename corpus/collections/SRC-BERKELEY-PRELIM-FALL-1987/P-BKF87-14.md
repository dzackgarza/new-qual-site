::: {.problem}
Show that the ring $M_n(F)$ of all $n\times n$ matrices over a field $F$ has no proper nonzero two-sided ideals.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $E_{pq}$ denote the matrix unit whose $(p,q)$ entry is $1$ and
whose other entries are $0$.

<1>1. Let $I\subseteq M_n(F)$ be a nonzero two-sided ideal. There are
$A=(a_{ij})\in I$ and indices $i,j$ such that
$$
a_{ij}\neq0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because $I$ is nonzero, choose a nonzero matrix $A\in I$. At least
one entry of a nonzero matrix is nonzero; call such an entry $a_{ij}$.
:::

<1>2. For every $p,q$,
$$
a_{ij}E_{pq}\in I.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $I$ is a two-sided ideal,
$$
E_{pi}AE_{jq}\in I.
$$
Multiplication by $E_{pi}$ keeps only row $i$ of $A$ and moves it to
row $p$, while multiplication by $E_{jq}$ keeps only column $j$ and
moves it to column $q$. Hence
$$
E_{pi}AE_{jq}=a_{ij}E_{pq}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Every matrix unit $E_{pq}$ belongs to $I$.

::: {.proof}
The scalar $a_{ij}$ is nonzero in the field $F$, so it is invertible.
By step <1>2,
$$
a_{ij}E_{pq}\in I.
$$
Because $I$ is a left ideal and
$$
a_{ij}^{-1}I_n\in M_n(F),
$$
we obtain
$$
E_{pq}
=
(a_{ij}^{-1}I_n)(a_{ij}E_{pq})
\in I.
$$
:::

<1>4. The ideal $I$ is all of $M_n(F)$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, each diagonal matrix unit $E_{pp}$ lies in $I$, so
$$
I_n=\sum_{p=1}^n E_{pp}\in I.
$$
For every $B\in M_n(F)$, the two-sided ideal property then gives
$$
B=BI_n\in I.
$$
Thus $I=M_n(F)$.
:::

<1>5. The only two-sided ideals of $M_n(F)$ are
$$
\boxed{0\text{ and }M_n(F)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The zero ideal is a two-sided ideal. Step <1>4 shows that every
nonzero two-sided ideal is the whole ring.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
