::: {.problem}
Find a curve $C\subset\mathbb R^2$ through $(3,2)$ with the following property. For $(x_0,y_0)\in C$, let $L(x_0,y_0)$ be the segment of the tangent line to $C$ at $(x_0,y_0)$ that lies in the first quadrant. Require that every $(x_0,y_0)\in C$ be the midpoint of $L(x_0,y_0)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Define
$$
C=\left\{(x,y)\in\RR^2:x>0,\ xy=6\right\}.
$$
Then $C$ is a smooth curve in the first quadrant and contains $(3,2)$.

::: pf-proof

The equation $xy=6$ with $x>0$ is the graph
$$
y=\frac{6}{x},
$$
so it defines a smooth curve in the first quadrant. Since
$$
3\cdot2=6,
$$
the point $(3,2)$ lies on $C$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $(x_0,y_0)\in C$, then the tangent line to $C$ at $(x_0,y_0)$ meets the positive coordinate axes at
$$
(2x_0,0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(0,2y_0).
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $y=6/x$ on $C$,
$$
y'(x_0)
=
-\frac{6}{x_0^2}
=
-\frac{y_0}{x_0},
$$
where the last equality uses $x_0y_0=6$. Thus the tangent line has equation
$$
Y-y_0
=
-\frac{y_0}{x_0}(X-x_0),
$$
or equivalently
$$
Y
=
2y_0-\frac{y_0}{x_0}X.
$$
Setting $Y=0$ gives $X=2x_0$, while setting $X=0$ gives $Y=2y_0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every point $(x_0,y_0)\in C$ is the midpoint of $L(x_0,y_0)$.

::: pf-proof

Both $x_0$ and $y_0$ are positive. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the tangent line has negative slope and intersects the coordinate axes at $(2x_0,0)$ and $(0,2y_0)$. Hence its portion in the first quadrant is exactly the segment joining these two points. Its midpoint is
$$
\left(
\frac{2x_0+0}{2},
\frac{0+2y_0}{2}
\right)
=
(x_0,y_0).
$$
Thus $(x_0,y_0)$ is the midpoint of $L(x_0,y_0)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} show that the curve
$$
\boxed{C=\left\{(x,y)\in\RR^2:x>0,\ xy=6\right\}}
$$
passes through $(3,2)$ and has the required midpoint property.

:::
