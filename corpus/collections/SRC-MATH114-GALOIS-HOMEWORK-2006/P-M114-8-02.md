::: {.problem}
Prove that the Galois group of $x ^ { 4 } - 5$ is isomorphic to $D _ { 4 }$ . Hint: prove that the degree of the splitting field is 8, then recall that the Galois group is a subgroup of $S _ { 4 }$
:::

Let
$$
f(x)
\coloneqq
x^4-5,
$$
and set
$$
\alpha
\coloneqq
\sqrt[4]{5}>0.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The polynomial $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.


::: pf-proof

Apply Eisenstein's criterion at the prime
$$
5.
$$
The leading coefficient is not divisible by $5$, every other nonleading coefficient is divisible by $5$, and the constant term
$$
-5
$$
is not divisible by
$$
25.
$$
Hence
$$
x^4-5
$$
is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]
=
4.
$$


::: pf-proof

The element $\alpha$ is a root of the irreducible quartic from step <1>1, so its minimal polynomial has degree $4$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The roots of $f$ in $\mathbb C$ are
$$
\boxed{
\alpha,
\quad
-\alpha,
\quad
i\alpha,
\quad
-i\alpha.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

These are the four fourth roots of $5$.
Each satisfies
$$
(\pm\alpha)^4
=
(\pm i\alpha)^4
=
5.
$$
They are distinct, so they are all roots of the quartic.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The splitting field of $f$ is
$$
\boxed{
F
=
\mathbb Q(\alpha,i).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The field
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha,i)
$$
contains all four roots in step <1>3, so it contains the splitting field.

Conversely, the splitting field contains
$$
\alpha
$$
and
$$
i\alpha.
$$
Since
$$
\alpha\ne0,
$$
it also contains
$$
i
=
\frac{i\alpha}{\alpha}.
$$
Thus it contains
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha,i).
$$
Hence equality holds.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The field
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha)
$$
is contained in $\mathbb R$, so
$$
i\notin\mathbb Q(\alpha).
$$


::: pf-proof

The generator
$$
\alpha
$$
is real, and every rational expression in a real number with rational coefficients is real.
Thus
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha)
\subseteq
\mathbb R.
$$
Since
$$
i
\notin
\mathbb R,
$$
it cannot lie in
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
[F:\mathbb Q(\alpha)]
=
2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The element
$$
i
$$
satisfies
$$
x^2+1
\in
\mathbb Q(\alpha)[x].
$$
By step <1>5 it is not already in the base field.
Therefore its minimal polynomial over
$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha)
$$
has degree $2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
[F:\mathbb Q]
=
8.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By the tower law and steps <1>2 and <1>6,
$$
\begin{aligned}
[F:\mathbb Q]
&=
[F:\mathbb Q(\alpha)]
[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\\
&=
2\cdot4\\
&=
8.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The extension
$$
F/\mathbb Q
$$
is Galois.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>4, $F$ is the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of
$$
x^4-5.
$$
The polynomial is separable because the characteristic is $0$.
Thus the splitting field is normal and separable.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently,
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)}
=
8.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a finite Galois extension, the order of the Galois group equals the field degree.
Apply step <1>7.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Galois group acts faithfully on the four roots of $f$.


::: pf-proof

Every
$$
\sigma
\in
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)
$$
permutes the roots of
$$
f,
$$
because it fixes the coefficients.
This gives a homomorphism
$$
\rho:
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)
\to
S_4.
$$

If an automorphism fixes all four roots, then in particular it fixes
$$
\alpha
$$
and
$$
i\alpha.
$$
Hence it fixes
$$
i
=
\frac{i\alpha}{\alpha},
$$
and therefore fixes the generators
$$
\alpha,i
$$
of $F$.
Thus it is the identity.
Hence
$$
\rho
$$
is injective.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)
$$
is a subgroup of $S_4$ of order $8$.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>10 gives an embedding into $S_4$, and step <1>9 gives the order.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every subgroup of $S_4$ of order $8$ is a Sylow $2$-subgroup.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
\abs{S_4}
=
24
=
2^3\cdot3.
$$
Thus the largest power of $2$ dividing
$$
\abs{S_4}
$$
is
$$
8.
$$
Any subgroup of order $8$ is therefore a Sylow $2$-subgroup.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The subgroup
$$
P
\coloneqq
\langle
r,s
\rangle
\le
S_4,
$$
where
$$
r=(1234),
\qquad
s=(13),
$$
is a dihedral group of order $8$.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
r^4=1,
\qquad
s^2=1.
$$
Conjugation gives
$$
srs
=
r^{-1}.
$$
Therefore every word reduces to one of
$$
1,r,r^2,r^3,
s,rs,r^2s,r^3s.
$$
These eight permutations are distinct, so
$$
\abs P=8.
$$
The relations
$$
r^4=s^2=1,
\qquad
srs=r^{-1}
$$
are the standard presentation of the dihedral group of the square.
Thus
$$
P\cong D_4,
$$
where
$$
\abs{D_4}=8.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_4$ is isomorphic to $D_4$.


::: pf-proof

By Sylow's conjugacy theorem, all Sylow $2$-subgroups of $S_4$ are conjugate.
Step <1>13 gives one Sylow $2$-subgroup isomorphic to $D_4$.
Conjugate subgroups are isomorphic.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)
\cong
D_4.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps <1>11--<1>12, the Galois group is a Sylow $2$-subgroup of
$$
S_4.
$$
Step <1>14 identifies the isomorphism type of every such subgroup as
$$
D_4.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>15 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
