::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(x)=x^4+ax^2+b\in\QQ[x],\qquad b\ne0.
$$

(a) Prove that if $\alpha$ is a root of $f(x)$, then $-\alpha$ and $\sqrt b/\alpha$ are also roots.

(b) Prove that the degree of the splitting field is $1$, $2$, $4$, or $8$.

(c) Prove that the Galois group is isomorphic to $\{1\}$, $\ZZ_2$, $\ZZ_2\times\ZZ_2$, $\ZZ_4$, or $D_4$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix a square root
$$
\sqrt b
\in
\CC.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $\alpha$ is a root of $f$, then
$$
\alpha\ne0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
f(0)=b
\ne
0,
$$
the number $0$ is not a root.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $\alpha$ is a root of $f$, then $-\alpha$ is also a root.


::: pf-proof

The polynomial is even:
$$
f(-x)
=
(-x)^4+a(-x)^2+b
=
x^4+ax^2+b
=
f(x).
$$
Thus
$$
f(\alpha)=0
\implies
f(-\alpha)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $\alpha$ is a root of $f$, then
$$
\beta
\coloneqq
\frac{\sqrt b}{\alpha}
$$
is also a root.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\alpha\ne0.
$$
Compute
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(\beta)
&=
\frac{b^2}{\alpha^4}
+
a\frac b{\alpha^2}
+
b\\
&=
\frac{b}{\alpha^4}
\left(
b+a\alpha^2+\alpha^4
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since
$$
f(\alpha)
=
\alpha^4+a\alpha^2+b
=
0,
$$
the parenthesized expression vanishes.
Hence
$$
f(\beta)=0.
$$
This proves part (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The roots of $f$ are contained in
$$
\{
\alpha,
-\alpha,
\beta,
-\beta
\},
$$
where
$$
\beta=\sqrt b/\alpha.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} show that all four displayed values are roots whenever distinct.
A quartic has at most four distinct roots in $\CC$.
Thus every root of $f$ is among them.
Repeated roots merely make some displayed values coincide.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Let $F$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $\QQ$.
Then
$$
\boxed{
F
=
\QQ(\alpha,\sqrt b).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, every root is one of
$$
\pm\alpha,
\qquad
\pm\frac{\sqrt b}{\alpha}.
$$
Hence
$$
\QQ(\alpha,\sqrt b)
$$
contains all roots and therefore contains $F$.

Conversely, $F$ contains
$$
\alpha
$$
and
$$
\beta=\frac{\sqrt b}{\alpha}.
$$
Therefore it contains
$$
\alpha\beta
=
\sqrt b.
$$
Thus
$$
\QQ(\alpha,\sqrt b)
\subseteq
F.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The degree
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]
$$
is one of
$$
1,2,4.
$$


::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial
$$
m_\alpha(x)
$$
over $\QQ$ divides the quartic
$$
f(x).
$$
Thus its degree is at most $4$.

It remains only to rule out degree $3$.
Suppose
$$
\deg m_\alpha=3.
$$
Then
$$
f(x)
=
m_\alpha(x)(x-r)
$$
for some
$$
r\in\QQ.
$$
Thus $r$ is a rational root of $f$.
Because
$$
b\ne0,
$$
step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
r\ne0.
$$
By evenness,
$$
-r
$$
is also a root of $f$.
Since
$$
-r\ne r,
$$
the root $-r$ must be a root of the cubic factor
$$
m_\alpha.
$$
But then
$$
m_\alpha
$$
has a rational root, contradicting its irreducibility.
Therefore degree $3$ is impossible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The degree
$$
[F:\QQ(\alpha)]
$$
is either $1$ or $2$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
F
=
\QQ(\alpha)(\sqrt b).
$$
The element
$$
\sqrt b
$$
satisfies
$$
x^2-b
\in
\QQ(\alpha)[x].
$$
Thus adjoining it has degree at most $2$.
Hence the degree is $1$ or $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
[F:\QQ]
\in
\{1,2,4,8\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By the tower law,
$$
[F:\QQ]
=
[F:\QQ(\alpha)]
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ].
$$
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives the second factor in
$$
\{1,2,4\},
$$
and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives the first factor in
$$
\{1,2\}.
$$
The possible products are
$$
1,2,4,8.
$$
This proves part (b).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The splitting-field extension
$$
F/\QQ
$$
is Galois.


::: pf-proof

The field
$$
\QQ
$$
has characteristic $0$, so every irreducible polynomial over it is separable.
The splitting field of $f$ is therefore the splitting field of its squarefree part and is a normal separable extension.
Hence
$$
F/\QQ
$$
is Galois.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The Galois group acts faithfully on the distinct roots of $f$ and therefore embeds in $S_4$.


::: pf-proof

Every automorphism fixes the coefficients of $f$ and hence permutes its roots.
If an automorphism fixes every root, then it fixes the field generated by all roots, namely the splitting field $F$.
Thus the action is faithful.

There are at most four distinct roots, so the permutation group embeds in
$$
S_4.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
\le
S_4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

If
$$
[F:\QQ]=1,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
\cong
\{1\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a finite Galois extension, the order of the Galois group equals the extension degree.
A group of order $1$ is trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

If
$$
[F:\QQ]=2,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
\cong
\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every group of order $2$ is cyclic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

If
$$
[F:\QQ]=4,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
\cong
\ZZ/4
\quad\text{or}\quad
\ZZ/2\times\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Up to isomorphism, the only groups of order $4$ are the cyclic group
$$
\ZZ/4
$$
and the Klein four group
$$
\ZZ/2\times\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

If
$$
[F:\QQ]=8,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
\cong
D_4.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, the Galois group is a subgroup of
$$
S_4.
$$
If it has order $8$, then it is a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_4$, because
$$
\abs{S_4}
=
24
=
2^3\cdot3.
$$
All Sylow $2$-subgroups of $S_4$ are conjugate.
One such subgroup is
$$
\langle
(1234),(13)
\rangle,
$$
which has presentation
$$
\langle
r,s
\mid
r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}
\rangle
$$
and is the dihedral group
$$
D_4
$$
of order $8$.
Therefore every order-$8$ subgroup of $S_4$ is isomorphic to $D_4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
\cong
\{1\},
\ \ZZ/2,
\ \ZZ/2\times\ZZ/2,
\ \ZZ/4,
\ \text{or }D_4.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the only possible orders
$$
1,2,4,8,
$$
and steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} list all possible isomorphism types at those orders under the faithful embedding into $S_4$.
This proves part (c).

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (a), step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves part (b), and step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} proves part (c).
