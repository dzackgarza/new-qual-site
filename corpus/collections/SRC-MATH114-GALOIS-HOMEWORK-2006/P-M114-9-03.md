::: {.problem}
Find the Galois groups of $x^6-1$ over $\mathbb{F}_5 , \mathbb { F } _ { 2 5 }$ and $\mathbb{F}_{125}$
:::

::: {.solution}
For $q\in\{5,25,125\}$, let $K_q$ be the splitting field of $x^6-1$ over $\mathbb F_q$.

<1>1. Since the characteristic is $5$, the polynomial $x^6-1$ is separable over each base field.

::: {.proof}
Its derivative is $6x^5=x^5$ in characteristic $5$. A common root of $x^6-1$ and $x^5$ would have to be both nonzero and zero, which is impossible. Thus the polynomial has no repeated roots.
:::

<1>2. For a finite field $\mathbb F_q$, all sixth roots of unity lie in $\mathbb F_{q^m}$ exactly when
\[
6\mid q^m-1.
\]

::: {.proof}
The multiplicative group $\mathbb F_{q^m}^{\times}$ is cyclic of order $q^m-1$. It contains all sixth roots of unity exactly when it contains an element of order $6$, which is equivalent to $6\mid q^m-1$.
:::

<1>3. For $q=5$, the least such $m$ is $2$.

::: {.proof}
Modulo $6$,
\[
5\equiv-1,
\qquad
5^2\equiv1.
\]
Thus $6\nmid5-1=4$, while $6\mid5^2-1=24$.
:::

<1>4. Hence
\[
K_5=\mathbb F_{25}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(K_5/\mathbb F_5)\cong\mathbb Z_2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 and step <1>2 identify the splitting field as the quadratic extension $\mathbb F_{5^2}$. Every finite-field extension $\mathbb F_{q^m}/\mathbb F_q$ is Galois with cyclic group of order $m$, generated by the Frobenius $x\mapsto x^q$. Here $m=2$.
:::

<1>5. Over $\mathbb F_{25}$, the polynomial already splits, so
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(K_{25}/\mathbb F_{25})=1}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since $25-1=24$ is divisible by $6$, step <1>2 with $m=1$ shows that all sixth roots of unity already lie in $\mathbb F_{25}$.
:::

<1>6. For $q=125$, the least extension degree is again $2$.

::: {.proof}
Modulo $6$,
\[
125\equiv5\equiv-1,
\]
so $6\nmid124$, whereas
\[
125^2\equiv1\pmod6.
\]
Thus step <1>2 gives minimal degree $2$.
:::

<1>7. Hence
\[
K_{125}=\mathbb F_{125^2}=\mathbb F_{15625}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(K_{125}/\mathbb F_{125})\cong\mathbb Z_2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply the finite-field Galois-group statement from step <1>4 with $q=125$ and extension degree $2$. The group is generated by $x\mapsto x^{125}$.
:::

<1>8. Therefore the three requested groups are
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Z_2,\qquad 1,\qquad\mathbb Z_2}
\]
over $\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_{25},\mathbb F_{125}$, respectively.

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>4, <1>5, and <1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 gives all three Galois groups.
:::
:::
