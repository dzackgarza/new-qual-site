::: {.problem}
Assume that $f(x) = g(x)h(x)$ for some separable polynomials $f(x), g(x), h(x) \in F[x]$.
Denote by $E_f$, $E_g$ and $E_h$ the splitting fields of the polynomials $f(x)$, $g(x)$ and $h(x)$ respectively.
Let
$$
(E_f/F) = (E_g/F)(E_h/F).
$$
Prove that the Galois group of $f(x)$ is isomorphic to the direct product of the Galois groups of $g(x)$ and $h(x)$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The source writes $(E/F)$ for the degree $[E : F]$ of a field extension $E/F$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. One has
$$
E_f=E_gE_h.
$$

::: {.proof}
The roots of
$$
f=gh
$$
are exactly the roots of $g$ together with the roots of $h$. Hence the
splitting field of $f$ is the smallest field containing both splitting fields
$E_g$ and $E_h$, namely their compositum.
:::

<1>2. The extensions
$$
E_g/F,qquad E_h/F,qquad E_f/F
$$
are finite Galois extensions.

::: {.proof}
Each field is the splitting field of a separable polynomial over $F$.
Therefore each extension is finite, normal, and separable.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
E_gcap E_h=F.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because $E_g/F$ and $E_h/F$ are finite Galois,
$$
[E_gE_h:F]
=
\frac{[E_g:F][E_h:F]}
{[E_g\cap E_h:F]}.
$$
By step <1>1 and the hypothesis,
$$
[E_gE_h:F]
=[E_f:F]
=[E_g:F][E_h:F].
$$
Comparing the two formulas gives
$$
[E_g\cap E_h:F]=1,
$$
so
$$
E_g\cap E_h=F.
$$
:::

<1>4. Define the restriction homomorphism
$$
\Phi:
\operatorname{Gal}(E_f/F)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}(E_g/F)
\times
\operatorname{Gal}(E_h/F)
$$
by
$$
\Phi(\sigma)
=
(\sigma|_{E_g},\sigma|_{E_h}).
$$
Then $\Phi$ is well-defined.

::: {.proof}
Since $E_g/F$ and $E_h/F$ are normal, every $F$-automorphism of the
overfield $E_f$ preserves $E_g$ and $E_h$. Thus the two restrictions are
indeed $F$-automorphisms of the corresponding splitting fields.
:::

<1>5. The map $\Phi$ is injective.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
\Phi(\sigma)=(1,1).
$$
Then $\sigma$ fixes every element of both $E_g$ and $E_h$. By step <1>1,
these two fields generate $E_f$. Hence $\sigma$ fixes every element of
$E_f$, so
$$
\sigma=1.
$$
Thus the kernel is trivial.
:::

<1>6. The domain and codomain of $\Phi$ have the same finite cardinality.

::: {.proof}
Because the three extensions are finite Galois,
$$
|\operatorname{Gal}(E_f/F)|
=[E_f:F].
$$
Also,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\operatorname{Gal}(E_g/F)
\times
\operatorname{Gal}(E_h/F)|
&=
[E_g:F][E_h:F]\\
&=
[E_f:F],
\end{aligned}
$$
where the final equality is the hypothesis.
:::

<1>7. The map $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>5, $\Phi$ is injective. By step <1>6, it is an injection between
finite sets of equal cardinality, hence is surjective as well.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(f/F)
\cong
\operatorname{Gal}(g/F)
\times
\operatorname{Gal}(h/F).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By definition,
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(f/F)
=
\operatorname{Gal}(E_f/F),
$$
and similarly for $g$ and $h$. Apply step <1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the required conclusion.
:::
