::: {.problem}
Let $q = p^{k}$ for some prime $p$, let $n$ be a number relatively prime to $p$, and let $m$ be the minimal positive integer such that
$$
q^{m} \equiv 1 \pmod{n}.
$$
Show that the Galois group of $x^{n} - 1$ over $\FF_{q}$ is isomorphic to $\ZZ_{m}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $K$ be the splitting field of $x^n-1$ over $\FF_q$. Since $\gcd(n,p)=1$, the polynomial is separable.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The field $\FF_{q^r}$ contains all $n$th roots of unity if and only if
$$
n\mid q^r-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

The multiplicative group $\FF_{q^r}^{\times}$ is cyclic of order $q^r-1$. Since $p\nmid n$, all $n$ roots of $x^n-1$ are distinct. A cyclic group contains all $n$th roots of unity exactly when it contains an element of order $n$, which occurs exactly when $n$ divides its order $q^r-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

By the definition of $m$, the least $r>0$ for which $x^n-1$ splits over $\FF_{q^r}$ is $r=m$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} says splitting over $\FF_{q^r}$ is equivalent to
$$
q^r\equiv1\pmod n.
$$
The problem defines $m$ to be the least positive integer satisfying this congruence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
K=\FF_{q^m}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that $\FF_{q^m}$ contains every root, hence contains $K$. Conversely, choose a primitive $n$th root $\zeta\in K$. Since $K$ is a finite extension of $\FF_q$, say $K=\FF_{q^d}$. Then $\zeta\in\FF_{q^d}^{\times}$ has order $n$, so step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives $n\mid q^d-1$, and minimality of $m$ implies $m\mid d$. But $K\subseteq\FF_{q^m}$ gives $d\mid m$. Hence $d=m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The extension $\FF_{q^m}/\FF_q$ is Galois of degree $m$, and its Galois group is generated by the Frobenius automorphism
$$
\operatorname{Fr}_q:x\longmapsto x^q.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every element of $\FF_{q^m}$ is a root of $x^{q^m}-x$, whose derivative is $-1$, so the extension is separable; finite fields are normal over their subfields because all conjugates remain in the same finite field. The Frobenius fixes $\FF_q$, and
$$
\operatorname{Fr}_q^r(x)=x^{q^r}.
$$
Its $m$th power is the identity on $\FF_{q^m}$. If $0<r<m$ and $\operatorname{Fr}_q^r$ were the identity, every element of $\FF_{q^m}$ would be a root of $x^{q^r}-x$, impossible because that polynomial has at most $q^r<q^m$ roots. Thus Frobenius has order exactly $m$. Since the Galois group has order $[\FF_{q^m}:\FF_q]=m$, Frobenius generates it.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(x^n-1/\FF_q)
\cong\ZZ_m.}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} the splitting field is $\FF_{q^m}$, and by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} its Galois group over $\FF_q$ is cyclic of order $m$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
