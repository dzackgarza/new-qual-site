::: {.problem}
For a cubic polynomial $f(x) = x^{3} + ax + b$ the discriminant is given by the formula
$$
D = -4a^{3} - 27b^{2}.
$$
Assume that $a$ and $b$ are real numbers. Prove that $D$ is negative if and only if $f(x)$ has exactly one real root.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let the roots of $f$ in $\CC$ be $r_1,r_2,r_3$. Since $f$ is monic, its discriminant is
$$
D=(r_1-r_2)^2(r_1-r_3)^2(r_2-r_3)^2.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $f$ has exactly one real root, then $D<0$.


::: pf-proof

Because the coefficients are real, the two nonreal roots are conjugate. Write the roots as
$$
r\in\RR,
\qquad
z=u+iv,
\qquad
\bar z=u-iv,
\qquad v\ne0.
$$
Then
$$
D=(r-z)^2(r-\bar z)^2(z-\bar z)^2.
$$
The first two factors multiply to
$$
((r-z)(r-\bar z))^2=\abs{r-z}^4>0,
$$
while
$$
(z-\bar z)^2=(2iv)^2=-4v^2<0.
$$
Hence $D<0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $D<0$, then $f$ has no repeated root.


::: pf-proof

The discriminant product preceding step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} vanishes exactly when two roots coincide. Since $D<0$, it is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If all three roots are real and distinct, then $D>0$.


::: pf-proof

Every difference $r_i-r_j$ is then a nonzero real number, so each square $(r_i-r_j)^2$ is positive. Their product is positive.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Every real cubic has at least one real root.


::: pf-proof

For the monic cubic $f(x)=x^3+ax+b$,
$$
\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=-\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{x\to+\infty}f(x)=+\infty.
$$
Continuity and the intermediate value theorem therefore give a real zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $D<0$, then $f$ has exactly one real root.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, at least one root is real. Since the coefficients are real, nonreal roots occur in conjugate pairs, so a cubic has either one real root and one nonreal conjugate pair, or three real roots counted with multiplicity. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} excludes repeated roots, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} excludes three distinct real roots because $D<0$. Thus exactly one root is real.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{D<0\iff f\text{ has exactly one real root}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves the forward implication from the root configuration to the sign, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves the converse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is the required equivalence.

:::
