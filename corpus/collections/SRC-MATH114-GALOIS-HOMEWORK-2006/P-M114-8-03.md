::: {.problem}
Prove that the Galois group of $x ^ { 4 } + 5 x ^ { 2 } + 5$ over Q is cyclic of order 4. Hint: use the formula for the roots.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
f(x)
\coloneqq
x^4+5x^2+5.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The polynomial $f$ is irreducible over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Apply Eisenstein's criterion at the prime
$$
5.
$$
Every nonleading coefficient is divisible by $5$, and the constant term
$$
5
$$
is not divisible by
$$
25.
$$
Hence $f$ is irreducible over $\QQ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Solving the quadratic equation in
$$
y=x^2
$$
gives
$$
y
=
\frac{-5\pm\sqrt5}{2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The equation
$$
y^2+5y+5=0
$$
has discriminant
$$
25-20=5.
$$
Thus the quadratic formula gives the two displayed values.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Choose roots $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ satisfying
$$
\alpha_1^2
=
\frac{-5+\sqrt5}{2},
$$
$$
\alpha_2^2
=
\frac{-5-\sqrt5}{2},
$$
and choose their signs so that
$$
\boxed{
\alpha_1\alpha_2
=
\sqrt5.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The product of the squares is
$$
\alpha_1^2\alpha_2^2
=
\frac{(-5+\sqrt5)(-5-\sqrt5)}4
=
\frac{25-5}{4}
=
5.
$$
Hence
$$
\alpha_1\alpha_2
=
\pm\sqrt5.
$$
Changing the sign of one chosen square root if necessary makes the product equal to
$$
\sqrt5.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The four roots of $f$ are
$$
\boxed{
\alpha_1,
\quad
\alpha_2,
\quad
-\alpha_1,
\quad
-\alpha_2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the square of any root must equal one of the two values defining
$$
\alpha_1^2
$$
and
$$
\alpha_2^2.
$$
Each value has exactly the two square roots
$$
\pm\alpha_i.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

One has
$$
\sqrt5
=
2\alpha_1^2+5
\in
\QQ(\alpha_1).
$$


::: pf-proof

From
$$
\alpha_1^2
=
\frac{-5+\sqrt5}{2},
$$
multiply by $2$ and add $5$:
$$
2\alpha_1^2+5
=
\sqrt5.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

One has
$$
\alpha_2
=
\frac{\sqrt5}{\alpha_1}
\in
\QQ(\alpha_1).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\alpha_1\alpha_2
=
\sqrt5.
$$
Since
$$
\alpha_1\ne0,
$$
$$
\alpha_2
=
\frac{\sqrt5}{\alpha_1}.
$$
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} places the numerator in
$$
\QQ(\alpha_1),
$$
so the quotient lies there as well.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The splitting field of $f$ is
$$
\boxed{
F
=
\QQ(\alpha_1).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
\alpha_2
\in
\QQ(\alpha_1).
$$
Therefore all four roots listed in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} lie in
$$
\QQ(\alpha_1).
$$
Thus this field contains the splitting field.

Conversely, the splitting field contains the root
$$
\alpha_1,
$$
so it contains
$$
\QQ(\alpha_1).
$$
Hence equality holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The extension
$$
F/\QQ
$$
has degree $4$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
f
$$
is the minimal polynomial of
$$
\alpha_1
$$
over $\QQ$.
Its degree is $4$.
By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
F=\QQ(\alpha_1).
$$
Therefore
$$
[F:\QQ]=4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The extension
$$
F/\QQ
$$
is Galois and
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)}
=
4.
$$


::: pf-proof

The field $F$ is the splitting field of the separable polynomial
$$
f
$$
over a field of characteristic $0$.
Hence it is normal and separable, so it is Galois.
Its Galois group has order equal to
$$
[F:\QQ]=4
$$
by step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

There is an automorphism
$$
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{
\sigma(\alpha_1)=\alpha_2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The field
$$
F=\QQ(\alpha_1)
$$
is the splitting field of the minimal polynomial of $\alpha_1$.
The assignment
$$
\alpha_1\mapsto\alpha_2
$$
to another root of that polynomial defines a $\QQ$-embedding
$$
F\hookrightarrow F.
$$
Because $F/\QQ$ is finite, every injective endomorphism is surjective.
Hence this embedding is an automorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The automorphism $\sigma$ sends
$$
\sqrt5
\longmapsto
-\sqrt5.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
\sqrt5
=
2\alpha_1^2+5.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma(\sqrt5)
&=
2\sigma(\alpha_1)^2+5\\
&=
2\alpha_2^2+5\\
&=
2
\left(
\frac{-5-\sqrt5}{2}
\right)
+5\\
&=
-\sqrt5.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

One has
$$
\sigma(\alpha_2)
=
-\alpha_1.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
\alpha_2
=
\frac{\sqrt5}{\alpha_1}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma(\alpha_2)
&=
\frac{\sigma(\sqrt5)}
{\sigma(\alpha_1)}\\
&=
\frac{-\sqrt5}{\alpha_2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using
$$
\alpha_1\alpha_2
=
\sqrt5
$$
from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\frac{-\sqrt5}{\alpha_2}
=
-\alpha_1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Hence
$$
\sigma^2(\alpha_1)
=
-\alpha_1.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref},
$$
\sigma(\alpha_1)
=
\alpha_2.
$$
Apply $\sigma$ once more and use step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}:
$$
\sigma^2(\alpha_1)
=
\sigma(\alpha_2)
=
-\alpha_1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

The automorphism $\sigma$ has order $4$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\sigma^2(\alpha_1)
=
-\alpha_1
\ne
\alpha_1,
$$
so
$$
\sigma^2
\ne
1.
$$
Applying $\sigma^2$ again gives
$$
\sigma^4(\alpha_1)
=
\alpha_1.
$$
Since
$$
F=\QQ(\alpha_1),
$$
an automorphism fixing $\alpha_1$ fixes all of $F$.
Thus
$$
\sigma^4=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)=4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
\cong
\ZZ/4.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, the Galois group has order $4$.
By step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}, it contains an element of order $4$.
Hence that element generates the entire group, so the group is cyclic of order $4$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
