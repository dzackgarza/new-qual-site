::: {.problem}
Let $f \left( x \right) \in \ZZ _ { p } \left[ x \right]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree 3. Prove that $f \left( x \right)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_{p^5}$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\alpha$ be a root of $f$ in an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_p$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Since $f$ is irreducible of degree $3$ over $\mathbb F_p$,
\[
\mathbb F_p(\alpha)\cong\mathbb F_{p^3}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb F_p$ is $f$, so
\[
[\mathbb F_p(\alpha):\mathbb F_p]=3.
\]
By the finite-field cardinality formula, this field has $p^3$ elements, hence is the unique field $\mathbb F_{p^3}$ inside the chosen algebraic closure.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The intersection of $\mathbb F_{p^3}$ and $\mathbb F_{p^5}$ inside the algebraic closure is
\[
\mathbb F_{p^3}\cap\mathbb F_{p^5}=\mathbb F_p.
\]


::: pf-proof

Let $L$ be the intersection. Then
\[
[L:\mathbb F_p]
\]
divides both $[\mathbb F_{p^3}:\mathbb F_p]=3$ and $[\mathbb F_{p^5}:\mathbb F_p]=5$ by the tower law. Since $\gcd(3,5)=1$, the degree is $1$, so $L=\mathbb F_p$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The compositum is
\[
\mathbb F_{p^5}(\alpha)
=\mathbb F_{p^3}\mathbb F_{p^5}
=\mathbb F_{p^{15}}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Both finite fields lie in $\mathbb F_{p^{15}}$ because $3$ and $5$ divide $15$. Their compositum has degree
\[
\frac{3\cdot5}{[\mathbb F_{p^3}\cap\mathbb F_{p^5}:\mathbb F_p]}
=15
\]
over $\mathbb F_p$ by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, so it is the full field $\mathbb F_{p^{15}}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
\[
[\mathbb F_{p^5}(\alpha):\mathbb F_{p^5}]
=\frac{15}{5}=3.
\]


::: pf-proof

Apply the tower law to
\[
\mathbb F_p\subset\mathbb F_{p^5}
\subset\mathbb F_{p^{15}}.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb F_{p^5}$ therefore has degree $3$.


::: pf-proof

The degree of the minimal polynomial equals the extension degree
\[
[\mathbb F_{p^5}(\alpha):\mathbb F_{p^5}],
\]
which is $3$ by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence
\[
\boxed{f(x)\text{ remains irreducible over }\mathbb F_{p^5}.}
\]


::: pf-proof

The polynomial $f$ has degree $3$, has $\alpha$ as a root, and has coefficients in $\mathbb F_p\subset\mathbb F_{p^5}$. Therefore the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb F_{p^5}$ divides $f$. By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} both have degree $3$, so they differ only by a nonzero scalar; since $f$ may be taken monic up to scaling, this proves irreducibility.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
