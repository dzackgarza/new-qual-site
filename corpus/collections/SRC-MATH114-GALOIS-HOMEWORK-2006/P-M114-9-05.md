::: {.problem}
Let $f \left( x \right) \in \ZZ _ { p } \left[ x \right]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree 3. Prove that $f \left( x \right)$ is irreducible over $\FF_{p^5}$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\alpha$ be a root of $f$ in an algebraic closure of $\FF_p$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Since $f$ is irreducible of degree $3$ over $\FF_p$,
$$
\FF_p(\alpha)\cong\FF_{p^3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\FF_p$ is $f$, so
$$
[\FF_p(\alpha):\FF_p]=3.
$$
By the finite-field cardinality formula, this field has $p^3$ elements, hence is the unique field $\FF_{p^3}$ inside the chosen algebraic closure.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The intersection of $\FF_{p^3}$ and $\FF_{p^5}$ inside the algebraic closure is
$$
\FF_{p^3}\cap\FF_{p^5}=\FF_p.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $L$ be the intersection. Then
$$
[L:\FF_p]
$$
divides both $[\FF_{p^3}:\FF_p]=3$ and $[\FF_{p^5}:\FF_p]=5$ by the tower law. Since $\gcd(3,5)=1$, the degree is $1$, so $L=\FF_p$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The compositum is
$$
\FF_{p^5}(\alpha)
=\FF_{p^3}\FF_{p^5}
=\FF_{p^{15}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both finite fields lie in $\FF_{p^{15}}$ because $3$ and $5$ divide $15$. Their compositum has degree
$$
\frac{3\cdot5}{[\FF_{p^3}\cap\FF_{p^5}:\FF_p]}
=15
$$
over $\FF_p$ by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, so it is the full field $\FF_{p^{15}}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
[\FF_{p^5}(\alpha):\FF_{p^5}]
=\frac{15}{5}=3.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the tower law to
$$
\FF_p\subset\FF_{p^5}
\subset\FF_{p^{15}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\FF_{p^5}$ therefore has degree $3$.


::: pf-proof

The degree of the minimal polynomial equals the extension degree
$$
[\FF_{p^5}(\alpha):\FF_{p^5}],
$$
which is $3$ by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence
$$
\boxed{f(x)\text{ remains irreducible over }\FF_{p^5}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The polynomial $f$ has degree $3$, has $\alpha$ as a root, and has coefficients in $\FF_p\subset\FF_{p^5}$. Therefore the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\FF_{p^5}$ divides $f$. By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} both have degree $3$, so they differ only by a nonzero scalar; since $f$ may be taken monic up to scaling, this proves irreducibility.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
