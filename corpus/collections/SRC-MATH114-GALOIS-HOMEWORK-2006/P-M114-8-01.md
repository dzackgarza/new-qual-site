::: {.problem}
Let F be the splitting field of the polynomial $x^4+25$ over $\QQ$ . List all subfields in $F$ and the corresponding subgroups in the Galois group.
:::

::: {.remark}
The retained solution packet lists $\QQ(\sqrt5)$ and $\QQ(\sqrt{-5})$ as quadratic subfields.
Direct factorization of $x^4+25$ shows that the correct quadratic generator is $\sqrt{10}$, so the corresponding fields are $\QQ(\sqrt{10})$ and $\QQ(\sqrt{-10})$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
f(x)
\coloneqq
x^4+25.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The roots of $f$ are
$$
\boxed{
\frac{\sqrt{10}}2(1+i),
\quad
\frac{\sqrt{10}}2(-1+i),
\quad
\frac{\sqrt{10}}2(-1-i),
\quad
\frac{\sqrt{10}}2(1-i).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Set
$$
c
\coloneqq
\frac{\sqrt{10}}2.
$$
Then
$$
\bigl(c(1+i)\bigr)^2
=
c^2(1+i)^2
=
\frac{10}{4}\cdot2i
=
5i.
$$
Squaring again gives
$$
\bigl(c(1+i)\bigr)^4
=
(5i)^2
=
-25.
$$
Thus
$$
c(1+i)
$$
is a root of
$$
x^4+25.
$$
Multiplying by the fourth roots of unity
$$
1,i,-1,-i
$$
produces the other three roots, which are exactly the displayed four numbers.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every root of $f$ lies in
$$
\QQ(i,\sqrt{10}).
$$


::: pf-proof

Each root in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} is a rational multiple of a product of
$$
\sqrt{10}
$$
with an element of
$$
\{1+i,-1+i,-1-i,1-i\}.
$$
Hence every root belongs to
$$
\QQ(i,\sqrt{10}).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If
$$
\alpha
\coloneqq
\frac{\sqrt{10}}2(1+i),
$$
then
$$
i
\in
\QQ(\alpha).
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\alpha^2=5i.
$$
Therefore
$$
i
=
\frac{\alpha^2}{5}
\in
\QQ(\alpha).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One also has
$$
\sqrt{10}
\in
\QQ(\alpha).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
i\in\QQ(\alpha).
$$
Since
$$
\alpha
=
\frac{\sqrt{10}}2(1+i),
$$
one gets
$$
\sqrt{10}
=
\frac{2\alpha}{1+i}
\in
\QQ(\alpha).
$$
The denominator is nonzero and hence invertible in the field.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the splitting field is
$$
\boxed{
F
=
\QQ(i,\sqrt{10}).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that
$$
\QQ(i,\sqrt{10})
$$
contains every root of $f$, so it contains the splitting field.

Conversely, the splitting field contains the root $\alpha$ from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.
Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} show that it therefore contains both
$$
i
$$
and
$$
\sqrt{10}.
$$
Hence it contains
$$
\QQ(i,\sqrt{10}).
$$
Thus equality holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The extension
$$
F/\QQ
$$
has degree $4$.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
[\QQ(i):\QQ]
=
2.
$$
The real elements of
$$
\QQ(i)
$$
are precisely the rational numbers.
Since
$$
\sqrt{10}
\notin
\QQ,
$$
one has
$$
\sqrt{10}
\notin
\QQ(i).
$$
Thus
$$
[F:\QQ(i)]
=
2.
$$
The tower law gives
$$
[F:\QQ]
=
2\cdot2
=
4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The Galois group has four elements determined independently by
$$
i\longmapsto\pm i
$$
and
$$
\sqrt{10}\longmapsto\pm\sqrt{10}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The field $F$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial over $\QQ$, so
$$
F/\QQ
$$
is Galois.
By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)}
=
4.
$$

Any $\QQ$-automorphism must send $i$ to a root
$$
\pm i
$$
of
$$
x^2+1,
$$
and must send $\sqrt{10}$ to a root
$$
\pm\sqrt{10}
$$
of
$$
x^2-10.
$$
All four sign choices preserve the field relations and define automorphisms.
Thus these are exactly the four elements.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
\sigma:
\begin{cases}
i\mapsto-i,\\
\sqrt{10}\mapsto\sqrt{10},
\end{cases}
$$
and
$$
\tau:
\begin{cases}
i\mapsto i,\\
\sqrt{10}\mapsto-\sqrt{10}.
\end{cases}
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
G
\coloneqq
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\QQ)
=
\{1,\sigma,\tau,\sigma\tau\}
\cong
C_2\times C_2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Both $\sigma$ and $\tau$ have order $2$ and commute.
Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} shows that the four resulting sign choices exhaust the Galois group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The fixed field of
$$
\langle\sigma\rangle
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
\QQ(\sqrt{10}).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The automorphism $\sigma$ fixes $\sqrt{10}$ and negates $i$.
Thus
$$
\QQ(\sqrt{10})
\subseteq
F^{\langle\sigma\rangle}.
$$
Both fields have degree $2$ over $\QQ$:
the fixed field has degree
$$
\frac{\abs G}{2}
=
2
$$
by the Galois correspondence, while
$$
\QQ(\sqrt{10})
$$
is quadratic.
Hence equality holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The fixed field of
$$
\langle\tau\rangle
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
\QQ(i).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The automorphism $\tau$ fixes $i$ and negates $\sqrt{10}$.
The same degree argument as in step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives
$$
F^{\langle\tau\rangle}
=
\QQ(i).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The fixed field of
$$
\langle\sigma\tau\rangle
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
\QQ(i\sqrt{10})
=
\QQ(\sqrt{-10}).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The automorphism
$$
\sigma\tau
$$
negates both $i$ and $\sqrt{10}$.
Therefore it fixes their product:
$$
(\sigma\tau)(i\sqrt{10})
=
(-i)(-\sqrt{10})
=
i\sqrt{10}.
$$
Thus
$$
\QQ(i\sqrt{10})
\subseteq
F^{\langle\sigma\tau\rangle}.
$$
Both fields have degree $2$ over $\QQ$, so equality follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The complete Galois correspondence is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{subgroup of }G
&
\text{fixed field}\\
\hline
G & \QQ\\
\langle\sigma\rangle & \QQ(\sqrt{10})\\
\langle\tau\rangle & \QQ(i)\\
\langle\sigma\tau\rangle & \QQ(\sqrt{-10})\\
\{1\} & F=\QQ(i,\sqrt{10}).
\end{array}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The Klein four group has exactly five subgroups: itself, the three order-$2$ subgroups, and the trivial subgroup.
Steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} identify the three nontrivial proper fixed fields.
The full group fixes $\QQ$, and the trivial subgroup fixes all of $F$.
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, this is the complete list of intermediate fields.

:::
