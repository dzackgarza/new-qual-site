::: {.problem}
Let $F \subset E$ be an extension of finite fields.
Prove that
$$
|E| = |F|^{(E/F)}.
$$
:::

::: {.remark}
The source writes $(E/F)$ for the degree $[E : F]$ of a field extension $E/F$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
\[
q=|F|,
\qquad
n=[E:F].
\]

<1>1. The field $E$ is an $n$-dimensional vector space over $F$.

::: {.proof}
This is the definition of the extension degree $[E:F]=n$.
:::

<1>2. Choose an $F$-basis
\[
e_1,\ldots,e_n
\]
of $E$. Every element $x\in E$ has a unique expression
\[
x=a_1e_1+\cdots+a_ne_n,
\qquad a_i\in F.
\]

::: {.proof}
Existence and uniqueness of such coordinates are exactly the spanning and linear-independence properties of a basis.
:::

<1>3. Hence the coordinate map
\[
F^n\longrightarrow E,
\qquad
(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\longmapsto
\sum_{i=1}^na_ie_i
\]
is a bijection.

::: {.proof}
Surjectivity follows from the spanning property in step <1>2, and injectivity follows from uniqueness of the basis coordinates.
:::

<1>4. Since each coordinate has $q=|F|$ possible values,
\[
|F^n|=q^n.
\]

::: {.proof}
There are $q$ independent choices for each of the $n$ entries of an ordered tuple in $F^n$, so the multiplication principle gives $q^n$ tuples.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\boxed{|E|=|F|^{[E:F]}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By the bijection in step <1>3, $|E|=|F^n|$. Apply step <1>4 and substitute $q=|F|$, $n=[E:F]$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the desired cardinality formula.
:::
:::
