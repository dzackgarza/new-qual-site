::: {.problem}
(a) With notation as in the previous problem, give the definition of the Cayley graph of $B(n)$ as generated by $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_n$.

(b) Compute the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the adjacency operator of the graph in part (a).
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this problem: the collection's `provenance` names `assets/attachments/202-Qual-SP21.pdf`, the examination paper, which carries no solutions. The argument below is the card's own and follows the card's previous prose; it uses the paper's own definition of $B(n)$ as the permutation group on $\theset{1,\dots,2n}$ generated by the transpositions $\tau_i=(2i-1\ \,2i)$, $1\le i\le n$.

Throughout $B(n)$ is that group, $\Gamma=\operatorname{Cay}\bigl(B(n),\theset{\tau_1,\dots,\tau_n}\bigr)$, and $A$ is the adjacency operator of $\Gamma$ acting on functions on $B(n)$ by $(Af)(g)=\sum_{i=1}^nf(g\tau_i)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #identification}
$B(n)\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^n$, by $\tau_i\mapsto e_i$.

::: pf-proof
The transpositions $\tau_i=(2i-1\ \,2i)$ have disjoint supports, so they commute, and each has order $2$. They generate $B(n)$ by definition, so $B(n)$ is abelian of exponent $2$ with $n$ generators, hence a quotient of $(\ZZ/2)^n$; distinct monomials $\tau_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots\tau_n^{\varepsilon_n}$ act differently on $\theset{1,\dots,2n}$, so there are $2^n$ of them, which is the order of $(\ZZ/2)^n$. So the map is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #graph}
$\Gamma$ is the $n$-dimensional cube graph: $g$ and $h$ are adjacent iff $h=g\tau_i$ for some $i$, iff $g$ and $h$ differ in exactly one coordinate of $(\ZZ/2)^n$.

::: pf-proof
Two vertices are adjacent when $h=g\tau_i$ by the definition of a Cayley graph, and $\tau_i\ne1$ so $g\ne g\tau_i$: no vertex is joined to itself, and each vertex has exactly $n$ neighbours $g\tau_1,\dots,g\tau_n$, which are distinct because the $\tau_i$ are distinct and multiplication is by a fixed element. Under the identification of step [](#identification){.pf-ref}, $g\tau_i$ is $g$ with the $i$-th coordinate flipped, so adjacency is exactly "differs in one coordinate".

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #characters}
For $S\subseteq\theset{1,\dots,n}$ let $\chi_S(\tau_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots\tau_n^{\varepsilon_n})=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i}$. The $\chi_S$ are the $2^n$ distinct linear characters of $B(n)$ and form a basis of the space of functions on $B(n)$.

::: pf-proof
$\chi_S$ is a homomorphism $B(n)\to\theset{\pm1}$ by step [](#identification){.pf-ref}, since the exponent vector enters linearly. Distinct subsets give distinct characters, because $\chi_S(\tau_i)=-1$ for $i\in S$ and $+1$ otherwise. A finite abelian group has exactly $\cardinality{G}$ characters, so these $2^n$ exhaust them, and distinct characters of a finite group are linearly independent (orthogonality of characters), so they are a basis of the $2^n$-dimensional space of complex functions on $B(n)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eigen}
$A\chi_S=(n-2\cardinality{S})\chi_S$.

::: pf-proof
$(A\chi_S)(g)=\sum_i\chi_S(g\tau_i)=\chi_S(g)\sum_i\chi_S(\tau_i)$, using that $\chi_S$ is a homomorphism. And $\chi_S(\tau_i)=-1$ for $i\in S$ and $1$ otherwise, so the sum is $(n-\cardinality S)-\cardinality S=n-2\cardinality S$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
The eigenvalues of the adjacency operator are $n,n-2,n-4,\dots,-n$, the eigenvalue $n-2k$ occurring with multiplicity $\binom nk$, and the eigenvectors are the $\chi_S$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#characters){.pf-ref} and [](#eigen){.pf-ref} the $2^n$ basis vectors $\chi_S$ are eigenvectors; the eigenvalue depends only on $k=\cardinality S$, and there are $\binom nk$ subsets of size $k$. A basis of eigenvectors diagonalizes $A$, so this is the complete list of eigenvalues.

:::
