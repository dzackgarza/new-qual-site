::: {.problem}
(a) Let $B(n)$ be the permutation group generated by the transpositions $\tau_i = (2i-1\ 2i)$, $1 \le i \le n$.
Show that every irreducible character $\chi$ of $B(n)$ takes values in $\{-1, 1\}$.

(b) Explicitly describe the dual group of $B(n)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: $B(n)$ is an elementary abelian $2$-group, so its irreducible characters are the homomorphisms to $\{\pm1\}$, and they are indexed by subsets of the generators. No retained set solves this exercise, and the sets searched are recorded in the audit trail.

Let $\tau_{i}=(2i-1\ \ 2i)$ for $1\le i\le n$ and $B(n)=\langle\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}\rangle$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #group}
$B(n)=\langle\tau_{1}\rangle\times\cdots\times\langle\tau_{n}\rangle\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^{n}$, and every element is uniquely $\tau_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}$ with $\varepsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}$.

::: pf-proof
The transpositions $\tau_{i}$ move disjoint pairs $\{2i-1,2i\}$, so they commute pairwise and each satisfies $\tau_{i}^{2}=1$. Hence the subgroup generated by them is their product, and the product is direct because the $\tau_{i}$ are independent: if $\tau_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}=1$ then applying both sides to the point $2i-1$ gives $\varepsilon_{i}=0$, since $\tau_{j}$ fixes $2i-1$ for $j\ne i$. So the $2^{n}$ products are distinct, each is its own inverse, and the group is a direct product of $n$ copies of $\ZZ/2\ZZ$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #one-dimensional}
(a) Every irreducible complex representation of $B(n)$ is one-dimensional, so every irreducible character is a homomorphism $B(n)\to\CC^{\times}$.

::: pf-proof
$B(n)$ is abelian, and a finite abelian group has a common eigenvector for the images of all its elements in any representation: the operators commute, so they are simultaneously triangularizable over $\CC$ (or, equivalently, an eigenspace of one of them is stable under the others), and a one-dimensional invariant subspace spans a one-dimensional subrepresentation. An irreducible representation is nonzero, so it equals that subrepresentation.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #values}
(a) $\chi(\tau_{i})\in\{\pm1\}$ for every $i$ and every irreducible character $\chi$, and $\chi(g)\in\{\pm1\}$ for every $g\in B(n)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#one-dimensional){.pf-ref} $\chi$ is a homomorphism, so $\chi(\tau_{i})^{2}=\chi(\tau_{i}^{2})=\chi(1)=1$ and $\chi(\tau_{i})=\pm1$. For $g=\tau_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}$ multiplicativity gives $\chi(g)=\prod_{i}\chi(\tau_{i})^{\varepsilon_{i}}$, a product of $1$'s and $-1$'s, hence $\pm1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #chi-S}
(b) For each $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ there is a character $\chi_{S}$ with $\chi_{S}(\tau_{i})=-1$ for $i\in S$ and $\chi_{S}(\tau_{i})=1$ otherwise, and $\chi_{S}(g)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_{i}}$ on the normal form of $g$; distinct subsets give distinct characters.

::: pf-proof
$B(n)$ is the direct product of the $\langle\tau_{i}\rangle$, so assigning an element of $\{\pm1\}$ to each generator extends uniquely to a homomorphism: $\chi_{S}$ is the composite of the projection to the $i$-th coordinate with $(-1)^{\cdot}$ on the coordinates in $S$, which is a homomorphism because each coordinate is. The formula on the normal form follows by multiplicativity. If $S\ne T$ pick $i$ in one and not the other; then $\chi_{S}(\tau_{i})\ne\chi_{T}(\tau_{i})$, so the characters differ.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dual}
(b) $\widehat{B(n)}=\{\chi_{S}\}$ has $2^{n}$ elements, so it is all of the dual group, and $\chi_{S}\chi_{T}=\chi_{S\triangle T}$ identifies it with $(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^{n}$.

::: pf-proof
The dual group is the group of homomorphisms $B(n)\to\CC^{\times}$ under pointwise multiplication, and every such homomorphism is determined by its values on the generators, which lie in $\{\pm1\}$ by step [](#values){.pf-ref}; so every element of the dual is one of the $2^{n}$ characters $\chi_{S}$ of step [](#chi-S){.pf-ref}, which are pairwise distinct. Since $(2^{n})=(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^{n}=\cardinality{B(n)}$ and a finite abelian group has as many irreducible characters as elements, the list is complete. Finally
$$
(\chi_{S}\chi_{T})(\tau_{i})=\chi_{S}(\tau_{i})\chi_{T}(\tau_{i})=(-1)^{1_{i\in S}+1_{i\in T}}=(-1)^{1_{i\in S\triangle T}}=\chi_{S\triangle T}(\tau_{i}),
$$
and two characters agreeing on the generators agree everywhere, so $\chi_{S}\chi_{T}=\chi_{S\triangle T}$; the map $S\mapsto\chi_{S}$ is therefore an isomorphism of groups $(\text{subsets},\triangle)\to\widehat{B(n)}$, and both sides are elementary abelian of order $2^{n}$.
:::
