::: {.problem}
Show that the infinite series $\sum_{n=0}^\infty x^n2^{-nx}$ converges uniformly on $[0,B]$ for any $B > 0$.
Does this series converge uniformly on $[0,\infty)$?
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $h(x)\coloneqq x2^{-x}=xe^{-x\ln 2}$ and $r\coloneqq1/(e\ln2)$. The terms of the series are $x^n2^{-nx}=h(x)^n$, by the exponent law $2^{-nx}=(2^{-x})^n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$0\le h(x)\le r<1$ for every $x\ge0$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s2-1}

The function $h$ has its global maximum on $[0,\infty)$ at $x = 1/\ln 2$.

::: pf-proof

Differentiation gives $h'(x)=e^{-x\ln2}(1-x\ln2)$. Since $\ln2>0$, this derivative is positive for $0\leq x<1/\ln2$ and negative for $x>1/\ln2$. Thus the unique global maximum occurs at $x=1/\ln2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2-1){.pf-ref} gives $0\le h(x)\le h(1/\ln2)=1/(e\ln2)=r$. Moreover $\ln2=\int_1^2dt/t>1/2$ and $e>2$, so $e\ln2>1$ and $r<1$.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #tail}

For every $N\geq0$,
$$
\sup_{x\geq0}\abs{\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty x^n2^{-nx}}
\leq\frac{r^{N+1}}{1-r}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $\abs{x^n2^{-nx}}=h(x)^n\leq r^n$ for every $x\ge0$, and $\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty r^n=r^{N+1}/(1-r)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The series converges uniformly on $[0, B]$ for every $B > 0$.

::: pf-proof

The bound of step [](#tail){.pf-ref} tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$ and holds in particular on $[0,B]$. (This is the Weierstrass M-test with $M_n=r^n$.)

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\boxed{\text{Yes}}$: the series converges uniformly on $[0, \infty)$.

::: pf-proof

The bound of step [](#tail){.pf-ref} holds on the entire half-line and tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$.

:::
