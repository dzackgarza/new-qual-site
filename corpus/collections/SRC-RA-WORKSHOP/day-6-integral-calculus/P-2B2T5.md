::: {.problem}
Use the definition of the Riemann integral to prove that if $f$ is bounded on $[a,b]$ and is continuous everywhere except for finitely many points in $(a,b)$, then $f\in\mathscr{R}$ on $[a,b]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $D=\{c_1,\ldots,c_k\}\subset(a,b)$ contain every discontinuity of $f$, and choose $M\geq0$ with $|f(x)|\leq M$ on $[a,b]$. Assume $a<b$; for $a=b$ the integral is zero.

<1>1. For each $\varepsilon>0$, there is a finite union $J$ of open intervals whose endpoints lie in $(a,b)$, containing $D$, with total length less than $\varepsilon/(4(M+1))$.

::: {.proof}
Since $D$ is finite and lies in $(a,b)$, choose around each $c_i$ an open interval with closure in $(a,b)$ and length less than $\varepsilon/(4k(M+1))$ when $k>0$. Their union has the required length. When $k=0$, take $J=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>2. The restriction of $f$ to $K:=[a,b]\setminus J$ is uniformly continuous.

::: {.proof}
The set $K$ is compact, and $f$ is continuous at every point of $K$. Thus the restriction is continuous on a compact metric space and hence uniformly continuous.
:::

<1>3. For each $\varepsilon>0$, there exists a partition $P$ of $[a,b]$ such that $U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon$.

<2>1. There exists a partition $P$ containing all endpoints of the component intervals of $J$ such that every subinterval of $P$ has length less than some $\delta>0$ chosen so that
\[
|f(x)-f(y)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2(b-a)}
\quad\text{for }x,y\in K,\ |x-y|<\delta.
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>2 to obtain $\delta>0$, then refine the finite partition consisting of $a,b$ and all endpoints of the component intervals of $J$ until each subinterval has length less than $\delta$.
:::

<2>2. The total contribution to $U(f,P)-L(f,P)$ from subintervals contained in $K$ is at most $\varepsilon/2$.

::: {.proof}
For each such subinterval, step <2>1 bounds the oscillation by $\varepsilon/(2(b-a))$. The sum of their lengths is at most $b-a$.
:::

<2>3. The total contribution from the remaining subintervals is strictly less than $\varepsilon/2$.

::: {.proof}
Every remaining subinterval lies in the closure of a component interval of $J$, because $P$ contains its endpoints. Their total length is at most the total length of $J$, which by step <1>1 is less than $\varepsilon/(4(M+1))$. Their oscillations are at most $2M$, so their contribution is less than $2M\varepsilon/(4(M+1))<\varepsilon/2$.
:::

<2>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <2>2 and <2>3 give $U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon$.
:::

<1>4. The function $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a,b]$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 establishes the Darboux criterion for Riemann integrability: for every $\varepsilon>0$, a partition has upper-minus-lower sum less than $\varepsilon$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves the required conclusion.
:::
