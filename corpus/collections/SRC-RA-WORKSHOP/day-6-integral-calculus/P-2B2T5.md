::: problem
Use the definition of the Riemann integral to prove that if $f$ is bounded on $[a,b]$ and is continuous everywhere except for finitely many points in $(a,b)$, then $f\in\mathscr{R}$ on $[a,b]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Setup: $f$ is bounded, say $|f| \le M$, and discontinuous only at finitely many points $c_1 < \cdots < c_k$ in $(a,b)$.
::: {.proof}
hypothesis.
:::

<1>2. Given $\eps > 0$, cover the discontinuity points by open intervals $J_1, \ldots, J_k$ of total length $< \frac{\eps}{4M}$.
::: {.proof}
possible since there are finitely many of them.
:::

<1>3. On the compact set $K = [a,b] \setminus \bigcup_i J_i$, $f$ is uniformly continuous.
::: {.proof}
$K$ is closed and bounded, hence compact, and $f$ is continuous on $K$ (all discontinuities lie in the removed open intervals).
:::

<1>4. Choose $\delta > 0$ such that $|f(x) - f(y)| < \frac{\eps}{2(b-a)}$ whenever $x, y \in K$ with $|x - y| < \delta$.
::: {.proof}
uniform continuity on $K$ (<1>3).
:::

<1>5. Build a partition $P$ containing the endpoints of the $J_i$ and with every sub-interval that lies inside $K$ of length $< \delta$; then $U(P) - L(P) < \eps$.
<2>1. The sub-intervals inside $K$ contribute at most $\frac{\eps}{2(b-a)}\sum \Delta x \le \eps/2$.
::: {.proof}
on each such sub-interval the oscillation is $< \frac{\eps}{2(b-a)}$ by <1>4, and the total length of the good sub-intervals is $\le b - a$.
:::
<2>2. The sub-intervals overlapping the bad set contribute at most $2M \cdot \frac{\eps}{4M} = \eps/2$.
::: {.proof}
on each, the oscillation is $\le 2M$ ($f$ bounded by $M$), and their total length is the total length of the $J_i$, $< \eps/(4M)$.
:::
<2>3. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<2>1 and <2>2 give $U(P) - L(P) < \eps/2 + \eps/2 = \eps$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
by the Riemann criterion, $U(P) - L(P) \to 0$ over fine partitions (<1>5 for every $\eps$) implies $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a,b]$.
:::
:::
