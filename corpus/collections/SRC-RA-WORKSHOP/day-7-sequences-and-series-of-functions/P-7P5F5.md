::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,1]\to\RR$ be continuous with $f(0)\neq f(1)$ and define $f_n(x)=f(x^n)$.
Prove that $f_n$ does not converge uniformly on $[0,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$f_n \to g$ pointwise, where $g(x) = f(0)$ for $x \in [0,1)$ and $g(1) = f(1)$.

::: pf-proof

For $0 \le x < 1$, $x^n \to 0$, and $f$ is continuous, so $f(x^n) \to f(0)$. At $x=1$, $f_n(1)=f(1^n) = f(1)$ for every $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$g$ is discontinuous at $1$.

::: pf-proof

$g(1) = f(1) \ne f(0) = \lim_{x \to 1^-}g(x)$ by the hypothesis $f(0) \ne f(1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $f_n$ converged uniformly, its limit would be continuous.

::: pf-proof

Each $f_n$ is continuous, as a composition of $x \mapsto x^n$ and $f$. By the uniform limit theorem, a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

A uniform limit is also a pointwise limit, so it would equal $g$ by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. But $g$ is discontinuous by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, contradicting step [](#s3){.pf-ref}. Hence the convergence is not uniform.

:::
