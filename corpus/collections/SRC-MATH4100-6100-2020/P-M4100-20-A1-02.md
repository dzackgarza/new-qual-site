::: {.problem}
(a) Let $q\ne0$ be rational and $x$ irrational. Prove that $q+x$ and $qx$ are irrational.

(b) Use the Archimedean property of $\RR$ to prove that between any two distinct real numbers there is both a rational and an irrational number.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $q\in\QQ$ and $x\notin\QQ$, then $q+x$ is irrational.


::: pf-proof

If $q+x$ were rational, then
$$
x=(q+x)-q
$$
would be rational, a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $q\in\QQ\setminus\{0\}$ and $x\notin\QQ$, then $qx$ is irrational.


::: pf-proof

If $qx$ were rational, then
$$
x=\frac{qx}{q}
$$
would be rational because $q\neq0$, a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} prove the two assertions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Let $x<y$. There exists $n\in\NN$ with
$$
n(y-x)>1.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the Archimedean property, choose
$$
n>\frac{1}{y-x}.
$$
Multiplying by $y-x>0$ gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

There exists $m\in\ZZ$ such that
$$
nx<m\leq nx+1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $t=nx$. By the Archimedean property, choose $N\in\NN$ with $N>\abs{t}$.
Then
$$
S=\{k\in\ZZ:k>t\}
$$
is nonempty, and every element of $S$ is greater than $-N$. Hence
$$
S+N=\{k+N:k\in S\}
$$
is a nonempty subset of $\NN$, so it has a least element. Equivalently, $S$
has a least element $m$. By minimality,
$$
m-1\leq t<m,
$$
because otherwise $m-1$ would also belong to $S$. Substituting $t=nx$ gives
the claimed inequalities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The rational number $m/n$ satisfies
$$
x<\frac mn<y.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives $nx<m$, hence $x<m/n$. Also, step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
nx+1<ny.
$$
Since $m\leq nx+1$, one has $m<ny$, hence $m/n<y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Between any two distinct real numbers there is a rational number.


::: pf-proof

For $x<y$, step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives such a rational number. If $y<x$, interchange the
two endpoints.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Between any two distinct real numbers there is an irrational number.


::: pf-proof

Assume $x<y$. By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, choose
$$
r\in\QQ
$$
with
$$
x-\sqrt2<r<y-\sqrt2.
$$
Then
$$
x<r+\sqrt2<y.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $r+\sqrt2$ is irrational.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} give respectively a rational and an irrational number
between the two real numbers.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} establish parts (a) and (b).

:::
