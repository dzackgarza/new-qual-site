::: {.problem}
Prove that if $\scriptstyle \sum _ { n = 0 } ^ { \infty } f _ { n } ( x )$ converges uniformly on a set A, then the sequence of functions $\left\{ f _ { n } \right\}$ must converge uniformly to 0 on A.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
S_N(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}f_n(x)
$$
denote the partial sums.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Since the series converges uniformly on $A$, the sequence $(S_N)$ is
uniformly Cauchy on $A$.


::: pf-proof

Uniform convergence implies the uniform Cauchy criterion: for every
$\varepsilon>0$, there exists $N$ such that for all $m,n\geq N$ and every
$x\in A$,
$$
\abs{S_m(x)-S_n(x)}<\varepsilon.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $n\geq1$,
$$
f_n=S_n-S_{n-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
S_n
=
f_0+f_1+\cdots+f_n
$$
and
$$
S_{n-1}
=
f_0+f_1+\cdots+f_{n-1}.
$$
Subtracting gives the identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The functions $f_n$ converge uniformly to $0$ on $A$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, choose $N$ such that
$$
\abs{S_m(x)-S_n(x)}<\varepsilon
$$
for all $m,n\geq N$ and every $x\in A$.

If $k\geq N+1$, then both $k$ and $k-1$ are at least $N$. Hence by step
[](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{f_k(x)}
=
\abs{S_k(x)-S_{k-1}(x)}
<
\varepsilon
$$
for every $x\in A$.

Therefore
$$
\sup_{x\in A}\abs{f_k(x)}
<\varepsilon
$$
for all $k\geq N+1$. This is uniform convergence of $f_k$ to $0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
