::: {.problem}
Let $(x_n)$ be bounded. Prove each claim twice, once from each equivalent definition
$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n
=
\sup\{x:x\text{ is a subsequential limit of }(x_n)\}
$$
and
$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{k\ge n}x_k.
$$

(a) If $|x_n|\le M$ for every $n$, then
$$
\left|\limsup x_n\right|\le M.
$$

(b) If $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$ are bounded and $a_n\le b_n$ for every $n$, then
$$
\limsup a_n\le\limsup b_n.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
L_x=\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Using the subsequential-limit definition, if $|x_n|\leq M$ for every
$n$, then
$$
|L_x|\leq M.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $(x_n)$ is bounded, the Bolzano--Weierstrass Theorem gives at least
one subsequential limit, so the set
$$
S=\{x:x\text{ is a subsequential limit of }(x_n)\}
$$
is nonempty.  If $x_{n_j}\to\ell$, then
$$
-M\leq x_{n_j}\leq M
$$
for every $j$.  Passing to the limit gives
$$
-M\leq\ell\leq M.
$$
Thus every element of $S$ lies in $[-M,M]$.  Therefore
$$
-M\leq\sup S\leq M.
$$
Since $L_x=\sup S$ by the first definition,
$$
|L_x|\leq M.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Using the tail-supremum definition, if $|x_n|\leq M$ for every $n$,
then
$$
|L_x|\leq M.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $n$,
$$
-M
\leq
\sup_{k\geq n}x_k
\leq
M.
$$
By the second definition,
$$
\sup_{k\geq n}x_k\longrightarrow L_x.
$$
Passing the two inequalities to the limit gives
$$
-M\leq L_x\leq M,
$$
and hence $|L_x|\leq M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Part (a) follows from either definition.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} prove the same claim independently from the two stated
definitions of $\limsup$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Using the subsequential-limit definition, if $a_n\leq b_n$ for every
$n$, then
$$
\limsup a_n\leq\limsup b_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\alpha$ be any subsequential limit of $(a_n)$.  Choose a subsequence
$$
a_{n_j}\longrightarrow\alpha.
$$
The corresponding sequence $(b_{n_j})$ is bounded because $(b_n)$ is bounded.
By Bolzano--Weierstrass, it has a convergent further subsequence
$$
b_{n_{j_\ell}}\longrightarrow\beta.
$$
Along the same further subsequence,
$$
a_{n_{j_\ell}}\longrightarrow\alpha,
$$
and
$$
a_{n_{j_\ell}}\leq b_{n_{j_\ell}}
$$
for every $\ell$.  Passing to the limit gives
$$
\alpha\leq\beta.
$$
The number $\beta$ is a subsequential limit of $(b_n)$, so by the first
definition,
$$
\beta\leq\limsup b_n.
$$
Hence every subsequential limit $\alpha$ of $(a_n)$ satisfies
$$
\alpha\leq\limsup b_n.
$$
Taking the supremum over all such $\alpha$ gives
$$
\limsup a_n\leq\limsup b_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Using the tail-supremum definition, if $a_n\leq b_n$ for every $n$,
then
$$
\limsup a_n\leq\limsup b_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $n$ and every $k\geq n$,
$$
a_k\leq b_k.
$$
Therefore
$$
\sup_{k\geq n}a_k
\leq
\sup_{k\geq n}b_k.
$$
By the second definition,
$$
\sup_{k\geq n}a_k\longrightarrow\limsup a_n
$$
and
$$
\sup_{k\geq n}b_k\longrightarrow\limsup b_n.
$$
Passing the inequality to the limit yields
$$
\limsup a_n\leq\limsup b_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Part (b) follows from either definition.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} prove the same monotonicity property independently from
the two definitions.

:::
