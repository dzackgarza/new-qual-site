::: {.problem}
Let $f : [ 0 , 1 ] \to \RR$ be continuous with $f ( 0 ) = f ( 1 )$

(a) Show that for each $n \in \NN$ there exist $x , y \in [ 0 , 1 ]$ satisfying $\abs{ x - y } = 1 / n$ and $f ( x ) = f ( y )$

(b) Show that if $h \in ( 0 , 1 / 2 )$ , but not of the form $1 / n$ for some $n \in \NN$ , then there does not necessarily exist $x , y \in [ 0 , 1 ]$ satisfying $\abs{ x - y } = h$ and $f ( x ) = f ( y )$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Fix $n\in\NN$ and define
$$
g(x)=f\left(x+\frac1n\right)-f(x),
\qquad
0\leq x\leq1-\frac1n.
$$
Then $g$ is continuous.


::: pf-proof

It is the difference of the two continuous functions
$$
x\longmapsto f\left(x+\frac1n\right)
$$
and
$$
x\longmapsto f(x).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The values of $g$ at the grid points satisfy
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
g\left(\frac kn\right)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The sum telescopes:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
g\left(\frac kn\right)
&=
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\left(
f\left(\frac{k+1}{n}\right)
-
f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)
\right)\\
&=
f(1)-f(0)\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The function $g$ has a zero on
$$
\left[0,1-\frac1n\right].
$$


::: pf-proof

If one of the numbers
$$
g(0),g(1/n),\ldots,g((n-1)/n)
$$
is zero, the claim is immediate.

Otherwise their sum is zero by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, so they cannot all have the same
sign. Hence there are two grid points at which $g$ has opposite signs. Since
$g$ is continuous on the interval joining those points, the Intermediate
Value Theorem gives a zero of $g$ between them.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every $n\in\NN$, there exist $x,y\in[0,1]$ such that
$$
\abs{x-y}=\frac1n
$$
and
$$
f(x)=f(y).
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose $x$ with $g(x)=0$ as in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and set
$$
y=x+\frac1n.
$$
Then $x,y\in[0,1]$,
$$
\abs{x-y}=\frac1n,
$$
and
$$
0=g(x)=f(y)-f(x).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Fix
$$
h\in\left(0,\frac12\right)
$$
with
$$
h\neq\frac1n
$$
for every $n\in\NN$. Set
$$
m=\left\lfloor\frac1h\right\rfloor
$$
and
$$
r=1-mh.
$$
Then
$$
m\geq2
$$
and
$$
0<r<h.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $0<h<1/2$,
$$
1/h>2,
$$
so $m\geq2$.
By definition of the floor,
$$
m\leq\frac1h<m+1.
$$
Multiplying by $h>0$ gives
$$
mh\leq1<(m+1)h,
$$
so
$$
0\leq r=1-mh<h.
$$
If $r=0$, then
$$
h=\frac1m,
$$
contrary to the hypothesis. Hence $0<r<h$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Choose a continuous piecewise-linear function
$$
\phi:[0,h]\to\RR
$$
satisfying
$$
\phi(0)=0,
\qquad
\phi(r)=-m,
\qquad
\phi(h)=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
0<r<h,
$$
there is a unique function that is linear on $[0,r]$ and on $[r,h]$ and has
the three displayed values. This function is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Define $F:[0,1]\to\RR$ by
$$
F(x)=k+\phi(x-kh)
$$
whenever
$$
kh\leq x\leq(k+1)h
$$
for some
$$
k=0,1,\ldots,m-1,
$$
and on the final interval
$$
mh\leq x\leq1
$$
define
$$
F(x)=m+\phi(x-mh).
$$


::: pf-proof

These formulas cover all of $[0,1]$ because
$$
mh<1<(m+1)h.
$$
At a shared endpoint $x=kh$ with $1\leq k\leq m$, the value from the left is
$$
(k-1)+\phi(h)
=
k,
$$
while the value from the right is
$$
k+\phi(0)
=
k.
$$
Thus the pieces agree.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The function $F$ is continuous on $[0,1]$ and satisfies
$$
F(0)=F(1).
$$


::: pf-proof

Continuity on each piece follows from continuity of $\phi$, and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}
shows compatibility at every shared endpoint. Hence $F$ is continuous.

Also,
$$
F(0)=\phi(0)=0.
$$
Since
$$
1=mh+r,
$$
the final formula gives
$$
F(1)
=
m+\phi(r)
=
m-m
=0.
$$
Thus
$$
F(0)=F(1).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

For every
$$
x\in[0,1-h],
$$
one has
$$
F(x+h)=F(x)+1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
x=kh+s
$$
with
$$
0\leq s<h.
$$
Because $x+h\leq1$, the translated point $x+h$ lies in the next $h$-block
with the same local coordinate $s$. Therefore
$$
F(x)=k+\phi(s)
$$
and
$$
F(x+h)
=(k+1)+\phi(s).
$$
Subtracting gives
$$
F(x+h)-F(x)=1.
$$
The same identity holds at block endpoints by continuity of the defining
formulas.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

There do not exist $x,y\in[0,1]$ with
$$
\abs{x-y}=h
$$
and
$$
F(x)=F(y).
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\abs{x-y}=h.
$$
After interchanging $x$ and $y$ if necessary, assume
$$
y=x+h.
$$
Then
$$
x\in[0,1-h].
$$
By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref},
$$
F(y)
=
F(x+h)
=
F(x)+1,
$$
so
$$
F(y)\neq F(x).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

For every admissible $h$, steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} construct a continuous function
$$
F:[0,1]\to\RR
$$
with
$$
F(0)=F(1)
$$
but no horizontal chord of length $h$. Thus such a chord does not necessarily
exist.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.
