::: {.problem}
Let $f \colon \RR \to \RR$.

(a) Prove that $f$ is continuous on $\RR$ if and only if $f^{-1}(G)$ is open whenever $G \subseteq \RR$ is an open set.

(b) Prove that if $f$ is continuous, then
$$
Z(f) := \{ x \in \RR : f(x) = 0 \}
$$
defines a closed set.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Suppose $f$ is continuous and $G\subseteq\RR$ is open. Then the preimage of $G$ under $f$ is open.


::: pf-proof

Let $x$ lie in the preimage. Since $f(x)\in G$ and $G$ is open, choose
$\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$
(f(x)-\varepsilon,f(x)+\varepsilon)\subseteq G.
$$
Continuity at $x$ gives $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{y-x}<\delta
\implies
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}<\varepsilon.
$$
Hence every such $y$ also lies in the preimage of $G$. Therefore an open
interval around $x$ is contained in the preimage, proving that the preimage is
open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Conversely, suppose the preimage of every open subset of $\RR$ is open.
Then $f$ is continuous at every $x\in\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $x\in\RR$ and let $\varepsilon>0$. The interval
$$
G=(f(x)-\varepsilon,f(x)+\varepsilon)
$$
is open and contains $f(x)$. Hence its preimage is an open set containing
$x$. Therefore there exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
(x-\delta,x+\delta)
$$
is contained in that preimage.

Thus
$$
\abs{y-x}<\delta
\implies
f(y)\in(f(x)-\varepsilon,f(x)+\varepsilon)
\implies
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}<\varepsilon.
$$
This is continuity at $x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore the two conditions in part (a) are equivalent.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves the forward implication, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves the converse.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Suppose $f$ is continuous. Then the complement of the zero set is the
preimage of the open set $\RR\setminus\{0\}$.


::: pf-proof

For $x\in\RR$,
$$
x\notin Z(f)
\iff
f(x)\neq0
\iff
f(x)\in\RR\setminus\{0\}.
$$
Thus the two sets are equal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The zero set
$$
Z(f)=\{x\in\RR:f(x)=0\}
$$
is closed.


::: pf-proof

The set $\RR\setminus\{0\}$ is open. By part (a), its preimage under the
continuous function $f$ is open. By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, that preimage is exactly the
complement of $Z(f)$. Hence $Z(f)$ is closed.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
