::: {.problem}
Let

$$
f ( x ) = \sum _ { n = 1 } ^ { \infty } { \frac { 1 } { x ^ { 2 } + n ^ { 2 } } } .
$$

(a) Show that $f$ is a continuous function on R.

(b) Is $f$ differentiable?
If so, is the derivative function $f ^ { \prime }$ continuous?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $x\in\RR$ and every $n\geq1$,
$$
0<
\frac1{x^2+n^2}
\leq
\frac1{n^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $x^2\geq0$,
$$
x^2+n^2\geq n^2.
$$
Taking reciprocals gives the inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac1{x^2+n^2}
$$
converges uniformly on $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the $n$th term is uniformly bounded by $1/n^2$. Since
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}
$$
converges, the Weierstrass $M$-test gives uniform convergence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The function
$$
f(x)
=
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac1{x^2+n^2}
$$
is continuous on $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Each summand is continuous on $\RR$. Hence every partial sum is continuous.
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the partial sums converge uniformly to $f$. Therefore $f$ is
continuous as a uniform limit of continuous functions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For the $n$th summand
$$
u_n(x)=\frac1{x^2+n^2},
$$
one has
$$
u_n'(x)
=
-\frac{2x}{(x^2+n^2)^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
(x^2+n^2)^{-1}
$$
by the chain rule.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For every $x\in\RR$ and every $n\geq1$,
$$
|u_n'(x)|
\leq
\frac1{n^3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The inequality
$$
x^2+n^2
\geq
2n|x|
$$
follows from
$$
(|x|-n)^2\geq0.
$$
Hence
$$
\frac{2|x|}{(x^2+n^2)^2}
\leq
\frac1{n(x^2+n^2)}
\leq
\frac1{n^3}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The derivative series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_n'(x)
$$
converges uniformly on $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
|u_n'(x)|
\leq
\frac1{n^3}
$$
uniformly in $x$. Since
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^3}
$$
converges, the Weierstrass $M$-test applies.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The partial sums
$$
S_N(x)
=
\sum_{n=1}^{N}u_n(x)
$$
converge at the point $x=0$.


::: pf-proof

At $x=0$,
$$
S_N(0)
=
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac1{n^2},
$$
and this numerical series converges.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The function $f$ is differentiable on $\RR$, with
$$
\boxed{
f'(x)
=
-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{2x}{(x^2+n^2)^2}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Each partial sum $S_N$ is differentiable. Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives uniform convergence
of the derivative sequence $S_N'$, while step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives convergence of
$S_N$ at one point. Therefore the standard theorem on differentiating a
uniformly convergent derivative sequence applies and identifies the derivative
of the limit with the uniform limit of the derivatives.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The derivative $f'$ is continuous on $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Each derivative partial sum
$$
S_N'(x)
=
-\sum_{n=1}^{N}
\frac{2x}{(x^2+n^2)^2}
$$
is continuous. By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, these functions converge uniformly to $f'$.
Hence $f'$ is continuous as a uniform limit of continuous functions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} proves differentiability and gives the derivative formula, while
step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} proves continuity of the derivative.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::
