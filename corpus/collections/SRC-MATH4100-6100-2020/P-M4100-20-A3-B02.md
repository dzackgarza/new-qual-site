::: {.problem}
Prove that
$$
\mathcal P(\NN)
$$
has the same cardinality as $\RR$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Define
$$
F:\mathcal P(\NN)\longrightarrow\RR,
\qquad
F(S)=\sum_{n\in S}\frac{2}{3^n}.
$$
Then $F$ is well-defined.


::: pf-proof

For every $S\subseteq\NN$,
$$
0
\leq
\sum_{n\in S}\frac{2}{3^n}
\leq
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{3^n}
=1.
$$
Hence the series converges to a real number.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map $F$ is injective.


::: pf-proof

Let $S,T\subseteq\NN$ be distinct, and let
$$
m=\min(S\mathbin{\triangle}T)
$$
be the least index at which their membership differs.  Interchanging $S$ and
$T$ if necessary, assume $m\in S$ and $m\notin T$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(S)-F(T)
&=
\frac{2}{3^m}
+
\sum_{n>m}
\frac{2(\mathbf 1_S(n)-\mathbf 1_T(n))}{3^n}\\
&\geq
\frac{2}{3^m}
-
\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{3^n}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The geometric tail is
$$
\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{3^n}
=
\frac{1}{3^m},
$$
so
$$
F(S)-F(T)
\geq
\frac{1}{3^m}
>0.
$$
Thus $F(S)\neq F(T)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore there is an injection
$$
\mathcal P(\NN)\hookrightarrow\RR.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the map $F$ from steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Fix an enumeration
$$
\QQ=\{q_1,q_2,q_3,\ldots\},
$$
and define
$$
G:\RR\longrightarrow\mathcal P(\NN),
\qquad
G(x)=\{n\in\NN:q_n<x\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For each real number $x$, the displayed set is a subset of $\NN$, so $G$ is
well-defined.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The map $G$ is injective.


::: pf-proof

Let $x<y$. By density of the rationals, choose
$$
q\in\QQ
$$
with
$$
x<q<y.
$$
Write $q=q_m$ in the fixed enumeration. Then
$$
m\notin G(x)
$$
because $q_m>x$, while
$$
m\in G(y)
$$
because $q_m<y$. Hence
$$
G(x)\neq G(y).
$$
Thus distinct real numbers have distinct images.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore there is an injection
$$
\RR\hookrightarrow\mathcal P(\NN).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the map $G$ from steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

There is a bijection
$$
\boxed{\mathcal P(\NN)\cong\RR}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives an injection
$$
\mathcal P(\NN)\hookrightarrow\RR,
$$
and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives an injection
$$
\RR\hookrightarrow\mathcal P(\NN).
$$
The Schröder--Bernstein theorem therefore gives a bijection between the two
sets.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is the required cardinality equality.

:::
