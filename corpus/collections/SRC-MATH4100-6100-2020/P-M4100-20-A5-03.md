::: {.problem}
Determine which of the following functions are metrics on $\RR$:
$$
d_1(x,y)=(x-y)^2,
\qquad
d_2(x,y)=\sqrt{\abs{x-y}},
$$
$$
d_3(x,y)=\abs{x^2-y^2},
\qquad
d_4(x,y)=\abs{x-2y},
$$
and
$$
d_5(x,y)=\frac{\abs{x-y}}{1+\abs{x-y}}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The function
$$
d_1(x,y)=(x-y)^2
$$
is not a metric.


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
x=0,\qquad y=1,\qquad z=2.
$$
Then
$$
d_1(0,2)=4,
$$
while
$$
d_1(0,1)+d_1(1,2)=1+1=2.
$$
Thus the triangle inequality fails.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The function
$$
d_2(x,y)=\sqrt{\abs{x-y}}
$$
satisfies nonnegativity, symmetry, and separation.


::: pf-proof

These properties follow directly from the corresponding properties of
$\abs{x-y}$ and the fact that the square-root function is nonnegative and vanishes
only at $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The function $d_2$ satisfies the triangle inequality.


::: pf-proof

For $x,y,z\in\RR$, the ordinary triangle inequality gives
$$
\abs{x-z}
\leq
\abs{x-y}+\abs{y-z}.
$$
Since the square-root function is increasing on $[0,\infty)$,
$$
\sqrt{\abs{x-z}}
\leq
\sqrt{\abs{x-y}+\abs{y-z}}.
$$
For $a,b\geq0$,
$$
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt a+\sqrt b,
$$
because
$$
(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2
=
a+b+2\sqrt{ab}
\geq
a+b.
$$
Applying this with
$$
a=\abs{x-y},
\qquad
b=\abs{y-z}
$$
gives
$$
d_2(x,z)
\leq
d_2(x,y)+d_2(y,z).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore $d_2$ is a metric.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} verify all metric axioms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The function
$$
d_3(x,y)=\abs{x^2-y^2}
$$
is not a metric.


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
x=1,
\qquad
y=-1.
$$
Then $x\neq y$, but
$$
d_3(1,-1)
=
\abs{1-1}
=0.
$$
Thus the separation axiom fails.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The function
$$
d_4(x,y)=\abs{x-2y}
$$
is not a metric.


::: pf-proof

For example,
$$
d_4(1,1)=\abs{1-2}=1\neq0.
$$
Thus a point need not have zero distance from itself. In addition,
$$
d_4(0,1)=2
\qquad\text{but}\qquad
d_4(1,0)=1,
$$
so symmetry also fails.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Let
$$
\phi(t)=\frac{t}{1+t},
\qquad t\geq0.
$$
Then $\phi$ is increasing.


::: pf-proof

If $0\leq s\leq t$, then
$$
\frac{s}{1+s}\leq\frac{t}{1+t}
$$
because cross-multiplication by the positive denominators gives
$$
s(1+t)\leq t(1+s)
\iff
s\leq t.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The function $\phi$ is subadditive:
$$
\phi(a+b)\leq\phi(a)+\phi(b)
$$
for all $a,b\geq0$.


::: pf-proof

Direct calculation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi(a)+\phi(b)-\phi(a+b)
&=
\frac{a}{1+a}
+
\frac{b}{1+b}
-
\frac{a+b}{1+a+b}\\
&=
\frac{ab(2+a+b)}
{(1+a)(1+b)(1+a+b)}
\geq0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The function
$$
d_5(x,y)=\frac{\abs{x-y}}{1+\abs{x-y}}
$$
satisfies the triangle inequality.


::: pf-proof

Set
$$
a=\abs{x-y},
\qquad
b=\abs{y-z}.
$$
The ordinary triangle inequality gives
$$
\abs{x-z}\leq a+b.
$$
By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
d_5(x,z)
=
\phi(\abs{x-z})
\leq
\phi(a+b).
$$
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
\phi(a+b)
\leq
\phi(a)+\phi(b)
=
d_5(x,y)+d_5(y,z).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The function $d_5$ is a metric.


::: pf-proof

Nonnegativity and symmetry are immediate from the absolute value. Also,
$$
d_5(x,y)=0
\iff
\abs{x-y}=0
\iff
x=y.
$$
The triangle inequality is step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The complete classification is
$$
\boxed{
d_2\text{ and }d_5\text{ are metrics; }
d_1,d_3,d_4\text{ are not.}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} answers all five cases.

:::
