::: {.problem}
Let $(a_n)$ be a real sequence.

(a) Prove that $(a_n)$ is unbounded above if and only if it has a subsequence tending to $+\infty$.

(b) Prove that $(a_n)$ is unbounded below if and only if it has a subsequence tending to $-\infty$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $(a_n)$ has a subsequence tending to $+\infty$, then $(a_n)$ is
unbounded above.


::: pf-proof

Let $(a_{n_k})$ satisfy
$$
a_{n_k}\longrightarrow+\infty.
$$
Given any $M\in\RR$, there exists $K$ such that
$$
a_{n_k}>M
$$
for every $k\geq K$. Thus some term of the original sequence exceeds $M$.
Since $M$ was arbitrary, $(a_n)$ is unbounded above.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $(a_n)$ is unbounded above, then every tail
$$
(a_n)_{n\geq N}
$$
is unbounded above.


::: pf-proof

Suppose some tail were bounded above by $M$. The finitely many terms
$$
a_1,\ldots,a_{N-1}
$$
have a finite maximum $M_0$. Then
$$
\max\{M,M_0\}
$$
would be an upper bound for the entire sequence, contradicting unboundedness.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $(a_n)$ is unbounded above, there is a subsequence $(a_{n_k})$ such
that
$$
a_{n_k}>k
$$
for every $k$.


::: pf-proof

Choose $n_1$ with $a_{n_1}>1$. Having chosen $n_k$, apply step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} to the
tail beginning at $n_k+1$. Since that tail is unbounded above, choose
$$
n_{k+1}>n_k
$$
such that
$$
a_{n_{k+1}}>k+1.
$$
This recursively defines the required subsequence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The subsequence in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} tends to $+\infty$.


::: pf-proof

Let $M\in\RR$. Choose $K\in\NN$ with $K>M$. Then for every $k\geq K$,
$$
a_{n_k}>k\geq K>M.
$$
Hence $a_{n_k}\to+\infty$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves one implication, and steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove the converse.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If $(a_n)$ has a subsequence tending to $-\infty$, then $(a_n)$ is
unbounded below.


::: pf-proof

Let $(a_{n_k})$ satisfy
$$
a_{n_k}\longrightarrow-\infty.
$$
Given any $M\in\RR$, there exists $K$ such that
$$
a_{n_k}<M
$$
for every $k\geq K$. Thus the original sequence has a term below every real
number $M$, so it is unbounded below.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If $(a_n)$ is unbounded below, then every tail is unbounded below.


::: pf-proof

If some tail beginning at $N$ were bounded below by $M$, then the finitely
many terms $a_1,\ldots,a_{N-1}$ would have a finite minimum $M_0$, and
$$
\min\{M,M_0\}
$$
would be a lower bound for the entire sequence. This contradicts unboundedness
below.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

If $(a_n)$ is unbounded below, there is a subsequence $(a_{n_k})$ such
that
$$
a_{n_k}<-k
$$
for every $k$.


::: pf-proof

Choose $n_1$ with $a_{n_1}<-1$. Having chosen $n_k$, apply step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} to the
tail beginning at $n_k+1$ and choose
$$
n_{k+1}>n_k
$$
with
$$
a_{n_{k+1}}<-(k+1).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The subsequence in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} tends to $-\infty$.


::: pf-proof

Let $M\in\RR$. Choose $K\in\NN$ with $K>-M$. Then for every $k\geq K$,
$$
a_{n_k}<-k\leq-K<M.
$$
Hence $a_{n_k}\to-\infty$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves one implication, and steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} prove the converse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::
