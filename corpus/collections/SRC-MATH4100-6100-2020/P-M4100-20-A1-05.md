::: {.problem}
Using the definition of convergence of a sequence, prove
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{3n+1}{2n+5}=\frac32,
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1{6n^2+1}=0,
$$
and
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac2{\sqrt{n+3}}=0.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{3n+1}{2n+5}=\frac32.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. For every $n\geq1$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\frac{3n+1}{2n+5}-\frac32
\right|
&=
\left|
\frac{2(3n+1)-3(2n+5)}{2(2n+5)}
\right|\\
&=
\frac{13}{4n+10}\\
&<
\frac{13}{4n}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Choose $N\in\NN$ with
$$
N>\frac{13}{4\varepsilon}.
$$
Then for every $n\geq N$,
$$
\left|
\frac{3n+1}{2n+5}-\frac32
\right|
<
\frac{13}{4n}
\leq
\frac{13}{4N}
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Hence the sequence converges to $3/2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1{6n^2+1}=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $N\in\NN$ such that
$$
N>\frac1{\sqrt{6\varepsilon}}.
$$
Then for every $n\geq N$,
$$
\left|
\frac1{6n^2+1}
\right|
<
\frac1{6n^2}
\leq
\frac1{6N^2}
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus the sequence converges to $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac2{\sqrt{n+3}}=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $N\in\NN$ such that
$$
N>\frac4{\varepsilon^2}.
$$
For every $n\geq N$,
$$
n+3>n\geq N>\frac4{\varepsilon^2},
$$
so
$$
\sqrt{n+3}>\frac2\varepsilon.
$$
Therefore
$$
\left|
\frac2{\sqrt{n+3}}
\right|
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Hence the sequence converges to $0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} establish the three requested limits directly from the
definition of convergence.
