::: {.problem}
(a) i. Show that $f(x) = x^{3}$ is continuous on all of $\RR$.

ii. Argue that $f$ is however not uniformly continuous on $\RR$.

(b) Show that $g(x) = 1/x^{2}$ is uniformly continuous on $[1, \infty)$, but not on the set $(0, 1]$.

(c) Show that $h(x) = \sqrt{x}$ is uniformly continuous on $[0, \infty)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Fix $a\in\RR$. If $\abs{x-a}<1$, then
$$
\abs{x^2+ax+a^2}
\leq
3(\abs{a}+1)^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The assumption gives $\abs{x}\leq\abs{a}+1$. Hence
$$
\abs{x^2+ax+a^2}
\leq
\abs{x}^2+\abs{a}\abs{x}+a^2
\leq
3(\abs{a}+1)^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The function $f(x)=x^3$ is continuous on $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$ and fix $a\in\RR$. Choose
$$
\delta
=
\min\left\{
1,
\frac{\varepsilon}{3(\abs{a}+1)^2}
\right\}.
$$
If $\abs{x-a}<\delta$, then by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{x^3-a^3}
=
\abs{x-a}\,\abs{x^2+ax+a^2}
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $f$ is continuous at every $a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Define
$$
x_n=n,
\qquad
y_n=n+\frac1{n^2}.
$$
Then
$$
\abs{x_n-y_n}\to0,
$$
while
$$
\abs{f(x_n)-f(y_n)}\geq3
$$
for every $n$.


::: pf-proof

Clearly
$$
\abs{x_n-y_n}=\frac1{n^2}\to0.
$$
Also,
$$
\left(n+\frac1{n^2}\right)^3-n^3
=
3+\frac3{n^3}+\frac1{n^6}
\geq3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The function $f(x)=x^3$ is not uniformly continuous on $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Take $\varepsilon=1$. If $f$ were uniformly continuous, some $\delta>0$
would satisfy
$$
\abs{x-y}<\delta
\implies
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}<1
$$
for all real $x,y$. Choose $n$ so large that $1/n^2<\delta$. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} then
gives a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves continuity and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} disproves uniform continuity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For $x,y\geq1$,
$$
\abs{
\frac1{x^2}-\frac1{y^2}
}
\leq
2\abs{x-y}.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{
\frac1{x^2}-\frac1{y^2}
}
&=
\abs{x-y}\frac{x+y}{x^2y^2}\\
&=
\abs{x-y}
\left(
\frac1{xy^2}+\frac1{x^2y}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $x,y\geq1$, each reciprocal term is at most $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The function
$$
g(x)=\frac1{x^2}
$$
is uniformly continuous on $[1,\infty)$.


::: pf-proof

Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose
$$
\delta=\frac\varepsilon2.
$$
If $x,y\geq1$ and $\abs{x-y}<\delta$, then step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{g(x)-g(y)}
\leq2\abs{x-y}
<
\varepsilon.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Let
$$
x_n=\frac1n,
\qquad
y_n=\frac1{n+1}.
$$
Then $x_n,y_n\in(0,1]$,
$$
\abs{x_n-y_n}\to0,
$$
but
$$
\abs{g(x_n)-g(y_n)}=2n+1.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
\abs{x_n-y_n}
=
\frac1{n(n+1)}
\to0,
$$
while
$$
g(x_n)=n^2,
\qquad
g(y_n)=(n+1)^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The function $g$ is not uniformly continuous on $(0,1]$.


::: pf-proof

Take $\varepsilon=1$. Given any $\delta>0$, choose $n$ so large that
$$
\frac1{n(n+1)}<\delta.
$$
Then step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{x_n-y_n}<\delta
$$
but
$$
\abs{g(x_n)-g(y_n)}=2n+1>1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves uniform continuity on $[1,\infty)$, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} disproves
it on $(0,1]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

For all $x,y\geq0$,
$$
\abs{\sqrt x-\sqrt y}
\leq
\sqrt{\abs{x-y}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Assume without loss of generality that $x\geq y$. Then
$$
(\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2
=
x+y-2\sqrt{xy}.
$$
Since $x\geq y\geq0$,
$$
\sqrt{xy}\geq y.
$$
Therefore
$$
(\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2
\leq
x-y
=
\abs{x-y}.
$$
Taking square roots gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The function
$$
h(x)=\sqrt x
$$
is uniformly continuous on $[0,\infty)$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
$$
\delta=\varepsilon^2.
$$
If $x,y\geq0$ and
$$
\abs{x-y}<\delta,
$$
then by step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{h(x)-h(y)}
\leq
\sqrt{\abs{x-y}}
<
\sqrt\delta
=
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus the same $\delta$ works for every pair $x,y$ in the domain.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Part (c) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} establish parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
