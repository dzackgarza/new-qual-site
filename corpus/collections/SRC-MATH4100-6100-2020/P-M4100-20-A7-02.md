::: {.problem}
(a) i. Show that $f ( x ) = x ^ { 3 }$ is continuous on all of R.

ii.
Argue that f is however not uniformly continuous on R.

(b) Show that $g ( x ) = 1 / x ^ { 2 }$ is uniformly continuous on $[ 1 , \infty )$ , but not on the set (0, 1].

(c) Show that $h ( x ) = { \sqrt { x } }$ is uniformly continuous on $[ 0 , \infty )$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Fix $a\in\RR$. If $|x-a|<1$, then
$$
|x^2+ax+a^2|
\leq
3(|a|+1)^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The assumption gives $|x|\leq|a|+1$. Hence
$$
|x^2+ax+a^2|
\leq
|x|^2+|a||x|+a^2
\leq
3(|a|+1)^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The function $f(x)=x^3$ is continuous on $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$ and fix $a\in\RR$. Choose
$$
\delta
=
\min\left\{
1,
\frac{\varepsilon}{3(|a|+1)^2}
\right\}.
$$
If $|x-a|<\delta$, then by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
|x^3-a^3|
=
|x-a|\,|x^2+ax+a^2|
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $f$ is continuous at every $a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Define
$$
x_n=n,
\qquad
y_n=n+\frac1{n^2}.
$$
Then
$$
|x_n-y_n|\to0,
$$
while
$$
|f(x_n)-f(y_n)|\geq3
$$
for every $n$.


::: pf-proof

Clearly
$$
|x_n-y_n|=\frac1{n^2}\to0.
$$
Also,
$$
\left(n+\frac1{n^2}\right)^3-n^3
=
3+\frac3{n^3}+\frac1{n^6}
\geq3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The function $f(x)=x^3$ is not uniformly continuous on $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Take $\varepsilon=1$. If $f$ were uniformly continuous, some $\delta>0$
would satisfy
$$
|x-y|<\delta
\implies
|f(x)-f(y)|<1
$$
for all real $x,y$. Choose $n$ so large that $1/n^2<\delta$. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} then
gives a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves continuity and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} disproves uniform continuity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For $x,y\geq1$,
$$
\left|
\frac1{x^2}-\frac1{y^2}
\right|
\leq
2|x-y|.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\frac1{x^2}-\frac1{y^2}
\right|
&=
|x-y|\frac{x+y}{x^2y^2}\\
&=
|x-y|
\left(
\frac1{xy^2}+\frac1{x^2y}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $x,y\geq1$, each reciprocal term is at most $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The function
$$
g(x)=\frac1{x^2}
$$
is uniformly continuous on $[1,\infty)$.


::: pf-proof

Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose
$$
\delta=\frac\varepsilon2.
$$
If $x,y\geq1$ and $|x-y|<\delta$, then step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
|g(x)-g(y)|
\leq2|x-y|
<
\varepsilon.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Let
$$
x_n=\frac1n,
\qquad
y_n=\frac1{n+1}.
$$
Then $x_n,y_n\in(0,1]$,
$$
|x_n-y_n|\to0,
$$
but
$$
|g(x_n)-g(y_n)|=2n+1.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
|x_n-y_n|
=
\frac1{n(n+1)}
\to0,
$$
while
$$
g(x_n)=n^2,
\qquad
g(y_n)=(n+1)^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The function $g$ is not uniformly continuous on $(0,1]$.


::: pf-proof

Take $\varepsilon=1$. Given any $\delta>0$, choose $n$ so large that
$$
\frac1{n(n+1)}<\delta.
$$
Then step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives
$$
|x_n-y_n|<\delta
$$
but
$$
|g(x_n)-g(y_n)|=2n+1>1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves uniform continuity on $[1,\infty)$, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} disproves
it on $(0,1]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

For all $x,y\geq0$,
$$
\left|\sqrt x-\sqrt y\right|
\leq
\sqrt{|x-y|}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Assume without loss of generality that $x\geq y$. Then
$$
(\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2
=
x+y-2\sqrt{xy}.
$$
Since $x\geq y\geq0$,
$$
\sqrt{xy}\geq y.
$$
Therefore
$$
(\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2
\leq
x-y
=
|x-y|.
$$
Taking square roots gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The function
$$
h(x)=\sqrt x
$$
is uniformly continuous on $[0,\infty)$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
$$
\delta=\varepsilon^2.
$$
If $x,y\geq0$ and
$$
|x-y|<\delta,
$$
then by step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref},
$$
|h(x)-h(y)|
\leq
\sqrt{|x-y|}
<
\sqrt\delta
=
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus the same $\delta$ works for every pair $x,y$ in the domain.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Part (c) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} establish parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
