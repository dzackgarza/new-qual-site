::: {.problem}
Using the definition of convergence, prove:

(a) $a_n\to a$ implies $|a_n|\to|a|$. Is the converse true?

(b) If $a_n\ge0$ and $a_n\to a$, then $\sqrt{a_n}\to\sqrt a$.

(c) If $(a_n)$ is bounded and $b_n\to0$, then $a_nb_n\to0$.

(d) If $0\le a_n\le b_n$ and $b_n\to0$, then $a_n\to0$; deduce the squeeze theorem.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $a_n\to a$, then
$$
|a_n|\to|a|.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the reverse triangle inequality,
$$
\bigl||a_n|-|a|\bigr|
\leq
|a_n-a|.
$$
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $a_n\to a$, there exists $N$ such that
$$
|a_n-a|<\varepsilon
$$
for every $n\geq N$. Hence
$$
\bigl||a_n|-|a|\bigr|<\varepsilon
$$
for every $n\geq N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The converse in part (a) is false.


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
a_n=(-1)^n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a=1.
$$
Then
$$
|a_n|=1=|a|
$$
for every $n$, so $|a_n|\to|a|$.  But $(a_n)$ does not converge to $1$,
since its odd-indexed terms equal $-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves the implication and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} disproves its converse.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $a_n\geq0$ for every $n$ and $a_n\to a$, then $a\geq0$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose $a<0$. Choose
$$
\varepsilon=-\frac a2>0.
$$
For all sufficiently large $n$,
$$
|a_n-a|<\varepsilon,
$$
so
$$
a_n<a+\varepsilon=\frac a2<0,
$$
contradicting $a_n\geq0$. Hence $a\geq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $a=0$, then
$$
\sqrt{a_n}\to0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $a_n\to0$, there exists $N$ such that
$$
|a_n|<\varepsilon^2
$$
for every $n\geq N$. Since $a_n\geq0$,
$$
0\leq\sqrt{a_n}<\varepsilon
$$
for every $n\geq N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If $a>0$, then
$$
\sqrt{a_n}\to\sqrt a.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $n$,
$$
\left|\sqrt{a_n}-\sqrt a\right|
=
\frac{|a_n-a|}{\sqrt{a_n}+\sqrt a}
\leq
\frac{|a_n-a|}{\sqrt a}.
$$
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $a_n\to a$, there exists $N$ such that
$$
|a_n-a|<\varepsilon\sqrt a
$$
for every $n\geq N$. Then
$$
\left|\sqrt{a_n}-\sqrt a\right|
<
\varepsilon.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that $a\geq0$. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} handles $a=0$, and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}
handles $a>0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

If $(a_n)$ is bounded and $b_n\to0$, then
$$
a_nb_n\to0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose $M\geq0$ such that
$$
|a_n|\leq M
$$
for every $n$. Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $b_n\to0$, there exists $N$ such
that
$$
|b_n|<\frac{\varepsilon}{M+1}
$$
for every $n\geq N$. Then
$$
|a_nb_n|
\leq
M|b_n|
<
\frac{M}{M+1}\varepsilon
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $a_nb_n\to0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Part (c) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

If
$$
0\leq a_n\leq b_n
$$
and $b_n\to0$, then $a_n\to0$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $b_n\to0$, there exists $N$ such that
$$
|b_n|<\varepsilon
$$
for every $n\geq N$. Because $b_n\geq a_n\geq0$,
$$
|a_n|=a_n\leq b_n=|b_n|<\varepsilon.
$$
Hence $a_n\to0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

If
$$
u_n\leq v_n\leq w_n
$$
for every $n$, and
$$
u_n\to L,
\qquad
w_n\to L,
$$
then
$$
\boxed{v_n\to L}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $N_1$ such that for $n\geq N_1$,
$$
L-\varepsilon<u_n<L+\varepsilon,
$$
and choose $N_2$ such that for $n\geq N_2$,
$$
L-\varepsilon<w_n<L+\varepsilon.
$$
For $n\geq N=\max\{N_1,N_2\}$,
$$
L-\varepsilon
<
u_n
\leq
v_n
\leq
w_n
<
L+\varepsilon.
$$
Therefore
$$
|v_n-L|<\varepsilon,
$$
so $v_n\to L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Part (d) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} proves the special case, and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} is the squeeze theorem.

:::
