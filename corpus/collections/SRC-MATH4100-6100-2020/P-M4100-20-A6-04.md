::: {.problem}
Assume $f:\RR\to\RR$ is continuous. Decide whether each claim is true or false, giving a proof or counterexample.

(a) If $f(x)\ge0$ for all $x<1$, then $f(1)\ge0$.

(b) If $f(r)=0$ for every rational $r$, then $f(x)=0$ for every real $x$.

(c) If $f(x_0)>0$ at one point $x_0$, then $f(x)>0$ for uncountably many real numbers $x$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Statement (a) is $\boxed{\mathrm{T}}$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
x_n=1-\frac1n.
$$
Then $x_n<1$ for every $n$, so $f(x_n)\geq0$. Also $x_n\to1$.
Continuity gives
$$
f(x_n)\longrightarrow f(1).
$$
The limit of a nonnegative convergent sequence is nonnegative, hence
$$
f(1)\geq0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Statement (b) is $\boxed{\mathrm{T}}$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $x\in\RR$. By density of $\QQ$, choose rationals $r_n\to x$.
Then $f(r_n)=0$ for every $n$, while continuity gives
$$
f(r_n)\longrightarrow f(x).
$$
Therefore $f(x)=0$. Since $x$ was arbitrary, $f(x)=0$ for every real $x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Suppose $f(x_0)>0$. Then there exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{x-x_0}<\delta
\implies
f(x)>\frac{f(x_0)}2>0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Set
$$
\varepsilon=\frac{f(x_0)}2.
$$
By continuity at $x_0$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{x-x_0}<\delta
\implies
\abs{f(x)-f(x_0)}<\varepsilon.
$$
Hence
$$
f(x)>f(x_0)-\varepsilon=\frac{f(x_0)}2>0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Statement (c) is $\boxed{\mathrm{T}}$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
(x_0-\delta,x_0+\delta)
\subseteq
\{x\in\RR:f(x)>0\}.
$$
Every nonempty open interval in $\RR$ is uncountable. Therefore the set on
the right is uncountable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The truth values are
$$
\boxed{\mathrm{T},\mathrm{T},\mathrm{T}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.

:::
