::: {.problem}
Suppose one reverses the usual order of quantifiers and says that a sequence $a_n$ “verconges” to $a$ when there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that for every $N\in\NN$, $n\ge N$ implies
$$
\abs{a_n-a}<\varepsilon.
$$
Give an example of a vercongent sequence, give a vercongent sequence that diverges in the usual sense, and characterize exactly what this altered definition describes.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $(a_n)$ verconges to $a$, then there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such
that
$$
\abs{a_n-a}<\varepsilon
$$
for every $n\in\NN$.


::: pf-proof

By definition, there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that for every
$N\in\NN$, every $n\geq N$ satisfies
$$
\abs{a_n-a}<\varepsilon.
$$
Taking $N=1$ gives the displayed inequality for every $n\geq1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every sequence that verconges to some real number is bounded.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{a_n}
\leq
\abs{a_n-a}+\abs{a}
<
\varepsilon+\abs{a}
$$
for every $n$.  Thus $(a_n)$ is bounded.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Conversely, every bounded sequence verconges to every real number $a$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\abs{a_n}\leq M
$$
for every $n$, and fix $a\in\RR$.  Choose
$$
\varepsilon=M+\abs{a}+1.
$$
Then for every $n$,
$$
\abs{a_n-a}
\leq
\abs{a_n}+\abs{a}
\leq
M+\abs{a}
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Therefore, for every $N$ and every $n\geq N$, the same inequality holds, so
$(a_n)$ verconges to $a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The altered definition describes exactly bounded sequences.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that vercongence implies boundedness, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that
boundedness implies vercongence.  Moreover, step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that the proposed
“limit” $a$ carries no information: a bounded sequence verconges to every
real number.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The constant sequence $a_n=0$ is an example of a vercongent sequence.


::: pf-proof

It is bounded, so step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} applies.  For example, it verconges to $0$ with
$\varepsilon=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The sequence
$$
a_n=(-1)^n
$$
is vercongent but divergent in the usual sense.


::: pf-proof

The sequence is bounded, hence vercongent by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.  In particular,
$$
\abs{a_n-0}=1<2
$$
for every $n$, so it verconges to $0$ with $\varepsilon=2$.

On the other hand, the subsequence of even-indexed terms is constantly $1$,
while the subsequence of odd-indexed terms is constantly $-1$.  Since these
subsequences have different limits, $(a_n)$ does not converge in the usual
sense.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} give the exact characterization and the two requested
examples.

:::
