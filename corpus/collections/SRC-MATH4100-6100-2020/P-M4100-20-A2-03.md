::: {.problem}
Let $(x_n)$ be bounded. Prove each claim twice, once from each equivalent definition
$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n
=
\inf\{x:x\text{ is a subsequential limit of }(x_n)\}
$$
and
$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{k\ge n}x_k.
$$

(a) If $|x_n|\le M$ for every $n$, then
$$
\left|\liminf x_n\right|\le M.
$$

(b) If
$$
\beta<\liminf x_n,
$$
then there is $N$ such that $x_n>\beta$ for every $n\ge N$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
L_x=\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Using the subsequential-limit definition, if $|x_n|\leq M$ for every
$n$, then
$$
|L_x|\leq M.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $(x_n)$ is bounded, it has at least one subsequential limit. Let
$$
S=\{x:x\text{ is a subsequential limit of }(x_n)\}.
$$
If $x_{n_j}\to\ell$, then
$$
-M\leq x_{n_j}\leq M
$$
for every $j$, so passing to the limit gives
$$
-M\leq\ell\leq M.
$$
Thus every element of $S$ lies in $[-M,M]$. Since
$$
L_x=\inf S,
$$
one has
$$
-M\leq L_x\leq M,
$$
and therefore $|L_x|\leq M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Using the tail-infimum definition, if $|x_n|\leq M$ for every $n$,
then
$$
|L_x|\leq M.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $n$,
$$
-M
\leq
\inf_{k\geq n}x_k
\leq
M.
$$
By the second definition,
$$
\inf_{k\geq n}x_k\longrightarrow L_x.
$$
Passing the inequalities to the limit gives
$$
-M\leq L_x\leq M,
$$
so $|L_x|\leq M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Part (a) follows from either definition.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} prove the same assertion independently.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Using the subsequential-limit definition, if
$$
\beta<L_x,
$$
then $x_n>\beta$ for all sufficiently large $n$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose not. Then there are infinitely many indices $n$ with
$$
x_n\leq\beta.
$$
Choose a subsequence $(x_{n_j})$ consisting entirely of such terms. Since the
original sequence is bounded, $(x_{n_j})$ is bounded. By Bolzano--Weierstrass,
it has a convergent further subsequence
$$
x_{n_{j_\ell}}\longrightarrow\alpha.
$$
Because
$$
x_{n_{j_\ell}}\leq\beta
$$
for every $\ell$, passing to the limit gives
$$
\alpha\leq\beta.
$$
But $\alpha$ is a subsequential limit of $(x_n)$, so by the first definition
$$
L_x
=
\inf S
\leq
\alpha
\leq
\beta,
$$
contradicting $\beta<L_x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Using the tail-infimum definition, if
$$
\beta<L_x,
$$
then $x_n>\beta$ for all sufficiently large $n$.


::: pf-proof

Set
$$
t_n=\inf_{k\geq n}x_k.
$$
Then
$$
t_n\longrightarrow L_x.
$$
Choose
$$
\varepsilon=\frac{L_x-\beta}{2}>0.
$$
There exists $N$ such that for every $n\geq N$,
$$
|t_n-L_x|<\varepsilon.
$$
Hence
$$
t_n
>
L_x-\varepsilon
=
\frac{L_x+\beta}{2}
>
\beta.
$$
Since $x_n\geq t_n$, it follows that
$$
x_n>\beta
$$
for every $n\geq N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Part (b) follows from either definition.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} prove the same eventual lower-bound property independently
from the two definitions.

:::

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (a) twice, and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves part (b) twice.
