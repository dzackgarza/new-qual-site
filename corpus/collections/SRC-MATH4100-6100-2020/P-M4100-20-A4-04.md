::: {.problem}
For each set below, decide whether it is compact. For every noncompact set, demonstrate failure of both sequential compactness and the open-cover definition by giving

1. a sequence in the set with no subsequence converging to a point of the set, and
2. an open cover with no finite subcover.

The sets are
$$
\QQ,
\qquad
\QQ\cap[0,1],
\qquad
\RR,
\qquad
\ZZ\cap[0,10],
$$
$$
\left\{1,\frac12,\frac13,\ldots\right\},
\qquad
\left\{1,\frac12,\frac23,\frac34,\frac45,\ldots\right\}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The set $\QQ$ is not compact.


::: pf-proof

For sequential compactness, let
$$
q_n=\frac{\lfloor 10^n\sqrt2\rfloor}{10^n}.
$$
Then $q_n\in\QQ$ and
$$
0\leq\sqrt2-q_n<10^{-n},
$$
so $q_n\to\sqrt2\notin\QQ$. Every subsequence of $(q_n)$ has the same
limit $\sqrt2$, so no subsequence converges to a point of $\QQ$.

For the open-cover criterion, the family
$$
\mathcal U=\{(-n,n):n\in\NN\}
$$
covers $\QQ$. Any finite subfamily is contained in $(-N,N)$ for some $N$,
and therefore misses rational numbers of absolute value larger than $N$.
Thus no finite subcover exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The set $\QQ\cap[0,1]$ is not compact.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\alpha=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}
$$
and define
$$
q_n=\frac{\lfloor10^n\alpha\rfloor}{10^n}.
$$
Then
$$
q_n\in\QQ\cap[0,1],
\qquad
q_n\longrightarrow\alpha,
$$
while $\alpha\notin\QQ$. Hence no subsequence can converge to a point of
$\QQ\cap[0,1]$.

For each $n\geq1$, let
$$
U_n=
(-\infty,\alpha-1/n)
\cup
(\alpha+1/n,\infty).
$$
The family $(U_n)$ covers $\QQ\cap[0,1]$: if $q$ is rational, then
$q\neq\alpha$, so for sufficiently large $n$ one has
$1/n<\abs{q-\alpha}$. The sets $U_n$ increase with $n$, so any finite subfamily
has union $U_N$ for its largest index $N$. By density of $\QQ$, there is a
rational
$$
q\in
(\alpha-1/N,\alpha+1/N)\cap[0,1],
$$
and this $q$ is not in $U_N$. Thus there is no finite subcover.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The set $\RR$ is not compact.


::: pf-proof

The sequence
$$
x_n=n
$$
has no convergent subsequence in $\RR$, because every subsequence is
unbounded.

The open cover
$$
\{(-n,n):n\in\NN\}
$$
has no finite subcover, since every finite union is bounded while $\RR$ is
unbounded.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The set
$$
\ZZ\cap[0,10]
$$
is compact.


::: pf-proof

It is finite. Every finite subset of $\RR$ is compact. Equivalently, it is
closed and bounded, so compact by the Heine--Borel theorem.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The set
$$
E=\left\{1,\frac12,\frac13,\ldots\right\}
=
\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}
$$
is not compact.


::: pf-proof

The sequence
$$
\frac1n\longrightarrow0,
$$
but $0\notin E$. Every subsequence indexed by strictly increasing
$n_k$ also satisfies $n_k\to\infty$, hence $1/n_k\to0$. Thus no subsequence
converges to a point of $E$.

For $m\geq2$, let
$$
U_m=(1/m,\infty).
$$
The family $(U_m)_{m\geq2}$ covers $E$: the point $1/n$ belongs to $U_m$
whenever $m>n$. The sets increase with $m$, so a finite subfamily has union
$U_M$ for its largest index $M$. But
$$
\frac1M\notin U_M.
$$
Hence no finite subcover exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The set
$$
F=
\left\{
1,\frac12,\frac23,\frac34,\frac45,\ldots
\right\}
$$
is compact.


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
F=
\{1\}
\cup
\left\{
\frac{n}{n+1}:n\in\NN
\right\}.
$$
It is bounded, since
$$
\frac12\leq x\leq1
$$
for every $x\in F$.

The sequence $n/(n+1)$ is strictly increasing and converges to $1$. Hence its
only limit point is $1$, which already belongs to $F$. Therefore $F$ is closed.
By Heine--Borel, $F$ is compact.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The classifications are
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{set}&\text{compact?}\\
\hline
\QQ&\text{no}\\
\QQ\cap[0,1]&\text{no}\\
\RR&\text{no}\\
\ZZ\cap[0,10]&\text{yes}\\
\{1/n:n\in\NN\}&\text{no}\\
\{1\}\cup\{n/(n+1):n\in\NN\}&\text{yes}
\end{array}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} establish each entry, and for every noncompact set they give
both a sequence violating sequential compactness and an open cover with no
finite subcover.

:::

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} summarizes all six cases.
