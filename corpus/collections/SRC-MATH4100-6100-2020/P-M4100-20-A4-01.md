::: {.problem}
Let
$$
E=\left\{\frac{(-1)^n n}{n+1}:n\in\NN\right\}.
$$

(a) Find the limit points of $E$.

(b) Is $E$ closed? Is it open?

(c) Does $E$ contain isolated points?

(d) Find the closure $\overline E$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
x_n=\frac{(-1)^n n}{n+1}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The even-indexed terms converge to $1$:
$$
x_{2k}=\frac{2k}{2k+1}\longrightarrow1.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
1-x_{2k}
=
\frac{1}{2k+1}
\longrightarrow0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The odd-indexed terms converge to $-1$:
$$
x_{2k-1}
=
-\frac{2k-1}{2k}
\longrightarrow-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
x_{2k-1}+1
=
\frac1{2k}
\longrightarrow0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Both $1$ and $-1$ are limit points of $E$.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} exhibit sequences of distinct points of $E$ converging to
$1$ and $-1$, respectively.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

No point other than $1$ and $-1$ is a limit point of $E$.


::: pf-proof

Let $y$ be a limit point of $E$. Recursively choose distinct points
$$
e_j\in E\setminus\{y\}
$$
such that
$$
|e_j-y|<\frac1j,
$$
which is possible because every punctured neighborhood of a limit point
contains infinitely many points of $E$. Write $e_j=x_{n_j}$. Since the points
$e_j$ are distinct, the indices $n_j$ are distinct and therefore unbounded.
Passing to a subsequence if necessary, arrange that $n_j$ is strictly
increasing. Thus
$$
e_j=x_{n_j}\longrightarrow y
$$
with $n_j\to\infty$.

Among the integers $n_j$, at least one parity occurs infinitely often. Passing
to that parity subsequence, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} or step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that the resulting
subsequence converges to $1$ or $-1$. Since every subsequence of a convergent
sequence has the same limit $y$, one must have
$$
y\in\{-1,1\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The set of limit points of $E$ is
$$
\boxed{\{-1,1\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives both points, and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} excludes all others.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Neither $1$ nor $-1$ belongs to $E$.


::: pf-proof

For every positive integer $n$,
$$
\left|\frac{(-1)^n n}{n+1}\right|
=
\frac{n}{n+1}
<1.
$$
Thus no element of $E$ equals $1$ or $-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The set $E$ is not closed.


::: pf-proof

A closed subset of $\RR$ contains all of its limit points. By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$1$ and $-1$ are limit points of $E$, while step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} shows that neither
belongs to $E$. Hence $E$ is not closed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The set $E$ is not open.


::: pf-proof

Fix any $x\in E$. Every open interval about $x$ contains uncountably many real
numbers, whereas $E$ is countable because it is the image of $\NN$. Therefore
no open interval about $x$ can be contained in $E$. Thus $x$ is not an
interior point of $E$, and $E$ is not open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} show that $E$ is neither closed nor open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Every point of $E$ is isolated.


::: pf-proof

Fix $x_n\in E$. Suppose $x_n$ were not isolated. Then for each $j$ one could
choose
$$
x_{m_j}\in E\setminus\{x_n\}
$$
with
$$
|x_{m_j}-x_n|<\frac1j
$$
and with the indices $m_j$ distinct. Hence
$$
x_{m_j}\longrightarrow x_n.
$$
The distinct positive integers $m_j$ are unbounded, so after passing to a
subsequence of one parity, steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} imply convergence to $1$ or $-1$.
Therefore
$$
x_n\in\{-1,1\},
$$
contradicting step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}. Hence $x_n$ is isolated.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Part (c) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} shows that every point of $E$ is isolated.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The closure of $E$ is
$$
\boxed{\overline E=E\cup\{-1,1\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For any subset of $\RR$, the closure is the union of the set with all of its
limit points. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} identifies the limit points as exactly $-1$ and $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Part (d) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} establish parts (a)--(d).

:::
