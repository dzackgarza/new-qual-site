::: {.problem}
(a) Suppose $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, twice differentiable on $(a, b)$, and $f''(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in (a, b)$. Prove carefully that $f$ has at most $2$ distinct zeros in $[a, b]$.

(b) Prove that the function $f(x) = x^{2} - \sin x$ has precisely two roots.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $f$ has three distinct zeros
$$
x_1<x_2<x_3
$$
in $[a,b]$.


::: pf-proof

This is the negation of the desired conclusion in part (a).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exist
$$
c_1\in(x_1,x_2),
\qquad
c_2\in(x_2,x_3)
$$
such that
$$
f'(c_1)=f'(c_2)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply Rolle's theorem to $f$ on $[x_1,x_2]$ and on $[x_2,x_3]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

There exists $d\in(c_1,c_2)$ such that
$$
f''(d)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The derivative $f'$ is continuous on $[c_1,c_2]$ and differentiable on $(c_1,c_2)$ because $f$ is twice differentiable.
Since
$$
f'(c_1)=f'(c_2)=0,
$$
Rolle's theorem applied to $f'$ gives such a point $d$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore $f$ has at most two distinct zeros in $[a,b]$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} contradicts the hypothesis that
$$
f''(x)\neq0
$$
for every $x\in(a,b)$.
Hence three distinct zeros cannot exist.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For
$$
F(x)=x^2-\sin x,
$$
one has
$$
F''(x)=2+\sin x\geq1
$$
for every $x\in\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Differentiating twice gives
$$
F'(x)=2x-\cos x,
$$
and
$$
F''(x)=2+\sin x.
$$
Since $\sin x\geq-1$, the lower bound follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The function $F$ has at most two real roots.


::: pf-proof

If it had three distinct roots, choose a closed interval containing all three.
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} verifies the hypotheses of part (a) on that interval, contradicting step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The number $0$ is a root of $F$.


::: pf-proof

Directly,
$$
F(0)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

There exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
0<x<\delta
\implies
F(x)<0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
F'(0)=-1,
$$
the definition of derivative gives $\delta>0$ such that
$$
0<\abs{x}<\delta
\implies
\abs{
\frac{F(x)-F(0)}x+1
}
<
\frac12.
$$
For $0<x<\delta$, this implies
$$
\frac{F(x)}x<-\frac12.
$$
Since $x>0$, one has $F(x)<0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

One has
$$
F(1)>0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\sin1<1,
$$
one has
$$
F(1)=1-\sin1>0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

There exists a root
$$
r\in(0,1)
$$
distinct from $0$.


::: pf-proof

Choose
$$
x_0\in(0,\min\{\delta,1\}).
$$
Then step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives $F(x_0)<0$, while step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives $F(1)>0$.
Continuity of $F$ and the Intermediate Value Theorem give
$$
r\in(x_0,1)
$$
with $F(r)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The function
$$
F(x)=x^2-\sin x
$$
has precisely two real roots.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} give two distinct roots, while step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} excludes any third root.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::
