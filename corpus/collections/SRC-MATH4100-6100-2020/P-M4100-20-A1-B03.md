::: {.problem}
Directly prove the equivalence of the Bolzano--Weierstrass Theorem and the Nested Interval Property.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Assume the Bolzano--Weierstrass Theorem, and let
$$
I_1\supseteq I_2\supseteq\cdots
$$
be a nested sequence of nonempty closed bounded intervals.


::: pf-proof

For each $n$, choose a point
$$
x_n\in I_n.
$$
Since every $I_n$ is contained in the bounded interval $I_1$, the sequence
$(x_n)$ is bounded.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The sequence $(x_n)$ has a convergent subsequence.


::: pf-proof

This is the Bolzano--Weierstrass Theorem applied to the bounded sequence from
step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.  Write
$$
x_{n_k}\longrightarrow x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The limit $x$ belongs to every interval $I_m$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $m$.  Since the subsequence indices satisfy $n_k\to\infty$, there exists
$K$ such that
$$
n_k\geq m
$$
for every $k\geq K$.  Nestedness then gives
$$
x_{n_k}\in I_{n_k}\subseteq I_m
$$
for every $k\geq K$.  The interval $I_m$ is closed, so the limit of the tail
$(x_{n_k})_{k\geq K}$ also belongs to $I_m$.  Hence $x\in I_m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} I_n\neq\varnothing.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that $x$ belongs to every $I_m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If the lengths of the intervals tend to zero, then the intersection is
a singleton.


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
I_n=[a_n,b_n]
$$
and suppose
$$
b_n-a_n\longrightarrow0.
$$
If $x,y$ belong to every $I_n$, then
$$
\abs{x-y}\leq b_n-a_n
$$
for every $n$.  Letting $n\to\infty$ gives $\abs{x-y}=0$, so $x=y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Thus the Bolzano--Weierstrass Theorem implies the Nested Interval
Property.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives nonempty intersection for every nested sequence of nonempty
closed bounded intervals, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives uniqueness when their lengths
tend to zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Assume now the Nested Interval Property, and let $(x_n)$ be a bounded
sequence.


::: pf-proof

Choose a closed bounded interval
$$
I_1=[a_1,b_1]
$$
containing every term of the sequence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

There exists a nested sequence of closed intervals
$$
I_1\supseteq I_2\supseteq I_3\supseteq\cdots
$$
such that each $I_k$ contains infinitely many terms of $(x_n)$ and
$$
\operatorname{length}(I_k)
=2^{-(k-1)}\operatorname{length}(I_1).
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose $I_k=[a_k,b_k]$ has been chosen and contains infinitely many terms of
the sequence.  Let
$$
m_k=\frac{a_k+b_k}{2}.
$$
The two closed half-intervals
$$
[a_k,m_k]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[m_k,b_k]
$$
cover $I_k$.  At least one of them contains infinitely many terms of the
sequence; otherwise their union would contain only finitely many such terms.
Choose one such half as $I_{k+1}$.  Its length is half that of $I_k$, and it is
contained in $I_k$.  Induction gives the construction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

There is a unique point
$$
x\in\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}I_k.
$$


::: pf-proof

The intervals constructed in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} are nested, nonempty, closed, and
bounded, and their lengths tend to zero.  The Nested Interval Property
therefore gives a nonempty intersection, and its shrinking-length clause makes
that intersection a singleton.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

There is a subsequence $(x_{n_k})$ such that
$$
x_{n_k}\in I_k
$$
for every $k$.


::: pf-proof

Choose $n_1$ with $x_{n_1}\in I_1$.  Having chosen $n_k$, use the fact that
$I_{k+1}$ contains infinitely many terms of the original sequence to choose
$$
n_{k+1}>n_k
$$
with
$$
x_{n_{k+1}}\in I_{k+1}.
$$
This recursively defines a subsequence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The subsequence $(x_{n_k})$ converges to the point $x$ from step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

Both $x_{n_k}$ and $x$ belong to $I_k$.  Hence
$$
\abs{x_{n_k}-x}
\leq
\operatorname{length}(I_k).
$$
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, the right-hand side tends to zero.  Therefore
$$
x_{n_k}\longrightarrow x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Thus the Nested Interval Property implies the Bolzano--Weierstrass
Theorem.


::: pf-proof

Every bounded sequence admits the convergent subsequence constructed in
steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves one implication and step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} proves the converse.

:::
