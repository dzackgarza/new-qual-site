::: problem
Let $X$ be the topological space constructed by attaching a closed 2-disk $\DD^2$ to the circle $S^1$ by a continuous map $\partial\DD^2 \to S^1$ of degree $d > 0$ on the boundary circle.

a.  Show that every continuous map $X \to X$ has a fixed point.

b.  Explain how to obtain all the connected covering spaces of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
X=S^1\cup_{\varphi}D^2,
\qquad
\deg(\varphi)=d>0.
\]

<1>1. With rational coefficients, the cellular chain complex of $X$ is
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathbb Q
\xrightarrow{\ d\ }
\mathbb Q
\xrightarrow{\ 0\ }
\mathbb Q
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence
\[
H_k(X;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&k=0,\\
0,&k>0.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the CW structure with one cell in each of dimensions $0,1,2$.
The boundary of the $1$-cell is zero, and the cellular boundary of the $2$-cell is multiplication by the degree of its attaching map, namely $d$.
Since $d\ne0$, multiplication by $d$ is an isomorphism of the one-dimensional vector space $\mathbb Q$.
The displayed homology groups follow.
:::

<1>2. Every continuous map $f:X\to X$ has a fixed point.
::: {.proof}
The space $X$ is a finite connected CW complex, so the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem applies.
By <1>1, the only nonzero rational homology group is
\[
H_0(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q.
\]
Because $X$ is connected, every self-map induces the identity on $H_0$.
Therefore the Lefschetz number is
\[
L(f)
=
\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(f_*:H_k(X;\mathbb Q)\to H_k(X;\mathbb Q)\bigr)
=1.
\]
Since $L(f)\ne0$, the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem implies that $f$ has a fixed point.
:::

<1>3. The fundamental group of $X$ is
\[
\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is $S^1$, with fundamental group generated by a loop $a$.
Attaching the $2$-cell along a degree-$d$ map imposes the relation $a^d=1$.
Seifert--van Kampen gives
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a\mid a^d=1\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>4. Up to isomorphism, there is exactly one connected covering of $X$ of degree $m$ for each positive divisor $m$ of $d$, and there are no other connected coverings.
::: {.proof}
As a finite CW complex, $X$ is path connected, locally path connected, and semilocally simply connected, so the classification theorem for connected covering spaces applies.
It identifies isomorphism classes of connected coverings with conjugacy classes of subgroups of $\pi_1(X)$.

By <1>3,
\[
\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z
\]
is cyclic and hence abelian.
For each positive divisor $m\mid d$, it has the unique subgroup
\[
H_m=\langle \overline m\rangle,
\]
whose order is $d/m$ and whose index is $m$.
These are all the subgroups of a finite cyclic group.
Thus there is exactly one connected $m$-sheeted cover for every $m\mid d$, and no connected covers of any other degree.
:::

<1>5. The $m$-sheeted cover for $m\mid d$ can be constructed explicitly by lifting the CW structure.
::: {.proof}
Start over the $1$-skeleton with the connected $m$-fold covering
\[
p_m:S^1\longrightarrow S^1,
\qquad
p_m(z)=z^m.
\]
The attaching map
\[
\varphi:S^1\to S^1
\]
has degree $d$.
It lifts through $p_m$ exactly when
\[
\varphi_*\pi_1(S^1)=d\mathbb Z
\subseteq
m\mathbb Z=(p_m)_*\pi_1(S^1),
\]
which is equivalent to $m\mid d$.

When $m\mid d$, there are $m$ lifts
\[
\widetilde\varphi_j:S^1\to S^1,
\qquad
j=0,\ldots,m-1,
\]
one for each choice of a point above the chosen basepoint.
Each lift has degree $d/m$ because
\[
p_m\circ\widetilde\varphi_j=\varphi
\]
and degrees multiply.

Attach one copy $D_j^2$ of the $2$-cell along each $\widetilde\varphi_j$.
The resulting space is
\[
\widetilde X_m
=
S^1\cup_{\widetilde\varphi_0}D_0^2
\cup\cdots\cup_{\widetilde\varphi_{m-1}}D_{m-1}^2.
\]
Map the covering circle by $p_m$, and map each open $2$-cell homeomorphically onto the open $2$-cell of $X$ using the lifted characteristic map.
The cell-lifting construction gives an $m$-sheeted covering
\[
\widetilde X_m\longrightarrow X.
\]
Its restriction over the $1$-skeleton corresponds to the subgroup $m\mathbb Z\le\mathbb Z$, so its subgroup in
\[
\pi_1(X)=\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z
\]
is precisely $H_m$ from <1>4. Hence these explicit covers realize every connected covering space of $X$.
:::
