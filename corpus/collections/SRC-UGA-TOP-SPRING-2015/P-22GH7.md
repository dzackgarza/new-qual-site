::: problem
Let $X$ be the topological space obtained as the quotient space of a regular $2n\dash$gon ($n \geq 2$) in $\RR^2$ by identifying opposite edges via translations in the plane.

First show that X is a compact, orientable surface without boundary, and then identify its genus as a function of $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $P$ be the regular $2n$-gon, with vertices
\[
v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_{2n-1}
\]
in cyclic order, and let
\[
e_i=[v_i,v_{i+1}],
\]
where all subscripts are taken modulo $2n$.

<1>1. The translation identifying $e_i$ with its opposite edge $e_{i+n}$ identifies
\[
v_i\sim v_{i+n+1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
v_{i+1}\sim v_{i+n}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Place the center of $P$ at the origin.
Since the polygon is regular,
\[
v_{i+n}=-v_i
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
v_{i+n+1}=-v_{i+1}.
\]
The vector from $v_i$ to $v_{i+1}$ is therefore the same as the vector from $v_{i+n+1}$ to $v_{i+n}$:
\[
v_{i+1}-v_i
=
v_{i+n}-v_{i+n+1}.
\]
Hence the translation carrying $e_i$ onto $e_{i+n}$ sends $v_i$ to $v_{i+n+1}$ and $v_{i+1}$ to $v_{i+n}$.
:::

<1>2. The quotient $X$ is a compact connected surface without boundary.
::: {.proof}
The quotient map
\[
q:P\to X
\]
is continuous and surjective.
Since $P$ is compact and connected, so is $X$.
The images of the vertex classes, paired open edges, and polygon interior give $X$ the structure of a finite CW complex, so $X$ is Hausdorff and second countable.

It remains to check the local surface condition.
An interior point of $P$ has a disk neighborhood on which $q$ is injective.
A point in the interior of an edge has two half-disk neighborhoods, one from each of the paired opposite edges; the edge identification glues their boundary intervals together, producing a disk neighborhood in $X$.

For a vertex class, choose pairwise disjoint sufficiently small sectors around all vertices in that class.
Intersecting each sector with a small link gives an interval.
The side identifications pair the endpoints of these intervals.
Every endpoint is paired exactly once, so the quotient link is a finite graph in which every vertex has degree $2$.
It is connected because its intervals come from one vertex-equivalence class, and the endpoint pairings are precisely the identifications generating that class.
Hence the quotient link is a circle.
The corresponding quotient of the sectors is therefore a disk neighborhood of the vertex class.

Thus every point of $X$ has a disk neighborhood.
In particular no point has a half-disk neighborhood, so $X$ has empty boundary.
:::

<1>3. The surface $X$ is orientable.
::: {.proof}
Give the interior of $P$ its standard orientation from $\mathbb R^2$.
The cyclic boundary orientations on a pair of opposite sides are opposite under the identifying translation: by <1>1, the boundary direction from $v_i$ to $v_{i+1}$ is sent to the direction from $v_{i+n+1}$ to $v_{i+n}$, which is opposite to the cyclic boundary direction on $e_{i+n}$.

Therefore each side pairing is orientation-reversing with respect to the induced boundary orientations.
Equivalently, the oriented half-disk charts on the two sides of every seam glue to an oriented disk chart.
Hence the orientation on the polygon interior extends across all seams and vertex neighborhoods, giving an orientation of $X$.
:::

<1>4. The quotient has one vertex if $n$ is even and two vertices if $n$ is odd.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, on the index set $\mathbb Z/(2n)$ the vertex relation is generated by
\[
i\sim i+(n+1).
\]
The second endpoint relation is the inverse shift, since
\[
-(n+1)\equiv n-1\pmod{2n}.
\]
Thus the vertex classes are the cosets of the subgroup generated by $n+1$ in $\mathbb Z/(2n)$, and their number is
\[
\gcd(2n,n+1)=\gcd(2,n+1).
\]
Consequently
\[
V=
\begin{cases}
1,&n\text{ even},\\
2,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
:::

<1>5. The Euler characteristic of $X$ is
\[
\chi(X)=
\begin{cases}
2-n,&n\text{ even},\\
3-n,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
The polygon quotient gives a CW decomposition of $X$ with one $2$-cell, one $1$-cell for each pair of opposite edges, and one $0$-cell for each vertex class.
Hence
\[
F=1,
\qquad
E=n,
\qquad
V=
\begin{cases}
1,&n\text{ even},\\
2,&n\text{ odd}
\end{cases}
\]
by <1>4. Therefore
\[
\chi(X)=V-E+F,
\]
which gives the stated two cases.
:::

<1>6. The genus of $X$ is
\[
\boxed{g=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>3, $X$ is a compact connected orientable surface without boundary.
The classification of compact surfaces therefore gives
\[
\chi(X)=2-2g.
\]
If $n$ is even, <1>5 yields
\[
2-2g=2-n,
\]
so $g=n/2$.
If $n$ is odd, <1>5 yields
\[
2-2g=3-n,
\]
so $g=(n-1)/2$.
These two formulas are exactly
\[
g=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.
\]
:::
