::: problem
Let $X$ denote the quotient space formed from the sphere $S^2$ by identifying two distinct points.

Compute the fundamental group and the homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the sphere as the suspension
\[
S^2=\Sigma S^1
=
\frac{S^1\times[0,1]}
{S^1\times\{0\}\sim N,
\ S^1\times\{1\}\sim S},
\]
and take the two points being identified to be the suspension points $N$ and $S$.
This causes no loss of generality because any two distinct points of $S^2$ are carried to $N,S$ by a homeomorphism of the sphere.

<1>1. The quotient $X$ has a CW structure with one cell in each of dimensions $0,1,2$.
The $2$-cell is attached to the $1$-cell by the loop
\[
aa^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Give $S^1$ its standard CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$ and one open $1$-cell $e$.
In the suspension, the set
\[
v\times[0,1]
\]
is an interval joining $N$ to $S$.
After identifying $N$ with $S$, this interval becomes a single $1$-cell $a$ whose two endpoints are attached to the unique $0$-cell.

The suspension of the open $1$-cell $e$ is an open $2$-cell.
Before quotienting, its characteristic disk can be viewed as the rectangle
\[
\overline e\times[0,1]
\]
with the top and bottom edges collapsed to the suspension points.
The two remaining boundary edges are the two copies of $v\times[0,1]$, traversed with opposite orientations around the boundary of the rectangle.
After $N\sim S$, these become $a$ and $a^{-1}$.
Hence the attaching loop of the $2$-cell is $aa^{-1}$.
:::

<1>2. The fundamental group is
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is a circle generated by $a$.
The $2$-cell imposes the relation
\[
aa^{-1}=1,
\]
which is already trivial.
Seifert--van Kampen therefore gives
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a\mid aa^{-1}=1\rangle
\cong
\langle a\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>3. The cellular chain complex of $X$ is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\ 0\ }
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\ 0\ }
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 there is one cell in each of dimensions $0,1,2$.
The unique $1$-cell begins and ends at the same $0$-cell, so $\partial_1=0$.

For the unique $2$-cell, the cellular boundary is the total signed degree with which its attaching loop traverses the $1$-cell.
By <1>1 the attaching word is
\[
aa^{-1},
\]
so the exponent sum is
\[
1-1=0.
\]
Thus $\partial_2=0$.
:::

<1>4. The homology groups are
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,1,2,\\
0,&k>2.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
All differentials in the cellular chain complex from <1>3 vanish.
Hence
\[
H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
There are no cells above dimension $2$.
:::

<1>5. In particular,
\[
X\simeq S^2\vee S^1.
\]
::: {.proof}
The attaching map $aa^{-1}:S^1\to S^1$ is null-homotopic.
Attaching a $2$-cell to a space by a null-homotopic attaching map yields a space homotopy equivalent to the wedge of the original space with $S^2$.
Here the original $1$-skeleton is $S^1$.
:::
