::: {.problem}
(a) Give the $\varepsilon$, $\delta$ definition for continuity of a function $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ at a point $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}$.

(b) Assume that $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous and that $\{a_n\}$ is a sequence of real numbers with $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = L$.
Prove that $\lim_{n \to \infty} f(a_n) = f(L)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
The function $f$ is continuous at $x_0$ if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that
\[
|x-x_0|<\delta\implies |f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon.
\]

:::

::: pf-step
Suppose $a_n\to L$ and $f$ is continuous at $L$. Let $\varepsilon>0$.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}
There exists $\delta>0$ such that
\[
|x-L|<\delta\implies |f(x)-f(L)|<\varepsilon.
\]

::: pf-proof
This is continuity of $f$ at $L$ from step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}
There exists $N\in\mathbb N$ such that
\[
n\ge N\implies |a_n-L|<\delta.
\]

::: pf-proof
This is the definition of $a_n\to L$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}
Hence, for every $n\ge N$,
\[
|f(a_n)-f(L)|<\varepsilon.
\]

::: pf-proof
Apply step [](#s3){.pf-ref} to $x=a_n$, using step [](#s4){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore $f(a_n)\to f(L)$.

::: pf-proof
The estimate in step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the definition of convergence of the sequence $f(a_n)$ to $f(L)$.
:::
