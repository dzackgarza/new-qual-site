::: {.problem}
(a) Give the $\varepsilon$, $\delta$ definition for continuity of a function $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ at a point $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}$.

(b) Assume that $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous and that $\{a_n\}$ is a sequence of real numbers with $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = L$.
Prove that $\lim_{n \to \infty} f(a_n) = f(L)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function $f$ is continuous at $x_0$ if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that
\[
|x-x_0|<\delta\implies |f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon.
\]
:::

<1>2. Suppose $a_n\to L$ and $f$ is continuous at $L$. Let $\varepsilon>0$.

<1>3. There exists $\delta>0$ such that
\[
|x-L|<\delta\implies |f(x)-f(L)|<\varepsilon.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is continuity of $f$ at $L$ from <1>1.
:::

<1>4. There exists $N\in\mathbb N$ such that
\[
n\ge N\implies |a_n-L|<\delta.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the definition of $a_n\to L$.
:::

<1>5. Hence, for every $n\ge N$,
\[
|f(a_n)-f(L)|<\varepsilon.
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply <1>3 to $x=a_n$, using <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $f(a_n)\to f(L)$.
::: {.proof}
The estimate in <1>5 is the definition of convergence of the sequence $f(a_n)$ to $f(L)$.
:::
