::: problem
Suppose that $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ is a linear transformation satisfying $T(1,1) = (5, 3)$ and $T(2,3) = (7, 9)$.
Find the standard matrix of $T$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Let
\[
u=T(1,0),\qquad v=T(0,1).
\]
Then
\[
u+v=(5,3),
\qquad
2u+3v=(7,9).
\]
::: {.proof}
This follows from linearity and the decompositions $(1,1)=(1,0)+(0,1)$ and $(2,3)=2(1,0)+3(0,1)$.
:::

<1>2. We have
\[
v=(-3,3).
\]
::: {.proof}
Subtract twice the first equation in <1>1 from the second:
\[
(2u+3v)-2(u+v)=v=(7,9)-2(5,3)=(-3,3).
\]
:::

<1>3. We have
\[
u=(8,0).
\]
::: {.proof}
From $u+v=(5,3)$ and <1>2,
\[
u=(5,3)-(-3,3)=(8,0).
\]
:::

<1>4. Therefore the standard matrix of $T$ is
\[
[T]=\begin{bmatrix}8&-3\\0&3\end{bmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The columns of the standard matrix are $T(1,0)=u$ and $T(0,1)=v$.
:::
:::
