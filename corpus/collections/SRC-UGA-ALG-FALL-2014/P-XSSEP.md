::: {.problem}
Let $f\in \QQ[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial and $L$ a finite Galois extension of $\QQ$.
Let $f(x) = g_1(x)g_2(x)\cdots g_r(x)$ be a factorization of $f$ into irreducibles in $L[x]$.

a. Prove that each of the factors $g_i(x)$ has the same degree.

b. Give an example showing that if $L$ is not Galois over $\QQ$, the conclusion of part (a) need not hold.
:::

::: {.problem}
Let $f\in \QQ[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial and $L$ a finite Galois extension of $\QQ$.
Let $f(x) = g_1(x)g_2(x)\cdots g_r(x)$ be a factorization of $f$ into irreducibles in $L[x]$.

(a) Prove that each of the factors $g_i(x)$ has the same degree.

(b) Give an example showing that if $L$ is not Galois over $\QQ$, the conclusion of part (a) need not hold.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper, so no retained solution set covers this problem and the argument is the card's own; it is the standard orbit argument, checked against the book's Galois correspondence.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #galois-closure}
Let $M$ be a splitting field of $f$ over $L$. Then $M/\QQ$ is finite Galois, and with $G=\Gal(M/\QQ)$ and $H=\Gal(M/L)$ one has $H\normal G$.

::: pf-proof
$M$ is the splitting field over $L$ of $f\in\QQ[x]\subseteq L[x]$, hence also the splitting field of $f$ over $\QQ$ together with $L$; since $M\supseteq L\supseteq\QQ$ and $M/\QQ$ is then generated by the roots of $f$ over a finite Galois extension, $M/\QQ$ is Galois: it is the splitting field of $f$ over $\QQ$ compositum with $L$, and a compositum of Galois extensions inside a Galois closure is Galois. The subgroup $H=\Gal(M/L)$ is normal in $G$ because $L/\QQ$ is Galois, by the fundamental theorem.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #orbits}
The roots of $g_i$ are exactly the elements of one orbit of $H$ on the roots of $f$, and $\deg g_i$ is the size of that orbit.

::: pf-proof
$f$ is separable, being irreducible over the field $\QQ$ of characteristic $0$, and $M$ contains all its roots. For a root $\alpha$ of $f$, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $L$ has as its roots exactly the distinct elements $\sigma(\alpha)$ with $\sigma\in H$: each $\sigma\in H$ fixes $L$ and so carries $\alpha$ to another root of the same minimal polynomial, and conversely every root of that minimal polynomial is $\tau(\alpha)$ for some $\tau\in\Gal(M/L)=H$ by the extension of embeddings to $M$. The minimal polynomial is one of the irreducible factors $g_i$, and its degree is the number of distinct conjugates $\abs{H\cdot\alpha}$, the stabiliser being trivial because the conjugates are distinct.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #transitive}
$G$ acts transitively on the roots of $f$.

::: pf-proof
$f$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ and $M/\QQ$ is Galois containing a root of $f$, so $f$ is the minimal polynomial of each of its roots; hence the roots are the $G$-translates of any one of them, by the same argument as step [](#orbits){.pf-ref} with $\QQ$ in place of $L$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(a) All the factors $g_i$ have the same degree.

::: pf-proof
For roots $\alpha,\beta$ of $f$ choose $\sigma\in G$ with $\sigma(\alpha)=\beta$, which step [](#transitive){.pf-ref} supplies. Normality of $H$ gives $\sigma H=H\sigma$, hence $\sigma(H\cdot\alpha)=H\cdot\sigma(\alpha)=H\cdot\beta$; so $\sigma$ carries the orbit of $\alpha$ onto the orbit of $\beta$ and the two orbits have the same cardinality. As $\alpha,\beta$ range over the roots, this says that all $H$-orbits have one size, and by step [](#orbits){.pf-ref} the degrees of the irreducible factors of $f$ over $L$ are all equal to that size.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(b) For $f(x)=x^3-2$ and $L=\QQ(\sqrt[3]{2})$ the factors over $L$ have degrees $1$ and $2$.

::: pf-proof
$f$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ by Eisenstein at the prime $2$. The extension $L/\QQ$ is not Galois, since $L\subseteq\RR$ contains the real root $\sqrt[3]{2}$ but neither non-real root $\zeta_3\sqrt[3]{2}$ nor $\zeta_3^2\sqrt[3]{2}$. Over $L$,
$$
x^3-2=\bigl(x-\sqrt[3]{2}\bigr)\bigl(x^2+\sqrt[3]{2}\,x+\sqrt[3]{4}\bigr),
$$
and the quadratic factor is irreducible over the real field $L$, its two roots being the non-real numbers $\zeta_3\sqrt[3]{2}$ and $\zeta_3^2\sqrt[3]{2}$. So the factorization into irreducibles over $L$ has factors of degrees $1$ and $2$, which are not equal, and $L$ is not Galois over $\QQ$.
:::
