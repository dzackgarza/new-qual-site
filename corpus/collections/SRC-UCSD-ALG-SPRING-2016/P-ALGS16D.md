::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be a field.

(a) Is the polynomial ring $K[x,y]$ Noetherian?
Why or why not?

(b) Find a subring $S \subseteq K[x,y]$ which is not Noetherian.
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #p1-s1}
$K[x,y]$ is Noetherian.

::: pf-proof
by the Hilbert basis theorem, if $R$ is Noetherian then $R[x]$ is Noetherian; $K$ is a field (hence Noetherian), so $K[x]$ is Noetherian, and then $K[x,y] = (K[x])[y]$ is Noetherian.
:::

:::

:::

**(b).**

::: pf

::: pf-step
Let $S = K[x, xy, xy^2, xy^3, \ldots]$, the subring of $K[x,y]$ generated by $x$ and all $xy^n$ for $n \ge 0$.

::: pf-proof
define the subring.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Consider the ideal $I = (x, xy, xy^2, xy^3, \ldots)$ of $S$.

::: pf-proof
the ideal generated by all the generators except the constants.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-s3}
$I$ is not finitely generated.

::: pf-proof
Suppose instead that $I$ were generated by finitely many elements. Each such element is a finite $K$-linear combination of monomials in the generators, so there is an $N$ such that every term of $x$-degree exactly $1$ occurring in those generators is a $K$-linear combination of $x,xy,\ldots,xy^N$. Every nonconstant element of $S$ has positive $x$-degree. Hence multiplying one of the chosen generators by a nonconstant coefficient of $S$ produces only terms of $x$-degree at least $2$. Therefore the $x$-degree-$1$ part of the ideal generated by the chosen finite set lies in the $K$-span of $x,xy,\ldots,xy^N$. But $xy^{N+1}\in I$ has $x$-degree $1$ and lies outside that span, a contradiction.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-s4}
Hence $S$ has an ideal that is not finitely generated, so $S$ is not Noetherian.

::: pf-proof
step [](#p2-s3){.pf-ref}.
:::
