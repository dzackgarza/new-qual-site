::: {.problem}
Given that $\pi$ is not the zero of a nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients, prove that $\pi^{2}$ cannot be written in the form $a\pi + b$, where $a$ and $b$ are rational.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $\pi^2=a\pi+b$ for some $a,b\in\QQ$, then $\pi$ is a root of a nonzero polynomial in $\QQ[x]$.


::: pf-proof

Define $g=x^2-ax-b\in\QQ[x]$. Its leading coefficient is $1$, so $g$ is nonzero. Under the assumed identity, evaluation yields $g(\pi)=\pi^2-a\pi-b=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There do not exist $a,b\in\QQ$ such that $\pi^2=a\pi+b$.


::: pf-proof

The given hypothesis says that no nonzero polynomial in $\QQ[x]$ vanishes at $\pi$. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} contradicts this hypothesis whenever such coefficients $a,b$ exist.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} is the requested conclusion.

:::
