::: {.problem}
Given that π is not the zero of a nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients, prove that $\pi ^ { 2 }$ cannot be written in the form $a \pi + b ,$ where a and b are rational.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $\pi^2=a\pi+b$ for some $a,b\in\mathbb Q$, then $\pi$ is a root of a nonzero polynomial in $\mathbb Q[x]$.

::: {.proof}
Define $g=x^2-ax-b\in\mathbb Q[x]$. Its leading coefficient is $1$, so $g$ is nonzero. Under the assumed identity, evaluation yields $g(\pi)=\pi^2-a\pi-b=0$.
:::

<1>2. There do not exist $a,b\in\mathbb Q$ such that $\pi^2=a\pi+b$.

::: {.proof}
The given hypothesis says that no nonzero polynomial in $\mathbb Q[x]$ vanishes at $\pi$. Step <1>1 contradicts this hypothesis whenever such coefficients $a,b$ exist.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 is the requested conclusion.
:::
:::
