::: {.problem}
Show that for every prime $p$ there exists a field of order $p^{2}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained solution outline for this chapter, **item 19, PDF p. 3** (`assets/attachments/Ch17Sltns.pdf`), restructured into steps; step [](#irreducible){.pf-ref} replaces the outline's reference to exercise 18 with a count, since the exercise it names is not proved in this card.

Throughout, $p$ is a prime, $\ZZ_{p}$ is the field of residues modulo $p$, and a polynomial is monic when its leading coefficient is $1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #field}
$\ZZ_{p}$ is a field.

::: pf-proof
Residues modulo $p$ form a commutative ring with $1$. Given $a\not\equiv0\pmod p$, $p$ does not divide $a$, so $\gcd(a,p)=1$ and Bézout gives $u,v$ with $ua+vp=1$; reducing modulo $p$ gives $ua\equiv1$, so $a$ is a unit. Hence every nonzero element is invertible and $\ZZ_{p}$ is a field.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #irreducible}
There is a monic quadratic polynomial in $\ZZ_{p}[x]$ irreducible over $\ZZ_{p}$.

::: pf-proof
Monic quadratics over $\ZZ_{p}$ are the $p^{2}$ polynomials $x^{2}+ax+b$ with $a,b\in\ZZ_{p}$.

A monic quadratic that is reducible factors into monic linear polynomials, that is $x^{2}+ax+b=(x-c)(x-d)$ with $c,d\in\ZZ_{p}$; conversely every such product is a reducible monic quadratic. The number of unordered pairs $\{c,d\}$ with repetition from a $p$-element set is $p(p+1)/2$: there are $p(p-1)/2$ pairs with $c\neq d$ and $p$ pairs with $c=d$, and distinct pairs give distinct products because a monic quadratic is determined by its two roots.

Since $p(p+1)/2<p^{2}$ — equivalently $p+1<2p$, which holds for every prime $p\ge2$ — there are fewer reducible monic quadratics than monic quadratics, so some $x^{2}+ax+b$ is irreducible over $\ZZ_{p}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #quotient}
$F=\ZZ_{p}[x]/\langle f(x)\rangle$ is a field, where $f(x)=x^{2}+ax+b$ is the irreducible quadratic of step [](#irreducible){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof
$\ZZ_{p}$ is a field by step [](#field){.pf-ref} and $\ZZ_{p}[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, so $f$ irreducible means $\langle f\rangle$ is a maximal ideal (Theorem 17.5, printed p. 311). The quotient of a commutative ring by a maximal ideal is a field (Corollary 1 of Theorem 17.5, printed p. 312), so $F$ is a field.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order}
$F$ has exactly $p^{2}$ elements.

::: pf-proof
Every element of $F$ is a coset $g(x)+\langle f\rangle$. Dividing $g$ by $f$ by the division algorithm gives $g=qf+r$ with $\deg r<2$ or $r=0$, so $g+\langle f\rangle=r+\langle f\rangle$ with $r(x)=cx+d$, $c,d\in\ZZ_{p}$; hence every element of $F$ is of the form $cx+d+\langle f\rangle$.

These $p^{2}$ cosets are distinct: if $cx+d+\langle f\rangle=c'x+d'+\langle f\rangle$, then $(c-c')x+(d-d')$ lies in $\langle f\rangle$, so it is a multiple $hf$ of $f$. Its degree is at most $1$ while $f$ has degree $2$, so $h=0$ and $c=c'$, $d=d'$. Hence $F$ has exactly $p^{2}$ elements.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
For every prime $p$ there is a field of order $p^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
Given $p$, steps [](#irreducible){.pf-ref}, [](#quotient){.pf-ref} and [](#order){.pf-ref} produce an irreducible quadratic $f\in\ZZ_{p}[x]$, a field $F=\ZZ_{p}[x]/\langle f\rangle$, and a bijection of $F$ with the $p^{2}$ pairs $(c,d)\in\ZZ_{p}^{2}$. So a field of order $p^{2}$ exists.
:::
