::: {.problem}
Show that for every prime p there exists a field of order $p ^ { 2 } .$
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $k=\mathbb F_p$.

<1>1. There exists a monic irreducible quadratic $q\in k[x]$.

::: {.proof}
If $p=2$, take $q=x^2+x+1$; it takes the value $1$ at both elements of $\mathbb F_2$, and hence has no root. If $p$ is odd, the squaring map on $k^\times$ has fibers of cardinality two, because $u^2=v^2$ implies $u=v$ or $u=-v$, and these are distinct for $v\neq0$. Thus exactly $(p-1)/2$ elements of $k^\times$ are squares. Choose a nonsquare $a\in k^\times$ and let $q=x^2-a$. It has no root in $k$. In either case $q$ has degree two and no root, so it is irreducible over $k$.
:::

<1>2. There exists a field of cardinality
\[
\boxed{p^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Let $q$ be as in step <1>1 and set $K=k[x]/(q)$. Since $q$ is irreducible, Bézout's identity shows that every nonzero class has an inverse: for $g$ not divisible by $q$, there exist $u,v\in k[x]$ with $ug+vq=1$. Thus $K$ is a field. Division by the monic quadratic $q$ gives every class a representative $a+bx$, with $a,b\in k$. If two such representatives give the same class, their difference is a multiple of $q$ of degree less than two, and therefore zero. There are exactly $p$ independent choices for each coefficient, so $|K|=p^2$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 constructs the required field for every prime $p$.
:::
