::: {.problem}
Show that the field given in Example 11 in this chapter, $\mathbb{Z}_3[x]/\langle x^2 + 1 \rangle$, is isomorphic to the field given in Example 9 in Chapter 13, $\mathbb{Z}_3[i]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $k=\mathbb F_3$ and $q=x^2+1\in k[x]$. Let $i$ satisfy $i^2=-1$ in the field $k[i]$.

<1>1. The polynomial $q$ is irreducible over $k$.

::: {.proof}
The squares in $\mathbb F_3$ are $0$ and $1$, so $x^2+1$ has no root in $k$. A quadratic over a field is irreducible precisely when it has no root.
:::

<1>2. Evaluation at $i$ induces a well-defined $k$-algebra morphism
\[
\phi:k[x]/(q)\longrightarrow k[i],\qquad [f]\longmapsto f(i).
\]

::: {.proof}
Evaluation $k[x]\to k[i]$ is a $k$-algebra morphism, and $q(i)=i^2+1=0$. Therefore the ideal $(q)$ lies in the evaluation kernel, so the universal property of the quotient supplies the stated morphism.
:::

<1>3. The morphism $\phi$ is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the domain $k[x]/(q)$ is a field. The unital morphism $\phi$ therefore has zero kernel: its kernel is a proper ideal of a field. Every element of $k[i]$ is a polynomial in $i$ with coefficients in $k$ by definition, hence belongs to the image of $\phi$. Thus $\phi$ is injective and surjective.
:::

<1>4. The requested isomorphism is
\[
\boxed{\mathbb F_3[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb F_3[i]}.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is the isomorphism constructed in steps <1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
