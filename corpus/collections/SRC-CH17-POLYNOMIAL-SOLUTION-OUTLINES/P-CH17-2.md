::: {.problem}
Suppose that D is an integral domain and F is a field containing D. If $f ( x ) \in D [ x ]$ and $f ( x )$ is irreducible over F but reducible over $D$ , what can you say about the factorization of $f ( x )$ over D?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

In any factorization $f=gh$ in $D[x]$, at least one of $g,h$ is a nonzero constant polynomial.


::: pf-proof

The inclusion $D\hookrightarrow F$ induces an injective ring homomorphism $D[x]\hookrightarrow F[x]$. Thus $f=gh$ also holds in $F[x]$. As $f$ is irreducible in $F[x]$, at least one of its factors is a unit of $F[x]$. The units of the polynomial ring over a field are exactly its nonzero constant polynomials; consequently that factor belongs to $D\setminus\{0\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The reducibility of $f$ over $D$ is witnessed by a factorization
$$
\boxed{f=cg,\qquad c\in D\setminus D^\times,\quad g\in D[x]\setminus D[x]^\times,\quad c\neq0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Reducibility in $D[x]$ supplies a factorization $f=uv$ with both factors nonunits in $D[x]$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, one factor is a nonzero constant $c\in D$. A constant polynomial is a unit of $D[x]$ precisely when it is a unit of $D$, so $c\notin D^\times$. Taking the other factor for $g$ gives the claimed factorization. In particular, the constant $c$ becomes invertible in $F[x]$, although it is not invertible in $D[x]$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} describes the necessary form of the factorization of $f$ over $D$.
