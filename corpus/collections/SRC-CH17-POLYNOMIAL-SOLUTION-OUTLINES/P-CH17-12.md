::: {.problem}
Determine which of the polynomials below is (are) irreducible over $\QQ$.

(a) $x^5 + 9x^4 + 12x^2 + 6$

(b) $x^4 + x + 1$

(c) $x^4 + 3x^2 + 3$

(d) $x^5 + 5x^2 + 1$

(e) $\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)x^5 + \left(\frac{9}{2}\right)x^4 + 15x^3 + \left(\frac{3}{7}\right)x^2 + 6x + \frac{3}{14}$
:::

::: {.solution}

For each letter, let $f_{\mathrm a},\ldots,f_{\mathrm e}\in\QQ[x]$ denote the polynomial in that part.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The polynomials in parts (a) and (c) are irreducible over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Both have integer coefficients and leading coefficient $1$. In part (a), the prime $3$ divides every nonleading coefficient, but $9$ does not divide the constant coefficient $6$. In part (c), the prime $3$ divides every nonleading coefficient, but $9$ does not divide the constant coefficient $3$. Eisenstein's criterion at $3$ applies to each polynomial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The polynomial in part (b) is irreducible over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Its reduction modulo $2$ is $h=x^4+x+1\in\FF_2[x]$. Since $h(0)=h(1)=1$, it has no linear factor. If the quartic $h$ were reducible, it would therefore have an irreducible quadratic factor. The only monic irreducible quadratic over $\FF_2$ is $q=x^2+x+1$. A factorization of $h$ would then have two irreducible quadratic factors, each equal to $q$, yielding $h=q^2=x^4+x^2+1$, a contradiction. Thus $h$ is irreducible. Since $f_{\mathrm b}$ is monic and retains degree four modulo $2$, Gauss's lemma and reduction modulo $2$ establish its irreducibility over $\QQ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The polynomial in part (d) is irreducible over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Its reduction modulo $2$ is $h=x^5+x^2+1$. Again $h(0)=h(1)=1$, so it has no linear factor. Any reducible polynomial of degree five with no linear factor has an irreducible factor of degree two. The only possible such factor over $\FF_2$ is $q=x^2+x+1$. Modulo $q$ one has $x^3=1$ and consequently $x^5=x^2$, giving $h\equiv x^2+x^2+1=1$. Thus $q$ does not divide $h$, so $h$ is irreducible. The monic integer polynomial $f_{\mathrm d}$ therefore is irreducible over $\QQ$ by the reduction criterion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The polynomial in part (e) is irreducible over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Multiplying by the nonzero rational scalar $14$ gives
\[
14f_{\mathrm e}=35x^5+63x^4+210x^3+6x^2+84x+3.
\]
Its leading coefficient is not divisible by $3$, every other coefficient is divisible by $3$, and its constant coefficient is not divisible by $9$. Eisenstein's criterion at $3$ proves that $14f_{\mathrm e}$, and hence $f_{\mathrm e}$, is irreducible over $\QQ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

All five polynomials are irreducible over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} establish irreducibility for parts (a)--(e).

:::
