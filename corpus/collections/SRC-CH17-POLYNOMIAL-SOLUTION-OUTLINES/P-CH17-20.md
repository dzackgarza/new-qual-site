::: {.problem}
Prove that, for every positive integer $n$, there are infinitely many polynomials of degree $n$ in $\ZZ[x]$ that are irreducible over $\QQ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix a positive integer $n$ and, for each prime number $p$, define $f_p=x^n+p\in\ZZ[x]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The polynomial $f_p$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ for every prime $p$.


::: pf-proof

The leading coefficient of $f_p$ is $1$, which is not divisible by $p$. Every nonleading coefficient is divisible by $p$, including the zero coefficients of $x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$, while its constant coefficient $p$ is not divisible by $p^2$. Eisenstein's criterion therefore proves that $f_p$ is irreducible over $\QQ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There are infinitely many distinct degree-$n$ polynomials in $\ZZ[x]$ that are irreducible over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, every $f_p$ is irreducible and has degree $n$. If $p$ and $q$ are distinct primes, the constant coefficients of $f_p$ and $f_q$ differ, so the polynomials are distinct. Since there are infinitely many primes, the family $\{f_p:p\text{ prime}\}$ is infinite.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} establishes the assertion for the arbitrary positive integer $n$.

:::
