::: {.problem}
Prove that, for every positive integer n, there are infinitely many polynomials of degree n in $\mathbb { Z } [ x ]$ that are irreducible over Q.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix a positive integer $n$ and, for each prime number $p$, define $f_p=x^n+p\in\mathbb Z[x]$.

<1>1. The polynomial $f_p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ for every prime $p$.

::: {.proof}
The leading coefficient of $f_p$ is $1$, which is not divisible by $p$. Every nonleading coefficient is divisible by $p$, including the zero coefficients of $x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$, while its constant coefficient $p$ is not divisible by $p^2$. Eisenstein's criterion therefore proves that $f_p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
:::

<1>2. There are infinitely many distinct degree-$n$ polynomials in $\mathbb Z[x]$ that are irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, every $f_p$ is irreducible and has degree $n$. If $p$ and $q$ are distinct primes, the constant coefficients of $f_p$ and $f_q$ differ, so the polynomials are distinct. Since there are infinitely many primes, the family $\{f_p:p\text{ prime}\}$ is infinite.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 establishes the assertion for the arbitrary positive integer $n$.
:::
:::
