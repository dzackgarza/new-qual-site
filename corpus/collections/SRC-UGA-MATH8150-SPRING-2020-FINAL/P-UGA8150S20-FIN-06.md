::: {.problem}
(a) Prove the following maximum modulus principle: suppose $U$ is a bounded domain and $f$ is a nonconstant continuous function on $\overline U$ whose restriction to $U$ is holomorphic. Show that for every $z_0\in U$,
$$
\abs{f(z_0)}<\sup\{\abs{f(z)}:z\in\partial U\}.
$$

(b) Furthermore, if $\abs{f(z)}$ is constant on $\partial U$, show that $f$ has a zero in $U$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The boundary $\partial U$ is compact, so
$$
M
=
\max_{z\in\partial U}\abs{f(z)}
$$
exists.


::: pf-proof

Because $U$ is bounded,
$$
\overline U
$$
is bounded. The boundary $\partial U$ is closed in $\CC$, hence closed
and bounded and therefore compact. Since $f$ is continuous on $\overline U$,
the function $\abs{f}$ attains its maximum on $\partial U$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\abs{f(z)}\le M
\qquad(z\in\overline U).
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $\overline U$ is compact, $\abs{f}$ attains a maximum at some point
$w\in\overline U$. If
$$
\abs{f(w)}>M,
$$
then $w$ cannot lie on $\partial U$, so $w\in U$. Thus $\abs{f}$ would attain
its global maximum at an interior point. By the maximum modulus theorem,
$f$ would be constant on the connected domain $U$, contrary to hypothesis.
Hence no value in $\overline U$ exceeds $M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

In fact, for every $z_0\in U$,
$$
\boxed{\abs{f(z_0)}<M}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{f(z_0)}\le M.
$$
If equality held for some $z_0\in U$, then $\abs{f}$ would attain its maximum
inside $U$. The maximum modulus theorem would again force $f$ to be
constant, contradicting the assumptions. Therefore the inequality is
strict.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

This proves part (a):
$$
\boxed{
\abs{f(z_0)}
<
\sup_{z\in\partial U}\abs{f(z)}
\qquad(z_0\in U)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the supremum over the boundary equals $M$, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
the strict inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Assume now that
$$
\abs{f(z)}=C
\qquad(z\in\partial U)
$$
for some constant $C\ge0$. Then
$$
C>0.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $C=0$, then part (a) would give, for every $z_0\in U$,
$$
\abs{f(z_0)}<0,
$$
which is impossible. Thus under the standing nonconstancy hypothesis,
$C$ must be positive.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $f$ has no zero in $U$. Then
$$
\frac1f
$$
is continuous on $\overline U$ and holomorphic on $U$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, $f$ is nonzero on $\partial U$. Under the temporary
assumption, it is also nonzero throughout $U$. Hence it has no zeros on
$\overline U$, so its reciprocal is continuous there and holomorphic in the
interior.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For every $z_0\in U$, part (a) applied to $f$ gives
$$
\abs{f(z_0)}<C.
$$


::: pf-proof

The boundary modulus is exactly $C$, so step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} applies directly.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

For every $z_0\in U$, part (a) applied to $1/f$ gives
$$
\frac1{\abs{f(z_0)}}
<
\frac1C,
$$
equivalently
$$
\abs{f(z_0)}>C.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, $1/f$ satisfies the same regularity hypotheses. It is
nonconstant because $f$ is nonconstant. On the boundary,
$$
\abs{\frac1{f(z)}}
=
\frac1C.
$$
Apply part (a) to $1/f$ and rearrange.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The inequalities in steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} contradict one another.


::: pf-proof

For any fixed $z_0\in U$, they assert simultaneously
$$
\abs{f(z_0)}<C
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\abs{f(z_0)}>C.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{f \text{ has at least one zero in } U}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The zero-free assumption in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} leads to the contradiction in step
[](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, so it is false.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark title="Variations"}
Part (b) alone, for a bounded region $G$: if $f$ is continuous on $\overline G$, holomorphic on $G$, and $\abs{f}=c$ on $\partial G$ for a constant $c\ge0$, then either $f$ is constant or $f$ has a zero in $G$.
:::
