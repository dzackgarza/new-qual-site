::: {.problem}
(a) Prove the following maximum modulus principle: suppose $U$ is a bounded domain and $f$ is a nonconstant continuous function on $\overline U$ whose restriction to $U$ is holomorphic. Show that for every $z_0\in U$,
\[
|f(z_0)|<\sup\{|f(z)|:z\in\partial U\}.
\]

(b) Furthermore, if $|f(z)|$ is constant on $\partial U$, show that $f$ has a zero in $U$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The boundary $\partial U$ is compact, so
\[
M
=
\max_{z\in\partial U}|f(z)|
\]
exists.

::: {.proof}
Because $U$ is bounded,
\[
\overline U
\]
is bounded. The boundary $\partial U$ is closed in $\mathbb C$, hence closed
and bounded and therefore compact. Since $f$ is continuous on $\overline U$,
the function $|f|$ attains its maximum on $\partial U$.
:::

<1>2. One has
\[
|f(z)|\le M
\qquad(z\in\overline U).
\]

::: {.proof}
Since $\overline U$ is compact, $|f|$ attains a maximum at some point
$w\in\overline U$. If
\[
|f(w)|>M,
\]
then $w$ cannot lie on $\partial U$, so $w\in U$. Thus $|f|$ would attain
its global maximum at an interior point. By the maximum modulus theorem,
$f$ would be constant on the connected domain $U$, contrary to hypothesis.
Hence no value in $\overline U$ exceeds $M$.
:::

<1>3. In fact, for every $z_0\in U$,
\[
\boxed{|f(z_0)|<M}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives
\[
|f(z_0)|\le M.
\]
If equality held for some $z_0\in U$, then $|f|$ would attain its maximum
inside $U$. The maximum modulus theorem would again force $f$ to be
constant, contradicting the assumptions. Therefore the inequality is
strict.
:::

<1>4. This proves part (a):
\[
\boxed{
|f(z_0)|
<
\sup_{z\in\partial U}|f(z)|
\qquad(z_0\in U)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the supremum over the boundary equals $M$, and step <1>3 gives
the strict inequality.
:::

<1>5. Assume now that
\[
|f(z)|=C
\qquad(z\in\partial U)
\]
for some constant $C\ge0$. Then
\[
C>0.
\]

::: {.proof}
If $C=0$, then part (a) would give, for every $z_0\in U$,
\[
|f(z_0)|<0,
\]
which is impossible. Thus under the standing nonconstancy hypothesis,
$C$ must be positive.
:::

<1>6. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $f$ has no zero in $U$. Then
\[
\frac1f
\]
is continuous on $\overline U$ and holomorphic on $U$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>5, $f$ is nonzero on $\partial U$. Under the temporary
assumption, it is also nonzero throughout $U$. Hence it has no zeros on
$\overline U$, so its reciprocal is continuous there and holomorphic in the
interior.
:::

<1>7. For every $z_0\in U$, part (a) applied to $f$ gives
\[
|f(z_0)|<C.
\]

::: {.proof}
The boundary modulus is exactly $C$, so step <1>4 applies directly.
:::

<1>8. For every $z_0\in U$, part (a) applied to $1/f$ gives
\[
\frac1{|f(z_0)|}
<
\frac1C,
\]
equivalently
\[
|f(z_0)|>C.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6, $1/f$ satisfies the same regularity hypotheses. It is
nonconstant because $f$ is nonconstant. On the boundary,
\[
\left|\frac1{f(z)}\right|
=
\frac1C.
\]
Apply part (a) to $1/f$ and rearrange.
:::

<1>9. The inequalities in steps <1>7 and <1>8 contradict one another.

::: {.proof}
For any fixed $z_0\in U$, they assert simultaneously
\[
|f(z_0)|<C
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|f(z_0)|>C.
\]
:::

<1>10. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\text{$f$ has at least one zero in $U$}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The zero-free assumption in step <1>6 leads to the contradiction in step
<1>9, so it is false.
:::

<1>11. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves part (a), and step <1>10 proves part (b).
:::
:::
