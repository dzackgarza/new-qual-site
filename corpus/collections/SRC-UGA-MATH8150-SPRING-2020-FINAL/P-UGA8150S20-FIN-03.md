::: {.problem}
(1) Show that if $f$ is analytic in an open set containing the disk $\abs{z-a}\le R$, then
$$
\abs{f(a)}^2\le \frac{1}{\pi R^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R
\abs{f(a+re^{i\theta})}^2 r\,dr\,d\theta.
$$

(2) Let $\Omega$ be a region and $M>0$. Let $\mathcal F$ be the family of all analytic functions $f$ on $\Omega$ such that
$$
\iint_\Omega \abs{f(z)}^2\,dx\,dy\le M.
$$
Show that $\mathcal F$ is a normal family.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $r$ with
$$
0<r\le R,
$$
Cauchy's integral formula gives
$$
f(a)
=
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
f(a+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $f$ is analytic on an open neighborhood of the closed disk
$$
\overline{D(a,R)},
$$
it is analytic on and inside every circle
$$
\abs{z-a}=r
\qquad(0<r\le R).
$$
Cauchy's formula at the center $a$ gives the displayed mean-value identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $0<r\le R$,
$$
\boxed{
\abs{f(a)}^2
\le
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
\abs{f(a+re^{i\theta})}^2\,d\theta}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply Cauchy--Schwarz to step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{f(a)}^2
&\le
\frac1{(2\pi)^2}
\left(
\int_0^{2\pi}1^2\,d\theta
\right)
\left(
\int_0^{2\pi}
\abs{f(a+re^{i\theta})}^2\,d\theta
\right)\\
&=
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
\abs{f(a+re^{i\theta})}^2\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The required area-mean inequality holds:
$$
\boxed{
\abs{f(a)}^2
\le
\frac1{\pi R^2}
\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R
\abs{f(a+re^{i\theta})}^2
r\,dr\,d\theta}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Multiply the inequality in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} by
$$
\frac{2r}{R^2}
$$
and integrate from $r=0$ to $r=R$. Since
$$
\int_0^R\frac{2r}{R^2}\,dr=1,
$$
the left-hand side remains $\abs{f(a)}^2$, while the right-hand side becomes
$$
\frac1{\pi R^2}
\int_0^R\int_0^{2\pi}
\abs{f(a+re^{i\theta})}^2
r\,d\theta\,dr.
$$
Tonelli's theorem allows the order of integration to be written as in the
problem statement.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let $K\subset\Omega$ be compact. Then there is $\rho>0$ such that
$$
\overline{D(z,\rho)}
\subset\Omega
\qquad(z\in K).
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $K$ is compact and $\Omega$ is open,
$$
d
=
\operatorname{dist}(K,\CC\setminus\Omega)
>0.
$$
Take, for example,
$$
\rho=\frac d2.
$$
Then every closed disk of radius $\rho$ centered at a point of $K$ lies in
$\Omega$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Every $f\in\mathcal F$ satisfies the uniform estimate
$$
\boxed{
\sup_{z\in K}\abs{f(z)}
\le
\sqrt{\frac{M}{\pi\rho^2}}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Fix $z\in K$ and apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} to the disk
$$
\overline{D(z,\rho)}.
$$
Since
$$
D(z,\rho)\subset\Omega,
$$
one obtains
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{f(z)}^2
&\le
\frac1{\pi\rho^2}
\iint_{D(z,\rho)}
\abs{f(w)}^2\,dA(w)\\
&\le
\frac1{\pi\rho^2}
\iint_{\Omega}
\abs{f(w)}^2\,dA(w)\\
&\le
\frac{M}{\pi\rho^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The bound is independent of both $z\in K$ and $f\in\mathcal F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The family $\mathcal F$ is locally bounded on $\Omega$.


::: pf-proof

For every compact $K\subset\Omega$, step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives a finite constant
$C_K$ such that
$$
\abs{f(z)}\le C_K
$$
for every $z\in K$ and every $f\in\mathcal F$. This is precisely local
boundedness.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathcal F\text{ is a normal family}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Montel's theorem states that a locally bounded family of holomorphic
functions on a region is normal: every sequence in the family has a
subsequence converging locally uniformly to a holomorphic function.
Apply Montel's theorem using step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (1), and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves part (2).

:::
