::: {.problem}
(1) Show that if $f$ is analytic in an open set containing the disk $|z-a|\le R$, then
\[
|f(a)|^2\le \frac{1}{\pi R^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R
|f(a+re^{i\theta})|^2 r\,dr\,d\theta.
\]

(2) Let $\Omega$ be a region and $M>0$. Let $\mathcal F$ be the family of all analytic functions $f$ on $\Omega$ such that
\[
\iint_\Omega |f(z)|^2\,dx\,dy\le M.
\]
Show that $\mathcal F$ is a normal family.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

For every $r$ with
\[
0<r\le R,
\]
Cauchy's integral formula gives
\[
f(a)
=
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
f(a+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.
\]


::: pf-proof

Since $f$ is analytic on an open neighborhood of the closed disk
\[
\overline{D(a,R)},
\]
it is analytic on and inside every circle
\[
|z-a|=r
\qquad(0<r\le R).
\]
Cauchy's formula at the center $a$ gives the displayed mean-value identity.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every $0<r\le R$,
\[
\boxed{
|f(a)|^2
\le
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
|f(a+re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Apply Cauchy--Schwarz to step <1>1:
\[
\begin{aligned}
|f(a)|^2
&\le
\frac1{(2\pi)^2}
\left(
\int_0^{2\pi}1^2\,d\theta
\right)
\left(
\int_0^{2\pi}
|f(a+re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta
\right)\\
&=
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
|f(a+re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The required area-mean inequality holds:
\[
\boxed{
|f(a)|^2
\le
\frac1{\pi R^2}
\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R
|f(a+re^{i\theta})|^2
r\,dr\,d\theta}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Multiply the inequality in step <1>2 by
\[
\frac{2r}{R^2}
\]
and integrate from $r=0$ to $r=R$. Since
\[
\int_0^R\frac{2r}{R^2}\,dr=1,
\]
the left-hand side remains $|f(a)|^2$, while the right-hand side becomes
\[
\frac1{\pi R^2}
\int_0^R\int_0^{2\pi}
|f(a+re^{i\theta})|^2
r\,d\theta\,dr.
\]
Tonelli's theorem allows the order of integration to be written as in the
problem statement.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let $K\subset\Omega$ be compact. Then there is $\rho>0$ such that
\[
\overline{D(z,\rho)}
\subset\Omega
\qquad(z\in K).
\]


::: pf-proof

Since $K$ is compact and $\Omega$ is open,
\[
d
=
\operatorname{dist}(K,\mathbb C\setminus\Omega)
>0.
\]
Take, for example,
\[
\rho=\frac d2.
\]
Then every closed disk of radius $\rho$ centered at a point of $K$ lies in
$\Omega$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every $f\in\mathcal F$ satisfies the uniform estimate
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{z\in K}|f(z)|
\le
\sqrt{\frac{M}{\pi\rho^2}}}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Fix $z\in K$ and apply step <1>3 to the disk
\[
\overline{D(z,\rho)}.
\]
Since
\[
D(z,\rho)\subset\Omega,
\]
one obtains
\[
\begin{aligned}
|f(z)|^2
&\le
\frac1{\pi\rho^2}
\iint_{D(z,\rho)}
|f(w)|^2\,dA(w)\\
&\le
\frac1{\pi\rho^2}
\iint_{\Omega}
|f(w)|^2\,dA(w)\\
&\le
\frac{M}{\pi\rho^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bound is independent of both $z\in K$ and $f\in\mathcal F$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The family $\mathcal F$ is locally bounded on $\Omega$.


::: pf-proof

For every compact $K\subset\Omega$, step <1>5 gives a finite constant
$C_K$ such that
\[
|f(z)|\le C_K
\]
for every $z\in K$ and every $f\in\mathcal F$. This is precisely local
boundedness.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
\[
\boxed{\mathcal F\text{ is a normal family}}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Montel's theorem states that a locally bounded family of holomorphic
functions on a region is normal: every sequence in the family has a
subsequence converging locally uniformly to a holomorphic function.
Apply Montel's theorem using step <1>6.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>3 proves part (1), and step <1>7 proves part (2).

:::
