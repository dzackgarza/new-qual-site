::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=\frac{1}{(z-1)z^2},
\]
which is analytic on $\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$.

(i) Expand $f$ in a Laurent series valid in a deleted neighborhood of (a) $z=0$ and (b) $z=1$.

(ii) Expand $f$ in Laurent series centered at $z=0$ valid in (a) $0<|z|<1$, (b) $1<|z|<2$, and (c) $|z|>2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Near $z=0$, one has
\[
\boxed{
f(z)
=
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n-2}
=
-\frac1{z^2}
-\frac1z
-1-z-z^2-\cdots
}
\]
for
\[
0<|z|<1.
\]

::: {.proof}
Rewrite
\[
f(z)
=
\frac1{(z-1)z^2}
=
-\frac1{z^2}\frac1{1-z}.
\]
For $|z|<1$, the geometric series gives
\[
\frac1{1-z}
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^n.
\]
Multiplying by $-z^{-2}$ gives the displayed Laurent expansion. Since
$z=0$ itself is a pole, the deleted neighborhood is
\[
0<|z|<1.
\]
:::

<1>2. Near $z=1$, with
\[
w=z-1,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
(-1)^n(n+1)(z-1)^{n-1}
}
\]
for
\[
0<|z-1|<1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
f(z)
=
\frac1{z-1}
-2
+3(z-1)
-4(z-1)^2
+\cdots.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since
\[
z=1+w,
\]
one has
\[
f(z)
=
\frac1{w(1+w)^2}.
\]
The differentiated geometric series gives
\[
\frac1{(1+w)^2}
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
(-1)^n(n+1)w^n
\qquad(|w|<1).
\]
Multiplying by $w^{-1}$ yields the formula. The radius is $1$ because the
other singularity, $z=0$, lies at distance $1$ from the center $z=1$.
:::

<1>3. The Laurent series centered at $0$ for
\[
0<|z|<1
\]
is exactly the series from step <1>1:
\[
\boxed{
f(z)
=
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n-2}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Part (ii)(a) asks for the same center and the same maximal annulus as
part (i)(a), so step <1>1 applies verbatim.
:::

<1>4. For
\[
|z|>1,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{-n-3}
=
\frac1{z^3}
+\frac1{z^4}
+\frac1{z^5}
+\cdots
}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Rewrite
\[
f(z)
=
\frac1{z^3(1-1/z)}
=
\frac1{z^3}
\frac1{1-1/z}.
\]
When $|z|>1$,
\[
\left|\frac1z\right|<1,
\]
so
\[
\frac1{1-1/z}
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{-n}.
\]
Multiplication by $z^{-3}$ gives the displayed series.
:::

<1>5. Thus on the requested annulus
\[
1<|z|<2,
\]
the expansion is
\[
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{-n-3}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The series in step <1>4 converges on the larger region
\[
|z|>1,
\]
so in particular it converges on the subannulus $1<|z|<2$.
:::

<1>6. On the requested region
\[
|z|>2,
\]
the expansion is again
\[
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{-n-3}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Again apply step <1>4. There is no singularity on the circle $|z|=2$, so
there is no new Laurent annulus centered at $0$ when crossing radius $2$.
The maximal exterior annulus is simply
\[
|z|>1.
\]
Uniqueness of Laurent expansions on an annulus also forces the same series
on both requested subregions.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 answer part (i), while steps <1>3, <1>5, and <1>6
answer the three regions in part (ii).
:::
:::
