::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(z)=\frac{1}{(z-1)z^2},
$$
which is analytic on $\CC\setminus\{0,1\}$.

(i) Expand $f$ in a Laurent series valid in a deleted neighborhood of (a) $z=0$ and (b) $z=1$.

(ii) Expand $f$ in Laurent series centered at $z=0$ valid in (a) $0<|z|<1$, (b) $1<|z|<2$, and (c) $|z|>2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Near $z=0$, one has
$$
\boxed{
f(z)
=
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n-2}
=
-\frac1{z^2}
-\frac1z
-1-z-z^2-\cdots
}
$$
for
$$
0<|z|<1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Rewrite
$$
f(z)
=
\frac1{(z-1)z^2}
=
-\frac1{z^2}\frac1{1-z}.
$$
For $|z|<1$, the geometric series gives
$$
\frac1{1-z}
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^n.
$$
Multiplying by $-z^{-2}$ gives the displayed Laurent expansion. Since
$z=0$ itself is a pole, the deleted neighborhood is
$$
0<|z|<1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Near $z=1$, with
$$
w=z-1,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
(-1)^n(n+1)(z-1)^{n-1}
}
$$
for
$$
0<|z-1|<1.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
f(z)
=
\frac1{z-1}
-2
+3(z-1)
-4(z-1)^2
+\cdots.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
z=1+w,
$$
one has
$$
f(z)
=
\frac1{w(1+w)^2}.
$$
The differentiated geometric series gives
$$
\frac1{(1+w)^2}
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
(-1)^n(n+1)w^n
\qquad(|w|<1).
$$
Multiplying by $w^{-1}$ yields the formula. The radius is $1$ because the
other singularity, $z=0$, lies at distance $1$ from the center $z=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Laurent series centered at $0$ for
$$
0<|z|<1
$$
is exactly the series from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}:
$$
\boxed{
f(z)
=
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n-2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Part (ii)(a) asks for the same center and the same maximal annulus as
part (i)(a), so step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} applies verbatim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For
$$
|z|>1,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{-n-3}
=
\frac1{z^3}
+\frac1{z^4}
+\frac1{z^5}
+\cdots
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Rewrite
$$
f(z)
=
\frac1{z^3(1-1/z)}
=
\frac1{z^3}
\frac1{1-1/z}.
$$
When $|z|>1$,
$$
\left|\frac1z\right|<1,
$$
so
$$
\frac1{1-1/z}
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{-n}.
$$
Multiplication by $z^{-3}$ gives the displayed series.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Thus on the requested annulus
$$
1<|z|<2,
$$
the expansion is
$$
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{-n-3}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The series in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} converges on the larger region
$$
|z|>1,
$$
so in particular it converges on the subannulus $1<|z|<2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

On the requested region
$$
|z|>2,
$$
the expansion is again
$$
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{-n-3}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Again apply step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. There is no singularity on the circle $|z|=2$, so
there is no new Laurent annulus centered at $0$ when crossing radius $2$.
The maximal exterior annulus is simply
$$
|z|>1.
$$
Uniqueness of Laurent expansions on an annulus also forces the same series
on both requested subregions.

:::

answer the three regions in part (ii).
