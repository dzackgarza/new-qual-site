::: {.problem}
Let $\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$ be a basis of a real vector space $V$, and let
$$
\Lambda = \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^n r_i e_i \;\middle|\; r_i \in \ZZ \right\} \cong \ZZ^n
$$
be the integer lattice generated by the basis.
Let $(\cdot)$ be a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on $V$ such that the Gram matrix $M = (e_i \cdot e_j)_{1 \le i, j \le n}$ has integer entries ($M \in M_n(\ZZ)$).
Define the dual lattice by
$$
\Lambda^\vee = \{v \in V \mid v \cdot x \in \ZZ \text{ for all } x \in \Lambda\}.
$$

(a) Show that $\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda^\vee$.

(b) Prove that $\det M \ne 0$ and that the dual basis vectors $e_i^* = \sum_{j=1}^n (M^{-1})_{ji} e_j$ form a $\ZZ$-basis for $\Lambda^\vee$.

(c) Prove that $|\Lambda^\vee / \Lambda| = |\det M|$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The integral lattice satisfies $\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^\vee$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Let $v \in \Lambda$, so $v = \sum_{i=1}^n r_i e_i$ with $r_i \in \ZZ$.

:::

::: pf-step

Let $x \in \Lambda$, so $x = \sum_{j=1}^n s_j e_j$ with $s_j \in \ZZ$.

:::

::: pf-step

Compute the bilinear product:
    $$v \cdot x = \left( \sum_{i=1}^n r_i e_i \right) \cdot \left( \sum_{j=1}^n s_j e_j \right) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n r_i s_j (e_i \cdot e_j) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n r_i s_j M_{ij}.$$

:::

::: pf-step

By hypothesis, $M_{ij} \in \ZZ$ for all $i, j$, and $r_i, s_j \in \ZZ$.

:::

::: pf-step

The sum of products of integers is an integer, so $v \cdot x \in \ZZ$.

:::

::: pf-step

Since $x \in \Lambda$ was arbitrary, $v \in \Lambda^\vee$.

:::

::: pf-step

Thus $\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda^\vee$.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The Gram matrix $M$ is invertible over $\RR$, so $\det M\neq0$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Suppose $c = (c_1, \dots, c_n)^T \in \RR^n$ satisfies $M c = 0$.

:::

::: pf-step

Define the vector $v = \sum_{j=1}^n c_j e_j \in V$.

:::

::: pf-step

For each basis vector $e_i$ ($1 \le i \le n$):
    $$e_i \cdot v = e_i \cdot \left( \sum_{j=1}^n c_j e_j \right) = \sum_{j=1}^n c_j (e_i \cdot e_j) = \sum_{j=1}^n M_{ij} c_j = (M c)_i = 0.$$

:::

::: pf-step

For any $w \in V$, write $w = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i e_i$. Then
    $$w \cdot v = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i (e_i \cdot v) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

:::

::: pf-step

Since the form $(\cdot)$ is non-degenerate, $w \cdot v = 0$ for all $w \in V$ implies $v = 0$.

:::

::: pf-step

Since $\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$ is a basis of $V$, $v = \sum c_j e_j = 0 \implies c_j = 0$ for all $j$.

:::

::: pf-step

Thus $\ker M = \{0\}$, so $M$ is invertible in $M_n(\RR)$, which proves $\det M \ne 0$.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The vectors $e_i^*=\sum_j(M^{-1})_{ji}e_j$ form a $\ZZ$-basis of $\Lambda^\vee$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

For each $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, define $e_i^* = \sum_{j=1}^n (M^{-1})_{ji} e_j \in V$.

:::

::: pf-step

Compute $e_k \cdot e_i^*$:
    $$e_k \cdot e_i^* = \sum_{j=1}^n (M^{-1})_{ji} (e_k \cdot e_j) = \sum_{j=1}^n M_{kj} (M^{-1})_{ji} = (M M^{-1})_{ki} = \delta_{ki}.$$

:::

::: pf-step

Let $v = \sum_{j=1}^n y_j e_j \in V$.

:::

::: pf-step

$v \in \Lambda^\vee \iff e_i \cdot v \in \ZZ$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ (since $\{e_i\}$ spans $\Lambda$ over $\ZZ$).

:::

::: pf-step

Compute $e_i \cdot v = (M y)_i$.

:::

::: pf-step

Thus $v \in \Lambda^\vee \iff M y = z \in \ZZ^n \iff y = M^{-1} z$ for some $z \in \ZZ^n$.

:::

::: pf-step

In terms of vectors in $V$:
    $$v = \sum_{j=1}^n y_j e_j = \sum_{j=1}^n \left( \sum_{i=1}^n (M^{-1})_{ji} z_i \right) e_j = \sum_{i=1}^n z_i \left( \sum_{j=1}^n (M^{-1})_{ji} e_j \right) = \sum_{i=1}^n z_i e_i^*.$$

:::

::: pf-step

Thus $\Lambda^\vee = \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^n z_i e_i^* \;\middle|\; z_i \in \ZZ \right\} = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n \ZZ e_i^*$.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The discriminant group has order
$$
\boxed{|\Lambda^\vee/\Lambda|=|\det M|}.
$$

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Express the basis $\{e_j\}$ of $\Lambda$ in terms of the basis $\{e_i^*\}$ of $\Lambda^\vee$:
    $$e_j = \sum_{i=1}^n (e_i \cdot e_j) e_i^* = \sum_{i=1}^n M_{ij} e_i^*.$$

:::

::: pf-step

Thus $\Lambda$ is the image of the $\ZZ$-linear map $M: \Lambda^\vee \to \Lambda^\vee$, so
    $$\Lambda^\vee / \Lambda \cong \ZZ^n / M \ZZ^n.$$

:::

::: pf-step

By the Smith Normal Form theorem for integer matrices, there exist unimodular matrices $P, Q \in \operatorname{GL}_n(\ZZ)$ ($\det P, \det Q \in \{\pm 1\}$) such that
    $$P M Q = D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, d_2, \dots, d_n), \quad d_i \in \ZZ_{>0}.$$

:::

::: pf-step

The quotient group is isomorphic to:
    $$\ZZ^n / M \ZZ^n \cong \ZZ^n / D \ZZ^n \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^n \ZZ / d_i \ZZ.$$

:::

::: pf-step

The cardinality of the quotient group is:
    $$|\Lambda^\vee / \Lambda| = \prod_{i=1}^n d_i = \det D.$$

:::

::: pf-step

Taking determinants:

:::

$$\det D = |\det(P M Q)| = |\det P| \cdot |\det M| \cdot |\det Q| = 1 \cdot |\det M| \cdot 1 = |\det M|.$$

:::
