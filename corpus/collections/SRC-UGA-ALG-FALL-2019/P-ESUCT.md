::: {.problem}
Let $\zeta_n \in \CC$ denote a primitive $n$-th root of unity, and let $p_n(x) \in \QQ[x]$ be its minimal polynomial over $\QQ$. You may assume that the roots of $p_n(x)$ are precisely the primitive $n$-th roots of unity in $\CC$.

(a) Show that the field extension $\QQ(\zeta_n)/\QQ$ is Galois, and prove that its Galois group is isomorphic to $(\ZZ/n\ZZ)^\times$.

(b) How many subfields are there of $\QQ(\zeta_{20})$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The extension $\QQ(\zeta_n)/\QQ$ is Galois.

::: pf-proof
The polynomial $x^n - 1 \in \QQ[x]$ has the roots $e^{2\pi i k/n} = \zeta_n^k$ for $0 \le k < n$, and every one of them lies in $\QQ(\zeta_n)$ because $\zeta_n$ does. Hence $\QQ(\zeta_n)$ contains all the roots of $x^n - 1$ and is generated over $\QQ$ by one of them, so it is the splitting field of that polynomial. Since $\QQ$ has characteristic $0$, every algebraic extension of $\QQ$ is separable, and a splitting field of a separable polynomial is normal and separable, hence Galois.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #degree}

$[\QQ(\zeta_n) : \QQ] = \varphi(n)$.

::: pf-proof
By hypothesis the roots of the minimal polynomial $p_n$ of $\zeta_n$ over $\QQ$ are exactly the primitive $n$-th roots of unity, and a polynomial with distinct roots has degree equal to their number. The primitive $n$-th roots of unity are the $\zeta_n^a$ with $1 \le a \le n$ and $\gcd(a,n) = 1$, so there are $\varphi(n)$ of them and $\varphi(n) = \deg p_n = [\QQ(\zeta_n):\QQ]$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #homomorphism}

The map $\Psi$ sending $\sigma$ to the class of $a$ with $\sigma(\zeta_n) = \zeta_n^a$ is a well-defined injective homomorphism into $(\ZZ/n\ZZ)^\times$.

::: pf-proof
An automorphism $\sigma$ fixing $\QQ$ is determined by $\sigma(\zeta_n)$, because $\QQ(\zeta_n) = \QQ[\zeta_n]$ is generated by $\zeta_n$ as a ring. Its value must be a root of $p_n$, hence a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta_n^a$ with $\gcd(a,n) = 1$, so the class $[a]_n$ is a unit and $\Psi$ is well defined; replacing $a$ by $a+n$ does not change $\zeta_n^a$, so the class depends only on $\sigma$. If $\sigma(\zeta_n) = \zeta_n^a$ and $\tau(\zeta_n) = \zeta_n^b$ then $(\sigma\circ\tau)(\zeta_n) = \sigma(\zeta_n^b) = \zeta_n^{ab}$, since $\sigma$ fixes $\QQ$; so $\Psi$ is a homomorphism. If $\Psi(\sigma) = [1]_n$ then $\sigma$ fixes $\QQ$ and $\zeta_n$, hence fixes all of $\QQ[\zeta_n]$ and is the identity, so $\Psi$ is injective.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #isomorphism}

$\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_n)/\QQ) \cong (\ZZ/n\ZZ)^\times$.

::: pf-proof
For a finite Galois extension the order of the Galois group equals the degree, so $\abs{\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_n)/\QQ)} = [\QQ(\zeta_n):\QQ] = \varphi(n)$ by the degree step. The group $(\ZZ/n\ZZ)^\times$ has order $\varphi(n)$ as well, by the definition of Euler's function as the count of residues coprime to $n$. An injective map between finite sets of equal size is a bijection, and a bijective group homomorphism is an isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The field $\QQ(\zeta_{20})$ has exactly eight intermediate fields including itself and $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

$\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_{20})/\QQ) \cong \ZZ/2\ZZ \times \ZZ/4\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
Since $20 = 4\cdot 5$ with $4$ and $5$ coprime, the Chinese remainder theorem for rings gives $\ZZ/20\ZZ \cong \ZZ/4\ZZ\times\ZZ/5\ZZ$, hence an isomorphism of unit groups $(\ZZ/20\ZZ)^\times \cong (\ZZ/4\ZZ)^\times\times(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^\times$. The units mod $4$ are $1$ and $3$, a group of order $2$, and the units mod $5$ form a cyclic group of order $4$ because $5$ is prime; so the product is $\ZZ/2\ZZ\times\ZZ/4\ZZ$, and composing with the isomorphism of part (a) gives the displayed identification.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Galois Correspondence: By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, the subfields $E$ with $\QQ \subseteq E \subseteq \QQ(\zeta_{20})$ correspond bijectively to the subgroups of $G \cong \ZZ/2\ZZ \times \ZZ/4\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
The fundamental theorem of Galois theory, applied to the finite Galois extension $\QQ(\zeta_{20})/\QQ$ of part (a), makes $E \mapsto \operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_{20})/E)$ a bijection from intermediate fields to subgroups of $G$ with inverse $H \mapsto \QQ(\zeta_{20})^H$; so counting subfields is counting subgroups of the group just identified.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subgroups}
$\ZZ/2\ZZ \times \ZZ/4\ZZ$ has exactly eight subgroups, listed above.

        - Order 1 (index 8): Exactly 1 subgroup: $\{(0, 0)\}$, corresponding to $\QQ(\zeta_{20})$.
        - Order 2 (index 4): The elements of order 2 in $\ZZ/2\ZZ \times \ZZ/4\ZZ$ are $(1, 0)$, $(0, 2)$, and $(1, 2)$. Each generates a distinct cyclic subgroup of order 2:
        $$H_1 = \langle (1, 0) \rangle, \quad H_2 = \langle (0, 2) \rangle, \quad H_3 = \langle (1, 2) \rangle.$$
        There are exactly 3 subgroups of order 2.
        - Order 4 (index 2):
            - Cyclic subgroups of order 4: The elements of order 4 are $(0, 1)$, $(0, 3)$, $(1, 1)$, and $(1, 3)$. They generate 2 cyclic subgroups:
            $$K_1 = \langle (0, 1) \rangle = \langle (0, 3) \rangle, \quad K_2 = \langle (1, 1) \rangle = \langle (1, 3) \rangle.$$
            - Non-cyclic subgroup ($\cong \ZZ/2\ZZ \times \ZZ/2\ZZ$): Generated by the elements of order 2:
            $$K_3 = \{(0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 2), (1, 2)\}.$$
            There are exactly $2 + 1 = 3$ subgroups of order 4.
        - Order 8 (index 1): Exactly 1 subgroup: $G$ itself, corresponding to $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
Write the group additively. A subgroup of order $2$ is generated by one element of order $2$, and the elements of order $2$ are those with $2(x,y) = (0,0)$ and $(x,y)\ne(0,0)$, namely $(1,0)$, $(0,2)$ and $(1,2)$, so there are exactly three. A subgroup of order $4$ either contains an element of order $4$, in which case it is generated by it, or consists of $0$ together with the three elements of order $2$; the elements of order $4$ are $(0,1)$, $(0,3)$, $(1,1)$, $(1,3)$ and they generate the two distinct cyclic subgroups listed, while $0$ with the three elements of order $2$ is the Klein subgroup listed. So there are three subgroups of order $4$ and, with the trivial subgroup and the whole group, $1 + 3 + 3 + 1 = 8$ in all.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\QQ(\zeta_{20})$ has exactly eight subfields.

::: pf-proof
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory the intermediate fields $\QQ \subseteq E \subseteq \QQ(\zeta_{20})$ are in bijection with the subgroups of $\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_{20})/\QQ)$, and that group is $\ZZ/2\ZZ\times\ZZ/4\ZZ$ by the preceding steps. The number of intermediate fields therefore equals the number of subgroups of that group, which is eight; both endpoints correspond to the whole group and to the trivial subgroup.
:::
