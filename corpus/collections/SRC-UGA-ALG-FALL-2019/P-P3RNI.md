::: problem
Let $G$ be a finite group with $n$ distinct conjugacy classes, and let $g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n$ be a set of representatives for the conjugacy classes of $G$.

Prove that if $g_i g_j = g_j g_i$ for all $1 \le i, j \le n$, then $G$ is abelian.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that $G$ is abelian by considering the subgroup $H = \langle g_1, \dots, g_n \rangle$ and applying the conjugate cover theorem for finite groups.

<1>1. Subgroup $H = \langle g_1, \dots, g_n \rangle$ generated by representatives:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define $H = \langle g_1, \dots, g_n \rangle \le G$ to be the subgroup of $G$ generated by the $n$ conjugacy class representatives.
    <2>2. By hypothesis, $g_i g_j = g_j g_i$ for all $1 \le i, j \le n$, so all generators of $H$ pairwise commute.
    <2>3. Since any group generated by pairwise commuting elements is abelian, $H$ is an abelian subgroup of $G$.

<1>2. $G$ is the union of conjugates of $H$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $x \in G$ be an arbitrary element.
    <2>2. Since $g_1, \dots, g_n$ represent all conjugacy classes of $G$, the conjugacy classes partition $G$:
    $$G = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Cl}(g_i).$$
    <2>3. Thus $x \in \operatorname{Cl}(g_k)$ for some $k \in \{1, \dots, n\}$.
    <2>4. By definition of conjugacy class, there exists an element $g \in G$ such that $x = g g_k g^{-1}$.
    <2>5. Since $g_k \in H$, $x = g g_k g^{-1} \in g H g^{-1}$.
    <2>6. Therefore:
    $$G \subseteq \bigcup_{g \in G} g H g^{-1} \subseteq G \implies G = \bigcup_{g \in G} g H g^{-1}.$$

<1>3. Lemma: No finite group is a union of conjugates of a proper subgroup.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $K < G$ be a proper subgroup with $[G : K] = m > 1$.
    <2>2. The number of distinct conjugate subgroups of $K$ in $G$ is $[G : N_G(K)] \le [G : K] = m$.
    <2>3. Each conjugate $g K g^{-1}$ contains $|K|$ elements and shares the identity element $e$ with all other conjugates.
    <2>4. The union of all conjugates contains at most
    $$\left| \bigcup_{g \in G} g K g^{-1} \right| \le 1 + [G : N_G(K)] (|K| - 1) \le 1 + [G : K] (|K| - 1) = 1 + |G| - [G : K].$$
    <2>5. Since $[G : K] \ge 2$, $1 + |G| - [G : K] \le |G| - 1 < |G|$.
    <2>6. Thus $\bigcup_{g \in G} g K g^{-1} \subsetneq G$.

<1>4. $H = G$ and $G$ is abelian:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. From <1>2, $G = \bigcup_{g \in G} g H g^{-1}$.
    <2>2. By the contrapositive of <1>3, $H$ cannot be a proper subgroup of $G$.
    <2>3. Thus $H = G$.
    <2>4. From <1>1, $H$ is abelian, so $G$ is abelian.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    *Proof:*
    $G$ is abelian.
:::
