::: {.problem}
Problem 2. Let $(X,\rho)$ be a compact metric space with at least two points, and let $C(X)$ be the space of continuous functions $X\to\RR$ with the uniform norm
$$
\|f\|=\sup_X|f(x)|.
$$
Let $D$ be a dense subset of $X$, and for each $y\in D$ define $f_y\in C(X)$ by
$$
f_y(x)=\rho(x,y).
$$
Let $A$ be the subalgebra of $C(X)$ generated by the collection $\{f_y:y\in D\}$, with pointwise addition and multiplication of functions.

(a) Prove that $A$ is dense in $C(X)$ under the uniform norm.

(b) Prove that $C(X)$ is separable.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The algebra $A$ separates points of $X$.


::: pf-proof

Let $x_1,x_2\in X$ be distinct and put $d=\rho(x_1,x_2)>0$. Since $D$ is dense, choose $y\in D$ with
$$
\rho(x_1,y)<\frac d3.
$$
Then the triangle inequality gives
$$
f_y(x_2)=\rho(x_2,y)
\ge d-\rho(x_1,y)>\frac{2d}{3},
$$
whereas $f_y(x_1)<d/3$. Thus $f_y(x_1)\ne f_y(x_2)$, and $f_y\in A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The algebra $A$ vanishes at no point of $X$: for every $x\in X$, there is $h\in A$ with $h(x)\ne0$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $x\in X$. Since $X$ has at least two points, choose $z\in X\setminus\{x\}$ and set $r=\rho(x,z)/2>0$. Density of $D$ gives $y\in D\cap B(z,r)$. Then
$$
f_y(x)=\rho(x,y)
\ge\rho(x,z)-\rho(z,y)>r>0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
\boxed{\overline A^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}=C(X)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The compact metric space $X$ is compact Hausdorff.
By steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the real subalgebra $A\subseteq C(X)$ separates points and vanishes nowhere.
The real Stone--Weierstrass theorem in its nonunital form therefore implies that $A$ is uniformly dense in $C(X)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The space $X$ has a countable dense subset $D_0$.


::: pf-proof

For every $n\ge1$, compactness gives a finite cover of $X$ by open balls of radius $1/n$. Choose one center from each ball and let $D_n$ be the resulting finite set.
Then
$$
D_0=\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty D_n
$$
is countable.
For every $x\in X$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, choosing $n$ with $1/n<\varepsilon$ gives a point of $D_n$ within $\varepsilon$ of $x$, so $D_0$ is dense.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Let $A_0$ be the real subalgebra generated by $\{f_y:y\in D_0\}$. There is a countable subset $A_{\QQ}\subseteq A_0$ that is dense in $A_0$.


::: pf-proof

Let $A_{\QQ}$ consist of all finite sums
$$
\sum_{k=1}^m q_k\prod_{j=1}^{r_k}f_{y_{k,j}},
\qquad
q_k\in\QQ,\quad y_{k,j}\in D_0,
$$
with the empty product allowed if the convention for generated subalgebras includes constants.
Because $\QQ$ and $D_0$ are countable and every expression uses only finitely many choices, $A_{\QQ}$ is countable.

Every element of $A_0$ has the same form with real coefficients $a_k$. Each fixed monomial
$$
M_k=\prod_{j=1}^{r_k}f_{y_{k,j}}
$$
has finite uniform norm because it is continuous on compact $X$. Approximating the finitely many coefficients $a_k$ by rationals $q_k$ makes
$$
\left\|\sum_{k=1}^m(a_k-q_k)M_k\right\|_\infty
\le\sum_{k=1}^m|a_k-q_k|\,\|M_k\|_\infty
$$
arbitrarily small.
Hence $A_{\QQ}$ is dense in $A_0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The Banach space $C(X)$ is $\boxed{\text{separable}}$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $D_0$ is dense in $X$, so step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} applied with $D=D_0$ shows that $A_0$ is dense in $C(X)$. By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, $A_{\QQ}$ is countable and dense in $A_0$. Therefore $A_{\QQ}$ is a countable dense subset of $C(X)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
