::: {.problem}
Suppose that $A,B\subseteq X$ are closed subsets of the topological space $X$, and both $A\cup B$ and $A\cap B$ are connected subsets of $X$.
Show that both $A$ and $B$ are connected subsets of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose for contradiction that $A$ is disconnected.
Proof: assume the contrary.

<1>2. Then there is a continuous surjective map $f: A \to \{0,1\}$ (the two-point discrete space).
Proof: a space is disconnected iff it admits a continuous surjection onto the discrete two-point space.

<1>3. Since $A \cap B$ is connected, $f|_{A \cap B}$ is constant; say $f|_{A \cap B} \equiv 0$.
Proof: a continuous map from a connected space to a discrete space is constant.

<1>4. Define $g: A \cup B \to \{0,1\}$ by $g|_A = f$ and $g|_B \equiv 0$.
Proof: definition.

<1>5. $g$ is well-defined.
Proof: on $A \cap B$, $f = 0$ (<1>3) agrees with the constant $0$ on $B$.

<1>6. $g$ is continuous.
Proof: $A$ and $B$ are closed, $g|_A = f$ and $g|_B = 0$ are continuous, and they agree on $A \cap B$; by the pasting (gluing) lemma, $g$ is continuous.

<1>7. $g$ is surjective.
Proof: $f$ is surjective, so $g$ takes both values $0$ and $1$.

<1>8. Hence $A \cup B$ is disconnected, contradicting the hypothesis.
Proof: <1>6 and <1>7 give a continuous surjection $A \cup B \to \{0,1\}$.

<1>9. Therefore $A$ is connected.
Proof: <1>1–<1>8.

<1>10. By symmetry, $B$ is connected.
Proof: the same argument with $A$ and $B$ interchanged.

<1>11. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>9 and <1>10.
:::
