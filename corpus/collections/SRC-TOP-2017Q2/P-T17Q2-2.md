::: {.problem}
Suppose that $A,B\subseteq X$ are closed subsets of the topological space $X$, and both $A\cup B$ and $A\cap B$ are connected subsets of $X$.
Show that both $A$ and $B$ are connected subsets of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A space is disconnected if and only if it admits a continuous surjection onto the discrete space $\{0,1\}$.
By the symmetry of the hypotheses in $A$ and $B$, it suffices to show that $A$ is connected.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #extends-to-union}
Every continuous $f\colon A\to\{0,1\}$ extends to a continuous $g\colon A\cup B\to\{0,1\}$.

::: pf-proof
Since $A\cap B$ is connected, $f|_{A\cap B}$ is constant, say with value $c$.
Define $g=f$ on $A$ and $g\equiv c$ on $B$; the definitions agree on $A\cap B$.
$A$ and $B$ are closed in $A\cup B$ and both pieces are continuous, so $g$ is continuous by the pasting lemma.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
If $f\colon A\to\{0,1\}$ were a continuous surjection, then the extension $g$ of step [](#extends-to-union){.pf-ref} would be a continuous surjection from the connected space $A\cup B$ onto $\{0,1\}$, which is impossible.
Hence $A$ is connected, and by symmetry so is $B$.
:::
