::: {.problem}
Show that if $f:(X,\mathcal T)\to(Y,\mathcal T')$ is injective and continuous, and $(Y,\mathcal T')$ is Hausdorff, then $(X,\mathcal T)$ is Hausdorff.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Select distinct points in the domain $X$: <2>1. Let $x_1, x_2 \in X$ be distinct points ($x_1 \neq x_2$).
::: {.proof}
setup for the Hausdorff ($T_2$) condition.
:::
<2>2. Since $f: X \to Y$ is injective, their images under $f$ are distinct in $Y$:
\[
y_1 = f(x_1) \neq f(x_2) = y_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of injectivity ($x_1 \neq x_2 \implies f(x_1) \neq f(x_2)$).
:::

<1>2. Separate the image points in the Hausdorff space $Y$: <2>1. Since $(Y, \mathcal{T}')$ is Hausdorff, there exist disjoint open sets $V_1, V_2 \in \mathcal{T}'$ such that:
\[
y_1 \in V_1, \quad y_2 \in V_2, \quad \text{and} \quad V_1 \cap V_2 = \emptyset.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of a Hausdorff space applied to distinct points $y_1 \neq y_2$.
:::

<1>3. Pull back the separating neighborhoods to $X$: <2>1. Define $U_1 = f^{-1}(V_1)$ and $U_2 = f^{-1}(V_2)$.
::: {.proof}
preimage definition.
:::
<2>2. Since $f: (X, \mathcal{T}) \to (Y, \mathcal{T}')$ is continuous, the preimages of open sets in $Y$ are open in $X$, so $U_1, U_2 \in \mathcal{T}$.
::: {.proof}
definition of continuity in topology.
:::
<2>3. Since $f(x_1) = y_1 \in V_1$, we have $x_1 \in f^{-1}(V_1) = U_1$.
::: {.proof}
definition of preimage.
:::
<2>4. Since $f(x_2) = y_2 \in V_2$, we have $x_2 \in f^{-1}(V_2) = U_2$.
::: {.proof}
definition of preimage.
:::
<2>5. The sets $U_1$ and $U_2$ are disjoint:
\[
U_1 \cap U_2 = f^{-1}(V_1) \cap f^{-1}(V_2) = f^{-1}(V_1 \cap V_2) = f^{-1}(\emptyset) = \emptyset.
\]
::: {.proof}
preimage preserves intersections and empty sets.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion: For any two distinct points $x_1 \neq x_2$ in $X$, there exist disjoint open sets $U_1, U_2 \in \mathcal{T}$ containing $x_1$ and $x_2$, respectively.
Hence $(X, \mathcal{T})$ is Hausdorff.
::: {.proof}
<1>1, <1>2, and <1>3.
:::
Q.E.D.
:::
