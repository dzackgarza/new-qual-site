::: {.problem}
Show that if $f:(X,\mathcal T)\to(Y,\mathcal T')$ is injective and continuous, and $(Y,\mathcal T')$ is Hausdorff, then $(X,\mathcal T)$ is Hausdorff.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $x_1,x_2\in X$ be distinct.

<1>1. There are disjoint $V_1,V_2\in\mathcal T'$ with $f(x_1)\in V_1$ and $f(x_2)\in V_2$.

::: {.proof}
Since $f$ is injective, $f(x_1)\neq f(x_2)$, and $Y$ is Hausdorff.
:::

<1>2. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
With $V_1,V_2$ from step <1>1, put $U_i=f^{-1}(V_i)$.
By continuity $U_1,U_2\in\mathcal T$, and $x_i\in U_i$.
Moreover $U_1\cap U_2=f^{-1}(V_1\cap V_2)=f^{-1}(\varnothing)=\varnothing$.
Hence $X$ is Hausdorff.
:::
:::
