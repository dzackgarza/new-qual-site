::: {.problem}
If $H:X\times[0,1]\to X$ is a homotopy with $H_0=H_1$ equal to the identity map, show that the map $\gamma:I\to X$ given by $\gamma(t)=H(x_0,t)$ is a loop in $X$ representing an element $g=[\gamma]\in\pi_1(X,x_0)$ which lies in the center of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$, i.e., $gh=hg$ for all $h\in\pi_1(X,x_0)$.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. The path $\gamma(t)=H(x_0,t)$ is a loop based at $x_0$.
::: {.proof}
Since $H_0=H_1=\operatorname{id}_X$, we have $\gamma(0)=H(x_0,0)=x_0=H(x_0,1)=\gamma(1)$.
:::

<1>2. Let $\alpha:I\to X$ be any loop based at $x_0$, and define $F:I\times I\to X$ by
$$
F(s,t)=H(\alpha(s),t).
$$
::: {.proof}
The map is continuous as the composite of $(s,t)\mapsto(\alpha(s),t)$ with $H$.
:::

<1>3. The four sides of the square under $F$ are $\alpha$, $\gamma$, $\alpha$, and $\gamma$.
::: {.proof}
Along the bottom, $F(s,0)=H_0(\alpha(s))=\alpha(s)$; along the top, $F(s,1)=H_1(\alpha(s))=\alpha(s)$. Along either vertical side, $\alpha(0)=\alpha(1)=x_0$, so $F(0,t)=F(1,t)=\gamma(t)$.
:::

<1>4. The loops $\alpha*\gamma$ and $\gamma*\alpha$ are path-homotopic relative endpoints.
::: {.proof}
The map $F$ fills the square whose boundary, with its induced orientation, is the loop $\alpha*\gamma*\bar\alpha*\bar\gamma$. Hence this commutator loop is null-homotopic. Multiplying the resulting equality in $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ by the appropriate inverse classes gives $[\alpha][\gamma]=[\gamma][\alpha]$.
:::

<1>5. Thus $g=[\gamma]$ lies in the center of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$.
::: {.proof}
The loop $\alpha$ in <1>2 was arbitrary, so <1>4 shows that $g$ commutes with every element represented by a based loop.
:::
:::
