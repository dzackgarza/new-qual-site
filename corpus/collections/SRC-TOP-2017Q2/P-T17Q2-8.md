::: {.problem}
Find a $\Delta$-complex structure for, and compute the (simplicial) homology groups of, the space $X$ obtained by gluing a $2$-disk $D^2$ to the $2$-sphere $S^2$ by a homeomorphism from $\partial D^2$ to the equator of $S^2$.
That is, $$X=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:x^2+y^2\le1\text{ and either }x^2+y^2+z^2=1\text{ or }z=0\}.$$ [Your description of a $\Delta$-complex structure need only contain enough detail to describe your homology calculations.]
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. Triangulate the equator by three vertices $v_0,v_1,v_2$ and three edges $e_{01},e_{12},e_{20}$. Add one interior vertex in each of the northern hemisphere, southern hemisphere, and attached disk, and cone the equatorial triangle to each of these three interior vertices.
::: {.proof}
Each of the three closed disks is thereby subdivided into three $2$-simplices, and the three triangulations agree on their common boundary equator. Hence this gives a simplicial, in particular a $\Delta$-complex, structure on $X$ with $6$ vertices, $12$ edges, and $9$ triangles.
:::

<1>2. Orient the common equatorial cycle
$$
E=e_{01}+e_{12}+e_{20}.
$$
For each of the three disks, let $C_N,C_S,C_D$ be the sum of its three oriented triangles, oriented so that
$$
\partial C_N=E,\qquad \partial C_S=-E,\qquad \partial C_D=E.
$$
::: {.proof}
Within each triangulated disk, the radial edges cancel in the sum of its three coherently oriented triangles, leaving exactly its oriented boundary. The two hemispheres induce opposite orientations on their common equator.
:::

<1>3. The kernel of $\partial_2:C_2\to C_1$ is free of rank $2$.
::: {.proof}
If a $2$-chain is a cycle, cancellation along each radial interior edge forces the three triangle coefficients inside each one of the three disks to be equal. Thus every $2$-cycle has the form $rC_N+sC_S+tC_D$. Its boundary is $(r-s+t)E$, so the cycle condition is the single relation $r-s+t=0$. Hence
$$
\ker\partial_2\cong\{(r,s,t)\in\mathbb Z^3:r-s+t=0\}\cong\mathbb Z^2.
$$
Since there are no $3$-simplices, $H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2$.
:::

<1>4. The image of $\partial_1:C_1\to C_0$ has rank $5$, so $\ker\partial_1$ has rank $12-5=7$.
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is connected and has $6$ vertices. For a connected finite graph, $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$, so $\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{im}\partial_1=6-1=5$. Since $C_1\cong\mathbb Z^{12}$, rank-nullity over the free abelian groups gives $\operatorname{rank}\ker\partial_1=7$.
:::

<1>5. The image of $\partial_2$ has rank $7$.
::: {.proof}
We have $C_2\cong\mathbb Z^9$ and $\ker\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z^2$ by <1>3. Therefore $\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{im}\partial_2=9-2=7$.
:::

<1>6. We have $H_1(X)=0$ and $H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
The northern and southern disks together form $S^2$, which is simply connected. The space $X$ is obtained from this $S^2$ by attaching the third $2$-disk along the equator. By Seifert--van Kampen, attaching a $2$-cell quotients the fundamental group by the normal closure of its attaching loop; since $\pi_1(S^2)=0$, we still have $\pi_1(X)=0$. Therefore $H_1(X)$, the abelianization of $\pi_1(X)$, is zero. The complex is connected, so $H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>7. Therefore
$$
H_i(X)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&i=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>3 and <1>6; there are no simplices above dimension $2$.
:::
