::: problem
Compute, by any means available, the fundamental group and all the homology groups of the space obtained by gluing one copy $A$ of $S^2$ to another copy $B$ of $S^2$ via a two-sheeted covering space map from the equator of $A$ onto the equator of $B$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
f:S^1_A\longrightarrow S^1_B
\]
be the two-sheeted covering used to identify the equator of $A$ with the equator of $B$, and let $X$ be the resulting quotient.
Choose orientations so that $f$ has degree $2$.

<1>1. The quotient $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, one $1$-cell, and four $2$-cells whose attaching maps have degrees $1,-1,2,-2$ on the $1$-skeleton.
::: {.proof}
Use the equator $S^1_B$ as the $1$-skeleton, with its standard CW structure consisting of one $0$-cell and one $1$-cell.
The northern and southern hemispheres of $B$ are two $2$-cells attached along the equator.
With compatible orientations, their attaching maps have degrees $1$ and $-1$.

The northern and southern hemispheres of $A$ give two further $2$-cells in the quotient.
Their boundary circles are identified with $S^1_B$ by the map $f$.
Hence their attaching maps have degrees $2$ and $-2$, with the sign change coming from the opposite boundary orientations of the two hemispheres.
Thus the cellular chain groups are
\[
C_2(X)\cong\ZZ^4,
\qquad
C_1(X)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
C_0(X)\cong\ZZ,
\]
with no cells in higher dimensions.
:::

<1>2. The fundamental group of $X$ is trivial.
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is a circle, so its fundamental group is generated by one element $t$.
Attaching the four $2$-cells adds the relations
\[
t=1,
\qquad
t^{-1}=1,
\qquad
t^2=1,
\qquad
t^{-2}=1.
\]
In particular, the first relation already kills the generator.
By the cellular form of the Seifert--van Kampen theorem,
\[
\pi_1(X)=1.
\]
:::

<1>3. The cellular boundary map $d_2:C_2(X)\to C_1(X)$ is
\[
d_2(a,b,c,d)=a-b+2c-2d.
\]
::: {.proof}
For a CW complex with one oriented $1$-cell, the coefficient of that $1$-cell in the boundary of a $2$-cell is the degree of its attaching map.
By <1>1, the four attaching degrees are $1,-1,2,-2$.
Hence, in the corresponding bases,
\[
d_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Also $d_1=0$, since the single $1$-cell is a loop at the unique $0$-cell.
:::

<1>4. The integral homology groups are
\[
H_n(X;\ZZ)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&n=0,\\
0,&n=1,\\
\ZZ^3,&n=2,\\
0,&n\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Because the first coefficient of $d_2$ is $1$, the map
\[
d_2:\ZZ^4\longrightarrow\ZZ
\]
is surjective.
Therefore
\[
H_1(X;\ZZ)
=\ker d_1/\operatorname{im}d_2
=\ZZ/\ZZ
=0.
\]
Since there are no $3$-cells,
\[
H_2(X;\ZZ)=\ker d_2.
\]
The kernel of a surjective homomorphism $\ZZ^4\to\ZZ$ is free of rank $3$, so
\[
H_2(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^3.
\]
The space is connected, hence $H_0(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ$, and there are no cells in dimensions at least $3$, so all higher homology groups vanish.
:::
