::: problem
a. Prove that every continuous map $f:S^2\to S^1\times S^1$ is homotopic to a constant map.

b. Prove that the quotient map $f:S^2\to\RP^2$ is not homotopic to a constant map.
(Here we regard $\RP^2$ as the quotient of $S^2$ by the antipodal map.)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every continuous map $f:S^2\to S^1\times S^1$ is null-homotopic.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
p:\RR^2\longrightarrow S^1\times S^1
\]
be the universal covering map.
Because $S^2$ is simply connected, the covering-space lifting criterion gives a lift
\[
\widetilde f:S^2\longrightarrow\RR^2
\]
such that
\[
p\circ\widetilde f=f.
\]
Since $\RR^2$ is contractible, there is a homotopy
\[
\widetilde H:S^2\times I\longrightarrow\RR^2
\]
from $\widetilde f$ to a constant map.
Then
\[
H=p\circ\widetilde H
\]
is a homotopy from $f$ to a constant map in $S^1\times S^1$.
:::

<1>2. The quotient map $q:S^2\to\RP^2$ is not null-homotopic.
::: {.proof}
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that there is a homotopy
\[
H:S^2\times I\longrightarrow\RP^2
\]
with
\[
H(-,0)=q
\]
and $H(-,1)$ constant.
Regard
\[
q:S^2\longrightarrow\RP^2
\]
as the two-sheeted covering map.
Since $\operatorname{id}_{S^2}$ is a lift of $H(-,0)=q$, the homotopy lifting property gives
\[
\widetilde H:S^2\times I\longrightarrow S^2
\]
with
\[
q\circ\widetilde H=H,
\qquad
\widetilde H(-,0)=\operatorname{id}_{S^2}.
\]

Because $H(-,1)$ is constant, the image of $\widetilde H(-,1)$ lies in a single fiber of $q$.
That fiber has two points, and $S^2$ is connected, so
\[
\widetilde H(-,1):S^2\longrightarrow S^2
\]
is constant.
Thus $\widetilde H$ would be a homotopy from $\operatorname{id}_{S^2}$ to a constant map.

This is impossible on second homology.
Indeed,
\[
H_2(S^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\]
the identity map induces the identity homomorphism on this group, while a constant map factors through a point and hence induces the zero homomorphism on $H_2$.
Homotopic maps induce the same map on homology, a contradiction.
Therefore $q$ is not homotopic to a constant map.
:::
:::
