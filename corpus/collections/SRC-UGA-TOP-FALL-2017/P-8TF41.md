::: problem
Let $\{Y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a sequence of connected subsets of a topological space $X$.
Suppose that $Y_n\cap Y_{n+1}$ is nonempty for all $n$.
Show that $\bigcup_n Y_n$ is connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\[
Y=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_n
\]
is disconnected.
Then there are disjoint nonempty subsets $U,V\subseteq Y$, open in the subspace topology on $Y$, such that
\[
Y=U\cup V.
\]

<1>1. For each $n$, the connected set $Y_n$ is contained entirely in $U$ or entirely in $V$.
::: {.proof}
For each $n$,
\[
Y_n=(Y_n\cap U)\cup(Y_n\cap V),
\]
where $Y_n\cap U$ and $Y_n\cap V$ are disjoint and open in the subspace topology on $Y_n$.
Since $Y_n$ is connected, one of these two sets is empty.
Thus either $Y_n\subseteq U$ or $Y_n\subseteq V$.
:::

<1>2. All the sets $Y_n$ lie in the same member of the separation.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $Y_1$ lies in either $U$ or $V$; interchange the names if necessary and assume $Y_1\subseteq U$.
Suppose $Y_n\subseteq U$.
Since
\[
Y_n\cap Y_{n+1}\neq\varnothing,
\]
the set $Y_{n+1}$ meets $U$.
By <1>1, $Y_{n+1}$ cannot then be contained in $V$, so $Y_{n+1}\subseteq U$.
Induction gives $Y_n\subseteq U$ for every $n$.
:::

<1>3. This contradicts that $U,V$ form a separation of $Y$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2,
\[
Y=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_n\subseteq U,
\]
so $V=\varnothing$, contrary to the assumption that both members of the separation are nonempty.
Therefore $Y$ is connected.
:::
:::
