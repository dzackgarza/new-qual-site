::: problem
Let $R$ be a commutative algebra over $\mathbb C$.
A derivation of $R$ is a $\mathbb C$-linear map $D:R\rightarrow R$ such that (i) $D(1)=0$ and (ii) $D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)$ for all $a,b\in R$.

- Describe all derivations of the polynomial ring $\mathbb C[x]$.

- Let $A$ be the subring (or $\mathbb C$-subalgebra) of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb C[x])$ generated by all derivations of $\mathbb C[x]$ and the left multiplications by $x$.
  Prove that $\mathbb C[x]$ is a simple left $A$-module.
  \> Note that the inclusion $A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb C[x])$ defines a natural left $A$-module structure on $\mathbb C[x]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Describe all derivations of $\CC[x]$, and show $\CC[x]$ is a simple left $A$-module.

<1>1. All derivations of $\CC[x]$.
<2>1. A derivation $D$ is determined by $D(x)$.
::: {.proof}
$D(1) = 0$ and the Leibniz rule give $D(x^n) = n x^{n-1} D(x)$ by induction, so $D$ is determined by its value on the generator $x$.
:::
<2>2. Conversely, for any $p(x) \in \CC[x]$, the map $D_p(f) = p(x) f'(x)$ is a derivation.
::: {.proof}
$D_p(1) = 0$ and $D_p(fg) = p(f'g + fg') = D_p(f)g + fD_p(g)$.
:::
<2>3. Hence the derivations of $\CC[x]$ are exactly $\theset{p(x)\frac{d}{dx} : p \in \CC[x]}$.
::: {.proof}
<1>1.1 and <1>1.2.
:::

<1>2. $\CC[x]$ is a simple left $A$-module.
<2>1. $A$ contains the derivation $\frac{d}{dx}$ and the multiplication operator $x$.
::: {.proof}
$\frac{d}{dx}$ is a derivation (with $p = 1$), and $x$ is a left multiplication.
:::
<2>2. The operators $x$ and $\frac{d}{dx}$ satisfy $[\frac{d}{dx}, x] = 1$.
::: {.proof}
$\frac{d}{dx}(x f) - x \frac{d}{dx}(f) = f + x f' - x f' = f$, so $[\frac{d}{dx}, x] = \operatorname{id}$.
:::
<2>3. $A$ is the Weyl algebra $A_1(\CC)$.
::: {.proof}
$A$ is generated by $x$ and $\frac{d}{dx}$ subject to the relation $[\frac{d}{dx}, x] = 1$, which is the defining presentation of the Weyl algebra.
:::
<2>4. $\CC[x]$ is a simple $A_1(\CC)$-module.
::: {.proof}
the Weyl algebra acts on $\CC[x]$ with $x$ acting by multiplication and $\frac{d}{dx}$ by differentiation; this module is simple (any nonzero submodule contains a nonzero polynomial, and applying $\frac{d}{dx}$ repeatedly gives a nonzero constant, hence $1$, hence the whole module).
:::
<2>5. Hence $\CC[x]$ is a simple left $A$-module.
::: {.proof}
<1>2.3 and <1>2.4.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 describes the derivations; <1>2 proves simplicity.
:::
:::
