::: {.problem}
Suppose $\mu$ is a Borel probability measure on the unit circle such that
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|z|=1} z^n\,d\mu(z)=0.
$$
For $f\in L^1(\mu)$, show that
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|z|=1} z^n f(z)\,d\mu(z)=0.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The conclusion holds whenever $f$ is a trigonometric polynomial on the unit circle.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
P(z)=\sum_{m=-N}^N c_m z^m.
$$
Then
$$
\int_{|z|=1} z^nP(z)\,d\mu(z)
=\sum_{m=-N}^N c_m
\int_{|z|=1} z^{n+m}\,d\mu(z).
$$
For each fixed $m$, one has $n+m\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$. By hypothesis each term in the finite sum tends to $0$, so the whole sum tends to $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Trigonometric polynomials are dense in $L^1(\mu)$.


::: pf-proof

Since the unit circle is a compact metric space and $\mu$ is a finite Borel measure, continuous functions are dense in $L^1(\mu)$. The algebra generated by $z$ and $\overline z=z^{-1}$ separates points of the unit circle, contains the constants, and is closed under complex conjugation. By the complex Stone--Weierstrass theorem, trigonometric polynomials are uniformly dense in the continuous functions. Uniform approximation implies $L^1(\mu)$ approximation because $\mu$ is finite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every $f\in L^1(\mu)$,
$$
\boxed{
\int_{|z|=1} z^n f(z)\,d\mu(z)
\longrightarrow0.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, choose a trigonometric polynomial $P$ such that
$$
\|f-P\|_{L^1(\mu)}<\varepsilon.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, for all sufficiently large $n$,
$$
\left|\int z^nP\,d\mu\right|<\varepsilon.
$$
Since $|z^n|=1$ on the unit circle,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int z^nf\,d\mu\right|
&\le
\left|\int z^n(f-P)\,d\mu\right|
+\left|\int z^nP\,d\mu\right|\\
&\le
\int|f-P|\,d\mu+\varepsilon\\
&<2\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, the integral tends to $0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
