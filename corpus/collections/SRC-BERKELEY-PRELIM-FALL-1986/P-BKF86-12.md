::: {.problem}
Let $\{U_1,U_2,\ldots\}$ be a cover of $\mathbb R^n$ by open sets.
Prove that there is a cover $\{V_1,V_2,\ldots\}$ such that

1. $V_j\subset U_j$ for each $j$;

2. each compact subset of $\mathbb R^n$ is disjoint from all but finitely many of the $V_j$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
V_1=U_1
$$
and, for $j\geq2$,
$$
V_j
=
U_j\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}U_i.
$$

<1>1. For every $j\geq1$,
$$
V_j\subseteq U_j.
$$

::: {.proof}
This follows immediately from the definition of $V_j$.
:::

<1>2. The family $\{V_1,V_2,\ldots\}$ covers $\RR^n$.

::: {.proof}
Let $x\in\RR^n$. Since $\{U_1,U_2,\ldots\}$ covers $\RR^n$, the set
$$
\{j\geq1:x\in U_j\}
$$
is nonempty. Let $j$ be its least element. Then $x\in U_j$ and $x\notin U_i$ for every $i<j$. Hence, by definition,
$$
x\in V_j.
$$
Thus every point of $\RR^n$ belongs to some $V_j$.
:::

<1>3. If $K\subseteq\RR^n$ is compact, then there is an integer $M$ such that
$$
K\cap V_j=\varnothing
$$
for every $j>M$.

::: {.proof}
The sets $U_j\cap K$ form an open cover of the compact space $K$. Hence there are indices
$$
j_1,\ldots,j_r
$$
such that
$$
K\subseteq U_{j_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{j_r}.
$$
Let
$$
M=\max\{j_1,\ldots,j_r\}.
$$

Suppose $j>M$ and $x\in K$. Then $x\in U_{j_s}$ for some $s$, with
$$
j_s\leq M<j.
$$
Therefore $x\in\bigcup_{i<j}U_i$, so the definition of $V_j$ implies $x\notin V_j$. Thus $K\cap V_j=\varnothing$ for every $j>M$.
:::

<1>4. Each compact subset of $\RR^n$ is disjoint from all but finitely many of the $V_j$.

::: {.proof}
For a compact set $K$, step <1>3 shows that only
$$
V_1,\ldots,V_M
$$
can meet $K$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives property (1), step <1>2 shows that the $V_j$ form a cover, and step <1>4 gives property (2).
:::
:::
