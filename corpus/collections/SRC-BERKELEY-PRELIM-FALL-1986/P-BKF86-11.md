::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be an $m\times n$ matrix with entries in a field $\mathbf F$.
Define the row rank and the column rank of $A$ and show from first principles that they are equal.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The row rank of $A$ is the dimension of the subspace of $\mathbf F^n$ spanned by the rows of $A$, and the column rank of $A$ is the dimension of the subspace of $\mathbf F^m$ spanned by the columns of $A$.

::: pf-proof

These are the definitions of the row space, column space, row rank, and column rank.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every matrix $A$,
$$
\operatorname{columnrank}(A)\leq\operatorname{rowrank}(A).
$$

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
r=\operatorname{rowrank}(A).
$$
If $r=0$, every row of $A$ is zero, hence $A=0$ and its column rank is also $0$.

Assume $r>0$. Choose rows
$$
R_1,\ldots,R_r
$$
of $A$ forming a basis of its row space, and let $B$ be the $r\times n$ matrix whose rows are $R_1,\ldots,R_r$. Every row $S_i$ of $A$ has a unique expression
$$
S_i=\sum_{k=1}^r\lambda_{ik}R_k.
$$
Let
$$
\Lambda=(\lambda_{ik})
$$
be the resulting $m\times r$ matrix. Row by row, the preceding equations say exactly that
$$
A=\Lambda B.
$$

For each column index $j$, let $b_j\in\mathbf F^r$ be the $j$th column of $B$. Then the $j$th column of $A$ is
$$
\Lambda b_j,
$$
which is a linear combination of the $r$ columns of $\Lambda$. Hence every column of $A$ lies in the span of those $r$ columns. Therefore the column space of $A$ has dimension at most $r$, proving
$$
\operatorname{columnrank}(A)\leq r=\operatorname{rowrank}(A).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every matrix $A$,
$$
\operatorname{rowrank}(A)\leq\operatorname{columnrank}(A).
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s2){.pf-ref} to the transpose $A^{\mathsf T}$. This gives
$$
\operatorname{columnrank}(A^{\mathsf T})
\leq
\operatorname{rowrank}(A^{\mathsf T}).
$$
The columns of $A^{\mathsf T}$ are exactly the transposes of the rows of $A$, so
$$
\operatorname{columnrank}(A^{\mathsf T})
=
\operatorname{rowrank}(A).
$$
Similarly,
$$
\operatorname{rowrank}(A^{\mathsf T})
=
\operatorname{columnrank}(A).
$$
Substitution gives the required inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The row rank and column rank of $A$ are equal.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} give the two opposite inequalities, hence
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{rowrank}(A)=\operatorname{columnrank}(A)
}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives the requested definitions, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves their equality.

:::
