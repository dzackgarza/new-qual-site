::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a subgroup of $S_5$, the group of all permutations of five objects.
Prove that if $G$ contains a $5$-cycle and a $2$-cycle, then $G=S_5$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\sigma\in G$ be a $5$-cycle and let $\tau\in G$ be a transposition.
Label the five objects by $\ZZ/5\ZZ$ in the cyclic order of $\sigma$, so that
$$
\sigma(k)=k+1.
$$
Write
$$
\tau=(r,s)
$$
and set
$$
d=s-r\in\ZZ/5\ZZ.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $k\in\ZZ/5\ZZ$, the group $G$ contains the transposition
$$
(r+k,s+k).
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $\sigma,\tau\in G$ and $G$ is a subgroup,
$$
\sigma^k\tau\sigma^{-k}\in G.
$$
Conjugation carries a transposition to the transposition of the images of its two entries, so
$$
\sigma^k(r,s)\sigma^{-k}
=
(r+k,s+k).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The graph on $\ZZ/5\ZZ$ whose edges are
$$
\{r+k,s+k\},
\qquad
k\in\ZZ/5\ZZ,
$$
is connected.

::: pf-proof

Since $r\neq s$, one has $d\neq0$ in $\ZZ/5\ZZ$. Because $5$ is prime, the elements
$$
0,d,2d,3d,4d
$$
are all distinct modulo $5$. Hence
$$
r,\ r+d,\ r+2d,\ r+3d,\ r+4d
$$
are all five vertices.

For each $j$, the pair
$$
\{r+jd,r+(j+1)d\}
$$
is one of the edges from step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, obtained by taking $k=jd$. Thus these edges give a path through all five vertices, so the graph is connected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The group $G$ contains every transposition of the five objects.

::: pf-proof

Let $a$ and $b$ be two distinct vertices. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, there is a path
$$
a=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_m=b
$$
in the graph. Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} says that every edge transposition
$$
(v_{i-1},v_i)
$$
lies in $G$.

Induct on the length of the path. For length $1$, $(a,b)$ is already an edge transposition. Suppose $m>1$. By induction,
$$
(v_0,v_{m-1})\in G,
$$
and the last edge gives
$$
(v_{m-1},v_m)\in G.
$$
Conjugating the latter transposition by the former gives
$$
(v_0,v_{m-1})(v_{m-1},v_m)(v_0,v_{m-1})
=
(v_0,v_m).
$$
Hence $(a,b)\in G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One has
$$
\boxed{G=S_5}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $G$ contains every transposition of the five objects. Every permutation is a product of transpositions, so $G$ contains every element of $S_5$. Since $G\leq S_5$, equality follows.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
