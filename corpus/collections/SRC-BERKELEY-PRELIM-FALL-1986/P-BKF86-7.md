::: {.problem}
Let $\mathbf F$ be a field containing $\mathbb Q$ such that $[\mathbf F:\mathbb Q]=2$.
Prove that there exists a unique integer $m$ such that $m$ has no multiple prime factors and $\mathbf F$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb Q(\sqrt m)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. There is an element $\alpha\in\mathbf F\setminus\QQ$ such that
$$
\mathbf F=\QQ(\alpha),
$$
and the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ has the form
$$
x^2+rx+s
$$
with $r,s\in\QQ$.

::: {.proof}
Choose any $\alpha\in\mathbf F\setminus\QQ$. Then
$$
1<[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]\leq[\mathbf F:\QQ]=2.
$$
Hence $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=2$ and therefore $\QQ(\alpha)=\mathbf F$. The monic minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ consequently has degree $2$, so it has the displayed form.
:::

<1>2. If
$$
\Delta=r^2-4s,
$$
then $\Delta\in\QQ^\times$ is not a square in $\QQ$ and
$$
\mathbf F=\QQ(\sqrt{\Delta}).
$$

::: {.proof}
From
$$
\alpha^2+r\alpha+s=0
$$
one obtains
$$
(2\alpha+r)^2=r^2-4s=\Delta.
$$
Also
$$
\alpha=\frac{(2\alpha+r)-r}{2},
$$
so
$$
\QQ(\alpha)=\QQ(2\alpha+r).
$$
Thus $\mathbf F$ is generated by a square root of $\Delta$.

If $\Delta=t^2$ for some $t\in\QQ$, then in the field $\mathbf F$,
$$
(2\alpha+r-t)(2\alpha+r+t)=0.
$$
Hence $2\alpha+r=\pm t$, which would imply $\alpha\in\QQ$, contrary to step <1>1. Therefore $\Delta$ is a nonzero nonsquare in $\QQ$.
:::

<1>3. There exists a squarefree integer $m$ such that
$$
\mathbf F\cong\QQ(\sqrt m).
$$

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
\Delta=\frac{a}{b}
$$
with relatively prime nonzero integers $a,b$ and $b>0$. If $\gamma^2=\Delta$, then
$$
(b\gamma)^2=ab,
$$
and conversely $\gamma=(b\gamma)/b$. Hence
$$
\QQ(\sqrt{\Delta})=\QQ(\sqrt{ab}).
$$

By unique factorization in $\ZZ$, write
$$
ab=mc^2
$$
with $c\in\ZZ_{>0}$ and $m$ a squarefree integer, with the sign of $m$ equal to the sign of $ab$. Then
$$
\QQ(\sqrt{ab})=\QQ(c\sqrt m)=\QQ(\sqrt m).
$$
Combining this with step <1>2 gives the required existence.
:::

<1>4. The squarefree integer $m$ in step <1>3 is unique.

::: {.proof}
Suppose $m$ and $n$ are squarefree integers and
$$
\QQ(\sqrt m)\cong\mathbf F\cong\QQ(\sqrt n).
$$
Composing the two field isomorphisms gives an isomorphism
$$
\varphi:\QQ(\sqrt m)\longrightarrow\QQ(\sqrt n).
$$
Every field isomorphism between fields of characteristic $0$ fixes $\QQ$, so for some $u,v\in\QQ$,
$$
\varphi(\sqrt m)=u+v\sqrt n.
$$
Squaring and using $\varphi(m)=m$ gives
$$
m=u^2+v^2n+2uv\sqrt n.
$$
Since $\QQ(\sqrt n)$ has degree $2$ over $\QQ$, one has $\sqrt n\notin\QQ$, and therefore
$$
uv=0.
$$
The case $v=0$ would give $\varphi(\sqrt m)\in\QQ$, impossible because $\varphi$ is an isomorphism and $\sqrt m\notin\QQ$. Thus $u=0$, so
$$
\frac{m}{n}=v^2
$$
is a square in $\QQ$.

For every prime $\ell$, squarefreeness gives
$$
\nu_\ell(m),\nu_\ell(n)\in\{0,1\}.
$$
Since $m/n$ is a rational square, $\nu_\ell(m)-\nu_\ell(n)$ is even. It lies in $\{-1,0,1\}$, so it must be $0$. Thus $m$ and $n$ have the same prime divisors. Moreover $m/n=v^2>0$, so they have the same sign. Therefore $m=n$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves existence and step <1>4 proves uniqueness.
