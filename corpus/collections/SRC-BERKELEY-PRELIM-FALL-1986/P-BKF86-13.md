::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R$ be defined by
\[
f(x,y)=\begin{cases}
x^{4/3}\sin(y/x) & \text{if } x\ne0,\\
0 & \text{if } x=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Determine all points at which $f$ is differentiable.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function $f$ is differentiable at every point $(x,y)$ with $x\neq0$.

::: {.proof}
On the open set
$$
\{(x,y)\in\RR^2:x\neq0\},
$$
the functions $x\mapsto x^{4/3}$ and $(x,y)\mapsto y/x$ are differentiable, and so is their composition with $\sin$. Hence
$$
f(x,y)=x^{4/3}\sin(y/x)
$$
is differentiable there.
:::

<1>2. For every $y_0\in\RR$, the function $f$ is differentiable at $(0,y_0)$, with derivative equal to the zero linear map.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
f(0,y_0)=0,
$$
it suffices to prove
$$
\frac{\abs{f(h,y_0+k)}}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2}}
\longrightarrow0
$$
as $(h,k)\to(0,0)$.

If $h=0$, the numerator is $0$. If $h\neq0$, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\abs{f(h,y_0+k)}}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2}}
&=
\frac{\abs{h}^{4/3}\abs{\sin((y_0+k)/h)}}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2}}\\
&\leq
\frac{\abs{h}^{4/3}}{\abs{h}}\\
&=
\abs{h}^{1/3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last quantity tends to $0$ as $(h,k)\to(0,0)$. Therefore
$$
f(0+h,y_0+k)-f(0,y_0)
=
o\bigl(\sqrt{h^2+k^2}\bigr),
$$
which is exactly differentiability at $(0,y_0)$ with derivative $0$.
:::

<1>3. The function is differentiable at
$$
\boxed{\text{every point of }\RR^2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 covers every point with nonzero first coordinate, and step <1>2 covers every point with first coordinate zero.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the required determination.
:::
:::
