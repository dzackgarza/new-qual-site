::: {.problem}
Evaluate
\[
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=1}\frac{z^{11}}{12z^{12}-4z^9+2z^6-4z^3+1}\,dz,
\]
where the direction of integration is counterclockwise.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
P(z)=12z^{12}-4z^9+2z^6-4z^3+1
$$
and
$$
R(z)=\frac{z^{11}}{P(z)}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Every zero of $P$ lies in the open unit disk.

::: pf-proof

On $\abs z=1$,
$$
\abs{12z^{12}}=12,
$$
while
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{-4z^9+2z^6-4z^3+1}
&\leq
4+2+4+1\\
&=11.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{-4z^9+2z^6-4z^3+1}
<
\abs{12z^{12}}
$$
on the unit circle. By Rouché's theorem, $P$ and $12z^{12}$ have the same number of zeros in the unit disk, counted with multiplicity. Since $P$ has degree $12$, all twelve of its zeros lie there. In particular, $P$ has no zero on the unit circle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The residue of $R$ at infinity is
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{\infty}R=-\frac1{12}.
$$

::: pf-proof

As $z\to\infty$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R(z)
&=
\frac{z^{11}}{12z^{12}-4z^9+2z^6-4z^3+1}\\
&=
\frac1{12z}+O\left(\frac1{z^4}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the Laurent expansion of $R$ at infinity is $1/12$. By the definition of the residue at infinity,
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{\infty}R=-\frac1{12}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The sum of the residues of $R$ at all of its finite poles is
$$
\frac1{12}.
$$

::: pf-proof

For a rational function, the sum of all finite residues together with the residue at infinity is zero. Hence step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\sum_{a\in\CC}\operatorname{Res}_{z=a}R
=
-\operatorname{Res}_{\infty}R
=
\frac1{12}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The required integral is
$$
\boxed{\frac1{12}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, every finite pole of $R$ lies inside $\abs z=1$, and none lies on the contour. The residue theorem and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} therefore give
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\abs z=1}R(z)\,dz
=
\sum_{\abs a<1}\operatorname{Res}_{z=a}R
=
\frac1{12}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is the required evaluation.

:::
