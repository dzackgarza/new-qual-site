::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the set of real numbers with the lower limit topology $\mathcal T$; that is, the topology generated by the basis $\{[a,b)\mid a,b\in X\}$.
Let $Y=X\times X$ have the product topology.

(1a) Find the closure of the interval $(0,1)$ in $X$.

(1b) Determine whether $Y$ is connected and/or compact.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
Every point of
\[
[0,1)
\]
lies in the closure of $(0,1)$ in the lower-limit topology.

::: pf-proof
Every point of $(0,1)$ belongs to its own closure.

It remains to consider $0$.
Let $U$ be any open neighborhood of $0$.
Since the intervals $[a,b)$ form a basis, there is a basic neighborhood
\[
[a,b)
\]
with
\[
0\in[a,b)\subseteq U.
\]
The condition $0\in[a,b)$ implies
\[
a\le0<b.
\]
Choose
\[
t\in(0,\min\{b,1\});
\]
this interval is nonempty because $b>0$.
Then
\[
t\in[a,b)\cap(0,1)\subseteq U\cap(0,1).
\]
Thus every neighborhood of $0$ meets $(0,1)$, so $0$ lies in its closure.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}
No point of
\[
\mathbb R\setminus[0,1)
\]
lies in the closure of $(0,1)$.

::: pf-proof
If $x<0$, then
\[
[x,0)
\]
is a basic open neighborhood of $x$ and is disjoint from $(0,1)$.

If $x\ge1$, then
\[
[x,x+1)
\]
is a basic open neighborhood of $x$ and is again disjoint from $(0,1)$.
Hence every point outside $[0,1)$ has an open neighborhood missing $(0,1)$ and therefore is not in the closure.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}_X((0,1))=[0,1).}
\]

::: pf-proof
The inclusion $[0,1)\subseteq\operatorname{Cl}_X((0,1))$ is step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, and the reverse inclusion is step [](#s2){.pf-ref}. This proves part (1a).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}
The subset $[0,1)$ is both open and closed in $X$.

::: pf-proof
It is open because it is itself one of the basic lower-limit intervals.

Its complement is
\[
(-\infty,0)\cup[1,\infty).
\]
For each $x<0$, the basic interval
\[
[x,0)
\]
lies in $(-\infty,0)$, so
\[
(-\infty,0)
=
\bigcup_{x<0}[x,0)
\]
is open.
Likewise every $x\ge1$ belongs to the basic interval $[x,x+1)$, and
\[
[1,\infty)
=
\bigcup_{x\ge1}[x,x+1)
\]
is open.
Thus the complement of $[0,1)$ is open, so $[0,1)$ is closed as well.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}
The product space
\[
Y=X\times X
\]
is disconnected.

::: pf-proof
The subset
\[
[0,1)\times X
\]
is nonempty and proper.
It is open because both factors are open, and it is closed because its complement is
\[
(X\setminus[0,1))\times X,
\]
which is open by step [](#s4){.pf-ref}. Thus $Y$ has a nontrivial clopen subset and is disconnected.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}
The Sorgenfrey line $X$ is not compact.

::: pf-proof
For $n\ge1$, let
\[
U_n=[-n,n).
\]
Each $U_n$ is a basic open set, and
\[
X=\bigcup_{n\ge1}U_n.
\]
Indeed, for any $x\in\mathbb R$, choose $n>|x|$.

However, any finite subfamily is contained in $U_N$ for the largest chosen index $N$, and
\[
N\notin[-N,N)=U_N.
\]
Hence no finite subfamily covers $X$.
Thus $X$ is not compact.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}
The product $Y=X\times X$ is not compact.

::: pf-proof
The first-coordinate projection
\[
\pi_1:Y\longrightarrow X
\]
is continuous and surjective.
If $Y$ were compact, its continuous image
\[
\pi_1(Y)=X
\]
would be compact, contradicting step [](#s6){.pf-ref}. Therefore $Y$ is not compact.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Hence in part (1b), $Y$ is neither connected nor compact.

::: pf-proof
Disconnectedness is step [](#s5){.pf-ref} and noncompactness is step [](#s7){.pf-ref}.
:::
