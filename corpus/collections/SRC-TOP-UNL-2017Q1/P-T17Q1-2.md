::: {.problem}
Let $X=\mathbb R^2$ have the Euclidean topology, let $C=(\mathbb R\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times\mathbb R)$, and let $\mathcal T_1$ be the subspace topology on $C$.
Define the function $f:X\to C$ by $f(p,q)=(p,0)$ if $p\ne0$ and $f(p,q)=(0,q)$ if $p=0$.
Let $\mathcal T_2$ be the quotient topology on $C$ induced by $f$.
Determine whether $\mathcal T_1=\mathcal T_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The two topologies are not equal.

<1>1. Let
\[
U=\{0\}\times(1/2,3/2)\subseteq C.
\]
Then $U$ is open in the subspace topology $\mathcal T_1$.
::: {.proof}
Consider the Euclidean-open rectangle
\[
W=(-1/4,1/4)\times(1/2,3/2)\subseteq\mathbb R^2.
\]
Because every point of $W$ has second coordinate strictly positive, $W$ does not meet the horizontal axis
\[
\mathbb R\times\{0\}.
\]
Its intersection with the vertical axis is exactly
\[
W\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb R)
=
\{0\}\times(1/2,3/2).
\]
Hence
\[
W\cap C=U.
\]
By the definition of the subspace topology, $U\in\mathcal T_1$.
:::

<1>2. The preimage of $U$ under $f$ is
\[
f^{-1}(U)=\{0\}\times(1/2,3/2).
\]
::: {.proof}
If $p\ne0$, then
\[
f(p,q)=(p,0),
\]
which lies on the horizontal axis and therefore cannot belong to $U$.

If $p=0$, then
\[
f(0,q)=(0,q),
\]
which belongs to $U$ exactly when
\[
q\in(1/2,3/2).
\]
This proves the formula for the preimage.
:::

<1>3. The set $f^{-1}(U)$ is not open in the Euclidean plane $X=\mathbb R^2$.
::: {.proof}
Take, for example,
\[
(0,1)\in f^{-1}(U).
\]
Every Euclidean open ball around $(0,1)$ contains points with first coordinate nonzero.
No such point belongs to
\[
\{0\}\times(1/2,3/2).
\]
Thus $(0,1)$ has no Euclidean-open neighborhood contained in $f^{-1}(U)$, so the preimage is not open.
:::

<1>4. The set $U$ is not open in the quotient topology $\mathcal T_2$.
::: {.proof}
By definition of the quotient topology induced by the surjection $f:X\to C$,
\[
V\in\mathcal T_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f^{-1}(V)\text{ is open in }X.
\]
For $V=U$, the preimage is not open by <1>3.
Therefore
\[
U\notin\mathcal T_2.
\]
:::

<1>5. Hence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal T_1\ne\mathcal T_2.}
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
U\in\mathcal T_1,
\]
while by <1>4,
\[
U\notin\mathcal T_2.
\]
Thus the two topologies cannot be equal.
:::
:::
