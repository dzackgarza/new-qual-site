::: {.problem}
If $X$ is a $\Delta$-complex with at most one $k$-simplex for each $0\le k\le5$, show that each of the homology groups $H_k(X)$ must be cyclic for all $0\le k\le5$.
Can all of these groups be non-trivial?
Explain why or why not.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
\cdots\longrightarrow C_{k+1}(X)
\overset{\partial_{k+1}}{\longrightarrow}
C_k(X)
\overset{\partial_k}{\longrightarrow}
C_{k-1}(X)
\longrightarrow\cdots
\]
be the simplicial chain complex of the $\Delta$-complex.

<1>1. For every $0\le k\le5$, the chain group $C_k(X)$ is cyclic.
::: {.proof}
The simplicial chain group $C_k(X)$ is the free abelian group on the $k$-simplices of $X$.
There is at most one such simplex by hypothesis.
Hence
\[
C_k(X)\cong0
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
C_k(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
In either case it is cyclic.
:::

<1>2. For every $0\le k\le5$, the cycle group
\[
Z_k(X)=\ker\partial_k
\]
is cyclic.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $C_k(X)$ is cyclic.
Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic, and
\[
Z_k(X)\le C_k(X).
\]
Thus $Z_k(X)$ is cyclic.
:::

<1>3. For every $0\le k\le5$, the homology group
\[
H_k(X)=Z_k(X)/B_k(X)
\]
is cyclic.
::: {.proof}
The boundary group
\[
B_k(X)=\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}
\]
is a subgroup of $Z_k(X)$ because $\partial_k\partial_{k+1}=0$.
By <1>2, $Z_k(X)$ is cyclic.
Every quotient of a cyclic group by a subgroup is cyclic.
Therefore $H_k(X)$ is cyclic for each $0\le k\le5$.
This argument also covers $k=5$ even if $X$ has simplices in dimensions greater than $5$, since only the inclusion
\[
B_5(X)\le Z_5(X)
\]
is used.
:::

<1>4. If $X$ has no $2$-simplex, then
\[
H_2(X)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
In this case
\[
C_2(X)=0,
\]
so
\[
Z_2(X)=\ker\partial_2=0.
\]
Hence $H_2(X)=0$.
:::

<1>5. If $X$ has a $2$-simplex, then
\[
\partial_2:C_2(X)\longrightarrow C_1(X)
\]
is injective.
::: {.proof}
Let $\sigma$ be the unique $2$-simplex.
Each of its three $1$-dimensional faces is a $1$-simplex of the $\Delta$-complex.
Since there is at most one $1$-simplex, there is exactly one edge $e$, and every face of $\sigma$ is identified with $e$, possibly with either orientation.

Choose an orientation of $e$.
In the simplicial boundary of $\sigma$, each of its three faces therefore contributes either $+e$ or $-e$.
Thus
\[
\partial_2(\sigma)
=(\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)e,
\qquad
\varepsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
The sum of three numbers each equal to $\pm1$ is odd, so it cannot be zero.
Hence
\[
\partial_2(\sigma)\ne0.
\]
Since
\[
C_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z\langle\sigma\rangle
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
C_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z\langle e\rangle,
\]
the map $\partial_2$ is multiplication by a nonzero integer and is therefore injective.
:::

<1>6. In fact
\[
H_2(X)=0
\]
for every $X$ satisfying the hypothesis.
::: {.proof}
If there is no $2$-simplex, use <1>4. If there is a $2$-simplex, <1>5 gives
\[
Z_2(X)=\ker\partial_2=0,
\]
so again $H_2(X)=0$.
Thus these are the only two cases, and both give trivial second homology.
:::

<1>7. Consequently, the groups
\[
H_0(X),H_1(X),\ldots,H_5(X)
\]
cannot all be nontrivial.
::: {.proof}
By <1>6, the second homology group is always trivial:
\[
H_2(X)=0.
\]
Therefore simultaneous nontriviality of all six groups is impossible.
:::
:::
