::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be a graph with five vertices $a_0,a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2$ and six edges $e_{ij}$ for $i\in\{0,1,2\}$ and $j\in\{1,2\}$, where $e_{ij}$ has endpoints $a_i$ and $b_j$.
Let $X$ be the CW complex obtained from $K$ by attaching a $2$-cell along each loop formed by a cycle of four distinct edges in $K$.
Find $\pi_1(X)$ and show that $\pi_1(X)$ is isomorphic to a familiar group.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
T=\{e_{01},e_{02},e_{11},e_{21}\}\subseteq K.
\]

<1>1. The subgraph $T$ is a spanning tree of $K$.
::: {.proof}
The graph $T$ contains all five vertices.
It is connected: both $b_1$ and $b_2$ are joined to $a_0$, while $a_1$ and $a_2$ are joined to $b_1$.

It has five vertices and four edges.
A connected graph with $v$ vertices and $v-1$ edges is a tree: if it contained a cycle, deleting one edge of that cycle would leave it connected with at most $v-2$ edges, whereas every connected graph on $v$ vertices has at least $v-1$ edges.
Hence $T$ is a spanning tree.
:::

Orient the two edges not in $T$ by
\[
x=e_{12}:a_1\longrightarrow b_2,
\qquad
y=e_{22}:a_2\longrightarrow b_2.
\]

<1>2. Collapsing $T$ to a point turns $K$ into a wedge of two circles with loop edges $x$ and $y$; consequently
\[
\pi_1(K)\cong F(x,y).
\]
::: {.proof}
A spanning tree in a connected graph is a contractible subcomplex.
Collapsing it identifies all vertices to one point and leaves precisely the two non-tree edges as loop edges.
Thus
\[
K/T\cong S^1\vee S^1.
\]

Equivalently, the standard maximal-tree presentation for a graph gives one free generator for each edge outside the tree and no relators.
Hence
\[
\pi_1(K)\cong\langle x,y\mid\ \rangle=F(x,y).
\]
:::

<1>3. The graph $K$ has exactly three cycles formed by four distinct edges.
::: {.proof}
The graph is the complete bipartite graph
\[
K_{3,2}
\]
with bipartition
\[
\{a_0,a_1,a_2\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\{b_1,b_2\}.
\]
Every cycle in a bipartite graph alternates between the two parts.
A four-cycle must therefore use both $b_1,b_2$ and exactly two distinct vertices among $a_0,a_1,a_2$.
Conversely, every choice of two $a$-vertices gives one four-cycle using all four connecting edges.

There are
\[
\binom32=3
\]
such choices, namely the pairs
\[
\{a_0,a_1\},
\qquad
\{a_0,a_2\},
\qquad
\{a_1,a_2\}.
\]
:::

<1>4. After collapsing the spanning tree $T$, the four-cycle through $a_0,a_1$ has attaching word $x$.
::: {.proof}
Orient the cycle as
\[
a_0\to b_1\to a_1\to b_2\to a_0.
\]
The first, second, and fourth edges lie in $T$ and therefore become constant after the tree collapse.
The third edge is
\[
a_1\to b_2=x.
\]
Hence the collapsed loop is precisely $x$.
:::

<1>5. After collapsing $T$, the four-cycle through $a_0,a_2$ has attaching word $y$.
::: {.proof}
Orient it as
\[
a_0\to b_1\to a_2\to b_2\to a_0.
\]
Again all edges except
\[
a_2\to b_2=y
\]
belong to $T$.
Thus the attaching word is $y$.
:::

<1>6. After collapsing $T$, the four-cycle through $a_1,a_2$ has attaching word
\[
yx^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Orient this cycle as
\[
a_1\to b_1\to a_2\to b_2\to a_1.
\]
The first two edges lie in $T$ and collapse to the basepoint.
The third is
\[
a_2\to b_2=y,
\]
and the last is
\[
b_2\to a_1=x^{-1}.
\]
Therefore the collapsed word is
\[
yx^{-1}.
\]
:::

<1>7. The Seifert--van Kampen theorem gives
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle x,y\mid x=1,\ y=1,\ yx^{-1}=1\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, the $1$-skeleton has fundamental group freely generated by $x,y$.
Attaching a $2$-cell along a loop quotients the fundamental group by the normal closure of that loop class.
There is one attached $2$-cell for each of the three four-cycles in <1>3, and their words are those computed in <1>4--<1>6.
Thus van Kampen gives the displayed presentation.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X)=1.}
\]
::: {.proof}
The first two relators in <1>7 already imply
\[
x=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y=1.
\]
Hence every word in the generators represents the identity.
The third relator is then automatic.
Thus the presented group has one element, so $\pi_1(X)$ is isomorphic to the familiar trivial group.
:::
