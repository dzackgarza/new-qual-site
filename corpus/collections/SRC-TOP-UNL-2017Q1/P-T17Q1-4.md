::: {.problem}
A continuous function $f:X\to Y$ is called *proper* if for every compact subset $C$ of $Y$, the preimage $f^{-1}(C)$ is compact in $X$.
Show that if $X$ and $Y$ are compact Hausdorff spaces and $f:X\to Y$ is a proper continuous function, then $f$ is a closed map.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every closed subset of the compact space $X$ is compact.
::: {.proof}
Let $A\subseteq X$ be closed and let $\mathcal U$ be an open cover of $A$.
Then
\[
\mathcal U\cup\{X\setminus A\}
\]
is an open cover of $X$.
Compactness of $X$ gives a finite subcover.
Discarding $X\setminus A$ if it occurs leaves finitely many members of $\mathcal U$ covering $A$.
Thus $A$ is compact.
:::

<1>2. If $A\subseteq X$ is compact, then $f(A)$ is compact in $Y$.
::: {.proof}
Let $\mathcal V$ be an open cover of $f(A)$.
Because $f$ is continuous, the family
\[
\{f^{-1}(V):V\in\mathcal V\}
\]
is an open cover of $A$.
Choose a finite subcover
\[
f^{-1}(V_1),\ldots,f^{-1}(V_n).
\]
Then
\[
V_1,\ldots,V_n
\]
cover $f(A)$.
Hence $f(A)$ is compact.
:::

<1>3. Every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed.
::: {.proof}
Let $K$ be compact in a Hausdorff space $Y$, and let $y\in Y\setminus K$.
For each $x\in K$, Hausdorffness gives disjoint open sets
\[
U_x\ni x,
\qquad
V_x\ni y.
\]
The sets $U_x$ cover $K$, so compactness gives
\[
K\subseteq U_{x_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{x_n}.
\]
Put
\[
V=V_{x_1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{x_n}.
\]
Then $V$ is an open neighborhood of $y$ and is disjoint from every $U_{x_i}$, hence from $K$.
Thus every point of $Y\setminus K$ has an open neighborhood contained in $Y\setminus K$.
Therefore $Y\setminus K$ is open and $K$ is closed.
:::

<1>4. The map $f:X\to Y$ is closed.
::: {.proof}
Let $A\subseteq X$ be closed.
By <1>1, $A$ is compact.
By <1>2, $f(A)$ is compact in $Y$.
By <1>3 and the Hausdorff hypothesis on $Y$, $f(A)$ is closed in $Y$.

Since this holds for every closed subset $A$ of $X$, the map $f$ is closed.
:::

<1>5. The proof does not require properness.
::: {.proof}
The argument in <1>1--<1>4 used only compactness of $X$, Hausdorffness of $Y$, and continuity of $f$.
Thus the stated conclusion follows a fortiori for a proper continuous map.
:::
:::
