::: {.problem}
Let $\lambda$ be a fixed complex number with $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)>1$.
How many solutions does the equation
$$
e^z=z+\lambda
$$
have in the left half-plane $\{z\in\CC:\operatorname{Re}(z)<0\}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.
Write $F(z)\coloneqq z+\lambda-e^z$ and $D\coloneqq\{z:\abs{z+\lambda}<1\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #location}
Every zero of $F$ in the open left half-plane lies in $D$, and $D$ itself lies in the open left half-plane.

::: pf-proof
If $F(z)=0$ then $\abs{z+\lambda}=\abs{e^z}=e^{\operatorname{Re}z}$, which is less than $1$ when $\operatorname{Re}z<0$; so such a zero lies in $D$. Conversely, for any $z$ one has $\operatorname{Re}z=\operatorname{Re}(z+\lambda)-\operatorname{Re}\lambda\le\abs{z+\lambda}-\operatorname{Re}\lambda$, so $z\in D$ gives $\operatorname{Re}z<1-\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0$ because $\operatorname{Re}\lambda>1$. Hence the zeros of $F$ in the left half-plane are exactly the zeros of $F$ in $D$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boundary}
On the circle $\abs{z+\lambda}=1$ one has $\abs{e^z}<\abs{z+\lambda}$.

::: pf-proof
There $\operatorname{Re}z\le\abs{z+\lambda}-\operatorname{Re}\lambda=1-\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0$, so $\abs{e^z}=e^{\operatorname{Re}z}\le e^{1-\operatorname{Re}\lambda}<1=\abs{z+\lambda}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rouche}
$F$ and $z\mapsto z+\lambda$ have the same number of zeros in $D$, counted with multiplicity, namely one.

::: pf-proof
Both are entire, and step [](#boundary){.pf-ref} shows the summand $-e^z=F-(z+\lambda)$ is dominated in modulus by $z+\lambda$ on the boundary circle, which is where $z+\lambda$ vanishes not at all. Rouché's theorem gives equal zero counts in $D$; $z+\lambda$ has the single zero $-\lambda$, of multiplicity one.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #simple}
The zero of $F$ is simple.

::: pf-proof
$F'(z)=1-e^z$. At a zero $z\in D$ one has $\abs{e^z}=\abs{z+\lambda}<1$, so $e^z\ne1$ and $F'(z)\ne0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The equation $e^z=z+\lambda$ has exactly $\boxed{1}$ solution in the left half-plane, and it is a simple zero of $z+\lambda-e^z$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#location){.pf-ref} the solutions in the left half-plane are the zeros of $F$ in $D$, and step [](#rouche){.pf-ref} counts exactly one of them, with multiplicity, which step [](#simple){.pf-ref} shows is one.
:::
