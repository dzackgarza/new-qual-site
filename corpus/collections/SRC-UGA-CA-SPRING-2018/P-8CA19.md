::: {.problem}
Show that
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\log x}{1+x^4}\,dx=-\frac{\pi^2}{8\sqrt{2}}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For $0<a<4$, a standard keyhole-contour computation gives
\[
I(a):=\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x^4}\,dx
=\frac\pi4\csc\frac{\pi a}{4}.
\]
Indeed, integrate $z^{a-1}/(1+z^4)$ around a keyhole contour with branch
$0<\arg z<2\pi$; the jump across the positive axis is
$1-e^{2\pi i(a-1)}$, and summing the residues at the four fourth roots of
$-1$ yields the displayed formula.

Differentiation under the integral sign is valid near $a=1$ by dominated
convergence, and therefore
\[
I'(1)=\int_0^\infty\frac{\log x}{1+x^4}\,dx.
\]
Differentiating the closed form,
\[
I'(a)=-\frac{\pi^2}{16}
\csc\frac{\pi a}{4}\cot\frac{\pi a}{4}.
\]
At $a=1$ this gives
\[
I'(1)=-\frac{\pi^2}{16}\sqrt2
=-\frac{\pi^2}{8\sqrt2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\int_0^\infty\frac{\log x}{1+x^4}\,dx
=-\frac{\pi^2}{8\sqrt2}.}
\]
:::
