::: {.problem}
Give a precise definition of the Borel sets on the real line.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this question; the answer is the card's own. For a family $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $\RR$, write $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$ for the intersection of all $\sigma$-algebras on $\RR$ containing $\mathcal{A}$, and $\mathcal{O}$ for the family of open subsets of $\RR$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #exists}
$\sigma(\mathcal{O})$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{O}$, and it is contained in every $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{O}$.

::: pf-proof
The family of $\sigma$-algebras on $\RR$ containing $\mathcal{O}$ is nonempty, since the power set $\mathcal{P}(\RR)$ is one. An intersection of $\sigma$-algebras is a $\sigma$-algebra: it contains $\varnothing$ and $\RR$ because each member does; it is closed under complement because each member is; and a countable union of its members lies in each member, hence in the intersection. It contains $\mathcal{O}$ because each member does. By construction it is contained in every $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{O}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #contains-intervals}
Every open interval and every closed set belongs to $\sigma(\mathcal{O})$.

::: pf-proof
An open interval is open, hence a member of $\mathcal{O}\subset\sigma(\mathcal{O})$. A closed set is the complement of an open set, and $F_{\sigma}$ and $G_{\delta}$ sets are countable unions and intersections of these, all within $\sigma(\mathcal{O})$ by the defining closure properties.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #open-is-countable-union}
Every open set is a countable union of open intervals with rational endpoints.

::: pf-proof
Let $U$ be open. The family $\mathcal{J}=\theset{(p,q)\mid p,q\in\QQ,\ (p,q)\subset U}$ is countable, since $\QQ^{2}$ is. Each member lies in $U$, so $\bigcup\mathcal{J}\subset U$. Conversely, given $x\in U$, openness gives $\varepsilon>0$ with $(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)\subset U$; choosing rationals $p,q$ with $x-\varepsilon<p<x<q<x+\varepsilon$ gives $x\in(p,q)\in\mathcal{J}$ and $(p,q)\subset(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)\subset U$. So $x\in\bigcup\mathcal{J}$ and $U=\bigcup\mathcal{J}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equivalence}
$\sigma(\mathcal{O})$ is also the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing every open interval.

::: pf-proof
Write $\mathcal{I}$ for the family of open intervals. Since $\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}$, every $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{O}$ contains $\mathcal{I}$, so $\sigma(\mathcal{I})\subset\sigma(\mathcal{O})$. Conversely, by step [](#open-is-countable-union){.pf-ref} each $U\in\mathcal{O}$ is a countable union of members of $\mathcal{I}$, hence lies in any $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{I}$; so $\mathcal{O}\subset\sigma(\mathcal{I})$ and therefore $\sigma(\mathcal{O})\subset\sigma(\mathcal{I})$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$\boxed{\mathcal{B}(\RR)=\sigma(\mathcal{O})}$, and $E\subseteq\RR$ is a Borel set exactly when $E\in\mathcal{B}(\RR)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#exists){.pf-ref} the family $\sigma(\mathcal{O})$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing the open subsets of $\RR$, and the definition of the Borel $\sigma$-algebra on the real line is that family; step [](#equivalence){.pf-ref} shows it is equally the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing the open intervals, which is the form the answer also uses. A subset of $\RR$ is called a Borel set exactly when it belongs to that $\sigma$-algebra, and by steps [](#contains-intervals){.pf-ref} and [](#open-is-countable-union){.pf-ref} these are the sets obtained from the open sets by countably many complements and countable unions and intersections.
:::
