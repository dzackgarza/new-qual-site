::: {.problem}
Let $H = \{ z \in \CC^{*} \st \abs{z} = 1 \}$. Prove that $\CC^{*}/H$ is isomorphic to $\RR^{+}$, the group of positive real numbers under multiplication.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is this collection's outline, written out; the statement is the one the outline records for Chapter 10 Exercise 37.

Write $\CC^{*}=\CC\setminus\{0\}$ and $\RR^{+}=(0,\infty)$, both groups under multiplication, and $H=\{z\in\CC^{*}\mid \abs{z}=1\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #well-defined}
$\phi(z)=\abs{z}$ defines a map $\CC^{*}\to\RR^{+}$.

::: pf-proof
For $z=a+bi\neq0$ we have $\abs{z}=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}>0$ because $a^{2}+b^{2}>0$; so the value is a positive real number, and it depends only on $z$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiplicative}
$\abs{zw}=\abs{z}\abs{w}$ for $z,w\in\CC^{*}$, so $\phi$ is a homomorphism.

::: pf-proof
Write $z=a+bi$, $w=c+di$; then $zw=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i$ and
$$
\abs{zw}^{2}=(ac-bd)^{2}+(ad+bc)^{2}=a^{2}c^{2}+b^{2}d^{2}+a^{2}d^{2}+b^{2}c^{2}=(a^{2}+b^{2})(c^{2}+d^{2})=\abs{z}^{2}\abs{w}^{2},
$$
the middle step by expanding and cancelling $-2abcd$ against $+2abcd$. Since all moduli are positive, taking positive square roots gives $\abs{zw}=\abs{z}\abs{w}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #surjective}
$\phi$ is surjective.

::: pf-proof
For $r\in\RR^{+}$ the complex number $r=r+0i$ lies in $\CC^{*}$ and $\phi(r)=\abs{r}=r$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #kernel}
$\ker\phi=H$.

::: pf-proof
$\phi(z)=1$ says $\abs{z}=1$, which is the definition of $H$; and every $z\in H$ satisfies $\phi(z)=1$. Note that $H$ is a subgroup of $\CC^{*}$: it is the kernel of a homomorphism, and also closed under multiplication and inversion because $\abs{zw}=\abs{z}\abs{w}$ and $\abs{z^{-1}}=\abs{z}^{-1}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\CC^{*}/H\cong\RR^{+}$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#well-defined){.pf-ref}, [](#multiplicative){.pf-ref} and [](#kernel){.pf-ref}, $\phi$ is a homomorphism of groups with kernel $H$, so the first isomorphism theorem gives an isomorphism $\CC^{*}/H\to\operatorname{im}\phi$; by step [](#surjective){.pf-ref} the image is $\RR^{+}$.
:::
