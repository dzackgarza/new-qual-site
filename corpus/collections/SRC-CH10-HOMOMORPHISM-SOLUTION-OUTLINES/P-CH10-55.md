::: {.problem}
Let $\ZZ[x]$ be the group of polynomials in $x$ with integer coefficients under addition.
Prove that the mapping from $\ZZ[x]$ into $\ZZ$ given by $f(x) \mapsto f(3)$ is a homomorphism.
Give a geometric description of the kernel of this homomorphism.
Generalize.
:::

::: {.solution}
The book's own answer is **Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra* (7th ed.), *Selected Answers*, Chapter 10 Exercise 53, printed p. A15** (`tmp/sources/Gallian-Contemporary-Abstract-Algebra-7e.pdf`, PDF p. 604): "$(f+g)(3)=f(3)+g(3)$. The kernel is the set of elements in $\ZZ[x]$ whose graphs pass through the point $(3,0)$." The steps below prove that, identify the kernel as a principal ideal, and give the generalization the exercise asks for. Write $\ZZ[x]$ additively and $\operatorname{ev}_{3}(f)=f(3)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #additive}
$\operatorname{ev}_{3}:\ZZ[x]\to\ZZ$ is a homomorphism of additive groups.

::: pf-proof
For $f,g\in\ZZ[x]$ the sum $f+g$ is the polynomial with $(f+g)(x)=f(x)+g(x)$, so $\operatorname{ev}_{3}(f+g)=(f+g)(3)=f(3)+g(3)=\operatorname{ev}_{3}(f)+\operatorname{ev}_{3}(g)$. Also $\operatorname{ev}_{3}(0)=0$. Hence $\operatorname{ev}_{3}$ preserves the group operation of $\ZZ[x]$ under addition.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #kernel-ideal}
$\ker\operatorname{ev}_{3}=\theset{f\in\ZZ[x]\mid f(3)=0}$ is the principal ideal $(x-3)\ZZ[x]$.

::: pf-proof
The kernel is a subgroup by step [](#additive){.pf-ref}. Since $\ZZ[x]$ is commutative, it is in fact an ideal, and the factor theorem for the ring $\ZZ[x]$ says $f(3)=0$ exactly when $x-3$ divides $f$ in $\ZZ[x]$: dividing $f$ by the monic polynomial $x-3$ gives $f=(x-3)q+r$ with $r\in\ZZ$ constant, and evaluating at $3$ gives $r=f(3)$. So $\ker\operatorname{ev}_{3}=\theset{(x-3)q\mid q\in\ZZ[x]}=(x-3)\ZZ[x]$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #geometric}
Geometrically, $\ker\operatorname{ev}_{3}$ is the set of polynomials whose graph passes through $(3,0)$.

::: pf-proof
The graph of $f$ passes through $(3,0)$ exactly when $f(3)=0$, which is the defining condition of the kernel; by step [](#kernel-ideal){.pf-ref} these are exactly the polynomials divisible by $x-3$, so the kernel consists of the polynomials with a factor vanishing at $3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #onto}
$\operatorname{ev}_{3}$ is onto $\ZZ$, so $\ZZ[x]/(x-3)\cong\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
For $n\in\ZZ$ the constant polynomial $n$ has $\operatorname{ev}_{3}(n)=n$, so the image is all of $\ZZ$. The first isomorphism theorem for additive groups gives $\ZZ[x]/\ker\operatorname{ev}_{3}\cong\ZZ$, and step [](#kernel-ideal){.pf-ref} identifies the kernel.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #general}
The argument generalizes: for any commutative ring $R$ and any $r\in R$, evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_{r}:\ZZ[x]\to R$, $f\mapsto f(r)$, is a ring homomorphism, and over $\ZZ[x]$ evaluation at an integer $a$ gives a group homomorphism with kernel $(x-a)\ZZ[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Evaluation is additive and multiplicative, $\operatorname{ev}_{r}(f+g)=f(r)+g(r)$ and $\operatorname{ev}_{r}(fg)=f(r)g(r)$, since $R$ is commutative, and $\operatorname{ev}_{r}(1)=1$; so it is a ring homomorphism, hence in particular a homomorphism of additive groups. With $R=\ZZ$ and $r=a\in\ZZ$ the factor theorem applied to $x-a$, which is monic, gives kernel $(x-a)\ZZ[x]$ exactly as in step [](#kernel-ideal){.pf-ref}, and the same argument at $a=3$ is the exercise. The book's answer records the case $a=3$ only.
:::
