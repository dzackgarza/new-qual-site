::: {.problem}
Suppose that $\ZZ_{10}$ and $\ZZ_{15}$ are both homomorphic images of a finite group $G$. What can be said about $\cardinality{G}$?
Generalize.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained solution outline for this chapter, **Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra*, seventh edition, Chapter 10 solution outlines, exercise 48, PDF p. 3** (`assets/attachments/Ch10Sltns.pdf`), restructured into steps; the outline's two one-line assertions are proved here, and its generalisation is stated with the hypothesis it needs.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #divides}
If $\overline G$ is a homomorphic image of a finite group $G$ then $\cardinality{\overline G}$ divides $\cardinality{G}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\phi:G\to\overline G$ be a surjective homomorphism. By the first isomorphism theorem $G/\ker\phi\cong\overline G$, so $\cardinality{G}=\cardinality{\ker\phi}\cdot\cardinality{\overline G}$ by Lagrange, and hence $\cardinality{\overline G}$ divides $\cardinality{G}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ten}
$10$ divides $\cardinality{G}$ and $15$ divides $\cardinality{G}$.

::: pf-proof
$\ZZ_{10}$ and $\ZZ_{15}$ are homomorphic images of $G$ by hypothesis, and their orders are $10$ and $15$; apply step [](#divides){.pf-ref} to each.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #thirty}
$30$ divides $\cardinality{G}$.

::: pf-proof
A number divisible by $10=2\cdot5$ and by $15=3\cdot5$ is divisible by $2\cdot3\cdot5=30$: the prime $5$ contributes exponent at least $1$ and the primes $2$ and $3$ each appear in one of the two divisors. Hence $30\mid\cardinality{G}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #general}
If $G$ has homomorphic images of orders $n_{1},\dots,n_{k}$ then $\operatorname{lcm}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})$ divides $\cardinality{G}$, and no larger conclusion follows.

::: pf-proof
Each $n_{i}$ divides $\cardinality{G}$ by step [](#divides){.pf-ref}, and the least common multiple of a family of divisors of $N$ divides $N$. That this is the most that follows is shown by $G=\ZZ_{30}$ itself, which maps onto $\ZZ_{10}$ and onto $\ZZ_{15}$ and has order exactly $\operatorname{lcm}(10,15)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\cardinality{G}$ is divisible by $30$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#thirty){.pf-ref} and [](#general){.pf-ref} give
$$
\boxed{30\mid\cardinality{G};\qquad\text{in general }\operatorname{lcm}\text{ of the orders of the homomorphic images divides }\cardinality{G}.}
$$
Note the direction: the order of $G$ is a multiple of $30$, not necessarily equal to it, and nothing is claimed about which groups of order divisible by $30$ have $\ZZ_{10}$ and $\ZZ_{15}$ as images.
:::
