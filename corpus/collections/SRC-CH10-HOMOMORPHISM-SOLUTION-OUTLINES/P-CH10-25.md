::: {.problem}
How many homomorphisms are there from $\ZZ_{20}$ onto $\ZZ_{10}$? How many are there to $\ZZ_{10}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained solution outline for this chapter, **Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra*, seventh edition, Chapter 10 solution outlines, exercise 25, PDF p. 2** (`assets/attachments/Ch10Sltns.pdf`), restructured into steps; the outline quotes the two facts about $\phi(1)$ that steps [](#determined){.pf-ref} and [](#image){.pf-ref} prove.

Throughout, $\ZZ_n$ is written additively with residue classes $0,1,\dots,n-1$, and for $k\in\ZZ_{10}$ the map $\phi_k:\ZZ_{20}\to\ZZ_{10}$ is $\phi_k(x)=kx \pmod{10}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #determined}
Every homomorphism $\phi:\ZZ_{20}\to\ZZ_{10}$ is $\phi_k$ for $k=\phi(1)$, and conversely every $\phi_k$ is a well-defined homomorphism; so homomorphisms $\ZZ_{20}\to\ZZ_{10}$ correspond bijectively to the ten elements $k\in\ZZ_{10}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\phi$ be a homomorphism and put $k=\phi(1)$. Since $\ZZ_{20}$ is generated by $1$, every $x\in\ZZ_{20}$ is $x\cdot1$ in the additive notation, and additivity of $\phi$ gives $\phi(x)=x\phi(1)=kx$; so $\phi=\phi_k$, and $\phi$ is determined by $k$.

Conversely, fix $k\in\ZZ_{10}$. If $x\equiv x'\pmod{20}$ then $x-x'=20t$ for some $t\in\ZZ$, so $k(x-x')=20kt=10(2kt)$ is divisible by $10$ and $kx=kx'$ in $\ZZ_{10}$; hence $\phi_k$ is well defined. It is additive because $\phi_k(x+y)=k(x+y)=kx+ky=\phi_k(x)+\phi_k(y)$.

The two constructions are inverse: the $k$ attached to $\phi_k$ is $\phi_k(1)=k$, and the map attached to $k=\phi(1)$ is $\phi$ itself. So $k\mapsto\phi_k$ is a bijection $\ZZ_{10}\to\Hom(\ZZ_{20},\ZZ_{10})$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #image}
For $k\in\ZZ_{10}$ with $d=\gcd(k,10)$, the image of $\phi_k$ is the cyclic subgroup $\langle k\rangle$ of $\ZZ_{10}$, and it consists of the $10/d$ residues $0,d,2d,\dots,(10/d-1)d$.

::: pf-proof
The image is $\{kx : x\in\ZZ_{20}\}=\{kx : x\in\ZZ\}$, which is by definition the subgroup generated by $k$.

Since $d$ divides $k$ and $10$, every element $kx$ of the image is a multiple of $d$ modulo $10$. Conversely, Bézout gives $u,v\in\ZZ$ with $uk+10v=d$, so $d\equiv uk\pmod{10}$ lies in the image; a subgroup containing $d$ contains all its multiples. Hence the image is $\{0,d,2d,\dots,(10/d-1)d\}$, whose $10/d$ displayed members are distinct because the residues $0,d,\dots,10-d$ lie in $[0,10)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #onto}
$\phi_k$ is onto if and only if $\gcd(k,10)=1$, which for $k\in\ZZ_{10}$ means $k\in\{1,3,7,9\}$; so exactly $4$ of the homomorphisms are onto.

::: pf-proof
Surjectivity says the image has $10$ elements, which by step [](#image){.pf-ref} says $10/d=10$, that is $d=\gcd(k,10)=1$. Among $0,1,\dots,9$ the residues coprime to $10$ are $1,3,7,9$: an even residue shares the factor $2$, the residue $5$ shares $5$, and $0$ is not coprime to $10$; each of $1,3,7,9$ is odd and not a multiple of $5$, so its only common divisor with $10$ is $1$. That gives four onto homomorphisms. Surjectivity is not excluded by the orders: $10$ divides $20$, so a homomorphism $\ZZ_{20}\to\ZZ_{10}$ can have image of order $10$, and $\phi_1$ does.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answers}
There are $10$ homomorphisms $\ZZ_{20}\to\ZZ_{10}$, and $4$ of them are onto.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#determined){.pf-ref} the homomorphisms are in bijection with the ten residues $k\in\ZZ_{10}$, so there are $10$ of them, which answers the second question. By step [](#onto){.pf-ref} exactly four of those $k$ give a surjection, which answers the first. The outline's observation that the order of $\phi(1)$ divides both $10$ and $20$ is the reason no $k$ is lost: every element of $\ZZ_{10}$ has order dividing $10$, and $10$ divides $20$.
:::
