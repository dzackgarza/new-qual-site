::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group of permutations.
For each $\sigma$ in $G$, define
$$
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) =
\begin{cases}
+1 & \text{if } \sigma \text{ is an even permutation,} \\
-1 & \text{if } \sigma \text{ is an odd permutation.}
\end{cases}
$$

Prove that $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)$ is a homomorphism from $G$ to the multiplicative group $\{+1, -1\}$.
What is the kernel?
Why does this homomorphism allow you to conclude that $A_n$ is a normal subgroup of $S_n$ of index 2?
Why does this prove Exercise 23 of Chapter 5?
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained solution outline for this chapter, **Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra*, seventh edition, Chapter 10 solution outlines, exercise 8, PDF p. 1** (`assets/attachments/Ch10Sltns.pdf`), restructured into steps. The two facts about parity that the outline uses are proved here as steps, since the outline cites them without proof: a product of two permutations of the same parity is even, and a product of permutations of opposite parity is odd.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #parity}
Parity is a homomorphism-like invariant: for permutations $\sigma,\tau$, the product $\sigma\tau$ is even if $\sigma$ and $\tau$ have the same parity and odd if they do not.

::: pf-proof
Write a permutation as a product of transpositions; its parity is the parity of the number of factors, and this is well defined (the sign of a permutation). If $\sigma$ is a product of $r$ transpositions and $\tau$ of $s$, then $\sigma\tau$ is a product of $r+s$ transpositions, whose parity is that of $r+s$: even when $r,s$ have the same parity, odd otherwise.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hom}
$\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma\tau)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)$ for all $\sigma,\tau\in G$.

::: pf-proof
Three cases, by step [](#parity){.pf-ref}. If both are odd, $\sigma\tau$ is even, so $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma\tau)=1=(-1)(-1)$. If both are even, $\sigma\tau$ is even, so $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma\tau)=1=(1)(1)$. If one is even and the other odd, $\sigma\tau$ is odd, so $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma\tau)=-1=1\cdot(-1)=(-1)\cdot1$. So $\operatorname{sgn}$ is a homomorphism $G\to\{+1,-1\}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #kernel}
$\ker\operatorname{sgn}$ is the set of even permutations in $G$, a subgroup of $G$.

::: pf-proof
$\ker\operatorname{sgn}=\{\sigma:\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=1\}$ is by definition the set of even permutations of $G$; it is a subgroup as the kernel of a homomorphism, and it is normal because kernels of homomorphisms are normal subgroups.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #an}
For $G=S_{n}$: $A_{n}$ is a normal subgroup of $S_{n}$ of index $2$.

::: pf-proof
$\ker\operatorname{sgn}=A_{n}$ by step [](#kernel){.pf-ref}, so $A_{n}\trianglelefteq S_{n}$. By the first isomorphism theorem $S_{n}/A_{n}\cong\operatorname{im}\operatorname{sgn}$, and $\operatorname{sgn}$ is onto $\{+1,-1\}$ because $S_{n}$ contains an odd permutation for $n\ge2$; hence $\cardinality{S_{n}/A_{n}}=2$, that is $[S_{n}:A_{n}]=2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ex23}
Every subgroup $H\le S_{n}$ consists either entirely of even permutations or of exactly half even and half odd permutations.

::: pf-proof
Restrict $\operatorname{sgn}$ to $H$. Its image is a subgroup of $\{+1,-1\}$, so it is $\{1\}$ or $\{1,-1\}$. In the first case every element of $H$ is even. In the second case $\ker(\operatorname{sgn}\restriction_{H})=H\cap A_{n}$ has index $2$ in $H$ by the first isomorphism theorem, so $H$ is the disjoint union of the two cosets of $H\cap A_{n}$, which have equal size; the elements of $H\cap A_{n}$ are the even permutations of $H$ and those of the other coset are the odd ones. So $H$ has exactly half of each.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
All four questions are answered.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#hom){.pf-ref}, [](#kernel){.pf-ref}, [](#an){.pf-ref} and [](#ex23){.pf-ref} give
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{sgn}\text{ is a homomorphism};\quad \ker\operatorname{sgn}=\{\text{even permutations of }G\};\quad A_{n}\trianglelefteq S_{n},\ [S_{n}:A_{n}]=2;\quad\text{Exercise 23 of Chapter 5 follows}.}
$$
:::
