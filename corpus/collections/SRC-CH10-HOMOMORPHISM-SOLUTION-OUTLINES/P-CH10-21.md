::: {.problem}
If $\phi$ is a homomorphism from $\ZZ_{30}$ onto a group of order $5$, determine the kernel of $\phi$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The book prints a selected answer to this exercise — **Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra* (7th ed.), *Selected Answers*, printed p. A14** (`tmp/sources/Gallian-Contemporary-Abstract-Algebra-7e.pdf`, PDF p. 603): "$\langle 5\rangle$". The steps below derive it, since the answer alone does not show why no other subgroup of order $6$ occurs. Write the domain additively as $\ZZ_{30}$, its identity as $0$, and the target group multiplicatively with identity $e$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #kernel-order}
$\cardinality{\ker\phi}=6$.

::: pf-proof
By the first isomorphism theorem $\ZZ_{30}/\ker\phi\cong\operatorname{im}\phi$, and $\phi$ is onto, so $\operatorname{im}\phi$ is the given group of order $5$. Lagrange's theorem applied to the quotient gives $\cardinality{\ZZ_{30}}=\cardinality{\ker\phi}\cdot\cardinality{\ZZ_{30}/\ker\phi}$, that is $30=\cardinality{\ker\phi}\cdot 5$, so $\cardinality{\ker\phi}=6$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #five-subgroup}
$\langle 5\rangle=\theset{0,5,10,15,20,25}$ is a subgroup of $\ZZ_{30}$ of order $6$.

::: pf-proof
The six listed residues are distinct, and they are the multiples of $5$ modulo $30$. The sum of two multiples of $5$ is a multiple of $5$, and $0$ is listed, so the set is a subgroup; $5\cdot 6=30\equiv0$ shows the order divides $6$ and the six distinct elements show it is $6$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #kernel-is-five}
$\ker\phi=\langle 5\rangle$.

::: pf-proof
A subgroup of the cyclic group $\ZZ_{30}$ is cyclic, so $\ker\phi=\langle a\rangle$ for some $a$, and the order of $\langle a\rangle$ is $30/\gcd(30,a)$. Step [](#kernel-order){.pf-ref} makes that order $6$, hence $\gcd(30,a)=5$, and therefore $\langle a\rangle=\langle\gcd(30,a)\rangle=\langle 5\rangle$, the subgroup of step [](#five-subgroup){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$\boxed{\ker\phi=\langle 5\rangle=\theset{0,5,10,15,20,25}}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#kernel-is-five){.pf-ref} identifies the kernel with $\langle 5\rangle$, and step [](#five-subgroup){.pf-ref} lists its elements, which answers the exercise: the kernel is determined by the data, not merely bounded.
:::
