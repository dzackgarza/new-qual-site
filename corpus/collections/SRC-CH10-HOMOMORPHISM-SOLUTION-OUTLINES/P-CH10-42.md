::: {.problem}
(Third Isomorphism Theorem) If $M$ and $N$ are normal subgroups of $G$ and $N \leq M$, prove that $(G/N)/(M/N) \approx G/M$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection retains its solution outline as `assets/attachments/Ch10Sltns.pdf`, and entry "# 42" of that outline is this exercise; the seventh edition prints the same statement as Chapter 10 Exercise 40, and the card keeps the outline's number. The outline's map $gN\mapsto gM$, its kernel computation, and the appeal to the first isomorphism theorem are the argument below. What the card adds is the well-definedness of that map, which the outline omits although it is the only place the hypothesis $N\leq M$ is used, and the verification that $M/N$ is a normal subgroup of $G/N$, without which the quotient $(G/N)/(M/N)$ is not defined and the theorem cannot be stated at all.

Let $e$ be the identity of $G$, and write cosets multiplicatively, so that $N=eN$ and $M=eM$ are the identities of $G/N$ and $G/M$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #quotients}
$N\trianglelefteq M$, $M\leq G$ and $M/N\trianglelefteq G/N$; hence $G/N$ and $M/N$ are groups and $(G/N)/(M/N)$ is a group.

::: pf-proof
$M$ and $N$ are normal subgroups of $G$ by hypothesis, so each is a subgroup of $G$ and $G/N$, $G/M$ are groups. $N\le M$ by hypothesis, so $N$ is a subgroup of $M$; it is normal there because for $m\in M$ and $n\in N$ the conjugate $mnm^{-1}$ lies in $N$, as $m\in G$ and $N\trianglelefteq G$. So $M/N$ is a group. Next $M/N=\theset{mN \st m\in M}$ is a subset of $G/N$, and it is a subgroup: it contains $N=eN$; it is closed under the operation of $G/N$, since $(mN)(m'N)=mm'N$ with $mm'\in M$; and it contains inverses, since $(mN)^{-1}=m^{-1}N$ with $m^{-1}\in M$. Finally $M/N$ is normal in $G/N$: for $gN\in G/N$ and $mN\in M/N$, the conjugate is $(gN)(mN)(gN)^{-1}=(gmg^{-1})N$, and $gmg^{-1}\in M$ because $M\trianglelefteq G$, so the conjugate lies in $M/N$. A quotient by a normal subgroup is a group, so $(G/N)/(M/N)$ is a group.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #well-defined}
The rule $\Phi(gN)=gM$ defines a function $\Phi\colon G/N\to G/M$.

::: pf-proof
Recall that for a subgroup $M\leq G$ one has $gM=g'M$ exactly when $g^{-1}g'\in M$: if $gM=g'M$ then $g'=g\cdot(g^{-1}g')\in gM$ gives $g^{-1}g'\in M$, and if $g^{-1}g'\in M$ then $g'\in gM$, whence $g'M\subseteq gM$, and symmetrically $gM\subseteq g'M$. Now let $gN=g'N$. Then $g^{-1}g'\in N$, and $N\le M$ gives $g^{-1}g'\in M$, so $gM=g'M$ by the criterion. Hence the value of $\Phi$ depends only on the coset $gN$ and not on the representative chosen; this is exactly where the hypothesis $N\le M$ is used, and without it the rule would assign two values to one coset.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #homomorphism}
$\Phi$ is a homomorphism.

::: pf-proof
For $gN,hN\in G/N$, the product in $G/N$ is $(gN)(hN)=ghN$, which is well defined because $N\trianglelefteq G$. Then $\Phi\bigl((gN)(hN)\bigr)=\Phi(ghN)=ghM=(gM)(hM)=\Phi(gN)\Phi(hN)$, the middle equality being the definition of the product in $G/M$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #onto}
$\Phi$ is onto.

::: pf-proof
An arbitrary element of $G/M$ is $gM$ for some $g\in G$, and $\Phi(gN)=gM$, so every element of the codomain is a value.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #kernel}
$\ker\Phi=M/N$.

::: pf-proof
Let $gN\in G/N$. Then $gN\in\ker\Phi$ exactly when $\Phi(gN)$ is the identity of $G/M$, that is, when $gM=M$; by the coset criterion of step [](#well-defined){.pf-ref} this is equivalent to $g^{-1}e=g^{-1}\in M$, that is, to $g\in M$. And $g\in M$ exactly when the coset $gN$ is an element of $M/N$: one inclusion is immediate, and if $gN=mN$ for some $m\in M$ then $g=m(m^{-1}g)$ with $m^{-1}g\in N\le M$, so $g\in M$. Hence $\ker\Phi=M/N$, which is a normal subgroup of $G/N$ by step [](#quotients){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #isomorphism}
$\boxed{(G/N)/(M/N)\approx G/M}$, by the first isomorphism theorem applied to $\Phi$.

::: pf-proof
The first isomorphism theorem says that for a homomorphism $\Phi\colon A\to B$ of groups the kernel is a normal subgroup of $A$ and $A/\ker\Phi\approx\im\Phi$, the isomorphism being $a\ker\Phi\mapsto\Phi(a)$. Take $A=G/N$ and $B=G/M$; by step [](#homomorphism){.pf-ref} $\Phi$ is a homomorphism, by step [](#kernel){.pf-ref} its kernel is $M/N$, and by step [](#onto){.pf-ref} its image is all of $G/M$. Therefore $(G/N)/(M/N)\approx G/M$, with the isomorphism carrying the coset $gN(M/N)$ of $G/N$ to $gM$. Both sides are groups by step [](#quotients){.pf-ref}, so this is an isomorphism of groups.
:::
