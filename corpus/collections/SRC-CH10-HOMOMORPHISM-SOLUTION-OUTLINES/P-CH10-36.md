::: {.problem}
Suppose that there is a homomorphism $\phi$ from $\ZZ \oplus \ZZ$ to a group $G$ such that $\phi((3, 2)) = a$ and $\phi((2, 1)) = b$. Determine $\phi((4, 4))$ in terms of $a$ and $b$. Assume that the operation of $G$ is addition.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection retains its solution outline as `assets/attachments/Ch10Sltns.pdf`, and entry "# 36" of that outline is this exercise, printed as Chapter 10 Exercise 36 in the seventh edition. The outline solves the system $c(3,2)+d(2,1)=(4,4)$ and applies additivity of $\phi$. The steps below state what those two moves need: that the coefficients $c,d$ exist and are unique, that a homomorphism carries integer combinations of arguments to the corresponding integer combinations of values, and that the expression so obtained is unambiguous in the additively written group $G$.

Write $u=(3,2)$ and $v=(2,1)$ in $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$, written additively, and write $G$ additively with identity $0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis}
For all integers $m,n$ the pair $(m,n)$ equals $cu+dv$ with $c=2n-m$ and $d=2m-3n$, and these coefficients are the only ones.

::: pf-proof
The two components of $cu+dv$ are $3c+2d$ and $2c+d$. Substituting $c=2n-m$ and $d=2m-3n$ gives $3(2n-m)+2(2m-3n)=6n-3m+4m-6n=m$ and $2(2n-m)+(2m-3n)=4n-2m+2m-3n=n$, so the pair is $(m,n)$ as claimed. For uniqueness, suppose $cu+dv=c'u+d'v$, and put $s=c-c'$ and $t=d-d'$; then $3s+2t=0$ and $2s+t=0$ in $\ZZ$. The second equation gives $t=-2s$, and substituting into the first gives $3s-4s=-s=0$, so $s=0$ and then $t=0$. Hence the representation is unique.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #linearity}
For every homomorphism $\phi\colon\ZZ\oplus\ZZ\to G$ and all $c,d\in\ZZ$, one has $\phi(cu+dv)=c\,\phi(u)+d\,\phi(v)$; moreover $G$'s operation is commutative on the image of $\phi$.

::: pf-proof
Since $u$ and $v$ are elements of the abelian group $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$, $\phi(u+v)=\phi(u)+\phi(v)$ and $\phi(0)=0$. Induction on $c\ge0$ from $\phi((c+1)u)=\phi(cu)+\phi(u)$ gives $\phi(cu)=c\,\phi(u)$; and $\phi(-u)=-\phi(u)$ because $\phi(u)+\phi(-u)=\phi(u-u)=\phi(0)=0$. So $\phi(cu+dv)=\phi(cu)+\phi(dv)=c\,\phi(u)+d\,\phi(v)$ for all integers $c,d$, applying the same two facts to $d$ and to $v$. For commutativity, $\phi(x)+\phi(y)=\phi(x+y)=\phi(y)+\phi(x)$ because $x+y=y+x$ in $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$, so any two elements of $\im\phi$ commute; in particular the summands of $c\,\phi(u)+d\,\phi(v)$ may be written in either order, and the additive expression for the answer is unambiguous.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$\boxed{\phi((4,4))=4a-4b}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#basis){.pf-ref} with $m=n=4$ gives $c=2\cdot4-4=4$ and $d=2\cdot4-3\cdot4=-4$, so $(4,4)=4u-4v$; directly, $4(3,2)-4(2,1)=(12,8)-(8,4)=(4,4)$. Step [](#linearity){.pf-ref} then gives $\phi((4,4))=4\,\phi(u)-4\,\phi(v)=4a-4b$. The value is forced rather than chosen, because the coefficients $4$ and $-4$ are the only ones by step [](#basis){.pf-ref}: any homomorphism with the two given values must carry $(4,4)$ to $4a-4b$. Nothing is lost by the supposition that $\phi$ exists either, since $u$ and $v$ span $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ with no relation between them, so $(m,n)\mapsto(2n-m)a+(2m-3n)b$ is consistent with the two given values and is a homomorphism into any abelian group containing $a$ and $b$.
:::
