::: problem
Let $A,B\subset\mathbb{R}$ be two Lebesgue measurable sets of finite measure.
For $x\in\mathbb{R}$ define
\[
f(x)=m\bigl(A\cap(B+x)\bigr),
\]
where $B+x=\{b+x:b\in B\}$ is the translate of the set $B$ by $x$.

Prove that $f$ is measurable, and
\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,dx=m(A)m(B).
\]
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Express the overlap as a parameter integral.
::: proof
For $x,y\in\mathbb R$,
\[
y\in B+x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
y-x\in B.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbf1_{B+x}(y)=\mathbf1_B(y-x),
\]
and therefore
\[
f(x)
=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_A(y)\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,dy.
\]
The map
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto \mathbf1_A(y)\mathbf1_B(y-x)
\]
is nonnegative and measurable on $\mathbb R^2$, because $(x,y)\mapsto y-x$ is continuous and the indicator functions are measurable.

By Tonelli's theorem, the function obtained by integrating this jointly measurable nonnegative function in $y$ is measurable in $x$. Thus $f$ is measurable.
:::

<1>2. Compute the integral of $f$.
::: proof
Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\,dx
&=\int_{\mathbb R}\int_{\mathbb R}
\mathbf1_A(y)\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,dy\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_A(y)
\left(\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,dx\right)dy.
\end{aligned}
\]
For fixed $y$, the change of variables $u=y-x$ yields
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,dx
=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_B(u)\,du
=m(B).
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\,dx
=m(B)\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_A(y)\,dy
=m(A)m(B).
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\int_{\mathbb R}m(A\cap(B+x))\,dx=m(A)m(B).}
\]
:::
:::
