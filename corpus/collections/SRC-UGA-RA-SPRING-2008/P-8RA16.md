::: {.problem}
Let $A,B\subset\RR$ be two Lebesgue measurable sets of finite measure.
For $x\in\RR$ define
$$
f(x)=m\bigl(A\cap(B+x)\bigr),
$$
where $B+x=\{b+x:b\in B\}$ is the translate of the set $B$ by $x$.

Prove that $f$ is measurable, and
$$
\int_{\RR}f(x)\,dx=m(A)m(B).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Express the overlap as a parameter integral.

::: pf-proof

For $x,y\in\RR$,
$$
y\in B+x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
y-x\in B.
$$
Hence
$$
\mathbf1_{B+x}(y)=\mathbf1_B(y-x),
$$
and therefore
$$
f(x)
=\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_A(y)\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,dy.
$$
The map
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto \mathbf1_A(y)\mathbf1_B(y-x)
$$
is nonnegative and measurable on $\RR^2$, because $(x,y)\mapsto y-x$ is continuous and the indicator functions are measurable.

By Tonelli's theorem, the function obtained by integrating this jointly measurable nonnegative function in $y$ is measurable in $x$. Thus $f$ is measurable.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Compute the integral of $f$.

::: pf-proof

Tonelli's theorem gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\RR}f(x)\,dx
&=\int_{\RR}\int_{\RR}
\mathbf1_A(y)\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,dy\,dx\\
&=\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_A(y)
\left(\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,dx\right)dy.
\end{aligned}
$$
For fixed $y$, the change of variables $u=y-x$ yields
$$
\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,dx
=\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_B(u)\,du
=m(B).
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_{\RR}f(x)\,dx
=m(B)\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_A(y)\,dy
=m(A)m(B).
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{\int_{\RR}m(A\cap(B+x))\,dx=m(A)m(B).}
$$

:::
