::: problem
(a) Provide an example of a function $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ which is continuous when restricted to any line through the origin, but is not continuous at the origin.
(Give complete explanations of why your example has the desired property.)

(b) Provide an example of a function $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ which is everywhere differentiable when restricted to any line through the origin but is not differentiable at the origin.
(Again, be sure to give complete justification that your example works.)
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Construct the example for part (a).
::: proof
Define
\[
f(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x^2y}{x^4+y^2},&(x,y)\ne(0,0),\\[1ex]
0,&(x,y)=(0,0).
\end{cases}
\]
On the line $(x,y)=t(a,b)$,
\[
f(ta,tb)=\frac{t a^2b}{t^2a^4+b^2}
\]
when $b\ne0$, and the restriction is identically $0$ when $b=0$. Thus every line restriction is continuous at $t=0$.

However, along the parabola $y=x^2$,
\[
f(x,x^2)=\frac12
\]
for every $x\ne0$. Hence $f$ is not continuous at the origin.
:::

<1>2. Construct the example for part (b).
::: proof
Define
\[
g(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x^3}{x^2+y^2},&(x,y)\ne(0,0),\\[1ex]
0,&(x,y)=(0,0).
\end{cases}
\]
On the line $(x,y)=t(a,b)$,
\[
g(ta,tb)=t\frac{a^3}{a^2+b^2},
\]
so every line restriction is differentiable everywhere.

If $g$ were differentiable at the origin, then its directional derivative as a function of the direction vector would be linear. But
\[
D_{(a,b)}g(0,0)=\frac{a^3}{a^2+b^2},
\]
which is not linear in $(a,b)$. Therefore $g$ is not differentiable at the origin.
:::
:::
