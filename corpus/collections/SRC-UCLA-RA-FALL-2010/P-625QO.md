::: {.problem}
Let $T$ be a linear transformation on $C_c(\RR)$ (continuous functions with compact support) that has the following two properties: $$\norm{Tf}_{L^\infty} \le \norm{f}_{L^\infty} \quad \text{and} \quad m\{x\in\RR: \abs{Tf(x)}>\lambda\} \le \frac{\norm{f}_{L^1}}{\lambda}$$ where $m$ denotes Lebesgue measure.
Prove that $$\int \abs{Tf(x)}^2\,dx \le C\int \abs{f(x)}^2\,dx$$ for all $f\in C_c(\RR)$ and some fixed number $C$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own: it is the diagonal case of the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, as the earlier prose said, written out here with the layer-cake identity and the constant made explicit. No retained solution set covers this exercise.

Throughout, $T$ is linear on $C_c(\RR)$ and satisfies
$$
\norm{Tf}_{\infty}\le\norm{f}_{\infty}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
m\{x:\abs{Tf(x)}>\lambda\}\le\frac{\norm{f}_{1}}{\lambda}\quad(\lambda>0),
$$
where $m$ is Lebesgue measure and the second hypothesis asserts in particular that each set $\{\abs{Tf}>\lambda\}$ is measurable. All integrals are over $\RR$ and are allowed the value $+\infty$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #layer-cake}
For a measurable $u\ge0$, $\displaystyle\int u^{2}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,m\{u>\lambda\}\,d\lambda$, in $[0,+\infty]$.

::: pf-proof
By Tonelli applied to the nonnegative measurable function $(x,\lambda)\mapsto\lambda\,\mathbf{1}_{\{u>\lambda\}}$ on $\RR\times(0,\infty)$,
$$
2\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,m\{u>\lambda\}\,d\lambda=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int\lambda\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\lambda<u(x)\}}\,dx\,d\lambda=\int\Big(2\int_{0}^{u(x)}\lambda\,d\lambda\Big)dx=\int u(x)^{2}\,dx .
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #truncate}
Let $f\ge0$ be continuous with compact support and let $\lambda>0$. Put $h=\min\{f,\lambda/2\}$ and $g=f-h$. Then $g,h\ge0$ are continuous with compact support, $\norm{h}_{\infty}\le\lambda/2$, $Tf=Tg+Th$, and $\{\abs{Tf}>\lambda\}\subset\{\abs{Tg}>\lambda/2\}$.

::: pf-proof
$h$ is continuous (a minimum of two continuous functions), $0\le h\le f$ has compact support, and $g=\max\{f-\lambda/2,0\}$ is continuous with compact support. Also $h\le\lambda/2$ everywhere, so $\norm{h}_{\infty}\le\lambda/2$. By linearity $Tf=Tg+Th$ and so $\abs{Tf}\le\abs{Tg}+\abs{Th}\le\abs{Tg}+\norm{Th}_{\infty}\le\abs{Tg}+\lambda/2$; hence $\abs{Tf(x)}>\lambda$ forces $\abs{Tg(x)}>\lambda/2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #weak}
Under that hypothesis, $m\{\abs{Tf}>\lambda\}\le2\norm{g}_{1}/\lambda$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#truncate){.pf-ref} the set $\{\abs{Tf}>\lambda\}$ is contained in $\{\abs{Tg}>\lambda/2\}$, so by monotonicity of $m$ and the weak-type hypothesis applied to $g$ at level $\lambda/2$,
$$
m\{\abs{Tf}>\lambda\}\le m\{\abs{Tg}>\lambda/2\}\le\frac{\norm{g}_{1}}{\lambda/2}=\frac{2\norm{g}_{1}}{\lambda}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #level-set}
$\displaystyle\norm{g}_{1}=\int_{\{f>\lambda/2\}}\Big(f-\frac{\lambda}{2}\Big)\le\int_{\{f>\lambda/2\}}f$.

::: pf-proof
$g=(f-\lambda/2)^{+}$, which is positive exactly where $f>\lambda/2$; integrating the two nonnegative functions $(f-\lambda/2)^{+}$ and $f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{f>\lambda/2\}}$ over that set gives the identity and then the inequality, since $f-\lambda/2\le f$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #real-nonnegative}
For continuous $f\ge0$ with compact support, $\int\abs{Tf}^{2}\le8\int f^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#layer-cake){.pf-ref}, [](#weak){.pf-ref} and [](#level-set){.pf-ref}, with $g=g_{\lambda}$ depending on $\lambda$,
$$
\int\abs{Tf}^{2}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,m\{\abs{Tf}>\lambda\}\,d\lambda\le4\int_{0}^{\infty}\norm{g_{\lambda}}_{1}\,d\lambda\le4\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\{f>\lambda/2\}}f(x)\,dx\,d\lambda .
$$
The double integral is over the region $\{(x,\lambda):0<\lambda<2f(x)\}$, so by Tonelli it equals
$$
\int_{\RR}f(x)\Big(\int_{0}^{2f(x)}d\lambda\Big)dx=2\int f(x)^{2}\,dx,
$$
giving $\int\abs{Tf}^{2}\le8\int f^{2}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #real}
For real-valued continuous $f$ with compact support, $\int\abs{Tf}^{2}\le16\int f^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
Write $f=f_{+}-f_{-}$ with $f_{\pm}\ge0$ continuous and compactly supported; then $Tf=Tf_{+}-Tf_{-}$ and $\abs{Tf}^{2}\le2\abs{Tf_{+}}^{2}+2\abs{Tf_{-}}^{2}$ by $\abs{a-b}^{2}\le2\abs{a}^{2}+2\abs{b}^{2}$. So
$$
\int\abs{Tf}^{2}\le2\int\abs{Tf_{+}}^{2}+2\int\abs{Tf_{-}}^{2}\le16\int f_{+}^{2}+16\int f_{-}^{2}=16\int f^{2},
$$
the last equality because $f_{+}f_{-}=0$ everywhere.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #complex}
For complex-valued continuous $f$ with compact support, $\int\abs{Tf}^{2}\le32\int\abs{f}^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
Write $f=u+iv$ with $u=\operatorname{Re}f$ and $v=\operatorname{Im}f$ real, continuous and compactly supported; by linearity $Tf=Tu+iTv$, so $\abs{Tf}^{2}\le2\abs{Tu}^{2}+2\abs{Tv}^{2}$ and step [](#real){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\int\abs{Tf}^{2}\le2\big(16\int u^{2}\big)+2\big(16\int v^{2}\big)=32\int\abs{f}^{2},
$$
since $u^{2}+v^{2}=\abs{f}^{2}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\displaystyle\int\abs{Tf(x)}^{2}dx\le C\int\abs{f(x)}^{2}dx$ for every $f\in C_c(\RR)$, with $C=32$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#real-nonnegative){.pf-ref}, [](#real){.pf-ref} and [](#complex){.pf-ref} cover nonnegative real, real and complex $f$, and $C=32$ is a fixed number independent of $f$.
:::
