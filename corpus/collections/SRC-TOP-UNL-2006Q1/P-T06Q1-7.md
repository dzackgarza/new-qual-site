::: {.problem}
Let $p:\widetilde X\to X$ be a covering map, and $f:Y\to X$ be a continuous map.
Define $$\widetilde Y=\{(y,\widetilde x)\in Y\times\widetilde X:f(y)=p(\widetilde x)\}\subseteq Y\times\widetilde X,$$ with the subspace topology inherited from $Y\times\widetilde X$, and define $q:\widetilde Y\to Y$ by $q(y,\widetilde x)=y$.
Show that $q$ is also a covering map.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $y_0\in Y$, choose an evenly covered open neighborhood $U$ of $f(y_0)$ with $p^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{\alpha\in A}U_\alpha$ and each $p_\alpha=p|_{U_\alpha}\colon U_\alpha\to U$ a homeomorphism, and put $V=f^{-1}(U)$, an open neighborhood of $y_0$.
For $\alpha\in A$ let $W_\alpha=\widetilde Y\cap(V\times U_\alpha)$.

<1>1. $q$ is continuous and surjective.

::: {.proof}
$q$ is the restriction of the projection $Y\times\widetilde X\to Y$.
For $y\in Y$, surjectivity of $p$ gives $\widetilde x$ with $p(\widetilde x)=f(y)$, and $q(y,\widetilde x)=y$.
:::

<1>2. The $W_\alpha$ are pairwise disjoint open subsets of $\widetilde Y$ with $q^{-1}(V)=\coprod_{\alpha\in A}W_\alpha$.

::: {.proof}
$V\times U_\alpha$ is open in $Y\times\widetilde X$, and the $U_\alpha$ are pairwise disjoint.
If $(y,\widetilde x)\in q^{-1}(V)$, then $p(\widetilde x)=f(y)\in U$, so $\widetilde x$ lies in exactly one $U_\alpha$.
:::

<1>3. Each $q|_{W_\alpha}\colon W_\alpha\to V$ is a homeomorphism.

::: {.proof}
Define $r_\alpha\colon V\to W_\alpha$ by $r_\alpha(y)=(y,p_\alpha^{-1}(f(y)))$; it lands in $\widetilde Y$ because $p(p_\alpha^{-1}(f(y)))=f(y)$, and it is continuous because its coordinates are.
$q(r_\alpha(y))=y$, and for $(y,\widetilde x)\in W_\alpha$, $p_\alpha(\widetilde x)=f(y)$ gives $r_\alpha(y)=(y,\widetilde x)$.
So $r_\alpha$ is a continuous inverse of $q|_{W_\alpha}$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>2 and <1>3, $V$ is evenly covered by $q$.
Since $y_0$ was arbitrary and $q$ is continuous and surjective by step <1>1, $q$ is a covering map.
:::
:::
