::: {.problem}
Find a cell structure for, and compute a presentation for, the fundamental group of the space $X$ described below.
$X$ is a quotient space of the disjoint union of two copies $T_1=T_2=S^1\times S^1$ of the 2-torus.
Fix a basepoint $x_0$ in $S^1$.
$X$ is obtained by gluing $T_1$ and $T_2$ together, identifying $(x_0,x)$ in $T_1$ with $(x,x_0)$ in $T_2$, for each $x\in S^1$.
For concreteness, use $(x_0,x_0)$ as your basepoint for $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Give each circle $S^1$ the CW structure with one $0$-cell $x_0$ and one oriented $1$-cell.

<1>1. Give $T_1=S^1\times S^1$ a CW structure with one vertex $v_1=(x_0,x_0)$, oriented loop $1$-cells
\[
a=S^1\times\{x_0\},
\qquad
b_1=\{x_0\}\times S^1,
\]
and one $2$-cell attached along
\[
[a,b_1]=ab_1a^{-1}b_1^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the standard product CW structure on the torus.
The boundary of a fundamental square traverses its four sides in the order
\[
a,b_1,a^{-1},b_1^{-1},
\]
so the attaching word is the displayed commutator.
:::

<1>2. Give $T_2=S^1\times S^1$ the analogous CW structure with one vertex $v_2$, loop $1$-cells
\[
b_2=S^1\times\{x_0\},
\qquad
c=\{x_0\}\times S^1,
\]
and one $2$-cell attached along
\[
[b_2,c].
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the same standard torus CW structure, with the names chosen to reflect the gluing in the problem.
:::

<1>3. The quotient identifying $(x_0,x)\in T_1$ with $(x,x_0)\in T_2$ has a CW structure with one vertex $v$, three loop $1$-cells $a,b,c$, and two $2$-cells attached along
\[
[a,b]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[b,c].
\]
::: {.proof}
The gluing map identifies the whole subcomplex
\[
b_1=\{x_0\}\times S^1\subseteq T_1
\]
with the whole subcomplex
\[
b_2=S^1\times\{x_0\}\subseteq T_2
\]
by the identity parameter $x\mapsto x$.
In particular it identifies $v_1$ with $v_2$ and identifies $b_1$ with $b_2$ preserving the chosen orientation.
Call the common image $b$.

The other $1$-cells $a$ and $c$ remain distinct, and the interiors of the two torus $2$-cells are not identified with anything.
Their attaching maps therefore descend from <1>1 and <1>2 to the words
\[
[a,b]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[b,c].
\]
This gives the asserted cell structure on $X$.
:::

<1>4. The fundamental group has presentation
\[
\pi_1(X,v)
\cong
\left\langle a,b,c\ \middle|\ [a,b]=1,\ [b,c]=1\right\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton in <1>3 is a wedge of three circles, so its fundamental group is the free group on $a,b,c$.
Attaching a $2$-cell quotients the fundamental group by the normal closure of its attaching loop.
The two $2$-cells therefore impose exactly the two commutator relations shown.
There are no higher cells that affect the fundamental group.
:::

<1>5. Equivalently,
\[
\pi_1(X,v)\cong F(a,c)\times\mathbb Z b,
\]
where $F(a,c)$ is the free group on $a,c$.
::: {.proof}
The presentation in <1>4 says precisely that $b$ commutes with both $a$ and $c$, while it imposes no relation involving only $a$ and $c$.

Define
\[
\Phi:F(a,c)\times\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\pi_1(X,v)
\]
by
\[
\Phi(w,n)=w(a,c)b^n.
\]
The commutator relations make this a homomorphism, and it is surjective because $a,b,c$ generate.

Conversely the assignments
\[
a\longmapsto(a,0),
\qquad
b\longmapsto(1,1),
\qquad
c\longmapsto(c,0)
\]
satisfy both defining commutator relations, so the presentation gives a homomorphism in the reverse direction.
The two maps are inverse on the generators, hence inverse homomorphisms.
:::
