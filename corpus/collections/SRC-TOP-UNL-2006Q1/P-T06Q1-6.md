::: {.problem}
Find a cell structure for, and compute a presentation for, the fundamental group of the space $X$ described below.
$X$ is a quotient space of the disjoint union of two copies $T_1=T_2=S^1\times S^1$ of the 2-torus.
Fix a basepoint $x_0$ in $S^1$.
$X$ is obtained by gluing $T_1$ and $T_2$ together, identifying $(x_0,x)$ in $T_1$ with $(x,x_0)$ in $T_2$, for each $x\in S^1$.
For concreteness, use $(x_0,x_0)$ as your basepoint for $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Give $S^1$ the CW structure with one $0$-cell $x_0$ and one oriented $1$-cell.
Then $T_1$ has the product CW structure with vertex $(x_0,x_0)$, loops $a=S^1\times\{x_0\}$ and $b_1=\{x_0\}\times S^1$, and one $2$-cell attached along $ab_1a^{-1}b_1^{-1}$, the boundary of the fundamental square; likewise $T_2$ has loops $b_2=S^1\times\{x_0\}$ and $c=\{x_0\}\times S^1$ and a $2$-cell attached along $[b_2,c]$.

<1>1. $X$ is a CW complex with one vertex $v$, three loops $a,b,c$, and two $2$-cells attached along $[a,b]$ and $[b,c]$.

::: {.proof}
The gluing identifies the subcomplex $b_1=\{x_0\}\times S^1\subseteq T_1$ with the subcomplex $b_2=S^1\times\{x_0\}\subseteq T_2$ by $x\mapsto x$, preserving orientation; call the common image $b$.
It identifies the two vertices, leaves $a$ and $c$ distinct, and does not touch the open $2$-cells, whose attaching words become $[a,b]$ and $[b,c]$.
:::

<1>2. $\pi_1(X,v)\cong\langle a,b,c\mid[a,b],\ [b,c]\rangle$.

::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is a wedge of three circles, with fundamental group free on $a,b,c$.
By the Seifert--van Kampen theorem, each $2$-cell of step <1>1 adds its attaching word as a relation.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the cell structure and step <1>2 the presentation.
Equivalently, $\pi_1(X,v)\cong F(a,c)\times\ZZ$, with $F(a,c)$ free on $a,c$ and $\ZZ$ generated by $b$: the map $F(a,c)\times\ZZ\to\pi_1(X,v)$, $(w,n)\mapsto w(a,c)b^n$, is a homomorphism because $b$ commutes with $a$ and $c$, and the assignment $a\mapsto(a,0)$, $b\mapsto(1,1)$, $c\mapsto(c,0)$ satisfies both relations and defines the inverse on generators.
:::
