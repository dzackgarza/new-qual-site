::: {.problem}
Find a $\Delta$-complex structure on the space $X$ obtained by identifying the boundaries of three copies of the unit disk together (using identity maps), and compute the simplicial homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the three disks as $D_1,D_2,D_3$, with all three boundary circles identified pointwise.

<1>1. Give the common boundary circle a $\Delta$-complex structure with one vertex $v$ and one oriented loop edge $e$.
For each $i=1,2,3$, give $D_i$ the cone $\Delta$-complex structure on this circle: add one cone vertex $w_i$, one oriented radial edge
\[
r_i:v\longrightarrow w_i,
\]
and one oriented $2$-simplex $\sigma_i$ whose base is $e$ and whose two remaining faces are both $r_i$.
These structures agree on the boundary circles, so after the prescribed boundary identifications they give a $\Delta$-complex structure on $X$.
::: {.proof}
Realize $\sigma_i$ as an ordered triangle $[v_0,v_1,v_2]$ with
\[
v_0,v_1\longmapsto v,
\qquad
v_2\longmapsto w_i.
\]
Map the base face $[v_0,v_1]$ to the loop $e$, and map both faces $[v_0,v_2]$ and $[v_1,v_2]$ homeomorphically onto $r_i$.

The quotient of the triangle by identifying these two radial faces is the cone on the one-edge circle $e$, hence is a disk.
Thus each $D_i$ has the asserted $\Delta$-complex structure.
Because the three boundary structures are literally the same one-edge circle and the problem glues the boundaries by identity maps, the three disk structures glue to a $\Delta$-complex structure on $X$.
:::

<1>2. With the orientations chosen in <1>1, the simplicial chain groups are
\[
C_2(X)=\mathbb Z\langle\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3\rangle,
\]
\[
C_1(X)=\mathbb Z\langle e,r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle,
\]
and
\[
C_0(X)=\mathbb Z\langle v,w_1,w_2,w_3\rangle.
\]
The nonzero boundary formulas are
\[
\partial_2\sigma_i=e,
\qquad
\partial_1r_i=w_i-v
\quad (i=1,2,3),
\]
while
\[
\partial_1e=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
For the ordered $2$-simplex $\sigma_i=[v_0,v_1,v_2]$,
\[
\partial_2\sigma_i
=[v_1,v_2]-[v_0,v_2]+[v_0,v_1].
\]
Both radial faces $[v_1,v_2]$ and $[v_0,v_2]$ map to $r_i$ with the same orientation, whereas the base maps to $e$.
Hence
\[
\partial_2\sigma_i=r_i-r_i+e=e.
\]

Since $r_i$ is oriented from $v$ to $w_i$,
\[
\partial_1r_i=w_i-v.
\]
The edge $e$ is a loop based at $v$, so
\[
\partial_1e=v-v=0.
\]
:::

<1>3. The second homology group is
\[
H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
There are no simplices in dimensions greater than $2$, so
\[
H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2.
\]
For a general $2$-chain,
\[
\partial_2(a_1\sigma_1+a_2\sigma_2+a_3\sigma_3)
=(a_1+a_2+a_3)e.
\]
Therefore
\[
\ker\partial_2
=
\{(a_1,a_2,a_3)\in\mathbb Z^3:a_1+a_2+a_3=0\}.
\]
This is free abelian of rank $2$, for example with basis
\[
\sigma_1-\sigma_3,
\qquad
\sigma_2-\sigma_3.
\]
Thus $H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2$.
:::

<1>4. The first homology group vanishes:
\[
H_1(X)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
c=ae+b_1r_1+b_2r_2+b_3r_3\in C_1(X).
\]
Then
\[
\partial_1c
=
b_1(w_1-v)+b_2(w_2-v)+b_3(w_3-v).
\]
Since $v,w_1,w_2,w_3$ form a basis of $C_0(X)$, this vanishes exactly when
\[
b_1=b_2=b_3=0.
\]
Hence
\[
\ker\partial_1=\mathbb Z\langle e\rangle.
\]

By <1>2,
\[
\partial_2\sigma_1=e,
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{im}\partial_2=\mathbb Z\langle e\rangle.
\]
Therefore
\[
H_1(X)
=
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=0.
\]
:::

<1>5. The zeroth homology group is
\[
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\partial_0=0$,
\[
H_0(X)=C_0(X)/\operatorname{im}\partial_1.
\]
The image of $\partial_1$ is generated by
\[
w_1-v,
\qquad
w_2-v,
\qquad
w_3-v.
\]
Thus the quotient identifies all four vertices and leaves one free generator.
Hence
\[
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>6. Consequently the simplicial homology groups are
\[
H_n(X)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,\\
0, & n=1,\\
\mathbb Z^2, & n=2,\\
0, & n\ge 3.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
The cases $n=0,1,2$ are <1>3--<1>5.
There are no simplices in dimensions $n\ge 3$, so $C_n(X)=0$ there and hence $H_n(X)=0$.
:::
