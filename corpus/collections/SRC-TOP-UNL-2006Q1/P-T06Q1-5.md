::: {.problem}
Recall that a topological space $X$ is locally connected if for every point $x\in X$ and every neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ there exists a connected neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ with $V\subseteq U$.

(a) Prove that a topological space $X$ is locally connected if and only if for every open set $U\subseteq X$ the components of $U$ are open.
Now let $p:X\to Y$ be a quotient map.

(b) Prove that if $C$ is a component of an open subset $U\subseteq Y$ then $p^{-1}(C)$ is a union of components of $p^{-1}(U)$.

(c) Deduce that if $X$ is locally connected then so is $Y$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose first that $X$ is locally connected.
If $U\subseteq X$ is open, then every component of $U$ is open in $X$.
::: {.proof}
Let $C$ be a component of $U$ and let $x\in C$.
Since $U$ is a neighborhood of $x$ and $X$ is locally connected, there is a connected neighborhood $V$ of $x$ such that
\[
V\subseteq U.
\]
Because $V$ is connected, lies in $U$, and meets the component $C$ at $x$, maximality of the component gives
\[
V\subseteq C.
\]
As $V$ is a neighborhood of $x$, there is an open set $W$ with
\[
x\in W\subseteq V\subseteq C.
\]
Thus every point of $C$ is interior to $C$, so $C$ is open.
:::

<1>2. Conversely, suppose every component of every open subset of $X$ is open.
Then $X$ is locally connected.
::: {.proof}
Let $x\in X$ and let $N$ be any neighborhood of $x$.
Choose an open set $U$ such that
\[
x\in U\subseteq N.
\]
Let $C$ be the component of $U$ containing $x$.
By hypothesis $C$ is open in $X$; by definition of component it is connected.
Therefore $C$ is a connected neighborhood of $x$ satisfying
\[
C\subseteq U\subseteq N.
\]
This is exactly local connectedness at $x$.
:::

<1>3. This proves part (a): $X$ is locally connected if and only if every component of every open subset of $X$ is open.
::: {.proof}
The two implications are <1>1 and <1>2.
:::

Now let
\[
p:X\longrightarrow Y
\]
be a quotient map, let $U\subseteq Y$ be open, and let $C$ be a component of $U$.

<1>4. If $D$ is a component of $p^{-1}(U)$ that meets $p^{-1}(C)$, then
\[
D\subseteq p^{-1}(C).
\]
::: {.proof}
Choose
\[
z\in D\cap p^{-1}(C).
\]
The restriction of $p$ to $D$ is continuous, so $p(D)$ is connected.
Also
\[
p(D)\subseteq U
\]
because $D\subseteq p^{-1}(U)$, and
\[
p(z)\in C.
\]
Thus the connected subset $p(D)$ of $U$ meets the component $C$ of $U$.
By maximality of components,
\[
p(D)\subseteq C.
\]
Taking inverse images gives
\[
D\subseteq p^{-1}(C).
\]
:::

<1>5. Hence $p^{-1}(C)$ is a union of components of $p^{-1}(U)$.
::: {.proof}
Every point $z\in p^{-1}(C)$ belongs to a unique component $D_z$ of $p^{-1}(U)$.
By <1>4,
\[
D_z\subseteq p^{-1}(C).
\]
Therefore
\[
p^{-1}(C)=\bigcup_{z\in p^{-1}(C)}D_z,
\]
which proves part (b).
:::

<1>6. Suppose $X$ is locally connected.
Then every component $C$ of every open subset $U\subseteq Y$ is open in $Y$.
::: {.proof}
Since $p$ is a quotient map, it is continuous, so $p^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$.
By <1>3 and local connectedness of $X$, every component of $p^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$.
By <1>5, $p^{-1}(C)$ is a union of such components, hence is open in $X$.

The defining property of a quotient map says that a subset $A\subseteq Y$ is open exactly when $p^{-1}(A)$ is open in $X$.
Therefore $C$ is open in $Y$.
:::

<1>7. Consequently $Y$ is locally connected.
::: {.proof}
By <1>6, every component of every open subset of $Y$ is open.
Applying the equivalence in <1>3 to $Y$ proves that $Y$ is locally connected, establishing part (c).
:::
