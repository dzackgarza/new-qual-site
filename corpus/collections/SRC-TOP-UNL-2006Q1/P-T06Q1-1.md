::: {.problem}
Suppose that $d,d'$ are metrics on the set $X$ with $d(x,y)\le d'(x,y)$ for every $x,y\in X$.
Show that the metric topology $(X,d)$ is coarser than the metric topology $(X,d')$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\mathcal T_d$ and $\mathcal T_{d'}$ be the two metric topologies.

<1>1. $B_{d'}(x,r)\subseteq B_d(x,r)$ for every $x\in X$ and $r>0$.

::: {.proof}
If $d'(x,y)<r$, then $d(x,y)\le d'(x,y)<r$.
:::

<1>2. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Let $U\in\mathcal T_d$ and $x\in U$, and choose $r>0$ with $B_d(x,r)\subseteq U$.
By step <1>1, $B_{d'}(x,r)\subseteq U$, so $U\in\mathcal T_{d'}$.
Hence $\mathcal T_d\subseteq\mathcal T_{d'}$.
:::
:::
