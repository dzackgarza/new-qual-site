::: {.problem}
Suppose that $d,d'$ are metrics on the set $X$ with $d(x,y)\le d'(x,y)$ for every $x,y\in X$.
Show that the metric topology $(X,d)$ is coarser than the metric topology $(X,d')$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For every $x\in X$ and every $r>0$,
\[
B_{d'}(x,r)\subseteq B_d(x,r).
\]
::: {.proof}
If $y\in B_{d'}(x,r)$, then
\[
d'(x,y)<r.
\]
By hypothesis,
\[
d(x,y)\le d'(x,y)<r,
\]
so $y\in B_d(x,r)$.
:::

<1>2. Every set open in the metric topology induced by $d$ is open in the metric topology induced by $d'$.
::: {.proof}
Let $U$ be $d$-open and let $x\in U$.
By the definition of the metric topology, there is an $r>0$ such that
\[
B_d(x,r)\subseteq U.
\]
By <1>1,
\[
B_{d'}(x,r)\subseteq B_d(x,r)\subseteq U.
\]
Thus every point $x\in U$ has a $d'$-open ball contained in $U$, so $U$ is $d'$-open.
:::

<1>3. Therefore the topology induced by $d$ is coarser than the topology induced by $d'$.
::: {.proof}
Writing $\mathcal T_d$ and $\mathcal T_{d'}$ for the two metric topologies, <1>2 proves
\[
\mathcal T_d\subseteq\mathcal T_{d'}.
\]
This is exactly the assertion that $(X,d)$ has the coarser topology.
:::
:::
