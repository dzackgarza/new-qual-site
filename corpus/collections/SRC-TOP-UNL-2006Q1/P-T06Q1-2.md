::: {.problem}
A topological space $(X,\mathcal T)$ is called *limit-point compact* if every infinite subset $A$ of $X$ has a limit point.
Show that every closed subset of a limit-point compact space is limit-point compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $C\subseteq X$ be closed and $A\subseteq C$ infinite.

<1>1. $A$ has a limit point $x\in X$, and $x\in C$.

::: {.proof}
$A$ is an infinite subset of the limit-point compact space $X$.
A limit point of $A$ lies in $\overline A\subseteq C$, since $C$ is closed.
:::

<1>2. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Every neighborhood of $x$ in $C$ has the form $U\cap C$ with $U$ open in $X$, and $U$ meets $A\setminus\{x\}\subseteq C$; so $x$ is a limit point of $A$ in $C$.
Hence $C$ is limit-point compact.
:::
:::
