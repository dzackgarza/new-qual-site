::: {.problem}
A topological space $(X,\mathcal T)$ is called *limit-point compact* if every infinite subset $A$ of $X$ has a limit point.
Show that every closed subset of a limit-point compact space is limit-point compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $C \subseteq X$ be closed, and let $A \subseteq C$ be an infinite subset.
::: {.proof}
take an arbitrary infinite subset of $C$.
:::

<1>2. $A$ is an infinite subset of $X$, so $A$ has a limit point $x \in X$.
::: {.proof}
$X$ is limit-point compact.
:::

<1>3. $x \in C$.
::: {.proof}
$A \subseteq C$ and $C$ is closed, so the closure of $A$ is contained in $C$; a limit point of $A$ lies in $\overline A \subseteq C$.
:::

<1>4. Hence every infinite subset of $C$ has a limit point in $C$, so $C$ is limit-point compact.
::: {.proof}
<1>1–<1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>4.
:::
:::
