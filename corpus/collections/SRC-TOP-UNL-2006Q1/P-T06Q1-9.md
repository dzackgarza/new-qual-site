::: {.problem}
Use Mayer--Vietoris sequences to compute the singular homology groups of the 2-torus $T=S^1\times S^1$.
(You may use knowledge of the homology groups of the circle $S^1$ in your calculations.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose a point $x_0$ in the interior of the standard square fundamental domain for the torus, and let $V$ be a small open disk around its image in $T$.
Set
\[
U=T\setminus\{x_0\}.
\]
Then $U$ and $V$ are open and cover $T$.

<1>1. The spaces in this cover have homotopy types
\[
U\simeq S^1\vee S^1,
\qquad
V\simeq *,
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq S^1.
\]
Hence
\[
H_n(U)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2, & n=1,\\
0, & n\ge 2,
\end{cases}
\]
\[
H_n(V)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,\\
0, & n\ge 1,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
H_n(U\cap V)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,1,\\
0, & n\ge 2.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
The disk $V$ is contractible.
Removing its center gives a punctured disk, so
\[
U\cap V=V\setminus\{x_0\}\simeq S^1.
\]

Use the usual square model of the torus, with opposite sides identified.
After removing the interior point $x_0$, radially retract the punctured square onto its boundary.
After the side identifications, that boundary is the standard $1$-skeleton of the torus, namely a wedge of two circles.
Thus
\[
U\simeq S^1\vee S^1.
\]
The displayed homology groups follow from these homotopy equivalences and the homology of a point, a circle, and a wedge of two circles.
:::

<1>2. The map
\[
H_1(U\cap V)\longrightarrow H_1(U)\oplus H_1(V)
\]
in the Mayer--Vietoris sequence is the zero map.
::: {.proof}
Let $a,b$ denote the two standard loop generators of the wedge $S^1\vee S^1\simeq U$.
A positively oriented loop around the puncture $x_0$ is homotopic in $U$ to the boundary of the square fundamental domain.
Under the usual edge identifications this boundary represents, up to orientation,
\[
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}\in\pi_1(U).
\]

Passing to first homology abelianizes the fundamental group, so the class of this loop is
\[
[a]+[b]-[a]-[b]=0
\]
in $H_1(U)$.
Thus the inclusion-induced map
\[
H_1(U\cap V)\longrightarrow H_1(U)
\]
is zero.
The map to $H_1(V)$ is also zero because $H_1(V)=0$.
Therefore the Mayer--Vietoris map to the direct sum is zero.
:::

<1>3. The second homology group is
\[
H_2(T)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
The relevant part of the Mayer--Vietoris sequence is
\[
H_2(U)\oplus H_2(V)
\longrightarrow
H_2(T)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_1(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
H_1(U)\oplus H_1(V).
\]
By <1>1 and <1>2 this becomes
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_2(T)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow
0.
\]
Exactness therefore makes $\delta$ an isomorphism, so
\[
H_2(T)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>4. The first homology group is
\[
H_1(T)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Continue the Mayer--Vietoris sequence:
\[
H_1(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
H_1(U)\oplus H_1(V)
\longrightarrow
H_1(T)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
H_0(U\cap V)
\xrightarrow{\phi}
H_0(U)\oplus H_0(V).
\]
By <1>1 and <1>2 this is
\[
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{0}
\mathbb Z^2
\longrightarrow
H_1(T)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\phi}
\mathbb Z^2.
\]

All three spaces $U$, $V$, and $U\cap V$ are path connected.
With the usual Mayer--Vietoris sign convention, $\phi$ sends the generator of $H_0(U\cap V)$ to
\[
(1,-1)\in H_0(U)\oplus H_0(V),
\]
so $\phi$ is injective.
Exactness gives
\[
\operatorname{im}\partial=\ker\phi=0,
\]
so the map
\[
H_1(U)\oplus H_1(V)\longrightarrow H_1(T)
\]
is surjective.
Its kernel is the image of the preceding zero map, hence is zero.
Therefore
\[
H_1(T)\cong H_1(U)\oplus H_1(V)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
:::

<1>5. The zeroth homology group is
\[
H_0(T)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
The torus is path connected, so its zeroth singular homology is $\mathbb Z$.
Equivalently, the end of the same Mayer--Vietoris sequence is
\[
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{1\mapsto(1,-1)}
\mathbb Z^2
\longrightarrow
H_0(T)
\longrightarrow 0,
\]
whose cokernel is $\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>6. For every $n\ge 3$,
\[
H_n(T)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
For $n\ge 3$, the relevant Mayer--Vietoris segment is
\[
H_n(U)\oplus H_n(V)
\longrightarrow
H_n(T)
\longrightarrow
H_{n-1}(U\cap V).
\]
Both outer groups vanish by <1>1, so exactness forces $H_n(T)=0$.
:::

<1>7. Thus
\[
H_n(T)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & n=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2, & n=1,\\
\mathbb Z, & n=2,\\
0, & n\ge 3.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>3--<1>6.
:::
:::
