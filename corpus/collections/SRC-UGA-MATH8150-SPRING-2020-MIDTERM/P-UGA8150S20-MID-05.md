::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(z)=2z^5+8z-1.
$$
Show that all five zeros of $f$ lie inside the disk $D(0,2)$ and that exactly one zero lies inside the disk $D(0,1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Two applications of Rouché's theorem: on $\abs z=2$ the term $2z^5$ dominates $8z-1$, so $f$ has as many zeros inside as $2z^5$, namely five; on $\abs z=1$ the term $8z$ dominates $2z^5-1$, so $f$ has as many zeros inside as $8z$, namely one. The strict inequalities also exclude zeros on either circle. The argument is this card's own: the checkout retains no solution set for this exam.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #setup}
$f(z)=2z^5+8z-1$ is a polynomial, hence holomorphic on $\CC$, and it has exactly five zeros counted with multiplicity.

::: pf-proof
A polynomial is entire. Its degree is $5$, so by the fundamental theorem of algebra it has exactly five zeros counted with multiplicity; in particular $f$ is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of every closed disc used below.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #outer}
On $\abs z=2$, $\abs{8z-1}\le17<64=\abs{2z^5}$; hence $f$ and $2z^5$ have the same number of zeros in $D(0,2)$, namely five, and $f$ has no zero on $\abs z=2$.

::: pf-proof
For $\abs z=2$ one has $\abs{8z-1}\le8\cdot2+1=17$ and $\abs{2z^5}=2\cdot2^5=64$, so the strict inequality holds on the whole circle. Rouché's theorem applies to the holomorphic functions $2z^5$ and $8z-1$ on the disc $D(0,2)$ with the boundary circle $\abs z=2$, and gives that $f=2z^5+(8z-1)$ has as many zeros in $D(0,2)$ as $2z^5$ does, counted with multiplicity. The function $2z^5$ has the single zero $0$, of multiplicity $5$, so the common count is five. Finally $f$ does not vanish on $\abs z=2$, since there $\abs{f(z)}\ge\abs{2z^5}-\abs{8z-1}\ge64-17>0$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #inner}
On $\abs z=1$, $\abs{2z^5-1}\le3<8=\abs{8z}$; hence $f$ and $8z$ have the same number of zeros in $D(0,1)$, namely one, and $f$ has no zero on $\abs z=1$.

::: pf-proof
For $\abs z=1$ one has $\abs{2z^5-1}\le2+1=3$ and $\abs{8z}=8$, so the strict inequality holds on the circle. Rouché's theorem applied to $8z$ and $2z^5-1$ on $D(0,1)$ gives that $f=8z+(2z^5-1)$ has as many zeros in $D(0,1)$ as $8z$ does; $8z$ has the simple zero $0$, so the count is one. And $f\ne0$ on $\abs z=1$, because there $\abs{f(z)}\ge\abs{8z}-\abs{2z^5-1}\ge8-3>0$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
All five zeros of $f$ lie in $D(0,2)$, and exactly one of them lies in $D(0,1)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#outer){.pf-ref} the zeros in $D(0,2)$ number five, which is the total number of zeros by step [](#setup){.pf-ref}; so every zero lies in $D(0,2)$, and none on the circle $\abs z=2$. By step [](#inner){.pf-ref} exactly one of them lies in $D(0,1)$, and none on $\abs z=1$.
:::
