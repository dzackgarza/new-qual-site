::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=2z^5+8z-1.
\]
Show that all five zeros of $f$ lie inside the disk $D(0,2)$ and that exactly one zero lies inside the disk $D(0,1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
p(z)=2z^5+8z-1.
\]
On $|z|=2$,
\[
|8z-1|\le 17<64=|2z^5|.
\]
By Rouché's theorem, $p$ and $2z^5$ have the same number of zeros in $D(0,2)$, counted with multiplicity, namely five.
The strict inequality also shows that $p$ has no zero on $|z|=2$.

On $|z|=1$,
\[
|2z^5-1|\le 3<8=|8z|.
\]
Again by Rouché's theorem, $p$ and $8z$ have the same number of zeros in $D(0,1)$, counted with multiplicity, namely one.
The strict inequality excludes zeros on $|z|=1$.

Thus all five zeros lie in $D(0,2)$ and exactly one lies in $D(0,1)$.
:::
