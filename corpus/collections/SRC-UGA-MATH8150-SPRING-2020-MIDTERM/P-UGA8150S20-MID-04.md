::: {.problem}
Compute
\[
I(b)=\int_0^{\pi/2}(\tan t)^{ib}\,dt
\]
for $b\in\mathbb R$.

Hint: a simple substitution reduces the integral to one treated in the homework and lectures.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $0<t<\pi/2$, $\tan t>0$, so we use the real logarithm in
\[
(\tan t)^{ib}=e^{ib\log(\tan t)}.
\]
Set $x=\tan t$. Then $dt=dx/(1+x^2)$ and
\[
I(b)=\int_0^\infty \frac{x^{ib}}{1+x^2}\,dx.
\]
Now let $u=x^2$. This gives
\[
I(b)=\frac12\int_0^\infty \frac{u^{a-1}}{1+u}\,du,
\qquad
 a=\frac{1+ib}{2}.
\]
Since $\Re a=1/2$, Euler's beta integral gives
\[
\int_0^\infty \frac{u^{a-1}}{1+u}\,du
=B(a,1-a)=\Gamma(a)\Gamma(1-a)
=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi a)}.
\]
But
\[
\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+i\frac{\pi b}{2}\right)
=\cosh\!\left(\frac{\pi b}{2}\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
I(b)=\frac{\pi}{2\cosh(\pi b/2)}
}.
\]
:::
