::: {.problem}
Assume $f$ is continuous in the region
$$
R_0\le \abs{z-a}<\infty,
\qquad
0\le \arg(z-a)\le \beta_0,
\qquad
0<\beta_0\le 2\pi,
$$
and that the limit
$$
\lim_{z\to\infty}(z-a)f(z)=A
$$
exists.
Show that
$$
\lim_{r\to+\infty}\int_{\gamma_r} f(z)\,dz=iA\beta_0,
$$
where
$$
\gamma_r:=\{z:z=a+re^{it},\ 0\le t\le\beta_0\}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
This is Problem 3 of the University of Georgia MATH 8150 midterm of Spring 2020 (`assets/attachments/Spring2020Midterm.pdf`). No published solution set is transcribed: the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #region}
For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $R\geq R_{0}$ such that $\abs{(z-a)f(z)-A}<\varepsilon$ for every $z$ with $R_{0}\leq\abs{z-a}$ , $R\leq\abs{z-a}$ and $0\leq\arg(z-a)\leq\beta_{0}$; in particular the bound holds simultaneously for all points of $\gamma_{r}$ once $r\geq R$.

::: pf-proof
This is the meaning of $\lim_{z\to\infty}(z-a)f(z)=A$ where the limit is taken in the given region: the region is the closed sector $\theset{a+se^{it}\mid s\geq R_{0},\ 0\leq t\leq\beta_{0}}$, and convergence to $A$ along it says exactly that the difference is eventually below $\varepsilon$ uniformly over that set. The arc $\gamma_{r}$ is contained in the region for $r\geq R_{0}$, so the same $R$ serves for every point of $\gamma_{r}$ and every $r\geq R$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #parametrise}
$\int_{\gamma_{r}}f(z)\,dz=i\int_{0}^{\beta_{0}}(a+re^{it}-a)f(a+re^{it})\,dt$.

::: pf-proof
On $\gamma_{r}$ one has $z=a+re^{it}$ with $t$ running over $[0,\beta_{0}]$, so $dz=ire^{it}\,dt$ and $(z-a)ire^{it}=ir^{2}e^{2it}$; hence $f(z)\,dz=if(z)re^{it}\,dt=i(z-a)f(z)\,dt$ because $re^{it}=z-a$. The integrand is continuous in $t$ since $f$ is continuous on the region and $t\mapsto a+re^{it}$ is continuous, so the integral is defined.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #bound}
$\abs{\int_{\gamma_{r}}f\,dz-iA\beta_{0}}\leq\beta_{0}\varepsilon$ for $r\geq R$.

::: pf-proof
Since $\int_{0}^{\beta_{0}}iA\,dt=iA\beta_{0}$,
$$\abs{\int_{\gamma_{r}}f\,dz-iA\beta_{0}}=\abs{\int_{0}^{\beta_{0}}\left(i(z-a)f(z)-iA\right)dt}\leq\int_{0}^{\beta_{0}}\abs{(z-a)f(z)-A}\,dt\leq\beta_{0}\varepsilon,$$
using step [](#parametrise){.pf-ref} and then step [](#region){.pf-ref} pointwise on the arc.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
$\lim_{r\to+\infty}\int_{\gamma_{r}}f(z)\,dz=iA\beta_{0}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Step [](#region){.pf-ref} supplies $R$, and step [](#bound){.pf-ref} gives $\abs{\int_{\gamma_{r}}f\,dz-iA\beta_{0}}\leq\beta_{0}\varepsilon$ for every $r\geq R$. As $\varepsilon$ was arbitrary, the limit is $iA\beta_{0}$.
:::
