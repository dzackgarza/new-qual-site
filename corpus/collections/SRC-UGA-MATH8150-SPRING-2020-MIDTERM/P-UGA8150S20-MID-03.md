::: {.problem}
Assume $f$ is continuous in the region
\[
R_0\le |z-a|<\infty,
\qquad
0\le \arg(z-a)\le \beta_0,
\qquad
0<\beta_0\le 2\pi,
\]
and that the limit
\[
\lim_{z\to\infty}(z-a)f(z)=A
\]
exists. Show that
\[
\lim_{r\to+\infty}\int_{\gamma_r} f(z)\,dz=iA\beta_0,
\]
where
\[
\gamma_r:=\{z:z=a+re^{it},\ 0\le t\le\beta_0\}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Parametrize the arc by
\[
z=a+re^{it},\qquad 0\le t\le \beta_0,
\]
so that $dz=ire^{it}\,dt$ and $z-a=re^{it}$. Then
\[
\int_{\gamma_r}f(z)\,dz
=i\int_0^{\beta_0}(z-a)f(z)\,dt.
\]
Because $(z-a)f(z)\to A$ as $z\to\infty$ within the stated sector, the convergence is uniform on the arc parameter $0\le t\le\beta_0$: for every $\varepsilon>0$, once $r$ is sufficiently large,
\[
\left|(a+re^{it}-a)f(a+re^{it})-A\right|<\varepsilon
\]
for every such $t$. Hence
\[
\left|
\int_{\gamma_r}f(z)\,dz-iA\beta_0
\right|
\le
\int_0^{\beta_0}\left|(z-a)f(z)-A\right|dt
\le \beta_0\varepsilon.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{r\to+\infty}\int_{\gamma_r}f(z)\,dz=iA\beta_0}.
\]
:::
