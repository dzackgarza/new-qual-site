::: {.problem}
1. Describe a space $X$ such that
   $$
   \pi_1(X)\cong\langle x,y,z\mid xyx^{-1}y^{-1},z^2\rangle.
   $$
2. Describe a space $X$ such that
   $$
   \pi_1(X)\cong\langle x,y\mid x^2,y^2,xyx^{-1}y^{-1}\rangle.
   $$
3. The retained source asks for connected spaces $X,Y$ such that
   $$
   H_i(X)\cong H_i(Y)\quad\text{for all }i,
   $$
   but also prints
   $$
   \pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(Y).
   $$
   This relation is retained exactly as extracted; any missing negation remains unrecovered.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For part (1), let
$$
X_1
\coloneqq
T^2\vee\RP^2.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X_1)
\cong
\langle x,y,z
\mid
xyx^{-1}y^{-1},
z^2
\rangle.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Use the standard CW structures:

- the torus has one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $x,y$, and one $2$-cell
  attached by
  $$
  xyx^{-1}y^{-1};
  $$
- the real projective plane has one $0$-cell, one $1$-cell $z$, and one
  $2$-cell attached by
  $$
  z^2.
  $$

Their wedge has one common $0$-cell and the three $1$-cells
$$
x,y,z.
$$
The two $2$-cells impose exactly the two displayed relators.
By van Kampen,
$$
\pi_1(X_1)
\cong
\langle x,y,z
\mid
[x,y],z^2
\rangle.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Equivalently,
$$
\pi_1(X_1)
\cong
\ZZ^2*\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The relation
$$
[x,y]=1
$$
makes the subgroup generated by $x,y$ equal to
$$
\ZZ^2.
$$
The generator $z$ is subject only to
$$
z^2=1.
$$
There is no relation involving $z$ together with $x$ or $y$, so the
resulting group is the free product
$$
\ZZ^2*\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For part (2), let $X_2$ be the presentation complex with one
$0$-cell, two $1$-cells $x,y$, and three $2$-cells attached along
$$
x^2,
\qquad
y^2,
\qquad
xyx^{-1}y^{-1}.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X_2)
\cong
\langle x,y
\mid
x^2,y^2,xyx^{-1}y^{-1}
\rangle.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The $1$-skeleton is
$$
S^1\vee S^1
$$
with free fundamental group
$$
F(x,y).
$$
Attaching a $2$-cell along a loop adds that loop as a relator in the
fundamental group.
Thus the three attaching maps impose precisely the three displayed
relations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The group in part (2) is the Klein four group:
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X_2)
\cong
\ZZ/2\times\ZZ/2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The relations
$$
x^2=1,
\qquad
y^2=1
$$
make both generators order at most $2$, while
$$
xyx^{-1}y^{-1}=1
$$
is equivalent to
$$
xy=yx.
$$
Hence every element has a unique representative among
$$
1,
\quad
x,
\quad
y,
\quad
xy,
$$
and the multiplication is that of
$$
\ZZ/2\times\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The literal printed statement in part (3) is satisfied, for example,
by taking
$$
\boxed{
X=Y=S^1.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Then trivially
$$
H_i(X)
\cong
H_i(Y)
$$
for every $i$, and
$$
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\pi_1(Y)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$
Thus the literal extracted condition is satisfied.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The contrastive word “but” strongly suggests that part (3) intended
$$
\pi_1(X)
\not\cong
\pi_1(Y).
$$
Under that reading, take
$$
\boxed{
X=T^2,
\qquad
Y=S^1\vee S^1\vee S^2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For the torus,
$$
H_0(T^2)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_1(T^2)\cong\ZZ^2,
\qquad
H_2(T^2)\cong\ZZ,
$$
and
$$
H_i(T^2)=0
\qquad(i>2).
$$

For
$$
Y=S^1\vee S^1\vee S^2,
$$
reduced homology of a wedge gives
$$
H_0(Y)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_1(Y)\cong\ZZ^2,
\qquad
H_2(Y)\cong\ZZ,
$$
and
$$
H_i(Y)=0
\qquad(i>2).
$$
Thus
$$
H_i(X)
\cong
H_i(Y)
$$
for every $i$.

However,
$$
\pi_1(T^2)
\cong
\ZZ^2,
$$
while
$$
\pi_1(S^1\vee S^1\vee S^2)
\cong
F_2,
$$
because the simply connected $S^2$ summand does not affect the fundamental
group.
The group
$$
\ZZ^2
$$
is abelian, while
$$
F_2
$$
is nonabelian, so they are not isomorphic.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the complete response is:

- part (1):
  $$
  X=T^2\vee\RP^2;
  $$
- part (2): the presentation $2$-complex with two $1$-cells and three
  $2$-cells attached by
  $$
  x^2,
  \quad
  y^2,
  \quad
  [x,y];
  $$
- part (3), literally:
  $$
  X=Y=S^1;
  $$
- part (3), under the likely intended missing-negation reading:
  $$
  X=T^2,
  \qquad
  Y=S^1\vee S^1\vee S^2.
  $$


::: pf-proof
