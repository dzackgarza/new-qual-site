::: {.problem}
How many subfields does the splitting field of \(x^5-2\) over \(\mathbb Q\) have?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\alpha\coloneqq2^{1/5},
\qquad
\zeta\coloneqq e^{2\pi i/5},
$$
and let $L$ be the splitting field of $x^5-2$ over $\QQ$.

<1>1. One has
$$
L=\QQ(\alpha,\zeta)
$$
and
$$
[L:\QQ]=20.
$$

::: {.proof}
The five roots of $x^5-2$ are
$$
\alpha,\zeta\alpha,\zeta^2\alpha,\zeta^3\alpha,\zeta^4\alpha,
$$
so the splitting field is $\QQ(\alpha,\zeta)$.

The polynomial $x^5-2$ is Eisenstein at $2$, hence
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=5.
$$
Also
$$
[\QQ(\zeta):\QQ]=\varphi(5)=4.
$$
The degree of
$$
\QQ(\alpha)\cap\QQ(\zeta)
$$
over $\QQ$ divides both $5$ and $4$, hence is $1$. Therefore the two
extensions are linearly disjoint over $\QQ$, and
$$
[L:\QQ]
=
5\cdot4
=
20.
$$
:::

<1>2. The Galois group $G\coloneqq\Gal(L/\QQ)$ is generated by
automorphisms $\sigma$ and $\tau$ satisfying
$$
\sigma^5=\tau^4=1,
\qquad
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^2.
$$
In particular,
$$
G\cong C_5\rtimes C_4.
$$

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
\sigma(\alpha)=\zeta\alpha,
\qquad
\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta,
$$
and
$$
\tau(\alpha)=\alpha,
\qquad
\tau(\zeta)=\zeta^2.
$$
By step <1>1,
$$
[L:\QQ(\zeta)]=5
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[L:\QQ(\alpha)]=4.
$$
Thus $x^5-2$ remains the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over
$\QQ(\zeta)$, so the assignment
$\alpha\mapsto\zeta\alpha$ defines a $\QQ(\zeta)$-automorphism
$\sigma$ of $L$. Likewise, the linear disjointness in step <1>1 lets the
cyclotomic automorphism
$$
\zeta\longmapsto\zeta^2
$$
extend to an automorphism $\tau$ of $L$ that fixes $\QQ(\alpha)$.
Their orders are respectively $5$ and $4$. Moreover,
$$
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}(\alpha)
=
\zeta^2\alpha
=
\sigma^2(\alpha),
$$
and both sides fix $\zeta$, so
$$
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^2.
$$

The subgroup generated by $\sigma$ and $\tau$ contains the $20$ distinct
elements
$$
\sigma^i\tau^j,
\qquad
0\leq i<5,
\quad
0\leq j<4.
$$
Indeed, equality of two such elements, evaluated first on $\zeta$, forces
the two exponents $j$ to agree modulo $4$; evaluation on $\alpha$ then
forces the exponents $i$ to agree modulo $5$.
By step <1>1, the whole Galois group has order $20$, so these automorphisms
generate all of $G$.
:::

<1>3. The group
$$
N\coloneqq\langle\sigma\rangle
$$
is the unique subgroup of $G$ of order $5$.

::: {.proof}
The number $n_5$ of Sylow $5$-subgroups satisfies
$$
n_5\equiv1\pmod5
$$
and
$$
n_5\mid4.
$$
Hence $n_5=1$. Since $N$ has order $5$, it is this unique subgroup.
:::

<1>4. There is exactly one subgroup of each of the orders
$$
5,\ 10,\ 20.
$$

::: {.proof}
Any subgroup whose order is divisible by $5$ contains a Sylow
$5$-subgroup, hence contains $N$ by step <1>3. By the correspondence
theorem, subgroups of $G$ containing $N$ correspond bijectively to
subgroups of
$$
G/N\cong C_4.
$$
The cyclic group $C_4$ has exactly one subgroup of each order $1,2,4$.
Their inverse images have orders $5,10,20$, respectively, so there is
exactly one subgroup of each of those orders.
:::

<1>5. The group $G$ has exactly five subgroups of order $4$.

::: {.proof}
These are the Sylow $2$-subgroups. Their number $n_2$ satisfies
$$
n_2\equiv1\pmod2
$$
and
$$
n_2\mid5,
$$
so $n_2$ is either $1$ or $5$.

If $n_2=1$, the Sylow $2$-subgroup $P$ would be normal. The subgroup
$N$ is also normal by step <1>3. Since their orders are coprime,
$$
N\cap P=\{1\},
$$
and normality would give
$$
[N,P]\subseteq N\cap P=\{1\}.
$$
Thus $N$ and $P$ would commute. Since $\langle\tau\rangle$ has order
$4$, uniqueness would give $P=\langle\tau\rangle$. But the relation in
step <1>2 gives
$$
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^2\neq\sigma,
$$
so the action is nontrivial. Therefore $n_2\neq1$, and hence
$$
n_2=5.
$$
:::

<1>6. The group $G$ has exactly five subgroups of order $2$.

::: {.proof}
Every element of $G$ has a unique form
$$
\sigma^i\tau^j
$$
with $0\leq i<5$ and $0\leq j<4$. An element of order $2$ must map to
an element of order dividing $2$ in
$$
G/N\cong C_4.
$$
It cannot lie in $N$, whose nonidentity elements have order $5$, so it must
have the form
$$
\sigma^i\tau^2.
$$

Since conjugation by $\tau^2$ sends $\sigma$ to $\sigma^4=\sigma^{-1}$,
$$
(\sigma^i\tau^2)^2
=
\sigma^i
\bigl(\tau^2\sigma^i\tau^{-2}\bigr)
\tau^4
=
\sigma^i\sigma^{-i}
=
1.
$$
Thus the five elements
$$
\sigma^i\tau^2,
\qquad
0\leq i<5,
$$
are precisely the five involutions of $G$. Each generates a distinct
subgroup of order $2$.
:::

<1>7. The total number of subgroups of $G$ is
$$
\boxed{14}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By Lagrange's theorem, a subgroup order divides $20$, so the only possible
orders are
$$
1,2,4,5,10,20.
$$
There is one trivial subgroup, five subgroups of order $2$ by step <1>6,
five subgroups of order $4$ by step <1>5, and one subgroup of each of the
orders $5,10,20$ by step <1>4. Therefore
$$
1+5+5+1+1+1=14.
$$
:::

<1>8. The splitting field $L$ has exactly
$$
\boxed{14}
$$
subfields containing $\QQ$.

::: {.proof}
The extension $L/\QQ$ is finite Galois. By the fundamental theorem of
Galois theory, its intermediate fields are in bijection with the subgroups
of $G=\Gal(L/\QQ)$. Step <1>7 counts those subgroups.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 gives the requested number of subfields.
:::
