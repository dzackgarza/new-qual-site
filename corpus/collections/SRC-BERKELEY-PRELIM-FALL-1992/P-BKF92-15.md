::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the group of real matrices
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&a^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a>0,
\]
and let $N$ be the subgroup with $a=1$.

(a) Prove that $N\triangleleft G$ and that $G/N\cong(\mathbb R,+)$.

(b) Find a proper normal subgroup of $G$ which properly contains $N$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For
$$
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&a^{-1}
\end{pmatrix}
\in G,
$$
define
$$
\phi(M)\coloneqq\log a.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map
$$
\phi:G\longrightarrow(\RR,+)
$$
is a group homomorphism.

::: pf-proof

If
$$
M_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&a^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
c&d\\
0&c^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},
$$
then the upper-left entry of $M_1M_2$ is $ac$. Hence
$$
\phi(M_1M_2)
=
\log(ac)
=
\log a+\log c
=
\phi(M_1)+\phi(M_2).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The homomorphism $\phi$ is surjective and
$$
\ker\phi=N.
$$

::: pf-proof

For every $t\in\RR$, the diagonal matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
e^t&0\\
0&e^{-t}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
lies in $G$ and maps to $t$, so $\phi$ is surjective.

Also,
$$
\phi(M)=0
\iff
\log a=0
\iff
a=1,
$$
which is exactly the defining condition for membership in $N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The subgroup $N$ is normal in $G$ and
$$
\boxed{G/N\cong(\RR,+)}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $N=\ker\phi$, so $N\triangleleft G$. The first isomorphism theorem and surjectivity of $\phi$ give
$$
G/N
\cong
\operatorname{im}\phi
=
(\RR,+).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Define
$$
H\coloneqq\phi^{-1}(\ZZ).
$$
Then $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$.

::: pf-proof

The subgroup $\ZZ$ is normal in the abelian group $(\RR,+)$. The inverse image of a normal subgroup under a group homomorphism is normal. Thus $H\triangleleft G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The inclusions
$$
N\subsetneq H\subsetneq G
$$
are both strict.

::: pf-proof

Since $0\in\ZZ$, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
N=\ker\phi\subseteq H.
$$
The matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
e&0\\
0&e^{-1}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
lies in $H$ because its image is $1$, but it does not lie in $N$, so the first inclusion is strict.

The matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{1/2}&0\\
0&e^{-1/2}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
lies in $G$ but not in $H$, because its image is $1/2\notin\ZZ$. Hence the second inclusion is strict.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Therefore a proper normal subgroup properly containing $N$ is
$$
\boxed{H=\phi^{-1}(\ZZ)}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} establish normality and both strict inclusions.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s6){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
