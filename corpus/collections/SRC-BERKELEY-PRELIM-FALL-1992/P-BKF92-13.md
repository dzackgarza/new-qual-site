::: {.problem}
Evaluate
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x(1+x^2)}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For $0\le a\le1$, define
$$
I(a)\coloneqq
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin(ax)}{x(1+x^2)}\,dx,
$$
where the integrand is interpreted at $x=0$ by its removable value $a$.

<1>1. The function $I$ is differentiable and
$$
I'(a)
=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\cos(ax)}{1+x^2}\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
For fixed $x$,
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}
\frac{\sin(ax)}{x(1+x^2)}
=
\frac{\cos(ax)}{1+x^2}.
$$
Moreover,
$$
\left|\frac{\cos(ax)}{1+x^2}\right|
\le
\frac1{1+x^2},
$$
and the right-hand side is integrable on $\RR$. Differentiation under the integral sign follows from dominated convergence.
:::

<1>2. For every $a\ge0$,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\cos(ax)}{1+x^2}\,dx
=
\pi e^{-a}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $a>0$, integrate
$$
F_a(z)=\frac{e^{iaz}}{1+z^2}
$$
over the positively oriented upper semicircle of radius $R>1$. The only enclosed pole is $z=i$, with residue
$$
\operatorname{Res}(F_a;i)
=
\frac{e^{ia i}}{2i}
=
\frac{e^{-a}}{2i}.
$$
On the semicircular arc,
$$
\abs{e^{iaz}}=e^{-a\operatorname{Im}z}\le1,
$$
while $\abs{1+z^2}\ge R^2-1$. Hence the arc integral has absolute value at most
$$
\frac{\pi R}{R^2-1}\longrightarrow0.
$$
The residue theorem therefore gives
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{iax}}{1+x^2}\,dx
=
2\pi i\frac{e^{-a}}{2i}
=
\pi e^{-a}.
$$
Its imaginary part is zero because $\sin(ax)/(1+x^2)$ is odd, so the real part is the displayed cosine integral.

For $a=0$, the identity is
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\pi.
$$
:::

<1>3. For $0\le a\le1$,
$$
I'(a)=\pi e^{-a}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>1 and <1>2.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
I(0)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The numerator $\sin(0\cdot x)$ is identically zero.
:::

<1>5. The requested integral is
$$
\boxed{\pi(1-e^{-1})}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>3--<1>4 and the fundamental theorem of calculus,
$$
\begin{aligned}
I(1)
&=I(0)+\int_0^1 I'(a)\,da\\
&=\pi\int_0^1 e^{-a}\,da\\
&=\pi(1-e^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
$$
By definition, $I(1)$ is the integral in the problem.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the requested value.
:::
