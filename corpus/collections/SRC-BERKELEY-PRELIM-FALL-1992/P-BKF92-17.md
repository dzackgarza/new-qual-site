::: {.problem}
Let $s\in\mathbb R$ and define on $\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]$
\[
u(re^{i\theta})=r^s\cos(s\theta),
\qquad r>0,\ -\pi<\theta<\pi.
\]
Prove that $u$ is harmonic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\Omega\coloneqq\CC\setminus(-\infty,0].
$$

<1>1. The principal logarithm
$$
\Log z=\log r+i\theta,
\qquad
z=re^{i\theta},\ -\pi<\theta<\pi,
$$
is holomorphic on $\Omega$.

::: {.proof}
This is the standard holomorphic branch of the logarithm on the slit plane $\Omega$.
:::

<1>2. The function
$$
F(z)\coloneqq e^{s\Log z}
$$
is holomorphic on $\Omega$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $\Log$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$. Multiplication by the real constant $s$ and composition with the entire exponential function preserve holomorphy.
:::

<1>3. For $z=re^{i\theta}\in\Omega$,
$$
\operatorname{Re}F(z)=r^s\cos(s\theta)=u(z).
$$

::: {.proof}
Using the definition of the principal logarithm,
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(re^{i\theta})
&=e^{s(\log r+i\theta)}\\
&=r^s e^{is\theta}\\
&=r^s\bigl(\cos(s\theta)+i\sin(s\theta)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking real parts gives the identity.
:::

<1>4. The function $u$ is harmonic on $\Omega$.

::: {.proof}
The real part of a holomorphic function is harmonic. By steps <1>2--<1>3, $u=\operatorname{Re}F$ with $F$ holomorphic on $\Omega$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\Delta u=0\text{ on }\Omega}.
$$
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
