::: {.problem}
How many Sylow $2$-subgroups does the dihedral group $D_n$ of order $2n$ have when $n$ is odd?
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the presentation
$$
D_n
=
\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle.
$$

<1>1. Every Sylow $2$-subgroup of $D_n$ has order $2$.

::: {.proof}
Since $n$ is odd,
$$
\abs{D_n}=2n
$$
is divisible by $2$ but not by $4$. Hence the largest power of $2$ dividing the group order is $2$.
:::

<1>2. For every $k=0,1,\ldots,n-1$, the element $sr^k$ has order $2$.

::: {.proof}
The relation $srs=r^{-1}$ implies
$$
sr^ks=r^{-k}.
$$
Therefore
$$
(sr^k)^2
=
sr^ksr^k
=
r^{-k}r^k
=1.
$$
The element $sr^k$ is not the identity because it lies outside the rotation subgroup $\langle r\rangle$.
:::

<1>3. The subgroups
$$
\langle sr^k\rangle,
\qquad
0\le k<n,
$$
are $n$ distinct Sylow $2$-subgroups.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1--<1>2, each subgroup $\langle sr^k\rangle$ has the required Sylow order $2$. The elements $sr^k$ are pairwise distinct, and a subgroup of order $2$ has exactly one nonidentity element. Hence the generated subgroups are pairwise distinct.
:::

<1>4. There are no other Sylow $2$-subgroups.

::: {.proof}
Every element of $D_n$ is either a rotation $r^k$ or a reflection $sr^k$. Since $\langle r\rangle$ has odd order $n$, no nonidentity rotation has order $2$. Therefore every subgroup of order $2$ is generated by one of the reflections listed in step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Hence the number of Sylow $2$-subgroups is
$$
\boxed{n}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 produces $n$ such subgroups, and step <1>4 proves the list is exhaustive.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the requested number.
:::
