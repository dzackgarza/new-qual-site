::: {.problem}
How many Sylow $2$-subgroups does the dihedral group $D_n$ of order $2n$ have when $n$ is odd?
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the presentation
$$
D_n
=
\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Every Sylow $2$-subgroup of $D_n$ has order $2$.

::: pf-proof

Since $n$ is odd,
$$
\abs{D_n}=2n
$$
is divisible by $2$ but not by $4$. Hence the largest power of $2$ dividing the group order is $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $k=0,1,\ldots,n-1$, the element $sr^k$ has order $2$.

::: pf-proof

The relation $srs=r^{-1}$ implies
$$
sr^ks=r^{-k}.
$$
Therefore
$$
(sr^k)^2
=
sr^ksr^k
=
r^{-k}r^k
=1.
$$
The element $sr^k$ is not the identity because it lies outside the rotation subgroup $\langle r\rangle$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The subgroups
$$
\langle sr^k\rangle,
\qquad
0\le k<n,
$$
are $n$ distinct Sylow $2$-subgroups.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, each subgroup $\langle sr^k\rangle$ has the required Sylow order $2$. The elements $sr^k$ are pairwise distinct, and a subgroup of order $2$ has exactly one nonidentity element. Hence the generated subgroups are pairwise distinct.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

There are no other Sylow $2$-subgroups.

::: pf-proof

Every element of $D_n$ is either a rotation $r^k$ or a reflection $sr^k$. Since $\langle r\rangle$ has odd order $n$, no nonidentity rotation has order $2$. Therefore every subgroup of order $2$ is generated by one of the reflections listed in step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Hence the number of Sylow $2$-subgroups is
$$
\boxed{n}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} produces $n$ such subgroups, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves the list is exhaustive.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives the requested number.

:::
