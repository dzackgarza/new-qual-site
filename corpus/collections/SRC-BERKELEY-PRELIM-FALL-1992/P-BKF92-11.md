::: {.problem}
Let $V$ be finite-dimensional over a field $F$, and let
\[
B:V\times V\to F
\]
be bilinear, not necessarily symmetric. Define
\[
V_1=\{x\in V:B(x,y)=0\text{ for all }y\in V\},
\]
\[
V_2=\{y\in V:B(x,y)=0\text{ for all }x\in V\}.
\]
Prove that
\[
\dim V_1=\dim V_2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $n=\dim_F V$, and fix a basis of $V$.

<1>1. There is a matrix $A\in M_n(F)$ such that, in the chosen coordinates,
$$
B(x,y)=x^TAy
$$
for all $x,y\in V$.

::: {.proof}
If $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is the chosen basis, define
$$
A_{ij}\coloneqq B(e_i,e_j).
$$
Bilinearity then gives the displayed formula for arbitrary coordinate vectors $x$ and $y$.
:::

<1>2. In these coordinates,
$$
V_1=\ker A^T.
$$

::: {.proof}
An element $x$ lies in $V_1$ exactly when
$$
x^TAy=0
$$
for every $y$. This is equivalent to the row vector $x^TA$ being zero, hence to
$$
A^Tx=0.
$$
:::

<1>3. In these coordinates,
$$
V_2=\ker A.
$$

::: {.proof}
An element $y$ lies in $V_2$ exactly when
$$
x^TAy=0
$$
for every $x$. This is equivalent to
$$
Ay=0.
$$
:::

<1>4. The kernels of $A$ and $A^T$ have the same dimension.

::: {.proof}
The rank of a matrix equals the rank of its transpose. Hence, by rank-nullity,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dim\ker A
&=n-\operatorname{rank}A\\
&=n-\operatorname{rank}A^T\\
&=\dim\ker A^T.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\dim V_1=\dim V_2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>2--<1>4.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required equality.
:::
:::
