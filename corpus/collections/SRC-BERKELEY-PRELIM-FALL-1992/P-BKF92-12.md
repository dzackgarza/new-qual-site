::: {.problem}
Let $(f_n)$ be a sequence of real-valued $C^1$ functions on $[0,1]$ such that for every $n$,
\[
|f_n'(x)|\le\frac1{\sqrt{x}}
\qquad(0<x\le1),
\]
and
\[
\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx=0.
\]
Prove that $(f_n)$ has a subsequence converging uniformly on $[0,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $n$ and every $0\le x<y\le1$,
$$
\abs{f_n(y)-f_n(x)}
\le
2(\sqrt y-\sqrt x)
\le
2\sqrt{y-x}.
$$

::: pf-proof

First suppose $x>0$. By the fundamental theorem of calculus and the derivative bound,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{f_n(y)-f_n(x)}
&\le\int_x^y\abs{f_n'(t)}\,dt\\
&\le\int_x^y t^{-1/2}\,dt\\
&=2(\sqrt y-\sqrt x).
\end{aligned}
$$
For $x=0$, apply this estimate with $x=\varepsilon>0$ and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, using continuity of $f_n$ at $0$.

Finally,
$$
\sqrt y
=
\sqrt{x+(y-x)}
\le
\sqrt x+\sqrt{y-x},
$$
which gives the second inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The family $(f_n)$ is equicontinuous on $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{f_n(y)-f_n(x)}
\le
2\sqrt{\abs{y-x}}
$$
for every $n$ and every $x,y\in[0,1]$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choosing
$$
\delta=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^2
$$
makes the right-hand side less than $\varepsilon$ whenever $\abs{x-y}<\delta$, uniformly in $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every $n$, there exists $c_n\in[0,1]$ such that
$$
f_n(c_n)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

If $f_n$ never vanished, continuity on the connected interval $[0,1]$ would force it to be everywhere strictly positive or everywhere strictly negative. Its integral would then have the same strict sign, contradicting
$$
\int_0^1f_n(x)\,dx=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The family $(f_n)$ is uniformly bounded:
$$
\abs{f_n(x)}\le2
$$
for all $n$ and all $x\in[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof

Choose $c_n$ as in step [](#s3){.pf-ref}. Then step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{f_n(x)}
=
\abs{f_n(x)-f_n(c_n)}
\le
2\sqrt{\abs{x-c_n}}
\le2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The sequence $(f_n)$ has a subsequence converging uniformly on $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref}, the sequence is equicontinuous and uniformly bounded on the compact interval $[0,1]$. The Arzelà--Ascoli theorem therefore gives a uniformly convergent subsequence.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
