::: {.problem}
Let $f,g:\mathbb R\to(0,\infty)$ be continuous with $g\le f$. Suppose the initial-value problem
\[
x'=f(x),
\qquad
x(0)=0,
\]
has a solution defined on all of $\mathbb R$. Prove that
\[
x'=g(x),
\qquad
x(0)=0,
\]
also has a solution defined on all of $\mathbb R$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
F(u)\coloneqq\int_0^u\frac{ds}{f(s)}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The function $F$ is strictly increasing and satisfies
$$
F'(u)=\frac1{f(u)}>0.
$$

::: pf-proof

The function $f$ is positive and continuous, so $1/f$ is positive and continuous. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives the derivative formula, and positivity of the derivative gives strict increase.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $x:\RR\to\RR$ is the assumed global solution of
$$
x'=f(x),
\qquad
x(0)=0,
$$
then
$$
F(x(t))=t
$$
for every $t\in\RR$.

::: pf-proof

By the chain rule and step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\frac{d}{dt}F(x(t))
=
F'(x(t))x'(t)
=
\frac{1}{f(x(t))}f(x(t))
=1.
$$
At $t=0$,
$$
F(x(0))=F(0)=0.
$$
Therefore $F(x(t))=t$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The function $F$ maps $\RR$ onto $\RR$, and hence
$$
\lim_{u\to\infty}F(u)=\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{u\to-\infty}F(u)=-\infty.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} shows that every $t\in\RR$ is a value of $F$, namely $F(x(t))$. Thus $F$ is onto. Since $F$ is strictly increasing by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, surjectivity forces the stated endpoint limits.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Define
$$
G(u)\coloneqq\int_0^u\frac{ds}{g(s)}.
$$
Then
$$
\lim_{u\to\infty}G(u)=\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{u\to-\infty}G(u)=-\infty.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $0<g\le f$,
$$
\frac1g\ge\frac1f.
$$
For $u>0$,
$$
G(u)
=
\int_0^u\frac{ds}{g(s)}
\ge
\int_0^u\frac{ds}{f(s)}
=F(u),
$$
so step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives $G(u)\to\infty$.

For $u<0$,
$$
G(u)
=
-\int_u^0\frac{ds}{g(s)}
\le
-\int_u^0\frac{ds}{f(s)}
=F(u),
$$
so step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives $G(u)\to-\infty$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The function $G:\RR\to\RR$ is a $C^1$ bijection with $G'(u)>0$ everywhere.

::: pf-proof

As in step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
G'(u)=\frac1{g(u)}>0,
$$
so $G$ is strictly increasing. Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives both endpoint limits, hence $G$ is onto. Strict increase gives injectivity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Define
$$
y(t)\coloneqq G^{-1}(t).
$$
Then $y$ is defined for every $t\in\RR$ and satisfies
$$
y'=g(y),
\qquad
y(0)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, the inverse is defined on all of $\RR$. Since $G'$ is everywhere nonzero, the inverse function theorem gives
$$
y'(t)
=
\frac{1}{G'(y(t))}
=
g(y(t)).
$$
Also $G(0)=0$, so $y(0)=G^{-1}(0)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

Hence the initial-value problem
$$
x'=g(x),
\qquad
x(0)=0
$$
has a solution defined on all of $\RR$.

::: pf-proof

The function $y$ constructed in step [](#s6){.pf-ref} is such a solution.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s7){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
