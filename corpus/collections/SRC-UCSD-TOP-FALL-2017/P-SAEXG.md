::: {.problem}
Up to equivalence, how many path-connected $2$-fold covering spaces does $S^1 \vee \RP^2$ have?
Describe the total spaces of these coverings.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X = S^1 \vee \RP^2$.

::: pf

::: pf-step
Compute the fundamental group of $X = S^1 \vee \RP^2$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
$S^1$ has fundamental group $\pi_1(S^1, s_0) \cong \langle a \rangle \cong \ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
The circle has fundamental group $\ZZ$, generated by the class of a loop that goes once around.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\RP^2$ has fundamental group $\pi_1(\RP^2, p_0) \cong \langle b \mid b^2 = 1 \rangle \cong \ZZ/2\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
The projective plane has fundamental group $\ZZ/2$, generated by the class of a line, which is a loop of order $2$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since $S^1$ and $\RP^2$ are locally contractible CW complexes, their wedge sum $X = S^1 \vee \RP^2$ at basepoint $x_0$ has fundamental group given by the free product: $$G = \pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \pi_1(S^1, s_0) * \pi_1(\RP^2, p_0) \cong \langle a, b \mid b^2 = 1 \rangle \cong \ZZ * (\ZZ/2\ZZ).$$

::: pf-proof
The Seifert–van Kampen theorem for the wedge of two nice spaces gives the free product of their fundamental groups, which is $\ZZ * (\ZZ/2\ZZ)$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}
Classification of path-connected 2-fold covering spaces.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
By covering space theory, isomorphism classes of path-connected 2-fold covering spaces of $X$ correspond bijectively to index 2 subgroups $H \le G$, which in turn correspond bijectively to non-trivial homomorphisms $\phi \colon G \to \ZZ/2\ZZ = \{\pm 1\}$ via $H = \ker(\phi)$.

::: pf-proof
The classification of covering spaces gives a bijection between connected $n$-fold covers and subgroups of index $n$, and an index-two subgroup is normal, so conjugacy plays no role; index-two subgroups correspond to surjective homomorphisms onto $\ZZ/2$ through their kernels, and a non-trivial homomorphism into a group of order two is surjective.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
A homomorphism $\phi \colon G \to \{\pm 1\}$ is determined uniquely by the images of the generators $a$ and $b$:

- $\phi(a) \in \{\pm 1\}$,

- $\phi(b) \in \{\pm 1\}$ (which always satisfies $(\phi(b))^2 = 1$).

::: pf-proof
A homomorphism out of $\langle a,b\mid b^2=1\rangle$ is determined by the images of the two generators, subject only to the relation; in $\ZZ/2$ every element squares to $1$, so the relation imposes no condition.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-3}
There are $2 \times 2 = 4$ homomorphisms in total.
Exactly 3 of them are non-trivial (surjective onto $\ZZ/2\ZZ$):

- Case 1: $\phi_1(a) = -1, \phi_1(b) = 1$.
  Subgroup $H_1 = \ker \phi_1 = \langle a^2, b, a b a^{-1} \rangle$.

- Case 2: $\phi_2(a) = 1, \phi_2(b) = -1$.
  Subgroup $H_2 = \ker \phi_2 = \langle a, b a b^{-1}, b^2 \rangle$.

- Case 3: $\phi_3(a) = -1, \phi_3(b) = -1$.
  Subgroup $H_3 = \ker \phi_3 = \langle a^2, b^2, ab, ba \rangle$.

::: pf-proof
Each generator has two possible images, giving $2\times2=4$ homomorphisms; the one sending both generators to $1$ is trivial, and the other three are surjective, with the kernels displayed.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Thus, there are exactly 3 distinct path-connected 2-fold covering spaces of $S^1 \vee \RP^2$.

::: pf-proof
The Galois correspondence identifies connected 2-fold covers with index-2 subgroups, equivalently with surjective homomorphisms to $\ZZ/2\ZZ$; there are exactly 3 such homomorphisms, listed in step [](#s2-3){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}
Identify the total space $\widetilde{X}_1$ for $\phi_1$ ($\phi_1(a) = -1, \phi_1(b) = 1$).

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
The loop $a$ has non-trivial monodromy (swaps the two sheets), while $b$ lifts to closed loops on both sheets.

::: pf-proof
The monodromy of a loop is the permutation $\phi$ assigns to its class: $\phi_1(a)=-1$ exchanges the two points of the fibre and $\phi_1(b)=1$ fixes them.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The basepoint lifts to 2 points $x_1, x_2$.

::: pf-proof
A two-fold cover has exactly two points over the basepoint.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The circle $S^1$ lifts to a single circle of twice the length connecting $x_1$ and $x_2$ (a single circle $\widetilde{S}^1 \cong S^1$).

::: pf-proof
Lifting the circle from $x_1$, one turn ends at $x_2$ because $\phi_1(a)=-1$, and a second turn returns to $x_1$, so the lift is a single circle of twice the length.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The $\RP^2$ lifts to two disjoint copies of $\RP^2$ (the monodromy of $\phi_1$ on the $\RP^2$ factor is the identity), one attached at $x_1$ and one attached at $x_2$.

::: pf-proof
Since $\phi_1(b)=1$, the lift of the projective plane starting at either point above the base is a closed loop, so it gives one copy over each sheet.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Thus, the total space is a circle with an $\RP^2$ attached at each of two distinct points: $$\widetilde{X}_1 \cong S^1 \cup_{p_1} \RP^2 \cup_{p_2} \RP^2 \simeq S^1 \vee \RP^2 \vee \RP^2.$$

::: pf-proof
Since $\phi_1(a) = -1$, the circle lifts to a single circle of double length; since $\phi_1(b) = 1$, each copy of $\RP^2$ lifts to two disjoint copies, one over each sheet, attached at the two basepoint lifts.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}
Identify the total space $\widetilde{X}_2$ for $\phi_2$ ($\phi_2(a) = 1, \phi_2(b) = -1$).

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
The loop $a$ has trivial monodromy (lifts to two separate circles), while $b$ has non-trivial monodromy (the standard 2-fold cover of $\RP^2$ is $S^2$).

::: pf-proof
The monodromy of a loop is $\phi$ applied to its class: $\phi_2(a)=1$ fixes both points of the fibre, and $\phi_2(b)=-1$ exchanges them.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The basepoint lifts to 2 points $x_1, x_2$.

::: pf-proof
A two-fold cover has exactly two points over the basepoint.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\RP^2$ lifts to the connected 2-fold cover $S^2$, with antipodal basepoints $x_1, x_2$.

::: pf-proof
The connected two-fold cover of $\RP^2$ is $S^2\to\RP^2$ by identification of antipodal points, whose two points over the base are antipodal.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The circle $S^1$ lifts to two disjoint circles, one attached at $x_1$ and one attached at $x_2$.

::: pf-proof
Since $\phi_2(a)=1$, the lift of the circle from either point above the base is closed, giving one circle attached at $x_1$ and one at $x_2$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Thus, the total space is the 2-sphere $S^2$ with a circle attached at each of two antipodal points: $$\widetilde{X}_2 \cong S^2 \cup_{x_1} S^1 \cup_{x_2} S^1 \simeq S^2 \vee S^1 \vee S^1.$$

::: pf-proof
Since $\phi_2(b) = -1$, the 2-fold cover of $\RP^2$ is $S^2$; since $\phi_2(a) = 1$, the circle lifts to two disjoint circles, one attached at each of the two antipodal basepoints.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}
Identify the total space $\widetilde X_3$ for $\phi_3(a)=\phi_3(b)=-1$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
The preimage of the $\RP^2$ summand is its connected double cover $S^2$, and the two points over the wedge point are antipodal points $x_1,x_2\in S^2$.

::: pf-proof
The restriction of the monodromy to $\pi_1(\RP^2)=\langle b\rangle$ is nontrivial, so the restricted cover is the universal double cover $S^2\to\RP^2$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The preimage of the $S^1$ summand is its connected double cover, hence a circle $C\cong S^1$ containing the two fiber points $x_1,x_2$.

::: pf-proof
The monodromy of $a$ swaps the two sheets, so the restricted $2$-fold cover of $S^1$ is connected. Its fiber over the wedge point consists of the two points where $C$ meets the lifted $S^2$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Thus the total space is
$$
\widetilde X_3\cong S^2\cup_{\{x_1,x_2\}} C,
$$
where the two selected points of $C$ are identified with the two antipodal points of $S^2$.

::: pf-proof
The original wedge is obtained by identifying the basepoints of the two summands, so its covering is obtained by gluing the restricted covers along the two points lying over that basepoint.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
This space has homotopy type
$$\boxed{\widetilde X_3\simeq S^2\vee S^1\vee S^1}.$$

::: pf-proof
The two points $x_1,x_2$ divide $C$ into two arcs $\alpha,\beta$, each joining $x_1$ to $x_2$. Choose an embedded arc $\gamma\subset S^2$ joining $x_1$ to $x_2$. Collapsing the contractible tree $\gamma$ to a point converts each of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ into a loop and leaves the sphere homotopy equivalent to $S^2$. Thus the quotient is $S^2\vee S^1\vee S^1$, and collapsing a contractible subcomplex is a homotopy equivalence here.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore there are exactly three connected double covers, with total spaces
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde X_1&\cong S^1\text{ with two copies of }\RP^2\text{ attached at the two fiber points},\\
\widetilde X_2&\cong S^2\text{ with one circle attached at each of the two fiber points},\\
\widetilde X_3&\cong S^2\cup_{\{x_1,x_2\}}S^1.
\end{aligned}}
$$
Their homotopy types are respectively
$$
S^1\vee\RP^2\vee\RP^2,
\qquad
S^2\vee S^1\vee S^1,
\qquad
S^2\vee S^1\vee S^1.
$$

::: pf-proof
The three nonzero homomorphisms $G\to\ZZ/2$ in step [](#s2){.pf-ref} give all index-$2$ subgroups, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, and step [](#s5){.pf-ref} identify their corresponding total spaces.
:::
