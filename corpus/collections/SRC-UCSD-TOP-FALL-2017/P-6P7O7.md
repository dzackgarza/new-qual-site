Let $X$ be an arbitrary topological space, and compute $\pi_1(\Sigma X)$.

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Let $X$ be an arbitrary topological space.
Prove that the suspension $\Sigma X$ has trivial fundamental group, $\pi_1(\Sigma X) = 1$, provided $\Sigma X$ is path-connected (or for any choice of basepoint in its path component).

<1>1. Definition of the unreduced suspension $\Sigma X$.
<2>1. $\Sigma X = (X \times [-1, 1]) / \sim$, where $(x, 1) \sim (x', 1)$ (collapsed to the north pole $N$) and $(x, -1) \sim (x', -1)$ (collapsed to the south pole $S$) for all $x, x' \in X$.
::: {.proof}
<2>2. This is the definition of the unreduced suspension: the two ends $X \times \{1\}$ and $X \times \{-1\}$ are each collapsed to a single point.
:::

<1>2. Decompose $\Sigma X$ into two open sets $U$ and $V$.
<2>1. Let $U = (\Sigma X) \setminus \{S\} = (X \times (-1, 1]) / \sim$.
<2>2. Let $V = (\Sigma X) \setminus \{N\} = (X \times [-1, 1)) / \sim$.
<2>3. $U$ and $V$ are open in $\Sigma X$ because $\{S\}$ and $\{N\}$ are closed singletons in the quotient topology.
<2>4. $U \cup V = \Sigma X$ since $N \neq S$ in $\Sigma X$ (assuming $[-1, 1]$ has distinct endpoints).
::: {.proof}
<2>5. Removing the south pole leaves $U$, and removing the north pole leaves $V$; since the two poles are distinct, every point of $\Sigma X$ lies in at least one of $U$ and $V$.
:::

<1>3. $U$ and $V$ are contractible spaces, hence simply connected.
<2>1. $U$ is the open cone $C_+ X \setminus \{S\}$, which deformation retracts to the cone vertex $N$ along straight lines in the interval coordinate: $H_U([(x, t)], s) = [(x, (1-s)t + s)]$ for $s \in [0, 1]$.
At $s = 1$, $H_U([(x, t)], 1) = [(x, 1)] = N$.
<2>2. Similarly, $V$ deformation retracts to the south cone vertex $S$ via $H_V([(x, t)], s) = [(x, (1-s)t - s)]$.
<2>3. Contractible spaces are path-connected and have trivial fundamental group: $\pi_1(U) = 1$ and $\pi_1(V) = 1$.
::: {.proof}
<2>4. The homotopies $H_U$ and $H_V$ are well-defined and continuous on the quotient spaces, and each collapses its cone to the corresponding vertex, so $U$ and $V$ are contractible.
:::

<1>4. Analyze the intersection $U \cap V = X \times (-1, 1)$.
<2>1. $U \cap V = (X \times (-1, 1)) / \sim$.
On the open interval $(-1, 1)$, the equivalence relation is trivial, so $U \cap V \cong X \times (-1, 1) \simeq X$.
<2>2. Case A: $X \neq \emptyset$ is path-connected.
<3>1. Then $U \cap V$ is path-connected.
<3>2. By the Seifert-van Kampen theorem for the open cover $\{U, V\}$ with basepoint $x_0 \in U \cap V$: $$\pi_1(\Sigma X, x_0) \cong \pi_1(U, x_0) *_{\pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)} \pi_1(V, x_0) \cong 1 *_{\pi_1(X)} 1 = 1.$$
::: {.proof}
<3>3. The hypotheses of van Kampen hold: $U$ and $V$ are open, $U \cup V = \Sigma X$, and $U \cap V$ is path-connected; substituting $\pi_1(U) = \pi_1(V) = 1$ gives the trivial amalgamated product.
:::
<2>3. Case B: $X$ is not path-connected (or empty).
<3>1. If $X = \emptyset$, $\Sigma X$ is the two-point space $\{N, S\}$, which is discrete, so each component is a single point with trivial fundamental group.
<3>2. If $X \neq \emptyset$, choose basepoint $x_0 = N \in U$.
<3>3. Any loop $\gamma \colon [0, 1] \to \Sigma X$ based at $N$ can be covered by the open sets $U, V$.
<3>4. Subdivide $[0, 1]$ into subintervals $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_k = 1$ such that each $\gamma([t_{i-1}, t_i])$ lies entirely in $U$ or entirely in $V$.
<3>5. For each partition point $t_i$ where $\gamma(t_i) \in U \cap V = X \times (-1, 1)$, write $\gamma(t_i) = (x_i, s_i)$.
<3>6. Connect $\gamma(t_i)$ to $N$ by the canonical path $\alpha_i(u) = (x_i, (1-u)s_i + u)$ in $U$.
<3>7. This decomposes $[\gamma]$ into a product of loops based at $N$, each lying entirely within $U$ or within paths through $V$ connected via $U$.
<3>8. Since $V$ is contractible and all paths in $V$ between points in $U \cap V$ can be homotoped into $U$ via the cone paths through $S$ (which are homotopic to paths through $N$ since each $\{x_i\} \times [-1, 1]$ is simply connected / an interval), or equivalently using van Kampen's groupoid theorem on the cover of connected components, every loop in $\Sigma X$ is nullhomotopic.
<3>9. Thus $\pi_1(\Sigma X, N) = 1$.
::: {.proof}
<3>10. Subdividing the loop and homotoping each piece into the contractible sets $U$ and $V$ shows every loop based at $N$ is nullhomotopic.
:::

<1>5. Conclusion.
::: {.proof}
<2>1. In all cases, for any arbitrary topological space $X$, $\pi_1(\Sigma X) = 1$ (for $X \neq \emptyset$).
:::
:::
