Let $X$ be an arbitrary topological space, and compute $\pi_1(\Sigma X)$.

::: {.solution}
Assume first that $X\ne\varnothing$, and let $\mathcal C=\pi_0^{\mathrm{path}}(X)$ be the set of path components of $X$. Fix one component $C_0\in\mathcal C$.

<1>1. Cover the suspension by the two open cones
$$
U=\Sigma X\setminus\{S\},\qquad V=\Sigma X\setminus\{N\}.
$$
Then $U$ and $V$ are contractible, while
$$
U\cap V\cong X\times(-1,1),
$$
whose path components are naturally indexed by $\mathcal C$.
::: {.proof}
Writing
$$
\Sigma X=(X\times[-1,1])/(X\times\{-1\}\sim S,\;X\times\{1\}\sim N),
$$
the two sets are the images of $X\times(-1,1]$ and $X\times[-1,1)$ and are open. Each contracts linearly in the interval coordinate to its cone point. No identifications occur for $-1<t<1$, so the intersection is $X\times(-1,1)$, and multiplication by a path-connected interval does not change path components.
:::

<1>2. Choose one point $x_C\in C$ for every $C\in\mathcal C$. The groupoid form of the Seifert--van Kampen theorem applied to $U\cup V$ shows that $\pi_1(\Sigma X)$ is a free group with one free generator for every component $C\ne C_0$.
::: {.proof}
The ordinary one-basepoint van Kampen theorem cannot be applied when $U\cap V$ is disconnected. Instead use the fundamental groupoid on the set
$$
A=\{(x_C,0):C\in\mathcal C\}\subset U\cap V.
$$
This set meets every path component of the intersection. Since both $U$ and $V$ are contractible, their fundamental groupoids on $A$ have exactly one morphism between every ordered pair of objects. In the intersection there are morphisms only between points belonging to the same component, and here there is exactly one chosen object in each component, so no distinct objects are joined there.

The groupoid van Kampen pushout therefore has, after choosing $C_0$ as a root object, one independent loop for every other object $C$: travel in $U$ from $x_{C_0}$ to $x_C$ and return in $V$. There are no relations among these loops, since the intersection contributes no morphisms between distinct chosen objects. Hence the vertex group is the free group on $\mathcal C\setminus\{C_0\}$.
:::

<1>3. Explicitly,
$$
\boxed{\pi_1(\Sigma X)\cong F\bigl(\mathcal C\setminus\{C_0\}\bigr).}
$$
::: {.proof}
This is the vertex-group computation in <1>2. A geometric free generator corresponding to $C\ne C_0$ is the loop that goes from the north pole to the south pole along the suspension arc through $x_C$ and returns from the south pole to the north pole along the suspension arc through $x_{C_0}$.
:::

<1>4. In particular, if $X$ is path-connected then $\pi_1(\Sigma X)=1$.
::: {.proof}
Then $\mathcal C$ has one element, so the free generating set in <1>3 is empty.
:::

<1>5. The old blanket assertion that the suspension of every nonempty space is simply connected is false: for $X=S^0$,
$$
\Sigma S^0\cong S^1,
\qquad
\pi_1(\Sigma S^0)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
::: {.proof}
$S^0$ has two path components, so <1>3 gives a free group on one generator, namely $\mathbb Z$. Equivalently, its suspension is visibly a circle formed by two arcs joining the suspension points.
:::

<1>6. More generally, if $X$ has exactly $r\ge1$ path components, then
$$
\pi_1(\Sigma X)\cong F_{r-1};
$$
for infinitely many components it is the free group on the cardinal set $\mathcal C\setminus\{C_0\}$.
::: {.proof}
This is the cardinality specialization of <1>3.
:::

<1>7. If $X=\varnothing$, then under the quotient definition $\Sigma X=(X\times I)/\!\sim$ is empty, so there is no based fundamental group until a basepoint convention is separately supplied.
::: {.proof}
The product $\varnothing\times I$ is empty, and a quotient of the empty space is empty.
:::
