::: problem
Let $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$. Show that $f$ is uniformly continuous on $(1, \infty)$ but is not uniformly continuous on $(0, \infty)$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove uniform continuity of $f(x) = 1/x$ on $(1, \infty)$ using a uniform $\delta$-$\varepsilon$ bound, and disprove uniform continuity on $(0, \infty)$ by exhibiting sequences with vanishing distance and non-vanishing image separation.

<1>1. Part 1: $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ is uniformly continuous on $(1, \infty)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\varepsilon > 0$ be given.
    <2>2. Choose $\delta = \varepsilon > 0$ (independent of $x, y$).
    <2>3. For any $x, y \in (1, \infty)$ with $|x - y| < \delta$:
    $$|f(x) - f(y)| = \left| \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{y} \right| = \frac{|x - y|}{x y}.$$
    <2>4. Since $x > 1$ and $y > 1$, the product $x y > 1$, so $\frac{1}{x y} < 1$.
    <2>5. Thus
    $$|f(x) - f(y)| = \frac{|x - y|}{x y} < |x - y| < \delta = \varepsilon.$$
    <2>6. Therefore $f$ is uniformly continuous on $(1, \infty)$.

<1>2. Part 2: $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ is not uniformly continuous on $(0, \infty)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. A function $f: E \to \mathbb{R}$ is uniformly continuous on $E$ if and only if for all sequences $(x_n)_{n=1}^\infty, (y_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subset E$, $\lim_{n \to \infty} |x_n - y_n| = 0 \implies \lim_{n \to \infty} |f(x_n) - f(y_n)| = 0$.
    <2>2. Choose $\varepsilon_0 = 1 > 0$.
    <2>3. Define the sequences $x_n = \frac{1}{n}$ and $y_n = \frac{1}{n + 1}$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}$. Both sequences lie in $(0, \infty)$.
    <2>4. Compute the distance between $x_n$ and $y_n$:
    $$|x_n - y_n| = \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{1}{n(n+1)} \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$
    <2>5. Compute the distance between the images:
    $$|f(x_n) - f(y_n)| = |n - (n+1)| = 1 \ge \varepsilon_0 \quad \text{for all } n \in \mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}.$$
    <2>6. Thus $\lim_{n \to \infty} |f(x_n) - f(y_n)| = 1 \ne 0$ despite $\lim_{n \to \infty} |x_n - y_n| = 0$.
    <2>7. Therefore $f$ is not uniformly continuous on $(0, \infty)$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    *Proof:*
    $f$ is uniformly continuous on $(1, \infty)$ and not uniformly continuous on $(0, \infty)$.
:::
