::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be a nonconstant meromorphic function. A complex number $w$ is a period if
$$
f(z+w)=f(z)
$$
for all $z$ where the expression is defined.

(a) Show that if $w_1,w_2$ are periods, then so is $n_1w_1+n_2w_2$ for all integers $n_1,n_2$.

(b) Show that every bounded region of the complex plane contains at most finitely many periods of $f$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\Lambda\coloneqq
\{w\in\CC:f(z+w)=f(z)\}
$$
denote the set of periods, with equality understood as equality of meromorphic functions.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #period-negation}
If $w\in\Lambda$, then $-w\in\Lambda$.

::: pf-proof
Since
$$
f(z+w)=f(z)
$$
for all $z$, replace $z$ by $z-w$ to obtain
$$
f(z)=f(z-w).
$$
Thus
$$
f(z-w)=f(z),
$$
so $-w$ is a period.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #period-addition}
If $w_1,w_2\in\Lambda$, then $w_1+w_2\in\Lambda$.

::: pf-proof
For every $z$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(z+w_1+w_2)
&=
f((z+w_1)+w_2)\\
&=
f(z+w_1)\\
&=
f(z).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $w_1+w_2$ is a period.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #integer-combinations}
If $w_1,w_2$ are periods and $n_1,n_2\in\ZZ$, then
$$
\boxed{n_1w_1+n_2w_2\in\Lambda}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The number $0$ is a period. Repeated use of [](#period-addition){.pf-ref} shows that every positive integer multiple of a period is a period, and [](#period-negation){.pf-ref} handles negative integer multiples. A final application of [](#period-addition){.pf-ref} gives the stated integer combination.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #regular-point}
Since $f$ is nonconstant and meromorphic, there is a point $z_0\in\CC$ at which $f$ is holomorphic and
$$
f'(z_0)\neq0.
$$

::: pf-proof
If $f$ has no poles, then it is entire. If $f'$ vanished everywhere, the usual constant-function theorem for holomorphic functions would make $f$ constant, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence such a $z_0$ exists.

If $f$ has a pole $p$, choose a punctured disk
$$
0<\abs{z-p}<r
$$
containing no other pole. The function is holomorphic there. Its derivative cannot vanish at every point of that punctured disk, because then $f$ would be constant there, whereas a constant holomorphic function on a punctured neighborhood is bounded and therefore cannot have a pole at $p$. Thus again there is a regular point $z_0$ with $f'(z_0)\neq0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #no-small-periods}
There is no sequence of distinct periods
$$
\delta_j\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}
$$
such that
$$
\delta_j\longrightarrow0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Suppose such a sequence existed. Choose $z_0$ as in [](#regular-point){.pf-ref}. Since $f$ is holomorphic at $z_0$, there is $r>0$ such that $f$ is holomorphic on
$$
\abs{z-z_0}<r.
$$
For all sufficiently large $j$, one has $\abs{\delta_j}<r$, and the period identity gives
$$
f(z_0+\delta_j)=f(z_0).
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{f(z_0+\delta_j)-f(z_0)}{\delta_j}=0.
$$
Letting $j\to\infty$ yields
$$
f'(z_0)=0,
$$
contradicting [](#regular-point){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Every bounded subset of $\CC$ contains only finitely many periods.

::: pf-proof
Suppose a bounded subset contained infinitely many distinct periods. Choose a sequence of distinct periods
$$
w_1,w_2,\ldots
$$
from that subset. As a bounded sequence in $\CC\cong\RR^2$, it has a convergent subsequence
$$
w_{n_j}\longrightarrow w.
$$
Set
$$
\delta_j\coloneqq w_{n_{j+1}}-w_{n_j}.
$$
By [](#integer-combinations){.pf-ref}, each $\delta_j$ is a period. Because the $w_{n_j}$ are distinct,
$$
\delta_j\neq0,
$$
and because the subsequence converges,
$$
\delta_j\longrightarrow0.
$$
This contradicts [](#no-small-periods){.pf-ref}.
:::
