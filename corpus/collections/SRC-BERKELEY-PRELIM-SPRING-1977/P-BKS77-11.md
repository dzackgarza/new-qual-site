::: {.problem}
Let $(a_n)$ be defined by
\[
1-x^2+x^4-x^6+\cdots
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n(x-3)^n,
\qquad 0<x<1.
\]
Find
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
F(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n(z-3)^n,
$$
and let $R$ be its radius of convergence.

<1>1. For $0<x<1$,
$$
1-x^2+x^4-x^6+\cdots=\frac1{1+x^2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The left-hand side is the geometric series
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-x^2)^k,
$$
whose ratio has modulus $x^2<1$. Hence its sum is
$$
\frac1{1-(-x^2)}
=
\frac1{1+x^2}.
$$
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
R\geq3.
$$

::: {.proof}
By hypothesis, the power series defining $F$ converges for every real $x\in(0,1)$. For such $x$,
$$
|x-3|=3-x.
$$
These distances approach $3$ as $x\downarrow0$. A power series converges at every point strictly inside its radius of convergence, so its radius must be at least the supremum of these distances, namely $3$.
:::

<1>3. The function $F$ agrees with
$$
h(z)\coloneqq\frac1{1+z^2}
$$
on the disk
$$
|z-3|<3.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $F$ is holomorphic on $|z-3|<3$. The poles of $h$ are at $\pm i$, and
$$
|3-i|=|3+i|=\sqrt{10}>3,
$$
so $h$ is also holomorphic on that disk. By step <1>1 and the defining identity in the problem,
$$
F(x)=h(x)
$$
for every $x\in(0,1)$. This set has accumulation points inside the disk. The identity theorem therefore gives $F=h$ throughout $|z-3|<3$.
:::

<1>4. The coefficients $a_n$ are the Taylor coefficients of $h(z)=1/(1+z^2)$ about $z=3$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 identifies $F$ with $h$ on a neighborhood of $3$. Taylor expansions of a holomorphic function about a fixed center are unique, so
$$
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n(z-3)^n
$$
near $z=3$.
:::

<1>5. The Taylor series of $h$ about $3$ has radius of convergence
$$
R=\sqrt{10}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The function $h$ is holomorphic on
$$
|z-3|<\sqrt{10},
$$
because its only singularities are $\pm i$, both at distance $\sqrt{10}$ from $3$. Hence its Taylor series about $3$ converges at least throughout this disk.

It cannot have radius greater than $\sqrt{10}$. If it did, its sum would define a holomorphic function on a neighborhood of $i$. By step <1>4 and the identity theorem, that sum would agree with $h$ on the punctured part of such a neighborhood. But $h$ has a pole at $i$, whereas a holomorphic function is bounded on some sufficiently small closed disk around $i$, a contradiction. Thus the radius is exactly $\sqrt{10}$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}
=
\frac1{\sqrt{10}}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By the Cauchy--Hadamard formula, the radius of convergence of
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n(z-3)^n
$$
satisfies
$$
\frac1R
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}.
$$
Apply step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 gives the requested value.
