::: {.problem}
On
\[
A=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x^2+y^2>1\},
\]
find differentiable real-valued functions $f,g$ such that
\[
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial y},
\]
but there is no real-valued function $h$ on $A$ satisfying
\[
f=\frac{\partial h}{\partial y},
\qquad
g=\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
\boxed{
f(x,y)=\frac{x}{x^2+y^2},
\qquad
g(x,y)=-\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}
}.
$$

<1>1. The functions $f$ and $g$ are differentiable on $A$.

::: {.proof}
On $A$ one has
$$
x^2+y^2>1,
$$
so the common denominator never vanishes. Thus both rational functions are smooth on $A$.
:::

<1>2. The equality
$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
=
\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}
$$
holds on $A$.

::: {.proof}
Direct differentiation gives
$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
=
\frac{(x^2+y^2)-2x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}
=
\frac{y^2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2},
$$
while
$$
\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}
=
-\frac{(x^2+y^2)-2y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}
=
\frac{y^2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
\gamma(t)=(2\cos t,2\sin t),
\qquad
0\leq t\leq2\pi.
$$
Then $\gamma$ is a closed curve in $A$ and
$$
\int_\gamma g\,dx+f\,dy=2\pi.
$$

::: {.proof}
The curve lies on the circle $x^2+y^2=4>1$, so it is contained in $A$. Along $\gamma$,
$$
g(\gamma(t))
=
-\frac{2\sin t}{4}
=
-\frac12\sin t,
$$
and
$$
f(\gamma(t))
=
\frac{2\cos t}{4}
=
\frac12\cos t.
$$
Also
$$
dx=-2\sin t\,dt,
\qquad
dy=2\cos t\,dt.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_\gamma g\,dx+f\,dy
&=
\int_0^{2\pi}
\left(
\sin^2t+\cos^2t
\right)\,dt\\
&=2\pi.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>4. There is no differentiable function $h:A\to\mathbb R$ satisfying
$$
h_y=f,
\qquad
h_x=g.
$$

::: {.proof}
Suppose such an $h$ existed. Along the curve $\gamma$ from step <1>3, the chain rule would give
$$
\frac{d}{dt}h(\gamma(t))
=
h_x(\gamma(t))x'(t)
+h_y(\gamma(t))y'(t).
$$
Hence
$$
\int_\gamma g\,dx+f\,dy
=
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d}{dt}h(\gamma(t))\,dt
=
h(\gamma(2\pi))-h(\gamma(0))
=0,
$$
because $\gamma(2\pi)=\gamma(0)$. This contradicts step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>2 verify the differential condition, and step <1>4 proves that no global potential $h$ exists.
:::
:::
