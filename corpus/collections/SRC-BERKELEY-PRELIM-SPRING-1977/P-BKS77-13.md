::: {.problem}
Let $A(\theta)$ solve
$$
\frac{dA}{d\theta}=BA(\theta),
\qquad
A(0)=I,
$$
where $B$ is a constant square matrix.

1. Find a necessary and sufficient condition on $B$ for $A(\theta)$ to be orthogonal for every $\theta$.
2. Find $A(\theta)$ when
   $$
   B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
   $$
   and give a geometric interpretation.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #necessity}
If $A(\theta)$ is orthogonal for every $\theta$, then
$$
\boxed{B^T+B=0}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Orthogonality means
$$
A(\theta)^TA(\theta)=I
$$
for every $\theta$. Differentiate at $\theta=0$:
$$
0
=
\evaluated{\frac{d}{d\theta}}_{\theta=0}
\bigl(A(\theta)^TA(\theta)\bigr).
$$
Using $A'(\theta)=BA(\theta)$ and $A(0)=I$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
0
&=
A'(0)^TA(0)+A(0)^TA'(0)\\
&=
B^T+B.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sufficiency}
Conversely, if $B^T+B=0$, then $A(\theta)$ is orthogonal for every $\theta$.

::: pf-proof
Set
$$
M(\theta)\coloneqq A(\theta)^TA(\theta).
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
M'(\theta)
&=
A'(\theta)^TA(\theta)+A(\theta)^TA'(\theta)\\
&=
A(\theta)^TB^TA(\theta)
+A(\theta)^TBA(\theta)\\
&=
A(\theta)^T(B^T+B)A(\theta)\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $M(\theta)$ is constant. Since
$$
M(0)=I,
$$
one has
$$
A(\theta)^TA(\theta)=I
$$
for every $\theta$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore the necessary and sufficient condition is
$$
\boxed{B^T=-B}.
$$

::: pf-proof
[](#necessity){.pf-ref} proves necessity and [](#sufficiency){.pf-ref} proves sufficiency.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square-of-generator}
For
$$
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
one has
$$
B^2=-I.
$$

::: pf-proof
Direct multiplication gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{pmatrix}^2
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix}
=-I.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix-flow}
The solution of the initial-value problem in part 2 is
$$
\boxed{
A(\theta)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For a constant matrix $B$, the solution is
$$
A(\theta)=e^{\theta B}.
$$
Using [](#square-of-generator){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
e^{\theta B}
&=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^kB^k}{k!}\\
&=
\left(
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^m\theta^{2m}}{(2m)!}
\right)I
+
\left(
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^m\theta^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}
\right)B\\
&=
\cos\theta\,I+\sin\theta\,B,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is the displayed matrix.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Geometrically, $A(\theta)$ is rotation of the plane through angle $-\theta$.

::: pf-proof
The standard counterclockwise rotation matrix through angle $\varphi$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\varphi&-\sin\varphi\\
\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Taking $\varphi=-\theta$ gives exactly the matrix in [](#matrix-flow){.pf-ref}.
:::
