::: {.problem}
Let $p$ be an odd prime and let $Q(p)$ be the set of integers $a$, $0\le a\le p-1$, for which
\[
x^2\equiv a\pmod p
\]
has a solution.
Show that
\[
|Q(p)|=\frac{p+1}{2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Among the nonzero residue classes modulo $p$, two elements have the same square if and only if they are negatives of one another.

::: {.proof}
Let $x,y\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Then
$$
x^2=y^2
$$
if and only if
$$
(x-y)(x+y)=0.
$$
Since $\mathbb F_p$ is a field, this holds exactly when
$$
x=y
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
x=-y.
$$
:::

<1>2. Every nonzero quadratic residue modulo $p$ has exactly two square roots in $\mathbb F_p^\times$.

::: {.proof}
If $a=x^2\neq0$, then both $x$ and $-x$ square to $a$. They are distinct because
$$
x=-x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2x=0,
$$
and $p$ is odd, so $2\neq0$ in $\mathbb F_p$ and $x\neq0$. Step <1>1 shows there are no other square roots.
:::

<1>3. There are exactly
$$
\frac{p-1}{2}
$$
nonzero quadratic residues modulo $p$.

::: {.proof}
There are $p-1$ nonzero elements of $\mathbb F_p$. By step <1>2, the squaring map
$$
\mathbb F_p^\times\longrightarrow\mathbb F_p^\times,
\qquad
x\longmapsto x^2,
$$
has fibers of size exactly $2$ over its image. Hence the image has cardinality
$$
\frac{p-1}{2}.
$$
:::

<1>4. The class $0$ is also a quadratic residue.

::: {.proof}
The congruence
$$
x^2\equiv0\pmod p
$$
has the solution $x\equiv0\pmod p$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{|Q(p)|=\frac{p+1}{2}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 counts the nonzero quadratic residues, and step <1>4 contributes the additional residue $0$:
$$
|Q(p)|
=
\frac{p-1}{2}+1
=
\frac{p+1}{2}.
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the desired count.
:::
:::
