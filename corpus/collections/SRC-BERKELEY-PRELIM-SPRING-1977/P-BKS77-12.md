::: {.problem}
Let $p$ be an odd prime and let $Q(p)$ be the set of integers $a$, $0\le a\le p-1$, for which
\[
x^2\equiv a\pmod p
\]
has a solution.
Show that
\[
|Q(p)|=\frac{p+1}{2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #square-fibers}
Among the nonzero residue classes modulo $p$, two elements have the same square if and only if they are negatives of one another.

::: pf-proof
Let $x,y\in\FF_p^\times$. Then
$$
x^2=y^2
$$
if and only if
$$
(x-y)(x+y)=0.
$$
Since $\FF_p$ is a field, this holds exactly when
$$
x=y
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
x=-y.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #two-square-roots}
Every nonzero quadratic residue modulo $p$ has exactly two square roots in $\FF_p^\times$.

::: pf-proof
If $a=x^2\neq0$, then both $x$ and $-x$ square to $a$. They are distinct because
$$
x=-x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2x=0,
$$
and $p$ is odd, so $2\neq0$ in $\FF_p$ and $x\neq0$. [](#square-fibers){.pf-ref} shows there are no other square roots.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nonzero-residues}
There are exactly
$$
\frac{p-1}{2}
$$
nonzero quadratic residues modulo $p$.

::: pf-proof
There are $p-1$ nonzero elements of $\FF_p$. By [](#two-square-roots){.pf-ref}, the squaring map
$$
\FF_p^\times\longrightarrow\FF_p^\times,
\qquad
x\longmapsto x^2,
$$
has fibers of size exactly $2$ over its image. Hence the image has cardinality
$$
\frac{p-1}{2}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #zero-residue}
The class $0$ is also a quadratic residue.

::: pf-proof
The congruence
$$
x^2\equiv0\pmod p
$$
has the solution $x\equiv0\pmod p$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore
$$
\boxed{|Q(p)|=\frac{p+1}{2}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
[](#nonzero-residues){.pf-ref} counts the nonzero quadratic residues, and [](#zero-residue){.pf-ref} contributes the additional residue $0$:
$$
|Q(p)|
=
\frac{p-1}{2}+1
=
\frac{p+1}{2}.
$$
:::
