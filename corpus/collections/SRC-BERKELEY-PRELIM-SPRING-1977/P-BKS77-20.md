::: {.problem}
Let $h:[0,1)\to\RR$ be uniformly continuous.
Prove that there exists a unique continuous function $g:[0,1]\to\RR$ such that
\[
g(x)=h(x)
\]
for every $x\in[0,1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $n\geq2$, set
$$
x_n\coloneqq1-\frac1n.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cauchy-sequence}
The sequence
$$
\bigl(h(x_n)\bigr)_{n\geq2}
$$
is Cauchy in $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Uniform continuity of $h$ gives $\delta>0$ such that
$$
|x-y|<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|h(x)-h(y)|<\varepsilon
$$
for all $x,y\in[0,1)$. Choose $N$ so large that
$$
\frac1N<\frac{\delta}{2}.
$$
If $m,n\geq N$, then
$$
|x_m-x_n|
=
\left|\frac1n-\frac1m\right|
\leq
\frac1n+\frac1m
\leq
\frac2N
<\delta.
$$
Therefore
$$
|h(x_m)-h(x_n)|<\varepsilon.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #endpoint-value}
There is a real number $L$ such that
$$
h(x_n)\longrightarrow L.
$$

::: pf-proof
By [](#cauchy-sequence){.pf-ref}, $(h(x_n))$ is Cauchy. The real numbers are complete, so every Cauchy sequence converges to a real number.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #left-limit}
One has
$$
\lim_{x\to1^-}h(x)=L.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $\varepsilon>0$. By uniform continuity, choose $\delta>0$ such that
$$
|x-y|<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|h(x)-h(y)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$
By [](#endpoint-value){.pf-ref}, choose $n$ so large that
$$
\frac1n<\frac{\delta}{2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|h(x_n)-L|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$
If
$$
1-\frac{\delta}{2}<x<1,
$$
then
$$
|x-x_n|
\leq
|1-x|+|1-x_n|
<
\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}
=\delta.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
|h(x)-L|
&\leq
|h(x)-h(x_n)|
+|h(x_n)-L|\\
&<
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
=\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is exactly the asserted left-hand limit.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #continuous-extension}
Define
$$
\boxed{
g(x)=
\begin{cases}
h(x),&0\leq x<1,\\
L,&x=1.
\end{cases}
}
$$
Then $g$ is continuous on $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof
Uniform continuity of $h$ implies ordinary continuity at every point of $[0,1)$, so $g$ is continuous there. At $x=1$, [](#left-limit){.pf-ref} says
$$
\lim_{x\to1^-}g(x)
=
\lim_{x\to1^-}h(x)
=L
=g(1).
$$
Thus $g$ is also continuous at the endpoint.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The continuous extension in [](#continuous-extension){.pf-ref} is unique.

::: pf-proof
Let $\widetilde g:[0,1]\to\RR$ be continuous and satisfy
$$
\widetilde g(x)=h(x)
$$
for $x<1$. Then
$$
\widetilde g(1)
=
\lim_{x\to1^-}\widetilde g(x)
=
\lim_{x\to1^-}h(x)
=L
$$
by continuity and [](#left-limit){.pf-ref}. Thus $\widetilde g$ agrees with $g$ also at $1$, and hence everywhere.
:::
