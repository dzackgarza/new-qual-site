::: {.problem}
Let $\QQ_+$ be the multiplicative group of positive rational numbers.

(a) Is $\QQ_+$ torsion-free?

(b) Is $\QQ_+$ free?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\mathcal P$ denote the set of prime numbers.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #prime-factorization}
Every $q\in\QQ_+$ has a unique expression
$$
q=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{v_p(q)},
$$
where
$$
v_p(q)\in\ZZ
$$
and all but finitely many $v_p(q)$ are zero.

::: pf-proof
Write
$$
q=\frac{a}{b}
$$
with positive coprime integers $a,b$. By unique factorization in $\ZZ$,
$$
a=\prod_p p^{\alpha_p},
\qquad
b=\prod_p p^{\beta_p},
$$
with nonnegative integer exponents of finite support. Then
$$
q=\prod_p p^{\alpha_p-\beta_p}.
$$
Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of the prime factorizations of $a$ and $b$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #valuation-isomorphism}
The map
$$
\Phi:\QQ_+
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P}\ZZ,
\qquad
\Phi(q)=\bigl(v_p(q)\bigr)_{p\in\mathcal P},
$$
is an isomorphism of abelian groups.

::: pf-proof
For positive rationals $q,r$,
$$
v_p(qr)=v_p(q)+v_p(r),
$$
so $\Phi$ is a homomorphism from multiplication to componentwise addition. [](#prime-factorization){.pf-ref} shows that $\Phi$ is injective.

Conversely, if $(e_p)_p$ is an integer vector of finite support, then
$$
\prod_p p^{e_p}
$$
is a positive rational number whose image under $\Phi$ is $(e_p)_p$. Thus $\Phi$ is surjective.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The group $\QQ_+$ is torsion-free.

::: pf-proof
Suppose
$$
q^m=1
$$
for some $q\in\QQ_+$ and positive integer $m$. Applying [](#valuation-isomorphism){.pf-ref} gives
$$
m\,v_p(q)=0
$$
in $\ZZ$ for every prime $p$. Hence
$$
v_p(q)=0
$$
for every $p$, so [](#prime-factorization){.pf-ref} gives $q=1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
As an abelian group, $\QQ_+$ is free abelian with basis the set of primes:
$$
\boxed{
\QQ_+
\cong
\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P}\ZZ
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The right-hand side is by definition the free abelian group on the basis indexed by the primes, and [](#valuation-isomorphism){.pf-ref} gives the isomorphism. Equivalently, every positive rational is uniquely a finite product of integer powers of primes.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If the word “free” is instead interpreted as “free group” in the nonabelian category of all groups, then $\QQ_+$ is not free.

::: pf-proof
The group $\QQ_+$ is abelian and is not cyclic, since no single positive rational can generate two distinct primes whose exponent vectors are linearly independent in the description of [](#valuation-isomorphism){.pf-ref}. A free group is abelian only when its free rank is at most $1$. Thus $\QQ_+$ is not a free group in that sense.
:::
