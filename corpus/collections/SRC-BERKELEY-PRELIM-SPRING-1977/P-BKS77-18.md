::: {.problem}
(a) In $\RR[x]$, consider the polynomials satisfying
$$
f(2)=f'(2)=f''(2)=0.
$$
Prove that they form an ideal and find its monic generator.

(b) Do the polynomials satisfying
$$
f(2)=0,
\qquad
f'(3)=0
$$
form an ideal?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
I\coloneqq
\{f\in\RR[x]:f(2)=f'(2)=f''(2)=0\}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #triple-divisibility}
If $f\in I$, then $(x-2)^3$ divides $f$.

::: pf-proof
Since $f(2)=0$, the factor theorem gives
$$
f(x)=(x-2)q(x)
$$
for some $q\in\RR[x]$. Differentiating,
$$
f'(x)=q(x)+(x-2)q'(x),
$$
so
$$
0=f'(2)=q(2).
$$
Thus
$$
q(x)=(x-2)r(x)
$$
for some $r$, and hence
$$
f(x)=(x-2)^2r(x).
$$
Differentiating twice gives
$$
f''(2)=2r(2).
$$
Since $f''(2)=0$, one has $r(2)=0$, so
$$
r(x)=(x-2)s(x)
$$
for some $s\in\RR[x]$. Therefore
$$
f(x)=(x-2)^3s(x).
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiples-belong}
Every multiple of $(x-2)^3$ belongs to $I$.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
f(x)=(x-2)^3s(x).
$$
Then $f(2)=0$. Differentiating once gives a factor $(x-2)^2$, and differentiating twice gives a factor $(x-2)$, so
$$
f'(2)=f''(2)=0.
$$
Thus $f\in I$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The set in part (a) is the principal ideal
$$
\boxed{I=((x-2)^3)},
$$
whose monic generator is $(x-2)^3$.

::: pf-proof
[](#triple-divisibility){.pf-ref} gives
$$
I\subseteq((x-2)^3),
$$
and [](#multiples-belong){.pf-ref} gives the reverse inclusion. A principal ideal is an ideal, and $(x-2)^3$ is monic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #counterexample-member}
For part (b), the polynomial
$$
f(x)\coloneqq(x-2)(x-4)
$$
satisfies
$$
f(2)=0,
\qquad
f'(3)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
The first equality is immediate. Also
$$
f(x)=x^2-6x+8,
$$
so
$$
f'(x)=2x-6
$$
and therefore $f'(3)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #failed-product}
The product
$$
h(x)\coloneqq(x-3)f(x)
$$
still satisfies $h(2)=0$, but
$$
h'(3)=-1\neq0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Since $f(2)=0$,
$$
h(2)=(2-3)f(2)=0.
$$
By the product rule,
$$
h'(x)=f(x)+(x-3)f'(x).
$$
Hence
$$
h'(3)=f(3)=(3-2)(3-4)=-1.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The polynomials in part (b) do not form an ideal.

::: pf-proof
[](#counterexample-member){.pf-ref} gives an element $f$ of the proposed set. If that set were an ideal, multiplication by the polynomial $x-3$ would keep the result in the set. [](#failed-product){.pf-ref} shows that it does not.
:::
