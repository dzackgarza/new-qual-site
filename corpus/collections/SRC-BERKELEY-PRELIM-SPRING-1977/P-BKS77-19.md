::: {.problem}
Suppose $u(x,t)$ is continuous with continuous second partial derivatives, and $u$ together with its first partial derivatives is periodic in $x$ with period $1$. Assume
$$
u_{xx}=u_{tt}.
$$
Prove that
$$
E(t)=\frac12\int_0^1\left(u_t^2+u_x^2\right)\,dx
$$
is independent of $t$.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #differentiate-energy}
The function $E$ is differentiable and
$$
E'(t)
=
\int_0^1
\left(
u_tu_{tt}+u_xu_{xt}
\right)\,dx.
$$

::: pf-proof
The first and second partial derivatives of $u$ are continuous, so the integrand
$$
\frac12(u_t^2+u_x^2)
$$
and its $t$-derivative are continuous on every compact rectangle. Differentiation under the integral sign is therefore valid and gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
E'(t)
&=
\frac12
\int_0^1
\left(
2u_tu_{tt}+2u_xu_{xt}
\right)\,dx\\
&=
\int_0^1
\left(
u_tu_{tt}+u_xu_{xt}
\right)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #use-wave-equation}
Using the wave equation,
$$
E'(t)
=
\int_0^1
\left(
u_tu_{xx}+u_xu_{xt}
\right)\,dx.
$$

::: pf-proof
Substitute
$$
u_{tt}=u_{xx}
$$
from the hypothesis into the expression in [](#differentiate-energy){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #integration-by-parts}
Integration by parts gives
$$
\int_0^1u_tu_{xx}\,dx
=
\left[u_tu_x\right]_{x=0}^{x=1}
-
\int_0^1u_{tx}u_x\,dx.
$$

::: pf-proof
For fixed $t$, apply integration by parts in the variable $x$ to
$$
\int_0^1u_t(x,t)\,u_{xx}(x,t)\,dx.
$$
Since
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u_t=u_{tx},
$$
the stated identity follows.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #periodic-boundary}
The boundary term in [](#integration-by-parts){.pf-ref} vanishes:
$$
\left[u_tu_x\right]_{x=0}^{x=1}=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
The first partial derivatives are periodic in $x$ with period $1$, so
$$
u_t(1,t)=u_t(0,t),
\qquad
u_x(1,t)=u_x(0,t).
$$
Hence
$$
u_t(1,t)u_x(1,t)-u_t(0,t)u_x(0,t)=0.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #zero-derivative}
One has
$$
E'(t)=0
$$
for every $t$.

::: pf-proof
Combine [](#use-wave-equation){.pf-ref}, [](#integration-by-parts){.pf-ref}, and [](#periodic-boundary){.pf-ref}:
$$
\begin{aligned}
E'(t)
&=
-\int_0^1u_{tx}u_x\,dx
+\int_0^1u_xu_{xt}\,dx\\
&=0,
\end{aligned}
$$
because $u_{tx}=u_{xt}$ by continuity of the second partial derivatives.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore
$$
\boxed{E(t)\text{ is independent of }t}.
$$

::: pf-proof
[](#zero-derivative){.pf-ref} shows that the derivative of $E$ vanishes identically, so $E$ is constant.
:::
