::: {.problem}
Suppose $u(x,t)$ is continuous with continuous second partial derivatives, and $u$ together with its first partial derivatives is periodic in $x$ with period $1$. Assume
\[
u_{xx}=u_{tt}.
\]
Prove that
\[
E(t)=\frac12\int_0^1\left(u_t^2+u_x^2\right)\,dx
\]
is independent of $t$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function $E$ is differentiable and
$$
E'(t)
=
\int_0^1
\left(
u_tu_{tt}+u_xu_{xt}
\right)\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
The first and second partial derivatives of $u$ are continuous, so the integrand
$$
\frac12(u_t^2+u_x^2)
$$
and its $t$-derivative are continuous on every compact rectangle. Differentiation under the integral sign is therefore valid and gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
E'(t)
&=
\frac12
\int_0^1
\left(
2u_tu_{tt}+2u_xu_{xt}
\right)\,dx\\
&=
\int_0^1
\left(
u_tu_{tt}+u_xu_{xt}
\right)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. Using the wave equation,
$$
E'(t)
=
\int_0^1
\left(
u_tu_{xx}+u_xu_{xt}
\right)\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
Substitute
$$
u_{tt}=u_{xx}
$$
from the hypothesis into the expression in step <1>1.
:::

<1>3. Integration by parts gives
$$
\int_0^1u_tu_{xx}\,dx
=
\left[u_tu_x\right]_{x=0}^{x=1}
-
\int_0^1u_{tx}u_x\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
For fixed $t$, apply integration by parts in the variable $x$ to
$$
\int_0^1u_t(x,t)\,u_{xx}(x,t)\,dx.
$$
Since
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u_t=u_{tx},
$$
the stated identity follows.
:::

<1>4. The boundary term in step <1>3 vanishes:
$$
\left[u_tu_x\right]_{x=0}^{x=1}=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The first partial derivatives are periodic in $x$ with period $1$, so
$$
u_t(1,t)=u_t(0,t),
\qquad
u_x(1,t)=u_x(0,t).
$$
Hence
$$
u_t(1,t)u_x(1,t)-u_t(0,t)u_x(0,t)=0.
$$
:::

<1>5. One has
$$
E'(t)=0
$$
for every $t$.

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>2--<1>4:
$$
\begin{aligned}
E'(t)
&=
-\int_0^1u_{tx}u_x\,dx
+\int_0^1u_xu_{xt}\,dx\\
&=0,
\end{aligned}
$$
because $u_{tx}=u_{xt}$ by continuity of the second partial derivatives.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{E(t)\text{ is independent of }t}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 shows that the derivative of $E$ vanishes identically, so $E$ is constant.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required conclusion.
:::
