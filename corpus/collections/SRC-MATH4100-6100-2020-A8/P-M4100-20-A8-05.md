::: {.problem}
(a) Suppose $f$ is continuous on $[ a , b ]$ , twice differentiable on $( a , b )$ , and $f ^ { \prime \prime } ( x ) \neq 0$ for all $x \in ( a , b )$ Prove carefully that $f$ has at most 2 distinct zeros in $[ a , b ]$

(b) Prove that the function $f ( x ) = x ^ { 2 } -$ sin x has precisely two roots.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $f$ has three distinct zeros
$$
x_1<x_2<x_3
$$
in $[a,b]$.


::: pf-proof

This is the negation of the desired conclusion in part (a).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exist
$$
c_1\in(x_1,x_2),
\qquad
c_2\in(x_2,x_3)
$$
such that
$$
f'(c_1)=f'(c_2)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply Rolle's theorem to $f$ on $[x_1,x_2]$ and on $[x_2,x_3]$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists $d\in(c_1,c_2)$ such that
$$
f''(d)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The derivative $f'$ is continuous on $[c_1,c_2]$ and differentiable on $(c_1,c_2)$ because $f$ is twice differentiable.
Since
$$
f'(c_1)=f'(c_2)=0,
$$
Rolle's theorem applied to $f'$ gives such a point $d$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $f$ has at most two distinct zeros in $[a,b]$.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>3 contradicts the hypothesis that
$$
f''(x)\neq0
$$
for every $x\in(a,b)$.
Hence three distinct zeros cannot exist.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step <1>4.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For
$$
F(x)=x^2-\sin x,
$$
one has
$$
F''(x)=2+\sin x\geq1
$$
for every $x\in\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Differentiating twice gives
$$
F'(x)=2x-\cos x,
$$
and
$$
F''(x)=2+\sin x.
$$
Since $\sin x\geq-1$, the lower bound follows.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The function $F$ has at most two real roots.


::: pf-proof

If it had three distinct roots, choose a closed interval containing all three.
Step <1>6 verifies the hypotheses of part (a) on that interval, contradicting step <1>4.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The number $0$ is a root of $F$.


::: pf-proof

Directly,
$$
F(0)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
0<x<\delta
\implies
F(x)<0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
F'(0)=-1,
$$
the definition of derivative gives $\delta>0$ such that
$$
0<|x|<\delta
\implies
\left|
\frac{F(x)-F(0)}x+1
\right|
<
\frac12.
$$
For $0<x<\delta$, this implies
$$
\frac{F(x)}x<-\frac12.
$$
Since $x>0$, one has $F(x)<0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
F(1)>0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\sin1<1,
$$
one has
$$
F(1)=1-\sin1>0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists a root
$$
r\in(0,1)
$$
distinct from $0$.


::: pf-proof

Choose
$$
x_0\in(0,\min\{\delta,1\}).
$$
Then step <1>9 gives $F(x_0)<0$, while step <1>10 gives $F(1)>0$.
Continuity of $F$ and the Intermediate Value Theorem give
$$
r\in(x_0,1)
$$
with $F(r)=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The function
$$
F(x)=x^2-\sin x
$$
has precisely two real roots.


::: pf-proof

Steps <1>8 and <1>11 give two distinct roots, while step <1>7 excludes any third root.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step <1>12.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>5 and <1>13 establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::
