::: {.problem}
(a) Suppose $f$ is continuous on $[ a , b ]$ , twice differentiable on $( a , b )$ , and $f ^ { \prime \prime } ( x ) \neq 0$ for all $x \in ( a , b )$ Prove carefully that $f$ has at most 2 distinct zeros in $[ a , b ]$

(b) Prove that the function $f ( x ) = x ^ { 2 } -$ sin x has precisely two roots.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $f$ has three distinct zeros
$$
x_1<x_2<x_3
$$
in $[a,b]$.

::: {.proof}
This is the negation of the desired conclusion in part (a).
:::

<1>2. There exist
$$
c_1\in(x_1,x_2),
\qquad
c_2\in(x_2,x_3)
$$
such that
$$
f'(c_1)=f'(c_2)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply Rolle's theorem to $f$ on $[x_1,x_2]$ and on $[x_2,x_3]$.
:::

<1>3. There exists $d\in(c_1,c_2)$ such that
$$
f''(d)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The derivative $f'$ is continuous on $[c_1,c_2]$ and differentiable on
$(c_1,c_2)$ because $f$ is twice differentiable. Since
$$
f'(c_1)=f'(c_2)=0,
$$
Rolle's theorem applied to $f'$ gives such a point $d$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $f$ has at most two distinct zeros in $[a,b]$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 contradicts the hypothesis that
$$
f''(x)\neq0
$$
for every $x\in(a,b)$. Hence three distinct zeros cannot exist.
:::

<1>5. Part (a) follows.

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. For
$$
F(x)=x^2-\sin x,
$$
one has
$$
F''(x)=2+\sin x\geq1
$$
for every $x\in\RR$.

::: {.proof}
Differentiating twice gives
$$
F'(x)=2x-\cos x,
$$
and
$$
F''(x)=2+\sin x.
$$
Since $\sin x\geq-1$, the lower bound follows.
:::

<1>7. The function $F$ has at most two real roots.

::: {.proof}
If it had three distinct roots, choose a closed interval containing all three.
Step <1>6 verifies the hypotheses of part (a) on that interval, contradicting
step <1>4.
:::

<1>8. The number $0$ is a root of $F$.

::: {.proof}
Directly,
$$
F(0)=0.
$$
:::

<1>9. There exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
0<x<\delta
\implies
F(x)<0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
F'(0)=-1,
$$
the definition of derivative gives $\delta>0$ such that
$$
0<|x|<\delta
\implies
\left|
\frac{F(x)-F(0)}x+1
\right|
<
\frac12.
$$
For $0<x<\delta$, this implies
$$
\frac{F(x)}x<-rac12.
$$
Since $x>0$, one has $F(x)<0$.
:::

<1>10. One has
$$
F(1)>0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
\sin1<1,
$$
one has
$$
F(1)=1-\sin1>0.
$$
:::

<1>11. There exists a root
$$
r\in(0,1)
$$
distinct from $0$.

::: {.proof}
Choose
$$
x_0\in(0,\min\{\delta,1\}).
$$
Then step <1>9 gives $F(x_0)<0$, while step <1>10 gives $F(1)>0$.
Continuity of $F$ and the Intermediate Value Theorem give
$$
r\in(x_0,1)
$$
with $F(r)=0$.
:::

<1>12. The function
$$
F(x)=x^2-\sin x
$$
has precisely two real roots.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>8 and <1>11 give two distinct roots, while step <1>7 excludes any
third root.
:::

<1>13. Part (b) follows.

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>12.
:::

<1>14. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>5 and <1>13 establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.
:::
