::: {.problem}
Construct a function $f : \mathbb { R } \to \mathbb { R }$ that is differentiable at a single point.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Define
$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x^2,&x\in\QQ,\\
0,&x\notin\QQ.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
This gives a well-defined real-valued function on all of $\RR$.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
f(0)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The number $0$ is rational, so
$$
f(0)=0^2=0.
$$
:::

<1>3. For every $h\neq0$,
$$
\left|
\frac{f(h)-f(0)}{h}
\right|
\leq
|h|.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $h\in\QQ$, then
$$
\frac{f(h)-f(0)}{h}
=
\frac{h^2}{h}
=h.
$$
If $h\notin\QQ$, then
$$
f(h)=0,
$$
so the quotient is $0$. In either case its absolute value is at most $|h|$.
:::

<1>4. The function $f$ is differentiable at $0$, with
$$
f'(0)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3 and the squeeze theorem,
$$
\lim_{h\to0}
\frac{f(h)-f(0)}{h}
=0.
$$
Thus the derivative exists and equals $0$.
:::

<1>5. Fix $a\neq0$. There are rational and irrational sequences
$$
q_n\to a,
\qquad
r_n\to a.
$$

::: {.proof}
Both $\QQ$ and $\RR\setminus\QQ$ are dense in $\RR$.
:::

<1>6. The function $f$ is discontinuous at every $a\neq0$.

::: {.proof}
Using the sequences from step <1>5,
$$
f(q_n)=q_n^2\longrightarrow a^2,
$$
while
$$
f(r_n)=0\longrightarrow0.
$$
Since $a\neq0$,
$$
a^2\neq0.
$$
Thus the two image sequences have different limits, so the sequential
criterion for continuity fails at $a$.
:::

<1>7. The function $f$ is not differentiable at any $a\neq0$.

::: {.proof}
Differentiability at a point implies continuity at that point. Step <1>6 shows
that $f$ is discontinuous at every nonzero point. Therefore it is not
differentiable there.
:::

<1>8. Hence $f$ is differentiable at exactly one point:
$$
\boxed{0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves differentiability at $0$, and step <1>7 excludes every other
point.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 gives the required example and verifies its differentiability set.
:::
:::
