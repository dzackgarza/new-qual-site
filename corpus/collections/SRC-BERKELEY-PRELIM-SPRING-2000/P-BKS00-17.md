::: {.problem}
Evaluate, for every real $t$,
\[
I(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{e^{itx}}{(x+i)^2}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For fixed $t\in\RR$, set
$$
f_t(z)=\frac{e^{itz}}{(z+i)^2}.
$$

<1>1. On either semicircle of radius $R>2$ in the half-plane appropriate
to the sign of $t$, the arc integral tends to zero as $R\to\infty$.

::: {.proof}
If $t\geq0$ and $\operatorname{Im}z\geq0$, then
$$
\abs{e^{itz}}
=
e^{-t\operatorname{Im}z}
\leq1.
$$
If $t<0$ and $\operatorname{Im}z\leq0$, the same inequality holds,
because $-t\operatorname{Im}z\leq0$.

On either semicircle $\abs{z}=R$,
$$
\abs{z+i}
\geq
R-1.
$$
The arc has length $\pi R$, so the estimation lemma gives
$$
\left|
\int_{\text{arc}}f_t(z)\,dz
\right|
\leq
\frac{\pi R}{(R-1)^2}
\longrightarrow0.
$$
:::

<1>2. If $t\geq0$, then
$$
I(t)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Close the segment $[-R,R]$ by the upper semicircle, oriented
counterclockwise. The only pole of $f_t$ is at $z=-i$, which lies below
the contour. Hence the residue theorem gives zero for the closed-contour
integral. Letting $R\to\infty$ and using step <1>1 yields $I(t)=0$.
:::

<1>3. If $t<0$, then
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i}f_t(z)
=
it e^t.
$$

::: {.proof}
The point $z=-i$ is a pole of order two. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i}f_t(z)
&=
\left.
\frac{d}{dz}e^{itz}
\right|_{z=-i}\\
&=
it e^{it(-i)}\\
&=
it e^t.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>4. If $t<0$, then
$$
I(t)=2\pi t e^t.
$$

::: {.proof}
Close $[-R,R]$ by the lower semicircle. The resulting contour is
clockwise and encloses the pole $-i$, so the residue theorem gives
$$
\int_{-R}^R f_t(x)\,dx
+
\int_{\text{lower arc}} f_t(z)\,dz
=
-2\pi i\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i}f_t(z).
$$
Letting $R\to\infty$ and using steps <1>1 and <1>3,
$$
I(t)
=
-2\pi i(it e^t)
=
2\pi t e^t.
$$
:::

<1>5. Thus
$$
\boxed{
I(t)
=
\begin{cases}
2\pi t e^t,&t<0,\\
0,&t\geq0.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
This combines steps <1>2 and <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the value for every real $t$.
:::
