::: {.problem}
Let $I_1,\ldots,I_n$ be disjoint, closed, nonempty intervals in $\mathbb R$.

1. Prove that if $p$ is a real polynomial of degree less than $n$ satisfying
   \[
   \int_{I_j}p(x)\,dx=0,
   \qquad j=1,\ldots,n,
   \]
   then $p=0$.
2. Prove that there is a nonzero real polynomial $p$ of degree $n$ satisfying all of these integral equations.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If a closed nonempty interval is allowed to be a singleton, the
printed assertion in part 1 is false.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
n=1,
\qquad
I_1=\{0\},
\qquad
p(x)=1.
$$
Then $p$ is a nonzero real polynomial of degree $0<1$, while
$$
\int_{I_1}p(x)\,dx
=
\int_{\{0\}}1\,dx
=0.
$$
Thus the literal statement requires the intervals to be nondegenerate,
equivalently to have positive length.
:::

<1>2. Assume henceforth that every $I_j$ has positive length. If a real
polynomial $p$ satisfies
$$
\int_{I_j}p(x)\,dx=0,
\qquad
1\leq j\leq n,
$$
then either $p=0$ or $p$ has at least one zero in each interval $I_j$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose $p\ne0$, and fix $j$. If $p$ had no zero on the interval
$I_j$, continuity and connectedness would force $p$ to have one strict
sign throughout $I_j$. Since $I_j$ has positive length, its integral
would then have that same strict sign, contradicting
$$
\int_{I_j}p(x)\,dx=0.
$$
Hence $p$ has a zero in $I_j$.
:::

<1>3. Under the positive-length hypothesis, part 1 holds: if
$\deg p<n$ and all $n$ moments vanish, then
$$
p=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $p\ne0$, step <1>2 gives points
$$
x_j\in I_j,
\qquad
p(x_j)=0,
\qquad
1\leq j\leq n.
$$
The intervals are pairwise disjoint, so the $x_j$ are $n$ distinct
real roots of $p$. A nonzero polynomial of degree less than $n$ cannot
have $n$ distinct roots. Therefore $p=0$.
:::

<1>4. For part 2, let
$$
J
=
\{j: I_j\text{ has positive length}\},
\qquad
r=\abs{J}.
$$
If $r=0$, then $p(x)=x^n$ satisfies all the required integral
equations.

::: {.proof}
If $r=0$, every $I_j$ is a singleton. Every integral over a singleton
is zero, so the degree-$n$ polynomial $x^n$ satisfies all $n$
equations.
:::

<1>5. Assume $r\geq1$. Let
$$
W_{r-1}
=
\left\{
a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{r-1}x^{r-1}
:
a_0,\ldots,a_{r-1}\in\RR
\right\}.
$$
The moment map
$$
T_J:W_{r-1}\longrightarrow\RR^r,
\qquad
T_J(q)
=
\left(
\int_{I_j}q(x)\,dx
\right)_{j\in J}
$$
is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
The space $W_{r-1}$ and the target $\RR^r$ both have dimension $r$.
If $T_J(q)=0$, then $q$ has zero integral on each of the $r$
positive-length intervals indexed by $J$. Step <1>3, applied to those
$r$ intervals, gives $q=0$ because $\deg q<r$. Hence $T_J$ is
injective, and equal dimensions make it an isomorphism.
:::

<1>6. There is a nonzero real polynomial $p$ of degree exactly $n$
satisfying all $n$ integral equations, even under the literal source
hypotheses.

::: {.proof}
If $r=0$, step <1>4 supplies such a polynomial. Assume $r\geq1$.
By step <1>5, there exists $q\in W_{r-1}$ such that
$$
T_J(q)
=
-T_J(x^n).
$$
Set
$$
p(x)=x^n+q(x).
$$
Since
$$
\deg q\leq r-1\leq n-1,
$$
the polynomial $p$ has
$$
\boxed{\deg p=n}.
$$
For every $j\in J$, the definition of $q$ gives
$$
\int_{I_j}p(x)\,dx=0.
$$
If $j\notin J$, then $I_j$ is a singleton, so the same integral is
automatically zero. Thus all $n$ equations hold.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves part 1 under the necessary positive-length correction,
while steps <1>4--<1>6 prove part 2 under the source's literal
hypotheses.
:::
:::

::: {.remark title="Erratum"}
The printed problem says only that the intervals are closed and nonempty.
If a singleton counts as an interval, part 1 is false by step <1>1. The
standard corrected formulation of part 1 requires every $I_j$ to have
positive length. Part 2 is valid as printed and is proved in steps
<1>4--<1>6.
:::
