::: {.problem}
Let $I_1,\ldots,I_n$ be disjoint, closed intervals of positive length in $\RR$.

(a) Prove that if $p$ is a real polynomial of degree less than $n$ satisfying
$$
\int_{I_j}p(x)\,dx=0,
\qquad j=1,\ldots,n,
$$
then $p=0$.

(b) Prove that there is a nonzero real polynomial $p$ of degree $n$ satisfying all of these integral equations.
:::

::: {.remark}
The exam asks only for closed nonempty intervals. Without positive length, part (a) fails: for $n=1$, $I_1=\{0\}$ and $p=1$, every integral over $I_1$ is $0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
If a real polynomial $p$ satisfies
$$
\int_{I_j}p(x)\,dx=0,
\qquad
1\leq j\leq n,
$$
then either $p=0$ or $p$ has at least one zero in each interval $I_j$.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $p\ne0$, and fix $j$. If $p$ had no zero on the interval
$I_j$, continuity and connectedness would force $p$ to have one strict
sign throughout $I_j$. Since $I_j$ has positive length, its integral
would then have that same strict sign, contradicting
$$
\int_{I_j}p(x)\,dx=0.
$$
Hence $p$ has a zero in $I_j$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}
Part 1 holds: if $\deg p<n$ and all $n$ integrals vanish, then
$$
p=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
If $p\ne0$, step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives points
$$
x_j\in I_j,
\qquad
p(x_j)=0,
\qquad
1\leq j\leq n.
$$
The intervals are pairwise disjoint, so the $x_j$ are $n$ distinct
real roots of $p$. A nonzero polynomial of degree less than $n$ cannot
have $n$ distinct roots. Therefore $p=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}
Let $W_{n-1}$ be the real vector space of polynomials of degree less
than $n$. The map
$$
T:W_{n-1}\longrightarrow\RR^n,
\qquad
T(q)
=
\left(
\int_{I_j}q(x)\,dx
\right)_{j=1}^n
$$
is a linear isomorphism.

::: pf-proof
The map $T$ is linear, and $W_{n-1}$ and $\RR^n$ both have dimension
$n$. Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} says that $\ker T=0$. Hence $T$ is injective, and
equal dimensions make it an isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}
There is a nonzero real polynomial $p$ of degree exactly $n$
satisfying all $n$ integral equations.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, there exists $q\in W_{n-1}$ such that
$$
T(q)
=
-\left(\int_{I_j}x^n\,dx\right)_{j=1}^n.
$$
Set
$$
p(x)=x^n+q(x).
$$
Since $\deg q\leq n-1$, the polynomial $p$ has
$$
\boxed{\deg p=n},
$$
and in particular $p\ne0$. For every $j$,
$$
\int_{I_j}p(x)\,dx
=
\int_{I_j}x^n\,dx+\int_{I_j}q(x)\,dx
=0.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
:::
