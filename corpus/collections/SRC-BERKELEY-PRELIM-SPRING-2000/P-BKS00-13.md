::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be a nonconstant entire function whose values on the real axis are real and nonnegative. Prove that every real zero of $f$ has even order.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $a\in\RR$ be a zero of $f$, and let $m\geq1$ be its order.

::: pf

::: pf-step

There is an entire function $g$ such that
$$
f(z)=(z-a)^m g(z)
$$
and
$$
g(a)\ne0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $f$ is nonconstant, it is not identically zero. The standard
factorization at a zero of order $m$ therefore gives the displayed
factorization with $g$ holomorphic near $a$ and $g(a)\ne0$. The
quotient extends holomorphically across $a$, and because $f$ is entire,
this defines an entire function $g$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

There exists $\delta>0$ such that $g(x)$ is real and has one
constant nonzero sign for all real $x$ with
$$
\abs{x-a}<\delta.
$$

::: pf-proof

For real $x\ne a$,
$$
g(x)
=
\frac{f(x)}{(x-a)^m}
\in
\RR,
$$
because both numerator and denominator are real. By continuity,
$g(a)\in\RR$ as well. Since $g(a)\ne0$, continuity gives
$\delta>0$ such that $g(x)$ has the same sign as $g(a)$ whenever
$x\in\RR$ and $\abs{x-a}<\delta$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The order $m$ cannot be odd.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $m$ were odd. Choose
$$
0<h<\delta.
$$
Then
$$
(a+h-a)^m=h^m>0,
\qquad
(a-h-a)^m=(-h)^m<0.
$$
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $g(a+h)$ and $g(a-h)$ have the same nonzero sign.
Consequently
$$
f(a+h)=h^m g(a+h)
$$
and
$$
f(a-h)=(-h)^m g(a-h)
$$
have opposite signs. One of them is therefore negative, contradicting
the hypothesis that $f(x)\geq0$ for every real $x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Every real zero of $f$ has even order.

::: pf-proof

The order $m$ is a positive integer, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} excludes odd
values. Hence $m$ is even. Since the real zero $a$ was arbitrary, the
claim holds for every real zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is the desired conclusion.

:::
