::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a complex $n\times n$ matrix such that the sequence $(A^m)_{m=1}^\infty$ converges to a matrix $B$. Prove that $B$ is similar to a diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are all $0$ or $1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The limit matrix satisfies
$$
B^2=B.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
A^m\longrightarrow B,
$$
the subsequence of even powers also satisfies
$$
A^{2m}\longrightarrow B.
$$
On the other hand,
$$
A^{2m}=(A^m)^2.
$$
Matrix multiplication is continuous, so
$$
(A^m)^2\longrightarrow B^2.
$$
Uniqueness of limits therefore gives $B^2=B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The minimal polynomial of $B$ divides
$$
t(t-1).
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} says
$$
B^2-B=0.
$$
Thus the polynomial
$$
t^2-t=t(t-1)
$$
annihilates $B$. By the defining divisibility property of the minimal
polynomial, the minimal polynomial of $B$ divides $t(t-1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The matrix $B$ is diagonalizable over $\CC$, and every
eigenvalue of $B$ belongs to
$$
\{0,1\}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The polynomial $t(t-1)$ splits over $\CC$ with two distinct roots.
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the minimal polynomial of $B$ also splits into distinct
linear factors. Hence $B$ is diagonalizable. Its eigenvalues are roots
of its minimal polynomial, so they are among $0$ and $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The matrix $B$ is similar to a diagonal matrix with only zeros
and ones on the diagonal.

::: pf-proof

Diagonalizability from step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives a basis of eigenvectors for
$B$. In that basis, the diagonal entries are the corresponding
eigenvalues, all of which lie in $\{0,1\}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is exactly the desired conclusion.

:::
