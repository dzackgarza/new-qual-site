::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite group and $p$ a prime. Suppose $a,b\in G$ have order $p$ and
\[
b\notin\langle a\rangle.
\]
Prove that $G$ contains at least $p^2-1$ elements of order $p$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
A=\langle a\rangle,
\qquad
B=\langle b\rangle.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The subgroups $A$ and $B$ are distinct and satisfy
$$
A\cap B=\{e\}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The hypothesis $b\notin A$ implies $B\ne A$. Both groups have prime
order $p$. Their intersection is a subgroup of each, so its order is
either $1$ or $p$. If it had order $p$, then $A=B$. Hence the
intersection is trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $A$ normalizes $B$, then $A$ centralizes $B$.

::: pf-proof

Conjugation gives a homomorphism
$$
A\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(B).
$$
Since $B\cong C_p$,
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(B)}=p-1.
$$
The image of the homomorphism has order dividing both
$$
\abs A=p
$$
and $p-1$. Hence the image is trivial, so every element of $A$
commutes with every element of $B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $A$ normalizes $B$, then $G$ contains at least
$$
p^2-1
$$
elements of order $p$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the product $AB$ is a subgroup and
$$
AB\cong A\times B\cong C_p\times C_p.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{AB}=p^2.
$$
Every nonidentity element of $C_p\times C_p$ has order $p$, so the
$p^2-1$ nonidentity elements of $AB$ all have order $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Suppose instead that $A$ does not normalize $B$. Then the orbit
of $B$ under conjugation by $A$ consists of exactly $p$ distinct
subgroups of order $p$.

::: pf-proof

The stabilizer of $B$ for the conjugation action is
$$
\operatorname{Stab}_A(B)
=
A\cap N_G(B).
$$
Because $A$ does not normalize $B$, this stabilizer is a proper subgroup
of the order-$p$ group $A$, hence is trivial. By orbit-stabilizer, the
orbit has size
$$
[A:\operatorname{Stab}_A(B)]
=p.
$$
Conjugation preserves subgroup order, so all orbit members have order
$p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

None of the $p$ conjugates from step [](#s4){.pf-ref} equals $A$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
a^iBa^{-i}=A
$$
for some integer $i$. Conjugating both sides by $a^{-i}$ gives
$$
B
=
a^{-i}Aa^i.
$$
But $A=\langle a\rangle$ is fixed by conjugation by every power of
$a$, so the right-hand side is $A$. This contradicts $A\ne B$ from
step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Any two distinct subgroups of order $p$ have no common
nonidentity element.

::: pf-proof

The intersection of two order-$p$ subgroups is a subgroup of each. If
it contains a nonidentity element, then its order is $p$, so both
subgroups equal their intersection and hence equal each other.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

If $A$ does not normalize $B$, then $G$ contains at least
$$
p^2-1
$$
elements of order $p$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref}, the $p$ conjugates of $B$, together with $A$,
give $p+1$ distinct subgroups of order $p$. Each contains exactly
$p-1$ nonidentity elements, all of order $p$, and step [](#s6){.pf-ref} shows that
these sets of nonidentity elements are pairwise disjoint. Hence their
union contains
$$
(p+1)(p-1)
=
p^2-1
$$
elements of order $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

In all cases, $G$ contains at least
$$
\boxed{p^2-1}
$$
elements of order $p$.

::: pf-proof

If $A$ normalizes $B$, use step [](#s3){.pf-ref}. Otherwise use step [](#s7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s8){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
