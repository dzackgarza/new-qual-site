::: {.problem}
Let $A_n=(a_{jk})$ be the $n\times n$ matrix with
\[
a_{jk}=\begin{cases}
1,& |j-k|=1,\\
0,& \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Prove that the eigenvalues of $A_n$ are symmetric with respect to the origin.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
D
=
\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots,(-1)^{n-1}).
$$

<1>1. The matrix $D$ is invertible and satisfies
$$
D^{-1}=D.
$$

::: {.proof}
Every diagonal entry of $D$ is either $1$ or $-1$. Hence
$$
D^2=I,
$$
which gives the claim.
:::

<1>2. The matrices $A_n$ and $-A_n$ are similar:
$$
DA_nD^{-1}=-A_n.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $D^{-1}=D$, the $(j,k)$ entry of $DA_nD$ is
$$
(-1)^{j-1}a_{jk}(-1)^{k-1}
=
(-1)^{j+k-2}a_{jk}.
$$
If $a_{jk}=0$, this entry is zero. If $a_{jk}=1$, then
$\abs{j-k}=1$, so $j$ and $k$ have opposite parity and therefore
$$
(-1)^{j+k-2}=-1.
$$
Thus every entry of $DA_nD$ is the negative of the corresponding entry
of $A_n$.
:::

<1>3. If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A_n$ with algebraic
multiplicity $m$, then $-\lambda$ is also an eigenvalue of $A_n$ with
algebraic multiplicity $m$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $A_n$ and $-A_n$ are similar, so they have the same
characteristic polynomial and hence the same eigenvalues with the same
algebraic multiplicities. The eigenvalues of $-A_n$ are precisely the
negatives of the eigenvalues of $A_n$, with multiplicities preserved.
Therefore $-\lambda$ occurs for $A_n$ with the same multiplicity as
$\lambda$.
:::

<1>4. The eigenvalues of $A_n$ are symmetric with respect to the
origin.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives the required pairing
$$
\lambda\longleftrightarrow-\lambda
$$
with multiplicity.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the desired conclusion.
:::
