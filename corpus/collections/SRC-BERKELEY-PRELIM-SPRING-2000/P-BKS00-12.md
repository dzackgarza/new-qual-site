::: {.problem}
Suppose $H_1$ and $H_2$ are distinct subgroups of a group $G$ with
\[
[G:H_1]=[G:H_2]=3.
\]
What are the possible values of
\[
[G:H_1\cap H_2]?
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
K=H_1\cap H_2.
$$

<1>1. The index $[H_1:K]$ is at most $3$.

::: {.proof}
Define a map on left-coset sets by
$$
H_1/K\longrightarrow G/H_2,
\qquad
hK\longmapsto hH_2.
$$
This is well-defined: if $hK=h'K$, then
$$
h'^{-1}h\in K\subseteq H_2,
$$
so $hH_2=h'H_2$.

The map is also injective. If $hH_2=h'H_2$ with $h,h'\in H_1$, then
$$
h'^{-1}h\in H_2.
$$
Since also $h'^{-1}h\in H_1$, it lies in $K$, so $hK=h'K$.
Therefore
$$
[H_1:K]
\leq
[G:H_2]
=3.
$$
:::

<1>2. The index $[H_1:K]$ is either $2$ or $3$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the only possibilities are $1,2,3$. If
$[H_1:K]=1$, then $H_1=K\subseteq H_2$. The index formula would give
$$
[G:H_1]
=
[G:H_2][H_2:H_1],
$$
hence
$$
3=3[H_2:H_1].
$$
Thus $[H_2:H_1]=1$ and $H_1=H_2$, contrary to hypothesis. Hence only
$2$ and $3$ remain.
:::

<1>3. The only possible values of $[G:K]$ are
$$
6
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
9.
$$

::: {.proof}
The index formula gives
$$
[G:K]
=
[G:H_1][H_1:K]
=
3[H_1:K].
$$
Now apply step <1>2.
:::

<1>4. The value $6$ occurs.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
G=S_3,
\qquad
H_1=\langle(12)\rangle,
\qquad
H_2=\langle(13)\rangle.
$$
Both subgroups have order $2$, hence index $3$, and they intersect
trivially. Therefore
$$
[G:H_1\cap H_2]
=
[S_3:\{e\}]
=6.
$$
:::

<1>5. The value $9$ occurs.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
G=C_3\times C_3,
\qquad
H_1=C_3\times\{0\},
\qquad
H_2=\{0\}\times C_3.
$$
Each subgroup has index $3$, and their intersection is the identity
subgroup. Hence
$$
[G:H_1\cap H_2]=9.
$$
:::

<1>6. The possible values are
$$
\boxed{6\text{ and }9}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 shows that no other values can occur, while steps <1>4 and
<1>5 realize both listed values.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 answers the question.
:::
