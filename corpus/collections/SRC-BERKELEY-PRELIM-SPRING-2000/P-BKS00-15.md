    The displayed sum is the squared Frobenius norm of DF(0), which

    at zero absorbs the o(||x||) remainder into the gap to one, giving
    ||F(x)||<||x|| on a sufficiently small ball.

::: {.problem}
Let $F=(F_1,\ldots,F_n):\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ be differentiable with $F(0)=0$. Suppose
\[
\sum_{j,k=1}^n
\left|
\frac{\partial F_j(0)}{\partial x_k}
\right|^2
=c<1.
\]
Prove that there is a ball $B$ centered at $0$ such that
\[
F(B)\subset B.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
A=DF(0).
$$

<1>1. For every $v\in\RR^n$,
$$
\norm{Av}_2
\leq
\sqrt c\,\norm{v}_2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
A=(a_{jk})_{j,k=1}^n,
\qquad
a_{jk}
=
\frac{\partial F_j(0)}{\partial x_k}.
$$
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\norm{Av}_2^2
&=
\sum_{j=1}^n
\left|
\sum_{k=1}^n a_{jk}v_k
\right|^2\\
&\leq
\sum_{j=1}^n
\left(
\sum_{k=1}^n\abs{a_{jk}}^2
\right)
\left(
\sum_{k=1}^n\abs{v_k}^2
\right)\\
&=
c\norm{v}_2^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking square roots gives the claim.
:::

<1>2. There exist a number $q$ with
$$
\sqrt c<q<1
$$
and a radius $r>0$ such that
$$
0<\norm{x}_2<r
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\norm{F(x)-Ax}_2
<
(q-\sqrt c)\norm{x}_2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $c<1$, choose for example
$$
q=\frac{1+\sqrt c}{2}.
$$
Because $F(0)=0$ and $F$ is differentiable at $0$,
$$
F(x)
=
Ax+o(\norm{x}_2)
$$
as $x\to0$. The definition of the little-$o$ term therefore gives
the stated radius $r$.
:::

<1>3. If
$$
B=\{x\in\RR^n:\norm{x}_2<r\},
$$
then
$$
F(B)\subseteq B.
$$

::: {.proof}
The point $0$ belongs to $B$ and $F(0)=0\in B$. If $x\in B$ is
nonzero, steps <1>1 and <1>2 give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\norm{F(x)}_2
&\leq
\norm{Ax}_2
+
\norm{F(x)-Ax}_2\\
&<
\sqrt c\,\norm{x}_2
+
(q-\sqrt c)\norm{x}_2\\
&=
q\norm{x}_2\\
&<
r.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $F(x)\in B$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 constructs the required ball centered at the origin.
:::
