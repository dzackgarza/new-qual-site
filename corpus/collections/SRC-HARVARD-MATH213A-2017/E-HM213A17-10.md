::: {.problem}
For $a,b\in\mathbb C$, regard $a$ and $b$ as vectors in $\mathbb R^2$.
Express the complex product $ab$ in terms of the dot product and the planar cross product of those vectors.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. With the standard oriented planar cross product, the dot and cross products of the vectors represented by $a$ and $b$ determine $\overline a b$, not $ab$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
a=x+iy,
\qquad
b=u+iv,
\]
and use
\[
a\cdot b=xu+yv,
\qquad
a\times b=xv-yu.
\]
Then
\[
\overline a b
=(x-iy)(u+iv)
=(xu+yv)+i(xv-yu),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\overline a b=a\cdot b+i(a\times b)}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
a\overline b=a\cdot b-i(a\times b).
\]
:::

<1>2. Consequently the source exercise, if interpreted as asking for $ab$ from only the two scalar quantities $a\cdot b$ and $a\times b$, is false as written.
::: {.proof}
Take first
\[
a=b=1.
\]
Then
\[
a\cdot b=1,
\qquad
a\times b=0,
\qquad
ab=1.
\]
Now rotate both vectors through $\pi/2$ and take
\[
a=b=i.
\]
The dot and cross products are still
\[
a\cdot b=1,
\qquad
a\times b=0,
\]
but now
\[
ab=-1.
\]
Thus no function of the pair $(a\cdot b,a\times b)$ alone can equal $ab$ for all $a,b\in\mathbb C$.
:::

<1>3. If one reflects one of the two vectors, then $ab$ can be written using the same operations.
::: {.proof}
The vector represented by $\overline b=u-iv$ is the reflection of the vector represented by $b$ across the real axis. Hence
\[
a\cdot\overline b=xu-yv,
\qquad
a\times\overline b=-xv-yu.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{ab=a\cdot\overline b-i(a\times\overline b)}.
\]
This uses the reflected vector $\overline b$, whereas <1>1 is the direct identity involving the original two vectors.
:::
:::
