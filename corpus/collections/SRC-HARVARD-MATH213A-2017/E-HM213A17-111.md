::: {.problem}
Does there exist a univalent map
\[
f:\mathbb C\setminus\Delta\to\mathbb C
\]
of the form
\[
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_n}{z^n}
\]
with
\[
b_2=\frac1{\sqrt2}?
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
No such univalent map exists.

<1>1. The area theorem would force every coefficient except $b_2$ to vanish.
::: {.proof}
For a normalized exterior univalent map
\[
f(z)=z+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{b_n}{z^n},
\]
the area theorem gives
\[
\sum_{n\ge1} n|b_n|^2\le1.
\]
If
\[
b_2=\frac1{\sqrt2},
\]
then the $n=2$ term alone contributes
\[
2|b_2|^2=2\cdot\frac12=1.
\]
Therefore every remaining nonnegative term must vanish:
\[
b_n=0\qquad(n\ne2).
\]
Hence the only possible map is
\[
f(z)=z+\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,z^2}.
\]
:::

<1>2. This forced candidate is not univalent on the exterior disk.
::: {.proof}
Its derivative is
\[
f'(z)=1-\frac{\sqrt2}{z^3}.
\]
Thus $f'(z)=0$ whenever
\[
z^3=\sqrt2.
\]
Every such critical point has modulus
\[
|z|=2^{1/6}>1,
\]
so it lies in the exterior domain. A holomorphic injective map has nonzero derivative everywhere in its domain. Hence this candidate is not univalent, contradicting the assumption.
:::

Therefore no normalized exterior univalent map can have $b_2=1/\sqrt2$.
:::
