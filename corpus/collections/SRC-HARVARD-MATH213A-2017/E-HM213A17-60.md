::: {.problem}
Let $f:\Delta\to\Delta$ be a proper analytic map, and let $F:\overline\Delta\to\overline\Delta$ be its continuous extension.
Suppose $f(0)=0$.
Show that for every continuous function $\phi:S^1\to\mathbb R$, the functions $\phi$ and $\phi\circ F$ have the same average over $S^1$.

Hint: use the harmonic extension of $\phi$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $u:\overline\Delta\to\mathbb R$ be the harmonic extension of $\phi$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The function $u\circ f$ is harmonic on $\Delta$ and extends
continuously to $\overline\Delta$ with boundary values $\phi\circ F$.

::: pf-proof

Since $u$ is harmonic and $f$ is holomorphic,
\[
\Delta(u\circ f)=|f'|^2(\Delta u)\circ f=0,
\]
so $u\circ f$ is harmonic.

A proper disk map is a finite Blaschke product, hence its continuous
extension satisfies $F(S^1)\subset S^1$. Since $u|_{S^1}=\phi$,
\[
(u\circ F)|_{S^1}=\phi\circ F.
\]
Continuity of $u$ and $F$ on the closed disk gives the claimed continuous
extension.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The average of $\phi$ over $S^1$ equals $u(0)$.

::: pf-proof

By the mean-value property for harmonic functions,
\[
u(0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\phi(e^{it})\,dt.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The average of $\phi\circ F$ over $S^1$ equals $(u\circ f)(0)$.

::: pf-proof

Apply the mean-value property to the harmonic function $u\circ f$ from
step [](#s1){.pf-ref}:
\[
(u\circ f)(0)
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\phi(F(e^{it}))\,dt.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The two averages are equal.

::: pf-proof

Since $f(0)=0$,
\[
(u\circ f)(0)=u(f(0))=u(0).
\]
Combining steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} therefore gives
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\phi(F(e^{it}))\,dt
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\phi(e^{it})\,dt.
\]

:::
