::: {.problem}
Determine the zeros of the Weierstrass $\wp$-function for each of the lattices
\[
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Zi
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\omega,
\]
where
\[
\omega=\frac{1+\sqrt{-3}}2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Modulo the relevant lattice, the zeros are:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\Lambda & \text{zeros of }\wp_\Lambda\\ \hline
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Zi
& (1+i)/2\quad\text{with multiplicity }2,\\[2mm]
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\omega
& \pm(1+\omega)/3\quad\text{each simple.}
\end{array}
}
\]
Thus in $\mathbb C$ one adds all lattice translates of the displayed points.

<1>1. If $u\Lambda=\Lambda$, then
\[
\wp_\Lambda(uz)=u^{-2}\wp_\Lambda(z).
\]
::: {.proof}
From the defining series,
\[
\wp_\Lambda(z)
=\frac1{z^2}
 +\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}
 \left(
 \frac1{(z-\lambda)^2}-\frac1{\lambda^2}
 \right).
\]
If multiplication by $u$ permutes $\Lambda$, write $\lambda=u\mu$ in the sum. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\wp_\Lambda(uz)
&=\frac1{u^2z^2}
 +\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}
 \left(
 \frac1{(uz-u\mu)^2}-\frac1{(u\mu)^2}
 \right)\\
&=u^{-2}\wp_\Lambda(z).
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>2. For the square lattice
\[
\Lambda_\square=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Zi,
\]
the point
\[
c=\frac{1+i}{2}
\]
is a zero of $\wp_{\Lambda_\square}$.
::: {.proof}
Multiplication by $i$ preserves $\Lambda_\square$, so <1>1 gives
\[
\wp(iz)=i^{-2}\wp(z)=-\wp(z).
\]
But
\[
ic=\frac{-1+i}{2}=c-1,
\]
so periodicity gives
\[
\wp(ic)=\wp(c).
\]
Combining the two identities,
\[
\wp(c)=-\wp(c),
\]
and hence
\[
\wp(c)=0.
\]
:::

<1>3. The square-lattice zero in <1>2 has multiplicity two and is the only zero modulo $\Lambda_\square$.
::: {.proof}
The point $c$ is a nonzero point of order two on
\[
\mathbb C/\Lambda_\square,
\]
because
\[
2c=1+i\in\Lambda_\square.
\]
McMullen's description of the critical points of $\wp$ therefore gives
\[
\wp'(c)=0.
\]
Thus the zero at $c$ has multiplicity at least two.

On the torus, $\wp$ has one pole, of order two, so it has exactly two zeros counted with multiplicity. Hence the zero at $c$ has multiplicity exactly two and exhausts all zeros modulo the lattice.
:::

<1>4. For the hexagonal lattice
\[
\Lambda_\hexagon=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\omega,
\qquad
\omega=e^{i\pi/3},
\]
the two points
\[
z_0=\frac{1+\omega}{3},
\qquad
-z_0
\]
are zeros.
::: {.proof}
Set
\[
\rho=\omega^2=e^{2\pi i/3}=\omega-1.
\]
Multiplication by $\rho$ preserves the lattice, since
\[
\rho\cdot1=\omega-1\in\Lambda_\hexagon,
\qquad
\rho\omega=\omega^3=-1\in\Lambda_\hexagon.
\]
By <1>1,
\[
\wp(\rho z)=\rho^{-2}\wp(z)=\rho\wp(z),
\]
because $\rho^3=1$.

Now
\[
(\rho-1)z_0
=\frac{(\omega-2)(1+\omega)}3
=-1\in\Lambda_\hexagon.
\]
Thus
\[
\rho z_0\equiv z_0\pmod{\Lambda_\hexagon}.
\]
Periodicity and the scaling identity give
\[
\wp(z_0)
=\wp(\rho z_0)
=\rho\wp(z_0).
\]
Since $\rho\ne1$,
\[
\wp(z_0)=0.
\]
The function $\wp$ is even, so
\[
\wp(-z_0)=0
\]
as well.
:::

<1>5. These are exactly the two hexagonal-lattice zeros, and both are simple.
::: {.proof}
The two points are distinct modulo the lattice: if
\[
z_0\equiv-z_0,
\]
then
\[
2z_0=\frac{2+2\omega}{3}
\]
would lie in $\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\omega$, which it does not.

Thus $\wp$ has at least two distinct zeros on the torus. As in <1>3, its unique double pole implies that the total zero multiplicity is exactly two. Therefore these are all the zeros, and each has multiplicity one.
:::
