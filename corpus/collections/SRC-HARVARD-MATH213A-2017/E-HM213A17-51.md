::: {.problem}
(a) Give a formula for the stereographic projection $z\in\mathbb C$ of a point on $S^2$ with latitude and longitude $(\alpha,\beta)$.

(b) Find the distance between Boston $(42^\circ\mathrm N,71^\circ\mathrm W)$ and Rio $(23^\circ\mathrm S,43^\circ\mathrm W)$, assuming the circumference of the Earth is $40{,}000$ km.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A point of latitude $\alpha$ and longitude $\beta$ on the unit sphere
has Cartesian coordinates
\[
P=(\cos\alpha\cos\beta,\,\cos\alpha\sin\beta,\,\sin\alpha).
\]
::: {.proof}
Latitude $\alpha$ gives height $\sin\alpha$ above the equatorial plane and
equatorial radius $\cos\alpha$. Resolving that equatorial component at
longitude $\beta$ gives the stated coordinates.
:::

<1>2. With stereographic projection from the north pole to the equatorial
plane, the complex coordinate of $P$ is
\[
\boxed{
z=e^{i\beta}\frac{\cos\alpha}{1-\sin\alpha}
=e^{i\beta}\tan\left(\frac\pi4+\frac\alpha2\right).}
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $N=(0,0,1)$. The line through $N$ and
\[
P=(X,Y,Z)
\]
meets the plane $Z=0$ at
\[
\left(\frac{X}{1-Z},\frac{Y}{1-Z},0\right).
\]
Substituting
\[
X+iY=\cos\alpha\,e^{i\beta},
\qquad
Z=\sin\alpha
\]
gives
\[
z=e^{i\beta}\frac{\cos\alpha}{1-\sin\alpha}.
\]
Multiplying numerator and denominator by $1+\sin\alpha$ yields
\[
\frac{\cos\alpha}{1-\sin\alpha}
=\frac{1+\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}
=\tan\left(\frac\pi4+\frac\alpha2\right),
\]
which is the desired formula.
:::

<1>3. If two points have latitudes $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ and longitudes
$\beta_1,\beta_2$, their central angle $\gamma$ satisfies
\[
\cos\gamma
=\sin\alpha_1\sin\alpha_2
+\cos\alpha_1\cos\alpha_2\cos(\beta_1-\beta_2).
\]
::: {.proof}
The points lie on the unit sphere, so the cosine of the angle between their
position vectors is their Euclidean dot product. Using the coordinates in
<1>1,
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1\cdot P_2
&=\cos\alpha_1\cos\alpha_2
  (\cos\beta_1\cos\beta_2+\sin\beta_1\sin\beta_2)
  +\sin\alpha_1\sin\alpha_2\\
&=\cos\alpha_1\cos\alpha_2\cos(\beta_1-\beta_2)
  +\sin\alpha_1\sin\alpha_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
This equals $\cos\gamma$.
:::

<1>4. For Boston and Rio,
\[
\alpha_B=42^\circ,
\quad
\beta_B=-71^\circ,
\quad
\alpha_R=-23^\circ,
\quad
\beta_R=-43^\circ,
\]
so
\[
\cos\gamma
=\sin42^\circ\sin(-23^\circ)
+\cos42^\circ\cos23^\circ\cos28^\circ
\approx0.3425464.
\]
Thus
\[
\gamma\approx1.2211704\text{ radians}
\approx69.9679^\circ.
\]
::: {.proof}
West longitudes are negative and south latitudes are negative, so the
longitude difference is
\[
|-71^\circ-(-43^\circ)|=28^\circ.
\]
Substitution into <1>3 gives the displayed value and hence the stated angle.
:::

<1>5. The great-circle distance between Boston and Rio is therefore
\[
\boxed{\frac{40{,}000}{2\pi}\gamma\approx7{,}774\text{ km}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
An arc subtending angle $\gamma$ occupies the fraction
$\gamma/(2\pi)$ of a full great circle. Multiplying that fraction by the
given circumference $40{,}000$ km gives
\[
d=40{,}000\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}
\approx7{,}774.2\text{ km}.
\]
:::
