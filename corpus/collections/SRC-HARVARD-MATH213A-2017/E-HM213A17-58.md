::: {.problem}
Let $f:\Delta\to\Delta$ be analytic and let $f^n=f\circ\cdots\circ f$ denote the $n$-fold iterate.
Show that either $f$ has a fixed point in $\Delta$, or
\[
|f^n(z)|\longrightarrow1
\]
for every $z\in\Delta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $f$ is conjugate to a rotation, then $f$ has a fixed point in
$\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
f=A^{-1}\circ R_\theta\circ A,
\qquad
R_\theta(z)=e^{i\theta}z,
\]
for some $A\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta)$. Since $R_\theta(0)=0$, the point
\[
p=A^{-1}(0)\in\Delta
\]
satisfies $f(p)=p$.
:::

<1>2. Suppose $f$ is not conjugate to a rotation. Then there is
$p\in\overline\Delta$ such that
\[
f^n(z)\longrightarrow p
\]
for every $z\in\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
This is McMullen's Theorem 2.30 (Denjoy--Wolff), immediately preceding
these exercises. The retained PDF states $p\in\overline\Delta$; the Markdown
extraction drops the bar over $\Delta$ in that theorem statement.
:::

<1>3. If the point $p$ from <1>2 lies in $\Delta$, then it is a fixed point
of $f$.
::: {.proof}
For any $z\in\Delta$,
\[
f^{n+1}(z)=f(f^n(z)).
\]
Letting $n\to\infty$ and using continuity of $f$ gives
\[
p=f(p).
\]
:::

<1>4. If $f$ has no fixed point in $\Delta$, then
\[
|f^n(z)|\longrightarrow1
\]
for every $z\in\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, such an $f$ cannot be conjugate to a rotation, so <1>2 applies.
By <1>3, its Denjoy--Wolff point cannot lie in $\Delta$. Hence
\[
p\in\partial\Delta,
\qquad |p|=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
|f^n(z)|\longrightarrow|p|=1
\]
for every $z\in\Delta$, as required.
:::
:::
