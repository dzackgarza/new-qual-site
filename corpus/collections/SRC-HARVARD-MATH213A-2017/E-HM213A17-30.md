::: {.problem}
Let $C(R)$ be the circular arc defined by $|z|=R$ and $\operatorname{Im}z>0$.
Prove that
\[
\int_{C(R)}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{as }R\to\infty.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Parametrize the upper semicircle by
\[
z=Re^{i\theta},\qquad 0\le\theta\le\pi.
\]
Then
\[
\left|\int_{C(R)}\frac{e^{iz}}z\,dz\right|
\le \frac{\pi}{R},
\]
so the integral tends to $0$ as $R\to\infty$.

<1>1. Under the parametrization $z=Re^{i\theta}$,
\[
\int_{C(R)}\frac{e^{iz}}z\,dz
=i\int_0^\pi e^{iRe^{i\theta}}\,d\theta.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
dz=iRe^{i\theta}\,d\theta=iz\,d\theta,
\]
we have $dz/z=i\,d\theta$, which gives the displayed identity.
:::

<1>2. Consequently,
\[
\left|\int_{C(R)}\frac{e^{iz}}z\,dz\right|
\le\int_0^\pi e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta.
\]
::: {.proof}
For $z=Re^{i\theta}$,
\[
i z=iR\cos\theta-R\sin\theta,
\]
so
\[
|e^{iz}|=e^{-R\sin\theta}.
\]
Apply the triangle inequality to <1>1.
:::

<1>3. The last integral is at most $\pi/R$.
::: {.proof}
On $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$, concavity of $\sin\theta$ gives
\[
\sin\theta\ge\frac{2\theta}{\pi}.
\]
By symmetry, on $\pi/2\le\theta\le\pi$,
\[
\sin\theta\ge\frac{2(\pi-\theta)}{\pi}.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\pi e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta
&\le2\int_0^{\pi/2}e^{-2R\theta/\pi}\,d\theta\\
&=\frac{\pi}{R}(1-e^{-R})\\
&\le\frac{\pi}{R}.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>4. Therefore
\[
\int_{C(R)}\frac{e^{iz}}z\,dz\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>3,
\[
0\le
\left|\int_{C(R)}\frac{e^{iz}}z\,dz\right|
\le\frac{\pi}{R}\longrightarrow0.
\]
:::
:::
