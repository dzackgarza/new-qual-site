::: {.problem}
For a locally univalent holomorphic map $f$, define
$$
S_f=N_f'-\frac12N_f^2,
\qquad
N_f=\frac{f''}{f'}.
$$
Prove the composition law
$$
S_{f\circ g}=S_g+(S_f\circ g)(g')^2.
$$

:::

::: {.solution}
The problem is Section 4.4 Exercise 23 of C. McMullen's Harvard Math 213a notes (4 December 2017), which states the law and no proof; the computation below is the card's own.

Throughout, $f$ and $g$ are locally univalent holomorphic maps, so $f'\circ g$ and $g'$ are nowhere zero and the nonlinearity $N_h=h''/h'$ is holomorphic; $S_h=N_h'-\tfrac12N_h^{2}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nonlinearity}
$N_{f\circ g}=N_g+(N_f\circ g)g'$.

::: pf-proof
Let $u=f\circ g$. By the chain rule $u'=(f'\circ g)g'$ and $u''=(f''\circ g)(g')^{2}+(f'\circ g)g''$. Hence
$$
N_u=\frac{u''}{u'}=\frac{(f''\circ g)(g')^{2}+(f'\circ g)g''}{(f'\circ g)g'}=\frac{(f''\circ g)g'}{f'\circ g}+\frac{g''}{g'}=(N_f\circ g)g'+N_g,
$$
dividing by the nonzero products $(f'\circ g)g'$ and using the definition of $N$ for $f$ and for $g$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #derivative}
$N_{f\circ g}'=N_g'+(N_f'\circ g)(g')^{2}+(N_f\circ g)g''$.

::: pf-proof
Differentiate step [](#nonlinearity){.pf-ref} term by term: $(N_g)'=N_g'$; and $(N_f\circ g)'=(N_f'\circ g)g'$, so the product $(N_f\circ g)g'$ has derivative $(N_f'\circ g)(g')^{2}+(N_f\circ g)g''$. Summing gives the display.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square}
$\tfrac12N_{f\circ g}^{2}=\tfrac12N_g^{2}+N_g(N_f\circ g)g'+\tfrac12(N_f\circ g)^{2}(g')^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
Squaring step [](#nonlinearity){.pf-ref} and using commutativity of multiplication of holomorphic functions,
$$
N_{f\circ g}^{2}=N_g^{2}+2N_g(N_f\circ g)g'+(N_f\circ g)^{2}(g')^{2},
$$
and halving gives the display.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cancel}
$N_g(N_f\circ g)g'=(N_f\circ g)g''$.

::: pf-proof
$N_g=g''/g'$, so $N_g(N_f\circ g)g'=\dfrac{g''}{g'}(N_f\circ g)g'=(N_f\circ g)g''$, since $g'\neq0$ wherever $g$ is locally univalent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #law}
$S_{f\circ g}=S_g+(S_f\circ g)(g')^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#derivative){.pf-ref} and [](#square){.pf-ref},
$$
S_{f\circ g}=N_{f\circ g}'-\tfrac12N_{f\circ g}^{2}
=\Big(N_g'-\tfrac12N_g^{2}\Big)+\Big((N_f'\circ g)-\tfrac12(N_f\circ g)^{2}\Big)(g')^{2}+(N_f\circ g)g''-N_g(N_f\circ g)g',
$$
and by step [](#cancel){.pf-ref} the last two terms cancel. The first bracket is $S_g$ and the second is $S_f\circ g$, by the definition of $S$ applied to $g$ and to $f$ at $g(z)$; so $S_{f\circ g}=S_g+(S_f\circ g)(g')^{2}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #differential}
Equivalently, for the quadratic differential $S_f(z)\,dz^{2}$ one has $S(f\circ g)=S(g)+g^{*}S(f)$.

::: pf-proof
$S_{f\circ g}(z)=S_g(z)+S_f(g(z))g'(z)^{2}$ by step [](#law){.pf-ref}, and the two terms are the coefficient functions of $S_g(z)\,dz^{2}$ and of the pullback $g^{*}(S_f(w)\,dw^{2})$, whose coefficient at $z$ is $S_f(g(z))g'(z)^{2}$.

:::
