::: {.problem}
Let $f$ and $g$ be nonconstant entire functions, and suppose
\[
f\circ g=f.
\]
Prove that
\[
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The identity $f\circ g=f$ forces every iterate of $g$ to preserve $f$.
This makes the family of iterates normal on all of $\mathbb C$. A nonlinear
entire function has nonempty Julia set, so $g$ must be affine, hence an
automorphism of the plane.

<1>1. For every integer $n\ge1$,
\[
f\circ g^n=f.
\]
::: {.proof}
The case $n=1$ is the hypothesis. If $f\circ g^n=f$, then
\[
f\circ g^{n+1}
=(f\circ g^n)\circ g
=f\circ g
=f.
\]
The result follows by induction.
:::

<1>2. The family of iterates
\[
\{g^n:n\ge1\}
\]
is normal in a neighborhood of every point of $\mathbb C$.
::: {.proof}
Fix $z_0\in\mathbb C$. Choose a disk $U$ about $z_0$ whose closure is
compact. Then
\[
K=f(\overline U)
\]
is compact.

Since $f$ is nonconstant entire, it is unbounded. We may therefore choose
three points $a_1,a_2,a_3\in\mathbb C$ such that the values
\[
f(a_1),\quad f(a_2),\quad f(a_3)
\]
are distinct and all lie outside $K$.

For $z\in U$, no iterate $g^n(z)$ can equal any $a_j$. Indeed, if
$g^n(z)=a_j$, then <1>1 gives
\[
f(z)=f(g^n(z))=f(a_j),
\]
but $f(z)\in K$ while $f(a_j)\notin K$, a contradiction.

Thus every function $g^n|_U$ omits the same three values
$a_1,a_2,a_3$. By Montel's theorem, the family
\[
\{g^n|_U:n\ge1\}
\]
is normal. Since $z_0$ was arbitrary, the iterates of $g$ form a normal
family on all of $\mathbb C$.
:::

<1>3. The function $g$ cannot be a nonlinear entire function.
::: {.proof}
Recall the standard Fatou--Julia theorem for entire iteration: if an entire
map is nonlinear (that is, either a polynomial of degree at least two or a
transcendental entire function), then its Julia set is nonempty. Equivalently,
its family of iterates fails to be normal in a neighborhood of at least one
point of $\mathbb C$.

But <1>2 shows that the iterates of $g$ are normal in a neighborhood of
every point. Hence $g$ is not nonlinear. Since $g$ is nonconstant, it must
have the form
\[
g(z)=az+b
\]
with $a\neq0$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $g\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)$.
::: {.proof}
For
\[
g(z)=az+b,
\qquad a\neq0,
\]
the inverse map is
\[
g^{-1}(w)=\frac{w-b}{a},
\]
which is entire. Thus $g$ is a biholomorphic self-map of $\mathbb C$, i.e.
\[
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C).
\]
:::
:::
