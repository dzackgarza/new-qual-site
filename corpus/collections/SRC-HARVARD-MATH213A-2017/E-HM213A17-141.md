::: {.problem}
Let $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$, let $[z_i]$ be finitely many distinct points of $E$, and prescribe at each $z_i$ the principal part
\[
F_i(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_i} a_{i,j}(z-z_i)^{-j}.
\]
Prove that there exists a meromorphic function
\[
f:E\to\widehat{\mathbb C}
\]
with poles at the $[z_i]$ and these prescribed principal parts if and only if
\[
\sum_i a_{i,1}=0.
\]

Hint: use the residue theorem in one direction and translates of the Weierstrass zeta function in the other.
:::

::: {.solution}
A meromorphic function with the prescribed principal parts exists exactly when
\[
\boxed{\sum_i a_{i,1}=0.}
\]

<1>1. The residue condition is necessary.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $f$ is meromorphic on the compact torus
\[
E=\mathbb C/\Lambda
\]
and has the prescribed principal parts. The holomorphic differential $dz$ on $\mathbb C$ is $\Lambda$-invariant, so it descends to $E$. At $[z_i]$ the residue of the meromorphic differential $f(z)\,dz$ is exactly the coefficient of $(z-z_i)^{-1}$ in the Laurent expansion of $f$, namely
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{[z_i]}(f\,dz)=a_{i,1}.
\]
The sum of the residues of a meromorphic differential on a compact Riemann surface is zero. Therefore
\[
\sum_i a_{i,1}=0.
\]
:::

<1>2. Derivatives of $\zeta$ provide elliptic functions with a single prescribed higher-order principal term.
::: {.proof}
Near $0$, the Weierstrass zeta function has expansion
\[
\zeta(w)=\frac1w+h(w),
\]
where $h$ is holomorphic. Hence for every $j\ge2$,
\[
\zeta^{(j-1)}(w)
=(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!\,w^{-j}+\text{holomorphic terms}.
\]
Define
\[
\phi_{i,j}(z)
=\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}
\zeta^{(j-1)}(z-z_i)
\qquad(j\ge2).
\]
Then $\phi_{i,j}$ has principal part
\[
(z-z_i)^{-j}
\]
at $z_i$.

Moreover, for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$ the zeta quasi-periodicity has the form
\[
\zeta(z+\lambda)=\zeta(z)+\eta(\lambda).
\]
Differentiating once kills the additive constant, so every derivative $\zeta^{(k)}$ with $k\ge1$ is $\Lambda$-periodic. Thus each $\phi_{i,j}$ is elliptic and descends to a meromorphic function on $E$.
:::

<1>3. If $\sum_i a_{i,1}=0$, the simple-pole part is also elliptic.
::: {.proof}
Set
\[
F_1(z)=\sum_i a_{i,1}\zeta(z-z_i).
\]
For $\lambda\in\Lambda$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_1(z+\lambda)-F_1(z)
&=\sum_i a_{i,1}
\bigl(\zeta(z-z_i+\lambda)-\zeta(z-z_i)\bigr)\\
&=\eta(\lambda)\sum_i a_{i,1}\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore $F_1$ is $\Lambda$-periodic. Since each translate $\zeta(z-z_i)$ has principal part $(z-z_i)^{-1}$ at $z_i$, the principal part of $F_1$ at $z_i$ is
\[
a_{i,1}(z-z_i)^{-1}.
\]
:::

<1>4. Summing the simple- and higher-order pieces realizes all prescribed principal parts.
::: {.proof}
Assume
\[
\sum_i a_{i,1}=0
\]
and define
\[
f(z)
=\sum_i a_{i,1}\zeta(z-z_i)
 +\sum_i\sum_{j=2}^{N_i}
 a_{i,j}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}
 \zeta^{(j-1)}(z-z_i).
\]
By <1>2 and <1>3, $f$ is $\Lambda$-periodic, so it descends to a meromorphic function on $E$.

Fix $i$. Because the points $[z_i]$ are distinct in $E$, every summand centered at $z_k$ with $k\ne i$ is holomorphic near $z_i$. The terms centered at $z_i$ have, by <1>2 and <1>3, principal parts
\[
a_{i,1}(z-z_i)^{-1}
\quad\text{and}\quad
 a_{i,j}(z-z_i)^{-j}
\quad(2\le j\le N_i).
\]
Hence the complete principal part of $f$ at $z_i$ is exactly
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{N_i}a_{i,j}(z-z_i)^{-j}=F_i(z).
\]
This proves sufficiency and completes the equivalence.
