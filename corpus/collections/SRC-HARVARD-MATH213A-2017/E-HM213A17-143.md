::: {.problem}
Let $g_2(\tau),g_3(\tau)$ be the Weierstrass invariants and let $\lambda(\tau)$ be the cross-ratio of the three roots of
\[
4x^3-g_2(\tau)x-g_3(\tau)=0
\]
together with $\infty$, in the source's ordering.
Using
\[
J(\tau)=\frac4{27}\frac{(\lambda^2-\lambda+1)^3}{\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2},
\]
prove that
\[
J(\tau)=\frac{g_2(\tau)^3}{g_2(\tau)^3-27g_3(\tau)^2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be the three roots of
\[
4x^3-g_2x-g_3=0
\]
and use the source's ordering
\[
\lambda=\frac{e_3-e_2}{e_1-e_2}.
\]
We suppress the argument $\tau$ until the end.

<1>1. Express the roots in terms of $\lambda$ and $d=e_1-e_2$.
::: {.proof}
The coefficient of $x^2$ in the cubic vanishes, so
\[
e_1+e_2+e_3=0.
\]
Set
\[
d=e_1-e_2\ne0.
\]
By definition of the cross-ratio,
\[
e_3-e_2=\lambda d.
\]
Thus
\[
e_1=e_2+d,
\qquad
e_3=e_2+\lambda d.
\]
Substituting into the zero-sum relation gives
\[
3e_2+(1+\lambda)d=0.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
 e_1=\frac{2-\lambda}{3}d,
\qquad
 e_2=-\frac{1+\lambda}{3}d,
\qquad
 e_3=\frac{2\lambda-1}{3}d.
}
\]
:::

<1>2. The invariant $g_2$ is
\[
 g_2=\frac43d^2(\lambda^2-\lambda+1).
\]
::: {.proof}
Factor the cubic as
\[
4(x-e_1)(x-e_2)(x-e_3)
=4x^3-g_2x-g_3.
\]
Comparing the coefficient of $x$ gives
\[
4(e_1e_2+e_1e_3+e_2e_3)=-g_2.
\]
Since $e_1+e_2+e_3=0$,
\[
e_1e_2+e_1e_3+e_2e_3
=-\frac12(e_1^2+e_2^2+e_3^2).
\]
Using the formulas from <1>1,
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_1^2+e_2^2+e_3^2
&=\frac{d^2}{9}
\left((2-\lambda)^2+(1+\lambda)^2+(2\lambda-1)^2\right)\\
&=\frac23d^2(\lambda^2-\lambda+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
g_2
=2(e_1^2+e_2^2+e_3^2)
=\frac43d^2(\lambda^2-\lambda+1).
\]
:::

<1>3. The discriminant satisfies
\[
 g_2^3-27g_3^2
=16d^6\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Divide the cubic by $4$:
\[
x^3-\frac{g_2}{4}x-\frac{g_3}{4}
=(x-e_1)(x-e_2)(x-e_3).
\]
For a monic depressed cubic $x^3+px+q$, its discriminant is
\[
-4p^3-27q^2
=(e_1-e_2)^2(e_1-e_3)^2(e_2-e_3)^2.
\]
Here this gives
\[
\frac{g_2^3-27g_3^2}{16}
=(e_1-e_2)^2(e_1-e_3)^2(e_2-e_3)^2.
\]
Now
\[
e_1-e_2=d,
\qquad
e_1-e_3=(1-\lambda)d,
\qquad
e_2-e_3=-\lambda d.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{g_2^3-27g_3^2}{16}
=d^6\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2,
\]
which is the claimed identity.
:::

<1>4. Substitution into the cross-ratio formula yields the desired expression for $J$.
::: {.proof}
Cubing the formula from <1>2 gives
\[
g_2^3
=\frac{64}{27}d^6(\lambda^2-\lambda+1)^3.
\]
Together with <1>3,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{g_2^3}{g_2^3-27g_3^2}
&=\frac{64d^6(\lambda^2-\lambda+1)^3/27}
{16d^6\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2}\\
&=\frac4{27}
\frac{(\lambda^2-\lambda+1)^3}
{\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2}\\
&=J.
\end{aligned}
\]
Restoring the dependence on $\tau$ gives
\[
\boxed{
J(\tau)
=\frac{g_2(\tau)^3}
{g_2(\tau)^3-27g_3(\tau)^2}.
}
\]
:::
