::: {.problem}
A circular triangle is a region $T\subset\widehat{\mathbb C}$ bounded by three circular arcs or lines.
Let $T_1,T_2\subset\widehat{\mathbb C}$ be two circular triangles with the same interior angles in the same cyclic order.
Prove that there exists a Möbius transformation sending $T_1$ to $T_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Label the vertices of $T_i$ as $a_i,b_i,c_i$ in the given cyclic order, and write the corresponding interior angles as
\[
\pi\alpha,\qquad \pi\beta,\qquad \pi\gamma.
\]
For each $i$ there is a conformal map
\[
f_i:\mathbb H\longrightarrow T_i
\]
whose boundary extension sends $0,1,\infty$ to $a_i,b_i,c_i$, respectively.
::: {.proof}
The boundary of a circular triangle is a Jordan curve made from three circular arcs, so the Riemann mapping theorem and the Carathéodory boundary theorem give a conformal map from $\mathbb H$ which extends homeomorphically to the boundary. The three preimages of the vertices occur on $\widehat{\mathbb R}$ in the same cyclic order. Precomposing by an element of $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ sends them to $0,1,\infty$ in that order.
:::

<1>2. The Schwarzian derivatives of the two maps in <1>1 are equal:
\[
S f_1=S f_2=Q(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),
\]
where
\[
Q(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
=\frac12\left[
\frac{1-\alpha^2}{z^2}
+\frac{1-\beta^2}{(z-1)^2}
+\frac{1-\gamma^2-(2-\alpha^2-\beta^2)}{z(z-1)}
\right]dz^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the Schwarzian characterization of circular triangles. Here is the argument. Schwarz reflection across each circular side continues $f_i$ across the corresponding interval of $\widehat{\mathbb R}$. Since reflection in a generalized circle is anti-Möbius and the Schwarzian is unchanged by postcomposition with a Möbius transformation, $S f_i$ continues to a meromorphic quadratic differential on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$ with possible poles only at $0,1,\infty$.

At a prevertex whose image has interior angle $\pi\delta$, the local reflection monodromy gives the same double-pole term as the model $z\mapsto z^\delta$; hence the coefficient of $dz^2/z^2$ is
\[
\frac{1-\delta^2}{2}.
\]
The same statement includes the parabolic limiting case $\delta=0$. Thus the double-pole coefficients at $0,1,\infty$ are respectively $(1-\alpha^2)/2$, $(1-\beta^2)/2$, and $(1-\gamma^2)/2$.

There is a unique quadratic differential on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$ having only those three double poles and those three leading coefficients. Indeed, after subtracting the displayed $Q(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ the difference has at most simple poles at $0,1,\infty$. Write it on $\mathbb C$ as $R(z)\,dz^2$. Then
\[
R(z)=\frac{A}{z}+\frac{B}{z-1}+P(z)
\]
for a polynomial $P$. Having at most a simple pole at $\infty$ as a quadratic differential means $R(z)=O(z^{-3})$. Hence $P=0$, while the coefficients of $z^{-1}$ and $z^{-2}$ in the expansion at infinity force $A+B=0$ and $B=0$. Thus $A=B=0$. The difference is therefore zero, and $S f_i=Q(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ for both $i$.
:::

<1>3. If two locally univalent holomorphic functions have the same Schwarzian derivative, then one is obtained from the other by postcomposition with a Möbius transformation.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
g=f_2\circ f_1^{-1}:T_1\longrightarrow T_2.
\]
The Schwarzian chain rule gives
\[
S(f_2)
=S(g\circ f_1)
=(Sg\circ f_1)(f_1')^2+S(f_1).
\]
By <1>2 the two outer Schwarzians are equal, and $f_1'\neq0$, so $Sg=0$.

On any coordinate disk put $u=g''/g'$. The equation $Sg=0$ is
\[
u'-\frac12u^2=0.
\]
If $u=0$, then $g$ is affine. Otherwise
\[
u(z)=-\frac{2}{z-c}
\]
for a constant $c$, so integration gives
\[
g(z)=A+\frac{B}{z-c},
\]
again a Möbius transformation. Thus $g$ agrees locally with a Möbius transformation; by analytic continuation on the connected region $T_1$, the same Möbius transformation works everywhere.
:::

<1>4. Consequently there is a Möbius transformation $M$ with
\[
M(T_1)=T_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, $f_2=M\circ f_1$ for some Möbius transformation $M$. Since each $f_i$ maps $\mathbb H$ onto $T_i$,
\[
M(T_1)=M(f_1(\mathbb H))=f_2(\mathbb H)=T_2.
\]
:::
