::: {.problem}
For a simply connected pointed region $(U,p)$, write its conformal radius as $|F'(0)|$, where
\[
F:(\Delta,0)\longrightarrow(U,p)
\]
is a Riemann map.

Find the conformal radii of:

(a) $(\mathbb H,i)$;

(b) $(\{z:-1<\operatorname{Re}z<1\},0)$;

(c) $(\{z\in\mathbb H:-\pi<\operatorname{Re}z<\pi\},i)$;

(d) $(\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus[-2,2],\infty)$; and

(e) $(S_\alpha,r)$ for $r>0$, where
\[
S_\alpha=\{z:\arg z\in(-\alpha,\alpha)\}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The conformal radii are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{pointed region}&\text{conformal radius}\\ \hline
(\mathbb H,i)&2\\[1mm]
(\{-1<\operatorname{Re}z<1\},0)&4/\pi\\[1mm]
(\{z\in\mathbb H:-\pi<\operatorname{Re}z<\pi\},i)
&4\tanh(1/2)\\[1mm]
(\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus[-2,2],\infty)&1\\[1mm]
(S_\alpha,r)&4\alpha r/\pi.
\end{array}}
\]
For the pointed region at infinity, the derivative is measured in the standard
local coordinate $w=1/z$ at $\infty$.

<1>1. If $\phi:(U,p)\to(V,q)$ is conformal, then
\[
r_U(p)=\frac{r_V(q)}{|\phi'(p)|},
\]
with derivatives interpreted in local coordinates.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
F:(\Delta,0)\to(U,p)
\]
be a Riemann map. Then $\phi\circ F$ is a Riemann map from $(\Delta,0)$
to $(V,q)$, so
\[
r_V(q)=|(\phi\circ F)'(0)|
=|\phi'(p)|\,|F'(0)|
=|\phi'(p)|r_U(p).
\]
Rearranging gives the formula.
:::

<1>2. For $(\mathbb H,i)$,
\[
r_{\mathbb H}(i)=2.
\]
::: {.proof}
The Cayley map
\[
F(z)=i\frac{1+z}{1-z}
\]
maps $\Delta$ conformally onto $\mathbb H$ and satisfies $F(0)=i$. Its
derivative is
\[
F'(z)=\frac{2i}{(1-z)^2},
\]
so
\[
r_{\mathbb H}(i)=|F'(0)|=2.
\]
More generally, affine automorphisms of $\mathbb H$ give
\[
r_{\mathbb H}(x+iy)=2y.
\]
:::

<1>3. For the vertical strip
\[
V=\{z:-1<\operatorname{Re}z<1\},
\]
one has
\[
r_V(0)=\frac4\pi.
\]
::: {.proof}
The function
\[
F(z)=\frac{4i}{\pi}\operatorname{artanh}z
=\frac{2i}{\pi}\log\frac{1+z}{1-z}
\]
maps $\Delta$ conformally onto $V$: the function $\operatorname{artanh}z$
maps the disk onto the horizontal strip $|\operatorname{Im}w|<\pi/4$, and
multiplication by $4i/\pi$ rotates and rescales it to $|\operatorname{Re}z|<1$.
Also $F(0)=0$ and
\[
F'(0)=\frac{4i}{\pi}.
\]
Hence
\[
r_V(0)=\frac4\pi.
\]
:::

<1>4. For
\[
W=\{z\in\mathbb H:-\pi<\operatorname{Re}z<\pi\},
\]
one has
\[
r_W(i)=4\tanh(1/2).
\]
::: {.proof}
The map
\[
\phi(z)=\sin(z/2)
\]
maps $W$ conformally onto $\mathbb H$. Indeed, after the affine change
$w=(z+\pi)/2$, the domain becomes the half-strip
\[
0<\operatorname{Re}w<\pi,
\qquad \operatorname{Im}w>0,
\]
which McMullen maps to a half-plane by the cosine; the identity
\[
-\cos((z+\pi)/2)=\sin(z/2)
\]
gives the stated map to $\mathbb H$.

At $p=i$,
\[
\phi(i)=i\sinh(1/2),
\qquad
|\phi'(i)|=\frac12\cosh(1/2).
\]
By <1>2,
\[
r_{\mathbb H}(\phi(i))=2\sinh(1/2).
\]
Therefore <1>1 gives
\[
r_W(i)
=\frac{2\sinh(1/2)}{\frac12\cosh(1/2)}
=4\tanh(1/2).
\]
:::

<1>5. For
\[
X=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus[-2,2]
\]
with basepoint $\infty$,
\[
r_X(\infty)=1
\]
in the local coordinate $w=1/z$ at infinity.
::: {.proof}
The Joukowski map
\[
F(\zeta)=\zeta+\frac1\zeta
\]
is conformal from the punctured disk $0<|\zeta|<1$ onto
$\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus[-2,2]$, and it extends by $F(0)=\infty$.
Injectivity follows because
\[
F(\zeta_1)=F(\zeta_2)
\]
implies either $\zeta_1=\zeta_2$ or $\zeta_1\zeta_2=1$, and the latter is
impossible when both moduli are less than $1$.

Using the target coordinate $w=1/z$ near infinity,
\[
(w\circ F)(\zeta)
=\frac1{\zeta+\zeta^{-1}}
=\frac{\zeta}{1+\zeta^2}.
\]
Thus
\[
(w\circ F)'(0)=1,
\]
so the conformal radius is $1$ in this standard coordinate.
:::

<1>6. For the sector
\[
S_\alpha=\{z:\arg z\in(-\alpha,\alpha)\}
\]
at the positive real point $r>0$,
\[
r_{S_\alpha}(r)=\frac{4\alpha r}{\pi}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Set
\[
\kappa=\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}.
\]
On the sector choose the branch of $z^\kappa$ with argument in
$(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. Then
\[
\phi(z)=iz^\kappa
\]
maps $S_\alpha$ conformally onto $\mathbb H$. At the positive real point
$r$,
\[
\phi(r)=ir^\kappa,
\qquad
|\phi'(r)|=\kappa r^{\kappa-1}.
\]
Using <1>2 and then <1>1,
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_{S_\alpha}(r)
&=\frac{2r^\kappa}{\kappa r^{\kappa-1}}\\
&=\frac{2r}{\kappa}
=\frac{4\alpha r}{\pi}.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::
:::
