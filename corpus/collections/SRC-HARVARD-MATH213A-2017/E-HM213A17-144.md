::: {.problem}
For $\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$, let
\[
S_\lambda=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{0,1,\infty,\lambda\}.
\]

(a) Prove that every holomorphic automorphism of $S_\lambda$ is the restriction of a Möbius transformation.

(b) Prove that $\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)$ always contains a subgroup isomorphic to
\[
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\]
and determine how this subgroup permutes $\{0,1,\infty,\lambda\}$.

(c) Find all $\lambda$ for which
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)|>4,
\]
and identify the automorphism group in each case.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
Q_\lambda=\{0,1,\infty,\lambda\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)
\cong
\begin{cases}
V_4,&\lambda\notin\{-1,\tfrac12,2,\tfrac{1+i\sqrt3}{2},\tfrac{1-i\sqrt3}{2}\},\\[1mm]
D_8,&\lambda\in\{-1,\tfrac12,2\},\\[1mm]
A_4,&\lambda\in\{\tfrac{1+i\sqrt3}{2},\tfrac{1-i\sqrt3}{2}\},
\end{cases}
}
\]
where $V_4\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2$ and $D_8$ denotes the dihedral group of order $8$.

<1>1. Every automorphism of $S_\lambda$ extends to a Möbius transformation preserving $Q_\lambda$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
f:S_\lambda\to S_\lambda
\]
be biholomorphic. At each point of $Q_\lambda$, the map $f$ has an isolated singularity when regarded as a meromorphic map into $\widehat{\mathbb C}$. It cannot be essential: by the Great Picard theorem, a meromorphic function with an essential singularity assumes every value of the sphere, with at most two exceptions, infinitely often near the singularity, whereas $f$ omits all four values in $Q_\lambda$. Thus every puncture is removable or a pole, and $f$ extends to a meromorphic map
\[
F:\widehat{\mathbb C}\to\widehat{\mathbb C}.
\]
The same argument extends $f^{-1}$ to a meromorphic map $G$ of the sphere. On the nonempty open set $S_\lambda$,
\[
G\circ F=\operatorname{id},
\qquad
F\circ G=\operatorname{id}.
\]
By the identity theorem these equalities hold on the whole sphere. Hence $F$ has degree $1$ and is a Möbius transformation. Since $F$ restricts to a bijection of the complements, it permutes $Q_\lambda$.

Conversely, every Möbius transformation preserving $Q_\lambda$ restricts to an automorphism of $S_\lambda$. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)
=\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb C)}(Q_\lambda).
\]
:::

<1>2. Three explicit involutions give a Klein four subgroup for every $\lambda$.
::: {.proof}
Consider
\[
A(z)=\frac{\lambda}{z},
\qquad
B(z)=\frac{\lambda(1-z)}{\lambda-z},
\qquad
C(z)=\frac{z-\lambda}{z-1}.
\]
Their actions on $Q_\lambda$ are respectively
\[
A:(0\ \infty)(1\ \lambda),
\]
\[
B:(0\ 1)(\infty\ \lambda),
\]
and
\[
C:(0\ \lambda)(1\ \infty).
\]
Each is an involution. These are the three double transpositions, and the product of any two is the third. Hence
\[
K=\{1,A,B,C\}\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
This also determines the permutation action requested in part (b).
:::

<1>3. The quotient by $K$ is the stabilizer of the cross-ratio parameter under the sixfold $S_3$ action.
::: {.proof}
A Möbius transformation is determined by the images of three points. Therefore the action of
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)
\]
on the four-point set $Q_\lambda$ is faithful, so we may regard it as a subgroup of $S_4$.

For an ordered quadruple, reordering changes its cross-ratio among the six values
\[
\lambda,
\quad\frac1\lambda,
\quad1-\lambda,
\quad\frac1{1-\lambda},
\quad\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1},
\quad\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}.
\]
The kernel of this $S_4$ action on cross-ratios is exactly the Klein four group of double transpositions from <1>2. Thus the action factors through
\[
S_4/K\cong S_3.
\]

A permutation of $Q_\lambda$ is induced by a Möbius self-map of that same four-point set exactly when the reordered quadruple has the same cross-ratio. Consequently there is an exact sequence
\[
1\longrightarrow K
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Stab}_{S_3}(\lambda)
\longrightarrow1.
\]
In particular,
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)|
=4\,|\operatorname{Stab}_{S_3}(\lambda)|.
\]
:::

<1>4. The only parameters with nontrivial $S_3$ stabilizer are the harmonic and equianharmonic values.
::: {.proof}
McMullen's cross-ratio analysis gives exactly five points of
\[
\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
\]
with nontrivial stabilizer under the six cross-ratio transformations.

The three harmonic values
\[
\lambda\in\left\{-1,\frac12,2\right\}
\]
have stabilizer of order $2$. Hence
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)|=8.
\]
These three puncture configurations are Möbius-equivalent. For $\lambda=-1$, the transformation
\[
R(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
\]
acts on
\[
\{0,1,\infty,-1\}
\]
as the $4$-cycle
\[
(0\ {-1}\ \infty\ 1).
\]
Thus the order-$8$ subgroup of $S_4$ contains a $4$-cycle and the normal Klein four subgroup; it is the dihedral group
\[
D_8.
\]

The two equianharmonic values are the roots of
\[
\lambda^2-\lambda+1=0,
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{1\pm i\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
Their $S_3$ stabilizer has order $3$, so
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)|=12.
\]
The exact sequence in <1>3 is then
\[
1\to V_4\to\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)\to C_3\to1.
\]
As a faithful order-$12$ subgroup of $S_4$ containing the normal Klein four group and a $3$-cycle, this is
\[
A_4.
\]

For every other $\lambda$, the $S_3$ stabilizer is trivial, so
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(S_\lambda)=K\cong V_4.
\]
This proves all three parts.
