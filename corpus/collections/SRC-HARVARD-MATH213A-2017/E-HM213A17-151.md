::: {.problem}
Prove that
\[
60\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}
=
64\pi^2\frac{\Gamma(5/4)^4}{\Gamma(3/4)^4}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z[i],
\qquad
G_2(\Lambda)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4},
\qquad
g_2=60G_2(\Lambda).
\]
Write
\[
E=\wp_\Lambda(1/2)>0.
\]

<1>1. For the square lattice,
\[
g_3=0,
\qquad
(e_1,e_2,e_3)=(E,-E,0),
\qquad
g_2=4E^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Multiplication by $i$ preserves $\Lambda$.  Hence
\[
G_3(\Lambda)
=\sum_{\lambda\ne0}\lambda^{-6}
=\sum_{\lambda\ne0}(i\lambda)^{-6}
=-G_3(\Lambda),
\]
so $G_3(\Lambda)=0$ and therefore $g_3=140G_3(\Lambda)=0$.

The square symmetry also gives
\[
e_2=-e_1,
\qquad
e_3=0.
\]
With $e_1=E>0$, the Weierstrass equation is therefore
\[
\wp_\Lambda'(z)^2
=4(\wp_\Lambda(z)-E)\wp_\Lambda(z)(\wp_\Lambda(z)+E)
=4\wp_\Lambda(z)^3-4E^2\wp_\Lambda(z).
\]
Comparing this with
\[
\wp_\Lambda'(z)^2=4\wp_\Lambda(z)^3-g_2\wp_\Lambda(z)-g_3
\]
gives $g_2=4E^2$.
:::

<1>2. If
\[
I=\int_1^\infty\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t^3-t}},
\]
then
\[
E=I^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
g_2=4I^4.
\]
::: {.proof}
The real half-period formula for the square lattice gives
\[
\frac12
=\int_E^\infty\frac{dx}{\sqrt{4x^3-g_2x}}.
\]
By <1>1,
\[
4x^3-g_2x=4x(x^2-E^2),
\]
so
\[
\frac12
=\frac12\int_E^\infty\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x^2-E^2)}}.
\]
Substituting $x=Et$ yields
\[
\frac12
=\frac1{2\sqrt E}
\int_1^\infty\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t(t^2-1)}}
=\frac I{2\sqrt E}.
\]
Thus $E=I^2$, and <1>1 gives $g_2=4I^4$.
:::

<1>3. We have
\[
\boxed{
60\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}
=64\pi^2\frac{\Gamma(5/4)^4}{\Gamma(3/4)^4}.
}
\]
::: {.proof}
In the integral from <1>2, put $u=t^{-2}$.  Since
\[
dt=-\frac12u^{-3/2}\,du,
\qquad
t^3-t=u^{-3/2}(1-u),
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
I
&=\frac12\int_0^1u^{-3/4}(1-u)^{-1/2}\,du\\
&=\frac12 B\left(\frac14,\frac12\right)\\
&=\frac{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(1/4)}{2\Gamma(3/4)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
60\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}
=g_2
&=4I^4\\
&=\frac{\pi^2\Gamma(1/4)^4}{4\Gamma(3/4)^4}\\
&=64\pi^2\frac{\Gamma(5/4)^4}{\Gamma(3/4)^4},
\end{aligned}
\]
because $\Gamma(5/4)=\frac14\Gamma(1/4)$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 reduces the square-lattice invariant to its positive half-period, step <1>2 expresses that half-period by the beta integral, and step <1>3 evaluates the integral and obtains the required identity.
