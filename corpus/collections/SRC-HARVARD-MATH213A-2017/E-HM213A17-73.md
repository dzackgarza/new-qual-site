::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal O(\CC)$ and $\mathcal M(\CC)$ be the rings of entire and meromorphic functions on $\CC$.

(a) Show that $\mathcal O(\CC)$ is integrally closed in $\mathcal M(\CC)$; that is, every $f\in\mathcal M(\CC)$ satisfying a monic polynomial in $\mathcal O(\CC)[X]$ actually lies in $\mathcal O(\CC)$.

(b) Is $\mathcal M(\CC)$ algebraically closed?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Let $f\in\mathcal M(\CC)$ satisfy a monic relation
\[
f^d+a_{d-1}f^{d-1}+\cdots+a_1f+a_0=0,
\qquad a_j\in\mathcal O(\CC).
\]
Then $f$ has no poles.

::: pf-proof

Suppose instead that $f$ has a pole of order $m>0$ at some point $p$. Write
\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(f)=-m.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(f^d)=-dm.
\]
For every $j<d$, holomorphicity of $a_j$ gives
\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(a_jf^j)
=\operatorname{ord}_p(a_j)+j\operatorname{ord}_p(f)
\ge-jm>-dm.
\]
Thus $f^d$ is the unique term in the monic relation with the lowest order at
$p$. Such a principal part cannot be cancelled by the remaining terms, a
contradiction. Hence no pole exists.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $\mathcal O(\CC)$ is integrally closed in
$\mathcal M(\CC)$.

::: pf-proof

A meromorphic function on $\CC$ with no poles is holomorphic
everywhere, hence entire. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, every meromorphic function integral over
$\mathcal O(\CC)$ has no poles, so it belongs to
$\mathcal O(\CC)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The field $\mathcal M(\CC)$ is not algebraically closed.

::: pf-proof

Consider
\[
P(X)=X^2-z\in\mathcal M(\CC)[X].
\]
If it had a root $h\in\mathcal M(\CC)$, then
\[
h^2=z.
\]
Taking orders at $0$ would give
\[
2\operatorname{ord}_0(h)
=\operatorname{ord}_0(z)
=1.
\]
The left side is an even integer, which is impossible. Hence $P$ has no root
in $\mathcal M(\CC)$, so this field is not algebraically closed.

:::
