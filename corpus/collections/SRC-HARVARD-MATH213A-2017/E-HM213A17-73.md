::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$ and $\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$ be the rings of entire and meromorphic functions on $\mathbb C$.

(a) Show that $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$ is integrally closed in $\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$; that is, every $f\in\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$ satisfying a monic polynomial in $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)[X]$ actually lies in $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$.

(b) Is $\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$ algebraically closed?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $f\in\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$ satisfy a monic relation
\[
f^d+a_{d-1}f^{d-1}+\cdots+a_1f+a_0=0,
\qquad a_j\in\mathcal O(\mathbb C).
\]
Then $f$ has no poles.
::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that $f$ has a pole of order $m>0$ at some point $p$. Write
\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(f)=-m.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(f^d)=-dm.
\]
For every $j<d$, holomorphicity of $a_j$ gives
\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(a_jf^j)
=\operatorname{ord}_p(a_j)+j\operatorname{ord}_p(f)
\ge-jm>-dm.
\]
Thus $f^d$ is the unique term in the monic relation with the lowest order at
$p$. Such a principal part cannot be cancelled by the remaining terms, a
contradiction. Hence no pole exists.
:::

<1>2. Therefore $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$ is integrally closed in
$\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$.
::: {.proof}
A meromorphic function on $\mathbb C$ with no poles is holomorphic
everywhere, hence entire. By <1>1, every meromorphic function integral over
$\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$ has no poles, so it belongs to
$\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$.
:::

<1>3. The field $\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$ is not algebraically closed.
::: {.proof}
Consider
\[
P(X)=X^2-z\in\mathcal M(\mathbb C)[X].
\]
If it had a root $h\in\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$, then
\[
h^2=z.
\]
Taking orders at $0$ would give
\[
2\operatorname{ord}_0(h)
=\operatorname{ord}_0(z)
=1.
\]
The left side is an even integer, which is impossible. Hence $P$ has no root
in $\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$, so this field is not algebraically closed.
:::
:::
