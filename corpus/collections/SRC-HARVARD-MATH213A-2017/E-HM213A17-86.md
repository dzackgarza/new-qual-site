::: {.problem}
(a) Evaluate
\[
\Gamma(1/3)\Gamma(2/3).
\]

(b) Find a formula for $\Gamma(3z)$ in terms of
\[
\Gamma(z),\qquad \Gamma(z+1/3),\qquad \Gamma(z+2/3).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The required formulas are
\[
\boxed{\Gamma(1/3)\Gamma(2/3)=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma(3z)
=\frac{3^{\,3z-1/2}}{2\pi}
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+1/3)\Gamma(z+2/3).}
\]

<1>1. Euler's reflection formula gives
\[
\Gamma(1/3)\Gamma(2/3)=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The source gives
\[
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)}.
\]
Substituting $z=1/3$ yields
\[
\Gamma(1/3)\Gamma(2/3)
=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi/3)}
=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt3/2}
=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}.
\]
:::

<1>2. Define, for $\operatorname{Re}w>0$,
\[
F(w)
=\frac{3^w}{2\pi\sqrt3}
\Gamma(w/3)
\Gamma((w+1)/3)
\Gamma((w+2)/3).
\]
Then $F(w+1)=wF(w)$.
::: {.proof}
Using $\Gamma(u+1)=u\Gamma(u)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(w+1)
&=\frac{3^{w+1}}{2\pi\sqrt3}
\Gamma((w+1)/3)
\Gamma((w+2)/3)
\Gamma(w/3+1)\\
&=\frac{3^{w+1}}{2\pi\sqrt3}
\Gamma((w+1)/3)
\Gamma((w+2)/3)
\frac w3\Gamma(w/3)\\
&=wF(w).
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>3. One has $F(1)=1$, and $F$ is bounded on the strip
$1\le\operatorname{Re}w\le2$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(1)
&=\frac{3}{2\pi\sqrt3}
\Gamma(1/3)\Gamma(2/3)\Gamma(1)\\
&=\frac{3}{2\pi\sqrt3}\cdot\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}
=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

If $1\le\operatorname{Re}w\le2$, then the real parts of
\[
\frac w3,\qquad \frac{w+1}{3},\qquad \frac{w+2}{3}
\]
lie in fixed compact subintervals of $(0,\infty)$. McMullen's bounds for
$\Gamma$ on vertical strips therefore bound each Gamma factor there, and
$|3^w|=3^{\operatorname{Re}w}$ is bounded as well. Hence $F$ is bounded on
the required strip.
:::

<1>4. Wielandt's characterization implies $F(w)=\Gamma(w)$.
::: {.proof}
McMullen's Theorem 3.21 states that a function on the right half-plane
satisfying
\[
F(w+1)=wF(w),\qquad F(1)=1,
\]
and bounded on $1\le\operatorname{Re}w\le2$ must equal $\Gamma(w)$.
The hypotheses were verified in <1>2 and <1>3, so
\[
\Gamma(w)
=\frac{3^w}{2\pi\sqrt3}
\Gamma(w/3)
\Gamma((w+1)/3)
\Gamma((w+2)/3).
\]
:::

<1>5. Replacing $w$ by $3z$ gives the triplication formula.
::: {.proof}
From <1>4,
\[
\Gamma(3z)
=\frac{3^{3z}}{2\pi\sqrt3}
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+1/3)\Gamma(z+2/3).
\]
Since $\sqrt3=3^{1/2}$,
\[
\Gamma(3z)
=\frac{3^{\,3z-1/2}}{2\pi}
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+1/3)\Gamma(z+2/3).
\]
Initially this identity holds where the displayed Gamma factors are
holomorphic; both sides are meromorphic functions, so it extends to all $z$
by analytic continuation.
:::
