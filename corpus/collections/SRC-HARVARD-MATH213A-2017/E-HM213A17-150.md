::: {.problem}
Prove that
\[
\lambda(i/2)=12\sqrt2-16.
\]

Hint: for $X_\tau=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z\oplus\tau\mathbb Z)$, interpret $\lambda(\tau)$ as the cross-ratio of the critical values of a degree-two map $X_\tau\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$.
For the square torus choose the critical values to be the roots of $z^4+1$, and use a degree-two map for $X_{i/2}$ whose critical values are
\[
\{-1,-1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2,1\}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
a=\frac1{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\Lambda=\mathbb Z\oplus i\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\Lambda'=\mathbb Z\oplus \frac i2\mathbb Z,
\]
and let
\[
q:X_i=\mathbb C/\Lambda\longrightarrow X_{i/2}=\mathbb C/\Lambda'
\]
be the natural degree-two quotient.  For
\[
c_1=\frac12,
\qquad
c_2=\frac i4,
\qquad
c_3=\frac12+\frac i4,
\]
write $v_0=f_{i/2}(0)$ and $v_j=f_{i/2}(c_j)$.

<1>1. The square-torus normalization in the hint may be chosen so that
\[
(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_0)=(-a,1,-1,a).
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $\wp_i$ be the Weierstrass function of the square lattice $\Lambda$ and set
\[
E=\wp_i(1/2)>0,
\qquad
u(z)=\frac{\wp_i(z)}E.
\]
For the square lattice, the marked half-period values are
\[
u(0)=\infty,
\qquad
u(1/2)=1,
\qquad
u(i/2)=-1,
\qquad
u((1+i)/2)=0.
\]
Indeed, for $\tau=i$ the square symmetry gives $e_2=-e_1$ and $e_3=0$.

Put
\[
\zeta=e^{\pi i/4}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f_i(z)=\zeta\,\frac{u(z)+i}{u(z)-i}.
\]
At the four ramification points this gives, respectively,
\[
\zeta,
\quad
i\zeta,
\quad
\zeta^{-1},
\quad
-\zeta,
\]
which are exactly the four roots of $w^4+1$.

The half-period addition formula for $\wp_i$, using $(e_1,e_2,e_3)=(E,-E,0)$, gives
\[
u(z+i/2)=\frac{1-u(z)}{1+u(z)},
\qquad
u(z+1/2)=\frac{u(z)+1}{u(z)-1}.
\]
Consequently a direct calculation gives
\[
f_i(z+i/2)=f_i(z)^{-1}.
\]

At $z=i/4$, evenness of $u$ and the first translation formula imply
\[
u(i/4)\in\{-1-\sqrt2,-1+\sqrt2\}.
\]
Along the open segment from $0$ to $i/2$, the value $\wp_i(iy)$ is real and has no critical point; it runs from $-\infty$ to $-E$.  Hence
\[
u(i/4)=-1-\sqrt2.
\]
Substitution into the formula for $f_i$ gives
\[
f_i(i/4)=1.
\]
The second translation formula then gives
\[
u(1/2+i/4)=-1+\sqrt2,
\qquad
f_i(1/2+i/4)=-1.
\]

Finally set
\[
g(w)=\frac12\left(w+w^{-1}\right).
\]
The construction in the hint is $f_{i/2}\circ q=g\circ f_i$.  Since
\[
g(\zeta)=a,
\qquad
g(i\zeta)=-a,
\qquad
g(1)=1,
\qquad
g(-1)=-1,
\]
the marked critical values are
\[
v_0=a,
\qquad
v_1=-a,
\qquad
v_2=1,
\qquad
v_3=-1.
\]
:::

<1>2. After the Möbius change of coordinate
\[
h(w)=\frac1{a-w},
\]
the marked critical values are
\[
h(v_0)=\infty,
\qquad
h(v_1)=a,
\qquad
h(v_2)=-(2+\sqrt2),
\qquad
h(v_3)=2-\sqrt2.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
h(v_0)=h(a)=\infty
\]
and
\[
h(v_1)
=\frac1{2a}
=\frac1{\sqrt2}
=a.
\]
Also
\[
h(v_2)
=\frac1{a-1}
=-(2+\sqrt2),
\]
while
\[
h(v_3)
=\frac1{a+1}
=2-\sqrt2.
\]
Postcomposition by $h$ does not change the marked cross-ratio.
:::

<1>3. The modular lambda value is
\[
\boxed{\lambda(i/2)=12\sqrt2-16}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By the marked cross-ratio formula and <1>2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda(i/2)
&=\frac{h(v_3)-h(v_2)}{h(v_1)-h(v_2)}\\
&=\frac{4}{2+\frac{3\sqrt2}{2}}\\
&=\frac{8}{4+3\sqrt2}\\
&=12\sqrt2-16.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 identify the suitably ordered critical values of the degree-two map, and step <1>3 evaluates their marked cross-ratio.
