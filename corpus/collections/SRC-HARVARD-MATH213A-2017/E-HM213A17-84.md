::: {.problem}
Prove or disprove:
\[
\Gamma'(3/2)=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The assertion is false. In fact
\[
\Gamma'(3/2)
=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\bigl(2-\gamma-2\log 2\bigr)>0,
\]
where $\gamma$ is Euler's constant in the normalization used in the source.

<1>1. If
\[
\psi(z)=\frac{\Gamma'(z)}{\Gamma(z)},
\]
then $\psi(1)=-\gamma$.
::: {.proof}
The source gives the product formula
\[
z\Gamma(z)
=e^{-\gamma z}
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(1+\frac zn\right)^{-1}e^{z/n}.
\]
Logarithmic differentiation yields
\[
\frac1z+\psi(z)
=-\gamma+
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(\frac1n-\frac1{n+z}\right).
\]
At $z=1$ the series telescopes to $1$. Hence
\[
1+\psi(1)=-\gamma+1,
\]
so $\psi(1)=-\gamma$.
:::

<1>2. One has
\[
\psi(3/2)=2-\gamma-2\log2.
\]
::: {.proof}
McMullen's duplication formula is
\[
2\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(2z)
=2^{2z}\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+1/2).
\]
Taking logarithmic derivatives gives
\[
2\psi(2z)
=2\log2+\psi(z)+\psi(z+1/2).
\]
Setting $z=1/2$ and using <1>1 gives
\[
2\psi(1)
=2\log2+\psi(1/2)+\psi(1),
\]
and therefore
\[
\psi(1/2)=-\gamma-2\log2.
\]

On the other hand, logarithmic differentiation of
$\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)$ gives
\[
\psi(z+1)=\psi(z)+\frac1z.
\]
Taking $z=1/2$ now yields
\[
\psi(3/2)
=\psi(1/2)+2
=2-\gamma-2\log2.
\]
:::

<1>3. The number $2-\gamma-2\log2$ is strictly positive.
::: {.proof}
The source defines Euler's constant by
\[
\gamma
=\lim_{N\to\infty}
\left(\sum_{k=1}^N\frac1k-\log(N+1)\right).
\]
Since
\[
\log(N+1)=\sum_{k=1}^N\log\left(1+\frac1k\right),
\]
this is equivalently
\[
\gamma
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\left(\frac1k-\log\left(1+\frac1k\right)\right).
\]
The first three summands are
\[
\sum_{k=1}^3\frac1k-\log4
=\frac{11}{6}-2\log2.
\]

For every $k\ge4$,
\[
\frac1k-\log\left(1+\frac1k\right)
=\int_0^{1/k}\frac{t}{1+t}\,dt
<\int_0^{1/k}t\,dt
=\frac1{2k^2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma-\left(\frac{11}{6}-2\log2\right)
&<\frac12\sum_{k=4}^{\infty}\frac1{k^2}\\
&<\frac12\int_3^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2}
=\frac16.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\gamma+2\log2<2,
\]
which is the desired strict positivity.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $\Gamma'(3/2)\neq0$.
::: {.proof}
The functional equation and $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt\pi$ give
\[
\Gamma(3/2)=\frac12\Gamma(1/2)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}>0.
\]
Combining this with <1>2 and <1>3,
\[
\Gamma'(3/2)
=\Gamma(3/2)\psi(3/2)
=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}
\bigl(2-\gamma-2\log2\bigr)>0.
\]
Hence the proposed equality is false.
:::
:::
