::: {.problem}
Let $f,g:\Delta\to\mathbb C$ be univalent maps satisfying
\[
f(0)=g(0)=0,
\qquad
f'(0)=g'(0)=1.
\]
If
\[
f(\Delta)\supseteq g(\Delta),
\]
prove that $f=g$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Since $g(\Delta)\subseteq f(\Delta)$ and $f$ is univalent, the map
\[
h=f^{-1}\circ g
\]
is a holomorphic self-map of $\Delta$. The normalizations force equality in
the Schwarz lemma.

<1>1. The map
\[
h=f^{-1}\circ g:\Delta\to\Delta
\]
is well-defined and holomorphic.
::: {.proof}
Because $f$ is univalent, it is a biholomorphism from $\Delta$ onto the
domain $f(\Delta)$, so its inverse
\[
f^{-1}:f(\Delta)\to\Delta
\]
is holomorphic. The inclusion
\[
g(\Delta)\subseteq f(\Delta)
\]
therefore makes $h=f^{-1}\circ g$ a holomorphic map from $\Delta$ to
$\Delta$.
:::

<1>2. One has
\[
h(0)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
h'(0)=1.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $f(0)=g(0)=0$,
\[
h(0)=f^{-1}(g(0))=f^{-1}(0)=0.
\]
Differentiating $g=f\circ h$ at $0$ gives
\[
g'(0)=f'(h(0))h'(0)=f'(0)h'(0).
\]
The normalization $f'(0)=g'(0)=1$ therefore yields
\[
h'(0)=1.
\]
:::

<1>3. The Schwarz lemma forces $h$ to be the identity.
::: {.proof}
The Schwarz lemma says that a holomorphic self-map of $\Delta$ fixing $0$
satisfies
\[
|h'(0)|\le1,
\]
with equality only for a rotation
\[
h(z)=e^{i\theta}z.
\]
By <1>2, $h'(0)=1$, so the rotation factor is $e^{i\theta}=1$. Hence
\[
h(z)=z
\]
for all $z\in\Delta$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $f=g$.
::: {.proof}
Since $g=f\circ h$ and $h=\operatorname{id}_\Delta$ by <1>3,
\[
g=f.
\]
:::
:::
