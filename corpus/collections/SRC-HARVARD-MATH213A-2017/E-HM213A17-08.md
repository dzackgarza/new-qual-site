::: {.problem}
What is the distance, in the spherical metric on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$, from $1$ to $1+i$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The spherical distance is
\[
d_{\mathrm{sph}}(1,1+i)
=2\arccos\sqrt{\frac56}.
\]
::: {.proof}
For points of $\widehat{\mathbb C}\cong\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)$ represented by nonzero vectors $v,w\in\mathbb C^2$, the spherical distance $\theta$ satisfies
\[
\cos\frac\theta2
=\frac{|\langle v,w\rangle|}{\|v\|\,\|w\|}.
\]
Represent
\[
1=[1:1],
\qquad
1+i=[1+i:1],
\]
so take
\[
v=(1,1),
\qquad
w=(1+i,1).
\]
Then
\[
|\langle v,w\rangle|
=|1(1-i)+1|
=|2-i|
=\sqrt5,
\]
while
\[
\|v\|=\sqrt2,
\qquad
\|w\|=\sqrt3.
\]
Therefore
\[
\cos\frac\theta2
=\frac{\sqrt5}{\sqrt6}
=\sqrt{\frac56},
\]
and hence
\[
\theta=2\arccos\sqrt{\frac56}.
\]
Numerically, this is approximately $0.841069$ radians.
