::: {.problem}
Let
\[
M(r)=\sup_{|z|=r}|f(z)|,
\]
where $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ is entire and not identically zero.
Suppose that, for some $r>0$ with $r\ne1$,
\[
M(r^2)^2=M(r)M(r^3).
\]
Prove that
\[
f(z)=az^n
\]
for some $a\ne0$ and integer $n\ge0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
R_1=\min(r,r^3),\qquad R_2=r^2,\qquad R_3=\max(r,r^3).
\]
Since $r\neq1$,
\[
R_1<R_2<R_3,
\qquad
\log R_2=\frac{\log R_1+\log R_3}{2}.
\]

<1>1. Let $h(z)=\alpha\log|z|+\beta$ be the harmonic radial function on
$R_1<|z|<R_3$ satisfying
\[
h=\log M(R_1)\quad\text{on }|z|=R_1,
\qquad
h=\log M(R_3)\quad\text{on }|z|=R_3.
\]
Then
\[
\log|f(z)|\le h(z)
\]
throughout the annulus.
::: {.proof}
The function $\log|f|$ is subharmonic. On each boundary circle it is at
most the corresponding value of $\log M$, hence at most $h$. The maximum
principle for subharmonic functions gives the inequality in the annulus.
This is the usual proof of Hadamard's three-circles theorem.
:::

<1>2. The hypothesis implies
\[
h(z)=\log M(R_2)
\]
on $|z|=R_2$.
::: {.proof}
Because $\log R_2$ is the midpoint of $\log R_1$ and $\log R_3$,
\[
h|_{|z|=R_2}
=\frac12\bigl(\log M(R_1)+\log M(R_3)\bigr).
\]
The given equality
\[
M(r^2)^2=M(r)M(r^3)
\]
is exactly
\[
2\log M(R_2)=\log M(R_1)+\log M(R_3),
\]
so the two quantities agree.
:::

<1>3. One has
\[
\log|f(z)|=h(z)
\]
throughout $R_1<|z|<R_3$.
::: {.proof}
Choose $z_0$ on $|z|=R_2$ with
\[
|f(z_0)|=M(R_2).
\]
By <1>2,
\[
\log|f(z_0)|-h(z_0)=0.
\]
By <1>1, the subharmonic function
\[
u(z)=\log|f(z)|-h(z)
\]
is at most zero on the annulus, so it attains its maximum at the interior
point $z_0$. The strong maximum principle for subharmonic functions forces
$u\equiv0$. In particular, $f$ has no zeros in this annulus.
:::

<1>4. On the annulus,
\[
z\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}=\alpha.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $f$ is nowhere zero there, $f'/f$ is holomorphic. From <1>3,
\[
\log|f(z)|=\alpha\log|z|+\beta.
\]
Writing $z=re^{i\theta}$, differentiation in the radial direction gives
\[
\Re\!\left(z\frac{f'}f\right)=\alpha,
\]
while differentiation in $\theta$ gives
\[
\Im\!\left(z\frac{f'}f\right)=0.
\]
Hence the holomorphic function $zf'/f$ is identically the real constant
$\alpha$.
:::

<1>5. The number $\alpha$ is an integer.
::: {.proof}
For any circle $|z|=\rho$ contained in the annulus, <1>4 gives
\[
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=\rho}\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz
=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=\rho}\frac{\alpha}{z}\,dz
=\alpha.
\]
The left side is the winding number of $f(|z|=\rho)$ about $0$, hence is an
integer. Thus $\alpha=n\in\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>6. There is a constant $a\neq0$ such that
\[
f(z)=az^n
\]
on the annulus.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4 and <1>5,
\[
\frac{f'}f=\frac nz.
\]
Thus
\[
\left(\frac{f(z)}{z^n}\right)'=0
\]
on the connected annulus, so $f(z)/z^n=a$ there for some nonzero constant
$a$.
:::

<1>7. In fact $n\ge0$ and the same identity holds on all of $\mathbb C$.
::: {.proof}
The Laurent expansion of the entire function $f$ about $0$ has no negative
powers. Since it agrees on an annulus with the single Laurent monomial
$az^n$, one must have $n\ge0$. Then both $f$ and $az^n$ are entire, and they
agree on a nonempty open annulus. The identity theorem gives
\[
f(z)=az^n
\]
for all $z\in\mathbb C$.
:::
