::: {.problem}
Compute the first four nonzero terms in the power series for $\tan z$ at $z=0$ by formally inverting the power series for
\[
\tan^{-1}z=\int\frac{dz}{1+z^2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The first four nonzero terms are
\[
\boxed{
\tan z
=z+\frac{z^3}{3}+\frac{2z^5}{15}+\frac{17z^7}{315}+O(z^9).}
\]

<1>1. The power series for the inverse tangent is
\[
\tan^{-1}u
=u-\frac{u^3}{3}+\frac{u^5}{5}-\frac{u^7}{7}+O(u^9).
\]
::: {.proof}
For $|u|<1$,
\[
\frac1{1+u^2}=1-u^2+u^4-u^6+O(u^8).
\]
Integrating term-by-term from $0$ to $u$ gives the displayed series.
:::

<1>2. Write the inverse series in the form
\[
u=\tan z=z+az^3+bz^5+cz^7+O(z^9).
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
u^3&=z^3+3az^5+(3b+3a^2)z^7+O(z^9),\\
u^5&=z^5+5az^7+O(z^9),\\
u^7&=z^7+O(z^9).
\end{aligned}
\]
::: {.proof}
These follow by formal multiplication, retaining only terms of degree at most
$7$.
:::

<1>3. Comparing the coefficient of $z^3$ in
\[
z=u-\frac{u^3}{3}+\frac{u^5}{5}-\frac{u^7}{7}+O(u^9)
\]
gives $a=1/3$.
::: {.proof}
The $z^3$ coefficient on the right is $a-1/3$, so it must vanish.
:::

<1>4. Comparing the coefficient of $z^5$ gives
\[
b=a-\frac15=\frac{2}{15}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $z^5$ coefficient is
\[
b-a+\frac15,
\]
which must be zero. Substitute $a=1/3$.
:::

<1>5. Comparing the coefficient of $z^7$ gives
\[
c=b+a^2-a+\frac17=\frac{17}{315}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $z^7$ coefficient on the right is
\[
c-(b+a^2)+a-\frac17.
\]
Setting it equal to zero and substituting
\[
a=\frac13,
\qquad
b=\frac{2}{15}
\]
gives
\[
c
=\frac{2}{15}+\frac19-\frac13+\frac17
=\frac{17}{315}.
\]
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
\tan z
=z+\frac{z^3}{3}+\frac{2z^5}{15}+\frac{17z^7}{315}+O(z^9).
\]
::: {.proof}
Insert the coefficients from <1>3--<1>5 into the ansatz of <1>2.
:::
:::
