::: {.problem}
Let
\[
p(z)=a_0z^3+a_1z^2+a_2z+a_3
\]
be a cubic polynomial.
Assume $a_0\ne0$ and $p'(z)$ has distinct zeros.

(a) Show that there are $A,B\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)$ such that
\[
A(p(B(z)))=z^3-3z.
\]

(b) Show how to solve
\[
z^3-3z=w
\]
by radicals, using the fact that if $z=a+a^{-1}$, then
\[
z^3-3z=a^3+a^{-3}.
\]

(c) Combining the previous calculations, solve
\[
z^3+az+b=0
\]
by radicals.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $r_+$ and $r_-$ be the two distinct zeros of $p'$. There is an
affine automorphism $B$ of $\mathbb C$ with
\[
B(1)=r_+,
\qquad
B(-1)=r_-.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $r_+\neq r_-$, the map
\[
B(z)=\frac{r_+-r_-}{2}z+\frac{r_++r_-}{2}
\]
has nonzero linear coefficient and therefore lies in
$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)$.
:::

<1>2. If $q=p\circ B$, then there are constants $\lambda\neq0$ and $\mu$
such that
\[
q(z)=\frac{\lambda}{3}(z^3-3z)+\mu.
\]
::: {.proof}
By the chain rule,
\[
q'(z)=p'(B(z))B'(z).
\]
Thus the zeros of $q'$ are exactly $1$ and $-1$. Since $q'$ is a quadratic
polynomial with distinct zeros $\pm1$, it has the form
\[
q'(z)=\lambda(z^2-1)
\]
for some $\lambda\neq0$. Integrating gives
\[
q(z)=\lambda\left(\frac{z^3}{3}-z\right)+\mu
=\frac{\lambda}{3}(z^3-3z)+\mu.
\]
:::

<1>3. Part (a) follows by taking
\[
A(w)=\frac{3}{\lambda}(w-\mu).
\]
::: {.proof}
The map $A$ is affine with nonzero linear coefficient, hence belongs to
$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)$. By <1>2,
\[
A(p(B(z)))
=\frac{3}{\lambda}\left(q(z)-\mu\right)
=z^3-3z.
\]
:::

<1>4. To solve
\[
z^3-3z=w,
\]
set $z=u+u^{-1}$ with $u\neq0$. Then $U=u^3$ must satisfy
\[
U^2-wU+1=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The identity supplied in the problem gives
\[
(u+u^{-1})^3-3(u+u^{-1})=u^3+u^{-3}.
\]
Hence the equation becomes
\[
u^3+u^{-3}=w.
\]
Writing $U=u^3$ and multiplying by $U$ gives
\[
U^2-wU+1=0.
\]
:::

<1>5. Therefore the solutions of $z^3-3z=w$ are obtained by radicals as
follows: choose
\[
U=\frac{w+\sqrt{w^2-4}}{2},
\]
choose any cube root $u$ of $U$, and set
\[
\boxed{z=u+u^{-1}}.
\]
The three choices of the cube root give the three roots, counted with
multiplicity.
::: {.proof}
The two roots of $U^2-wU+1=0$ are
\[
U_\pm=\frac{w\pm\sqrt{w^2-4}}{2},
\]
and satisfy $U_+U_-=1$, so neither is zero. If $u^3=U_+$, then
$(u^{-1})^3=U_-$. Thus <1>4 gives
\[
(u+u^{-1})^3-3(u+u^{-1})=U_++U_-=w.
\]
Replacing $u$ by $\omega u$, where $\omega^3=1$, gives all three roots of
the cubic, with repetitions precisely when the cubic has a multiple root.
Choosing $U_-$ instead merely replaces $u$ by a reciprocal and produces the
same set of $z$-values.
:::

<1>6. For the depressed cubic
\[
z^3+az+b=0,
\]
put
\[
\Delta=\frac{b^2}{4}+\frac{a^3}{27}.
\]
If $a\neq0$, choose
\[
U^3=-\frac b2+\sqrt\Delta,
\qquad
V=-\frac{a}{3U}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{z=U+V}
\]
is a root.
::: {.proof}
Because
\[
\left(-\frac b2+\sqrt\Delta\right)
\left(-\frac b2-\sqrt\Delta\right)
=-\frac{a^3}{27},
\]
the assumption $a\neq0$ implies $U\neq0$, and
\[
V^3
=-\frac{a^3}{27U^3}
=-\frac b2-\sqrt\Delta.
\]
Hence
\[
UV=-\frac a3,
\qquad
U^3+V^3=-b.
\]
Therefore
\[
(U+V)^3+a(U+V)+b
=U^3+V^3+(3UV+a)(U+V)+b
=0.
\]
This is Cardano's radical formula, with the cube roots paired by the condition
$UV=-a/3$.
:::

<1>7. All three roots of $z^3+az+b=0$ are
\[
z_k=\omega^kU+\omega^{-k}V,
\qquad k=0,1,2,
\]
where $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$. If $a=0$, the equation reduces directly to
$z^3=-b$.
::: {.proof}
For each $k$,
\[
(\omega^kU)(\omega^{-k}V)=UV=-\frac a3
\]
and
\[
(\omega^kU)^3+(\omega^{-k}V)^3=U^3+V^3=-b.
\]
The calculation in <1>6 therefore shows that every $z_k$ is a root. A cubic
has three roots counted with multiplicity, so these give all of them. When
$a=0$, no pairing is needed: the roots are simply the three cube roots of
$-b$.
:::
