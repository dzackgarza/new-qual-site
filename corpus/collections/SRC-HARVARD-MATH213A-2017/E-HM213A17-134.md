::: {.problem}
Let $X=\mathbb C/\Lambda$ be a complex torus and define
\[
F:X\to X,
\qquad
[z]\mapsto[2z].
\]

(a) Show that $F$ has a dense forward orbit: there exists $p\in X$ such that
\[
\overline{\{F^n(p):n>0\}}=X.
\]

(b) Let $R$ be the rational function in the Weierstrass double-angle formula
\[
\wp(2z)=R(\wp(z)).
\]
Prove that $R$ has a dense forward orbit on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The source's parenthetical description of a dense orbit writes $\{F^n(p):n>0\}=X$ without closure.
Literal equality is impossible because the forward orbit is countable while the torus is uncountable; density is the stated and intended property.
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose a real basis $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ of the lattice. Then
\[
\Phi:\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow X,
\qquad
(s,t)\longmapsto[s\lambda_1+t\lambda_2]
\]
is a homeomorphism, and under this identification $F$ is coordinatewise doubling modulo $1$.

<1>1. There is a point of $\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$ with dense orbit under
\[
D(s,t)=(2s,2t)\pmod{\mathbb Z^2}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
\mathcal A=\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}.
\]
Form an infinite word
\[
\mathbf u=(u_1,u_2,u_3,\ldots),
\qquad
u_j=(a_j,b_j)\in\mathcal A,
\]
by concatenating, for $m=1,2,3,\ldots$, all words of length $m$ over the alphabet $\mathcal A$. Then every finite word over $\mathcal A$ occurs in $\mathbf u$, in fact arbitrarily far out.

Define
\[
s=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{a_j}{2^j},
\qquad
t=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{b_j}{2^j}.
\]
The sequence $\mathbf u$ is not eventually constant in either coordinate, so these binary expansions cause no terminal-expansion ambiguity.

Multiplication by $2$ modulo $1$ shifts binary digits to the left. Thus
\[
D^n(s,t)
\]
has binary digit pairs
\[
u_{n+1},u_{n+2},u_{n+3},\ldots.
\]

Now let
\[
Q=I_{\varepsilon_1\cdots\varepsilon_m}
\times
I_{\delta_1\cdots\delta_m}
\]
be a dyadic rectangle, where the first factor consists of numbers whose first $m$ binary digits are $\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_m$, and similarly for the second factor. The length-$m$ word
\[
((\varepsilon_1,\delta_1),\ldots,(\varepsilon_m,\delta_m))
\]
occurs somewhere in $\mathbf u$, arbitrarily far from the beginning. Hence for some $n>0$,
\[
D^n(s,t)\in Q.
\]
Every nonempty open set in the torus contains a dyadic rectangle, so the forward orbit of $(s,t)$ is dense.
:::

<1>2. Part (a) follows for every complex torus $X=\mathbb C/\Lambda$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
p=\Phi(s,t),
\]
with $(s,t)$ from <1>1. Since
\[
F\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ D,
\]
we have
\[
F^n(p)=\Phi(D^n(s,t)).
\]
The homeomorphism $\Phi$ carries the dense orbit from <1>1 to a dense forward orbit of $F$ on $X$.
:::

<1>3. The Weierstrass map semiconjugates torus doubling to the rational duplication map:
\[
\wp\circ F=R\circ\wp.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the double-angle identity
\[
\wp(2z)=R(\wp(z)).
\]
Iterating gives
\[
\wp(F^n(p))=R^n(\wp(p))
\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
:::

<1>4. The rational map $R$ has a dense forward orbit on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$.
::: {.proof}
Set
\[
q=\wp(p),
\]
where $p$ has dense $F$-orbit as in <1>2. Let
\[
V\subset\widehat{\mathbb C}
\]
be any nonempty open set. The map
\[
\wp:X\to\widehat{\mathbb C}
\]
is continuous and surjective, so
\[
\wp^{-1}(V)
\]
is a nonempty open subset of $X$. Density of the $F$-orbit gives some $n>0$ with
\[
F^n(p)\in\wp^{-1}(V).
\]
Using <1>3,
\[
R^n(q)
=\wp(F^n(p))
\in V.
\]
Thus the forward orbit of $q$ under $R$ meets every nonempty open subset of the sphere and is therefore dense.
:::
