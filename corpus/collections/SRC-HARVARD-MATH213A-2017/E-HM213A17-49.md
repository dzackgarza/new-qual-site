::: {.problem}
Prove that every proper holomorphic map
\[
f:\Delta\to\Delta
\]
of degree two can be written
\[
f=A\circ S\circ B,
\]
where $A,B\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta)$ and $S(z)=z^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The map $f$ has a critical point $c\in\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that $f'$ never vanished. Then $f$ would be a covering map.
Since the target $\Delta$ is simply connected, such a covering from the
connected disk would be an isomorphism. Its degree would then be $1$, contrary
to the hypothesis that $f$ has degree $2$. Hence $f'(c)=0$ for some
$c\in\Delta$.
:::

<1>2. The point $c$ is the unique point of $f^{-1}(f(c))$, counted with
multiplicity two.
::: {.proof}
Because $f'(c)=0$, the local degree of $f$ at $c$ is at least $2$. The degree
of a proper holomorphic map is the number of points in any fiber counted with
multiplicity, and the total degree of $f$ is $2$. Therefore the local degree at
$c$ is exactly $2$ and there can be no other point in the fiber over $f(c)$.
:::

<1>3. Choose automorphisms $B_0,A_0\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta)$ with
\[
B_0(0)=c,
\qquad
A_0(f(c))=0,
\]
and set
\[
g=A_0\circ f\circ B_0.
\]
Then $g$ is a proper degree-two disk map whose only zero is $0$, with
multiplicity two.
::: {.proof}
Pre- and post-composition by automorphisms preserve properness and degree.
Moreover
\[
g(0)=A_0(f(c))=0.
\]
By <1>2, the fiber of $f$ over $f(c)$ consists only of $c$ with multiplicity
two. Applying $B_0^{-1}$ in the domain and $A_0$ in the range therefore shows
that the zero divisor of $g$ is exactly $2[0]$.
:::

<1>4. There is a real number $\theta$ such that
\[
g(z)=e^{i\theta}z^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
By McMullen's Theorem 2.26, every proper holomorphic map
$\Delta\to\Delta$ is a finite Blaschke product. A degree-two Blaschke product
has two zeros counted with multiplicity. By <1>3 both zeros of $g$ are at
$0$, so its Blaschke-product representation is
\[
g(z)=e^{i\theta}
\left(\frac{z-0}{1-0\cdot z}\right)^2
=e^{i\theta}z^2.
\]
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
f=A\circ S\circ B
\]
for suitable $A,B\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta)$ and $S(z)=z^2$.
::: {.proof}
From <1>4,
\[
A_0\circ f\circ B_0=R_\theta\circ S,
\qquad
R_\theta(z)=e^{i\theta}z.
\]
Hence
\[
f
=A_0^{-1}\circ R_\theta\circ S\circ B_0^{-1}.
\]
Both $A_0^{-1}\circ R_\theta$ and $B_0^{-1}$ are automorphisms of the disk.
Taking
\[
A=A_0^{-1}\circ R_\theta,
\qquad
B=B_0^{-1}
\]
gives the required form.
:::
:::
