::: {.problem}
Fix $\rho\ge0$, and let $\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$ be the set of entire functions of order at most $\rho$.

(a) Show that $\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$ is a ring.

(b) Show that $\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$ is integrally closed in $\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For an entire function $h$, write
\[
M_h(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|h(z)|.
\]

<1>1. If $f,g\in\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$, then
$f+g\in\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$.
::: {.proof}
The maximum-modulus inequality gives
\[
M_{f+g}(r)\le M_f(r)+M_g(r)
\le2\max\{M_f(r),M_g(r)\}.
\]
Hence
\[
\log\log M_{f+g}(r)
\le
\log\!\left(\log2+\max\{\log M_f(r),\log M_g(r)\}\right)
\]
for large $r$. Dividing by $\log r$ and taking limsup shows
\[
\rho(f+g)\le\max\{\rho(f),\rho(g)\}\le\rho.
\]
The zero function causes no exception.
:::

<1>2. If $f,g\in\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$, then
$fg\in\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$.
::: {.proof}
One has
\[
M_{fg}(r)\le M_f(r)M_g(r).
\]
Thus
\[
\log M_{fg}(r)
\le\log M_f(r)+\log M_g(r)
\le2\max\{\log M_f(r),\log M_g(r)\}.
\]
Taking another logarithm, dividing by $\log r$, and passing to limsup gives
\[
\rho(fg)\le\max\{\rho(f),\rho(g)\}\le\rho,
\]
as also noted in the source immediately before these exercises.
:::

<1>3. Therefore $\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$ is a ring.
::: {.proof}
It contains the constant functions, is closed under additive inverses, and
<1>1--<1>2 give closure under addition and multiplication.
:::

<1>4. Let $f\in\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$ satisfy a monic relation
\[
f^d+a_{d-1}f^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0=0,
\qquad
a_j\in\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C).
\]
Then $f$ is entire.
::: {.proof}
Since every coefficient $a_j$ is entire, this is in particular a monic
integral relation over $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$. By the preceding exercise,
$\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$ is integrally closed in
$\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$. Hence $f\in\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$.
:::

<1>5. For every $z\in\mathbb C$,
\[
|f(z)|\le1+\max_{0\le j<d}|a_j(z)|.
\]
::: {.proof}
Set
\[
A(z)=\max_{0\le j<d}|a_j(z)|.
\]
Suppose $|f(z)|>1+A(z)$. Then $|f(z)|>1$ and, using the monic relation,
\[
|f(z)|^d
\le A(z)\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|f(z)|^j
=A(z)\frac{|f(z)|^d-1}{|f(z)|-1}.
\]
Because $|f(z)|-1>A(z)$, the right side is strictly smaller than
$|f(z)|^d$, a contradiction. This is the usual Cauchy bound for roots of a
monic polynomial.
:::

<1>6. The entire function $f$ has order at most $\rho$.
::: {.proof}
Taking maxima on $|z|=r$ in <1>5 gives
\[
M_f(r)
\le1+\max_{0\le j<d}M_{a_j}(r).
\]
There are only finitely many coefficients, each of order at most $\rho$.
Therefore the finite maximum on the right has order at most $\rho$, and
adding the constant $1$ does not increase order. Hence
\[
\rho(f)\le\rho.
\]
Thus $f\in\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$.
:::

<1>7. Consequently $\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$ is integrally closed in
$\mathcal M(\mathbb C)$.
::: {.proof}
Every meromorphic element integral over
$\mathcal O_\rho(\mathbb C)$ belongs to that ring by <1>4 and <1>6.
:::
