::: {.problem}
Let $\{F_p\}$ be a sequence of entire functions such that $F_p(1)=0$, each $F_p$ has no other zeros, and
\[
F_p(z)\longrightarrow1
\]
uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb C\setminus\{1\}$.
Let $a_n\ne0$ with $a_n\to\infty$.
Show that there exist indices $p_n$ such that
\[
G(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{p_n}(z/a_n)
\]
converges absolutely on $\mathbb C$ and defines an entire function whose zeros are exactly the $a_n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose $p_n$ so that
\[
\sup_{|w|\le1/2}|F_{p_n}(w)-1|<2^{-n}.
\]
Then
\[
G(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{p_n}(z/a_n)
\]
converges locally uniformly and absolutely, and its zero set is precisely the
prescribed sequence $\{a_n\}$, with repetitions interpreted as multiplicities.

<1>1. The indices $p_n$ can be chosen with the displayed estimate.
::: {.proof}
The closed disk
\[
K=\{w:|w|\le1/2\}
\]
is a compact subset of $\mathbb C\setminus\{1\}$. By hypothesis,
\[
F_p(w)\longrightarrow1
\]
uniformly on $K$. Hence, for each $n$, there exists an index $p_n$ such that
\[
\sup_{|w|\le1/2}|F_{p_n}(w)-1|<2^{-n}.
\]
:::

<1>2. For every $n$ and every $z$ with $|z|\le |a_n|/2$,
\[
|F_{p_n}(z/a_n)-1|<2^{-n}.
\]
::: {.proof}
If $|z|\le |a_n|/2$, then
\[
\left|\frac z{a_n}\right|\le\frac12.
\]
The estimate is therefore exactly the one obtained in <1>1.
:::

<1>3. The series
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|F_{p_n}(z/a_n)-1|
\]
converges uniformly on every compact subset of $\mathbb C$.
::: {.proof}
Let $K_R=\{z:|z|\le R\}$. Since $a_n\to\infty$, there exists $N$ such that
\[
|a_n|/2\ge R
\qquad(n\ge N).
\]
For $z\in K_R$ and $n\ge N$, <1>2 gives
\[
|F_{p_n}(z/a_n)-1|<2^{-n}.
\]
Thus the tail is uniformly dominated on $K_R$ by the convergent geometric
series
\[
\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{-n}.
\]
The finitely many preceding terms cause no issue, so the full series converges
uniformly on $K_R$.
:::

<1>4. The infinite product defining $G$ converges locally uniformly to an
entire function.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, on every compact set the series of deviations from $1$,
\[
\sum_n |F_{p_n}(z/a_n)-1|,
\]
converges uniformly. The standard convergence criterion for infinite products
therefore shows that
\[
\prod_n F_{p_n}(z/a_n)
\]
converges uniformly on compact subsets. Each factor is entire, so the locally
uniform limit $G$ is entire.
:::

<1>5. Every $a_m$ is a zero of $G$.
::: {.proof}
At $z=a_m$, the $m$-th factor is
\[
F_{p_m}(a_m/a_m)=F_{p_m}(1)=0.
\]
Hence
\[
G(a_m)=0.
\]
If a value occurs several times in the sequence, the corresponding number of
factors vanish there, giving the expected multiplicity.
:::

<1>6. The function $G$ has no zeros outside the prescribed sequence.
::: {.proof}
Fix $z\notin\{a_n:n\ge1\}$. Then every factor
\[
F_{p_n}(z/a_n)
\]
is nonzero, because each $F_{p_n}$ has its only zero at $1$.

By <1>3, for all sufficiently large $n$ we have
\[
|F_{p_n}(z/a_n)-1|<1/2
\]
and the sum of these deviations converges. Hence the corresponding series of
logarithms converges absolutely after choosing the branch near $1$, so the tail
product converges to a nonzero number. Multiplying by the finitely many initial
nonzero factors remains nonzero. Thus
\[
G(z)\neq0.
\]
Therefore the zeros of $G$ are exactly the prescribed $a_n$.
:::
