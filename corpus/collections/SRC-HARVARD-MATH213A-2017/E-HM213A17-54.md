::: {.problem}
Let $a,b\in\Delta\subset\mathbb P^1$ be represented by vectors $A,B\in\mathbb C^2$, and let $\delta$ be the distance between $a$ and $b$ in the hyperbolic metric
\[
\rho_\Delta=\frac{2|dz|}{1-|z|^2}.
\]
Prove that
\[
\cosh(\delta/2)=\frac{|A\cdot B|}{\sqrt{|A^2|\,|B^2|}},
\]
where
\[
A\cdot B=A_0\overline{B_0}-A_1\overline{B_1},
\qquad
A^2=A\cdot A,
\qquad
B^2=B\cdot B.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The quantity
\[
\frac{|A\cdot B|}{\sqrt{|A^2|\,|B^2|}}
\]
depends only on the projective points represented by $A$ and $B$.
::: {.proof}
If $A$ is replaced by $\lambda A$ and $B$ by $\mu B$, with
$\lambda,\mu\neq0$, then
\[
(\lambda A)\cdot(\mu B)
=\lambda\overline\mu(A\cdot B),
\]
while
\[
(\lambda A)^2=|\lambda|^2A^2,
\qquad
(\mu B)^2=|\mu|^2B^2.
\]
The common factor $|\lambda||\mu|$ therefore cancels from the ratio.
:::

<1>2. We may consequently take
\[
A=(a,1),
\qquad
B=(b,1).
\]
Then
\[
\frac{|A\cdot B|}{\sqrt{|A^2|\,|B^2|}}
=\frac{|1-a\overline b|}
{\sqrt{(1-|a|^2)(1-|b|^2)}}.
\]
::: {.proof}
For these representatives,
\[
A\cdot B=a\overline b-1,
\]
and
\[
A^2=|a|^2-1,
\qquad
B^2=|b|^2-1.
\]
Since $a,b\in\Delta$, both squared norms are negative, so
\[
|A^2|=1-|a|^2,
\qquad
|B^2|=1-|b|^2.
\]
Taking absolute values gives the stated expression.
:::

<1>3. Let
\[
\phi_b(z)=\frac{z-b}{1-\overline b z}.
\]
Then $\phi_b\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta)$, $\phi_b(b)=0$, and if
\[
r=|\phi_b(a)|,
\]
then
\[
\delta=d_\Delta(a,b)=d_\Delta(r,0).
\]
::: {.proof}
Automorphisms of the unit disk are isometries for the hyperbolic metric.
Thus
\[
d_\Delta(a,b)
=d_\Delta(\phi_b(a),0).
\]
A rotation about the origin is also a disk automorphism, so it is an
isometry and sends $\phi_b(a)$ to the positive real number
$r=|\phi_b(a)|$. Hence the distance equals $d_\Delta(r,0)$.
:::

<1>4. The radial hyperbolic distance from $0$ to $r\in[0,1)$ is
\[
d_\Delta(0,r)
=\log\frac{1+r}{1-r}
=2\operatorname{artanh}r.
\]
Hence
\[
\cosh(\delta/2)=\frac1{\sqrt{1-r^2}}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Along the radial segment $[0,r]$, the metric is
\[
\frac{2\,dt}{1-t^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
d_\Delta(0,r)
=\int_0^r\frac{2\,dt}{1-t^2}
=\log\frac{1+r}{1-r}.
\]
Thus $r=\tanh(\delta/2)$, and the identity
$1-\tanh^2x=\operatorname{sech}^2x$ gives
\[
\cosh(\delta/2)=\frac1{\sqrt{1-r^2}}.
\]
:::

<1>5. For $r=|\phi_b(a)|$,
\[
1-r^2
=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|b|^2)}{|1-a\overline b|^2}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By definition,
\[
r^2=\frac{|a-b|^2}{|1-\overline b a|^2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
1-r^2
&=\frac{|1-\overline b a|^2-|a-b|^2}
{|1-\overline b a|^2}\\
&=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|b|^2)}{|1-\overline b a|^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\cosh(\delta/2)
=\frac{|A\cdot B|}{\sqrt{|A^2|\,|B^2|}}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combining <1>4 and <1>5 gives
\[
\cosh(\delta/2)
=\frac{|1-a\overline b|}
{\sqrt{(1-|a|^2)(1-|b|^2)}}.
\]
By <1>2 this is precisely the projective expression in the statement.
:::
