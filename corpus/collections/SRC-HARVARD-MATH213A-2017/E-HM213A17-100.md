::: {.problem}
Let $U\subset\widehat{\mathbb C}$ be a region such that
\[
|\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus U|\ge3.
\]
Prove that the universal cover of $U$ is conformally equivalent to the unit disk $\Delta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
\pi:(X,p)\longrightarrow(U,u_0)
\]
be the abstract universal cover. We construct a covering map
$X\to\Delta$ by an extremal argument and then prove that it is surjective.

<1>1. There exists a nonconstant holomorphic map
\[
F:(X,p)\longrightarrow(\Delta,0)
\]
which is a covering map onto its image.
::: {.proof}
Choose three distinct points
\[
a,b,c\in\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus U.
\]
Set
\[
Y=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{a,b,c\}.
\]
The preceding construction in the source gives a universal covering
\[
q:\Delta\longrightarrow Y.
\]

The inclusion $j:U\hookrightarrow Y$ composed with $\pi$ gives a map
\[
j\circ\pi:X\to Y.
\]
Since $X$ is simply connected, this map lifts through $q$ to a holomorphic
map
\[
F:X\to\Delta
\]
with $F(p)=0$ after choosing a suitable point of the fiber $q^{-1}(u_0)$ and
postcomposing by a disk automorphism if necessary.

More concretely, $F(X)$ is the connected component of $q^{-1}(U)$ containing
$F(p)$. The restriction of $q$ to that component is a covering of $U$, and
the usual lifting property shows that $F:X\to F(X)$ is its universal covering.
Thus $F$ is a covering map onto its image.
:::

<1>2. Let $\mathcal F$ be the family of all holomorphic maps
\[
F:(X,p)\to(\Delta,0)
\]
which are covering maps onto their images. Then $\mathcal F$ is a normal
family, and a nonconstant locally uniform limit of members of $\mathcal F$
again belongs to $\mathcal F$.
::: {.proof}
Normality is immediate locally: every member of $\mathcal F$ takes values in
the bounded domain $\Delta$, so Montel's theorem applies in every coordinate
disk of $X$; a diagonal argument gives subsequential convergence on compact
subsets of $X$.

For the closure statement, let $F_n\in\mathcal F$ converge locally uniformly
to a nonconstant $F$. Since each covering map has no critical points,
$F_n'$ never vanishes in local coordinates. Hurwitz's theorem applied to the
derivatives shows that $F'$ never vanishes either. Thus $F$ is a local
biholomorphism.

The covering property is stable under this normalized nonconstant limit:
inverse germs of the $F_n$ are univalent, and on every compact chain of
coordinate disks they form normal families. Their limits give analytic
continuation of every inverse germ of $F$ along every path in $F(X)$; Hurwitz
prevents two inverse branches from colliding. The path-lifting criterion for
coverings therefore shows that
\[
F:X\to F(X)
\]
is a covering map. Hence $F\in\mathcal F$.
:::

<1>3. Fix a local coordinate $\zeta$ near $p$ with $\zeta(p)=0$. There is
an extremal map $F\in\mathcal F$ satisfying
\[
|F'(p)|=\sup_{H\in\mathcal F}|H'(p)|>0,
\]
where derivatives are taken in the coordinate $\zeta$.
::: {.proof}
The family is nonempty by <1>1, and every member is a local biholomorphism,
so the supremum is positive.

Choose a small coordinate disk $D$ about $p$ whose closure is compact in
$X$. Since every $H\in\mathcal F$ satisfies $|H|<1$, Cauchy's estimate on
$D$ gives a uniform upper bound on $|H'(p)|$. Hence the supremum is finite.

Choose $F_n\in\mathcal F$ with $|F_n'(p)|$ tending to the supremum. By
normality, pass to a locally uniformly convergent subsequence $F_n\to F$.
Derivatives converge locally as well, so $|F'(p)|$ equals the positive
supremum. In particular $F$ is nonconstant, and by the closure statement in
<1>2 we have $F\in\mathcal F$.
:::

<1>4. The extremal map $F$ from <1>3 is surjective onto $\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that some $a\in\Delta$ is omitted by $F$. Since $F(p)=0$,
we have $a\neq0$.

Let
\[
\phi_a(w)=\frac{w-a}{1-\overline a\,w},
\]
so $\phi_a$ is a disk automorphism sending $a$ to $0$. The map
\[
F_1=\phi_a\circ F
\]
omits $0$. Since $X$ is simply connected, $F_1$ has a holomorphic square
root
\[
h:X\to\Delta,
\qquad h^2=F_1.
\]
Equivalently, $h$ is the lift of $F_1$ through the covering
$s(w)=w^2$ of $\Delta^*$. Because $F$ is a covering onto its image, the
lifting property shows that $h$ is also a covering onto its image.

Put
\[
u=h(p).
\]
Then
\[
|u|^2=|F_1(p)|=|\phi_a(0)|=|a|.
\]
Set $r=|u|=\sqrt{|a|}$, so $0<r<1$, and let
\[
\psi_u(w)=\frac{w-u}{1-\overline u\,w}.
\]
The normalized map
\[
H=\psi_u\circ h
\]
belongs to $\mathcal F$ and satisfies $H(p)=0$.

We now compare derivatives at $p$. Since
\[
|\phi_a'(0)|=1-|a|^2=1-r^4,
\]
the identity $h^2=F_1$ gives
\[
2|u|\,|h'(p)|=(1-r^4)|F'(p)|.
\]
Also
\[
|\psi_u'(u)|=\frac1{1-|u|^2}=\frac1{1-r^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
|H'(p)|
&=\frac{|h'(p)|}{1-r^2}\\
&=\frac{1-r^4}{2r(1-r^2)}|F'(p)|\\
&=\frac{1+r^2}{2r}|F'(p)|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $0<r<1$,
\[
1+r^2>2r,
\]
so
\[
|H'(p)|>|F'(p)|.
\]
This contradicts the extremality of $F$. Hence $F$ omits no point of
$\Delta$, and therefore
\[
F(X)=\Delta.
\]
:::

<1>5. The universal cover $X$ is conformally equivalent to $\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>4,
\[
F:X\to\Delta
\]
is a covering map. The target $\Delta$ is simply connected. A connected
covering of a simply connected space has one sheet, so $F$ is a homeomorphism.
Since $F$ is holomorphic and locally biholomorphic, its inverse is holomorphic.
Thus
\[
X\cong\Delta
\]
conformally, as required.
:::
