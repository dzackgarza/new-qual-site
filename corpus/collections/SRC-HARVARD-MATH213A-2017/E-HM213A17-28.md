::: {.problem}
For each integer $n>0$, give an example of an analytic function
\[
f:\Delta^*\to\mathbb C
\]
such that:

1. $f$ is nowhere zero;
2. $f$ has an essential singularity at $z=0$;
3. $\operatorname{Res}(f'/f,0)=n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For the prescribed integer $n>0$, take
\[
\boxed{f(z)=z^n e^{1/z}}.
\]

<1>1. The function $f$ is analytic and nowhere zero on $\Delta^*$.
::: {.proof}
Both factors $z^n$ and $e^{1/z}$ are analytic on $\Delta^*$. They are also
both nonzero there: $z^n\neq0$ for $z\neq0$, and the exponential function
never vanishes. Hence their product is analytic and nowhere zero on
$\Delta^*$.
:::

<1>2. The singularity of $f$ at $0$ is essential.
::: {.proof}
The Laurent expansion is
\[
f(z)=z^n e^{1/z}
=z^n\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-k}}{k!}
=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n-k}}{k!}.
\]
There are infinitely many negative powers of $z$, namely all terms with
$k>n$. Therefore the isolated singularity at $0$ is essential.
:::

<1>3. The logarithmic derivative of $f$ has residue $n$ at $0$.
::: {.proof}
Direct differentiation gives
\[
f'(z)
=nz^{n-1}e^{1/z}-z^{n-2}e^{1/z}.
\]
Dividing by the nowhere-zero function $f(z)=z^ne^{1/z}$ yields
\[
\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}=\frac nz-\frac1{z^2}.
\]
The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ is $n$, so
\[
\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{f'}f,0\right)=n.
\]
:::
:::
