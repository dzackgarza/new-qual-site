::: {.problem}
Show that for every polynomial $p(z)$ there exists $z$ with $\abs{z}=1$ such that
$$
\abs{p(z)-\frac1z}\ge 1.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
\abs{p(z)-\frac1z}<1
$$
for every $\abs{z}=1$. Then
$$
\abs{zp(z)-1}<1
$$
on the unit circle.

::: pf-proof

For $\abs{z}=1$,
$$
\abs{zp(z)-1}
=\abs{z}\abs{p(z)-\frac1z}
=\abs{p(z)-\frac1z}.
$$
Thus the assumed strict inequality gives the displayed estimate.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Rouché's theorem applied on $\abs{z}=1$ gives a contradiction.

::: pf-proof

Set
$$
q(z)=zp(z).
$$
On $\abs{z}=1$, step [](#s1){.pf-ref} says
$$
\abs{q(z)-1}<1=\abs{1}.
$$
Hence Rouché's theorem implies that
$$
q(z)=1+(q(z)-1)
$$
and the constant function $1$ have the same number of zeros in the unit disk,
counted with multiplicity. The constant function has no zeros, so $q$ should
have none.

But
$$
q(0)=0\cdot p(0)=0,
$$
so $q$ has a zero at the origin. This contradiction proves that the strict
inequality cannot hold at every point of the unit circle. Therefore some
$\abs{z}=1$ satisfies
$$
\abs{p(z)-\frac1z}\ge1.
$$

:::
