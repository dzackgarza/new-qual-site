::: {.problem}
Let $f:U\to V$ be a proper local homeomorphism between open sets $U,V\subset\mathbb C$.
Prove that $f$ is a covering map.
Here proper means that $f^{-1}(K)$ is compact whenever $K\subset V$ is compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Under the convention that a covering map is surjective, the statement as written needs an additional hypothesis, for example that $V$ is connected and $U$ is nonempty, or more directly that $f$ is surjective.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
V=D(0,1)\sqcup D(3,1),
\qquad
U=D(0,1),
\]
and let $f:U\hookrightarrow V$ be the inclusion. It is a local homeomorphism. It is also proper: $U$ is both open and closed in $V$, so for every compact $K\subset V$ the set
\[
f^{-1}(K)=K\cap U
\]
is closed in the compact set $K$, hence compact. But $f$ is not surjective onto $V$, so it is not a covering map under the stated convention.
:::

<1>2. For any proper local homeomorphism $f:U\to V$, the image $f(U)$ is both open and closed in $V$.
::: {.proof}
It is open because every local homeomorphism is an open map.

To prove that it is closed, let $y_n=f(x_n)$ be a sequence in $f(U)$ converging to $y\in V$. Choose $r>0$ such that the closed disk
\[
K=\overline{D(y,r)}
\]
lies in $V$. For all sufficiently large $n$, we have $y_n\in K$, hence $x_n\in f^{-1}(K)$. Properness makes $f^{-1}(K)$ compact, so a subsequence $x_{n_j}$ converges to some $x\in f^{-1}(K)$. Continuity gives
\[
f(x)=\lim_j f(x_{n_j})=y.
\]
Thus $y\in f(U)$, proving that $f(U)$ is closed.

Consequently $f(U)$ is a union of connected components of $V$. In particular, if $V$ is connected and $U\ne\varnothing$, then $f(U)=V$.
:::

<1>3. Every fiber of $f$ is finite.
::: {.proof}
Fix $y\in f(U)$. Since $\{y\}$ is compact and $f$ is proper, the fiber
\[
F=f^{-1}(y)
\]
is compact. Local homeomorphy makes $F$ discrete: for each $x\in F$ there is a neighborhood on which $f$ is injective, and that neighborhood meets $F$ only at $x$. A compact discrete space is finite, so write
\[
F=\{x_1,\ldots,x_m\}.
\]
:::

<1>4. Every $y\in f(U)$ has an evenly covered neighborhood.
::: {.proof}
For each point $x_i$ of the finite fiber in <1>3, choose pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods $W_i\subset U$ such that
\[
f|_{W_i}:W_i\longrightarrow O_i
\]
is a homeomorphism onto an open neighborhood $O_i$ of $y$.

Choose $r>0$ with $\overline{D(y,r)}\subset V$ and put
\[
K=\overline{D(y,r)},
\qquad
C=f^{-1}(K)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^m W_i.
\]
The set $f^{-1}(K)$ is compact by properness, and $C$ is closed in it, so $C$ is compact. No point of $C$ maps to $y$, because the whole fiber over $y$ is contained in the union of the $W_i$. Hence $f(C)$ is compact, therefore closed in $V$, and $y\notin f(C)$.

Now set
\[
N=D(y,r)\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^m O_i\setminus f(C).
\]
This is an open neighborhood of $y$. If $x\in f^{-1}(N)$, then $f(x)\in K$, while $f(x)\notin f(C)$; hence $x\notin C$ and therefore $x\in W_i$ for some $i$. Thus
\[
f^{-1}(N)
=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^m\bigl(W_i\cap f^{-1}(N)\bigr).
\]
Because $N\subset O_i$, each restriction
\[
f:W_i\cap f^{-1}(N)\longrightarrow N
\]
is a homeomorphism. Therefore $N$ is evenly covered.
:::

<1>5. Hence a proper local homeomorphism is a covering map onto its image, and the source claim follows whenever $V$ is connected and $U$ is nonempty.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, the image is a union of connected components of $V$, and by <1>4 every point of the image has an evenly covered neighborhood. Thus
\[
f:U\longrightarrow f(U)
\]
is a covering map. If $V$ is connected and $U\ne\varnothing$, then <1>2 gives $f(U)=V$, so $f:U\to V$ is a covering map.
:::
