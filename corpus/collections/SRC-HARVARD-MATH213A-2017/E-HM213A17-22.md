::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R^{n+1}\to\mathbb R^{n+1}$ be continuous, and suppose $f$ does not vanish on the unit sphere $S^n$.
Define
\[
F:S^n\to S^n,
\qquad
F(x)=\frac{f(x)}{|f(x)|}.
\]
Prove that if $F$ has nonzero degree, then $f$ has a zero in the unit ball.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Assume, for contradiction, that $f$ has no zero in the closed unit ball $B^{n+1}$.
::: {.proof}
Under this assumption the normalization
\[
G:B^{n+1}\longrightarrow S^n,
\qquad
G(x)=\frac{f(x)}{|f(x)|},
\]
is continuous. On the boundary $S^n=\partial B^{n+1}$ it agrees with $F$, so $G$ is an extension of $F$ over the ball.
:::

<1>2. Any map $S^n\to S^n$ that extends continuously over $B^{n+1}$ is null-homotopic.
::: {.proof}
Using the extension $G$ from <1>1, define
\[
H:S^n\times[0,1]\longrightarrow S^n,
\qquad
H(x,t)=G((1-t)x).
\]
Then
\[
H(x,0)=G(x)=F(x),
\qquad
H(x,1)=G(0),
\]
so $H$ is a homotopy from $F$ to the constant map with value $G(0)$.
:::

<1>3. The assumption in <1>1 contradicts $\deg(F)\neq0$.
::: {.proof}
Degree is invariant under homotopy, while a constant map $S^n\to S^n$ has degree $0$. By <1>2, $F$ is homotopic to a constant map, hence
\[
\deg(F)=0,
\]
contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore $f$ must vanish at some point of $B^{n+1}$.
:::
:::
