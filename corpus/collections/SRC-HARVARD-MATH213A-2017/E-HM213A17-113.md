::: {.problem}
Let $D_n$ be the union of the open unit disks centered at $0$ and at $2-1/n$.
Let
\[
f_n:(\Delta,0)\longrightarrow(D_n,0)
\]
be the Riemann map normalized by $f_n'(0)>0$.
Prove that
\[
f_n(z)\longrightarrow z
\]
uniformly on compact subsets of $\Delta$.
In particular, show that the image of the limiting map is smaller than the limit of the images of the $f_n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The maps $f_n$ converge locally uniformly to the identity on $\Delta$.

<1>1. The family $(f_n)$ is normal, and its derivatives at the origin satisfy
\[
f_n'(0)\ge1.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every $D_n$ is contained in the disk $B(0,3)$, so
\[
|f_n(z)|<3
\qquad(z\in\Delta).
\]
Hence $(f_n)$ is a normal family.

Also the first unit disk is contained in every $D_n$:
\[
\Delta\subset D_n.
\]
Therefore the inverse Riemann map restricts to a holomorphic self-map
\[
h_n:=f_n^{-1}|_\Delta:\Delta\to\Delta
\]
fixing $0$. By the Schwarz lemma,
\[
|h_n'(0)|\le1.
\]
Since $h_n'(0)=1/f_n'(0)$ and $f_n'(0)>0$, this gives
\[
f_n'(0)\ge1.
\]
:::

<1>2. Every locally uniformly convergent subsequence of $(f_n)$ converges to the identity.
::: {.proof}
Take any subsequence. By <1>1 it has a further subsequence, still denoted $(f_{n_k})$, converging locally uniformly to a holomorphic map
\[
g:\Delta\to\mathbb C.
\]
Then
\[
g(0)=0,
\qquad
g'(0)=\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{n_k}'(0)\ge1.
\]
In particular, $g$ is nonconstant.

Set
\[
K=\overline\Delta\cup\overline{B(2,1)}.
\]
For every $w\in D_n$, either $w\in\Delta$ or $w\in B(2-1/n,1)$. In the latter case its distance from $\overline{B(2,1)}$ is at most $1/n$. Thus
\[
\sup_{w\in D_n}\operatorname{dist}(w,K)\le\frac1n.
\]
For each fixed $z\in\Delta$, the points $f_{n_k}(z)$ therefore converge to a point of $K$, so
\[
g(\Delta)\subset K.
\]

Because $g$ is nonconstant, $g(\Delta)$ is open and connected. But
\[
\operatorname{int}K
=\Delta\,\sqcup\,B(2,1),
\]
the disjoint union of the two open tangent disks. Since $0\in g(\Delta)$, connectedness forces
\[
g(\Delta)\subset\Delta.
\]
Thus $g$ is a holomorphic self-map of $\Delta$ fixing $0$. The Schwarz lemma gives
\[
|g'(0)|\le1.
\]
Combined with $g'(0)\ge1$ and the positive normalization, this yields
\[
g'(0)=1.
\]
The equality case of the Schwarz lemma now gives
\[
g(z)=z.
\]
:::

<1>3. The whole sequence converges to the identity locally uniformly.
::: {.proof}
If not, there would be a compact set $L\Subset\Delta$, an $\varepsilon>0$, and a subsequence such that
\[
\sup_{z\in L}|f_n(z)-z|\ge\varepsilon
\]
along that subsequence. Normality would give a further locally uniformly convergent subsequence, which by <1>2 must converge to the identity, a contradiction.
:::

<1>4. The image of the limiting map is strictly smaller than the limiting images.
::: {.proof}
The limiting map is the identity, so its image is only
\[
\Delta.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
2\in B(2-1/n,1)\subset D_n=f_n(\Delta)
\]
for every $n\ge2$, because $|2-(2-1/n)|=1/n<1$. Thus the fixed point $2$ belongs to all sufficiently large images $f_n(\Delta)$ but not to the image of the limiting map. In particular,
\[
\operatorname{im}(\lim f_n)=\Delta
\]
is strictly smaller than the set-theoretic lower limit of the images, and hence than the geometric limit of the domains.
:::
