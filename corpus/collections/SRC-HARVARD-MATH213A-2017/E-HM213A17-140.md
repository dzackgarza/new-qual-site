::: {.problem}
Continue with the lattice generators $\lambda_i$, zeta quasi-periods $\eta_i$, and the canonical product $\sigma$.
Show that
\[
\sigma(z+\lambda_i)=\exp(a_i+b_i z)\sigma(z)
\]
for suitable constants $a_i,b_i\in\mathbb C$, and express $a_i$ and $b_i$ in terms of $\lambda_i$ and $\eta_i$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The quasi-periodicity is
\[
\boxed{
\sigma(z+\lambda_i)
=-\exp\!\left(\eta_i\left(z+\frac{\lambda_i}{2}\right)\right)\sigma(z).
}
\]
Consequently one may take
\[
\boxed{b_i=\eta_i,
\qquad
a_i\equiv \frac{\eta_i\lambda_i}{2}+\pi i\pmod{2\pi i}.}
\]

<1>1. The quotient $\sigma(z+\lambda_i)/\sigma(z)$ is an everywhere nonzero entire function after removing its apparent singularities.
::: {.proof}
The zeros of $\sigma$ are exactly the lattice points, all simple. Translation by $\lambda_i\in\Lambda$ permutes those zeros. Hence the numerator and denominator of
\[
R_i(z)=\frac{\sigma(z+\lambda_i)}{\sigma(z)}
\]
have the same simple zeros. Every apparent zero or pole of the quotient is therefore removable, and the resulting entire function $R_i$ has no zeros.
:::

<1>2. Its logarithmic derivative is the constant $\eta_i$.
::: {.proof}
Using the preceding identity
\[
\zeta=\frac{\sigma'}{\sigma}
\]
and the quasi-period relation
\[
\zeta(z+\lambda_i)=\zeta(z)+\eta_i,
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{R_i'(z)}{R_i(z)}
=\frac{\sigma'(z+\lambda_i)}{\sigma(z+\lambda_i)}
 -\frac{\sigma'(z)}{\sigma(z)}
=\eta_i.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{d}{dz}\bigl(e^{-\eta_i z}R_i(z)\bigr)=0,
\]
so for some $C_i\ne0$,
\[
R_i(z)=C_i e^{\eta_i z}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\sigma(z+\lambda_i)=C_i e^{\eta_i z}\sigma(z).
\]
:::

<1>3. The canonical product $\sigma$ is odd.
::: {.proof}
Pair in the defining product the factors indexed by $\lambda$ and $-\lambda$. Their product is
\[
\left(1-\frac{z^2}{\lambda^2}\right)
\exp\!\left(\frac{z^2}{\lambda^2}\right),
\]
which is even in $z$. Since the remaining initial factor is $z$, the full canonical product satisfies
\[
\sigma(-z)=-\sigma(z).
\]
:::

<1>4. Oddness determines the constant $C_i$.
::: {.proof}
Put
\[
z=-\frac{\lambda_i}{2}
\]
in the formula from <1>2. Since $\lambda_i/2\notin\Lambda$, the value $\sigma(\lambda_i/2)$ is nonzero. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma(\lambda_i/2)
&=C_i e^{-\eta_i\lambda_i/2}\sigma(-\lambda_i/2)\\
&=-C_i e^{-\eta_i\lambda_i/2}\sigma(\lambda_i/2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Cancelling gives
\[
C_i=-e^{\eta_i\lambda_i/2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\sigma(z+\lambda_i)
=-e^{\eta_i(z+\lambda_i/2)}\sigma(z).
\]
Writing the minus sign as $e^{\pi i}$ yields
\[
b_i=\eta_i,
\qquad
a_i\equiv\frac{\eta_i\lambda_i}{2}+\pi i\pmod{2\pi i},
\]
which is exactly the ambiguity inherent in the expression $e^{a_i+b_i z}$.
:::
