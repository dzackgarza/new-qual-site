::: {.problem}
State and prove a necessary and sufficient condition for a meromorphic $1$-form
\[
\omega=\omega(z)\,dz
\]
on $\mathbb C$ to be the logarithmic derivative of a meromorphic function; that is, for
\[
\omega=d\log f=\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz
\]
for some meromorphic $f$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A meromorphic $1$-form $\omega$ on $\mathbb C$ is of the form
\[
\omega=\frac{f'}f\,dz
\]
for a nonzero meromorphic function $f$ if and only if every pole of
$\omega$ is simple and has integer residue.

<1>1. If $\omega=(f'/f)\,dz$ for a nonzero meromorphic $f$, then every
pole of $\omega$ is simple with integer residue.
::: {.proof}
Fix $a\in\mathbb C$. Since $f$ is meromorphic and not identically zero, in
a neighborhood of $a$ it can be written
\[
f(z)=(z-a)^m u(z),
\]
where $m\in\mathbb Z$ and $u$ is holomorphic with $u(a)\neq0$. Here
$m>0$ is the order of a zero, $m<0$ is minus the order of a pole, and
$m=0$ means that $f$ is holomorphic and nonzero at $a$.

Taking the logarithmic derivative gives
\[
\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}
=\frac{m}{z-a}+\frac{u'(z)}{u(z)}.
\]
The second term is holomorphic near $a$. Hence, whenever $f'/f$ has a pole
at $a$, that pole is simple and
\[
\operatorname{Res}_a\left(\frac{f'}f\,dz\right)=m\in\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>2. Conversely, suppose every pole of $\omega$ is simple and has
integer residue. Let $P$ be the discrete set of poles and choose
$z_0\in\mathbb C\setminus P$.
::: {.proof}
The set of poles of a meromorphic form is discrete, so
$\mathbb C\setminus P$ is path connected. On this punctured plane we may
integrate $\omega$ along piecewise smooth paths beginning at $z_0$.
:::

<1>3. For $z\in\mathbb C\setminus P$, the formula
\[
f(z)=\exp\left(\int_{z_0}^z\omega\right)
\]
is independent of the chosen path.
::: {.proof}
If $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are two paths from $z_0$ to $z$, their
difference is a closed cycle $\gamma$. By the residue theorem,
\[
\int_\gamma\omega
=2\pi i\sum_{a\in P}n(\gamma,a)\operatorname{Res}_a(\omega),
\]
where only finitely many terms are nonzero and each winding number
$n(\gamma,a)$ is an integer. By hypothesis each residue is also an integer,
so
\[
\int_\gamma\omega\in2\pi i\mathbb Z.
\]
Exponentiation therefore removes the ambiguity:
\[
\exp\left(\int_{\gamma_1}\omega\right)
=\exp\left(\int_{\gamma_2}\omega\right).
\]
Thus $f$ is well-defined on $\mathbb C\setminus P$.
:::

<1>4. On $\mathbb C\setminus P$, the function $f$ is holomorphic,
nowhere zero, and satisfies $f'/f\,dz=\omega$.
::: {.proof}
Locally on a simply connected disk avoiding $P$, choose a primitive $H$ of
$\omega$. The definition in <1>3 then has the form
\[
f(z)=Ce^{H(z)}
\]
for some nonzero constant $C$. Hence $f$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero,
and
\[
\frac{f'}f=H'=\omega/dz.
\]
:::

<1>5. The function $f$ extends meromorphically across every point of $P$.
::: {.proof}
Let $a\in P$ and write
\[
\operatorname{Res}_a(\omega)=m\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Because the pole is simple,
\[
\omega
=\left(\frac{m}{z-a}+h(z)\right)dz
\]
near $a$, with $h$ holomorphic. Choose a holomorphic primitive $H$ of $h$
on a small disk about $a$. On the punctured disk, <1>4 implies
\[
f(z)=C(z-a)^m e^{H(z)}
\]
for some $C\neq0$.

If $m\ge0$, this extends holomorphically across $a$, with a zero of order
$m$ when $m>0$. If $m<0$, it extends meromorphically with a pole of order
$-m$. Thus $f$ extends meromorphically across every pole of $\omega$.
:::

<1>6. The extended meromorphic function satisfies
\[
\omega=d\log f
\]
on all of $\mathbb C$.
::: {.proof}
The equality holds on $\mathbb C\setminus P$ by <1>4. Near every
$a\in P$, the local expression from <1>5 gives
\[
\frac{f'}f
=\frac{m}{z-a}+h(z),
\]
which is exactly the local coefficient of $\omega$. Hence the equality holds
meromorphically across $P$ as well.
:::
