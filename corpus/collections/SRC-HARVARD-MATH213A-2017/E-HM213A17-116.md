::: {.problem}
For $\operatorname{Re}a>0$ and $\operatorname{Re}b>0$, prove that
\[
B(a,b)=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}.
\]

Hint: fix $b$ and show that
\[
F(a)=\Gamma(a+b)B(a,b)
\]
satisfies $F(a+1)=aF(a)$, using
\[
x^a(1-x)^{b+1}=(x^a-x^{a+1})(1-x)^b.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $b$ with $\operatorname{Re}b>0$ and set
\[
F(a)=\Gamma(a+b)B(a,b),
\qquad \operatorname{Re}a>0.
\]
We show that $F(a)/\Gamma(b)$ satisfies Wielandt's characterization of the Gamma function.

<1>1. The beta integral satisfies
\[
(a+b)B(a+1,b)=aB(a,b).
\]
::: {.proof}
From
\[
1=x+(1-x)
\]
inside the beta integral,
\[
B(a,b)=B(a+1,b)+B(a,b+1).
\]
Also integration by parts gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
B(a+1,b)
&=\int_0^1x^a(1-x)^{b-1}\,dx\\
&=\frac{a}{b}\int_0^1x^{a-1}(1-x)^b\,dx\\
&=\frac{a}{b}B(a,b+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The boundary term vanishes because $\operatorname{Re}a,\operatorname{Re}b>0$. Hence
\[
bB(a+1,b)=aB(a,b+1)
=a\bigl(B(a,b)-B(a+1,b)\bigr),
\]
which rearranges to
\[
(a+b)B(a+1,b)=aB(a,b).
\]
This is the integrated form of the identity suggested in the hint.
:::

<1>2. The function $F$ satisfies the Gamma functional equation
\[
F(a+1)=aF(a).
\]
::: {.proof}
Using <1>1 and $\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(a+1)
&=\Gamma(a+b+1)B(a+1,b)\\
&=(a+b)\Gamma(a+b)B(a+1,b)\\
&=a\Gamma(a+b)B(a,b)\\
&=aF(a).
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>3. The normalized function
\[
G(a)=\frac{F(a)}{\Gamma(b)}
\]
satisfies $G(1)=1$ and is bounded on the strip $1\le\operatorname{Re}a\le2$.
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
B(1,b)=\int_0^1(1-x)^{b-1}\,dx=\frac1b,
\]
the Gamma functional equation gives
\[
G(1)
=\frac{\Gamma(b+1)B(1,b)}{\Gamma(b)}
=\frac{b\Gamma(b)}{b\Gamma(b)}
=1.
\]

For $1\le\operatorname{Re}a\le2$,
\[
|B(a,b)|
\le\int_0^1x^{\operatorname{Re}a-1}(1-x)^{\operatorname{Re}b-1}\,dx
\le\int_0^1(1-x)^{\operatorname{Re}b-1}\,dx
=\frac1{\operatorname{Re}b}.
\]
McMullen's Gamma estimate
\[
|\Gamma(z)|\le\Gamma(\operatorname{Re}z)
\]
shows that $\Gamma(a+b)$ is uniformly bounded on this strip, because
\[
1+\operatorname{Re}b
\le\operatorname{Re}(a+b)
\le2+\operatorname{Re}b.
\]
Thus $G$ is bounded on $1\le\operatorname{Re}a\le2$.
:::

<1>4. Wielandt's uniqueness theorem identifies $G$ with $\Gamma$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2,
\[
G(a+1)=aG(a).
\]
By <1>3, $G(1)=1$ and $G$ is bounded on the fundamental strip. Wielandt's characterization therefore gives
\[
G(a)=\Gamma(a)
\qquad(\operatorname{Re}a>0).
\]
Thus
\[
\Gamma(a+b)B(a,b)=\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b),
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
B(a,b)=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}
}.
\]
:::
:::
