::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal S$ be the normalized univalent maps $f:\Delta\to\mathbb C$ with $f(0)=0$ and $f'(0)=1$.
Show that the maps $f\in\mathcal S$ whose images $f(\Delta)$ are Jordan domains are dense in $\mathcal S$ for uniform convergence on compact subsets of $\Delta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $f\in\mathcal S$. For $0<r<1$, define
\[
f_r(z)=\frac{f(rz)}r.
\]
Then each $f_r$ lies in $\mathcal S$, its image is a Jordan domain, and
\[
f_r\longrightarrow f
\]
uniformly on compact subsets as $r\to1^-$. Thus the desired maps are dense.

<1>1. Each $f_r$ is normalized and univalent.
::: {.proof}
Since $f$ is univalent and $z\mapsto rz$ is injective on $\Delta$, the map
$f_r$ is univalent. Moreover
\[
f_r(0)=0,
\qquad
f_r'(0)=f'(0)=1.
\]
Hence $f_r\in\mathcal S$.
:::

<1>2. The image $f_r(\Delta)$ is a Jordan domain.
::: {.proof}
Choose $\rho$ with
\[
r<\rho<1.
\]
The map $f$ is holomorphic and injective on the open disk $\rho\Delta$, so it is a homeomorphism there onto its image. In particular, its restriction to the compact circle
\[
rS^1
\]
is continuous and injective. Therefore
\[
\Gamma_r:=f(rS^1)
\]
is a Jordan curve.

The restriction of $f$ to the closed disk $r\overline\Delta$ is a homeomorphism onto its image, so
\[
\partial f(r\Delta)=f(rS^1)=\Gamma_r.
\]
Since $f(r\Delta)$ is bounded and connected, it is the bounded complementary component of $\Gamma_r$. Hence $f(r\Delta)$ is a Jordan domain. Multiplication by $1/r$ preserves Jordan domains, and therefore
\[
f_r(\Delta)=\frac1r f(r\Delta)
\]
is a Jordan domain as well.
:::

<1>3. One has $f_r\to f$ uniformly on every compact subset of $\Delta$ as $r\to1^-$.
::: {.proof}
Let $K\Subset\Delta$. Choose $R<1$ such that
\[
K\subset\{z:|z|\le R\}.
\]
For $r$ sufficiently close to $1$, both $z$ and $rz$ lie in a fixed compact disk contained in $\Delta$. Since $f$ is uniformly continuous there,
\[
\sup_{z\in K}|f(rz)-f(z)|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Also $1/r\to1$, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{z\in K}|f_r(z)-f(z)|
&\le \frac1r\sup_{z\in K}|f(rz)-f(z)|
 +\left|\frac1r-1\right|\sup_{z\in K}|f(z)|\\
&\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $f_r\to f$ locally uniformly.
:::
