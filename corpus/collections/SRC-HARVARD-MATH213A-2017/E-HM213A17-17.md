::: {.problem}
Prove that the distributional derivative of $f(z)=1/z$ satisfies
\[
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline z}=C\delta_0,
\]
and evaluate the constant $C$.
Equivalently, show that
\[
-\int_{\mathbb C} f(z)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}\,|dz|^2=C\phi(0)
\]
for every $\phi\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb C)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The constant is
\[
\boxed{C=\pi}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline z}\left(\frac1z\right)
=\pi\delta_0
\]
in the sense of distributions.

<1>1. Away from the origin, the function $1/z$ has vanishing
$\overline z$-derivative.
::: {.proof}
On $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ the function $z\mapsto1/z$ is holomorphic.
Hence
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline z}\left(\frac1z\right)=0
\]
pointwise there. Thus any distributional contribution is supported at the
origin.
:::

<1>2. Let $\phi\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb C)$. For sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$,
\[
2i\int_{\mathbb C\setminus D(0,\varepsilon)}
\frac1z\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}\,|dz|^2
=-\int_{|z|=\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(z)}z\,dz,
\]
where the circle on the right is oriented counterclockwise.
::: {.proof}
Choose $R>0$ so large that $\phi$ vanishes near $|z|=R$, and set
\[
A_{\varepsilon,R}=\{z:\varepsilon<|z|<R\}.
\]
On this annulus,
\[
d\left(\frac{\phi(z)}z\,dz\right)
=\frac1z\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}
\,d\overline z\wedge dz.
\]
Since
\[
d\overline z\wedge dz=2i\,dx\wedge dy
=2i\,|dz|^2,
\]
Stokes' theorem gives
\[
2i\int_{A_{\varepsilon,R}}
\frac1z\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}\,|dz|^2
=\int_{\partial A_{\varepsilon,R}}\frac{\phi(z)}z\,dz.
\]
The outer boundary integral is zero because $\phi$ vanishes there. The inner
boundary of the annulus has clockwise orientation, hence its contribution is
the negative of the counterclockwise circle integral. This proves the stated
identity.
:::

<1>3. As $\varepsilon\to0$,
\[
\int_{|z|=\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(z)}z\,dz
\longrightarrow2\pi i\,\phi(0).
\]
::: {.proof}
Parametrize the circle by $z=\varepsilon e^{it}$, $0\le t\le2\pi$. Then
\[
\frac{dz}{z}=i\,dt,
\]
so
\[
\int_{|z|=\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(z)}z\,dz
=i\int_0^{2\pi}\phi(\varepsilon e^{it})\,dt.
\]
Smoothness of $\phi$ implies uniform convergence
\[
\phi(\varepsilon e^{it})\longrightarrow\phi(0)
\]
as $\varepsilon\to0$, and therefore the integral tends to
$2\pi i\phi(0)$.
:::

<1>4. The punctured-disk integral converges to the full distributional
pairing:
\[
\int_{\mathbb C\setminus D(0,\varepsilon)}
\frac1z\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}\,|dz|^2
\longrightarrow
\int_{\mathbb C}
\frac1z\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}\,|dz|^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
The function $1/z$ is locally integrable on $\mathbb C$: in polar
coordinates,
\[
\int_{|z|<\varepsilon}\frac1{|z|}\,|dz|^2
=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\varepsilon\frac1r\,r\,dr\,d\theta
=2\pi\varepsilon.
\]
Since $\partial\phi/\partial\overline z$ is bounded, the contribution from
$|z|<\varepsilon$ tends to zero.
:::

<1>5. Hence
\[
-\int_{\mathbb C}\frac1z
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}\,|dz|^2
=\pi\phi(0).
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>2--<1>4 and let $\varepsilon\to0$. From <1>2 and <1>3,
\[
2i\int_{\mathbb C}\frac1z
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}\,|dz|^2
=-2\pi i\phi(0).
\]
Dividing by $2i$ yields
\[
\int_{\mathbb C}\frac1z
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\overline z}\,|dz|^2
=-\pi\phi(0),
\]
which is exactly
\[
\left\langle
\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline z}\left(\frac1z\right),\phi
\right\rangle
=\pi\phi(0).
\]
Therefore $C=\pi$.
:::
