::: {.problem}
For a locally univalent holomorphic map $f$, define its nonlinearity by
\[
N_f=\frac{f''}{f'}.
\]
Prove the composition law
\[
N_{f\circ g}
=N_g+(N_f\circ g)g'.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
By the ordinary chain rule,
\[
(f\circ g)'=(f'\circ g)g'.
\]
Differentiating once more gives
\[
(f\circ g)''
=(f''\circ g)(g')^2+(f'\circ g)g''.
\]
Because $f$ and $g$ are locally univalent, the relevant first derivatives do not vanish, so division by $(f\circ g)'$ is legitimate. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{f\circ g}
&=\frac{(f\circ g)''}{(f\circ g)'}\\
&=\frac{(f''\circ g)(g')^2+(f'\circ g)g''}
        {(f'\circ g)g'}\\
&=\left(\frac{f''}{f'}\circ g\right)g'
  +\frac{g''}{g'}\\
&=(N_f\circ g)g'+N_g.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is exactly the asserted cocycle law. In differential-form notation it reads
\[
N(f\circ g)=N(g)+g^*N(f).
\]
:::
