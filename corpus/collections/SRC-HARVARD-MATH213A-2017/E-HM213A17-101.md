::: {.problem}
Let $U\subset\mathbb C$ be a connected open set and let $p\in U$.
Let $\mathcal F$ be the family of all univalent maps
\[
f:U\to\mathbb C
\]
such that
\[
f(p)=0,
\qquad
f'(p)=1.
\]
Show that $\mathcal F$ is compact in the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of $U$.
:::

::: {.solution}
We first prove local boundedness uniformly over $\mathcal F$ by propagating
the normalization at $p$ along finite chains of disks. Montel then gives
normality, and Hurwitz gives closedness.

<1>1. Let $D(q,R)\Subset U$. For $f\in\mathcal F$, define
\[
F_{f,q,R}(\zeta)
=\frac{f(q+R\zeta)-f(q)}{R f'(q)}.
\]
Then $F_{f,q,R}\in S$, the normalized schlicht class on $\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
Because $f$ is univalent on $U$, its derivative never vanishes, so the
denominator is nonzero. The map $F_{f,q,R}$ is univalent on $\Delta$ and
satisfies
\[
F_{f,q,R}(0)=0,
\qquad
F_{f,q,R}'(0)=1.
\]
Thus it belongs to the class $S$ used in the source.
:::

<1>2. Fix $0<r<1$. There is a constant $C_r$, independent of
$f,q,R$, such that for $|\zeta|\le r$,
\[
|F_{f,q,R}(\zeta)|\le C_r,
\qquad
|F_{f,q,R}'(\zeta)|\le C_r.
\]
::: {.proof}
McMullen's distortion theorem for the schlicht class gives, for
$|\zeta|=s<1$,
\[
|F(\zeta)|\le\frac{s}{(1-s)^2}
\]
and
\[
|F'(\zeta)|\le\frac{1+s}{(1-s)^3}.
\]
Taking the maximum of these expressions for $0\le s\le r$ gives a constant
$C_r$ depending only on $r$.
:::

<1>3. Suppose $D(q,R)\Subset U$ and $q'$ satisfies
\[
|q'-q|\le R/2.
\]
Uniform upper bounds for $|f(q)|$ and $|f'(q)|$ over $f\in\mathcal F$
imply uniform upper bounds for $|f(q')|$ and $|f'(q')|$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
q'=q+R\zeta,
\qquad |\zeta|\le1/2.
\]
By <1>1,
\[
f(q')-f(q)
=R f'(q)F_{f,q,R}(\zeta),
\]
so <1>2 with $r=1/2$ gives
\[
|f(q')|
\le |f(q)|+R|f'(q)|C_{1/2}.
\]
Similarly,
\[
f'(q')
=f'(q)F_{f,q,R}'(\zeta),
\]
and hence
\[
|f'(q')|\le |f'(q)|C_{1/2}.
\]
Thus bounds at $q$ propagate to $q'$ by constants depending only on the
chosen disk.
:::

<1>4. For every compact set $K\Subset U$, the family $\mathcal F$ is
uniformly bounded on $K$.
::: {.proof}
At the basepoint,
\[
f(p)=0,
\qquad
f'(p)=1
\]
for every $f\in\mathcal F$, so uniform bounds are available there.

Because $U$ is connected and open, it is path connected. For any
$q\in U$, choose a compact path from $p$ to $q$. Cover that path by finitely
many disks compactly contained in $U$, chosen successively so that the center
of the next disk lies in the half-radius subdisk of the preceding one. Repeated
application of <1>3 propagates uniform bounds for $f$ and $f'$ from $p$ to a
neighborhood of $q$.

Doing this for each point of the compact set $K$ and then taking a finite
subcover gives a constant $C_K$ such that
\[
|f(z)|\le C_K
\qquad(z\in K,\ f\in\mathcal F).
\]
:::

<1>5. Every sequence in $\mathcal F$ has a subsequence converging uniformly
on compact subsets of $U$ to a holomorphic function.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, $\mathcal F$ is locally uniformly bounded. Montel's theorem therefore
makes $\mathcal F$ a normal family. Hence every sequence has a subsequence
converging uniformly on compact subsets to a holomorphic function
$f:U\to\mathbb C$.
:::

<1>6. Any locally uniform limit of a sequence in $\mathcal F$ again belongs
to $\mathcal F$.
::: {.proof}
Let $f_n\in\mathcal F$ and suppose $f_n\to f$ locally uniformly. Then
derivatives also converge locally uniformly, so
\[
f(p)=0,
\qquad
f'(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n'(p)=1.
\]
Thus $f$ is nonconstant.

Hurwitz's theorem for univalent functions says that a locally uniform limit of
univalent holomorphic functions is either univalent or constant. Since $f$ is
not constant, it is univalent. Therefore
\[
f\in\mathcal F.
\]
:::

<1>7. Therefore $\mathcal F$ is compact in the compact-open topology.
::: {.proof}
By <1>5 every sequence has a locally uniformly convergent subsequence, and by
<1>6 every such limit remains in $\mathcal F$. The compact-open topology on
holomorphic functions over the second-countable domain $U$ is metrizable, so
sequential compactness is equivalent to compactness. Hence $\mathcal F$ is
compact.
:::
