::: {.problem}
Let $\sigma$ be the canonical Weierstrass sigma function of a lattice.
Prove that
\[
\wp'(z)=-\frac{\sigma(2z)}{\sigma(z)^4}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
G(z)=-\frac{\sigma(2z)}{\sigma(z)^4}.
\]
We prove that $G$ and $\wp'$ are the same elliptic function.

<1>1. The function $G$ is $\Lambda$-periodic.
::: {.proof}
For a basis period $\lambda_i$, the preceding exercise gives
\[
\sigma(z+\lambda_i)
=-e^{\eta_i(z+\lambda_i/2)}\sigma(z).
\]
Applying this identity twice, with an arbitrary variable $w$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma(w+2\lambda_i)
&=-e^{\eta_i(w+3\lambda_i/2)}\sigma(w+\lambda_i)\\
&=e^{2\eta_i(w+\lambda_i)}\sigma(w).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking $w=2z$ yields
\[
\sigma(2z+2\lambda_i)
=e^{4\eta_i z+2\eta_i\lambda_i}\sigma(2z).
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\sigma(z+\lambda_i)^4
=e^{4\eta_i z+2\eta_i\lambda_i}\sigma(z)^4.
\]
The factors cancel, so
\[
G(z+\lambda_i)=G(z)
\qquad(i=1,2).
\]
Hence $G$ is elliptic for $\Lambda$.
:::

<1>2. Modulo $\Lambda$, $G$ has a triple pole at $0$ and simple zeros at the three nonzero points of order two.
::: {.proof}
The sigma function has simple zeros precisely at the lattice points.

At $z=0$, both $\sigma(2z)$ and $\sigma(z)$ vanish simply. Thus the quotient
\[
\frac{\sigma(2z)}{\sigma(z)^4}
\]
has pole order $4-1=3$.

If $c\notin\Lambda$ and $2c\in\Lambda$, then $\sigma(2c)=0$ while $\sigma(c)\ne0$. Since the zero of $\sigma$ at $2c$ is simple and multiplication by $2$ is locally biholomorphic, $\sigma(2z)$ has a simple zero at $c$. Thus $G$ has a simple zero at each of the three nonzero classes in
\[
E[2]=\tfrac12\Lambda/\Lambda.
\]
There are no other zeros or poles modulo $\Lambda$.
:::

<1>3. The elliptic function $\wp'$ has the same divisor.
::: {.proof}
Near a lattice point, periodicity reduces to the expansion at $0$:
\[
\wp(z)=z^{-2}+O(z^2),
\qquad
\wp'(z)=-2z^{-3}+O(z).
\]
Thus $\wp'$ has a triple pole at $0$ modulo $\Lambda$. McMullen's description of the critical points of $\wp$ shows that the zeros of $\wp'$ are exactly the three nonzero points of order two, each simple. Hence $G$ and $\wp'$ have identical zeros and poles with identical multiplicities.
:::

<1>4. Their quotient is constant, and the normalized principal term makes that constant $1$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1--<1>3,
\[
H(z)=\frac{G(z)}{\wp'(z)}
\]
is an elliptic function with no zeros or poles. It therefore descends to a holomorphic function on the compact torus $\mathbb C/\Lambda$ and is constant.

From the canonical product,
\[
\sigma(z)=z+o(z)
\qquad(z\to0).
\]
Consequently
\[
G(z)
=-\frac{2z+o(z)}{(z+o(z))^4}
\sim -\frac2{z^3}.
\]
Since also
\[
\wp'(z)\sim-\frac2{z^3},
\]
we have
\[
\lim_{z\to0}\frac{G(z)}{\wp'(z)}=1.
\]
Thus $H\equiv1$, and therefore
\[
\boxed{\wp'(z)=-\frac{\sigma(2z)}{\sigma(z)^4}.}
\]
:::
