::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be an entire function of finite order with simple zeros exactly at
\[
z=n+im,
\qquad (n,m)\in\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Show that there are polynomials $P,Q$ such that
\[
f(z+1)=e^{P(z)}f(z),
\qquad
f(z+i)=e^{Q(z)}f(z).
\]
Prove that at least one of $P$ and $Q$ is nonzero.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z.
\]

<1>1. The translated functions $f(z+1)$ and $f(z+i)$ are entire of finite
order.
::: {.proof}
They are entire because $f$ is entire. If $M_f(r)$ denotes the maximum
modulus of $f$, then
\[
M_{f(\cdot+1)}(r)\le M_f(r+1),
\qquad
M_{f(\cdot+i)}(r)\le M_f(r+1).
\]
Thus a finite-order growth bound for $f$ gives the same finite order for each
translate.
:::

<1>2. The functions $f(z+1)$ and $f(z)$ have exactly the same zeros, with
the same multiplicities.
::: {.proof}
The zeros of $f(z+1)$ occur when
\[
z+1\in\Lambda,
\]
equivalently when $z\in\Lambda-1=\Lambda$. All these zeros are simple by
hypothesis, just as for $f$.
:::

<1>3. There is a polynomial $P$ such that
\[
f(z+1)=e^{P(z)}f(z).
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1--<1>2, the two functions are nonzero entire functions of finite
order with the same zero divisor. Apply Hadamard's factorization theorem to
both. Their canonical-product factors, including all zero multiplicities,
are the same; only the polynomial exponential factors can differ. Hence
their quotient is
\[
\frac{f(z+1)}{f(z)}=e^{P(z)}
\]
for some polynomial $P$. The quotient extends holomorphically and without
zeros across the common zero set, so the identity holds everywhere.
:::

<1>4. There is a polynomial $Q$ such that
\[
f(z+i)=e^{Q(z)}f(z).
\]
::: {.proof}
Translation by $i$ also preserves the lattice:
\[
\Lambda-i=\Lambda.
\]
Thus $f(z+i)$ and $f(z)$ have the same simple zeros. Repeat the Hadamard
factorization argument from <1>3.
:::

<1>5. The polynomials $P$ and $Q$ cannot both be zero.
::: {.proof}
If $P=Q=0$, then <1>3--<1>4 give
\[
f(z+1)=f(z),
\qquad
f(z+i)=f(z).
\]
Hence $f$ is doubly periodic with periods $1$ and $i$. It is bounded on the
compact fundamental square
\[
\{x+iy:0\le x\le1,\ 0\le y\le1\},
\]
and periodicity then makes it bounded on all of $\mathbb C$. Liouville's
theorem forces $f$ to be constant. This is impossible because $f$ has the
nonempty simple zero set $\Lambda$. Therefore at least one of $P,Q$ is
nonzero.
:::
:::
