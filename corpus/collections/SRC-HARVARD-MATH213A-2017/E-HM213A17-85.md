::: {.problem}
Show that the entire function
\[
\frac1{\Gamma(z)}
\]
has order $1$, but that there is no constant $C>0$ such that
\[
\frac1{\Gamma(z)}=O(e^{C|z|}).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The function $1/\Gamma$ has order exactly $1$, but it is not of finite
exponential type.

<1>1. The source's product formula writes $1/\Gamma$ as a genus-one
canonical product times an exponential factor.
::: {.proof}
McMullen gives
\[
\frac1{\Gamma(z)}
=ze^{\gamma z}
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(1+\frac zn\right)e^{-z/n}.
\]
The infinite product is the canonical product of genus one associated to the
zero sequence
\[
-1,-2,-3,\ldots.
\]
Its critical exponent is $1$: indeed
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^{1+\varepsilon}}<\infty
\qquad(\varepsilon>0),
\]
whereas the harmonic series diverges.
:::

<1>2. The order of $1/\Gamma$ is at most $1$.
::: {.proof}
By McMullen's Theorem 3.13, the canonical product in <1>1 has order equal to
the critical exponent of its zero sequence, namely $1$.

The factor $e^{\gamma z}$ also has order $1$, while multiplication by the
polynomial $z$ does not increase order. Since the order of a product of
finite-order entire functions is at most the maximum of their orders,
\[
\rho(1/\Gamma)\le1.
\]
:::

<1>3. The order of $1/\Gamma$ is at least $1$.
::: {.proof}
The entire function $1/\Gamma$ has zeros at
\[
0,-1,-2,-3,\ldots,
\]
all simple. The nonzero zeros therefore have critical exponent $1$.
McMullen's Corollary 3.12 says that the critical exponent of the zero set of
an entire function is at most its order. Hence
\[
1\le\rho(1/\Gamma).
\]
Together with <1>2 this gives
\[
\boxed{\rho(1/\Gamma)=1}.
\]
:::

<1>4. Along the negative half-integers, $1/\Gamma$ grows like a Gamma
value on the positive real axis.
::: {.proof}
Euler's reflection formula in the source is
\[
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)}.
\]
For
\[
z_n=\frac12-n,
\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots,
\]
we have $|\sin(\pi z_n)|=1$, so
\[
\left|\frac1{\Gamma(z_n)}\right|
=\frac{\Gamma(n+1/2)}{\pi}.
\]
:::

<1>5. No estimate $1/\Gamma(z)=O(e^{C|z|})$ can hold for any fixed
$C>0$.
::: {.proof}
Stirling's formula gives, for real $s\to+\infty$,
\[
\Gamma(s)
\sim
\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s}}
\left(\frac{s}{e}\right)^s.
\]
Taking $s=n+1/2$ and logarithms,
\[
\log\Gamma(n+1/2)
=(n+1/2)\log(n+1/2)-(n+1/2)+O(\log n).
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{\log\Gamma(n+1/2)}{n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Since $|z_n|=n-1/2$, for every fixed $C>0$ we have, for all sufficiently
large $n$,
\[
\log\left|\frac1{\Gamma(z_n)}\right|
>C|z_n|.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{|1/\Gamma(z_n)|}{e^{C|z_n|}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus no global bound of the form
\[
\frac1{\Gamma(z)}=O(e^{C|z|})
\]
is possible.
:::
