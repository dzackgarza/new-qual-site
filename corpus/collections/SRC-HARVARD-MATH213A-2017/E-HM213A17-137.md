::: {.problem}
Let $T\subset\mathbb C$ be the triangle with vertices
\[
0,\qquad 1,\qquad 1+i.
\]
Give an explicit formula for a conformal map
\[
f:T\to\mathbb H
\]
in terms of the Weierstrass $\wp$-function for a suitable lattice.
:::

::: {.solution}
Take the square lattice
\[
\Lambda=2\mathbb Z\oplus 2i\mathbb Z
\]
and write $\wp=\wp_\Lambda$. Then an explicit conformal map is
\[
\boxed{f(z)=-\wp(z)^2.}
\]

<1>1. The square-lattice Weierstrass function satisfies
\[
\wp(iz)=-\wp(z),
\qquad
\wp(\overline z)=\overline{\wp(z)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Multiplication by $i$ permutes $\Lambda$. Reindexing the defining series of the Weierstrass function therefore gives
\[
\wp(iz)=i^{-2}\wp(z)=-\wp(z).
\]
Likewise, complex conjugation permutes $\Lambda$, and conjugating the defining series term by term gives
\[
\wp(\overline z)=\overline{\wp(z)}.
\]
We will also use the usual identities
\[
\wp(z+\lambda)=\wp(z)
\quad(\lambda\in\Lambda),
\qquad
\wp(-z)=\wp(z).
\]
:::

<1>2. The three sides of $T$ are carried by $f$ to the extended real axis.
::: {.proof}
On the horizontal side $z=x$, $0<x<1$, <1>1 gives
\[
\wp(x)=\overline{\wp(x)},
\]
so $\wp(x)$ is real.

On the vertical side write $z=1+iy$, $0<y<1$. Then
\[
\overline z=1-iy=2-z.
\]
Since $2\in\Lambda$, periodicity and evenness give
\[
\wp(\overline z)=\wp(2-z)=\wp(-z)=\wp(z).
\]
Together with <1>1, this again says that $\wp(z)$ is real.

Finally, on the diagonal side write $z=t(1+i)$, $0<t<1$. Then
\[
\overline z=-iz,
\]
and hence
\[
\overline{\wp(z)}
=\wp(\overline z)
=\wp(-iz)
=\wp(iz)
=-\wp(z).
\]
Thus $\wp(z)$ is purely imaginary there.

Consequently $-\wp(z)^2$ is real on every open side. At $z=0$ it tends to $\infty$. At the opposite vertex,
\[
i(1+i)=-1+i=(1+i)-2,
\]
so periodicity and <1>1 give
\[
\wp(1+i)=\wp(i(1+i))=-\wp(1+i),
\]
and therefore $\wp(1+i)=0$.
:::

<1>3. The map $f$ is injective on the interior of $T$.
::: {.proof}
McMullen's quotient description of the Weierstrass function says
\[
\wp(a)=\wp(b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b\equiv \pm a\pmod\Lambda.
\]
Suppose $z,w\in T$ and $f(z)=f(w)$. Then
\[
\wp(w)=\pm\wp(z).
\]
If the sign is positive, then
\[
w\equiv\pm z\pmod\Lambda.
\]
If it is negative, <1>1 gives $-\wp(z)=\wp(iz)$, so
\[
w\equiv\pm iz\pmod\Lambda.
\]

Write
\[
z=x+iy,
\qquad
w=u+iv,
\qquad
0<y<x<1,
\quad
0<v<u<1.
\]
Because every lattice coordinate is an even integer, these inequalities rule out all four congruences except $w\equiv z$: the coordinate differences in $w-z$ lie in $(-1,1)$; both coordinates of $w+z$ lie in $(0,2)$; the real coordinate of $w-iz$ is $u+y\in(0,2)$; and the imaginary coordinate of $w+iz$ is $v+x\in(0,2)$. Hence the only possible lattice congruence is $w=z$.
:::

<1>4. The image of the interior of $T$ lies in the upper half-plane.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
f(\overline z)=\overline{f(z)}.
\]
If $f(z)$ were real at an interior point, then
\[
f(z)=f(\overline z).
\]
The same fiber argument as in <1>3 would force
\[
\overline z\equiv \pm z
\quad\text{or}\quad
\overline z\equiv\pm iz
\pmod\Lambda,
\]
which is impossible for $0<\operatorname{Im}z<\operatorname{Re}z<1$. Thus $\operatorname{Im}f$ never vanishes in $T$ and, since $T$ is connected, has constant sign.

For $z=re^{i\pi/8}$ with $r>0$ small, the Laurent expansion
\[
\wp(z)=z^{-2}+O(z^2)
\]
gives
\[
f(z)=-z^{-4}+O(1)=ir^{-4}+O(1),
\]
whose imaginary part is positive for sufficiently small $r$. Therefore
\[
f(T)\subset\mathbb H.
\]
:::

<1>5. The inclusion in <1>4 is onto.
::: {.proof}
The function $f$ is holomorphic on $T$ and injective by <1>3, so $f(T)$ is a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb H$.

By <1>2, $f$ extends continuously to every point of $\partial T\setminus\{0\}$ with real boundary value, while
\[
f(z)\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad(z\to0,\ z\in T).
\]
Hence a sequence in $T$ that leaves every compact subset of $T$ has image approaching the boundary
\[
\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}
\]
of $\mathbb H$ in the Riemann sphere. Thus $f:T\to\mathbb H$ is proper.

Its image is therefore closed in $\mathbb H$ as well as open. Since $\mathbb H$ is connected,
\[
f(T)=\mathbb H.
\]
Together with injectivity, this proves that
\[
\boxed{f(z)=-\wp_{\,2\mathbb Z\oplus2i\mathbb Z}(z)^2}
\]
is the required conformal map.
:::
