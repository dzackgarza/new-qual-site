::: {.problem}
Let
\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3},
\qquad
\Lambda=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Zi,
\qquad
L=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\omega.
\]
Prove that
\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-6}=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_{\lambda\in L\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Both sums converge absolutely, since in a rank-two lattice
\[
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}|\lambda|^{-s}<\infty
\qquad(s>2).
\]
Hence we may freely reindex them by lattice automorphisms.

<1>1. For the square lattice,
\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-6}=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Multiplication by $i$ permutes
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Zi.
\]
Thus, writing
\[
S_6=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-6},
\]
absolute convergence permits the change of variables $\lambda=i\mu$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_6
&=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}(i\mu)^{-6}\\
&=i^{-6}S_6\\
&=-S_6.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
2S_6=0,
\]
and hence $S_6=0$.
:::

<1>2. For the hexagonal lattice,
\[
\sum_{\lambda\in L\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
\omega^3=1,
\qquad
1+\omega+\omega^2=0,
\]
multiplication by $\omega$ preserves
\[
L=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\omega:
\]
indeed $\omega\cdot1=\omega$ and
\[
\omega^2=-1-\omega\in L.
\]
Set
\[
S_4=\sum_{\lambda\in L\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}.
\]
Reindexing by $\lambda=\omega\mu$ gives
\[
S_4
=\omega^{-4}S_4
=\omega^2S_4.
\]
Since $\omega^2\ne1$,
\[
(1-\omega^2)S_4=0,
\]
so $S_4=0$.
:::
:::
