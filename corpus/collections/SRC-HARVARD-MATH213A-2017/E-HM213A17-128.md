::: {.problem}
Let $\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a lattice.
Choose $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in\Lambda$ successively so that $a_1$ is a shortest nonzero vector and, for $i>1$, $a_i$ is a shortest vector linearly independent from $a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1}$.

Must one always have
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Za_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb Za_n?
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
No. A counterexample is provided by the $D_4$ lattice
\[
\Lambda
=D_4
:=\left\{(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\in\mathbb Z^4:
x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4\equiv0\pmod2\right\}.
\]

Choose
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_1&=e_1+e_2,\\
a_2&=e_1-e_2,\\
a_3&=e_3+e_4,\\
a_4&=e_3-e_4.
\end{aligned}
\]

<1>1. The shortest nonzero vectors of $D_4$ have length $\sqrt2$.
::: {.proof}
Every vector of $D_4$ has integral coordinates. A vector of Euclidean length $1$ would therefore have to be one of
\[
\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_4.
\]
Each of these has odd coordinate sum, so none lies in $D_4$. Hence every nonzero vector of $D_4$ has squared norm at least $2$.

On the other hand,
\[
e_i\pm e_j\in D_4
\qquad(i\ne j)
\]
and has squared norm $2$. Thus the lattice minimum is exactly
\[
\sqrt2.
\]
:::

<1>2. The displayed $a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4$ are valid successive choices in the problem.
::: {.proof}
Each $a_i$ has norm $\sqrt2$, so by <1>1 every $a_i$ is a shortest nonzero lattice vector. They are linearly independent: relative to the standard basis their column matrix is block diagonal,
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0&0\\
1&-1&0&0\\
0&0&1&1\\
0&0&1&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is $4\ne0$.

Consequently, after $a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1}$ have been chosen, the vector $a_i$ is linearly independent from them and has the smallest possible nonzero lattice length $\sqrt2$. Hence it is certainly a shortest lattice vector with the required independence property.
:::

<1>3. These four vectors do not generate $D_4$ as a lattice.
::: {.proof}
Their integer span is
\[
L=\mathbb Za_1+\mathbb Za_2+\mathbb Za_3+\mathbb Za_4.
\]
From
\[
m(e_1+e_2)+n(e_1-e_2)
=(m+n,m-n,0,0),
\]
the first two coordinates of every vector of $L$ have the same parity. Similarly the last two coordinates have the same parity. Thus
\[
L
=\{x\in\mathbb Z^4:
x_1\equiv x_2\pmod2,
\ x_3\equiv x_4\pmod2\}.
\]
This is a proper sublattice of $D_4$: for example,
\[
e_1+e_3=(1,0,1,0)
\]
has even coordinate sum and hence lies in $D_4$, but it does not lie in $L$.

Equivalently, the determinant of the four $a_i$ is $4$, whereas $D_4$ has index $2$ in $\mathbb Z^4$ and therefore covolume $2$. Hence
\[
[D_4:L]=\frac4{2}=2.
\]
Thus valid successive shortest independent vectors need not form a lattice basis.
:::
