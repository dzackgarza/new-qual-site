::: {.problem}
Let
\[
A=\{z\in S^1:\operatorname{Im}z\ge0\}.
\]
Find a conformal map
\[
f:\mathbb C\setminus\Delta\longrightarrow\mathbb C\setminus A.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
One such map is
\[
\boxed{
f(z)=\frac{z(z+1+i)}{1+(1-i)z}
}.
\]

<1>1. The Möbius map
\[
Q(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
\]
maps the exterior domain $E=\{z:|z|>1\}$ conformally onto the punctured right half-plane
\[
H_R\setminus\{1\},
\qquad
H_R=\{\zeta:\operatorname{Re}\zeta>0\}.
::: {.proof}
For $|z|>1$,
\[
\operatorname{Re}Q(z)
=\frac{|z|^2-1}{|z+1|^2}>0.
\]
Conversely, if $\operatorname{Re}\zeta>0$ and $\zeta\ne1$, then
\[
Q^{-1}(\zeta)=\frac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta}
\]
is finite and satisfies
\[
\left|\frac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta}\right|^2-1
=\frac{4\operatorname{Re}\zeta}{|1-\zeta|^2}>0.
\]
Thus $Q$ is a conformal bijection from $E$ onto $H_R\setminus\{1\}$; the omitted point $1$ is the image of $\infty$ on the Riemann sphere.
:::

<1>2. The Möbius map
\[
M(w)=i\frac{1-w}{1+w}
\]
maps $\mathbb C\setminus A$ conformally onto
\[
\bigl(\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\bigr)\setminus\{-i\}.
\]
::: {.proof}
For $w=e^{i\theta}$ with $0\le\theta\le\pi$,
\[
M(w)
=i\frac{1-e^{i\theta}}{1+e^{i\theta}}
=\tan\frac\theta2.
\]
Hence, on the Riemann sphere,
\[
M(A)=[0,\infty],
\]
where $w=-1$ maps to $\infty$. Also
\[
M(\infty)=-i.
\]
Since $M$ is a Möbius transformation, deleting $A$ and then deleting the source point $\infty$ gives exactly
\[
M(\mathbb C\setminus A)
=\bigl(\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\bigr)\setminus\{-i\}.
\]
:::

<1>3. The map
\[
T(\zeta)
=-\left(\frac{\zeta+i}{\sqrt2}\right)^2
\]
is a conformal bijection
\[
H_R\setminus\{1\}
\longrightarrow
\bigl(\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\bigr)\setminus\{-i\}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The affine map
\[
P(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta+i}{\sqrt2}
\]
is an automorphism of $H_R$ and sends
\[
1\longmapsto p:=\frac{1+i}{\sqrt2}.
\]
The map
\[
S(u)=-u^2
\]
is a conformal bijection from $H_R$ onto $\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)$: its inverse is the branch
\[
u=\sqrt{-t}
\]
with positive real part. Finally,
\[
S(p)=-p^2=-i.
\]
Therefore $T=S\circ P$ has the asserted restriction to the punctured domains.
:::

<1>4. The composition
\[
f=M^{-1}\circ T\circ Q
\]
is therefore a conformal bijection from $\mathbb C\setminus\Delta$ onto $\mathbb C\setminus A$, and it simplifies to the displayed rational function.
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
M^{-1}(t)=\frac{i-t}{i+t},
\]
we have
\[
f(z)
=\frac{i+\frac12(Q(z)+i)^2}{i-\frac12(Q(z)+i)^2}.
\]
Now
\[
Q(z)+i
=\frac{(1+i)(z+i)}{z+1},
\]
so
\[
\frac12(Q(z)+i)^2
=i\frac{(z+i)^2}{(z+1)^2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(z)
&=\frac{(z+1)^2+(z+i)^2}{(z+1)^2-(z+i)^2}\\
&=\frac{z(z+1+i)}{1+(1-i)z}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Steps <1>1--<1>3 show that every factor in the composition is conformal and bijective on the indicated domain, so this $f$ has precisely the required source and target.
:::
