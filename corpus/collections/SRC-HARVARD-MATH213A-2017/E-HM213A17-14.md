::: {.problem}
When is a polynomial $p(x,y)$ expressible in the form
\[
p(x,y)=f(x+iy)+g(x-iy),
\]
where $f$ and $g$ are holomorphic?
:::

::: {.solution}
Such a representation exists exactly when $p$ is harmonic:
\[
\boxed{\Delta p=p_{xx}+p_{yy}=0.}
\]
Equivalently, after rewriting $p$ uniquely as a polynomial in
$z=x+iy$ and $\overline z=x-iy$, no monomial
$z^j\overline z^{\,k}$ with $j,k>0$ may occur.

<1>1. If
\[
p(x,y)=f(z)+g(\overline z),
\]
with $f$ and $g$ holomorphic, then $p$ is harmonic.
::: {.proof}
Using Wirtinger derivatives,
\[
\partial_{\overline z}f(z)=0,
\qquad
\partial_z g(\overline z)=0.
\]
Hence
\[
\partial_z\partial_{\overline z}p=0.
\]
Since
\[
\Delta=\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2
=4\partial_z\partial_{\overline z},
\]
we obtain $\Delta p=0$.
:::

<1>2. Rewrite $p$ uniquely as
\[
p(x,y)=P(z,\overline z)
=\sum_{j,k\ge0}c_{jk}z^j\overline z^{\,k}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The linear change of variables
\[
z=x+iy,
\qquad
\overline z=x-iy
\]
is invertible, with
\[
x=\frac{z+\overline z}{2},
\qquad
y=\frac{z-\overline z}{2i}.
\]
Therefore substitution gives an isomorphism
\[
\mathbb C[x,y]\cong\mathbb C[z,\overline z]
\]
of polynomial rings, and the displayed expansion is unique.
:::

<1>3. If $p$ is harmonic, then $c_{jk}=0$ whenever $j,k>0$.
::: {.proof}
By harmonicity and <1>1's identity for the Laplacian,
\[
0=\partial_z\partial_{\overline z}P
=\sum_{j,k\ge1}jk\,c_{jk}
z^{j-1}\overline z^{\,k-1}.
\]
Distinct monomials in the polynomial ring
$\mathbb C[z,\overline z]$ are linearly independent, so
\[
jk\,c_{jk}=0
\]
for every $j,k\ge1$. Since $jk\neq0$, all such $c_{jk}$ vanish.
:::

<1>4. Every harmonic polynomial therefore has the required form.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3,
\[
P(z,\overline z)
=c_{00}
+\sum_{j\ge1}c_{j0}z^j
+\sum_{k\ge1}c_{0k}\overline z^{\,k}.
\]
Define the one-variable polynomials
\[
f(w)=c_{00}+\sum_{j\ge1}c_{j0}w^j,
\qquad
g(w)=\sum_{k\ge1}c_{0k}w^k.
\]
They are entire, and
\[
p(x,y)=f(x+iy)+g(x-iy).
\]
Together with <1>1, this proves the equivalence.
:::
:::
