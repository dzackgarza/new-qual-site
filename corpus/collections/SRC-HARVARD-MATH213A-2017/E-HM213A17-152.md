::: {.problem}
Relate the square-lattice identity
\[
60\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}
=
64\pi^2\frac{\Gamma(5/4)^4}{\Gamma(3/4)^4}
\]
to the conformal radius of the square with side length $2$, namely
\[
\frac{4\Gamma(3/4)^2}{\pi^{3/2}}
=1.078705\ldots.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
g_2
=60\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}
\]
and let $r$ denote the conformal radius of the square with side length $2$.

<1>1. The square-lattice identity can be written as
\[
g_2=\frac{\pi^6}{\Gamma(3/4)^8}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Euler's reflection formula and the gamma recurrence give
\[
\Gamma(1/4)\Gamma(3/4)=\pi\sqrt2
\]
and
\[
\Gamma(5/4)
=\frac14\Gamma(1/4)
=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{4\Gamma(3/4)}.
\]
Substituting this into the square-lattice identity yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_2
&=64\pi^2
\frac{\Gamma(5/4)^4}{\Gamma(3/4)^4}\\
&=64\pi^2
\frac{\pi^4}{64\Gamma(3/4)^8}\\
&=\frac{\pi^6}{\Gamma(3/4)^8}.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>2. The conformal radius satisfies
\[
r^4=\frac{256\Gamma(3/4)^8}{\pi^6}.
\]
::: {.proof}
From the stated formula
\[
r=\frac{4\Gamma(3/4)^2}{\pi^{3/2}},
\]
raising both sides to the fourth power gives
\[
r^4
=\frac{4^4\Gamma(3/4)^8}{\pi^6}
=\frac{256\Gamma(3/4)^8}{\pi^6}.
\]
:::

<1>3. The two identities are related by
\[
\boxed{
g_2=\left(\frac4r\right)^4,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
r=4g_2^{-1/4}.
}
\]
::: {.proof}
Multiplying the identities in <1>1 and <1>2 gives
\[
g_2r^4=256=4^4.
\]
Since $g_2>0$ and $r>0$, taking the positive fourth root gives the stated relation.
:::

<1>4. The relation in <1>3 is also the direct link between the two Schwarz--Christoffel integrals.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
I=\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^3-x}}.
\]
The square-lattice period calculation gives
\[
g_2=4I^4.
\]
Hence
\[
4g_2^{-1/4}=\frac{2\sqrt2}{I}.
\]
Using
\[
I
=\frac12B\left(\frac14,\frac12\right)
=\frac{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(1/4)}{2\Gamma(3/4)}
\]
and Euler's reflection formula gives
\[
\frac{2\sqrt2}{I}
=\frac{4\Gamma(3/4)^2}{\pi^{3/2}}
=r.
\]
Thus the elliptic period determining the square-lattice Eisenstein invariant is the reciprocal-scaled Schwarz--Christoffel integral determining the conformal radius.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 give the exact algebraic relation, and step <1>4 identifies the common period integral underlying the two formulas.
:::
