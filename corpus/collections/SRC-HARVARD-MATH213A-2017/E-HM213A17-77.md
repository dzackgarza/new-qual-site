::: {.problem}
Express
\[
f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac1{(z-n)^4}
\]
in terms of trigonometric functions, and use the result to evaluate
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^4}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Recall the partial-fraction identity
\[
S_2(z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{(z-n)^2}
=\pi^2\csc^2(\pi z).
\]

::: pf-proof

This is the Mittag--Leffler expansion of $\pi^2\csc^2(\pi z)$. Both sides are meromorphic with double poles at the
integers and the same principal parts.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

On compact subsets of $\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z$, the series for
$S_2$ may be differentiated twice term by term.

::: pf-proof

If a compact set $K$ stays a positive distance from $\mathbb Z$, then for
large $|n|$ and $z\in K$ one has $|z-n|\ge |n|/2$. The differentiated terms
are bounded by constant multiples of $|n|^{-3}$ and $|n|^{-4}$,
respectively. These majorants are summable, so the differentiated series
converge uniformly on $K$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Therefore
\[
6f(z)=\frac{d^2}{dz^2}\bigl(\pi^2\csc^2(\pi z)\bigr).
\]

::: pf-proof

For each summand,
\[
\frac{d^2}{dz^2}(z-n)^{-2}=6(z-n)^{-4}.
\]
Now apply step [](#s2){.pf-ref} to the identity in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One obtains the trigonometric formula
\[
\boxed{
f(z)
=\pi^4\csc^4(\pi z)
-\frac{2\pi^4}{3}\csc^2(\pi z).}
\]

::: pf-proof

Put $u=\pi z$. Since
\[
\frac{d}{du}\csc^2u=-2\csc^2u\cot u,
\]
one more derivative gives
\[
\frac{d^2}{du^2}\csc^2u
=4\csc^2u\cot^2u+2\csc^4u.
\]
Thus step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives
\[
f(z)
=\frac{\pi^4}{6}
\bigl(4\csc^2u\cot^2u+2\csc^4u\bigr).
\]
Using $\cot^2u=\csc^2u-1$ reduces this to the displayed expression.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The Laurent expansion at $z=0$ has constant term
\[
2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^4}
\]
on the series side.

::: pf-proof

Separate the $n=0$ term:
\[
f(z)=\frac1{z^4}
+\sum_{n\ne0}\frac1{(z-n)^4}.
\]
The second sum is holomorphic near $0$, and its value at $0$ is
\[
\sum_{n\ne0}\frac1{n^4}
=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^4}.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The constant Laurent term of the trigonometric expression in step [](#s4){.pf-ref}
is $\pi^4/45$.

::: pf-proof

The standard expansion at the origin is
\[
\csc^2(\pi z)
=\frac1{\pi^2z^2}
+\frac13
+\frac{\pi^2z^2}{15}
+O(z^4).
\]
Squaring gives
\[
\csc^4(\pi z)
=\frac1{\pi^4z^4}
+\frac{2}{3\pi^2z^2}
+\frac{11}{45}
+O(z^2).
\]
Substitution into step [](#s4){.pf-ref} yields
\[
f(z)
=\frac1{z^4}
+\left(\frac{11}{45}-\frac{2}{9}\right)\pi^4
+O(z^2)
=\frac1{z^4}+\frac{\pi^4}{45}+O(z^2).
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{90}.}
\]

::: pf-proof

Equating the constant terms from steps [](#s5){.pf-ref} and [](#s6){.pf-ref} gives
\[
2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{45}.
\]
Dividing by $2$ proves the formula.

:::
