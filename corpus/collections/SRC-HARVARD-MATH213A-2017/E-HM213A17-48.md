::: {.problem}
Find the zeros, critical points, and critical values of the Chebyshev polynomial $T_n(z)$ for $n\ge1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Recall from the source that
\[
T_n(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta).
\]

<1>1. The zeros of $T_n$ are
\[
\boxed{
\cos\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2n},
\qquad k=1,\ldots,n.}
\]
::: {.proof}
For
\[
\theta_k=\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2n},
\]
we have
\[
T_n(\cos\theta_k)
=\cos(n\theta_k)
=\cos\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2}
=0.
\]
The numbers $\theta_k$ lie strictly between $0$ and $\pi$ and are distinct,
so their cosines are distinct. Since $T_n$ has degree $n$, these are all of
its zeros.
:::

<1>2. For $0<\theta<\pi$,
\[
T_n'(\cos\theta)
=n\frac{\sin(n\theta)}{\sin\theta}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Differentiate
\[
T_n(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)
\]
with respect to $\theta$. This gives
\[
-\sin\theta\,T_n'(\cos\theta)
=-n\sin(n\theta).
\]
Since $\sin\theta\neq0$ on $(0,\pi)$, division by $\sin\theta$ yields the
formula.
:::

<1>3. For $n\ge2$, the critical points are
\[
\boxed{
\cos\frac{k\pi}{n},
\qquad k=1,\ldots,n-1.}
\]
For $n=1$ there are no critical points.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, if
\[
\theta_k=\frac{k\pi}{n},
\qquad 1\le k\le n-1,
\]
then
\[
T_n'(\cos\theta_k)
=n\frac{\sin(k\pi)}{\sin(k\pi/n)}
=0.
\]
These $n-1$ points are distinct because cosine is strictly decreasing on
$[0,\pi]$. The derivative $T_n'$ has degree $n-1$, so these are all of its
zeros. When $n=1$, $T_1(z)=z$ and $T_1'(z)=1$, so there are none.
:::

<1>4. At the critical point $\cos(k\pi/n)$,
\[
T_n\left(\cos\frac{k\pi}{n}\right)=(-1)^k.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using the defining cosine identity,
\[
T_n\left(\cos\frac{k\pi}{n}\right)
=\cos(k\pi)
=(-1)^k.
\]
:::

<1>5. Hence the critical values are
\[
\boxed{\{-1,1\}}
\]
for $n\ge3$, while for $n=2$ the only critical value is $-1$, and for
$n=1$ there are no critical values.
::: {.proof}
For $n\ge3$, the indices $k=1,2$ already produce the two values $-1$ and
$1$, and <1>4 shows that no other value occurs. For $n=2$, only $k=1$ is
available, giving $-1$. The case $n=1$ follows from <1>3.
:::
:::
