::: {.problem}
(a) Verify that the spherical metric
$$
\rho=\frac{2\abs{dz}}{1+\abs{z}^2}
$$
is invariant under
$$
h(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}.
$$

(b) Show that the rotations
$$
g_\theta(z)=e^{i\theta}z
$$
together with $h$ generate the full group
$$
SU(2)/(\pm I)\subset PSL_2(\CC).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

For
$$
h(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
$$
one has
$$
h'(z)=\frac{2}{(z+1)^2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Differentiate the quotient:
$$
h'(z)
=\frac{(z+1)-(z-1)}{(z+1)^2}
=\frac{2}{(z+1)^2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The spherical metric is invariant under $h$.

::: pf-proof

First compute
$$
1+\abs{h(z)}^2
=1+\frac{\abs{z-1}^2}{\abs{z+1}^2}
=\frac{\abs{z+1}^2+\abs{z-1}^2}{\abs{z+1}^2}.
$$
Since
$$
\abs{z+1}^2+\abs{z-1}^2=2(1+\abs{z}^2),
$$
we obtain
$$
1+\abs{h(z)}^2
=\frac{2(1+\abs{z}^2)}{\abs{z+1}^2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{2\abs{h'(z)}\,\abs{dz}}{1+\abs{h(z)}^2}
=
\frac{2\cdot 2\abs{dz}/\abs{z+1}^2}
{2(1+\abs{z}^2)/\abs{z+1}^2}
=\frac{2\abs{dz}}{1+\abs{z}^2}.
$$
Thus $h^*\rho=\rho$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The rotation
$$
g_\theta(z)=e^{i\theta}z
$$
is represented in $SU(2)$ by
$$
R_\theta=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i\theta/2}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta/2}
\end{pmatrix},
$$
while $h$ is represented projectively by
$$
H=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Both matrices have determinant $1$ and are unitary, hence lie in
$SU(2)$. Their associated Möbius transformations are
$$
z\longmapsto
\frac{e^{i\theta/2}z}{e^{-i\theta/2}}
=e^{i\theta}z
$$
and
$$
z\longmapsto\frac{z-1}{z+1},
$$
respectively. Multiplying a matrix by $-I$ does not change its Möbius
transformation, so these represent the required elements of
$SU(2)/(\pm I)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Conjugating the rotations by $H$ gives the one-parameter subgroup
$$
X_\beta
=H R_\beta H^{-1}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos(\beta/2)&i\sin(\beta/2)\\
i\sin(\beta/2)&\cos(\beta/2)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
H^{-1}
=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
-1&1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
direct multiplication gives
$$
H R_\beta H^{-1}
=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i\beta/2}+e^{-i\beta/2}&
e^{i\beta/2}-e^{-i\beta/2}\\
e^{i\beta/2}-e^{-i\beta/2}&
e^{i\beta/2}+e^{-i\beta/2}
\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is the displayed matrix.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every matrix in $SU(2)$ has the form
$$
U=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
-\overline b&\overline a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\abs{a}^2+\abs{b}^2=1.
$$

::: pf-proof

If the first row of a unitary determinant-one matrix is $(a,b)$, then
the second row must be a unit vector orthogonal to it. The determinant
condition fixes that row as $(-\overline b,\overline a)$, and unitarity
gives $\abs{a}^2+\abs{b}^2=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Every such matrix admits an Euler factorization
$$
U=R_\alpha X_\beta R_\gamma
$$
for suitable real $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$.

::: pf-proof

Multiplication gives
$$
R_\alpha X_\beta R_\gamma
=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i(\alpha+\gamma)/2}\cos(\beta/2)&
i e^{i(\alpha-\gamma)/2}\sin(\beta/2)\\
i e^{-i(\alpha-\gamma)/2}\sin(\beta/2)&
e^{-i(\alpha+\gamma)/2}\cos(\beta/2)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Choose $\beta\in[0,\pi]$ so that
$$
\cos(\beta/2)=\abs{a},
\qquad
\sin(\beta/2)=\abs{b}.
$$
When $a,b\neq0$, write
$$
a=\abs{a}e^{iA},
\qquad
b=\abs{b}e^{iB}.
$$
The choices
$$
\alpha=A+B-\frac\pi2,
\qquad
\gamma=A-B+\frac\pi2
$$
make the upper row equal to $(a,b)$, and the lower row then has the
required $SU(2)$ form. If $a=0$ or $b=0$, the unused phase may be chosen
arbitrarily and the same equations give a factorization.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the rotations $g_\theta$ together with $h$ generate
$SU(2)/(\pm I)$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, every $X_\beta$ lies in the subgroup generated by the
$R_\theta$ and $H$. By step [](#s6){.pf-ref}, every element of $SU(2)$ is a product of
three matrices from these generated one-parameter subgroups. Passing to
projective classes shows that the Möbius transformations $g_\theta$ and
$h$ generate the full subgroup $SU(2)/(\pm I)\subset PSL_2(\CC)$.

:::
