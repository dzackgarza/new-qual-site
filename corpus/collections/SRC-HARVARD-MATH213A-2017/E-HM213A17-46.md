::: {.problem}
(a) Verify that the spherical metric
\[
\rho=\frac{2|dz|}{1+|z|^2}
\]
is invariant under
\[
h(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}.
\]

(b) Show that the rotations
\[
g_\theta(z)=e^{i\theta}z
\]
together with $h$ generate the full group
\[
SU(2)/(\pm I)\subset PSL_2(\mathbb C).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For
\[
h(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
\]
one has
\[
h'(z)=\frac{2}{(z+1)^2}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Differentiate the quotient:
\[
h'(z)
=\frac{(z+1)-(z-1)}{(z+1)^2}
=\frac{2}{(z+1)^2}.
\]
:::

<1>2. The spherical metric is invariant under $h$.
::: {.proof}
First compute
\[
1+|h(z)|^2
=1+\frac{|z-1|^2}{|z+1|^2}
=\frac{|z+1|^2+|z-1|^2}{|z+1|^2}.
\]
Since
\[
|z+1|^2+|z-1|^2=2(1+|z|^2),
\]
we obtain
\[
1+|h(z)|^2
=\frac{2(1+|z|^2)}{|z+1|^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{2|h'(z)|\,|dz|}{1+|h(z)|^2}
=
\frac{2\cdot 2|dz|/|z+1|^2}
{2(1+|z|^2)/|z+1|^2}
=\frac{2|dz|}{1+|z|^2}.
\]
Thus $h^*\rho=\rho$.
:::

<1>3. The rotation
\[
g_\theta(z)=e^{i\theta}z
\]
is represented in $SU(2)$ by
\[
R_\theta=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i\theta/2}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta/2}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
while $h$ is represented projectively by
\[
H=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Both matrices have determinant $1$ and are unitary, hence lie in
$SU(2)$. Their associated Möbius transformations are
\[
z\longmapsto
\frac{e^{i\theta/2}z}{e^{-i\theta/2}}
=e^{i\theta}z
\]
and
\[
z\longmapsto\frac{z-1}{z+1},
\]
respectively. Multiplying a matrix by $-I$ does not change its Möbius
transformation, so these represent the required elements of
$SU(2)/(\pm I)$.
:::

<1>4. Conjugating the rotations by $H$ gives the one-parameter subgroup
\[
X_\beta
=H R_\beta H^{-1}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos(\beta/2)&i\sin(\beta/2)\\
i\sin(\beta/2)&\cos(\beta/2)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
H^{-1}
=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
-1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]
direct multiplication gives
\[
H R_\beta H^{-1}
=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i\beta/2}+e^{-i\beta/2}&
e^{i\beta/2}-e^{-i\beta/2}\\
e^{i\beta/2}-e^{-i\beta/2}&
e^{i\beta/2}+e^{-i\beta/2}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is the displayed matrix.
:::

<1>5. Every matrix in $SU(2)$ has the form
\[
U=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
-\overline b&\overline a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
|a|^2+|b|^2=1.
\]
::: {.proof}
If the first row of a unitary determinant-one matrix is $(a,b)$, then
the second row must be a unit vector orthogonal to it. The determinant
condition fixes that row as $(-\overline b,\overline a)$, and unitarity
gives $|a|^2+|b|^2=1$.
:::

<1>6. Every such matrix admits an Euler factorization
\[
U=R_\alpha X_\beta R_\gamma
\]
for suitable real $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$.
::: {.proof}
Multiplication gives
\[
R_\alpha X_\beta R_\gamma
=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i(\alpha+\gamma)/2}\cos(\beta/2)&
i e^{i(\alpha-\gamma)/2}\sin(\beta/2)\\
i e^{-i(\alpha-\gamma)/2}\sin(\beta/2)&
e^{-i(\alpha+\gamma)/2}\cos(\beta/2)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Choose $\beta\in[0,\pi]$ so that
\[
\cos(\beta/2)=|a|,
\qquad
\sin(\beta/2)=|b|.
\]
When $a,b\neq0$, write
\[
a=|a|e^{iA},
\qquad
b=|b|e^{iB}.
\]
The choices
\[
\alpha=A+B-\frac\pi2,
\qquad
\gamma=A-B+\frac\pi2
\]
make the upper row equal to $(a,b)$, and the lower row then has the
required $SU(2)$ form. If $a=0$ or $b=0$, the unused phase may be chosen
arbitrarily and the same equations give a factorization.
:::

<1>7. Therefore the rotations $g_\theta$ together with $h$ generate
$SU(2)/(\pm I)$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, every $X_\beta$ lies in the subgroup generated by the
$R_\theta$ and $H$. By <1>6, every element of $SU(2)$ is a product of
three matrices from these generated one-parameter subgroups. Passing to
projective classes shows that the Möbius transformations $g_\theta$ and
$h$ generate the full subgroup $SU(2)/(\pm I)\subset PSL_2(\mathbb C)$.
:::
