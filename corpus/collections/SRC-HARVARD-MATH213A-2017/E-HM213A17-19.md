::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ be analytic and let $U\subset\mathbb C$ be a bounded region.
Suppose $|f(z)|$ is constant on $\partial U$.
Show that either $f$ is constant or $f$ has a zero in $U$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
\[
|f(z)|=c\qquad(z\in\partial U).
\]
If $c=0$, then $f$ is identically zero.

::: pf-proof

If $c=0$, then $|f|=0$ on $\partial U$. Since $f$ is entire, it is
continuous on the compact set $\overline U$. If $|f|$ were positive
somewhere in $U$, its maximum on $\overline U$ would be positive and would
occur at an interior point. The maximum modulus principle would force $f$ to
be constant, contradicting its zero boundary values unless that constant were
zero. Hence $|f|=0$ throughout $U$, and therefore $f\equiv0$ on $U$ and,
by the identity theorem, on all of $\mathbb C$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Assume $c>0$ and suppose that $f$ has no zero in $U$. Then
\[
|f(z)|\le c
\qquad(z\in U).
\]

::: pf-proof

The closure $\overline U$ is compact because $U$ is bounded. The entire
function $f$ is continuous on $\overline U$, so $|f|$ attains its maximum
there.

If some point of $U$ had modulus strictly larger than $c$, then the maximum
of $|f|$ on $\overline U$ would occur at an interior point of $U$, because
$|f|=c$ on $\partial U$. The maximum modulus principle would then force $f$
to be constant. In that event there is nothing further to prove. Therefore,
in the only remaining case,
\[
|f(z)|\le c
\]
throughout $U$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Under the same hypotheses,
\[
|f(z)|\ge c
\qquad(z\in U).
\]

::: pf-proof

Because $f$ has no zero in $U$ and $c>0$ on $\partial U$, it has no zero on
$\overline U$. Hence
\[
g(z)=\frac1{f(z)}
\]
is holomorphic on $U$ and continuous on $\overline U$. On the boundary,
\[
|g(z)|=\frac1c.
\]
Applying the same maximum-modulus argument as in step [](#s2){.pf-ref} to $g$ gives
\[
|g(z)|\le\frac1c
\]
throughout $U$. Equivalently,
\[
|f(z)|\ge c.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore, if $f$ has no zero in $U$, then $f$ is constant.

::: pf-proof

Combining steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives
\[
|f(z)|=c
\]
for every $z\in U$.

If $f$ were nonconstant, the open mapping theorem would imply that $f(U)$ is
open in $\mathbb C$. But
\[
f(U)\subset\{w\in\mathbb C:|w|=c\},
\]
and a circle has empty interior. This is impossible. Thus $f$ is constant.

Consequently, every nonconstant $f$ satisfying the boundary-modulus
hypothesis must have a zero in $U$.

:::
