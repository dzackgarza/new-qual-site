::: {.problem}
Let $B\subset\mathbb C$ be an annulus bounded by Jordan curves $C_1$ and $C_2$.
Prove that there are an arc $\alpha\subset B$ joining $C_1$ to $C_2$ and a loop $\beta\subset B$ separating $C_1$ from $C_2$ such that
\[
L(\alpha)L(\beta)\le\operatorname{area}(B).
\]

Hint: take a conformal map $f:A(R)\to B$ and consider
\[
\int_{A(R)} |f'(z)|\,|z|^{-1}\,|dz|^2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
f:A(R)=\{1<|z|<R\}\longrightarrow B
\]
be a conformal map, and set
\[
I=\int_{A(R)}\frac{|f'(z)|}{|z|}\,dA(z).
\]
We will select a radial segment and a concentric circle whose image lengths
are controlled by two different averages of the same integral $I$.

<1>1. For $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$, let
\[
\ell(\theta)
=\int_1^R|f'(re^{i\theta})|\,dr.
\]
Then
\[
I=\int_0^{2\pi}\ell(\theta)\,d\theta.
\]
::: {.proof}
In polar coordinates $z=re^{i\theta}$,
\[
dA=r\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
I
&=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_1^R
\frac{|f'(re^{i\theta})|}{r}\,r\,dr\,d\theta\\
&=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_1^R
|f'(re^{i\theta})|\,dr\,d\theta\\
&=\int_0^{2\pi}\ell(\theta)\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>2. There is an angle $\theta_0$ for which the image of the radial segment
\[
\{re^{i\theta_0}:1<r<R\}
\]
has length at most $I/(2\pi)$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, the average value of $\ell(\theta)$ over $[0,2\pi)$ is
\[
\frac{I}{2\pi}.
\]
Hence for some $\theta_0$,
\[
\ell(\theta_0)\le\frac{I}{2\pi}.
\]

The image radial segment approaches the two boundary components of $B$;
since these are Jordan curves, the conformal map extends to the boundary
components, so its closure is an arc $\alpha$ joining $C_1$ to $C_2$. Its
length is
\[
L(\alpha)=\ell(\theta_0)\le\frac{I}{2\pi}.
\]
:::

<1>3. For $1<r<R$, let
\[
m(r)=\int_0^{2\pi}|f'(re^{i\theta})|\,r\,d\theta.
\]
Then
\[
I=\int_1^R m(r)\,\frac{dr}{r}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The quantity $m(r)$ is exactly the Euclidean length of the loop
\[
\theta\longmapsto f(re^{i\theta}).
\]
Also
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^R m(r)\frac{dr}{r}
&=\int_1^R\int_0^{2\pi}
|f'(re^{i\theta})|\,r\,d\theta\,\frac{dr}{r}\\
&=\int_1^R\int_0^{2\pi}|f'(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta\,dr\\
&=I
\end{aligned}
\]
by <1>1.
:::

<1>4. There is a radius $r_0\in(1,R)$ such that
\[
m(r_0)\le\frac{I}{\log R}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The measure $dr/r$ of the interval $(1,R)$ is
\[
\int_1^R\frac{dr}{r}=\log R.
\]
Thus <1>3 says that the average of $m(r)$ with respect to this measure is
$I/\log R$. Hence some $r_0$ satisfies
\[
m(r_0)\le\frac{I}{\log R}.
\]

The circle $|z|=r_0$ separates the two boundary components of $A(R)$, so
its image
\[
\beta=f(\{|z|=r_0\})
\]
is a loop in $B$ separating $C_1$ from $C_2$, and
\[
L(\beta)=m(r_0).
\]
:::

<1>5. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
I^2\le 2\pi\log R\,\operatorname{area}(B).
\]
::: {.proof}
By Cauchy--Schwarz on $A(R)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
I^2
&=\left(
\int_{A(R)}|f'(z)|\frac1{|z|}\,dA
\right)^2\\
&\le
\left(\int_{A(R)}|f'(z)|^2\,dA\right)
\left(\int_{A(R)}\frac{1}{|z|^2}\,dA\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Because $f$ is conformal and bijective,
\[
\int_{A(R)}|f'(z)|^2\,dA
=\operatorname{area}(B).
\]
Also
\[
\int_{A(R)}\frac1{|z|^2}\,dA
=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_1^R\frac1{r^2}r\,dr\,d\theta
=2\pi\log R.
\]
Combining these gives the claimed inequality.
:::

<1>6. The selected curves satisfy
\[
L(\alpha)L(\beta)\le\operatorname{area}(B).
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>4,
\[
L(\alpha)L(\beta)
\le
\frac{I}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{I}{\log R}
=\frac{I^2}{2\pi\log R}.
\]
Applying <1>5 gives
\[
L(\alpha)L(\beta)
\le\operatorname{area}(B).
\]
:::
