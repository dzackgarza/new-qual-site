::: {.problem}
Let $f:\Delta\to\Delta$ be holomorphic with $f(0)=0$.
Prove that either $f$ is a rotation, or
$$
f^n(z)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(n\to\infty),
$$
where $f^n=f\circ f\circ\cdots\circ f$ denotes the $n$-fold iterate.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

By Schwarz's lemma,
$$
\abs{f(z)}\le \abs{z}
\qquad(z\in\Delta).
$$
Moreover, if equality holds at any nonzero point, then $f$ is a rotation.

::: pf-proof

The hypotheses of Schwarz's lemma apply because $f:\Delta\to\Delta$ is
holomorphic and $f(0)=0$. Its equality case says that if
$\abs{f(z_0)}=\abs{z_0}$ for some $z_0\neq0$, then
$$
f(z)=e^{i\theta}z
$$
for some real $\theta$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Assume $f$ is not a rotation. Then
$$
g(z)=
\begin{cases}
f(z)/z,&z\neq0,\\
f'(0),&z=0
\end{cases}
$$
is holomorphic on $\Delta$ and satisfies
$$
\abs{g(z)}<1
\qquad(z\in\Delta).
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $f(0)=0$, the quotient $f(z)/z$ has a removable singularity at $0$,
whose value after removal is $f'(0)$. Thus $g$ is holomorphic. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$\abs{g(z)}\le1$. If equality held at any point, then the equality case of
Schwarz's lemma would make $f$ a rotation, contrary to assumption. Hence the
inequality is strict everywhere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Fix $z\in\Delta$ and set $r=\abs{z}$. If $r>0$, there is a constant
$q=q(r)<1$ such that
$$
\abs{f(w)}\le q\abs{w}
\qquad(\abs{w}\le r).
$$

::: pf-proof

The continuous function $\abs{g}$ attains a maximum on the compact disk
$\{\abs{w}\le r\}$. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref} this maximum is strictly less than $1$; call it
$q$. Since $f(w)=wg(w)$, the stated estimate follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For every $z\in\Delta$,
$$
f^n(z)\longrightarrow0.
$$

::: pf-proof

The case $z=0$ is immediate because every iterate fixes $0$. Let $z\neq0$
and put $r=\abs{z}$. From step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{f^k(z)}\le r
$$
for every $k\ge0$. Therefore the estimate in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} applies at every step of
the orbit, and induction gives
$$
\abs{f^n(z)}\le q^n\abs{z}.
$$
Since $0\le q<1$, the right-hand side tends to zero. Hence
$f^n(z)\to0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus exactly the required dichotomy holds: either
$f(z)=e^{i\theta}z$ is a rotation, or all iterates converge pointwise to
$0$ on $\Delta$.

::: pf-proof

If $f$ is a rotation, this is the first alternative. Otherwise step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves
the second.

:::
