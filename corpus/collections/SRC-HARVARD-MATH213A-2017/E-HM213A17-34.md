::: {.problem}
Suppose analytic functions $f$ and $g$ on a region $U$ satisfy
$$
\abs{f(z)}=\abs{g(z)}
\qquad(z\in U).
$$
Prove that $f(z)=a g(z)$ for some constant $a$ with $\abs{a}=1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $g\equiv0$, then $\abs{f}=\abs{g}$ gives $f\equiv0$, and the conclusion holds
for any constant $a$ with $\abs{a}=1$. Assume henceforth that $g$ is not
identically zero.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The zeros of $f$ and $g$ coincide.

::: pf-proof

For every $z\in U$,
$$
\abs{f(z)}=\abs{g(z)}.
$$
Thus $f(z)=0$ if and only if $g(z)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

At every common zero, $f$ and $g$ have the same order of vanishing.

::: pf-proof

Let $z_0$ be a common zero. Since neither function is identically zero on the
region $U$, write locally
$$
f(z)=(z-z_0)^m F(z),
\qquad
g(z)=(z-z_0)^n G(z),
$$
where $F(z_0)\neq0$ and $G(z_0)\neq0$. On a punctured neighborhood of
$z_0$, the equality of moduli gives
$$
\abs{z-z_0}^{m-n}\abs{\frac{F(z)}{G(z)}}=1.
$$
The second factor tends to the finite nonzero number
$\abs{F(z_0)/G(z_0)}$. If $m>n$, the left side tends to $0$; if $m<n$, it
tends to $\infty$. Both are impossible. Hence $m=n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The quotient $h=f/g$, initially defined away from the zeros of $g$,
extends to an analytic function on all of $U$.

::: pf-proof

Away from the zeros of $g$, the quotient is analytic. At a common zero
$z_0$, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives local factorizations with the same power:
$$
f(z)=(z-z_0)^mF(z),
\qquad
g(z)=(z-z_0)^mG(z).
$$
Thus on the punctured neighborhood
$$
h(z)=\frac{F(z)}{G(z)},
$$
and the right side is analytic at $z_0$ because $G(z_0)\neq0$. It therefore
provides the removable extension of $h$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The extended function $h$ satisfies $\abs{h(z)}=1$ for every $z\in U$.

::: pf-proof

Away from the common zeros,
$$
\abs{h(z)}=\frac{\abs{f(z)}}{\abs{g(z)}}=1.
$$
The extension is continuous, so the same equality holds at the removable
points as well.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The function $h$ is constant.

::: pf-proof

If $h$ were nonconstant, the open mapping theorem would imply that $h(U)$ is
open in $\CC$. But step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
h(U)\subset S^1,
$$
and the unit circle contains no nonempty open subset of $\CC$.
Therefore $h$ is constant, say $h\equiv a$. Since $\abs{h}=1$, one has
$\abs{a}=1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $f=ag$ on $U$ for a constant $a$ with $\abs{a}=1$.

::: pf-proof

By definition of the extended quotient, $f=hg$ everywhere on $U$. Apply
step [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

:::
