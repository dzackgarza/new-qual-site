::: {.problem}
Suppose analytic functions $f$ and $g$ on a region $U$ satisfy
\[
|f(z)|=|g(z)|
\qquad(z\in U).
\]
Prove that $f(z)=a g(z)$ for some constant $a$ with $|a|=1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $g\equiv0$, then $|f|=|g|$ gives $f\equiv0$, and the conclusion holds
for any constant $a$ with $|a|=1$. Assume henceforth that $g$ is not
identically zero.

<1>1. The zeros of $f$ and $g$ coincide.
::: {.proof}
For every $z\in U$,
\[
|f(z)|=|g(z)|.
\]
Thus $f(z)=0$ if and only if $g(z)=0$.
:::

<1>2. At every common zero, $f$ and $g$ have the same order of vanishing.
::: {.proof}
Let $z_0$ be a common zero. Since neither function is identically zero on the
region $U$, write locally
\[
f(z)=(z-z_0)^m F(z),
\qquad
g(z)=(z-z_0)^n G(z),
\]
where $F(z_0)\neq0$ and $G(z_0)\neq0$. On a punctured neighborhood of
$z_0$, the equality of moduli gives
\[
|z-z_0|^{m-n}\left|\frac{F(z)}{G(z)}\right|=1.
\]
The second factor tends to the finite nonzero number
$|F(z_0)/G(z_0)|$. If $m>n$, the left side tends to $0$; if $m<n$, it
tends to $\infty$. Both are impossible. Hence $m=n$.
:::

<1>3. The quotient $h=f/g$, initially defined away from the zeros of $g$,
extends to an analytic function on all of $U$.
::: {.proof}
Away from the zeros of $g$, the quotient is analytic. At a common zero
$z_0$, <1>2 gives local factorizations with the same power:
\[
f(z)=(z-z_0)^mF(z),
\qquad
g(z)=(z-z_0)^mG(z).
\]
Thus on the punctured neighborhood
\[
h(z)=\frac{F(z)}{G(z)},
\]
and the right side is analytic at $z_0$ because $G(z_0)\neq0$. It therefore
provides the removable extension of $h$.
:::

<1>4. The extended function $h$ satisfies $|h(z)|=1$ for every $z\in U$.
::: {.proof}
Away from the common zeros,
\[
|h(z)|=\frac{|f(z)|}{|g(z)|}=1.
\]
The extension is continuous, so the same equality holds at the removable
points as well.
:::

<1>5. The function $h$ is constant.
::: {.proof}
If $h$ were nonconstant, the open mapping theorem would imply that $h(U)$ is
open in $\mathbb C$. But <1>4 gives
\[
h(U)\subset S^1,
\]
and the unit circle contains no nonempty open subset of $\mathbb C$.
Therefore $h$ is constant, say $h\equiv a$. Since $|h|=1$, one has
$|a|=1$.
:::

<1>6. Hence $f=ag$ on $U$ for a constant $a$ with $|a|=1$.
::: {.proof}
By definition of the extended quotient, $f=hg$ everywhere on $U$. Apply
<1>5.
:::
:::
