::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_n}{z^n}
\]
be a univalent map from $\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\Delta$ to $\widehat{\mathbb C}$ fixing $\infty$ with derivative $1$ there.
If $w$ is not in the image of $f$, prove that
\[
|w|\le2.
\]

Hint: apply the area-theorem bound $|b_1|\le1$ to a normalized square root of $f(z^2)-w$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
G(z)=\sqrt{f(z^2)-w}
\]
with the branch normalized by $G(z)/z\to1$ at infinity. Then $G\in\Sigma$
and its first negative Laurent coefficient is $-w/2$. The area theorem gives
the result.

<1>1. The function $f(z^2)-w$ has a holomorphic square root on
$\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\overline\Delta$ normalized by
\[
G(z)=z+O(z^{-1}).
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $w$ is omitted by $f$, the function
\[
Q(z)=f(z^2)-w
\]
has no zeros for $|z|>1$. Near infinity,
\[
Q(z)=z^2-w+O(z^{-2})
=z^2\left(1-wz^{-2}+O(z^{-4})\right).
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{Q(z)}{z^2}\longrightarrow1
\qquad(z\to\infty).
\]
The nonvanishing function $Q(z)/z^2$ has winding number zero around $0$
on a large circle, and hence on every circle in the exterior domain by
homotopy through nonvanishing values. It therefore has a holomorphic
logarithm there. Define
\[
G(z)
=z\exp\left(\frac12\log\frac{Q(z)}{z^2}\right),
\]
choosing the logarithm tending to $0$ at infinity. Then $G^2=Q$ and
$G(z)/z\to1$.
:::

<1>2. The map $G$ is univalent on the exterior disk.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $G(z_1)=G(z_2)$. Squaring gives
\[
f(z_1^2)-w=f(z_2^2)-w.
\]
Since $f$ is univalent,
\[
z_1^2=z_2^2,
\]
so $z_2=\pm z_1$.

Because $Q(-z)=Q(z)$, the quotient
\[
\frac{G(-z)}{G(z)}
\]
has square $1$ and is holomorphic on the connected exterior domain. Hence it
is constant. From the normalization at infinity,
\[
\frac{G(-z)}{G(z)}\longrightarrow-1,
\]
so
\[
G(-z)=-G(z).
\]
Also $G$ never vanishes because $Q$ never vanishes. Therefore
$G(z_1)=G(-z_1)$ is impossible, and we must have $z_2=z_1$. Thus $G$ is
univalent.
:::

<1>3. The Laurent expansion of $G$ begins
\[
G(z)=z-\frac{w}{2z}+O(z^{-3}).
\]
::: {.proof}
Using
\[
f(z^2)
=z^2+\frac{b_1}{z^2}+\frac{b_2}{z^4}+\cdots,
\]
we have
\[
Q(z)
=z^2\left(1-wz^{-2}+b_1z^{-4}+O(z^{-6})\right).
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(z)
&=z\left(1-wz^{-2}+O(z^{-4})\right)^{1/2}\\
&=z\left(1-\frac w2z^{-2}+O(z^{-4})\right)\\
&=z-\frac{w}{2z}+O(z^{-3}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, if
\[
G(z)=z+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c_n}{z^n},
\]
then
\[
c_1=-\frac w2.
\]
:::

<1>4. The area theorem implies $|w|\le2$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and <1>2, $G\in\Sigma$. McMullen's area-theorem corollary gives
\[
|c_1|\le1.
\]
Using <1>3,
\[
\frac{|w|}{2}=|c_1|\le1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{|w|\le2}.
\]
:::
