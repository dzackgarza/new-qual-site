::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic in a region $U$, and suppose
\[
\int_U |f(z)|\,|dz|^2<\infty.
\]
Prove that there is a constant $M$ such that for every $z\in U$,
\[
|f(z)|\le \frac{M}{d(z,\partial U)^2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
L=\int_U |f(w)|\,|dw|^2<\infty.
\]
Then one may take
\[
\boxed{M=\frac{L}{\pi}}.
\]

<1>1. Fix $z\in U$ and let
\[
0<r<d(z,\partial U).
\]
Then the disk $D(z,r)$ is contained in $U$.
::: {.proof}
By the definition of distance to the boundary, no point at distance less
than $d(z,\partial U)$ from $z$ can lie outside the component of $U$
containing $z$. Hence every disk of radius strictly smaller than that
distance lies in $U$.
:::

<1>2. The analytic mean-value formula gives
\[
f(z)=\frac1{\pi r^2}\int_{D(z,r)}f(w)\,|dw|^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
The real and imaginary parts of $f$ are harmonic. Each therefore satisfies
the area mean-value property on the disk $D(z,r)$ from <1>1. Combining the
two real identities yields the displayed complex-valued formula.
:::

<1>3. Consequently
\[
|f(z)|
\le \frac{L}{\pi r^2}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Taking absolute values in <1>2 and using the triangle inequality gives
\[
|f(z)|
\le \frac1{\pi r^2}
\int_{D(z,r)}|f(w)|\,|dw|^2.
\]
Since $D(z,r)\subset U$, the last integral is at most $L$.
:::

<1>4. Letting $r\uparrow d(z,\partial U)$ gives
\[
|f(z)|
\le
\frac{L}{\pi\,d(z,\partial U)^2}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The bound in <1>3 holds for every radius
$r<d(z,\partial U)$. Taking the limit from below yields the stated
estimate. Thus the required constant is $M=L/\pi$, independent of $z$.
:::
:::
