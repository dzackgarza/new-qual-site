::: {.problem}
Continue with the lattice
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z\lambda_1\oplus\mathbb Z\lambda_2
\]
and its Weierstrass zeta function $\zeta$.

(a) Show that there are constants $\eta_1,\eta_2\in\mathbb C$ such that
\[
\zeta(z+\lambda_i)=\zeta(z)+\eta_i
\qquad(i=1,2).
\]

(b) Using the residue theorem, prove the Legendre relation
\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\eta_1&\eta_2\\
\lambda_1&\lambda_2
\end{pmatrix}
=2\pi i.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For the sign in the displayed Legendre relation, take the ordered basis $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ to be positively oriented in the real plane $\mathbb C\cong\mathbb R^2$. Then
\[
\boxed{\eta_1\lambda_2-\eta_2\lambda_1=2\pi i.}
\]
If the ordered basis is reversed, the left side and the orientation of its fundamental parallelogram both reverse, so the corresponding formula has the opposite sign.

<1>1. Translation by a lattice period changes $\zeta$ by a constant.
::: {.proof}
From the preceding exercise,
\[
\zeta'(z)=-\wp(z).
\]
For any $\lambda\in\Lambda$, periodicity of $\wp$ gives
\[
\frac{d}{dz}\bigl(\zeta(z+\lambda)-\zeta(z)\bigr)
=-\wp(z+\lambda)+\wp(z)=0
\]
away from the lattice poles. Hence the difference is constant there, and therefore as a meromorphic identity on $\mathbb C$. Thus there is a constant $\eta(\lambda)$ such that
\[
\zeta(z+\lambda)=\zeta(z)+\eta(\lambda).
\]
Taking $\lambda=\lambda_i$ defines
\[
\eta_i=\eta(\lambda_i),
\qquad i=1,2.
\]
:::

<1>2. The zeta function has residue $1$ at every lattice point.
::: {.proof}
The preceding exercise identifies
\[
\zeta(z)=\frac{\sigma'(z)}{\sigma(z)}.
\]
The canonical product $\sigma$ has a simple zero at every point of $\Lambda$. The logarithmic derivative of a holomorphic function at a simple zero has residue $1$. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=\lambda}\zeta(z)=1
\qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda).
\]
:::

<1>3. The residue theorem on a centered fundamental parallelogram gives
\[
\int_{\partial P}\zeta(z)\,dz=2\pi i.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
P=\left\{s\lambda_1+t\lambda_2:
-\frac12\le s,t\le\frac12\right\}.
\]
No lattice point lies on $\partial P$, and $0$ is the unique lattice point in its interior. With the positive orientation induced by the positively oriented ordered basis $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$, the residue theorem and <1>2 give
\[
\int_{\partial P}\zeta(z)\,dz
=2\pi i\operatorname{Res}_{0}\zeta
=2\pi i.
\]
:::

<1>4. Pairing opposite edges evaluates the same boundary integral as
\[
\eta_1\lambda_2-\eta_2\lambda_1.
\]
::: {.proof}
Set
\[
a=-\frac{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}{2}.
\]
Traverse the boundary as
\[
a\to a+\lambda_1
\to a+\lambda_1+\lambda_2
\to a+\lambda_2
\to a.
\]
For the bottom and top edges, translate the top edge by $-\lambda_2$. Using <1>1,
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{\mathrm{bottom}}+I_{\mathrm{top}}
&=\int_a^{a+\lambda_1}\zeta(z)\,dz
 +\int_{a+\lambda_1}^{a}\zeta(z+\lambda_2)\,dz\\
&=\int_a^{a+\lambda_1}\zeta(z)\,dz
 -\int_a^{a+\lambda_1}(\zeta(z)+\eta_2)\,dz\\
&=-\eta_2\lambda_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Similarly, translate the right edge by $-\lambda_1$ and pair it with the left edge:
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{\mathrm{right}}+I_{\mathrm{left}}
&=\int_a^{a+\lambda_2}\zeta(z+\lambda_1)\,dz
 +\int_{a+\lambda_2}^{a}\zeta(z)\,dz\\
&=\int_a^{a+\lambda_2}(\zeta(z)+\eta_1)\,dz
 -\int_a^{a+\lambda_2}\zeta(z)\,dz\\
&=\eta_1\lambda_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Adding the two pairs gives
\[
\int_{\partial P}\zeta(z)\,dz
=\eta_1\lambda_2-\eta_2\lambda_1
=\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\eta_1&\eta_2\\
\lambda_1&\lambda_2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
By <1>3 this equals $2\pi i$, which proves the stated Legendre relation with the positive-orientation convention.
