::: {.problem}
Let
\[
T=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:x^2+y^2+z^2=1,\ x,y,z\ge 0\}
\]
be the spherical triangle, and let $\alpha=z\,dx\wedge dz$.

(a) Find a smooth $1$-form $\beta$ on $\mathbb R^3$ such that $\alpha=d\beta$.

(b) Define consistent orientations for $T$ and $\partial T$.

(c) Using those orientations, compute $\int_T\alpha$ and $\int_{\partial T}\beta$ directly and check that they agree.
Explain why they should agree.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The smooth $1$-form
\[
\beta=xz\,dz
\]
satisfies $d\beta=\alpha$ on $\mathbb R^3$.

::: pf-proof

Since $d(dz)=0$,
\[
d\beta=d(xz)\wedge dz=(z\,dx+x\,dz)\wedge dz=z\,dx\wedge dz=\alpha.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Orient $T$ by the outward unit normal
\[
n=(x,y,z).
\]
Give $\partial T$ the induced boundary orientation: if $\nu$ is the outward-pointing unit conormal to an edge inside $T$, then a tangent vector $\tau$ is positive when $(\nu,\tau)$ is a positive basis of $TT$.

::: pf-proof

Equivalently, along an edge we require $\nu\times\tau=n$.

Writing
\[
e_1=(1,0,0),\qquad e_2=(0,1,0),\qquad e_3=(0,0,1),
\]
the three positively oriented edges are therefore
\[
e_1\longrightarrow e_2\quad(z=0),\qquad
e_2\longrightarrow e_3\quad(x=0),\qquad
e_3\longrightarrow e_1\quad(y=0).
\]
Indeed, on these edges the outward conormals are respectively $-e_z,-e_x,-e_y$, and the indicated tangent directions satisfy $\nu\times\tau=n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

With the orientation in step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
\[
\int_T\alpha=-\frac13.
\]

::: pf-proof

On the interior of $T$, write it as the graph
\[
\Phi(x,z)=\bigl(x,\sqrt{1-x^2-z^2},z\bigr)
\]
over
\[
D=\{(x,z):x\ge0,\ z\ge0,\ x^2+z^2\le1\}.
\]
If $y=\sqrt{1-x^2-z^2}$, then
\[
\Phi_x\times\Phi_z
=\left(-\frac{x}{y},-1,-\frac{z}{y}\right)
=-\frac1y\,n,
\]
so the $(x,z)$-orientation is opposite to the chosen outward orientation. Hence
\[
\int_T\alpha
=-\int_D z\,dx\,dz
=-\int_0^1\int_0^{\sqrt{1-x^2}}z\,dz\,dx
=-\frac12\int_0^1(1-x^2)\,dx
=-\frac13.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

With the induced boundary orientation in step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
\[
\int_{\partial T}\beta=-\frac13.
\]

::: pf-proof

On the edges $z=0$ and $x=0$, the form $\beta=xz\,dz$ vanishes identically.

The remaining edge is $y=0$, oriented from $e_3$ to $e_1$. Parametrize it by
\[
\gamma(t)=(\sin t,0,\cos t),\qquad 0\le t\le\frac\pi2.
\]
This has precisely the required orientation. Along $\gamma$,
\[
x=\sin t,\qquad z=\cos t,\qquad dz=-\sin t\,dt,
\]
and therefore
\[
\gamma^*\beta
=-\sin^2 t\cos t\,dt.
\]
Thus
\[
\int_{\partial T}\beta
=-\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2t\cos t\,dt
=-\frac13.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The two direct computations agree, as required by Stokes' theorem.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $\alpha=d\beta$, and by step [](#s2){.pf-ref} the orientations on $T$ and $\partial T$ are compatible. Hence Stokes' theorem gives
\[
\int_T\alpha=\int_Td\beta=\int_{\partial T}\beta.
\]
Steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} compute both sides directly as $-1/3$.

:::
