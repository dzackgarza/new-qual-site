::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic on the unit disk $\Delta$, with $f(0)=0$ and
\[
\int_\Delta |f(z)|^2\,|dz|^2<\infty.
\]
Prove that
\[
\int_\Delta |\operatorname{Re}f|^2\,|dz|^2
=
\int_\Delta |\operatorname{Im}f|^2\,|dz|^2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
\[
f=u+iv,
\qquad
u=\operatorname{Re}f,
\quad
v=\operatorname{Im}f.
\]
The key observation is
\[
u^2-v^2=\operatorname{Re}(f^2).
\]
We will show that
\[
\int_\Delta f(z)^2\,|dz|^2=0.
\]

<1>1. The function $f^2$ is integrable on $\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
By hypothesis,
\[
\int_\Delta |f(z)|^2\,|dz|^2<\infty.
\]
Since
\[
|f(z)^2|=|f(z)|^2,
\]
the function $f^2$ is absolutely integrable. Thus polar-coordinate
integration and Fubini's theorem may be applied to $f^2$.
:::

<1>2. For every $0<r<1$,
\[
\int_0^{2\pi} f(re^{i\theta})^2\,d\theta=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The function $f^2$ is analytic on $\Delta$, and
\[
f(0)^2=0.
\]
By the mean-value formula for analytic functions,
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(re^{i\theta})^2\,d\theta
=f(0)^2
=0.
\]
:::

<1>3. One has
\[
\int_\Delta f(z)^2\,|dz|^2=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using $|dz|^2=dx\,dy$ and polar coordinates,
\[
\int_\Delta f(z)^2\,|dz|^2
=\int_0^1
r\left(\int_0^{2\pi}f(re^{i\theta})^2\,d\theta\right)dr.
\]
The interchange is justified by <1>1. The inner integral vanishes for
every $r$ by <1>2, so the whole integral is zero.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
\[
\int_\Delta u^2\,|dz|^2
=
\int_\Delta v^2\,|dz|^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Taking real parts in <1>3 gives
\[
0
=\operatorname{Re}\int_\Delta f^2\,|dz|^2
=\int_\Delta \operatorname{Re}(f^2)\,|dz|^2
=\int_\Delta (u^2-v^2)\,|dz|^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\int_\Delta |\operatorname{Re}f|^2\,|dz|^2
=
\int_\Delta |\operatorname{Im}f|^2\,|dz|^2.
\]
:::
:::
