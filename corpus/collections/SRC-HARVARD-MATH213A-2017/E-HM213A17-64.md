::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f_n:\widehat{\mathbb C}\to\widehat{\mathbb C}
\]
be an unbounded sequence in $\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{\mathbb C})$.
Prove that, after passing to a subsequence, there are $p,q\in\widehat{\mathbb C}$ such that
\[
f_n(z)\longrightarrow p
\]
uniformly on compact subsets of $\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{q\}$.
Give an example for which $p=q=0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Choose matrix representatives $A_n\in SL_2(\mathbb C)$ for $f_n$.
After passing to a subsequence, their norms satisfy
\[
\|A_n\|\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
::: {.proof}
The quotient map $SL_2(\mathbb C)\to PSL_2(\mathbb C)$ has finite fibers
$\{\pm I\}$. If the norms of determinant-one representatives stayed bounded,
a subsequence would converge in the finite-dimensional matrix space. Since
determinant is continuous and equal to $1$, the limit would still lie in
$SL_2(\mathbb C)$, so the corresponding Möbius transformations would have a
convergent subsequence in $\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{\mathbb C})$. This
contradicts unboundedness. Hence an unbounded subsequence of norms exists; pass
to it.
:::

<1>2. After normalizing
\[
B_n=\frac{A_n}{\|A_n\|},
\]
and passing to a further subsequence, one has $B_n\to B$ for a nonzero
rank-one matrix $B$.
::: {.proof}
The matrices $B_n$ lie on the unit sphere of the finite-dimensional matrix
space, which is compact. Thus a subsequence converges to some $B$ with
$\|B\|=1$, so $B\neq0$. Moreover
\[
\det B_n
=\frac{\det A_n}{\|A_n\|^2}
=\frac1{\|A_n\|^2}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence $\det B=0$. A nonzero singular $2\times2$ matrix has rank one.
:::

<1>3. Let
\[
q=\mathbb P(\ker B),
\qquad
p=\mathbb P(\operatorname{im}B).
\]
Then $f_n(z)\to p$ for every $z\neq q$.
::: {.proof}
Multiplying a matrix by a nonzero scalar does not change its projective
transformation, so
\[
f_n([v])=[A_nv]=[B_nv].
\]
If $[v]\neq q$, then $Bv\neq0$. Since $B$ has rank one, every nonzero
$Bv$ spans the same line $\operatorname{im}B$. Thus
\[
[B_nv]\longrightarrow[Bv]=p.
\]
:::

<1>4. The convergence is uniform on compact subsets of
$\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{q\}$.
::: {.proof}
Let $K$ be such a compact set. Choose unit-vector representatives for points
of $K$ and consider the compact set of all such representatives. Because $K$
does not meet the projective kernel of $B$, there is a constant $c>0$ such
that
\[
\|Bv\|\ge c
\]
for every such unit representative $v$. Operator-norm convergence
$B_n\to B$ is uniform on the unit sphere, so $B_nv\to Bv$ uniformly for
$[v]\in K$, and $B_nv$ stays uniformly away from zero. Projectivization is
uniformly continuous on that set, hence
\[
[B_nv]\longrightarrow[Bv]=p
\]
uniformly on $K$.
:::

<1>5. The sequence
\[
f_n(z)=\frac1{nz}
\]
has $p=q=0$.
::: {.proof}
Each $f_n$ is a Möbius transformation of $\widehat{\mathbb C}$. For every
$z\neq0$,
\[
f_n(z)=\frac1{nz}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and also $f_n(\infty)=0$. The convergence is uniform on compact subsets of
$\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{0\}$ because such a compact set stays a
positive spherical distance from $0$. At the exceptional point,
$f_n(0)=\infty$. Thus both the limiting point and the exceptional point are
$0$.

For example, determinant-one representatives are
\[
A_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&i/\sqrt n\\
i\sqrt n&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose norms tend to infinity, so this is indeed an unbounded sequence in
$PSL_2(\mathbb C)$.
:::
