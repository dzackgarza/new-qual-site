::: {.problem}
Show that for every $\alpha>0$,
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-x^\alpha}\,dx
=\frac1\alpha\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac1\alpha\right).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Make the substitution
\[
t=x^\alpha.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\alpha>0$, the map $x\mapsto x^\alpha$ is an increasing
homeomorphism of $(0,\infty)$ onto itself. Its inverse is
\[
x=t^{1/\alpha},
\]
and therefore
\[
dx=\frac1\alpha t^{1/\alpha-1}\,dt.
\]
:::

<1>2. The integral becomes
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-x^\alpha}\,dx
=\frac1\alpha
\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^{1/\alpha-1}\,dt.
\]
::: {.proof}
Substitute the identities from <1>1. The endpoints remain $0$ and
$\infty$ because $x\mapsto x^\alpha$ maps those endpoints to themselves.
:::

<1>3. The remaining integral is the Gamma function at $1/\alpha$.
::: {.proof}
McMullen's Mellin-transform representation states that for $s>0$,
\[
\Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^{s-1}\,dt.
\]
Taking $s=1/\alpha>0$ in <1>2 gives
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-x^\alpha}\,dx
=\frac1\alpha\Gamma\!\left(\frac1\alpha\right).
\]
:::
:::
