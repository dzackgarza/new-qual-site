::: {.problem}
Given $w\in\mathbb C$, find an explicit sequence $z_n\to0$ such that
\[
e^{1/z_n}\to w.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
If $w\neq0$, choose any logarithm $L\in\mathbb C$ with $e^L=w$ and set
\[
z_n=\frac{1}{L+2\pi i n},\qquad n\ge1.
\]
Then $z_n\to0$ and in fact $e^{1/z_n}=w$ for every $n$.

If $w=0$, take
\[
z_n=-\frac1n.
\]
Then $z_n\to0$ and $e^{1/z_n}=e^{-n}\to0$.

<1>1. Suppose $w\neq0$. There is $L\in\mathbb C$ such that $e^L=w$.
::: {.proof}
Write $w=re^{i\theta}$ with $r>0$. Then
\[
L=\log r+i\theta
\]
satisfies $e^L=w$.
:::

<1>2. For
\[
z_n=\frac{1}{L+2\pi i n},
\]
one has $z_n\to0$ and $e^{1/z_n}=w$ for every $n$.
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
|L+2\pi i n|\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
we have $z_n\to0$. Moreover
\[
e^{1/z_n}
=e^{L+2\pi i n}
=e^L e^{2\pi i n}
=w.
\]
Thus the required convergence is exact term-by-term.
:::

<1>3. If $w=0$, the sequence $z_n=-1/n$ has the required property.
::: {.proof}
Clearly $z_n\to0$, while
\[
e^{1/z_n}=e^{-n}\longrightarrow0=w.
\]
:::
:::
