::: {.problem}
Let $a>b>0$, and let $E\subset\mathbb C$ be the ellipse with major axis $[-a,a]$ and minor axis $[-ib,ib]$.
Let $I\subset[-a,a]$ be the line segment joining the foci of $E$, and let $B$ be the annular region between $E$ and $I$.

Find $R>1$ and an explicit conformal map
\[
f:A(R)=\{z:1<|z|<R\}\longrightarrow B.
\]

Hint: use a map of the form $z+c/z$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
c=\sqrt{a^2-b^2},
\qquad
R=\frac{a+b}{c}
=\sqrt{\frac{a+b}{a-b}}.
\]
Then $c$ is the focal distance of the ellipse, and an explicit conformal map
is
\[
\boxed{
F(z)=\frac c2\left(z+\frac1z\right),
\qquad 1<|z|<R.}
\]

<1>1. The number $R$ satisfies $R>1$ and
\[
\frac c2\left(R+R^{-1}\right)=a,
\qquad
\frac c2\left(R-R^{-1}\right)=b.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $a>b>0$,
\[
c=\sqrt{(a-b)(a+b)}>0
\]
and
\[
R=\frac{a+b}{\sqrt{(a-b)(a+b)}}
=\sqrt{\frac{a+b}{a-b}}>1.
\]
Also
\[
R^{-1}=\sqrt{\frac{a-b}{a+b}}=\frac{a-b}{c}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac c2(R+R^{-1})
=\frac12((a+b)+(a-b))=a,
\]
and similarly
\[
\frac c2(R-R^{-1})
=\frac12((a+b)-(a-b))=b.
\]
:::

<1>2. The inner boundary circle $|z|=1$ is mapped onto the focal segment
\[
I=[-c,c].
\]
::: {.proof}
For $z=e^{i\theta}$,
\[
F(e^{i\theta})
=\frac c2(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta})
=c\cos\theta.
\]
As $\theta$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$, $c\cos\theta$ runs over the segment
$[-c,c]$.

The foci of the ellipse
\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1
\]
are at $\pm\sqrt{a^2-b^2}=\pm c$, so this is exactly the segment $I$ from
the statement.
:::

<1>3. The outer boundary circle $|z|=R$ is mapped onto the ellipse $E$.
::: {.proof}
For $z=Re^{i\theta}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(z)
&=\frac c2\left(Re^{i\theta}+R^{-1}e^{-i\theta}\right)\\
&=\frac c2(R+R^{-1})\cos\theta
+i\frac c2(R-R^{-1})\sin\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]
By <1>1 this becomes
\[
F(Re^{i\theta})
=a\cos\theta+ib\sin\theta,
\]
which parametrizes the ellipse with major axis $[-a,a]$ and minor axis
$[-ib,ib]$.
:::

<1>4. The derivative of $F$ does not vanish on $A(R)$.
::: {.proof}
We have
\[
F'(z)=\frac c2\left(1-\frac1{z^2}\right).
\]
Thus $F'(z)=0$ only at $z=\pm1$, both of which lie on the inner boundary
and not in the open annulus $1<|z|<R$.
:::

<1>5. The map $F$ is injective on $A(R)$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $F(z_1)=F(z_2)$. Then
\[
z_1+z_1^{-1}=z_2+z_2^{-1}.
\]
Multiplying by $z_1z_2$ and factoring gives
\[
(z_1-z_2)(z_1z_2-1)=0.
\]
Hence either $z_1=z_2$ or $z_2=1/z_1$.

If $z_1\in A(R)$, then $|z_1|>1$, so
\[
|1/z_1|<1.
\]
Thus the second possibility cannot also lie in $A(R)$. Therefore
$z_1=z_2$, proving injectivity.
:::

<1>6. The image $F(A(R))$ is exactly the annular region $B$ between $I$
and $E$.
::: {.proof}
For every radius $1<r<R$, the same calculation as in <1>3 shows that
$F(|z|=r)$ is the ellipse
\[
x=\frac c2(r+r^{-1})\cos\theta,
\qquad
y=\frac c2(r-r^{-1})\sin\theta,
\]
with foci $\pm c$.

As $r$ increases from $1$ to $R$, the semimajor and semiminor axes
\[
\frac c2(r+r^{-1}),
\qquad
\frac c2(r-r^{-1})
\]
increase continuously from $(c,0)$ to $(a,b)$. Hence these nested confocal
ellipses foliate exactly the region between the focal segment $I$ and the
outer ellipse $E$.

Thus
\[
F(A(R))=B.
\]
:::

<1>7. Therefore $F:A(R)\to B$ is conformal.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, $F$ is locally conformal; by <1>5 it is injective; and by <1>6 its
image is $B$. Hence $F$ is an analytic homeomorphism from $A(R)$ onto $B$.
:::
