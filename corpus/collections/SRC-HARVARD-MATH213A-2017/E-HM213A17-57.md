::: {.problem}
Let
\[
A(R)=\{z:1<|z|<R\}.
\]

(a) Give an explicit analytic covering map
\[
\pi:\mathbb H\to A(R).
\]

(b) Compute the hyperbolic metric on $A(R)$.

(c) Show that $|z|=\sqrt R$ is a closed geodesic in $A(R)$, and compute its hyperbolic length.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $L=\log R$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

An explicit universal covering is
\[
\boxed{
\pi(\zeta)
=\exp\!\left(-\frac{iL}{\pi}\Log\zeta\right),
\qquad \zeta\in\mathbb H,}
\]
where $0<\arg\zeta<\pi$.

::: pf-proof

The principal logarithm on $\mathbb H$ maps it conformally to the horizontal
strip
\[
0<\Im u<\pi.
\]
Multiplication by $-iL/\pi$ maps this strip conformally to
\[
V=\{w:0<\Re w<L\}.
\]
Finally, $w\mapsto e^w$ is a covering $V\to A(R)$, since
\[
1<|e^w|=e^{\Re w}<e^L=R,
\]
with deck transformations $w\mapsto w+2\pi i k$. Their composition is the
displayed covering map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The hyperbolic metric on the strip $V=\{0<\Re w<L\}$ is
\[
\rho_V(w)
=\frac{\pi}{L}
\frac{|dw|}{\sin(\pi\Re w/L)}.
\]

::: pf-proof

The map
\[
F(w)=\exp\!\left(\frac{i\pi w}{L}\right)
\]
sends $V$ conformally onto $\mathbb H$. If $w=u+iv$, then
\[
|F'(w)|=\frac{\pi}{L}|F(w)|,
\qquad
\Im F(w)=|F(w)|\sin(\pi u/L).
\]
Pulling back the metric $|d\zeta|/\Im\zeta$ on $\mathbb H$ therefore gives
\[
F^*\rho_{\mathbb H}
=\frac{\pi}{L}
\frac{|dw|}{\sin(\pi u/L)}.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The hyperbolic metric on $A(R)$ is
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{A(R)}(z)
=\frac{\pi}{\log R}
\frac{|dz|}
{|z|\sin\!\left(\pi\log|z|/\log R\right)}.}
\]

::: pf-proof

Use the covering $z=e^w$ from $V$ to $A(R)$. Then
\[
|dw|=\frac{|dz|}{|z|},
\qquad
\Re w=\log|z|.
\]
Substituting these identities into step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives exactly the displayed metric.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The line $\Re w=L/2$ is a geodesic in $V$.

::: pf-proof

Under the conformal isometry $F$ from step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
\[
w=\frac L2+iv
\quad\longmapsto\quad
F(w)=i e^{-\pi v/L},
\]
which is the positive imaginary axis. The latter is a hyperbolic geodesic in
$\mathbb H$, so its inverse image is a geodesic in $V$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

This geodesic descends to the closed curve $|z|=\sqrt R$ in the
annulus.

::: pf-proof

Under $z=e^w$, the line $\Re w=L/2$ satisfies
\[
|z|=e^{L/2}=\sqrt R.
\]
The deck translation $w\mapsto w+2\pi i$ identifies one period of the line,
so its image is a closed geodesic in $A(R)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Its hyperbolic length is
\[
\boxed{\frac{2\pi^2}{\log R}}.
\]

::: pf-proof

On $\Re w=L/2$, the sine factor in step [](#s2){.pf-ref} equals $1$. One period is
$w=L/2+iv$, $0\le v\le2\pi$. Hence
\[
\operatorname{length}
=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\pi}{L}\,dv
=\frac{2\pi^2}{L}
=\frac{2\pi^2}{\log R}.
\]

:::
