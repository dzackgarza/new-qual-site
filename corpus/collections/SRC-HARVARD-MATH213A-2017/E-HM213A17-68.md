::: {.problem}
Prove that there is no nonzero holomorphic function
\[
f:\Delta\to\Delta
\]
whose zeros include
\[
a_n=1-\frac1{n+1},
\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots.
\]

Hint: consider $f(0)/B_n(0)$, where $B_n:\Delta\to\Delta$ is a proper map of degree $n$ with zeros at $a_1,\ldots,a_n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $a\in(0,1)$, write
\[
\beta_a(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-az}.
\]

<1>1. If $h:\Delta\to\Delta$ is holomorphic and $h(a)=0$, then
\[
\frac{h(z)}{\beta_a(z)}
\]
extends holomorphically across $z=a$ and has modulus at most $1$ on
$\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
The quotient has a removable singularity at $a$ because both numerator and
denominator vanish there. Let $\psi_a=\beta_a^{-1}$ and define
\[
H(\zeta)=h(\psi_a(\zeta)).
\]
Then $H:\Delta\to\Delta$ and $H(0)=0$. Schwarz's lemma gives
\[
|H(\zeta)|\le|\zeta|.
\]
Substituting $\zeta=\beta_a(z)$ yields
\[
|h(z)|\le|\beta_a(z)|,
\]
which is the desired bound for the quotient.
:::

<1>2. For
\[
B_n(z)=\prod_{k=1}^n\beta_{a_k}(z),
\]
the quotient $f/B_n$ is holomorphic on $\Delta$ and has modulus at most
$1$.
::: {.proof}
The function $f$ vanishes at $a_1$. By <1>1, dividing by
$\beta_{a_1}$ leaves a holomorphic disk-bounded function. It still vanishes
at $a_2,\ldots,a_n$, because the removed factor is nonzero at those distinct
points. Repeat <1>1 successively for $a_2,\ldots,a_n$. The resulting function
is exactly $f/B_n$ and has modulus at most $1$.
:::

<1>3. One has
\[
|B_n(0)|=\frac1{n+1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
a_k=1-\frac1{k+1}=\frac{k}{k+1}
\]
and $\beta_{a_k}(0)=-a_k$,
\[
|B_n(0)|
=\prod_{k=1}^n a_k
=\prod_{k=1}^n\frac{k}{k+1}
=\frac1{n+1}.
\]
:::

<1>4. Any disk-bounded holomorphic function with all the zeros $a_k$ must
vanish at $0$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2,
\[
\left|\frac{f(0)}{B_n(0)}\right|\le1.
\]
Using <1>3 gives
\[
|f(0)|\le\frac1{n+1}
\]
for every $n$. Hence $f(0)=0$.
:::

<1>5. If $f$ were not identically zero, divide off its finite-order zero at
$0$ to obtain a contradiction.
::: {.proof}
Assume $f\not\equiv0$, and let $m\ge0$ be the order of its zero at $0$.
By <1>4, $m\ge1$. Repeated application of Schwarz's lemma at the origin shows
that
\[
g(z)=\frac{f(z)}{z^m}
\]
is holomorphic on $\Delta$ and satisfies $|g(z)|\le1$. Moreover
$g(0)\neq0$ by the definition of $m$. Since none of the $a_k$ equals $0$,
the function $g$ still vanishes at every $a_k$.

Applying <1>4 to $g$ now gives $g(0)=0$, a contradiction. Therefore the
only holomorphic map $f:\Delta\to\Delta$ with all the prescribed zeros is
$f\equiv0$.
:::
