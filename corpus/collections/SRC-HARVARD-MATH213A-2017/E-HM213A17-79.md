::: {.problem}
Prove that
\[
\frac{\coth\pi}{1^7}+\frac{\coth(2\pi)}{2^7}+\frac{\coth(3\pi)}{3^7}+\cdots
=\frac{19\pi^7}{56700}.
\]

Hint: you may use
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}
\frac{\cot(\pi z)\coth(\pi z)}{z^7}
=-\frac{19\pi^6}{14175}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Set
\[
h(z)=\frac{\cot(\pi z)\coth(\pi z)}{z^7}.
\]
Besides its pole at $0$, the poles of $h$ are the nonzero points of $\mathbb Z\cup i\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
The poles of $\cot(\pi z)$ are the integers, each simple with residue $1/\pi$. The poles of $\coth(\pi z)$ are the points $ik$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, again each simple with residue $1/\pi$. Away from $0$ these two pole sets are disjoint.
:::

<1>2. For every integer $n\ge1$, the four poles $n,-n,in,-in$ all have residue
\[
\frac{\coth(\pi n)}{\pi n^7}.
\]
::: {.proof}
At $z=n$,
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=n}h
=\frac1\pi\frac{\coth(\pi n)}{n^7}.
\]
Since both $\coth$ and $z^7$ are odd, the same value is obtained at $z=-n$.

At $z=in$, use
\[
\cot(iw)=-i\coth(w),
\qquad
i^7=-i.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=in}h
=\frac1\pi\frac{\cot(\pi in)}{(in)^7}
=\frac1\pi\frac{-i\coth(\pi n)}{-in^7}
=\frac{\coth(\pi n)}{\pi n^7}.
\]
Oddness gives the same value at $z=-in$.
:::

<1>3. Let $Q_N$ be the positively oriented boundary of the square
\[
|\operatorname{Re}z|\le N+\frac12,
\qquad
|\operatorname{Im}z|\le N+\frac12.
\]
Then
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{Q_N}h(z)\,dz=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The boundary of $Q_N$ stays a fixed positive distance from both pole lattices $\mathbb Z$ and $i\mathbb Z$. More explicitly, for $z=x+iy$,
\[
|\cot(\pi z)|^2
=\frac{\cos^2(\pi x)+\sinh^2(\pi y)}{\sin^2(\pi x)+\sinh^2(\pi y)},
\]
and
\[
|\coth(\pi z)|^2
=\frac{\sinh^2(\pi x)+\cos^2(\pi y)}{\sinh^2(\pi x)+\sin^2(\pi y)}.
\]
On the vertical sides $x=\pm(N+1/2)$ the first expression is at most $1$ and the second is uniformly bounded; on the horizontal sides $y=\pm(N+1/2)$ the second expression is at most $1$ and the first is uniformly bounded. Thus for some constant $C$ independent of $N$,
\[
|h(z)|\le \frac{C}{|z|^7}
\le \frac{C}{(N+1/2)^7}
\qquad(z\in Q_N).
\]
The perimeter of $Q_N$ is $8(N+1/2)$, so
\[
\left|\int_{Q_N}h(z)\,dz\right|
\le \frac{8C}{(N+1/2)^6}\longrightarrow0.
\]
:::

<1>4. The desired series equals $19\pi^7/56700$.
::: {.proof}
By the residue theorem and <1>2,
\[
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{Q_N}h(z)\,dz
=\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}h
+\frac4\pi\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\coth(\pi n)}{n^7}.
\]
The series converges absolutely because $\coth(\pi n)$ is bounded and $\sum n^{-7}$ converges. Hence <1>3 and the residue supplied in the hint give
\[
0
=-\frac{19\pi^6}{14175}
+\frac4\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\coth(\pi n)}{n^7}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\coth(\pi n)}{n^7}
=\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{19\pi^6}{14175}
=\boxed{\frac{19\pi^7}{56700}}.
\]
:::
