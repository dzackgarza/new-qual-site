::: {.problem}
Let $\Lambda_1,\Lambda_2\subset\mathbb C$ be lattices.
Suppose
\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_1\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}
=
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_2\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_1\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-6}
=
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_2\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-6}.
\]
Must $\Lambda_1=\Lambda_2$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Yes.

<1>1. The two lattices have the same Weierstrass invariants $g_2$ and $g_3$.
::: {.proof}
For a lattice $\Lambda$, McMullen writes
\[
G_2(\Lambda)
=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-4},
\qquad
G_3(\Lambda)
=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-6},
\]
and the Weierstrass invariants are
\[
g_2(\Lambda)=60G_2(\Lambda),
\qquad
g_3(\Lambda)=140G_3(\Lambda).
\]
The two equalities in the hypothesis therefore imply
\[
g_2(\Lambda_1)=g_2(\Lambda_2),
\qquad
g_3(\Lambda_1)=g_3(\Lambda_2).
\]
Denote the common values by $g_2,g_3$.
:::

<1>2. The pair $(g_2,g_3)$ determines the lattice itself.
::: {.proof}
McMullen's period reconstruction theorem states that the lattice with invariants $g_2,g_3$ is generated by the values
\[
\pm\int_\gamma
\frac{dx}{\sqrt{4x^3-g_2x-g_3}},
\]
where $\gamma$ ranges over oriented loops in the $x$-plane enclosing exactly two roots of
\[
4x^3-g_2x-g_3.
\]
The cubic is nonsingular because it comes from a lattice, so these period integrals are well-defined with the branch chosen continuously near each such loop.

This construction depends only on the numerical pair $(g_2,g_3)$, not on any prior choice of lattice. Since $\Lambda_1$ and $\Lambda_2$ have the same pair by <1>1, the theorem reconstructs the same period subgroup of $\mathbb C$ from each. Hence
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_1=\Lambda_2}.
\]
:::

<1>3. Equivalently, equality can be seen directly from the Weierstrass functions.
::: {.proof}
Both $\wp_{\Lambda_1}$ and $\wp_{\Lambda_2}$ satisfy
\[
(\wp')^2=4\wp^3-g_2\wp-g_3
\]
and have the same normalized principal part
\[
\wp(z)=\frac1{z^2}+O(z^2)
\]
at $0$. The differential equation recursively determines the Laurent series, so the two functions agree near $0$, and hence everywhere by analytic continuation.

Finally, the full period group of $\wp_\Lambda$ is exactly $\Lambda$: every element of $\Lambda$ is a period, while any period $a$ sends the pole at $0$ to a pole at $a$, and the poles of $\wp_\Lambda$ are precisely the lattice points. Thus equality of the two Weierstrass functions also forces
\[
\Lambda_1=\Lambda_2.
\]
:::
:::
