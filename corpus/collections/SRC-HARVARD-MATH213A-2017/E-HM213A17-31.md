::: {.problem}
Prove the identity
\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^{2n}\theta\,d\theta
=2\pi\frac{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)}{2\cdot4\cdot6\cdots(2n)}
\]
by applying the residue theorem to
\[
\int_{S^1}(z+1/z)^{2n}\,\frac{dz}{z}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
I_n=\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^{2n}\theta\,d\theta.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
I_n
=2\pi\frac{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)}
{2\cdot4\cdot6\cdots(2n)}.}
\]

<1>1. On the unit circle,
\[
\int_{S^1}(z+1/z)^{2n}\frac{dz}{z}
=i4^n I_n.
\]
::: {.proof}
Write $z=e^{i\theta}$ with $0\le\theta\le2\pi$. Then
\[
z+\frac1z=e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}=2\cos\theta
\]
and
\[
\frac{dz}{z}=i\,d\theta.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{S^1}(z+1/z)^{2n}\frac{dz}{z}
&=i\int_0^{2\pi}(2\cos\theta)^{2n}\,d\theta\\
&=i4^n I_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>2. The integrand has only one pole inside $S^1$, at $z=0$, and its
residue there is
\[
\binom{2n}{n}.
\]
::: {.proof}
We have
\[
(z+1/z)^{2n}\frac1z
=z^{-2n-1}(1+z^2)^{2n}.
\]
The residue is the coefficient of $z^{-1}$, hence the coefficient of
$z^{2n}$ in $(1+z^2)^{2n}$. By the binomial theorem this coefficient is
\[
\binom{2n}{n}.
\]
:::

<1>3. Hence
\[
I_n=\frac{2\pi}{4^n}\binom{2n}{n}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By the residue theorem and <1>2,
\[
\int_{S^1}(z+1/z)^{2n}\frac{dz}{z}
=2\pi i\binom{2n}{n}.
\]
Comparing with <1>1 and cancelling $i$ gives
\[
4^n I_n=2\pi\binom{2n}{n},
\]
which is the claimed formula.
:::

<1>4. Finally,
\[
\frac1{4^n}\binom{2n}{n}
=\frac{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)}
{2\cdot4\cdot6\cdots(2n)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Indeed,
\[
\frac1{4^n}\binom{2n}{n}
=\frac{(2n)!}{4^n(n!)^2}
=\frac{(1\cdot3\cdots(2n-1))(2\cdot4\cdots2n)}
{(2\cdot4\cdots2n)^2},
\]
because $2\cdot4\cdots2n=2^n n!$. Cancelling one even product gives the
desired ratio. Combining this with <1>3 proves the identity.
:::
:::
