::: {.problem}
Compute the Laurent series centered at $z=0$ such that
\[
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_nz^n
=\frac{1}{z(z-1)(z-2)}
\]
in the region $1<|z|<2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $1<|z|<2$,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{1}{z(z-1)(z-2)}
=-\frac1{2z}
-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}z^{-n}
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+2}}.}
\]

<1>1. The rational function has the partial-fraction decomposition
\[
\frac{1}{z(z-1)(z-2)}
=\frac1{2z}-\frac1{z-1}+\frac1{2(z-2)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The coefficient of $1/z$ is
\[
\left.\frac1{(z-1)(z-2)}\right|_{z=0}=\frac12.
\]
The coefficient of $1/(z-1)$ is
\[
\left.\frac1{z(z-2)}\right|_{z=1}=-1,
\]
and the coefficient of $1/(z-2)$ is
\[
\left.\frac1{z(z-1)}\right|_{z=2}=\frac12.
\]
This gives the stated decomposition.
:::

<1>2. On the region $|z|>1$,
\[
-\frac1{z-1}
=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $|1/z|<1$,
\[
\frac1{z-1}
=\frac1z\frac1{1-z^{-1}}
=\frac1z\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{-k}
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n}.
\]
Multiplying by $-1$ gives the claimed expansion.
:::

<1>3. On the region $|z|<2$,
\[
\frac1{2(z-2)}
=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+2}}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $|z/2|<1$,
\[
\frac1{2(z-2)}
=-\frac14\frac1{1-z/2}
=-\frac14\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac z2\right)^n
=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+2}}.
\]
:::

<1>4. Combining <1>1--<1>3 gives the Laurent series on
$1<|z|<2$.
::: {.proof}
Substitution into the partial fractions gives
\[
\frac{1}{z(z-1)(z-2)}
=\frac1{2z}
-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n}
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+2}}.
\]
The first term combines with the $n=1$ term of the negative-power sum:
\[
\frac1{2z}-\frac1z=-\frac1{2z}.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{1}{z(z-1)(z-2)}
=-\frac1{2z}
-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}z^{-n}
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+2}},
\]
and both geometric series converge precisely under the simultaneous
conditions $|z|>1$ and $|z|<2$.
:::
:::
