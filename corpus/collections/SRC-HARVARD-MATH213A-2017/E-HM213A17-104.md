::: {.problem}
Let $f:\Delta\to\mathbb C$ be holomorphic and suppose
\[
\int_\Delta |f'(z)|^2\,|dz|^2<\infty.
\]
Prove that for almost every $\zeta\in S^1$, the radial limit
\[
F(\zeta)=\lim_{r\to1^-} f(r\zeta)
\]
exists and is finite.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write points of $S^1$ as $\zeta=e^{i\theta}$. Fubini's theorem shows that
for almost every $\theta$, the radial restriction of $f'$ is square-integrable
near $r=1$. Cauchy--Schwarz upgrades this to integrability of the radial
derivative of $f$, which makes the radial values Cauchy as $r\to1^-$.

<1>1. In polar coordinates, the hypothesis is
\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1
|f'(re^{i\theta})|^2 r\,dr\,d\theta<\infty.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $|dz|^2=dA$ and $dA=r\,dr\,d\theta$ in polar coordinates,
\[
\int_\Delta |f'(z)|^2\,dA(z)
=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1
|f'(re^{i\theta})|^2r\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
The left side is finite by hypothesis.
:::

<1>2. For almost every $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$,
\[
\int_{1/2}^1|f'(re^{i\theta})|^2\,dr<\infty.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and Fubini's theorem, for almost every $\theta$,
\[
\int_0^1|f'(re^{i\theta})|^2r\,dr<\infty.
\]
For $r\in[1/2,1)$ we have $r\ge1/2$, so
\[
\int_{1/2}^1|f'(re^{i\theta})|^2\,dr
\le
2\int_{1/2}^1|f'(re^{i\theta})|^2r\,dr<\infty.
\]
:::

<1>3. For every $\theta$ satisfying <1>2,
\[
\int_{1/2}^1|f'(re^{i\theta})|\,dr<\infty.
\]
::: {.proof}
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{1/2}^1|f'(re^{i\theta})|\,dr
&\le
\left(\int_{1/2}^1|f'(re^{i\theta})|^2\,dr\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_{1/2}^1 1\,dr\right)^{1/2}\\
&<\infty
\end{aligned}
\]
by <1>2.
:::

<1>4. For such a $\theta$, the radial path
\[
r\longmapsto f(re^{i\theta})
\]
is Cauchy as $r\to1^-$.
::: {.proof}
Its derivative is
\[
\frac d{dr}f(re^{i\theta})
=e^{i\theta}f'(re^{i\theta}).
\]
Hence, for $1/2<r<s<1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|f(se^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})|
&\le\int_r^s|f'(te^{i\theta})|\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
By <1>3, the function $t\mapsto|f'(te^{i\theta})|$ is integrable on
$[1/2,1)$. Therefore the right-hand side tends to $0$ uniformly as
$r,s\to1^-$. Thus the radial values form a Cauchy family in $\mathbb C$.
:::

<1>5. For almost every $\zeta\in S^1$, the radial limit exists and is
finite.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, the conclusions of <1>3 and <1>4 hold for almost every angle
$\theta$. Since $\mathbb C$ is complete, the Cauchy family
\[
f(re^{i\theta})
\]
has a finite limit as $r\to1^-$. Writing $\zeta=e^{i\theta}$ gives
\[
F(\zeta)=\lim_{r\to1^-}f(r\zeta)\in\mathbb C
\]
for almost every $\zeta\in S^1$.
:::
