::: {.problem}
Suppose
\[
(x,y)=(\wp(z),\wp'(z))
\]
satisfies
\[
y^2=4x^3+ax+b,
\qquad a,b\in\mathbb R,
\]
and the cubic $4x^3+ax+b$ has exactly one real root.
What can you conclude about the shape of the lattice $\Lambda$ defining $\wp$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The lattice is rhombic rather than rectangular.
More precisely, after choosing a primitive positive real period $\alpha$, one can choose a second period of the form
\[
\boxed{
\beta=\frac{\alpha}{2}+i\gamma,
\qquad \gamma>0,
}
\]
so that
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z\alpha\oplus\mathbb Z\beta.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z\beta\oplus\mathbb Z\overline\beta,
\]
and these two generators have equal length; a fundamental parallelogram is therefore a rhombus symmetric about the real axis.

<1>1. The lattice is invariant under complex conjugation.
::: {.proof}
The Weierstrass equation in McMullen's normalization is
\[
y^2=4x^3-g_2x-g_3.
\]
Comparing with
\[
y^2=4x^3+ax+b,
\qquad a,b\in\mathbb R,
\]
gives
\[
g_2=-a\in\mathbb R,
\qquad
g_3=-b\in\mathbb R.
\]

For the conjugate lattice
\[
\overline\Lambda=\{\overline\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda\},
\]
absolute convergence of the Eisenstein sums gives
\[
g_2(\overline\Lambda)=\overline{g_2(\Lambda)}=g_2(\Lambda),
\qquad
g_3(\overline\Lambda)=\overline{g_3(\Lambda)}=g_3(\Lambda).
\]
The preceding reconstruction result shows that the pair $(g_2,g_3)$ determines the lattice uniquely.
Hence
\[
\overline\Lambda=\Lambda.
\]
:::

<1>2. Any conjugation-invariant lattice has either rectangular or rhombic shape.
::: {.proof}
Since $\Lambda$ has rank two and is not contained in the imaginary axis, there is some $\lambda\in\Lambda$ with nonzero real part.
Then
\[
\lambda+\overline\lambda=2\operatorname{Re}\lambda
\]
is a nonzero real lattice vector.
Thus
\[
\Lambda\cap\mathbb R=\mathbb Z\alpha
\]
for a unique primitive generator $\alpha>0$.

Choose $\beta\in\Lambda$ with $\operatorname{Im}\beta>0$ whose class generates
\[
\Lambda/\mathbb Z\alpha\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
and replace $\beta$ by $\beta+k\alpha$ so that
\[
0\le\operatorname{Re}\beta<\alpha.
\]
Because $\overline\beta\in\Lambda$, write
\[
\overline\beta=m\alpha+n\beta,
\qquad m,n\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Comparing imaginary parts gives
\[
n=-1.
\]
Hence
\[
\beta+\overline\beta=m\alpha,
\]
or
\[
2\operatorname{Re}\beta=m\alpha.
\]
The normalization $0\le\operatorname{Re}\beta<\alpha$ forces
\[
m=0\quad\text{or}\quad m=1.
\]

If $m=0$, then $\operatorname{Re}\beta=0$, so $\alpha$ is real and $\beta$ is purely imaginary: the lattice is rectangular.

If $m=1$, then
\[
\operatorname{Re}\beta=\frac\alpha2,
\]
and
\[
\overline\beta=\alpha-\beta.
\]
Thus
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z\beta\oplus\mathbb Z\overline\beta,
\]
with $|\beta|=|\overline\beta|$, giving a rhombic fundamental parallelogram.
:::

<1>3. The rectangular case is impossible under the one-real-root hypothesis.
::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z\alpha\oplus\mathbb Z\beta,
\qquad
\alpha\in\mathbb R,
\quad
\beta\in i\mathbb R.
\]
McMullen's real-lattice analysis then shows that $\wp$ is real on the horizontal and vertical half-period lines.
In particular the three nonzero order-two points
\[
\frac\alpha2,
\qquad
\frac\beta2,
\qquad
\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}
\]
have real $\wp$-values
\[
e_1,e_2,e_3.
\]
These are the three distinct roots of
\[
4x^3-g_2x-g_3
=4x^3+ax+b.
\]
Hence the cubic would have three real roots, contrary to the assumption that it has exactly one.

Therefore the only remaining case in <1>2 is the rhombic one, proving the asserted shape of $\Lambda$.
