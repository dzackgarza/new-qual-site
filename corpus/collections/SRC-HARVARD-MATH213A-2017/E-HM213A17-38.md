::: {.problem}
Use the preceding two exercises to prove that a uniform limit of harmonic functions on a domain $U\subset\mathbb C$ is harmonic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $u_n$ be harmonic on $U$ and suppose $u_n\to u$ uniformly. We show
that every point of $U$ has a disk on which $u$ is the real part of a
holomorphic function.

<1>1. Fix $z_0\in U$ and choose $R>0$ such that
\[
\overline{D(z_0,R)}\subset U.
\]
For each $n$, choose a harmonic conjugate $v_n$ of $u_n$ on
$D(z_0,R)$, normalized by
\[
v_n(z_0)=0,
\]
and set
\[
F_n=u_n+i v_n.
\]
::: {.proof}
The disk $D(z_0,R)$ is simply connected. Every harmonic function on a
simply connected planar domain has a harmonic conjugate, unique up to an
additive real constant. Choosing that constant so that $v_n(z_0)=0$
makes $F_n$ holomorphic on the disk.
:::

<1>2. Let
\[
D_1=D(z_0,R/2).
\]
For $n,m$ define
\[
c_{nm}=u_n(z_0)-u_m(z_0)
\]
and
\[
G_{nm}=F_n-F_m-c_{nm}.
\]
Then $G_{nm}$ is holomorphic on $D(z_0,R)$ and satisfies
$G_{nm}(z_0)=0$.
::: {.proof}
Holomorphicity is immediate from <1>1. At $z_0$ the imaginary parts of
$F_n$ and $F_m$ both vanish, while their real-part difference is exactly
$c_{nm}$. Hence
\[
G_{nm}(z_0)=0.
\]
:::

<1>3. The preceding $L^2$ exercise implies
\[
\int_{D_1}|\operatorname{Im}G_{nm}|^2\,|dz|^2
=
\int_{D_1}|\operatorname{Re}G_{nm}|^2\,|dz|^2.
\]
Moreover, both sides tend to $0$ as $n,m\to\infty$.
::: {.proof}
After translating $z_0$ to $0$ and rescaling $D_1$ to the unit disk,
the preceding exercise applies to $G_{nm}$ because $G_{nm}(z_0)=0$.

Set
\[
\varepsilon_{nm}
=\sup_{z\in D_1}|u_n(z)-u_m(z)|.
\]
Uniform convergence of $u_n$ implies $\varepsilon_{nm}\to0$. Since
$|c_{nm}|\le\varepsilon_{nm}$,
\[
|\operatorname{Re}G_{nm}(z)|
=|(u_n-u_m)(z)-c_{nm}|
\le2\varepsilon_{nm}
\]
on $D_1$. Therefore
\[
\int_{D_1}|\operatorname{Re}G_{nm}|^2\,|dz|^2
\le4\,\operatorname{Area}(D_1)\,\varepsilon_{nm}^2
\longrightarrow0.
\]
The equality from the preceding exercise gives the same conclusion for
the imaginary part.
:::

<1>4. Consequently
\[
\int_{D_1}|G_{nm}(z)|\,|dz|^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>3,
\[
\int_{D_1}|G_{nm}|^2\,|dz|^2
=
\int_{D_1}
\left(|\operatorname{Re}G_{nm}|^2
+|\operatorname{Im}G_{nm}|^2\right)|dz|^2
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz then gives
\[
\int_{D_1}|G_{nm}|\,|dz|^2
\le
\operatorname{Area}(D_1)^{1/2}
\left(\int_{D_1}|G_{nm}|^2\,|dz|^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
which tends to zero.
:::

<1>5. On the smaller disk
\[
D_2=D(z_0,R/4),
\]
one has
\[
\sup_{D_2}|G_{nm}|\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply the preceding pointwise-bound exercise to the holomorphic function
$G_{nm}$ on $D_1$. It gives
\[
|G_{nm}(z)|
\le
\frac{1}{\pi\,d(z,\partial D_1)^2}
\int_{D_1}|G_{nm}(w)|\,|dw|^2.
\]
For $z\in D_2$,
\[
d(z,\partial D_1)\ge R/4.
\]
The integral tends to zero by <1>4, so the displayed bound tends to zero
uniformly on $D_2$.
:::

<1>6. The sequence $F_n$ is uniformly Cauchy on $D_2$.
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
F_n-F_m=G_{nm}+c_{nm},
\]
<1>5 gives uniform convergence of the first term to zero, while
\[
|c_{nm}|
\le\sup_U|u_n-u_m|\longrightarrow0
\]
because $u_n$ converges uniformly. Hence
\[
\sup_{D_2}|F_n-F_m|\longrightarrow0.
\]
:::

<1>7. The limit $u$ is harmonic on $D_2$, and therefore on all of $U$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>6, $F_n$ converges uniformly on $D_2$ to a function $F$. A uniform
limit of holomorphic functions is holomorphic, so $F$ is holomorphic on
$D_2$. Since
\[
\operatorname{Re}F_n=u_n\longrightarrow u,
\]
we have
\[
\operatorname{Re}F=u
\]
on $D_2$. The real part of a holomorphic function is harmonic, hence $u$
is harmonic near $z_0$. Since $z_0\in U$ was arbitrary, $u$ is harmonic
throughout $U$.
:::
