::: {.problem}
Let $T\subset\mathbb C$ be a square.
Find a formula, which may involve an integral, for a holomorphic covering map
\[
f:\mathbb H\longrightarrow\mathbb C\setminus T.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
E=\{w:|w|>1\}.
\]
Choose on $E$ the branch
\[
q(w)=\sqrt{1-w^{-4}}
\]
with $q(w)\to1$ as $w\to\infty$, and define
\[
\Phi(w)
=w+\int_\infty^w\bigl(q(\zeta)-1\bigr)\,d\zeta.
\]
Then $\Phi$ maps $E$ conformally onto the complement of a square $S$. If
\[
A(z)=az+b,
\qquad a\ne0,
\]
is an orientation-preserving similarity carrying $S$ onto the prescribed square $T$, then
\[
\boxed{
f(z)=A\!\left(\Phi(e^{-iz})\right)
}
\]
is the required covering map $\mathbb H\to\mathbb C\setminus T$.

<1>1. The formula for $\Phi$ is single-valued and satisfies
\[
\Phi'(w)=\sqrt{1-w^{-4}},
\qquad
\Phi(w)=w+O(w^{-3})
\]
at infinity.
::: {.proof}
The chosen square root is holomorphic on $E$: in the coordinate $u=1/w$, it is the branch of
\[
\sqrt{1-u^4}
\]
equal to $1$ at $u=0$. Moreover
\[
q(w)-1=O(w^{-4}).
\]
Its Laurent expansion contains only powers $w^{-4k}$, so in particular its residue is zero. Hence its integral around the generator of $\pi_1(E)$ vanishes, and the integral defining $\Phi$ is path-independent. Differentiation gives the displayed formula for $\Phi'$, and integration of $O(w^{-4})$ gives the stated asymptotic expansion.
:::

<1>2. The unit circle is mapped by $\Phi$ to four straight segments meeting at right angles.
::: {.proof}
On the first quadrant of the unit circle, write
\[
w=e^{i\theta},
\qquad 0<\theta<\frac\pi2.
\]
Then
\[
1-w^{-4}
=1-e^{-4i\theta}
=2i e^{-2i\theta}\sin(2\theta).
\]
For the branch fixed in <1>1,
\[
q(e^{i\theta})
=\sqrt{2\sin(2\theta)}\,e^{i(\pi/4-\theta)}.
\]
Since $dw=i e^{i\theta}\,d\theta$,
\[
d\Phi
=q(w)\,dw
=\sqrt{2\sin(2\theta)}\,e^{3\pi i/4}\,d\theta.
\]
Thus the image of this quarter-circle is a straight segment.

Also
\[
q(iw)=q(w).
\]
The functions $\Phi(iw)$ and $i\Phi(w)$ have the same derivative with respect to $w$, and their asymptotics at infinity agree; hence
\[
\Phi(iw)=i\Phi(w).
\]
Therefore the four quarter-arcs map to four congruent straight segments obtained successively by rotation through $\pi/2$. They form a square, denoted $S$.
:::

<1>3. The map
\[
\Phi:E\longrightarrow\mathbb C\setminus S
\]
is a conformal homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
This is precisely the exterior Schwarz--Christoffel map for a square. Indeed, each vertex of the exterior square has interior angle
\[
\frac{3\pi}{2},
\]
so the Schwarz--Christoffel derivative has exponent
\[
\frac{3}{2}-1=\frac12
\]
at each of the four prevertices $1,i,-1,-i$. Hence, up to an affine transformation, its derivative is
\[
\prod_{\omega^4=1}\left(1-\frac{\omega}{w}\right)^{1/2}
=\sqrt{1-w^{-4}},
\]
with the normalization at infinity chosen in <1>1. McMullen's Schwarz--Christoffel theorem therefore identifies $\Phi$ as the conformal map from the exterior disk onto the exterior of the square found in <1>2.
:::

<1>4. The map
\[
p(z)=e^{-iz}
\]
is a holomorphic covering map from $\mathbb H$ onto $E$.
::: {.proof}
If $z=x+iy\in\mathbb H$, then
\[
|e^{-iz}|=e^y>1,
\]
so $p(\mathbb H)\subset E$. Conversely, if $w\in E$, choose a logarithm
\[
\log w=\log|w|+i\arg w.
\]
Then
\[
z=i\log w
\]
has imaginary part $\log|w|>0$ and satisfies $e^{-iz}=w$. The derivative
\[
p'(z)=-ie^{-iz}
\]
never vanishes, and the fibers differ by integer multiples of $2\pi$. Thus $p$ is the standard universal covering of the exterior disk.
:::

<1>5. The displayed formula is a covering of the required square complement.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, $\Phi$ is a conformal homeomorphism from $E$ onto $\mathbb C\setminus S$, and the similarity $A$ is a conformal homeomorphism
\[
A:\mathbb C\setminus S\longrightarrow\mathbb C\setminus T.
\]
Composing these homeomorphisms with the covering $p$ from <1>4 gives
\[
f=A\circ\Phi\circ p:\mathbb H\longrightarrow\mathbb C\setminus T,
\]
which is again a holomorphic covering map.
:::
