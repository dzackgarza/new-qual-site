::: {.problem}
Show that the figure-eight knot cannot arise as the link obtained by intersecting an analytic curve in $\mathbb C^2$ through the origin with a sufficiently small $3$-sphere centered at the origin.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A knot arising from a singular analytic plane-curve germ has Alexander polynomial whose zeros are roots of unity.
::: {.proof}
This is a standard consequence of the Milnor fibration and the monodromy theorem for isolated plane-curve singularities; see J. Milnor, *Singular Points of Complex Hypersurfaces*, cited as [Mil1] in the source notes.

Indeed, after replacing a defining equation by its reduced equation, a singular plane-curve germ has an isolated singularity at the origin. For a sufficiently small sphere, its link complement is fibered over $S^1$ by the Milnor fibration. If $h$ denotes the monodromy of the fiber, then, up to multiplication by a unit $\pm t^m$,
\[
\Delta_K(t)=\det\bigl(tI-h_*\mid H_1(F;\mathbb Z)\bigr).
\]
The monodromy theorem states that every eigenvalue of $h_*$ is a root of unity. Hence every zero of $\Delta_K$ is a root of unity.

If the analytic curve is smooth at the origin instead, the holomorphic implicit-function theorem makes it locally equivalent to a coordinate axis, whose link is the unknot. Thus any nontrivial knot arising from an analytic curve is covered by the singular case above.
:::

<1>2. The Alexander polynomial of the figure-eight knot is
\[
\Delta_{4_1}(t)=t^2-3t+1
\]
up to multiplication by a unit $\pm t^m$.
::: {.proof}
A standard genus-one Seifert surface for the figure-eight knot has Seifert matrix
\[
V=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(V-tV^T)
&=
\det\begin{pmatrix}
1-t&t\\
-1&t-1
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=-(t^2-3t+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since the Alexander polynomial is defined only up to $\pm t^m$, this gives the stated normalization.
:::

<1>3. The zeros of $\Delta_{4_1}$ are not roots of unity.
::: {.proof}
The quadratic formula gives
\[
\lambda_\pm=\frac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\lambda_+>1.
\]
Every root of unity has absolute value $1$, so $\lambda_+$ is not a root of unity.
:::

<1>4. Hence the figure-eight knot is not the link of an analytic plane curve singularity.
::: {.proof}
If the figure-eight knot arose as such a link, <1>1 would force every zero of its Alexander polynomial to be a root of unity. This contradicts <1>2--<1>3. The smooth case was already excluded in <1>1 because it gives the unknot. Therefore no analytic curve germ in $\mathbb C^2$ has the figure-eight knot as its sufficiently small link.
:::
:::
