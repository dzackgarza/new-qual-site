::: {.problem}
Fix $\tau\in\mathbb H$ and define
\[
\theta(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}
\exp(2\pi i n^2\tau)\exp(2\pi i n z).
\]
Show that $\theta$ is an entire function of order $2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
\[
\tau=u+iv,
\qquad v>0.
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The defining series converges uniformly on compact subsets of
$\mathbb C$.

::: pf-proof

If $|\Im z|\le R$, then the $n$th summand has modulus
\[
\left|
e^{2\pi i n^2\tau}e^{2\pi i n z}
\right|
=e^{-2\pi v n^2-2\pi n\Im z}
\le e^{-2\pi v n^2+2\pi|n|R}.
\]
The sequence on the right is summable over $n\in\mathbb Z$, because the
negative quadratic term dominates the linear term. The Weierstrass
$M$-test therefore gives uniform convergence on every horizontal compact
strip, hence on every compact subset of $\mathbb C$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently $\theta$ is entire.

::: pf-proof

Every summand is entire in $z$, and by step [](#s1){.pf-ref} the series converges uniformly on
compact subsets. Therefore its sum is entire.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

There is a constant $C=C(\tau)>0$ such that
\[
M(r)\le C\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi r^2}{2v}\right),
\]
where $M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|\theta(z)|$.

::: pf-proof

For $|z|\le r$,
\[
|\theta(z)|
\le\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}
e^{-2\pi v n^2+2\pi|n|r}.
\]
Completing the square gives
\[
-2\pi v n^2+2\pi|n|r
=-2\pi v\left(|n|-\frac r{2v}\right)^2
+\frac{\pi r^2}{2v}.
\]
Thus
\[
|\theta(z)|
\le e^{\pi r^2/(2v)}
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}
e^{-2\pi v(|n|-r/(2v))^2}.
\]
The shifted Gaussian sum is bounded uniformly in the real shift
$r/(2v)$, so it is at most a constant $C(v)$. This proves the estimate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The order of $\theta$ is at most $2$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
\[
\log M(r)=O(r^2).
\]
Hence
\[
\limsup_{r\to\infty}
\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}\le2.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The function $\theta$ is not identically zero.

::: pf-proof

The series is $1$-periodic. On the real interval $[0,1]$ it converges
uniformly, so termwise integration is valid:
\[
\int_0^1\theta(x)\,dx
=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{2\pi i n^2\tau}
\int_0^1e^{2\pi i n x}\,dx
=1.
\]
Thus $\theta\not\equiv0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The theta series satisfies
\[
\theta(z+2\tau)
=e^{-2\pi i(z+\tau)}\theta(z).
\]

::: pf-proof

Starting from the definition,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta(z+2\tau)
&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}
e^{2\pi i\tau(n^2+2n)}e^{2\pi i n z}\\
&=e^{-2\pi i(\tau+z)}
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}
e^{2\pi i\tau(n+1)^2}e^{2\pi i(n+1)z}\\
&=e^{-2\pi i(z+\tau)}\theta(z).
\end{aligned}
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

For every integer $k\ge1$,
\[
\theta(z+2k\tau)
=e^{-2\pi i(kz+k^2\tau)}\theta(z).
\]

::: pf-proof

Iterate step [](#s6){.pf-ref}. The product of the successive factors is
\[
\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}
e^{-2\pi i(z+(2j+1)\tau)}
=e^{-2\pi i(kz+k^2\tau)}.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

The order of $\theta$ is at least $2$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, choose $z_0$ with $\theta(z_0)\neq0$. From step [](#s7){.pf-ref},
\[
|\theta(z_0+2k\tau)|
=e^{2\pi(k\Im z_0+k^2v)}|\theta(z_0)|.
\]
For all sufficiently large $k$, this is at least $e^{\pi v k^2}$. Put
\[
r_k=|z_0+2k\tau|.
\]
Since $r_k\le C_1k$ for a constant $C_1$, one gets
\[
\log M(r_k)
\ge \pi v k^2
\ge C_2r_k^2
\]
for some $C_2>0$ and all large $k$. Therefore
\[
\limsup_{r\to\infty}
\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}\ge2.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $\theta$ has order exactly $2$.

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s8){.pf-ref}.

:::
