::: {.problem}
Give an example of a homeomorphism
\[
f:\Delta\to\Delta
\]
that is distance-decreasing for the hyperbolic metric but does not extend continuously to $S^1$.

Hint: in polar coordinates, try
\[
f(r,\theta)=(R(r),T(r)+\theta),
\]
with $T(r)\to\infty$ as $r\to1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
It is convenient to use hyperbolic polar radius rather than Euclidean
radius. Put
\[
t=2\operatorname{artanh}r
=\log\frac{1+r}{1-r},
\qquad r=\tanh(t/2).
\]
Define
\[
S(t)=\operatorname{arsinh}t
\]
and
\[
T(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\le t\le2,\\[1mm]
\frac12\log(t/2),&t\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
In hyperbolic polar coordinates define
\[
F(t,\theta)=(S(t),\theta+T(t)).
\]
Equivalently, in Euclidean polar coordinates,
\[
f(re^{i\theta})
=\tanh\!\left(\frac12\operatorname{arsinh}
\left(2\operatorname{artanh}r\right)\right)
e^{\,i(\theta+T(2\operatorname{artanh}r))}.
\]

<1>1. In the coordinate $t=2\operatorname{artanh}r$, the hyperbolic metric
on $\Delta$ is
\[
ds^2=dt^2+\sinh^2t\,d\theta^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
McMullen's disk metric is
\[
ds=\frac{2|dz|}{1-|z|^2}.
\]
In Euclidean polar coordinates,
\[
|dz|^2=dr^2+r^2d\theta^2.
\]
Since
\[
r=\tanh(t/2),
\qquad
\frac{dr}{dt}=\frac12(1-r^2),
\]
the radial part becomes
\[
\frac{4dr^2}{(1-r^2)^2}=dt^2.
\]
Also
\[
\frac{2r}{1-r^2}=\sinh t,
\]
so the angular part is $\sinh^2t\,d\theta^2$.
:::

<1>2. The map $F$ defines a homeomorphism $\Delta\to\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
The function
\[
S(t)=\operatorname{arsinh}t
\]
is a strictly increasing homeomorphism $[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$. The
function $T$ is continuous and vanishes near $0$.

Thus away from the origin the map
\[
(t,\theta)\longmapsto(S(t),\theta+T(t))
\]
has the continuous inverse
\[
(s,\phi)\longmapsto
\left(\sinh s,\phi-T(\sinh s)\right).
\]
At $t=0$, both source and target angular coordinates collapse to the origin,
and $S(t)\to0$, so the map and its inverse extend continuously there. Hence
$F$ is a homeomorphism of the disk.
:::

<1>3. For $0<t<2$, the differential of $F$ has hyperbolic operator norm
at most $1$.
::: {.proof}
Use the orthonormal frames
\[
e_t=\partial_t,
\qquad
e_\theta=\frac1{\sinh t}\partial_\theta
\]
in the source and the analogous frame at radius $S(t)$ in the target.

For $t<2$, $T'(t)=0$. Therefore the differential is diagonal in these
frames, with factors
\[
S'(t)=\frac1{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\le1
\]
and
\[
\frac{\sinh S(t)}{\sinh t}
=\frac{t}{\sinh t}\le1,
\]
because $\sinh S(t)=t$ and $\sinh t\ge t$. Hence the operator norm is at
most $1$.
:::

<1>4. For $t>2$, the differential of $F$ has hyperbolic operator norm
strictly less than $1$.
::: {.proof}
For $t>2$,
\[
T'(t)=\frac1{2t}.
\]
In the same orthonormal frames as in <1>3, the differential matrix is
\[
A(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac1{\sqrt{1+t^2}}&0\\[3mm]
\sinh(S(t))T'(t)&\dfrac{\sinh S(t)}{\sinh t}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac1{\sqrt{1+t^2}}&0\\[3mm]
\dfrac12&\dfrac{t}{\sinh t}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For $t\ge2$,
\[
\frac1{1+t^2}\le\frac15.
\]
Also
\[
\sinh t
\ge t+\frac{t^3}{6},
\]
so
\[
\frac{t}{\sinh t}
\le\frac1{1+t^2/6}
\le\frac35.
\]
Thus the squared Frobenius norm satisfies
\[
\|A(t)\|_{\mathrm F}^2
\le\frac15+\frac14+\frac9{25}
=\frac{81}{100}<1.
\]
The operator norm is at most the Frobenius norm, hence
\[
\|A(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1.
\]
:::

<1>5. The homeomorphism $F$ is distance-decreasing for the hyperbolic
metric.
::: {.proof}
The map is locally Lipschitz and piecewise $C^1$; the only seam is $t=2$.
By <1>3 and <1>4, its differential has hyperbolic operator norm at most $1$
almost everywhere.

Therefore the hyperbolic length of the image of any rectifiable path is at
most the length of the original path. Taking infima over paths joining two
points gives
\[
d_{\Delta}(F(x),F(y))\le d_{\Delta}(x,y).
\]
Thus $F$ is distance-decreasing.
:::

<1>6. The map does not extend continuously to $S^1$.
::: {.proof}
Along the radial ray $\theta=0$, let $t\to\infty$. Since
\[
S(t)=\operatorname{arsinh}t\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
the Euclidean radius of $F(t,0)$ tends to $1$. But
\[
T(t)=\frac12\log(t/2)\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
so its angle keeps winding around the circle.

More explicitly, take
\[
t_n=2e^{4\pi n}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
s_n=2e^{4\pi n+2\pi}.
\]
Then
\[
T(t_n)=2\pi n,
\qquad
T(s_n)=2\pi n+\pi.
\]
Both source sequences approach the same boundary point $1\in S^1$ along the
positive real radius, but their images satisfy
\[
F(t_n,0)\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
F(s_n,0)\longrightarrow-1.
\]
Hence there is no limit at the boundary point $1$, so no continuous extension
to $S^1$ exists.
:::
