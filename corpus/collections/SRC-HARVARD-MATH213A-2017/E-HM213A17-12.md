::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ be given in polar coordinates by
\[
f(r,\theta)=(r^\alpha,n\theta),
\]
where $\alpha>0$ and $n\in\mathbb Z$.
Compute $\partial f/\partial z$ and $\partial f/\partial\overline z$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $z=re^{i\theta}\neq0$,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}
=\frac{\alpha+n}{2}r^{\alpha-1}e^{i(n-1)\theta},
\qquad
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline z}
=\frac{\alpha-n}{2}r^{\alpha-1}e^{i(n+1)\theta}.}
\]
At the origin, in the source's $C^1$ convention for Wirtinger derivatives,
these extend as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{case}&f_z(0)&f_{\overline z}(0)\\ \hline
\alpha>1&0&0\\
\alpha=1,\ n=1&1&0\\
\alpha=1,\ n=-1&0&1.
\end{array}
\]
In all remaining cases, $f$ is not $C^1$ at $0$, so the Wirtinger
derivatives there are not defined in that convention.

<1>1. For $z=re^{i\theta}\neq0$, the Wirtinger operators are
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
=\frac{e^{-i\theta}}2
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
-\frac{i}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right),
\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline z}
=\frac{e^{i\theta}}2
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
+\frac{i}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right).
\]
::: {.proof}
From
\[
x=r\cos\theta,
\qquad
y=r\sin\theta,
\]
one obtains
\[
\partial_x
=\cos\theta\,\partial_r
-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}\,\partial_\theta,
\qquad
\partial_y
=\sin\theta\,\partial_r
+\frac{\cos\theta}{r}\,\partial_\theta.
\]
Substituting these into
\[
\partial_z=\frac12(\partial_x-i\partial_y),
\qquad
\partial_{\overline z}=\frac12(\partial_x+i\partial_y)
\]
gives the two formulas.
:::

<1>2. For $z\neq0$, the derivatives have the boxed values above.
::: {.proof}
The polar description means
\[
f(re^{i\theta})=r^\alpha e^{in\theta}.
\]
Therefore
\[
f_r=\alpha r^{\alpha-1}e^{in\theta},
\qquad
f_\theta=inr^\alpha e^{in\theta}.
\]
Applying <1>1 gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_z
&=\frac{e^{-i\theta}}2
\left(\alpha r^{\alpha-1}e^{in\theta}
-\frac{i}{r}inr^\alpha e^{in\theta}\right)\\
&=\frac{\alpha+n}{2}r^{\alpha-1}e^{i(n-1)\theta},
\end{aligned}
\]
and similarly
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_{\overline z}
&=\frac{e^{i\theta}}2
\left(\alpha r^{\alpha-1}e^{in\theta}
+\frac{i}{r}inr^\alpha e^{in\theta}\right)\\
&=\frac{\alpha-n}{2}r^{\alpha-1}e^{i(n+1)\theta}.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>3. If $\alpha>1$, then $f$ is $C^1$ at $0$ with real differential
$Df_0=0$, so $f_z(0)=f_{\overline z}(0)=0$.
::: {.proof}
Since $|f(z)|=|z|^\alpha$,
\[
\frac{|f(z)-f(0)|}{|z|}=|z|^{\alpha-1}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so $f$ is real differentiable at $0$ with derivative zero. The formulas in
<1>2 satisfy
\[
|f_z(z)|\le \frac{|\alpha+n|}{2}|z|^{\alpha-1},
\qquad
|f_{\overline z}(z)|\le \frac{|\alpha-n|}{2}|z|^{\alpha-1},
\]
and both tend to zero. Hence the first derivatives extend continuously to
$0$, so $f$ is $C^1$ there and both Wirtinger derivatives vanish.
:::

<1>4. If $\alpha=1$, then $f$ is real differentiable at $0$ exactly for
$n=1$ or $n=-1$.
::: {.proof}
For $z=re^{i\theta}$,
\[
f(z)=r e^{in\theta}.
\]
If $f$ were real differentiable at $0$, its differential would be a real
linear map $L:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$, hence
\[
L(z)=az+b\overline z
\]
for some $a,b\in\mathbb C$. Dividing
\[
f(re^{i\theta})=L(re^{i\theta})+o(r)
\]
by $r$ and letting $r\to0$ gives
\[
e^{in\theta}=ae^{i\theta}+be^{-i\theta}
\]
for every $\theta$. The Fourier modes on the two sides can agree only when
$n=1$ or $n=-1$. In those cases $f(z)=z$ and $f(z)=\overline z$,
respectively, yielding the stated Wirtinger derivatives.
:::

<1>5. If $0<\alpha<1$, then $f$ is not real differentiable at $0$.
::: {.proof}
Real differentiability would imply $|f(z)|=O(|z|)$ as $z\to0$. But
\[
\frac{|f(z)|}{|z|}=|z|^{\alpha-1}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
which is impossible. Thus the source's $C^1$ Wirtinger derivatives are not
defined at the origin in this case.
:::
:::
