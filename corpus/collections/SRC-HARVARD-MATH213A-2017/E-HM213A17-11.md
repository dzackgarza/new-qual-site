::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be one-to-one and analytic on a neighborhood of the unit circle $S^1\subset\mathbb C$.
Relate
\[
\int_{S^1}\overline{f(z)}\,f'(z)\,dz
\]
to the area of the region enclosed by $f(S^1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $A$ denote the ordinary (unsigned) area of the bounded component of
$\mathbb C\setminus f(S^1)$. Then
\[
\left|\int_{S^1}\overline{f(z)}\,f'(z)\,dz\right|=2A.
\]
More precisely, if $f(S^1)$ is given the orientation induced from the
counterclockwise orientation of $S^1$, then
\[
\int_{S^1}\overline{f(z)}\,f'(z)\,dz
=2iA_{\mathrm{sgn}},
\]
where $A_{\mathrm{sgn}}=A$ for counterclockwise orientation and
$A_{\mathrm{sgn}}=-A$ for clockwise orientation.

<1>1. The restriction of $f$ to $S^1$ parametrizes a $C^1$ Jordan curve.
::: {.proof}
Because $f$ is one-to-one on a neighborhood of $S^1$, its restriction to
$S^1$ is injective. Since $S^1$ is compact and $\mathbb C$ is Hausdorff,
\[
f|_{S^1}:S^1\longrightarrow f(S^1)
\]
is a homeomorphism. Hence $f(S^1)$ is a Jordan curve.

Moreover $f'(z)\neq0$ on $S^1$. Indeed, if $f'(z_0)=0$, then the local
power-series expansion at $z_0$ has the form
\[
f(z)-f(z_0)=(z-z_0)^m h(z),
\qquad m\ge2,\quad h(z_0)\neq0,
\]
so sufficiently near $z_0$ the map has local multiplicity $m$ and cannot be
one-to-one. Thus the parametrization
\[
\gamma(t)=f(e^{it}),\qquad 0\le t\le2\pi,
\]
is regular and $C^1$.
:::

<1>2. The given integral is the line integral
\[
\int_{f(S^1)}\overline w\,dw
\]
with the orientation induced by $f$.
::: {.proof}
Along the parametrization $w=f(z)$ one has $dw=f'(z)\,dz$. Therefore
\[
\int_{S^1}\overline{f(z)}\,f'(z)\,dz
=\int_{f(S^1)}\overline w\,dw.
\]
:::

<1>3. If $w=x+iy$, then
\[
\int_{f(S^1)}\overline w\,dw
=i\int_{f(S^1)}(x\,dy-y\,dx).
\]
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
\overline w\,dw
=(x-iy)(dx+i\,dy)
=x\,dx+y\,dy+i(x\,dy-y\,dx),
\]
the real part integrates to zero around the closed curve because
\[
x\,dx+y\,dy=\frac12\,d(x^2+y^2).
\]
Hence only the displayed imaginary term remains.
:::

<1>4. Green's theorem gives
\[
\int_{f(S^1)}(x\,dy-y\,dx)=2A_{\mathrm{sgn}}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $\Omega$ be the bounded component enclosed by the Jordan curve
$f(S^1)$. For the counterclockwise boundary orientation, Green's theorem
applied to the $1$-form $x\,dy-y\,dx$ gives
\[
\int_{\partial\Omega}(x\,dy-y\,dx)
=\iint_\Omega
\left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial x}
-\frac{\partial(-y)}{\partial y}\right)\,dx\,dy
=2\iint_\Omega dx\,dy
=2A.
\]
Reversing the orientation changes the line integral by a sign. Thus for the
orientation induced by $f$ the value is $2A_{\mathrm{sgn}}$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\int_{S^1}\overline{f(z)}\,f'(z)\,dz=2iA_{\mathrm{sgn}},
\qquad
A=\frac12\left|\int_{S^1}\overline{f(z)}\,f'(z)\,dz\right|.
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>2--<1>4. The absolute-value formula removes the orientation
ambiguity. This ambiguity is genuine under the stated hypotheses: $f(z)=z$
induces the counterclockwise orientation, whereas $f(z)=1/z$, which is
holomorphic and one-to-one on an annular neighborhood of $S^1$, induces the
clockwise orientation.
:::
