::: {.problem}
Use the Little Picard theorem to prove that there is a constant $M>0$ such that every holomorphic map
\[
f:\Delta\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}
\]
satisfies
\[
\|f'\|\le M,
\]
where the derivative is measured from the hyperbolic metric on $\Delta$ to the spherical metric on the range.

Hint: if the derivative were unbounded over such maps, rescale to obtain a nonconstant entire map $\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose, to the contrary, that no universal bound exists. We will use McMullen's rescaling argument to produce a nonconstant entire function omitting both $0$ and $1$, contradicting Little Picard.

<1>1. We may choose holomorphic maps
\[
f_n:\Delta\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}
\]
for which the hyperbolic-to-spherical derivative norm attains a maximum
\[
M_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
D_f(z)
=\frac{|f'(z)|(1-|z|^2)}{1+|f(z)|^2}.
\]
This is the derivative norm from the hyperbolic metric on $\Delta$ to the spherical metric, in McMullen's normalization.

If no common bound exists, choose maps and points with $D_f$ arbitrarily large. Replacing such a map $f$ by
\[
z\longmapsto f(rz),
\qquad r<1
\]
with $r$ sufficiently close to $1$ preserves an arbitrarily large value of the derivative norm at the corresponding interior point. The new map is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline\Delta$, and its factor $1-|z|^2$ forces $D_f(z)\to0$ as $|z|\to1$. Thus $D_f$ attains its maximum at an interior point. In this way we obtain $f_n$ with maxima $M_n\to\infty$.
:::

<1>2. After moving a maximizing point to $0$ and rescaling, there are maps
\[
g_n:\Delta(M_n)\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}
\]
such that
\[
g_n^\#(0)=1
\]
and, for $|z|<M_n$,
\[
g_n^\#(z)
\le\frac{1}{1-|z|^2/M_n^2},
\]
where
\[
g^\#=\frac{|g'|}{1+|g|^2}
\]
is the Euclidean-to-spherical derivative.
::: {.proof}
Let $p_n$ maximize $D_{f_n}$, and choose an automorphism
\[
A_n\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta)
\]
with $A_n(0)=p_n$. Since the hyperbolic metric is invariant under disk automorphisms, for
\[
F_n=f_n\circ A_n
\]
we have
\[
D_{F_n}(0)=M_n,
\qquad
D_{F_n}(u)\le M_n
\quad(u\in\Delta).
\]
At the origin, the hyperbolic factor is $1$, so
\[
F_n^\#(0)=M_n.
\]

Now define
\[
g_n(z)=F_n(z/M_n),
\qquad |z|<M_n.
\]
Then
\[
g_n^\#(0)=\frac{F_n^\#(0)}{M_n}=1.
\]
For $u=z/M_n$, the maximality inequality says
\[
F_n^\#(u)(1-|u|^2)\le M_n.
\]
Hence
\[
g_n^\#(z)
=\frac{F_n^\#(u)}{M_n}
\le\frac{1}{1-|z|^2/M_n^2}.
\]
:::

<1>3. A subsequence converges spherically and locally uniformly on $\mathbb C$ to a nonconstant meromorphic map
\[
g:\mathbb C\to\widehat{\mathbb C}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Fix $R>0$. For all sufficiently large $n$, $R<M_n/2$, and <1>2 gives
\[
g_n^\#(z)\le\frac{1}{1-1/4}=\frac43
\qquad(|z|\le R).
\]
McMullen's compactness statement for maps with uniformly bounded spherical derivative therefore makes $(g_n)$ normal on every fixed disk. A diagonal subsequence converges spherically and locally uniformly on all of $\mathbb C$ to a meromorphic map $g$.

Moreover spherical derivatives converge at the normalized basepoint under this locally uniform convergence, so
\[
g^\#(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_n^\#(0)=1.
\]
Thus $g$ is nonconstant.
:::

<1>4. The limit $g$ omits $0$, $1$, and $\infty$.
::: {.proof}
Every $g_n$ is holomorphic and takes values in
\[
\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}.
\]
McMullen's omitted-value lemma states that a value omitted by every member of a spherically convergent sequence is also omitted by a nonconstant limit. Therefore
\[
g(\mathbb C)\cap\{0,1,\infty\}=\varnothing.
\]
In particular, omission of $\infty$ means that $g$ is an entire function
\[
g:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}.
\]
It is nonconstant by <1>3.
:::

<1>5. This contradicts Little Picard, so a universal $M$ exists.
::: {.proof}
The Little Picard theorem says that a nonconstant entire function can omit at most one complex value. The entire function produced in <1>4 omits both $0$ and $1$, a contradiction. Hence the assumed unbounded sequence cannot exist. Therefore there is a constant $M>0$ such that every holomorphic
\[
f:\Delta\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}
\]
satisfies
\[
\|f'\|\le M.
\]
:::
