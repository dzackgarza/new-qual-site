::: {.problem}
Evaluate
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x^6}{(1+x^4)^2}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The value of the integral is
\[
\boxed{\frac{3\pi\sqrt2}{8}}.
\]

<1>1. Let
\[
F(z)=\frac{z^6}{(1+z^4)^2}.
\]
On the upper semicircle $|z|=R$, the integral of $F$ tends to zero as
$R\to\infty$.
::: {.proof}
For $R$ sufficiently large,
\[
|1+z^4|\ge |z|^4-1=R^4-1
\]
on $|z|=R$. Hence
\[
|F(z)|\le \frac{R^6}{(R^4-1)^2}=O(R^{-2}).
\]
The semicircle has length $\pi R$, so the $ML$ estimate gives an
$O(R^{-1})$ bound for its contribution, which tends to zero.
:::

<1>2. The poles of $F$ in the open upper half-plane are the two double
poles
\[
a_1=e^{i\pi/4},
\qquad
a_2=e^{3i\pi/4}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The poles are the roots of
\[
z^4=-1=e^{i(\pi+2\pi k)},
\]
namely
\[
e^{i(\pi/4+k\pi/2)},\qquad k=0,1,2,3.
\]
Exactly the first two have positive imaginary part. Since every root of
$1+z^4$ is simple and the denominator is squared, these are double poles of
$F$.
:::

<1>3. If $a^4=-1$, then
\[
\operatorname{Res}(F,a)=\frac{3}{16a}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Factor
\[
1+z^4=(z-a)q(z).
\]
Then
\[
F(z)=\frac{1}{(z-a)^2}\frac{z^6}{q(z)^2},
\]
so, because the pole is double,
\[
\operatorname{Res}(F,a)
=\left.\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{z^6}{q(z)^2}\right)\right|_{z=a}.
\]
From
\[
(1+z^4)'=4z^3
\]
we get
\[
q(a)=4a^3.
\]
Differentiating
\[
1+z^4=(z-a)q(z)
\]
twice and evaluating at $z=a$ gives
\[
12a^2=2q'(a),
\qquad
q'(a)=6a^2.
\]
Put
\[
h(z)=\frac{z^6}{q(z)^2}.
\]
Then
\[
h(a)=\frac{a^6}{(4a^3)^2}=\frac1{16}
\]
and logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
\frac{h'(a)}{h(a)}
=\frac6a-2\frac{q'(a)}{q(a)}
=\frac6a-2\frac{6a^2}{4a^3}
=\frac3a.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Res}(F,a)=h'(a)=\frac{3}{16a}.
\]
:::

<1>4. The sum of the upper-half-plane residues is
\[
-\frac{3\sqrt2}{16}i.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>3,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}(F,a_1)+\operatorname{Res}(F,a_2)
&=\frac3{16}\left(e^{-i\pi/4}+e^{-3i\pi/4}\right)\\
&=\frac3{16}\left(
\frac{1-i}{\sqrt2}+\frac{-1-i}{\sqrt2}
\right)\\
&=-\frac{3\sqrt2}{16}i.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>5. Hence
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x^6}{(1+x^4)^2}\,dx
=\frac{3\pi\sqrt2}{8}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Integrate $F$ over the positively oriented upper-half-plane semicircle of
radius $R$. By the residue theorem,
\[
\int_{-R}^{R}F(x)\,dx
+\int_{\text{semicircle}}F(z)\,dz
=2\pi i\left(-\frac{3\sqrt2}{16}i\right).
\]
By <1>1 the semicircle contribution tends to zero. Letting $R\to\infty$
therefore gives
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x^6}{(1+x^4)^2}\,dx
=2\pi i\left(-\frac{3\sqrt2}{16}i\right)
=\frac{3\pi\sqrt2}{8}.
\]
:::
