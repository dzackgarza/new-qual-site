::: {.problem}
Let
\[
E(a,b)=\left\{x+iy:\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}\le1\right\},
\qquad a>b>0.
\]
Find an explicit conformal homeomorphism
\[
\mathbb C\setminus\Delta\longrightarrow\mathbb C\setminus E(a,b).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
\alpha=\frac{a+b}{2},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{a-b}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
F(z)=\alpha z+\frac{\beta}{z}
=\frac{a+b}{2}z+\frac{a-b}{2z}
}
\]
is a conformal homeomorphism from the exterior disk onto the exterior of $E(a,b)$.

<1>1. The unit circle is mapped homeomorphically onto the boundary ellipse.
::: {.proof}
For $z=e^{i\theta}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(e^{i\theta})
&=\alpha(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)
 +\beta(\cos\theta-i\sin\theta)\\
&=(\alpha+\beta)\cos\theta
 +i(\alpha-\beta)\sin\theta\\
&=a\cos\theta+ib\sin\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the standard one-to-one parametrization of
\[
\partial E(a,b)
=\left\{x+iy:\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1\right\}.
\]
:::

<1>2. The map $F$ is conformal and injective on $\{z:|z|>1\}$.
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
\frac{\beta}{\alpha}=\frac{a-b}{a+b}<1,
\]
the equation
\[
F'(z)=\alpha-\frac{\beta}{z^2}=0
\]
would force
\[
|z|^2=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}<1.
\]
Thus $F'$ has no zero for $|z|>1$.

For injectivity, suppose $F(z_1)=F(z_2)$. Then
\[
0
=\alpha(z_1-z_2)+\beta\left(\frac1{z_1}-\frac1{z_2}\right)
=(z_1-z_2)\left(\alpha-\frac{\beta}{z_1z_2}\right).
\]
If $z_1\ne z_2$, then
\[
z_1z_2=\frac{\beta}{\alpha},
\]
which is impossible when $|z_1|,|z_2|>1$. Hence $z_1=z_2$.
:::

<1>3. The image is exactly $\mathbb C\setminus E(a,b)$.
::: {.proof}
The rational map $F$ extends continuously to the exterior closed disk in the Riemann sphere,
\[
\{z:|z|\ge1\}\cup\{\infty\},
\]
with $F(\infty)=\infty$. The injectivity argument in <1>2 remains valid when $|z_1|,|z_2|\ge1$, and <1>1 gives injectivity on the boundary circle. Thus this extension is a homeomorphism onto its image.

By <1>1 its boundary is $\partial E(a,b)$. The Jordan curve theorem gives exactly two complementary components of this ellipse. The interior of the exterior closed disk on the sphere,
\[
\{z:|z|>1\}\cup\{\infty\},
\]
contains $\infty$, so it maps onto the complementary component containing $\infty$, namely
\[
\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus E(a,b).
\]
Restricting to finite points gives
\[
F:\mathbb C\setminus\Delta
\longrightarrow
\mathbb C\setminus E(a,b)
\]
as required.
:::
