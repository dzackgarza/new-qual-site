::: {.problem}
Give an example of an entire function of finite order $\rho$ such that
\[
\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}
\]
does not converge to $\rho$ as $r\to\infty$, where
\[
M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers $N_k$ so rapidly
that
\[
N_{k+1}>e^{N_k^2}
\qquad(k\ge1),
\]
and define
\[
\boxed{
f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{N_k}}{N_k!}.}
\]
Then $f$ has order $1$, but
\[
\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}
\]
has limsup $1$ and liminf $0$.

<1>1. The series defining $f$ converges to an entire function.
::: {.proof}
The series is a subseries of the exponential power series. For $|z|\le R$,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\left|\frac{z^{N_k}}{N_k!}\right|
\le
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{R^n}{n!}
=e^R.
\]
Thus the series converges uniformly on every compact disk, so $f$ is entire.
:::

<1>2. The order of $f$ is at most $1$.
::: {.proof}
All coefficients of $f$ are nonnegative. Hence for $|z|=r$,
\[
|f(z)|
\le\sum_k\frac{r^{N_k}}{N_k!}
\le e^r.
\]
Therefore
\[
M(r)\le e^r,
\]
and so
\[
\limsup_{r\to\infty}
\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}
\le1.
\]
:::

<1>3. Along $r=2N_k$, the logarithmic growth quotient tends to $1$.
::: {.proof}
At $r=2N_k$, the $k$-th term gives
\[
M(2N_k)
\ge\frac{(2N_k)^{N_k}}{N_k!}.
\]
Since $N_k!\le N_k^{N_k}$,
\[
M(2N_k)\ge2^{N_k}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\log\log M(2N_k)
\ge\log(N_k\log2),
\]
and therefore
\[
\liminf_{k\to\infty}
\frac{\log\log M(2N_k)}{\log(2N_k)}\ge1.
\]
Together with <1>2, this shows that the order of $f$ is exactly
\[
\rho(f)=1.
\]
:::

<1>4. Set
\[
r_k=\sqrt{N_{k+1}}.
\]
The contribution to $M(r_k)$ from terms with index $j\le k$ satisfies
\[
\sum_{j\le k}\frac{r_k^{N_j}}{N_j!}
\le k r_k^{N_k}.
\]
::: {.proof}
For large $k$ we have $r_k>1$, and $N_j\le N_k$ for $j\le k$. Since
$N_j!\ge1$,
\[
\frac{r_k^{N_j}}{N_j!}
\le r_k^{N_j}
\le r_k^{N_k}.
\]
Summing the first $k$ terms gives the bound.
:::

<1>5. The tail with $j\ge k+1$ is bounded independently of $k$ for all
sufficiently large $k$.
::: {.proof}
We use the elementary factorial estimate
\[
n!\ge (n/e)^n.
\]
For $j\ge k+1$ we have $N_j\ge N_{k+1}=r_k^2$, and hence
\[
\frac{r_k^{N_j}}{N_j!}
\le\left(\frac{e r_k}{N_j}\right)^{N_j}
\le\left(\frac{e}{r_k}\right)^{N_j}.
\]
For large $k$, $e/r_k<1/2$, so
\[
\sum_{j\ge k+1}\frac{r_k^{N_j}}{N_j!}
\le\sum_{j\ge k+1}2^{-N_j}<1.
\]
:::

<1>6. Along $r=r_k$, the logarithmic growth quotient tends to $0$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4 and <1>5,
\[
M(r_k)\le k r_k^{N_k}+1.
\]
Thus, for large $k$,
\[
\log M(r_k)
\le N_k\log r_k+O(\log k),
\]
and therefore
\[
\log\log M(r_k)
\le \log N_k+\log\log r_k+O(1).
\]

But
\[
\log r_k=\frac12\log N_{k+1}>\frac12N_k^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{\log N_k}{\log r_k}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{\log\log r_k}{\log r_k}\longrightarrow0.
\]
It follows that
\[
\frac{\log\log M(r_k)}{\log r_k}\longrightarrow0.
\]
:::

<1>7. The required quotient does not converge to the order.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3,
\[
\limsup_{r\to\infty}
\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}=1=\rho(f),
\]
while <1>6 gives a sequence $r_k\to\infty$ along which the quotient tends
to $0$. Therefore the quotient has no limit, as required.
:::
