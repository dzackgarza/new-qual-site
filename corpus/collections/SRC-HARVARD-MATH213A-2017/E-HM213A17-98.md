::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=z+a_2z^2+a_3z^3+\cdots
\]
be univalent on $\Delta$, and suppose
\[
\sup_{z\in\Delta}|f(z)|\le M.
\]
Show that
\[
|a_2|\le2\sqrt{1-\frac1M}.
\]

Hint: the normalized exterior map obtained from $f(z^{-2})^{-1/2}$ omits the disk of radius $M^{-1/2}$; apply the area theorem.
:::

::: {.solution}
Construct the normalized exterior map suggested in the hint. Its first
negative Laurent coefficient is $-a_2/2$, while boundedness of $f$ forces a
disk of area $\pi/M$ into the omitted set. The area theorem then gives the
desired coefficient estimate.

<1>1. There is a holomorphic nonvanishing function $h$ on $\Delta$ with
$h(0)=1$ such that
\[
f(w)=w h(w)^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $f$ is univalent and $f(0)=0$, the origin is the only zero of $f$ in
$\Delta$, and it is simple because $f'(0)=1$. Thus
\[
q(w)=\frac{f(w)}{w}
\]
extends holomorphically to $\Delta$, satisfies $q(0)=1$, and never vanishes.
Because $\Delta$ is simply connected, $q$ admits a holomorphic square root
$h$ with $h(0)=1$. Hence
\[
f(w)=w h(w)^2.
\]
:::

<1>2. For $|z|>1$, define
\[
G(z)=\frac{z}{h(z^{-2})}.
\]
Then $G$ is a normalized univalent exterior map, so $G\in\Sigma$.
::: {.proof}
The function $G$ is holomorphic for $|z|>1$ and near infinity satisfies
$G(z)=z+O(z^{-1})$, because $h(0)=1$.

Moreover, by <1>1,
\[
G(z)^2
=\frac{z^2}{h(z^{-2})^2}
=\frac1{f(z^{-2})}.
\]
Suppose $G(z_1)=G(z_2)$. Squaring and using the preceding identity gives
\[
f(z_1^{-2})=f(z_2^{-2}).
\]
Since $f$ is univalent,
\[
z_1^{-2}=z_2^{-2},
\]
so $z_2=\pm z_1$. But the definition shows $G(-z)=-G(z)$, and $G$ never
vanishes on $|z|>1$. Thus $G(z_1)=G(-z_1)$ is impossible, so $z_2=z_1$.
Hence $G$ is univalent and normalized at infinity, i.e. $G\in\Sigma$.
:::

<1>3. The Laurent expansion of $G$ at infinity begins
\[
G(z)=z-\frac{a_2}{2z}+O(z^{-3}).
\]
::: {.proof}
From
\[
f(w)=w+a_2w^2+a_3w^3+\cdots,
\]
we obtain
\[
f(z^{-2})
=z^{-2}\left(1+a_2z^{-2}+a_3z^{-4}+\cdots\right).
\]
Using the branch fixed in <1>2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(z)
&=f(z^{-2})^{-1/2}\\
&=z\left(1+a_2z^{-2}+a_3z^{-4}+\cdots\right)^{-1/2}\\
&=z\left(1-\frac{a_2}{2}z^{-2}+O(z^{-4})\right)\\
&=z-\frac{a_2}{2z}+O(z^{-3}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, in the notation
\[
G(z)=z+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{b_n}{z^n},
\]
we have
\[
b_1=-\frac{a_2}{2}.
\]
:::

<1>4. The omitted compact set $K(G)=\mathbb C\setminus G(\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\overline\Delta)$ contains the disk
\[
B(0,M^{-1/2}).
\]
::: {.proof}
For $|z|>1$, put $w=z^{-2}\in\Delta$. By hypothesis,
\[
|f(w)|\le M.
\]
Using $G(z)^2=1/f(w)$ from <1>2,
\[
|G(z)|=|f(w)|^{-1/2}\ge M^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus no point with modulus less than $M^{-1/2}$ belongs to the image of
$G$, so
\[
B(0,M^{-1/2})\subset K(G).
\]
Consequently
\[
\operatorname{area}K(G)\ge\frac{\pi}{M}.
\]
:::

<1>5. The area theorem gives
\[
\operatorname{area}K(G)
\le \pi\left(1-\frac{|a_2|^2}{4}\right).
\]
::: {.proof}
McMullen's area theorem says that for
\[
G(z)=z+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{b_n}{z^n}\in\Sigma,
\]
\[
\operatorname{area}K(G)
=\pi\left(1-\sum_{n\ge1}n|b_n|^2\right).
\]
Discarding all terms except $n=1$ gives
\[
\operatorname{area}K(G)
\le\pi(1-|b_1|^2).
\]
By <1>3, $|b_1|=|a_2|/2$, proving the claim.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
|a_2|\le2\sqrt{1-\frac1M}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Combining <1>4 and <1>5,
\[
\frac\pi M
\le\pi\left(1-\frac{|a_2|^2}{4}\right).
\]
Canceling $\pi$ and rearranging gives
\[
\frac{|a_2|^2}{4}\le1-\frac1M.
\]
Taking square roots yields
\[
|a_2|\le2\sqrt{1-\frac1M}.
\]
:::
