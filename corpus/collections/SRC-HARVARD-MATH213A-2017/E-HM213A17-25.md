::: {.problem}
Let $E\subset(-1,1)$ be a compact set of zero linear measure, and let
\[
f:\Delta\setminus E\to\mathbb C
\]
be a bounded analytic function.
Show that $f$ extends to an analytic function on the whole unit disk $\Delta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $|f|\le M$ on $\Delta\setminus E$. We prove that every point of $E$
has a neighborhood on which $f$ is the restriction of a holomorphic
function.

<1>1. Fix $x_0\in E$. There is a disk
\[
D=D(x_0,r),\qquad \overline D\subset\Delta,
\]
such that $\partial D\cap E=\varnothing$.
::: {.proof}
The circle centered at the real point $x_0$ meets the real axis only at
$x_0-r$ and $x_0+r$. Since $E$ has linear measure zero, we may choose $r>0$
small enough that $\overline{D(x_0,r)}\subset\Delta$ and neither
$x_0-r$ nor $x_0+r$ belongs to $E$. Hence $\partial D$ is disjoint from
$E$.
:::

<1>2. Put $K=E\cap\overline D$. For every $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently
small, $K$ can be covered by finitely many pairwise disjoint closed squares
$Q_1,\ldots,Q_N\subset D$ whose boundaries avoid $E$ and whose total
perimeter is less than $4\varepsilon$.
::: {.proof}
Because $K\subset\mathbb R$ has linear measure zero, it has a finite cover
by open intervals with total length less than $\varepsilon$. Taking the
connected components of their union gives finitely many pairwise disjoint
open intervals $I_j$ with
\[
K\subset\bigcup_j I_j,
\qquad
\sum_j |I_j|<\varepsilon.
\]
Their endpoints may be chosen outside $E$, since $\mathbb R\setminus E$ is
dense. As $K$ is a positive distance from $\partial D$, after taking
$\varepsilon$ small we may replace each $I_j$ by the closed square $Q_j$
centered on the real axis whose horizontal side has length $|I_j|$.
The squares remain inside $D$, are disjoint because their horizontal
projections are disjoint, and their boundaries do not meet $E$: their
horizontal sides lie off the real axis and their vertical sides meet the
real axis only at the endpoints of $I_j$. Finally,
\[
\sum_j\operatorname{length}(\partial Q_j)
=4\sum_j|I_j|<4\varepsilon.
\]
:::

<1>3. For every $z\in D\setminus E$,
\[
f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D}
\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta.
\]
::: {.proof}
Fix $z\in D\setminus E$. Since $K$ is compact and $z\notin K$,
\[
\delta=\operatorname{dist}(z,K)>0.
\]
Choose the squares in <1>2 so small that they all lie in the
$\delta/2$-neighborhood of $K$ and none contains $z$. On
\[
\Omega=D\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^N Q_j
\]
the function
\[
\zeta\longmapsto \frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}
\]
is holomorphic except for its simple pole at $z$, whose residue is $f(z)$.
The residue theorem therefore gives
\[
2\pi i f(z)
=\int_{\partial D}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta
-\sum_{j=1}^N
\int_{\partial Q_j}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta.
\]
For $\zeta\in Q_j$ we have $|\zeta-z|\ge\delta/2$, so
\[
\left|
\sum_{j=1}^N
\int_{\partial Q_j}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta
\right|
\le
\frac{2M}{\delta}
\sum_{j=1}^N\operatorname{length}(\partial Q_j)
<\frac{8M}{\delta}\varepsilon.
\]
Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ proves the formula.
:::

<1>4. The function
\[
F_D(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D}
\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-w}\,d\zeta
\qquad(w\in D)
\]
is holomorphic on $D$ and agrees with $f$ on $D\setminus E$.
::: {.proof}
Because $\partial D\cap E=\varnothing$, the boundary values of $f$ in the
integral are defined and holomorphic in a neighborhood of $\partial D$.
The Cauchy integral therefore defines a holomorphic function of $w\in D$.
By <1>3, $F_D(w)=f(w)$ for every $w\in D\setminus E$.
:::

<1>5. These local extensions glue to a holomorphic extension of $f$ to all
of $\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
Perform <1>1--<1>4 at every point of $E$. If two resulting disks overlap,
their holomorphic extensions both agree with $f$ on the overlap minus $E$.
That set is dense in the overlap because $E$ has empty interior, so by
continuity the two extensions agree everywhere on the overlap. Hence the
local extensions glue with the original $f$ on $\Delta\setminus E$ to a
single holomorphic function on $\Delta$.
:::
