::: {.problem}
Let $u:\Delta\to\mathbb R$ be smooth and suppose
\[
\Delta u(z)>0
\qquad(z\in\Delta).
\]
Show that $u$ satisfies the maximum principle.
:::

::: {.solution}
The strict inequality $\Delta u>0$ rules out every interior local maximum.
Consequently, on any relatively compact subdomain, the maximum of $u$ is
attained on the boundary.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Suppose, for contradiction, that $u$ has a local maximum at
$z_0=(x_0,y_0)\in\Delta$.

::: pf-proof

At an interior local maximum, the restrictions
\[
x\longmapsto u(x,y_0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y\longmapsto u(x_0,y)
\]
each have a local maximum at $x_0$ and $y_0$, respectively.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

At such a point one must have
\[
u_{xx}(z_0)\le0,
\qquad
u_{yy}(z_0)\le0.
\]

::: pf-proof

This is the ordinary one-variable second-derivative test applied to the
two coordinate restrictions from step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Smoothness of $u$ ensures that
these second derivatives exist.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Hence
\[
\Delta u(z_0)
=u_{xx}(z_0)+u_{yy}(z_0)
\le0,
\]
contradicting the hypothesis $\Delta u(z_0)>0$.

::: pf-proof

Add the two inequalities from step [](#s2){.pf-ref}. The result is incompatible with the
strict positivity of the Laplacian at every point of $\Delta$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $u$ satisfies the maximum principle.

::: pf-proof

The contradiction in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that $u$ has no interior local maximum.
In particular, if $V\Subset\Delta$ is a bounded domain on whose closure
$u$ is considered, continuity gives a maximum on $\overline V$, and that
maximum cannot occur in $V$. Hence
\[
\max_{\overline V}u
=\max_{\partial V}u.
\]
This is the maximum principle for $u$.

:::
