::: {.problem}
Let $\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{\mathbb C})$ be finite.
A rational map
\[
f:\widehat{\mathbb C}\to\widehat{\mathbb C}
\]
is a quotient map for $\Gamma$ if $f(x)=f(y)$ if and only if $\gamma(x)=y$ for some $\gamma\in\Gamma$.

(a) Find a quotient map for the dihedral group $D_{2m}$ generated by
\[
z\mapsto\zeta_m z,
\qquad
z\mapsto 1/z,
\qquad
\zeta_m=e^{2\pi i/m}.
\]

(b) Let $\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{\mathbb C})$ be the subgroup leaving $\{0,1,\infty\}$ invariant.
Give generators for $\Gamma$ and find a quotient map normalized by
\[
f(\infty)=\infty,\qquad f(\zeta_6)=0,\qquad f(-1)=1.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For part (a), a quotient map is
\[
\boxed{F_m(z)=z^m+z^{-m}.}
\]

For part (b), the group is generated by
\[
s(z)=1-z,
\qquad
t(z)=\frac1z,
\]
and the required normalized quotient map is
\[
\boxed{
J(z)=\frac{4(z^2-z+1)^3}{27z^2(1-z)^2}.}
\]

<1>1. We use the following finite-group criterion: if a finite group
$G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{\mathbb C})$ leaves a nonconstant
rational map $F$ invariant and
\[
\deg F=|G|,
\]
then $F$ is a quotient map for $G$.
::: {.proof}
Invariance gives one implication immediately:
\[
y=\gamma(x),\ \gamma\in G
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F(y)=F(x).
\]

For the converse, suppose $F(x)=F(y)=c$. The set of critical values of
$F$ is finite. Also, the set of points with nontrivial $G$-stabilizer is
finite: each nonidentity Möbius transformation has at most two fixed
points, and $G$ is finite. Hence we may choose regular values
\[
c_j\longrightarrow c
\]
avoiding the images of all points with nontrivial stabilizer.

By the local form of a nonconstant holomorphic map, after passing to a
subsequence if necessary there are points
\[
x_j\to x,
\qquad
y_j\to y,
\qquad
F(x_j)=F(y_j)=c_j.
\]
For each $j$, the point $x_j$ has trivial stabilizer, so its orbit has
exactly $|G|$ distinct points. All of them lie in the fiber
$F^{-1}(c_j)$. Since $c_j$ is a regular value and $\deg F=|G|$, that
fiber has exactly $|G|$ points. Therefore
\[
F^{-1}(c_j)=G\cdot x_j,
\]
so $y_j=\gamma_j(x_j)$ for some $\gamma_j\in G$.

Because $G$ is finite, one element $\gamma$ occurs along a subsequence.
Taking limits gives
\[
y=\gamma(x).
\]
Thus the fibers of $F$ are exactly the $G$-orbits.
:::

<1>2. The map
\[
F_m(z)=z^m+z^{-m}
\]
is invariant under the generators
\[
r(z)=\zeta_m z,
\qquad
\iota(z)=1/z
\]
of $D_{2m}$.
::: {.proof}
Since $\zeta_m^m=1$,
\[
F_m(\zeta_m z)
=(\zeta_m z)^m+(\zeta_m z)^{-m}
=z^m+z^{-m}.
\]
Also
\[
F_m(1/z)=z^{-m}+z^m=F_m(z).
\]
Thus $F_m$ is invariant under the whole group generated by $r$ and
$\iota$.
:::

<1>3. The rational map $F_m$ has degree $2m$, equal to
$|D_{2m}|$; hence it is a quotient map.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
F_m(z)=\frac{z^{2m}+1}{z^m}.
\]
The numerator and denominator are coprime, so
\[
\deg F_m=2m.
\]
The dihedral group generated by an order-$m$ rotation and inversion has
$2m$ elements. The criterion in <1>1 now applies.

Equivalently, one can see the fibers directly: if
$F_m(z)=F_m(w)$ and $u=z^m$, $v=w^m$, then
\[
u+u^{-1}=v+v^{-1},
\]
so
\[
(u-v)(uv-1)=0.
\]
Thus $w=\zeta_m^kz$ or $w=\zeta_m^k/z$, exactly the dihedral orbit.
:::

<1>4. The subgroup of Möbius transformations preserving
$\{0,1,\infty\}$ is generated by
\[
s(z)=1-z,
\qquad
t(z)=1/z,
\]
and has order $6$.
::: {.proof}
The map $s$ swaps $0$ and $1$ and fixes $\infty$, while $t$ swaps $0$
and $\infty$ and fixes $1$. These two transpositions generate all
permutations of the three-point set.

A Möbius transformation is uniquely determined by its values on three
distinct points, so the action on $\{0,1,\infty\}$ is faithful. Hence this
subgroup is isomorphic to $S_3$ and has order $6$.
:::

<1>5. The rational function
\[
J(z)=\frac{4(z^2-z+1)^3}{27z^2(1-z)^2}
\]
is invariant under both generators $s$ and $t$.
::: {.proof}
Set
\[
q(z)=z^2-z+1.
\]
For $s(z)=1-z$,
\[
q(1-z)=q(z),
\]
and the denominator $z^2(1-z)^2$ is symmetric under
$z\leftrightarrow1-z$. Hence $J(1-z)=J(z)$.

For $t(z)=1/z$,
\[
q(1/z)=\frac{q(z)}{z^2}
\]
and
\[
\left(\frac1z\right)^2
\left(1-\frac1z\right)^2
=\frac{(1-z)^2}{z^4}.
\]
Therefore
\[
J(1/z)
=\frac{4q(z)^3/z^6}{27(1-z)^2/z^4}
=\frac{4q(z)^3}{27z^2(1-z)^2}
=J(z).
\]
Thus $J$ is invariant under the whole group.
:::

<1>6. The map $J$ has degree $6$, so it is a quotient map for this
$S_3$-group.
::: {.proof}
The numerator has degree $6$ and the denominator degree $4$. They have no
common zero, since the roots of $z^2-z+1$ are neither $0$ nor $1$.
Hence
\[
\deg J=6.
\]
By <1>4 the group also has order $6$, and by <1>5 the map is invariant.
The criterion in <1>1 therefore shows that its fibers are exactly the
group orbits.
:::

<1>7. The quotient map $J$ has the required normalization.
::: {.proof}
Since the numerator has degree two more than the denominator,
\[
J(\infty)=\infty.
\]
For $\zeta_6=e^{\pi i/3}$ one has
\[
\zeta_6^2-\zeta_6+1=0,
\]
so
\[
J(\zeta_6)=0.
\]
Finally,
\[
J(-1)
=\frac{4(1+1+1)^3}{27\cdot1\cdot(1+1)^2}
=\frac{4\cdot27}{27\cdot4}
=1.
\]
:::
