::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal U$ be the set of positive harmonic functions $u$ on the unit disk $\Delta$, normalized by $u(0)=1$.
Show that $\mathcal U$ is compact: every sequence in $\mathcal U$ has a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets of $\Delta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $(u_n)$ be a sequence in $\mathcal U$.

<1>1. For each $n$, choose a harmonic conjugate $v_n$ so that
\[
F_n=u_n+iv_n
\]
is holomorphic on $\Delta$ and $v_n(0)=0$.
::: {.proof}
The disk is simply connected, so every harmonic function on it has a harmonic
conjugate. Adding a real constant to the conjugate does not change
holomorphicity, so normalize by $v_n(0)=0$. Since $u_n(0)=1$,
\[
F_n(0)=1.
\]
Also $\Re F_n=u_n>0$.
:::

<1>2. The family $(F_n)$ is uniformly bounded on every compact subset of
$\Delta$.
::: {.proof}
By the estimate of the preceding exercise, applied to $F_n$,
\[
|F_n(z)|
\le |F_n(0)|\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}
=\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}.
\]
Thus for every $0<r<1$,
\[
\sup_n\sup_{|z|\le r}|F_n(z)|
\le\frac{1+r}{1-r}<\infty.
\]
:::

<1>3. A subsequence $(F_{n_k})$ converges locally uniformly on $\Delta$ to
a holomorphic function $F$.
::: {.proof}
The local bounds from <1>2 make $(F_n)$ a normal family. Equivalently, apply
McMullen's Corollary 1.19 on an exhaustion of the disk by compact subdisks and
take a diagonal subsequence. The resulting subsequence converges uniformly on
every compact subset to a holomorphic limit $F$.
:::

<1>4. The corresponding subsequence $(u_{n_k})$ converges locally uniformly
to $u=\Re F$.
::: {.proof}
Taking real parts preserves uniform convergence, so
\[
u_{n_k}=\Re F_{n_k}\longrightarrow\Re F=u
\]
uniformly on each compact subset. The real part of a holomorphic function is
harmonic, hence $u$ is harmonic.
:::

<1>5. The limit $u$ belongs to $\mathcal U$.
::: {.proof}
Since every $u_{n_k}>0$, local uniform convergence gives $u\ge0$. Also
\[
u(0)=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{n_k}(0)=1.
\]
Thus $u$ is not identically zero. By the minimum principle for harmonic
functions, a nonnegative harmonic function that vanishes at an interior point
would be identically zero; therefore $u>0$ throughout $\Delta$. Hence
$u\in\mathcal U$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $\mathcal U$ is compact for the topology of uniform
convergence on compact subsets.
::: {.proof}
Starting from an arbitrary sequence in $\mathcal U$, <1>3--<1>5 produced a
subsequence converging locally uniformly to another member of $\mathcal U$.
This is the asserted compactness.
:::
:::
