::: {.problem}
For $k\ge0$, let $M_k$ be the space of rational $k$-differentials
\[
f(z)\,dz^k
\]
on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$ whose only poles are at $0$, $1$, and $\infty$, each of order at most $k$.

(a) Determine $\dim M_k$ and give a basis for $M_k$.

(b) Give a finite set of generators for the graded ring
\[
\bigoplus_{k\ge0}M_k.
\]

This is the ring of modular forms for $\Gamma(2)$ in the source's model.
:::

::: {.solution}
For every $k\ge0$,
\[
\boxed{\dim M_k=k+1.}
\]
A basis is
\[
\boxed{
\omega_{k,j}
=\frac{z^j}{z^k(z-1)^k}\,dz^k,
\qquad 0\le j\le k.
}
\]
Moreover, if
\[
u=\frac{dz}{z(z-1)},
\qquad
v=\frac{dz}{z-1},
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\bigoplus_{k\ge0}M_k\cong\mathbb C[u,v],
\qquad \deg u=\deg v=1.}
\]

<1>1. Every element of $M_k$ has the form
\[
\frac{P(z)}{z^k(z-1)^k}\,dz^k
\]
for a polynomial $P$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
\omega=f(z)\,dz^k\in M_k.
\]
At the finite points $0$ and $1$, the differential $dz^k$ is nonvanishing, so the pole-order condition says that $f$ has poles of order at most $k$ at those two points and no other finite poles. Therefore
\[
P(z)=z^k(z-1)^k f(z)
\]
is a rational function with no finite poles. Hence $P$ is a polynomial, and
\[
\omega
=\frac{P(z)}{z^k(z-1)^k}\,dz^k.
\]
:::

<1>2. The condition at $\infty$ is exactly
\[
\deg P\le k.
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the local coordinate
\[
w=\frac1z
\]
near $\infty$. Since
\[
dz=-w^{-2}dw,
\]
we have
\[
z^k(z-1)^k
=\frac{(1-w)^k}{w^{2k}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega
&=\frac{P(1/w)}{(1-w)^k/w^{2k}}
(-1)^k w^{-2k}dw^k\\
&=(-1)^k\frac{P(1/w)}{(1-w)^k}\,dw^k.
\end{aligned}
\]
The factor $(1-w)^{-k}$ is a unit at $w=0$. If $d=\deg P$, then $P(1/w)$ has a pole of order $d$ at $w=0$. Thus the pole order of $\omega$ at $\infty$ is exactly $d$. The requirement that this order be at most $k$ is therefore equivalent to
\[
\deg P\le k.
\]
:::

<1>3. This gives the dimension and basis of $M_k$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and <1>2, the map
\[
P(z)\longmapsto
\frac{P(z)}{z^k(z-1)^k}\,dz^k
\]
is a linear isomorphism from the vector space of polynomials of degree at most $k$ onto $M_k$. The polynomials
\[
1,z,\ldots,z^k
\]
form a basis of that space. Hence
\[
\dim M_k=k+1
\]
and the corresponding basis of $M_k$ is
\[
\omega_{k,j}
=\frac{z^j}{z^k(z-1)^k}\,dz^k,
\qquad0\le j\le k.
\]
:::

<1>4. The two degree-one forms $u$ and $v$ generate every homogeneous piece.
::: {.proof}
Both
\[
u=\frac{dz}{z(z-1)},
\qquad
v=\frac{dz}{z-1}
\]
belong to $M_1$. For $0\le j\le k$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
u^{k-j}v^j
&=\left(\frac{dz}{z(z-1)}\right)^{k-j}
\left(\frac{dz}{z-1}\right)^j\\
&=\frac{z^j}{z^k(z-1)^k}\,dz^k\\
&=\omega_{k,j}.
\end{aligned}
\]
By <1>3 these monomials form a basis of $M_k$. Hence every graded piece, and therefore the whole graded ring, is generated by $u$ and $v$.
:::

<1>5. There is no algebraic relation between $u$ and $v$.
::: {.proof}
Any homogeneous polynomial relation of degree $k$ would have the form
\[
\sum_{j=0}^k c_j u^{k-j}v^j=0.
\]
By <1>4 this is
\[
\sum_{j=0}^k c_j\omega_{k,j}=0.
\]
The basis statement in <1>3 forces every $c_j=0$. A relation in the graded direct sum decomposes into homogeneous relations, so there are no nonzero relations at all. Thus
\[
\bigoplus_{k\ge0}M_k\cong\mathbb C[u,v]
\]
as a graded ring, with both generators in degree $1$.
:::
