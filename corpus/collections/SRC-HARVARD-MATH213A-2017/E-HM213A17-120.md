::: {.problem}
Let $\phi$ be holomorphic, and let $u_1,u_2$ be linearly independent solutions of
\[
u''+\phi u=0.
\]

(a) Show that the Wronskian
\[
W(z)=\det
\begin{pmatrix}
u_1&u_2\\
u_1'&u_2'
\end{pmatrix}
\]
is constant.

(b) Let $f=u_1/u_2$.
Show that
\[
S_f=C\phi
\]
for a universal constant $C$, determine $C$, and explain why $S_f$ is independent of the chosen basis $u_1,u_2$ of the solution space.
:::

::: {.solution}
The Wronskian is constant, and
\[
\boxed{S_f=2\phi}.
\]
Thus $C=2$.

<1>1. The Wronskian
\[
W=u_1u_2'-u_2u_1'
\]
is constant.
::: {.proof}
Differentiate:
\[
\begin{aligned}
W'
&=u_1'u_2'+u_1u_2''-u_2'u_1'-u_2u_1''\\
&=u_1u_2''-u_2u_1''.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since both solutions satisfy
\[
u_j''=-\phi u_j,
\]
we get
\[
W'
=-\phi u_1u_2+\phi u_2u_1
=0.
\]
Hence $W$ is constant. Because $u_1,u_2$ are linearly independent, this constant is nonzero.
:::

<1>2. On every open set where $u_2\ne0$, the quotient $f=u_1/u_2$ satisfies
\[
N_f=-2\frac{u_2'}{u_2}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using the sign convention for $W$ in <1>1,
\[
f'
=\frac{u_1'u_2-u_1u_2'}{u_2^2}
=-\frac{W}{u_2^2}.
\]
Since $W$ is a nonzero constant,
\[
N_f
=\frac{f''}{f'}
=\frac{d}{dz}\log f'
=-2\frac{u_2'}{u_2}.
\]
:::

<1>3. The Schwarzian is
\[
S_f=2\phi.
\]
::: {.proof}
From <1>2,
\[
N_f'
=-2\left(\frac{u_2''}{u_2}-\left(\frac{u_2'}{u_2}\right)^2\right),
\]
while
\[
\frac12N_f^2
=2\left(\frac{u_2'}{u_2}\right)^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_f
&=N_f'-\frac12N_f^2\\
&=-2\frac{u_2''}{u_2}\\
&=2\phi,
\end{aligned}
\]
because $u_2''=-\phi u_2$. This identity holds wherever $u_2\ne0$, and hence throughout by analytic continuation of the resulting quadratic differential.
:::

<1>4. The Schwarzian does not depend on the chosen basis of solutions.
::: {.proof}
If $(v_1,v_2)$ is another basis, then for constants $a,b,c,d$ with $ad-bc\ne0$,
\[
\frac{v_1}{v_2}
=\frac{a u_1+b u_2}{c u_1+d u_2}
=M\!\left(\frac{u_1}{u_2}\right),
\]
where
\[
M(w)=\frac{aw+b}{cw+d}
\]
is a Möbius transformation. Möbius transformations have zero Schwarzian. Hence the Schwarzian chain rule gives
\[
S_{M\circ f}
=S_f+(S_M\circ f)(f')^2
=S_f.
\]
Equivalently, <1>3 already shows that every basis gives the same intrinsic value $2\phi$.
:::
