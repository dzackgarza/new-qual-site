::: {.problem}
Let $\rho(z)|dz|$ be the hyperbolic metric on
\[
X=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}.
\]
Show that there are constants $A,B>0$ such that, for all sufficiently small nonzero $z$,
\[
\frac{A}{|z\log|z||}
\le \rho(z)
\le \frac{B}{|z\log|z||}.
\]

Hint: write the covering map using its period as a holomorphic map from the punctured disk and compare it with the standard hyperbolic metric on a punctured disk.
:::

::: {.solution}
Near the puncture $0$ one has
\[
\boxed{
\rho(z)\asymp\frac1{|z\log|z||}.
}
\]
More explicitly, there are $A,B,\varepsilon>0$ such that
\[
\frac{A}{|z\log|z||}
\le \rho(z)
\le \frac{B}{|z\log|z||}
\qquad(0<|z|<\varepsilon).
\]

<1>1. The period $2$ of the modular covering factors it through a punctured disk.
::: {.proof}
The universal covering
\[
\lambda:\mathbb H\to X
\]
is invariant under the parabolic deck transformation
\[
\tau\longmapsto\tau+2.
\]
Set
\[
q=e^{\pi i\tau}.
\]
Then $q(\tau+2)=q(\tau)$, and the quotient
\[
\mathbb H/\langle\tau\mapsto\tau+2\rangle
\]
is biholomorphic to the punctured disk $\Delta^*$. Hence there is a holomorphic covering map
\[
f:\Delta^*\to X
\]
such that
\[
\lambda(\tau)=f(e^{\pi i\tau}).
\]
For the cusp normalization used in the source, the end $\operatorname{Im}\tau\to\infty$ maps to the puncture $0\in\widehat{\mathbb C}$. Thus $f$ extends holomorphically across $q=0$ with
\[
f(0)=0.
\]
The loop $q=re^{2\pi it}$ represents the primitive quotient translation $\tau\mapsto\tau+2$, which is the primitive peripheral loop about this puncture. Therefore the local degree of the extension at $0$ is one, so
\[
f'(0)\ne0.
\]
:::

<1>2. The hyperbolic metric on $\Delta^*$ is
\[
\frac{|dq|}{|q\log|q||}.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the standard cusp metric recalled in the hint. Since $f:\Delta^*\to X$ is a holomorphic covering and both metrics are the complete hyperbolic metrics in the source's normalization, $f$ is a local isometry. Therefore
\[
f^*\bigl(\rho(z)|dz|\bigr)
=\frac{|dq|}{|q\log|q||}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\rho(f(q))\,|f'(q)|
=\frac1{|q\log|q||}.
\]
:::

<1>3. The local coordinate $f$ compares $q$ and $z=f(q)$ up to fixed constants.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
a=f'(0)\ne0.
\]
Then
\[
f(q)=aq+O(q^2),
\qquad
f'(q)=a+O(q).
\]
Hence, after shrinking to $0<|q|<\delta$, there are constants $c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4>0$ such that
\[
c_1|q|\le |f(q)|\le c_2|q|,
\qquad
c_3\le |f'(q)|\le c_4.
\]
Moreover
\[
\log|f(q)|
=\log|q|+\log|a+O(q)|.
\]
The second term is bounded as $q\to0$, whereas $\log|q|\to-\infty$. Thus, after shrinking $\delta$ again, there are constants $c_5,c_6>0$ with
\[
c_5|\log|q||
\le |\log|f(q)||
\le c_6|\log|q||.
\]
Since $f'(0)\ne0$, the inverse function theorem also shows that $f$ maps a small disk about $0$ biholomorphically onto a neighborhood of $0$. Hence every sufficiently small nonzero $z$ can be written uniquely as $z=f(q)$ with $q$ small.
:::

<1>4. Substitute these comparisons into the local-isometry identity.
::: {.proof}
From <1>2,
\[
\rho(f(q))
=\frac1{|f'(q)|\,|q|\,|\log|q||}.
\]
The estimates of <1>3 show that the denominator on the right is comparable, by positive constants independent of $q$, to
\[
|f(q)|\,|\log|f(q)||.
\]
Thus there are $A,B>0$ such that
\[
\frac{A}{|f(q)\log|f(q)||}
\le \rho(f(q))
\le \frac{B}{|f(q)\log|f(q)||}
\]
for all sufficiently small nonzero $q$. Writing $z=f(q)$ gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{A}{|z\log|z||}
\le \rho(z)
\le \frac{B}{|z\log|z||}
}
\]
for all sufficiently small nonzero $z$.
:::
