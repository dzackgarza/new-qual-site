::: {.problem}
Let $\rho(z)\abs{dz}$ be the hyperbolic metric on
$$
X=\widehat{\CC}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}.
$$
Show that there are constants $A,B>0$ such that, for all sufficiently small nonzero $z$,
$$
\frac{A}{\abs{z\log\abs{z}}}
\le \rho(z)
\le \frac{B}{\abs{z\log\abs{z}}}.
$$

Hint: write the covering map using its period as a holomorphic map from the punctured disk and compare it with the standard hyperbolic metric on a punctured disk.
:::

::: {.solution}
Near the puncture $0$ one has
$$
\boxed{
\rho(z)\asymp\frac1{\abs{z\log\abs{z}}}.
}
$$
More explicitly, there are $A,B,\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$
\frac{A}{\abs{z\log\abs{z}}}
\le \rho(z)
\le \frac{B}{\abs{z\log\abs{z}}}
\qquad(0<\abs{z}<\varepsilon).
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The period $2$ of the modular covering factors it through a punctured disk.

::: pf-proof

The universal covering
$$
\lambda:\halfplane\to X
$$
is invariant under the parabolic deck transformation
$$
\tau\longmapsto\tau+2.
$$
Set
$$
q=e^{\pi i\tau}.
$$
Then $q(\tau+2)=q(\tau)$, and the quotient
$$
\halfplane/\langle\tau\mapsto\tau+2\rangle
$$
is biholomorphic to the punctured disk $\Delta^*$. Hence there is a holomorphic covering map
$$
f:\Delta^*\to X
$$
such that
$$
\lambda(\tau)=f(e^{\pi i\tau}).
$$
The modular function satisfies $\lambda(\tau)\to0$ as $\operatorname{Im}\tau\to\infty$, so the end $\operatorname{Im}\tau\to\infty$ maps to the puncture $0\in\widehat{\CC}$. Thus $f$ extends holomorphically across $q=0$ with
$$
f(0)=0.
$$
The loop $q=re^{2\pi it}$ represents the primitive quotient translation $\tau\mapsto\tau+2$, which is the primitive peripheral loop about this puncture. Therefore the local degree of the extension at $0$ is one, so
$$
f'(0)\ne0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The hyperbolic metric on $\Delta^*$ is
$$
\frac{\abs{dq}}{\abs{q\log\abs{q}}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Under $q=e^{\pi i\tau}$ one has $\abs{dq}=\pi\abs{q}\,\abs{d\tau}$ and $\abs{\log\abs{q}}=\pi\operatorname{Im}\tau$, so this metric is the quotient of the curvature $-1$ metric $\abs{d\tau}/\operatorname{Im}\tau$ on $\halfplane$ by $\tau\mapsto\tau+2$. Taking $\rho(z)\abs{dz}$ with curvature $-1$ as well, the holomorphic covering $f:\Delta^*\to X$ is a local isometry. Therefore
$$
f^*\bigl(\rho(z)\abs{dz}\bigr)
=\frac{\abs{dq}}{\abs{q\log\abs{q}}}.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\rho(f(q))\,\abs{f'(q)}
=\frac1{\abs{q\log\abs{q}}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The local coordinate $f$ compares $q$ and $z=f(q)$ up to fixed constants.

::: pf-proof

Write
$$
a=f'(0)\ne0.
$$
Then
$$
f(q)=aq+O(q^2),
\qquad
f'(q)=a+O(q).
$$
Hence, after shrinking to $0<\abs{q}<\delta$, there are constants $c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4>0$ such that
$$
c_1\abs{q}\le \abs{f(q)}\le c_2\abs{q},
\qquad
c_3\le \abs{f'(q)}\le c_4.
$$
Moreover
$$
\log\abs{f(q)}
=\log\abs{q}+\log\abs{a+O(q)}.
$$
The second term is bounded as $q\to0$, whereas $\log\abs{q}\to-\infty$. Thus, after shrinking $\delta$ again, there are constants $c_5,c_6>0$ with
$$
c_5\abs{\log\abs{q}}
\le \abs{\log\abs{f(q)}}
\le c_6\abs{\log\abs{q}}.
$$
Since $f'(0)\ne0$, the inverse function theorem also shows that $f$ maps a small disk about $0$ biholomorphically onto a neighborhood of $0$. Hence every sufficiently small nonzero $z$ can be written uniquely as $z=f(q)$ with $q$ small.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Substitute these comparisons into the local-isometry identity.

::: pf-proof

From step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\rho(f(q))
=\frac1{\abs{f'(q)}\,\abs{q}\,\abs{\log\abs{q}}}.
$$
The estimates of step [](#s3){.pf-ref} show that the denominator on the right is comparable, by positive constants independent of $q$, to
$$
\abs{f(q)}\,\abs{\log\abs{f(q)}}.
$$
Thus there are $A,B>0$ such that
$$
\frac{A}{\abs{f(q)\log\abs{f(q)}}}
\le \rho(f(q))
\le \frac{B}{\abs{f(q)\log\abs{f(q)}}}
$$
for all sufficiently small nonzero $q$. Writing $z=f(q)$ gives
$$
\boxed{
\frac{A}{\abs{z\log\abs{z}}}
\le \rho(z)
\le \frac{B}{\abs{z\log\abs{z}}}
}
$$
for all sufficiently small nonzero $z$.

:::
