::: {.problem}
Formulate and prove an infinite product formula for
\[
\sin^2(\sqrt z).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function $z\mapsto\sin^2(\sqrt z)$ is entire and has the power-series expression
\[
\sin^2(\sqrt z)=\frac{1-\cos(2\sqrt z)}2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using the cosine series,
\[
\cos(2\sqrt z)
=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k2^{2k}z^k}{(2k)!},
\]
which is an entire power series in $z$. Thus $(1-\cos(2\sqrt z))/2$ is entire. Since $\sin^2 w=(1-\cos 2w)/2$, it equals $\sin^2(\sqrt z)$ for either choice of the square root.
:::

<1>2. One has the infinite product
\[
\boxed{
\sin^2(\sqrt z)
=z\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(1-\frac{z}{\pi^2n^2}\right)^2
}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The sine product formula is
\[
\frac{\sin u}{u}
=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(1-\frac{u^2}{\pi^2n^2}\right).
\]
Squaring gives
\[
\sin^2u
=u^2\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(1-\frac{u^2}{\pi^2n^2}\right)^2.
\]
Now put $u^2=z$. Both sides depend only on $u^2$, so the result is independent of the choice of square root and gives the displayed formula.
:::

<1>3. The product in <1>2 converges locally uniformly in $z$.
::: {.proof}
For a compact set $K\subset\mathbb C$, choose $R$ with $|z|\le R$ on $K$. Then
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\sup_{z\in K}\left|\frac{z}{\pi^2n^2}\right|
\le
\frac{R}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}
<\infty.
\]
Hence $\prod_n(1-z/(\pi^2n^2))$ converges uniformly on $K$, and so does its square. This also directly exhibits the zero at $z=0$ with multiplicity one and the zeros $z=\pi^2n^2$ with multiplicity two.
:::
:::
