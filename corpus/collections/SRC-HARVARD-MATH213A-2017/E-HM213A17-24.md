::: {.problem}
Let $p(z)$ be a polynomial of degree $d\ge2$ with distinct roots $r_1,\ldots,r_d$.
Show that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^d\frac{1}{p'(r_i)}=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For each root $r_i$, the meromorphic function $1/p(z)$ has a simple
pole at $r_i$ with residue
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=r_i}\frac1{p(z)}=\frac1{p'(r_i)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since the roots are distinct, $p'(r_i)\neq0$. Thus
\[
p(z)=(z-r_i)q_i(z)
\]
with $q_i(r_i)=p'(r_i)$. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=r_i}\frac1{p(z)}
=\lim_{z\to r_i}\frac{z-r_i}{p(z)}
=\frac1{q_i(r_i)}
=\frac1{p'(r_i)}.
\]
:::

<1>2. If $R$ is large enough that all roots of $p$ lie inside
$|z|<R$, then
\[
\int_{|z|=R}\frac{dz}{p(z)}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(R\to\infty).
\]
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
p(z)=a_dz^d+O(z^{d-1}),
\qquad a_d\neq0.
\]
Hence for all sufficiently large $R$ there is a constant $C>0$ such that
\[
\left|\frac1{p(z)}\right|\le \frac{C}{R^d}
\qquad(|z|=R).
\]
The ML estimate then gives
\[
\left|\int_{|z|=R}\frac{dz}{p(z)}\right|
\le 2\pi R\frac{C}{R^d}
=2\pi C R^{1-d}\longrightarrow0,
\]
because $d\ge2$.
:::

<1>3. Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^d\frac1{p'(r_i)}=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
For every sufficiently large $R$, the residue theorem and <1>1 give
\[
\int_{|z|=R}\frac{dz}{p(z)}
=2\pi i\sum_{i=1}^d\frac1{p'(r_i)}.
\]
The right-hand side is independent of $R$, while by <1>2 the left-hand side
tends to zero as $R\to\infty$. Hence the sum must vanish.
:::
:::
