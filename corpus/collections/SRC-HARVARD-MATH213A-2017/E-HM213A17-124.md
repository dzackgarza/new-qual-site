::: {.problem}
Let $f,g$ be entire functions satisfying
\[
f^n+g^n=1
\]
for an integer $n>2$.
Prove that $f$ and $g$ are constant.

Hint: consider $f/g$.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $g\equiv0$, then $f^n\equiv1$, so $f$ is constant. Assume therefore that $g\not\equiv0$ and consider the meromorphic function
\[
h=\frac{f}{g}:\mathbb C\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb C}.
\]

<1>1. The function $h$ omits every $n$th root of $-1$.
::: {.proof}
Let $\zeta\in\mathbb C$ satisfy
\[
\zeta^n=-1.
\]
Suppose for contradiction that
\[
h(z_0)=\zeta.
\]
Then $h(z_0)$ is finite, so $g(z_0)\ne0$, and
\[
f(z_0)=\zeta g(z_0).
\]
Substituting into Fermat's equation gives
\[
1
=f(z_0)^n+g(z_0)^n
=(\zeta^n+1)g(z_0)^n
=0,
\]
a contradiction. Thus every root of $z^n=-1$ is omitted by $h$.
:::

<1>2. The meromorphic function $h$ is constant.
::: {.proof}
Because $n>2$, the equation
\[
\zeta^n=-1
\]
has at least three distinct roots. By <1>1, the meromorphic function $h$ omits all of them. McMullen's meromorphic form of Little Picard states that a nonconstant meromorphic function on $\mathbb C$ can omit at most two values of the Riemann sphere. Hence $h$ must be constant, say
\[
h\equiv c.
\]
:::

<1>3. Both $f$ and $g$ are constant.
::: {.proof}
From $f=cg$ and
\[
f^n+g^n=1
\]
we obtain
\[
(c^n+1)g^n=1.
\]
In particular $c^n+1\ne0$, and therefore
\[
g^n\equiv\frac{1}{c^n+1}
\]
is constant and nonzero. Differentiating gives
\[
ng^{n-1}g'=0.
\]
Since $g$ never vanishes, $g'\equiv0$, so $g$ is constant. Then $f=cg$ is constant as well.
:::
:::
