::: {.problem}
Show that there exists a constant $\alpha\in[0,1]$ such that
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k+\alpha}
=\log n+o(1)
\qquad(n\to\infty).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
There is an $\alpha\in(0,1)$ for which
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k+\alpha}-\log n\longrightarrow0.
\]
We obtain it by varying continuously between the ordinary harmonic sum and
its unit shift.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For $\alpha\in[0,1]$,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k+\alpha}-\log n
=\left(H_n-\log n\right)
-\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{\alpha}{k(k+\alpha)},
\]
where $H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n1/k$.

::: pf-proof

For each $k$,
\[
\frac1k-\frac1{k+\alpha}
=\frac{\alpha}{k(k+\alpha)}.
\]
Summing this identity from $1$ to $n$ and rearranging gives the displayed
formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The limit
\[
L(\alpha)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}
\left(\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k+\alpha}-\log n\right)
\]
exists for every $\alpha\in[0,1]$, and
\[
L(\alpha)
=\gamma-
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha}{k(k+\alpha)}.
\]

::: pf-proof

Euler's constant is
\[
\gamma
=\lim_{n\to\infty}(H_n-\log(n+1)).
\]
Since
\[
\log(n+1)-\log n\longrightarrow0,
\]
we also have
\[
H_n-\log n\longrightarrow\gamma.
\]

For $0\le\alpha\le1$,
\[
0\le\frac{\alpha}{k(k+\alpha)}\le\frac1{k^2}.
\]
Thus the series
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha}{k(k+\alpha)}
\]
converges uniformly on $[0,1]$ by the Weierstrass $M$-test. Taking limits in
step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives the formula for $L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The function $L:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ is continuous.

::: pf-proof

Each summand
\[
\frac{\alpha}{k(k+\alpha)}
\]
is continuous in $\alpha$, and the series of these summands converges
uniformly on $[0,1]$ by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}. Its sum is therefore continuous, and so is
$L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Euler's constant satisfies
\[
0<\gamma<1.
\]

::: pf-proof

For every $k\ge1$,
\[
\int_k^{k+1}\frac{dx}{x}<\frac1k,
\]
so
\[
H_n-\log(n+1)>0,
\]
and hence $\gamma\ge0$. The inequality is strict in the limit, for example
because already the first summand
\[
1-\log2
\]
is positive.

For the upper bound, for $k\ge2$,
\[
\frac1k<\int_{k-1}^{k}\frac{dx}{x}.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{k=2}^n\frac1k<\log n,
\]
and therefore
\[
H_n-\log(n+1)
<1+\log n-\log(n+1)<1.
\]
Passing to the limit gives $\gamma<1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

One has
\[
L(0)>0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
L(1)<0.
\]

::: pf-proof

At $\alpha=0$, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
\[
L(0)=\gamma>0.
\]
At $\alpha=1$,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac1{k(k+1)}
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac1k-\frac1{k+1}\right)
=1.
\]
Thus
\[
L(1)=\gamma-1<0
\]
by step [](#s4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

There exists $\alpha\in(0,1)$ such that $L(\alpha)=0$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $L$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, and by step [](#s5){.pf-ref} it changes sign
between the endpoints. The intermediate value theorem therefore gives some
\[
\alpha\in(0,1)
\]
with $L(\alpha)=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

This $\alpha$ has the required asymptotic property.

::: pf-proof

By the definition of $L$ and step [](#s6){.pf-ref},
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k+\alpha}-\log n
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k+\alpha}
=\log n+o(1).
\]

:::
