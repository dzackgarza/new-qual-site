::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f:\Delta\to\mathbb C,
\qquad
f(z)=e^{z^2}.
\]
A disk $B(p,r)\subset\mathbb C$ is called unramified for $f$ if there is a holomorphic map
\[
h:B(p,r)\to\Delta
\]
such that $f(h(w))=w$ for every $w\in B(p,r)$.

Find the radius of the largest unramified disk for $f$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The largest possible radius is
\[
\boxed{\frac{e-1}{2}}.
\]

<1>1. Every unramified disk has radius at most $(e-1)/2$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
f(z)=e^{z^2}.
\]
Since $z^2$ maps $\Delta$ onto $\Delta$, the image of $f$ is
\[
\Omega=e^\Delta:=\{e^u:|u|<1\}.
\]
For $u\in\Delta$,
\[
|e^u-1|
\le \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|u|^n}{n!}
=e^{|u|}-1
<e-1.
\]
Hence
\[
\Omega\subset B(1,e-1).
\]

Now suppose $B(p,r)$ is unramified. Then $B(p,r)\subset\Omega$, because every point of the disk is of the form $f(h(w))$. Also
\[
1\notin B(p,r).
\]
Indeed, the only solution in $\Delta$ of $e^{z^2}=1$ is $z=0$, and $f'(0)=0$; differentiating $f\circ h=\operatorname{id}$ at $1$ would give the contradiction
\[
1=f'(0)h'(1)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
r\le |p-1|.
\]
On the other hand, the inclusion
\[
B(p,r)\subset B(1,e-1)
\]
implies
\[
|p-1|+r\le e-1.
\]
Combining the two inequalities gives
\[
2r\le e-1,
\]
so
\[
r\le\frac{e-1}{2}.
\]
:::

<1>2. Let
\[
c=\frac{e+1}{2},
\qquad
R=\frac{e-1}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
B(c,R)\subset e^\Delta.
\]
::: {.proof}
Consider the right half-disk
\[
H=\{z:|z|<1,\ \operatorname{Re}z>0\}.
\]
The exponential is injective on $\overline H$, so it maps $H$ conformally onto a Jordan domain $V=e^H$. We show that the boundary of $V$ never enters $B(c,R)$.

On the diameter of $\partial H$, write $z=iy$ with $|y|\le1$. Then
\[
\operatorname{Re}(e^{iy})=\cos y\le1=c-R,
\]
so
\[
|e^{iy}-c|\ge R,
\]
with equality only at $y=0$.

On the semicircular part of $\partial H$, write
\[
z=x+iy,
\qquad
x^2+y^2=1,
\qquad
0\le x\le1.
\]
Since
\[
c^2-R^2=e,
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{-x}\bigl(|e^z-c|^2-R^2\bigr)
&=e^x+e^{1-x}-(e+1)\cos y\\
&=:\Phi(x),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $|y|=\sqrt{1-x^2}$. We claim $\Phi(x)\ge0$ on $[0,1]$.

Set
\[
s=\sqrt{1-x^2}.
\]
Then
\[
\Phi'(x)
=e^x-e^{1-x}-(e+1)x\frac{\sin s}{s},
\]
with $\sin s/s=1$ at $s=0$. If $0\le x\le1/2$, the first difference is nonpositive, so $\Phi'(x)<0$.

If $1/2\le x\le1$, convexity of $\sinh$ on $[0,1/2]$ gives
\[
\sinh(x-1/2)
\le2(x-1/2)\sinh(1/2).
\]
Thus
\[
e^x-e^{1-x}
\le4\sqrt e\,(x-1/2)\sinh(1/2).
\]
Since $2(x-1/2)\le x$, $\tanh(1/2)<1/2$, and
\[
\frac{\sin s}{s}\ge1-\frac{s^2}{6}\ge\frac56,
\]
the last quantity is strictly smaller than
\[
2\sqrt e\,\cosh(1/2)\,x\frac{\sin s}{s}
=(e+1)x\frac{\sin s}{s}.
\]
Hence $\Phi'(x)<0$ throughout $[0,1]$. Since
\[
\Phi(1)=e+1-(e+1)=0,
\]
we get $\Phi(x)\ge0$, proving
\[
|e^z-c|\ge R
\]
on the whole semicircle.

Therefore
\[
\partial V\cap B(c,R)=\varnothing.
\]
But $1<c<e$, so $\log c\in(0,1)$ and hence $c\in V$. If any point of the connected disk $B(c,R)$ lay outside $V$, the line segment from $c$ to that point would meet $\partial V$ inside $B(c,R)$, a contradiction. Thus
\[
B(c,R)\subset V\subset e^\Delta.
\]
:::

<1>3. The disk $B(c,R)$ is unramified for $f$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, the principal logarithm maps $B(c,R)$ holomorphically into $\Delta$. Moreover $1$ lies on the boundary of $B(c,R)$, so
\[
\log w\ne0
\qquad(w\in B(c,R)).
\]
Because the disk is simply connected, the zero-free holomorphic function $\log w$ has a holomorphic square root there. Choose one branch and set
\[
h(w)=\sqrt{\log w}.
\]
Then
\[
|h(w)|^2=|\log w|<1,
\]
so $h:B(c,R)\to\Delta$, and
\[
f(h(w))
=e^{h(w)^2}
=e^{\log w}
=w.
\]
Thus $B(c,R)$ is unramified. Together with <1>1, this proves that the largest radius is exactly $(e-1)/2$.
:::
