::: {.problem}
On $[0,\pi]^2$, consider
$$
f_t=f_{xx}+f_{yy}+11f.
$$

(a) Show that $\sin(nx)\sin(my)$ is an eigenfunction of $D_x^2+D_y^2+11$ and find its eigenvalue.

(b) Solve the PDE with initial condition
$$
f(x,y,0)=\sin(4x)\sin(7y),
$$
and decide whether the solution dies out.

(c) Do the same for
$$
f(x,y,0)=\sin(3x)\sin y.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
T=D_x^2+D_y^2+11.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every positive integers $n,m$,
$$
\boxed{\lambda_{n,m}=11-n^2-m^2}
$$
is the eigenvalue of $T$ corresponding to the eigenfunction
$\sin(nx)\sin(my)$.


::: pf-proof

Direct differentiation gives
$$
D_x^2\bigl(\sin(nx)\sin(my)\bigr)
=-n^2\sin(nx)\sin(my)
$$
and
$$
D_y^2\bigl(\sin(nx)\sin(my)\bigr)
=-m^2\sin(nx)\sin(my).
$$
Therefore
$$
T\bigl(\sin(nx)\sin(my)\bigr)
=(11-n^2-m^2)\sin(nx)\sin(my).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For the initial condition in part (b), the eigenvalue is
$$
\lambda_{4,7}=-54.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\lambda_{4,7}=11-4^2-7^2=11-16-49=-54.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The solution in part (b) is
$$
\boxed{f(x,y,t)=e^{-54t}\sin(4x)\sin(7y)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Seek a solution of the form
$$
f(x,y,t)=a(t)\sin(4x)\sin(7y).
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, substitution into $f_t=T(f)$ gives
$$
a'(t)=-54a(t).
$$
The initial condition requires $a(0)=1$, hence $a(t)=e^{-54t}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The solution in part (b) dies out as $t\to\infty$.


::: pf-proof

Since $e^{-54t}\to0$ as $t\to\infty$ and the spatial sine factors are bounded
by $1$ in absolute value, the solution in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} converges uniformly to
zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For the initial condition in part (c), the eigenvalue is
$$
\lambda_{3,1}=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\lambda_{3,1}=11-3^2-1^2=11-9-1=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The solution in part (c) is
$$
\boxed{f(x,y,t)=e^t\sin(3x)\sin y}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Writing
$$
f(x,y,t)=a(t)\sin(3x)\sin y
$$
and using step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives the scalar equation
$$
a'(t)=a(t).
$$
The initial condition gives $a(0)=1$, so $a(t)=e^t$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The solution in part (c) grows exponentially rather than dying out.


::: pf-proof

At every point where $\sin(3x)\sin y\ne0$, the absolute value of the solution
from step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is
$$
e^t|\sin(3x)\sin y|,
$$
which tends to infinity as $t\to\infty$. Thus this mode is amplified rather
than damped.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove part (b), and
steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} prove part (c).

:::
