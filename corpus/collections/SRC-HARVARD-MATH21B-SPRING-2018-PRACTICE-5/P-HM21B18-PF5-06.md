::: {.problem}
(a) Find all eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
$$
B=\begin{pmatrix}
2&1&0&0\\0&2&1&0\\0&0&2&1\\1&0&0&2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

(b) Is $\dot x=Bx$ asymptotically stable?

(c) For
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}
1/3&0&2/3\\
0&2/3&1/3\\
2/3&0&1/3
\end{pmatrix},
$$
find an eigenvector belonging to the eigenvalue of largest modulus.

(d) Is the discrete system $x(n+1)=Ax(n)$ asymptotically stable?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The eigenvalues of $B$ are $3,2+i,1,2-i$, with eigenspaces
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_3&=\operatorname{span}_{\CC}\{(1,1,1,1)^T\},\\
E_{2+i}&=\operatorname{span}_{\CC}\{(1,i,-1,-i)^T\},\\
E_1&=\operatorname{span}_{\CC}\{(1,-1,1,-1)^T\},\\
E_{2-i}&=\operatorname{span}_{\CC}\{(1,-i,-1,i)^T\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus all eigenvectors are the nonzero vectors in these four one-dimensional eigenspaces.


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Put $P=B-2I$. Then
$$
P(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)^T=(x_2,x_3,x_4,x_1)^T.
$$


::: pf-proof

This follows directly from the displayed matrix for $B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every fourth root of unity $\mu$, the vector
$$
v_\mu=(1,\mu,\mu^2,\mu^3)^T
$$
satisfies $Pv_\mu=\mu v_\mu$ and hence $Bv_\mu=(2+\mu)v_\mu$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
Pv_\mu=(\mu,\mu^2,\mu^3,1)^T
      =\mu(1,\mu,\mu^2,\mu^3)^T,
$$
because $\mu^4=1$. Since $B=2I+P$, the second assertion follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The four vectors $v_1,v_i,v_{-1},v_{-i}$ form a basis of $\CC^4$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} they are eigenvectors of $P$ for the four distinct eigenvalues $1,i,-1,-i$. Eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent, so these four vectors form a basis of the four-dimensional space $\CC^4$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Applying step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} to $\mu=1,i,-1,-i$ gives respectively the four eigenvalue--eigenvector pairs listed in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that there are no further eigenvalues, and the distinctness of the four eigenvalues makes each eigenspace one-dimensional.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The continuous system $\dot x=Bx$ is not asymptotically stable.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $Bv=3v$ for $v=(1,1,1,1)^T$. Hence $x(t)=e^{3t}v$ is a solution of $\dot x=Bx$ and does not tend to $0$ as $t\to\infty$. Scaling $v$ gives arbitrarily small initial conditions with the same behavior, so the zero solution is not asymptotically stable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The eigenvalue of $A$ having largest modulus is $1$, and one corresponding eigenvector is
$$
\boxed{(1,1,1)^T}.
$$


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s3-1}

$A(1,1,1)^T=(1,1,1)^T$.


::: pf-proof

Each row of $A$ has sum $1$, so multiplication by $(1,1,1)^T$ leaves that vector fixed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-2}

The other two eigenvalues of $A$ are $2/3$ and $-1/3$.


::: pf-proof

The trace is $\operatorname{tr}(A)=4/3$, while expansion along the second column gives
$$
\det A=\frac23\left(\frac19-\frac49\right)=-\frac29.
$$
By step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref} one eigenvalue is $1$. If $r,s$ are the remaining two eigenvalues, then
$$
r+s=\frac43-1=\frac13,
\qquad
rs=-\frac29.
$$
Thus $r$ and $s$ are the roots of
$$
t^2-\frac13t-\frac29
=\left(t-\frac23\right)\left(t+\frac13\right),
$$
so $\{r,s\}=\{2/3,-1/3\}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref} supplies an eigenvector for eigenvalue $1$, and step [](#s3-2){.pf-ref} shows that the remaining eigenvalues have moduli $2/3$ and $1/3$. Hence $1$ has the largest modulus.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The discrete system $x(n+1)=Ax(n)$ is not asymptotically stable.


::: pf-proof

Let $v=(1,1,1)^T$. By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $Av=v$, so the solution with initial condition $x(0)=v$ satisfies $x(n)=v$ for every $n$. Scaling $v$ gives arbitrarily small nonzero initial conditions whose solutions do not tend to $0$. Therefore the zero solution is not asymptotically stable.

:::

:::
