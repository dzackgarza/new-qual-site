::: {.problem}
(a) Find all eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}
2&1&0&0\\0&2&1&0\\0&0&2&1\\1&0&0&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

(b) Is $\dot x=Bx$ asymptotically stable?

(c) For
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
1/3&0&2/3\\
0&2/3&1/3\\
2/3&0&1/3
\end{pmatrix},
\]
find an eigenvector belonging to the eigenvalue of largest modulus.

(d) Is the discrete system $x(n+1)=Ax(n)$ asymptotically stable?
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

The eigenvalues of $B$ are $3,2+i,1,2-i$, with eigenspaces
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_3&=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{(1,1,1,1)^T\},\\
E_{2+i}&=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{(1,i,-1,-i)^T\},\\
E_1&=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{(1,-1,1,-1)^T\},\\
E_{2-i}&=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{(1,-i,-1,i)^T\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus all eigenvectors are the nonzero vectors in these four one-dimensional eigenspaces.


::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Put $P=B-2I$. Then
\[
P(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)^T=(x_2,x_3,x_4,x_1)^T.
\]


::: pf-proof

This follows directly from the displayed matrix for $B$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every fourth root of unity $\mu$, the vector
\[
v_\mu=(1,\mu,\mu^2,\mu^3)^T
\]
satisfies $Pv_\mu=\mu v_\mu$ and hence $Bv_\mu=(2+\mu)v_\mu$.


::: pf-proof

Step <2>1 gives
\[
Pv_\mu=(\mu,\mu^2,\mu^3,1)^T
      =\mu(1,\mu,\mu^2,\mu^3)^T,
\]
because $\mu^4=1$. Since $B=2I+P$, the second assertion follows.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The four vectors $v_1,v_i,v_{-1},v_{-i}$ form a basis of $\mathbb C^4$.


::: pf-proof

By step <2>2 they are eigenvectors of $P$ for the four distinct eigenvalues $1,i,-1,-i$. Eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent, so these four vectors form a basis of the four-dimensional space $\mathbb C^4$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Applying step <2>2 to $\mu=1,i,-1,-i$ gives respectively the four eigenvalue--eigenvector pairs listed in step <1>1. Step <2>3 shows that there are no further eigenvalues, and the distinctness of the four eigenvalues makes each eigenspace one-dimensional.

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The continuous system $\dot x=Bx$ is not asymptotically stable.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>1, $Bv=3v$ for $v=(1,1,1,1)^T$. Hence $x(t)=e^{3t}v$ is a solution of $\dot x=Bx$ and does not tend to $0$ as $t\to\infty$. Scaling $v$ gives arbitrarily small initial conditions with the same behavior, so the zero solution is not asymptotically stable.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The eigenvalue of $A$ having largest modulus is $1$, and one corresponding eigenvector is
\[
\boxed{(1,1,1)^T}.
\]


::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

$A(1,1,1)^T=(1,1,1)^T$.


::: pf-proof

Each row of $A$ has sum $1$, so multiplication by $(1,1,1)^T$ leaves that vector fixed.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The other two eigenvalues of $A$ are $2/3$ and $-1/3$.


::: pf-proof

The trace is $\operatorname{tr}(A)=4/3$, while expansion along the second column gives
\[
\det A=\frac23\left(\frac19-\frac49\right)=-\frac29.
\]
By step <2>1 one eigenvalue is $1$. If $r,s$ are the remaining two eigenvalues, then
\[
r+s=\frac43-1=\frac13,
\qquad
rs=-\frac29.
\]
Thus $r$ and $s$ are the roots of
\[
t^2-\frac13t-\frac29
=\left(t-\frac23\right)\left(t+\frac13\right),
\]
so $\{r,s\}=\{2/3,-1/3\}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <2>1 supplies an eigenvector for eigenvalue $1$, and step <2>2 shows that the remaining eigenvalues have moduli $2/3$ and $1/3$. Hence $1$ has the largest modulus.

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The discrete system $x(n+1)=Ax(n)$ is not asymptotically stable.


::: pf-proof

Let $v=(1,1,1)^T$. By step <1>3, $Av=v$, so the solution with initial condition $x(0)=v$ satisfies $x(n)=v$ for every $n$. Scaling $v$ gives arbitrarily small nonzero initial conditions whose solutions do not tend to $0$. Therefore the zero solution is not asymptotically stable.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>1 answers part (a), step <1>2 answers part (b), step <1>3 answers part (c), and step <1>4 answers part (d).

:::
