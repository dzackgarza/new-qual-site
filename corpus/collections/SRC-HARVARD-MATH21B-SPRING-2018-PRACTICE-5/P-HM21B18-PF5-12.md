::: {.problem}
(a) Find the Fourier series on $[-\pi,\pi]$ of
$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
\pi,&-1\le x\le1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

(b) Use Parseval's theorem to evaluate
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sin^2 n}{n^2}.
$$

(c) For $0\le a\le\pi$, repeat the computation with
$$
f_a(x)=
\begin{cases}
\pi,&-a\le x\le a,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
$$
and find
$$
g(a)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sin^2(na)}{n^2}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

For a $2\pi$-periodic function, write
$$
f(x)\sim \frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\bigl(a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\bigr),
$$
where
$$
a_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\cos(nx)\,dx,
\qquad
b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The Fourier series in part (a) is
$$
\boxed{
f(x)\sim 1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin n}{n}\cos(nx)
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

$b_n=0$ for every $n\ge1$.


::: pf-proof

The function $f$ is even, while $\sin(nx)$ is odd. Their product is odd, so its integral over $[-\pi,\pi]$ is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

$a_0=2$ and, for $n\ge1$,
$$
a_n=\frac{2\sin n}{n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $f$ is supported on $[-1,1]$ and equals $\pi$ there,
$$
a_0=\frac1\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\pi\,dx=2.
$$
For $n\ge1$, evenness gives
$$
a_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^1\pi\cos(nx)\,dx
=2\left[\frac{\sin(nx)}n\right]_{0}^{1}
=\frac{2\sin n}{n}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Substituting the coefficients from steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} into the Fourier expansion gives the series in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. At the jump points $x=\pm1$ the series converges to the midpoint value $\pi/2$, and at every continuity point it converges to $f(x)$.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Parseval's theorem gives
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^2 n}{n^2}
=\frac{\pi-1}{2}
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s2-1}

The normalized squared $L^2$ norm is
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx=2\pi.
$$


::: pf-proof

The function has magnitude $\pi$ on an interval of length $2$ and vanishes elsewhere, so
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac1\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\pi^2\,dx
=2\pi.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-2}

Parseval's identity reduces to
$$
2\pi=2+4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^2 n}{n^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Parseval's theorem in the normalization fixed above is
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac{a_0^2}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2+b_n^2).
$$
Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives $a_0=2$, $a_n=2\sin n/n$, and $b_n=0$, while step [](#s2-1){.pf-ref} gives the left-hand side.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Solving the identity in step [](#s2-2){.pf-ref} for the series gives $(\pi-1)/2$.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every $0\le a\le\pi$,
$$
\boxed{
g(a)=\frac{a(\pi-a)}2
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s3-1}

The Fourier coefficients of $f_a$ are
$$
a_0=2a,
\qquad
a_n=\frac{2\sin(na)}n,
\qquad
b_n=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The function $f_a$ is even, so $b_n=0$. Moreover,
$$
a_0=\frac1\pi\int_{-a}^{a}\pi\,dx=2a,
$$
and, for $n\ge1$,
$$
a_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^a\pi\cos(nx)\,dx
=\frac{2\sin(na)}n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-2}

Parseval's identity gives
$$
2\pi a=2a^2+4g(a).
$$


::: pf-proof

As in step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref} of part (b),
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f_a(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac1\pi\int_{-a}^{a}\pi^2\,dx
=2\pi a.
$$
Using the coefficients from step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref} in Parseval's identity yields
$$
2\pi a
=\frac{(2a)^2}{2}
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4\sin^2(na)}{n^2}
=2a^2+4g(a).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Solving the identity in step [](#s3-2){.pf-ref} gives $g(a)=a(\pi-a)/2$. The same formula gives $0$ at the endpoints $a=0$ and $a=\pi$, as does the defining series.

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives the Fourier series, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} evaluates the sum in part (b), and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} evaluates the parameterized sum in part (c).

:::
