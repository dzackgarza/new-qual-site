::: {.problem}
(a) Find the Fourier series on $[-\pi,\pi]$ of
\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
\pi,&-1\le x\le1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

(b) Use Parseval's theorem to evaluate
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sin^2 n}{n^2}.
\]

(c) For $0\le a\le\pi$, repeat the computation with
\[
f_a(x)=
\begin{cases}
\pi,&-a\le x\le a,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
and find
\[
g(a)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sin^2(na)}{n^2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For a $2\pi$-periodic function, write
\[
f(x)\sim \frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\bigl(a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\bigr),
\]
where
\[
a_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\cos(nx)\,dx,
\qquad
b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx.
\]

<1>1. The Fourier series in part (a) is
\[
\boxed{
f(x)\sim 1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin n}{n}\cos(nx)
}.
\]

<2>1. $b_n=0$ for every $n\ge1$.

::: {.proof}
The function $f$ is even, while $\sin(nx)$ is odd. Their product is odd, so its integral over $[-\pi,\pi]$ is zero.
:::

<2>2. $a_0=2$ and, for $n\ge1$,
\[
a_n=\frac{2\sin n}{n}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since $f$ is supported on $[-1,1]$ and equals $\pi$ there,
\[
a_0=\frac1\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\pi\,dx=2.
\]
For $n\ge1$, evenness gives
\[
a_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^1\pi\cos(nx)\,dx
=2\left[\frac{\sin(nx)}n\right]_{0}^{1}
=\frac{2\sin n}{n}.
\]
:::

<2>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Substituting the coefficients from steps <2>1 and <2>2 into the Fourier expansion gives the series in step <1>1. At the jump points $x=\pm1$ the series converges to the midpoint value $\pi/2$, and at every continuity point it converges to $f(x)$.
:::

<1>2. Parseval's theorem gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^2 n}{n^2}
=\frac{\pi-1}{2}
}.
\]

<2>1. The normalized squared $L^2$ norm is
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx=2\pi.
\]

::: {.proof}
The function has magnitude $\pi$ on an interval of length $2$ and vanishes elsewhere, so
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac1\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\pi^2\,dx
=2\pi.
\]
:::

<2>2. Parseval's identity reduces to
\[
2\pi=2+4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^2 n}{n^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Parseval's theorem in the normalization fixed above is
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac{a_0^2}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2+b_n^2).
\]
Step <1>1 gives $a_0=2$, $a_n=2\sin n/n$, and $b_n=0$, while step <2>1 gives the left-hand side.
:::

<2>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Solving the identity in step <2>2 for the series gives $(\pi-1)/2$.
:::

<1>3. For every $0\le a\le\pi$,
\[
\boxed{
g(a)=\frac{a(\pi-a)}2
}.
\]

<2>1. The Fourier coefficients of $f_a$ are
\[
a_0=2a,
\qquad
a_n=\frac{2\sin(na)}n,
\qquad
b_n=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
The function $f_a$ is even, so $b_n=0$. Moreover,
\[
a_0=\frac1\pi\int_{-a}^{a}\pi\,dx=2a,
\]
and, for $n\ge1$,
\[
a_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^a\pi\cos(nx)\,dx
=\frac{2\sin(na)}n.
\]
:::

<2>2. Parseval's identity gives
\[
2\pi a=2a^2+4g(a).
\]

::: {.proof}
As in step <2>1 of part (b),
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f_a(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac1\pi\int_{-a}^{a}\pi^2\,dx
=2\pi a.
\]
Using the coefficients from step <2>1 in Parseval's identity yields
\[
2\pi a
=\frac{(2a)^2}{2}
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4\sin^2(na)}{n^2}
=2a^2+4g(a).
\]
:::

<2>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Solving the identity in step <2>2 gives $g(a)=a(\pi-a)/2$. The same formula gives $0$ at the endpoints $a=0$ and $a=\pi$, as does the defining series.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the Fourier series, step <1>2 evaluates the sum in part (b), and step <1>3 evaluates the parameterized sum in part (c).
:::
