::: {.problem}
(a) Suppose a $2\times2$ matrix $A$ has eigenvalues $2,3$ with eigenvectors
$$
v_1=(1,1)^T,
\qquad
v_2=(1,-1)^T.
$$
For each of $B=A^T$, $B=A^2-A$, and $B=A^{-1}$, decide whether $2,3$ are eigenvalues of $B$ and whether $v_1,v_2$ are both eigenvectors of $B$.

(b) Match the systems $\dot x=A_ix$ for
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_1&=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},&
A_2&=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix},&
A_3&=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-3\end{pmatrix},\\
A_4&=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&-3\end{pmatrix},&
A_5&=\begin{pmatrix}-\tfrac12&0\\0&\tfrac13\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
to the source portraits A--F. Qualitatively, A consists of vertical trajectories converging to a line of equilibria; B is a saddle with horizontal unstable and vertical stable axes; C is a repelling node; D is an attracting node; E is a repelling improper node; and F is a saddle with horizontal stable and vertical unstable axes.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The matrix $A$ in part (a) is symmetric.


::: pf-proof

The given eigenvectors satisfy
$$
v_1^Tv_2=(1,1)\binom1{-1}=0.
$$
After normalizing them, they form an orthonormal eigenbasis. Hence for an orthogonal matrix $Q$ with these normalized vectors as columns,
$$
A=Q\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix}Q^T.
$$
Taking transposes shows $A^T=A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For $B=A^T$, both $2,3$ are eigenvalues and both $v_1,v_2$ are eigenvectors.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $A^T=A$. Therefore $A^T$ has exactly the same eigenvalue-eigenvector pairs as $A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For $B=A^2-A$, the eigenvalues are $2,6$, and both $v_1,v_2$ are eigenvectors.


::: pf-proof

If $Av=\lambda v$, then
$$
(A^2-A)v=(\lambda^2-\lambda)v.
$$
Thus $v_1$ has eigenvalue $2^2-2=2$ and $v_2$ has eigenvalue $3^2-3=6$. Hence the joint assertion “$2$ and $3$ are eigenvalues” is false, while both given vectors remain eigenvectors.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For $B=A^{-1}$, the eigenvalues are $1/2,1/3$, and both $v_1,v_2$ are eigenvectors.


::: pf-proof

Since the eigenvalues $2,3$ are nonzero, $A$ is invertible. From $Av_j=\lambda_jv_j$ one obtains
$$
A^{-1}v_j=\lambda_j^{-1}v_j.
$$
Thus the eigenvalues are $1/2,1/3$, so the joint assertion “$2$ and $3$ are eigenvalues” is false, while both given vectors remain eigenvectors.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The box choices in part (a) are therefore
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cc}
B&2,3\text{ both eigenvalues}&v_1,v_2\text{ both eigenvectors}\\ \hline
A^T&\text{yes}&\text{yes}\\
A^2-A&\text{no}&\text{yes}\\
A^{-1}&\text{no}&\text{yes}
\end{array}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This table is the summary of steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The system for $A_1$ matches portrait $\mathrm E$.


::: pf-proof

The matrix $A_1$ has the repeated positive eigenvalue $1$ and only one eigendirection, because
$$
A_1-I=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
$$
has one-dimensional kernel. Its solutions have the form
$$
y(t)=c_2e^t,
\qquad
x(t)=e^t(c_1+2c_2t),
$$
so the origin is a repelling improper node, which is portrait E.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The system for $A_2$ matches portrait $\mathrm C$.


::: pf-proof

Its equations are $x'=2x$, $y'=3y$. Both eigendirections expand in forward time, so the origin is a repelling node, which is portrait C.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The system for $A_3$ matches portrait $\mathrm A$.


::: pf-proof

Its equations are $x'=0$, $y'=-3y$. Every point on the $x$-axis is an equilibrium, and every off-axis trajectory is a vertical line converging to that axis. This is portrait A.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The system for $A_4$ matches portrait $\mathrm B$.


::: pf-proof

Its equations are $x'=2x$, $y'=-3y$. Thus the horizontal eigendirection is unstable and the vertical eigendirection is stable, giving the saddle in portrait B.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The system for $A_5$ matches portrait $\mathrm F$.


::: pf-proof

Its equations are $x'=-x/2$, $y'=y/3$. Thus the horizontal eigendirection is stable and the vertical eigendirection is unstable, giving the oppositely oriented saddle in portrait F.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Hence the part (b) matching, in the order $A_1,\ldots,A_5$, is
$$
\boxed{\mathrm{E,C,A,B,F}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the sequence established in steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
