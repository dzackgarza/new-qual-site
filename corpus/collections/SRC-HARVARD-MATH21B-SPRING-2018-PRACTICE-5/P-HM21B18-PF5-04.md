::: {.problem}
Solve by row reduction
$$
x+v=10,
\qquad
y+z=6,
\qquad
x+u+v=6.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Use the column order $x,y,z,u,v$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The augmented matrix row reduces to
$$
\left[
\begin{array}{ccccc|c}
1&0&0&0&1&10\\
0&1&1&0&0&6\\
1&0&0&1&1&6
\end{array}
\right]
\mathop{\sim}^{R_3\leftarrow R_3-R_1}
\left[
\begin{array}{ccccc|c}
1&0&0&0&1&10\\
0&1&1&0&0&6\\
0&0&0&1&0&-4
\end{array}
\right].
$$


::: pf-proof

The first matrix is the augmented matrix of the given system. Replacing the third row by the third row minus the first is an elementary row operation. In the resulting matrix the leading entries are $1$ in columns $1$, $2$, and $4$, and each pivot is the only nonzero entry in its column, so the matrix is in reduced row-echelon form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

All solutions are
$$
\boxed{(x,y,z,u,v)=(10-t,\,6-s,\,s,\,-4,\,t),\qquad s,t\in\RR.}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the reduced system is
$$
x+v=10,\qquad y+z=6,\qquad u=-4.
$$
Thus $z$ and $v$ are free variables. Setting $z=s$ and $v=t$ gives $x=10-t$, $y=6-s$, and $u=-4$. Conversely, every solution of the reduced system has this form, and every such choice of $s,t$ satisfies the reduced system. Since the row operation in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} preserves the solution set, these are exactly the solutions of the original system.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives all solutions of the system.

:::
