::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
\dot x=x-y^2,
\qquad
\dot y=y-x.
$$

(a) Find the nullclines and all equilibrium points.

(b) Analyze the stability of all equilibrium points.

(c) Choose which phase portrait A--D belongs to the system.
:::

::: {.remark}
Part (c) refers to four candidate phase portraits A--D that are printed in the source exam.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The nullclines are
$$
x=y^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x=y,
$$
and the equilibrium points are $\boxed{(0,0)}$ and $\boxed{(1,1)}$.


::: pf-proof

The $x$-nullcline is defined by $x-y^2=0$, hence by $x=y^2$. The $y$-nullcline is defined by $y-x=0$, hence by $x=y$. At an equilibrium both equations hold, so $y=y^2$. Thus $y\in\{0,1\}$ and $x=y$, giving exactly $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The equilibrium $(0,0)$ is an unstable repelling equilibrium, while $(1,1)$ is an unstable saddle.


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s2-1}

The Jacobian of the vector field is
$$
J(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2y\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate $(x-y^2,y-x)$ with respect to $(x,y)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-2}

At $(0,0)$ the Jacobian has the repeated eigenvalue $1$, and at $(1,1)$ its eigenvalues are $1+\sqrt2$ and $1-\sqrt2$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
J(0,0)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose characteristic polynomial is $(1-\lambda)^2$. It also gives
$$
J(1,1)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose characteristic polynomial is
$$
(1-\lambda)^2-2.
$$
The corresponding roots are $1\pm\sqrt2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-3}

The classifications in step [](#s2){.pf-ref} follow.


::: pf-proof

Both equilibria are hyperbolic: no eigenvalue in step [](#s2-2){.pf-ref} has zero real part. The planar linearization theorem therefore gives the same local stability type as the linearized systems. At $(0,0)$ both eigenvalues have positive real part, so trajectories are locally repelled from the equilibrium. At $(1,1)$ one eigenvalue is positive and one is negative, so the equilibrium is a saddle. In particular, neither equilibrium is asymptotically stable.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s2-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s2-3){.pf-ref} compute the linearizations and determine the local stability type at both equilibria.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The source phase portrait is $\boxed{\mathrm{A}}$.


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s3-1}

At the test points $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ the vector field points northwest and southeast, respectively.


::: pf-proof

Direct substitution gives
$$
(\dot x,\dot y)(0,1)=(-1,1),
\qquad
(\dot x,\dot y)(1,0)=(1,-1).
$$

:::

Among the four portraits in the retained source PDF, only portrait A has the equilibrium and direction data from steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, and [](#s3-1){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

Portrait A shows a repelling equilibrium at the lower fixed point and a saddle at the upper fixed point, with the arrows at the two test regions oriented as in step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref}. The other three portraits fail at least one of these local stability or arrow-direction conditions.
