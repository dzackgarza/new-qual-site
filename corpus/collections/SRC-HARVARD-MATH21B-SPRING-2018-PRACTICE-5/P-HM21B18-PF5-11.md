::: {.problem}
Consider
\[
\dot x=x-y^2,
\qquad
\dot y=y-x.
\]

(a) Find the nullclines and all equilibrium points.

(b) Analyze the stability of all equilibrium points.

(c) Choose which phase portrait A--D belongs to the system.
:::

::: {.remark}
Part (c) refers to four candidate phase portraits A--D that are printed in the source exam.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The nullclines are
\[
x=y^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x=y,
\]
and the equilibrium points are $\boxed{(0,0)}$ and $\boxed{(1,1)}$.

::: {.proof}
The $x$-nullcline is defined by $x-y^2=0$, hence by $x=y^2$. The $y$-nullcline is defined by $y-x=0$, hence by $x=y$. At an equilibrium both equations hold, so $y=y^2$. Thus $y\in\{0,1\}$ and $x=y$, giving exactly $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$.
:::

<1>2. The equilibrium $(0,0)$ is an unstable repelling equilibrium, while $(1,1)$ is an unstable saddle.

<2>1. The Jacobian of the vector field is
\[
J(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2y\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Differentiate $(x-y^2,y-x)$ with respect to $(x,y)$.
:::

<2>2. At $(0,0)$ the Jacobian has the repeated eigenvalue $1$, and at $(1,1)$ its eigenvalues are $1+\sqrt2$ and $1-\sqrt2$.

::: {.proof}
Step <2>1 gives
\[
J(0,0)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose characteristic polynomial is $(1-\lambda)^2$. It also gives
\[
J(1,1)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose characteristic polynomial is
\[
(1-\lambda)^2-2.
\]
The corresponding roots are $1\pm\sqrt2$.
:::

<2>3. The classifications in step <1>2 follow.

::: {.proof}
Both equilibria are hyperbolic: no eigenvalue in step <2>2 has zero real part. The planar linearization theorem therefore gives the same local stability type as the linearized systems. At $(0,0)$ both eigenvalues have positive real part, so trajectories are locally repelled from the equilibrium. At $(1,1)$ one eigenvalue is positive and one is negative, so the equilibrium is a saddle. In particular, neither equilibrium is asymptotically stable.
:::

<2>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <2>1--<2>3 compute the linearizations and determine the local stability type at both equilibria.
:::

<1>3. The source phase portrait is $\boxed{\mathrm{A}}$.

<2>1. At the test points $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ the vector field points northwest and southeast, respectively.

::: {.proof}
Direct substitution gives
\[
(\dot x,\dot y)(0,1)=(-1,1),
\qquad
(\dot x,\dot y)(1,0)=(1,-1).
\]
:::

<2>2. Among the four portraits in the retained source PDF, only portrait A has the equilibrium and direction data from steps <1>1, <1>2, and <2>1.

::: {.proof}
Portrait A shows a repelling equilibrium at the lower fixed point and a saddle at the upper fixed point, with the arrows at the two test regions oriented as in step <2>1. The other three portraits fail at least one of these local stability or arrow-direction conditions.
:::

<2>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <2>2 uniquely identifies portrait A from the vector-field data.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 answers part (a), step <1>2 answers part (b), and step <1>3 answers part (c).
:::
