::: {.problem}
Using least squares, find the best function
\[
f(x,y)=a\sin x+b\cos y
\]
for the data
\[
(0,0,1),\quad(\pi/2,0,2),\quad(\pi/2,\pi/2,4).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\beta=\binom ab,
\qquad
z=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\4\end{pmatrix}.
\]

<1>1. The least-squares problem is to minimize $\|M\beta-z\|^2$.

::: {.proof}
At the three data points, the model values are respectively
\[
b,
\qquad
a+b,
\qquad
a,
\]
because $\sin0=0$, $\sin(\pi/2)=1$, $\cos0=1$, and $\cos(\pi/2)=0$. These are exactly the entries of $M\beta$.
:::

<1>2. The normal equations are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\binom ab
=\binom63.
\]

::: {.proof}
The least-squares minimizer satisfies $M^TM\beta=M^Tz$. Direct multiplication gives
\[
M^TM=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M^Tz=\binom63.
\]
:::

<1>3. The unique least-squares coefficients are
\[
\boxed{a=3,\qquad b=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The determinant of the matrix in step <1>2 is $3\ne0$, so the normal equations have a unique solution. They read
\[
2a+b=6,
\qquad
a+2b=3.
\]
Solving gives $a=3$ and $b=0$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore the best-fitting function is
\[
\boxed{f(x,y)=3\sin x}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Substitute the coefficients from step <1>3 into $f(x,y)=a\sin x+b\cos y$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the requested least-squares fit.
:::
:::
