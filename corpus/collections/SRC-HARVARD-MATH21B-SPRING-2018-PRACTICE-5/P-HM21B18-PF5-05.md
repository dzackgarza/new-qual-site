::: {.problem}
Using least squares, find the best function
$$
f(x,y)=a\sin x+b\cos y
$$
for the data
$$
(0,0,1),\quad(\pi/2,0,2),\quad(\pi/2,\pi/2,4).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\beta=\binom ab,
\qquad
z=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\4\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The least-squares problem is to minimize $\norm{M\beta-z}^2$.


::: pf-proof

At the three data points, the model values are respectively
$$
b,
\qquad
a+b,
\qquad
a,
$$
because $\sin0=0$, $\sin(\pi/2)=1$, $\cos0=1$, and $\cos(\pi/2)=0$. These are exactly the entries of $M\beta$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The normal equations are
$$
\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\binom ab
=\binom63.
$$


::: pf-proof

The least-squares minimizer satisfies $M^TM\beta=M^Tz$. Direct multiplication gives
$$
M^TM=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M^Tz=\binom63.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The unique least-squares coefficients are
$$
\boxed{a=3,\qquad b=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The determinant of the matrix in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} is $3\ne0$, so the normal equations have a unique solution. They read
$$
2a+b=6,
\qquad
a+2b=3.
$$
Solving gives $a=3$ and $b=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore the best-fitting function is
$$
\boxed{f(x,y)=3\sin x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute the coefficients from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} into $f(x,y)=a\sin x+b\cos y$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is the requested least-squares fit.

:::
