::: problem
Give a list (together with a brief explanation) of all surfaces (orientable or not, and with or without boundary) that are homotopy equivalent to the wedge of two circles.
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the standard classification convention that a surface here is compact and connected, possibly with boundary.

<1>1. A surface homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^1$ has
\[
\chi=-1,
\qquad
H_1(-;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^2,
\qquad
H_2(-;\ZZ)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The wedge $S^1\vee S^1$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell and two $1$-cells, so
\[
\chi(S^1\vee S^1)=1-2=-1.
\]
Its reduced homology is concentrated in degree $1$, where it is $\ZZ^2$.
Euler characteristic and homology are homotopy invariants.
:::

<1>2. No closed orientable surface is homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^1$.
::: {.proof}
A closed orientable surface has
\[
H_2(-;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\]
whereas <1>1 gives $H_2(S^1\vee S^1;\ZZ)=0$.
:::

<1>3. No closed nonorientable surface is homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^1$.
::: {.proof}
The closed nonorientable surface of genus $k\ge1$ has
\[
H_1(-;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^{k-1}\oplus\ZZ/2\ZZ.
\]
This group has $2$-torsion, while <1>1 gives the torsion-free group $\ZZ^2$.
:::

<1>4. For an orientable surface of genus $g$ with $b\ge1$ boundary components, the condition $\chi=-1$ is equivalent to
\[
2g+b=3.
\]
Hence the only orientable possibilities are
\[
(g,b)=(0,3)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(g,b)=(1,1).
\]
::: {.proof}
The classification theorem gives
\[
\chi=2-2g-b.
\]
Combining this with <1>1 gives $2-2g-b=-1$, or $2g+b=3$.
With $g\ge0$ and $b\ge1$, the two displayed pairs are the only solutions.
They are respectively a pair of pants and a torus with one open disk removed.
:::

<1>5. For a nonorientable surface of genus $k$ with $b\ge1$ boundary components, the condition $\chi=-1$ is equivalent to
\[
k+b=3.
\]
Hence the only nonorientable possibilities are
\[
(k,b)=(1,2)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(k,b)=(2,1).
\]
::: {.proof}
The classification theorem gives
\[
\chi=2-k-b.
\]
Thus <1>1 yields $k+b=3$.
With $k\ge1$ and $b\ge1$, the only solutions are the two displayed pairs.
They are respectively $\RP^2$ with two open disks removed and the Klein bottle with one open disk removed.
:::

<1>6. Each of the four surfaces in <1>4 and <1>5 is homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^1$.
::: {.proof}
Every compact connected surface with nonempty boundary admits a deformation retraction onto a finite connected graph obtained from a handle decomposition with no $2$-cell.
For such a graph $G$,
\[
\chi(G)=1-r,
\]
where $r$ is its first Betti number, equivalently the number of circles in a wedge to which $G$ is homotopy equivalent.

Each of the four candidate surfaces has Euler characteristic $-1$, so its spine has
\[
1-r=-1,
\qquad r=2.
\]
Hence its spine, and therefore the surface itself, is homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^1$.
:::

<1>7. The complete list is therefore:

- the pair of pants $S^2$ with three open disks removed;

- the torus with one open disk removed;

- $\RP^2$ with two open disks removed;

- the Klein bottle with one open disk removed.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2 and <1>3 exclude all closed surfaces, <1>4 and <1>5 exhaust the compact connected surfaces with boundary having the required Euler characteristic, and <1>6 verifies that all four candidates have the required homotopy type.
:::
