::: problem
Prove or disprove: The fundamental group of a Klein bottle is an abelian group.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The standard polygonal CW structure of the Klein bottle gives
\[
\pi_1(K)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}=b^{-1}\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The Klein bottle has one $0$-cell, two oriented $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell whose attaching word is
\[
aba^{-1}b.
\]
The cellular presentation of the fundamental group therefore gives
\[
\pi_1(K)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b=1\rangle
=
\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}=b^{-1}\rangle.
:::

<1>2. There is a homomorphism
\[
\rho:\pi_1(K)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\RR^2)
\]
with
\[
\rho(a)=A,
\qquad
\rho(b)=B,
\]
where
\[
A(x,y)=(x+1,-y),
\qquad
B(x,y)=(x,y+1).
\]
::: {.proof}
It suffices, by the presentation in <1>1, to verify the defining relation.
Since
\[
A^{-1}(x,y)=(x-1,-y),
\]
one computes
\[
(ABA^{-1})(x,y)
=A(x-1,-y+1)
=(x,y-1)
=B^{-1}(x,y).
\]
Thus $ABA^{-1}=B^{-1}$, so the assignment extends to a homomorphism from the presented group.
:::

<1>3. The isometries $A$ and $B$ do not commute.
::: {.proof}
At the origin,
\[
(A\circ B)(0,0)=(1,-1),
\qquad
(B\circ A)(0,0)=(1,1).
\]
Hence $AB\ne BA$.
:::

<1>4. The fundamental group of the Klein bottle is nonabelian.
::: {.proof}
If $a$ and $b$ commuted in $\pi_1(K)$, then their images under every homomorphism would commute.
But <1>2 and <1>3 give a homomorphism for which
\[
\rho(a)\rho(b)=AB\ne BA=\rho(b)\rho(a).
\]
Therefore $ab\ne ba$ in $\pi_1(K)$, disproving the claim that the group is abelian.
:::
:::
