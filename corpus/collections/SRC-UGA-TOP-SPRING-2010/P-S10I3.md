::: {.problem}
Let $(X, d)$ be a compact metric space, and let $f : X \to X$ be an isometry (this means that $d(f(x), f(y)) = d(x, y)$ for all $x, y \in X$). Show that $f$ must be a surjection.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $f$ is not surjective, there are $p\in X$ and $\delta>0$ such that
\[
d(p,f(X))\ge\delta.
\]

::: pf-proof

Assume $f$ is not surjective and choose $p\in X\setminus f(X)$.
Because $X$ is compact and $f$ is continuous, $f(X)$ is compact, hence closed in the metric space $X$.
Therefore
\[
\delta\definedas d(p,f(X))>0.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The forward orbit
\[
p,f(p),f^2(p),\ldots
\]
is $\delta$-separated.

::: pf-proof

Let $n>m\ge0$.
Since $f$ is an isometry,
\[
d(f^n(p),f^m(p))
=d(f^{n-m}(p),p).
\]
Because $n-m\ge1$, the point $f^{n-m}(p)$ lies in $f(X)$.
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
\[
d(f^{n-m}(p),p)\ge\delta.
\]
Hence distinct points in the orbit are at least $\delta$ apart.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

A compact metric space cannot contain an infinite $\delta$-separated subset.

::: pf-proof

The open balls
\[
\{B_{\delta/2}(x):x\in X\}
\]
cover $X$.
Compactness gives a finite subcover.
Each ball of radius $\delta/2$ contains at most one point of a $\delta$-separated set, so every $\delta$-separated subset of $X$ is finite.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$f$ is surjective.

::: pf-proof

If $f$ were not surjective, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} would produce the infinite $\delta$-separated orbit of $p$, contradicting step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::
