::: {.exercise}
(a) State the classification theorem for finitely generated modules over a Euclidean domain, and give a brief sketch of the main steps of the existence proof.

(b) Give an explicit example of one abelian group in each isomorphism class of abelian groups of order 360.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. State the classification theorem for finitely generated modules over a Euclidean domain.
::: proof
Let $R$ be a Euclidean domain and let $M$ be a finitely generated
$R$-module. Then there are uniquely determined data, up to multiplication of
the $d_i$ by units,
$$
r\ge0,
\qquad
0\ne d_1,\ldots,d_t\in R,
$$
with
$$
d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_t
$$
and each $d_i$ a nonunit, such that
$$
\boxed{
M\cong R^r\oplus R/(d_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus R/(d_t).}
$$
The integer $r$ is the free rank, and the $d_i$ are the invariant factors of
the torsion submodule.

Equivalently, after factoring the $d_i$ into powers of irreducibles, the
torsion part may be written uniquely as a direct sum of modules
$R/(p^a)$; this is the elementary-divisor form.
:::

<1>2. Sketch the existence proof via Smith normal form.
::: proof
Choose generators $m_1,\ldots,m_n$ of $M$. They give a surjection
$$
\pi:R^n\twoheadrightarrow M.
$$
Its kernel $K$ is a submodule of the finite-rank free module $R^n$. Since a
Euclidean domain is a PID and submodules of finite-rank free modules over a
PID are free and finitely generated, choose a basis of $K$. This gives a
finite presentation
$$
R^m\xrightarrow{A}R^n\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0
$$
for some matrix $A$ over $R$.

Elementary row and column operations on $A$ correspond to changing bases in
$R^n$ and $R^m$, so they do not change the isomorphism type of the cokernel.
Using the Euclidean algorithm, repeatedly choose a nonzero entry of least
Euclidean size, use division with remainder to clear its row and column, and
iterate. If the current pivot does not divide a later diagonal entry, a
further row/column combination followed by the Euclidean algorithm decreases
the pivot. This process terminates and produces Smith normal form
$$
UAV
=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_t,0,\ldots,0)
$$
with
$$
d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_t.
$$

Taking cokernels now gives
$$
M\cong
R/(d_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus R/(d_t)\oplus R^{n-t},
$$
which is the asserted decomposition. Uniqueness follows, for example, from
the determinantal divisors: the ideals generated by the $k\times k$ minors
are invariant under row and column operations and determine
$d_1\cdots d_k$ for every $k$.
:::

<1>3. Reduce the classification of abelian groups of order $360$ to their primary components.
::: proof
Factor
$$
360=2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5.
$$
Every finite abelian group $A$ of this order decomposes uniquely as
$$
A=A_{(2)}\times A_{(3)}\times A_{(5)},
$$
where the three factors have orders $8$, $9$, and $5$ respectively.

The abelian groups of order $8$ correspond to the partitions of $3$:
$$
C_8,
\qquad
C_4\times C_2,
\qquad
C_2\times C_2\times C_2.
$$
The abelian groups of order $9$ correspond to the partitions of $2$:
$$
C_9,
\qquad
C_3\times C_3.
$$
There is only one group of order $5$, namely $C_5$.
Therefore there are
$$
3\cdot2\cdot1=6
$$
isomorphism classes in total.
:::

<1>4. Give one explicit representative of each isomorphism class.
::: proof
Combining each possible $2$-primary factor with each possible $3$-primary
factor and with $C_5$ gives exactly the six groups
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&C_8\times C_9\times C_5,\\
&C_8\times C_3\times C_3\times C_5,\\
&C_4\times C_2\times C_9\times C_5,\\
&C_4\times C_2\times C_3\times C_3\times C_5,\\
&C_2\times C_2\times C_2\times C_9\times C_5,\\
&C_2\times C_2\times C_2\times C_3\times C_3\times C_5.
\end{aligned}}
$$
Their primary decompositions are pairwise different, so no two are
isomorphic, and step <1>3 shows that the list is exhaustive.
:::
:::
