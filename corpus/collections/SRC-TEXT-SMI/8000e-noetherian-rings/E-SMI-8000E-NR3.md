::: {.exercise}
If $R$ is a ring and

$$
0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0
$$

an exact sequence of $R$-modules, prove $B$ is noetherian if and only if both $A$ and $C$ are.

[If $A$, $C$ are finitely generated, prove $B$ is too.] Conclude that if $N$ is a submodule of $M$, then $N$ and $M/N$ are both noetherian modules if and only if $M$ is.
:::


::: {.solution}
Write the exact sequence as
$$
0\longrightarrow A\xrightarrow{i}B\xrightarrow{q}C\longrightarrow0.
$$
We identify $A$ with the submodule $i(A)\subseteq B$.

<1>1. If $B$ is noetherian, then $A$ is noetherian.
::: {.proof}
Every submodule of $A$ is also a submodule of $B$. Since $B$ is noetherian,
every such submodule is finitely generated. Hence $A$ is noetherian.
:::

<1>2. If $B$ is noetherian, then $C$ is noetherian.
::: {.proof}
Let $D\subseteq C$ be a submodule. Its preimage
$$
q^{-1}(D)\subseteq B
$$
is finitely generated because $B$ is noetherian. If
$$
q^{-1}(D)=Rb_1+\cdots+Rb_r,
$$
then surjectivity of
$$
q:q^{-1}(D)\to D
$$
shows that
$$
D=Rq(b_1)+\cdots+Rq(b_r).
$$
Thus every submodule of $C$ is finitely generated, so $C$ is noetherian.
:::

<1>3. If $A$ and $C$ are noetherian, then $B$ is noetherian.
::: {.proof}
Let $N\subseteq B$ be any submodule. Since $A$ is noetherian,
$$
N\cap A
$$
is generated by finitely many elements
$$
a_1,\ldots,a_r.
$$
The image
$$
q(N)\subseteq C
$$
is finitely generated because $C$ is noetherian; write
$$
q(N)=Rc_1+\cdots+Rc_s.
$$
Choose $b_j\in N$ with
$$
q(b_j)=c_j.
$$

For any $x\in N$, write
$$
q(x)=\sum_{j=1}^s r_j c_j.
$$
Then
$$
x-\sum_{j=1}^s r_jb_j\in\ker q=A,
$$
and it also lies in $N$, so it belongs to $N\cap A$. Hence it is an
$R$-linear combination of $a_1,\ldots,a_r$. Therefore
$$
N=Ra_1+\cdots+Ra_r+Rb_1+\cdots+Rb_s.
$$
Thus every submodule of $B$ is finitely generated, and $B$ is noetherian.
:::

<1>4. If $A$ and $C$ are merely finitely generated, then $B$ is finitely generated.
::: {.proof}
Choose generators
$$
a_1,\ldots,a_r
$$
of $A$ and
$$
c_1,\ldots,c_s
$$
of $C$. Lift each $c_j$ to some $b_j\in B$. For any $b\in B$, express
$$
q(b)=\sum_j r_j c_j.
$$
Then
$$
b-\sum_jr_jb_j\in A,
$$
so it is generated by the $a_i$. Hence
$$
B=Ra_1+\cdots+Ra_r+Rb_1+\cdots+Rb_s.
$$
:::

<1>5. Apply the result to a submodule and its quotient.
::: {.proof}
For any submodule $N\subseteq M$, there is an exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/N\longrightarrow0.
$$
Steps <1>1--<1>3 therefore give
$$
\boxed{
M\text{ is noetherian}
\iff
N\text{ and }M/N\text{ are noetherian}.}
$$
:::
:::
