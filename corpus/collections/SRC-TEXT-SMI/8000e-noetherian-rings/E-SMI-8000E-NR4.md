::: {.exercise}
If $R$ is a noetherian ring, prove any finitely generated $R$ module $M$ is noetherian.
:::


::: {.solution}
Suppose $M$ is generated by $m$ elements. Then there is a surjective
$R$-linear map
$$
\pi:R^m\twoheadrightarrow M.
$$

<1>1. Every finite free module $R^m$ is noetherian.
::: {.proof}
Because $R$ is a noetherian ring, $R$ is noetherian as an $R$-module. We
induct on $m$. The case $m=1$ is exactly the hypothesis.

For $m>1$, use the exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow R^{m-1}
\longrightarrow R^m
\longrightarrow R
\longrightarrow0,
$$
where the last map is projection onto the final coordinate. By induction
$R^{m-1}$ is noetherian, and $R$ is noetherian. The two-out-of-three result
[[E-SMI-8000E-NR3]] therefore shows that $R^m$ is noetherian.
:::

<1>2. Pass to the quotient $M$.
::: {.proof}
Since $\pi$ is surjective,
$$
M\cong R^m/\ker\pi.
$$
A quotient of a noetherian module is noetherian by
[[E-SMI-8000E-NR3]]. Hence $M$ is noetherian.

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\text{every finitely generated module over a noetherian ring is noetherian}.}
$$
:::
:::
