::: {.exercise}
For an $R$ module $M$ and ideal $I$, let $IM$ be the submodule generated by all products $rx$, with $r$ in $I$ and $x$ in $M$.
Prove $R^n / IR^n$ is isomorphic to $(R/I)^n$ as $R$ modules, and as $R/I$ modules.
If $R^n \cong R^m$, deduce $n = m$, assuming it holds when $R$ is a field.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. Identify $IR^n$ coordinatewise.
::: proof
Let $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ be the standard basis of $R^n$. Every generator of
$IR^n$ has the form
$$
r(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=(rx_1,\ldots,rx_n)
$$
with $r\in I$, so every coordinate of an element of $IR^n$ lies in $I$.
Conversely, if
$$
(a_1,\ldots,a_n)
$$
has each $a_j\in I$, then
$$
(a_1,\ldots,a_n)=\sum_{j=1}^n a_je_j\in IR^n.
$$
Thus
$$
IR^n=I^n
$$
inside $R^n$.
:::

<1>2. Construct the quotient isomorphism.
::: proof
Define
$$
\Phi:R^n\longrightarrow(R/I)^n
$$
by coordinatewise reduction:
$$
\Phi(r_1,\ldots,r_n)
=(r_1+I,\ldots,r_n+I).
$$
This is a surjective $R$-module homomorphism. By step <1>1,
$$
\ker\Phi=IR^n.
$$
The first isomorphism theorem therefore gives
$$
\boxed{R^n/IR^n\cong(R/I)^n}
$$
as $R$-modules.
:::

<1>3. The same map is an isomorphism of $R/I$-modules.
::: proof
The ideal $I$ annihilates the quotient $R^n/IR^n$, so scalar multiplication
by $R$ factors through $R/I$: define
$$
(r+I)(x+IR^n)=rx+IR^n.
$$
This is well defined because changing $r$ by an element of $I$ changes $rx$
by an element of $IR^n$.

The standard scalar action on $(R/I)^n$ is also coordinatewise, and the map
induced by $\Phi$ satisfies
$$
\Phi((r+I)(x+IR^n))=(r+I)\Phi(x+IR^n).
$$
Hence the isomorphism is $R/I$-linear as well.
:::

<1>4. An isomorphism $R^n\cong R^m$ descends modulo every ideal.
::: proof
Let
$$
F:R^n\xrightarrow{\sim}R^m
$$
be $R$-linear. Then
$$
F(IR^n)=IR^m.
$$
Indeed, $F(ix)=iF(x)$ gives one inclusion, and applying the same argument to
$F^{-1}$ gives the reverse inclusion. Thus $F$ induces an isomorphism
$$
R^n/IR^n\xrightarrow{\sim}R^m/IR^m.
$$
By steps <1>2--<1>3,
$$
(R/I)^n\cong(R/I)^m
$$
as $R/I$-modules.
:::

<1>5. Reduce rank invariance to the field case.
::: proof
Choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $R$. Then
$$
k=R/\mathfrak m
$$
is a field. Applying step <1>4 with $I=\mathfrak m$ gives
$$
k^n\cong k^m.
$$
By the assumed invariance of dimension for vector spaces over a field,
$$
n=m.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{R^n\cong R^m\Longrightarrow n=m.}
$$
:::
