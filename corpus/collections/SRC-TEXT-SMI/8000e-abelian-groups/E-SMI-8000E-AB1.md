::: {.exercise}
Prove: if $A \to B \to C \to 0$ is an exact sequence of abelian groups, and if $A$ and $C$ are finitely generated, then so is $B$.

[Hint: use the images of some generators of $A$, and some choice of preimages of some generators of $C$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $A \xrightarrow{\alpha} B \xrightarrow{\beta} C \to 0$ be the exact sequence.
Proof: name the maps.

<1>2. Let $a_1, \ldots, a_m$ generate $A$ and $c_1, \ldots, c_n$ generate $C$.
Proof: $A$ and $C$ are finitely generated.

<1>3. For each $c_j$, choose a preimage $b_j \in B$ with $\beta(b_j) = c_j$.
Proof: $\beta$ is surjective (exactness at $C$).

<1>4. Let $S = \{\alpha(a_1), \ldots, \alpha(a_m), b_1, \ldots, b_n\}$.
Proof: the candidate generating set.

<1>5. $S$ generates $B$.
Proof: for any $b \in B$, $\beta(b) = \sum_j n_j c_j$ (for integers $n_j$), so $b - \sum_j n_j b_j \in \ker \beta = \operatorname{im} \alpha$; hence $b - \sum_j n_j b_j = \sum_i m_i \alpha(a_i)$, so $b = \sum_i m_i \alpha(a_i) + \sum_j n_j b_j$ is in the subgroup generated by $S$.

<1>6. Hence $B$ is finitely generated.
Proof: <1>4 and <1>5.

<1>7. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>6.
:::
