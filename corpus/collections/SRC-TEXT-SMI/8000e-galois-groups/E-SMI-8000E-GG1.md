::: {.exercise}
(i) Prove that the polynomial $f(X) = X^2 + X + 1$ is irreducible over $k = \ZZ/2$, and hence that the quotient $k[X]/(f)$ defines a field of 4 elements.

(ii) Prove that the splitting field of $X^2 + X + 1$ over $\ZZ/2$ has dimension 2, hence the Galois group is $\ZZ/2$, a solvable group. Then determine whether the root field is a radical extension under the standard definition.
:::

::: remark
The printed exercise asks one to prove that the root field is **not** a
radical extension. Under the standard definition this is false: if $\alpha$
is a nontrivial root, then $\alpha^3=1\in\mathbf F_2$. Thus
$\mathbf F_4=\mathbf F_2(\alpha)$ is a one-step radical extension. The local
packet does not record a different convention that would change this
conclusion.
:::

::: solution
Put
$$
k=\mathbf F_2.
$$

<1>1. The polynomial $f(X)=X^2+X+1$ is irreducible over $k$.
::: proof
A quadratic polynomial over a field is reducible exactly when it has a root
in that field. The only elements of $\mathbf F_2$ are $0$ and $1$, and
$$
f(0)=1,
\qquad
f(1)=1+1+1=1
$$
in characteristic $2$. Thus $f$ has no root in $\mathbf F_2$ and is
irreducible.
:::

<1>2. The quotient $k[X]/(f)$ is a field with four elements.
::: proof
Because $f$ is irreducible, the ideal $(f)$ is maximal in $k[X]$, so
$$
K=k[X]/(f)
$$
is a field. Let
$$
\alpha=X+(f)\in K.
$$
Every residue class has a unique representative of degree less than $2$,
so every element is uniquely of the form
$$
a+b\alpha,
\qquad a,b\in\mathbf F_2.
$$
Hence
$$
|K|=2^2=4.
$$
:::

<1>3. This field is the splitting field of $f$ and has degree $2$ over $k$.
::: proof
By construction,
$$
\alpha^2+\alpha+1=0.
$$
In characteristic $2$, the other root is
$$
\alpha+1,
$$
because
$$
(X-\alpha)(X-(\alpha+1))
=(X+\alpha)(X+\alpha+1)
=X^2+X+1.
$$
Thus $f$ splits in $K$, and $K=k(\alpha)$ is generated by one root, so $K$
is the splitting field. Since the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $k$
is the irreducible quadratic $f$,
$$
\boxed{[K:k]=2.}
$$
:::

<1>4. The Galois group is cyclic of order $2$.
::: proof
The polynomial $f$ is separable: its derivative is
$$
f'(X)=2X+1=1.
$$
Hence the splitting field $K/k$ is Galois. Its Galois group has order
$$
[K:k]=2.
$$
More explicitly, the nontrivial automorphism is the Frobenius map
$$
\varphi(x)=x^2.
$$
It fixes $\mathbf F_2$ and sends
$$
\alpha\longmapsto\alpha^2=\alpha+1,
$$
so it exchanges the two roots. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)\cong C_2.}
$$
In particular the Galois group is solvable.
:::

<1>5. Under the standard definition, the root field is a radical extension.
::: proof
From
$$
\alpha^2+\alpha+1=0
$$
we obtain
$$
\alpha^2=\alpha+1.
$$
Multiplying by $\alpha$ gives
$$
\alpha^3=\alpha^2+\alpha=1.
$$
Thus
$$
\alpha^3\in k.
$$
Since
$$
K=k(\alpha),
$$
the extension is obtained by adjoining an element whose positive power lies
in the base field. Hence, under the standard definition,
$$
\boxed{K/k\text{ is a radical extension}.}
$$
This directly contradicts the final sentence of the printed problem, which
is why that sentence has been corrected on this card.
:::
:::
