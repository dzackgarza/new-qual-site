::: {.exercise}
Show that $X^5 - X - 1$ factors mod 2 into an irreducible quadratic and an irreducible cubic.
Show there is also a transposition, and deduce that the Galois group over $\QQ$ is $S(5)$.

[Fact from the sheet: if $f$ is a monic polynomial of degree $n$ over $\ZZ$ which is separable mod $p$, with irreducible factors mod $p$ of degrees $a, b, \ldots, c$, then the Galois group of $f$ over $\QQ$, as a subgroup of $S(n)$, contains a cycle with decomposition of type $a, b, \ldots, c$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
f(X)=X^5-X-1\in\mathbf Z[X]
$$
and let
$$
G=\operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbf Q)\le S_5.
$$

<1>1. Modulo $2$, the polynomial factors as an irreducible quadratic times an irreducible cubic.
::: {.proof}
Reducing modulo $2$ gives
$$
\overline f(X)=X^5+X+1.
$$
Direct multiplication shows
$$
X^5+X+1
=(X^2+X+1)(X^3+X^2+1).
$$
The quadratic $X^2+X+1$ has no root in $\mathbf F_2$, so it is irreducible.
The cubic $X^3+X^2+1$ also has no root in $\mathbf F_2$:
$$
g(0)=1,
\qquad
g(1)=1+1+1=1.
$$
A cubic over a field is reducible exactly when it has a root, so this cubic is
irreducible as well.

The two factors are distinct, so the reduction is separable. By the cycle-type
fact from the sheet, $G$ contains an element with disjoint cycle structure
$$
(2)(3).
$$
:::

<1>2. The group $G$ contains a transposition.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\sigma=(ab)(cde)\in G
$$
be the element supplied by step <1>1. Since the two cycles are disjoint,
$$
\sigma^3
=(ab)^3(cde)^3
=(ab).
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{G\text{ contains a transposition}.}
$$
:::

<1>3. Modulo $3$, the polynomial is irreducible.
::: {.proof}
Modulo $3$,
$$
\overline f(X)=X^5+2X+2.
$$
It has no root in $\mathbf F_3$, since
$$
\overline f(0)=\overline f(1)=\overline f(2)=2.
$$
If a polynomial of degree five is reducible and has no linear factor, then it
must have an irreducible factor of degree two. The monic irreducible
quadratics over $\mathbf F_3$ are
$$
X^2+1,
\qquad
X^2+X+2,
\qquad
X^2+2X+2.
$$
Division gives remainders
$$
2,
\qquad
X+2,
\qquad
X+2,
$$
respectively. Hence none divides $\overline f$.

Therefore $\overline f$ is irreducible of degree five over $\mathbf F_3$.
In particular it is separable, and the cycle-type fact gives a $5$-cycle in
$G$.
:::

<1>4. A subgroup of $S_5$ containing a $5$-cycle and a transposition is all of $S_5$.
::: {.proof}
After relabeling the five roots, write the $5$-cycle as
$$
\rho=(1\,2\,3\,4\,5)
$$
and let
$$
\tau=(i\,j)
$$
be a transposition in $G$. Conjugating by powers of $\rho$ gives
$$
\rho^k\tau\rho^{-k}
=(\rho^k(i)\,\rho^k(j)).
$$
These transpositions are the edges of the graph on
$\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ obtained by repeatedly translating the fixed nonzero
difference $j-i$ modulo $5$. Since $5$ is prime, that graph is connected.

Transpositions along the edges of a connected graph generate the full
symmetric group on its vertices: along a path one obtains arbitrary
transpositions by conjugating adjacent edge transpositions. Hence the
subgroup generated by $\rho$ and $\tau$ is $S_5$.
Since both lie in $G$,
$$
S_5\le G\le S_5.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(X^5-X-1/\mathbf Q)\cong S_5.}
$$
:::
