::: {.exercise}
Prove every submodule of $R^m$ is finitely generated, and in fact isomorphic to $R^n$ where $n$ is at most equal to $m$.
[Hint: read the proof for $\ZZ$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove by induction on $m\ge1$ that every submodule $M\subseteq R^m$ is isomorphic to $R^n$ for some $n\le m$; a module isomorphic to $R^n$ is generated by $n$ elements.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Every submodule of $R$ is isomorphic to $R^0$ or $R^1$.

::: pf-proof

A submodule of $R$ is an ideal $I$, and $I=aR$ for some $a\in R$ by [[E-SMI-8000E-ED1]]. If $a=0$, then $I=0\cong R^0$. If $a\ne0$, the map $R\to aR$, $r\mapsto ar$, is $R$-linear and surjective, and it is injective because $R$ is a domain; hence $I\cong R$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If every submodule of $R^{m-1}$ is isomorphic to $R^k$ for some $k\le m-1$, then every submodule of $R^m$ is isomorphic to $R^n$ for some $n\le m$.

::: pf-proof

Let $M\subseteq R^m$ be a submodule and let $\pi\colon R^m\to R$, $(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\mapsto x_1$. Its kernel is $0\times R^{m-1}\cong R^{m-1}$, so $M\cap\ker\pi\cong R^k$ with $k\le m-1$ by the induction hypothesis. The image $\pi(M)$ is an ideal of $R$, so $\pi(M)=aR$ for some $a\in R$.

If $a=0$, then $M=M\cap\ker\pi\cong R^k$ with $k\le m-1$.

If $a\ne0$, choose $v\in M$ with $\pi(v)=a$. We claim
$$
M=(M\cap\ker\pi)\oplus Rv
\quad\text{and}\quad
Rv\cong R.
$$
For $x\in M$, write $\pi(x)=ra$; then $x-rv\in M\cap\ker\pi$, so $M=(M\cap\ker\pi)+Rv$. If $rv\in\ker\pi$, then $ra=0$, so $r=0$ because $R$ is a domain and $a\ne0$; this shows both that the sum is direct and that $r\mapsto rv$ is an isomorphism $R\to Rv$. Hence $M\cong R^k\oplus R\cong R^{k+1}$ with $k+1\le m$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref} complete the induction. A submodule $M\cong R^n$ of $R^m$ is generated by the images of the $n$ standard basis vectors, so it is finitely generated.

:::
