::: {.exercise}
Conclude that if $M$ is a finitely generated $R$ module, with minimal set of generators $x_1, \ldots, x_m$, then $M$ is isomorphic to some product of the form

$$
(R/Ra_1) \times \cdots \times (R/Ra_m),
$$

where some $a_i$ may be zero, but not units.

[Clarification from the sheet: you may wish to prove first that if $M$ is generated by $x_1, \ldots, x_m$, then $M$ is isomorphic to some product of cyclic modules $R/Ra_i$, and then afterwards prove that if $m$ is the minimum cardinality of a set of generators, none of the $a_i$ is a unit.]
:::


::: {.solution}
Let $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ be a generating set of $M$. Define the surjection
$$
\pi:R^m\longrightarrow M,
\qquad
e_i\longmapsto x_i.
$$
Then
$$
M\cong R^m/\ker\pi.
$$

<1>1. Realize $M$ as the cokernel of a map between finite free modules.
::: {.proof}
Because $R$ is Euclidean, every submodule of a finite free module is finitely
generated. Hence
$$
N=\ker\pi\subseteq R^m
$$
is generated by finitely many elements, say $y_1,\ldots,y_n$. Define
$$
f:R^n\longrightarrow R^m,
\qquad
f(e_j)=y_j.
$$
Then
$$
\operatorname{im}f=N,
$$
so
$$
M\cong R^m/N=\operatorname{coker}f.
$$
:::

<1>2. Diagonalize the presentation map without changing its cokernel up to isomorphism.
::: {.proof}
By the preceding matrix-diagonalization exercise over a Euclidean domain,
there are automorphisms
$$
g:R^n\xrightarrow{\sim}R^n,
\qquad
h:R^m\xrightarrow{\sim}R^m
$$
such that
$$
d=hfg
$$
is represented in the standard bases by a diagonal matrix. By
[[E-SMI-8000E-ED6]],
$$
\operatorname{coker}d\cong\operatorname{coker}f\cong M.
$$
After padding the diagonal list with zeros if necessary, write
$$
\operatorname{im}d
=Ra_1e_1+\cdots+Ra_me_m
$$
for some $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in R$; entries corresponding to missing diagonal
positions are simply $a_i=0$.
:::

<1>3. Decompose $M$ as a product of cyclic modules.
::: {.proof}
By step <1>2 and [[E-SMI-8000E-ED7]],
$$
\begin{aligned}
M
&\cong\operatorname{coker}d\\
&=R^m/(Ra_1e_1+\cdots+Ra_me_m)\\
&\cong(R/Ra_1)\times\cdots\times(R/Ra_m).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every finitely generated $R$-module generated by $m$ elements has a
decomposition into $m$ cyclic factors of this form, allowing $a_i=0$.
:::

<1>4. If $m$ is minimal, none of the $a_i$ is a unit.
::: {.proof}
If some $a_i$ were a unit, then
$$
Ra_i=R,
$$
so
$$
R/Ra_i=0.
$$
The product from step <1>3 would then have a zero factor and could be generated
by the remaining at most $m-1$ coordinate generators. Transporting those
generators through the isomorphism would give a generating set of $M$ with at
most $m-1$ elements, contradicting the minimality of $m$.

Hence, for a minimal generating set,
$$
\boxed{
M\cong(R/Ra_1)\times\cdots\times(R/Ra_m),
\quad
\text{where each }a_i\text{ is either }0\text{ or a nonunit}.}
$$
:::
