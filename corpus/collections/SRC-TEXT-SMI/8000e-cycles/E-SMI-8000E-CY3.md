::: {.exercise}
Prove that $S(n)$ is generated by 2-cycles.
[Hint: $(a_1 a_k)(a_1 a_2 \ldots a_{k-1}) = (a_1 a_2 \ldots a_k)$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every permutation is a product of disjoint cycles.
::: {.proof}
Let $\sigma \in S(n)$. The orbits of $\langle\sigma\rangle$ partition $\{1, \ldots, n\}$. On an orbit $O = \{a, \sigma(a), \ldots, \sigma^{k-1}(a)\}$ of size $k$, $\sigma$ acts as the cycle $(a\ \sigma(a)\ \cdots\ \sigma^{k-1}(a))$. Hence $\sigma$ is the product of these cycles over the orbits of size at least $2$, and cycles on distinct orbits are disjoint.
:::

<1>2. For $k \ge 2$, $(a_1 a_2 \cdots a_k) = (a_1 a_k)(a_1 a_{k-1}) \cdots (a_1 a_2)$.
::: {.proof}
Induct on $k$. For $k = 2$ both sides are $(a_1 a_2)$. For $k > 2$, the identity $(a_1 a_k)(a_1 a_2 \cdots a_{k-1}) = (a_1 a_2 \cdots a_k)$ from the hint and the induction hypothesis for $(a_1 a_2 \cdots a_{k-1})$ give the formula.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
By step <1>1 every permutation is a product of cycles, and by step <1>2 every cycle is a product of transpositions. Hence $S(n)$ is generated by $2$-cycles.
:::
:::
