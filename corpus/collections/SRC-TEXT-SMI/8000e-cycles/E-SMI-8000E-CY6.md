::: {.exercise}
Prove that $A(n)$ is generated by 3-cycles.
[Hint: if $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$ are all different, $(ac)(ab) = (abc)$ and $(cd)(ab) = (abc)(bcd)$.]
:::

::: solution
Let $H$ be the subgroup of $S_n$ generated by all $3$-cycles.

<1>1. Every $3$-cycle lies in $A_n$.
::: proof
For distinct $a,b,c$,
$$
(abc)=(ac)(ab).
$$
Thus a $3$-cycle is a product of two transpositions, hence is even. Therefore
$$
H\le A_n.
$$
:::

<1>2. The product of any two transpositions is a product of $3$-cycles.
::: proof
Let $\tau$ and $\sigma$ be transpositions.

If $\tau=\sigma$, then
$$
\tau\sigma=1,
$$
which is the empty product of $3$-cycles.

If they share exactly one point, relabel them as
$$
\tau=(ac),
\qquad
\sigma=(ab)
$$
with $a,b,c$ distinct. Then
$$
\tau\sigma=(ac)(ab)=(abc),
$$
a single $3$-cycle.

If they are disjoint, write
$$
\tau=(cd),
\qquad
\sigma=(ab)
$$
with $a,b,c,d$ all distinct. The identity from the hint gives
$$
(cd)(ab)=(abc)(bcd),
$$
a product of two $3$-cycles.

These cases exhaust all pairs of transpositions.
:::

<1>3. Every even permutation belongs to $H$.
::: proof
Let $\pi\in A_n$. By definition of $A_n$, there is an even-length
transposition decomposition
$$
\pi=\tau_1\tau_2\cdots\tau_{2r}.
$$
Group the factors in pairs:
$$
\pi
=(\tau_1\tau_2)(\tau_3\tau_4)\cdots
(\tau_{2r-1}\tau_{2r}).
$$
By step <1>2, each parenthesized pair is a product of $3$-cycles. Hence
$\pi\in H$, so
$$
A_n\le H.
$$
Together with step <1>1 this yields
$$
\boxed{A_n=H,}
$$
so $A_n$ is generated by $3$-cycles.
:::
:::
