::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal B=\{[a,b):a,b\in\RR\}$; let $\RR_\ell$ denote $\RR$ equipped with the topology generated by the basis $\mathcal B$ (you do not need to prove $\mathcal B$ is a basis).
Decide (with proof) if the following sets are compact in $\RR_\ell$:

(a) $A=\{1/n:n\in\NN\}\cup\{0\}$.

(b) $B=\{-1/n:n\in\NN\}\cup\{0\}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a-compact}
(a) $A$ is compact in $\RR_\ell$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\mathcal U$ be an open cover of $A$, and choose $U_0\in\mathcal U$ with $0\in U_0$.
Some basic set $[a,b)$ with $a\le0<b$ lies in $U_0$, so $[0,b)\subseteq U_0$.
Then $1/n\in U_0$ for all $n>1/b$, and finitely many members of $\mathcal U$ cover the remaining points $1,\tfrac12,\ldots,\tfrac1N$; with $U_0$ they form a finite subcover.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-not-compact}
(b) $B$ is not compact in $\RR_\ell$.

::: pf-proof
The sets $U_0=[0,1)$ and $U_n=[-1/n,-1/(n+1))$ for $n\ge1$ are open in $\RR_\ell$ and cover $B$.
$U_0\cap B=\{0\}$ and $U_n\cap B=\{-1/n\}$ for $n\ge1$, so a finite subfamily covers only finitely many points of the infinite set $B$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Steps [](#a-compact){.pf-ref} and [](#b-not-compact){.pf-ref} decide (a) and (b).
:::
