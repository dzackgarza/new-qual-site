::: {.problem}
Prove that every contractible space is path-connected.
**Caution:** $X$ being contractible does *not* imply that $X$ strong deformation retracts to a point.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Since $X$ is contractible, there are a point $x_0\in X$ and a homotopy
$$
H:X\times I\to X,
\qquad H(x,0)=x,\quad H(x,1)=x_0.
$$
::: {.proof}
Contractibility means precisely that the identity map of $X$ is homotopic to a constant map.
:::

<1>2. For every $x\in X$, the path $t\mapsto H(x,t)$ joins $x$ to $x_0$.
::: {.proof}
Its endpoint values are $H(x,0)=x$ and $H(x,1)=x_0$.
:::

<1>3. Any two points $x,y\in X$ can therefore be joined by a path.
::: {.proof}
Concatenate the path $t\mapsto H(x,t)$ from $x$ to $x_0$ with the reverse of the path $t\mapsto H(y,t)$ from $y$ to $x_0$.
:::

<1>4. Hence every contractible space is path-connected.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the definition of path-connectedness, using <1>3>. No strong deformation retraction is needed.
:::
:::
