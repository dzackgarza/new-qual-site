::: {.problem}
Suppose $Y$ is Hausdorff, path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply-connected, and that its universal cover $\widetilde Y$ is compact.
Prove that $\pi_1(Y)$ is finite.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $p:\widetilde Y\to Y$ be the universal covering map and fix $y_0\in Y$.
::: {.proof}
Under the stated hypotheses the universal cover exists and is connected and simply connected.
:::

<1>2. The total space $\widetilde Y$ is Hausdorff.
::: {.proof}
A covering space of a Hausdorff space is Hausdorff. Indeed, distinct points lying over different base points are separated by inverse images of disjoint base neighborhoods; distinct points in the same fiber are separated by distinct sheets over an evenly covered neighborhood.
:::

<1>3. The fiber $F=p^{-1}(y_0)$ is a closed discrete subspace of $\widetilde Y$.
::: {.proof}
Since $Y$ is Hausdorff, the singleton $\{y_0\}$ is closed, so $F=p^{-1}(\{y_0\})$ is closed. An evenly covered neighborhood of $y_0$ meets each sheet in exactly one point of the fiber, so every point of $F$ is isolated in the subspace topology.
:::

<1>4. The fiber $F$ is finite.
::: {.proof}
By hypothesis $\widetilde Y$ is compact, so its closed subspace $F$ is compact. A compact discrete space is finite: the cover by singleton open sets has a finite subcover only when there are finitely many points.
:::

<1>5. The cardinality of $F$ equals $|\pi_1(Y,y_0)|$.
::: {.proof}
For a connected covering, the number of sheets is the index of the subgroup $p_*\pi_1(\widetilde Y,\tilde y_0)$ in $\pi_1(Y,y_0)$. Since $\widetilde Y$ is simply connected, this subgroup is trivial. Thus
$$
|F|=[\pi_1(Y,y_0):1]=|\pi_1(Y,y_0)|.
$$
:::

<1>6. Therefore $\pi_1(Y)$ is finite.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4 the fiber has finitely many points, and <1>5 identifies that number with the order of the fundamental group.
:::
:::
