::: {.problem}
Suppose $Y$ is Hausdorff, path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply-connected, and that its universal cover $\widetilde Y$ is compact.
Prove that $\pi_1(Y)$ is finite.
:::

::: {.solution}
Under the stated hypotheses $Y$ has a universal covering $p\colon\widetilde Y\to Y$, with $\widetilde Y$ connected and simply connected.
Fix $y_0\in Y$ and let $F=p^{-1}(y_0)$.

<1>1. $F$ is finite.

::: {.proof}
$\{y_0\}$ is closed because $Y$ is Hausdorff, so $F$ is closed in the compact space $\widetilde Y$ and hence compact.
Over an evenly covered neighborhood of $y_0$, each sheet meets $F$ in exactly one point, so $F$ is discrete.
A compact discrete space is finite, since its cover by singletons has a finite subcover.
:::

<1>2. $\abs{F}=\abs{\pi_1(Y,y_0)}$.

::: {.proof}
For a connected covering, the number of sheets is the index of $p_*\pi_1(\widetilde Y,\tilde y_0)$ in $\pi_1(Y,y_0)$, and this subgroup is trivial because $\widetilde Y$ is simply connected.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 show that $\pi_1(Y,y_0)$ is finite.
:::
:::
