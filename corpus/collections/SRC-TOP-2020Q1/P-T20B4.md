::: {.problem}
Consider the simple closed curve $A$ contained in the torus $T$, as shown below.
Sometimes $A$ is a called a $(1,1)$-curve.
Use the long exact sequence of relative homology groups to compute $H_n(T,A)$ for all $n$.
(You may state the homology groups $\widetilde H_n(T)$ and $\widetilde H_n(S^1)$ without proof.)

![](../../assets/Topology/figures/2020Q1-topology-B4.png)
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $T=S^1\times S^1$ with $H_1(T)\cong\ZZ^2$ generated by the two circle factors, and let $i\colon A\hookrightarrow T$ be the inclusion; $A\cong S^1$.

<1>1. $i_*\colon H_1(A)\cong\ZZ\to H_1(T)\cong\ZZ^2$ sends a generator to $(1,1)$; it is injective with cokernel $\ZZ$.

::: {.proof}
A $(1,1)$-curve winds once around each circle factor, so its class is $(1,1)$.
This vector is primitive, so $i_*$ is injective and $\ZZ^2/\langle(1,1)\rangle\cong\ZZ$, for example via $(a,b)\mapsto a-b$.
:::

<1>2. $i_*\colon H_0(A)\to H_0(T)$ is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
$A$ and $T$ are connected and $A$ is nonempty, so both groups are $\ZZ$, generated by the class of a point of $A$.
:::

<1>3. The long exact sequence of the pair $(T,A)$ gives the exact sequence
$$0\to H_2(T)\to H_2(T,A)\to H_1(A)\xrightarrow{i_*}H_1(T)\to H_1(T,A)\to0,$$
and $H_0(T,A)=0$.

::: {.proof}
$H_2(A)=0$ because $A\cong S^1$.
By step <1>2, $H_0(A)\to H_0(T)$ is injective, so $H_1(T,A)\to H_0(A)$ is zero; and it is surjective, so $H_0(T,A)=0$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $i_*$ is injective, so exactness in step <1>3 makes $H_2(T)\cong\ZZ\to H_2(T,A)$ an isomorphism and identifies $H_1(T,A)$ with $\operatorname{coker}i_*\cong\ZZ$.
For $n\ge3$, $H_n(T)$ and $H_{n-1}(A)$ vanish, so $H_n(T,A)=0$.
Hence
$$\boxed{H_n(T,A;\ZZ)\cong\begin{cases}\ZZ,&n=1,2,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}}$$
:::
:::
