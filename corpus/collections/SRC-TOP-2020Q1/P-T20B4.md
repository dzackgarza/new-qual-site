::: {.problem}
Consider the simple closed curve $A$ contained in the torus $T$, as shown below.
Sometimes $A$ is a called a $(1,1)$-curve.
Use the long exact sequence of relative homology groups to compute $H_n(T,A)$ for all $n$.
(You may state the homology groups $\widetilde H_n(T)$ and $\widetilde H_n(S^1)$ without proof.)

![](../../assets/Topology/figures/2020Q1-topology-B4.png)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The curve $A$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$, and the inclusion $i:A\hookrightarrow T^2$ induces
$$
i_*:H_1(A;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\longrightarrow H_1(T^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad 1\longmapsto(1,1).
$$
::: {.proof}
A $(1,1)$-curve winds once around each standard circle factor of the torus. Thus its homology class is the primitive vector $(1,1)$.
:::

<1>2. The map $i_*$ in degree $1$ is injective, and its cokernel is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
The vector $(1,1)$ is primitive in $\mathbb Z^2$. Hence the quotient $\mathbb Z^2/\langle(1,1)\rangle$ is free of rank one, for example via $(a,b)\mapsto a-b$.
:::

<1>3. Since $A$ and $T^2$ are connected, the map $H_0(A)\to H_0(T^2)$ is an isomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Both groups are $\mathbb Z$, and inclusion of a nonempty connected subspace induces the identity on the component generator.
:::

<1>4. The long exact sequence of the pair gives
$$
0\to H_2(T^2)\to H_2(T^2,A)\to H_1(A)\xrightarrow{i_*}H_1(T^2)\to H_1(T^2,A)\to0.
$$
::: {.proof}
Here $H_2(A)=0$, while <1>3> makes the next map $H_0(A)\to H_0(T^2)$ an isomorphism, so the displayed portion is exact with zeros at both ends.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
H_2(T^2,A)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(T^2,A)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
::: {.proof}
Because $i_*$ is injective by <1>2>, exactness in <1>4> makes $H_2(T^2)\to H_2(T^2,A)$ an isomorphism. Exactness also identifies $H_1(T^2,A)$ with $\operatorname{coker}i_*\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>6. Moreover $H_0(T^2,A)=0$ and $H_n(T^2,A)=0$ for $n\ge3$. Hence
$$
\boxed{H_n(T^2,A;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&n=1,2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}}
$$
::: {.proof}
The degree-$0$ relative group vanishes by <1>3>. For $n\ge3$, both $H_n(T^2)$ and $H_{n-1}(A)$ vanish, so the long exact sequence gives zero.
:::
:::
