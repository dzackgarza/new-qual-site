::: {.problem}
Suppose $Y$ is Hausdorff, and $f:X\to Y$ and $g:X\to Y$ are continuous functions.
Prove that the set $A=\{x\in X:f(x)=g(x)\}$ is closed in $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Define $F:X\to Y\times Y$ by
$$
F(x)=(f(x),g(x)).
$$
::: {.proof}
The map $F$ is continuous because its coordinate maps $f$ and $g$ are continuous.
:::

<1>2. The diagonal
$$
\Delta_Y=\{(y,y):y\in Y\}
$$
is closed in $Y\times Y$.
::: {.proof}
Let $(y_1,y_2)\notin\Delta_Y$, so $y_1\ne y_2$. Since $Y$ is Hausdorff, choose disjoint open neighborhoods $U$ of $y_1$ and $V$ of $y_2$. Then $U\times V$ is an open neighborhood of $(y_1,y_2)$ disjoint from $\Delta_Y$. Thus the complement of the diagonal is open.
:::

<1>3. We have
$$
A=F^{-1}(\Delta_Y).
$$
::: {.proof}
A point $x$ lies in the inverse image exactly when $(f(x),g(x))$ lies on the diagonal, i.e. exactly when $f(x)=g(x)$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $A$ is closed in $X$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 the diagonal is closed, and by <1>1 the map $F$ is continuous, so <1>3 is a closed inverse image.
:::
:::
