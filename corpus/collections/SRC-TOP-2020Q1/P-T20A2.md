::: {.problem}
(a) State the definition of what it means for a topological space $X$ to be regular.

(b) Prove that if $X$ and $Y$ are regular, then the space $X\times Y$ with the product topology is regular.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. (a) A space $X$ is \dfn{regular} if it is $T_1$ and, whenever $x\in X$ and $C\subseteq X$ is closed with $x\notin C$, there are disjoint open sets $U\ni x$ and $W\supseteq C$.

<1>2. A $T_1$ space $X$ is regular if and only if for every $x\in X$ and every open $U\ni x$ there is an open $V\ni x$ with $\overline V\subseteq U$.

::: {.proof}
If $X$ is regular and $x\in U$, separate $x$ from $X\setminus U$ by disjoint open $V\ni x$ and $W\supseteq X\setminus U$; then $\overline V\subseteq X\setminus W\subseteq U$.
Conversely, given $x\notin C$ with $C$ closed, choose $V\ni x$ with $\overline V\subseteq X\setminus C$; then $V$ and $X\setminus\overline V$ separate $x$ and $C$.
:::

<1>3. (b) If $X$ and $Y$ are regular, then $X\times Y$ is regular.

::: {.proof}
Singletons are closed in $X$ and $Y$, so $\{(x,y)\}=\{x\}\times\{y\}$ is closed in $X\times Y$, which is therefore $T_1$.
Let $O$ be an open neighborhood of $(x,y)$, and choose open $U\ni x$ and $V\ni y$ with $U\times V\subseteq O$.
By step <1>2, there are open $U'\ni x$ and $V'\ni y$ with $\overline{U'}\subseteq U$ and $\overline{V'}\subseteq V$.
Since $\overline{U'\times V'}=\overline{U'}\times\overline{V'}$, we get $\overline{U'\times V'}\subseteq U\times V\subseteq O$, and step <1>2 shows that $X\times Y$ is regular.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 answers (a), and step <1>3 proves (b).
:::
:::
