::: {.problem}
(a) State the definition of what it means for a topological space $X$ to be regular.

(b) Prove that if $X$ and $Y$ are regular, then the space $X\times Y$ with the product topology is regular.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A space $X$ is regular if it is $T_1$ and, for every $x\in X$ and every open neighborhood $U\ni x$, there is an open neighborhood $V\ni x$ such that
$$
\overline V\subseteq U.
$$
::: {.proof}
This is equivalent to the usual formulation that a point and a disjoint closed set can be separated by disjoint open neighborhoods. We use this standard $T_1$ convention for regularity.
:::

<1>2. If $X$ and $Y$ are regular, then $X\times Y$ is $T_1$.
::: {.proof}
Regular spaces are $T_1$, so singletons $\{x\}\subseteq X$ and $\{y\}\subseteq Y$ are closed. Hence $\{(x,y)\}=\{x\}\times\{y\}$ is closed in the product.
:::

<1>3. Let $(x,y)\in X\times Y$ and let $O$ be an open neighborhood of $(x,y)$. Choose basic open sets $U\ni x$ and $V\ni y$ with
$$
(x,y)\in U\times V\subseteq O.
$$
::: {.proof}
Products of open sets form a basis for the product topology.
:::

<1>4. By regularity of the factors, choose open $U'\ni x$ and $V'\ni y$ satisfying
$$
\overline{U'}\subseteq U,
\qquad
\overline{V'}\subseteq V.
$$
::: {.proof}
Apply the neighborhood-closure characterization of regularity from <1>1> in each factor.
:::

<1>5. Then
$$
\overline{U'\times V'}=\overline{U'}\times\overline{V'}\subseteq U\times V\subseteq O.
$$
::: {.proof}
Closure commutes with a product of two subsets: a basic product neighborhood of $(a,b)$ meets $U'\times V'$ exactly when its two coordinate neighborhoods meet $U'$ and $V'$ respectively.
:::

<1>6. Thus $X\times Y$ is regular.
::: {.proof}
The open neighborhood $U'\times V'$ of $(x,y)$ has closure contained in the arbitrary neighborhood $O$, by <1>5>. Together with the $T_1$ property from <1>2>, this is regularity.
:::
:::
