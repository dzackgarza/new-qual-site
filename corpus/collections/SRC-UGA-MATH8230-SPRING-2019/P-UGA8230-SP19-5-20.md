::: {.problem}
Let $F,G\in C^\infty(M)$ on a symplectic manifold.
Prove that
$$
X_{\{F,G\}}=[X_F,X_G],
$$
where the right-hand side is the commutator $X_F\circ X_G-X_G\circ X_F$ under the identification of vector fields with derivations on $C^\infty(M)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Recall the conventions
$$
\iota_{X_H}\omega=-dH
$$
and
$$
\{F,G\}
=
\omega(X_F,X_G)
=
dG(X_F).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For arbitrary vector fields $X,Y$ and any $2$-form $\eta$,
$$
\boxed{
\iota_{[X,Y]}\eta
=
\mathcal L_X(\iota_Y\eta)
-\iota_Y(\mathcal L_X\eta)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Evaluate both sides on an arbitrary vector field $Z$. By the definition of Lie derivative of a $1$-form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(\mathcal L_X(\iota_Y\eta)\bigr)(Z)
&=
X\bigl(\eta(Y,Z)\bigr)
-\eta(Y,[X,Z]).
\end{aligned}
$$
Also, by the definition of Lie derivative of a $2$-form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(\iota_Y(\mathcal L_X\eta)\bigr)(Z)
&=
(\mathcal L_X\eta)(Y,Z)\\
&=
X\bigl(\eta(Y,Z)\bigr)
-\eta([X,Y],Z)
-\eta(Y,[X,Z]).
\end{aligned}
$$
Subtracting gives
$$
\eta([X,Y],Z)
=
(\iota_{[X,Y]}\eta)(Z).
$$
Since this holds for every $Z$, the identity follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every Hamiltonian vector field preserves the symplectic form:
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{X_F}\omega=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Cartan's formula gives
$$
\mathcal L_{X_F}\omega
=
d(\iota_{X_F}\omega)
+\iota_{X_F}(d\omega).
$$
Since
$$
\iota_{X_F}\omega=-dF
$$
and
$$
d\omega=0,
$$
we obtain
$$
\mathcal L_{X_F}\omega
=
-d^2F
=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
\iota_{[X_F,X_G]}\omega
=
-d\bigl(X_FG\bigr).
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} with
$$
X=X_F,
\qquad
Y=X_G,
\qquad
\eta=\omega.
$$
Using step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
\iota_{[X_F,X_G]}\omega
&=
\mathcal L_{X_F}(\iota_{X_G}\omega)
-\iota_{X_G}(\mathcal L_{X_F}\omega)\\
&=
\mathcal L_{X_F}(-dG).
\end{aligned}
$$
Lie derivative commutes with exterior differentiation, so
$$
\mathcal L_{X_F}(-dG)
=
-d(\mathcal L_{X_F}G)
=
-d(X_FG).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The derivative $X_FG$ is the Poisson bracket:
$$
\boxed{X_FG=\{F,G\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition of the action of a vector field on a function,
$$
X_FG
=
dG(X_F).
$$
The sign convention for Hamiltonian vector fields gives
$$
dG(X_F)
=
\omega(X_F,X_G)
=
\{F,G\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\iota_{[X_F,X_G]}\omega
=
-d\{F,G\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} into step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
X_{\{F,G\}}
=
[X_F,X_G]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition, the Hamiltonian vector field of $\{F,G\}$ is the unique vector field $X$ satisfying
$$
\iota_X\omega=-d\{F,G\}.
$$
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} shows that
$$
[X_F,X_G]
$$
satisfies this equation. Nondegeneracy of $\omega$ gives uniqueness, so it must equal
$$
X_{\{F,G\}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is the required identity.

:::
