::: {.problem}
Let $\omega_0$ be the standard area form on $S^2$, so that for $x\in S^2$ and $v,w\in T_xS^2=\{x\}^\perp\subset\RR^3$,
$$
(\omega_0)_x(v,w)=x\cdot(v\times w).
$$
Let $M=S^2\times S^2$ with product symplectic form
$$
\omega=\pi_1^*\omega_0+\pi_2^*\omega_0.
$$
Use without proof that $H_2(M;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$, generated by
$$
A=[S^2\times\{y\}],\qquad B=[\{x\}\times S^2].
$$

(a) If $\alpha:S^2\to S^2$ is the antipodal map, prove that
$$
L=\{(x,\alpha(x)):x\in S^2\}
$$
is a Lagrangian submanifold of $M$.
Determine its homology class in terms of $A$ and $B$.

(b) Prove that for positive integers $m,n$ there is a symplectic submanifold of $M$ representing $mA+nB$.
Hint: start with a union of horizontal and vertical spheres and smooth their transverse intersections symplectically.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\iota:S^2\longrightarrow S^2\times S^2,
\qquad
\iota(x)=(x,\alpha(x)),
$$
where $\alpha(x)=-x$ is the antipodal map.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The antipodal map satisfies
$$
\boxed{\alpha^*\omega_0=-\omega_0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $x\in S^2$ and $v,w\in T_xS^2$,
$$
d\alpha_x(v)=-v,
\qquad
d\alpha_x(w)=-w.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\alpha^*\omega_0)_x(v,w)
&=
(\omega_0)_{-x}(-v,-w)\\
&=
(-x)\cdot((-v)\times(-w))\\
&=
-x\cdot(v\times w)\\
&=
-(\omega_0)_x(v,w).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The antidiagonal
$$
L=\iota(S^2)
$$
is Lagrangian in $(M,\omega)$.


::: pf-proof

The map $\iota$ is an embedding, and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\iota^*\omega
&=
\iota^*(\pi_1^*\omega_0+\pi_2^*\omega_0)\\
&=
\omega_0+\alpha^*\omega_0\\
&=
0
\end{aligned}
$$
by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Thus every tangent space of $L$ is isotropic. Since
$$
\dim L=2=\frac12\dim M,
$$
$L$ is Lagrangian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The homology class of $L$ is
$$
\boxed{[L]=A-B}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
[L]=aA+bB.
$$
The first projection restricts to
$$
\pi_1\circ\iota=\operatorname{id}_{S^2},
$$
so
$$
(\pi_1)_*[L]=[S^2].
$$
Since
$$
(\pi_1)_*A=[S^2],
\qquad
(\pi_1)_*B=0,
$$
it follows that $a=1$.

Likewise,
$$
\pi_2\circ\iota=\alpha.
$$
The antipodal map on $S^2$ has degree $-1$, as also follows from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} because it reverses the area orientation. Hence
$$
(\pi_2)_*[L]=-[S^2].
$$
Since
$$
(\pi_2)_*A=0,
\qquad
(\pi_2)_*B=[S^2],
$$
we obtain $b=-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Fix distinct points
$$
x_1,\ldots,x_n\in S^2,
\qquad
y_1,\ldots,y_m\in S^2,
$$
and let
$$
\Sigma_0
\coloneqq
\bigcup_{j=1}^m(S^2\times\{y_j\})
\cup
\bigcup_{i=1}^n(\{x_i\}\times S^2).
$$
Then every component is symplectic and
$$
[\Sigma_0]=mA+nB
$$
as a singular $2$-cycle.


::: pf-proof

On a horizontal sphere,
$$
\omega\restrict_{S^2\times\{y_j\}}=\omega_0,
$$
and on a vertical sphere,
$$
\omega\restrict_{\{x_i\}\times S^2}=\omega_0,
$$
so every component is symplectic.

The horizontal components are disjoint from one another, as are the vertical components. Each horizontal component represents $A$, and each vertical component represents $B$. Therefore the union, regarded as a singular cycle, represents
$$
mA+nB.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The only singularities of $\Sigma_0$ are the $mn$ transverse intersection points
$$
(x_i,y_j),
$$
and every such intersection is positive and symplectically orthogonal.


::: pf-proof

At $(x_i,y_j)$,
$$
T_{(x_i,y_j)}(S^2\times\{y_j\})
=
T_{x_i}S^2\oplus0
$$
and
$$
T_{(x_i,y_j)}(\{x_i\}\times S^2)
=
0\oplus T_{y_j}S^2.
$$
These planes are complementary and are $\omega$-orthogonal because $\omega$ is the product form. Their symplectic orientations together give the product orientation of $M$, so the local intersection number is $+1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

A positive orthogonal symplectic node in a symplectic four-manifold admits a local symplectic smoothing that agrees with the two branches outside an arbitrarily small ball.


::: pf-proof

Choose a product Darboux chart centered at the node in which
$$
(M,\omega)
\cong
(\CC^2,\omega_{\mathrm{std}})
$$
and the two local branches are the complex coordinate axes
$$
\{z_2=0\}
\cup
\{z_1=0\}.
$$

Inside a smaller ball, replace the nodal complex curve
$$
z_1z_2=0
$$
by
$$
z_1z_2=\varepsilon
$$
for sufficiently small nonzero $\varepsilon$. This is a smooth complex curve because the gradient of
$$
(z_1,z_2)\longmapsto z_1z_2
$$
cannot vanish on the level set $z_1z_2=\varepsilon$. Hence its tangent planes are complex lines and therefore symplectic for $\omega_{\mathrm{std}}$.

Choose a radial cutoff function $\chi$ which is $1$ on the smaller ball and $0$ near the boundary of the Darboux ball, and use the zero set
$$
z_1z_2=\varepsilon\chi
$$
in the intervening annulus. Away from the origin, the zero set of $z_1z_2$ is a disjoint union of smooth branches, and its differential is nonzero. For sufficiently small $\abs\varepsilon$, the implicit-function theorem therefore makes the cutoff zero set a pair of graphs that are arbitrarily $C^1$-close to the coordinate axes throughout the annulus. Since nondegeneracy of the restriction of a two-form to an oriented two-plane is an open condition, these graphs remain symplectic. Where $\chi=0$ they agree exactly with the original axes.

Thus the replacement is a smooth symplectic surface and is identical to the original two branches near the boundary of the Darboux ball.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Smoothing all $mn$ nodes of $\Sigma_0$ produces a smooth embedded symplectic surface
$$
\Sigma\subset M.
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose pairwise disjoint Darboux balls around the finitely many points $(x_i,y_j)$. Apply the local construction of step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} independently in each ball. Outside these balls leave $\Sigma_0$ unchanged.

Each local replacement agrees with the old branches near the boundary of its ball, so the pieces glue to a smooth embedded surface. It is symplectic on the unchanged pieces by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and on each smoothing piece by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The smoothed surface represents the same homology class:
$$
\boxed{[\Sigma]=mA+nB}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Inside each Darboux ball, the old nodal pair of discs and the smoothed surface have the same oriented boundary in the boundary three-sphere. Their difference is therefore a relative $2$-cycle supported in a ball. Since
$$
H_2(B^4,\partial B^4;\ZZ)=0,
$$
the two local pieces determine the same relative homology class. Replacing all nodes therefore does not change the global homology class. Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the class of the original cycle as $mA+nB$.

:::
