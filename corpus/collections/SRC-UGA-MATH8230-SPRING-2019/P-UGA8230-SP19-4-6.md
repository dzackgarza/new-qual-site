::: {.problem}
Suppose that $V=(V_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$ is a time-dependent vector field whose time-one flow $\psi_{V,1}$ is defined on all of $M$.
Construct another time-dependent vector field $W=(W_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$ such that
$$
\psi_{W,1}=(\psi_{V,1})^{-1}.
$$
Suggestion: arrange that whenever $\gamma:[0,1]\to M$ satisfies $\dot\gamma(t)=V_t(\gamma(t))$, the reversed curve $\bar\gamma(t)=\gamma(1-t)$ satisfies $\dot{\bar\gamma}(t)=W_t(\bar\gamma(t))$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Define
$$
\boxed{
W_t=-V_{1-t}}
$$
for $0\le t\le1$. The inverse
$$
(\psi_{V,1})^{-1}
$$
is understood on its natural domain $\psi_{V,1}(M)$; if $\psi_{V,1}$ is onto, this domain is all of $M$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If
$$
\dot\gamma(t)=V_t(\gamma(t)),
$$
then the reversed curve
$$
\bar\gamma(t)=\gamma(1-t)
$$
satisfies
$$
\dot{\bar\gamma}(t)=W_t(\bar\gamma(t)).
$$


::: pf-proof

By the chain rule,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot{\bar\gamma}(t)
&=
-\dot\gamma(1-t)\\
&=
-V_{1-t}(\gamma(1-t))\\
&=
W_t(\bar\gamma(t)).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $x\in M$, the $W$-trajectory beginning at
$$
y=\psi_{V,1}(x)
$$
exists on $[0,1]$ and ends at $x$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\gamma_x(t)=\psi_{V,t}(x).
$$
By hypothesis this $V$-trajectory exists for $0\le t\le1$. Its reversed curve
$$
\bar\gamma_x(t)=\gamma_x(1-t)
$$
is a $W$-trajectory by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Moreover,
$$
\bar\gamma_x(0)
=
\gamma_x(1)
=
\psi_{V,1}(x)
=y
$$
and
$$
\bar\gamma_x(1)
=
\gamma_x(0)
=x.
$$
Uniqueness for the time-dependent ODE therefore gives
$$
\psi_{W,1}(y)=x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The map $\psi_{V,1}:M\to\psi_{V,1}(M)$ is injective.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\psi_{V,1}(x_0)=\psi_{V,1}(x_1)=y.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, reversing each of the two $V$-trajectories from $x_0$ and $x_1$ gives a $W$-trajectory with the same initial value $y$. Uniqueness for the $W$-equation implies that these reversed trajectories coincide. Evaluating at time $1$ gives
$$
x_0=x_1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

On $\psi_{V,1}(M)$,
$$
\boxed{
\psi_{W,1}=(\psi_{V,1})^{-1}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every
$$
y\in\psi_{V,1}(M)
$$
has the form
$$
y=\psi_{V,1}(x)
$$
for a unique $x$ by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\psi_{W,1}(y)=x.
$$
This is exactly the defining property of the inverse map.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves the required inverse-flow identity for the time-dependent vector field
$$
W_t=-V_{1-t}.
$$

:::
