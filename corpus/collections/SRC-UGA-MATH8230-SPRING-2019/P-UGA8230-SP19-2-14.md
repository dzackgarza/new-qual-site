::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that in the symplectic vector space $(\RR^4,\omega_0)$ every subspace is symplectic, isotropic, or coisotropic.
Hint: if $V$ is the subspace, consider the possible values of the rank of $\omega_0\restrict_{V\times V}$.

(b) Give an example of a subspace of $\RR^6$ that is neither symplectic, nor isotropic, nor coisotropic with respect to $\omega_0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

For a subspace $V$ of a symplectic vector space, write
$$
V^\omega
\coloneqq
\{x:\omega(x,v)=0\text{ for every }v\in V\}.
$$
Recall that $V$ is isotropic when $V\subseteq V^\omega$, coisotropic when $V^\omega\subseteq V$, and symplectic when $\omega\restrict_{V\times V}$ is nondegenerate.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

In a four-dimensional symplectic vector space,
$$
\dim V^\omega=4-\dim V.
$$


::: pf-proof

Nondegeneracy of $\omega$ identifies the ambient vector space with its dual by
$$
x\longmapsto\omega(x,-).
$$
Under this isomorphism, $V^\omega$ corresponds to the annihilator of $V$, whose dimension is $4-\dim V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every isotropic subspace of $\RR^4$ has dimension at most $2$.


::: pf-proof

If $V$ is isotropic, then $V\subseteq V^\omega$. Hence by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim V
\le
\dim V^\omega
=
4-\dim V,
$$
so $\dim V\le2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $\dim V\le2$, then $V$ is either isotropic or symplectic.


::: pf-proof

The rank of an alternating bilinear form is even.

If $\dim V=0$ or $1$, the restriction $\omega_0\restrict_V$ has rank $0$, so $V$ is isotropic.

If $\dim V=2$, the restriction has rank either $0$ or $2$. Rank $0$ means
$$
\omega_0\restrict_{V\times V}=0,
$$
so $V$ is isotropic. Rank $2$ means the restriction is nondegenerate, so $V$ is symplectic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Every three-dimensional subspace $V\subset\RR^4$ is coisotropic.


::: pf-proof

The restriction $\omega_0\restrict_V$ has even rank at most $2$. It cannot have rank $0$, because then $V$ would be isotropic, contradicting step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. Hence its rank is $2$, so
$$
\dim\ker(\omega_0\restrict_V)=1.
$$
But
$$
\ker(\omega_0\restrict_V)
=
V\cap V^{\omega_0}.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim V^{\omega_0}=1.
$$
Thus
$$
V\cap V^{\omega_0}=V^{\omega_0},
$$
which is exactly
$$
V^{\omega_0}\subseteq V.
$$
So $V$ is coisotropic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The only four-dimensional subspace of $\RR^4$ is $\RR^4$ itself, which is symplectic.


::: pf-proof

The ambient form $\omega_0$ is nondegenerate by definition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore every subspace of $(\RR^4,\omega_0)$ is symplectic, isotropic, or coisotropic.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} exhaust the possible dimensions $0,1,2,3,4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

In $(\RR^6,\omega_0)$, with coordinates
$$
(p_1,p_2,p_3,q_1,q_2,q_3),
\qquad
\omega_0
=
\sum_{j=1}^3dp_j\wedge dq_j,
$$
set
$$
V
\coloneqq
\operatorname{span}
\{\partial_{p_1},\partial_{q_1},\partial_{p_2}\}.
$$
Then $V$ is neither symplectic nor isotropic.


::: pf-proof

It is not isotropic because
$$
\omega_0(\partial_{p_1},\partial_{q_1})=1.
$$
It is not symplectic because
$$
\partial_{p_2}\in
\ker(\omega_0\restrict_{V\times V}),
$$
so the restriction is degenerate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The same subspace $V$ is not coisotropic.


::: pf-proof

A direct calculation gives
$$
V^{\omega_0}
=
\operatorname{span}
\{\partial_{p_2},\partial_{p_3},\partial_{q_3}\}.
$$
Indeed, orthogonality to
$$
\partial_{p_1},\quad
\partial_{q_1},\quad
\partial_{p_2}
$$
forces respectively the $q_1$-, $p_1$-, and $q_2$-coefficients to vanish, and imposes no other conditions. Since
$$
\partial_{p_3}\notin V,
$$
one has $V^{\omega_0}\nsubseteq V$. Thus $V$ is not coisotropic.

:::

:::

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves part (a), while steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} provide and verify the example required in part (b).
