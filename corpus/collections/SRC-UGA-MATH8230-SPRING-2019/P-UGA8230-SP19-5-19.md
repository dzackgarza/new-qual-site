::: {.problem}
Let $F,G:M\to\RR$ be autonomous Hamiltonians on a symplectic manifold, with flows $\phi_F^s$ and $\phi_G^t$.
Prove, without using the identity $X_{\{F,G\}}=[X_F,X_G]$, that if the Poisson bracket $\{F,G\}$ is constant, then the two flows commute wherever both sides are defined:
$$
\phi_F^s(\phi_G^t(x))=\phi_G^t(\phi_F^s(x)).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume
$$
\{F,G\}=c
$$
for some constant $c\in\RR$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Along the Hamiltonian flow of $F$,
$$
\boxed{
G\circ\phi_F^s=G+cs}
$$
wherever the flow is defined.


::: pf-proof

For any point $x$ and every time $s$ for which the trajectory is defined,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac d{ds}
G(\phi_F^s(x))
&=
dG_{\phi_F^s(x)}
\left(
X_F(\phi_F^s(x))
\right)\\
&=
\{F,G\}(\phi_F^s(x))\\
&=
c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Integrating in $s$ and using
$$
\phi_F^0=\operatorname{id}
$$
gives
$$
G(\phi_F^s(x))
=
G(x)+cs.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For fixed $s$, the Hamiltonian
$$
K_s
\coloneqq
G\circ\phi_F^{-s}
$$
satisfies
$$
K_s=G-cs.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} with time $-s$:
$$
G\circ\phi_F^{-s}
=
G-cs.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Hamiltonians $K_s$ and $G$ have the same Hamiltonian vector field and hence the same flow.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
K_s-G=-cs
$$
is constant on $M$. Therefore
$$
dK_s=dG.
$$
Since Hamiltonian vector fields are characterized by
$$
\iota_{X_H}\omega=-dH,
$$
nondegeneracy of $\omega$ gives
$$
X_{K_s}=X_G.
$$
Their flows therefore agree by uniqueness of solutions to the ODE.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The flow of $K_s=G\circ\phi_F^{-s}$ is
$$
\phi_F^s\circ\phi_G^t\circ\phi_F^{-s}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For fixed $s$, the map
$$
\phi_F^s
$$
is a symplectomorphism on the region where it is defined. Proposition 5.12 applied to
$$
H=G,
\qquad
\psi=\phi_F^s
$$
says that the Hamiltonian
$$
G\circ(\phi_F^s)^{-1}
=
G\circ\phi_F^{-s}
=
K_s
$$
has flow
$$
\phi_F^s\circ\phi_G^t\circ\phi_F^{-s}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The two flows commute:
$$
\boxed{
\phi_F^s\circ\phi_G^t
=
\phi_G^t\circ\phi_F^s}
$$
wherever both sides are defined.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the flow of $K_s$ is
$$
\phi_G^t.
$$
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the same flow is
$$
\phi_F^s\circ\phi_G^t\circ\phi_F^{-s}.
$$
Thus
$$
\phi_F^s\circ\phi_G^t\circ\phi_F^{-s}
=
\phi_G^t.
$$
Composing on the right with $\phi_F^s$ gives the required commutation identity.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves the claim without using
$$
X_{\{F,G\}}=[X_F,X_G].
$$

:::
