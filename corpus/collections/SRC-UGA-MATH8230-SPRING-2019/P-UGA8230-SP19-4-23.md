::: {.problem}
Let $(M,\omega)$ be a symplectic manifold and let $C\subset M$ be a compact coisotropic submanifold.

(a) Let $J$ be an $\omega$-compatible almost complex structure with metric $g_J(v,w)=\omega(v,Jw)$.
For $x\in C$, set
$$
E_x=J(T_xC^\omega)
$$
and let $V_x$ be the $g_J$-orthogonal complement of $E_x\oplus T_xC^\omega$ in $T_xM$.
Prove
$$
T_xM=E_x\oplus T_xC,\qquad T_xC=T_xC^\omega\oplus V_x,
$$
and prove that both $E_x\oplus T_xC^\omega$ and $V_x$ are symplectic subspaces of $T_xM$.

(b) Suppose $(M',\omega')$ is another symplectic manifold containing the same $C$ as a coisotropic submanifold, and $\omega'\restrict_{TC}=\omega\restrict_{TC}$.
Show that there are neighborhoods $U\subset M$ and $U'\subset M'$ of $C$ and a symplectomorphism $F:U\to U'$ restricting to the identity on $C$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix $x\in C$ and abbreviate
$$
W\coloneqq T_xC^\omega.
$$
Since $C$ is coisotropic,
$$
W\subseteq T_xC.
$$
Let
$$
E=J(W)
$$
and let $V$ be the $g_J$-orthogonal complement of $E\oplus W$ in $T_xM$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The subspace $E\oplus W$ is $J$-invariant.


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
J(W)=E.
$$
Since $J^2=-I$,
$$
J(E)
=
J^2(W)
=
-W.
$$
Hence $J(E\oplus W)=E\oplus W$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
E\cap T_xC=\{0\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
Jw\in T_xC
$$
for some $w\in W$. Because
$$
w\in T_xC^\omega,
$$
one has
$$
\omega(w,Jw)=0.
$$
But compatibility gives
$$
\omega(w,Jw)=g_J(w,w).
$$
Positive definiteness of $g_J$ therefore forces $w=0$, hence $Jw=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The first decomposition is
$$
\boxed{T_xM=E\oplus T_xC}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} the sum is direct. Since
$$
\dim W
=
\operatorname{codim}T_xC
$$
in a symplectic vector space and $J$ preserves dimension,
$$
\dim E
=
\operatorname{codim}T_xC.
$$
Thus
$$
\dim E+\dim T_xC
=
\dim T_xM,
$$
so the direct sum is all of $T_xM$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The orthogonal complement $V$ is $J$-invariant.


::: pf-proof

For a compatible complex structure,
$$
g_J(Ju,Jv)=g_J(u,v).
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the subspace $E\oplus W$ is $J$-invariant. Hence its $g_J$-orthogonal complement is also $J$-invariant.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

One has
$$
V\subseteq T_xC.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $v\in V$ and $w\in W$. Since
$$
Jw\in E
$$
and $v$ is $g_J$-orthogonal to $E$,
$$
0
=
g_J(v,Jw)
=
\omega(v,J(Jw))
=
-\omega(v,w).
$$
Thus $v\in W^\omega$. Since
$$
W=T_xC^\omega,
$$
the double symplectic-orthogonal identity gives
$$
W^\omega=T_xC.
$$
Hence $v\in T_xC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The second decomposition is
$$
\boxed{T_xC=W\oplus V}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $V$ is $g_J$-orthogonal to $W$,
$$
V\cap W=\{0\}.
$$
Also
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dim V
&=
\dim T_xM-\dim(E\oplus W)\\
&=
\dim T_xM-2\dim W\\
&=
\dim T_xC-\dim W,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the last equality uses
$$
\dim W=\operatorname{codim}T_xC.
$$
Together with step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, this shows
$$
T_xC=W\oplus V.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The subspace $E\oplus W$ is symplectic.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} it is $J$-invariant. If
$$
0\ne u\in E\oplus W,
$$
then
$$
Ju\in E\oplus W
$$
and
$$
\omega(u,Ju)
=
g_J(u,u)
>0.
$$
Thus no nonzero $u$ lies in the kernel of
$$
\omega\restrict_{(E\oplus W)\times(E\oplus W)}.
$$
Hence the restriction is nondegenerate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The subspace $V$ is symplectic.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $V$ is $J$-invariant. The same argument as in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives, for every nonzero $v\in V$,
$$
\omega(v,Jv)
=
g_J(v,v)
>0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\omega\restrict_{V\times V}
$$
is nondegenerate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For part (b), the characteristic subbundle
$$
K\coloneqq TC^\omega
$$
is also
$$
TC^{\omega'}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $C$ is coisotropic for both forms,
$$
TC^\omega
=
\ker(\omega\restrict_{TC}),
\qquad
TC^{\omega'}
=
\ker(\omega'\restrict_{TC}).
$$
The hypothesis
$$
\omega'\restrict_{TC}=\omega\restrict_{TC}
$$
therefore gives equality of the two kernels.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Choose a smooth vector subbundle
$$
H\subset TC
$$
such that
$$
TC=K\oplus H.
$$
Then the common restriction
$$
\omega\restrict_H=\omega'\restrict_H
$$
is symplectic.


::: pf-proof

A smooth complement exists by choosing any bundle metric on $TC$ and taking the orthogonal complement of $K$.

Suppose $h\in H$ satisfies
$$
\omega(h,h')=0
$$
for every $h'\in H$. Since $K$ is the kernel of $\omega\restrict_{TC}$, one also has
$$
\omega(h,k)=0
$$
for every $k\in K$. Hence
$$
\omega(h,c)=0
$$
for every $c\in TC$, so
$$
h\in K.
$$
But
$$
H\cap K=\{0\},
$$
so $h=0$. Thus $\omega\restrict_H$ is nondegenerate. The same form is obtained from $\omega'$ by hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Define symplectic orthogonal bundles
$$
S\coloneqq H^\omega\subset TM\restrict_C,
\qquad
S'\coloneqq H^{\omega'}\subset TM'\restrict_C.
$$
Then
$$
TM\restrict_C=H\oplus S,
\qquad
TM'\restrict_C=H\oplus S',
$$
and $K$ is Lagrangian in both $S$ and $S'$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, $H$ is symplectic for both ambient forms. The symplectic orthogonal complement of a symplectic subspace is a symplectic complement, giving the two direct-sum decompositions.

Since
$$
K\subset TC
$$
is the kernel of the restricted form, every $k\in K$ is $\omega$-orthogonal and $\omega'$-orthogonal to $H$. Hence
$$
K\subset S\cap S'.
$$
If
$$
r=\operatorname{rank}K,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{rank}S
=
\dim M-\operatorname{rank}H
=
2r,
$$
because
$$
\operatorname{rank}H
=
\dim C-r
$$
and
$$
r=\operatorname{codim}C.
$$
Thus $K$ has half the dimension of each symplectic bundle $S,S'$. It is isotropic because
$$
K\subset TC
$$
and $K$ is the kernel of the restricted form. Therefore $K$ is Lagrangian in both.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

There is a smooth symplectic vector-bundle isomorphism
$$
\Phi:S\longrightarrow S'
$$
whose restriction to $K$ is the identity.


::: pf-proof

Choose smooth compatible complex structures on the symplectic vector bundles
$$
(S,\omega\restrict_S)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(S',\omega'\restrict_{S'}).
$$
Their images of $K$ are smooth Lagrangian complements to $K$. Fiberwise, Corollary 2.21 applied with the identity map on $K$ gives the unique symplectic linear isomorphism from $S_x$ to $S'_x$ that fixes $K_x$ and maps the chosen complement in $S_x$ to the chosen complement in $S'_x$.

The formula in Corollary 2.21 is built from the bundle maps
$$
\theta_{\omega}^{K},
\qquad
\theta_{\omega'}^{K},
$$
and the chosen smooth complements, so these fiberwise maps depend smoothly on $x$. They therefore assemble to the required smooth bundle isomorphism $\Phi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Define
$$
\widehat F:TM\restrict_C\longrightarrow TM'\restrict_C
$$
by
$$
\widehat F(h+s)=h+\Phi(s),
\qquad
h\in H, s\in S.
$$
Then $\widehat F$ is a symplectic vector-bundle isomorphism and
$$
\widehat F\restrict_{TC}=\operatorname{id}_{TC}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The decompositions in step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} are symplectic orthogonal direct sums. On $H$, the two symplectic forms agree by step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, so the identity
$$
H\to H
$$
is symplectic. On $S$, the map $\Phi$ is symplectic by step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}. Therefore their direct sum $\widehat F$ is symplectic.

Finally,
$$
TC=H\oplus K.
$$
The map $\widehat F$ is the identity on $H$ by definition and the identity on $K$ by step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}. Hence it restricts to the identity on all of $TC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

There are neighborhoods
$$
U\subset M,
\qquad
U'\subset M'
$$
of $C$ and a symplectomorphism
$$
\boxed{F:U\longrightarrow U'}
$$
such that
$$
F\restrict_C=\operatorname{id}_C.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply Theorem 4.16 with
$$
f=\operatorname{id}_C
$$
and with the symplectic bundle isomorphism $\widehat F$ from step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}. Its restriction to $TC$ is exactly
$$
f_*=\operatorname{id}_{TC},
$$
so all hypotheses of the theorem are satisfied. Since $C$ is compact, the theorem gives the required neighborhoods and symplectomorphism.

:::
