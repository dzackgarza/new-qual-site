::: {.problem}
Let $\omega$ be a linear $2$-form on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$, and define
\[
V^\omega=\{v\in V:(\forall w\in V)\,\omega(v,w)=0\}=\ker\theta_\omega.
\]
Prove that there is a linear symplectic form $\bar\omega$ on $V/V^\omega$, uniquely characterized by
\[
\pi^*\bar\omega=\omega,
\]
where $\pi:V\to V/V^\omega$ is the quotient projection.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Define
$$
\bar\omega([v],[w])
\coloneqq
\omega(v,w),
$$
where $[v]=v+V^\omega$ and $[w]=w+V^\omega$.

::: {.proof}
This gives a candidate bilinear form on the quotient. The next step proves that it is independent of the chosen representatives.
:::

<1>2. The formula in step <1>1 is well defined.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
v'=v+k,
\qquad
w'=w+\ell,
$$
with $k,\ell\in V^\omega$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega(v',w')
&=
\omega(v,w)
+\omega(k,w)
+\omega(v,\ell)
+\omega(k,\ell).
\end{aligned}
$$
By definition of $V^\omega$,
$$
\omega(k,w)=\omega(k,\ell)=0.
$$
Since $\omega$ is alternating,
$$
\omega(v,\ell)=-\omega(\ell,v)=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\omega(v',w')=\omega(v,w).
$$
:::

<1>3. The form $\bar\omega$ is alternating and bilinear.

::: {.proof}
Both properties follow immediately from the corresponding properties of $\omega$ and the definition in step <1>1.
:::

<1>4. The form $\bar\omega$ is nondegenerate.

::: {.proof}
Suppose $[v]\in V/V^\omega$ satisfies
$$
\bar\omega([v],[w])=0
$$
for every $[w]\in V/V^\omega$. By step <1>1, this means
$$
\omega(v,w)=0
$$
for every $w\in V$. Hence
$$
v\in V^\omega,
$$
so $[v]=0$ in the quotient. Therefore the kernel of $\bar\omega$ is zero.
:::

<1>5. The quotient projection satisfies
$$
\boxed{\pi^*\bar\omega=\omega}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $v,w\in V$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\pi^*\bar\omega)(v,w)
&=
\bar\omega(\pi(v),\pi(w))\\
&=
\bar\omega([v],[w])\\
&=
\omega(v,w)
\end{aligned}
$$
by step <1>1.
:::

<1>6. The form $\bar\omega$ is uniquely characterized by
$$
\pi^*\bar\omega=\omega.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $\eta$ be any bilinear form on $V/V^\omega$ satisfying
$$
\pi^*\eta=\omega.
$$
Since $\pi$ is surjective, every pair of quotient vectors has the form $([v],[w])$. Then
$$
\eta([v],[w])
=
(\pi^*\eta)(v,w)
=
\omega(v,w)
=
\bar\omega([v],[w]).
$$
Thus $\eta=\bar\omega$.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2--<1>4 show that $\bar\omega$ is a well-defined linear symplectic form, step <1>5 proves the pullback identity, and step <1>6 proves uniqueness.
:::
:::
