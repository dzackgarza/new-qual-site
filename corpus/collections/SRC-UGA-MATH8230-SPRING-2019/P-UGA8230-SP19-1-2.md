::: {.problem}
Take $n=3$, let $\vec A:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$ be smooth, and let $e,m$ be constants with $m>0$.
Consider the Hamiltonian
\[
H(\vec p,\vec q)=\frac{1}{2m}\|\vec p-e\vec A(\vec q)\|^2.
\]
Show that Hamilton's equations give rise to the second-order equation
\[
m\ddot{\vec q}=e\dot{\vec q}\times(\nabla\times\vec A)
\]
for $\vec q(t)$, where $\times$ denotes the cross product.
:::

Set
$$
\vec w
\coloneqq
\vec p-e\vec A(\vec q).
$$
With the convention of the notes, Hamilton's equations are
$$
\dot{\vec p}=-\nabla_qH,
\qquad
\dot{\vec q}=\nabla_pH.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The second Hamilton equation gives
$$
\vec w=m\dot{\vec q},
$$
equivalently
$$
\vec p=m\dot{\vec q}+e\vec A(\vec q).
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
H(\vec p,\vec q)
=
\frac1{2m}\vec w\cdot\vec w,
$$
differentiation with respect to $\vec p$ gives
$$
\nabla_pH=\frac1m\vec w.
$$
Thus
$$
\dot{\vec q}=\frac1m\vec w.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For each $j=1,2,3$,
$$
\dot p_j
=
e\sum_{k=1}^3
\dot q_k\frac{\partial A_k}{\partial q_j}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiating $H$ with respect to $q_j$ gives
$$
\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_j}
=
-\frac e m
\sum_{k=1}^3
w_k\frac{\partial A_k}{\partial q_j}.
$$
By step <1>1, $w_k=m\dot q_k$, so
$$
-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_j}
=
e\sum_{k=1}^3
\dot q_k\frac{\partial A_k}{\partial q_j}.
$$
Hamilton's equation $\dot p_j=-\partial H/\partial q_j$ gives the result.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Differentiating the momentum identity of step <1>1 gives
$$
\dot p_j
=
m\ddot q_j
+e\sum_{k=1}^3
\frac{\partial A_j}{\partial q_k}\dot q_k.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
p_j=m\dot q_j+eA_j(\vec q(t))
$$
with respect to $t$ and apply the chain rule.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently,
$$
m\ddot q_j
=
e\sum_{k=1}^3
\dot q_k
\left(
\frac{\partial A_k}{\partial q_j}
-\frac{\partial A_j}{\partial q_k}
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

Equate the two expressions for $\dot p_j$ from steps <1>2 and <1>3 and move the $A_j$-derivative term to the right-hand side.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every vector $v\in\RR^3$,
$$
\left[v\times(\nabla\times\vec A)\right]_j
=
\sum_{k=1}^3
v_k
\left(
\frac{\partial A_k}{\partial q_j}
-\frac{\partial A_j}{\partial q_k}
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

Using the Levi--Civita symbol,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left[v\times(\nabla\times\vec A)\right]_j
&=
\sum_{k,\ell}
\varepsilon_{jk\ell}v_k
\sum_{r,s}
\varepsilon_{\ell rs}
\frac{\partial A_s}{\partial q_r}\\
&=
\sum_{k,r,s}
(\delta_{jr}\delta_{ks}-\delta_{js}\delta_{kr})
v_k
\frac{\partial A_s}{\partial q_r}\\
&=
\sum_k
v_k
\left(
\frac{\partial A_k}{\partial q_j}
-\frac{\partial A_j}{\partial q_k}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
m\ddot{\vec q}
=
e\dot{\vec q}\times(\nabla\times\vec A)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step <1>5 with $v=\dot{\vec q}$ to the component identity in step <1>4.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>6 is the required Lorentz-force equation.

:::

:::
