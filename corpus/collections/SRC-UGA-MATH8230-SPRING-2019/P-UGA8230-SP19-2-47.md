::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal L_n$ be the set of Lagrangian subspaces of $(\mathbb R^{2n},\omega_0)$, and fix $L_0\in\mathcal L_n$.

(a) Prove that for each $L\in\mathcal L_n$ there is $A\in U(n)$ such that $A(L_0)=L$, and that the subgroup
\[
\{A\in U(n):A(L_0)=L_0\}
\]
is isomorphic to $O(n)$.

(b) Convince yourself that part (a) gives $\mathcal L_n$ the structure of a smooth manifold diffeomorphic to $U(n)/O(n)$ and yields an exact sequence
\[
\pi_1(O(n))\to\pi_1(U(n))\to\pi_1(\mathcal L_n)\to\pi_0(O(n)).
\]

(c) Show that $\pi_1(\mathcal L_n)\cong\mathbb Z$, and find an explicit loop in $\mathcal L_n$ representing a generator.
This generator should map to the nontrivial element of $\pi_0(O(n))$ under the last map above.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $J_0$ denote the standard complex structure on $\RR^{2n}$, so that
$$
g_0(v,w)=\omega_0(v,J_0w)
$$
is the standard Euclidean inner product and $U(n)$ is the group of linear maps preserving both $g_0$ and $J_0$.

<1>1. If $L\in\mathcal L_n$ and $e_1,\dots,e_n$ is an orthonormal basis of $L$, then
$$
e_1,\dots,e_n,J_0e_1,\dots,J_0e_n
$$
is an orthonormal basis of $\RR^{2n}$.

::: {.proof}
Because $L$ is Lagrangian,
$$
\omega_0(e_i,e_j)=0.
$$
Hence
$$
g_0(e_i,J_0e_j)
=
\omega_0(e_i,J_0^2e_j)
=
-\omega_0(e_i,e_j)
=0.
$$
Also $J_0$ is orthogonal, so the vectors $J_0e_i$ are orthonormal. Thus the displayed $2n$ vectors are orthonormal, hence form a basis.
:::

<1>2. The action of $U(n)$ on $\mathcal L_n$ is transitive.

::: {.proof}
Choose orthonormal bases
$$
e_1^0,\dots,e_n^0
$$
of $L_0$ and
$$
e_1,\dots,e_n
$$
of $L$. By step <1>1, each extends by its $J_0$-images to an orthonormal basis of $\RR^{2n}$.

Define $A$ by
$$
A e_j^0=e_j,
\qquad
A(J_0e_j^0)=J_0e_j.
$$
Then $A$ is orthogonal and commutes with $J_0$, so $A\in U(n)$. By construction,
$$
A(L_0)=L.
$$
:::

<1>3. The stabilizer
$$
G_{L_0}
\coloneqq
\{A\in U(n):A(L_0)=L_0\}
$$
is isomorphic to $O(n)$.

::: {.proof}
Restriction gives a homomorphism
$$
\rho:G_{L_0}\longrightarrow O(L_0,g_0|_{L_0}),
\qquad
\rho(A)=A|_{L_0}.
$$
It is injective because a unitary map commutes with $J_0$, so its values on $L_0$ determine its values on
$$
J_0L_0,
$$
and step <1>1 gives
$$
\RR^{2n}=L_0\oplus J_0L_0.
$$

Conversely, if $Q\in O(L_0)$, define
$$
\widetilde Q(x+J_0y)
=
Qx+J_0Qy,
\qquad
x,y\in L_0.
$$
This map is orthogonal, commutes with $J_0$, and preserves $L_0$, so
$$
\widetilde Q\in G_{L_0}.
$$
Thus $\rho$ is an isomorphism. After choosing an orthonormal basis of $L_0$, its target is $O(n)$.
:::

<1>4. The orbit map identifies
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_n\cong U(n)/O(n)}
$$
as a smooth homogeneous space, and the quotient map
$$
q:U(n)\longrightarrow\mathcal L_n,
\qquad
q(A)=A(L_0),
$$
is a fiber bundle with fiber $O(n)$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives transitivity and step <1>3 identifies the stabilizer with the closed Lie subgroup $O(n)$. The standard homogeneous-space theorem therefore identifies the orbit with the smooth quotient by the stabilizer. The quotient projection of a Lie group by a closed Lie subgroup is a principal bundle.
:::

<1>5. The long exact homotopy sequence contains
$$
\pi_1(O(n))
\longrightarrow
\pi_1(U(n))
\xrightarrow{q_*}
\pi_1(\mathcal L_n)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
\pi_0(O(n)).
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply the long exact homotopy sequence to the fiber bundle of step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. The map
$$
\pi_1(O(n))\longrightarrow\pi_1(U(n))
$$
is zero.

::: {.proof}
The determinant induces an isomorphism
$$
\det_*:\pi_1(U(n))\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ.
$$
Every loop in $O(n)$ based at the identity lies in the identity component $SO(n)$, on which the determinant is constantly $1$. Hence its image in $U(n)$ has zero determinant winding number. Since $\det_*$ is injective, its class in $\pi_1(U(n))$ is zero.
:::

<1>7. Choose an orthonormal basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$ of $L_0$. For $0\le t\le1$, let $A_t\in U(n)$ rotate the complex line
$$
\operatorname{span}_{\RR}\{e_1,J_0e_1\}
$$
through angle $\pi t$ and act as the identity on the other complex coordinate lines. Then
$$
\gamma(t)\coloneqq A_t(L_0)
$$
is a loop in $\mathcal L_n$.

::: {.proof}
At $t=0$, $A_0=I$. At $t=1$,
$$
A_1e_1=-e_1,
\qquad
A_1e_j=e_j
\quad(j\ge2),
$$
so $A_1(L_0)=L_0$. Hence
$$
\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)=L_0.
$$
:::

<1>8. The boundary class $\partial[\gamma]$ is the nontrivial element of
$$
\pi_0(O(n))\cong\ZZ/2.
$$

::: {.proof}
The path $A_t$ is a lift of $\gamma$ beginning at the identity. Its endpoint $A_1$ lies in the stabilizer $O(n)$ and restricts to
$$
\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\dots,1)
$$
on $L_0$. Its determinant is $-1$, so it lies in the nonidentity component of $O(n)$. By the definition of the connecting map for the fiber bundle, this component is $\partial[\gamma]$.
:::

<1>9. The image under $q_*$ of a standard generator of
$$
\pi_1(U(n))\cong\ZZ
$$
is
$$
2[\gamma].
$$

::: {.proof}
A standard generator of $\pi_1(U(n))$ is the loop $B_t$ rotating the first complex coordinate through angle $2\pi t$ and fixing the remaining coordinates. Its determinant is
$$
e^{2\pi it},
$$
so it has determinant winding number $1$.

The quotient loop $q(B_t)$ rotates the real line $\RR e_1$ through a full angle $2\pi$. It is exactly the concatenation of two copies of the loop that rotates this line through angle $\pi$, namely $\gamma$. Hence
$$
q_*[B]=2[\gamma].
$$
:::

<1>10. Every element of $\pi_1(\mathcal L_n)$ is an integral power of $[\gamma]$.

::: {.proof}
Since $U(n)$ is connected and $O(n)$ has two components, exactness extends step <1>5 to
$$
\pi_1(U(n))
\xrightarrow{q_*}
\pi_1(\mathcal L_n)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
\ZZ/2
\longrightarrow0.
$$
By step <1>6, $q_*$ is injective.

Let $x\in\pi_1(\mathcal L_n)$. If
$$
\partial x=0,
$$
then exactness gives
$$
x=q_*(k)
$$
for some $k\in\ZZ$, and step <1>9 gives
$$
x=[\gamma]^{2k}.
$$
If
$$
\partial x=1,
$$
then $x$ and $[\gamma]$ lie in the same fiber of the connecting map. Exactness for the principal $O(n)$-bundle says that this fiber is a coset of
$$
\operatorname{im}q_*.
$$
Hence
$$
x=q_*(k)[\gamma]
$$
for some $k\in\ZZ$. By step <1>9,
$$
x=[\gamma]^{2k+1}.
$$
:::

<1>11. The class $[\gamma]$ has infinite order.

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
\mu:\mathcal L_n\longrightarrow S^1
$$
by
$$
\mu(A(L_0))=\det(A)^2.
$$
This is well defined: if
$$
A(L_0)=A'(L_0),
$$
then
$$
A^{-1}A'\in O(n),
$$
whose determinant is $\pm1$, so
$$
\det(A')^2=\det(A)^2.
$$

For the lift $A_t$ from step <1>7,
$$
\det(A_t)=e^{\pi it}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\mu(\gamma(t))
=
e^{2\pi it},
$$
which has winding number $1$. Hence
$$
\mu_*[\gamma]=1\in\ZZ,
$$
so $[\gamma]$ has infinite order.
:::

<1>12. Therefore, for $n\ge1$,
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(\mathcal L_n)\cong\ZZ}
$$
with generator represented by the loop $\gamma$ from step <1>7.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>10 shows that $[\gamma]$ generates the fundamental group, and step <1>11 shows that it has infinite order.
:::

<1>13. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 prove part (a), steps <1>4--<1>5 prove part (b), and steps <1>6--<1>12 prove part (c), including the required nontrivial image in $\pi_0(O(n))$ from step <1>8.
:::
