::: {.problem}
Let $(V,\omega)$ be a symplectic vector space, let $M,L\le V$ be complementary Lagrangian subspaces, and let $h$ be an inner product on $L$.
Define
$$
\theta_h:L\to L^*,\qquad \theta_h(\ell)=h(\ell,\cdot),
$$
and
$$
\theta_\omega^L:M\to L^*,\qquad \theta_\omega^L(m)=\omega(m,\cdot)|_L.
$$
With respect to $V=M\oplus L$, define
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-(\theta_\omega^L)^{-1}\theta_h\\
\theta_h^{-1}\theta_\omega^L&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
For $g_J(v,w)=\omega(v,Jw)$, prove that
$$
g_J(m+\ell,m'+\ell')
=h\!\left(\theta_h^{-1}(\theta_\omega^L m),\theta_h^{-1}(\theta_\omega^L m')\right)+h(\ell',\ell)
$$
for all $m,m'\in M$ and $\ell,\ell'\in L$.
Use that $M$ and $L$ are Lagrangian.
:::

::: {.solution}

For $m\in M$, write
$$
a_m
\coloneqq
\theta_h^{-1}(\theta_\omega^L m)
\in L.
$$
For $\ell\in L$, write
$$
b_\ell
\coloneqq
(\theta_\omega^L)^{-1}(\theta_h\ell)
\in M.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The block definition of $J$ gives
$$
J(m'+\ell')
=
-b_{\ell'}+a_{m'}.
$$


::: pf-proof

With respect to the decomposition $V=M\oplus L$,
$$
J
\begin{pmatrix}
m'\\
\ell'
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-(\theta_\omega^L)^{-1}\theta_h\ell'\\
\theta_h^{-1}\theta_\omega^Lm'
\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is exactly $-b_{\ell'}+a_{m'}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Since $M$ and $L$ are Lagrangian,
$$
\omega(m,b_{\ell'})=0,
\qquad
\omega(\ell,a_{m'})=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both $m$ and $b_{\ell'}$ lie in $M$, and
$$
\omega|_{M\times M}=0
$$
because $M$ is Lagrangian. Likewise, both $\ell$ and $a_{m'}$ lie in $L$, and
$$
\omega|_{L\times L}=0
$$
because $L$ is Lagrangian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The first surviving mixed term is
$$
\omega(m,a_{m'})
=
h(a_m,a_{m'}).
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition of $\theta_\omega^L$,
$$
\omega(m,a_{m'})
=
(\theta_\omega^Lm)(a_{m'}).
$$
Since
$$
\theta_\omega^Lm
=
\theta_h(a_m),
$$
the right-hand side equals
$$
(\theta_h(a_m))(a_{m'})
=
h(a_m,a_{m'}).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The second surviving mixed term is
$$
-\omega(\ell,b_{\ell'})
=
h(\ell',\ell).
$$


::: pf-proof

Alternation of $\omega$ gives
$$
-\omega(\ell,b_{\ell'})
=
\omega(b_{\ell'},\ell).
$$
By definition of $\theta_\omega^L$,
$$
\omega(b_{\ell'},\ell)
=
(\theta_\omega^Lb_{\ell'})(\ell).
$$
Since
$$
\theta_\omega^Lb_{\ell'}
=
\theta_h(\ell'),
$$
this is
$$
(\theta_h(\ell'))(\ell)
=
h(\ell',\ell).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
g_J(m+\ell,m'+\ell')
=
h(a_m,a_{m'})
+h(\ell',\ell)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition of $g_J$ and step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_J(m+\ell,m'+\ell')
&=
\omega(m+\ell,-b_{\ell'}+a_{m'})\\
&=
-\omega(m,b_{\ell'})
+\omega(m,a_{m'})
-\omega(\ell,b_{\ell'})
+\omega(\ell,a_{m'}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The first and fourth terms vanish by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. Substitute steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} into the two remaining terms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Replacing $a_m$ and $a_{m'}$ by their definitions gives
$$
\boxed{
g_J(m+\ell,m'+\ell')
=
h\!\left(
\theta_h^{-1}(\theta_\omega^Lm),
\theta_h^{-1}(\theta_\omega^Lm')
\right)
+h(\ell',\ell)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} with
$$
a_m=\theta_h^{-1}(\theta_\omega^Lm),
\qquad
a_{m'}=\theta_h^{-1}(\theta_\omega^Lm').
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is exactly the required identity.

:::
