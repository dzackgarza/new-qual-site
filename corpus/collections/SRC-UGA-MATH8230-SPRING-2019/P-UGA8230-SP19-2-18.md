::: {.problem}
Let $L$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space and equip $L^*\oplus L$ with its standard symplectic form, with $L$ identified with $\{0\}\oplus L$.

(a) Show that if $N\le L^*\oplus L$ satisfies $N\oplus L=L^*\oplus L$, then there is a unique linear map $A_N:L^*\to L$ such that
$$
N=\{(\alpha,A_N\alpha):\alpha\in L^*\}.
$$

(b) Prove that $N$ is Lagrangian if and only if $A_N:L^*\to L$ is symmetric, meaning that under the canonical identification $L^{**}\cong L$ one has $A_N^*=A_N$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The canonical symplectic form on $L^*\oplus L$ is
$$
\omega_L((\alpha,x),(\beta,y))
=
\alpha(y)-\beta(x).
$$
Let
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1:L^*\oplus L\to L^*,
\qquad
\operatorname{pr}_2:L^*\oplus L\to L
$$
be the two projections.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The restriction
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1|_N:N\longrightarrow L^*
$$
is an isomorphism.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
N\oplus L=L^*\oplus L,
$$
one has
$$
N\cap L=\{0\}.
$$
But
$$
\ker(\operatorname{pr}_1|_N)
=
N\cap(\{0\}\oplus L)
=
N\cap L
=
\{0\},
$$
so the restriction is injective.

Moreover,
$$
\dim N
=
\dim(L^*\oplus L)-\dim L
=
\dim L^*.
$$
Thus the injective map $\operatorname{pr}_1|_N$ is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There is a unique linear map
$$
A_N:L^*\to L
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{
N=\{(\alpha,A_N\alpha):\alpha\in L^*\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, define
$$
A_N
\coloneqq
\operatorname{pr}_2
\circ
(\operatorname{pr}_1|_N)^{-1}.
$$
For each $\alpha\in L^*$, the unique element of $N$ whose first component is $\alpha$ is then
$$
(\alpha,A_N\alpha).
$$
Hence $N$ is exactly the graph of $A_N$.

If another linear map $A:L^*\to L$ had the same graph, then for every $\alpha$ both
$$
(\alpha,A\alpha)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(\alpha,A_N\alpha)
$$
would be the unique element of $N$ with first component $\alpha$. Therefore $A=A_N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For $\alpha,\beta\in L^*$,
$$
\omega_L
\bigl(
(\alpha,A_N\alpha),
(\beta,A_N\beta)
\bigr)
=
\alpha(A_N\beta)-\beta(A_N\alpha).
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute the two graph vectors into the defining formula for $\omega_L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The subspace $N$ is Lagrangian if and only if
$$
\alpha(A_N\beta)=\beta(A_N\alpha)
$$
for every $\alpha,\beta\in L^*$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim N=\dim L
=
\frac12\dim(L^*\oplus L).
$$
Thus $N$ is Lagrangian if and only if it is isotropic. By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, isotropy is exactly the condition
$$
\alpha(A_N\beta)-\beta(A_N\alpha)=0
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta\in L^*$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Under the canonical identification $L^{**}\cong L$,
$$
A_N^*=A_N
$$
if and only if
$$
\alpha(A_N\beta)=\beta(A_N\alpha)
$$
for every $\alpha,\beta\in L^*$.


::: pf-proof

The dual map
$$
A_N^*:L^*\longrightarrow L^{**}
$$
is characterized by
$$
(A_N^*\beta)(\alpha)
=
\beta(A_N\alpha).
$$
Under the canonical map
$$
\iota:L\longrightarrow L^{**},
\qquad
\iota(x)(\alpha)=\alpha(x),
$$
the equality $A_N^*=A_N$ means
$$
A_N^*\beta=\iota(A_N\beta)
$$
for every $\beta\in L^*$. Evaluating both sides on $\alpha\in L^*$ gives precisely
$$
\beta(A_N\alpha)=\alpha(A_N\beta).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
N\text{ is Lagrangian}
\iff
A_N^*=A_N}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} prove part (a), and steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} prove part (b).

:::
