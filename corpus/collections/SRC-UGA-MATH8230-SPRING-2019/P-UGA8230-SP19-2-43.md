::: {.problem}
Let $\omega$ be a linear symplectic form on $\RR^{2n}$, and let $A$ be the $2n\times2n$ matrix determined by
$$
\omega(v,w)=(Av)\cdot w.
$$

(a) Show that $A$ is skew-symmetric and invertible.

(b) If
$$
O=(AA^T)^{-1/2}A
$$
is the orthogonal part of the polar decomposition of $A$, prove using part (a) that $O^2=-I$.
Carefully justify the commutation of the matrices that you use.

(c) Prove that the complex structure $O$ is $\omega$-compatible.
:::

::: {.solution}

Set
$$
P\coloneqq(AA^T)^{1/2}.
$$
Then $P$ is the positive-definite symmetric square root of $AA^T$, and
$$
O=P^{-1}A.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The matrix $A$ is skew-symmetric:
$$
\boxed{A^T=-A}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Alternation of $\omega$ gives, for all $v,w\in\RR^{2n}$,
$$
(Av)\cdot w
=
\omega(v,w)
=
-\omega(w,v)
=
-(Aw)\cdot v.
$$
In matrix notation this is
$$
v^TA^Tw=-v^TAw
$$
for all $v,w$. Hence
$$
A^T=-A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The matrix $A$ is invertible.


::: pf-proof

If $Av=0$, then for every $w$,
$$
\omega(v,w)
=(Av)\cdot w
=0.
$$
Nondegeneracy of $\omega$ implies $v=0$. Thus $A$ has trivial kernel, and since it is a square finite-dimensional matrix, it is invertible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
AA^T=-A^2,
$$
and $A$ commutes with $P$ and $P^{-1}$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
AA^T=A(-A)=-A^2.
$$
Hence $A$ commutes with $AA^T$.

Because $AA^T$ is positive definite by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, it is orthogonally diagonalizable with positive eigenvalues. The matrices
$$
P=(AA^T)^{1/2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
P^{-1}=(AA^T)^{-1/2}
$$
are obtained from $AA^T$ by applying the scalar functions $t\mapsto t^{1/2}$ and $t\mapsto t^{-1/2}$ on its spectrum. Any matrix commuting with $AA^T$ preserves its eigenspaces, hence commutes with these spectral functions. Therefore $A$ commutes with both $P$ and $P^{-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The orthogonal polar factor satisfies
$$
\boxed{O^2=-I}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
O^2
&=
P^{-1}AP^{-1}A\\
&=
P^{-2}A^2\\
&=
(AA^T)^{-1}A^2\\
&=
(-A^2)^{-1}A^2\\
&=
-I.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $O$ is a complex structure.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The matrix $O$ is orthogonal and
$$
O^T=-O.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $O$ is the orthogonal factor in the polar decomposition, $O^TO=I$. Alternatively,
$$
\begin{aligned}
OO^T
&=
P^{-1}AA^TP^{-1}\\
&=
P^{-1}P^2P^{-1}\\
&=
I.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $O^{-1}=O^T$. Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives $O^{-1}=-O$, hence
$$
O^T=-O.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The bilinear form
$$
g_O(v,w)\coloneqq\omega(v,Ow)
$$
is given by
$$
\boxed{g_O(v,w)=(Pv)\cdot w}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
O=P^{-1}A,
$$
one has
$$
A=PO.
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $P$ commutes with $A$, hence also with $O=P^{-1}A$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_O(v,w)
&=
(Av)\cdot Ow\\
&=
(POv)\cdot Ow\\
&=
(OPv)\cdot Ow\\
&=
(Pv)\cdot w,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the last equality uses orthogonality of $O$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The form $g_O$ is an inner product.


::: pf-proof

The matrix $P$ is symmetric positive definite. Hence step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
g_O(v,w)
=(Pv)\cdot w
=
v\cdot(Pw)
=
g_O(w,v),
$$
so $g_O$ is symmetric, and for every nonzero $v$,
$$
g_O(v,v)
=(Pv)\cdot v
>0.
$$
Thus $g_O$ is positive definite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Consequently, $O$ is $\omega$-compatible.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $O$ is a complex structure, and by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} the associated form
$$
(v,w)\longmapsto\omega(v,Ow)
$$
is an inner product. This is exactly the definition of $\omega$-compatibility.

:::
