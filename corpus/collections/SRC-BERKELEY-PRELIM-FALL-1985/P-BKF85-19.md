::: {.problem}
An $n\times n$ real matrix $T$ is called positive definite if it is symmetric and
\[
\langle Tx,x\rangle>0
\]
for every nonzero $x\in\mathbb R^n$.
Let $A$ and $B$ be positive-definite real matrices.

1. Show that there is a basis $\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}$ of $\mathbb R^n$ and real numbers $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$ such that
\[
\langle Av_i,v_j\rangle=
\begin{cases}
1,&i=j,\\
0,&i\ne j,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
\langle Bv_i,v_j\rangle=
\begin{cases}
\lambda_i,&i=j,\\
0,&i\ne j.
\end{cases}
\]

2. Deduce that there is an invertible real matrix $U$ such that
\[
U^TAU=I
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
U^TBU
\]
is diagonal.
:::
