::: {.problem}
Let $(M,d)$ be a nonempty complete metric space and let $S:M\to M$.
Suppose $S^2=S\circ S$ is a strict contraction: there exists $\lambda<1$ such that
\[
d(S^2(x),S^2(y))\le \lambda d(x,y)
\]
for all $x,y\in M$.
Show that $S$ has a unique fixed point in $M$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The map $S^2$ has a unique fixed point $p\in M$.

::: {.proof}
If $M$ consists of a single point, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise there exist distinct $x,y\in M$, and the contraction inequality forces $\lambda\geq0$, since its left-hand side is nonnegative while $d(x,y)>0$. Thus
$$
0\leq\lambda<1.
$$
By hypothesis, $S^2:M\to M$ is therefore a contraction on the nonempty complete metric space $M$. The Banach fixed-point theorem gives a unique point $p\in M$ such that
$$
S^2(p)=p.
$$
:::

<1>2. The point $S(p)$ is also fixed by $S^2$.

::: {.proof}
Using $S^2(p)=p$,
$$
S^2(S(p))
=
S^3(p)
=
S(S^2(p))
=
S(p).
$$
Thus $S(p)$ is a fixed point of $S^2$.
:::

<1>3. The point $p$ is fixed by $S$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $S^2$ has only one fixed point. Step <1>2 shows that $S(p)$ is such a fixed point, while $p$ itself is one. Hence
$$
S(p)=p.
$$
:::

<1>4. The fixed point of $S$ is unique.

::: {.proof}
If $q\in M$ satisfies
$$
S(q)=q,
$$
then
$$
S^2(q)=S(q)=q.
$$
Thus every fixed point of $S$ is a fixed point of $S^2$. Step <1>1 says the latter has only the fixed point $p$, so $q=p$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3 and <1>4 give existence and uniqueness of the fixed point of $S$.
:::
:::
