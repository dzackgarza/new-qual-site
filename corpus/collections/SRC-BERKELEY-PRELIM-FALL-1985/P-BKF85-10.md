::: {.problem}
Let $f,f_n:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$.
Assume that whenever $x_n\to x$,
\[
f_n(x_n)\longrightarrow f(x).
\]
Show that $f$ is continuous.

The functions $f_n$ are not assumed to be continuous.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For every fixed $a\in\RR$,
$$
f_n(a)\longrightarrow f(a).
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply the hypothesis to the constant sequence
$$
x_n=a,
$$
which converges to $a$.
:::

<1>2. Let $y_m\to x$. There exists a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers $(n_m)$ such that
$$
\abs{f_{n_m}(y_m)-f(y_m)}<\frac1m
$$
for every $m$.

::: {.proof}
For each fixed $m$, step <1>1 applied at the point $y_m$ gives
$$
f_n(y_m)\longrightarrow f(y_m)
\qquad
\text{as }n\to\infty.
$$
Choose $n_1$ so that the desired inequality holds for $m=1$. Having chosen $n_{m-1}$, choose
$$
n_m>n_{m-1}
$$
large enough that
$$
\abs{f_{n_m}(y_m)-f(y_m)}<\frac1m.
$$
:::

<1>3. Define a sequence $(z_n)$ by
$$
z_{n_m}=y_m
$$
for every $m$, and
$$
z_n=x
$$
when $n$ is not one of the indices $n_m$. Then
$$
z_n\longrightarrow x.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $y_m\to x$, there is $M$ such that
$$
\abs{y_m-x}<\varepsilon
$$
for all $m\geq M$. For $n\geq n_M$, either $z_n=x$, or $n=n_m$ for some $m\geq M$, in which case $z_n=y_m$. Thus
$$
\abs{z_n-x}<\varepsilon
$$
for all $n\geq n_M$.
:::

<1>4. For every sequence $y_m\to x$,
$$
f(y_m)\longrightarrow f(x).
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply the hypothesis to the sequence $(z_n)$ from step <1>3. Then
$$
f_n(z_n)\longrightarrow f(x).
$$
Passing to the subsequence $n=n_m$ gives
$$
f_{n_m}(y_m)\longrightarrow f(x).
$$
By step <1>2,
$$
\abs{f_{n_m}(y_m)-f(y_m)}<\frac1m\longrightarrow0.
$$
Therefore $f(y_m)\to f(x)$.
:::

<1>5. The function $f$ is continuous on $\RR$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 shows that for every $x\in\RR$ and every sequence $y_m\to x$, one has
$$
f(y_m)\to f(x).
$$
By the sequential criterion for continuity in $\RR$, $f$ is continuous at every $x$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
