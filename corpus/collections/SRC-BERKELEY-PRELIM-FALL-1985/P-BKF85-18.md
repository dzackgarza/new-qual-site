::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group.
For $X\subseteq G$, define
\[
C(X)=\{y\in G:xy=yx\text{ for every }x\in X\}.
\]
Prove:

1. If $X\subseteq Y$, then $C(Y)\subseteq C(X)$.

2. $X\subseteq C(C(X))$.

3. $C(X)=C(C(C(X)))$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $X\subseteq Y$, then
$$
C(Y)\subseteq C(X).
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $g\in C(Y)$. By definition, $g$ commutes with every element of $Y$. Since $X\subseteq Y$, it therefore commutes with every element of $X$. Hence
$$
g\in C(X).
$$
Thus $C(Y)\subseteq C(X)$.
:::

<1>2. For every subset $X\subseteq G$,
$$
X\subseteq C(C(X)).
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $x\in X$. If $y\in C(X)$, then by definition
$$
xy=yx.
$$
Thus $x$ commutes with every element $y$ of $C(X)$, which means
$$
x\in C(C(X)).
$$
Therefore $X\subseteq C(C(X))$.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
C(C(C(X)))\subseteq C(X).
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives
$$
X\subseteq C(C(X)).
$$
Applying the order-reversing property from step <1>1 to this inclusion yields
$$
C(C(C(X)))\subseteq C(X).
$$
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
C(X)\subseteq C(C(C(X))).
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>2 to the subset $C(X)$ in place of $X$. This gives
$$
C(X)
\subseteq
C(C(C(X))).
$$
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
C(X)=C(C(C(X))).
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine the two inclusions from steps <1>3 and <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1, <1>2, and <1>5 prove the three requested assertions.
:::
:::
