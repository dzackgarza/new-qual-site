::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group.
For $X\subseteq G$, define
\[
C(X)=\{y\in G:xy=yx\text{ for every }x\in X\}.
\]
Prove:

1. If $X\subseteq Y$, then $C(Y)\subseteq C(X)$.

2. $X\subseteq C(C(X))$.

3. $C(X)=C(C(C(X)))$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $X\subseteq Y$, then
$$
C(Y)\subseteq C(X).
$$

::: pf-proof

Let $g\in C(Y)$. By definition, $g$ commutes with every element of $Y$. Since $X\subseteq Y$, it therefore commutes with every element of $X$. Hence
$$
g\in C(X).
$$
Thus $C(Y)\subseteq C(X)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every subset $X\subseteq G$,
$$
X\subseteq C(C(X)).
$$

::: pf-proof

Let $x\in X$. If $y\in C(X)$, then by definition
$$
xy=yx.
$$
Thus $x$ commutes with every element $y$ of $C(X)$, which means
$$
x\in C(C(X)).
$$
Therefore $X\subseteq C(C(X))$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

One has
$$
C(C(C(X)))\subseteq C(X).
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
X\subseteq C(C(X)).
$$
Applying the order-reversing property from step [](#s1){.pf-ref} to this inclusion yields
$$
C(C(C(X)))\subseteq C(X).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One has
$$
C(X)\subseteq C(C(C(X))).
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s2){.pf-ref} to the subset $C(X)$ in place of $X$. This gives
$$
C(X)
\subseteq
C(C(C(X))).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore
$$
C(X)=C(C(C(X))).
$$

::: pf-proof

Combine the two inclusions from steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref}, [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} prove the three requested assertions.

:::
