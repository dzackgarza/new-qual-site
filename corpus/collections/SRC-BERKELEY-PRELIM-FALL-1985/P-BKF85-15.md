::: {.problem}
Fix $0\le a\le1$.
Determine all nonnegative continuous functions $f$ on $[0,1]$ satisfying
\[
\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx=1,
\qquad
\int_0^1 x f(x)\,dx=a,
\qquad
\int_0^1 x^2f(x)\,dx=a^2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Any function satisfying the three displayed integral conditions would satisfy
$$
\int_0^1 (x-a)^2f(x)\,dx=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Expand the square and use the three hypotheses:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1 (x-a)^2f(x)\,dx
&=
\int_0^1x^2f(x)\,dx
-2a\int_0^1xf(x)\,dx
+a^2\int_0^1f(x)\,dx\\
&=
a^2-2a^2+a^2\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. If $f$ satisfied the hypotheses, then
$$
(x-a)^2f(x)=0
$$
for every $x\in[0,1]$.

::: {.proof}
The function
$$
g(x)\coloneqq(x-a)^2f(x)
$$
is continuous and nonnegative on $[0,1]$. By step <1>1 its integral is $0$. If $g(x_0)>0$ at some point, continuity would make $g$ positive on a nontrivial interval relative to $[0,1]$, forcing its integral to be positive. Hence $g\equiv0$.
:::

<1>3. Any such $f$ would be identically zero.

::: {.proof}
For every $x\neq a$, step <1>2 and $(x-a)^2>0$ give
$$
f(x)=0.
$$
Thus $f$ vanishes on $[0,1]\setminus\{a\}$. Since $f$ is continuous, taking points $x\neq a$ tending to $a$ from within $[0,1]$ gives
$$
f(a)=0.
$$
This also covers the endpoint cases $a=0$ and $a=1$ by one-sided convergence. Therefore $f\equiv0$.
:::

<1>4. No nonnegative continuous function satisfies all three conditions.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 would imply
$$
\int_0^1f(x)\,dx=0,
$$
contradicting the required value $1$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
The set of functions requested in the problem is empty by step <1>4.
:::
:::
