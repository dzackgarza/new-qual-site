::: {.problem}
Evaluate
\[
\int_0^{2\pi} e^{e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Under the substitution
$$
z=e^{i\theta},
$$
the integral becomes
$$
\frac1i\oint_{\abs{z}=1}\frac{e^z}{z}\,dz.
$$

::: pf-proof

As $\theta$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$, the point $z=e^{i\theta}$ traverses the unit circle once counterclockwise. Moreover,
$$
dz=iz\,d\theta,
\qquad
d\theta=\frac{dz}{iz}.
$$
Substituting this into the integral gives the displayed contour integral.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

One has
$$
\frac1i\oint_{\abs{z}=1}\frac{e^z}{z}\,dz
=
2\pi.
$$

::: pf-proof

By Cauchy's integral formula,
$$
\oint_{\abs{z}=1}\frac{e^z}{z}\,dz
=
2\pi i\,e^0
=
2\pi i.
$$
Multiplying by $1/i$ gives $2\pi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^{2\pi} e^{e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta=2\pi
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} is the required evaluation.
