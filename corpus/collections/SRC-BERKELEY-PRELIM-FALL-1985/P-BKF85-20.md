::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be differentiable on $[0,1]$ and suppose
\[
\sup_{0<x<1}|f'(x)|=M<\infty.
\]
For every positive integer $n$, prove that
\[
\left|
\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(j/n)-\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx
\right|
\le \frac{M}{2n}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $j=0,\ldots,n-1$ and every
$$
x\in\left[\frac jn,\frac{j+1}n\right],
$$
one has
$$
\abs{f(j/n)-f(x)}
\leq
M\left(x-\frac jn\right).
$$

::: pf-proof

If $x=j/n$, the inequality is immediate. If $x>j/n$, the mean value theorem applied to $f$ on
$$
\left[\frac jn,x\right]
$$
gives some point $\xi$ between $j/n$ and $x$ such that
$$
f(x)-f(j/n)
=
f'(\xi)\left(x-\frac jn\right).
$$
Since $0<\xi<1$ whenever the interval is nontrivial,
$$
\abs{f'(\xi)}\leq M,
$$
which gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For each $j=0,\ldots,n-1$,
$$
\left|
\frac1n f(j/n)
-\int_{j/n}^{(j+1)/n}f(x)\,dx
\right|
\leq
\frac{M}{2n^2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since the interval has length $1/n$,
$$
\frac1n f(j/n)
=
\int_{j/n}^{(j+1)/n}f(j/n)\,dx.
$$
Hence, by step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\frac1n f(j/n)
-\int_{j/n}^{(j+1)/n}f(x)\,dx
\right|
&\leq
\int_{j/n}^{(j+1)/n}
\abs{f(j/n)-f(x)}\,dx\\
&\leq
M\int_{j/n}^{(j+1)/n}
\left(x-\frac jn\right)\,dx\\
&=
\frac{M}{2n^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The total left-endpoint Riemann-sum error satisfies
$$
\left|
\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(j/n)
-\int_0^1f(x)\,dx
\right|
\leq
\frac{M}{2n}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Decompose the integral over the $n$ subintervals and apply the triangle inequality together with step [](#s2){.pf-ref}:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(j/n)
-\int_0^1f(x)\,dx
\right|
&\leq
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
\left|
\frac1n f(j/n)
-\int_{j/n}^{(j+1)/n}f(x)\,dx
\right|\\
&\leq
n\cdot\frac{M}{2n^2}\\
&=
\frac{M}{2n}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} is the required estimate.
