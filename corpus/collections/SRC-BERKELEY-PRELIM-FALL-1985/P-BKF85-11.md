::: {.problem}
Let $G\le S_6$ and suppose $G$ contains an element of order $6$.
Prove that $G$ has a normal subgroup of index $2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every permutation of order $6$ in $S_6$ is odd.

::: {.proof}
Let $\sigma\in S_6$ have order $6$. The order of a permutation is the least common multiple of the lengths of the cycles in its disjoint-cycle decomposition.

Since $3$ divides the order of $\sigma$, its cycle decomposition contains a cycle of length divisible by $3$. With only six letters, such a cycle has length $3$ or $6$.

If $\sigma$ contains a $6$-cycle, then it is itself a $6$-cycle. Its sign is
$$
(-1)^{6-1}=-1.
$$

Otherwise $\sigma$ contains a $3$-cycle. Since its order is also divisible by $2$, the remaining three letters must contain an even cycle. The only possibility is a $2$-cycle, leaving one fixed point. Thus the cycle type is
$$
3+2+1.
$$
A $3$-cycle is even and a $2$-cycle is odd, so their disjoint product is odd. Hence every order-$6$ element of $S_6$ is odd.
:::

<1>2. The restriction
$$
\operatorname{sgn}|_G:G\longrightarrow\{\pm1\}
$$
is surjective.

::: {.proof}
By hypothesis, $G$ contains an element $\sigma$ of order $6$. Step <1>1 gives
$$
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=-1.
$$
The identity element maps to $1$, so the image contains both elements of $\{\pm1\}$.
:::

<1>3. The subgroup
$$
H\coloneqq G\cap A_6
$$
is normal in $G$ and has index $2$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
H
=
\ker(\operatorname{sgn}|_G),
$$
it is normal in $G$. By step <1>2, the restricted sign homomorphism has image of order $2$. The first isomorphism theorem gives
$$
G/H\cong\{\pm1\},
$$
so
$$
[G:H]=2.
$$
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 supplies the required normal subgroup of index $2$.
:::
