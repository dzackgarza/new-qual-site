::: {.problem}
Let $A$ and $B$ be endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ over a field $F$.
Prove or disprove each statement:

1. Every eigenvector of $AB$ is also an eigenvector of $BA$.

2. Every eigenvalue of $AB$ is also an eigenvalue of $BA$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Statement 1 is false.

::: {.proof}
Take $V=F^2$ and, in the standard basis, let
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then
$$
AB=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
BA=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
For
$$
e_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\
0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
one has
$$
ABe_1=e_1,
$$
so $e_1$ is an eigenvector of $AB$. But
$$
BAe_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\
1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is not a scalar multiple of $e_1$. Thus $e_1$ is not an eigenvector of $BA$.
:::

<1>2. Let $\lambda\neq0$ be an eigenvalue of $AB$. Then $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $BA$.

::: {.proof}
Choose a nonzero vector $v$ such that
$$
ABv=\lambda v.
$$
One must have $Bv\neq0$, because otherwise $ABv=0$, contradicting $\lambda v\neq0$. Applying $B$ to the eigenvalue equation gives
$$
BA(Bv)
=
B(ABv)
=
\lambda Bv.
$$
Thus the nonzero vector $Bv$ is an eigenvector of $BA$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$.
:::

<1>3. If $0$ is an eigenvalue of $AB$, then $0$ is an eigenvalue of $BA$.

::: {.proof}
If $0$ is an eigenvalue of $AB$, then $AB$ is not invertible. Suppose, for contradiction, that $BA$ were invertible.

If $Ax=0$, then
$$
BAx=0,
$$
so invertibility of $BA$ gives $x=0$. Hence $A$ is injective and therefore invertible because $V$ is finite-dimensional.

Also, for every $y\in V$, surjectivity of $BA$ gives some $x\in V$ such that
$$
BAx=y;
$$
thus $y=B(Ax)$ lies in the image of $B$. Hence $B$ is surjective and therefore invertible. Thus both $A$ and $B$ would be invertible, forcing $AB$ to be invertible, a contradiction.

Therefore $BA$ is not invertible, so $0$ is an eigenvalue of $BA$.
:::

<1>4. Statement 2 is true.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 handles every nonzero eigenvalue of $AB$, and step <1>3 handles the eigenvalue $0$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Statement 1 is disproved in step <1>1, while statement 2 is proved in step <1>4.
:::
