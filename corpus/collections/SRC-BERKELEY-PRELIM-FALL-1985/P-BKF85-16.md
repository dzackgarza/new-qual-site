::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(x)=x^5-8x^3+9x-3,
\qquad
g(x)=x^4-5x^2-6x+3.
\]
Prove that there is an integer $d$ such that $f$ and $g$ have a common root in
\[
\mathbb Q(\sqrt d).
\]
Determine $d$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The two polynomials have the common quadratic factor
$$
q(x)=x^2-3x+1.
$$

::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2-3x+1)(x^3+3x^2-3)
&=
x^5-8x^3+9x-3\\
&=
f(x),
\end{aligned}
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2-3x+1)(x^2+3x+3)
&=
x^4-5x^2-6x+3\\
&=
g(x).
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence every root of $q$ is a common root of $f$ and $g$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The roots of $q$ are
$$
\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The quadratic formula applied to
$$
x^2-3x+1=0
$$
gives
$$
x
=
\frac{3\pm\sqrt{9-4}}2
=
\frac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The required integer is
$$
\boxed{d=5}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, both $f$ and $g$ vanish at
$$
\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}\in\QQ(\sqrt5).
$$
Thus $d=5$ has the required property. The other root lies in the same quadratic field.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives the required quadratic field and integer.

:::
