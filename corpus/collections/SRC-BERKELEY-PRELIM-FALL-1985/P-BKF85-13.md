::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic on the right half-plane
\[
H=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Re}z>0\}
\]
and suppose
\[
|f(z)|\le1\qquad(z\in H),
\qquad
f(1)=0.
\]
What is the largest possible value of $|f'(1)|$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The map
$$
\phi(z)\coloneqq\frac{z-1}{z+1}
$$
maps the right half-plane $H$ biholomorphically onto the unit disk $\mathbb D$, with inverse
$$
\psi(w)\coloneqq\frac{1+w}{1-w}.
$$

::: pf-proof

For $z\in H$,
$$
\abs{z+1}^2-\abs{z-1}^2
=
4\operatorname{Re}z
>0.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{\phi(z)}<1.
$$
Solving
$$
w=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
$$
for $z$ gives
$$
z=\frac{1+w}{1-w}=\psi(w).
$$
Thus $\phi$ and $\psi$ are inverse biholomorphisms. In particular,
$$
\psi(0)=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Define
$$
g(w)\coloneqq f(\psi(w)),
\qquad
w\in\mathbb D.
$$
Then
$$
g(0)=0,
\qquad
\abs{g(w)}\leq1,
$$
and therefore
$$
\abs{g'(0)}\leq1.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $\psi$ maps $\mathbb D$ into $H$, so $g$ is analytic on $\mathbb D$. The hypotheses on $f$ give
$$
g(0)=f(1)=0
$$
and $\abs{g(w)}\leq1$. Schwarz's lemma therefore yields $\abs{g'(0)}\leq1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every admissible $f$ satisfies
$$
\abs{f'(1)}\leq\frac12.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\psi'(w)=\frac{2}{(1-w)^2},
$$
one has
$$
\psi'(0)=2.
$$
By the chain rule,
$$
g'(0)
=
f'(\psi(0))\psi'(0)
=
2f'(1).
$$
Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives $\abs{2f'(1)}\leq1$, proving the bound.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The bound $1/2$ is attained.

::: pf-proof

Take
$$
f_0(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}.
$$
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $f_0$ maps $H$ into $\mathbb D$, so $\abs{f_0(z)}<1$ on $H$, and
$$
f_0(1)=0.
$$
Moreover,
$$
f_0'(z)=\frac{2}{(z+1)^2},
$$
so
$$
\abs{f_0'(1)}=\frac12.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The largest possible value is
$$
\boxed{\frac12}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} supplies the universal upper bound, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} attains it.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required extremal value.

:::
