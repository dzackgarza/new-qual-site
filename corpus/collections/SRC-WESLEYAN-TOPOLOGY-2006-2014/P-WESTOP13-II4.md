::: {.problem}
1. Calculate the simplicial homology groups of $S^1$.

2. Calculate the simplicial homology groups of the torus using the following $\Delta$-complex from the source diagram.
   Start with a square whose four corners are identified to one vertex $y$.
   Label both vertical sides by $a$ and orient them upward; label both horizontal sides by $b$ and orient them to the right.
   Add the diagonal edge $c$, oriented from the lower-left corner to the upper-right corner.
   Let $U$ be the upper triangle and $L$ the lower triangle.

3. Let $T_0^2$ be the torus with the interior of a disk removed. Use the Mayer--Vietoris sequence for
   $$
   T^2=T_0^2\cup D^2,
   \qquad
   T_0^2\cap D^2\simeq S^1,
   $$
   to calculate the homology groups of $T_0^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Give $S^1$ the one-vertex $\Delta$-complex structure with one vertex $v$ and one oriented edge $e$ whose two endpoints are both $v$.
Its chain complex is
$$
0\longrightarrow \ZZ\langle e\rangle
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
\ZZ\langle v\rangle
\longrightarrow0,
$$
with $\partial_1e=0$.


::: pf-proof

The boundary of the oriented edge is terminal vertex minus initial vertex.
Since both are the same vertex $v$,
$$
\partial_1e=v-v=0.
$$
There are no simplices of dimension at least $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_n(S^1)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&n=0,1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

The boundary map in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} is zero, so
$$
H_1(S^1)=\ker\partial_1\cong\ZZ
$$
and
$$
H_0(S^1)=C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1\cong\ZZ.
$$
All higher chain groups vanish.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For the source $\Delta$-complex on the torus,
$$
C_2=\ZZ\langle U,L\rangle,
\qquad
C_1=\ZZ\langle a,b,c\rangle,
\qquad
C_0=\ZZ\langle y\rangle.
$$
Moreover,
$$
\partial_1=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The recovered source diagram has one vertex after all four corners of the square are identified, three edge classes $a,b,c$, and two triangles $U,L$.
Since both endpoints of each edge are the single vertex $y$, every $1$-simplex has zero boundary.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Orient both triangles counterclockwise in the square.
Then
$$
\partial_2U=c-b-a,
\qquad
\partial_2L=a+b-c.
$$


::: pf-proof

For the lower triangle $L$, the counterclockwise boundary runs along the bottom edge in the positive $b$ direction, then the right edge in the positive $a$ direction, then back along the diagonal opposite to its positive $c$ direction.
Hence
$$
\partial_2L=b+a-c.
$$

For the upper triangle $U$, the counterclockwise boundary runs along the diagonal in the positive $c$ direction, then along the top edge opposite to the positive $b$ direction, then down the left edge opposite to the positive $a$ direction.
Hence
$$
\partial_2U=c-b-a.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The kernel of $\partial_2$ is generated by $U+L$, and the image of $\partial_2$ is generated by $a+b-c$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\partial_2U=-(a+b-c),
\qquad
\partial_2L=a+b-c.
$$
Thus for integers $r,s$,
$$
\partial_2(rU+sL)=(s-r)(a+b-c).
$$
This vanishes exactly when $r=s$, so
$$
\ker\partial_2=\ZZ\langle U+L\rangle.
$$
The image is visibly the cyclic subgroup generated by $a+b-c$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The torus has homology
$$
\boxed{
H_n(T^2)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&n=0,2,\\
\ZZ^2,&n=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Since there are no $3$-simplices, step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
H_2(T^2)=\ker\partial_2\cong\ZZ.
$$
Since $\partial_1=0$,
$$
H_1(T^2)
=\frac{\ZZ\langle a,b,c\rangle}
{\ZZ\langle a+b-c\rangle}
\cong\ZZ^2.
$$
The relation vector $(1,1,-1)$ is primitive, so the quotient is free of rank $2$.
Finally,
$$
H_0(T^2)=\ZZ
$$
because the $\Delta$-complex has one vertex, and all higher groups vanish.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The space $T_0^2$ is connected, so
$$
H_0(T_0^2)\cong\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

Removing the interior of a disk from the connected surface $T^2$ leaves a connected surface with one boundary component.
Hence its zeroth homology is $\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

In the Mayer--Vietoris sequence for
$$
T^2=T_0^2\cup D^2,
\qquad
T_0^2\cap D^2\simeq S^1,
$$
the connecting map
$$
\delta:H_2(T^2)\longrightarrow H_1(S^1)
$$
is an isomorphism.


::: pf-proof

Both groups are infinite cyclic by steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}. At the chain level, represent the fundamental class of the oriented torus by the sum of a fundamental $2$-chain on $T_0^2$ and the oriented disk that fills its boundary.
The Mayer--Vietoris connecting homomorphism sends this class to the common boundary circle of those two chains.
That circle represents a generator of
$$
H_1(T_0^2\cap D^2)\cong H_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ.
$$
Thus $\delta$ sends a generator to a generator up to sign and is therefore an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

One has
$$
H_2(T_0^2)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The relevant part of the Mayer--Vietoris sequence is
$$
0=H_2(S^1)
\longrightarrow
H_2(T_0^2)\oplus H_2(D^2)
\longrightarrow
H_2(T^2)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_1(S^1).
$$
Since $H_2(D^2)=0$, the map
$$
H_2(T_0^2)\to H_2(T^2)
$$
is injective.
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, $\ker\delta=0$, so exactness forces its image to be $0$.
Hence $H_2(T_0^2)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

One has
$$
H_1(T_0^2)\cong\ZZ^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The next part of Mayer--Vietoris is
$$
H_2(T^2)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_1(S^1)
\longrightarrow
H_1(T_0^2)
\longrightarrow
H_1(T^2)
\longrightarrow
H_0(S^1).
$$
Since $\delta$ is surjective by step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, the map
$$
H_1(S^1)\to H_1(T_0^2)
$$
is zero.
Thus
$$
H_1(T_0^2)\to H_1(T^2)
$$
is injective.

The next map
$$
H_0(S^1)\longrightarrow H_0(T_0^2)\oplus H_0(D^2)
$$
is, up to sign, the diagonal map
$$
\ZZ\longrightarrow\ZZ\oplus\ZZ,
\qquad
1\longmapsto(1,-1),
$$
because all three spaces are connected.
It is injective.
Exactness therefore makes
$$
H_1(T^2)\to H_0(S^1)
$$
the zero map, so
$$
H_1(T_0^2)\to H_1(T^2)
$$
is also surjective.
Hence it is an isomorphism.
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
H_1(T_0^2)\cong\ZZ^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

For every $n\ge3$,
$$
H_n(T_0^2)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $n\ge3$, the Mayer--Vietoris sequence has
$$
H_n(S^1)=0,
\qquad
H_n(D^2)=0,
\qquad
H_n(T^2)=0.
$$
Exactness therefore forces $H_n(T_0^2)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_n(T_0^2)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&n=0,\\
\ZZ^2,&n=1,\\
0,&n\ge2.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::
