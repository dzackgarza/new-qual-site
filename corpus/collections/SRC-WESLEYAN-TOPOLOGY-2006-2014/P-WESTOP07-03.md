::: {.problem}
Let $(X_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of topological spaces.

1. Define the product and box topologies on $\prod_i X_i$. If the $X_i$ are metric spaces, define the uniform topology on the product.
2. State the characteristic property that makes the product topology the distinguished topology on $\prod_i X_i$.
3. Give, with justification, a sequence that converges in the product topology but not in the uniform or box topologies.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The product topology on
$$
X
\coloneqq
\prod_{i\in I}X_i
$$
has as a basis all sets
$$
\prod_{i\in I}U_i,
$$
where each
$$
U_i\subseteq X_i
$$
is open and
$$
U_i=X_i
$$
for all but finitely many
$$
i.
$$


::: pf-proof

Equivalently, the product topology is generated by the subbasic sets
$$
\pi_i^{-1}(U),
$$
where
$$
\pi_i:X\to X_i
$$
is a coordinate projection and
$$
U\subseteq X_i
$$
is open. Finite intersections of such subbasic sets are exactly the stated basic products.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The box topology on
$$
X
$$
has as a basis all products
$$
\boxed{
\prod_{i\in I}U_i}
$$
with every
$$
U_i\subseteq X_i
$$
open, with no finiteness restriction.


::: pf-proof

This is the definition of the box topology.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If each
$$
X_i
$$
has a metric
$$
d_i,
$$
replace it by the bounded equivalent metric
$$
\rho_i(x,y)
\coloneqq
\min\{d_i(x,y),1\}.
$$
The uniform topology on
$$
X
$$
is the metric topology induced by
$$
\boxed{
\rho(x,y)
\coloneqq
\sup_{i\in I}
\rho_i(x_i,y_i)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Each
$$
\rho_i
$$
induces the same topology as
$$
d_i,
$$
and the supremum is finite because
$$
0\le\rho_i\le1.
$$
The resulting metric measures the largest coordinatewise displacement uniformly over all coordinates.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The product topology is the coarsest topology on
$$
\prod_iX_i
$$
for which every coordinate projection
$$
\pi_i:\prod_jX_j\to X_i
$$
is continuous.


::: pf-proof

Every subbasic set of the product topology has the form
$$
\pi_i^{-1}(U),
$$
so every projection is continuous.

Conversely, any topology making every
$$
\pi_i
$$
continuous must contain every
$$
\pi_i^{-1}(U),
$$
and therefore must contain the topology generated by these sets, namely the product topology.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Equivalently, for every topological space $Y$, a map
$$
f:Y\to\prod_iX_i
$$
is continuous in the product topology if and only if every coordinate map
$$
\pi_i\circ f:Y\to X_i
$$
is continuous.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
f
$$
is continuous, then each
$$
\pi_i\circ f
$$
is continuous because
$$
\pi_i
$$
is.

Conversely, if every coordinate map is continuous, then the preimage under
$$
f
$$
of each subbasic set
$$
\pi_i^{-1}(U)
$$
is
$$
(\pi_i\circ f)^{-1}(U),
$$
which is open. Hence
$$
f
$$
is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Let
$$
X_i=\RR
$$
for
$$
i\in\NN,
$$
with the usual metric, and define
$$
x^{(n)}
=
(x^{(n)}_1,x^{(n)}_2,\ldots)
$$
by
$$
x^{(n)}_j
=
\begin{cases}
0,&j<n,\\
1,&j\ge n.
\end{cases}
$$
Then
$$
x^{(n)}\to0
$$
in the product topology.


::: pf-proof

Fix a coordinate
$$
j.
$$
For every
$$
n>j,
$$
one has
$$
x^{(n)}_j=0.
$$
Thus every coordinate sequence converges to
$$
0.
$$
By the characteristic property in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, this is exactly convergence to
$$
0
$$
in the product topology.

Equivalently, a basic product neighborhood of
$$
0
$$
restricts only finitely many coordinates, and for all sufficiently large
$$
n
$$
those finitely many coordinates of
$$
x^{(n)}
$$
are all zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The sequence
$$
x^{(n)}
$$
does not converge to
$$
0
$$
in the uniform topology.


::: pf-proof

Using the bounded metrics
$$
\rho_j(s,t)=\min\{\abs{s-t},1\},
$$
one has
$$
\rho(x^{(n)},0)
=
\sup_{j\in\NN}
\rho_j(x^{(n)}_j,0)
=1
$$
for every
$$
n,
$$
because every
$$
x^{(n)}
$$
has coordinates equal to
$$
1.
$$
Therefore the uniform distance from
$$
0
$$
never tends to zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The sequence
$$
x^{(n)}
$$
does not converge to
$$
0
$$
in the box topology.


::: pf-proof

The set
$$
U
\coloneqq
\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}
\left(-\frac12,\frac12\right)
$$
is a box-open neighborhood of
$$
0.
$$
Every
$$
x^{(n)}
$$
has some coordinate equal to
$$
1,
$$
indeed all coordinates
$$
j\ge n
$$
equal
$$
1.
$$
Hence
$$
x^{(n)}\notin U
$$
for every
$$
n.
$$
Thus the sequence does not converge to
$$
0
$$
in the box topology.

:::
