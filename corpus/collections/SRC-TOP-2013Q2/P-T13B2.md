::: {.problem}
(a) Explain in detail how the Seifert--van Kampen theorem may be used to calculate the fundamental group of a wedge sum $X\vee Y$ of two spaces under suitable assumptions on the spaces.
Clarify what assumptions on the spaces you are using and how you are using them.

(b) Describe the presentation complex $X_G$ of the group $G=\langle a,b,c:a^2=1\rangle$ as a wedge sum of familiar spaces.
Explain carefully what results you are using.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #van-kampen-wedge}
(a) Let $X$ and $Y$ be path-connected, with basepoints $x_0\in X$ and $y_0\in Y$ having open neighborhoods $N_X\subseteq X$ and $N_Y\subseteq Y$ that deformation retract onto $x_0$ and $y_0$. Then $\pi_1(X\vee Y)\cong\pi_1(X)*\pi_1(Y)$.

::: pf-proof
In $X\vee Y$, with $x_0$ and $y_0$ identified, the sets $U=X\cup N_Y$ and $V=N_X\cup Y$ are open.
The deformation retraction of $N_Y$ onto $y_0$, extended by the identity on $X$, deformation retracts $U$ onto $X$; likewise $V$ deformation retracts onto $Y$, and $U\cap V=N_X\vee N_Y$ deformation retracts onto the wedgepoint.
So $U$, $V$, and $U\cap V$ are open and path-connected, and $\pi_1(U\cap V)=1$.
The Seifert--van Kampen theorem identifies $\pi_1(U\cup V)$ with the free product of $\pi_1(U)$ and $\pi_1(V)$ amalgamated over the trivial group, that is, $\pi_1(U)*\pi_1(V)\cong\pi_1(X)*\pi_1(Y)$.
The hypothesis on $N_X$ and $N_Y$ holds when $X$ and $Y$ are CW complexes and $x_0$, $y_0$ are $0$-cells, because CW complexes are locally contractible in this sense.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #presentation-complex-structure}
The presentation complex $X_G$ of $G=\langle a,b,c\mid a^2=1\rangle$ has one $0$-cell, three $1$-cells $a,b,c$, and one $2$-cell attached along $a^2$.

::: pf-proof
The presentation complex has one $1$-cell loop for each generator and one $2$-cell for each relator, attached by the loop spelling the relator.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #presentation-complex-decomposition}
(b) $X_G\cong\RP^2\vee S^1\vee S^1$.

::: pf-proof
$\RP^2$ has a CW structure with one cell in each dimension $0,1,2$, the $2$-cell attached to the $1$-skeleton $S^1$ by a map of degree $2$.
So by step [](#presentation-complex-structure){.pf-ref} the subcomplex formed by the $0$-cell, the loop $a$, and the $2$-cell is $\RP^2$.
The loops $b$ and $c$ carry no $2$-cells and meet the rest of $X_G$ only at the $0$-cell, so each is a circle wedged at that point.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#van-kampen-wedge){.pf-ref} answers (a), and step [](#presentation-complex-decomposition){.pf-ref} answers (b).
Applying step [](#van-kampen-wedge){.pf-ref} twice to $\RP^2\vee S^1\vee S^1$ gives $\pi_1(X_G)\cong\ZZ/2*\ZZ*\ZZ\cong\langle a,b,c\mid a^2=1\rangle$, as it must for a presentation complex.
:::
