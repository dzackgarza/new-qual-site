::: {.problem}
(a) Explain in detail how the Seifert--van Kampen theorem may be used to calculate the fundamental group of a wedge sum $X\vee Y$ of two spaces under suitable assumptions on the spaces.
Clarify what assumptions on the spaces you are using and how you are using them.

(b) Describe the presentation complex $X_G$ of the group $G=\langle a,b,c:a^2=1\rangle$ as a wedge sum of familiar spaces.
Explain carefully what results you are using.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For part (a), assume $X$ and $Y$ are path-connected CW complexes and that the chosen wedgepoints are $0$-cells.
::: {.proof}
These hypotheses ensure that the wedge has convenient open neighborhoods to which Seifert--van Kampen applies. They are stronger than necessary but sufficient and standard.
:::

<1>2. Choose contractible open neighborhoods $N_X\subseteq X$ and $N_Y\subseteq Y$ of the wedgepoints that deformation-retract to those points, and form open subsets
$$
U=X\cup N_Y,\qquad V=N_X\cup Y
$$
of $X\vee Y$.
::: {.proof}
After replacing the CW complexes by small open stars of the $0$-cells if necessary, such neighborhoods exist. Then $U$ deformation-retracts onto $X$, $V$ deformation-retracts onto $Y$, and $U\cap V=N_X\cup N_Y$ is contractible.
:::

<1>3. Seifert--van Kampen gives
$$
\pi_1(X\vee Y)\cong \pi_1(X)*\pi_1(Y).
$$
::: {.proof}
The sets $U,V,U\cap V$ are path-connected and open, and the intersection has trivial fundamental group. Van Kampen identifies $\pi_1(U\cup V)$ with the pushout of $\pi_1(U)$ and $\pi_1(V)$ over the trivial group, namely their free product. The deformation retractions of <1>2 identify these groups with $\pi_1(X)$ and $\pi_1(Y)$.
:::

<1>4. The presentation complex of
$$
G=\langle a,b,c\mid a^2=1\rangle
$$
has one $0$-cell, three oriented $1$-cells $a,b,c$, and one $2$-cell attached along the loop $a^2$.
::: {.proof}
This is the definition of the standard presentation $2$-complex: one loop for each generator and one $2$-cell for each relator, attached by a loop spelling that relator.
:::

<1>5. The subcomplex consisting of the $a$-loop and the $2$-cell attached by $a^2$ is $\mathbb{RP}^2$.
::: {.proof}
The standard CW structure on $\mathbb{RP}^2$ has one cell in each dimension $0,1,2$, with the $2$-cell attached to the $1$-skeleton $S^1$ by the degree-$2$ map. This is exactly the subcomplex in question.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
X_G\cong \mathbb{RP}^2\vee S^1\vee S^1.
$$
::: {.proof}
The $b$- and $c$-loops have no $2$-cells attached to them, and all three loops share only the unique $0$-cell. Hence adjoining those two loops to the $\mathbb{RP}^2$ subcomplex forms the displayed wedge sum.
:::

<1>7. As a check,
$$
\pi_1(X_G)\cong (\mathbb Z/2)*\mathbb Z*\mathbb Z\cong\langle a,b,c\mid a^2=1\rangle.
$$
::: {.proof}
Apply part (a) twice and use $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb Z/2$ and $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::
:::
