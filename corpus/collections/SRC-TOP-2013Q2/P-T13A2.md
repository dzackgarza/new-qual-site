::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that every compact subspace of a Hausdorff space is closed.
Show by example that the Hausdorff hypothesis cannot be removed.

(b) Prove that every compact Hausdorff space is normal.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #point-away-from-compact}
If $K$ is a compact subspace of a Hausdorff space $X$ and $x\in X\setminus K$, then $x$ has an open neighborhood disjoint from $K$.

::: pf-proof
For each $y\in K$ we have $x\neq y$, so choose disjoint open sets $U_y\ni x$ and $V_y\ni y$.
The sets $V_y$ cover the compact space $K$, so $K\subseteq V_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{y_n}$ for some $y_1,\ldots,y_n\in K$.
Then $U=U_{y_1}\cap\cdots\cap U_{y_n}$ is an open neighborhood of $x$.
If $z\in U\cap K$, then $z\in V_{y_i}\cap U_{y_i}$ for some $i$, which is empty.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #indiscrete-counterexample}
In the two-point space $X=\{0,1\}$ with the indiscrete topology $\{\varnothing,X\}$, the compact subset $\{0\}$ is not closed.

::: pf-proof
Every subset of a finite space is compact, and the complement $\{1\}$ of $\{0\}$ is not open.
This space is not Hausdorff.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #separate-compact-closed-sets}
If $X$ is compact Hausdorff and $A,B\subseteq X$ are disjoint closed sets, then there are disjoint open sets $U\supseteq A$ and $V\supseteq B$.

::: pf-proof
$A$ and $B$ are compact as closed subsets of a compact space.
For every $(a,b)\in A\times B$, choose disjoint open neighborhoods $U_{a,b}\ni a$ and $V_{a,b}\ni b$.
Fix $a\in A$; compactness of $B$ gives $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ with $B\subseteq\bigcup_iV_{a,b_i}$.
Put $U_a=\bigcap_iU_{a,b_i}$ and $V_a=\bigcup_iV_{a,b_i}$; then $a\in U_a$, $B\subseteq V_a$, and $U_a\cap V_a=\varnothing$.
Compactness of $A$ gives $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ with $A\subseteq\bigcup_jU_{a_j}$.
Let $U=\bigcup_jU_{a_j}$ and $V=\bigcap_jV_{a_j}$.
Then $A\subseteq U$, $B\subseteq V$, and a point of $U\cap V$ would lie in some $U_{a_j}\cap V_{a_j}=\varnothing$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
For (a), step [](#point-away-from-compact){.pf-ref} shows that $X\setminus K$ is open, so $K$ is closed, and step [](#indiscrete-counterexample){.pf-ref} is the example showing the Hausdorff hypothesis is needed.
For (b), step [](#separate-compact-closed-sets){.pf-ref} separates disjoint closed sets, and points are closed in a Hausdorff space, so $X$ is normal.
:::
