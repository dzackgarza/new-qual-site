::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained from the 2-sphere $S^2$ by identifying the north and south poles (i.e., by identifying two diametrically opposite points).

(a) Show that $X$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^2$.

(b) Describe all connected covering spaces of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $N,S$ be the poles, and give $S^2$ the CW structure with $0$-cells $N,S$, $1$-cells $e,f$ the two semicircles of a meridian circle from $N$ to $S$, and $2$-cells the two complementary hemispheres.

<1>1. $X$ is a CW complex with one $0$-cell, two loops $e,f$, and two $2$-cells attached along $ef^{-1}$ and $fe^{-1}$.

::: {.proof}
The quotient identifies only the two $0$-cells.
Each hemisphere boundary traverses one semicircle from $N$ to $S$ and the other in reverse, and the two hemispheres induce opposite orientations on the meridian circle.
:::

<1>2. $X\simeq S^1\vee S^2$, and $\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ$.

::: {.proof}
Change the free basis of $\pi_1$ of the $1$-skeleton from $e,f$ to $a=e$ and $b=ef^{-1}$, realized by a homotopy equivalence of the $1$-skeleton that extends over the $2$-cells.
In this basis one $2$-cell is attached along $b$ and the other along $b^{-1}$.
The $1$-cell $b$ together with the first $2$-cell is a disk; collapsing it is a homotopy equivalence.
What remains is the circle $a$ and a $2$-cell with constant attaching map, that is, $S^1\vee S^2$.
By the Seifert--van Kampen theorem, $\pi_1(S^1\vee S^2)\cong\pi_1(S^1)*\pi_1(S^2)\cong\ZZ$.
:::

<1>3. Connected covering spaces of $X$, up to isomorphism, correspond to the subgroups $n\ZZ$ ($n\ge1$) and $0$ of $\ZZ$.

::: {.proof}
$X$ is a connected CW complex, hence path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected, so the classification of covering spaces applies.
$\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ$ is abelian, so conjugacy classes of subgroups are subgroups, and the subgroups of $\ZZ$ are $n\ZZ$ for $n\ge1$ and $0$.
:::

<1>4. For $n\ge1$, the cover corresponding to $n\ZZ$ is a necklace of $n$ spheres: copies $S^2_1,\ldots,S^2_n$ of $S^2$ with the south pole of $S^2_i$ identified with the north pole of $S^2_{i+1}$, indices mod $n$; the covering map sends each $S^2_i$ to $X$ by the quotient map $S^2\to X$.

::: {.proof}
Over the complement of the identified pole, the map is $n$ disjoint copies of the homeomorphism $S^2\setminus\{N,S\}\to X\setminus\{[N]\}$.
A small neighborhood of $[N]$ in $X$ is two open disks joined at their centers; its preimage is $n$ disjoint such pairs, one at each junction of the necklace, each mapped homeomorphically.
Hence the map is an $n$-sheeted connected covering.
Its fundamental group is infinite cyclic, generated by the loop that runs once around the necklace, which maps to $n$ times the generator of $\pi_1(X)$ given by a path in one sphere from $N$ to $S$; so its induced subgroup is $n\ZZ$.
:::

<1>5. The cover corresponding to $0$ is the universal cover: an infinite chain of spheres $S^2_i$, $i\in\ZZ$, with the south pole of $S^2_i$ identified with the north pole of $S^2_{i+1}$.

::: {.proof}
The local argument of step <1>4 applies unchanged, so this is a covering of $X$.
Every loop in the chain is compact, so it lies in a finite subchain of spheres, each meeting the next in one point; such a subchain is simply connected by van Kampen.
Hence the chain is simply connected.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves (a).
For (b), by step <1>3 every connected covering of $X$ is isomorphic to exactly one of the necklaces of step <1>4 or the chain of step <1>5.
:::
