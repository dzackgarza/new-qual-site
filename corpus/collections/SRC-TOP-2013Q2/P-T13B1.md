::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained from the 2-sphere $S^2$ by identifying the north and south poles (i.e., by identifying two diametrically opposite points).

(a) Show that $X$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^2$.

(b) Describe all connected covering spaces of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Give $S^2$ a CW structure with two $0$-cells $N,S$, two $1$-cells $e,f$ joining $N$ to $S$, and two $2$-cells attached along the circle $e\cup f$.
::: {.proof}
Take $e$ and $f$ to be the two semicircles of an equator through $N$ and $S$, and take the complementary hemispheres as the two $2$-cells.
:::

<1>2. After identifying $N\sim S$, the quotient $X$ has one $0$-cell, two loop $1$-cells $e,f$, and two $2$-cells whose attaching words are $ef^{-1}$ and $fe^{-1}$.
::: {.proof}
The quotient only identifies the two vertices. Each hemisphere boundary traverses one semicircle from $N$ to $S$ and the other in the reverse direction; the two hemispheres have opposite orientations.
:::

<1>3. The CW complex in <1>2 is homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^2$.
::: {.proof}
Change the free basis of the $1$-skeleton from $e,f$ to $a=e$ and $b=ef^{-1}$. In this basis one $2$-cell is attached along $b$ and the other along $b^{-1}$. The subcomplex consisting of the $1$-cell $b$ together with one of these $2$-cells is a disk attached along its boundary and is contractible; collapsing it is an elementary CW collapse up to homotopy. The remaining $1$-cell $a$ is a circle, while the remaining $2$-cell now has constant attaching map, so it contributes an $S^2$ wedged at the unique vertex. Hence $X\simeq S^1\vee S^2$.
:::

<1>4. Consequently $\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
Homotopy equivalent spaces have isomorphic fundamental groups, and $\pi_1(S^1\vee S^2)\cong\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z$ by van Kampen.
:::

<1>5. Connected covering spaces of $X$ are classified, up to isomorphism of coverings, by subgroups of $\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
The space $X$ is a connected CW complex, hence path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected. The standard classification theorem for connected covering spaces therefore applies. Since $\mathbb Z$ is abelian, conjugacy classes of subgroups are just subgroups.
:::

<1>6. The subgroups are $n\mathbb Z$ for $n=1,2,\dots$, together with the trivial subgroup $0$.
::: {.proof}
These are exactly the subgroups of the infinite cyclic group.
:::

<1>7. For $n\ge1$, the cover corresponding to $n\ZZ$ is a necklace of $n$ spheres: copies $S^2_1,\ldots,S^2_n$ of $S^2$ with the south pole of $S^2_i$ identified with the north pole of $S^2_{i+1}$, indices mod $n$; the covering map sends each $S^2_i$ to $X$ by the quotient map $S^2\to X$.
::: {.proof}
Over the complement of the identified pole, the map is $n$ disjoint copies of the homeomorphism $S^2\setminus\{N,S\}\to X\setminus\{[N]\}$.
A small neighborhood of $[N]$ in $X$ is two open disks joined at their centers; its preimage is $n$ disjoint such pairs, one at each junction of the necklace, each mapped homeomorphically.
Hence the map is an $n$-sheeted connected covering.
Its $\pi_1$ is infinite cyclic, generated by the loop that runs once around the necklace, which maps to $n$ times the generator of $\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ$ (the loop through one sphere from $N$ to $S$); so its induced subgroup is $n\ZZ$.
:::

<1>8. The cover corresponding to the trivial subgroup is the universal cover: an infinite chain of spheres $S^2_i$, $i\in\ZZ$, with the south pole of $S^2_i$ identified with the north pole of $S^2_{i+1}$.
::: {.proof}
The local argument of <1>7 applies unchanged, so this is a covering of $X$.
Every loop in the chain is compact, so it lies in a finite subchain of spheres, each meeting the next in one point; such a subchain is simply connected by van Kampen.
Hence the chain is simply connected, and it is the universal cover.
:::

<1>9. These are all connected covering spaces of $X$ up to covering isomorphism.
::: {.proof}
By <1>5--<1>6, every connected cover corresponds to exactly one of the listed subgroups.
:::
