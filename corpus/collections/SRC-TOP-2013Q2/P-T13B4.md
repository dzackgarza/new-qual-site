::: {.problem}
(a) Describe how to construct a cell structure on the 2-sphere $S^2$ consisting of one 0-cell, one 1-cell and two 2-cells, and explain how to use this cell structure to calculate the simplicial homology groups of $S^2$.

(b) Explain how a long exact sequence may be used to calculate all of the (singular) homology groups $H_i(S^n)$ of the $n$-sphere $S^n$ (and calculate these groups for all $i$ and $n$).
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For part (a), take the equator $S^1\subset S^2$ as the $1$-skeleton, choose one point $v$ on it as the unique $0$-cell, let $e=S^1\setminus\{v\}$ be the unique open $1$-cell, and take the northern and southern open hemispheres as the two $2$-cells.
::: {.proof}
The closure of the $1$-cell is the equatorial circle, attached to the single vertex at both ends. Each open hemisphere is an open disk whose boundary is the equator, so this is a CW structure with one $0$-cell, one $1$-cell, and two $2$-cells. The resulting cellular chain complex computes the singular homology of $S^2$; any triangulation compatible with this decomposition gives the same homology groups.
:::

<1>2. With orientations chosen so that the two hemisphere boundaries induce opposite orientations on the equator, the cellular chain complex is
$$
0\longrightarrow \mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow 0,
$$
where
$$
\partial_2(a,b)=a-b,
\qquad
\partial_1=0.
$$
::: {.proof}
There is one $0$-cell and one loop $1$-cell, so $C_0\cong C_1\cong\mathbb Z$ and $\partial_1=0$. Each $2$-cell attaches once around the equatorial $1$-cell; the two induced boundary orientations are opposite, so their incidence numbers are $+1$ and $-1$.
:::

<1>3. Hence
$$
H_2(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad H_1(S^2)=0,\qquad H_0(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
::: {.proof}
The kernel of $(a,b)\mapsto a-b$ is generated by $(1,1)$, so $H_2\cong\mathbb Z$. The map $\partial_2$ is surjective, hence $H_1=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2=0$. Finally $H_0=C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>4. For part (b), write
$$
S^n=U\cup V
$$
where $U$ and $V$ are open neighborhoods of the northern and southern closed hemispheres chosen so that each is contractible and $U\cap V\simeq S^{n-1}$.
::: {.proof}
For example, take $U=S^n\setminus\{\text{south pole}\}$ and $V=S^n\setminus\{\text{north pole}\}$. Each is homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^n$, while their intersection deformation-retracts onto the equator $S^{n-1}$.
:::

<1>5. The reduced Mayer--Vietoris long exact sequence gives natural isomorphisms
$$
\widetilde H_i(S^n)\cong \widetilde H_{i-1}(S^{n-1})
$$
for all $i$ and all $n\ge1$.
::: {.proof}
The relevant segment is
$$
\widetilde H_i(U)\oplus\widetilde H_i(V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_i(S^n)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_{i-1}(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_{i-1}(U)\oplus\widetilde H_{i-1}(V).
$$
Since $U$ and $V$ are contractible, their reduced homology groups vanish. Exactness therefore identifies the middle map with an isomorphism, and $U\cap V\simeq S^{n-1}$.
:::

<1>6. The base case is
$$
\widetilde H_i(S^0)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
0,&i\neq0.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
The space $S^0$ consists of two points, so $H_0(S^0)\cong\mathbb Z^2$ and its reduced $H_0$ is the kernel of the augmentation $\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z$, which is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$; all higher homology groups vanish.
:::

<1>7. Inductively, for every $n\ge1$,
$$
\widetilde H_i(S^n)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=n,\\
0,&i\neq n.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
Apply the degree-shifting isomorphism in <1>5 repeatedly until reaching the base case <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Therefore the unreduced singular homology groups are
$$
H_i(S^n)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,n,\quad n\ge1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
$$
and for $n=0$, $H_0(S^0)\cong\mathbb Z^2$ with all higher groups zero.
::: {.proof}
For $n\ge1$, the sphere is path-connected, so $H_0(S^n)\cong\mathbb Z$, while reduced and unreduced homology agree in positive degrees. The $n=0$ case is <1>6 before reduction.
:::
