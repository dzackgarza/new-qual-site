::: {.problem}
(a) Describe how to construct a cell structure on the 2-sphere $S^2$ consisting of one 0-cell, one 1-cell and two 2-cells, and explain how to use this cell structure to calculate the simplicial homology groups of $S^2$.

(b) Explain how a long exact sequence may be used to calculate all of the (singular) homology groups $H_i(S^n)$ of the $n$-sphere $S^n$ (and calculate these groups for all $i$ and $n$).
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $S^2$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$ on the equator, one $1$-cell $e$ equal to the equator minus $v$, and two $2$-cells $u,w$, the open northern and southern hemispheres. With $u,w$ oriented so that their boundaries induce opposite orientations on the equator, the cellular chain complex is
$$0\to\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{\partial_2}\ZZ\xrightarrow{\partial_1}\ZZ\to0,\qquad\partial_2(a,b)=a-b,\qquad\partial_1=0.$$

::: {.proof}
The closure of $e$ is the equator, attached to $v$ at both ends, so $\partial_1=0$.
Each closed hemisphere is a disk whose boundary maps homeomorphically onto the equator, so each $2$-cell has incidence number $\pm1$ with $e$, with opposite signs for the chosen orientations.
:::

<1>2. $H_0(S^2)\cong\ZZ$, $H_1(S^2)=0$, and $H_2(S^2)\cong\ZZ$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1: $H_0=C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1\cong\ZZ$; $\partial_2$ is surjective, so $H_1=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2=0$; and $H_2=\ker\partial_2=\ZZ(1,1)\cong\ZZ$.
Cellular homology agrees with singular homology, which agrees with the simplicial homology of any $\Delta$-complex structure on $S^2$.
:::

<1>3. For $n\ge1$, $\widetilde H_i(S^n)\cong\widetilde H_{i-1}(S^{n-1})$ for all $i$.

::: {.proof}
Let $U=S^n\setminus\{\text{south pole}\}$ and $V=S^n\setminus\{\text{north pole}\}$.
Each is homeomorphic to $\RR^n$, hence contractible, and $U\cap V$ deformation retracts onto the equator $S^{n-1}$.
The reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence contains the exact segment
$$\widetilde H_i(U)\oplus\widetilde H_i(V)\to\widetilde H_i(S^n)\to\widetilde H_{i-1}(U\cap V)\to\widetilde H_{i-1}(U)\oplus\widetilde H_{i-1}(V),$$
whose outer terms vanish, so the connecting map $\widetilde H_i(S^n)\to\widetilde H_{i-1}(U\cap V)\cong\widetilde H_{i-1}(S^{n-1})$ is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>4. For every $n\ge0$, $\widetilde H_i(S^n)\cong\ZZ$ if $i=n$ and $\widetilde H_i(S^n)=0$ otherwise.

::: {.proof}
$S^0$ is two points, so $H_0(S^0)\cong\ZZ^2$, $\widetilde H_0(S^0)$ is the kernel $\ZZ$ of the augmentation $\ZZ^2\to\ZZ$, and all higher groups vanish.
Induction on $n$ using step <1>3 gives the claim.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 answer (a).
For (b), step <1>4, together with $H_0(S^n)\cong\ZZ$ for the path-connected spaces $S^n$ with $n\ge1$ and the equality of reduced and unreduced homology in positive degrees, gives
$$H_i(S^n)\cong\begin{cases}\ZZ,&i=0\text{ or }i=n,\ n\ge1,\\\ZZ^2,&i=n=0,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$
:::
