::: {.problem}
Let $Y$ be the standard 3-simplex $\Delta^3$ with a total ordering on its four vertices.
Let $X$ be the $\Delta$-complex obtained from $Y$ by identifying, for each $k\le 3$, all of its $k$-dimensional faces such that the identifications respect the vertex ordering.
Thus $X$ has a single $k$-simplex for each $k\le 3$.
Compute the simplicial homology groups of the $\Delta$-complex $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The chain groups of the $\Delta$-complex $X$ are free abelian groups of rank 1 in dimensions $0 \le k \le 3$:
\[
C_3(X) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_3,\quad C_2(X) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_2,\quad C_1(X) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_1,\quad C_0(X) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_0,
\]
and $C_k(X) = 0$ for $k \ge 4$ or $k < 0$.
Proof: $X$ has exactly one $k$-simplex $\sigma_k$ for each $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$.

<1>2. Compute the boundary map $d_1: C_1(X) \to C_0(X)$.
<2>1. Under the identification respecting vertex order, the single 1-simplex $\sigma_1$ corresponds to an edge $[v_i, v_j]$ with $i < j$.
Proof: definition of the quotient $\Delta$-complex.
<2>2. $d_1(\sigma_1) = v_j - v_i = \sigma_0 - \sigma_0 = 0$.
Proof: all 0-simplices $v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3$ are identified to $\sigma_0$.
<2>3. Hence $d_1 = 0$.
Proof: <2>2.

<1>3. Compute the boundary map $d_2: C_2(X) \to C_1(X)$.
<2>1. The single 2-simplex $\sigma_2$ corresponds to any face $[v_i, v_j, v_k]$ with $i < j < k$.
Proof: definition of the quotient $\Delta$-complex.
<2>2. The simplicial boundary is $d_2([v_i, v_j, v_k]) = [v_j, v_k] - [v_i, v_k] + [v_i, v_j]$.
Proof: standard simplicial boundary formula $\sum_{r=0}^2 (-1)^r [v_0, \dots, \hat{v}_r, \dots, v_2]$.
<2>3. Since each edge $[v_a, v_b]$ with $a < b$ maps to $\sigma_1$:
\[
d_2(\sigma_2) = \sigma_1 - \sigma_1 + \sigma_1 = \sigma_1.
\]
Proof: all edges are identified to $\sigma_1$ respecting vertex ordering.
<2>4. Hence $d_2: \mathbb{Z}\sigma_2 \to \mathbb{Z}\sigma_1$ is an isomorphism (multiplication by 1).
Proof: <2>3.

<1>4. Compute the boundary map $d_3: C_3(X) \to C_2(X)$.
<2>1. The 3-simplex $\sigma_3$ is the single 3-simplex $[v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3]$.
Proof: definition of $X$.
<2>2. The simplicial boundary is:
\[
d_3(\sigma_3) = [v_1, v_2, v_3] - [v_0, v_2, v_3] + [v_0, v_1, v_3] - [v_0, v_1, v_2].
\]
Proof: standard simplicial boundary formula $\sum_{r=0}^3 (-1)^r [v_0, \dots, \hat{v}_r, \dots, v_3]$.
<2>3. Since each of the four 2-faces is identified to $\sigma_2$ preserving vertex order:
\[
d_3(\sigma_3) = \sigma_2 - \sigma_2 + \sigma_2 - \sigma_2 = 0.
\]
Proof: alternating sum of 4 copies of $\sigma_2$.
<2>4. Hence $d_3 = 0$.
Proof: <2>3.

<1>5. Compute the homology groups $H_k(X) = \ker(d_k)/\operatorname{im}(d_{k+1})$:
<2>1. $H_0(X) = \ker(d_0)/\operatorname{im}(d_1) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_0 / 0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
Proof: $\ker(d_0) = C_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $d_1 = 0$ by <1>2.
<2>2. $H_1(X) = \ker(d_1)/\operatorname{im}(d_2) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_1 / \mathbb{Z}\sigma_1 \cong 0$.
Proof: $\ker(d_1) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_1$ by <1>2 and $\operatorname{im}(d_2) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_1$ by <1>3.
<2>3. $H_2(X) = \ker(d_2)/\operatorname{im}(d_3) = 0 / 0 \cong 0$.
Proof: $\ker(d_2) = 0$ by <1>3 and $\operatorname{im}(d_3) = 0$ by <1>4.
<2>4. $H_3(X) = \ker(d_3)/\operatorname{im}(d_4) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_3 / 0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
Proof: $\ker(d_3) = \mathbb{Z}\sigma_3$ by <1>4 and $C_4(X) = 0$.
<2>5. $H_k(X) = 0$ for all $k \ge 4$.
Proof: $C_k(X) = 0$ for $k \ge 4$.

<1>6. Conclusion:
\[
H_k(X) \cong \begin{cases}
\mathbb{Z} & k = 0, 3, \\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>5.
