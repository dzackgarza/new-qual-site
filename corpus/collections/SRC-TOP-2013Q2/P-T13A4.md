::: {.problem}
A space is locally connected if for each point $x\in X$ and every neighborhood $U$ of $x$, there is a connected neighborhood $V$ of $x$ contained in $U$.

(a) Prove that $X$ is locally connected if and only if for every open set $U$ of $X$, each connected component of $U$ is open in $X$.

(b) Prove that if $p:X\to Y$ is a quotient map and $X$ is locally connected, then $Y$ is locally connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Assume first that $X$ is locally connected, let $U\subseteq X$ be open, and let $C$ be a connected component of $U$.
::: {.proof}
We prove that $C$ is open in $X$.
:::

<1>2. For every $x\in C$, there exists a connected neighborhood $N_x$ of $x$ with $N_x\subseteq U$.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly local connectedness applied to the open neighborhood $U$ of $x$.
:::

<1>3. Each $N_x$ lies in $C$.
::: {.proof}
The set $N_x$ is connected, contained in $U$, and meets $C$ at $x$. Hence $C\cup N_x$ is connected. By maximality of the connected component $C$ in $U$, we have $N_x\subseteq C$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $C$ is open.
::: {.proof}
Since $N_x$ is a neighborhood of $x$, it contains an open set $O_x$ with $x\in O_x\subseteq N_x\subseteq C$. Thus every point of $C$ is interior to $C$, so $C$ is open.
:::

<1>5. Conversely, assume that every connected component of every open subset of $X$ is open.
::: {.proof}
We prove local connectedness directly.
:::

<1>6. Let $x\in X$ and let $U$ be a neighborhood of $x$. Then there is an open set $O$ with $x\in O\subseteq U$.
::: {.proof}
This is the definition of neighborhood.
:::

<1>7. Let $C$ be the connected component of $O$ containing $x$. Then $C$ is a connected open neighborhood of $x$ contained in $U$.
::: {.proof}
By hypothesis $C$ is open in $X$; by definition it is connected, and $x\in C\subseteq O\subseteq U$.
:::

<1>8. Hence $X$ is locally connected.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the local connectedness condition.
:::

<1>9. Now let $p:X\to Y$ be a quotient map with $X$ locally connected.
::: {.proof}
We prove that every component of every open subset of $Y$ is open and invoke part (a).
:::

<1>10. Let $U\subseteq Y$ be open and let $C$ be a connected component of $U$.
::: {.proof}
Since $p$ is quotient, $p^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$.
:::

<1>11. The components of $p^{-1}(U)$ are open in $X$.
::: {.proof}
By part (a), because $X$ is locally connected.
:::

<1>12. Let $W$ be the union of all components $D$ of $p^{-1}(U)$ for which $p(D)\subseteq C$. Then $W$ is open and saturated with respect to $p$ over $U$.
::: {.proof}
It is a union of open components, hence open. If $x\in W$ and $p(x')=p(x)$ with $x'\in p^{-1}(U)$, let $D'$ be the component containing $x'$. The point $p(x)=p(x')$ lies in both $p(D)$ and $p(D')$. Since continuous images of connected sets are connected, $p(D)$ and $p(D')$ are connected subsets of $U$ meeting $C$; hence both lie in the component $C$. Thus $D'$ is also included in $W$, proving saturation.
:::

<1>13. We have $p(W)=C$.
::: {.proof}
Certainly $p(W)\subseteq C$. Conversely, if $y\in C$, choose $x\in p^{-1}(y)$ and let $D$ be the component of $p^{-1}(U)$ containing $x$. Then $p(D)$ is connected and contains $y\in C$, so $p(D)\subseteq C$. Hence $D\subseteq W$ and $y\in p(W)$.
:::

<1>14. The component $C$ is open in $Y$.
::: {.proof}
Because $W$ is saturated, $p^{-1}(p(W))=W$ is open. Since $p$ is a quotient map, $p(W)=C$ is open.
:::

<1>15. Therefore $Y$ is locally connected.
::: {.proof}
By <1>10--<1>14, every component of every open subset of $Y$ is open; apply part (a).
:::
:::
