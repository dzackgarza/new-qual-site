::: problem
Let $K$ be a field with $729$ elements.
Let $\mathbb{F}_3$ be the prime field of $K$ and set $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{F}_3)$.
Consider the action of $G$ on $K$ given by $\sigma \cdot u = \sigma(u)$.
Describe the orbits of this action, calculate their sizes, and calculate how many orbits there are of each size.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. One has
\[
K\cong\mathbb F_{3^6},
\qquad
G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb F_3)
=\langle\operatorname{Fr}\rangle\cong C_6,
\]
where \(\operatorname{Fr}(x)=x^3\).
::: {.proof}
Since \(729=3^6\), there is, up to unique isomorphism, a single field with \(729\) elements. The Galois group of \(\mathbb F_{3^6}/\mathbb F_3\) is cyclic of order \(6\), generated by Frobenius.
:::

<1>2. For \(u\in K\), its orbit is
\[
\mathcal O(u)=\{u,u^3,u^{3^2},\dots\},
\]
and its orbit size is
\[
|\mathcal O(u)|=[\mathbb F_3(u):\mathbb F_3].
\]
::: {.proof}
The orbit size is the least positive integer \(d\) such that \(\operatorname{Fr}^d(u)=u\), i.e. \(u^{3^d}=u\). This is exactly the least \(d\) for which \(u\in\mathbb F_{3^d}\), hence the degree of the finite field \(\mathbb F_3(u)\) over \(\mathbb F_3\).
:::

<1>3. The possible orbit sizes are \(1,2,3,6\).
::: {.proof}
The field \(\mathbb F_3(u)\) is an intermediate field of \(\mathbb F_{3^6}/\mathbb F_3\). Intermediate finite fields are precisely \(\mathbb F_{3^d}\) for divisors \(d\mid6\), so \(d\in\{1,2,3,6\}\).
:::

<1>4. There are exactly \(3\) elements of orbit size \(1\), hence \(3\) such orbits.
::: {.proof}
Orbit size \(1\) means \(u\in\mathbb F_3\). There are \(3\) such elements, each fixed by all of \(G\).
:::

<1>5. There are exactly
\[
3^2-3=6
\]
elements of orbit size \(2\), hence \(6/2=3\) such orbits.
::: {.proof}
The elements of degree dividing \(2\) are exactly those in \(\mathbb F_9\). Removing the \(3\) elements of \(\mathbb F_3\) leaves \(6\) elements of exact degree \(2\). Each orbit has size \(2\) by <1>2.
:::

<1>6. There are exactly
\[
3^3-3=24
\]
elements of orbit size \(3\), hence \(24/3=8\) such orbits.
::: {.proof}
The only proper subfield of \(\mathbb F_{27}\) containing \(\mathbb F_3\) is \(\mathbb F_3\), because the only proper divisor of \(3\) is \(1\). Thus the elements of exact degree \(3\) are \(\mathbb F_{27}\setminus\mathbb F_3\), of cardinality \(27-3=24\).
:::

<1>7. There are exactly
\[
729-(9+27-3)=696
\]
elements of orbit size \(6\), hence \(696/6=116\) such orbits.
::: {.proof}
An element has degree strictly less than \(6\) exactly when it lies in one of the proper intermediate fields \(\mathbb F_9\) or \(\mathbb F_{27}\). Their intersection is \(\mathbb F_3\), so
\[
|\mathbb F_9\cup\mathbb F_{27}|=9+27-3=33.
\]
All remaining \(729-33=696\) elements have exact degree \(6\), and each corresponding orbit has size \(6\).
:::

<1>8. Thus the orbit distribution is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\text{orbit size}&1&2&3&6\\\hline
\text{number of orbits}&3&3&8&116.
\end{array}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>4--<1>7. As a check, the accounted-for elements total
\[
3\cdot1+3\cdot2+8\cdot3+116\cdot6=729.
\]
:::
:::
