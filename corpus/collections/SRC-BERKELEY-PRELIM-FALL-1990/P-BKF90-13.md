::: {.problem}
Suppose $f$ is analytic on the open upper half-plane, satisfies
\[
|f(z)|\le1
\]
for all $z$, and $f(i)=0$.
How large can $|f(2i)|$ be?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\phi(z)\coloneqq\frac{z-i}{z+i}.
$$

<1>1. The map $\phi$ is a biholomorphism from the upper half-plane onto $\DD$, and
$$
\phi(i)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The Cayley transform $\phi$ maps the real axis to the unit circle and the upper half-plane to the unit disk. Direct substitution gives $\phi(i)=0$.
:::

<1>2. The function
$$
h\coloneqq f\circ\phi^{-1}:\DD\to\overline{\DD}
$$
satisfies $h(0)=0$ and therefore
$$
\abs{h(w)}\le\abs w
$$
for every $w\in\DD$.

::: {.proof}
The function $h$ is holomorphic and $h(0)=f(i)=0$. Since $\abs h\le1$, Schwarz's lemma gives the displayed inequality.
:::

<1>3. Every admissible $f$ satisfies
$$
\abs{f(2i)}\le\frac13.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
\abs{f(2i)}
=
\abs{h(\phi(2i))}
\le
\abs{\phi(2i)}.
$$
Now
$$
\phi(2i)=\frac{2i-i}{2i+i}=\frac13,
$$
so the asserted bound follows.
:::

<1>4. The bound $1/3$ is attained.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
f(z)=\phi(z)=\frac{z-i}{z+i}.
$$
By step <1>1, this function is holomorphic on the upper half-plane, satisfies $\abs{f(z)}<1$ there, and has $f(i)=0$. Moreover
$$
\abs{f(2i)}=\frac13.
$$
:::

<1>5. Hence the largest possible value is
$$
\boxed{\frac13}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives the upper bound, and step <1>4 shows that it is sharp.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 answers the extremal question.
:::
