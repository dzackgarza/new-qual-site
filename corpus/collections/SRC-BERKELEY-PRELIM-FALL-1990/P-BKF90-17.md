::: {.problem}
Let
\[
G=\{a\in\mathbb R:a>0,\ a\ne1\}
\]
and define
\[
a*b=a^{\log b}.
\]
Does $G$ form a group under this operation?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The map
$$
\ell:G\longrightarrow\RR^\times,
\qquad
\ell(a)=\log a,
$$
is a bijection.

::: pf-proof

The logarithm is a bijection from $(0,\infty)$ to $\RR$. The excluded element $1$ is exactly the unique positive real number with logarithm $0$. Therefore restricting the logarithm gives a bijection
$$
(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\}
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\RR\setminus\{0\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For all $a,b\in G$,
$$
\ell(a*b)=\ell(a)\ell(b).
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $a>0$,
$$
\ell(a*b)
=
\log\left(a^{\log b}\right)
=
(\log b)(\log a)
=
\ell(a)\ell(b).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The operation $*$ makes $G$ a group.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $\ell$ transports $*$ to ordinary multiplication on the group $\RR^\times$. Hence closure, associativity, the identity, and inverses all follow from the corresponding properties in $\RR^\times$.

Explicitly, the identity is
$$
e,
$$
because $\log e=1$, and the inverse of $a\in G$ is
$$
\exp\left(\frac{1}{\log a}\right),
$$
whose logarithm is the multiplicative inverse of $\log a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore the answer is
$$
\boxed{\text{yes}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves that all group axioms hold.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} answers the question.
