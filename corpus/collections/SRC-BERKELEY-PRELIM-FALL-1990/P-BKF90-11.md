::: {.problem}
Suppose $f$ maps a compact interval $I$ into itself and
\[
|f(x)-f(y)|<|x-y|
\]
whenever $x\ne y$.
Must there exist a constant $M<1$ such that
\[
|f(x)-f(y)|\le M|x-y|
\]
for all $x,y\in I$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Consider
$$
I=[0,1],
\qquad
f(x)=x-\frac{x^2}{2}.
$$
Then $f$ maps $I$ into itself.

::: pf-proof

For $0\le x\le1$,
$$
f(x)=x\left(1-\frac x2\right)\ge0.
$$
Moreover $f'(x)=1-x\ge0$, so $f$ is increasing on $[0,1]$, and therefore
$$
0=f(0)\le f(x)\le f(1)=\frac12\le1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $x\ne y$ in $I$, then
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}<\abs{x-y}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Assume $0\le x<y\le1$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(y)-f(x)
&=(y-x)-\frac{y^2-x^2}{2}\\
&=(y-x)\left(1-\frac{x+y}{2}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $0<x+y\le2$, the factor satisfies
$$
0\le1-\frac{x+y}{2}<1.
$$
Hence
$$
0\le f(y)-f(x)<y-x.
$$
Taking absolute values gives the claim; the case $y<x$ follows by symmetry.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

No constant $M<1$ satisfies
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le M\abs{x-y}
$$
for all $x,y\in I$.

::: pf-proof

For $y>0$, taking $x=0$ gives
$$
\frac{\abs{f(y)-f(0)}}{\abs{y-0}}
=
1-\frac y2.
$$
As $y\downarrow0$, this ratio tends to $1$. Thus for every $M<1$, one may choose $y>0$ sufficiently small that
$$
1-\frac y2>M,
$$
which violates the proposed uniform bound.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore the answer is
$$
\boxed{\text{no}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref} verify all hypotheses, while step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that the proposed conclusion fails.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} answers the question.

:::
