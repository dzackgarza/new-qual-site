::: {.problem}
Let $n$ be a positive integer, and let $P_{2n+1}$ be the vector space of real polynomials of degree at most $2n+1$.
Prove that there exist unique real numbers $c_1,\ldots,c_n$ such that for every $p\in P_{2n+1}$,
\[
\int_{-1}^1p(x)\,dx
=2p(0)+\sum_{k=1}^n c_k\bigl(p(k)+p(-k)-2p(0)\bigr).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every odd polynomial $p\in P_{2n+1}$, the proposed identity holds for every choice of $c_1,\ldots,c_n$.

::: pf-proof

If $p$ is odd, then
$$
\int_{-1}^1p(x)\,dx=0,
\qquad
p(0)=0,
$$
and for every $k$,
$$
p(k)+p(-k)=0.
$$
Thus both sides of the proposed identity are zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For the constant polynomial $p=1$, the proposed identity also holds for every choice of $c_1,\ldots,c_n$.

::: pf-proof

The left-hand side is $2$. On the right-hand side,
$$
2p(0)=2
$$
and every summand is
$$
c_k(1+1-2)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For $j=1,\ldots,n$, the identity for the monomial $p(x)=x^{2j}$ is equivalent to
$$
\sum_{k=1}^n c_k k^{2j}=\frac{1}{2j+1}.
$$

::: pf-proof

For $p(x)=x^{2j}$,
$$
\int_{-1}^1x^{2j}\,dx=\frac{2}{2j+1},
\qquad
p(0)=0,
$$
and
$$
p(k)+p(-k)=2k^{2j}.
$$
Dividing the resulting identity by $2$ gives the displayed equation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The system
$$
\sum_{k=1}^n c_k k^{2j}=\frac{1}{2j+1},
\qquad 1\le j\le n,
$$
has a unique solution $(c_1,\ldots,c_n)\in\RR^n$.

::: pf-proof

Its coefficient matrix is
$$
M=(k^{2j})_{1\le j,k\le n}.
$$
Factoring $k^2$ from the $k$th column gives
$$
\det M
=
\left(\prod_{k=1}^n k^2\right)
\det\bigl((k^2)^{j-1}\bigr)_{1\le j,k\le n}.
$$
The remaining determinant is the Vandermonde determinant in the pairwise distinct numbers
$$
1^2,2^2,\ldots,n^2,
$$
so
$$
\det M
=
\left(\prod_{k=1}^n k^2\right)
\prod_{1\le r<s\le n}(s^2-r^2)
\ne0.
$$
Hence $M$ is invertible, and the system has exactly one solution.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The coefficients from step [](#s4){.pf-ref} satisfy the proposed identity for every $p\in P_{2n+1}$.

::: pf-proof

The vector space $P_{2n+1}$ has basis
$$
1,x,x^2,\ldots,x^{2n+1}.
$$
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the identity holds for $1$. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, it holds for every odd monomial. By steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref}, it holds for each even monomial
$$
x^2,x^4,\ldots,x^{2n}.
$$
Both sides of the proposed identity depend linearly on $p$, so the identity holds on the entire span, namely all of $P_{2n+1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

These coefficients are unique.

::: pf-proof

Any coefficients satisfying the identity for every $p\in P_{2n+1}$ must in particular satisfy it for $p(x)=x^{2j}$, $1\le j\le n$. By step [](#s3){.pf-ref} they must solve the linear system of step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, whose solution is unique.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

Therefore there exist unique real numbers $c_1,\ldots,c_n$ with the required property.

::: pf-proof

Existence is step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, and uniqueness is step [](#s6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s7){.pf-ref} is exactly the assertion.

:::
