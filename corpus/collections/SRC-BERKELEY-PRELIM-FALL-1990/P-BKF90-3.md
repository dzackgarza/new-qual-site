::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a ring with identity, and let $I$ be the left ideal generated by
\[
\{ab-ba:a,b\in R\}.
\]
Prove that $I$ is a two-sided ideal.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
[a,b]\coloneqq ab-ba.
$$

<1>1. For all $a,b,r\in R$,
$$
[a,b]r=[a,br]-b[a,r].
$$

::: {.proof}
Expanding the right-hand side gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
[a,br]-b[a,r]
&=(abr-bra)-b(ar-ra)\\
&=abr-bra-bar+bra\\
&=abr-bar\\
&=[a,b]r.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. If $a,b,r\in R$, then $[a,b]r\in I$.

::: {.proof}
Both $[a,br]$ and $[a,r]$ are commutators, so they belong to the generating set of $I$ and therefore to $I$. Since $I$ is a left ideal, $b[a,r]\in I$. Step <1>1 then implies
$$
[a,b]r=[a,br]-b[a,r]\in I.
$$
:::

<1>3. For every $x\in I$ and $r\in R$, one has $xr\in I$.

::: {.proof}
Because $I$ is the left ideal generated by the commutators, there are finitely many elements $s_j,a_j,b_j\in R$ such that
$$
x=\sum_j s_j[a_j,b_j].
$$
Hence
$$
xr=\sum_j s_j\bigl([a_j,b_j]r\bigr).
$$
By step <1>2, every factor $[a_j,b_j]r$ lies in $I$. Since $I$ is a left ideal, each term $s_j([a_j,b_j]r)$ lies in $I$, and therefore so does their sum.
:::

<1>4. The ideal $I$ is two-sided.

::: {.proof}
By definition, $I$ is a left ideal. Step <1>3 shows that it is also closed under multiplication on the right by arbitrary elements of $R$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{I\text{ is a two-sided ideal of }R}.
$$
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
