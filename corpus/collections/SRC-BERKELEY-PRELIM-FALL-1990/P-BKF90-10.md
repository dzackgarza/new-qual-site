::: {.problem}
Determine the Jordan canonical form of
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\
0&4&5\\
0&0&4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
A\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\
0&4&5\\
0&0&4
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The characteristic polynomial is
$$
\chi_A(t)=(t-1)(t-4)^2.
$$

::: pf-proof

The matrix $A$ is upper triangular, so its eigenvalues, counted with algebraic multiplicity, are its diagonal entries $1,4,4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The eigenspace for the eigenvalue $4$ has dimension $1$.

::: pf-proof

One has
$$
A-4I=
\begin{pmatrix}
-3&2&3\\
0&0&5\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $(x,y,z)^T\in\ker(A-4I)$, the second row gives $z=0$, and then the first row gives
$$
-3x+2y=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\ker(A-4I)
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}2\\3\\0\end{pmatrix}
\right\},
$$
which is one-dimensional.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The eigenvalue $4$ contributes one Jordan block of size $2$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, the algebraic multiplicity of $4$ is $2$. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, its geometric multiplicity is $1$, which equals the number of Jordan blocks for that eigenvalue. Hence there is exactly one block, and its size must be $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The Jordan canonical form is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&4&1\\
0&0&4
\end{pmatrix}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The simple eigenvalue $1$ contributes a $1\times1$ block, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives the single $2\times2$ Jordan block for $4$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives the requested Jordan canonical form.

:::
