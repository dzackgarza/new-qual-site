::: {.problem}
Prove that
\[
\sqrt2+\sqrt[3]3
\]
is irrational.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
r\coloneqq\sqrt2+\sqrt[3]3
$$
is rational.

::: pf-proof

This is the negation of the desired conclusion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Under this assumption,
$$
(3r^2+2)\sqrt2=r^3+6r-3.
$$

::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\sqrt[3]3=r-\sqrt2.
$$
Cubing both sides gives
$$
3
=
(r-\sqrt2)^3
=
r^3-3r^2\sqrt2+6r-2\sqrt2.
$$
Rearranging yields the displayed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The number $\sqrt2$ is rational.

::: pf-proof

Since $r\in\QQ$ and
$$
3r^2+2>0,
$$
step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\sqrt2
=
\frac{r^3+6r-3}{3r^2+2}
\in\QQ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

This is a contradiction.

::: pf-proof

It is standard that $\sqrt2$ is irrational, contradicting step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\sqrt2+\sqrt[3]3\notin\QQ}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The assumption in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} leads to the contradiction in step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, so it is false.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
