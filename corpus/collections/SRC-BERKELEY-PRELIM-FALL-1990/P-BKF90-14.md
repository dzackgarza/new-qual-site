::: {.problem}
Prove that
\[
\sqrt2+\sqrt[3]3
\]
is irrational.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
r\coloneqq\sqrt2+\sqrt[3]3
$$
is rational.

::: {.proof}
This is the negation of the desired conclusion.
:::

<1>2. Under this assumption,
$$
(3r^2+2)\sqrt2=r^3+6r-3.
$$

::: {.proof}
From step <1>1,
$$
\sqrt[3]3=r-\sqrt2.
$$
Cubing both sides gives
$$
3
=
(r-\sqrt2)^3
=
r^3-3r^2\sqrt2+6r-2\sqrt2.
$$
Rearranging yields the displayed identity.
:::

<1>3. The number $\sqrt2$ is rational.

::: {.proof}
Since $r\in\QQ$ and
$$
3r^2+2>0,
$$
step <1>2 gives
$$
\sqrt2
=
\frac{r^3+6r-3}{3r^2+2}
\in\QQ.
$$
:::

<1>4. This is a contradiction.

::: {.proof}
It is standard that $\sqrt2$ is irrational, contradicting step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\sqrt2+\sqrt[3]3\notin\QQ}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The assumption in step <1>1 leads to the contradiction in step <1>4, so it is false.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
