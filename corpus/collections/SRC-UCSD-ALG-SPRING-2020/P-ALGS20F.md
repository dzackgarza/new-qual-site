::: problem
Let $F$ be a field with algebraic closure $\overline{F}$.
Let $F \subseteq K \subseteq \overline{F}$ and $F \subseteq L \subseteq \overline{F}$, where $K$ and $L$ are fields with $[K:F] < \infty$ and $[L:F] < \infty$.
Prove that the following conditions are equivalent:

(1) $K \otimes_F L$ is a field.

(2) Given any $F$-linearly independent elements $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_m \in K$, then $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_m$ are linearly independent over $L$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let
\[
\mu:K\otimes_F L\longrightarrow KL,
\qquad k\otimes \ell\longmapsto k\ell.
\]
Then \(\mu\) is surjective.
::: {.proof}
Its image is the \(F\)-subalgebra of \(KL\) generated by products \(k\ell\). This image contains \(K\) and \(L\), since \(k=\mu(k\otimes1)\) and \(\ell=\mu(1\otimes\ell)\). It is finite-dimensional over \(F\), because \(K\otimes_F L\) is finite-dimensional over \(F\). As a subring of the field \(KL\), the image is a domain; a finite-dimensional domain over a field is itself a field. Hence the image is a field containing \(K\) and \(L\), so it contains the compositum \(KL\). Therefore it equals \(KL\), and \(\mu\) is surjective.
:::

<1>2. Assume \(K\otimes_F L\) is a field. Then \(\mu\) is injective.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, \(\mu\) is a unital surjective homomorphism from the field \(K\otimes_F L\) to the field \(KL\). Its kernel is a proper ideal because \(\mu(1)=1\), so its kernel is zero.
:::

<1>3. Under the hypothesis of <1>2, every \(F\)-linearly independent family \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m\in K\) is linearly independent over \(L\).
::: {.proof}
Extend \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m\) to an \(F\)-basis of \(K\). Then the corresponding tensors form part of an \(L\)-basis of
\[
K\otimes_F L.
\]
Suppose
\[
\ell_1\alpha_1+\cdots+\ell_m\alpha_m=0
\]
with \(\ell_i\in L\). Then
\[
u=\sum_{i=1}^m\alpha_i\otimes\ell_i
\]
lies in \(\ker\mu\). By <1>2, \(u=0\). Since the \(\alpha_i\otimes1\) are \(L\)-linearly independent in \(K\otimes_F L\), all \(\ell_i=0\). Thus the \(\alpha_i\) remain linearly independent over \(L\).
:::

<1>4. Conversely, assume every \(F\)-linearly independent family in \(K\) remains linearly independent over \(L\). Choose an \(F\)-basis \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d\) of \(K\). Then \(\mu\) is injective.
::: {.proof}
The elements \(\alpha_1\otimes1,\ldots,\alpha_d\otimes1\) form an \(L\)-basis of \(K\otimes_F L\). If
\[
u=\sum_{i=1}^d\alpha_i\otimes\ell_i\in\ker\mu,
\]
then
\[
0=\mu(u)=\sum_{i=1}^d\ell_i\alpha_i.
\]
By hypothesis the \(F\)-basis \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d\) is linearly independent over \(L\), so every \(\ell_i=0\). Hence \(u=0\), and \(\ker\mu=0\).
:::

<1>5. Under the hypothesis of <1>4, \(K\otimes_F L\) is a field.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and <1>4, \(\mu\) is an isomorphism
\[
K\otimes_F L\cong KL.
\]
Since \(KL\) is a field, so is \(K\otimes_F L\).
:::

<1>6. Therefore conditions (1) and (2) are equivalent.
::: {.proof}
The implication (1) \(\Rightarrow\) (2) is <1>2--<1>3, and the converse is <1>4--<1>5.
:::
:::
