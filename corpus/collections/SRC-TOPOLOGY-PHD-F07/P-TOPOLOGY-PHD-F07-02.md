::: {.problem}
Define what it means for $(X,d)$ to be a metric space.
Then $d:X\times X\to\mathbb R$: is $d$ continuous?
Discuss.
If you cannot answer in general do it for $X=\mathbb R$ with the usual topology.
:::

::: remark
The source page prints an extra colon immediately before the arrow in the map notation; the map is rendered here with the conventional $\to$ notation.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A metric on a set $X$ is a function
\[
d:X\times X\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
\]
such that for all $x,y,z\in X$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&d(x,y)=0\iff x=y,\\
&d(x,y)=d(y,x),\\
&d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z).
\end{aligned}
\]
A metric space is a pair $(X,d)$ equipped with the topology generated by its open balls.
::: {.proof}
These are respectively positive definiteness, symmetry, and the triangle inequality, together with the metric topology associated to $d$.
:::

<1>2. For all $x,x',y,y'\in X$,
\[
\bigl|d(x,y)-d(x',y')\bigr|
\le d(x,x')+d(y,y').
\]
::: {.proof}
The triangle inequality gives
\[
d(x,y)
\le d(x,x')+d(x',y')+d(y',y),
\]
so by symmetry,
\[
d(x,y)-d(x',y')
\le d(x,x')+d(y,y').
\]
Interchanging $(x,y)$ with $(x',y')$ gives
\[
d(x',y')-d(x,y)
\le d(x,x')+d(y,y').
\]
Combining these two inequalities yields the claimed absolute-value estimate.
:::

<1>3. The function
\[
d:X\times X\longrightarrow\mathbb R
\]
is continuous for the product topology on $X\times X$.
::: {.proof}
Fix $(x,y)\in X\times X$ and let $\epsilon>0$.
Set
\[
U=B(x,\epsilon/2),
\qquad
V=B(y,\epsilon/2).
\]
Then $U\times V$ is an open neighborhood of $(x,y)$ in the product topology.
For every $(x',y')\in U\times V$, <1>2 gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl|d(x',y')-d(x,y)\bigr|
&\le d(x',x)+d(y',y)\\
&<\frac\epsilon2+\frac\epsilon2\\
&=\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
d(U\times V)\subseteq
(d(x,y)-\epsilon,d(x,y)+\epsilon).
\]
This is continuity of $d$ at $(x,y)$, and the point was arbitrary.
:::

<1>4. Hence the answer is yes for every metric space, not merely for $X=\mathbb R$.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly <1>3.
:::
