::: {.problem}
Compute the first three homology groups of the hollow sphere $S^2$.
You may use simplicial theory and a triangulation but do not simply say that $H_1(S^2)$ is the abelianization of $\pi_1(S^2)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Triangulate $S^2$ as the boundary of a tetrahedron with vertices
\[
v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3.
\]
Write
\[
e_{ij}=[v_i,v_j]
\qquad(i<j)
\]
for the oriented edges and
\[
F_0=[v_1,v_2,v_3],\quad
F_1=[v_0,v_2,v_3],\quad
F_2=[v_0,v_1,v_3],\quad
F_3=[v_0,v_1,v_2]
\]
for the oriented faces.
The simplicial chain groups are
\[
C_2\cong\mathbb Z^4,
\qquad
C_1\cong\mathbb Z^6,
\qquad
C_0\cong\mathbb Z^4,
\]
and $C_3=0$.

<1>1. The image of
\[
\partial_1:C_1\to C_0
\]
is exactly the subgroup of $0$-chains whose coefficients sum to zero.
::: {.proof}
For every $i<j$,
\[
\partial_1 e_{ij}=v_j-v_i,
\]
whose coefficients sum to zero.
Thus
\[
\operatorname{im}\partial_1
\subseteq
\left\{\sum_{i=0}^3 a_i v_i:\sum_{i=0}^3a_i=0\right\}.
\]

Conversely, if
\[
c=a_0v_0+a_1v_1+a_2v_2+a_3v_3
\]
has
\[
a_0+a_1+a_2+a_3=0,
\]
then
\[
c
=a_1(v_1-v_0)+a_2(v_2-v_0)+a_3(v_3-v_0)
=\partial_1(a_1e_{01}+a_2e_{02}+a_3e_{03}).
\]
Hence the reverse inclusion also holds.
:::

<1>2. The zeroth homology group is
\[
H_0(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\partial_0=0$,
\[
H_0(S^2)=C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1.
\]
Define the augmentation
\[
\varepsilon:C_0\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\varepsilon\left(\sum a_iv_i\right)=\sum a_i.
\]
This is surjective, and by <1>1 its kernel is precisely $\operatorname{im}\partial_1$.
Therefore the first isomorphism theorem gives
\[
H_0(S^2)
=C_0/\ker\varepsilon
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>3. Every simplicial $1$-cycle is a simplicial boundary.
::: {.proof}
Write an arbitrary $1$-chain as
\[
c
=a_{01}e_{01}+a_{02}e_{02}+a_{03}e_{03}
+a_{12}e_{12}+a_{13}e_{13}+a_{23}e_{23}.
\]
The equation $\partial_1c=0$ is equivalent to
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{01}+a_{02}+a_{03}&=0,\\
a_{01}-a_{12}-a_{13}&=0,\\
a_{02}+a_{12}-a_{23}&=0,\\
a_{03}+a_{13}+a_{23}&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equation follows from the first three, and the first three give
\[
a_{01}=a_{12}+a_{13},
\qquad
a_{02}=-a_{12}+a_{23},
\qquad
a_{03}=-a_{13}-a_{23}.
\]

Now
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_2F_3&=e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01},\\
\partial_2F_2&=e_{13}-e_{03}+e_{01},\\
\partial_2F_1&=e_{23}-e_{03}+e_{02}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting the cycle relations above yields
\[
c
=a_{12}\partial_2F_3
+a_{13}\partial_2F_2
+a_{23}\partial_2F_1.
\]
Hence
\[
c\in\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
\]
Since every boundary is automatically a cycle because
\[
\partial_1\partial_2=0,
\]
we conclude
\[
\ker\partial_1=\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
\]
:::

<1>4. The first homology group is
\[
H_1(S^2)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
By definition,
\[
H_1(S^2)
=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
\]
The numerator and denominator are equal by <1>3, so the quotient is the zero group.
:::

<1>5. The kernel of
\[
\partial_2:C_2\to C_1
\]
is infinite cyclic, generated by
\[
F_0-F_1+F_2-F_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
The face boundaries are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_2F_0&=e_{23}-e_{13}+e_{12},\\
\partial_2F_1&=e_{23}-e_{03}+e_{02},\\
\partial_2F_2&=e_{13}-e_{03}+e_{01},\\
\partial_2F_3&=e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let
\[
z=aF_0+bF_1+cF_2+dF_3.
\]
If $\partial_2z=0$, comparing respectively the coefficients of
\[
e_{23},\quad e_{13},\quad e_{12}
\]
gives
\[
a+b=0,
\qquad
-a+c=0,
\qquad
a+d=0.
\]
Thus
\[
b=-a,
\qquad
c=a,
\qquad
d=-a.
\]
The remaining edge coefficients then vanish automatically.
Hence every $2$-cycle has the form
\[
z=a(F_0-F_1+F_2-F_3).
\]
Conversely, the displayed generator has zero boundary by the four formulas above.
Therefore
\[
\ker\partial_2
=\mathbb Z(F_0-F_1+F_2-F_3)
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>6. The second homology group is
\[
H_2(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
There are no $3$-simplices in the boundary of a tetrahedron, so
\[
C_3=0
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
\operatorname{im}\partial_3=0.
\]
Therefore, by <1>5,
\[
H_2(S^2)
=\ker\partial_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3
=\ker\partial_2
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>7. Hence the first three homology groups of the hollow sphere are
\[
\boxed{H_0(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad H_1(S^2)=0,\qquad H_2(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z.}
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the combination of <1>2, <1>4, and <1>6. The computation used only the simplicial chain complex of a tetrahedral triangulation and did not use the fundamental group.
