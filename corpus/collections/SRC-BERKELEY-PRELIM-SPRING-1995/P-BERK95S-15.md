::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a finite field. Suppose the subfield generated by
\[
\{x^3:x\in F\}
\]
is a proper subfield of $F$. Prove that
\[
|F|=4.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
q\coloneqq\abs F=p^n
$$
with $p$ prime, and let $K$ be the subfield of $F$ generated by
$\{x^3:x\in F\}$.

<1>1. The characteristic $p$ is either $2$ or $3$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose that $p\ne 2,3$, and let $a\in F$. Since $K$ contains every
cube and contains the prime field,
$$
(a+1)^3+(a-1)^3-2a^3=6a
$$
belongs to $K$. Because $6$ is invertible in the prime field, it
follows that $a\in K$. Thus $F=K$, contrary to the hypothesis.
:::

<1>2. The characteristic is $p=2$.

::: {.proof}
If $p=3$, then the Frobenius map
$$
F\longrightarrow F,\qquad x\longmapsto x^3
$$
is an automorphism of the finite field $F$. Hence every element of $F$
is a cube, so again $K=F$, contrary to the hypothesis. Step <1>1
therefore leaves only $p=2$.
:::

<1>3. The integer $n$ is even, and the set of nonzero cubes has
cardinality
$$
\frac{2^n-1}{3}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $q=2^n$. The group $F^\times$ is cyclic of order
$2^n-1$. If cubing were surjective on $F^\times$, then every nonzero
element of $F$ would be a cube and hence lie in $K$, forcing $K=F$.
Thus the endomorphism
$$
F^\times\longrightarrow F^\times,\qquad x\longmapsto x^3
$$
is not surjective. For a cyclic group of order $2^n-1$, this happens
exactly when $3\mid 2^n-1$. Since $2\equiv-1\pmod 3$, this divisibility
forces $n$ to be even. Its kernel then has order $3$, so its image, the
set of nonzero cubes, has cardinality $(2^n-1)/3$.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\frac{2^n-1}{3}\le 2^{n/2}-1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because $K$ is a proper subfield of $F=\FF_{2^n}$, there is a
proper divisor $d$ of $n$ such that
$$
\abs K=2^d.
$$
Hence $d\le n/2$. Every nonzero cube belongs to $K^\times$, so step
<1>3 gives
$$
\frac{2^n-1}{3}\le \abs{K^\times}
=2^d-1
\le 2^{n/2}-1.
$$
:::

<1>5. The field has cardinality
$$
\boxed{\abs F=4}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Set $m\coloneqq 2^{n/2}$. By step <1>3, $n$ is a positive even
integer, so $m\ge2$. Step <1>4 becomes
$$
\frac{m^2-1}{3}\le m-1.
$$
Since $m-1>0$, cancelling this factor gives
$$
m+1\le3.
$$
Thus $m=2$, so $n=2$ and $\abs F=2^2=4$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 proves the required cardinality.
