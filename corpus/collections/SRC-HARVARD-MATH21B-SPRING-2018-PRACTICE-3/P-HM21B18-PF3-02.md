::: {.problem}
(a) Let $A$ be a real $3\times3$ matrix and $B=A^3+A$. Decide which assertions are always true: invertible $A$ implies invertible $B$; diagonalizable $A$ implies diagonalizable $B$; symmetric $A$ implies symmetric $B$; $\det A=0$ implies $\det B=0$; $\operatorname{tr}A=0$ implies $\operatorname{tr}B=0$; eigenvalue $1$ of $A$ implies eigenvalue $2$ of $B$.

(b) Match
\[
f''+f=2\sin(2t),\quad f'=2\sin t,\quad f'-f=2\sin(2t),\quad f''+f=2\sin t
\]
to source graphs A--D. In the source, graph A is a bounded periodic curve
oscillating between about $2$ and $6$, graph B grows rapidly and positively,
graph C oscillates with increasing amplitude, and graph D is a bounded
non-sinusoidal oscillation.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. In part (a), “invertible $A$ implies invertible $B$” is false.

::: {.proof}
Take
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The upper-left $2\times2$ block $R$ satisfies $R^2=-I_2$, so
$R^3+R=0$. Therefore
\[
B=A^3+A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&2
\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is singular, whereas $A$ is invertible.
:::

<1>2. In part (a), “diagonalizable $A$ implies diagonalizable $B$” is true.

::: {.proof}
If $A=SDS^{-1}$ with $D$ diagonal, then
\[
B=A^3+A=S(D^3+D)S^{-1}.
\]
Since $D^3+D$ is diagonal, $B$ is diagonalizable.
:::

<1>3. In part (a), “symmetric $A$ implies symmetric $B$” is true.

::: {.proof}
If $A^T=A$, then
\[
B^T=(A^3+A)^T=(A^T)^3+A^T=A^3+A=B.
\]
:::

<1>4. In part (a), “$\det A=0$ implies $\det B=0$” is true.

::: {.proof}
If $\det A=0$, choose $0\ne v\in\ker A$. Then
\[
Bv=(A^3+A)v=0.
\]
Thus $B$ has a nontrivial kernel and is singular.
:::

<1>5. In part (a), “$\operatorname{tr}A=0$ implies
$\operatorname{tr}B=0$” is false.

::: {.proof}
For
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)
\]
one has $\operatorname{tr}A=0$, but
\[
B=A^3+A=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,-10),
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{tr}B=-6\ne0.
\]
:::

<1>6. In part (a), “eigenvalue $1$ of $A$ implies eigenvalue $2$ of $B$”
is true.

::: {.proof}
If $Av=v$ for some $v\ne0$, then
\[
Bv=(A^3+A)v=v+v=2v.
\]
Hence $2$ is an eigenvalue of $B$.
:::

<1>7. In part (b), $f''+f=2\sin(2t)$ matches graph D.

::: {.proof}
A particular solution is $-\frac23\sin(2t)$, so every solution has the
form
\[
f(t)=C_1\cos t+C_2\sin t-\frac23\sin(2t).
\]
Thus every solution is bounded and periodic, and in general it contains both
frequencies $1$ and $2$. This is the bounded non-sinusoidal behavior of graph
D.
:::

<1>8. In part (b), $f'=2\sin t$ matches graph A.

::: {.proof}
Integrating gives
\[
f(t)=C-2\cos t.
\]
This is a single shifted cosine with amplitude $2$. Choosing $C=4$ gives
values between $2$ and $6$, exactly the behavior shown by graph A.
:::

<1>9. In part (b), $f'-f=2\sin(2t)$ matches graph B.

::: {.proof}
Solving the first-order equation gives
\[
f(t)=Ce^t-\frac25\sin(2t)-\frac45\cos(2t).
\]
For $C>0$, the $Ce^t$ term dominates and produces rapid positive exponential
growth, which is the behavior of graph B.
:::

<1>10. In part (b), $f''+f=2\sin t$ matches graph C.

::: {.proof}
The forcing is resonant with the homogeneous equation. A particular solution
is $-t\cos t$, so
\[
f(t)=C_1\cos t+C_2\sin t-t\cos t.
\]
The oscillation therefore has amplitude growing linearly in $t$, matching
graph C.
:::

<1>11. The complete answers are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{(a) only statements 2, 3, 4, and 6 are always true;}\\
\text{(b) }D,\ A,\ B,\ C.
\end{gathered}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>6 establish part (a), and steps <1>7--<1>10 establish part
(b).
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>11 records every requested decision and match.
