::: {.problem}
(a) Solve
\[
u_t=u_{xx}+u
\]
on $[0,\pi]$ with
\[
u(x,0)=\sin x+4\sin(7x)+2\sin(13x).
\]

(b) Solve
\[
u_{tt}=u_{xx}+u
\]
with
\[
u(x,0)=2\sin(5x),
\qquad
u_t(x,0)=3\sin(5x)+6\sin(8x).
\]
Use sine-Fourier modes in $x$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For a sine mode
\[
u_n(x,t)=a_n(t)\sin(nx),
\]
the operator \(u_{xx}+u\) acts by multiplication by \(1-n^2\) in the
spatial variable.

::: {.proof}
Since
\[
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}\sin(nx)=-n^2\sin(nx),
\]
one has
\[
(u_n)_{xx}+u_n
=(1-n^2)a_n(t)\sin(nx).
\]
:::

<1>2. For the modified heat equation, the coefficient of the \(n\)-th sine
mode is multiplied by
\[
e^{(1-n^2)t}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Substituting the mode from step <1>1 into
\[
u_t=u_{xx}+u
\]
gives the scalar equation
\[
a_n'(t)=(1-n^2)a_n(t).
\]
Therefore
\[
a_n(t)=a_n(0)e^{(1-n^2)t}.
\]
:::

<1>3. The solution in part (a) is
\[
\boxed{
u(x,t)
=\sin x
+4e^{-48t}\sin(7x)
+2e^{-168t}\sin(13x).}
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>2 to the three nonzero initial sine coefficients. For
\(n=1,7,13\), the exponents \(1-n^2\) are respectively
\[
0,\qquad -48,\qquad -168.
\]
At \(t=0\) the displayed expression is exactly the prescribed initial
condition, and each mode satisfies the PDE by step <1>2.
:::

<1>4. For the modified wave equation, the \(n\)-th sine coefficient satisfies
\[
a_n''+(n^2-1)a_n=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
Substitution of \(u_n=a_n(t)\sin(nx)\) into
\[
u_{tt}=u_{xx}+u
\]
and use of step <1>1 give
\[
a_n''(t)=(1-n^2)a_n(t),
\]
which is the displayed harmonic-oscillator equation.
:::

<1>5. The mode-\(5\) coefficient is
\[
a_5(t)
=2\cos(\sqrt{24}\,t)
+\frac{3}{\sqrt{24}}\sin(\sqrt{24}\,t),
\]
and the mode-\(8\) coefficient is
\[
a_8(t)
=\frac{6}{\sqrt{63}}\sin(\sqrt{63}\,t).
\]

::: {.proof}
For \(n=5\), step <1>4 gives
\[
a_5''+24a_5=0.
\]
The initial conditions are \(a_5(0)=2\) and \(a_5'(0)=3\), which determine
the displayed oscillator solution.

For \(n=8\),
\[
a_8''+63a_8=0,
\]
with \(a_8(0)=0\) and \(a_8'(0)=6\), giving the second displayed formula.
All other sine modes have zero initial position and velocity and therefore
vanish identically.
:::

<1>6. The solution in part (b) is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u(x,t)
={}&
\left(
2\cos(\sqrt{24}\,t)
+\frac{3}{\sqrt{24}}\sin(\sqrt{24}\,t)
\right)\sin(5x)\\
&+\frac{6}{\sqrt{63}}\sin(\sqrt{63}\,t)\sin(8x).
\end{aligned}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine the two nonzero modal coefficients from step <1>5. At \(t=0\),
the cosine term gives \(2\sin(5x)\) and all sine-in-time terms vanish.
Differentiating in \(t\) at \(0\) gives
\[
3\sin(5x)+6\sin(8x),
\]
the prescribed initial velocity. Step <1>4 verifies the PDE mode by mode.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3 and <1>6 give the solutions requested in parts (a) and (b).
:::
:::

::: {.remark}
The retained source solution correctly displays the factor
\(e^{(1-n^2)t}\), but its preceding prose says the modal equation is
\(a_n'=-\lambda_n a_n\) and the factor is \(e^{-\lambda_n t}\) after
defining \(\lambda_n=1-n^2\). Step <1>2 gives the correct sign directly.
:::
