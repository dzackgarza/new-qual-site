::: {.problem}
(a) Solve
$$
u_t=u_{xx}+u
$$
on $[0,\pi]$ with
$$
u(x,0)=\sin x+4\sin(7x)+2\sin(13x).
$$

(b) Solve
$$
u_{tt}=u_{xx}+u
$$
with
$$
u(x,0)=2\sin(5x),
\qquad
u_t(x,0)=3\sin(5x)+6\sin(8x).
$$
Use sine-Fourier modes in $x$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For a sine mode
$$
u_n(x,t)=a_n(t)\sin(nx),
$$
the operator $u_{xx}+u$ acts by multiplication by $1-n^2$ in the
spatial variable.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}\sin(nx)=-n^2\sin(nx),
$$
one has
$$
(u_n)_{xx}+u_n
=(1-n^2)a_n(t)\sin(nx).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For the modified heat equation, the coefficient of the $n$-th sine
mode is multiplied by
$$
e^{(1-n^2)t}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substituting the mode from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} into
$$
u_t=u_{xx}+u
$$
gives the scalar equation
$$
a_n'(t)=(1-n^2)a_n(t).
$$
Therefore
$$
a_n(t)=a_n(0)e^{(1-n^2)t}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The solution in part (a) is
$$
\boxed{
u(x,t)
=\sin x
+4e^{-48t}\sin(7x)
+2e^{-168t}\sin(13x).}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} to the three nonzero initial sine coefficients. For
$n=1,7,13$, the exponents $1-n^2$ are respectively
$$
0,\qquad -48,\qquad -168.
$$
At $t=0$ the displayed expression is exactly the prescribed initial
condition, and each mode satisfies the PDE by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For the modified wave equation, the $n$-th sine coefficient satisfies
$$
a_n''+(n^2-1)a_n=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitution of $u_n=a_n(t)\sin(nx)$ into
$$
u_{tt}=u_{xx}+u
$$
and use of step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} give
$$
a_n''(t)=(1-n^2)a_n(t),
$$
which is the displayed harmonic-oscillator equation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The mode-$5$ coefficient is
$$
a_5(t)
=2\cos(\sqrt{24}\,t)
+\frac{3}{\sqrt{24}}\sin(\sqrt{24}\,t),
$$
and the mode-$8$ coefficient is
$$
a_8(t)
=\frac{6}{\sqrt{63}}\sin(\sqrt{63}\,t).
$$


::: pf-proof

For $n=5$, step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
a_5''+24a_5=0.
$$
The initial conditions are $a_5(0)=2$ and $a_5'(0)=3$, which determine
the displayed oscillator solution.

For $n=8$,
$$
a_8''+63a_8=0,
$$
with $a_8(0)=0$ and $a_8'(0)=6$, giving the second displayed formula.
All other sine modes have zero initial position and velocity and therefore
vanish identically.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The solution in part (b) is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u(x,t)
={}&
\left(
2\cos(\sqrt{24}\,t)
+\frac{3}{\sqrt{24}}\sin(\sqrt{24}\,t)
\right)\sin(5x)\\
&+\frac{6}{\sqrt{63}}\sin(\sqrt{63}\,t)\sin(8x).
\end{aligned}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine the two nonzero modal coefficients from step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}. At $t=0$,
the cosine term gives $2\sin(5x)$ and all sine-in-time terms vanish.
Differentiating in $t$ at $0$ gives
$$
3\sin(5x)+6\sin(8x),
$$
the prescribed initial velocity. Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} verifies the PDE mode by mode.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} give the solutions requested in parts (a) and (b).

:::

::: {.remark}
The retained source solution correctly displays the factor
$e^{(1-n^2)t}$, but its preceding prose says the modal equation is
$a_n'=-\lambda_n a_n$ and the factor is $e^{-\lambda_n t}$ after
defining $\lambda_n=1-n^2$. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives the correct sign directly.
:::
