::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\pi/2<x<\pi,\\
0,&-\pi/2<x<\pi/2,\\
-1,&-\pi<x<-\pi/2.
\end{cases}
$$
(a) Find its Fourier series on $[-\pi,\pi]$. (b) If $a_0,a_n,b_n$ are its Fourier coefficients, compute
$$
\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n^2+\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n^2.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Use the Fourier-series convention
$$
f(x)\sim \frac{a_0}{2}
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\bigr).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

All cosine coefficients, including the constant coefficient, vanish:
$$
a_0=0,\qquad a_n=0\quad(n\ge1).
$$


::: pf-proof

The function is odd:
$$
f(-x)=-f(x).
$$
Hence its integral over $[-\pi,\pi]$ is zero, so $a_0=0$. Since
$\cos(nx)$ is even, the product $f(x)\cos(nx)$ is odd, and therefore
$$
a_n
=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\cos(nx)\,dx
=0
$$
for every $n\ge1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $n\ge1$,
$$
\boxed{
b_n
=\frac{2}{n\pi}
\left(\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}-\cos(n\pi)\right).}
$$


::: pf-proof

Because both $f$ and $\sin(nx)$ are odd, their product is even. On
$(0,\pi/2)$ the function $f$ vanishes, while on $(\pi/2,\pi)$ it equals
$1$. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n
&=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac2\pi\int_{\pi/2}^{\pi}\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac{2}{n\pi}
\left(\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}-\cos(n\pi)\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Fourier series is
$$
\boxed{
f(x)\sim
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{2}{n\pi}
\left(\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}-(-1)^n\right)\sin(nx).}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that only sine terms occur, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives their
coefficients. Since $\cos(n\pi)=(-1)^n$, substitution into the Fourier
series gives the displayed expression.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The requested coefficient-square sum is
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n^2
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n^2=1.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Parseval's identity in the convention above is
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac{a_0^2}{2}
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2+b_n^2).
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, all $a$-coefficients vanish, so the right-hand side equals the
sum requested in the problem. Moreover, $|f|=1$ on
$(-\pi,-\pi/2)\cup(\pi/2,\pi)$ and vanishes elsewhere. Therefore
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac1\pi\left(\frac\pi2+\frac\pi2\right)
=1.
$$

:::
