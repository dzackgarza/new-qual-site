::: {.problem}
Let
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

(a) Compute $A^2$ and $A^{-1}$;

(b) find the eigenvalues of $A^T+A$;

(c) find the QR decomposition;

(d) find the characteristic polynomial;

(e) determine algebraic and geometric multiplicities and decide whether $A$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\boxed{
A^2=\begin{pmatrix}4&8\\0&4\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1/2\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives
$$
A^2
=
\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\0&2\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\0&2\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}4&8\\0&4\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Also
$$
\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\0&2\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1/2\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix}
=I_2,
$$
so the second displayed matrix is $A^{-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The eigenvalues of $A^T+A$ are
$$
\boxed{6\text{ and }2.}
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
A^T+A
=
\begin{pmatrix}4&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its characteristic polynomial is
$$
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda-4&-2\\
-2&\lambda-4
\end{pmatrix}
=(\lambda-4)^2-4
=(\lambda-6)(\lambda-2).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The QR decomposition with positive diagonal entries in $R$ is
$$
\boxed{
A=QR,
\qquad
Q=I_2,
\qquad
R=\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The first column of $A$ is $a_1=(2,0)^T$, so Gram--Schmidt gives
$q_1=(1,0)^T$. For the second column $a_2=(2,2)^T$,
$$
a_2-\langle a_2,q_1\rangle q_1
=
\begin{pmatrix}2\\2\end{pmatrix}
-2\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}0\\2\end{pmatrix},
$$
so $q_2=(0,1)^T$. Hence $Q=(q_1\ q_2)=I_2$ and therefore $R=Q^TA=A$.
Its diagonal entries are positive.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The characteristic polynomial of $A$ is
$$
\boxed{
p_A(\lambda)=\det(\lambda I-A)
=(\lambda-2)^2
=\lambda^2-4\lambda+4.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $A$ is upper triangular, its diagonal entries are both $2$. Thus
$$
\det(\lambda I-A)
=
\det\begin{pmatrix}\lambda-2&-2\\0&\lambda-2\end{pmatrix}
=(\lambda-2)^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The eigenvalue $2$ has algebraic multiplicity $2$ and geometric
multiplicity $1$, so
$$
\boxed{A\text{ is not diagonalizable}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives algebraic multiplicity $2$. Moreover,
$$
A-2I
=
\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose kernel is
$$
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}x\\0\end{pmatrix}:x\in\RR
\right\}.
$$
Hence the eigenspace has dimension $1$. A $2\times2$ matrix is
diagonalizable only if it has two linearly independent eigenvectors, so
$A$ is not diagonalizable.

:::
