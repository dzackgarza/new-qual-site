::: {.problem}
Find the general solutions of
$$
f'+f=t^2+1,
\qquad
f''+9f=t^2+1,
$$
$$
f''+2f'+f=t^2,
\qquad
f''-f=t^2+e^t.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The general solution of
$$
f'+f=t^2+1
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
f(t)=C_1e^{-t}+t^2-2t+3.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The homogeneous equation $f'+f=0$ has solution $C_1e^{-t}$. For a
particular solution, put
$$
f_p(t)=at^2+bt+c.
$$
Then
$$
f_p'+f_p
=at^2+(b+2a)t+(c+b).
$$
Matching coefficients with $t^2+1$ gives
$$
a=1,\qquad b=-2,\qquad c=3.
$$
Adding the homogeneous and particular solutions gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The general solution of
$$
f''+9f=t^2+1
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
f(t)=C_1\cos(3t)+C_2\sin(3t)+\frac{t^2}{9}+\frac7{81}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The characteristic equation of the homogeneous problem is
$r^2+9=0$, with roots $\pm3i$. Hence the homogeneous solutions are
$\cos(3t)$ and $\sin(3t)$. For
$$
f_p(t)=at^2+bt+c,
$$
one has
$$
f_p''+9f_p
=9at^2+9bt+(2a+9c).
$$
Matching coefficients gives
$$
a=\frac19,\qquad b=0,\qquad
2a+9c=1,
$$
hence $c=7/81$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The general solution of
$$
f''+2f'+f=t^2
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
f(t)=C_1e^{-t}+C_2te^{-t}+t^2-4t+6.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The homogeneous characteristic polynomial is
$$
r^2+2r+1=(r+1)^2,
$$
so the homogeneous solution is $(C_1+C_2t)e^{-t}$. For
$f_p=at^2+bt+c$,
$$
f_p''+2f_p'+f_p
=at^2+(b+4a)t+(c+2b+2a).
$$
Matching with $t^2$ gives
$$
a=1,\qquad b=-4,\qquad c=6.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The general solution of
$$
f''-f=t^2+e^t
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
f(t)=C_1e^t+C_2e^{-t}+\frac12te^t-t^2-2.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The homogeneous characteristic equation is $r^2-1=0$, so the homogeneous
solution is $C_1e^t+C_2e^{-t}$.

For the polynomial forcing, $f_{p,1}=-t^2-2$ satisfies
$$
f_{p,1}''-f_{p,1}=t^2.
$$
Because $e^t$ is a homogeneous mode, use the resonant ansatz
$f_{p,2}=Ate^t$. Then
$$
f_{p,2}''-f_{p,2}=2Ae^t,
$$
so $A=1/2$. Adding the homogeneous solution and both particular solutions
gives the displayed formula.

:::
