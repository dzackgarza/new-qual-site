::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
\dot x=y-x^2,
\qquad
\dot y=x^2-y^2.
$$
(a) Find the nullclines; (b) find all equilibria; (c) analyze their stability; (d) choose the matching source phase portrait A--D.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The nullclines are
$$
\boxed{
\dot x=0:\ y=x^2,
\qquad
\dot y=0:\ y^2=x^2.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The first component vanishes exactly when
$$
y-x^2=0,
$$
and the second vanishes exactly when
$$
x^2-y^2=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The equilibrium points are
$$
\boxed{(0,0),\ (1,1),\ (-1,1).}
$$


::: pf-proof

At an equilibrium both nullcline equations from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} hold. Thus
$$
y=x^2
$$
and either $y=x$ or $y=-x$.

If $y=x$, then $x^2=x$, so $x=0$ or $1$, giving $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$.
If $y=-x$, then $x^2=-x$, so $x=0$ or $-1$, giving $(0,0)$ and
$(-1,1)$. These are all intersections of the nullclines.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Jacobian is
$$
J(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2x&1\\
2x&-2y
\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate the vector field
$$
F(x,y)=(y-x^2,\ x^2-y^2)
$$
with respect to $x$ and $y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The equilibrium $(1,1)$ is locally asymptotically stable.


::: pf-proof

At $(1,1)$,
$$
J(1,1)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&1\\
2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its characteristic polynomial is
$$
(\lambda+2)^2-2,
$$
so its eigenvalues are
$$
-2\pm\sqrt2.
$$
Both are strictly negative. Since the equilibrium is hyperbolic, the
linearization theorem implies local asymptotic stability.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The equilibrium $(-1,1)$ is unstable.


::: pf-proof

At $(-1,1)$,
$$
J(-1,1)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&1\\
-2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its trace is $0$ and determinant is $-2$, so its eigenvalues are
$\pm\sqrt2$. Hence it is a hyperbolic saddle, and therefore unstable by the
linearization theorem.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The region
$$
R=\{(x,y):0<x<1,\ x^2<y<x\}
$$
is forward invariant, and every trajectory starting in $R$ converges to
$(1,1)$.


::: pf-proof

Inside $R$,
$$
\dot x=y-x^2>0
$$
and, since $0<y<x$,
$$
\dot y=x^2-y^2=(x-y)(x+y)>0.
$$
On the lower boundary $y=x^2$, let $g=y-x^2$. Then
$$
\dot g
=\dot y-2x\dot x
=x^2-x^4
>0
\qquad(0<x<1),
$$
so the vector field points into $g>0$. On the upper boundary $y=x$, let
$h=x-y$. Then
$$
\dot h
=\dot x-\dot y
=x-x^2
>0
\qquad(0<x<1),
$$
so the vector field points into $h>0$. Thus trajectories cannot leave $R$
through either curved boundary. They cannot leave through $x=0$ because
$\dot x>0$ in $R$. If a trajectory in the closure reached $x=1$, the
inequalities $x^2\le y\le x$ would force $y=1$, giving the equilibrium
$(1,1)$. Hence $R$ is forward invariant. Since both coordinates increase
and remain below $1$, each trajectory has a limit $(L,M)$ in the closure of
$R$.

By continuity of the vector field,
$$
\dot x(t)\longrightarrow M-L^2,
\qquad
\dot y(t)\longrightarrow L^2-M^2.
$$
The two derivatives are positive along the trajectory. If either limiting
derivative were positive, the corresponding convergent coordinate could not
remain bounded; hence both limits are zero. Thus $(L,M)$ is an equilibrium.
Since $L$ is at least the positive initial $x$-coordinate, the limit cannot
be $(0,0)$. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} then forces $(L,M)=(1,1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The equilibrium $(0,0)$ is unstable.


::: pf-proof

Fix the neighborhood
$$
U=\{(x,y):x^2+y^2<1/16\}.
$$
For every $\delta>0$, choose
$$
0<x_0<\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{\delta}{2}\right\},
\qquad
y_0=\frac{x_0}{2}.
$$
Then $x_0^2<y_0<x_0$, so $(x_0,y_0)\in R$, and its Euclidean norm is less
than $\delta$. By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, its trajectory converges to $(1,1)$, hence
eventually leaves $U$. Therefore arbitrarily small initial data leave the
fixed neighborhood $U$, which is exactly failure of Lyapunov stability.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The matching source phase portrait is
$$
\boxed{\text{A}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} show that the three marked equilibria have,
respectively, a sink at $(1,1)$, a saddle at $(-1,1)$, and an unstable
nonhyperbolic equilibrium at the origin. Inspection of the four retained
source portraits shows that portrait A is the unique candidate with this
stability pattern and local flow orientation.

:::
