::: {.problem}
The pseudodeterminant of a matrix is the product of its nonzero eigenvalues.

(a) Find the pseudodeterminant of the $10\times10$ matrix all of whose entries are $9$.

(b) Give a counterexample to multiplicativity of pseudodeterminants: find matrices $A,B$ for which
\[
\operatorname{pdet}(AB)\ne\operatorname{pdet}(A)\operatorname{pdet}(B).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\mathbf 1=(1,\ldots,1)^T\in\RR^{10}$, and let $M$ denote the matrix in part (a).

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The eigenvalues of $M$ are $90$ with multiplicity $1$ and $0$ with multiplicity $9$.


::: pf-proof

Since every entry of $M$ is $9$, one has
\[
M=9\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T.
\]
Hence
\[
M\mathbf 1=9\mathbf 1(\mathbf 1^T\mathbf 1)=9\mathbf 1\cdot10=90\mathbf 1.
\]
On the other hand, if $x\in\mathbf 1^\perp$, then $\mathbf 1^Tx=0$, so
\[
Mx=9\mathbf 1(\mathbf 1^Tx)=0.
\]
Because $\RR^{10}=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf 1)\oplus\mathbf 1^\perp$ and $\dim\mathbf 1^\perp=9$, these are all ten eigenvalues with the stated multiplicities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The pseudodeterminant of $M$ is $\boxed{90}$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the only nonzero eigenvalue of $M$, counted with multiplicity, is $90$. The definition of pseudodeterminant therefore gives $\operatorname{pdet}(M)=90$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

A counterexample in part (b) is
\[
\boxed{A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1,0),\qquad B=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,2)}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The eigenvalues of a diagonal matrix are its diagonal entries. Thus
\[
\operatorname{pdet}(A)=2,
\qquad
\operatorname{pdet}(B)=2.
\]
Moreover,
\[
AB=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0),
\]
so $\operatorname{pdet}(AB)=1$. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{pdet}(AB)=1\ne4=\operatorname{pdet}(A)\operatorname{pdet}(B),
\]
which disproves multiplicativity.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
