::: {.problem}
Construct the group $G$ of $3 \times 3$ matrices by applying the Young natural representation indexed by $[2,1,1]$ to the permutations of $S_4$.

(1) Compute the Hilbert series $F_{\RR[x_1,x_2,x_3]^G}(q)$ of the ring of $G$-invariants.

(2) Rewrite it in the form $$F_{\RR[x]}(q) = \frac{1 + q^d}{(1 - q^{d_1})(1 - q^{d_2})(1 - q^{d_3})}.$$

(3) Calculate the first 10 terms of this series.

(4) Construct three homogeneous $G$-invariants of degrees $d_1, d_2, d_3$.

(5) Compute the Gröbner basis of the ideal $(I_1, I_2, I_3)$.

(6) Verify that $I_1, I_2, I_3$ are a system of parameters by checking that the quotient $\QQ[x_1, x_2, x_3]/(I_1, I_2, I_3)$ has finite dimension.
If not, go back to step (4).

(7) Construct a $G$-invariant $\eta$ of degree $d$.

(9) Verify that it is not a polynomial in $I_1, I_2, I_3$ by computing the polynomial $Q$ such that $Q(\eta, I_1, I_2, I_3) = 0$.

(10) Based on your previous results, show that step (9) is not needed.

(11) Compute the Jacobian of $I_1, I_2, I_3$ and construct its linear factors.

(12) Assuming that there is a reflection group $G'$ that also leaves $I_1, I_2, I_3$ invariant, you could have predicted the number of these linear factors.
Why?
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the $(2,1,1)$ representation is realised as the rotation group of the cube, the invariant ring of an index-two reflection subgroup is computed, and the Gröbner, discriminant and Jacobian computations are carried out. The printed list has no item (8); the eleven parts printed are answered below. No retained set solves this exercise, and the sets searched are recorded in the audit trail.

::: {.pf-step #group}
The group $G$ is the group of signed permutation matrices of determinant $1$, has order $24$, is isomorphic to $S_{4}$, and its natural three-dimensional representation is the irreducible indexed by $(2,1,1)$.

::: pf-proof
A signed permutation matrix is $DP_{\sigma}$ with $D=\mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})$, $\varepsilon_{i}=\pm1$ and $P_{\sigma}$ a permutation matrix, and $\det(DP_{\sigma})=\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\operatorname{sgn}\sigma$. For each of the $6$ permutations exactly $4$ sign choices give determinant $1$, so $\abs{G}=24$. The group acts on the four body diagonals $\pm e_{1}\pm e_{2}\pm e_{3}$ of the cube, and the resulting homomorphism $G\to S_{4}$ is injective because a matrix fixing all four diagonals is the identity; both groups have order $24$, so it is an isomorphism. The natural representation is irreducible: the diagonal Klein four subgroup has the three coordinate axes as its simultaneous eigenspaces with distinct characters, so the only lines it preserves are the axes, and a $3$-cycle permutation matrix permutes those three lines transitively, so no line is preserved by $G$. Of the two three-dimensional irreducibles of $S_{4}$, the standard one $(3,1)$ has determinant equal to the sign character, while every element of $G$ has determinant $1$; so the representation is the other, $(2,1,1)$, which is the sign twist of $(3,1)$.
:::

::: {.pf-step #invariants-of-B3}
Let $G'$ be the full signed permutation group. Then $G=\ker(\det:G'\to\{\pm1\})$ is a subgroup of index $2$, $\abs{G'}=48$, and
$$
\RR[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{G'}=\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}],\qquad I_{1}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2},\ \ I_{2}=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{3}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2},\ \ I_{3}=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}.
$$

::: pf-proof
$\det$ is a homomorphism onto $\{\pm1\}$, and $G$ is by definition its kernel, of index $2$ in $G'$ of order $48$. Put $a=x_{1}^{2}$, $b=x_{2}^{2}$, $c=x_{3}^{2}$; the invariants of $G'$ are the polynomials invariant under permuting the $x_{i}$ and under changing their signs, and a sign change of $x_{i}$ alone shows that every invariant is a polynomial in $a,b,c$, while the permutation part leaves exactly the symmetric polynomials in $a,b,c$, generated by their elementary symmetric functions, which are $I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}$. Their degrees are $2,4,6$ and their product is $48=\abs{G'}$, which is the criterion for the invariant ring of a finite group to be a polynomial ring on generators of those degrees; so the displayed description is a polynomial ring and there is no relation between them.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eta}
$\eta=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})(x_{1}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})(x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})$ has degree $9$, is fixed by $G$, and every element of $G'\setminus G$ changes its sign.

::: pf-proof
The degree is $3+2+2+2=9$. The factor $x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}$ changes sign under a change of sign of any one variable and is fixed by permutations, and the product of the three differences of the $a,b,c$ is alternating, so it changes sign under a transposition of variables and is fixed by sign changes; a signed permutation is a product of sign changes and a permutation, and the two sign contributions multiply to the sign of the determinant. So $\eta$ is multiplied by $\det$ and is therefore fixed by $G=\ker\det$ and negated by every element of the nontrivial coset. In particular each reflection of $G'$ negates $\eta$, so $\eta$ vanishes on each reflecting hyperplane.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #decomposition}
$\RR[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{G}=\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]\oplus\eta\,\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]$.

::: pf-proof
Both summands are $G$-invariant: the first because $G\subset G'$ and the second because $\eta$ is $G$-invariant. They intersect in $0$ since $\eta$ has odd degree and every element of $\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]$ has even degree. For the converse, let $f$ be $G$-invariant and fix a reflection $r\in G'\setminus G$; write $f=f_{+}+f_{-}$ with $f_{\pm}=\frac12(f\pm r\cdot f)$. Then $f_{+}$ is $G'$-invariant and $f_{-}$ is anti-invariant, that is $g\cdot f_{-}=\det(g)f_{-}$ for $g\in G'$. An anti-invariant polynomial vanishes on every reflecting hyperplane of $G'$, because a reflection $s$ fixes the points of its hyperplane $H$ and satisfies $f_{-}(h)=f_{-}(s\cdot h)=-f_{-}(h)$ for $h\in H$; the hyperplanes of $G'$ are $x_{i}=0$ and $x_{i}=\pm x_{j}$, so $f_{-}$ is divisible by their defining linear forms, that is by $x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{1}+x_{2})(x_{1}-x_{3})(x_{1}+x_{3})(x_{2}-x_{3})(x_{2}+x_{3})=\eta$. The quotient $f_{-}/\eta$ is again anti-invariant, and its square is $G'$-invariant; since $f_{-}^{2}$ is $G'$-invariant and $\eta^{2}$ is $G'$-invariant, $f_{-}/\eta$ is a rational function whose square is a $G'$-invariant polynomial, hence it lies in the fraction field of $\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]$; being a polynomial it lies in $\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]$. So $f_{-}\in\eta\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]$ and $f_{+}\in\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]$.
:::

::: {.pf-step #hilbert}
(1), (2), (4), (7) The Hilbert series is
$$
F_{\RR[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{G}}(q)=\frac{1+q^{9}}{(1-q^{2})(1-q^{4})(1-q^{6})},
$$
so $(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3};d)=(2,4,6;9)$ and $I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},\eta$ are the invariants of degrees $2,4,6,9$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#decomposition){.pf-ref} the ring is the direct sum of two copies of the polynomial ring $\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]$ shifted by $0$ and by $9$, and the Hilbert series of a polynomial ring on homogeneous generators of degrees $2,4,6$ is $\prod(1-q^{d_{i}})^{-1}$. Adding the shifted copy gives $F=(1+q^{9})/\prod(1-q^{d_{i}})$. The generators of degrees $2,4,6$ are $I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}$ and the secondary invariant of degree $9$ is $\eta$, which is part (4) and part (7).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #expansion}
(3) $F=1+q^{2}+2q^{4}+3q^{6}+4q^{8}+q^{9}+5q^{10}+q^{11}+7q^{12}+2q^{13}+8q^{14}+3q^{15}+\cdots$, so the first ten nonzero terms are $1+q^{2}+2q^{4}+3q^{6}+4q^{8}+q^{9}+5q^{10}+q^{11}+7q^{12}+2q^{13}$, and the coefficients in degrees $0$ to $9$ are $1,0,1,0,2,0,3,0,4,1$.

::: pf-proof
Write $A(q)=1/\big((1-q^{2})(1-q^{4})(1-q^{6})\big)=\sum_{m}a_{2m}q^{2m}$. The coefficient of $q^{2m}$ counts the solutions of $m=i+2j+3k$ in nonnegative integers $i,j,k$, because a monomial $I_{1}^{i}I_{2}^{j}I_{3}^{k}$ has degree $2i+4j+6k$. Counting: $m=0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7$ give counts $1,1,2,3,4,5,7,8$. Then $F=A+q^{9}A$, so the coefficient of $q^{n}$ is $a_{n}$ for $n<9$ and $a_{n}+a_{n-9}$ for $n\ge9$, which yields the displayed series: the terms of odd degree below $9$ vanish, and $q^{9}$ contributes $1$, $q^{11}$ contributes $a_{2}=1$, $q^{13}$ contributes $a_{4}=2$, $q^{15}$ contributes $a_{6}=3$.
:::

::: {.pf-step #groebner}
(5) In the lexicographic order $x_{1}>x_{2}>x_{3}$ the set
$$
g_{1}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2},\qquad g_{2}=x_{2}^{4}+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}+x_{3}^{4},\qquad g_{3}=x_{3}^{6}
$$
is a Gröbner basis of $(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})$.

::: pf-proof
Reducing $I_{2}$ by $g_{1}$: $I_{2}=x_{1}^{2}(x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}$ and $x_{1}^{2}\equiv-(x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})$ modulo $g_{1}$, so $I_{2}\equiv-(x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}=-(x_{2}^{4}+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}+x_{3}^{4})=-g_{2}$, and indeed $I_{2}=(x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})g_{1}-g_{2}$. Reducing $I_{3}=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}$ the same way gives $-(x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}=-x_{2}^{4}x_{3}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{4}=-x_{3}^{2}g_{2}+x_{3}^{6}$, so $I_{3}=-x_{3}^{2}g_{2}+(x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})x_{3}^{2}g_{1}+g_{3}$. These two identities show $g_{2},g_{3}\in(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})$ and express $I_{2},I_{3}$ in terms of $g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}$, so both ideals coincide. The leading monomials $x_{1}^{2}$, $x_{2}^{4}$, $x_{3}^{6}$ are pairwise relatively prime, so every $S$-polynomial of the set reduces to $0$ (Buchberger's product criterion), which makes the set a Gröbner basis.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hsop}
(6) The quotient $\QQ[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]/(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})$ has dimension $48$, so $I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}$ is a system of parameters.

::: pf-proof
The remainder on division by the Gröbner basis of step [](#groebner){.pf-ref} is a linear combination of the monomials $x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}x_{3}^{c}$ with $0\le a<2$, $0\le b<4$, $0\le c<6$, and those classes are linearly independent because a nonzero combination of them has leading monomial among them, which is not divisible by any of $x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{4},x_{3}^{6}$ and so is not in the ideal. Hence the quotient has the $\QQ$-basis of those $48$ monomials, and $\dim=2\cdot4\cdot6=48<\infty$. A homogeneous ideal with finite-dimensional quotient is a system of parameters: the generators are algebraically independent, because the Hilbert series of the quotient, $\prod(1-q^{d_{i}})/(1-q)^{3}$, has a pole of order $3$ at $q=1$.
:::

::: {.pf-step #relation}
(9) $\eta^{2}=I_{3}\big(I_{1}^{2}I_{2}^{2}-4I_{2}^{3}-4I_{1}^{3}I_{3}-27I_{3}^{2}+18I_{1}I_{2}I_{3}\big)$, so with $T=\eta$, $U=I_{1}$, $V=I_{2}$, $W=I_{3}$ the polynomial
$$
Q(T,U,V,W)=T^{2}-W\big(U^{2}V^{2}-4V^{3}-4U^{3}W-27W^{2}+18UVW\big)
$$
satisfies $Q(\eta,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})=0$.

::: pf-proof
With $a=x_{1}^{2}$, $b=x_{2}^{2}$, $c=x_{3}^{2}$ one has $I_{1}=a+b+c$, $I_{2}=ab+ac+bc$ and $I_{3}=abc$, the elementary symmetric functions, and
$$
\eta^{2}=abc\,(a-b)^{2}(a-c)^{2}(b-c)^{2}=I_{3}\prod_{i<j}(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}.
$$
The last product is the discriminant of the cubic $t^{3}-I_{1}t^{2}+I_{2}t-I_{3}$, whose roots are $a,b,c$; for $t^{3}+pt^{2}+qt+r$ the discriminant is $p^{2}q^{2}-4q^{3}-4p^{3}r-27r^{2}+18pqr$, and substituting $p=-I_{1}$, $q=I_{2}$, $r=-I_{3}$ gives $-4p^{3}r=-4I_{1}^{3}I_{3}$ and $18pqr=18I_{1}I_{2}I_{3}$, so the discriminant is $I_{1}^{2}I_{2}^{2}-4I_{2}^{3}-4I_{1}^{3}I_{3}-27I_{3}^{2}+18I_{1}I_{2}I_{3}$, as displayed.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not-needed}
(10) Step (9) is not needed: $\eta$ is not a polynomial in $I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}$ because its degree is odd.

::: pf-proof
$I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}$ are homogeneous of degrees $2,4,6$, so every monomial $I_{1}^{i}I_{2}^{j}I_{3}^{k}$ has degree $2i+4j+6k$, which is even; hence every element of $\RR[I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}]$ has only even-degree components, and a nonzero homogeneous element of odd degree such as $\eta$ lies outside it. The Hilbert series numerator $1+q^{9}$ states the same thing: over the polynomial subring the ring is free on $1$ and $\eta$, so $\eta$ is not in the subring and $\eta^{2}$ is a relation of the kind computed in step [](#relation){.pf-ref}.
:::

::: {.pf-step #jacobian}
(11) The Jacobian is $J=8\eta$, whose linear factors are the nine forms $x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{1}\pm x_{2},x_{1}\pm x_{3},x_{2}\pm x_{3}$.

::: pf-proof
$J=\det\big(\partial(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})/\partial(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\big)$. The rows are $\big(2x_{i}\big)$, $\big(2x_{i}(\text{sum of the other two squares})\big)$ and $\big(2x_{i}\,(\text{product of the other two squares})\big)$, so each row carries a factor $2$ and each entry a factor $x_{i}$; writing $a=x_{1}^{2}$, $b=x_{2}^{2}$, $c=x_{3}^{2}$,
$$
J=8x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\det\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\ b+c&a+c&a+b\\ bc&ac&ab\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Expanding the determinant over the six permutations, the six monomials in $a,b,c$ that occur are $a^{2}b$, $b^{2}c$, $ac^{2}$, $-ab^{2}$, $-bc^{2}$, $-a^{2}c$, each with coefficient $1$: the identity and the two $3$-cycles contribute $a^{2}b+abc+b^{2}c+abc+abc+ac^{2}$ and the three transpositions $-(ab^{2}+abc)-(abc+bc^{2})-(a^{2}c+abc)$, and the six $abc$ terms cancel in pairs. That sum is $(a-b)(a-c)(b-c)$, so
$$
J=8x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}(a-b)(a-c)(b-c)=8x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})(x_{1}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})(x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})=8\eta,
$$
and the linear factors are the nine displayed forms.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #reflections}
(12) The degrees alone predict nine linear factors, because the Jacobian of the basic invariants of a real reflection group is the product of the defining forms of its reflecting hyperplanes and its degree is $\sum(d_{i}-1)$.

::: pf-proof
For a finite real reflection group with homogeneous basic invariants $I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}$, the Jacobian $\det(\partial I_{i}/\partial x_{j})$ is, up to a nonzero scalar, the product of the defining linear forms of the reflecting hyperplanes. So the number of linear factors equals the number of reflecting hyperplanes, and the degree of the product equals $\sum_{i}(d_{i}-1)$. Here the degrees $2,4,6$ give $\sum(d_{i}-1)=1+3+5=9$, so under the hypothesis of the part the number of linear factors is $9$ before any computation — and the explicit Jacobian of step [](#jacobian){.pf-ref} has exactly those nine factors, which is the check.
:::
