::: {.problem}
Let $P(T)$ be the Young idempotent corresponding to the row group of the tableau
$$
T = \begin{array}{ccc}
5 & & \\
2 & 6 & 7 \\
1 & 3 & 4
\end{array}.
$$
Let $\chi$ be the character of the representation resulting from the action of $S_7$ on the left ideal $\CC[S_7]P(T)$.
Give the expansion of $\chi$ in terms of the character basis $\{\chi^\lambda\}_{\lambda \vdash 7}$.
Hint: Use the Frobenius map.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the row symmetrizer gives the permutation module of the Young subgroup, and its Frobenius characteristic is a product of complete symmetric functions expanded by Pieri's rule. No retained set solves this exercise, and the sets searched are recorded in the audit trail.

Read the tableau $T$ with its rows as drawn, so that the row of length $1$ is the last one: the shape is $\lambda=(3,3,1)$ and the row group permutes the entries within each row.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #row-group}
The row group of $T$ is $R_{T}\cong S_{3}\times S_{3}\times S_{1}$, acting on the rows $\{1,3,4\}$, $\{2,6,7\}$ and $\{5\}$.

::: pf-proof
The rows of $T$ have lengths $3,3,1$, so their entries are permuted independently by the symmetric groups on those three sets, and these sets are disjoint and cover $\{1,\ldots,7\}$; the row group is their direct product, the Young subgroup $S_{(3,3,1)}=S_{3}\times S_{3}\times S_{1}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #permutation-module}
$\CC[S_{7}]P(T)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{3}\times S_{3}\times S_{1}}^{S_{7}}\mathbf 1$, whose Frobenius characteristic is $h_{3}h_{3}h_{1}$.

::: pf-proof
For the row symmetrizer, $\CC[S_{7}]P(T)$ is the permutation module on the cosets of the row group: the module generated by $P(T)$ under left multiplication is spanned by the translates $gP(T)$ for $g\in S_{7}$, and $gP(T)$ depends only on the coset $gS_{(3,3,1)}$, since multiplying on the right by a row permutation fixes $P(T)$ up to a scalar. So it is $\CC[S_{7}/S_{(3,3,1)}]\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{(3,3,1)}}^{S_{7}}\mathbf 1$. The Frobenius characteristic of $\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{\mu}}^{S_{n}}\mathbf 1$ is $h_{\mu}$, and $h_{r}=s_{(r)}$, so the characteristic is $h_{3}h_{3}h_{1}=s_{(3)}s_{(3)}s_{(1)}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #first-pieri}
$s_{(3)}s_{(3)}=s_{(6)}+s_{(5,1)}+s_{(4,2)}+s_{(3,3)}$.

::: pf-proof
Pieri's rule: $s_{\mu}s_{(b)}=\sum_{\nu}s_{\nu}$ over partitions $\nu$ of $6$ containing $\mu=(3)$ such that $\nu/\mu$ is a horizontal strip, that is $b=3$ boxes added with no two in the same column. The partitions of $6$ containing $(3)$ are $(6)$, $(5,1)$, $(4,2)$, $(3,3)$, $(4,1,1)$, $(3,2,1)$, $(3,1,1,1)$; the first four add their boxes in distinct columns ($(6)$ in columns $4,5,6$; $(5,1)$ in columns $4,5$ and $1$; $(4,2)$ in column $4$ and columns $1,2$; $(3,3)$ in columns $1,2,3$), while each of the last three puts two added boxes in column $1$. So exactly the four terms displayed occur.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #second-pieri}
Multiplying by $s_{(1)}$ term by term gives
$$
s_{(3)}s_{(3)}s_{(1)}=s_{(7)}+2s_{(6,1)}+2s_{(5,2)}+s_{(5,1,1)}+2s_{(4,3)}+s_{(4,2,1)}+s_{(3,3,1)}.
$$

::: pf-proof
$s_{(1)}=h_{1}$, and adding a single box to a partition always satisfies the horizontal-strip condition, so $s_{\mu}s_{(1)}$ is the sum of $s_{\nu}$ over the partitions $\nu$ of $7$ obtained by adding one box to $\mu$. The four additions of step [](#first-pieri){.pf-ref} are
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_{(6)}s_{(1)}&=s_{(7)}+s_{(6,1)},\\
s_{(5,1)}s_{(1)}&=s_{(6,1)}+s_{(5,2)}+s_{(5,1,1)},\\
s_{(4,2)}s_{(1)}&=s_{(5,2)}+s_{(4,3)}+s_{(4,2,1)},\\
s_{(3,3)}s_{(1)}&=s_{(4,3)}+s_{(3,3,1)},
\end{aligned}
$$
one line for each way of adding a box to a row whose length increases while the diagram stays a partition; collecting the coefficients gives the displayed expansion.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #characters}
The character of $\CC[S_{7}]P(T)$ is
$$
\chi=\chi^{(7)}+2\chi^{(6,1)}+2\chi^{(5,2)}+\chi^{(5,1,1)}+2\chi^{(4,3)}+\chi^{(4,2,1)}+\chi^{(3,3,1)}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The Frobenius map sends the irreducible character $\chi^{\lambda}$ to $s_{\lambda}$ and is additive, so the coefficient of $\chi^{\lambda}$ in $\chi$ is the coefficient of $s_{\lambda}$ in $\operatorname{ch}\chi$, which step [](#second-pieri){.pf-ref} computes. As a check on the coefficients, the dimensions of these irreducibles are $1,6,14,15,14,35,21$ for $(7),(6,1),(5,2),(5,1,1),(4,3),(4,2,1),(3,3,1)$, so the dimension of the representation is
$$
1+2(6)+2(14)+15+2(14)+35+21=140=\frac{7!}{3!\,3!\,1!}=\big[S_{7}:S_{3}\times S_{3}\times S_{1}\big],
$$
which is the dimension of the induced module; hence
$$
\boxed{\chi=\chi^{(7)}+2\chi^{(6,1)}+2\chi^{(5,2)}+\chi^{(5,1,1)}+2\chi^{(4,3)}+\chi^{(4,2,1)}+\chi^{(3,3,1)}.}
$$
:::
