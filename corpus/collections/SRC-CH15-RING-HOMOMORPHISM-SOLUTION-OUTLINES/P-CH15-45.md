::: {.problem}
Is there a ring homomorphism from the reals to some ring whose kernel is the integers?
:::

::: {.solution}

No.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #kernel-ideal}

The kernel of a ring homomorphism $\phi\colon R\to S$ is an ideal of $R$.

::: pf-proof

The kernel $K=\{r:\phi(r)=0\}$ is an additive subgroup, since
$\phi(r-r')=\phi(r)-\phi(r')=0$. For $r\in R$ and $k\in K$,
$\phi(rk)=\phi(r)\phi(k)=\phi(r)\cdot 0=0$ and
$\phi(kr)=\phi(k)\phi(r)=0$, so $rk,kr\in K$. Hence $K$ is an ideal of $R$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #z-not-ideal}

$\ZZ$ is not an ideal of $\RR$.

::: pf-proof

An ideal $I$ of $\RR$ is closed under multiplication by arbitrary elements:
$r\in\RR$, $x\in I$ implies $rx\in I$. Now $1\in\ZZ$ and $\pi\in\RR$, but
$\pi\cdot 1=\pi\notin\ZZ$ since $\pi$ is not an integer. So $\ZZ$ fails the
defining closure and is not an ideal of $\RR$. The same computation shows in
general that a subring of a field is an ideal only if it is $\{0\}$ or the whole
field.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}

There is no ring homomorphism from $\RR$ to any ring with kernel $\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $\phi\colon\RR\to S$ is a ring homomorphism with $\ker\phi=\ZZ$. By step
[](#kernel-ideal){.pf-ref} the kernel is an ideal of $\RR$, so $\ZZ$ would be
an ideal of $\RR$, contradicting step [](#z-not-ideal){.pf-ref}. Equivalently,
the only ideals of the field $\RR$ are $\{0\}$ and $\RR$, and $\ZZ$ is neither.
So no such homomorphism exists.

:::
