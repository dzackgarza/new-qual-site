::: {.problem}
Show that if $m$ and $n$ are distinct positive integers, then $m\ZZ$ is not ring isomorphic to $n\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own: the collection's provenance is a set of solution outlines. Let $m\ne n$ be positive integers and consider $m\ZZ$ and $n\ZZ$ as rings with the inherited operations.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #generator}
If $\varphi:m\ZZ\to n\ZZ$ is a ring isomorphism then $\varphi(m)=\pm n$.

::: pf-proof
As an additive group $m\ZZ$ is cyclic with generator $m$, and an isomorphism of rings is in particular an isomorphism of additive groups, so it carries generators to generators. The generators of the additive group $n\ZZ\cong\ZZ$ are $\pm n$, hence the display.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #contradiction}
No such $\varphi$ exists.

::: pf-proof
Write $\varepsilon=\pm1$ with $\varphi(m)=\varepsilon n$. On one hand $m^2=m\cdot m$, a product in the ring, so $\varphi(m^2)=\varphi(m)^2=\varepsilon^2n^2=n^2$. On the other hand $m^2$ is also the sum of $m$ copies of $m$ in the additive group, so $\varphi(m^2)=m\varphi(m)=m\varepsilon n$. Hence $n^2=\varepsilon mn$, and dividing by $n>0$ gives $n=\varepsilon m$. Since $m,n>0$, $\varepsilon$ must be $+1$ and $n=m$, contradicting that they are distinct. So no ring isomorphism exists.
:::
