::: {.problem}
Let $f\colon \RR^3 \to \RR^3$ be the function defined by the equation $f(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$, where

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{pmatrix}$$

Which of the following best describes the image of $f$?

(A) A point
(B) A line through the origin
(C) A plane through the origin
(D) The union of a plane through the origin and a line through the origin, with the line perpendicular to the plane
(E) All of $\RR^3$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $\mathbf c_1,\mathbf c_2,\mathbf c_3$ are the columns of $A$, then
$$
\mathbf c_3=2\mathbf c_2-\mathbf c_1.
$$


::: pf-proof

The columns are
$$
\mathbf c_1=(1,4,7)^T,
\qquad
\mathbf c_2=(2,5,8)^T,
\qquad
\mathbf c_3=(3,6,9)^T,
$$
and direct calculation gives
$2\mathbf c_2-\mathbf c_1=(3,6,9)^T=\mathbf c_3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The vectors $\mathbf c_1$ and $\mathbf c_2$ are linearly independent.


::: pf-proof

They are nonzero and are not scalar multiples: equality
$\mathbf c_2=\lambda\mathbf c_1$ would force $\lambda=2$ from the first
coordinate, but then the second coordinate would be $8$, not $5$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Hence the image of $f$ is a two-dimensional subspace of $\RR^3$.


::: pf-proof

The image of the matrix map $f(\mathbf x)=A\mathbf x$ is the span of the
columns of $A$. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that this span is generated by
$\mathbf c_1,\mathbf c_2$, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that those two generators are
independent. Thus the image has dimension $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore the image is
$$
\boxed{\text{a plane through the origin}},
$$
so the answer is (C).


::: pf-proof

Every two-dimensional linear subspace of $\RR^3$ is a plane through the
origin. Apply this to the image identified in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the required description.

:::

:::
