::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the group generated by the elements $x$ and $y$ and subject to the following relations: $x^2 = y^3$, $y^6 = 1$, and $x^{-1}yx = y^{-1}$. Express in simplest form the inverse of the element $z = x^{-2}yx^3y^3$.

(A) $y^{-2}x^{-1}$
(B) $xy^2$
(C) $xy$
(D) $yx$
(E) $y^2x$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The element $z$ simplifies to
$$
z=x^{-1}y^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Insert parentheses so that the defining relations can be applied:
$$
\begin{aligned}
z
&=x^{-2}yx^3y^3\\
&=x^{-1}(x^{-1}yx)x^2y^3\\
&=x^{-1}y^{-1}x^2y^3\\
&=x^{-1}y^{-1}y^3y^3\\
&=x^{-1}y^{-1}y^6\\
&=x^{-1}y^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here the third equality uses $x^{-1}yx=y^{-1}$, the fourth uses $x^2=y^3$, and the sixth uses $y^6=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
z=(yx)^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For any group elements $a,b$, $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}$. Hence
$$
(yx)^{-1}=x^{-1}y^{-1},
$$
which equals $z$ by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{z^{-1}=yx},
$$
so the answer is (D).


::: pf-proof

Taking inverses in the identity of step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives $z^{-1}=yx$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the requested inverse.

:::

:::
