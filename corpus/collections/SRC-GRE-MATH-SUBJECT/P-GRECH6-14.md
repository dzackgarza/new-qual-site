::: {.problem}
Let $H$ be the set of all group homomorphisms $\phi\colon \ZZ_3 \to \ZZ_6$. How many functions does $H$ contain?

(A) $1$
(B) $2$
(C) $3$
(D) $4$
(E) $6$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

A homomorphism $\phi\colon\ZZ_3\to\ZZ_6$ is determined uniquely by $\phi(\overline1)$.


::: pf-proof

The additive group $\ZZ_3$ is generated by $\overline1$. For every $k\in\ZZ$,
$$
\phi(\overline{k})=k\phi(\overline1),
$$
so fixing the image of $\overline1$ fixes the entire homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The possible values of $\phi(\overline1)$ are exactly $\overline0$, $\overline2$, and $\overline4$ in $\ZZ_6$.


::: pf-proof

Since $3\overline1=\overline0$ in $\ZZ_3$, a homomorphism must satisfy
$$
3\phi(\overline1)=\overline0
$$
in $\ZZ_6$. For $a\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$, the congruence $3a\equiv0\pmod6$ holds exactly when $a$ is even, namely for $a=0,2,4$. Conversely, each such choice defines a homomorphism by $\phi(\overline{k})=k\overline a$, because the relation $3\overline1=0$ is respected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Hence $H$ contains
$$
\boxed{3}
$$
functions, so the answer is (C).


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, distinct allowed images of $\overline1$ give distinct homomorphisms, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives exactly three allowed images.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the requested cardinality.

:::

:::

:::
