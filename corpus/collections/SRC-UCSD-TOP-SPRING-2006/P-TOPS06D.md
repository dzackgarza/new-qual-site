::: problem
Let $X$ be a CW complex with one $0$-cell, one $1$-cell, two $2$-cells and one $4$-cell.
Assume that the attaching maps of the two $2$-cells to the $1$-cell have degree $2$ and $4$ respectively.

(a) Calculate the Euler characteristic of $X$.

(b) Calculate the integral and mod $2$ homology of $X \times \mathbb{RP}^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Cellular chain complex and homology of $X$:
<2>1. The cellular chain groups of $X$ are:
$C_0 = \mathbb{Z}\langle e^0 \rangle$, $C_1 = \mathbb{Z}\langle e^1 \rangle$, $C_2 = \mathbb{Z}\langle e_1^2, e_2^2 \rangle$, $C_3 = 0$, $C_4 = \mathbb{Z}\langle e^4 \rangle$.
Proof: given CW structure of $X$.
<2>2. The cellular boundary maps are:
- $d_1 = 0$ (single 0-cell).
- $d_2(e_1^2) = 2e^1$ and $d_2(e_2^2) = 4e^1$, so $\operatorname{im}(d_2) = 2\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Z}$.
- $d_3 = 0$ and $d_4 = 0$ (since $C_3 = 0$).
Proof: degrees of attaching maps.
<2>3. The integral homology groups of $X$ are:
- $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$
- $H_1(X) = \ker(d_1)/\operatorname{im}(d_2) = \mathbb{Z} / 2\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$
- $H_2(X) = \ker(d_2)/\operatorname{im}(d_3) = \{(a, b) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : 2a + 4b = 0\} \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (generated by $(-2, 1)$)
- $H_3(X) = 0$
- $H_4(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and $H_k(X) = 0$ for $k > 4$.
Proof: homology = kernel / image.

<1>2. Part (a): Calculate the Euler characteristic $\chi(X)$:
<2>1. Using the cell counts:
\[
\chi(X) = c_0 - c_1 + c_2 - c_3 + c_4 = 1 - 1 + 2 - 0 + 1 = 3.
\]
Proof: Euler characteristic formula $\chi(X) = \sum (-1)^n c_n$.

<1>3. Part (b): Integral homology of $X \times \mathbb{RP}^2$:
<2>1. The homology of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is $H_0(\mathbb{RP}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, and $H_k(\mathbb{RP}^2) = 0$ for $k \ge 2$.
Proof: standard homology of real projective plane.
<2>2. Apply the Künneth Theorem for integral homology:
\[
0 \to \bigoplus_{i+j=k} H_i(X) \otimes H_j(\mathbb{RP}^2) \to H_k(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2) \to \bigoplus_{i+j=k-1} \operatorname{Tor}_1(H_i(X), H_j(\mathbb{RP}^2)) \to 0.
\]
Proof: Künneth formula for CW complexes.
<2>3. Evaluate $H_k(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z})$ degree by degree:
- $k=0$: $\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
- $k=1$: $(\mathbb{Z}_2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}) \oplus (\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_2$.
- $k=2$: $(\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}) \oplus (\mathbb{Z}_2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_2$.
- $k=3$: $(\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_2) \oplus \operatorname{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}_2, \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_2$.
- $k=4$: $\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
- $k=5$: $\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$.
- $k \ge 6$: $0$.
Proof: tensor product and Tor evaluations.

<1>4. Part (b): Mod 2 homology of $X \times \mathbb{RP}^2$:
<2>1. Mod 2 boundary maps on $C_*(X; \mathbb{Z}_2)$ are all zero because $2 \equiv 4 \equiv 0 \pmod 2$.
Thus $H_0(X; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, $H_1(X; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, $H_2(X; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^2$, $H_3(X; \mathbb{Z}_2) = 0$, $H_4(X; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$.
Proof: cellular chain complex over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
<2>2. For $\mathbb{RP}^2$, $H_0(\mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, $H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, $H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$.
Proof: mod 2 homology of $\mathbb{RP}^2$.
<2>3. By the Künneth Theorem over the field $\mathbb{Z}_2$:
- $H_0(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$
- $H_1(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^2$
- $H_2(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^4$
- $H_3(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^3$
- $H_4(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^3$
- $H_5(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$
- $H_6(X \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, and $0$ for $k \ge 7$.
Proof: graded tensor product of $\mathbb{Z}_2$-vector spaces.

<1>5. Conclusion:
$\chi(X) = 3$, with integral and mod 2 homology as listed above. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 through <1>4.
:::
