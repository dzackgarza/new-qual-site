::: {.problem}
Let $X=Y=\mathbb R$ with their Borel Lebesgue measures $m_X,m_Y$.
Prove that every open subset of
\[
X\times Y=\mathbb R^2
\]
is measurable for the product measure $m_X\times m_Y$; equivalently, the product measure is a Borel measure.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\]
denote the Borel $\sigma$-algebra on $\mathbb R$.
The product measure
\[
m_X\times m_Y
\]
is defined on the product $\sigma$-algebra
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\otimes
\mathcal B(\mathbb R).
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Every open rectangle
\[
U\times V
\subseteq
\mathbb R^2
\]
with $U,V$ open in $\mathbb R$ belongs to
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\otimes
\mathcal B(\mathbb R).
\]


::: pf-proof

Open subsets of $\mathbb R$ are Borel.
Hence
\[
U,V
\in
\mathcal B(\mathbb R).
\]
By definition, the product $\sigma$-algebra is generated by measurable
rectangles
\[
A\times B
\]
with
\[
A,B\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R).
\]
Thus every open rectangle belongs to it.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Let
\[
\mathcal Q
\coloneqq
\{
(a,b)\times(c,d):
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Q,
\ a<b,
\ c<d
\}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal Q
\]
is a countable basis for the Euclidean topology on
\[
\mathbb R^2.
\]


::: pf-proof

The rational numbers are countable, so
\[
\mathbb Q^4
\]
is countable.
Hence $\mathcal Q$ is countable.

Let
\[
O\subseteq\mathbb R^2
\]
be open and let
\[
(x,y)\in O.
\]
There exists
\[
\varepsilon>0
\]
such that
\[
(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)
\times
(y-\varepsilon,y+\varepsilon)
\subseteq
O.
\]
Using density of $\mathbb Q$, choose rational numbers
\[
a<x<b,
\qquad
c<y<d
\]
with
\[
(a,b)
\subseteq
(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)
\]
and
\[
(c,d)
\subseteq
(y-\varepsilon,y+\varepsilon).
\]
Then
\[
(x,y)
\in
(a,b)\times(c,d)
\subseteq
O.
\]
Thus $\mathcal Q$ is a basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Every open set
\[
O\subseteq\mathbb R^2
\]
is a countable union of members of
\[
\mathcal Q.
\]


::: pf-proof

Define
\[
\mathcal Q_O
\coloneqq
\{
Q\in\mathcal Q:
Q\subseteq O
\}.
\]
Because
\[
\mathcal Q
\]
is countable, so is
\[
\mathcal Q_O.
\]

By the basis property from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, every point of $O$ lies in some
\[
Q\in\mathcal Q_O.
\]
Therefore
\[
O
=
\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal Q_O}Q,
\]
a countable union.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Every open subset of
\[
\mathbb R^2
\]
belongs to
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\otimes
\mathcal B(\mathbb R).
\]


::: pf-proof

Each
\[
Q\in\mathcal Q
\]
is an open rectangle, hence belongs to the product $\sigma$-algebra by
step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, any open set is a countable union of such rectangles.
A $\sigma$-algebra is closed under countable unions.
Hence every open set belongs to
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\otimes
\mathcal B(\mathbb R).
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Consequently
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R^2)
\subseteq
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\otimes
\mathcal B(\mathbb R).
\]


::: pf-proof

The Borel $\sigma$-algebra on
\[
\mathbb R^2
\]
is generated by the open subsets.
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows all open subsets already lie in the product
$\sigma$-algebra.
Therefore the $\sigma$-algebra they generate is contained in it.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Conversely,
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\otimes
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\subseteq
\mathcal B(\mathbb R^2).
\]


::: pf-proof

Let
\[
\pi_1,\pi_2:
\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R
\]
be the coordinate projections.
They are continuous.
Hence for every Borel set
\[
A\subseteq\mathbb R,
\]
\[
\pi_1^{-1}(A)
=
A\times\mathbb R
\]
is Borel in
\[
\mathbb R^2,
\]
and similarly
\[
\pi_2^{-1}(B)
=
\mathbb R\times B
\]
is Borel.

Therefore
\[
A\times B
=
\pi_1^{-1}(A)
\cap
\pi_2^{-1}(B)
\]
is Borel in $\mathbb R^2$.
Since such rectangles generate the product $\sigma$-algebra, the stated
containment follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B(\mathbb R^2)
=
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\otimes
\mathcal B(\mathbb R).
}
\]


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence every open subset of
\[
\mathbb R^2
\]
is measurable for the product measure
\[
m_X\times m_Y.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
m_X\times m_Y
\text{ is a Borel measure on }\mathbb R^2.
}
\]


::: pf-proof

The product measure is defined on
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R)
\otimes
\mathcal B(\mathbb R),
\]
which equals
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbb R^2)
\]
by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the desired conclusion.

:::
