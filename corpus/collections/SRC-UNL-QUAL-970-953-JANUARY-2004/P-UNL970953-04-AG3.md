::: {.problem}
Let $C\subset\CP^2$ be the zero locus of a homogeneous polynomial of degree $5$ and assume that $C$ is smooth.
Let $D$ be a divisor on $C$ of degree $11$.
What is the most you can say about
\[
\dim\Gamma(D)?
\]
For example: is it positive; can you give a lower bound; can you give an upper bound; or can you determine it exactly?
Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
\[
\ell(D)
\coloneqq
\dim_{\CC}
\Gamma(C,\mathcal O_C(D)).
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The canonical bundle of the smooth plane quintic $C$ is
\[
K_C
\cong
\mathcal O_C(2).
\]


::: pf-proof

The canonical bundle of the projective plane is
\[
K_{\mathbb{CP}^2}
\cong
\mathcal O_{\mathbb{CP}^2}(-3).
\]
The curve $C$ is a smooth divisor of degree $5$, so
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathbb{CP}^2}(C)
\cong
\mathcal O_{\mathbb{CP}^2}(5).
\]
By the adjunction formula,
\[
K_C
\cong
\left(
K_{\mathbb{CP}^2}
\otimes
\mathcal O_{\mathbb{CP}^2}(C)
\right)|_C.
\]
Therefore
\[
K_C
\cong
\mathcal O_C(-3+5)
=
\mathcal O_C(2).
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
\[
\deg K_C=10.
\]


::: pf-proof

The line bundle
\[
\mathcal O_C(1)
\]
has degree equal to the degree of the plane curve $C$, namely $5$.
Therefore
\[
\deg\mathcal O_C(2)
=
2\deg\mathcal O_C(1)
=
10.
\]
Apply step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The genus of $C$ is
\[
\boxed{g(C)=6}.
\]


::: pf-proof

For a smooth projective curve,
\[
\deg K_C=2g-2.
\]
Using step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
\[
2g-2=10.
\]
Hence
\[
2g=12
\]
and therefore
\[
g=6.
\]
Equivalently, this is the standard smooth-plane-curve formula
\[
g
=
\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}2
\]
with $d=5$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Riemann--Roch gives
\[
\ell(D)-\ell(K_C-D)
=
\deg D+1-g.
\]


::: pf-proof

This is the Riemann--Roch theorem for divisors on a smooth projective
curve. Applied to the divisor $D$ on $C$, it states
\[
h^0(C,\mathcal O_C(D))
-
h^0(C,\mathcal O_C(K_C-D))
=
\deg D+1-g(C).
\]
The two $h^0$ terms are exactly
\[
\ell(D)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\ell(K_C-D).
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The divisor
\[
K_C-D
\]
has degree
\[
-1.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and the hypothesis
\[
\deg D=11,
\]
one has
\[
\deg(K_C-D)
=
\deg K_C-\deg D
=
10-11
=
-1.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

One has
\[
\ell(K_C-D)=0.
\]


::: pf-proof

Suppose there were a nonzero section
\[
s\in
H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(K_C-D)).
\]
Its zero divisor
\[
(s)_0
\]
is effective and represents the divisor class $K_C-D$. Therefore
\[
\deg(K_C-D)
=
\deg (s)_0
\ge
0.
\]
This contradicts step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, which gives degree $-1$.

Hence no nonzero section exists, so
\[
\ell(K_C-D)=0.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\dim\Gamma(D)
=
\ell(D)
=
6.
}
\]


::: pf-proof

Substitute
\[
\deg D=11,
\qquad
g=6,
\qquad
\ell(K_C-D)=0
\]
from the hypothesis and steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} into Riemann--Roch:
\[
\ell(D)-0
=
11+1-6
=
6.
\]
Thus the dimension is determined exactly, not merely bounded.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is the strongest possible answer to the question.
