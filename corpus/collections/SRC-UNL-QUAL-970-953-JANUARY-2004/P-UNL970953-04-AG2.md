::: {.problem}
Find the multiplicity of each singular point of the zero locus of
\[
xyz(x+y)-x^4-y^4
\]
in $\CP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
F(x,y,z)
\coloneqq
xyz(x+y)-x^4-y^4.
\]

<1>1. The partial derivatives are
\[
F_x
=
yz(2x+y)-4x^3,
\]
\[
F_y
=
xz(x+2y)-4y^3,
\]
and
\[
F_z
=
xy(x+y).
\]

::: {.proof}
Differentiate
\[
F=x^2yz+xy^2z-x^4-y^4
\]
term by term.
:::

<1>2. Any singular point
\[
[x:y:z]\in\mathbb{CP}^2
\]
must satisfy
\[
xy(x+y)=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
A singular point of the projective plane curve
\[
F=0
\]
must satisfy
\[
F=F_x=F_y=F_z=0.
\]
By step <1>1,
\[
F_z=xy(x+y),
\]
so the displayed equation is necessary.
:::

<1>3. If
\[
x=0,
\]
then the only point of the curve is
\[
[0:0:1].
\]

::: {.proof}
Substituting $x=0$ into the defining equation gives
\[
F(0,y,z)
=
-y^4.
\]
Thus $F=0$ forces
\[
y=0.
\]
Projective coordinates cannot all vanish, so necessarily
\[
z\ne0.
\]
Hence the point is
\[
[0:0:1].
\]
:::

<1>4. If
\[
y=0,
\]
then the only point of the curve is again
\[
[0:0:1].
\]

::: {.proof}
Now
\[
F(x,0,z)
=
-x^4.
\]
Thus $F=0$ forces $x=0$, and projectivity again gives the point
\[
[0:0:1].
\]
:::

<1>5. If
\[
x+y=0,
\]
then the only point of the curve is again
\[
[0:0:1].
\]

::: {.proof}
Write
\[
y=-x.
\]
Then the term
\[
xyz(x+y)
\]
vanishes, while
\[
x^4+y^4
=
x^4+(-x)^4
=
2x^4.
\]
Hence
\[
F(x,-x,z)
=
-2x^4.
\]
The equation $F=0$ forces $x=0$, hence also $y=0$, so the projective point
is
\[
[0:0:1].
\]
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
P
\coloneqq
[0:0:1]
\]
is the only possible singular point.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, every singular point lies in one of the three cases
\[
x=0,
\qquad
y=0,
\qquad
x+y=0.
\]
Steps <1>3--<1>5 show that every case gives only $P$.
:::

<1>7. The point
\[
P=[0:0:1]
\]
is indeed singular.

::: {.proof}
Substituting
\[
x=y=0,
\qquad
z=1
\]
gives
\[
F(P)=0.
\]
Using the formulas in step <1>1,
\[
F_x(P)=F_y(P)=F_z(P)=0.
\]
Thus $P$ is singular.
:::

<1>8. In the affine chart
\[
z=1,
\]
the local equation at $P$ is
\[
f(x,y)
=
x^2y+xy^2-x^4-y^4.
\]

::: {.proof}
The point $P$ lies in the standard affine chart
\[
U_z=\{z\ne0\}.
\]
Dehomogenize by setting
\[
z=1.
\]
Then
\[
F(x,y,1)
=
xy(x+y)-x^4-y^4
=
x^2y+xy^2-x^4-y^4.
\]
The point $P$ corresponds to the affine origin $(0,0)$.
:::

<1>9. The lowest-degree nonzero homogeneous part of $f$ is
\[
f_3(x,y)
=
x^2y+xy^2
=
xy(x+y),
\]
which has degree $3$.

::: {.proof}
The equation in step <1>8 decomposes by total degree as
\[
f
=
\underbrace{x^2y+xy^2}_{\text{degree }3}
+
\underbrace{(-x^4-y^4)}_{\text{degree }4}.
\]
There are no nonzero terms of total degree $0$, $1$, or $2$, and the
degree-$3$ part is nonzero.
:::

<1>10. The multiplicity of $P$ on the curve is
\[
\boxed{3}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For an affine plane curve with local equation
\[
f(x,y)=0
\]
at the origin, the multiplicity is the least total degree of a nonzero
homogeneous component of $f$.

By step <1>9, that least degree is $3$. Hence
\[
\operatorname{mult}_P(F=0)=3.
\]
:::

<1>11. Thus the curve has exactly one singular point:
\[
\boxed{
[0:0:1],
}
\]
and it is a triple point.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>6--<1>7 show uniqueness and singularity, while step <1>10 gives
its multiplicity.
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>11 is the requested classification of all singular points and
their multiplicities.
:::
