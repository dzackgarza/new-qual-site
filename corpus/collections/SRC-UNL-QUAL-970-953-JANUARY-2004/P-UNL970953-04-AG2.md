::: {.problem}
Find the multiplicity of each singular point of the zero locus of
$$
xyz(x+y)-x^4-y^4
$$
in $\CP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
F(x,y,z)
\coloneqq
xyz(x+y)-x^4-y^4.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The partial derivatives are
$$
F_x
=
yz(2x+y)-4x^3,
$$
$$
F_y
=
xz(x+2y)-4y^3,
$$
and
$$
F_z
=
xy(x+y).
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
F=x^2yz+xy^2z-x^4-y^4
$$
term by term.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Any singular point
$$
[x:y:z]\in\CP^2
$$
must satisfy
$$
xy(x+y)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

A singular point of the projective plane curve
$$
F=0
$$
must satisfy
$$
F=F_x=F_y=F_z=0.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
F_z=xy(x+y),
$$
so the displayed equation is necessary.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If
$$
x=0,
$$
then the only point of the curve is
$$
[0:0:1].
$$


::: pf-proof

Substituting $x=0$ into the defining equation gives
$$
F(0,y,z)
=
-y^4.
$$
Thus $F=0$ forces
$$
y=0.
$$
Projective coordinates cannot all vanish, so necessarily
$$
z\ne0.
$$
Hence the point is
$$
[0:0:1].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If
$$
y=0,
$$
then the only point of the curve is again
$$
[0:0:1].
$$


::: pf-proof

Now
$$
F(x,0,z)
=
-x^4.
$$
Thus $F=0$ forces $x=0$, and projectivity again gives the point
$$
[0:0:1].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If
$$
x+y=0,
$$
then the only point of the curve is again
$$
[0:0:1].
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
y=-x.
$$
Then the term
$$
xyz(x+y)
$$
vanishes, while
$$
x^4+y^4
=
x^4+(-x)^4
=
2x^4.
$$
Hence
$$
F(x,-x,z)
=
-2x^4.
$$
The equation $F=0$ forces $x=0$, hence also $y=0$, so the projective point
is
$$
[0:0:1].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
P
\coloneqq
[0:0:1]
$$
is the only possible singular point.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, every singular point lies in one of the three cases
$$
x=0,
\qquad
y=0,
\qquad
x+y=0.
$$
Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} show that every case gives only $P$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The point
$$
P=[0:0:1]
$$
is indeed singular.


::: pf-proof

Substituting
$$
x=y=0,
\qquad
z=1
$$
gives
$$
F(P)=0.
$$
Using the formulas in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
F_x(P)=F_y(P)=F_z(P)=0.
$$
Thus $P$ is singular.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

In the affine chart
$$
z=1,
$$
the local equation at $P$ is
$$
f(x,y)
=
x^2y+xy^2-x^4-y^4.
$$


::: pf-proof

The point $P$ lies in the standard affine chart
$$
U_z=\{z\ne0\}.
$$
Dehomogenize by setting
$$
z=1.
$$
Then
$$
F(x,y,1)
=
xy(x+y)-x^4-y^4
=
x^2y+xy^2-x^4-y^4.
$$
The point $P$ corresponds to the affine origin $(0,0)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The lowest-degree nonzero homogeneous part of $f$ is
$$
f_3(x,y)
=
x^2y+xy^2
=
xy(x+y),
$$
which has degree $3$.


::: pf-proof

The equation in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} decomposes by total degree as
$$
f
=
\underbrace{x^2y+xy^2}_{\text{degree }3}
+
\underbrace{(-x^4-y^4)}_{\text{degree }4}.
$$
There are no nonzero terms of total degree $0$, $1$, or $2$, and the
degree-$3$ part is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The multiplicity of $P$ on the curve is
$$
\boxed{3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For an affine plane curve with local equation
$$
f(x,y)=0
$$
at the origin, the multiplicity is the least total degree of a nonzero
homogeneous component of $f$.

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, that least degree is $3$. Hence
$$
\operatorname{mult}_P(F=0)=3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Thus the curve has exactly one singular point:
$$
\boxed{
[0:0:1],
}
$$
and it is a triple point.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} show uniqueness and singularity, while step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives
its multiplicity.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} is the requested classification of all singular points and
their multiplicities.

:::
