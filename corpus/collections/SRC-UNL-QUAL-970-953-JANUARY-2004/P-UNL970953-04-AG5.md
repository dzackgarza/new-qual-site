::: {.problem}
(a) Show that the closed points of any scheme of finite type over a field are dense.

(b) Give an example of a nonempty scheme whose closed points are not dense.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $k$ be a field.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let
$$
A
$$
be a finitely generated $k$-algebra and let
$$
D(f)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A
$$
be a nonempty principal open subset. Then
$$
A_f\ne0.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
A_f=0,
$$
then some power of $f$ is zero in $A$. Hence every prime ideal of $A$
contains $f$, so
$$
D(f)=\varnothing,
$$
contrary to hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Choose a maximal ideal
$$
\mathfrak n
\subset
A_f.
$$
Then
$$
K
\coloneqq
A_f/\mathfrak n
$$
is a finite algebraic extension of $k$.


::: pf-proof

The ring $A_f$ is again a finitely generated $k$-algebra:
$$
A_f
\cong
A[T]/(fT-1).
$$
Its quotient
$$
K=A_f/\mathfrak n
$$
is a field and is finitely generated as a $k$-algebra. Zariski's lemma
therefore implies that $K/k$ is finite algebraic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let
$$
\mathfrak m
\coloneqq
\mathfrak n\cap A.
$$
Then
$$
f\notin\mathfrak m
$$
and $\mathfrak m$ is a maximal ideal of $A$.


::: pf-proof

Because $f$ is invertible in $A_f$, it belongs to no proper ideal of
$A_f$. Thus
$$
f\notin\mathfrak n,
$$
and consequently
$$
f\notin\mathfrak m.
$$

The natural map
$$
A/\mathfrak m
\longrightarrow
A_f/\mathfrak n
=
K
$$
is injective. Hence $A/\mathfrak m$ is a $k$-subalgebra of the
finite-dimensional $k$-vector space $K$. It is therefore finite-dimensional
over $k$.

Moreover, $A/\mathfrak m$ is an integral domain because
$\mathfrak m$ is prime, being the contraction of the prime ideal
$\mathfrak n$.

A finite-dimensional domain over a field is itself a field: if
$$
0\ne a\in A/\mathfrak m,
$$
multiplication by $a$ is an injective endomorphism of the finite-dimensional
$k$-vector space $A/\mathfrak m$, hence surjective; therefore
$$
ab=1
$$
for some $b$. Thus every nonzero element is invertible.

Hence $A/\mathfrak m$ is a field and $\mathfrak m$ is maximal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The point
$$
x_{\mathfrak m}
\in
\operatorname{Spec}A
$$
defined by $\mathfrak m$ lies in $D(f)$ and is a closed point.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
f\notin\mathfrak m,
$$
so
$$
x_{\mathfrak m}\in D(f).
$$
Since $\mathfrak m$ is maximal, the corresponding point is closed in
$$
\operatorname{Spec}A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Let $X$ be a scheme of finite type over $k$, and let
$$
U\subseteq X
$$
be a nonempty open subset. Then $U$ contains a point whose residue field is
a finite extension of $k$.


::: pf-proof

Choose
$$
x\in U.
$$
Because $X$ is a scheme, there is an affine open neighborhood
$$
V=\operatorname{Spec}A
$$
of $x$. Since $X$ is of finite type over $k$, the $k$-algebra $A$ is
finitely generated.

The set
$$
U\cap V
$$
is a nonempty open subset of the affine scheme $V$. Principal opens form a
basis, so there is some $f\in A$ with
$$
x\in D(f)\subseteq U\cap V.
$$

By steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $D(f)$ contains a maximal ideal
$$
\mathfrak m
$$
whose residue field
$$
\kappa(\mathfrak m)=A/\mathfrak m
$$
is finite over $k$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

A point of a scheme locally of finite type over $k$ whose residue
field is finite over $k$ is closed.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
y\in X
$$
have
$$
[\kappa(y):k]<\infty.
$$
The morphism
$$
\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(y)
\longrightarrow
X
$$
is then a finite-type point. For schemes locally of finite type over a
field, finite-type points are precisely closed points.

Equivalently, this is the standard Jacobson property for schemes locally
of finite type over a field; see Stacks Project, Lemma 33.14.1.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Every nonempty open subset of $X$ contains a closed point.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
U\subseteq X
$$
be nonempty. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
y\in U
$$
with residue field finite over $k$. Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} says that $y$ is closed in
$X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore the closed points of every finite-type $k$-scheme are
dense.


::: pf-proof

A subset $S$ of a topological space is dense if and only if every nonempty
open subset meets $S$.

Take $S$ to be the set of closed points of $X$. Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} says precisely
that every nonempty open subset of $X$ meets $S$. Hence
$$
\overline S=X.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For part (b), let
$$
R=k[[t]]
$$
and
$$
Y=\operatorname{Spec}R.
$$
Then $Y$ is nonempty and has exactly one closed point, namely
$$
(t).
$$


::: pf-proof

The formal power-series ring
$$
k[[t]]
$$
is a local ring with unique maximal ideal
$$
(t).
$$
Closed points of an affine scheme
$$
\operatorname{Spec}R
$$
correspond exactly to maximal ideals of $R$. Thus the only closed point of
$Y$ is $(t)$.

The spectrum is nonempty because $(0)$ is a prime ideal: $k[[t]]$ is an
integral domain.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The unique closed point
$$
(t)
$$
is not dense in $Y$.


::: pf-proof

The singleton
$$
\{(t)\}
$$
is already closed because $(t)$ is maximal. Hence its closure is itself:
$$
\overline{\{(t)\}}
=
\{(t)\}.
$$

But
$$
(0)\in\operatorname{Spec}k[[t]]
$$
and
$$
(0)\ne(t).
$$
Therefore
$$
\overline{\{(t)\}}
\ne
Y.
$$
So the set of closed points is not dense.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}k[[t]]
}
$$
is a nonempty scheme whose closed points are not dense.


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the conclusion of steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} provides the counterexample
requested in part (b).

:::
