::: {.problem}
Prove that
$$
(x^n+y^n+z^n)\subset\CC[x,y,z]
$$
is a prime ideal for every integer $n>0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Set
$$
F(x,y,z)
\coloneqq
x^n+y^n+z^n.
$$
Regard $F$ as a polynomial in $x$ with coefficients in
$$
R\coloneqq\CC[y,z].
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Choose
$$
\xi\in\CC
$$
with
$$
\xi^n=-1,
$$
and set
$$
p\coloneqq y-\xi z\in R.
$$
Then $p$ is a prime element of $R$.


::: pf-proof

The quotient by $(p)$ is
$$
R/(p)
\cong
\CC[z],
$$
via the substitution
$$
y=\xi z.
$$
Since $\CC[z]$ is an integral domain, $(p)$ is a prime ideal in
$R$, hence $p$ is a prime element.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
p\mid y^n+z^n
$$
but
$$
p^2\nmid y^n+z^n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Over $\CC$,
$$
y^n+z^n
=
\prod_{\eta^n=-1}
(y-\eta z).
$$
The $n$ roots $\eta$ of
$$
t^n+1
$$
are distinct because the derivative
$$
nt^{n-1}
$$
has no common root with $t^n+1$ in characteristic zero.

The chosen factor
$$
p=y-\xi z
$$
therefore occurs exactly once in the displayed factorization. Hence $p$
divides $y^n+z^n$, while $p^2$ does not.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $F$ factors nontrivially in
$$
R[x]:
\qquad
F=A B,
$$
with
$$
\deg_x A>0,
\qquad
\deg_x B>0.
$$
Then, after multiplying the factors by inverse nonzero constants, both
$A$ and $B$ may be taken monic in $x$.


::: pf-proof

The polynomial $F$ is monic in $x$. If
$$
a\in R
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b\in R
$$
are the leading coefficients of $A$ and $B$, then
$$
ab=1.
$$
Thus both $a$ and $b$ are units of
$$
R=\CC[y,z].
$$
The only units of this polynomial ring are the nonzero constants in
$$
\CC.
$$
Multiplying $A$ by $a^{-1}$ and $B$ by $a$ preserves their product and
makes both factors monic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Reducing the factorization in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} modulo $p$ gives
$$
x^n
=
\overline A\,\overline B
$$
in
$$
(R/(p))[x]
\cong
\CC[z][x].
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
y^n+z^n\equiv0\pmod p.
$$
Therefore
$$
F
=
x^n+y^n+z^n
\equiv
x^n
\pmod p.
$$
Reducing the equality
$$
F=AB
$$
modulo $p$ gives the displayed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If
$$
r=\deg_x A,
\qquad
s=\deg_x B,
$$
then
$$
\overline A=x^r,
\qquad
\overline B=x^s.
$$


::: pf-proof

The reductions $\overline A$ and $\overline B$ remain monic, so their
$x$-degrees remain $r$ and $s$.

In the domain
$$
\CC[z][x],
$$
the element $x$ is prime because the quotient by $(x)$ is
$$
\CC[z],
$$
an integral domain. Since
$$
\overline A\,\overline B=x^n,
$$
every irreducible factor of $\overline A$ and $\overline B$ must be
associated to $x$. Thus each factor is a unit times a power of $x$.
Because both are monic,
$$
\overline A=x^r,
\qquad
\overline B=x^s.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The constant terms of both $A$ and $B$ are divisible by $p$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
r>0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
s>0,
$$
the polynomials
$$
x^r
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x^s
$$
have zero constant term.

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, the reductions of $A$ and $B$ modulo $p$ have zero constant
term. Therefore the constant term of each original polynomial lies in
$(p)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} contradicts step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
a_0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b_0
$$
be the constant terms of $A$ and $B$ as polynomials in $x$. Since
$$
F=AB,
$$
their product is the constant term of $F$:
$$
a_0b_0
=
y^n+z^n.
$$

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
p\mid a_0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p\mid b_0.
$$
Hence
$$
p^2\mid a_0b_0
=
y^n+z^n,
$$
contradicting step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
F=x^n+y^n+z^n
$$
is irreducible in
$$
\CC[x,y,z].
$$


::: pf-proof

Any nontrivial factorization would have positive $x$-degree in both
factors after removing unit factors, and steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} show that no such
factorization exists.

Since $F$ is monic in $x$, it cannot have a nonconstant factor lying
entirely in $R=\CC[y,z]$: such a factor would divide the leading
coefficient $1$. Thus all possible nontrivial factorizations have already
been excluded.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The ring
$$
\CC[x,y,z]
$$
is a unique factorization domain, so the irreducible element $F$ is prime.


::: pf-proof

A polynomial ring over a field is a UFD, and adjoining polynomial variables
to a UFD preserves unique factorization. Hence
$$
\CC[x,y,z]
$$
is a UFD.

In a UFD every irreducible element is prime: if an irreducible $q$ divides
$ab$, unique factorization of $a$ and $b$ shows that the irreducible factor
$q$ must occur in one of those two factorizations, so $q$ divides $a$ or
$b$. This standard implication is also Stacks Project, Lemma 10.120.5.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
(x^n+y^n+z^n)
\subset
\CC[x,y,z]
\text{ is prime}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

A principal ideal $(q)$ in a domain is prime exactly when $q$ is a prime
element. Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} shows that
$$
q=F=x^n+y^n+z^n
$$
is prime.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} is the desired conclusion for every integer $n>0$.

:::
