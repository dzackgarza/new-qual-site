::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal F$ be the structure sheaf of $\CP^2$, and let $\mathcal I$ be the sheaf of ideals defining two distinct reduced points $p,q\in\CP^2$.
Define a presheaf $\mathcal G$ by
$$
\mathcal G(U)=\mathcal F(U)/\mathcal I(U)
$$
for every Zariski-open set $U\subseteq\CP^2$.

(a) Show that $\mathcal G$ is not a sheaf.

(b) Determine the associated sheaf $\mathcal G^+$; that is, determine $\mathcal G^+(U)$ for every Zariski-open set $U\subseteq\CP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
X=\CP^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Z=\{p,q\}.
$$
The ideal sheaf $\mathcal I$ is the ideal sheaf of the reduced closed
subscheme $Z\subset X$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let
$$
U_p=X\setminus\{q\},
\qquad
U_q=X\setminus\{p\}.
$$
Then
$$
U_p\cup U_q=X
$$
and on their intersection
$$
U_p\cap U_q=X\setminus\{p,q\}
$$
one has
$$
\mathcal I|_{U_p\cap U_q}
=
\mathcal F|_{U_p\cap U_q}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The two sets $\{p\}$ and $\{q\}$ are Zariski closed, so $U_p$ and
$U_q$ are Zariski open and clearly cover $X$.

The quotient sheaf
$$
\mathcal F/\mathcal I
$$
is supported on the closed subscheme $Z$. Thus away from $p$ and $q$ the
ideal sheaf is the unit ideal:
$$
\mathcal I_x=\mathcal F_x
\qquad
(x\notin Z).
$$
Equivalently, after restricting to
$$
X\setminus Z,
$$
one has
$$
\mathcal I=\mathcal F.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Consequently
$$
\mathcal G(U_p\cap U_q)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\mathcal I(U_p\cap U_q)
=
\mathcal F(U_p\cap U_q).
$$
Therefore
$$
\mathcal G(U_p\cap U_q)
=
\frac{\mathcal F(U_p\cap U_q)}
{\mathcal I(U_p\cap U_q)}
=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let
$$
s_p
\coloneqq
[1]
\in
\mathcal G(U_p)
$$
and
$$
s_q
\coloneqq
[0]
\in
\mathcal G(U_q).
$$
Then $s_p$ and $s_q$ have equal restrictions to $U_p\cap U_q$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the target of both restriction maps is the zero group.
Hence both restricted sections are zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The compatible pair
$$
(s_p,s_q)
$$
does not come from any section of $\mathcal G(X)$.


::: pf-proof

Every global regular function on projective space is constant:
$$
\mathcal F(X)
=
H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)
=
\CC.
$$
Moreover, the only constant regular function vanishing at both $p$ and $q$
is zero, so
$$
\mathcal I(X)=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\mathcal G(X)
=
\CC.
$$

Suppose a class
$$
c\in\CC
$$
glued the pair in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.
Its restriction to $U_p$ would satisfy
$$
[c]=[1]
\quad\text{in }\mathcal G(U_p),
$$
so
$$
c-1\in\mathcal I(U_p).
$$
Every section of $\mathcal I(U_p)$ vanishes at $p$, hence
$$
c-1=0,
\qquad
c=1.
$$

Its restriction to $U_q$ would satisfy
$$
[c]=[0]
\quad\text{in }\mathcal G(U_q),
$$
so
$$
c\in\mathcal I(U_q).
$$
Evaluating at $q$ gives
$$
c=0.
$$
This contradicts $c=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal G
\text{ is not a sheaf}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} exhibit compatible local sections on the open cover
$$
X=U_p\cup U_q
$$
which admit no global gluing. This violates the sheaf gluing axiom.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every point $x\in X$, the stalk of the presheaf $\mathcal G$
is
$$
\mathcal G_x
\cong
\mathcal F_x/\mathcal I_x.
$$


::: pf-proof

Taking a stalk is a filtered colimit over neighborhoods of $x$.
Filtered colimits of modules are exact, so
$$
\varinjlim_{x\in U}
\frac{\mathcal F(U)}{\mathcal I(U)}
\cong
\frac{
\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathcal F(U)
}{
\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathcal I(U)
}
=
\mathcal F_x/\mathcal I_x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

These stalks are
$$
\mathcal G_x
\cong
\begin{cases}
\CC,&x=p,\\
\CC,&x=q,\\
0,&x\notin\{p,q\}.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $p$ and $q$ are reduced closed points over $\CC$,
$$
\mathcal F_p/\mathcal I_p
\cong
\kappa(p)
\cong
\CC
$$
and similarly
$$
\mathcal F_q/\mathcal I_q
\cong
\CC.
$$

If
$$
x\notin\{p,q\},
$$
then the ideal sheaf is the unit ideal near $x$, so
$$
\mathcal I_x=\mathcal F_x
$$
and the quotient stalk is zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
i_p:\{p\}\hookrightarrow X,
\qquad
i_q:\{q\}\hookrightarrow X.
$$
Then the sheaf
$$
\mathcal Q
\coloneqq
(i_p)_*\CC
\oplus
(i_q)_*\CC
$$
has the same stalks as in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

The skyscraper sheaf
$$
(i_p)_*\CC
$$
has stalk $\CC$ at $p$ and zero stalk at every other point.
Likewise
$$
(i_q)_*\CC
$$
has stalk $\CC$ at $q$ and zero elsewhere.
Taking their direct sum gives exactly the stalk description in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The natural morphism of presheaves
$$
\mathcal G
\longrightarrow
\mathcal F/\mathcal I
$$
induces an isomorphism after sheafification:
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal G^+
\cong
\mathcal F/\mathcal I
\cong
(i_p)_*\CC
\oplus
(i_q)_*\CC.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The cokernel of a morphism of sheaves of modules is obtained by
sheafifying the presheaf cokernel. Here the inclusion
$$
\mathcal I\hookrightarrow\mathcal F
$$
has presheaf cokernel
$$
U
\longmapsto
\mathcal F(U)/\mathcal I(U)
=
\mathcal G(U).
$$
Hence its sheafification is precisely the quotient sheaf
$$
\mathcal F/\mathcal I.
$$

Since $Z=\{p,q\}$ is reduced, its structure sheaf is the direct sum of
the two residue fields:
$$
\mathcal O_Z
\cong
(i_p)_*\CC
\oplus
(i_q)_*\CC.
$$
But
$$
\mathcal O_Z
\cong
\mathcal F/\mathcal I,
$$
which gives the displayed isomorphisms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Explicitly, for every Zariski-open set $U\subseteq X$,
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal G^+(U)
\cong
\bigoplus_{r\in U\cap\{p,q\}}
\CC.
}
$$
Thus
$$
\mathcal G^+(U)
\cong
\begin{cases}
0,&p,q\notin U,\\
\CC,&\text{exactly one of }p,q\text{ lies in }U,\\
\CC\oplus\CC,&p,q\in U.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, $\mathcal G^+$ is the direct sum of the two skyscraper
sheaves. A section of a skyscraper sheaf over $U$ is the residue field if
its support point belongs to $U$, and is zero otherwise. Summing the two
contributions gives the formula.

:::

sheaf required in part (b).
