::: {.problem}
How many isomorphism classes of groups of order 45 are there? Describe a representative from each class with full justification.
:::

::: {.solution}
Throughout $G$ is a group of order $45 = 3^{2}\cdot 5$, $n_{p}$ is the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$, $P$ is a Sylow $3$-subgroup and $Q$ a Sylow $5$-subgroup. The collection's `provenance` is the Spring 2019 examination paper, and no solutions sheet accompanies it, so the argument below is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #sylow-counts}
$n_{5} = n_{3} = 1$, so $P$ and $Q$ are the unique Sylow subgroups of their orders and are normal in $G$.

::: pf-proof
The Sylow theorems give $n_{5} \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ and $n_{5} \mid 9$: the divisors of $9$ are $1, 3, 9$, of which only $1 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$, so $n_{5} = 1$. Similarly $n_{3} \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $n_{3} \mid 5$: the divisors of $5$ are $1, 5$, of which only $1 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, so $n_{3} = 1$. A subgroup that is the unique one of its order is carried to itself by every automorphism $g \mapsto gxg^{-1}$, so it is normal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product}
$G \cong P \times Q$.

::: pf-proof
$\cardinality{P} = 9$ and $\cardinality{Q} = 5$, so $P \cap Q$ has order dividing both, that is dividing $\gcd(9,5) = 1$, and $P \cap Q = \theset{e}$. Both $P$ and $Q$ are normal by step [](#sylow-counts){.pf-ref}, so $PQ$ is a subgroup, with $\cardinality{PQ} = \cardinality{P}\cardinality{Q}/\cardinality{P\cap Q} = 45 = \cardinality{G}$; hence $PQ = G$. A group generated by two normal subgroups with trivial intersection is their direct product, the map $(x,y) \mapsto xy$ being an isomorphism: it is a homomorphism because elements of $P$ commute with elements of $Q$ — for $x \in P$ and $y \in Q$ the commutator $xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ lies in $P$ (as $P$ is normal) and in $Q$ (as $Q$ is normal), hence is $e$ — and it is bijective because $PQ = G$ and $P \cap Q = \theset{e}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #factors}
$Q \cong \ZZ/5\ZZ$, and $P$ is isomorphic to $\ZZ/9\ZZ$ or to $\ZZ/3\ZZ \times \ZZ/3\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
$\cardinality{Q} = 5$ is prime, so $Q$ is cyclic, generated by any nonidentity element. A group of order $p^{2}$ is abelian: its centre is nontrivial by the class equation, and if the centre were proper then $G/Z(G)$ would be cyclic of order $p$, which forces $G$ abelian — a contradiction — so $G = Z(G)$. Hence $P$ is abelian of order $9$, and by the fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups it is $\ZZ/9\ZZ$ or $\ZZ/3\ZZ \times \ZZ/3\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
There are exactly $\boxed{2}$ isomorphism classes of groups of order $45$, represented by $\ZZ/45\ZZ$ and $\ZZ/3\ZZ \times \ZZ/15\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#product){.pf-ref} and [](#factors){.pf-ref} every such $G$ is $P \times Q$ with $Q \cong \ZZ/5$ and $P \in \theset{\ZZ/9, \ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/3}$. In the first case $G \cong \ZZ/9 \times \ZZ/5 \cong \ZZ/45$ by the Chinese remainder theorem, since $\gcd(9,5) = 1$. In the second $G \cong \ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/5 \cong \ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/15$, again by the Chinese remainder theorem applied to $\ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/5$. The two are not isomorphic: $\ZZ/45$ has an element of order $45$, while every element of $\ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/15$ is killed by $15$, so its exponent is $15$. Since the two cases are exhaustive and give non-isomorphic groups, the count is two.

:::
