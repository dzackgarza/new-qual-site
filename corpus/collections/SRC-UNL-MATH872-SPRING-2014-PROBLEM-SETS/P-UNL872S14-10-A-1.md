::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 79 #1.

Let
\[
p:\widetilde X\to X
\]
be a covering space and let $A\subset X$. Put
\[
\widetilde A=p^{-1}(A).
\]
Show that the restriction
\[
p|_{\widetilde A}:\widetilde A\to A
\]
is a covering space.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Fix $a\in A$. Since $p:\widetilde X\to X$ is a covering, there is an open neighborhood $U\subset X$ of $a$ that is evenly covered.

::: {.proof}
This is the defining local property of a covering map.
:::

<1>2. Write
\[
p^{-1}(U)
=
\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_\lambda,
\]
where each $U_\lambda$ is open in $\widetilde X$ and
\[
p|_{U_\lambda}:U_\lambda\xrightarrow{\sim}U
\]
is a homeomorphism.

::: {.proof}
This is what it means for $U$ to be evenly covered.
:::

<1>3. The set
\[
V=U\cap A
\]
is an open neighborhood of $a$ in the subspace $A$.

::: {.proof}
Because $U$ is open in $X$, its intersection with $A$ is open in the subspace topology on $A$. Since $a\in U\cap A$, it is a neighborhood of $a$.
:::

<1>4. One has
\[
\boxed{
(p|_{\widetilde A})^{-1}(V)
=
\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
(U_\lambda\cap\widetilde A)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since
\[
\widetilde A=p^{-1}(A),
\]
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
(p|_{\widetilde A})^{-1}(V)
&=
p^{-1}(U\cap A)\cap\widetilde A\\
&=
p^{-1}(U)\cap p^{-1}(A)\\
&=
\left(
\bigsqcup_{\lambda}U_\lambda
\right)
\cap\widetilde A\\
&=
\bigsqcup_{\lambda}
(U_\lambda\cap\widetilde A).
\end{aligned}
\]
The union remains disjoint because the $U_\lambda$ are pairwise disjoint.
:::

<1>5. Each
\[
U_\lambda\cap\widetilde A
\]
is open in $\widetilde A$.

::: {.proof}
The set $U_\lambda$ is open in $\widetilde X$. Intersecting it with the subspace $\widetilde A$ gives an open subset of $\widetilde A$.
:::

<1>6. For every $\lambda$,
\[
\boxed{
p|_{U_\lambda\cap\widetilde A}:
U_\lambda\cap\widetilde A
\xrightarrow{\sim}
V}
\]
is a homeomorphism.

::: {.proof}
The map
\[
p|_{U_\lambda}:U_\lambda\to U
\]
is a homeomorphism by step <1>2. Under this homeomorphism,
\[
U_\lambda\cap\widetilde A
=
U_\lambda\cap p^{-1}(A)
\]
is exactly the inverse image of
\[
U\cap A=V.
\]
Restricting a homeomorphism to the inverse image of a subspace gives a homeomorphism onto that subspace.
:::

<1>7. Thus $V$ is evenly covered by
\[
p|_{\widetilde A}:\widetilde A\to A.
\]

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4--<1>6 give a disjoint union of open sheets over $V$, each mapped homeomorphically onto $V$.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
p|_{\widetilde A}:\widetilde A\to A
\text{ is a covering map}.}
\]

::: {.proof}
Every point $a\in A$ has an evenly covered neighborhood by step <1>7. This is exactly the definition of a covering map.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
