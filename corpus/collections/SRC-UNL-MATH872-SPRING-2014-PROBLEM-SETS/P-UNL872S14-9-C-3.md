::: {.problem}
Let X be the topological space obtained by taking a quotient of a Euclidean triangle, identifying the three vertices.
Find a CW complex structure for X and use this structure to compute the fundamental group for X.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let the triangle have vertices
\[
v_0,v_1,v_2,
\]
and orient its three boundary edges cyclically.

<1>1. After identifying the three vertices, there is a single \(0\)-cell
\[
\boxed{v}.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is exactly the quotient relation imposed on the three vertices.
:::

<1>2. The interiors of the three triangle edges remain three distinct open
\(1\)-cells.

::: {.proof}
The quotient identifies only the three vertices. No interior point of one
edge is identified with an interior point of another edge.
:::

<1>3. Denote the three oriented \(1\)-cells by
\[
\boxed{a,b,c}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Choose the cyclic orientations
\[
v_0\to v_1,\qquad
v_1\to v_2,\qquad
v_2\to v_0.
\]
After the vertices are identified, each edge becomes a loop based at \(v\).
:::

<1>4. The interior of the triangle is one open \(2\)-cell
\[
\boxed{e^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The quotient does not identify distinct interior points of the triangle.
Hence the open triangle interior descends homeomorphically to one open
\(2\)-cell.
:::

<1>5. Thus \(X\) has a CW structure with
\[
\boxed{
1\text{ zero-cell},\quad
3\text{ one-cells},\quad
1\text{ two-cell}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>1--<1>4.
:::

<1>6. The \(1\)-skeleton is
\[
\boxed{
X^{(1)}
\cong
S^1\vee S^1\vee S^1}.
\]

::: {.proof}
There is one vertex and three loop edges.
:::

<1>7. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X^{(1)},v)
\cong
F(a,b,c)},
\]
the free group on \(a,b,c\).

::: {.proof}
The fundamental group of a wedge of three circles is the free group of rank
three.
:::

<1>8. The attaching map of the \(2\)-cell reads the boundary word
\[
\boxed{abc}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Traversing the oriented boundary of the original triangle once follows the
three oriented edges in the order
\[
a,\quad b,\quad c.
\]
After the vertices are identified, this becomes the based loop \(abc\) in the
\(1\)-skeleton.
:::

<1>9. Seifert--van Kampen therefore gives
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b,c\mid abc=1\rangle}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Attaching one \(2\)-cell along the loop \(abc\) quotients
\[
\pi_1(X^{(1)})
\]
by the normal closure of that attaching word.
:::

<1>10. The relation
\[
abc=1
\]
is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
c=b^{-1}a^{-1}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Multiply
\[
abc=1
\]
on the left by \(b^{-1}a^{-1}\), or solve directly:
\[
c=(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}.
\]
:::

<1>11. Hence the generator \(c\) can be eliminated from the presentation.

::: {.proof}
A presentation relation expressing one generator uniquely as a word in the
others permits a Tietze transformation deleting that generator and the
relation.
:::

<1>12. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid\ \rangle
\cong
F_2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>11 to the presentation in step <1>9. The remaining presentation
has two generators and no relators.
:::

<1>13. In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\text{ is the free group on two generators}.}
\]

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>12.
:::

<1>14. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the CW structure and step <1>12 gives the fundamental group.
:::
