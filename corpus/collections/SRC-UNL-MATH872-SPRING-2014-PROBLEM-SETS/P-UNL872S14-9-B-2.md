::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 19 #17.

(a) Show that the mapping cylinder of every map
$$
f:S^1\to S^1
$$
is a CW complex.

(b) Construct a $2$-dimensional CW complex that contains both an annulus
$$
S^1\times I
$$
and a Möbius band as deformation retracts.
:::

::: {.solution}

## Part (a)

Let
$$
M_f
=
\left(
S^1_{\mathrm{target}}
\sqcup
(S^1_{\mathrm{domain}}\times I)
\right)
/((x,0)\sim f(x))
$$
be the mapping cylinder.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Choose a point
$$
x_0\in S^1_{\mathrm{domain}}
$$
and give the domain circle the CW structure with one $0$-cell $x_0$ and
one $1$-cell.


::: pf-proof

A circle is obtained from one $0$-cell by attaching one $1$-cell with
both endpoints sent to the $0$-cell.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Give the target circle the CW structure with one $0$-cell
$$
y_0=f(x_0)
$$
and one $1$-cell.


::: pf-proof

Again use the standard one-vertex, one-edge CW structure on $S^1$, choosing
the vertex to be the specified point $y_0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

With these CW structures, the map
$$
f:S^1_{\mathrm{domain}}\to S^1_{\mathrm{target}}
$$
is cellular.


::: pf-proof

A map is cellular if
$$
f(X^{(n)})\subseteq Y^{(n)}
$$
for every $n$. In dimension $0$,
$$
f(x_0)=y_0,
$$
so the domain $0$-skeleton maps to the target $0$-skeleton. In dimension
$1$, both whole circles are their own $1$-skeleta, so the condition is
automatic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The cylinder
$$
S^1_{\mathrm{domain}}\times I
$$
has a product CW structure with:
two $0$-cells,
three $1$-cells, and one $2$-cell.


::: pf-proof

Take the standard CW structure on $I$ with two $0$-cells and one open
$1$-cell. The product of CW structures gives cells
$$
e^p\times e^q
$$
of dimension $p+q$. Thus:
$$
1\cdot2=2
$$
zero-cells,
$$
1\cdot1+1\cdot2=3
$$
one-cells, and
$$
1\cdot1=1
$$
two-cell.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The subspace
$$
S^1_{\mathrm{domain}}\times\{0\}
$$
is a subcomplex of this product CW structure.


::: pf-proof

It is the product of the domain CW complex with the endpoint $0$, which is
a $0$-cell of the chosen CW structure on $I$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The mapping cylinder $M_f$ is the pushout obtained by attaching the
CW complex
$$
S^1_{\mathrm{domain}}\times I
$$
to the target circle along the cellular map
$$
f:S^1_{\mathrm{domain}}\times\{0\}\to
S^1_{\mathrm{target}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the defining quotient of the mapping cylinder.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Attaching a CW complex along a cellular map from a subcomplex produces
a CW complex.


::: pf-proof

Attach the cells not in the subcomplex in increasing dimension. Their
characteristic maps have boundaries landing in the union of the target and
lower-dimensional cells because the gluing map is cellular. This is the
standard CW pushout construction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{M_f\text{ is a CW complex}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} to the cellular pushout in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Moreover, $M_f$ is $2$-dimensional.


::: pf-proof

The target circle has cells only in dimensions $0,1$, and the product
cylinder in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} has no cells above dimension $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Both $A$ and $M$ deformation retract onto their core circles.


::: pf-proof

For the annulus, use
$$
H_A((z,t),u)
=
(z,(1-u)t).
$$
For the Möbius band, use the quotient model
$$
M=[0,1]\times[-1,1]/(0,t)\sim(1,-t)
$$
and the homotopy
$$
H_M([s,t],u)
=
[s,(1-u)t].
$$
Both homotopies fix the respective core circle pointwise.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Choose a homeomorphism
$$
\phi:C_A\xrightarrow{\sim}C_M
$$
and form the quotient
$$
\boxed{
Z=A\cup_{\phi}M}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Identify each point $x\in C_A$ with $\phi(x)\in C_M$. This glues the
annulus and Möbius band along their core circles.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The space $Z$ has a $2$-dimensional CW structure.


::: pf-proof

Give $A$ and $M$ finite $2$-dimensional CW structures in which the
core circles are subcomplexes, and choose $\phi$ to be cellular after
subdivision if necessary. Gluing CW complexes along a common subcomplex gives
a CW complex. No cells above dimension $2$ are introduced.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The space $Z$ deformation retracts onto the annulus $A$.


::: pf-proof

Keep every point of $A$ fixed. On the Möbius-band summand, apply the
deformation retraction
$$
H_M:M\times I\to M
$$
from step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} onto its core $C_M$, which is identified with
$$
C_A\subset A.
$$
Because $H_M$ fixes $C_M$ pointwise, this homotopy agrees with the
identity homotopy on $A$ along the gluing circle. Hence the two homotopies
paste to a deformation retraction
$$
Z\to A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

The space $Z$ also deformation retracts onto the Möbius band $M$.


::: pf-proof

Now keep $M$ fixed and apply the annulus deformation retraction
$$
H_A:A\times I\to A
$$
onto $C_A$, identified with $C_M\subset M$. Since $H_A$ fixes the core
circle pointwise, the homotopies paste to a deformation retraction
$$
Z\to M.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
Z=A\cup_{S^1}M}
$$
is the required $2$-dimensional CW complex containing both an annulus and a
Möbius band as deformation retracts.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} gives the required dimension and CW structure, while steps
[](#s1-13){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} give the two deformation retractions.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} constructs the space required in
part (b).

:::
