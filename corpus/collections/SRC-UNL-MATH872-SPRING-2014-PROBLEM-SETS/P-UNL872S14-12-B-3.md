::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a path-connected compact Hausdorff space.
The \dfn{cone} on $X$ is the quotient $CX$ of $X \times I$ by the equivalence relation making $(p,0)$ equivalent to $(q,0)$ for all $p,q \in X$, and the \dfn{suspension} of $X$ is the quotient $SX$ of $X \times I$ by the equivalence relation making $(p,0)$ equivalent to $(q,0)$ and $(p,1)$ equivalent to $(q,1)$ for all $p,q \in X$.

(a) If $X$ is a $\Delta$-complex, find $\Delta$-complex structures for $CX$ and $SX$.

(b) Show that the reduced homology groups of $CX$ satisfy $\tilde H_i(CX) = 0$ for all $i$.

(c) Show that the reduced homology groups of $SX$ satisfy $\tilde H_i(SX) \cong \tilde H_{i-1}(X)$ for all $i$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let the simplices of the given $\Delta$-complex $X$ be denoted by
$$
\sigma_\alpha^n.
$$

## Part (a): $\Delta$-complex structures

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Form the cone $CX$ by adjoining one new vertex
$$
\boxed{v}
$$
to $X$.


::: pf-proof

The quotient
$$
CX=(X\times I)/(X\times\{0\})
$$
collapses the entire bottom copy $X\times\{0\}$ to one point, the cone
vertex $v$. The top copy $X\times\{1\}$ remains a copy of $X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Retain every simplex
$$
\sigma_\alpha^n
$$
of the base $X\subset CX$.


::: pf-proof

The quotient map is injective on
$$
X\times\{1\},
$$
so the base is canonically embedded in $CX$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every $n$-simplex
$$
\sigma_\alpha^n=[v_0,\dots,v_n]
$$
of $X$, add the $(n+1)$-simplex
$$
\boxed{
v*\sigma_\alpha^n
=
[v,v_0,\dots,v_n]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The cone on the geometric simplex $\Delta^n$ is naturally homeomorphic to
$\Delta^{n+1}$. Coning the characteristic map of $\sigma_\alpha^n$
therefore gives a characteristic map for an $(n+1)$-simplex in $CX$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The faces of
$$
v*\sigma_\alpha^n
$$
are the base face
$$
\sigma_\alpha^n
$$
and the coned faces
$$
v*\tau
$$
where $\tau$ ranges over the faces of $\sigma_\alpha^n$.


::: pf-proof

This is the ordinary face structure of the simplex
$$
[v,v_0,\dots,v_n].
$$
Deleting the cone vertex $v$ gives the base simplex, while deleting one of
the vertices $v_i$ gives the cone on the corresponding face of
$\sigma_\alpha^n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence the simplices in steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} define a
$\Delta$-complex structure on $CX$.


::: pf-proof

The face identifications among the base simplices are those already present
in $X$. Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that every face of every new cone simplex is either
a base simplex or the cone on a lower-dimensional base face, so the
identifications are compatible and affine on faces.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For the suspension $SX$, take two cone vertices
$$
\boxed{v_-,v_+}
$$
and retain the equatorial copy of $X$.


::: pf-proof

The suspension quotient collapses
$$
X\times\{0\}
$$
to a south vertex $v_-$ and
$$
X\times\{1\}
$$
to a north vertex $v_+$. The middle slice
$$
X\times\{1/2\}
$$
is an embedded copy of $X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For every simplex $\sigma_\alpha^n\subset X$, add two
$(n+1)$-simplices
$$
\boxed{
v_-*\sigma_\alpha^n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
v_+*\sigma_\alpha^n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The lower half of the suspension is a cone on $X$, and the upper half is
another cone on $X$. Apply the cone construction from steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} to
each half.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The two cone $\Delta$-complexes agree exactly on their common base
$X$, so together they define a $\Delta$-complex structure
$$
\boxed{
SX=C_-X\cup_X C_+X}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every lower cone simplex meets every upper cone simplex only along faces in
the equatorial copy of $X$. Both use the original face identifications of
$X$ there. Hence the union is a valid $\Delta$-complex.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Define
$$
H:CX\times I\to CX
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
H([x,t],u)
=
[x,(1-u)t]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

At $t=0$, all points $(x,0)$ represent the cone vertex. The formula sends
all such representatives to
$$
[x,0],
$$
independent of $x$, so it is well defined on the quotient.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

At $u=0$,
$$
H(-,0)=\operatorname{id}_{CX}.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
H([x,t],0)
=
[x,t].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

At $u=1$,
$$
H([x,t],1)=v
$$
for every point of $CX$.


::: pf-proof

At $u=1$,
$$
H([x,t],1)
=
[x,0],
$$
which is the cone vertex $v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{CX\text{ is contractible}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} give a homotopy from the identity map to the constant map
at $v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_i(CX)=0
\qquad\text{for every }i}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Reduced homology is homotopy invariant, and the reduced homology of a point
vanishes in every degree. Apply step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
U
=
\{[x,t]\in SX:t<3/4\}
$$
together with the south suspension point, and let
$$
V
=
\{[x,t]\in SX:t>1/4\}
$$
together with the north suspension point.


::: pf-proof

More formally, take the images in the quotient of the saturated open subsets
$$
X\times[0,3/4)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
X\times(1/4,1]
$$
of $X\times I$. These form an open cover of $SX$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

The set $U$ is contractible.


::: pf-proof

It is a truncated cone on $X$ with apex $v_-$. The same contraction as
in step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} pushes every point to the south vertex.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

The set $V$ is contractible.


::: pf-proof

It is a truncated cone on $X$ with apex $v_+$. Contract it vertically
toward the north vertex.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

The intersection $U\cap V$ deformation retracts onto the equatorial
copy of $X$.


::: pf-proof

The intersection is represented by
$$
X\times(1/4,3/4).
$$
No suspension identification occurs in this interval, so
$$
U\cap V
\cong
X\times(1/4,3/4).
$$
The interval factor deformation retracts onto $1/2$, giving
$$
U\cap V\simeq X\times\{1/2\}\cong X.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_j(U)=
\widetilde H_j(V)=0}
$$
for every $j$, and
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_j(U\cap V)
\cong
\widetilde H_j(X)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The first assertion follows from steps [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}, and the second from
step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref} by homotopy invariance.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

The reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence contains
$$
\widetilde H_i(U)\oplus\widetilde H_i(V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_i(SX)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
\widetilde H_{i-1}(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_{i-1}(U)\oplus\widetilde H_{i-1}(V).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the reduced Mayer--Vietoris long exact sequence for the open cover
$$
SX=U\cup V.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

By step [](#s1-18){.pf-ref}, the sequence in step [](#s1-19){.pf-ref} reduces to
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_i(SX)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
\widetilde H_{i-1}(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both reduced homology groups of $U$ and $V$ vanish.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

Hence the connecting homomorphism is an isomorphism:
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_i(SX)
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\widetilde H_{i-1}(U\cap V)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Exactness of
$$
0\to A\xrightarrow{\delta}B\to0
$$
means that $\delta$ is injective and surjective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

Using step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref},
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_i(SX)
\cong
\widetilde H_{i-1}(X)}
$$
for every $i$.


::: pf-proof

Compose the isomorphism from step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref} with the homotopy-invariance
isomorphism
$$
\widetilde H_{i-1}(U\cap V)
\cong
\widetilde H_{i-1}(X)
$$
from step [](#s1-18){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $X$ is path connected, the formula in step [](#s1-22){.pf-ref} also
correctly gives
$$
\widetilde H_0(SX)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

A suspension of a nonempty space is path connected, so
$$
\widetilde H_0(SX)=0.
$$
Also
$$
\widetilde H_{-1}(X)=0
$$
under the standard reduced-homology convention for nonempty spaces. Thus the
shift formula is consistent in degree $0$.

:::
