::: {.problem}
In each part of this problem, a group G is given by a presentation, together with a familiar group H. Prove that the groups G and H are isomorphic.

(a) $\mathrm { G } = < \mathrm { a } \presentationbar \mathrm { a } ^ { 3 } > ;$ H is the cyclic group of order 3.

(b) $\mathrm { G = < a , b , c \mid a ^ { 2 } , b ^ { 2 } , c ^ { 2 } , ( a b ) ^ { 3 } , ( a c ) ^ { 2 } , ( b c ) ^ { 3 } > } ;$ H is the symmetric group permuting 4 objects.
:::

::: {.solution}

## Part (a)

Let
$$
G_3=\langle a\mid a^3=1\rangle.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

There is a surjective homomorphism
$$
\phi:G_3\to\ZZ/3
$$
sending
$$
a\longmapsto \overline1.
$$


::: pf-proof

The element $\overline1\in\ZZ/3$ satisfies
$$
3\overline1=0,
$$
so the relation $a^3=1$ is respected. The image contains the generator
$\overline1$, hence the map is surjective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every element of $G_3$ is represented by one of
$$
\boxed{1,\ a,\ a^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every word in the single generator reduces to $a^n$. The relation
$$
a^3=1
$$
reduces the exponent modulo $3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\cardinality{G_3}\le3.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives at most three distinct normal forms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Hence
$$
\boxed{G_3\cong\ZZ/3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives a surjection from $G_3$ onto a group of three elements,
while step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} says $G_3$ has at most three elements. Therefore $G_3$
has exactly three elements and the surjection is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The assignments
$$
\boxed{
a\longmapsto(12),\qquad
b\longmapsto(23),\qquad
c\longmapsto(34)}
$$
define a homomorphism
$$
\Phi:G\to S_4.
$$


::: pf-proof

Each transposition has order $2$. Also
$$
(12)(23)=(123)
$$
has order $3$,
$$
(12)(34)
$$
has order $2$, and
$$
(23)(34)=(234)
$$
has order $3$. Thus all defining relators of $G$ map to the identity, so
the presentation universal property gives $\Phi$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The homomorphism $\Phi$ is surjective.


::: pf-proof

The adjacent transpositions
$$
(12),\quad(23),\quad(34)
$$
generate the full symmetric group $S_4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Let
$$
H=\langle a,b\rangle\le G.
$$
Within $H$, one has the braid relation
$$
\boxed{aba=bab}.
$$


::: pf-proof

From
$$
(ab)^3=1
$$
and $a^2=b^2=1$,
$$
(ab)^3=1
\iff
abab=(ab)^{-1}=ba.
$$
Multiplying on the left by $a$ and on the right by $a$ gives
$$
bab=aba.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Every element of $H$ is represented by one of
$$
\boxed{
1,\ a,\ b,\ ab,\ ba,\ aba}.
$$


::: pf-proof

First cancel every occurrence of $aa$ or $bb$ using
$$
a^2=b^2=1.
$$
A reduced word therefore alternates $a,b$.

If its length is at least $4$, it begins with either
$$
abab
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
baba.
$$
From
$$
(ab)^3=1
$$
one gets
$$
abab=ba.
$$
Similarly, since
$$
(ba)^3=((ab)^{-1})^3=1,
$$
one gets
$$
baba=ab.
$$
Thus every alternating word of length at least $4$ shortens by two.
Repeating leaves length at most $3$. The only reduced words of length at
most $3$ are
$$
1,\ a,\ b,\ ab,\ ba,\ aba,\ bab,
$$
and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} identifies the last two.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{\cardinality{H}\le6}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives at most six representatives.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The relations involving $c$ imply
$$
\boxed{ac=ca}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{cbc=bcb}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $a^2=c^2=1$,
$$
(ac)^2=1
$$
means
$$
acac=1.
$$
Multiplying by $a$ on the left and $c$ on the right gives
$$
ca=ac.
$$

Likewise $b^2=c^2=1$ and
$$
(bc)^3=1
$$
give the standard braid relation
$$
bcb=cbc
$$
by the same argument as in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Consider the four right cosets
$$
\boxed{
H,\qquad
Hc,\qquad
Hcb,\qquad
Hcba}.
$$


::: pf-proof

These are well-defined right cosets of the subgroup $H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Right multiplication by $a,b,c$ sends the coset $H$ into the
four-element set in step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

Since $a,b\in H$,
$$
Ha=Hb=H.
$$
Also
$$
Hc=Hc.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

For the coset $Hc$,
$$
\boxed{
Hca=Hc,\qquad
Hcb=Hcb,\qquad
Hcc=H}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using $ca=ac$ from step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref},
$$
Hca=Hac=Hc.
$$
The second equality is the definition of the next listed coset. Since
$c^2=1$,
$$
Hcc=H.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

For the coset $Hcb$,
$$
\boxed{
Hcba=Hcba,\qquad
Hcbb=Hc,\qquad
Hcbc=Hcb}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The first equality is the definition of the fourth listed coset. Since
$b^2=1$,
$$
Hcbb=Hc.
$$
For the last equality, step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives
$$
cbc=bcb.
$$
Hence
$$
Hcbc
=
Hbcb
=
Hcb,
$$
because $b\in H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

For the coset $Hcba$,
$$
\boxed{
Hcbaa=Hcb,\qquad
Hcbab=Hcba,\qquad
Hcbac=Hcba}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $a^2=1$,
$$
Hcbaa=Hcb.
$$

For multiplication by $b$, use the braid relation
$$
bab=aba
$$
from step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}:
$$
Hcbab
=
Hcaba.
$$
Since $ca=ac$,
$$
Hcaba
=
Hacba
=
Hcba.
$$

For multiplication by $c$,
$$
Hcbac
=
Hcbca
$$
using $ac=ca$. Then
$$
cb c=bcb
$$
gives
$$
Hcbca
=
Hb cba
=
Hcba,
$$
because $b\in H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Every element of $G$ lies in one of the four cosets in step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

The identity element lies in $H$. Any word in $a,b,c$ is obtained from
the identity by successive right multiplication by generators. Steps
[](#s1-12){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} show inductively that after each multiplication the current
element remains in one of the four listed right cosets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{[G:H]\le4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} shows that at most four right cosets cover $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Combining steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-17){.pf-ref},
$$
\boxed{\cardinality{G}\le24}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group is the union of at most four right cosets of $H$, each having
$\cardinality{H}\le6$ elements. Thus
$$
\cardinality{G}
\le
4\cdot6
=
24.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

Since
$$
\cardinality{S_4}=24
$$
and $\Phi:G\twoheadrightarrow S_4$, one has
$$
\boxed{G\cong S_4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

A surjection onto $S_4$ forces
$$
\cardinality{G}\ge24.
$$
Step [](#s1-18){.pf-ref} gives the reverse inequality. Hence $\cardinality{G}=24$, so the surjective
homomorphism $\Phi$ has trivial kernel and is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-19){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
