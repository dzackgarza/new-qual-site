::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be an octagon in $\RR^2$. Label its eight boundary edges,
in counterclockwise order around the boundary, by
$$
a,\ b,\ a^{-1},\ b^{-1},\ c,\ d,\ c^{-1},\ d^{-1},
$$
where opposite orientations indicate the edge identifications. Let $M$ be
the corresponding quotient space.

(a) The resulting surface is homeomorphic to the frosting on a doughnut with
$g$ holes. Determine $g$.

(b) Compute $\pi_1(M)$.

(c) Compute the abelianization
$$
\pi_1(M)^{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$

(d) Show that $M$ is not homotopy equivalent to the torus $T^2$, the
sphere $S^2$, the projective plane $\RP^2$, or the Klein bottle.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The boundary word is
$$
\boxed{
aba^{-1}b^{-1}cdc^{-1}d^{-1}
=
[a,b][c,d]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The first four edge labels form
$$
aba^{-1}b^{-1}=[a,b],
$$
and the last four form
$$
cdc^{-1}d^{-1}=[c,d].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The quotient $M$ is the closed orientable surface of genus $2$.


::: pf-proof

The standard fundamental polygon for a closed orientable surface of genus
$g$ has boundary word
$$
[a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_g,b_g].
$$
Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} has exactly two commutator blocks, so the quotient is the genus-two
surface.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{g=2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The quotient has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, four
$1$-cells
$$
a,b,c,d,
$$
and one $2$-cell.


::: pf-proof

Under the polygon edge identifications all eight vertices become one point.
The four paired edge classes give four $1$-cells, and the octagon interior
gives one $2$-cell.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The $1$-skeleton is
$$
\boxed{
S^1_a\vee S^1_b\vee S^1_c\vee S^1_d}.
$$


::: pf-proof

There is one vertex and four loop edges by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence
$$
\pi_1(M^{(1)})
\cong
F(a,b,c,d),
$$
the free group on four generators.


::: pf-proof

The fundamental group of a wedge of four circles is free of rank four.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The $2$-cell is attached along the loop representing
$$
\boxed{[a,b][c,d]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Walking once around the octagon boundary reads exactly the word from step
[](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Seifert--van Kampen gives
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(M)
\cong
\left\langle
a,b,c,d
\ \middle|\
[a,b][c,d]=1
\right\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Attaching a $2$-cell to the $1$-skeleton quotients the free fundamental
group by the normal closure of its attaching word. Apply steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In the abelianization, both commutators vanish:
$$
[a,b]=1,
\qquad
[c,d]=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition, the abelianization is the quotient by the commutator
subgroup, so every commutator becomes trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore the defining relator of step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} imposes no relation after
abelianization.


::: pf-proof

Its image is
$$
[a,b][c,d]
=
1\cdot1
=
1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(M)^{\mathrm{ab}}
\cong
\ZZ^4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The abelianization of the free group on $a,b,c,d$ is $\ZZ^4$, and
step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} shows that the surface relator contributes no further abelian
relation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Equivalently,
$$
\boxed{
H_1(M;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every path-connected space,
$$
H_1(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The torus has
$$
\boxed{H_1(T^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Use the standard homology of
$$
S^1\times S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

The sphere has
$$
\boxed{H_1(S^2;\ZZ)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The sphere is simply connected, so its first homology vanishes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

The projective plane has
$$
\boxed{
H_1(\RP^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Its standard cellular chain complex gives
$$
H_1(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

The Klein bottle has
$$
\boxed{
H_1(K;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

A standard presentation is
$$
\pi_1(K)
=
\langle x,y\mid xyx^{-1}=y^{-1}\rangle.
$$
After abelianizing, the relation becomes
$$
y=-y,
$$
equivalently
$$
2y=0.
$$
Thus the abelianization is
$$
\ZZ\langle x\rangle
\oplus
\ZZ/2\langle y\rangle.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

None of the groups in steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} is isomorphic to
$$
\ZZ^4.
$$


::: pf-proof

Their free ranks are respectively
$$
2,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1,
$$
whereas $\ZZ^4$ has free rank $4$. In addition, two of them have
torsion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Therefore $M$ is not homotopy equivalent to
$$
T^2,\qquad
S^2,\qquad
\RP^2,\qquad
K.
$$


::: pf-proof

Homotopy equivalent spaces have isomorphic homology groups. By step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref},
$$
H_1(M)\cong\ZZ^4,
$$
while steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-17){.pf-ref} show that none of the four comparison spaces has
isomorphic first homology.

:::
