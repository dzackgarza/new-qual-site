::: {.problem}
Let \(X\) be an octagon in \(\mathbb R^2\). Label its eight boundary edges,
in counterclockwise order around the boundary, by
\[
a,\ b,\ a^{-1},\ b^{-1},\ c,\ d,\ c^{-1},\ d^{-1},
\]
where opposite orientations indicate the edge identifications. Let \(M\) be
the corresponding quotient space.

(a) The resulting surface is homeomorphic to the frosting on a doughnut with
\(g\) holes. Determine \(g\).

(b) Compute \(\pi_1(M)\).

(c) Compute the abelianization
\[
\pi_1(M)^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]

(d) Show that \(M\) is not homotopy equivalent to the torus \(T^2\), the
sphere \(S^2\), the projective plane \(\mathbb{RP}^2\), or the Klein bottle.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The boundary word is
\[
\boxed{
aba^{-1}b^{-1}cdc^{-1}d^{-1}
=
[a,b][c,d]}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The first four edge labels form
\[
aba^{-1}b^{-1}=[a,b],
\]
and the last four form
\[
cdc^{-1}d^{-1}=[c,d].
\]
:::

<1>2. The quotient \(M\) is the closed orientable surface of genus \(2\).

::: {.proof}
The standard fundamental polygon for a closed orientable surface of genus
\(g\) has boundary word
\[
[a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_g,b_g].
\]
Step <1>1 has exactly two commutator blocks, so the quotient is the genus-two
surface.
:::

<1>3. Therefore
\[
\boxed{g=2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>2.
:::

<1>4. The quotient has a CW structure with one \(0\)-cell, four
\(1\)-cells
\[
a,b,c,d,
\]
and one \(2\)-cell.

::: {.proof}
Under the polygon edge identifications all eight vertices become one point.
The four paired edge classes give four \(1\)-cells, and the octagon interior
gives one \(2\)-cell.
:::

<1>5. The \(1\)-skeleton is
\[
\boxed{
S^1_a\vee S^1_b\vee S^1_c\vee S^1_d}.
\]

::: {.proof}
There is one vertex and four loop edges by step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Hence
\[
\pi_1(M^{(1)})
\cong
F(a,b,c,d),
\]
the free group on four generators.

::: {.proof}
The fundamental group of a wedge of four circles is free of rank four.
:::

<1>7. The \(2\)-cell is attached along the loop representing
\[
\boxed{[a,b][c,d]}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Walking once around the octagon boundary reads exactly the word from step
<1>1.
:::

<1>8. Seifert--van Kampen gives
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(M)
\cong
\left\langle
a,b,c,d
\ \middle|\
[a,b][c,d]=1
\right\rangle}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Attaching a \(2\)-cell to the \(1\)-skeleton quotients the free fundamental
group by the normal closure of its attaching word. Apply steps <1>6 and
<1>7.
:::

<1>9. In the abelianization, both commutators vanish:
\[
[a,b]=1,
\qquad
[c,d]=1.
\]

::: {.proof}
By definition, the abelianization is the quotient by the commutator
subgroup, so every commutator becomes trivial.
:::

<1>10. Therefore the defining relator of step <1>8 imposes no relation after
abelianization.

::: {.proof}
Its image is
\[
[a,b][c,d]
=
1\cdot1
=
1.
\]
:::

<1>11. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(M)^{\mathrm{ab}}
\cong
\mathbb Z^4}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The abelianization of the free group on \(a,b,c,d\) is \(\mathbb Z^4\), and
step <1>10 shows that the surface relator contributes no further abelian
relation.
:::

<1>12. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
H_1(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For every path-connected space,
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]
Apply step <1>11.
:::

<1>13. The torus has
\[
\boxed{H_1(T^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Use the standard homology of
\[
S^1\times S^1.
\]
:::

<1>14. The sphere has
\[
\boxed{H_1(S^2;\mathbb Z)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The sphere is simply connected, so its first homology vanishes.
:::

<1>15. The projective plane has
\[
\boxed{
H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Its standard cellular chain complex gives
\[
H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
:::

<1>16. The Klein bottle has
\[
\boxed{
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
A standard presentation is
\[
\pi_1(K)
=
\langle x,y\mid xyx^{-1}=y^{-1}\rangle.
\]
After abelianizing, the relation becomes
\[
y=-y,
\]
equivalently
\[
2y=0.
\]
Thus the abelianization is
\[
\mathbb Z\langle x\rangle
\oplus
\mathbb Z/2\langle y\rangle.
\]
:::

<1>17. None of the groups in steps <1>13--<1>16 is isomorphic to
\[
\mathbb Z^4.
\]

::: {.proof}
Their free ranks are respectively
\[
2,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1,
\]
whereas \(\mathbb Z^4\) has free rank \(4\). In addition, two of them have
torsion.
:::

<1>18. Therefore \(M\) is not homotopy equivalent to
\[
T^2,\qquad
S^2,\qquad
\mathbb{RP}^2,\qquad
K.
\]

::: {.proof}
Homotopy equivalent spaces have isomorphic homology groups. By step <1>12,
\[
H_1(M)\cong\mathbb Z^4,
\]
while steps <1>13--<1>17 show that none of the four comparison spaces has
isomorphic first homology.
:::

<1>19. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 answers part (a), step <1>8 answers part (b), step <1>11 answers
part (c), and step <1>18 answers part (d).
:::
:::
