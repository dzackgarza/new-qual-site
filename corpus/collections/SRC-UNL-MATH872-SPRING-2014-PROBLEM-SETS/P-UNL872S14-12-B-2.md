::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 158 #31.

Suppose the basepoints
$$
x_0\in X,
\qquad
y_0\in Y
$$
that are identified to form
$$
X\vee Y
$$
are deformation retracts of neighborhoods
$$
U\subseteq X,
\qquad
V\subseteq Y.
$$
Use the Mayer--Vietoris sequence to prove that for every $n$,
$$
\widetilde H_n(X\vee Y)
\cong
\widetilde H_n(X)\oplus\widetilde H_n(Y).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
Z=X\vee Y
$$
and let
$$
q:X\sqcup Y\to Z
$$
be the quotient identifying $x_0$ with $y_0$. Denote the common wedge
point by $z_0$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

We may take $U$ and $V$ to be open neighborhoods of the
basepoints that deformation retract onto $x_0$ and $y_0$, respectively.


::: pf-proof

The hypothesis says the basepoints are deformation retracts of
neighborhoods. Replacing these neighborhoods by suitable open neighborhoods
inside them does not alter the Mayer--Vietoris argument; alternatively, one
may use the excisive-subspace form of Mayer--Vietoris. We henceforth use
open neighborhoods $U,V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Define
$$
A=q(X\sqcup V)
$$
and
$$
B=q(U\sqcup Y).
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{Z=A\cup B}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The set $A$ contains the entire $X$-summand, while $B$ contains the
entire $Y$-summand. Together they contain every point of the wedge.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The sets $A$ and $B$ are open in $Z$.


::: pf-proof

The preimage
$$
q^{-1}(A)=X\sqcup V
$$
is open in the disjoint union $X\sqcup Y$: the whole component $X$ is
open in itself and $V$ is open in $Y$. It is saturated for the quotient
relation because both copies of the basepoint are included. Since $q$ is a
quotient map, $A$ is open. The same argument applies to
$$
q^{-1}(B)=U\sqcup Y.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\boxed{A\cap B=q(U\sqcup V)=U\vee V}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Away from the wedge point, the $X$- and $Y$-summands are disjoint. A
point of the $X$-summand lies in both $A$ and $B$ exactly when it lies
in $U$; similarly, a point of the $Y$-summand lies in both exactly when
it lies in $V$. The basepoints are identified, so the intersection is the
wedge $U\vee V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The intersection $A\cap B$ deformation retracts onto the wedge point
$z_0$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
H_X:U\times I\to U
$$
be a deformation retraction of $U$ onto $x_0$, and let
$$
H_Y:V\times I\to V
$$
be a deformation retraction of $V$ onto $y_0$. Since both homotopies fix
their basepoints, they agree after the two basepoints are identified. Hence
they combine to a deformation retraction
$$
(U\vee V)\times I\to U\vee V
$$
onto $z_0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_k(A\cap B)=0
\quad\text{for all }k}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, $A\cap B$ is contractible. Reduced homology of a
contractible space is zero in every degree.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The space $A$ deformation retracts onto the $X$-summand.


::: pf-proof

The subset $A$ is obtained from $X$ by adjoining the neighborhood $V$
at the basepoint. Use the given deformation retraction of $V$ onto
$y_0$, while fixing every point of $X$. Since the homotopy fixes
$y_0=x_0$, it descends to a deformation retraction
$$
A\to X.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Similarly, $B$ deformation retracts onto the $Y$-summand.


::: pf-proof

Use the deformation retraction of $U$ onto $x_0$, fixing $Y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_n(A)
\cong
\widetilde H_n(X)}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_n(B)
\cong
\widetilde H_n(Y)}
$$
for every $n$.


::: pf-proof

Apply homotopy invariance to steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence for
$$
Z=A\cup B
$$
contains, for every $n$,
$$
\widetilde H_n(A\cap B)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_n(A)\oplus\widetilde H_n(B)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_n(Z)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_{n-1}(A\cap B).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the reduced Mayer--Vietoris long exact sequence for the open cover
from steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, the sequence in step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} reduces to
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_n(A)\oplus\widetilde H_n(B)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_n(Z)
\longrightarrow
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both reduced homology groups of the intersection vanish.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore the middle map is an isomorphism:
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_n(A)\oplus\widetilde H_n(B)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\widetilde H_n(Z)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Exactness of
$$
0\to G\to H\to0
$$
means the map $G\to H$ is both injective and surjective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Substituting step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} and $Z=X\vee Y$, one obtains
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde H_n(X\vee Y)
\cong
\widetilde H_n(X)\oplus\widetilde H_n(Y)}
$$
for every $n$.


::: pf-proof

Replace
$$
\widetilde H_n(A)
$$
and
$$
\widetilde H_n(B)
$$
by the isomorphic groups from step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} in the isomorphism of step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The isomorphism is induced by the inclusions of the two wedge
summands.


::: pf-proof

The Mayer--Vietoris map
$$
\widetilde H_n(A)\oplus\widetilde H_n(B)\to\widetilde H_n(Z)
$$
is the sum of the two inclusion-induced maps. Under the deformation
retractions $A\simeq X$ and $B\simeq Y$, these are exactly the maps
induced by the canonical inclusions
$$
X\hookrightarrow X\vee Y,
\qquad
Y\hookrightarrow X\vee Y.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} is the desired decomposition.
