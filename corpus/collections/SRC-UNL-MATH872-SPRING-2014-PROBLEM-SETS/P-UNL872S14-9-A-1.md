::: {.problem}
Let X be $\RR ^ { 3 } - ( \{ ( \mathrm { x } , 0 , 0 ) | \mathrm { x } \in \RR \} \cup \{ ( 1 , 1 , 1 ) , ( 2 , 3 , 4 ) \} )$with the Euclidean subspace topology.
Then X deformation retracts to a subspace that is homeomorphic to a wedge product of m copies of${ \mathrm { \dot { s } } } ^ { 1 }$and n copies$\mathrm { o f } \mathrm { S } ^ { 2 }$.
What are m and n? Use your answer to compute$\pi _ { 1 } ( \mathrm { X } )$

(Formal proof not needed for this problem.)
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
L=\{(x,0,0):x\in\RR\},
\qquad
p=(1,1,1),
\qquad
q=(2,3,4).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The complement
$$
\RR^3-L
$$
has the homotopy type of $S^1$.


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
\RR^3-L
\cong
\RR\times(\RR^2-\{0\})
$$
using the $x$-coordinate and the $(y,z)$-coordinates. The factor
$\RR$ is contractible, while
$$
\RR^2-\{0\}
$$
deformation retracts radially onto $S^1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

A small meridian circle linking the deleted line once gives the
$S^1$-summand in the stated wedge decomposition.


::: pf-proof

Take a small circle in a plane perpendicular to $L$, centered on $L$.
It represents the generator of
$$
\pi_1(\RR^3-L)\cong\ZZ
$$
from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

A small sphere surrounding the deleted point $p$, disjoint from
$L$ and $q$, gives one $S^2$-summand.


::: pf-proof

Choose a small closed ball $B_p$ around $p$ whose closure misses
$$
L\cup\{q\}.
$$
Removing the center $p$ from the ball leaves
$$
B_p-\{p\},
$$
which deformation retracts radially onto
$$
\partial B_p\cong S^2.
$$
In the usual spine construction for the complement, this sphere is joined to
the rest of the spine by an arc, producing a wedge summand.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Likewise, a small sphere surrounding $q$ gives a second
$S^2$-summand.


::: pf-proof

Repeat step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} with a small ball $B_q$ disjoint from
$$
L\cup\{p\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore the deformation retract described in the problem has
homotopy type
$$
\boxed{
S^1\vee S^2\vee S^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The deleted line contributes the meridional circle from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, and the
two isolated deleted points contribute the two spheres from steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. Joining the three pieces to a common basepoint by disjoint arcs gives
the stated wedge.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence
$$
\boxed{m=1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{n=2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compare step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} with the form
$$
\bigvee^m S^1\vee\bigvee^n S^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The $S^2$-summands do not contribute to the fundamental group.


::: pf-proof

Each $S^2$ is simply connected. By van Kampen, wedging simply connected
spaces onto a connected space does not change the fundamental group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\pi_1(S^1)
\cong
\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Use steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives the requested integers, and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the fundamental
group.

:::
