::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 38 # 5.

For a space \(X\), show that the following three conditions are equivalent:

(a) Every map \(S^1\to X\) is homotopic to a constant map whose image is a point.

(b) Every map \(S^1\to X\) extends to a map \(D^2\to X\).

(c) \(\pi_1(X,x_0)=0\) for every \(x_0\in X\).

Deduce that \(X\) is simply connected if and only if all maps \(S^1\to X\) are homotopic to one another.

(Hint: You may wish to use the following lemma in your proofs:

Lemma \(\ast\): If \(g:X\to Y\) is a continuous surjection, \(X\) is compact, and \(Y\) is Hausdorff, then \(g\) is a quotient map.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Let \(i:S^1\hookrightarrow D^2\) denote the boundary inclusion.

<1>1. Condition (a) implies condition (b).

::: {.proof}
Let \(f:S^1\to X\). By (a), choose a homotopy
\[
H:S^1\times I\to X
\]
from \(f\) to a constant map \(c_{x_0}\):
\[
H(s,0)=f(s),
\qquad
H(s,1)=x_0.
\]
Define
\[
q:S^1\times I\to D^2,
\qquad
q(s,t)=(1-t)s,
\]
viewing \(S^1\) as the unit circle in \(\mathbb C\). The map \(q\) is a
continuous surjection from a compact space to the Hausdorff space \(D^2\),
so the lemma in the problem says that \(q\) is a quotient map.

The only nontrivial fiber of \(q\) is
\[
q^{-1}(0)=S^1\times\{1\},
\]
and \(H\) is constant on this fiber. Therefore \(H\) factors uniquely
through a continuous map
\[
F:D^2\to X
\]
with
\[
F\circ q=H.
\]
Since
\[
q(s,0)=s,
\]
we get
\[
F\circ i=f.
\]
Thus \(f\) extends over \(D^2\).
:::

<1>2. Condition (b) implies condition (c).

::: {.proof}
Fix \(x_0\in X\), and let
\[
f:(S^1,s_0)\to(X,x_0)
\]
be any based loop. By (b), \(f\) extends to
\[
F:D^2\to X.
\]
Because \(D^2\) is convex, the homotopy
\[
R(z,t)=(1-t)z+ts_0
\]
contracts \(D^2\) to the boundary point \(s_0\) while fixing \(s_0\).
Hence
\[
K(s,t)=F(R(s,t))
\]
is a based homotopy from \(f\) to the constant loop at \(x_0\). Thus every
based loop at \(x_0\) is nullhomotopic, so
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)=0.
\]
Since \(x_0\) was arbitrary, (c) holds.
:::

<1>3. Condition (c) implies condition (a).

::: {.proof}
Let \(f:S^1\to X\), choose \(s_0\in S^1\), and put
\[
x_0=f(s_0).
\]
Then \(f\) is a based loop
\[
(S^1,s_0)\to(X,x_0).
\]
By (c),
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)=0,
\]
so \(f\) is based-homotopic to the constant loop at \(x_0\). Forgetting the
basepoint condition gives an ordinary homotopy from \(f\) to a constant map.
Thus (a) holds.
:::

<1>4. Therefore conditions (a), (b), and (c) are equivalent.

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>1 through <1>3.
:::

<1>5. If \(X\) is simply connected, then all maps \(S^1\to X\) are
homotopic to one another.

::: {.proof}
Simple connectedness gives path connectedness and condition (c). By step
<1>4, every map \(S^1\to X\) is homotopic to a constant map. Any two
constant maps \(c_x,c_y\) are homotopic because a path
\[
\gamma:I\to X
\]
from \(x\) to \(y\) gives the homotopy
\[
H(s,t)=\gamma(t).
\]
Hence all maps \(S^1\to X\) are homotopic.
:::

<1>6. Conversely, if all maps \(S^1\to X\) are homotopic, then \(X\) is
path connected.

::: {.proof}
For arbitrary \(x,y\in X\), the constant maps \(c_x,c_y:S^1\to X\) are
homotopic. Evaluating such a homotopy at one fixed \(s_0\in S^1\) gives a
path from \(x\) to \(y\).
:::

<1>7. Under the hypothesis of step <1>6,
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)=0
\]
for every \(x_0\in X\).

::: {.proof}
Every map \(S^1\to X\) is homotopic to a constant map, so condition (a)
holds. By step <1>4, condition (c) follows.
:::

<1>8. Hence
\[
\boxed{
X\text{ is simply connected}
\iff
\text{all maps }S^1\to X\text{ are homotopic}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The forward implication is step <1>5. The reverse implication follows from
steps <1>6 and <1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves the three-way equivalence, and step <1>8 proves the final
deduction.
:::
:::
