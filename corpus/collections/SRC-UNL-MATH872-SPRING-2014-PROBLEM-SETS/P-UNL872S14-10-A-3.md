::: {.problem}
(a) Show (by pictures) a $2$-sheeted covering space of the Klein bottle by the torus.
(That is, $p \colon T^{2} \to K^{2}$.)

(b) Show (by pictures) a simply connected covering space of the Klein bottle.

(c) Using your answer in either (a) or (b) and the path lifting theorem, show that the fundamental group of the Klein bottle is not abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}

Use the standard quotient model
$$
K=\RR^2/G,
$$
where $G$ is generated by
$$
A(x,y)=(x+1,y),
\qquad
B(x,y)=(-x,y+1).
$$
Let
$$
q:\RR^2\to K
$$
be the quotient map.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The transformations satisfy
$$
\boxed{BAB^{-1}=A^{-1}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
B^{-1}(x,y)=(-x,y-1),
$$
one computes
$$
\begin{aligned}
BAB^{-1}(x,y)
&=
+BA(-x,y-1)\
&=
+B(1-x,y-1)\
&=
+(x-1,y)\
&=
+A^{-1}(x,y).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\boxed{B^2(x,y)=(x,y+2)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply $B$ twice:
$$
B^2(x,y)
=
B(-x,y+1)
=
(x,y+2).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The subgroup
$$
H=\langle A,B^2\rangle
$$
acts on $\RR^2$ by translations
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto(x+m,y+2n),
\qquad
m,n\in\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
A^m(B^2)^n(x,y)=(x+m,y+2n).
$$
Since $A$ commutes with $B^2$, these are exactly the elements of
$H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The quotient
$$
\boxed{\RR^2/H\cong T^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group $H$ is the rank-two translation lattice generated by
$$
(1,0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(0,2).
$$
A fundamental domain is the rectangle
$$
[0,1]\times[0,2],
$$
with opposite sides identified by translations. This quotient is a torus.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The subgroup $H$ has index $2$ in $G$.


::: pf-proof

Every word in $A^{\pm1},B^{\pm1}$ can be rewritten, using
$$
BA=A^{-1}B
$$
from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, in the form
$$
A^mB^n.
$$
If $n$ is even, this element lies in $H$. If $n$ is odd, it lies
in the coset $HB$. Thus
$$
G=H\sqcup HB,
$$
so
$$
[G:H]=2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore there is a two-sheeted covering
$$
\boxed{
p:T^2=\RR^2/H\longrightarrow
K=\RR^2/G}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $H\le G$, the quotient map by $H$ factors through the quotient
by $G$:
$$
\RR^2
\longrightarrow
\RR^2/H
\xrightarrow{p}
\RR^2/G.
$$
For a properly discontinuous free action, a subgroup of index $2$
produces a two-sheeted covering. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives the index.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Geometrically, part (a) is obtained from the rectangle
$$
[0,1]\times[0,2]
$$
by identifying both pairs of opposite sides by translations, and mapping its
two unit-height halves to one standard Klein-bottle square.


::: pf-proof

The torus quotient uses the translation identifications
$$
(x,0)\sim(x,2),
\qquad
(0,y)\sim(1,y).
$$
The Klein-bottle quotient additionally identifies the two height-one layers
by the glide reflection represented by $B$. Hence the two halves of the
height-two rectangle form the two sheets over the usual height-one
Klein-bottle square.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The map
$$
\boxed{q:\RR^2\to K}
$$
is a covering map.


::: pf-proof

The group $G$ acts freely and properly discontinuously on $\RR^2$. Every point has a sufficiently small disk whose nontrivial translates
under $G$ are disjoint. The quotient map by such an action is a covering
map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Since $\RR^2$ is simply connected, the covering in step
[](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the universal cover of the Klein bottle.


::: pf-proof

The plane is contractible and therefore simply connected. A connected
covering with simply connected total space is universal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Let
$$
\alpha(s)=q(s,0),
\qquad
\beta(s)=q(0,s),
\qquad
0\le s\le1.
$$
These are based loops in $K$ at
$$
x_0=q(0,0).
$$


::: pf-proof

For $\alpha$,
$$
q(1,0)=q(A(0,0))=q(0,0).
$$
For $\beta$,
$$
q(0,1)=q(B(0,0))=q(0,0).
$$
Thus both paths close up in the quotient.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The lift of $\alpha*\beta$ starting at $(0,0)$ ends at
$$
\boxed{(1,1)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The first half lifts to the path
$$
s\longmapsto(s,0),
$$
ending at $(1,0)$. A lift of $\beta$ starting at $(1,0)$ is
$$
s\longmapsto(1,s),
$$
because
$$
q(1,s)=q(A(0,s))=q(0,s)=\beta(s).
$$
Hence the concatenated lift ends at $(1,1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The lift of $\beta*\alpha$ starting at $(0,0)$ ends at
$$
\boxed{(-1,1)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The first half lifts to
$$
s\longmapsto(0,s),
$$
ending at $(0,1)$. A lift of $\alpha$ starting at $(0,1)$ is
$$
s\longmapsto(-s,1),
$$
because
$$
q(-s,1)
=
q(B(s,0))
=
q(s,0)
=
\alpha(s).
$$
Thus the lift ends at $(-1,1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The loops $\alpha*\beta$ and $\beta*\alpha$ are not
path-homotopic relative to the basepoint.


::: pf-proof

If two based loops are path-homotopic, then the homotopy-lifting theorem
implies that their lifts starting at the same point have the same endpoint:
the lifted endpoints vary continuously in the discrete fiber over the
basepoint and hence remain constant during the homotopy.

Steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} give distinct endpoints
$$
(1,1)\ne(-1,1).
$$
Therefore the two loops are not path-homotopic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
[\alpha][\beta]\ne[\beta][\alpha]}
$$
in $\pi_1(K,x_0)$.


::: pf-proof

The products in the fundamental group are represented by the concatenations
$\alpha*\beta$ and $\beta*\alpha$. Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} shows these
classes are different.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\pi_1(K)\text{ is not abelian}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} exhibits two elements that do not commute.

:::
