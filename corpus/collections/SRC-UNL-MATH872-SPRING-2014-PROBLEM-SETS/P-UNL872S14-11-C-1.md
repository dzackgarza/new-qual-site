::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 131 #4.

Compute the simplicial homology groups of the triangular parachute obtained from
\[
\Delta^2=[v_0,v_1,v_2]
\]
by identifying its three vertices to a single point.

A complete proof, including the chain groups and boundary maps, is required.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X$ denote the quotient $\Delta$-complex.

<1>1. The quotient has exactly one $0$-simplex, which we denote by
\[
\boxed{v}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By construction, the three vertices $v_0,v_1,v_2$ of the original $2$-simplex are all identified.
:::

<1>2. The three open edges remain three distinct $1$-simplices.
Denote them
\[
\boxed{
e_{01}=[v_0,v_1],\qquad
e_{12}=[v_1,v_2],\qquad
e_{02}=[v_0,v_2]}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The quotient identifies only the vertices, not the interiors of the three edges.
Hence the three open $1$-simplices remain distinct, though each now has both endpoints equal to $v$.
:::

<1>3. There is one $2$-simplex,
\[
\boxed{\sigma=[v_0,v_1,v_2]}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The interior of the original $2$-simplex is unaffected by the vertex identifications.
:::

<1>4. Therefore the simplicial chain groups are
\[
\boxed{
C_k(X)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z\langle\sigma
\rangle,&k=2,\\
\mathbb Z\langle e_{01},e_{12},e_{02}
\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^3,&k=1,\\
\mathbb Z\langle v
\rangle,&k=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
The simplicial chain group is the free abelian group on the oriented $k$-simplices of the $\Delta$-complex.
Steps <1>1--<1>3 list all of them.
:::

<1>5. The boundary map
\[
\partial_1:C_1(X)\to C_0(X)
\]
is the zero map.

::: {.proof}
For each edge,
\[
\partial_1[v_i,v_j]
=
[v_j]-[v_i].
\]
Both endpoints map to the single vertex $v$, so
\[
\partial_1(e_{ij})
=
v-v
=
0.
\]
Thus $\partial_1=0$ on the basis and hence everywhere.
:::

<1>6. The boundary of the $2$-simplex is
\[
\boxed{
\partial_2\sigma
=
e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The simplicial boundary formula gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_2[v_0,v_1,v_2]
&=
[v_1,v_2]
-
[v_0,v_2]
+
[v_0,v_1]\\
&=
e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>7. In the ordered basis
\[
(e_{01},e_{12},e_{02})
\]
of $C_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^3$, the map $\partial_2$ is
\[
\boxed{
\partial_2(n)
=
(n,n,-n)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 gives
\[
\partial_2(1)
=
e_{01}+e_{12}-e_{02},
\]
whose coordinate vector is
\[
(1,1,-1).
\]
Multiply by $n$.
:::

<1>8. The map
\[
\partial_2:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z^3
\]
is injective.

::: {.proof}
If
\[
\partial_2(n)=(n,n,-n)=0,
\]
then $n=0$.
:::

<1>9. Hence
\[
\boxed{H_2(X)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since there are no $3$-simplices,
\[
\operatorname{im}\partial_3=0.
\]
By step <1>8,
\[
\ker\partial_2=0.
\]
Thus
\[
H_2(X)
=
\ker\partial_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3
=
0.
\]
:::

<1>10. Since $\partial_1=0$,
\[
\ker\partial_1
=
C_1(X)
=
\mathbb Z^3.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>5.
:::

<1>11. Therefore
\[
H_1(X)
\cong
\boxed{
\mathbb Z^3/
\langle(1,1,-1)
\rangle}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By definition,
\[
H_1(X)
=
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
\]
Use step <1>10 for the numerator and step <1>7 for the image of $\partial_2$.
:::

<1>12. Define
\[
\Phi:\mathbb Z^3\to\mathbb Z^2
\]
by
\[
\boxed{
\Phi(x,y,z)
=
(x-y, x+z)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is a homomorphism of free abelian groups.
:::

<1>13. The map $\Phi$ is surjective.

::: {.proof}
For any
\[
(u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2,
\]
take
\[
(x,y,z)=(0,-u,v).
\]
Then
\[
\Phi(0,-u,v)
=
(u,v).
\]
:::

<1>14. The kernel of $\Phi$ is
\[
\boxed{
\ker\Phi
=
\langle(1,1,-1)
\rangle}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The equations
\[
x-y=0,
\qquad
x+z=0
\]
give
\[
y=x,
\qquad
z=-x.
\]
Hence every kernel element has the form
\[
x(1,1,-1).
\]
Conversely, $((1,1,-1))$ clearly lies in the kernel.
:::

<1>15. Therefore
\[
\boxed{H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By the first isomorphism theorem and steps <1>13--<1>14,
\[
\mathbb Z^3/
\langle(1,1,-1)
\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Apply step <1>11.
:::

<1>16. The zeroth homology is
\[
\boxed{H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The space $X$ is connected because it contains one $2$-simplex whose quotient contains all lower-dimensional cells.
Equivalently,
\[
H_0(X)
=
C_0(X)/\operatorname{im}\partial_1
=
\mathbb Z/0
\cong
\mathbb Z
\]
by step <1>5.
:::

<1>17. For all $k>2$,
\[
\boxed{H_k(X)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
There are no simplices in dimensions above $2$, so
\[
C_k(X)=0
\]
for $k>2$.
:::

<1>18. Thus
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>9, <1>15, <1>16, and <1>17.
:::

<1>19. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>18 is the complete simplicial homology computation.
:::
