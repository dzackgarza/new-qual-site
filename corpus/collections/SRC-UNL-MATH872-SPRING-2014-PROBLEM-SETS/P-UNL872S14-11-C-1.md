::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 131 #4.

Compute the simplicial homology groups of the triangular parachute obtained from
$$
\Delta^2=[v_0,v_1,v_2]
$$
by identifying its three vertices to a single point.

A complete proof, including the chain groups and boundary maps, is required.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $X$ denote the quotient $\Delta$-complex.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The quotient has exactly one $0$-simplex, which we denote by
$$
\boxed{v}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By construction, the three vertices $v_0,v_1,v_2$ of the original $2$-simplex are all identified.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The three open edges remain three distinct $1$-simplices.
Denote them
$$
\boxed{
e_{01}=[v_0,v_1],\qquad
e_{12}=[v_1,v_2],\qquad
e_{02}=[v_0,v_2]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The quotient identifies only the vertices, not the interiors of the three edges.
Hence the three open $1$-simplices remain distinct, though each now has both endpoints equal to $v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

There is one $2$-simplex,
$$
\boxed{\sigma=[v_0,v_1,v_2]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The interior of the original $2$-simplex is unaffected by the vertex identifications.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the simplicial chain groups are
$$
\boxed{
C_k(X)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ\langle\sigma
\rangle,&k=2,\\
\ZZ\langle e_{01},e_{12},e_{02}
\rangle\cong\ZZ^3,&k=1,\\
\ZZ\langle v
\rangle,&k=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

The simplicial chain group is the free abelian group on the oriented $k$-simplices of the $\Delta$-complex.
Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} list all of them.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The boundary map
$$
\partial_1:C_1(X)\to C_0(X)
$$
is the zero map.


::: pf-proof

For each edge,
$$
\partial_1[v_i,v_j]
=
[v_j]-[v_i].
$$
Both endpoints map to the single vertex $v$, so
$$
\partial_1(e_{ij})
=
v-v
=
0.
$$
Thus $\partial_1=0$ on the basis and hence everywhere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The boundary of the $2$-simplex is
$$
\boxed{
\partial_2\sigma
=
e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The simplicial boundary formula gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_2[v_0,v_1,v_2]
&=
[v_1,v_2]
-
[v_0,v_2]
+
[v_0,v_1]\\
&=
e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

In the ordered basis
$$
(e_{01},e_{12},e_{02})
$$
of $C_1(X)\cong\ZZ^3$, the map $\partial_2$ is
$$
\boxed{
\partial_2(n)
=
(n,n,-n)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\partial_2(1)
=
e_{01}+e_{12}-e_{02},
$$
whose coordinate vector is
$$
(1,1,-1).
$$
Multiply by $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The map
$$
\partial_2:\ZZ\to\ZZ^3
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\partial_2(n)=(n,n,-n)=0,
$$
then $n=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Hence
$$
\boxed{H_2(X)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since there are no $3$-simplices,
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_3=0.
$$
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
\ker\partial_2=0.
$$
Thus
$$
H_2(X)
=
\ker\partial_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3
=
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Since $\partial_1=0$,
$$
\ker\partial_1
=
C_1(X)
=
\ZZ^3.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore
$$
H_1(X)
\cong
\boxed{
\ZZ^3/
\langle(1,1,-1)
\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
H_1(X)
=
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
$$
Use step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} for the numerator and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} for the image of $\partial_2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
\Phi:\ZZ^3\to\ZZ^2
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi(x,y,z)
=
(x-y, x+z)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is a homomorphism of free abelian groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The map $\Phi$ is surjective.


::: pf-proof

For any
$$
(u,v)\in\ZZ^2,
$$
take
$$
(x,y,z)=(0,-u,v).
$$
Then
$$
\Phi(0,-u,v)
=
(u,v).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

The kernel of $\Phi$ is
$$
\boxed{
\ker\Phi
=
\langle(1,1,-1)
\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The equations
$$
x-y=0,
\qquad
x+z=0
$$
give
$$
y=x,
\qquad
z=-x.
$$
Hence every kernel element has the form
$$
x(1,1,-1).
$$
Conversely, $((1,1,-1))$ clearly lies in the kernel.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{H_1(X)\cong\ZZ^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the first isomorphism theorem and steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-14){.pf-ref},
$$
\ZZ^3/
\langle(1,1,-1)
\rangle
\cong
\ZZ^2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

The zeroth homology is
$$
\boxed{H_0(X)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The space $X$ is connected because it contains one $2$-simplex whose quotient contains all lower-dimensional cells.
Equivalently,
$$
H_0(X)
=
C_0(X)/\operatorname{im}\partial_1
=
\ZZ/0
\cong
\ZZ
$$
by step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

For all $k>2$,
$$
\boxed{H_k(X)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no simplices in dimensions above $2$, so
$$
C_k(X)=0
$$
for $k>2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_k(X)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
\ZZ^2,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-17){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-18){.pf-ref} is the complete simplicial homology computation.
