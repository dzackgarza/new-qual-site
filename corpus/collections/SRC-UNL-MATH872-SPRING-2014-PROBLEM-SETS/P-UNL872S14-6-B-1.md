::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 39 #16(a),(b),(f).

Show that there are no retractions \(r:X\to A\) in the following cases:

(a) \(X=\mathbb R^3\), with \(A\subset\mathbb R^3\) any subspace homeomorphic to \(S^1\).

(b) \(X=S^1\times D^2\), with \(A\) its boundary torus
\[
A=S^1\times S^1.
\]

(f) \(X\) is the Möbius band and \(A\) is its boundary circle.

Hints for (f): The boundary of $X$ is the set of points that have an open neighborhood homeomorphic to the half disc
\[
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2\mid x^2+y^2<1\text{ and }x\ge0\}.
\]
Let $x_0$ be a basepoint of $X$ on the boundary circle.
Let $f$ be the loop in $X$ at $x_0$ that goes around the center circle of $X$ once, and let $g$ be the loop at $x_0$ that goes around the boundary circle of $X$ once.
How are $f$ and $g$ related?
Where does the map induced by a retraction $r:X\to A$ send $[f]$ and $[g]$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If \(i:A\hookrightarrow X\) is an inclusion and there is a retraction
\[
r:X\to A,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{i_*:\pi_1(A,a_0)\to\pi_1(X,a_0)\text{ is injective}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since \(r\) is a retraction,
\[
r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_A.
\]
Applying the fundamental-group functor gives
\[
r_*\circ i_*
=
\operatorname{id}_{\pi_1(A,a_0)}.
\]
Thus \(i_*\) has a left inverse and is therefore injective.
:::

<1>2. In part (a),
\[
\pi_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\pi_1(\mathbb R^3)=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
By hypothesis,
\[
A\cong S^1,
\]
so
\[
\pi_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The space \(\mathbb R^3\) is contractible, hence has trivial fundamental
group.
:::

<1>3. Therefore no retraction
\[
\mathbb R^3\to A
\]
exists in part (a).

::: {.proof}
The inclusion-induced map
\[
i_*:\mathbb Z\to0
\]
cannot be injective, contradicting the necessary condition in step <1>1.
:::

<1>4. In part (b),
\[
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1)
\cong
\mathbb Z^2
\]
and
\[
\pi_1(S^1\times D^2)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
\]

::: {.proof}
The torus is a product of two circles, so its fundamental group is
\[
\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z.
\]
Since \(D^2\) is contractible,
\[
S^1\times D^2
\simeq
S^1,
\]
so its fundamental group is \(\mathbb Z\).
:::

<1>5. Under the standard generators, the inclusion
\[
i:S^1\times\partial D^2
\hookrightarrow
S^1\times D^2
\]
induces
\[
\boxed{
i_*:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
(m,n)\longmapsto m}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The first generator of the boundary torus runs around the \(S^1\) factor of
the solid torus and survives. The second generator is the meridian
\[
\{\ast\}\times\partial D^2,
\]
which bounds the disk
\[
\{\ast\}\times D^2
\]
inside the solid torus and is therefore nullhomotopic. Hence
\[
i_*(1,0)=1,
\qquad
i_*(0,1)=0.
\]
:::

<1>6. Therefore no retraction
\[
S^1\times D^2\to S^1\times S^1
\]
exists in part (b).

::: {.proof}
By step <1>5, the nonzero element
\[
(0,1)
\]
lies in the kernel of \(i_*\). Thus \(i_*\) is not injective, contradicting
step <1>1.
:::

<1>7. Let \(X\) be the Möbius band. Then
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The Möbius band deformation retracts onto its central circle. Therefore its
fundamental group is the fundamental group of \(S^1\), namely \(\mathbb Z\).
:::

<1>8. Let \(A=\partial X\) be the boundary circle of the Möbius band. Then
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The boundary of a Möbius band is a single circle.
:::

<1>9. If \(f\) is a loop going once around the central circle and \(g\) is
a loop going once around the boundary circle, then in \(\pi_1(X)\),
\[
\boxed{[g]=[f]^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Use the standard quotient model
\[
X=[0,1]\times[-1,1]/(0,t)\sim(1,-t).
\]
The central circle is represented by
\[
s\longmapsto[s,0],
\]
which goes once around the quotient.

The boundary consists of the two horizontal edges
\[
[0,1]\times\{1\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[0,1]\times\{-1\},
\]
joined by the endpoint identifications. Going once around the single
boundary component traverses the \(s\)-direction twice before returning to
the starting point. After deformation retraction to the central circle, this
becomes the loop that winds twice. Hence
\[
[g]=[f]^2.
\]
:::

<1>10. Consequently, under the identifications
\[
\pi_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
the inclusion-induced map is
\[
\boxed{
i_*:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
n\longmapsto2n}.
\]

::: {.proof}
A generator of \(\pi_1(A)\) is represented by \(g\), and a generator of
\(\pi_1(X)\) is represented by \(f\). Step <1>9 says
\[
i_*([g])=[f]^2,
\]
which is multiplication by \(2\) in additive notation.
:::

<1>11. There is no retraction from the Möbius band onto its boundary circle.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
r:X\to A
\]
were a retraction. Then
\[
r_*\circ i_*
=
\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z}.
\]
Every homomorphism
\[
r_*:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z
\]
is multiplication by some integer \(k\). By step <1>10,
\[
(r_*\circ i_*)(1)
=
r_*(2)
=
2k.
\]
The identity condition would require
\[
2k=1,
\]
which has no integer solution. Contradiction.
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3, <1>6, and <1>11 prove the three assigned cases (a), (b), and
(f), respectively.
:::
