::: {.problem}
For a CW complex $X$, show that if the $1$-skeleton $X^{(1)}$ is path-connected, then so is $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
$$
x\in X.
$$
Then $x$ lies in some open cell
$$
e^n\subseteq X
$$
for a finite integer $n\ge0$.


::: pf-proof

The open cells of a CW complex partition the underlying space.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $n\le1$, then
$$
x\in X^{(1)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition, the $1$-skeleton is the union of all cells of dimensions
$0$ and $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Suppose $n\ge2$, and let
$$
\Phi:D^n\to X^{(n)}
$$
be a characteristic map for the cell $e^n$, so that
$$
\Phi(\operatorname{int}D^n)=e^n
$$
and
$$
\Phi(S^{n-1})\subseteq X^{(n-1)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the definition of an $n$-cell attachment in a CW complex.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Choose
$$
u\in\operatorname{int}D^n
$$
with
$$
\Phi(u)=x.
$$
Choose any point
$$
v\in S^{n-1}
$$
lying on the ray from the origin through $u$.


::: pf-proof

Since $\Phi$ maps the open disk homeomorphically onto $e^n$, the point
$u$ exists and is unique. Because $u\ne0$ unless $x$ is the image of
the origin, one may take
$$
v=\frac{u}{\norm{u}}.
$$
If $u=0$, choose any $v\in S^{n-1}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The line segment
$$
\lambda(t)=(1-t)u+tv
$$
lies in $D^n$ and joins $u$ to $v$.


::: pf-proof

The disk $D^n$ is convex, so every convex combination of $u$ and $v$
lies in $D^n$. At $t=0$ the segment is $u$, and at $t=1$ it is
$v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
\gamma=\Phi\circ\lambda
$$
is a path in $X$ from $x$ to the point
$$
\Phi(v)\in X^{(n-1)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Continuity follows from continuity of $\Phi$ and $\lambda$. The endpoint
lies in the lower skeleton by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

By repeating step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} finitely many times, every point of $X$ can be
joined by a path in $X$ to some point of $X^{(1)}$.


::: pf-proof

Starting in a cell of dimension $n$, step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} lowers the skeleton
dimension by at least one. Repeating reaches dimension $1$ after at most
$n-1$ iterations.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let $x,y\in X$. Choose paths
$$
\alpha:x\leadsto x_1,
\qquad
\beta:y\leadsto y_1
$$
with
$$
x_1,y_1\in X^{(1)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} separately to $x$ and $y$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $X^{(1)}$ is path connected, there is a path
$$
\delta:x_1\leadsto y_1
$$
lying entirely in $X^{(1)}$.


::: pf-proof

This is the hypothesis of the problem.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The concatenation
$$
\alpha*\delta*\overline\beta
$$
is a path in $X$ from $x$ to $y$.


::: pf-proof

The path $\alpha$ goes from $x$ to $x_1$, the path $\delta$ goes
from $x_1$ to $y_1$, and the reverse path $\overline\beta$ goes from
$y_1$ to $y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Hence
$$
\boxed{X\text{ is path connected}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The points $x,y\in X$ were arbitrary, and step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} joins any such pair
by a path.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
