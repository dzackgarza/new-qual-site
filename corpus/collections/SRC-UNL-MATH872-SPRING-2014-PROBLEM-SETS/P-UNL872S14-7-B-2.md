::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 52 #1.

Show that the free product
\[
G*H
\]
of nontrivial groups \(G\) and \(H\) has trivial center, and that the only
elements of \(G*H\) of finite order are conjugates of finite-order elements of
\(G\) and \(H\).
:::

::: {.solution}
Recall that every nonidentity element of \(G*H\) has a unique reduced word
\[
x_1x_2\cdots x_n,
\]
where each \(x_i\) is a nonidentity element of one factor and consecutive
letters lie in different factors.

<1>1. Every nonidentity element \(w\in G*H\) is conjugate to a cyclically
reduced word.

::: {.proof}
Write \(w=x_1\cdots x_n\) in reduced form.

If \(n\le1\), then \(w\) is already cyclically reduced.

If \(n\ge2\) and \(x_1,x_n\) lie in different factors, the word is
cyclically reduced by definition.

If \(x_1,x_n\) lie in the same factor, conjugate by \(x_1^{-1}\):
\[
x_1^{-1}wx_1
=
x_2\cdots x_nx_1.
\]
The final two letters \(x_nx_1\) lie in the same factor, so they either
multiply to a nonidentity single letter or cancel completely. In either case
the reduced length strictly decreases.

Repeating this process terminates and yields a cyclically reduced conjugate.
:::

<1>2. Let
\[
u=x_1\cdots x_n
\]
be cyclically reduced with \(n\ge2\). Then for every \(m\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
u^m
=
(x_1\cdots x_n)^m
}
\]
is already reduced and has length \(mn\).

::: {.proof}
Because \(u\) is cyclically reduced, the final letter \(x_n\) and the initial
letter \(x_1\) lie in different free factors. Thus when two copies of \(u\)
are concatenated, no cancellation or multiplication occurs at the join.
The same is true at every join in \(u^m\). Hence the whole product is
reduced and contains exactly \(mn\) letters.
:::

<1>3. A cyclically reduced word of length at least \(2\) has infinite order.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, every positive power \(u^m\) has positive reduced length
\(mn\), so
\[
u^m\ne1
\]
for every \(m\ge1\).
:::

<1>4. If \(w\in G*H\) has finite order, then \(w\) is conjugate to an
element of \(G\) or of \(H\).

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, \(w\) is conjugate to a cyclically reduced word \(u\).
Conjugation preserves order.

If the reduced length of \(u\) were at least \(2\), step <1>3 would give
infinite order. Therefore the reduced length of \(u\) is \(1\), so
\[
u\in G\setminus\{1\}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
u\in H\setminus\{1\}.
\]
:::

<1>5. If \(w\) has finite order, then the factor element to which it is
conjugate also has finite order.

::: {.proof}
If
\[
w=sus^{-1},
\]
then
\[
w^m=su^ms^{-1}.
\]
Thus
\[
w^m=1
\iff
u^m=1.
\]
Hence \(w\) and \(u\) have the same order.
:::

<1>6. Conversely, every conjugate of a finite-order element of \(G\) or \(H\)
has finite order.

::: {.proof}
Conjugation preserves order by the calculation in step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Therefore the finite-order elements of \(G*H\) are exactly the
conjugates of finite-order elements of the two factors.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4--<1>6 give both inclusions.
:::

<1>8. Let \(z\in Z(G*H)\) be central. If \(z\ne1\), choose a cyclically
reduced conjugate
\[
u=s^{-1}zs.
\]
Then \(u\) is also central.

::: {.proof}
The center is invariant under conjugation:
if \(z\) commutes with every group element, then for every \(x\),
\[
(s^{-1}zs)x
=
s^{-1}z(sxs^{-1})s
=
s^{-1}(sxs^{-1})zs
=
x(s^{-1}zs).
\]
Thus \(u\in Z(G*H)\).
:::

<1>9. A nontrivial cyclically reduced central element cannot have reduced
length \(1\).

::: {.proof}
Suppose first that
\[
u\in G\setminus\{1\}.
\]
Choose
\[
h\in H\setminus\{1\},
\]
possible because \(H\) is nontrivial.

The word
\[
uh
\]
is reduced with letters from \(G,H\), while
\[
hu
\]
is reduced with letters from \(H,G\). Uniqueness of reduced normal form gives
\[
uh\ne hu.
\]
Thus \(u\) is not central.

The case
\[
u\in H\setminus\{1\}
\]
is symmetric, using a nonidentity \(g\in G\).
:::

<1>10. A nontrivial cyclically reduced central element cannot have reduced
length \(n\ge2\).

::: {.proof}
Write
\[
u=x_1x_2\cdots x_n
\]
cyclically reduced.

Assume first that
\[
x_1\in G.
\]
Choose a nonidentity
\[
h\in H.
\]

Because \(u\) is cyclically reduced and \(n\ge2\), the last letter \(x_n\)
lies in the factor opposite to \(x_1\), namely \(H\).

The product
\[
hu
\]
is reduced and begins with the two letters
\[
h,\ x_1
\]
from \(H,G\).

For
\[
uh,
\]
the final letter \(x_n\) and \(h\) lie in the same factor \(H\), so after
reduction either they combine to one \(H\)-letter or cancel. In either case,
the reduced word for \(uh\) begins with
\[
x_1\in G,
\]
whereas the reduced word for \(hu\) begins with
\[
h\in H.
\]
Hence
\[
uh\ne hu.
\]

If \(x_1\in H\), the same argument uses a nonidentity element of \(G\).
Thus no cyclically reduced word of length at least \(2\) is central.
:::

<1>11. Hence
\[
\boxed{Z(G*H)=\{1\}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Suppose \(z\) were a nonidentity central element. Step <1>8 would give a
nonidentity cyclically reduced central conjugate \(u\). Its reduced length is
either \(1\) or at least \(2\), excluded by steps <1>9 and <1>10.
Therefore no such \(z\) exists.
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 classifies the finite-order elements, and step <1>11 proves that
the center is trivial.
:::
:::
