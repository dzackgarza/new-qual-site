::: {.problem}
For the symmetric group permuting 3 objects, $\mathrm { S } _ { 3 } = < \mathrm { a , b } \mid \mathrm { a } ^ { 2 } = \mathrm { b } ^ { 2 } = ( \mathrm { a b } ) ^ { 3 } = 1 > :$ Find the presentation complex, the Cayley complex, and all connected covering spaces of the presentation complex.
Describe all of the cells and attaching maps of these CW complexes, in addition to drawing pictures.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
G=S_3
=
\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=(ab)^3=1
\rangle.
$$
Take, for concreteness,
$$
a=(12),
\qquad
b=(23).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The presentation complex $X$ has one $0$-cell, two oriented $1$-cells $(a,b)$, and three $2$-cells attached by the words
$$
\boxed{a^2,\qquad b^2,\qquad (ab)^3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard CW complex associated to the given presentation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The fundamental group of $X$ is $S_3$.


::: pf-proof

By the presentation theorem for presentation complexes,
$$
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=(ab)^3=1
\rangle
\cong
S_3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For any subgroup $H\le G$, the connected covering corresponding to $H$ has one $0$-cell for each right coset
$$
Hg\in H\backslash G.
$$


::: pf-proof

The fiber over the unique $0$-cell of $X$ is the coset set
$$
H\backslash G.
$$
This is the usual Schreier construction for the covering associated to a subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

From each coset $Hg$, there is one oriented lift of the $a$-cell
$$
Hg\longrightarrow Hga
$$
and one oriented lift of the $b$-cell
$$
Hg\longrightarrow Hgb.
$$


::: pf-proof

Lift the two oriented generator loops from each point of the fiber.
Their endpoints are obtained by right multiplication by the corresponding generator.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For each coset $Hg$, attach one lift of each base $2$-cell, along the closed edge-paths beginning at $Hg$ and labeled
$$
\boxed{a^2,\qquad b^2,\qquad (ab)^3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

A relator represents the trivial element of $G$, hence right multiplication by its word returns every coset to itself.
Therefore the lifted relator path is closed at every vertex, and each base $2$-cell has exactly one lift over each sheet.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The universal cover, corresponding to $H=\{1\}$, is the Cayley complex of $S_3$.


::: pf-proof

When $H$ is trivial, the vertices are the six group elements.
Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} are exactly the Cayley graph and Cayley $2$-complex construction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The Cayley graph has six vertices and twelve $1$-cells: six labeled $a$ and six labeled $b$.


::: pf-proof

There is one lifted $a$-cell and one lifted $b$-cell for each of the six vertices by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Since $a^2=b^2=1$, the vertices are paired by the $a$-action and by the $b$-action.
Each paired pair is joined by two parallel oriented lifts of the corresponding generator edge.


::: pf-proof

For example, if $h=ga$, then the lifted $a$-edge at $g$ runs from $g$ to $h$, while the lifted $a$-edge at $h$ runs from
$$
h
$$
to
$$
ha=gaa=g.
$$
These are distinct lifts of the single open $a$-cell and have the same two endpoints.
The same argument applies to $b$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Collapsing each parallel pair in the picture to a single edge produces a hexagon whose edge labels alternate
$$
\boxed{a,b,a,b,a,b}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The simple right-multiplication graph has one $a$-neighbor and one $b$-neighbor at each vertex, hence is connected and $2$-regular on six vertices.
It is therefore a $6$-cycle.
Successive multiplication alternates the labels $(a,b)$, and
$$
(ab)^3=1
$$
closes the cycle after six steps.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The Cayley complex has eighteen $2$-cells: six lifts of each of the three base $2$-cells.


::: pf-proof

The universal cover has six sheets, and every open cell in a covering lifts to one open cell over each sheet.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

For each $a$-paired pair of vertices, the two $a^2$-cells based at those vertices attach to the same $2$-edge $a$-circle, one from each side; together they form a $2$-sphere.
There are three such $a$-spheres.
The same is true for $b$.


::: pf-proof

At a vertex $g$, the path labeled $a^2$ traverses the two parallel $a$-edges joining $g$ and $ga$, returning to $g$. Starting at $ga$ traverses the same $2$-edge circle with the other cyclic starting point.
The two lifted disks therefore share their whole boundary and form $S^2$. The $a$-action has three orbits of size two; similarly for $b$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The six lifts of the $(ab)^3$-cell attach along the lifted alternating length-six relator paths in the Cayley graph.


::: pf-proof

Starting from each of the six vertices, follow successively
$$
a,b,a,b,a,b.
$$
Because
$$
(ab)^3=1,
$$
the path closes.
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} attaches one $2$-cell along each such lifted loop.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The Cayley complex is simply connected.


::: pf-proof

It is the universal cover of the presentation complex $X$, so its fundamental group is trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Up to conjugacy, the subgroups of $S_3$ are exactly
$$
\boxed{
S_3,\qquad
A_3=\langle ab
\rangle,\qquad
\langle a
\rangle,\qquad
\{1\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The subgroup orders divide $6$. There is one subgroup of order $3$, namely
$$
A_3.
$$
There are three subgroups of order $2$, generated by the three transpositions, and these are conjugate.
The remaining subgroup orders $1$ and $6$ give the trivial and full subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Therefore there are exactly four connected covering spaces of $X$, up to unbased isomorphism, of degrees
$$
\boxed{1, 2, 3, 6}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Connected covering spaces up to unbased isomorphism correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups of
$$
\pi_1(X)\cong S_3.
$$
The covering degree is the subgroup index.
Apply step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

The degree-$1$ cover is $X$ itself.


::: pf-proof

It corresponds to the full subgroup $S_3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

The degree-$2$ cover corresponds to
$$
H=A_3=\langle ab
\rangle.
$$
Its $1$-skeleton has two vertices.
Both $a$ and $b$ interchange the two vertices.


::: pf-proof

The two right cosets are
$$
H
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Ha.
$$
Both $a$ and $b$ are odd permutations, so right multiplication by either swaps the even and odd cosets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Thus the degree-$2$ $1$-skeleton consists of two vertices joined by two parallel $a$-edges and two parallel $b$-edges.


::: pf-proof

Each generator has two lifted open $1$-cells, one starting at each vertex.
Step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref} shows both run between the two vertices in opposite orientations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

The degree-$2$ cover has two lifts of each relator $2$-cell, attached along the lifted words
$$
a^2,\qquad b^2,\qquad (ab)^3
$$
from the two vertices.


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} with
$$
[G:H]=2.
$$
This completely specifies the cells and attaching maps.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

The degree-$3$ cover may be represented by
$$
H=\langle a
\rangle.
$$
Let its three right cosets be
$$
C_0=H,\qquad
C_1=Hb,\qquad
C_2=Hba.
$$


::: pf-proof

The subgroup $H$ has order $2$, hence index $3$. The displayed cosets contain all six elements of $S_3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

Right multiplication by $a$ acts on the three cosets by
$$
\boxed{
C_0\mapsto C_0,\qquad
C_1\leftrightarrow C_2},
$$
while right multiplication by $b$ acts by
$$
\boxed{
C_0\leftrightarrow C_1,\qquad
C_2\mapsto C_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $a\in H$,
$$
Ha=H.
$$
Also
$$
(Hb)a=Hba=C_2
$$
and
$$
(Hba)a=Hb=C_1.
$$
For $b$,
$$
Hb=C_1,\qquad
(Hb)b=H=C_0.
$$
Finally,
$$
(Hba)b=Hbab.
$$
Using
$$
bab=aba,
$$
one has
$$
Hbab=Haba=Hba=C_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

Hence the degree-$3$ $1$-skeleton has:
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{one }a\text{-loop at }C_0,\text{ and two parallel }a\text{-edges between }C_1,C_2;\\
\text{one }b\text{-loop at }C_2,\text{ and two parallel }b\text{-edges between }C_0,C_1.
\end{array}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Translate the coset action in step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref} into the Schreier graph rule from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-23}

The degree-$3$ cover has three lifts of each relator $2$-cell, attached from $C_0,C_1,C_2$ along the paths labeled
$$
a^2,\qquad b^2,\qquad (ab)^3.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} with three cosets.
This specifies all nine $2$-cells and their attaching maps.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-24}

The degree-$6$ cover is the Cayley complex described in steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

It corresponds to the trivial subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-25}

These four covers exhaust all connected covering spaces of the presentation complex.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} proves there are exactly four unbased isomorphism classes, and steps [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-24){.pf-ref} construct one representative of each.

:::
