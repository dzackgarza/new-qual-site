::: {.problem}
(a) Explain how the 2-sphere $\mathrm { S } ^ { 2 }$ is (homeomorphic to) a quotient of the square I x I with a choice of identifications among the edges on the boundary of the square (i.e. identifying sets of edges with arrows).
(Show your map by pictures only.)

(b) Let X be the quotient $\mathrm { o f } \mathrm { { S } } ^ { 2 }$obtained by identifying the north and south poles to a single point.
Using part (a) and the Seifert-van Kampen Theorem, compute$\pi _ { 1 } ( \mathrm { X } )$ . (This part does need proof details.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Let \(D^2\) denote a closed disk.

## Part (a)

<1>1. The quotient
\[
\boxed{
D^2/\partial D^2}
\]
is homeomorphic to \(S^2\).

::: {.proof}
View \(S^2\) as the one-point compactification of the open disk
\[
\operatorname{int}(D^2)\cong\mathbb R^2.
\]
Collapsing the boundary circle \(\partial D^2\) to one point adds exactly one
point at infinity to the open disk. Hence
\[
D^2/\partial D^2
\cong
S^2.
\]
:::

<1>2. Thus a square model for \(S^2\) is obtained by collapsing the entire
boundary of
\[
I^2
\]
to one point.

::: {.proof}
The square \(I^2\) is homeomorphic to \(D^2\). Transport the quotient from
step <1>1 across such a homeomorphism.
:::

<1>3. Equivalently, after subdividing the boundary into four edges, all four
boundary edges are identified to the same boundary point in the quotient.

::: {.proof}
This is just the square version of the relation
\[
\partial D^2\sim\{\ast\}.
\]
It is the requested edge-identification picture: every boundary point lies in
one equivalence class.
:::

## Part (b)

Let
\[
q:S^2\to X
\]
identify the north and south poles
\[
N,S\in S^2
\]
to a single point
\[
x_0=q(N)=q(S).
\]

<1>4. Choose an embedded arc
\[
\gamma:[0,1]\to S^2
\]
from \(N\) to \(S\), with no self-intersections.

::: {.proof}
For example, take a meridian semicircle from the north pole to the south
pole.
:::

<1>5. The image
\[
C=q(\gamma([0,1]))
\]
is a circle in \(X\).

::: {.proof}
The quotient identifies only the two endpoints
\[
\gamma(0)=N
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\gamma(1)=S.
\]
Thus
\[
C
\cong
[0,1]/(0\sim1)
\cong
S^1.
\]
:::

<1>6. Cutting \(S^2\) open along the arc \(\gamma\) gives a closed disk
\(D\).

::: {.proof}
An embedded arc joining two distinct points of \(S^2\) has a regular
neighborhood that is a disk strip. Cutting the sphere along the arc produces
a simply connected compact surface with one boundary component, hence a
disk. Concretely, for a meridian arc this is visible by flattening the sphere
along the meridian.
:::

<1>7. The boundary of \(D\) consists of two copies
\[
\gamma_+
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\gamma_-
\]
of the arc, traversed in opposite directions.

::: {.proof}
Cutting along an arc duplicates the arc into the two sides of the cut.
The induced boundary orientation traverses one copy from \(N\) to \(S\) and
the other from \(S\) back to \(N\).
:::

<1>8. After identifying \(N\sim S\), the quotient \(X\) is obtained from the
circle \(C\) by attaching the disk \(D\) along the loop
\[
\boxed{aa^{-1}},
\]
where \(a\) denotes one positive traversal of \(C\).

::: {.proof}
The copy \(\gamma_+\) maps to one traversal \(a\) of \(C\). The second copy
\(\gamma_-\) maps to the same geometric circle with the opposite
orientation, hence to \(a^{-1}\). Therefore the attaching map around
\(\partial D\) represents
\[
aa^{-1}.
\]
:::

<1>9. The attaching loop in step <1>8 is nullhomotopic in \(C\).

::: {.proof}
In
\[
\pi_1(C,x_0)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
the word
\[
aa^{-1}
\]
represents the identity.
:::

<1>10. Let \(U\subset X\) be a small open neighborhood of \(C\), and let
\(V\subset X\) be the union of the interior of the attached disk with a thin
collar of its boundary. Then
\[
U\simeq C,
\qquad
V\simeq D^2,
\]
and \(U\cap V\) deformation retracts onto the attaching circle
\[
\partial D.
\]

::: {.proof}
Choose the usual open neighborhoods in a CW complex obtained by thickening
the \(1\)-skeleton and the \(2\)-cell. The collar neighborhood of the
attaching map gives the stated deformation retractions.
:::

<1>11. Under the deformation retractions in step <1>10, the inclusion
\[
U\cap V\hookrightarrow U
\]
induces the trivial homomorphism on fundamental groups.

::: {.proof}
A generator of
\[
\pi_1(U\cap V)\cong\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
maps to the attaching loop
\[
aa^{-1}
\]
in \(C\), which is nullhomotopic by step <1>9.
:::

<1>12. Also
\[
\pi_1(V)=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
The space \(V\) deformation retracts onto the disk \(D^2\), which is
contractible.
:::

<1>13. Seifert--van Kampen gives
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X,x_0)
\cong
\pi_1(C,x_0)/
\langle\!\langle aa^{-1}\rangle\!\rangle}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply van Kampen to the open cover \(X=U\cup V\). Since
\[
\pi_1(V)=0,
\]
the fundamental group is obtained from \(\pi_1(U)\cong\pi_1(C)\) by
quotienting by the normal closure of the image of
\[
\pi_1(U\cap V),
\]
which step <1>11 identifies with the attaching word \(aa^{-1}\).
:::

<1>14. Since the relation \(aa^{-1}=1\) is already trivial,
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\pi_1(S^1)
\cong
\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The normal closure of the identity element is trivial, so the quotient in
step <1>13 does not impose any new relation on the generator \(a\).
:::

<1>15. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 give the square quotient model for \(S^2\), and steps
<1>4--<1>14 compute the fundamental group of the pole-identification quotient
using Seifert--van Kampen.
:::
:::
