::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 158 #29.

Let \(M_g\) be the closed orientable surface of genus \(g\), embedded in
\(\RR^3\) in the standard way, and let \(R\) be the compact region that
it bounds. Form a closed \(3\)-manifold \(X\) by gluing two copies of \(R\)
along their common boundary \(M_g\) by the identity map.

Compute the homology groups of \(X\) using the Mayer--Vietoris sequence.
Also compute the relative homology groups
\[
H_i(R,M_g).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The handlebody \(R\) deformation retracts onto a wedge of \(g\) circles:
\[
\boxed{R\simeq\bigvee_{j=1}^gS^1}.
\]


::: pf-proof

A standard genus-\(g\) handlebody is a regular neighborhood of a graph with
one vertex and \(g\) loop edges. Collapsing the normal disk fibers of this
regular neighborhood gives a deformation retraction onto that graph.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_k(R)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
\ZZ^g,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Apply homotopy invariance to step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and use the homology of a wedge of
\(g\) circles.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The boundary surface \(M_g\) has homology
\[
\boxed{
H_k(M_g)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,2,\\
\ZZ^{2g},&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

This is the standard homology computation for a closed orientable surface of
genus \(g\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The inclusion
\[
i:M_g\hookrightarrow R
\]
induces a surjection
\[
\boxed{
i_*:H_1(M_g)\twoheadrightarrow H_1(R)}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Choose the usual symplectic basis
\[
a_1,b_1,\dots,a_g,b_g
\]
for \(H_1(M_g)\), where each \(a_j\) is a meridian bounding a disk in the
handlebody and each \(b_j\) runs along the \(j\)-th handle.

Under inclusion,
\[
i_*[a_j]=0
\]
because \(a_j\) bounds a compressing disk in \(R\), while the classes
\[
i_*[b_1],\dots,i_*[b_g]
\]
form a basis of
\[
H_1(R)\cong\ZZ^g
\]
under the deformation retraction in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Hence \(i_*\) is surjective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The kernel of \(i_*\) is free abelian of rank \(g\):
\[
\boxed{\ker i_*\cong\ZZ^g}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The map in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is a surjection
\[
\ZZ^{2g}\twoheadrightarrow\ZZ^g.
\]
Its kernel is free because it is a subgroup of a free abelian group, and
rank-nullity gives rank \(2g-g=g\). Concretely it is generated by the
meridian classes \([a_1],\dots,[a_g]\).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Write
\[
X=R_1\cup R_2
\]
for the two copies of \(R\). After thickening the two pieces slightly,
Mayer--Vietoris has intersection homotopy equivalent to \(M_g\).


::: pf-proof

Take collar neighborhoods of the common boundary in the two copies. Their
intersection deformation retracts onto \(M_g\), while each enlarged piece
deformation retracts onto its copy of \(R\). Thus the ordinary
Mayer--Vietoris sequence applies with the homology groups of \(R\) and
\(M_g\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The Mayer--Vietoris map
\[
H_1(M_g)
\longrightarrow
H_1(R_1)\oplus H_1(R_2)
\]
is
\[
\boxed{x\longmapsto(i_*x,-i_*x)}.
\]


::: pf-proof

For a union \(X=A\cup B\), the Mayer--Vietoris map from the intersection is
the difference of the two inclusion-induced maps. Since the two boundary
identifications are the identity, both inclusions induce the same map \(i_*\)
after identifying \(R_1,R_2\) with \(R\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The kernel of the map in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is
\[
\boxed{\ker i_*\cong\ZZ^g}.
\]


::: pf-proof

One has
\[
(i_*x,-i_*x)=(0,0)
\iff
i_*x=0.
\]
Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The cokernel of the map in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is
\[
\boxed{\ZZ^g}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By surjectivity of \(i_*\),
\[
\operatorname{im}(i_*,-i_*)
=
\{(u,-u):u\in\ZZ^g\},
\]
the anti-diagonal subgroup of
\[
\ZZ^g\oplus\ZZ^g.
\]
The homomorphism
\[
\ZZ^g\oplus\ZZ^g\to\ZZ^g,
\qquad
(u,v)\mapsto u+v,
\]
is surjective with exactly this kernel. The first isomorphism theorem gives
the claimed cokernel.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

In degree \(3\), Mayer--Vietoris gives
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_3(X)
\longrightarrow
H_2(M_g)
\longrightarrow
0.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
\[
H_3(R_1)\oplus H_3(R_2)=0
\]
and
\[
H_2(R_1)\oplus H_2(R_2)=0.
\]
Insert these groups into the Mayer--Vietoris sequence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Hence
\[
\boxed{H_3(X)\cong\ZZ}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref},
\[
H_3(X)\cong H_2(M_g),
\]
and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
\[
H_2(M_g)\cong\ZZ.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

In degree \(2\), Mayer--Vietoris gives
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_2(X)
\longrightarrow
H_1(M_g)
\xrightarrow{(i_*,-i_*)}
H_1(R_1)\oplus H_1(R_2).
\]


::: pf-proof

Again use
\[
H_2(R_1)\oplus H_2(R_2)=0
\]
in the exact sequence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{H_2(X)\cong\ZZ^g}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Exactness in step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} identifies
\[
H_2(X)
\cong
\ker(i_*,-i_*),
\]
which is \(\ZZ^g\) by step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The low-degree Mayer--Vietoris sequence contains
\[
H_1(M_g)
\xrightarrow{(i_*,-i_*)}
H_1(R_1)\oplus H_1(R_2)
\longrightarrow
H_1(X)
\longrightarrow
H_0(M_g)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_0(R_1)\oplus H_0(R_2).
\]


::: pf-proof

This is the usual Mayer--Vietoris sequence in degrees \(1\) and \(0\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

The map
\[
\delta:H_0(M_g)\to H_0(R_1)\oplus H_0(R_2)
\]
is injective.


::: pf-proof

All three spaces are connected, so each \(H_0\) is \(\ZZ\). The
Mayer--Vietoris map is
\[
1\longmapsto(1,-1),
\]
which is injective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_1(X)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}(i_*,-i_*)
\cong
\ZZ^g}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By exactness and injectivity in step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}, the map
\[
H_1(R_1)\oplus H_1(R_2)\to H_1(X)
\]
is surjective with kernel
\[
\operatorname{im}(i_*,-i_*).
\]
Apply step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Finally,
\[
\boxed{H_0(X)\cong\ZZ}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The double \(X\) is connected. Equivalently, exactness of Mayer--Vietoris
after step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} gives the standard connected \(H_0\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Thus
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,3,\\
\ZZ^g,&k=1,2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-17){.pf-ref}. The space is
\(3\)-dimensional, so higher homology vanishes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

For the pair \((R,M_g)\), the long exact sequence begins in high
degree as
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_3(R,M_g)
\longrightarrow
H_2(M_g)
\longrightarrow
H_2(R)=0.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
\[
H_3(R)=H_2(R)=0.
\]
Insert these into the long exact sequence of the pair.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{H_3(R,M_g)\cong\ZZ}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-19){.pf-ref} identifies
\[
H_3(R,M_g)\cong H_2(M_g),
\]
and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives \(H_2(M_g)\cong\ZZ\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

The next part of the pair sequence is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_2(R,M_g)
\longrightarrow
H_1(M_g)
\xrightarrow{i_*}
H_1(R)
\longrightarrow
H_1(R,M_g)
\longrightarrow
H_0(M_g)
\longrightarrow
H_0(R).
\]


::: pf-proof

Use \(H_2(R)=0\) from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} in the long exact sequence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

The final map
\[
H_0(M_g)\to H_0(R)
\]
is an isomorphism.


::: pf-proof

Both spaces are connected and the inclusion sends their unique path
component to the unique path component.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-23}

Hence
\[
\boxed{H_1(R,M_g)=0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the map
\[
i_*:H_1(M_g)\to H_1(R)
\]
is surjective. Exactness in step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref} then makes
\[
H_1(R)\to H_1(R,M_g)
\]
the zero map. Exactness together with the injective map from
\(H_1(R,M_g)\) into \(H_0(M_g)\), whose image must be the kernel of the
isomorphism in step [](#s1-22){.pf-ref}, forces
\[
H_1(R,M_g)=0.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-24}

Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
H_2(R,M_g)
\cong
\ker i_*
\cong
\ZZ^g}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Exactness in step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref} identifies
\[
H_2(R,M_g)
\]
with the kernel of \(i_*\). Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-25}

One also has
\[
\boxed{H_0(R,M_g)=0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The tail of the long exact sequence is
\[
H_0(M_g)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_0(R)\to H_0(R,M_g)\to0.
\]
The first map is an isomorphism by step [](#s1-22){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-26}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_k(R,M_g)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=3,\\
\ZZ^g,&k=2,\\
0,&k\ne2,3.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-20){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-23){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-24){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-25){.pf-ref}. There is no homology above
degree \(3\).

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-18){.pf-ref} computes the homology of the double \(X\), and step [](#s1-26){.pf-ref}
computes the relative groups \(H_*(R,M_g)\).

:::
