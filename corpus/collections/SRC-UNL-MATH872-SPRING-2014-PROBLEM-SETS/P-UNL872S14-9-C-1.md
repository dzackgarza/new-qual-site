::: {.problem}
Show that if the map $f \colon S^{n-1} \to X$ is used to attach an $n$-cell, with $n > 2$, to $X$ to form a space $Y$, then the inclusion $X \to Y$ induces an isomorphism from $\pi_{1}(X)$ to $\pi_{1}(Y)$. Show that the same is true if we attach any (finite or infinite) collection of cells of dimension $> 2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix a basepoint $x_0\in X$.

## One attached cell

Suppose
$$
Y=X\cup_f D^n,
\qquad
f:S^{n-1}\to X,
\qquad
n>2.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Choose open sets $U,V\subseteq Y$ such that
$$
X\subseteq U,
\qquad
U\simeq X,
$$
$$
V\simeq D^n,
$$
and
$$
U\cap V\simeq S^{n-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take $V$ to be the image of the open $n$-cell together with a thin collar
of its boundary. Take $U$ to be $X$ together with the complementary thin
collar inside the attached disk.

Radially push the collar in $U$ back to the attaching sphere and then into
$X$; this gives $U\simeq X$. The set $V$ deformation retracts onto the
disk, and the collar intersection deformation retracts onto the attaching
sphere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\boxed{\pi_1(V)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The set $V$ is homotopy equivalent to the contractible disk $D^n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
\boxed{\pi_1(U\cap V)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
U\cap V\simeq S^{n-1}.
$$
Since
$$
n>2,
$$
one has
$$
n-1\ge2,
$$
and $S^{n-1}$ is simply connected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Seifert--van Kampen gives
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(Y,x_0)
\cong
\pi_1(U,x_0)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Van Kampen identifies
$$
\pi_1(Y)
$$
as the pushout of
$$
\pi_1(U)
\leftarrow
\pi_1(U\cap V)
\rightarrow
\pi_1(V).
$$
By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the latter two groups are trivial. Thus the pushout is
just $\pi_1(U)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Since $U\simeq X$,
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(U,x_0)
\cong
\pi_1(X,x_0)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Homotopy equivalent based path-connected spaces have isomorphic fundamental
groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Under the isomorphisms in steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, the map
$$
\pi_1(X,x_0)\to\pi_1(Y,x_0)
$$
is exactly the homomorphism induced by the inclusion
$$
X\hookrightarrow Y.
$$


::: pf-proof

The deformation retraction $U\simeq X$ fixes $X$, so the identification
$$
\pi_1(U)\cong\pi_1(X)
$$
is induced by the inclusion $X\hookrightarrow U$. The van Kampen map
$$
\pi_1(U)\to\pi_1(Y)
$$
is induced by $U\hookrightarrow Y$. Their composite is induced by
$$
X\hookrightarrow Y.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X,x_0)
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
\pi_1(Y,x_0)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Attaching finitely many cells of dimensions $>2$ preserves the
fundamental group.


::: pf-proof

Attach the cells one at a time. Each step is covered by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}. A finite
composition of isomorphisms is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Every loop
$$
\gamma:S^1\to Y
$$
has image contained in
$$
X\cup
\bigcup_{\alpha\in F}e_\alpha^{n_\alpha}
$$
for some finite set $F\subseteq A$.


::: pf-proof

The image $\gamma(S^1)$ is compact. In a relative CW complex, every compact
subset meets only finitely many open cells. Therefore it meets only finitely
many of the attached cells.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The inclusion
$$
i:X\hookrightarrow Y
$$
induces a surjection on $\pi_1$.


::: pf-proof

Let $[\gamma]\in\pi_1(Y,x_0)$. By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, $\gamma$ lies in a finite
subcomplex
$$
Y_F
=
X\cup
\bigcup_{\alpha\in F}e_\alpha^{n_\alpha}.
$$
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
\pi_1(X,x_0)\to\pi_1(Y_F,x_0)
$$
is an isomorphism. Thus $\gamma$ is homotopic in $Y_F\subseteq Y$ to a
loop in $X$. Hence every element of $\pi_1(Y)$ is in the image of
$i_*$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Suppose a loop
$$
\gamma:S^1\to X
$$
is nullhomotopic in $Y$. Then it is already nullhomotopic in a finite
subcomplex
$$
Y_F
=
X\cup
\bigcup_{\alpha\in F}e_\alpha^{n_\alpha}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose a nullhomotopy
$$
H:D^2\to Y.
$$
The image $H(D^2)$ is compact, so by the same compactness property used in
step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, it meets only finitely many attached open cells. Thus the whole
homotopy lands in some $Y_F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The inclusion-induced map
$$
i_*:\pi_1(X,x_0)\to\pi_1(Y,x_0)
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
i_*([\gamma])=1.
$$
Then $\gamma$ is nullhomotopic in $Y$. By step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}, it is
nullhomotopic in some finite $Y_F$. Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} says
$$
\pi_1(X)\to\pi_1(Y_F)
$$
is an isomorphism, so $[\gamma]=1$ already in $\pi_1(X)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Hence, after attaching any finite or infinite family of cells of
dimension $>2$,
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X,x_0)
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
\pi_1(Y,x_0)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Surjectivity is step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} and injectivity is step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves the one-cell case, step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} the finite case, and step
[](#s1-13){.pf-ref} the arbitrary case.

:::
