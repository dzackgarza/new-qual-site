::: {.problem}
For each integer $n$, let $\omega_n:I\to S^1$ be the loop
$$
\omega_n(s)=\bigl(\cos(2\pi ns),\sin(2\pi ns)\bigr).
$$

Define
$$
C(t)=(1-t)\frac12+t\frac{m}{m+n}
$$
and, whenever the displayed expressions are defined, set
$$
H(s,t)=\begin{cases}
\bigl(\cos(2\pi m\,s/C(t)),\sin(2\pi m\,s/C(t))\bigr),&0\le s\le C(t),\\[4pt]
\bigl(\cos(2\pi n\,(s-C(t))/(1-C(t))),\sin(2\pi n\,(s-C(t))/(1-C(t)))\bigr),&C(t)\le s\le1.
\end{cases}
$$

(a) Prove that, when this formula defines a homotopy, $H$ is a path homotopy from $\omega_m*\omega_n$ to $\omega_{m+n}$.

(b) For some integers $m,n$, the formula in part (a) does not define such a homotopy. Determine exactly which values fail, and construct a path homotopy $\omega_m*\omega_n\simeq\omega_{m+n}$ for those cases.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

At $t=0$, one has
$$
C(0)=\frac12,
$$
and
$$
\boxed{H(s,0)=(\omega_m*\omega_n)(s)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

When $0\le s\le1/2$,
$$
H(s,0)
=
\bigl(
\cos(2\pi m(2s)),
\sin(2\pi m(2s))
\bigr)
=
\omega_m(2s).
$$
When $1/2\le s\le1$,
$$
H(s,0)
=
\bigl(
\cos(2\pi n(2s-1)),
\sin(2\pi n(2s-1))
\bigr)
=
\omega_n(2s-1).
$$
This is exactly the standard concatenation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Suppose
$$
mn>0.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
0<
\frac{m}{m+n}
<1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $m,n>0$, then
$$
0<m<m+n.
$$
If $m,n<0$, then dividing the inequalities
$$
m+n<m<0
$$
by the negative number $m+n$ reverses their direction and again gives
$$
0<\frac{m}{m+n}<1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Under the hypothesis $mn>0$,
$$
\boxed{0<C(t)<1\qquad(0\le t\le1)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The number $C(t)$ is the convex combination
$$
C(t)
=(1-t)\frac12
+t\frac{m}{m+n}.
$$
Both endpoints lie strictly in $(0,1)$ by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, so every convex
combination does as well.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $mn>0$, the two formulas defining $H$ agree at
$$
s=C(t).
$$


::: pf-proof

For the first formula,
$$
H(C(t),t)
=
\bigl(
\cos(2\pi m),
\sin(2\pi m)
\bigr)
=(1,0).
$$
For the second,
$$
H(C(t),t)
=
\bigl(
\cos0,
\sin0
\bigr)
=(1,0).
$$
Thus the two branches agree along their common boundary.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $mn>0$, the map
$$
H:I\times I\to S^1
$$
is continuous.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, neither denominator
$$
C(t)
\qquad\text{nor}\qquad
1-C(t)
$$
vanishes. Hence each branch is continuous on its closed region
$$
\{(s,t):0\le s\le C(t)\}
$$
or
$$
\{(s,t):C(t)\le s\le1\}.
$$
These two closed regions cover $I\times I$, and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that the
two branch formulas agree on their intersection. The pasting lemma therefore
gives continuity of $H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If $mn>0$, then for every $t$,
$$
\boxed{
H(0,t)=H(1,t)=(1,0)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

At $s=0$, the first formula has phase $0$. At $s=1$, the second formula
has phase $2\pi n$. Since $n\in\ZZ$, both values are $(1,0)$.
Thus the homotopy fixes the endpoints of the loops.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If $mn>0$, then
$$
\boxed{H(s,1)=\omega_{m+n}(s)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

At $t=1$,
$$
C(1)=\frac{m}{m+n}.
$$
For
$$
0\le s\le C(1),
$$
the phase in the first branch is
$$
2\pi m\frac{s}{m/(m+n)}
=
2\pi(m+n)s.
$$
Thus the first branch equals $\omega_{m+n}(s)$.

For
$$
C(1)\le s\le1,
$$
one has
$$
1-C(1)
=
\frac{n}{m+n}.
$$
Hence the phase in the second branch is
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\pi n
\frac{s-m/(m+n)}{n/(m+n)}
&=
2\pi\bigl((m+n)s-m\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $m\in\ZZ$, subtracting $2\pi m$ does not change the point of
$S^1$. Thus this branch also equals
$$
\omega_{m+n}(s).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore, when
$$
\boxed{mn>0},
$$
the displayed $H$ is a path homotopy
$$
\boxed{
\omega_m*\omega_n
\simeq
\omega_{m+n}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives continuity, step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives a homotopy relative to the
endpoints, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} identifies the initial path, and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} identifies
the final path.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

If
$$
m+n=0,
$$
the formula for $C(t)$ is undefined.


::: pf-proof

The term
$$
\frac{m}{m+n}
$$
has zero denominator. This includes the case $m=n=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Suppose
$$
mn<0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
m+n\ne0.
$$
Then
$$
\frac{m}{m+n}\notin[0,1].
$$


::: pf-proof

If $m>0>n$, then either $m+n>0$, in which case
$$
\frac{m}{m+n}>1,
$$
or $m+n<0$, in which case the quotient is negative. The case
$$
m<0<n
$$
is symmetric.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Under the hypotheses of step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, there is
$$
t_0\in(0,1)
$$
such that
$$
C(t_0)\in\{0,1\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The function $C(t)$ is continuous,
$$
C(0)=\frac12,
$$
and by step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} its endpoint $C(1)$ lies outside $[0,1]$. The
intermediate value theorem therefore forces $C$ to cross either $0$ or
$1$ at some $t_0\in(0,1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

If $m=0$ and $n\ne0$, then
$$
C(1)=0;
$$
if $n=0$ and $m\ne0$, then
$$
C(1)=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute into
$$
C(1)=\frac{m}{m+n}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Consequently, the literal formula for $H$ defines a homotopy
exactly when
$$
\boxed{mn>0}.
$$
It fails for exactly the pairs
$$
\boxed{mn\le0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $mn>0$, step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves that the formula works.

If $m+n=0$, step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} shows that $C$ is undefined.

If $mn<0$ and $m+n\ne0$, step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} produces a time at which one
denominator $C(t)$ or $1-C(t)$ vanishes; after that the moving breakpoint
also leaves the interval $[0,1]$.

If exactly one of $m,n$ is zero, step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} shows that at $t=1$ one
branch has a zero denominator. Thus the displayed formula is not defined on
all of $I\times I$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Define a lift
$$
a:I\to\RR
$$
of the concatenated loop by
$$
a(s)
=
\begin{cases}
2ms,&0\le s\le\frac12,\\[4pt]
m+2n\left(s-\frac12\right),&\frac12\le s\le1.
\end{cases}
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
e^{2\pi ia(s)}
=(\omega_m*\omega_n)(s)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

On the first half,
$$
e^{2\pi i a(s)}
=
e^{2\pi i m(2s)}
=
\omega_m(2s).
$$
On the second half,
$$
\begin{aligned}
e^{2\pi ia(s)}
&=
e^{2\pi i m}
e^{2\pi i n(2s-1)}\\
&=
\omega_n(2s-1),
\end{aligned}
$$
since $e^{2\pi im}=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Define
$$
b(s)=(m+n)s.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
e^{2\pi ib(s)}
=
\omega_{m+n}(s)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the definition of $\omega_{m+n}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

The two lifted paths have the same endpoints:
$$
\boxed{
a(0)=b(0)=0,
\qquad
a(1)=b(1)=m+n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The formulas in steps [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} give these values directly.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Define
$$
A(s,t)
=(1-t)a(s)+tb(s)
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
K(s,t)
=
\bigl(
\cos(2\pi A(s,t)),
\sin(2\pi A(s,t))
\bigr)}.
$$
Then $K$ is a path homotopy
$$
\omega_m*\omega_n
\simeq
\omega_{m+n}
$$
for every pair of integers $m,n$.


::: pf-proof

The function $A$ is continuous because $a$ and $b$ are continuous,
and hence $K$ is continuous. By steps [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-15){.pf-ref},
$$
K(s,0)
=(\omega_m*\omega_n)(s)
$$
and
$$
K(s,1)
=
\omega_{m+n}(s).
$$
By step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref},
$$
A(0,t)=0
$$
and
$$
A(1,t)=m+n
$$
for every $t$. Since $m+n\in\ZZ$,
$$
K(0,t)=K(1,t)=(1,0).
$$
Thus $K$ is a path homotopy relative to endpoints.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

In particular, for every exceptional pair
$$
\boxed{mn\le0},
$$
the homotopy $K$ in step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref} supplies the required replacement for the
invalid formula in part (a).


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} identifies exactly the exceptional pairs, while step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref} works
for all integers $m,n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves part (a) in precisely the range where the displayed formula
is valid. Steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-18){.pf-ref} answer part (b).

:::
