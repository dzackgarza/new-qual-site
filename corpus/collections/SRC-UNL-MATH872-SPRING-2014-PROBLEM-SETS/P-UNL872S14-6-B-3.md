::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 38 #8.

Does the Borsuk--Ulam theorem hold for the torus? In other words, for every
continuous map
$$
f:S^1\times S^1\to\RR^2,
$$
must there exist
$$
(x,y)\in S^1\times S^1
$$
such that
$$
f(x,y)=f(-x,-y)?
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Define
$$
f:S^1\times S^1\to\RR^2
$$
by
$$
\boxed{f(x,y)=x},
$$
where $S^1\subset\RR^2$ is the unit circle.


::: pf-proof

This is the composition of the projection
$$
S^1\times S^1\to S^1,
\qquad
(x,y)\mapsto x,
$$
with the inclusion
$$
S^1\hookrightarrow\RR^2.
$$
Hence $f$ is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $((x,y)\in S^1\times S^1)$,
$$
\boxed{f(-x,-y)=-x=-f(x,y)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the definition in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
f(-x,-y)=-x,
$$
while
$$
f(x,y)=x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every $x\in S^1$,
$$
\boxed{x\ne -x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $x=-x$, then
$$
2x=0,
$$
so $x=0$, contradicting
$$
\|x\|=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore for every $((x,y)\in S^1\times S^1)$,
$$
\boxed{f(x,y)\ne f(-x,-y)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence the Borsuk--Ulam conclusion does not hold for the torus:
$$
\boxed{\text{the answer is no}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives an explicit continuous map for which step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that
no antipodal pair has the same image.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} answers the exercise.

:::
