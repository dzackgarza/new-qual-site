::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 82 #31.

Show that the normal connected covering spaces of
$$
S^1\vee S^1
$$
are precisely the Cayley graphs of groups with two generators.

More generally, show that the normal connected covering spaces of a wedge of $n$ circles are precisely the Cayley graphs of groups with $n$ generators.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
X_n=\bigvee_{i=1}^n S^1
$$
with oriented circle generators
$$
a_1,\dots,a_n.
$$
Then
$$
\pi_1(X_n,x_0)\cong F_n=\langle a_1,\dots,a_n
\rangle.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
$$
p:(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\to(X_n,x_0)
$$
be a connected normal covering, and let
$$
N=p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)
\trianglelefteq F_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Connected covering spaces correspond to subgroups of $F_n$. Normality of the cover is equivalent to normality of the corresponding subgroup.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Set
$$
\boxed{G=F_n/N}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $N\trianglelefteq F_n$, the quotient group is defined.
It is generated by the images
$$
\overline a_1,\dots,\overline a_n
$$
of the free generators.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The vertices of $\widetilde X$ can be identified with the elements of $G$.


::: pf-proof

The fiber over the wedge point is naturally identified with the right coset space
$$
N\backslash F_n.
$$
Since $N$ is normal,
$$
N\backslash F_n
=
F_n/N
=
G.
$$
The lifts of the unique vertex of $X_n$ are exactly the vertices of the covering graph.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Under the identification in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the lifted $a_i$-edge beginning at a vertex $g\in G$ ends at
$$
\boxed{g\overline a_i}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Lift the loop $a_i$ from the vertex corresponding to the coset $Ng$.
The endpoint corresponds to the coset
$$
Ng a_i,
$$
which becomes
$$
g\overline a_i
$$
in the quotient group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore $\widetilde X$ is the Cayley graph
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cay}(G;\overline a_1,\dots,\overline a_n)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

A Cayley graph has one vertex for each group element and, for every generator $\overline a_i$, an oriented edge
$$
g\longrightarrow g\overline a_i
$$
from every vertex $g$. Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} give exactly this graph, with labels and orientations inherited from the base wedge.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Conversely, let $G$ be any group generated by
$$
g_1,\dots,g_n.
$$
Let
$$
\Gamma=\operatorname{Cay}(G;g_1,\dots,g_n).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the usual labeled oriented Cayley graph: vertices are the elements of $G$, and from each $h\in G$ there is an oriented $i$-edge
$$
h\to hg_i.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

There is a covering map
$$
\boxed{p:\Gamma\to X_n}
$$
sending every vertex to the wedge point and every $i$-edge orientation-preservingly onto the $i$th circle.


::: pf-proof

At each vertex of $\Gamma$, for each $i$ there is exactly one outgoing and one incoming $i$-edge.
Thus the labeled oriented star of every vertex is identical to the star of the wedge point in $X_n$. Hence a sufficiently small star neighborhood of each vertex maps homeomorphically onto a small star neighborhood of $x_0$, while interior points of edges have obvious evenly covered neighborhoods.
Therefore $p$ is a covering map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The Cayley graph $\Gamma$ is connected.


::: pf-proof

The generators $g_1,\dots,g_n$ generate $G$. Thus for every $g\in G$, a word in the $g_i^{\pm1}$ gives an edge path from the identity vertex to $g$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The group $G$ acts on $\Gamma$ by left multiplication:
$$
k\cdot h=kh
$$
on vertices, and by sending the edge
$$
h\to hg_i
$$
to
$$
kh\to khg_i.
$$


::: pf-proof

Left multiplication preserves incidence, generator labels, and orientations, so it defines graph automorphisms.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every left multiplication map from step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is a deck transformation of
$$
p:\Gamma\to X_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The covering map depends only on the edge label, and left multiplication preserves labels.
Hence
$$
p(k\cdot z)=p(z)
$$
for every point $z\in\Gamma$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The deck group acts transitively on the fiber over the wedge point.


::: pf-proof

The fiber is the vertex set $G$. For any vertices $h_1,h_2$, left multiplication by
$$
h_2h_1^{-1}
$$
sends $h_1$ to $h_2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore the covering
$$
p:\Gamma\to X_n
$$
is normal.


::: pf-proof

A connected covering is normal exactly when its deck transformation group acts transitively on one fiber.
Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives this property.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Thus every Cayley graph of an $n$-generated group is a connected normal covering of the wedge of $n$ circles.


::: pf-proof

Connectedness is step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, the covering property is step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, and normality is step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Conversely, every connected normal covering of the wedge of $n$ circles is a Cayley graph of an $n$-generated group.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, with
$$
G=F_n/N.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\text{connected normal covers of }\bigvee_{i=1}^nS^1
\iff
\text{Cayley graphs of groups generated by }n\text{ elements}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

For $n=2$, this gives the stated classification for
$$
S^1\vee S^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Specialize step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} to two generators.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} prove the general and two-circle statements.

:::
