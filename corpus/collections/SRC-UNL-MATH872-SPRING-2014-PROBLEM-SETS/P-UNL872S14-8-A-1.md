::: {.problem}
Use the methods of section 8.A to compute the fundamental group of the Mobius band.
:::

::: {.solution}

Use the standard quotient model
$$
M
=
[0,1]\times[-1,1]/(0,t)\sim(1,-t)
$$
for the Möbius band.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
$$
C
=
\{[s,0]:0\le s\le1\}
\subset M.
$$
Then $C$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$.


::: pf-proof

Along the line $t=0$, the endpoint identification becomes
$$
(0,0)\sim(1,0).
$$
Thus
$$
C
\cong
[0,1]/(0\sim1)
\cong
S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
H:M\times I\to M
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
H([s,t],u)
=
[s,(1-u)t]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This formula is the natural linear contraction in the fiber direction. Its
well-definedness under the quotient relation is checked in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The homotopy $H$ is well defined.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
(0,t)\sim(1,-t).
$$
Then
$$
H([0,t],u)
=
[0,(1-u)t],
$$
while
$$
H([1,-t],u)
=
[1,-(1-u)t].
$$
By the defining Möbius identification,
$$
(0,(1-u)t)
\sim
(1,-(1-u)t).
$$
Hence the two representatives determine the same point of $M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
\boxed{H(-,0)=\operatorname{id}_M}.
$$


::: pf-proof

At $u=0$,
$$
H([s,t],0)
=
[s,t].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

One has
$$
\boxed{H(M,1)\subseteq C}.
$$


::: pf-proof

At $u=1$,
$$
H([s,t],1)
=
[s,0]
\in C.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The homotopy fixes $C$ pointwise.


::: pf-proof

If $[s,0]\in C$, then for every $u$,
$$
H([s,0],u)
=
[s,(1-u)0]
=
[s,0].
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $C$ is a deformation retract of $M$.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} are precisely the three defining properties of a
deformation retraction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence the inclusion
$$
i:C\hookrightarrow M
$$
is a homotopy equivalence.


::: pf-proof

A deformation retract inclusion is a homotopy equivalence, with homotopy
inverse given by the retraction
$$
r([s,t])=[s,0].
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(M)
\cong
\pi_1(C)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Homotopy equivalent spaces have isomorphic fundamental groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Since
$$
C\cong S^1,
$$
one obtains
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(M)
\cong
\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The fundamental group of the circle is infinite cyclic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

A generator is represented by the core loop
$$
\boxed{
\gamma(s)=[s,0],
\qquad
0\le s\le1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Under the homeomorphism
$$
C\cong S^1,
$$
this loop traverses the core circle exactly once.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives the fundamental group, and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} identifies a concrete
generator.

:::
