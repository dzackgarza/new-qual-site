::: {.problem}
Compute the simplicial homology groups of the Klein bottle, torus, projective plane, and Mobius band.
:::

All homology groups use integer coefficients.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Give the torus the standard \(\Delta\)-complex structure obtained by
triangulating a square by a diagonal and identifying opposite sides
orientation-preservingly. It has one vertex \(v\), three \(1\)-simplices
\(a,b,c\), and two \(2\)-simplices \(\sigma,\tau\).


::: pf-proof

After opposite sides are identified, all four square vertices become one
vertex. The two pairs of boundary edges give \(a,b\), and the diagonal gives
\(c\). The diagonal cuts the square into two triangles.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For this torus structure,
\[
\partial_1=0,
\qquad
\partial_2\sigma=a+b-c,
\qquad
\partial_2\tau=a+b-c.
\]


::: pf-proof

Every \(1\)-simplex has both endpoints equal to \(v\), so
\(\partial_1=0\). Choosing compatible orientations of the two triangles
gives the displayed boundary formulas.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus
\[
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\langle(1,1,-1)\rangle
\subset\mathbb Z^3
\]
and
\[
\ker\partial_2
=
\langle\sigma-\tau\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]


::: pf-proof

In the ordered basis \((a,b,c)\), both columns of the matrix of
\(\partial_2\) are \((1,1,-1)^T\). Hence
\[
x\sigma+y\tau\in\ker\partial_2
\iff x+y=0.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_k(T^2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,2,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&k=1,\\
0,&k>2.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Since \(\partial_1=0\), \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\). Step <1>3 gives
\[
H_2=\ker\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Also
\[
H_1
=
\mathbb Z^3/\langle(1,1,-1)\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z^2,
\]
because the relation vector is primitive.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Give the Klein bottle the analogous square \(\Delta\)-complex,
identifying one pair of opposite sides preserving orientation and the other
pair reversing orientation. Again there is one vertex, three \(1\)-simplices
\(a,b,c\), and two \(2\)-simplices \(\sigma,\tau\).


::: pf-proof

This is the standard square model of the Klein bottle with a diagonal added.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

With suitable orientations,
\[
\partial_1=0,
\qquad
\partial_2\sigma=a+b-c,
\qquad
\partial_2\tau=a-b-c.
\]


::: pf-proof

All \(1\)-simplices are loops at the unique vertex. The reversing side
identification changes the sign of the \(b\)-edge in the second triangle.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The two boundary vectors
\[
(1,1,-1),
\qquad
(1,-1,-1)
\]
are linearly independent, so
\[
\boxed{H_2(K)=0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

If
\[
x(1,1,-1)+y(1,-1,-1)=0,
\]
the second coordinate gives \(x=y\), while the first gives \(x+y=0\).
Thus \(x=y=0\), so \(\partial_2\) is injective.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The quotient
\[
\mathbb Z^3/
\langle
(1,1,-1),(1,-1,-1)
\rangle
\]
is
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Set
\[
u=a-c,\qquad v=b,\qquad w=c.
\]
The two relations become
\[
u+v=0,
\qquad
u-v=0.
\]
Hence \(u=-v\) and \(2v=0\), while \(w\) remains free.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_k(K)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

The Klein bottle is connected, \(\partial_1=0\), step <1>8 computes
\(H_1\), and step <1>7 computes \(H_2\).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The standard \(\Delta\)-complex for \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) has one
vertex \(v\), one \(1\)-simplex \(e\), and one \(2\)-simplex \(\sigma\), with
\[
\boxed{\partial_1e=0,\qquad \partial_2\sigma=2e}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The standard disk model identifies antipodal boundary points. The boundary
of the \(2\)-simplex wraps twice around the unique \(1\)-simplex, so the
attaching map has degree \(2\).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z/2,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

The chain complex is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\times2}
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{0}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its homology gives the displayed groups.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Möbius band \(M\) deformation retracts onto its central circle
\(S^1\).


::: pf-proof

Use
\[
M=[0,1]\times[-1,1]/(0,t)\sim(1,-t).
\]
The homotopy
\[
H([s,t],u)=[s,(1-u)t]
\]
is well defined and at \(u=1\) lands on the core
\[
\{[s,0]\}.
\]
It fixes that core throughout.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_k(M)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Simplicial homology is homotopy invariant. By step <1>12,
\[
M\simeq S^1.
\]
Use the homology of the circle.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Collecting the computations,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&H_0&H_1&H_2&H_k,\ k>2\\
\hline
T^2&\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z^2&\mathbb Z&0\\
K&\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2&0&0\\
\mathbb{RP}^2&\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z/2&0&0\\
M&\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z&0&0.
\end{array}}
\]


::: pf-proof

This combines steps <1>4, <1>9, <1>11, and <1>13.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>14 gives all requested homology groups.

:::
