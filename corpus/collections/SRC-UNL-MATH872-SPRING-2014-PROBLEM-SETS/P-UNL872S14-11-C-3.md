::: {.problem}
Let X be the standard 3-simplex $\Delta ^ { 3 }$ with the "standard" Δ-complex structure (that is, with four 0-simplices, six 1-simplices, four 2-simplices, and one 3-simplex).
Compute the simplicial homology groups of X, and compare them to the simplicial homology groups computed in class for the 2-sphere S 2 .
:::

::: {.solution}

Orient the unique $3$-simplex as
$$
\sigma=[v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3].
$$
For $i<j$, write
$$
e_{ij}=[v_i,v_j],
$$
and for $i<j<k$, write
$$
f_{ijk}=[v_i,v_j,v_k].
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The simplicial chain groups are
$$
\boxed{
C_k(X)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=3,\\
\ZZ^4,&k=2,\\
\ZZ^6,&k=1,\\
\ZZ^4,&k=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

These ranks are exactly the numbers of $k$-dimensional faces of a
tetrahedron: one tetrahedron, four triangular faces, six edges, and four
vertices.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The top boundary is
$$
\boxed{
\partial_3\sigma
=
f_{123}-f_{023}+f_{013}-f_{012}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the simplicial boundary formula
$$
\partial[v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3]
=
\sum_{i=0}^{3}
(-1)^i
[v_0,\dots,\widehat v_i,\dots,v_3].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The map
$$
\partial_3:C_3(X)\to C_2(X)
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

The image of the generator $\sigma$ in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} is nonzero in the free
abelian group $C_2(X)$. Hence multiplication by any nonzero integer cannot
send it to zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{H_3(X)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $4$-simplices, so
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_4=0.
$$
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\ker\partial_3=0.
$$
Thus
$$
H_3(X)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The four $2$-simplex boundaries are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_2f_{012}&=e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01},\\
\partial_2f_{013}&=e_{13}-e_{03}+e_{01},\\
\partial_2f_{023}&=e_{23}-e_{03}+e_{02},\\
\partial_2f_{123}&=e_{23}-e_{13}+e_{12}.
\end{aligned}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the simplicial boundary formula to each oriented triangle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The first three vectors in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} are linearly independent in
$C_1(X)$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
a\,\partial f_{012}
+
b\,\partial f_{013}
+
c\,\partial f_{023}
=0.
$$
The coefficient of $e_{12}$ is $a$, so $a=0$. The coefficient of
$e_{13}$ is then $b$, so $b=0$. The coefficient of $e_{23}$ is then
$c$, so $c=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\partial_2\ge3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} exhibits three linearly independent elements in the image.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Since
$$
\partial_2\partial_3=0,
$$
the nonzero vector $\partial_3\sigma$ lies in $\ker\partial_2$.


::: pf-proof

This is the chain-complex identity
$$
\partial^2=0
$$
applied to $\sigma$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\partial_2\le3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The domain $C_2(X)$ has rank $4$. Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives a nonzero element of
its kernel, so the kernel has rank at least $1$. Hence the image has rank
at most $4-1=3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Combining steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=3}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
\ker\partial_2
=
\operatorname{im}\partial_3
=
\ZZ\langle
f_{123}-f_{023}+f_{013}-f_{012}
\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both $\ker\partial_2$ and $\operatorname{im}\partial_3$ have rank $1$,
and the latter is contained in the former by $\partial^2=0$. The generator
of $\operatorname{im}\partial_3$ is primitive because its coordinates are
$\pm1$, so there is no proper finite-index inclusion between these rank-one
subgroups. Thus they are equal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Hence
$$
\boxed{H_2(X)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
H_2(X)
=
\ker\partial_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3.
$$
Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} identifies numerator and denominator.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For each edge,
$$
\boxed{
\partial_1e_{ij}=v_j-v_i}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the simplicial boundary formula in degree $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The three boundaries
$$
v_1-v_0,\qquad
v_2-v_0,\qquad
v_3-v_0
$$
are linearly independent.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
a(v_1-v_0)+b(v_2-v_0)+c(v_3-v_0)=0,
$$
comparison of the coefficients of $v_1,v_2,v_3$ gives
$$
a=b=c=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\partial_1=3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} gives rank at least $3$. Every edge boundary has total
coefficient sum $0$, so
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_1
\subseteq
\left\{
\sum_{i=0}^3 n_iv_i:\sum n_i=0
\right\},
$$
which is a rank-three subgroup of $C_0(X)$. Thus the rank is exactly $3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Since
$$
C_1(X)\cong\ZZ^6,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\ker\partial_1=3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The kernel of a homomorphism between free abelian groups is free, and
rank-nullity gives
$$
6-3=3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

One has
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\ker\partial_1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The chain identity gives
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\subseteq
\ker\partial_1.
$$
By steps [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}, both groups have rank $3$. To rule out a finite
index discrepancy, note that the three triangle boundaries
$$
\partial f_{012},\quad
\partial f_{013},\quad
\partial f_{023}
$$
generate every edge cycle: if
$$
z=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}e_{ij}
$$
has $\partial z=0$, subtract suitable multiples of these three triangle
boundaries to eliminate $e_{12},e_{13},e_{23}$. The remaining cycle is a
linear combination of $e_{01},e_{02},e_{03}$, and the condition
$\partial z=0$ then forces all remaining coefficients to vanish. Hence
every $1$-cycle is a $2$-boundary.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{H_1(X)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
H_1(X)
=
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

The image of $\partial_1$ is the subgroup
$$
\left\{
\sum_{i=0}^3n_iv_i:\sum n_i=0
\right\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every edge boundary has coefficient sum $0$, so the image is contained in
this subgroup. Conversely, the three generators
$$
v_1-v_0,\quad
v_2-v_0,\quad
v_3-v_0
$$
from step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} generate every vector whose coordinate sum is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{H_0(X)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $\partial_0=0$,
$$
H_0(X)
=
C_0(X)/\operatorname{im}\partial_1.
$$
The quotient by the subgroup of coefficient-sum-zero vectors is identified
with $\ZZ$ by the total coefficient-sum homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_k(\Delta^3)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
0,&k>0.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-17){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-19){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

The boundary
$$
\partial\Delta^3
$$
is a $\Delta$-complex homeomorphic to $S^2$ with the same four
$2$-simplices, six $1$-simplices, and four $0$-simplices, but with no
$3$-simplex.


::: pf-proof

The boundary of a tetrahedron is the union of its four triangular faces and
is homeomorphic to the $2$-sphere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

For $S^2=\partial\Delta^3$,
$$
\ker\partial_2
=
\ZZ\langle
f_{123}-f_{023}+f_{013}-f_{012}
\rangle
$$
but now
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_3=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The map $\partial_2$ is identical to the one computed for the tetrahedron,
so its kernel is the same rank-one group from step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}. But
$$
C_3(\partial\Delta^3)=0,
$$
so there are no $3$-boundaries.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-23}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_k(S^2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-22){.pf-ref} gives
$$
H_2(S^2)\cong\ZZ.
$$
The lower-dimensional boundary maps are unchanged, so steps [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-19){.pf-ref}
give
$$
H_1(S^2)=0,\qquad H_0(S^2)\cong\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-24}

The difference between the two computations is exactly that in
$\Delta^3$, the fundamental $2$-cycle of the boundary sphere is itself
the boundary of the $3$-simplex:
$$
\boxed{
\partial_3\sigma
=
f_{123}-f_{023}+f_{013}-f_{012}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. In the sphere alone there is no $3$-simplex to fill this
cycle, so it represents the generator of $H_2(S^2)$; in $\Delta^3$ it
is a boundary and hence represents zero.

:::
