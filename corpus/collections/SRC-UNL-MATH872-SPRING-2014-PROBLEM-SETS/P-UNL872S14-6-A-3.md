::: {.problem}
Let
\[
p:\mathbb R^2\to S^1\times S^1
\]
be defined by
\[
p(x,y)=\bigl((\cos 2\pi x,\sin 2\pi x),(\cos 2\pi y,\sin 2\pi y)\bigr).
\]

(a) Let \(f:I\to S^1\times S^1\) be the loop based at \(((1,0),(1,0))\) defined by
\[
f(s)=\bigl((\cos2\pi s,\sin2\pi s),(\cos4\pi s,\sin4\pi s)\bigr).
\]
Find a path
\[
g:(I,0)\to(\mathbb R^2,(0,0))
\]
satisfying \(p\circ g=f\). Sketch the image of \(g\) in \(\mathbb R^2\) and the image of \(f\) in \(S^1\times S^1\), viewed as a torus.

(b) Generalize the proof that \(\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z\), together with the path- and homotopy-lifting theorems, to prove
\[
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1)\cong\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
e(t)=(\cos2\pi t,\sin2\pi t),
\]
so
\[
p(x,y)=(e(x),e(y)).
\]

<1>1. For part (a), define
\[
\boxed{g(s)=(s,2s)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Clearly
\[
g(0)=(0,0).
\]
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(g(s))
&=
p(s,2s)\\
&=
\bigl(
(\cos2\pi s,\sin2\pi s),
(\cos4\pi s,\sin4\pi s)
\bigr)\\
&=
f(s).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(g\) is a lift of \(f\) starting at \((0,0)\).
:::

<1>2. The image of \(g\) is the line segment
\[
\boxed{
\{(s,2s):0\le s\le1\}}
\]
from \((0,0)\) to \((1,2)\).

::: {.proof}
This is immediate from the formula in step <1>1.
:::

<1>3. The image of \(f\) on the torus winds once around the first circle
factor and twice around the second.

::: {.proof}
The first coordinate of \(f\) is
\[
s\longmapsto e(s),
\]
which traverses \(S^1\) once counterclockwise. The second coordinate is
\[
s\longmapsto e(2s),
\]
which traverses \(S^1\) twice counterclockwise. Hence the loop has winding
pair \((1,2)\).
:::

<1>4. The map
\[
p:\mathbb R^2\to S^1\times S^1
\]
is a covering map.

::: {.proof}
The standard exponential map
\[
e:\mathbb R\to S^1
\]
is a covering map. The product of two covering maps is a covering map:
if \(U_1,U_2\subset S^1\) are evenly covered neighborhoods, then
\[
U_1\times U_2
\]
is evenly covered by
\[
e\times e=p.
\]
:::

<1>5. Let
\[
x_0=((1,0),(1,0)).
\]
For every based loop
\[
\gamma:(I,\partial I)\to(S^1\times S^1,x_0),
\]
there is a unique lift
\[
\widetilde\gamma:I\to\mathbb R^2
\]
with
\[
\widetilde\gamma(0)=(0,0).
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply the path-lifting theorem to the covering map in step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. The endpoint of such a lift lies in
\[
\mathbb Z^2.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since \(\gamma\) is a loop,
\[
p(\widetilde\gamma(1))
=
\gamma(1)
=
x_0
=
p(0,0).
\]
The fiber of \(p\) over \(x_0\) is exactly
\[
p^{-1}(x_0)
=
\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z.
\]
Hence
\[
\widetilde\gamma(1)\in\mathbb Z^2.
\]
:::

<1>7. Define
\[
\Phi:
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1,x_0)
\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z^2
\]
by
\[
\boxed{
\Phi([\gamma])
=
\widetilde\gamma(1)},
\]
where \(\widetilde\gamma\) is the lift from step <1>5.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 shows that the endpoint has values in \(\mathbb Z^2\). It remains
to verify that the endpoint depends only on the homotopy class, which is done
in step <1>8.
:::

<1>8. The map \(\Phi\) is well-defined.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
\gamma_0\simeq\gamma_1
\]
by a path homotopy relative to endpoints. Let
\[
H:I\times I\to S^1\times S^1
\]
be such a homotopy. By the homotopy-lifting theorem, there is a unique lift
\[
\widetilde H:I\times I\to\mathbb R^2
\]
whose restriction at \(t=0\) is the lift \(\widetilde\gamma_0\) starting at
\((0,0)\).

Because
\[
H(0,t)=x_0
\]
for all \(t\), the path
\[
t\longmapsto\widetilde H(0,t)
\]
lies in the discrete fiber \(\mathbb Z^2\). It starts at \((0,0)\), so it is
constant. Hence each slice
\[
s\longmapsto\widetilde H(s,t)
\]
is the lift of \(H(-,t)\) starting at \((0,0)\).

Similarly,
\[
H(1,t)=x_0,
\]
so
\[
t\longmapsto\widetilde H(1,t)
\]
is a continuous path in the discrete set \(\mathbb Z^2\), hence constant.
Thus
\[
\widetilde\gamma_0(1)
=
\widetilde H(1,0)
=
\widetilde H(1,1)
=
\widetilde\gamma_1(1).
\]
Therefore \(\Phi\) depends only on \([\gamma]\).
:::

<1>9. The map \(\Phi\) is a group homomorphism.

::: {.proof}
Let
\[
\Phi([\gamma])=(m,n),
\qquad
\Phi([\eta])=(r,s).
\]
Let \(\widetilde\gamma\) and \(\widetilde\eta\) be the lifts starting at
\((0,0)\).

The lift of the concatenation
\[
\gamma*\eta
\]
starting at \((0,0)\) first follows \(\widetilde\gamma\), ending at
\((m,n)\). The second half is the translate
\[
(m,n)+\widetilde\eta,
\]
because integer translation is a deck transformation of \(p\). Therefore
the final endpoint is
\[
(m,n)+(r,s).
\]
Hence
\[
\Phi([\gamma][\eta])
=
\Phi([\gamma])+\Phi([\eta]).
\]
:::

<1>10. The map \(\Phi\) is surjective.

::: {.proof}
Given any
\[
(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2,
\]
define
\[
\widetilde\omega_{m,n}(s)=(ms,ns).
\]
Its projection
\[
\omega_{m,n}=p\circ\widetilde\omega_{m,n}
\]
is a based loop, because
\[
\widetilde\omega_{m,n}(1)=(m,n)\in p^{-1}(x_0).
\]
By definition,
\[
\Phi([\omega_{m,n}])
=(m,n).
\]
:::

<1>11. If
\[
\Phi([\gamma])=(0,0),
\]
then the lift \(\widetilde\gamma\) is a loop in \(\mathbb R^2\).

::: {.proof}
The lift starts at \((0,0)\) by construction, and the hypothesis says its
endpoint is also \((0,0)\).
:::

<1>12. Every loop in \(\mathbb R^2\) is nullhomotopic.

::: {.proof}
The plane is convex. If
\[
\lambda:I\to\mathbb R^2
\]
is a loop based at \(0\), then
\[
K(s,t)=(1-t)\lambda(s)
\]
is a based contraction to the constant loop.
:::

<1>13. The map \(\Phi\) is injective.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
\Phi([\gamma])=(0,0).
\]
By step <1>11, the lift \(\widetilde\gamma\) is a loop. By step <1>12 it has
a based nullhomotopy
\[
\widetilde H:I\times I\to\mathbb R^2.
\]
Composing with the covering map gives a based nullhomotopy
\[
p\circ\widetilde H
\]
of \(\gamma\). Hence
\[
[\gamma]=1.
\]
Thus the kernel of \(\Phi\) is trivial.
:::

<1>14. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1,x_0)
\cong
\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>9, <1>10, and <1>13, \(\Phi\) is a bijective group
homomorphism.
:::

<1>15. Under this isomorphism, the loop in part (a) represents
\[
\boxed{(1,2)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Its lift from step <1>1 has endpoint
\[
g(1)=(1,2),
\]
so step <1>7 sends its homotopy class to \((1,2)\).
:::

<1>16. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 through <1>3 answer part (a), and step <1>14 proves part (b).
:::
