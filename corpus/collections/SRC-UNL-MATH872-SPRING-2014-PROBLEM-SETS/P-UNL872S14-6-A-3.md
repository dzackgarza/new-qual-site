::: {.problem}
Let
$$
p:\RR^2\to S^1\times S^1
$$
be defined by
$$
p(x,y)=\bigl((\cos 2\pi x,\sin 2\pi x),(\cos 2\pi y,\sin 2\pi y)\bigr).
$$

(a) Let $f:I\to S^1\times S^1$ be the loop based at $((1,0),(1,0))$ defined by
$$
f(s)=\bigl((\cos2\pi s,\sin2\pi s),(\cos4\pi s,\sin4\pi s)\bigr).
$$
Find a path
$$
g:(I,0)\to(\RR^2,(0,0))
$$
satisfying $p\circ g=f$. Sketch the image of $g$ in $\RR^2$ and the image of $f$ in $S^1\times S^1$, viewed as a torus.

(b) Generalize the proof that $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ$, together with the path- and homotopy-lifting theorems, to prove
$$
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1)\cong\ZZ\times\ZZ.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
e(t)=(\cos2\pi t,\sin2\pi t),
$$
so
$$
p(x,y)=(e(x),e(y)).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For part (a), define
$$
\boxed{g(s)=(s,2s)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Clearly
$$
g(0)=(0,0).
$$
Moreover,
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(g(s))
&=
p(s,2s)\\
&=
\bigl(
(\cos2\pi s,\sin2\pi s),
(\cos4\pi s,\sin4\pi s)
\bigr)\\
&=
f(s).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $g$ is a lift of $f$ starting at $(0,0)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The image of $g$ is the line segment
$$
\boxed{
\{(s,2s):0\le s\le1\}}
$$
from $(0,0)$ to $(1,2)$.


::: pf-proof

This is immediate from the formula in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The image of $f$ on the torus winds once around the first circle
factor and twice around the second.


::: pf-proof

The first coordinate of $f$ is
$$
s\longmapsto e(s),
$$
which traverses $S^1$ once counterclockwise. The second coordinate is
$$
s\longmapsto e(2s),
$$
which traverses $S^1$ twice counterclockwise. Hence the loop has winding
pair $(1,2)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The map
$$
p:\RR^2\to S^1\times S^1
$$
is a covering map.


::: pf-proof

The standard exponential map
$$
e:\RR\to S^1
$$
is a covering map. The product of two covering maps is a covering map:
if $U_1,U_2\subset S^1$ are evenly covered neighborhoods, then
$$
U_1\times U_2
$$
is evenly covered by
$$
e\times e=p.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Let
$$
x_0=((1,0),(1,0)).
$$
For every based loop
$$
\gamma:(I,\partial I)\to(S^1\times S^1,x_0),
$$
there is a unique lift
$$
\widetilde\gamma:I\to\RR^2
$$
with
$$
\widetilde\gamma(0)=(0,0).
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the path-lifting theorem to the covering map in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The endpoint of such a lift lies in
$$
\ZZ^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $\gamma$ is a loop,
$$
p(\widetilde\gamma(1))
=
\gamma(1)
=
x_0
=
p(0,0).
$$
The fiber of $p$ over $x_0$ is exactly
$$
p^{-1}(x_0)
=
\ZZ\times\ZZ.
$$
Hence
$$
\widetilde\gamma(1)\in\ZZ^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Define
$$
\Phi:
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1,x_0)
\longrightarrow
\ZZ^2
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi([\gamma])
=
\widetilde\gamma(1)},
$$
where $\widetilde\gamma$ is the lift from step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} shows that the endpoint has values in $\ZZ^2$. It remains
to verify that the endpoint depends only on the homotopy class, which is done
in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The map $\Phi$ is well-defined.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\gamma_0\simeq\gamma_1
$$
by a path homotopy relative to endpoints. Let
$$
H:I\times I\to S^1\times S^1
$$
be such a homotopy. By the homotopy-lifting theorem, there is a unique lift
$$
\widetilde H:I\times I\to\RR^2
$$
whose restriction at $t=0$ is the lift $\widetilde\gamma_0$ starting at
$(0,0)$.

Because
$$
H(0,t)=x_0
$$
for all $t$, the path
$$
t\longmapsto\widetilde H(0,t)
$$
lies in the discrete fiber $\ZZ^2$. It starts at $(0,0)$, so it is
constant. Hence each slice
$$
s\longmapsto\widetilde H(s,t)
$$
is the lift of $H(-,t)$ starting at $(0,0)$.

Similarly,
$$
H(1,t)=x_0,
$$
so
$$
t\longmapsto\widetilde H(1,t)
$$
is a continuous path in the discrete set $\ZZ^2$, hence constant.
Thus
$$
\widetilde\gamma_0(1)
=
\widetilde H(1,0)
=
\widetilde H(1,1)
=
\widetilde\gamma_1(1).
$$
Therefore $\Phi$ depends only on $[\gamma]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The map $\Phi$ is a group homomorphism.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\Phi([\gamma])=(m,n),
\qquad
\Phi([\eta])=(r,s).
$$
Let $\widetilde\gamma$ and $\widetilde\eta$ be the lifts starting at
$(0,0)$.

The lift of the concatenation
$$
\gamma*\eta
$$
starting at $(0,0)$ first follows $\widetilde\gamma$, ending at
$(m,n)$. The second half is the translate
$$
(m,n)+\widetilde\eta,
$$
because integer translation is a deck transformation of $p$. Therefore
the final endpoint is
$$
(m,n)+(r,s).
$$
Hence
$$
\Phi([\gamma][\eta])
=
\Phi([\gamma])+\Phi([\eta]).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The map $\Phi$ is surjective.


::: pf-proof

Given any
$$
(m,n)\in\ZZ^2,
$$
define
$$
\widetilde\omega_{m,n}(s)=(ms,ns).
$$
Its projection
$$
\omega_{m,n}=p\circ\widetilde\omega_{m,n}
$$
is a based loop, because
$$
\widetilde\omega_{m,n}(1)=(m,n)\in p^{-1}(x_0).
$$
By definition,
$$
\Phi([\omega_{m,n}])
=(m,n).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

If
$$
\Phi([\gamma])=(0,0),
$$
then the lift $\widetilde\gamma$ is a loop in $\RR^2$.


::: pf-proof

The lift starts at $(0,0)$ by construction, and the hypothesis says its
endpoint is also $(0,0)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Every loop in $\RR^2$ is nullhomotopic.


::: pf-proof

The plane is convex. If
$$
\lambda:I\to\RR^2
$$
is a loop based at $0$, then
$$
K(s,t)=(1-t)\lambda(s)
$$
is a based contraction to the constant loop.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The map $\Phi$ is injective.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\Phi([\gamma])=(0,0).
$$
By step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}, the lift $\widetilde\gamma$ is a loop. By step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} it has
a based nullhomotopy
$$
\widetilde H:I\times I\to\RR^2.
$$
Composing with the covering map gives a based nullhomotopy
$$
p\circ\widetilde H
$$
of $\gamma$. Hence
$$
[\gamma]=1.
$$
Thus the kernel of $\Phi$ is trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1,x_0)
\cong
\ZZ\times\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}, $\Phi$ is a bijective group
homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Under this isomorphism, the loop in part (a) represents
$$
\boxed{(1,2)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Its lift from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} has endpoint
$$
g(1)=(1,2),
$$
so step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} sends its homotopy class to $(1,2)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} answer part (a), and step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
