::: {.problem}
Let Y be a simply connected and LPC space.
Let G and H be subgroups of the group Homeo(Y) of all homeomorphisms of Y. Suppose that G and H are conjugate in Homeo(Y), and that both G and H give covering space actions on Y. Show that Y/G and Y/H are homeomorphic spaces.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Since $G$ and $H$ are conjugate in $\operatorname{Homeo}(Y)$, choose
$$
\varphi\in\operatorname{Homeo}(Y)
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{H=\varphi G\varphi^{-1}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the definition of conjugate subgroups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Define a set map
$$
F:Y/G\to Y/H
$$
by
$$
\boxed{F([y]_G)=[\varphi(y)]_H}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The expression makes sense once well-definedness is checked in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The map $F$ is well defined.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
[y']_G=[y]_G.
$$
Then
$$
y'=g(y)
$$
for some $g\in G$. Hence
$$
\varphi(y')
=
\varphi g(y)
=
(\varphi g\varphi^{-1})(\varphi(y)).
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\varphi g\varphi^{-1}\in H.
$$
Thus
$$
[\varphi(y')]_H
=
[\varphi(y)]_H.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Let
$$
q_G:Y\to Y/G,
\qquad
q_H:Y\to Y/H
$$
be the quotient maps.
Then
$$
\boxed{F\circ q_G=q_H\circ\varphi}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $y\in Y$,
$$
F(q_G(y))
=
F([y]_G)
=
[\varphi(y)]_H
=
q_H(\varphi(y)).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The map $F$ is continuous.


::: pf-proof

The composite
$$
q_H\circ\varphi:Y\to Y/H
$$
is continuous.
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} it equals
$$
F\circ q_G.
$$
Since $q_G$ is a quotient map, continuity of $F\circ q_G$ implies continuity of $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The inverse homeomorphism $\varphi^{-1}$ conjugates $H$ back to $G$:
$$
\boxed{G=\varphi^{-1}H\varphi}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Rearrange
$$
H=\varphi G\varphi^{-1}
$$
from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore $\varphi^{-1}$ induces a continuous map
$$
E:Y/H\to Y/G
$$
given by
$$
\boxed{E([y]_H)=[\varphi^{-1}(y)]_G}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Repeat steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} using step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} in place of step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

One has
$$
\boxed{E\circ F=\operatorname{id}_{Y/G}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every orbit class $[y]_G$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(E\circ F)([y]_G)
&=
E([\varphi(y)]_H)\\
&=
[\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(y))]_G\\
&=
[y]_G.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

One also has
$$
\boxed{F\circ E=\operatorname{id}_{Y/H}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $[y]_H$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(F\circ E)([y]_H)
&=
F([\varphi^{-1}(y)]_G)\\
&=
[\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(y))]_H\\
&=
[y]_H.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Hence
$$
\boxed{Y/G\cong Y/H}
$$
as topological spaces.


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, the maps $F$ and $E$ are continuous.
Steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} show that they are inverse bijections.
Thus $F$ is a homeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
