::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 53 #8.

Compute the fundamental group of the space obtained from two tori
$$
S^1\times S^1
$$
by identifying a circle
$$
S^1\times\{x_0\}
$$
in one torus with the corresponding circle
$$
S^1\times\{x_0\}
$$
in the other torus.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
T_1=S^1_a\times S^1_b,
\qquad
T_2=S^1_c\times S^1_d,
$$
and identify
$$
S^1_a\times\{x_0\}
$$
with
$$
S^1_c\times\{x_0\}.
$$
Call the quotient space $X$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The two torus groups are
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(T_1)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid[a,b]=1\rangle}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(T_2)
\cong
\langle c,d\mid[c,d]=1\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Each torus is a product of two circles, so its fundamental group is
$$
\ZZ^2.
$$
The standard presentation of $\ZZ^2$ has two commuting generators.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The intersection circle has
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(S^1)\cong\langle t\rangle\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the fundamental group of a circle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Under the two inclusion maps,
$$
\boxed{
t\longmapsto a}
$$
in $\pi_1(T_1)$ and
$$
\boxed{
t\longmapsto c}
$$
in $\pi_1(T_2)$.


::: pf-proof

The gluing circle is the first $S^1$-factor in each torus by construction.
Its fundamental-group generator is therefore the corresponding first-factor
generator.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Choose open neighborhoods $U,V\subset X$ of the two torus images so
that
$$
U\simeq T_1,
\qquad
V\simeq T_2,
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq S^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Thicken the common gluing circle slightly in each torus. The resulting open
sets deformation retract to the respective tori, while their overlap
deformation retracts to the identified circle.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

By Seifert--van Kampen,
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\pi_1(T_1)
*_{\pi_1(S^1)}
\pi_1(T_2)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply van Kampen to the open cover from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. The pushout of the two
fundamental groups over the intersection group is precisely the amalgamated
free product.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Using steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, this gives the presentation
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\left\langle
a,b,c,d
\ \presentationbar\
[a,b]=1,\ [c,d]=1,\ a=c
\right\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take the free product of the two torus presentations and impose the
additional relation identifying the two images of the intersection
generator:
$$
a=c.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Eliminating $c$, one obtains
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\left\langle
a,b,d
\ \presentationbar\
[a,b]=1,\ [a,d]=1
\right\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The relation $a=c$ allows every occurrence of $c$ to be replaced by
$a$. The second torus relation
$$
[c,d]=1
$$
then becomes
$$
[a,d]=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
F_2=\langle b,d\rangle
$$
be the free group on $b,d$. There is a homomorphism
$$
\Phi:\ZZ\times F_2\to\pi_1(X)
$$
given by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi(n,w)=a^n w(b,d)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The element $a$ commutes with both $b$ and $d$ by the relations in
step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, hence it commutes with every word in $F_2$. Therefore the
displayed formula respects multiplication in the direct product.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $\Phi$ is surjective.


::: pf-proof

The group in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is generated by
$$
a,b,d,
$$
all of which lie in the image:
$$
a=\Phi(1,1),
\qquad
b=\Phi(0,b),
\qquad
d=\Phi(0,d).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
\Psi:\pi_1(X)\to\ZZ\times F_2
$$
on generators by
$$
\boxed{
\Psi(a)=(1,1),
\qquad
\Psi(b)=(0,b),
\qquad
\Psi(d)=(0,d)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

In the direct product, $(1,1)$ commutes with every element of
$\{0\}\times F_2$. Hence
$$
[(1,1),(0,b)]=1
$$
and
$$
[(1,1),(0,d)]=1.
$$
Thus the defining relators of the presentation in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} are satisfied,
so the presentation universal property gives $\Psi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The maps $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are inverse isomorphisms.


::: pf-proof

The composite
$$
\Psi\circ\Phi
$$
fixes the generators of $\ZZ\times F_2$, while
$$
\Phi\circ\Psi
$$
fixes the generators $a,b,d$ of $\pi_1(X)$. Hence both composites are
identity maps.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\ZZ\times F_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} is the requested computation.

:::
