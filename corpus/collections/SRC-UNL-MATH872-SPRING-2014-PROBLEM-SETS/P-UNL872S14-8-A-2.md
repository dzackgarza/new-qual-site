::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 53 #4.

Let
$$
X\subset\RR^3
$$
be the union of $n$ distinct lines through the origin. Compute
$$
\pi_1(\RR^3-X).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume $n\ge1$, and let
$$
L_1,\dots,L_n
$$
be the distinct lines.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Each line $L_i$ meets the unit sphere $S^2$ in exactly two antipodal points.


::: pf-proof

A line through the origin has the form
$$
L_i=\{tv_i:t\in\RR\}
$$
for some unit vector $v_i$. Its intersection with $S^2$ is
$$
\{v_i,-v_i\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since the lines are distinct, the $2n$ points
$$
P
=
X\cap S^2
$$
are all distinct.


::: pf-proof

If two distinct lines through the origin shared a nonzero point, they would
coincide. Thus their pairs of unit direction vectors are disjoint.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The complement
$$
\RR^3-X
$$
deformation retracts onto
$$
S^2-P.
$$


::: pf-proof

For
$$
x\in\RR^3-X,
$$
one has $x\ne0$ because the origin lies in every $L_i\subset X$.
Define
$$
H(x,t)
=
\left(
(1-t)+\frac{t}{\|x\|}
\right)x.
$$
The scalar coefficient is positive for all $t\in[0,1]$, so $H(x,t)$ lies
on the same ray from the origin as $x$. If some $H(x,t)$ lay in one of
the lines $L_i$, then $x$ would lie in the same line, contrary to
$x\notin X$. Thus the homotopy remains in the complement.

At $t=0$,
$$
H(x,0)=x,
$$
and at $t=1$,
$$
H(x,1)=\frac{x}{\|x\|}\in S^2-P.
$$
If $x\in S^2-P$, then $\|x\|=1$, so $H(x,t)=x$ for every $t$.
Hence this is a deformation retraction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Choose one puncture
$$
p_\infty\in P.
$$
Stereographic projection from $p_\infty$ gives a homeomorphism
$$
\boxed{
S^2-\{p_\infty\}
\cong
\RR^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard stereographic projection homeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Under the homeomorphism in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
S^2-P
$$
corresponds to
$$
\boxed{
\RR^2-\{q_1,\dots,q_{2n-1}\}}
$$
for $2n-1$ distinct points $q_j$.


::: pf-proof

Removing $p_\infty$ turns the sphere into the plane. The remaining
$2n-1$ points of $P$ map to $2n-1$ distinct finite points.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The plane with $m$ points removed deformation retracts onto a wedge
of $m$ circles:
$$
\boxed{
\RR^2-\{q_1,\dots,q_m\}
\simeq
\bigvee_{j=1}^{m}S^1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose pairwise disjoint small closed disks $D_j$ centered at the removed
points and choose a basepoint $x_0$ outside them. Join $x_0$ to one point
of each boundary circle $\partial D_j$ by pairwise disjoint arcs whose
interiors avoid all the disks. The complement of the disk interiors
deformation retracts onto the union of these boundary circles and joining
arcs, which is a graph homotopy equivalent to a wedge of $m$ circles.
Equivalently, collapse a maximal tree in that graph.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
S^2-P
\simeq
\bigvee_{j=1}^{2n-1}S^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} to the planar model from step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} with
$$
m=2n-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(S^2-P)
\cong
F_{2n-1}},
$$
the free group on $2n-1$ generators.


::: pf-proof

The fundamental group of a wedge of $r$ circles is the free group $F_r$.
Apply step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(\RR^3-X)
\cong
F_{2n-1}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives a homotopy equivalence
$$
\RR^3-X
\simeq
S^2-P,
$$
and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} computes the latter fundamental group.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For $n=1$, this specializes to
$$
\pi_1(\RR^3-L_1)\cong\ZZ,
$$
as expected.


::: pf-proof

The formula gives
$$
F_{2\cdot1-1}
=
F_1
\cong
\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is the required computation.

:::
