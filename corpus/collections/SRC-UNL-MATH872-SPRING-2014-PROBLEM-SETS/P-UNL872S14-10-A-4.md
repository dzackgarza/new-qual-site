::: {.problem}
Lifting criterion and unique lifting property, proof deconstruction.
The following questions refer to the proofs of the lifting criterion (LC) and the unique lifting property (ULP) in Hatcher, pp. 62--63.
Paragraph 1 of LC begins "The 'only if' statement is", paragraph 2 line 3 of LC begins "path-connected open neighborhood", and line 4 of ULP begins "continuity of $\tilde f_1$".

(a) LC paragraph 1, line 3: what theorem is used in the definition of the function $\tilde f \colon Y \to \tilde X$?

(b) LC paragraph 1, lines 6--7: what theorem is Hatcher applying in order to know that a loop $h_1$ exists such that $h_1$ is path homotopic to $h_0$ and the lift $\tilde h_1$ at $\tilde x_0$ is a loop?

(c) LC paragraph 1: where is the hypothesis that $\operatorname{im} f_* \subseteq \operatorname{im} p_*$ used in the proof that $\tilde f$ is well-defined?

(d) LC paragraph 2: in order to prove that $\tilde f$ is continuous, Hatcher is proving another condition that is equivalent to the definition of continuity.
State this condition, and give a reference to the theorem in Munkres that shows the equivalence.
Also, Hatcher does not show the condition for all open neighborhoods of $f(y)$, only for evenly covered ones; write the missing sentences that show the condition holds for all open neighborhoods of $f(y)$.

(e) LC paragraphs 1--2: exactly where are the hypotheses that $Y$ is path-connected and locally path-connected, respectively, used in this proof?

(f) ULP lines 4--5: Hatcher is again applying the theorem from Munkres you found in part (d).
Write a couple of extra lines explaining more clearly why an open set $N$ exists with the properties listed in these lines.

(g) ULP: exactly where is the hypothesis that $Y$ is connected used in this proof?
:::

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

In part (a), the theorem used to define
$$
\widetilde f(y)
$$
is the path lifting theorem.


::: pf-proof

For a chosen path
$$
\gamma:I\to Y
$$
from $y_0$ to $y$, Hatcher considers the path
$$
f\circ\gamma:I\to X
$$
starting at $x_0$. The path lifting theorem for the covering
$$
p:(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\to(X,x_0)
$$
gives a unique lift
$$
\widetilde{f\gamma}:I\to\widetilde X
$$
starting at $\widetilde x_0$. He then defines
$$
\widetilde f(y)=\widetilde{f\gamma}(1).
$$
Thus existence and uniqueness of this lifted path are exactly the path
lifting theorem.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

In part (b), the theorem used to obtain the loop $h_1$ is
Proposition 1.31, specifically its characterization of
$$
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0).
$$


::: pf-proof

Hatcher forms the loop
$$
h_0=(f\gamma_0)\cdot\overline{(f\gamma)}
$$
at $x_0$. The hypothesis of the lifting criterion gives
$$
[h_0]
\in
f_*\pi_1(Y,y_0)
\subseteq
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0).
$$
Proposition 1.31 says that an element of
$$
\pi_1(X,x_0)
$$
lies in the image of $p_*$ exactly when it can be represented by a loop
whose lift starting at $\widetilde x_0$ is itself a loop. Hence there is
a loop $h_1$ representing the same homotopy class as $h_0$, with a
lift $\widetilde h_1$ based at $\widetilde x_0$ that closes up.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

In part (c), the inclusion
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{im}f_*
\subseteq
\operatorname{im}p_*}
$$
is used exactly to deduce
$$
[h_0]\in\operatorname{im}p_*.
$$


::: pf-proof

The loop
$$
\gamma_0\cdot\overline\gamma
$$
is based at $y_0$, so
$$
[h_0]
=
f_*[\gamma_0\cdot\overline\gamma]
\in
\operatorname{im}f_*.
$$
The assumed subgroup inclusion then puts $[h_0]$ in
$$
\operatorname{im}p_*.
$$
Only after this step can Proposition 1.31 be applied as in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} to
replace $h_0$ by a homotopic loop whose lift is a loop. This is the
precise use of the subgroup hypothesis in the well-definedness argument.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The continuity condition used in part (d) is:
for every $y\in Y$ and every neighborhood $N$ of
$\widetilde f(y)$, there exists a neighborhood $V$ of $y$
such that
$$
\boxed{\widetilde f(V)\subseteq N}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the neighborhood formulation of continuity, equivalent to the
inverse-image definition. It is Munkres, *Topology*, Theorem 18.1, condition
(4).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hatcher proves the condition in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} for neighborhoods of
$\widetilde f(y)$ that are sheets over evenly covered neighborhoods of
$f(y)$.


::: pf-proof

He chooses an evenly covered open neighborhood $U\subset X$ of
$$
f(y)
$$
and the sheet $\widetilde U$ over $U$ containing
$$
\widetilde f(y).
$$
Then local path-connectedness supplies a path-connected open neighborhood
$V$ of $y$ with
$$
f(V)\subseteq U.
$$
His path-lifting computation shows
$$
\widetilde f(V)\subseteq\widetilde U
$$
and in fact
$$
\widetilde f|_V
=
(p|_{\widetilde U})^{-1}\circ f|_V.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The argument in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} implies the neighborhood criterion for an
arbitrary open neighborhood $N$ of $\widetilde f(y)$.


::: pf-proof

Let $N\subseteq\widetilde X$ be any open neighborhood of
$\widetilde f(y)$. Choose an evenly covered neighborhood $U$ of
$$
f(y)
$$
and let $\widetilde U$ be the sheet over $U$ containing
$\widetilde f(y)$.

Then
$$
N\cap\widetilde U
$$
is an open neighborhood of $\widetilde f(y)$ inside the sheet. Since
$$
p|_{\widetilde U}:\widetilde U\to U
$$
is a homeomorphism,
$$
W
=
p(N\cap\widetilde U)
$$
is an open neighborhood of $f(y)$ contained in $U$. Because an open
subset of an evenly covered neighborhood is again evenly covered, $W$ is
evenly covered, with the sheet
$$
(p|_{\widetilde U})^{-1}(W)
=
N\cap\widetilde U.
$$

Applying Hatcher's argument from step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} to $W$, there is a neighborhood
$V$ of $y$ such that
$$
\widetilde f(V)
\subseteq
N\cap\widetilde U
\subseteq N.
$$
Thus the full neighborhood criterion from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

In part (e), path-connectedness of $Y$ is used when Hatcher chooses,
for each $y\in Y$, a path
$$
\gamma
$$
from $y_0$ to $y$.


::: pf-proof

The definition
$$
\widetilde f(y)
=
\widetilde{f\gamma}(1)
$$
requires such a path for every point $y$. Existence of these paths is
exactly path-connectedness of $Y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

In part (e), local path-connectedness of $Y$ is used in the
continuity proof when Hatcher chooses a path-connected open neighborhood
$V$ of $y$ satisfying
$$
f(V)\subseteq U.
$$


::: pf-proof

The set
$$
f^{-1}(U)
$$
is an open neighborhood of $y$. Local path-connectedness says that
inside any neighborhood of $y$ there is a smaller open path-connected
neighborhood. Hence one can choose
$$
y\in V\subseteq f^{-1}(U)
$$
with $V$ open and path-connected.

Path-connectedness of this particular $V$ is then used to join $y$
to every $y'\in V$ by a path $\eta$ staying inside $V$, so that
the lift over the chosen sheet can be described by
$$
(p|_{\widetilde U})^{-1}\circ f\circ\eta.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

In part (f), if $\widetilde U_1$ and $\widetilde U_2$ are the
sheets containing
$$
\widetilde f_1(y)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\widetilde f_2(y),
$$
then one may take
$$
\boxed{
N
=
\widetilde f_1^{-1}(\widetilde U_1)
\cap
\widetilde f_2^{-1}(\widetilde U_2)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By continuity of $\widetilde f_1$,
$$
\widetilde f_1^{-1}(\widetilde U_1)
$$
is an open neighborhood of $y$, since
$$
\widetilde f_1(y)\in\widetilde U_1.
$$
Likewise,
$$
\widetilde f_2^{-1}(\widetilde U_2)
$$
is an open neighborhood of $y$. Their intersection $N$ is therefore
an open neighborhood of $y$, and by construction
$$
\widetilde f_1(N)\subseteq\widetilde U_1,
\qquad
\widetilde f_2(N)\subseteq\widetilde U_2.
$$
This is exactly another application of the neighborhood criterion from
Munkres Theorem 18.1.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

In part (g), connectedness of $Y$ is used only at the final step
of the unique lifting proof.


::: pf-proof

Hatcher first proves that
$$
E
=
\{y\in Y:\widetilde f_1(y)=\widetilde f_2(y)\}
$$
is open, and that its complement
$$
Y\setminus E
=
\{y:\widetilde f_1(y)\ne\widetilde f_2(y)\}
$$
is also open. Thus $E$ is both open and closed.

The hypothesis that the two lifts agree at one point says
$$
E\ne\varnothing.
$$
Connectedness of $Y$ is then used to conclude that the only nonempty
clopen subset is all of $Y$:
$$
E=Y.
$$
Therefore
$$
\widetilde f_1=\widetilde f_2.
$$
No earlier step of this argument needs connectedness.

:::
