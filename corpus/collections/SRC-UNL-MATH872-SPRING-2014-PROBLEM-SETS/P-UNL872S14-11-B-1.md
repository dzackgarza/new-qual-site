::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 131 #2.

Show that the $\Delta$-complex obtained from $\Delta^3$ by the order-preserving edge identifications
\[
[v_0,v_1]\sim[v_1,v_3]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[v_0,v_2]\sim[v_2,v_3]
\]
deformation retracts onto a Klein bottle.

Also find other pairs of edge identifications that produce $\Delta$-complexes deformation retracting onto a torus, a $2$-sphere, and $\RP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
\[
K=[v_0,v_1,v_2]\cup[v_1,v_2,v_3]
\subset\Delta^3.
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The tetrahedron $\Delta^3$ deformation retracts onto $K$, fixing $K$ pointwise.


::: pf-proof

The set $K$ is the union of two adjacent boundary faces and is a closed $2$-disk.
The tetrahedron is a $3$-ball, and one may collapse it linearly away from the complementary edge $[v_0,v_3]$ onto these two faces.
Equivalently, after identifying $\Delta^3$ with a cone on a boundary face and subdividing along $[v_1,v_2]$, the standard elementary collapse removes the two faces containing $[v_0,v_3]$ together with the $3$-simplex, leaving exactly $K$. This gives a deformation retraction relative to $K$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If only boundary edges lying in $K$ are identified, the deformation retraction in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} descends to the quotient.


::: pf-proof

Let
\[
q:\Delta^3\to Q
\]
be such a quotient.
Every nontrivial equivalence class is contained in $K$, and the deformation retraction fixes $K$ pointwise.
Hence equivalent points remain equivalent throughout the homotopy.
By the universal property of quotient maps, the homotopy descends to a deformation retraction of $Q$ onto the quotient $q(K)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The disk $K$ has boundary cycle
\[
\boxed{
[v_0,v_1],
[v_1,v_3],
[v_3,v_2],
[v_2,v_0]}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The two triangles in $K$ share the diagonal $[v_1,v_2]$, which is interior to their union.
The four remaining edges occur in the displayed cyclic order.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Under the identifications
\[
[v_0,v_1]\sim[v_1,v_3],
\qquad
[v_0,v_2]\sim[v_2,v_3],
\]
the quotient polygon $K/\!\sim$ has boundary word
\[
\boxed{a,a,b,b}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The first identification is order preserving, so the first two boundary edges in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} receive the same orientation and label $a$.

For the second pair, the ordered edges
\[
[v_0,v_2]\sim[v_2,v_3]
\]
are both the reverses of the boundary-oriented edges
\[
[v_2,v_0]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[v_3,v_2].
\]
Hence these two boundary edges also receive the same orientation and a common label $b$. Thus the cyclic boundary word is $aabb$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The polygon with boundary word
\[
aabb
\]
is a Klein bottle.


::: pf-proof

The word
\[
a^2b^2
\]
is the standard fundamental polygon for the connected sum
\[
\RP^2#\RP^2,
\]
which is homeomorphic to the Klein bottle.
Equivalently, the usual cut-and-reglue move converts $aabb$ to the standard Klein-bottle word
\[
aba^{-1}b.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore the original quotient of $\Delta^3$ deformation retracts onto a Klein bottle.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the quotient retracts onto $K/\!\sim$.
Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} identify this quotient with the Klein bottle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For a torus, use the order-preserving identifications
\[
\boxed{
[v_0,v_1]\sim[v_2,v_3],
\qquad
[v_0,v_2]\sim[v_1,v_3]}.
\]


::: pf-proof

With the boundary orientation from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the edge
\[
[v_3,v_2]
\]
is the reverse of $[v_2,v_3]$, so the first pair contributes
\[
a, a^{-1}
\]
to opposite positions of the boundary word.
Likewise $[v_2,v_0]$ is the reverse of $[v_0,v_2]$, while $[v_1,v_3]$ occurs with the boundary orientation, so the second pair contributes
\[
b, b^{-1}.
\]
Thus the boundary word is
\[
\boxed{aba^{-1}b^{-1}},
\]
the standard torus polygon.
Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} then gives a deformation retraction of the corresponding tetrahedron quotient onto $T^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

For a $2$-sphere, use
\[
\boxed{
[v_2,v_0]\sim[v_2,v_3],
\qquad
[v_1,v_0]\sim[v_1,v_3]}.
\]


::: pf-proof

In the boundary cycle from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, these identifications pair adjacent edges with opposite orientations.
The quotient $1$-skeleton is a tree with three vertices and two edges.
The quotient of $K$ is obtained by attaching its single $2$-cell along a loop that traverses each tree edge once in each direction.

Collapse the tree to a point.
Since collapsing a contractible subcomplex is a homotopy equivalence, the quotient becomes one $2$-cell with its entire boundary collapsed to a point, namely
\[
D^2/\partial D^2\cong S^2.
\]
Thus the corresponding tetrahedron quotient deformation retracts onto $S^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For $\RP^2$, use
\[
\boxed{
[v_0,v_2]\sim[v_3,v_1],
\qquad
[v_3,v_2]\sim[v_0,v_1]}.
\]


::: pf-proof

These identifications produce two vertex classes
\[
\{v_0,v_3\},
\qquad
\{v_1,v_2\},
\]
and two edge classes joining them.
Thus the quotient $1$-skeleton is a circle subdivided into two edges.

Writing the two oriented edge classes as $a$ and $b$, the boundary cycle of the single $2$-cell becomes
\[
abab=(ab)^2.
\]
Hence the $2$-cell is attached to the circle by a map of degree $2$. The resulting CW complex is the standard cell structure
\[
S^1\cup_{\deg 2}D^2
\cong
\RP^2.
\]
Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} again lifts this identification to a deformation retraction of the tetrahedron quotient.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Thus explicit edge-pair choices are:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{target} & \text{edge identifications}\\
\hline
K &
[v_0,v_1]\sim[v_1,v_3],[v_0,v_2]\sim[v_2,v_3]\\
T^2 &
[v_0,v_1]\sim[v_2,v_3],[v_0,v_2]\sim[v_1,v_3]\\
S^2 &
[v_2,v_0]\sim[v_2,v_3],[v_1,v_0]\sim[v_1,v_3]\\
\RP^2 &
[v_0,v_2]\sim[v_3,v_1],[v_3,v_2]\sim[v_0,v_1].
\end{array}}
\]


::: pf-proof

The Klein-bottle case is steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, and the other three cases are steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::
