::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 39 #10.

From the isomorphism
$$
\pi_1(X\times Y,(x_0,y_0))
\cong
\pi_1(X,x_0)\times\pi_1(Y,y_0),
$$
it follows that loops in
$$
X\times\{y_0\}
$$
and
$$
\{x_0\}\times Y
$$
represent commuting elements of
$$
\pi_1(X\times Y,(x_0,y_0)).
$$
Construct an explicit path homotopy demonstrating this.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
f:(I,\partial I)\to(X,x_0)
$$
and
$$
g:(I,\partial I)\to(Y,y_0)
$$
be arbitrary based loops. Define loops in $(X\times Y)$ by
$$
\alpha(s)=(f(s),y_0),
\qquad
\beta(s)=(x_0,g(s)).
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Define two paths $(p,q:I\to I^2)$ by
$$
p(s)
=
\begin{cases}
(2s,0),&0\le s\le\frac12,\\
(1,2s-1),&\frac12\le s\le1,
\end{cases}
$$
and
$$
q(s)
=
\begin{cases}
(0,2s),&0\le s\le\frac12,\\
(2s-1,1),&\frac12\le s\le1.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

The path $p$ traverses the bottom edge of the square from $((0,0))$ to
$((1,0))$, then the right edge to $((1,1))$. The path $q$ traverses the
left edge first, then the top edge. Both begin at $((0,0))$ and end at
$((1,1))$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Define
$$
L:I\times I\to I^2,
\qquad
L(s,t)=(1-t)p(s)+tq(s).
$$
Then $L$ is a path homotopy from $p$ to $q$ relative to endpoints.


::: pf-proof

The square $I^2$ is convex, so the convex combination lies in $I^2$.
Continuity is immediate. Moreover,
$$
L(s,0)=p(s),
\qquad
L(s,1)=q(s).
$$
Since
$$
p(0)=q(0)=(0,0),
\qquad
p(1)=q(1)=(1,1),
$$
one has
$$
L(0,t)=(0,0),
\qquad
L(1,t)=(1,1)
$$
for every $t$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Define
$$
F:I^2\to X\times Y,
\qquad
F(u,v)=(f(u),g(v)).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is continuous because both coordinate functions are continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
\boxed{F\circ p=\alpha*\beta}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $0\le s\le1/2$, then
$$
(F\circ p)(s)
=
F(2s,0)
=
(f(2s),g(0))
=
(f(2s),y_0)
=
\alpha(2s).
$$
If $1/2\le s\le1$, then
$$
(F\circ p)(s)
=
F(1,2s-1)
=
(f(1),g(2s-1))
=
(x_0,g(2s-1))
=
\beta(2s-1).
$$
This is exactly the standard concatenation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

One has
$$
\boxed{F\circ q=\beta*\alpha}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $0\le s\le1/2$,
$$
(F\circ q)(s)
=
F(0,2s)
=
(x_0,g(2s))
=
\beta(2s).
$$
For $1/2\le s\le1$,
$$
(F\circ q)(s)
=
F(2s-1,1)
=
(f(2s-1),y_0)
=
\alpha(2s-1).
$$
Thus $F\circ q=\beta*\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Define
$$
H=F\circ L:I\times I\to X\times Y.
$$
Then $H$ is a path homotopy
$$
\boxed{\alpha*\beta\simeq\beta*\alpha}
$$
relative to the basepoint.


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $H$ is continuous. By steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
H(s,0)=\alpha*\beta(s),
\qquad
H(s,1)=\beta*\alpha(s).
$$
At either endpoint of the path parameter,
$$
H(0,t)
=
F(0,0)
=
(x_0,y_0),
$$
and
$$
H(1,t)
=
F(1,1)
=
(x_0,y_0).
$$
Thus the homotopy fixes endpoints.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the corresponding fundamental-group elements commute:
$$
\boxed{[\alpha][\beta]=[\beta][\alpha]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Path-homotopic based loops represent the same element of the fundamental
group. Apply step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives the requested explicit path homotopy.

:::
