::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 79 #3.

Let
$$
p:\widetilde X\to X
$$
be a covering space such that every fiber
$$
p^{-1}(x)
$$
is finite and nonempty. Show that
$$
\widetilde X\text{ is compact Hausdorff}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
X\text{ is compact Hausdorff}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Assume first that $\widetilde X$ is compact Hausdorff. Then $X$ is compact.


::: pf-proof

The fibers are nonempty, so $p$ is surjective. Hence
$$
X=p(\widetilde X)
$$
is the continuous image of a compact space.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Fix distinct $x,y\in X$. There are disjoint open sets $O_x,O_y\subseteq\widetilde X$ containing the finite fibers $p^{-1}(x)$ and $p^{-1}(y)$, respectively.


::: pf-proof

The two fibers are finite, disjoint, and therefore closed. A compact Hausdorff space is normal, so the two closed sets have disjoint open neighborhoods.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

There is an open neighborhood $U_x$ of $x$ such that
$$
\boxed{p^{-1}(U_x)\subseteq O_x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose an evenly covered neighborhood $U$ of $x$:
$$
p^{-1}(U)=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^m W_j,
$$
where each $p\restrict_{W_j}:W_j\to U$ is a homeomorphism. There are only finitely many sheets because the fiber over $x$ is finite.

For each $j$, the set
$$
p(O_x\cap W_j)
$$
is an open neighborhood of $x$ in $U$. Let $U_x$ be the intersection of these finitely many neighborhoods. Then the inverse image of $U_x$ in every sheet lies in $O_x$, hence $p^{-1}(U_x)\subseteq O_x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Similarly, there is an open neighborhood $U_y$ of $y$ with
$$
\boxed{p^{-1}(U_y)\subseteq O_y}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Repeat step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} with $y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The neighborhoods $U_x$ and $U_y$ are disjoint.


::: pf-proof

If $z\in U_x\cap U_y$, choose $\widetilde z\in p^{-1}(z)$, which is possible because every fiber is nonempty. Then
$$
\widetilde z\in O_x\cap O_y,
$$
contradicting $O_x\cap O_y=\varnothing$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Thus $X$ is Hausdorff.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} separate every pair of distinct points by disjoint open neighborhoods.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde X\text{ compact Hausdorff}
\Longrightarrow
X\text{ compact Hausdorff}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compactness is step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and Hausdorffness is step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Assume conversely that $X$ is compact Hausdorff. Then $\widetilde X$ is Hausdorff.


::: pf-proof

Let $\widetilde x\ne\widetilde y$. If
$$
p(\widetilde x)\ne p(\widetilde y),
$$
separate the two images by disjoint open sets in $X$ and pull them back.

If
$$
p(\widetilde x)=p(\widetilde y),
$$
choose an evenly covered neighborhood of their common image. The two points lie in distinct sheets, which are disjoint open neighborhoods in $\widetilde X$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every $x\in X$, choose an evenly covered open neighborhood $U_x$. There is an open neighborhood $V_x$ of $x$ such that
$$
\boxed{
x\in V_x\subseteq\overline{V_x}\subseteq U_x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compact Hausdorff spaces are regular, so one may shrink the open neighborhood $U_x$ to an open $V_x$ whose closure lies inside $U_x$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Choose finitely many points $x_1,\dots,x_N$ such that
$$
\boxed{X=\bigcup_{j=1}^N V_{x_j}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The sets $V_x$ cover the compact space $X$, so there is a finite subcover.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

For each $j$,
$$
\boxed{
p^{-1}(\overline{V_{x_j}})
\text{ is compact}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $U_{x_j}$ is evenly covered,
$$
p^{-1}(U_{x_j})
=
\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_j}W_{j,\lambda},
$$
with each sheet homeomorphic to $U_{x_j}$. The index set $\Lambda_j$ is finite because the fiber over $x_j$ is finite.

For each sheet,
$$
W_{j,\lambda}\cap p^{-1}(\overline{V_{x_j}})
$$
is homeomorphic to the compact set $\overline{V_{x_j}}$. Hence $p^{-1}(\overline{V_{x_j}})$ is a finite union of compact sets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

One has
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde X
=
\bigcup_{j=1}^N p^{-1}(\overline{V_{x_j}})}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The sets $V_{x_j}$ cover $X$, so their closures also cover $X$. Taking inverse images gives the displayed finite cover of $\widetilde X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Hence $\widetilde X$ is compact.


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}, $\widetilde X$ is a finite union of compact sets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
X\text{ compact Hausdorff}
\Longrightarrow
\widetilde X\text{ compact Hausdorff}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Hausdorffness is step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and compactness is step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} prove the two implications.

:::
