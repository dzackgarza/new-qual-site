::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 80 #12.

Let (a,b) be the generators of [
pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)
] corresponding to the two circle summands.
Draw the covering space of [
S^1\vee S^1
] corresponding to the normal subgroup generated by [
a^2,qquad b^2,qquad (ab)^4,
] and prove that the covering space is the correct one.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let [
F=pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)conglangle a,b
angle
] and let [
N=langle!langle a^2,b^2,(ab)^4
angle!
angle
] be the indicated normal closure.

<1>1. The quotient group is [
F/N
cong
\boxed{
langle a,bmid a^2=1, b^2=1, (ab)^4=1
angle}.
]

::: {.proof}
Quotienting a free group by the normal closure of a set of words gives the presentation obtained by imposing those words as relators.
:::

<1>2. Set [
r=ab.
] Then in the quotient, [
\boxed{r^4=1}
qquad	ext{and}qquad
\boxed{ara=r^{-1}}.
]

::: {.proof}
The first relation is ((ab)^4=1). For the second, [
ara
=
a(ab)a
=
ba
=
(ab)^{-1}
=
r^{-1},
] using (a^2=b^2=1).
:::

<1>3. Every element of (F/N) can be written in one of the forms [
\boxed{r^k}
qquad	ext{or}qquad
\boxed{ar^k},
qquad
0le k<4.
]

::: {.proof}
Since [
b=ar,
] every word in (a,b) can be written using (a,r). The relation [
ara=r^{-1}
] is equivalent to [
ra=ar^{-1},
] so every (a) can be moved to the left.
Then (a^2=1) reduces the exponent of (a) to (0) or (1), and (r^4=1) reduces the exponent of (r) modulo (4). Thus there are at most eight elements.
:::

<1>4. The quotient (F/N) has exactly eight elements and is isomorphic to the dihedral group [
\boxed{D_8}.
]

::: {.proof}
Let (D_8) be the symmetry group of a square.
Choose two reflections (A,B) whose axes meet at angle (pi/4). Then [
A^2=B^2=1
] and [
AB
] is a rotation through (pi/2), hence has order (4). Therefore the presentation in step <1>1 admits a surjective homomorphism onto (D_8).

Step <1>3 shows that the presented group has at most eight elements, while (D_8) has eight.
The surjection is therefore an isomorphism.
:::

<1>5. Define a labeled graph (widetilde X) with vertex set (D_8). For every (gin D_8), add an (a)-edge from (g) to (ga) and a (b)-edge from (g) to (gb).

::: {.proof}
This is the Cayley graph of (D_8) with respect to the generators (a,b).
:::

<1>6. As an unlabeled graph, (widetilde X) is an (8)-cycle.

::: {.proof}
Because [
a^2=b^2=1,
] each vertex is incident to exactly one geometric (a)-edge and one geometric (b)-edge.
Thus every vertex has degree (2).

The graph is connected because (a,b) generate (D_8). A finite connected (2)-regular graph is a cycle.
Since there are eight vertices, it is an (8)-cycle.
:::

<1>7. Around the cycle, the edge labels alternate [
\boxed{a,b,a,b,a,b,a,b}.
]

::: {.proof}
Start at the identity (e) and successively follow the edges [
a, b, a, b, a, b, a, b.
] After each pair (ab), one has multiplied by (r=ab).
Thus the successive even vertices are [
e, r, r^2, r^3, r^4=e.
] Since (r) has order (4), no earlier return occurs.
The intervening edges are the (a)-edges.
Hence the full (8)-cycle alternates the two labels.
:::

<1>8. Map every vertex of (widetilde X) to the wedge point of (S^1\vee S^1), every (a)-edge homeomorphically and orientation-preservingly onto the (a)-circle, and every (b)-edge similarly onto the (b)-circle.

::: {.proof}
At each vertex there is exactly one incoming and one outgoing oriented lift of each generator edge.
Therefore a small star neighborhood of every vertex maps homeomorphically onto a small star neighborhood of the wedge point.
Interior points of edges have the obvious evenly covered neighborhoods.
Hence this labeling defines a covering map [
p:widetilde X	o S^1\vee S^1.
]
:::

<1>9. The covering (p) has eight sheets and is connected.

::: {.proof}
There are eight vertices over the unique vertex of the base graph, so the cover has eight sheets.
Step <1>6 shows that (widetilde X) is connected.
:::

<1>10. Let [
\varphi:F	o D_8
] be the quotient homomorphism sending the free generators (a,b) to the identically named generators of (D_8). A based loop in (S^1\vee S^1) labeled by a word (win F) lifts from the identity vertex (e) to the vertex [
\boxed{\varphi(w)}.
]

::: {.proof}
Reading the loop one generator at a time, each (a^{pm1}) or (b^{pm1}) moves the lifted endpoint by right multiplication by the corresponding generator.
Since (a^{-1}=a) and (b^{-1}=b) in (D_8), after reading the entire word (w), the endpoint is precisely its value (\varphi(w)).
:::

<1>11. Such a lifted loop closes at (e) if and only if [
\boxed{winker\varphi}.
]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>10, the endpoint is (e) exactly when [
\varphi(w)=e,
] which is the definition of membership in the kernel.
:::

<1>12. One has [
\boxed{ker\varphi=N}.
]

::: {.proof}
The homomorphism (\varphi) is exactly the quotient map [
F	o F/Ncong D_8
] from steps <1>1--<1>4. Its kernel is therefore (N).
:::

<1>13. Consequently, [
\boxed{
p_*pi_1(widetilde X,e)=N}.
]

::: {.proof}
For a covering of a graph, the image subgroup consists exactly of the homotopy classes of based loops whose lifts from the chosen point are loops.
By steps <1>11 and <1>12, these are exactly the words in (N).
:::

<1>14. The covering is normal.

::: {.proof}
The corresponding subgroup (N) is normal by construction as a normal closure.
Equivalently, the Cayley graph has the deck action of (D_8) by left multiplication, transitive on the fiber.
:::

<1>15. Thus the required picture is an alternating (a,b)-labeled octagon: [
\boxed{
	ext{eight vertices in a cycle, with edge labels }a,b,a,b,a,b,a,b}.
]

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>5--<1>8 construct the octagonal covering, and step <1>13 proves that its corresponding subgroup is exactly the prescribed normal subgroup.
:::

<1>16. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>15 gives the covering and the preceding steps prove its correctness.
