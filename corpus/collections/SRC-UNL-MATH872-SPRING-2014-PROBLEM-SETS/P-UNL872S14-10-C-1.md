::: {.problem}
Hatcher p. 80 #12.

Let $(a,b)$ be the generators of
$$
\pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)
$$
corresponding to the two circle summands.
Draw the covering space of
$$
S^1\vee S^1
$$
corresponding to the normal subgroup generated by
$$
a^2,\qquad b^2,\qquad (ab)^4,
$$
and prove that the covering space is the correct one.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
F=\pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)\cong\langle a,b
\rangle
$$
and let
$$
N=\langle\!\langle a^2,b^2,(ab)^4
\rangle\!\rangle
$$
be the indicated normal closure.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The quotient group is
$$
F/N
\cong
\boxed{
\langle a,b\mid a^2=1, b^2=1, (ab)^4=1
\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Quotienting a free group by the normal closure of a set of words gives the presentation obtained by imposing those words as relators.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Set
$$
r=ab.
$$
Then in the quotient,
$$
\boxed{r^4=1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{ara=r^{-1}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The first relation is $((ab)^4=1)$. For the second,
$$
ara
=
a(ab)a
=
ba
=
(ab)^{-1}
=
r^{-1},
$$
using $(a^2=b^2=1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Every element of $F/N$ can be written in one of the forms
$$
\boxed{r^k}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\boxed{ar^k},
\qquad
0\le k<4.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
b=ar,
$$
every word in $(a,b)$ can be written using $(a,r)$. The relation
$$
ara=r^{-1}
$$
is equivalent to
$$
ra=ar^{-1},
$$
so every $a$ can be moved to the left.
Then $a^2=1$ reduces the exponent of $a$ to $0$ or $1$, and $r^4=1$ reduces the exponent of $r$ modulo $4$. Thus there are at most eight elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The quotient $F/N$ has exactly eight elements and is isomorphic to the dihedral group
$$
\boxed{D_8}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $D_8$ be the symmetry group of a square.
Choose two reflections $A,B$ whose axes meet at angle $\pi/4$. Then
$$
A^2=B^2=1
$$
and
$$
AB
$$
is a rotation through $\pi/2$, hence has order $4$. Therefore the presentation in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} admits a surjective homomorphism onto $D_8$.

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that the presented group has at most eight elements, while $D_8$ has eight.
The surjection is therefore an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Define a labeled graph $\widetilde X$ with vertex set $D_8$. For every $g\in D_8$, add an $a$-edge from $g$ to $ga$ and a $b$-edge from $g$ to $gb$.


::: pf-proof

This is the Cayley graph of $D_8$ with respect to the generators $(a,b)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

As an unlabeled graph, $\widetilde X$ is an $8$-cycle.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
a^2=b^2=1,
$$
each vertex is incident to exactly one geometric $a$-edge and one geometric $b$-edge.
Thus every vertex has degree $2$.

The graph is connected because $a,b$ generate $D_8$. A finite connected $2$-regular graph is a cycle.
Since there are eight vertices, it is an $8$-cycle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Around the cycle, the edge labels alternate
$$
\boxed{a,b,a,b,a,b,a,b}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Start at the identity $e$ and successively follow the edges
$$
a, b, a, b, a, b, a, b.
$$
After each pair $(ab)$, one has multiplied by $(r=ab)$.
Thus the successive even vertices are
$$
e, r, r^2, r^3, r^4=e.
$$
Since $r$ has order $4$, no earlier return occurs.
The intervening edges are the $a$-edges.
Hence the full $8$-cycle alternates the two labels.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Map every vertex of $\widetilde X$ to the wedge point of $(S^1\vee S^1)$, every $a$-edge homeomorphically and orientation-preservingly onto the $a$-circle, and every $b$-edge similarly onto the $b$-circle.


::: pf-proof

At each vertex there is exactly one incoming and one outgoing oriented lift of each generator edge.
Therefore a small star neighborhood of every vertex maps homeomorphically onto a small star neighborhood of the wedge point.
Interior points of edges have the obvious evenly covered neighborhoods.
Hence this labeling defines a covering map
$$
p:\widetilde X\to S^1\vee S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The covering $p$ has eight sheets and is connected.


::: pf-proof

There are eight vertices over the unique vertex of the base graph, so the cover has eight sheets.
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} shows that $\widetilde X$ is connected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Let
$$
\varphi:F\to D_8
$$
be the quotient homomorphism sending the free generators $(a,b)$ to the identically named generators of $D_8$. A based loop in $(S^1\vee S^1)$ labeled by a word $w\in F$ lifts from the identity vertex $e$ to the vertex
$$
\boxed{\varphi(w)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Reading the loop one generator at a time, each $a^{\pm1}$ or $b^{\pm1}$ moves the lifted endpoint by right multiplication by the corresponding generator.
Since $a^{-1}=a$ and $b^{-1}=b$ in $D_8$, after reading the entire word $w$, the endpoint is precisely its value $\varphi(w)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Such a lifted loop closes at $e$ if and only if
$$
\boxed{w\in\ker\varphi}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, the endpoint is $e$ exactly when
$$
\varphi(w)=e,
$$
which is the definition of membership in the kernel.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

One has
$$
\boxed{\ker\varphi=N}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The homomorphism $\varphi$ is exactly the quotient map
$$
F\to F/N\cong D_8
$$
from steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. Its kernel is therefore $N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,e)=N}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a covering of a graph, the image subgroup consists exactly of the homotopy classes of based loops whose lifts from the chosen point are loops.
By steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}, these are exactly the words in $N$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The covering is normal.


::: pf-proof

The corresponding subgroup $N$ is normal by construction as a normal closure.
Equivalently, the Cayley graph has the deck action of $D_8$ by left multiplication, transitive on the fiber.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Thus the required picture is an alternating $(a,b)$-labeled octagon:
$$
\boxed{
\text{eight vertices in a cycle, with edge labels }a,b,a,b,a,b,a,b}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} construct the octagonal covering, and step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} proves that its corresponding subgroup is exactly the prescribed normal subgroup.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} gives the covering and the preceding steps prove its correctness.
