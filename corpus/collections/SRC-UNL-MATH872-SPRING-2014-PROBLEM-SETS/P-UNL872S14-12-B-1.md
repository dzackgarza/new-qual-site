::: {.problem}
Use the Mayer-Vietoris Theorem to compute the homology groups of the following spaces:

(a) The space obtained from two tori glued along a circle from problem (9.C.2)/Hatcher p. 53 #8.

(b) The square $I\times I$ with all four boundary edges glued in a clockwise orientation.

(c) The mapping cylinder of the function $f:I\to I$ defined by
\[
f(t)=
\begin{cases}
3t, & t\in[0,1/3],\\
-3t+2, & t\in[1/3,2/3],\\
3t-2, & t\in[2/3,1].
\end{cases}
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
All homology groups use integer coefficients.

## Part (a)

Let
\[
X=T_1\cup_C T_2,
\]
where
\[
T_1\cong T_2\cong S^1\times S^1
\]
and the common circle is
\[
C=S^1\times\{x_0\}.
\]

<1>1. The relevant homology groups are
\[
H_2(T_i)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1(T_i)\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
H_1(C)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_0(C)\cong H_0(T_i)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

::: {.proof}
Use the standard homology groups of the torus and circle.
:::

<1>2. Choose bases
\[
H_1(T_i)
=
\mathbb Z\langle a_i,b_i\rangle
\]
so that \(a_i\) is represented by the gluing circle \(S^1\times\{x_0\}\).
Then the Mayer--Vietoris map
\[
H_1(C)\to H_1(T_1)\oplus H_1(T_2)
\]
is
\[
\boxed{
1\longmapsto(a_1,-a_2)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For a union \(A\cup B\), the Mayer--Vietoris map from the intersection is
the difference of the two inclusion-induced maps. Each inclusion of \(C\)
into a torus sends its generator to the primitive class \(a_i\).
:::

<1>3. The map in step <1>2 is injective.

::: {.proof}
The vector
\[
(a_1,-a_2)
\]
is nonzero and primitive in the free abelian group
\[
H_1(T_1)\oplus H_1(T_2)\cong\mathbb Z^4.
\]
Hence the map from \(\mathbb Z\) has trivial kernel.
:::

<1>4. The Mayer--Vietoris sequence in degree \(3\) gives
\[
\boxed{H_3(X)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The relevant segment is
\[
H_3(T_1)\oplus H_3(T_2)
\to
H_3(X)
\to
H_2(C).
\]
Both outer groups are zero.
:::

<1>5. The natural map
\[
H_2(T_1)\oplus H_2(T_2)\to H_2(X)
\]
is an isomorphism.

::: {.proof}
The relevant exact segment is
\[
0=H_2(C)
\to
H_2(T_1)\oplus H_2(T_2)
\to
H_2(X)
\to
H_1(C)
\to
H_1(T_1)\oplus H_1(T_2).
\]
The final map is injective by step <1>3, so the connecting map
\[
H_2(X)\to H_1(C)
\]
has zero image. Exactness then makes the middle map both injective and
surjective.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
\boxed{H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>5 and
\[
H_2(T_1)\oplus H_2(T_2)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
:::

<1>7. The cokernel of the map in step <1>2 is
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Z^3}.
\]

::: {.proof}
One is quotienting \(\mathbb Z^4\) by the primitive rank-one subgroup
generated by
\[
(a_1,-a_2).
\]
A primitive rank-one summand splits off, so the quotient is free of rank
\(4-1=3\).
:::

<1>8. The map
\[
H_0(C)\to H_0(T_1)\oplus H_0(T_2)
\]
is injective.

::: {.proof}
All three spaces are connected, so under the standard identifications it is
\[
1\longmapsto(1,-1).
\]
:::

<1>9. Hence
\[
\boxed{H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^3}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The low-degree exact sequence is
\[
H_1(C)
\to
H_1(T_1)\oplus H_1(T_2)
\to
H_1(X)
\to
H_0(C)
\to
H_0(T_1)\oplus H_0(T_2).
\]
By step <1>8 the final map is injective, so
\[
H_1(X)
\]
is the cokernel of the first map. Apply step <1>7.
:::

<1>10. One has
\[
\boxed{H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The space \(X\) is connected because two connected tori are glued along the
nonempty connected circle \(C\).
:::

<1>11. Thus in part (a),
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z^3,&k=1,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&k=2,\\
0,&k\ge3.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>4, <1>6, <1>9, and <1>10.
:::

## Part (b)

Let \(Y\) be the quotient of the square with all four boundary edges
identified in the clockwise direction.

<1>12. The space \(Y\) is the mapping cone of the degree-\(4\) map
\[
\boxed{
d_4:S^1\to S^1,
\qquad
z\longmapsto z^4}.
\]

::: {.proof}
After all four boundary edges are identified in the same clockwise
orientation, the boundary of the square becomes one circle, and traversing
the boundary of the square once traverses that quotient circle four times in
the positive direction. Thus the square is a \(2\)-cell attached to a circle
by a degree-\(4\) map, which is exactly the mapping cone of \(d_4\).
:::

<1>13. Choose an open cover
\[
Y=U\cup V
\]
of the mapping cone so that
\[
U\simeq S^1,
\qquad
V\simeq\{\ast\},
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq S^1.
\]

::: {.proof}
Use the standard mapping-cone model
\[
C_{d_4}
=
\left(S^1\sqcup(S^1\times I)\right)/
((z,0)\sim d_4(z),\ S^1\times\{1\}\text{ collapsed}).
\]
Let \(U\) contain the target circle and the lower two-thirds of the cylinder;
it deformation retracts to the target \(S^1\). Let \(V\) contain the cone tip
and the upper two-thirds of the cylinder; it is contractible. Their
intersection is a middle annulus and deformation retracts to a copy of the
domain \(S^1\).
:::

<1>14. Under the deformation retractions in step <1>13, the
Mayer--Vietoris map
\[
H_1(U\cap V)\to H_1(U)\oplus H_1(V)
\]
is
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{(\times4,\,0)}
\mathbb Z\oplus0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The inclusion of the middle cylinder into \(U\), followed by the deformation
retraction of \(U\) to the target circle, is homotopic to the attaching map
\(d_4\), which induces multiplication by \(4\) on \(H_1(S^1)\). The
inclusion into the contractible space \(V\) induces the zero map.
:::

<1>15. Therefore
\[
\boxed{H_2(Y)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The exact sequence contains
\[
0
=
H_2(U)\oplus H_2(V)
\to
H_2(Y)
\to
H_1(U\cap V)
\xrightarrow{\times4}
H_1(U).
\]
Multiplication by \(4\) on \(\mathbb Z\) is injective, so the middle group
has zero image and hence is zero.
:::

<1>16. One has
\[
\boxed{H_1(Y)\cong\mathbb Z/4}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The next part of the exact sequence is
\[
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\times4}
\mathbb Z
\to
H_1(Y)
\to
H_0(U\cap V)
\to
H_0(U)\oplus H_0(V).
\]
The final \(H_0\)-map is
\[
1\mapsto(1,-1)
\]
and is injective. Thus
\[
H_1(Y)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}(\times4)
\cong
\mathbb Z/4.
\]
:::

<1>17. The space \(Y\) is connected, so
\[
\boxed{H_0(Y)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The mapping cone is connected because the target circle and cone are joined
along a nonempty cylinder.
:::

<1>18. Hence in part (b),
\[
\boxed{
H_k(Y)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z/4,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>15--<1>17.
:::

## Part (c)

Let \(M_f\) be the mapping cylinder of the given map
\[
f:I\to I.
\]

<1>19. Use the standard mapping-cylinder model
\[
M_f
=
\left(
I_{\mathrm{target}}
\sqcup
(I_{\mathrm{domain}}\times I)
\right)/
((x,1)\sim f(x)).
\]

::: {.proof}
This is the definition of the mapping cylinder.
:::

<1>20. There is an open cover
\[
M_f=U\cup V
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
U\simeq I,
\qquad
V\simeq I,
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq I}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Let \(U\) contain the target copy \(I_{\mathrm{target}}\) and the upper
two-thirds of the cylinder. Sliding vertically toward the target gives a
deformation retraction
\[
U\simeq I_{\mathrm{target}}\cong I.
\]

Let \(V\) be the lower two-thirds of the cylinder, slightly thickened so that
the two sets are open. It deformation retracts onto a horizontal copy of the
domain interval. The overlap is a middle strip, which also deformation
retracts onto a horizontal interval.
:::

<1>21. Therefore
\[
H_k(U)
=
H_k(V)
=
H_k(U\cap V)
=
0
\qquad(k>0),
\]
and all three \(H_0\)-groups are \(\mathbb Z\).

::: {.proof}
Each of the three spaces is homotopy equivalent to the contractible interval.
:::

<1>22. For every \(k\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{H_k(M_f)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For \(k\ge2\), the Mayer--Vietoris sequence has
\[
0
\to
H_k(M_f)
\to
0
\]
by step <1>21.
:::

<1>23. The relevant low-degree sequence is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_1(M_f)
\longrightarrow
H_0(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
H_0(U)\oplus H_0(V)
\longrightarrow
H_0(M_f)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

::: {.proof}
Insert the vanishing \(H_1\)-groups from step <1>21 into Mayer--Vietoris.
:::

<1>24. The map
\[
H_0(U\cap V)\to H_0(U)\oplus H_0(V)
\]
is
\[
\boxed{
1\longmapsto(1,-1)}
\]
and is injective.

::: {.proof}
All three sets are connected, so the two inclusions send the unique component
of the intersection to the unique component of each larger set. The
Mayer--Vietoris sign convention gives the difference map.
:::

<1>25. Hence
\[
\boxed{H_1(M_f)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Exactness in step <1>23 and injectivity from step <1>24 give the result.
:::

<1>26. Also,
\[
\boxed{H_0(M_f)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The cokernel of
\[
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{1\mapsto(1,-1)}
\mathbb Z^2
\]
is \(\mathbb Z\). Equivalently, the mapping cylinder is connected.
:::

<1>27. Therefore in part (c),
\[
\boxed{
H_k(M_f)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
0,&k>0.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>22, <1>25, and <1>26.
:::

<1>28. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>11 answers part (a), step <1>18 answers part (b), and step <1>27
answers part (c), all via Mayer--Vietoris.
:::
