::: {.problem}
Use the proof of the Classification of Surfaces theorem to determine which of the spaces listed in that classification is homeomorphic to the Klein bottle.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Use the standard square model of the Klein bottle with boundary word
$$
\boxed{aba^{-1}b}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take a square. Identify one pair of opposite sides in the same direction and
the other pair in opposite directions. Reading the oriented boundary gives
the standard Klein-bottle word
$$
aba^{-1}b.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Klein bottle is a closed connected surface.


::: pf-proof

The quotient of the compact square by pairing all boundary edges has no
boundary and is connected because the square is connected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Klein bottle is nonorientable.


::: pf-proof

In the square model, one pair of opposite sides is glued with the same
boundary orientation. Such a gluing reverses a local orientation when one
crosses that identified edge. Equivalently, the image of a vertical strip
through the square is a Möbius band embedded in the quotient. A surface
containing a Möbius band is nonorientable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The standard square cell structure on the Klein bottle has
$$
\boxed{
c_0=1,\qquad c_1=2,\qquad c_2=1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

All four vertices are identified to one vertex. The two pairs of side edges
give two $1$-cells, and the square interior gives one $2$-cell.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\chi(K)=1-2+1=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the cellular formula for Euler characteristic to step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

By the classification of closed connected surfaces, every closed
connected nonorientable surface is homeomorphic to
$$
\boxed{
N_g
=
\#^g\RP^2}
$$
for a unique integer $g\ge1$.


::: pf-proof

This is the nonorientable case of the Classification of Surfaces theorem.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The Euler characteristic of $N_g$ is
$$
\boxed{\chi(N_g)=2-g}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The projective plane has
$$
\chi(\RP^2)=1.
$$
For connected closed surfaces,
$$
\chi(M\#N)
=
\chi(M)+\chi(N)-2.
$$
Inducting on $g$ gives
$$
\chi(\#^g\RP^2)
=
g\cdot1-2(g-1)
=
2-g.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since the Klein bottle is nonorientable, it is homeomorphic to some
$N_g$, and Euler characteristic forces
$$
0
=
\chi(K)
=
\chi(N_g)
=
2-g.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{g=2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Solve
$$
2-g=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Hence the Klein bottle is homeomorphic to
$$
\boxed{
\RP^2\#\RP^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
N_2
=
\#^2\RP^2
=
\RP^2\#\RP^2.
$$
Combine with step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In the polygon-normal-form proof of the classification theorem, the
same conclusion is expressed by converting
$$
aba^{-1}b
$$
to the nonorientable normal-form word
$$
\boxed{aabb}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The cut-and-paste moves in the classification proof collect
orientation-reversing edge pairs into square blocks. For the Klein-bottle
polygon there are exactly two such crosscap blocks, so its word reduces to
$$
a^2b^2.
$$
A polygon with boundary word
$$
a^2b^2
$$
is exactly the standard fundamental polygon for
$$
\RP^2\#\RP^2.
$$
This agrees with the invariant computation above.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives the member of the Classification of Surfaces list that is
homeomorphic to the Klein bottle.
