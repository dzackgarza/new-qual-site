::: {.problem}
Let
$$
B=\langle a,b\mid aba=bab\rangle
$$
be the braid group on three strands.
Let
$$
H=\langle x\mid\ \rangle,\qquad K=\langle y\mid\ \rangle,\qquad L=\langle z\mid\ \rangle,
$$
and define homomorphisms $c:L\to H$ and $d:L\to K$ by
$$
c(z)=x^2,\qquad d(z)=y^3.
$$
Let $G=H*_L K$ be the free product of $H$ and $K$ amalgamated along $L$ via $c$ and $d$.
Prove that $B\cong G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

The groups
$$
H=\langle x\mid\ \rangle,
\qquad
K=\langle y\mid\ \rangle,
\qquad
L=\langle z\mid\ \rangle
$$
are all infinite cyclic.


::: pf-proof

Each presentation has one generator and no relators, so each group is the
free group on one generator, namely $\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The amalgamated free product has presentation
$$
\boxed{
G
\cong
\langle x,y\mid x^2=y^3\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The free product
$$
H*K
$$
has presentation
$$
\langle x,y\mid\ \rangle.
$$
Amalgamating along $L$ imposes the relation that the two images of its
generator agree:
$$
c(z)=d(z).
$$
By definition,
$$
c(z)=x^2,
\qquad
d(z)=y^3.
$$
Hence the single added relation is
$$
x^2=y^3.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In the braid group
$$
B=\langle a,b\mid aba=bab\rangle,
$$
define
$$
\boxed{
X=aba,
\qquad
Y=ab}.
$$


::: pf-proof

These are elements of $B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The elements $X,Y$ satisfy
$$
\boxed{X^2=Y^3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using
$$
aba=bab,
$$
write the second copy of $X$ as $bab$:
$$
X^2
=
(aba)(aba)
=
(aba)(bab)
=
ababab
=
(ab)^3
=
Y^3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore there is a homomorphism
$$
\Phi:G\to B
$$
defined by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi(x)=aba,
\qquad
\Phi(y)=ab}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the images satisfy the defining relation
$$
x^2=y^3
$$
of the presentation in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. The universal property of presented groups
therefore gives $\Phi$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In $G$, define
$$
\boxed{
A=y^{-1}x,
\qquad
B'=x^{-1}y^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

These are elements of $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

One has
$$
\boxed{AB'=y}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compute directly:
$$
AB'
=
(y^{-1}x)(x^{-1}y^2)
=
y^{-1}y^2
=
y.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

One has
$$
\boxed{AB'A=x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
AB'A
=
y(y^{-1}x)
=
x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

One has
$$
\boxed{B'AB'=x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

First,
$$
B'A
=
(x^{-1}y^2)(y^{-1}x)
=
x^{-1}yx.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
B'AB'
&=
(x^{-1}yx)(x^{-1}y^2)\\
&=
x^{-1}y^3\\
&=
x^{-1}x^2\\
&=
x,
\end{aligned}
$$
using the defining relation
$$
y^3=x^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Thus
$$
\boxed{AB'A=B'AB'}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both sides equal $x$ by steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore there is a homomorphism
$$
\Psi:B\to G
$$
defined by
$$
\boxed{
\Psi(a)=y^{-1}x,
\qquad
\Psi(b)=x^{-1}y^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} says that the proposed images satisfy the braid relation
$$
aba=bab.
$$
Thus the presentation of $B$ gives the homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The composite
$$
\Psi\circ\Phi:G\to G
$$
fixes $x$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
\Phi(x)=aba.
$$
Applying $\Psi$,
$$
(\Psi\circ\Phi)(x)
=
AB'A
=
x
$$
by step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The same composite fixes $y$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\Phi(y)=ab,
$$
one has
$$
(\Psi\circ\Phi)(y)
=
AB'
=
y
$$
by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\Psi\circ\Phi=\operatorname{id}_G}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group $G$ is generated by $x,y$, and the composite fixes both
generators by steps [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

The composite
$$
\Phi\circ\Psi:B\to B
$$
fixes $a$.


::: pf-proof

Using the formulas from steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\Phi\circ\Psi)(a)
&=
\Phi(y^{-1}x)\\
&=
(ab)^{-1}(aba)\\
&=
b^{-1}a^{-1}aba\\
&=
b^{-1}ba\\
&=
a.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

The composite fixes $b$.


::: pf-proof

From
$$
X=aba=(ab)a=Ya
$$
inside $B$, one has
$$
X^{-1}Y^2
=
a^{-1}Y^{-1}Y^2
=
a^{-1}Y
=
a^{-1}ab
=
b.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\Phi\circ\Psi)(b)
&=
\Phi(x^{-1}y^2)\\
&=
X^{-1}Y^2\\
&=
b.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\Phi\circ\Psi=\operatorname{id}_B}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group $B$ is generated by $a,b$, and steps [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} show that
the composite fixes both.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
B\cong G}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The homomorphisms
$$
\Phi:G\to B
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Psi:B\to G
$$
are inverse by steps [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-17){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-18){.pf-ref} proves the required isomorphism.
