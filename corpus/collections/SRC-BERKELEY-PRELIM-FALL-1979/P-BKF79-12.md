::: {.problem}
For a function $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$, consider:

1. $f$ is continuous;
2. the graph of $f$ is connected in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R$.

Prove or disprove each implication
\[
(1)\Longrightarrow(2),
\qquad
(2)\Longrightarrow(1).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume $n\geq1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #implication-one-true}
The implication $(1)\Longrightarrow(2)$ is true.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $f:\RR^n\to\RR$ is continuous. The map
$$
\Phi:\RR^n\longrightarrow\RR^n\times\RR,
\qquad
\Phi(x)=(x,f(x)),
$$
is continuous, and its image is exactly the graph
$$
\Gamma_f
\coloneqq
\{(x,f(x)):x\in\RR^n\}.
$$
The space $\RR^n$ is path connected, hence connected. Therefore its
continuous image $\Gamma_f$ is connected.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #g-discontinuous}
Define $g:\RR\to\RR$ by
$$
g(t)
\coloneqq
\begin{cases}
\sin(1/t),&t\neq0,\\
0,&t=0.
\end{cases}
$$
Then $g$ is discontinuous at $0$.

::: pf-proof
For
$$
t_m
\coloneqq
\frac{1}{\pi/2+2\pi m},
$$
one has $t_m\to0$ but
$$
g(t_m)=1
$$
for every $m$, whereas $g(0)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #graph-g-connected}
The graph $\Gamma_g$ is connected.

::: pf-proof
Set
$$
G_+
\coloneqq
\{(t,\sin(1/t)):t>0\},
\qquad
G_-
\coloneqq
\{(t,\sin(1/t)):t<0\}.
$$
Each of $G_+$ and $G_-$ is connected because it is the continuous
image of a connected interval. Moreover,
$$
\left(\frac{1}{m\pi},0\right)\longrightarrow(0,0)
$$
through points of $G_+$, and
$$
\left(-\frac{1}{m\pi},0\right)\longrightarrow(0,0)
$$
through points of $G_-$. Hence $(0,0)$ lies in the closure of each
set.

Adjoining a closure point to a connected set preserves
connectedness. Therefore
$$
G_+\cup\{(0,0)\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
G_-\cup\{(0,0)\}
$$
are connected. They meet at $(0,0)$, so their union is connected. That
union is exactly $\Gamma_g$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #counterexample-exists}
For every $n\geq1$, there is a discontinuous
$f:\RR^n\to\RR$ whose graph is connected.

::: pf-proof
Define
$$
f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
\coloneqq
g(x_1),
$$
where $g$ is the function from step [](#g-discontinuous){.pf-ref}. The restriction of $f$ to
the first coordinate axis is $g$, so step [](#g-discontinuous){.pf-ref} shows that $f$ is
discontinuous at the origin.

The map
$$
\Gamma_g\times\RR^{n-1}
\longrightarrow
\Gamma_f,
\qquad
((t,g(t)),y_2,\ldots,y_n)
\longmapsto
(t,y_2,\ldots,y_n,g(t)),
$$
is a homeomorphism. By step [](#graph-g-connected){.pf-ref}, $\Gamma_g$ is connected, and
$\RR^{n-1}$ is connected. Their product is therefore connected, so
$\Gamma_f$ is connected.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #implication-two-false}
The implication $(2)\Longrightarrow(1)$ is false.

::: pf-proof
The function constructed in step [](#counterexample-exists){.pf-ref} has connected graph but is not
continuous.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#implication-one-true){.pf-ref} proves the first implication, and step [](#implication-two-false){.pf-ref} disproves the
second.
:::
