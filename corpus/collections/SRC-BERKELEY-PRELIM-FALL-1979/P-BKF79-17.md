::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ complex matrix. Prove that there exists a unitary matrix $U$ such that
\[
B=UAU^{-1}
\]
is upper triangular.
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove the assertion by induction on $n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #base-case-n1}
The assertion holds for $n=1$.

::: pf-proof
Every $1\times1$ matrix is already upper triangular, so one may take
$U=(1)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #unitary-eigenvector-basis}
Suppose $n>1$. There is a unitary matrix $Q$ whose first column
is a unit eigenvector of $A$.

::: pf-proof
By the fundamental theorem of algebra, the characteristic polynomial
of $A$ has a root $\lambda\in\CC$. Choose a nonzero vector $v_1$ with
$$
Av_1=\lambda v_1
$$
and normalize it so that $\norm{v_1}=1$. Extend $v_1$ to an
orthonormal basis
$$
v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n
$$
of $\CC^n$, and let $Q$ be the matrix with these vectors as its
columns. Then $Q$ is unitary.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #block-triangular-form}
Relative to this basis,
$$
Q^*AQ
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda&r\\
0&C
\end{pmatrix}
$$
for some row $r$ and some $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ complex matrix $C$.

::: pf-proof
The first column of $Q^*AQ$ is the coordinate vector of $Av_1$ in
the orthonormal basis from step [](#unitary-eigenvector-basis){.pf-ref}. Since
$Av_1=\lambda v_1$, that column is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The remaining entries form the indicated row block $r$ and square
block $C$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #induction-hypothesis-block}
There is a unitary $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ matrix $W$ such that
$$
WCW^{-1}
$$
is upper triangular.

::: pf-proof
This is the induction hypothesis applied to the complex matrix $C$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #combined-unitary}
Set
$$
V=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&W
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U=VQ^*.
$$
Then $U$ is unitary and $UAU^{-1}$ is upper triangular.

::: pf-proof
Both $V$ and $Q^*$ are unitary, hence so is their product $U$. Using
step [](#block-triangular-form){.pf-ref},
$$
UAU^{-1}
=
V(Q^*AQ)V^{-1}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda&rW^{-1}\\
0&WCW^{-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
By step [](#induction-hypothesis-block){.pf-ref}, the lower-right block is upper triangular. Therefore the
whole matrix is upper triangular.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#base-case-n1){.pf-ref} is the base case, and steps [](#unitary-eigenvector-basis){.pf-ref} through [](#combined-unitary){.pf-ref} prove the
induction step. Hence the assertion holds for every $n\geq1$.
:::
