::: {.problem}
An accurate map of California is spread out flat on a table in Evans Hall, in Berkeley. Prove that there is exactly one point on the map lying directly over the point it represents.
:::

::: {.solution}
Identify horizontal position with the Euclidean plane, and let
$C\subset\RR^2$ be the closed region occupied by California.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #similarity-contraction}
Let
$$
T:C\longrightarrow\RR^2
$$
send a point $x\in C$ to the physical horizontal position, on the
table, of the point of the map representing $x$. Then there is a
constant $\lambda$ with
$$
0<\lambda<1
$$
such that
$$
\norm{T(x)-T(y)}
=
\lambda\norm{x-y}
$$
for all $x,y\in C$.

::: pf-proof
An accurate map differs from the region it represents by a uniform
change of scale followed by a rigid motion in the horizontal plane.
Thus
$$
T(x)=b+\lambda Rx
$$
for some vector $b$ and some orthogonal linear map $R$. The map is
strictly smaller than the state, so its scale factor satisfies
$0<\lambda<1$. Orthogonality of $R$ gives the displayed distance
identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #image-in-c}
One has
$$
T(C)\subseteq C.
$$

::: pf-proof
The physical image $T(C)$ is precisely the placed map of California.
It lies on a table in Evans Hall, hence physically inside California.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fixed-point-exists}
The map $T:C\to C$ has a fixed point.

::: pf-proof
Choose any $x_0\in C$ and define
$$
x_{n+1}=T(x_n).
$$
Step [](#image-in-c){.pf-ref} shows inductively that every $x_n$ lies in $C$. By step
[](#similarity-contraction){.pf-ref},
$$
\norm{x_{n+1}-x_n}
=
\lambda^n\norm{x_1-x_0}.
$$
Hence for $m>n$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\norm{x_m-x_n}
&\leq
\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}\norm{x_{k+1}-x_k}\\
&\leq
\frac{\lambda^n}{1-\lambda}\norm{x_1-x_0}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $(x_n)$ is Cauchy. Since $C$ is closed in $\RR^2$, it is
complete, so
$$
x_n\longrightarrow p
$$
for some $p\in C$. The similarity $T$ is continuous, and therefore
$$
T(p)
=
T\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n\right)
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n+1}
=
p.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fixed-point-unique}
The fixed point of $T$ is unique.

::: pf-proof
If $p,q\in C$ are fixed points, then step [](#similarity-contraction){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\norm{p-q}
=
\norm{T(p)-T(q)}
=
\lambda\norm{p-q}.
$$
Since $0<\lambda<1$, this forces
$$
\norm{p-q}=0,
$$
so $p=q$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fixed-point-meaning}
A point $p\in C$ is fixed by $T$ if and only if the point of
the map representing $p$ lies directly over $p$ itself.

::: pf-proof
By definition, $T(p)$ is the physical horizontal location of the map
point representing $p$. Thus $T(p)=p$ says exactly that these two
horizontal positions coincide.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #exactly-one-point}
Exactly one point on the map lies directly over the point it
represents.

::: pf-proof
Existence follows from step [](#fixed-point-exists){.pf-ref}, uniqueness from step [](#fixed-point-unique){.pf-ref}, and step
[](#fixed-point-meaning){.pf-ref} identifies fixed points with the points described in the
problem.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#exactly-one-point){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
:::
