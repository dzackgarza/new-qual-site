::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the abelian group generated by $x,y,z$ subject to
\[
15x+3y=0,
\]
\[
3x+7y+4z=0,
\]
\[
18x+14y+8z=0.
\]

1. Express $G$ as a direct product of two cyclic groups.
2. Express $G$ as a direct product of cyclic groups of prime-power order.
3. How many elements of $G$ have order $2$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $C_m=\ZZ/m\ZZ$ and let
$$
R=
\begin{pmatrix}
15&3&0\\
3&7&4\\
18&14&8
\end{pmatrix}
$$
be the relation matrix with respect to the ordered generators
$x,y,z$.

<1>1. The determinantal divisors of $R$ are
$$
\Delta_1=1,
\qquad
\Delta_2=12,
\qquad
\Delta_3=144.
$$

::: {.proof}
The greatest common divisor of all entries of $R$ is $1$, because the
entries include $3$ and $7$. Thus $\Delta_1=1$.

The nonzero $2\times2$ minors, up to sign, are
$$
96,\ 60,\ 12,\ 156,\ 120,\ 24,\ 84,\ 48.
$$
Their greatest common divisor is $12$, so $\Delta_2=12$.

Finally,
$$
\det R
=
15(7\cdot8-4\cdot14)
-3(3\cdot8-4\cdot18)
=
144.
$$
Hence $\Delta_3=\abs{\det R}=144$.
:::

<1>2. The Smith invariant factors of $R$ are
$$
d_1=1,
\qquad
d_2=12,
\qquad
d_3=12.
$$

::: {.proof}
For a full-rank integer matrix, the Smith invariant factors satisfy
$$
\Delta_k=d_1d_2\cdots d_k.
$$
Using step <1>1,
$$
d_1=\Delta_1=1,
\qquad
d_2=\frac{\Delta_2}{\Delta_1}=12,
\qquad
d_3=\frac{\Delta_3}{\Delta_2}=12.
$$
:::

<1>3. For part 1,
$$
\boxed{
G\cong C_{12}\times C_{12}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The presentation identifies $G$ with the quotient of $\ZZ^3$ by the
subgroup generated by the rows of $R$. By the Smith normal form
theorem and step <1>2,
$$
G
\cong
C_1\times C_{12}\times C_{12}
\cong
C_{12}\times C_{12}.
$$
:::

<1>4. For part 2,
$$
\boxed{
G\cong C_4\times C_4\times C_3\times C_3
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $12=4\cdot3$ and $\gcd(4,3)=1$, the Chinese remainder theorem
gives
$$
C_{12}\cong C_4\times C_3.
$$
Apply this to both factors in step <1>3 and reorder the direct
factors.
:::

<1>5. For part 3, the number of elements of order $2$ is
$$
\boxed{3}.
$$

::: {.proof}
In $C_{12}$, the elements annihilated by $2$ are exactly $0$ and
$6$. Therefore the elements of $C_{12}\times C_{12}$ whose order
divides $2$ are
$$
(0,0),\qquad(6,0),\qquad(0,6),\qquad(6,6).
$$
The first has order $1$, and each of the other three has order $2$.
Step <1>3 transfers this count to $G$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3, <1>4, and <1>5 answer parts 1, 2, and 3, respectively.
