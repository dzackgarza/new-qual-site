::: {.problem}
Solve the system
\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=-3x+10y,
\qquad
\frac{dy}{dt}=-3x+8y.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The system is
$$
\frac{d}{dt}
\begin{pmatrix}
x\\
y
\end{pmatrix}
=
A
\begin{pmatrix}
x\\
y
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
-3&10\\
-3&8
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is exactly the given pair of differential equations written in
matrix form.
:::

<1>2. The eigenvalues of $A$ are $2$ and $3$.

::: {.proof}
The characteristic polynomial is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det(A-\lambda I)
&=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
-3-\lambda&10\\
-3&8-\lambda
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=
(-3-\lambda)(8-\lambda)+30\\
&=
\lambda^2-5\lambda+6\\
&=
(\lambda-2)(\lambda-3).
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>3. Eigenvectors for the eigenvalues $2$ and $3$ may be chosen as
$$
v_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
2\\
1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
5\\
3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $\lambda=2$,
$$
(A-2I)
\begin{pmatrix}
2\\
1
\end{pmatrix}
=
0.
$$
For $\lambda=3$,
$$
(A-3I)
\begin{pmatrix}
5\\
3
\end{pmatrix}
=
0.
$$
:::

<1>4. The general solution is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
x(t)\\
y(t)
\end{pmatrix}
=
C_1e^{2t}
\begin{pmatrix}
2\\
1
\end{pmatrix}
+
C_2e^{3t}
\begin{pmatrix}
5\\
3
\end{pmatrix},
}
$$
where $C_1,C_2$ are arbitrary constants.

::: {.proof}
For an eigenpair $(\lambda,v)$ of $A$, the function
$$
u(t)=e^{\lambda t}v
$$
satisfies
$$
u'(t)
=
\lambda e^{\lambda t}v
=
e^{\lambda t}Av
=
Au(t).
$$
Thus the two displayed eigenmodes are solutions. Their eigenvectors
are linearly independent because they correspond to distinct
eigenvalues, so these two solutions form a basis of the
two-dimensional solution space of the first-order system.
:::

<1>5. Equivalently,
$$
\boxed{
x(t)=2C_1e^{2t}+5C_2e^{3t},
\qquad
y(t)=C_1e^{2t}+3C_2e^{3t}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Read off the two coordinates in step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the complete family of solutions.
:::
:::
