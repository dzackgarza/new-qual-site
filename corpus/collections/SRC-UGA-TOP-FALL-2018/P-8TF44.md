::: problem
Recall that a covering space
\[
\pi:\widetilde X\longrightarrow X
\]
between path-connected spaces is called normal (or regular) provided that, for one and hence any $x_0\in X$, the deck transformation group of $\pi$ acts transitively on $\pi^{-1}(x_0)$.
Give examples both of a $3$-sheeted normal covering space of $S^1\vee S^1$, and of a path-connected $3$-sheeted non-normal covering space of $S^1\vee S^1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
\[
R=S^1\vee S^1
\]
with oriented loop-edges labeled $a$ and $b$, meeting at the vertex $v$.
A three-sheeted graph cover of $R$ can be specified by permutations
\[
\sigma_a,\sigma_b\in S_3.
\]
Take three vertices $v_1,v_2,v_3$ over $v$; for each $x\in\{a,b\}$ and each $i$, attach one oriented edge labeled $x$ from $v_i$ to $v_{\sigma_x(i)}$, and map it homeomorphically to the $x$-edge of $R$.

<1>1. This permutation construction gives a $3$-sheeted covering of $R$, and it is path connected exactly when the subgroup generated by $\sigma_a$ and $\sigma_b$ acts transitively on $\{1,2,3\}$.
::: {.proof}
At each vertex $v_i$, there is exactly one outgoing and one incoming lift of each oriented base edge $a$ and $b$.
Hence a sufficiently small star neighborhood of $v_i$ maps homeomorphically to a star neighborhood of $v$, so the resulting graph map is a covering map.
Its fiber over $v$ is exactly
\[
\{v_1,v_2,v_3\},
\]
so it has three sheets.

Lifting a word in $a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}$ moves the sheet index by the corresponding word in $\sigma_a^{\pm1},\sigma_b^{\pm1}$.
Thus two vertices lie in the same path component precisely when their indices lie in the same orbit of
\[
\langle\sigma_a,\sigma_b\rangle.
\]
This proves the connectedness criterion.
:::

<1>2. The choice
\[
\sigma_a=(123),
\qquad
\sigma_b=1
\]
gives a connected normal $3$-sheeted cover.
::: {.proof}
The lifted $a$-edges form a $3$-cycle through the vertices $v_1,v_2,v_3$, and the lifted $b$-edges are one loop at each vertex.
Since $(123)$ acts transitively on the three indices, <1>1 shows that the covering graph is connected.

Define
\[
T(v_i)=v_{i+1}
\]
with indices read modulo $3$, and send every labeled edge to the corresponding labeled edge after this cyclic shift.
Because the $a$-edge pattern is the same $3$-cycle and the $b$-edge pattern consists of identical loops, $T$ is a deck transformation.
The cyclic group
\[
\langle T\rangle\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ
\]
acts transitively on the fiber $\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}$.
By the definition in the problem, this cover is normal.
:::

<1>3. The choice
\[
\sigma_a=(12),
\qquad
\sigma_b=(123)
\]
gives a path-connected $3$-sheeted cover.
::: {.proof}
The permutation $(123)$ alone is transitive on $\{1,2,3\}$, so the monodromy group
\[
\langle(12),(123)\rangle
\]
is transitive.
By <1>1, the covering graph is path connected.
Explicitly, the lifted $b$-edges form a $3$-cycle; the lifted $a$-edges consist of the two-edge cycle over sheets $1,2$ together with an $a$-loop at sheet $3$.
:::

<1>4. The cover in <1>3 is not normal.
::: {.proof}
A deck transformation permutes the fiber over $v$.
Let
\[
\tau\in S_3
\]
be the induced permutation.
Because a deck transformation commutes with lifting each base loop, $\tau$ must commute with both monodromy permutations:
\[
\tau(12)=(12)\tau,
\qquad
\tau(123)=(123)\tau.
\]
But $(12)$ and $(123)$ generate all of $S_3$.
Therefore $\tau$ lies in the center of $S_3$, which is trivial.
Hence every deck transformation fixes all three points of the fiber.
A deck transformation of a connected covering that fixes one point is the identity, so the deck group is trivial.
It cannot act transitively on a three-point fiber.
Thus this connected $3$-sheeted cover is non-normal.
:::
