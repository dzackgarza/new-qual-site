::: {.exercise}
Suppose $n=4^a(8k+7)$, where $a$ and $k$ are positive integers. Show that $n$ cannot be written as a sum of three squares. The converse, that every $n$ not of this form is a sum of three squares, is a theorem of Legendre and Gauss.
:::

::: {.remark}
The source requires $a,k>0$, but its stated converse then fails: $7$ is not of that form and is not a sum of three squares. The three-square theorem excludes $4^a(8k+7)$ with $a,k\geq0$.
:::
