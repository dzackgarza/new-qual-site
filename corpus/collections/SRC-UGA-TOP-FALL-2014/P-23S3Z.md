::: problem
Prove that, for every continuous map $f : B^2 \to B^2$, there is a point $x$ such that $f (x) = x$.

> This is the $n = 2$ case of the Brouwer fixed point theorem; your proof shouldn't appeal to either of the Brouwer or the Lefschetz fixed point theorems.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose, for contradiction, that
\[
f:B^2\to B^2
\]
has no fixed point.
Then
\[
g:B^2\to S^1,
\qquad
g(x)=\frac{x-f(x)}{\|x-f(x)\|},
\]
is a well-defined continuous map.
::: {.proof}
The assumption that $f$ has no fixed point says
\[
x-f(x)\ne0
\]
for every $x\in B^2$.
Thus the denominator never vanishes.
The numerator is continuous, normalization is continuous on $\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}$, and the normalized vector has norm $1$.
Hence $g$ is a continuous map into $S^1$.
:::

<1>2. On the boundary $S^1=\partial B^2$, the map $g|_{S^1}$ is homotopic to the identity map of $S^1$.
::: {.proof}
For $x\in S^1$ and $0\le t\le1$, set
\[
H(x,t)=\frac{x-tf(x)}{\|x-tf(x)\|}.
\]
We first show that the denominator never vanishes.
If
\[
x=tf(x),
\]
then, because $\|x\|=1$ and $\|f(x)\|\le1$,
\[
1=t\|f(x)\|\le t\le1.
\]
Hence equality holds throughout, so
\[
t=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f(x)=x,
\]
contradicting the fixed-point-free assumption.

Therefore $H$ is well-defined and continuous.
At the endpoints,
\[
H(x,0)=x
\]
and
\[
H(x,1)
=
\frac{x-f(x)}{\|x-f(x)\|}
=
g(x).
\]
Thus
\[
g|_{S^1}\simeq\operatorname{id}_{S^1}.
\]
:::

<1>3. Consequently,
\[
(g|_{S^1})_*:\pi_1(S^1)\to\pi_1(S^1)
\]
is the identity homomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism on fundamental groups, up to the standard basepoint-change isomorphism when the homotopy moves the basepoint.
Since $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z$ is abelian, this basepoint change has no effect on the induced endomorphism.
By <1>2,
\[
g|_{S^1}\simeq\operatorname{id}_{S^1},
\]
so
\[
(g|_{S^1})_*=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z}.
\]
In particular it is nonzero.
:::

<1>4. But because $g|_{S^1}$ extends over $B^2$, its induced map on $\pi_1$ must be zero.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
i:S^1\hookrightarrow B^2
\]
be the inclusion.
Then
\[
g|_{S^1}=g\circ i.
\]
Functoriality of the fundamental group gives
\[
(g|_{S^1})_*=g_*\circ i_*.
\]
Since $B^2$ is contractible,
\[
\pi_1(B^2)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
i_*:\pi_1(S^1)\to\pi_1(B^2)
\]
is the zero homomorphism, and hence
\[
(g|_{S^1})_*=0.
\]
:::

<1>5. The contradiction proves that every continuous map $f:B^2\to B^2$ has a fixed point.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives
\[
(g|_{S^1})_*=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z},
\]
while <1>4 gives
\[
(g|_{S^1})_*=0.
\]
These cannot both hold.
Thus the assumption in <1>1 that $f$ is fixed-point-free is false.
Hence there exists
\[
x\in B^2
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
\]
:::
:::
