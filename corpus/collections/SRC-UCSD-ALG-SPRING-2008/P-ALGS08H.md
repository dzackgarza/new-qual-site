::: problem
Show that the center of a simple ring with identity element is a field.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Algebraic structure of the center:
<2>1. Let $R$ be a simple ring with identity $1 \neq 0$ (its only two-sided ideals are $\{0\}$ and $R$).
The center $Z(R) = \{z \in R \mid zr = rz \text{ for all } r \in R\}$ is a commutative ring containing $1 \neq 0$.
::: {.proof}
definition of center of a unital ring.
:::

<1>2. Existence of two-sided inverse in $R$:
<2>1. Let $z \in Z(R)$ with $z \neq 0$.
Consider the principal two-sided ideal generated by $z$ in $R$:
\[
I = R z R = \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^k r_i z s_i \;\middle|\; r_i, s_i \in R, \, k \in \mathbb{N} \right\}.
\]
Because $z \in Z(R)$, for every $r_i, s_i \in R$ we have $r_i z s_i = z (r_i s_i)$, so $I = zR = Rz$.
::: {.proof}
$z$ commutes with all elements of $R$.
:::
<2>2. The ideal $I$ is non-zero because $z = z \cdot 1 \in I$ and $z \neq 0$.
Since $R$ is a simple ring, the only non-zero two-sided ideal is $R$ itself, so $I = R$.
::: {.proof}
simplicity of $R$.
:::
<2>3. Since $1 \in R = zR$, there exists an element $w \in R$ such that:
\[
z w = 1.
\]
Because $z \in Z(R)$, we also have $w z = z w = 1$, so $w$ is a two-sided inverse of $z$ in $R$.
::: {.proof}
commutativity of $z$ with $w$.
:::

<1>3. Proof that $w \in Z(R)$:
<2>1. Let $r \in R$ be arbitrary. Compute using $w z = z w = 1$ and $z r = r z$:
\[
rw = (w z) r w = w (z r) w = w (r z) w = w r (z w) = wr \cdot 1 = wr.
\]
Thus $rw = wr$ for all $r \in R$.
::: {.proof}
associativity of multiplication in $R$ and $z \in Z(R)$.
:::
<2>2. Therefore $w \in Z(R)$, meaning that every non-zero element $z \in Z(R)$ is invertible with its inverse in $Z(R)$.
::: {.proof}
definition of $Z(R)$.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
$Z(R)$ is a non-trivial commutative ring in which every non-zero element is invertible, hence $Z(R)$ is a field. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>3.
:::
:::
