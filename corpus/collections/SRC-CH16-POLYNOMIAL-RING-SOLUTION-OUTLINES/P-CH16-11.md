::: {.problem}
If $\phi : R \to S$ is a ring homomorphism, define $\bar{\phi} : R[x] \to S[x]$ by $(a_{n}x^{n} + \cdots + a_{1}x + a_{0}) \mapsto \phi(a_{n})x^{n} + \cdots + \phi(a_{0})$. Show that $\bar{\phi}$ is a ring homomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $f=\sum a_ix^i$ and $g=\sum b_jx^j$ with finitely many nonzero coefficients, padding with zeros so that the two sums run over a common range. Then $\overline\varphi(f)$ is by definition the polynomial whose coefficient of $x^i$ is $\varphi(a_i)$; additivity reduces to $\varphi(a_i+b_i)=\varphi(a_i)+\varphi(b_i)$ coefficientwise, and multiplicativity to the convolution formula for the coefficients of a product together with $\varphi$ respecting finite sums and products. The collection retains its solution outline as `assets/attachments/Ch16Sltns.pdf`, whose entry "# 11" on PDF p. 1 is this exercise: it writes the addition coefficientwise and ends "Similarly, you can show that $\overline\varphi$ preserves multiplication." The card supplies that multiplicativity through the convolution formula for the coefficients of a product, adds well-definedness, and makes no claim about $1$; the wording is its own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #well}
$\overline\varphi$ is well defined: a polynomial has a unique expression $\sum_{i\ge0}a_ix^i$ with finitely many nonzero $a_i$, and $f\mapsto\sum_i\varphi(a_i)x^i$ is then an element of $S[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Polynomials over a ring are equal exactly when their coefficient sequences are equal, so the sequence $(a_i)$ of coefficients of $f$ is unique, and all but finitely many of its entries are $0$. A homomorphism of additive groups carries $0$ to $0$: from $\varphi(0_R)=\varphi(0_R+0_R)=\varphi(0_R)+\varphi(0_R)$ one cancels $\varphi(0_R)$ in the group $(S,+)$ and gets $\varphi(0_R)=0_S$. So $\varphi(a_i)=0$ for all but finitely many $i$, the sum $\sum_i\varphi(a_i)x^i$ is a polynomial over $S$, and it depends only on $f$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #add}
$\overline\varphi(f+g)=\overline\varphi(f)+\overline\varphi(g)$.

::: pf-proof
Write $f+g=\sum_i(a_i+b_i)x^i$. Then $\overline\varphi(f+g)=\sum_i\varphi(a_i+b_i)x^i=\sum_i\bigl(\varphi(a_i)+\varphi(b_i)\bigr)x^i$, since $\varphi$ respects addition, and the last sum is $\sum_i\varphi(a_i)x^i+\sum_i\varphi(b_i)x^i=\overline\varphi(f)+\overline\varphi(g)$ by the definition of addition in $S[x]$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #mul}
$\overline\varphi(fg)=\overline\varphi(f)\overline\varphi(g)$.

::: pf-proof
If $f=\sum_ia_ix^i$ and $g=\sum_jb_jx^j$ then $fg=\sum_kc_kx^k$ with $c_k=\sum_{i+j=k}a_ib_j$, a finite sum. Hence
$$
\overline\varphi(fg)=\sum_k\varphi(c_k)x^k=\sum_k\Bigl(\sum_{i+j=k}\varphi(a_i)\varphi(b_j)\Bigr)x^k,
$$
using that $\varphi$ respects finite sums and products. The right-hand side is exactly the coefficient of $x^k$ in $\bigl(\sum_i\varphi(a_i)x^i\bigr)\bigl(\sum_j\varphi(b_j)x^j\bigr)$, so $\overline\varphi(fg)=\overline\varphi(f)\overline\varphi(g)$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
$\overline\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism $R[x]\to S[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#well){.pf-ref}, [](#add){.pf-ref} and [](#mul){.pf-ref} verify the three requirements: a well-defined map of additive groups respecting addition and multiplication. Nothing is claimed about $1$: with Gallian's convention a ring homomorphism need not carry $1$ to $1$, and indeed $\overline\varphi(1_R)=\varphi(1_R)$, which need not be $1_S$ unless $\varphi$ is unital.
:::
