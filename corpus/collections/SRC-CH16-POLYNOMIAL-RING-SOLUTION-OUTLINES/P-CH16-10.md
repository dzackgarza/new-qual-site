::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring.
Show that $R[x]$ has a subring isomorphic to $R$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own: the collection's provenance is a set of solution outlines. Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $R[x]$ its polynomial ring.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #embedding}
The map $\varphi:R\to R[x]$ sending $r$ to the constant polynomial $r$ is an injective ring homomorphism.

::: pf-proof
The constant polynomials add and multiply as the elements do: $\varphi(r)+\varphi(s)=\varphi(r+s)$ and $\varphi(r)\varphi(s)=\varphi(rs)$, because the product of two constant polynomials is constant with product coefficient; and $\varphi(1_R)=1_{R[x]}$. It is injective because a constant polynomial determines its coefficient, so $\varphi(r)=0$ only for $r=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subring}
$\varphi(R)$ is a subring of $R[x]$ isomorphic to $R$.

::: pf-proof
The image of a ring homomorphism is a subring: it contains $\varphi(1)=1$ and is closed under addition, multiplication and additive inverses because those operations are carried to the corresponding operations of the image. Since $\varphi$ is injective by step [](#embedding){.pf-ref}, it is an isomorphism of $R$ onto its image, so $\varphi(R)\le R[x]$ is a subring isomorphic to $R$. This is the subring the problem asks for, namely the constant polynomials.
:::
