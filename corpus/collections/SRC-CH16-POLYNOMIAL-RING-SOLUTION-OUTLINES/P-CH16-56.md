::: {.problem}
For any field $F$ recall that $F(x)$ denotes the field of quotients of the ring $F[x]$. Prove that there is no element in $F(x)$ whose square is $x$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The retained outline is `assets/attachments/Ch16Sltns.pdf`; its entry "# 56" covers the square-root question printed as Gallian 7e Chapter 16 Exercise 46. The outline uses a reduced fraction and shows that its numerator and denominator must both be divisible by $x$. The cancellations supporting that contradiction are checked below.

Suppose instead that $(f/g)^{2}=x$ for some $f/g\in F(x)$. Write $f,g\in F[x]$ with $g\ne0$ and with $f$ and $g$ having no nonconstant common polynomial factor.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #equation}
$f^{2}=xg^{2}$ in $F[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Multiplication in the field of quotients gives $(f/g)^{2}=f^{2}/g^{2}$. Equality of $f^{2}/g^{2}$ and $x/1$ in $F(x)$ yields $f^{2}=xg^{2}$ after multiplication by the nonzero polynomial $g^{2}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #numerator}
There is an $h\in F[x]$ such that $f=xh$.

::: pf-proof
Evaluating the identity from step [](#equation){.pf-ref} at $0$ gives $f(0)^{2}=0$, whence $f(0)=0$ in $F$. By the factor theorem for polynomials over a field, $x-0=x$ divides $f$, so $f=xh$ for some $h\in F[x]$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #denominator}
The polynomial $g$ is also divisible by $x$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #cancellation}
$g^{2}=xh^{2}$ for the polynomial $h$ supplied by step [](#numerator){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof
Substitute $f=xh$ into step [](#equation){.pf-ref} to obtain $x^{2}h^{2}=xg^{2}$, hence $x(xh^{2}-g^{2})=0$. Since $F$ is a field, $F[x]$ is an integral domain. The polynomial $x$ is nonzero, so $xh^{2}-g^{2}=0$, and $g^{2}=xh^{2}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$g(0)=0$, and consequently $g=xk$ for some $k\in F[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Evaluating $g^{2}=xh^{2}$ at $0$ gives $g(0)^{2}=0$, hence $g(0)=0$. The factor theorem therefore gives $g=xk$ for some $k\in F[x]$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
The hypothesized square root cannot exist.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#numerator){.pf-ref} and [](#denominator){.pf-ref} supply $h,k\in F[x]$ with $f=xh$ and $g=xk$. Thus $x$, a nonconstant polynomial, is a common factor of $f$ and $g$, contradicting the reduced-form choice. The assumption that some element of $F(x)$ has square $x$ is therefore false.
:::
