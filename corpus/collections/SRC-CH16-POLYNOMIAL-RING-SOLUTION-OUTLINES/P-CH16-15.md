::: {.problem}
Show that the polynomial $2x + 1$ in $\ZZ_{4}[x]$ has a multiplicative inverse in $\ZZ_{4}[x]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #compute}

$(2x+1)(2x+1)=1$ in $\ZZ_{4}[x]$.

::: pf-proof

Multiply in $\ZZ_{4}[x]$, where coefficients are reduced modulo $4$:
$(2x+1)^{2}=4x^{2}+4x+1$. The coefficient $4$ is the zero element of
$\ZZ_{4}$, so both higher terms vanish and the product is the constant
polynomial $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inverse}

$2x+1$ is a unit of $\ZZ_{4}[x]$, with inverse $2x+1$.

::: pf-proof

A unit is an element with a two-sided multiplicative inverse, and $2x+1$ is its
own: by step [](#compute){.pf-ref} the product is $1$, and $\ZZ_{4}[x]$ is
commutative, so the product is $1$ in either order.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #why}

This is possible because $\ZZ_{4}$ has zero-divisors, so degrees need not add.

::: pf-proof

Over a field $F$ one shows $\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g$, which forbids a nonconstant
unit of $F[x]$. In $\ZZ_{4}[x]$ the leading coefficients can annihilate: here
$2\cdot 2=0$ in $\ZZ_{4}$, which is exactly what collapses the degree of
$(2x+1)^{2}$ from $2$ to $0$.

:::
