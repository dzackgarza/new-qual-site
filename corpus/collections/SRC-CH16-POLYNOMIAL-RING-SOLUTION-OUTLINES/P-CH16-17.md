::: {.problem}
Let $p$ be a prime.
Are there any nonconstant polynomials in $\ZZ_{p}[x]$ that have multiplicative inverses?
Explain your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's `provenance` is the textbook's Google Books record and no solutions sheet is retained, so the argument below is the card's own, structured from the outline already on the card.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #domain}
For nonzero $f, g \in \ZZ_{p}[x]$ the product $fg$ is nonzero, $\deg(fg) = \deg f + \deg g$, and the leading coefficient of $fg$ is the product of the leading coefficients.

::: pf-proof
Let $a_{n}x^{n}$ and $b_{m}x^{m}$ be the leading terms of $f$ and $g$. Every other product of a term of $f$ with a term of $g$ has degree below $n+m$, so the coefficient of $x^{n+m}$ in $fg$ is exactly $a_{n}b_{m}$. Since $\ZZ_{p}$ is a field, $a_{n} \ne 0$ and $b_{m} \ne 0$ give $a_{n}b_{m} \ne 0$, so the coefficient of $x^{n+m}$ is nonzero and no term of higher degree occurs.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #degree-zero}
If $f$ is a unit of $\ZZ_{p}[x]$ then $\deg f = 0$.

::: pf-proof
Let $g$ be an inverse, $fg = 1$. The polynomial $1$ is nonzero of degree $0$, and $f, g$ are nonzero because their product is, so step [](#domain){.pf-ref} gives $\deg f + \deg g = \deg(fg) = 0$. Degrees are nonnegative integers, and $\deg g \ge 0$, so $\deg f = 0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #constants}
Every nonzero constant is a unit of $\ZZ_{p}[x]$, so the units are exactly the nonzero constants.

::: pf-proof
A nonzero constant is an element $c$ of the field $\ZZ_{p}$ and has inverse $c^{-1} \in \ZZ_{p} \subseteq \ZZ_{p}[x]$. Together with step [](#degree-zero){.pf-ref}, which excludes every polynomial of positive degree, this identifies the unit group as $\ZZ_{p}^{\times}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
No: no nonconstant polynomial in $\ZZ_{p}[x]$ has a multiplicative inverse.

::: pf-proof
A nonconstant polynomial has degree at least $1$, and step [](#degree-zero){.pf-ref} shows a unit has degree $0$; step [](#constants){.pf-ref} shows the surviving constants really are units, so the question is settled in both directions.

:::
