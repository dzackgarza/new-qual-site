::: {.problem}
Are there any nonconstant polynomials in $\ZZ[x]$ that have multiplicative inverses?
Explain your answers.
:::

::: {.solution}
The retained outline is `assets/attachments/Ch16Sltns.pdf`; its entry "# 16" covers the nonconstant-polynomial question printed as Gallian 7e Chapter 16 Exercise 14. The outline argues through a product and its coefficients. After source review, the card uses the more direct degree-rule calculation for that product and also proves which constants are units.

Suppose throughout that $f\in\ZZ[x]$ has a multiplicative inverse $g\in\ZZ[x]$, so $fg=1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #constant}
The polynomials $f$ and $g$ are constant.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #degree-rule}
$\deg f+\deg g=0$.

::: pf-proof
Since $\ZZ$ is an integral domain, the degree rule for nonzero polynomials over an integral domain applies. Both $f$ and $g$ are nonzero because $fg=1$, so $\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g$. But $fg=1$ is the constant polynomial of degree $0$, and the required equality follows.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\deg f=\deg g=0$, so $f$ and $g$ are constant polynomials.

::: pf-proof
Degrees of nonzero polynomials are nonnegative integers. Step [](#degree-rule){.pf-ref} gives a sum of two such integers equal to $0$, whence both terms are $0$. Thus $\deg f=\deg g=0$, which says exactly that $f$ and $g$ are constant.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #constant-units}
Among constant polynomials, precisely $1$ and $-1$ are units in $\ZZ[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Write the constant polynomial $f$ as an integer $a$. Its constant inverse $g$, supplied by step [](#constant){.pf-ref}, is likewise an integer $b$, and $fg=1$ becomes $ab=1$ in $\ZZ$. The only integer divisors of $1$ are $\pm1$, so $(a,b)=(1,1)$ or $(a,b)=(-1,-1)$. Conversely, $1\cdot1=1$ and $(-1)(-1)=1$, proving that both $1$ and $-1$ really are units.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\boxed{\text{No nonconstant polynomial in }\ZZ[x]\text{ has a multiplicative inverse; the units are exactly }\pm1}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#constant){.pf-ref} shows that every unit must be constant, so no nonconstant polynomial is a unit. Step [](#constant-units){.pf-ref} then determines exactly which constants are units.
:::
