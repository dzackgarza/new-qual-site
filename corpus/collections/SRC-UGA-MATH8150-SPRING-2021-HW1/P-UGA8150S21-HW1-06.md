::: {.problem}
Use $n$-th roots of unity (i.e. solutions of $z^{n} - 1 = 0$) to show that
$$
2^{n-1} \sin\frac{\pi}{n} \sin\frac{2\pi}{n} \cdots \sin\frac{(n-1)\pi}{n} = n.
$$

[Hint: $1 - \cos 2\theta = 2\sin^{2}\theta$, $\sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$.]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The $n$th roots of unity are
$$
\boxed{
1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\dots,\zeta^{n-1}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The solutions of
$$
z^n=1
$$
are
$$
e^{2\pi ik/n},
\qquad
0\le k\le n-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
z^n-1
=
(z-1)\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(z-\zeta^k)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The polynomial $z^n-1$ is monic of degree $n$ and has exactly the roots
listed in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For $z\ne1$,
$$
\frac{z^n-1}{z-1}
=
\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(z-\zeta^k).
$$


::: pf-proof

Divide the factorization in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} by $z-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Taking the limit as $z\to1$,
$$
\boxed{
n
=
\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-\zeta^k)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The left-hand side satisfies
$$
\lim_{z\to1}\frac{z^n-1}{z-1}
=
\evaluated{\frac{d}{dz}z^n}_{z=1}
=
n.
$$
The right-hand side is continuous at $z=1$, so its limit is the displayed
product.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Taking absolute values,
$$
\boxed{
n
=
\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\abs{1-\zeta^k}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $n>0$,
$$
\abs{n}=n.
$$
Apply multiplicativity of the complex modulus to step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For every real $\theta$,
$$
\boxed{
\abs{1-e^{i\theta}}
=
2\abs{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Square the modulus:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{1-e^{i\theta}}^2
&=
(1-e^{i\theta})(1-e^{-i\theta})\\
&=
2-2\cos\theta\\
&=
4\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Take nonnegative square roots.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\abs{1-\zeta^k}
=
2\sin\frac{k\pi}{n}}
$$
for
$$
1\le k\le n-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
\theta=\frac{2\pi k}{n}
$$
in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}. Then
$$
0<\frac{k\pi}{n}<\pi,
$$
so the sine is positive and the absolute value may be removed.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Substituting step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} into step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
n
=
\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}
2\sin\frac{k\pi}{n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Replace every factor in the product from step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Pulling out the $n-1$ factors of $2$,
$$
\boxed{
2^{n-1}
\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}
\sin\frac{k\pi}{n}
=
n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are exactly $n-1$ factors in the product.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Equivalently,
$$
\boxed{
\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}
\sin\frac{k\pi}{n}
=
\frac{n}{2^{n-1}}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Divide step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} by $2^{n-1}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is the identity requested in the problem.

:::
