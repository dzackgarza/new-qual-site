::: {.problem}
(a) Let $z, w$ be complex numbers, such that $\bar{z} w \neq 1$.
Prove that
$$\abs{\frac{w - z}{1 - \bar{w} z}} < 1 \quad \text{if } \abs{z} < 1 \text{ and } \abs{w} < 1,$$
and also that
$$\abs{\frac{w - z}{1 - \bar{w} z}} = 1 \quad \text{if } \abs{z} = 1 \text{ or } \abs{w} = 1.$$

(b) Prove that for fixed $w$ in the unit disk $\DD \coloneqq \theset{z : \abs{z} < 1}$, the
mapping $$F: z \mapsto \frac{w - z}{1 - \bar{w} z}$$ satisfies the
following conditions:

(i) $F$ maps $\DD$ to itself and is holomorphic.

(ii) $F$ interchanges $0$ and $w$, namely, $F(0) = w$ and
$F(w) = 0$.

(iii) $\abs{F(z)} = 1$ if $\abs{z} = 1$.

(iv) $F: \DD \to \DD$ is bijective.
[Hint: Calculate $F \circ F$.]
:::

::: {.solution}

## Part (a)

Let
$$
N=\abs{1-\overline wz}^2-\abs{w-z}^2.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Expanding the first modulus,
$$
\abs{1-\overline wz}^2
=
1
-\overline wz
-w\overline z
+
\abs{w}^2\abs{z}^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Multiply by the complex conjugate:
$$
(1-\overline wz)(1-w\overline z).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Expanding the second modulus,
$$
\abs{w-z}^2
=
\abs{w}^2+\abs{z}^2-w\overline z-\overline wz.
$$


::: pf-proof

Multiply
$$
(w-z)(\overline w-\overline z).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Subtracting,
$$
\boxed{
N
=
(1-\abs{w}^2)(1-\abs{z}^2)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
N
&=
1-\abs{w}^2-\abs{z}^2+\abs{w}^2\abs{z}^2\\
&=
(1-\abs{w}^2)(1-\abs{z}^2).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If
$$
\abs{w}<1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\abs{z}<1,
$$
then
$$
\boxed{
\abs{1-\overline wz}^2>\abs{w-z}^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both factors on the right-hand side of step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} are positive, so
$$
N>0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\abs{
\frac{w-z}{1-\overline wz}
}
<1}
$$
when $\abs{w}<1$ and $\abs{z}<1$.


::: pf-proof

The denominator is nonzero because
$$
\abs{\overline wz}
=
\abs{w}\abs{z}
<1.
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{w-z}<\abs{1-\overline wz}.
$$
Divide by the positive denominator modulus.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If
$$
\abs{w}=1
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\abs{z}=1,
$$
then
$$
\boxed{
\abs{1-\overline wz}^2
=
\abs{w-z}^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

At least one factor on the right-hand side of step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is zero, so
$$
N=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Under the standing assumption
$$
\overline zw\ne1,
$$
the denominator
$$
1-\overline wz
$$
is nonzero.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
1-\overline wz=0,
$$
then
$$
\overline wz=1.
$$
Taking complex conjugates gives
$$
w\overline z=1,
$$
which is exactly
$$
\overline zw=1,
$$
contrary to the hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore, if $\abs{w}=1$ or $\abs{z}=1$,
$$
\boxed{
\abs{
\frac{w-z}{1-\overline wz}
}
=1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Use steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and take nonnegative square roots.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For every $z\in\DD$,
$$
1-\overline wz\ne0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\abs{w}<1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\abs{z}<1,
$$
one has
$$
\abs{\overline wz}
=
\abs{w}\abs{z}
<1.
$$
Thus $\overline wz\ne1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Hence $F$ is holomorphic on $\DD$.


::: pf-proof

The numerator and denominator are affine holomorphic functions of $z$, and
the denominator has no zeros on $\DD$ by step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

For every $z\in\DD$,
$$
\boxed{\abs{F(z)}<1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} with the fixed $w\in\DD$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
F(\DD)\subseteq\DD}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the content of step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

One has
$$
\boxed{F(0)=w}.
$$


::: pf-proof

$$
F(0)
=
\frac{w}{1}
=
w.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

One has
$$
\boxed{F(w)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

$$
F(w)
=
\frac{w-w}{1-\abs{w}^2}
=
0.
$$
The denominator is nonzero because $\abs{w}<1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

If
$$
\abs{z}=1,
$$
then
$$
\boxed{\abs{F(z)}=1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}. Since $\abs{w}<1$, the denominator cannot vanish on the unit
circle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Conversely, if
$$
\abs{F(z)}=1
$$
and the expression is defined, then
$$
\boxed{\abs{z}=1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{1-\overline wz}^2-\abs{w-z}^2
=
(1-\abs{w}^2)(1-\abs{z}^2).
$$
The hypothesis
$$
\abs{F(z)}=1
$$
means the two moduli on the left are equal, so the left side is zero.
Because
$$
1-\abs{w}^2>0,
$$
one must have
$$
1-\abs{z}^2=0.
$$
Thus $\abs{z}=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\abs{F(z)}=1
\iff
\abs{z}=1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The forward implication is step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}, and the reverse implication is step
[](#s1-15){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

The numerator of $F(F(z))$ is
$$
w-F(z)
=
\frac{(1-\abs{w}^2)z}{1-\overline wz}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compute
$$
\begin{aligned}
w-F(z)
&=
w-\frac{w-z}{1-\overline wz}\\
&=
\frac{w(1-\overline wz)-(w-z)}
{1-\overline wz}\\
&=
\frac{z-\abs{w}^2z}
{1-\overline wz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

The denominator of $F(F(z))$ is
$$
1-\overline wF(z)
=
\frac{1-\abs{w}^2}{1-\overline wz}.
$$


::: pf-proof

$$
\begin{aligned}
1-\overline wF(z)
&=
1-\overline w
\frac{w-z}{1-\overline wz}\\
&=
\frac{
1-\overline wz-\abs{w}^2+\overline wz
}{
1-\overline wz
}\\
&=
\frac{1-\abs{w}^2}{1-\overline wz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{F(F(z))=z}
$$
for every $z\in\DD$.


::: pf-proof

Divide the expressions from steps [](#s1-18){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-19){.pf-ref}:
$$
F(F(z))
=
\frac{(1-\abs{w}^2)z}
{1-\abs{w}^2}
=
z.
$$
The scalar $1-\abs{w}^2$ is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

Hence $F:\DD\to\DD$ is injective.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
F(z_1)=F(z_2),
$$
apply $F$ to both sides:
$$
F(F(z_1))
=
F(F(z_2)).
$$
By step [](#s1-20){.pf-ref},
$$
z_1=z_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

The map $F:\DD\to\DD$ is surjective.


::: pf-proof

Given $y\in\DD$, set
$$
z=F(y).
$$
By step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref},
$$
z\in\DD.
$$
Then step [](#s1-20){.pf-ref} gives
$$
F(z)
=
F(F(y))
=
y.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-23}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
F:\DD\to\DD
\text{ is a biholomorphic involution}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Holomorphicity is step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, mapping into the disk is step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref},
bijectivity is steps [](#s1-21){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-22){.pf-ref}, and involutivity is step [](#s1-20){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

claims in part (b).
