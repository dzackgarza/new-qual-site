::: {.problem}
Describe geometrically the sets of points z in the complex plane defined by the following relations: (a) $| z - 1 | = 1 .$ (b) $| z - 1 | = 2 | z - 2 |$ (c) $1 / z = { \bar { z } } .$ (d) $\mathrm { R e } ( z ) = 3$ (e) $\operatorname { I m } ( z ) = a$ with $a \in \mathbb { R } .$ (f) $\operatorname { R e } ( z ) > a$ with $a \in \mathbb { R }$

(g) $| z - 1 | < 2 | z - 2 |$
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
\[
z=x+iy,
\qquad
x,y\in\mathbb R.
\]

## Part (a)

<1>1. The relation
\[
|z-1|=1
\]
becomes
\[
\boxed{
(x-1)^2+y^2=1}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since
\[
z-1=(x-1)+iy,
\]
one has
\[
|z-1|^2=(x-1)^2+y^2.
\]
Square the equation.
:::

<1>2. Therefore the locus is the circle of radius \(1\) centered at
\[
\boxed{(1,0)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 is the standard circle equation.
:::

## Part (b)

<1>3. Squaring
\[
|z-1|=2|z-2|
\]
gives
\[
(x-1)^2+y^2
=
4\bigl((x-2)^2+y^2\bigr).
\]

::: {.proof}
Both sides are nonnegative, so squaring is equivalent.
:::

<1>4. Expanding,
\[
3x^2-14x+15+3y^2=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
\[
x^2-2x+1+y^2
=
4x^2-16x+16+4y^2.
\]
Move all terms to one side.
:::

<1>5. Completing the square,
\[
\boxed{
\left(x-\frac73\right)^2+y^2
=
\frac49}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Divide step <1>4 by \(3\):
\[
x^2-\frac{14}{3}x+5+y^2=0.
\]
Since
\[
x^2-\frac{14}{3}x
=
\left(x-\frac73\right)^2-\frac{49}{9},
\]
one obtains
\[
\left(x-\frac73\right)^2+y^2
=
\frac{49}{9}-5
=
\frac49.
\]
:::

<1>6. Therefore the locus is the circle centered at
\[
\boxed{\left(\frac73,0\right)}
\]
with radius
\[
\boxed{\frac23}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Read the center and radius from step <1>5.
:::

## Part (c)

<1>7. The relation
\[
\frac1z=\overline z
\]
implies
\[
\boxed{|z|^2=1}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The equation already requires \(z\ne0\). Multiply both sides by \(z\):
\[
1=z\overline z=|z|^2.
\]
:::

<1>8. Conversely, if
\[
|z|=1,
\]
then
\[
\frac1z=\overline z.
\]

::: {.proof}
From
\[
z\overline z=1,
\]
divide by \(z\ne0\).
:::

<1>9. Thus the locus is the unit circle
\[
\boxed{|z|=1}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>7--<1>8.
:::

## Part (d)

<1>10. The equation
\[
\operatorname{Re}z=3
\]
is
\[
\boxed{x=3}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For \(z=x+iy\),
\[
\operatorname{Re}z=x.
\]
:::

<1>11. Hence the locus is the vertical line through
\[
\boxed{x=3}.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is the geometric meaning of step <1>10.
:::

## Part (e)

<1>12. The equation
\[
\operatorname{Im}z=a
\]
is
\[
\boxed{y=a}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For \(z=x+iy\),
\[
\operatorname{Im}z=y.
\]
:::

<1>13. Hence the locus is the horizontal line
\[
\boxed{y=a}.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is the geometric meaning of step <1>12.
:::

## Part (f)

<1>14. The inequality
\[
\operatorname{Re}z>a
\]
is
\[
\boxed{x>a}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Again,
\[
\operatorname{Re}z=x.
\]
:::

<1>15. Hence the locus is the open right half-plane bounded by the vertical
line
\[
\boxed{x=a}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The strict inequality excludes the boundary line.
:::

## Part (g)

<1>16. Squaring
\[
|z-1|<2|z-2|
\]
gives
\[
(x-1)^2+y^2
<
4\bigl((x-2)^2+y^2\bigr).
\]

::: {.proof}
Both sides are nonnegative, so the strict inequality is preserved by
squaring.
:::

<1>17. Rearranging as in part (b),
\[
\boxed{
\left(x-\frac73\right)^2+y^2
<
\frac49}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The algebra in steps <1>4--<1>5 is unchanged, with equality replaced by a
strict inequality.
:::

<1>18. Therefore the locus is the open disk centered at
\[
\boxed{\left(\frac73,0\right)}
\]
with radius
\[
\boxed{\frac23}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Step <1>17 is the standard equation of the interior of that circle.
:::

<1>19. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2, <1>6, <1>9, <1>11, <1>13, <1>15, and <1>18 give the seven
requested geometric descriptions.
:::
:::
