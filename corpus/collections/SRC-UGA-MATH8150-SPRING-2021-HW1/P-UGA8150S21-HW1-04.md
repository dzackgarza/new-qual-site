::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that if $| w _ { 1 } | = c | w _ { 2 } |$ where $c > 0 .$ , then $\left| w _ { 1 } - c ^ { 2 } w _ { 2 } \right| = c | w _ { 1 } - w _ { 2 } |$

(b) Prove that if $c > 0 , c \neq 1$ and $z _ { 1 } \neq z _ { 2 }$ , then $\left| { \frac { z - z _ { 1 } } { z - z _ { 2 } } } \right| = c$ represents a circle.
Find its center and radius.
[Hint: an easy way is to use part (a)]
:::

::: {.solution}

## Part (a)

Assume
$$
|w_1|=c|w_2|,
\qquad
c>0.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Squaring the hypothesis gives
$$
\boxed{|w_1|^2=c^2|w_2|^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both sides are nonnegative.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Expanding,
$$
|w_1-c^2w_2|^2
=
|w_1|^2
-2c^2\operatorname{Re}(w_1\overline{w_2})
+c^4|w_2|^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Use
$$
|a-b|^2
=
|a|^2+|b|^2-2\operatorname{Re}(a\overline b)
$$
with
$$
a=w_1,
\qquad
b=c^2w_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
|w_1-c^2w_2|^2
=
c^2
\left(
|w_1|^2+|w_2|^2
-2\operatorname{Re}(w_1\overline{w_2})
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

In step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, replace
$$
|w_1|^2
$$
by
$$
c^2|w_2|^2.
$$
Then
$$
c^2|w_2|^2+c^4|w_2|^2
=
c^2\bigl(|w_2|^2+c^2|w_2|^2\bigr)
=
c^2\bigl(|w_2|^2+|w_1|^2\bigr).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
|w_1-c^2w_2|^2
=
c^2|w_1-w_2|^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The parenthesized expression in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is exactly
$$
|w_1-w_2|^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Taking nonnegative square roots,
$$
\boxed{
|w_1-c^2w_2|
=
c|w_1-w_2|}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $c>0$,
$$
\sqrt{c^2|w_1-w_2|^2}
=
c|w_1-w_2|.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The equation
$$
\left|
\frac{z-z_1}{z-z_2}
\right|
=
c
$$
is equivalent to
$$
\boxed{
|z-z_1|
=
c|z-z_2|}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The displayed quotient equation requires $z\ne z_2$. Multiply both sides
by the positive number $|z-z_2|$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Apply part (a) with
$$
w_1=z-z_1,
\qquad
w_2=z-z_2.
$$
Then every point of the locus satisfies
$$
|(z-z_1)-c^2(z-z_2)|
=
c|z_1-z_2|.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives the hypothesis
$$
|w_1|=c|w_2|.
$$
Also
$$
w_1-w_2
=
(z-z_1)-(z-z_2)
=
z_2-z_1,
$$
whose modulus is $|z_1-z_2|$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The left-hand side in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is
$$
\left|
(1-c^2)z-(z_1-c^2z_2)
\right|.
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand:
$$
(z-z_1)-c^2(z-z_2)
=
(1-c^2)z-z_1+c^2z_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Since $c\ne1$,
$$
1-c^2\ne0,
$$
so step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is equivalent to
$$
\boxed{
\left|
z-
\frac{z_1-c^2z_2}{1-c^2}
\right|
=
\frac{c|z_1-z_2|}{|1-c^2|}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Factor
$$
1-c^2
$$
from the expression in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and divide both sides by
$$
|1-c^2|.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Conversely, if step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} holds, then
$$
|z-z_1|
=
c|z-z_2|.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
w_1=z-z_1,
\qquad
w_2=z-z_2.
$$
Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is equivalent to
$$
|w_1-c^2w_2|
=
c|w_1-w_2|.
$$
Square both sides and subtract:
$$
0
=
|w_1-c^2w_2|^2
-
c^2|w_1-w_2|^2.
$$
A direct expansion gives
$$
0
=
(1-c^2)
\left(
|w_1|^2-c^2|w_2|^2
\right).
$$
Since
$$
c\ne1,
$$
one obtains
$$
|w_1|^2=c^2|w_2|^2.
$$
Taking nonnegative square roots yields
$$
|w_1|=c|w_2|.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore the locus is exactly the circle with center
$$
\boxed{
z_c
=
\frac{z_1-c^2z_2}{1-c^2}}
$$
and radius
$$
\boxed{
R
=
\frac{c|z_1-z_2|}{|1-c^2|}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} establish equivalence with the standard circle equation
$$
|z-z_c|=R.
$$
Because $z_1\ne z_2$ and $c>0$,
$$
R>0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives the center and radius in
part (b).
