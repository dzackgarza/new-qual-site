::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that if $\abs{w_{1}} = c\abs{w_{2}}$ where $c > 0$, then $\abs{w_{1} - c^{2}w_{2}} = c\abs{w_{1} - w_{2}}$.

(b) Prove that if $c > 0$, $c \neq 1$ and $z_{1} \neq z_{2}$, then $\abs{\frac{z - z_{1}}{z - z_{2}}} = c$ represents a circle.
Find its center and radius.
[Hint: an easy way is to use part (a).]
:::

::: {.solution}

## Part (a)

Assume
$$
\abs{w_1}=c\abs{w_2},
\qquad
c>0.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Squaring the hypothesis gives
$$
\boxed{\abs{w_1}^2=c^2\abs{w_2}^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both sides are nonnegative.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Expanding,
$$
\abs{w_1-c^2w_2}^2
=
\abs{w_1}^2
-2c^2\operatorname{Re}(w_1\overline{w_2})
+c^4\abs{w_2}^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Use
$$
\abs{a-b}^2
=
\abs{a}^2+\abs{b}^2-2\operatorname{Re}(a\overline b)
$$
with
$$
a=w_1,
\qquad
b=c^2w_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{w_1-c^2w_2}^2
=
c^2
\left(
\abs{w_1}^2+\abs{w_2}^2
-2\operatorname{Re}(w_1\overline{w_2})
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

In step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, replace
$$
\abs{w_1}^2
$$
by
$$
c^2\abs{w_2}^2.
$$
Then
$$
c^2\abs{w_2}^2+c^4\abs{w_2}^2
=
c^2\bigl(\abs{w_2}^2+c^2\abs{w_2}^2\bigr)
=
c^2\bigl(\abs{w_2}^2+\abs{w_1}^2\bigr).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\abs{w_1-c^2w_2}^2
=
c^2\abs{w_1-w_2}^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The parenthesized expression in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is exactly
$$
\abs{w_1-w_2}^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Taking nonnegative square roots,
$$
\boxed{
\abs{w_1-c^2w_2}
=
c\abs{w_1-w_2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $c>0$,
$$
\sqrt{c^2\abs{w_1-w_2}^2}
=
c\abs{w_1-w_2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The equation
$$
\abs{
\frac{z-z_1}{z-z_2}
}
=
c
$$
is equivalent to
$$
\boxed{
\abs{z-z_1}
=
c\abs{z-z_2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The displayed quotient equation requires $z\ne z_2$. Multiply both sides
by the positive number $\abs{z-z_2}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Apply part (a) with
$$
w_1=z-z_1,
\qquad
w_2=z-z_2.
$$
Then every point of the locus satisfies
$$
\abs{(z-z_1)-c^2(z-z_2)}
=
c\abs{z_1-z_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives the hypothesis
$$
\abs{w_1}=c\abs{w_2}.
$$
Also
$$
w_1-w_2
=
(z-z_1)-(z-z_2)
=
z_2-z_1,
$$
whose modulus is $\abs{z_1-z_2}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The left-hand side in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is
$$
\abs{
(1-c^2)z-(z_1-c^2z_2)
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand:
$$
(z-z_1)-c^2(z-z_2)
=
(1-c^2)z-z_1+c^2z_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Since $c\ne1$,
$$
1-c^2\ne0,
$$
so step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is equivalent to
$$
\boxed{
\abs{
z-
\frac{z_1-c^2z_2}{1-c^2}
}
=
\frac{c\abs{z_1-z_2}}{\abs{1-c^2}}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Factor
$$
1-c^2
$$
from the expression in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and divide both sides by
$$
\abs{1-c^2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Conversely, if step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} holds, then
$$
\abs{z-z_1}
=
c\abs{z-z_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
w_1=z-z_1,
\qquad
w_2=z-z_2.
$$
Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is equivalent to
$$
\abs{w_1-c^2w_2}
=
c\abs{w_1-w_2}.
$$
Square both sides and subtract:
$$
0
=
\abs{w_1-c^2w_2}^2
-
c^2\abs{w_1-w_2}^2.
$$
A direct expansion gives
$$
0
=
(1-c^2)
\left(
\abs{w_1}^2-c^2\abs{w_2}^2
\right).
$$
Since
$$
c\ne1,
$$
one obtains
$$
\abs{w_1}^2=c^2\abs{w_2}^2.
$$
Taking nonnegative square roots yields
$$
\abs{w_1}=c\abs{w_2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore the locus is exactly the circle with center
$$
\boxed{
z_c
=
\frac{z_1-c^2z_2}{1-c^2}}
$$
and radius
$$
\boxed{
R
=
\frac{c\abs{z_1-z_2}}{\abs{1-c^2}}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} establish equivalence with the standard circle equation
$$
\abs{z-z_c}=R.
$$
Because $z_1\ne z_2$ and $c>0$,
$$
R>0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives the center and radius in
part (b).
