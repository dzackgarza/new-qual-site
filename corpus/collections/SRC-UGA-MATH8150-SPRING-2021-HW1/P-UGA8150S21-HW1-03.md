::: {.problem}
Prove that the equation $z^3 + 2z + 4 = 0$ has its roots outside the unit circle.
[Hint: what is the maximum value of the modulus of the first two terms if $\abs{z} \le 1$?]
:::

::: {.solution}

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
$$
z^3+2z+4=0
$$
for some $z$ with
$$
\abs{z}\le1.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Rearranging the equation,
$$
\boxed{
z^3+2z=-4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Subtract $4$ from both sides.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\abs{z^3+2z}=4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take absolute values in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}:
$$
\abs{-4}=4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

By the triangle inequality,
$$
\abs{z^3+2z}
\le
\abs{z}^3+2\abs{z}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply
$$
\abs{a+b}\le\abs{a}+\abs{b}
$$
with
$$
a=z^3,
\qquad
b=2z.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Since $\abs{z}\le1$,
$$
\abs{z}^3\le1
$$
and
$$
2\abs{z}\le2.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $0\le r\le1$,
$$
r^3\le1
$$
and
$$
2r\le2.
$$
Set $r=\abs{z}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\abs{z^3+2z}
\le3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}:
$$
\abs{z^3+2z}
\le
1+2
=
3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} contradict one another.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{z^3+2z}=4,
$$
while step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{z^3+2z}\le3.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore no root of
$$
z^3+2z+4
$$
lies in the closed unit disk.


::: pf-proof

The assumption $\abs{z}\le1$ led to the contradiction in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence every root satisfies
$$
\boxed{\abs{z}>1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The complement of the closed unit disk is precisely the set
$$
\{z:\abs{z}>1\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
