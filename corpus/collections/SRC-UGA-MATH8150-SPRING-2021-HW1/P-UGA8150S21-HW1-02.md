::: {.problem}
Prove that $\abs{z_1 + z_2} \geq \abs{\abs{z_1} - \abs{z_2}}$ and explain when equality holds.
:::

::: {.remark title="Variations"}
The form $\abs{z-w} \geq \abs{\abs{z}-\abs{w}}$ is the case $z_1 = z$, $z_2 = -w$, since $\abs{-w} = \abs{w}$.
By the final step of the proof, equality holds exactly when $z$ and $w$ point in the same direction, allowing either one to be zero.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

By the triangle inequality,
$$
\abs{z_1}
=
\abs{(z_1+z_2)-z_2}
\le
\abs{z_1+z_2}+\abs{z_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the triangle inequality to the two complex numbers
$$
z_1+z_2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
-z_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Therefore
$$
\abs{z_1}-\abs{z_2}
\le
\abs{z_1+z_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Subtract $\abs{z_2}$ from both sides of step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Interchanging $z_1$ and $z_2$ gives
$$
\abs{z_2}-\abs{z_1}
\le
\abs{z_1+z_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} with the two variables reversed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Combining steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\boxed{
\abs{z_1+z_2}
\ge
\abs{\abs{z_1}-\abs{z_2}}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The two inequalities say that
$$
\abs{z_1+z_2}
$$
is at least both
$$
\abs{z_1}-\abs{z_2}
$$
and
$$
\abs{z_2}-\abs{z_1}.
$$
The maximum of these two numbers is
$$
\abs{\abs{z_1}-\abs{z_2}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Equality in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is equivalent to
$$
\boxed{
\abs{z_1+z_2}^2
=
\bigl(\abs{z_1}-\abs{z_2}\bigr)^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both sides of step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} are nonnegative, so equality is preserved by
squaring.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

One has
$$
\abs{z_1+z_2}^2
=
\abs{z_1}^2+\abs{z_2}^2
+
2\operatorname{Re}(z_1\overline{z_2}).
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand
$$
(z_1+z_2)(\overline{z_1}+\overline{z_2}).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Also,
$$
\bigl(\abs{z_1}-\abs{z_2}\bigr)^2
=
\abs{z_1}^2+\abs{z_2}^2
-
2\abs{z_1}\abs{z_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand the square.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore equality holds if and only if
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}(z_1\overline{z_2})
=
-\abs{z_1}\abs{z_2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compare the right-hand sides of steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and cancel the common
terms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

If both $z_1,z_2\ne0$, the condition in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} holds if and only
if $z_1$ and $z_2$ point in opposite directions.


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
z_1\overline{z_2}
=
\abs{z_1}\abs{z_2}e^{i\theta},
$$
where $\theta$ is the oriented angle from $z_2$ to $z_1$. Then
$$
\operatorname{Re}(z_1\overline{z_2})
=
\abs{z_1}\abs{z_2}\cos\theta.
$$
Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is therefore equivalent to
$$
\cos\theta=-1,
$$
hence
$$
\theta\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Equivalently, when both numbers are nonzero, equality holds exactly
when
$$
\boxed{
z_1=-t z_2
\quad\text{for some }t>0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Two nonzero complex numbers point in opposite directions exactly when their
ratio is a negative real number.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

If either $z_1=0$ or $z_2=0$, equality also holds.


::: pf-proof

If $z_2=0$, for example,
$$
\abs{z_1+z_2}
=
\abs{z_1}
=
\abs{\abs{z_1}-0}.
$$
The case $z_1=0$ is symmetric.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Hence equality holds exactly when the two complex numbers are
collinear with opposite directions, allowing either one to be zero.


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves the inequality and step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} gives the equality
criterion.

:::
