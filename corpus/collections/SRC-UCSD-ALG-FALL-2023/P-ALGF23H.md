::: problem
Suppose $p$ is a prime and $p \nmid n$.
Let $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ be an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$.
Suppose $\zeta \in \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p^{\times}$ is an element of (multiplicative) order $n$.

(a) Prove that $x^n - 1 = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(x - \zeta^i)$ in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$.

(b) Let $E_{n,p} \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ be a splitting field of $x^n - 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
Prove that $E_{n,p}/\mathbb{F}_p$ is a Galois extension and $\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ can be embedded into the group of automorphisms $\operatorname{Aut}(\langle \zeta \rangle)$ of the cyclic group $\langle \zeta \rangle$.

(c) Prove that $\operatorname{Gal}(E_{n,p}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is isomorphic to the cyclic subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$ which is generated by $p + n\mathbb{Z}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $E=E_{n,p}$ and $C=\langle\zeta\rangle$.

<1>1. The roots of $x^n-1$ are exactly the $n$ distinct elements of $C$, and $E=\mathbb{F}_p(\zeta)$.
::: {.proof}
The powers $1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}$ are distinct because $\zeta$ has order $n$.
Each is a root of $x^n-1$.
The factor theorem, applied to these $n$ distinct roots of a monic polynomial of degree $n$, gives
\[
x^n-1=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(x-\zeta^i).
\]
The field generated by all these roots is $\mathbb{F}_p(\zeta)$, proving both assertions and part (a).
:::

<1>2. The extension $E/\mathbb{F}_p$ is Galois, and restriction gives an injective homomorphism
\[
\rho:\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{F}_p)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(C).
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $E$ is the splitting field of a polynomial with distinct roots.
Thus the extension is finite, normal, and separable, hence Galois.
An $\mathbb{F}_p$-automorphism of $E$ permutes the roots of $x^n-1$ and respects multiplication, so it restricts to an automorphism of $C$.
Restriction respects composition.
If an automorphism restricts to the identity on $C$, it fixes $\zeta$ and $\mathbb{F}_p$, hence fixes $E=\mathbb{F}_p(\zeta)$.
Therefore $\rho$ is injective, proving part (b).
:::

<1>3. If $d=[E:\mathbb{F}_p]$, then the Frobenius map
\[
\varphi:E\longrightarrow E,\qquad a\longmapsto a^p,
\]
generates $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{F}_p)$ and has order $d$.
::: {.proof}
The field $E$ has $p^d$ elements, since it is a $d$-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
In characteristic $p$, the binomial theorem gives $(a+b)^p=a^p+b^p$.
Consequently $\varphi$ is a field homomorphism fixing $\mathbb{F}_p$.
It is injective and hence, on the finite set $E$, bijective.

The multiplicative group $E^{\times}$ has order $p^d-1$.
Lagrange's theorem gives $a^{p^d}=a$ for every $a\in E$, including $a=0$, so $\varphi^d$ is the identity.
If $1\le r<d$ and $\varphi^r$ were the identity, all $p^d$ elements of $E$ would be roots of the nonzero polynomial $x^{p^r}-x$ of degree $p^r<p^d$, a contradiction.
Thus $\varphi$ has order $d$.

Every $\mathbb{F}_p$-automorphism of $E=\mathbb{F}_p(\zeta)$ is determined by the image of $\zeta$, which must be a root of its minimal polynomial of degree $d$.
There are therefore at most $d$ such automorphisms.
The $d$ distinct powers of $\varphi$ exhaust them.
:::

<1>4. Under the usual identification $\operatorname{Aut}(C)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$, the image of $\rho$ is $\langle p+n\mathbb{Z}\rangle$.
::: {.proof}
For $n>1$, an automorphism of $C$ is uniquely determined by
\[
\zeta\longmapsto\zeta^a,
\qquad \gcd(a,n)=1.
\]
Indeed, $\zeta^a$ generates $C$ exactly when $\gcd(a,n)=1$, and composition of these automorphisms multiplies the exponents modulo $n$.
This proves the stated identification.
The Frobenius automorphism sends $\zeta$ to $\zeta^p$, and $p+n\mathbb{Z}$ is a unit because $p\nmid n$.
By <1>3, every element of the Galois group is a power of Frobenius, so its image is precisely $\langle p+n\mathbb{Z}\rangle$.
Together with the injectivity in <1>2, this proves part (c).

When $n=1$, we have $\zeta=1$ and $E=\mathbb{F}_p$.
Both the Galois group and the unit group of the one-element ring $\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}$ are trivial, so part (c) holds in this case as well.
:::
