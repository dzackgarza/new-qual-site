::: {.problem}
Prove that there is no one-to-one conformal map from the punctured disk
$$
\{z\in\CC:0<\abs{z}<1\}
$$
onto the annulus
$$
\{z\in\CC:1<\abs{z}<2\}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
This is the problem "Prove that there is no one-to-one conformal map of the punctured disk $\theset{0<\abs{z}<1}$ onto the annulus $\theset{1<\abs{w}<2}$", printed in the retained complex-analysis preliminary compilation (`assets/attachments/complex_prelim.pdf`) as the grouping of Fall 2011 problem 7 and Fall 2010 problem 3. The argument below is the card's own, and it is the argument the retained compilation gives.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #extension}
If $f\colon\DD\setminus\{0\}\to A=\theset{1<\abs{w}<2}$ is a conformal bijection, then $f$ extends to a holomorphic $g$ on $\DD$ with $g(\DD\setminus\{0\})=A$ and $g$ injective on $\DD\setminus\{0\}$.

::: pf-proof
$\abs{f(z)}<2$ on its domain, so $f$ is bounded near the punctured point $0$; the removable singularity theorem gives a holomorphic $g$ on $\DD$ agreeing with $f$ off $0$. Since $g$ agrees with $f$ on $\DD\setminus\{0\}$, it maps that punctured disc onto $A$ and is injective there, $f$ being injective.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #open}
$g(\DD)$ is open and $g(0)\in\overline{A}$; hence $g(0)\in A$.

::: pf-proof
$g$ is holomorphic and nonconstant, so $g(\DD)$ is open by the open mapping theorem. Since $g(\DD\setminus\{0\})=A$ and $g$ is continuous at $0$, $g(0)$ is a limit of points of $A$, that is $g(0)\in\overline{A}=\theset{1\leq\abs{w}\leq2}$. If $\abs{g(0)}=1$ or $\abs{g(0)}=2$, then the open set $g(\DD)$ contains $g(0)$ but no point of either circle, while $g(\DD)=A\cup\{g(0)\}\subset\overline{A}$; a point of $g(\DD)$ on the circle $\abs{w}=1$ would have a neighbourhood inside $g(\DD)$ crossing into $\abs{w}<1$, which is disjoint from $\overline{A}\supset g(\DD)$. So $g(0)\in A$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #preimage}
There is $z_{0}\in\DD\setminus\{0\}$ with $g(z_{0})=g(0)$.

::: pf-proof
$g(0)=\omega\in A=g(\DD\setminus\{0\})$, so $\omega=f(z_{0})=g(z_{0})$ for some $z_{0}\in\DD\setminus\{0\}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #contradiction}
The existence of such an $f$ is impossible.

::: pf-proof
Choose disjoint open discs $V,W\subset\DD$ with $z_{0}\in V$ and $0\in W$, possible since $z_{0}\neq0$. By the open mapping theorem $g(V)$ and $g(W)$ are open neighbourhoods of $\omega$, so their intersection contains a point $\eta\neq\omega$. Choose $v\in V$ and $w\in W$ with $g(v)=g(w)=\eta$. Since $\eta\neq\omega=g(0)$ we have $w\neq0$, and $v\neq0$ because $0\notin V$; so $v,w\in\DD\setminus\{0\}$ and $f(v)=f(w)=\eta$ with $v\neq w$, contradicting the injectivity of $f$. Hence no conformal bijection of the punctured disc onto $A$ exists.
:::
