::: {.problem}
Prove that there is no one-to-one conformal map from the punctured disk
\[
\{z\in\mathbb C:0<|z|<1\}
\]
onto the annulus
\[
\{z\in\mathbb C:1<|z|<2\}.
\]
:::


::: {.solution}
Assume, toward a contradiction, that
\[
f:\{0<|z|<1\}\longrightarrow\{1<|w|<2\}
\]
is a conformal bijection.

Because $|f(z)|<2$, the isolated singularity at $0$ is removable. Hence $f$ extends to a holomorphic function $g$ on the unit disk $\mathbb D$. Since $0$ is a limit point of $\mathbb D\setminus\{0\}$ and
\[
g(\mathbb D\setminus\{0\})=\{1<|w|<2\},
\]
we have
\[
g(0)\in\{1\le |w|\le 2\}.
\]
The function $g$ is nonconstant, so the open mapping theorem implies that $g(\mathbb D)$ is open. Therefore $g(0)$ cannot lie on either boundary circle $|w|=1$ or $|w|=2$; otherwise $g(\mathbb D)$, while contained in the closed annulus, would fail to contain a neighborhood of $g(0)$. Thus
\[
\omega:=g(0)\in\{1<|w|<2\}.
\]

Since $f$ is onto, there is some $z_0\in\mathbb D\setminus\{0\}$ with
\[
f(z_0)=\omega=g(0).
\]
Choose disjoint open disks $V,W\subset\mathbb D$ with $z_0\in V$ and $0\in W$. By the open mapping theorem, $g(V)$ and $g(W)$ are open neighborhoods of $\omega$. Hence their intersection contains some $\eta\ne\omega$. Choose $v\in V$ and $w\in W$ with
\[
g(v)=g(w)=\eta.
\]
Because $\eta\ne\omega=g(0)$, we have $w\ne0$; also $v\ne0$ since $0\notin V$. Therefore
\[
f(v)=f(w)=\eta,
\]
with $v\ne w$, contradicting the injectivity of $f$.

Hence no such conformal bijection exists.
:::
