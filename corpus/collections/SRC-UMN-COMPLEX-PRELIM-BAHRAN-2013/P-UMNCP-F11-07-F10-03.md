::: {.problem}
Prove that there is no one-to-one conformal map from the punctured disk
$$
\{z\in\CC:0<\abs{z}<1\}
$$
onto the annulus
$$
\{z\in\CC:1<\abs{z}<2\}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume, toward a contradiction, that
$$
f:\{0<\abs{z}<1\}\longrightarrow\{1<\abs{w}<2\}
$$
is a conformal bijection.

Because $\abs{f(z)}<2$, the isolated singularity at $0$ is removable.
Hence $f$ extends to a holomorphic function $g$ on the unit disk $\DD$.
Since $0$ is a limit point of $\DD\setminus\{0\}$ and
$$
g(\DD\setminus\{0\})=\{1<\abs{w}<2\},
$$
we have
$$
g(0)\in\{1\le \abs{w}\le 2\}.
$$
The function $g$ is nonconstant, so the open mapping theorem implies that $g(\DD)$ is open.
Therefore $g(0)$ cannot lie on either boundary circle $\abs{w}=1$ or $\abs{w}=2$; otherwise $g(\DD)$, while contained in the closed annulus, would fail to contain a neighborhood of $g(0)$.
Thus
$$
\omega:=g(0)\in\{1<\abs{w}<2\}.
$$

Since $f$ is onto, there is some $z_0\in\DD\setminus\{0\}$ with
$$
f(z_0)=\omega=g(0).
$$
Choose disjoint open disks $V,W\subset\DD$ with $z_0\in V$ and $0\in W$.
By the open mapping theorem, $g(V)$ and $g(W)$ are open neighborhoods of $\omega$.
Hence their intersection contains some $\eta\ne\omega$.
Choose $v\in V$ and $w\in W$ with
$$
g(v)=g(w)=\eta.
$$
Because $\eta\ne\omega=g(0)$, we have $w\ne0$; also $v\ne0$ since $0\notin V$.
Therefore
$$
f(v)=f(w)=\eta,
$$
with $v\ne w$, contradicting the injectivity of $f$.

Hence no such conformal bijection exists.
:::
