::: {.problem}
Let \(g:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\) be entire, and let \(k,n\) be integers such that
\[
\frac{g^{(n)}(z)}{2+|z|^k}
\]
is bounded on \(\mathbb C\). Prove that \(g\) is a polynomial and estimate its degree in terms of \(k\) and \(n\).
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
h(z)coloneqq g^{(n)}(z).
$$
The hypothesis means that there is a constant
$$
C>0
$$
such that
$$
|h(z)|
\le
C(2+|z|^k)
$$
for every
$$
z\in\CC.
$$

<1>1. For every
$$
R\ge1,
$$
one has
$$
\max_{|z|=R}|h(z)|
\le
3CR^k.
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
|z|=R\ge1,
$$
then
$$
2+|z|^k
=
2+R^k
\le
3R^k.
$$
Substitute this into the global growth estimate for $h$.
:::

<1>2. For every integer
$$
m>k,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{h^{(m)}(0)=0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Fix
$$
m>k.
$$
By Cauchy's estimate on the circle
$$
|z|=R,
$$
for every
$$
R\ge1,
$$
one has
$$
|h^{(m)}(0)|
\le
\frac{m!}{R^m}
\max_{|z|=R}|h(z)|.
$$
Using step <1>1,
$$
|h^{(m)}(0)|
\le
3Cm!R^{k-m}.
$$
Since
$$
k-m<0,
$$
the right-hand side tends to $0$ as
$$
R\to\infty.
$$
Therefore
$$
h^{(m)}(0)=0.
$$
:::

<1>3. The entire function $h$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k$.

::: {.proof}
The Taylor expansion of $h$ at the origin is
$$
h(z)
=
\sum_{m=0}^\infty
\frac{h^{(m)}(0)}{m!}z^m.
$$
By step <1>2,
$$
h^{(m)}(0)=0
$$
for every
$$
m>k.
$$
Hence the Taylor series terminates:
$$
h(z)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{k}
\frac{h^{(m)}(0)}{m!}z^m.
$$
Thus $h$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k$.
:::

<1>4. If
$$
g^{(n)}
$$
is a polynomial of degree at most $k$, then $g$ is a polynomial of degree at most
$$
n+k.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
g^{(n)}=h,
$$
step <1>3 gives
$$
g^{(n)}(z)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_jz^j
$$
for suitable coefficients $a_j$.

Integrating this identity $n$ times shows that $g$ is the sum of a polynomial of degree at most
$$
k+n
$$
and an arbitrary polynomial of degree at most
$$
n-1.
$$
Thus $g$ itself is a polynomial of degree at most $k+n$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
g\text{ is a polynomial and }
\deg g\le n+k.}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>3 and <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
