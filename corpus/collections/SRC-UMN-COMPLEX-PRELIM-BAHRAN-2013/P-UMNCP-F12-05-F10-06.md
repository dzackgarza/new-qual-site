::: {.problem}
Let $g:\CC\to\CC$ be entire, and let $k,n$ be integers such that
$$
\frac{g^{(n)}(z)}{2+\abs{z}^k}
$$
is bounded on $\CC$. Prove that $g$ is a polynomial and estimate its degree in terms of $k$ and $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
h(z)\coloneqq g^{(n)}(z).
$$
The hypothesis means that there is a constant
$$
C>0
$$
such that
$$
\abs{h(z)}
\le
C(2+\abs{z}^k)
$$
for every
$$
z\in\CC.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every
$$
R\ge1,
$$
one has
$$
\max_{\abs{z}=R}\abs{h(z)}
\le
3CR^k.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\abs{z}=R\ge1,
$$
then
$$
2+\abs{z}^k
=
2+R^k
\le
3R^k.
$$
Substitute this into the global growth estimate for $h$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every integer
$$
m>k,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{h^{(m)}(0)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Fix
$$
m>k.
$$
By Cauchy's estimate on the circle
$$
\abs{z}=R,
$$
for every
$$
R\ge1,
$$
one has
$$
\abs{h^{(m)}(0)}
\le
\frac{m!}{R^m}
\max_{\abs{z}=R}\abs{h(z)}.
$$
Using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{h^{(m)}(0)}
\le
3Cm!R^{k-m}.
$$
Since
$$
k-m<0,
$$
the right-hand side tends to $0$ as
$$
R\to\infty.
$$
Therefore
$$
h^{(m)}(0)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The entire function $h$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k$.


::: pf-proof

The Taylor expansion of $h$ at the origin is
$$
h(z)
=
\sum_{m=0}^\infty
\frac{h^{(m)}(0)}{m!}z^m.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
h^{(m)}(0)=0
$$
for every
$$
m>k.
$$
Hence the Taylor series terminates:
$$
h(z)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{k}
\frac{h^{(m)}(0)}{m!}z^m.
$$
Thus $h$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If
$$
g^{(n)}
$$
is a polynomial of degree at most $k$, then $g$ is a polynomial of degree at most
$$
n+k.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
g^{(n)}=h,
$$
step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
g^{(n)}(z)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_jz^j
$$
for suitable coefficients $a_j$.

Integrating this identity $n$ times shows that $g$ is the sum of a polynomial of degree at most
$$
k+n
$$
and an arbitrary polynomial of degree at most
$$
n-1.
$$
Thus $g$ itself is a polynomial of degree at most $k+n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
g\text{ is a polynomial and }
\deg g\le n+k.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
