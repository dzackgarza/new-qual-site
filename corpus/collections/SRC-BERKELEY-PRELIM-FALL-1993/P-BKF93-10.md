::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ be continuous. Let $A$ be the set of all real numbers $a$ for which there exists a sequence $x_n\to\infty$ such that
\[
f(x_n)\to a.
\]
Prove that if $a,b\in A$, then every point of the interval with endpoints $a$ and $b$ also belongs to $A$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

It is enough to consider the case
$$
a<b
$$
and a point
$$
c\in(a,b).
$$

::: pf-proof

If $a=b$, the interval with endpoints $a$ and $b$ is the singleton
$\{a\}\subseteq A$. If $a\neq b$, interchange the names of $a$ and $b$ if
necessary so that $a<b$. The endpoints already lie in $A$ by hypothesis, so
only the points strictly between them require proof.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

There are sequences $(u_n)$ and $(v_n)$ in $[0,\infty)$ such that
$$
u_n\geq n,
\qquad
v_n\geq n,
$$
and
$$
f(u_n)<c<f(v_n)
$$
for every $n$.

::: pf-proof

Because $a\in A$, choose a sequence $x_j\to\infty$ with
$f(x_j)\to a$. Since $a<c$, for every sufficiently large $j$ one has
$f(x_j)<c$. Since also $x_j\to\infty$, for each $n$ one may choose an index
$j_n$ large enough that
$$
x_{j_n}\geq n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f(x_{j_n})<c.
$$
Set $u_n\coloneqq x_{j_n}$.

Similarly, since $b\in A$ and $c<b$, choose $y_j\to\infty$ with
$f(y_j)\to b$ and, for each $n$, an index $k_n$ such that
$$
y_{k_n}\geq n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f(y_{k_n})>c.
$$
Set $v_n\coloneqq y_{k_n}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every $n$, there is $z_n\geq n$ such that
$$
f(z_n)=c.
$$

::: pf-proof

The function $f$ is continuous on the closed interval whose endpoints are
$u_n$ and $v_n$. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, its values at those endpoints lie on opposite
sides of $c$. The intermediate value theorem therefore gives a point $z_n$
between $u_n$ and $v_n$ with $f(z_n)=c$. Since both endpoints are at least
$n$, one has $z_n\geq n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The point $c$ belongs to $A$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives $z_n\geq n$, hence $z_n\to\infty$, while
$$
f(z_n)=c
$$
for every $n$. Thus $f(z_n)\to c$, which is exactly the condition
$c\in A$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, the endpoints need no proof, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives every strict
intermediate point. Hence the entire interval with endpoints $a$ and $b$ lies
in $A$.

:::
