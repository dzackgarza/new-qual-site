::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ be continuous and define
\[
h(z)=\int_0^1 f(t)\cos(zt)\,dt.
\]

1. Prove that $h$ is entire.
2. Prove that $h\equiv0$ only if $f\equiv0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
M\coloneqq\max_{0\leq t\leq1}\abs{f(t)}.
$$

<1>1. For every $z\in\CC$,
$$
h(z)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^m z^{2m}}{(2m)!}
\int_0^1 f(t)t^{2m}\,dt.
$$

::: {.proof}
The cosine power series gives
$$
\cos(zt)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^m z^{2m}t^{2m}}{(2m)!}.
$$
Fix $R>0$. For $\abs z\leq R$ and $0\leq t\leq1$, the absolute value of
the $m$th summand after multiplication by $f(t)$ is at most
$$
M\frac{R^{2m}}{(2m)!}.
$$
The series of these bounds converges. Hence the series for
$f(t)\cos(zt)$ converges uniformly for
$$
(z,t)\in\{z:\abs z\leq R\}\times[0,1].
$$
Termwise integration is therefore valid and gives the displayed expansion.
:::

<1>2. The function $h$ is entire.

::: {.proof}
Set
$$
\mu_{2m}\coloneqq\int_0^1 f(t)t^{2m}\,dt.
$$
Then
$$
\abs{\mu_{2m}}\leq M.
$$
Thus the series from step <1>1 is dominated in absolute value by
$$
M\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\abs{z}^{2m}}{(2m)!},
$$
which converges for every $z\in\CC$. It is therefore a power series with
infinite radius of convergence, so it defines an entire function. Step <1>1
identifies that function with $h$.
:::

<1>3. If $h\equiv0$, then
$$
\int_0^1 f(t)t^{2m}\,dt=0
$$
for every integer $m\geq0$.

::: {.proof}
If $h$ is identically zero, every coefficient of its power series at $0$
vanishes. By step <1>1, the coefficient of $z^{2m}$ is
$$
\frac{(-1)^m}{(2m)!}
\int_0^1f(t)t^{2m}\,dt.
$$
Hence every displayed moment is zero.
:::

<1>4. If $h\equiv0$, then
$$
\int_0^1 f(t)p(t^2)\,dt=0
$$
for every real polynomial $p$.

::: {.proof}
Every polynomial $p(t^2)$ is a finite linear combination of
$1,t^2,t^4,\ldots$. Apply linearity to the vanishing moments in step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. There is a sequence of real polynomials $(p_j)$ such that
$$
p_j(t^2)\longrightarrow f(t)
$$
uniformly for $t\in[0,1]$.

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
F(s)\coloneqq f(\sqrt{s}),
\qquad
0\leq s\leq1.
$$
The function $F$ is continuous. By the Weierstrass approximation theorem,
there are real polynomials $p_j$ converging uniformly to $F$ on $[0,1]$.
Substituting $s=t^2$ gives the claimed uniform convergence.
:::

<1>6. If $h\equiv0$, then $f\equiv0$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4,
$$
\int_0^1 f(t)p_j(t^2)\,dt=0
$$
for every $j$. Step <1>5 and boundedness of $f$ imply
$$
\int_0^1 f(t)p_j(t^2)\,dt
\longrightarrow
\int_0^1 f(t)^2\,dt.
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_0^1 f(t)^2\,dt=0.
$$
The continuous function $f^2$ is nonnegative, so a zero integral forces
$f(t)^2=0$ for every $t\in[0,1]$. Hence $f\equiv0$.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves part 1, and step <1>6 proves part 2.
:::
