::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a commutative integral domain with identity, and let $G$ be a finite subgroup of the unit group $R^*$. Prove that $G$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
K\coloneqq\Frac(R).
$$
Since $R$ is an integral domain, the natural map $R\hookrightarrow K$ is
injective, so regard $G$ as a finite subgroup of $K^\times$.

<1>1. If $e$ is the exponent of $G$, then $G$ contains an element of order
$e$.

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
e=\prod_{p}p^{a_p}.
$$
For each prime $p$ dividing $e$, the definition of the least common multiple
implies that some $g_p\in G$ has order divisible by $p^{a_p}$. If the order
of $g_p$ is
$$
p^{a_p}m_p
$$
with $p\nmid m_p$, then
$$
h_p\coloneqq g_p^{m_p}
$$
has order $p^{a_p}$.

Because $G$ is abelian, the elements $h_p$ commute. Their orders are pairwise
coprime, so
$$
h\coloneqq\prod_p h_p
$$
has order
$$
\prod_p p^{a_p}=e.
$$
:::

<1>2. Every element of $G$ is a root of
$$
t^e-1\in K[t].
$$

::: {.proof}
By definition of the exponent, the order of every $g\in G$ divides $e$.
Hence
$$
g^e=1
$$
for every $g\in G$.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\abs{G}\leq e.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, all $\abs{G}$ distinct elements of $G$ are roots in the field
$K$ of the nonzero polynomial $t^e-1$, which has degree $e$. A nonzero
polynomial over a field has at most as many roots as its degree, so
$\abs{G}\leq e$.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
e=\abs{G}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives an element $h\in G$ of order $e$. By Lagrange's theorem,
$$
e\mid\abs{G},
$$
so $e\leq\abs{G}$. Combined with step <1>3, this gives
$e=\abs{G}$.
:::

<1>5. The group $G$ is cyclic.

::: {.proof}
The element $h$ from step <1>1 has order $e$, and step <1>4 gives
$e=\abs{G}$. Therefore
$$
\abs{\langle h\rangle}=\abs{G},
$$
so $G=\langle h\rangle$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
