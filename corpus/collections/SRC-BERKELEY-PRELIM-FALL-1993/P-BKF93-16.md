::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be continuous on $[0,1]\times[0,1]$. Let $\mathcal F$ be the family of functions
\[
f(x)=\int_0^1 g(y)K(x,y)\,dy,
\]
where $g$ is continuous on $[0,1]$ and $|g(y)|\le1$ everywhere. Prove that $\mathcal F$ is equicontinuous.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{x-x'}<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\abs{K(x,y)-K(x',y)}<\varepsilon
$$
for all $x,x',y\in[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof

The function $K$ is continuous on the compact set $[0,1]\times[0,1]$, so it
is uniformly continuous. Apply uniform continuity to the two points
$(x,y)$ and $(x',y)$, whose distance is $\abs{x-x'}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

With $\delta$ as in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, every $f\in\mathcal F$ satisfies
$$
\abs{x-x'}<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\abs{f(x)-f(x')}<\varepsilon.
$$

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
f(x)=\int_0^1 g(y)K(x,y)\,dy
$$
with $\abs{g(y)}\leq1$. If $\abs{x-x'}<\delta$, then step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{f(x)-f(x')}
&=
\abs{\int_0^1
g(y)\bigl(K(x,y)-K(x',y)\bigr)\,dy}\\
&\leq
\int_0^1
\abs{g(y)}
\abs{K(x,y)-K(x',y)}\,dy\\
&<
\int_0^1\varepsilon\,dy\\
&=
\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same $\delta$ works for every admissible $g$ and hence for every
$f\in\mathcal F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The family $\mathcal F$ is equicontinuous.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} is precisely the uniform-in-$f$ $\varepsilon$--$\delta$ condition
for equicontinuity on $[0,1]$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves the claim.

:::
