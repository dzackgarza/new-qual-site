::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(z)=z^{-2}(1-z^2)^{-1}e^z.
$$
Evaluate
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz,
$$
The depicted curve has a counterclockwise outer loop around $-1$ and $0$,
a counterclockwise inner loop around $0$, and a clockwise loop around $1$.
Thus its winding numbers at $-1$, $0$, and $1$ are respectively $1$, $2$,
and $-1$.

The figure appears in [de Souza and Silva, *Berkeley Problems in
Mathematics*, Problem 5.11.17 (Fa93), p. 81](https://hoclieuxanh.com/download-file/61e72d2e-93b4-4bce-ba30-e7a6c03ac0eb/Barkaley%20Problems%20%28chap%201%20_%207%29.pdf#page=86).
The retained exam PDF lacks it.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The function
$$
f(z)=\frac{e^z}{z^2(1-z^2)}
$$
has singularities only at $0$, $1$, and $-1$, with
$$
\Res_{z=0}f(z)=1,
\qquad
\Res_{z=1}f(z)=-\frac e2,
\qquad
\Res_{z=-1}f(z)=\frac{e^{-1}}2.
$$

::: pf-proof

At $0$ there is a double pole. Writing
$$
f(z)=\frac1{z^2}\frac{e^z}{1-z^2},
$$
its residue is
$$
\evaluated{\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{e^z}{1-z^2}\right)}_{z=0}
=1.
$$

The poles at $1$ and $-1$ are simple. Since
$$
1-z^2=-(z-1)(z+1),
$$
one obtains
$$
\Res_{z=1}f(z)
=
\lim_{z\to1}
\frac{-e^z}{z^2(z+1)}
=
-\frac e2.
$$
Similarly, using $1-z^2=(1-z)(z+1)$,
$$
\Res_{z=-1}f(z)
=
\lim_{z\to-1}
\frac{e^z}{z^2(1-z)}
=
\frac{e^{-1}}2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The figure gives
$$
\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(-1)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(0)=2,\qquad
\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(1)=-1.
$$
Consequently,
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz
=
\boxed{2+\frac{e+e^{-1}}2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The residue theorem with winding numbers gives
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz
=
\sum_{a\in\{0,1,-1\}}
\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(a)\Res_{z=a}f(z).
$$
Substituting the three residues from step [](#s1){.pf-ref} and the
winding numbers read from the figure gives
$$
2\cdot1+(-1)\left(-\frac e2\right)
+1\cdot\frac{e^{-1}}2
=2+\frac{e+e^{-1}}2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

The integral is $2+\frac{e+e^{-1}}2$.
