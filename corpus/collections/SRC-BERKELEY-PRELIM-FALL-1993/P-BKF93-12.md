::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=z^{-2}(1-z^2)^{-1}e^z.
\]
Evaluate
\[
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz,
\]
where $\gamma$ is the curve depicted in Problem 12 of the Berkeley Fall 1993 source exam.

The retained extraction does not preserve that curve.
:::

::: {.solution}
The retained source PDF itself does not contain the curve $\gamma$: where
the figure should occur, it prints "../Fig/Pr/Fa93-12.ps not found". Thus the
retained source does not determine the path, its orientation, or its winding
data, so no unique numerical value can be recovered. The singularity data can
nevertheless be determined exactly.

<1>1. The function
$$
f(z)=\frac{e^z}{z^2(1-z^2)}
$$
has singularities only at $0$, $1$, and $-1$, with
$$
\Res_{z=0}f(z)=1,
\qquad
\Res_{z=1}f(z)=-\frac e2,
\qquad
\Res_{z=-1}f(z)=\frac{e^{-1}}2.
$$

::: {.proof}
At $0$ there is a double pole. Writing
$$
f(z)=\frac1{z^2}\frac{e^z}{1-z^2},
$$
its residue is
$$
\left.\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{e^z}{1-z^2}\right)\right|_{z=0}
=1.
$$

The poles at $1$ and $-1$ are simple. Since
$$
1-z^2=-(z-1)(z+1),
$$
one obtains
$$
\Res_{z=1}f(z)
=
\lim_{z\to1}
\frac{-e^z}{z^2(z+1)}
=
-\frac e2.
$$
Similarly, using $1-z^2=(1-z)(z+1)$,
$$
\Res_{z=-1}f(z)
=
\lim_{z\to-1}
\frac{e^z}{z^2(1-z)}
=
\frac{e^{-1}}2.
$$
:::

<1>2. If the missing curve $\gamma$ is closed, piecewise smooth, and avoids
$0,1,-1$, then
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz
=
\boxed{
\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(0)
-\frac e2\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(1)
+\frac{e^{-1}}2\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(-1)
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The residue theorem with winding numbers gives
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz
=
\sum_{a\in\{0,1,-1\}}
\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(a)\Res_{z=a}f(z).
$$
Substituting the three residues from step <1>1 gives the displayed formula.
:::

<1>3. A unique numerical value cannot be determined from the retained
Berkeley Fall 1993 source.

::: {.proof}
The integral depends on the missing geometric data of $\gamma$. Even in the
closed-curve case, step <1>2 depends on the three winding numbers, and the
retained PDF supplies none of them. The source therefore does not determine a
single numerical answer.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 determines all residue data recoverable from the printed integrand,
step <1>2 gives the exact closed-contour formula, and step <1>3 records the
unrecoverable dependence on the missing figure.
:::
:::
