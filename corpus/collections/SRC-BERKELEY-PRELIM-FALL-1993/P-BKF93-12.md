::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=z^{-2}(1-z^2)^{-1}e^z.
\]
Evaluate
\[
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz,
\]
where $\gamma$ is the curve depicted in Problem 12 of the Berkeley Fall 1993 source exam.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The function
$$
f(z)=\frac{e^z}{z^2(1-z^2)}
$$
has singularities only at $0$, $1$, and $-1$, with
$$
\Res_{z=0}f(z)=1,
\qquad
\Res_{z=1}f(z)=-\frac e2,
\qquad
\Res_{z=-1}f(z)=\frac{e^{-1}}2.
$$

::: pf-proof

At $0$ there is a double pole. Writing
$$
f(z)=\frac1{z^2}\frac{e^z}{1-z^2},
$$
its residue is
$$
\left.\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{e^z}{1-z^2}\right)\right|_{z=0}
=1.
$$

The poles at $1$ and $-1$ are simple. Since
$$
1-z^2=-(z-1)(z+1),
$$
one obtains
$$
\Res_{z=1}f(z)
=
\lim_{z\to1}
\frac{-e^z}{z^2(z+1)}
=
-\frac e2.
$$
Similarly, using $1-z^2=(1-z)(z+1)$,
$$
\Res_{z=-1}f(z)
=
\lim_{z\to-1}
\frac{e^z}{z^2(1-z)}
=
\frac{e^{-1}}2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $\gamma$ is closed, piecewise smooth, and avoids
$0,1,-1$, then
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz
=
\boxed{
\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(0)
-\frac e2\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(1)
+\frac{e^{-1}}2\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(-1)
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The residue theorem with winding numbers gives
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz
=
\sum_{a\in\{0,1,-1\}}
\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(a)\Res_{z=a}f(z).
$$
Substituting the three residues from step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives the integral in terms of the winding numbers
$\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(0)$, $\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(1)$, and
$\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(-1)$ of $\gamma$ about the three poles of $f$.

:::
