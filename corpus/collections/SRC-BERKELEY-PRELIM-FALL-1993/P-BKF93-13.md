::: {.problem}
Show that there are at least two nonisomorphic nonabelian groups of order $40$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Let
$$
G\coloneqq C_{20}\rtimes C_2,
$$
where the nonidentity element of $C_2$ acts on $C_{20}$ by inversion. Then
$G$ is a nonabelian group of order $40$.

::: pf-proof

Write
$$
C_{20}=\langle a\rangle,
\qquad
C_2=\langle s\rangle,
$$
with
$$
sas^{-1}=a^{-1}.
$$
The inversion map is an automorphism of $C_{20}$ of order $2$, so the
semidirect product is defined and has
$$
\abs{G}=20\cdot2=40.
$$
Since $a$ has order $20$, one has $a\neq a^{-1}$, and therefore
$sa\neq as$. Thus $G$ is nonabelian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The group $G$ has exactly $21$ elements of order $2$.

::: pf-proof

Inside the cyclic subgroup $\langle a\rangle$, the unique involution is
$a^{10}$. Every element outside that subgroup has the form $a^is$, and
$$
(a^is)^2
=
a^is a^is
=
a^ia^{-i}
=1.
$$
The $20$ elements $a^is$ are distinct and nonidentity. Hence $G$ has
$20+1=21$ involutions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Let
$$
H\coloneqq C_5\rtimes C_8,
$$
where a generator of $C_8$ acts on $C_5$ by inversion. Then $H$ is a
nonabelian group of order $40$.

::: pf-proof

Write
$$
C_5=\langle c\rangle,
\qquad
C_8=\langle b\rangle,
$$
and define the action by
$$
bcb^{-1}=c^{-1}.
$$
Since inversion has order $2$, the assignment of the generator $b$ to
inversion defines a homomorphism $C_8\to\Aut(C_5)$. Hence the semidirect
product exists and has
$$
\abs{H}=5\cdot8=40.
$$
Because $c\neq c^{-1}$, the relation $bcb^{-1}=c^{-1}$ shows that $H$ is
nonabelian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The group $H$ has exactly one element of order $2$.

::: pf-proof

Every element of $H$ has a unique form
$$
c^i b^j,
\qquad
0\leq i<5,
\quad
0\leq j<8.
$$
The relation $bcb^{-1}=c^{-1}$ gives
$$
(c^ib^j)^2
=
c^{\,i(1+(-1)^j)}b^{2j}.
$$
If $j$ is odd, then $b^{2j}\neq1$, so the square is not $1$. If $j$ is
even, the square equals
$$
c^{2i}b^{2j}.
$$
For this to be $1$, one must have $j=0$ or $j=4$ and $i=0$. The case
$(i,j)=(0,0)$ is the identity, while $(i,j)=(0,4)$ gives the involution
$b^4$. Thus $b^4$ is the unique involution in $H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The groups $G$ and $H$ are not isomorphic.

::: pf-proof

An isomorphism preserves element orders and hence preserves the number of
involutions. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $G$ has $21$ involutions, whereas by step [](#s4){.pf-ref},
$H$ has exactly one. Therefore $G\not\cong H$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} construct two nonabelian groups of order $40$, and step
[](#s5){.pf-ref} proves that they are nonisomorphic.

:::
