::: {.problem}
Let $n>1$ be an integer. Is there a differentiable function
\[
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R
\]
such that
\[
f'=f^n
\]
and $f(0)>0$?
:::

::: {.solution}
No such function exists.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If such a function existed and
$$
a\coloneqq f(0)>0,
$$
then
$$
f(t)>0
$$
for every $t\geq0$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose instead that $f$ vanishes somewhere. Since $f(0)>0$ and $f$ is
continuous, let $\tau>0$ be the first zero. Then
$$
f(t)>0
\qquad
0\leq t<\tau.
$$
On this interval,
$$
f'(t)=f(t)^n>0,
$$
so $f$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\tau)$. Hence
$$
f(t)\geq f(0)=a>0
$$
there, and continuity gives $f(\tau)\geq a>0$, contradicting
$f(\tau)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $t\geq0$,
$$
f(t)^{1-n}
=
a^{1-n}-(n-1)t.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $f(t)>0$, so $f^{1-n}$ is differentiable. The chain rule and
the differential equation give
$$
\frac{d}{dt}f(t)^{1-n}
=(1-n)f(t)^{-n}f'(t)
=
1-n.
$$
Integrating from $0$ to $t$ yields the displayed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The identity in step [](#s2){.pf-ref} is impossible on all of $[0,\infty)$.

::: pf-proof

Take
$$
T\coloneqq\frac{a^{1-n}}{n-1}>0.
$$
Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
f(T)^{1-n}=0.
$$
But step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives $f(T)>0$, so $f(T)^{1-n}>0$. This is a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} contradicts the existence of the required differentiable function.
Therefore the answer is no.

:::
