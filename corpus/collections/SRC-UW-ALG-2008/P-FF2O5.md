::: {.problem}
- Let $R$ be a commutative principal ideal domain.
  Show that any $R$-module $M$ generated by two elements takes the form $R/(a)\oplus R/(b)$ for some $a,b\in R$.
  What more can you say about $a$ and $b$?

- Give a necessary and sufficient condition for two direct sums as in part (a) to be isomorphic as $R$-modules.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. There is a surjective homomorphism
\[
\pi:R^2\twoheadrightarrow M.
\]
::: {.proof}
Choose generators $m_1,m_2$ of $M$ and define
\[
\pi(r,s)=rm_1+sm_2.
\]
By the choice of $m_1,m_2$, this map is surjective.
:::

<1>2. If $K=\ker\pi$, then
\[
M\cong R^2/K,
\]
and $K$ is a finitely generated free $R$-module of rank at most $2$.
::: {.proof}
The first isomorphism theorem gives $M\cong R^2/K$. Since $R$ is a PID, every submodule of the free module $R^2$ is free of rank at most $2$. In particular $K$ is finitely generated.
:::

<1>3. There are bases of $K$ and $R^2$ for which the inclusion $K\hookrightarrow R^2$ has Smith normal form
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
 d_1&0\\
 0&d_2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad d_1\mid d_2,
\]
where zero diagonal entries are allowed.
::: {.proof}
Apply Smith normal form over the PID $R$ to a matrix representing the inclusion $K\hookrightarrow R^2$. Since the rank of $K$ is at most $2$, after adjoining zero columns or diagonal entries as necessary, the cokernel is represented by a diagonal $2\times2$ matrix with invariant factors $d_1,d_2$ satisfying $d_1\mid d_2$. A zero invariant factor records a free summand, using $R/(0)=R$.
:::

<1>4. Consequently
\[
M\cong R/(d_1)\oplus R/(d_2),
\qquad d_1\mid d_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, $M$ is the cokernel of the inclusion $K\hookrightarrow R^2$. Replacing a presentation matrix on the left and right by invertible matrices does not change its cokernel up to isomorphism. Thus <1>3 gives
\[
M\cong\operatorname{coker}\!\begin{pmatrix}d_1&0\\0&d_2\end{pmatrix}
\cong R/(d_1)\oplus R/(d_2).
\]
:::

<1>5. The invariant factors $d_1,d_2$ in <1>4 are unique up to multiplication by units of $R$.
::: {.proof}
Uniqueness is the uniqueness part of Smith normal form over a PID: once the diagonal entries are arranged so that
\[
d_1\mid d_2,
\]
the principal ideals $(d_1)$ and $(d_2)$ are determined by the cokernel. Equivalently, replacing either $d_i$ by an associate does not change $R/(d_i)$, and these are the only ambiguities in invariant-factor form.
:::

<1>6. Hence, if
\[
d_1\mid d_2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
e_1\mid e_2,
\]
then
\[
R/(d_1)\oplus R/(d_2)
\cong
R/(e_1)\oplus R/(e_2)
\]
if and only if $d_1$ is associate to $e_1$ and $d_2$ is associate to $e_2$.
::: {.proof}
If corresponding invariant factors are associates, the corresponding cyclic quotients are isomorphic, so the direct sums are isomorphic. Conversely, if the direct sums are isomorphic, uniqueness in <1>5 forces their invariant factors to agree up to associates.
:::

<1>7. For arbitrary $a,b\in R$, the direct sum
\[
R/(a)\oplus R/(b)
\]
has invariant factors obtained from the Smith normal form of $\operatorname{diag}(a,b)$.
More explicitly, one may choose $d_1,d_2$ with
\[
(d_1)=(a,b),
\qquad
d_1\mid d_2,
\qquad
d_1d_2\sim ab,
\]
where $\sim$ denotes association and the conventions $d_2=0$ when exactly one of $a,b$ is zero and $d_1=d_2=0$ when both are zero are understood.
::: {.proof}
For a $2\times2$ diagonal presentation matrix, the first determinantal divisor is a generator of the ideal generated by its $1\times1$ minors, namely
\[
(a,b).
\]
The product of the two invariant factors is associate to the determinant $ab$. These two determinantal divisors determine the Smith normal form. The zero cases give the stated free summands directly.
:::

<1>8. Therefore two arbitrary sums
\[
R/(a)\oplus R/(b)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
R/(c)\oplus R/(d)
\]
are isomorphic if and only if their two Smith invariant factors, computed as in <1>7, agree pairwise up to associates.
:::
