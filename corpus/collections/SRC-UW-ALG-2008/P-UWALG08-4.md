::: {.problem}
By looking at degrees of polynomials, show that any $\CC$-subalgebra of the ring $\CC[x]$ of polynomials in one variable over the complex field $\CC$ is finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $A\subseteq\CC[x]$ be a $\CC$-subalgebra.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $A\subseteq\CC$ then $A=\CC$ is generated by $1$.

::: {.pf-proof}

$A$ is a $\CC$-subspace of $\CC$, and $1\in A$, so $A=\CC$; and $\CC=\CC[1]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Otherwise, let $d=\min\{\deg f: f\in A,\ f\notin\CC\}>0$ and choose $f\in A$ with $\deg f=d$; then $\CC[x]$ is a free $\CC[f]$-module with basis $1,x,\dots,x^{d-1}$.

::: {.pf-proof}

The set is nonempty because $A$ contains some nonconstant polynomial, and its degrees are positive integers, so the minimum exists and is $\ge1$.

Write $f=cx^d+g$ with $\deg g<d$ and $c\ne0$. Given $h\in\CC[x]$, the division algorithm on degrees in steps of $d$ writes
$$
h=qf+r,\qquad q\in\CC[x],\quad \deg r<d,
$$
by induction on $\deg h$: subtract a suitable multiple of $f$ to lower the degree by $d$ each time. So $\CC[x]=(\CC[f])\cdot\{1,x,\dots,x^{d-1}\}$.

For linear independence over $\CC[f]$: suppose $\sum_{i<d}p_i(x)f(x)^i=0$ with $p_i\in\CC[f]$, polynomials in $f$, and suppose some $p_i\ne0$; let $i_0$ be the largest index with $p_{i_0}\ne0$. The term $p_{i_0}f^{i_0}$ has degree $\deg p_{i_0}+i_0d$, and all other nonzero terms have degree $\deg p_i+id<\deg p_{i_0}+i_0 d$ because $\deg p_i$ is finite and $i<i_0$; more precisely the top-degree coefficient of the sum is the leading coefficient of $p_{i_0}f^{i_0}$, which is nonzero, so the sum cannot vanish. Hence $1,x,\dots,x^{d-1}$ is a $\CC[f]$-basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$A$ is a free $\CC[f]$-module of rank $r\le d$, and $A=\CC[f,g_1,\dots,g_r]$ for a $\CC[f]$-basis $g_1,\dots,g_r$.

::: {.pf-proof}

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $\CC[x]$ is free of rank $d$ over $\CC[f]$. Since $f\in A$, the ring $\CC[f]$ is a subring of $A$, so $A$ is a $\CC[f]$-submodule of $\CC[x]$.

The ring $\CC[f]$ is a polynomial ring in one variable over a field, hence a principal ideal domain. A submodule of a free module of finite rank over a PID is free of rank at most that rank, so $A$ is a free $\CC[f]$-module of some rank $r\le d$.

Let $g_1,\dots,g_r$ be a $\CC[f]$-basis of $A$. Every element of $A$ is $\sum p_i(f)g_i$ with $p_i\in\CC[f]$, hence lies in the $\CC$-algebra generated by $f,g_1,\dots,g_r$; and conversely that algebra is contained in $A$ because $A$ is closed under multiplication and contains $f,g_1,\dots,g_r$. So
$$
A=\CC[f,g_1,\dots,g_r].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Conclusion: every $\CC$-subalgebra of $\CC[x]$ is finitely generated, by at most $d+1$ elements.

::: {.pf-proof}

In the case of step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $A=\CC[1]$. Otherwise step [](#s3){.pf-ref} exhibits $A$ as generated by the $r+1\le d+1$ elements $f,g_1,\dots,g_r$. Either way $A$ is a finitely generated $\CC$-algebra.

The bound comes from the degrees and from nothing else: the minimal degree $d$ of a nonconstant element controls the rank of $\CC[x]$ over $\CC[f]$, and hence the number of generators needed.

:::
