::: {.problem}
(a) Let $A\subseteq\RR$ be nonempty and bounded below. Prove
$$
\inf A=-\sup(-A)
$$
and
$$
\inf A=\sup\{b:b\text{ is a lower bound for }A\}.
$$

(b) Let $A,B\subseteq\RR$ be nonempty and bounded above. Prove that $A\subseteq B$ implies $\sup A\le\sup B$, and that if $\sup A<\sup B$, then some $b\in B$ is an upper bound for $A$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

A real number $m$ is a lower bound for $A$ if and only if $-m$ is an
upper bound for
$$
-A\coloneqq\{-a:a\in A\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The statement
$$
m\leq a
$$
for every $a\in A$ is equivalent, after multiplying by $-1$, to
$$
-a\leq -m
$$
for every $a\in A$.  This is exactly the assertion that $-m$ is an upper
bound for $-A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\boxed{\inf A=-\sup(-A)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
s=\sup(-A).
$$
Since $s$ is an upper bound for $-A$, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that $-s$ is a lower
bound for $A$.

If $m$ is any lower bound for $A$, then step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that $-m$ is an upper
bound for $-A$.  By the least-upper-bound property of $s$,
$$
s\leq -m.
$$
Multiplying by $-1$ gives
$$
m\leq -s.
$$
Thus $-s$ is the greatest lower bound of $A$, so
$$
\inf A=-s=-\sup(-A).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let
$$
L=\{b\in\RR:b\text{ is a lower bound for }A\}.
$$
Then $\inf A$ is an upper bound for $L$.


::: pf-proof

Every $b\in L$ is a lower bound for $A$.  Since $\inf A$ is the greatest
lower bound,
$$
b\leq\inf A.
$$
Hence $\inf A$ is an upper bound for $L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The number $\inf A$ belongs to $L$.


::: pf-proof

By definition, $\inf A$ is itself a lower bound for $A$.  Therefore
$\inf A\in L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

One has
$$
\boxed{
\inf A
=
\sup\{b:b\text{ is a lower bound for }A\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $\inf A$ is an upper bound for $L$.  By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$\inf A\in L$.  Therefore no smaller number can be an upper bound for $L$.
Hence
$$
\sup L=\inf A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} are the two required identities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If $A\subseteq B$, then
$$
\boxed{\sup A\leq\sup B}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $\sup B$ is an upper bound for $B$, it is also an upper bound for the
subset $A$.  Since $\sup A$ is the least upper bound for $A$,
$$
\sup A\leq\sup B.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Suppose
$$
\sup A<\sup B.
$$
Then there exists $b\in B$ such that
$$
b>\sup A.
$$


::: pf-proof

If every $b\in B$ satisfied $b\leq\sup A$, then $\sup A$ would be an upper
bound for $B$.  By the least-upper-bound property this would imply
$$
\sup B\leq\sup A,
$$
contrary to the hypothesis.  Hence some $b\in B$ satisfies
$b>\sup A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The element $b$ from step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is an upper bound for $A$.


::: pf-proof

For every $a\in A$,
$$
a\leq\sup A<b.
$$
Hence $a\leq b$ for every $a\in A$, so $b$ is an upper bound for $A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves the first assertion, and steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} prove the second.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::
