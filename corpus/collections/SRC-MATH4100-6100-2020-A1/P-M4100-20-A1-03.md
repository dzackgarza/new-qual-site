::: {.problem}
(a) For $A,B\subseteq\RR$, verify
$$
(A\cap B)^c=A^c\cup B^c,
\qquad
(A\cup B)^c=A^c\cap B^c.
$$

(b) Use induction to prove the finite-union version
$$
(A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_n)^c=A_1^c\cap\cdots\cap A_n^c.
$$
Explain why induction alone does not establish the corresponding infinite-union identity, and then prove or disprove
$$
\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right)^c
=
\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty A_n^c.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For subsets $A,B\subseteq\RR$,
$$
(A\cap B)^c=A^c\cup B^c.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $x\in\RR$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
x\in(A\cap B)^c
&\iff x\notin A\cap B\\
&\iff (x\notin A)\text{ or }(x\notin B)\\
&\iff x\in A^c\cup B^c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the two sets have exactly the same elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For subsets $A,B\subseteq\RR$,
$$
(A\cup B)^c=A^c\cap B^c.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $x\in\RR$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
x\in(A\cup B)^c
&\iff x\notin A\cup B\\
&\iff (x\notin A)\text{ and }(x\notin B)\\
&\iff x\in A^c\cap B^c.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} are the two required identities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every $n\geq1$,
$$
\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^n A_k\right)^c
=
\bigcap_{k=1}^n A_k^c.
$$


::: pf-proof

Proceed by induction on $n$.

For $n=1$, both sides equal $A_1^c$.

Assume the identity holds for some $n$. Then by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n+1}A_k\right)^c
&=
\left(
\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}A_k\right)
\cup A_{n+1}
\right)^c\\
&=
\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}A_k\right)^c
\cap A_{n+1}^c\\
&=
\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}A_k^c\right)
\cap A_{n+1}^c\\
&=
\bigcap_{k=1}^{n+1}A_k^c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the result holds for $n+1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Induction alone does not prove the infinite-union identity.


::: pf-proof

Induction proves a statement $P(n)$ for each finite integer $n$. The expression
$$
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n
$$
is not the union corresponding to any finite stage $P(n)$, so no finite number
of induction steps reaches the countable union. A separate argument is
required.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The infinite De Morgan identity
$$
\boxed{
\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\right)^c
=
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n^c
}
$$
is true.


::: pf-proof

Let $x\in\RR$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
x\in
\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\right)^c
&\iff
x\notin\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\\
&\iff
x\notin A_n\text{ for every }n\\
&\iff
x\in A_n^c\text{ for every }n\\
&\iff
x\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n^c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the two sets are equal.

:::
