::: {.problem}
Let $a_n\to a$.

(a) Prove that if $a_n\le b$ for every $n$, then $a\le b$.

(b) Prove that if $(a_n)$ is increasing, then $a_n\le a$ for every $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $a_n\leq b$ for every $n$, then
$$
\boxed{a\leq b}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose instead that $a>b$.  Set
$$
\varepsilon=\frac{a-b}{2}>0.
$$
Since $a_n\to a$, there exists $N$ such that for every $n\geq N$,
$$
|a_n-a|<\varepsilon.
$$
Hence
$$
a_n>a-\varepsilon
=
\frac{a+b}{2}
>
b,
$$
contradicting the hypothesis $a_n\leq b$.  Therefore $a\leq b$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Fix $k\in\NN$.  If $(a_n)$ is increasing, then for every $n\geq k$,
$$
-a_n\leq -a_k.
$$


::: pf-proof

Monotonicity gives
$$
a_k\leq a_n
$$
whenever $n\geq k$. Multiplying by $-1$ reverses the inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The tail sequence $(-a_n)_{n\geq k}$ converges to $-a$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $a_n\to a$, for all sufficiently large $n$,
$$
|a_n-a|<\varepsilon.
$$
But
$$
|-a_n-(-a)|=|a_n-a|,
$$
so $-a_n\to-a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every $k$,
$$
a_k\leq a.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the convergent tail sequence $(-a_n)_{n\geq k}$ is bounded above
by $-a_k$.  Applying step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} to that tail and using step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
-a\leq -a_k.
$$
Multiplying by $-1$ yields
$$
a_k\leq a.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

The index $k$ in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} was arbitrary.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
