::: {.problem}
Use the triangle inequality to prove that for $x,y\in\mathbb R$,
\[
\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|\le |x-y|.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. One has
$$
|x|-|y|\le |x-y|.
$$

::: {.proof}
By the triangle inequality,
$$
|x|
=|(x-y)+y|
\le |x-y|+|y|.
$$
Subtracting $|y|$ from both sides gives
$$
|x|-|y|\le |x-y|.
$$
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
|y|-|x|\le |x-y|.
$$

::: {.proof}
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ in step <1>1 gives
$$
|y|-|x|\le |y-x|.
$$
Since $|y-x|=|x-y|$, the desired inequality follows.
:::

<1>3. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|\le |x-y|}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 give
$$
-|x-y|
\le
|x|-|y|
\le
|x-y|.
$$
Taking absolute values yields the stated reverse triangle inequality.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
