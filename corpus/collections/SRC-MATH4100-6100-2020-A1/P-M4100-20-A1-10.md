::: {.problem}
(a) Determine convergence or divergence of
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt n),
\qquad
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt n}{n},
\]
and
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(n^{1/n}-1\right)^n.
\]

(b) Let $a_n>0$. Prove that if $\lim na_n$ exists and is nonzero, then $\sum a_n$ diverges, while if $\lim n^2a_n$ exists, then $\sum a_n$ converges.

(c) If $a_n>0$ and $\sum a_n$ converges, prove that both
\[
\sum a_n^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum\frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n}
\]
converge.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt n)
$$
diverges.

::: {.proof}
Its $N$th partial sum telescopes:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt n)
&=
(\sqrt2-1)+(\sqrt3-\sqrt2)+\cdots+(\sqrt{N+1}-\sqrt N)\\
&=
\sqrt{N+1}-1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $\sqrt{N+1}-1\to\infty$, the series diverges.
:::

<1>2. The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt n}{n}
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
Rationalizing the numerator gives
$$
\frac{\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt n}{n}
=
\frac{1}{n(\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt n)}.
$$
Since
$$
\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt n\geq2\sqrt n,
$$
one has
$$
0<
\frac{\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt n}{n}
\leq
\frac{1}{2n^{3/2}}.
$$
The $p$-series $\sum n^{-3/2}$ converges, so the comparison test proves the
claim.
:::

<1>3. The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n^{1/n}-1)^n
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
c_n=(n^{1/n}-1)^n.
$$
Then
$$
c_n^{1/n}=n^{1/n}-1.
$$
Since
$$
n^{1/n}\longrightarrow1,
$$
one has
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_n^{1/n}=0<1.
$$
The root test therefore implies convergence.
:::

<1>4. Part (a) follows.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 determine the three series respectively as divergent,
convergent, and convergent.
:::

<1>5. Let $a_n>0$ and suppose
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}na_n=L
$$
exists and is nonzero. Then $L>0$.

::: {.proof}
Each $na_n$ is positive, so its finite limit satisfies $L\geq0$. Since the
limit is nonzero, $L>0$.
:::

<1>6. Under the hypotheses of step <1>5,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n
$$
diverges.

::: {.proof}
Since $na_n\to L>0$, there exists $N$ such that for $n\geq N$,
$$
na_n>\frac L2.
$$
Hence
$$
a_n>\frac{L}{2n}
$$
for $n\geq N$. The harmonic series diverges, so comparison gives divergence
of $\sum a_n$.
:::

<1>7. Let $a_n>0$ and suppose
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^2a_n=L
$$
exists. Then
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
Convergence of $n^2a_n$ implies that this sequence is bounded. Thus there are
$M>0$ and $N$ such that
$$
n^2a_n\leq M
$$
for every $n\geq N$. Hence
$$
0<a_n\leq\frac{M}{n^2}.
$$
Since $\sum n^{-2}$ converges, the comparison test shows that the tail
$\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_n$ converges. Adding finitely many initial terms does
not affect convergence.
:::

<1>8. Part (b) follows.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 proves the first assertion and step <1>7 proves the second.
:::

<1>9. If $a_n>0$ and $\sum a_n$ converges, then
$$
a_n\longrightarrow0.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the necessary term test for convergence of a series.
:::

<1>10. Under the hypotheses of step <1>9,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n^2
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>9, there exists $N$ such that
$$
0<a_n<1
$$
for every $n\geq N$. Hence
$$
0<a_n^2\leq a_n
$$
for $n\geq N$. Since $\sum a_n$ converges, comparison gives convergence of
$\sum a_n^2$.
:::

<1>11. For every $n$,
$$
\frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n}
\leq
\frac12\left(a_n+\frac1{n^2}\right).
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply
$$
2\sqrt{uv}\leq u+v
$$
with
$$
u=a_n,
\qquad
v=\frac1{n^2}.
$$
Then
$$
2\frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n}
\leq
a_n+\frac1{n^2}.
$$
:::

<1>12. Under the hypotheses of step <1>9,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n}
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>11,
$$
0<
\frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n}
\leq
\frac12a_n+\frac1{2n^2}.
$$
Both series on the right converge, so the comparison test proves convergence.
:::

<1>13. Part (c) follows.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>10 and <1>12 prove the two requested convergence statements.
:::

<1>14. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4, <1>8, and <1>13 establish parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
:::
:::
