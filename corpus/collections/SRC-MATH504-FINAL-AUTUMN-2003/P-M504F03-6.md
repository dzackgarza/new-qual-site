::: {.problem}
Let $K/F$ be a field extension, and fix $a \in K$.
Show that $F(a)$ is algebraic over $F$ if and only if $[F(a) : F]$ is finite.
Use this to prove that sums, differences, products, and quotients of algebraic elements are algebraic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose that $F(a)/F$ is algebraic.
Then $a$ is algebraic over $F$, so it satisfies a monic polynomial
$$
g(x) = x^n + b_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \cdots + b_1 x + b_0 \in F[x].
$$
Thus $a^n$ is an $F$-linear combination of $1, a, \ldots, a^{n-1}$, and by induction, so is $a^m$ for every $m \ge n$.
Hence $F[a]$ is spanned by $1, a, \ldots, a^{n-1}$.
Since $F[a]$ is a finite-dimensional domain over $F$, it is a field, so $F[a] = F(a)$ and $[F(a):F] \le n$.
(Alternatively, if $g$ is the minimal polynomial of $a$, then $F(a) \cong F[x]/(g(x))$, an $n$-dimensional vector space over $F$.)

Conversely, suppose that $[F(a):F] = d$ is finite, and let $b \in F(a)$.
The $d+1$ elements $1, b, \ldots, b^d$ cannot be linearly independent over $F$, so there are $c_0, \ldots, c_d \in F$, not all zero, with
$$
c_d b^d + c_{d-1} b^{d-1} + \cdots + c_1 b + c_0 = 0.
$$
Thus $b$ is a root of the nonzero polynomial $c_d x^d + \cdots + c_0 \in F[x]$, so $b$ is algebraic over $F$.
Since $b \in F(a)$ was arbitrary, $F(a)$ is algebraic over $F$.

Now let $a, b \in K$ be algebraic over $F$.
We claim that $[F(a,b):F]$ is finite.
First, $F(a,b) = F(a)(b)$.
The element $b$ is a root of a nonzero polynomial with coefficients in $F \subseteq F(a)$, so $b$ is algebraic over $F(a)$, and by the first part $[F(a)(b):F(a)]$ is finite.
Likewise $[F(a):F]$ is finite.
By the tower law,
$$
[F(a,b):F] = [F(a,b):F(a)]\,[F(a):F]
$$
is finite.
For every $c \in F(a,b)$, the degree $[F(c):F]$ divides $[F(a,b):F]$, hence is finite, so $c$ is algebraic over $F$ by the first part.
The field $F(a,b)$ contains $a+b$, $a-b$, $ab$ and, if $b \ne 0$, $a/b$, so all of these elements are algebraic over $F$.
:::
