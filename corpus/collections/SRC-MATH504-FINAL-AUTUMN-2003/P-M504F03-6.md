::: {.problem}
Let $K/F$ be a field extension, and fix $a \in K$.
Show that $F(a)$ is algebraic over $F$ if and only if $[F(a) : F]$ is finite.
Use this to prove that sums, differences, products, and quotients of algebraic elements are algebraic.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: an algebraic element spans a finite-dimensional $F[a]$, a finite-dimensional domain is a field, and the tower law puts $F(a,b)$ in finite degree.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #algebraicfinite}

If $a$ is algebraic over $F$ then $[F(a):F]$ is finite.

::: pf-proof

Let $g(x)=x^{n}+b_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{0}\in F[x]$ be monic with $g(a)=0$. Then $a^{n}=-(b_{n-1}a^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{0})$ lies in the $F$-span of $1,a,\dots,a^{n-1}$, and by induction so does $a^{m}$ for every $m\ge n$: if $a^{m}$ does, then $a^{m+1}=a\cdot a^{m}$ lies in the span of $a,a^{2},\dots,a^{n}$, hence of $1,\dots,a^{n-1}$ after replacing $a^{n}$. So $F[a]=\operatorname{span}_{F}\{1,a,\dots,a^{n-1}\}$, of dimension at most $n$. $F[a]$ is a domain, being a subring of the field $K$, so multiplication by a nonzero element of $F[a]$ is an injective $F$-linear endomorphism of a finite-dimensional space, hence surjective; therefore every nonzero element of $F[a]$ is invertible in $F[a]$ and $F[a]$ is a field. A field containing $F$ and $a$ contains $F(a)$, so $F[a]=F(a)$ and $[F(a):F]\le n<\infty$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #finitealgebraic}

If $[F(a):F]=d$ is finite then every $b\in F(a)$ is algebraic over $F$.

::: pf-proof

The $d+1$ elements $1,b,b^{2},\dots,b^{d}$ in the $d$-dimensional $F$-space $F(a)$ are linearly dependent, so there are $c_{0},\dots,c_{d}\in F$, not all zero, with $c_{0}+c_{1}b+\cdots+c_{d}b^{d}=0$. The polynomial $c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{d}x^{d}$ is nonzero and has $b$ as a root, so $b$ is algebraic over $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #iff}

$F(a)$ is algebraic over $F$ if and only if $[F(a):F]$ is finite.

::: pf-proof

If $F(a)$ is algebraic then $a\in F(a)$ is algebraic, so step [](#algebraicfinite){.pf-ref} gives finiteness. If the degree is finite, step [](#finitealgebraic){.pf-ref} applied to each $b\in F(a)$ makes $F(a)$ algebraic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #closure}

Sums, differences, products and quotients of elements algebraic over $F$ are algebraic over $F$.

::: pf-proof

Let $a,b\in K$ be algebraic. Then $b$ is a root of a nonzero polynomial over $F\subseteq F(a)$, so $b$ is algebraic over $F(a)$ and $[F(a)(b):F(a)]$ is finite by step [](#iff){.pf-ref}; and $[F(a):F]$ is finite. By the tower law applied to $F\subseteq F(a)\subseteq F(a)(b)=F(a,b)$, the degree $[F(a,b):F]$ is the product of two finite numbers, hence finite. Every $c\in F(a,b)$ then has finite degree over $F$ by step [](#iff){.pf-ref} again, so is algebraic. Since $a+b$, $a-b$, $ab$ lie in $F(a,b)$, and $a/b$ too whenever $b\ne0$:
$$
\boxed{F(a)\ \text{algebraic}\iff[F(a):F]<\infty;\quad \text{algebraic elements form a subfield of } K.}
$$

:::
