::: {.problem}
Assume that $G$ is a group of order $231 = 3 \times 7 \times 11$. Show that $G$ contains a normal Sylow 7-subgroup and a central Sylow 11-subgroup.
:::

::: {.solution}
By the Sylow theorems, $n_3 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $n_3 \mid 77$, so $n_3 \in \{1, 7\}$.
Next, $n_7 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$ and $n_7 \mid 33$, so $n_7 = 1$.
Finally, $n_{11} \equiv 1 \pmod{11}$ and $n_{11} \mid 21$, so $n_{11} = 1$.
Thus $G$ has a normal Sylow $7$-subgroup and a normal Sylow $11$-subgroup.

Let $P$ be the normal Sylow $11$-subgroup.
Then $P \cong C_{11}$; let $x$ be a generator.
To show that $P$ is central, it suffices to show that $x$ commutes with every element of $G$.
Since $P$ is normal, conjugation $y \mapsto gyg^{-1}$ restricts to an automorphism of $P$ for each $g \in G$, and this defines a group homomorphism
$$
G \longrightarrow \Aut(C_{11}) \cong C_{10}.
$$
The order of the image divides both $10$ and $\abs{G} = 231$, so the image is trivial.
That is, $gxg^{-1} = x$ for all $g \in G$, so $x$ is central, and hence $P$ is central.
:::
