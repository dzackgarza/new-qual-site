::: {.problem}
Let $p$ be a prime and $n$ a positive integer.
Use the fundamental theorem of Galois theory and facts about the extension $\mathbf{F}_{p^n}/\mathbf{F}_p$ to classify the subfields of $\mathbf{F}_{p^n}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this problem: the collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so the argument is the card's own. Let $p$ be a prime, $n\ge1$, and $K=\FF_{p^n}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #galois}
$K/\FF_p$ is Galois with cyclic Galois group of order $n$, generated by the Frobenius $\varphi(x)=x^p$.

::: pf-proof
$K$ is the splitting field over $\FF_p$ of $x^{p^n}-x$, whose roots are exactly the elements of $K$ and are all distinct since the derivative $p^nx^{p^n-1}=-x^{p^n-1}$ shares no root with it; a splitting field of a polynomial with distinct roots over a finite field is separable, so $K/\FF_p$ is Galois. Every $\FF_p$-automorphism of $K$ is a power of $\varphi$: it fixes $\FF_p$ and sends a root of $x^{p^n}-x$ to a root of the same polynomial, and $\varphi$ has order exactly $n$ because $\varphi^n=\mathrm{id}$ and no smaller power fixes all of $K$. Hence $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\FF_p)=\langle\varphi\rangle\cong C_n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subgroups}
The subgroups of $C_n=\langle\varphi\rangle$ are exactly $\langle\varphi^m\rangle$ for $m\mid n$, of order $n/m$.

::: pf-proof
A cyclic group of order $n$ has exactly one subgroup of each order dividing $n$, namely the set of elements whose order divides that number; in the generator $\varphi$ the subgroup of order $n/m$ is $\langle\varphi^{m}\rangle$. So the subgroups correspond bijectively to the divisors $m$ of $n$, the index being $m$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fixed}
The fixed field of $H=\langle\varphi^m\rangle$ is a subfield of order $p^m$, and every subfield of $K$ arises this way.

::: pf-proof
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, $H\mapsto K^H$ is a bijection from subgroups of the Galois group to intermediate fields, with $[K:K^H]=\abs{H}$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(K/K^H)=H$. For $H=\langle\varphi^m\rangle$ of order $n/m$ this gives $[K:K^H]=n/m$, hence $\abs{K^H}=p^{n/(n/m)}=p^m$ because $\abs{K}=p^n$ and degrees multiply. Conversely every intermediate field $F$ has $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ a subgroup, so $F=K^H$ for that $H$; and since every subfield of $K$ contains $1$ and hence $\FF_p$, the intermediate fields are exactly all the subfields.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The subfields of $\FF_{p^n}$ are exactly the fields $\FF_{p^m}$ for $m\mid n$, one for each divisor.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#fixed){.pf-ref} gives, for each divisor $m$ of $n$, a subfield of $K$ with $p^m$ elements, and says that every subfield arises so. A subfield with $p^m$ elements is isomorphic to $\FF_{p^m}$ by uniqueness of the finite field of a given order, and as a subset of $K$ it is that copy. So the subfields are exactly the $\FF_{p^m}$ with $m\mid n$, and there are as many as there are divisors of $n$.
:::
