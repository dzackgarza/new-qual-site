::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field and let $p(x)$ be an irreducible polynomial in $F[x]$.
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $p(x)$.
Show that the Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ acts transitively on the roots of $p(x)$.
(For full credit, do this without using the fundamental theorem of Galois theory.)
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix an algebraic closure $\overline F$ containing $K$, and let $\alpha,\beta\in K$ be roots of $p$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

There is an $F$-isomorphism
$$
\varphi:F(\alpha)\longrightarrow F(\beta)
$$
such that $\varphi(\alpha)=\beta$.


::: pf-proof

Because $p$ is irreducible and has both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ as roots, it is the minimal polynomial over $F$ of each root. Evaluation therefore gives $F$-isomorphisms
$$
F[x]/(p)\xrightarrow{\sim}F(\alpha),
\qquad
F[x]/(p)\xrightarrow{\sim}F(\beta),
$$
sending the class of $x$ to $\alpha$ and $\beta$, respectively. Composing one with the inverse of the other gives $\varphi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map $\varphi$ extends to an $F$-embedding
$$
\sigma:K\longrightarrow\overline F
$$
with $\sigma(\alpha)=\beta$.


::: pf-proof

The extension $K/F(\alpha)$ is algebraic. By the standard extension theorem for embeddings into an algebraically closed field, the embedding $\varphi:F(\alpha)\to\overline F$ extends to an embedding $\sigma:K\to\overline F$. This is the same extension-of-embeddings theorem used in the proof of [[PR-OZYUC|the characterization of normal algebraic extensions]]. Since $\varphi$ fixes $F$, so does $\sigma$, and $\sigma(\alpha)=\varphi(\alpha)=\beta$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The embedding $\sigma$ is an element of $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$.


::: pf-proof

The field $K$ is the splitting field over $F$ of the nonzero polynomial $p$, so $K/F$ is finite and normal by [[PR-TZN4M|the splitting-field characterization of finite normal extensions]]. Hence [[PR-OZYUC]] gives
$$
\sigma(K)=K.
$$
Thus $\sigma:K\to K$ is an $F$-automorphism, i.e. $\sigma\in G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The action of $G$ on the roots of $p$ is $\boxed{\text{transitive}}$.


::: pf-proof

The roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$ were arbitrary. By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, there is $\sigma\in G$ with $\sigma(\alpha)=\beta$. Hence every root lies in the $G$-orbit of every other root.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves the required transitivity without using the fundamental theorem of Galois theory.

:::
