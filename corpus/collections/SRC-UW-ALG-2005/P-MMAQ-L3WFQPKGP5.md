::: {.problem}
For any group $G$ we define $\Omega(G)$ to be the image of the group homomorphism $\rho:G\rightarrow\Aut(G)$ where $\rho$ maps $g\in G$ to the conjugation automorphism $x\mapsto gxg\inv$.
Starting with a group $G_0$, we define $G_1=\Omega(G_0)$ and $G_{i+1}=\Omega(G_i)$ for all $i\geq 0$.
If $G_0$ is of order $p^e$ for a prime $p$ and integer $e\geq 2$, prove that $G_{e-1}$ is the trivial group.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For every group $G$,
\[
\Omega(G)\cong G/Z(G).
\]
::: {.proof}
The homomorphism
\[
\rho:G\longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(G),
\qquad
\rho(g)(x)=gxg^{-1},
\]
has image $\Omega(G)$ by definition. Its kernel consists exactly of the elements commuting with every element of $G$, hence is $Z(G)$. The first isomorphism theorem gives
\[
\Omega(G)\cong G/Z(G).
\]
:::

<1>2. If $H$ is a finite $p$-group of order $p^m$, then either $\Omega(H)=1$ or
\[
|\Omega(H)|\le p^{m-2}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $\Omega(H)\cong H/Z(H)$. Since $H$ is a finite $p$-group, $Z(H)$ is nontrivial whenever $H$ is nontrivial, so $|H/Z(H)|\le p^{m-1}$.

It remains to exclude the case $|H/Z(H)|=p$. If $H/Z(H)$ were cyclic, say generated by $hZ(H)$, then every element of $H$ would have the form $h^r z$ with $z\in Z(H)$. Thus any two elements $h^r z$ and $h^s z'$ commute, so $H$ would be abelian. Then $Z(H)=H$, making $H/Z(H)$ trivial, a contradiction. Therefore a nontrivial quotient $H/Z(H)$ cannot have order $p$. Since its order is a power of $p$, a nontrivial $\Omega(H)$ has order at most $p^{m-2}$.
:::

<1>3. For every $i\ge0$, either $G_i=1$ or
\[
|G_i|\le p^{e-2i}.
\]
::: {.proof}
We use induction on $i$. For $i=0$, $|G_0|=p^e$. Suppose the claim holds for $i$. If $G_i=1$, then $G_{i+1}=\Omega(G_i)=1$. Otherwise $G_i$ is a finite $p$-group of order at most $p^{e-2i}$. Applying <1>2,
\[
G_{i+1}=1
\quad\text{or}\quad
|G_{i+1}|\le p^{e-2i-2}=p^{e-2(i+1)}.
\]
This proves the induction step.
:::

<1>4. The group $G_{e-1}$ is trivial.
::: {.proof}
If $G_{e-1}$ were nontrivial, then <1>3 would give
\[
1<|G_{e-1}|\le p^{e-2(e-1)}=p^{2-e}\le1,
\]
because $e\ge2$. This is impossible. Hence $G_{e-1}=1$.
:::
