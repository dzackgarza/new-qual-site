::: problem
Let $U$ be an infinite-dimensional vector space over a field $k$, $f: U\to U$ a linear map, and $\theset{u_1, \cdots, u_m} \subset U$ vectors such that $U$ is generated by the vectors $u_1,\ldots,u_m$ and $f^d(u_1),\ldots,f^d(u_m)$ for all $d\in\mathbb N$.

Prove that $U$ can be written as a direct sum $U \cong V\oplus W$ such that

1. $V$ has a basis consisting of some vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ and $f^d(v_1),\ldots,f^d(v_n)$ for all $d\in\mathbb N$, and

2. $W$ is finite-dimensional.

Moreover, prove that for any other decomposition $U \cong V' \oplus W'$, one has $W' \cong W$.
:::


::: solution
The two occurrences of $d$ in the source are quantified by $d\in\mathbb N$; thus the hypothesis says that $U$ is generated by finitely many vectors under all iterates of $f$.

Let $R=k[t]$ and make $U$ an $R$-module by
\[
t\cdot u=f(u).
\]
The hypothesis says exactly that $U$ is a finitely generated $R$-module, generated by $u_1,\ldots,u_m$.
Since $R=k[t]$ is a PID, the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID gives
\[
U\cong R^n\oplus T,
\]
where $T$ is the torsion submodule and is a finite direct sum of modules of the form $R/(p_j(t))$ with $p_j\ne0$.

Let $V\cong R^n$ be the free summand and choose an $R$-basis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ of $V$. Then, as a $k$-vector space,
\[
V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n k[t]v_i,
\]
and each $k[t]v_i$ has the $k$-basis
\[
v_i,f(v_i),f^2(v_i),\ldots.
\]
Thus $V$ has the required basis consisting of the iterates $f^d(v_i)$ for $d\in\mathbb N$.

Set $W=T$. Since
\[
T\cong\bigoplus_j R/(p_j(t)),
\]
and
\[
\dim_k R/(p_j)=\deg p_j<\infty,
\]
the space $W$ is finite-dimensional. This proves the existence of a decomposition
\[
U\cong V\oplus W
\]
with the stated properties.

However, the final uniqueness assertion is false as printed if $V$ and $W$ are required only to be vector subspaces. Take
\[
U=k[t],\qquad f(q(t))=tq(t).
\]
Then $U$ is infinite-dimensional and generated by $1$ under the iterates of $f$. We have one valid decomposition
\[
U=U\oplus0.
\]
But we also have
\[
U=tk[t]\oplus k\cdot1.
\]
The subspace $tk[t]$ has the orbit basis
\[
t,t^2,t^3,\ldots,
\]
so it satisfies condition (1), while $k\cdot1$ is one-dimensional. Hence the two finite-dimensional complements have dimensions $0$ and $1$ and are not isomorphic.

A standard corrected uniqueness statement is obtained by requiring the summands to be $f$-invariant, equivalently $k[t]$-submodules. In that case every finite-dimensional $f$-invariant submodule is torsion, while the free summand has no nonzero torsion. Therefore in any decomposition
\[
U=V'\oplus W'
\]
with $V'$ free and $W'$ finite-dimensional and $f$-invariant, the torsion submodule of $U$ is exactly $W'$. Hence
\[
W'=T=W
\]
(as $k[t]$-modules, in particular as vector spaces), so the corrected uniqueness claim holds.
:::
