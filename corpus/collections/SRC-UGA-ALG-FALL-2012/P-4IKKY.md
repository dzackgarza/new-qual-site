::: {.problem}
Let $U$ be an infinite-dimensional vector space over a field $k$, $f: U\to U$ a linear map, and $\theset{u_1, \cdots, u_m} \subset U$ vectors such that $U$ is generated by the vectors $u_1,\ldots,u_m$ and $f^d(u_1),\ldots,f^d(u_m)$ for all $d\in\NN$.

Prove that $U$ can be written as a direct sum $U = V\oplus W$ of $f$-invariant subspaces such that

1. $V$ has a basis consisting of some vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ and $f^d(v_1),\ldots,f^d(v_n)$ for all $d\in\NN$, and

2. $W$ is finite-dimensional.

Moreover, prove that for any other such decomposition $U = V' \oplus W'$ into $f$-invariant subspaces, one has $W' \cong W$.

:::

::: {.solution}

Equip $U$ with its $k[t]$-module structure given by $t\cdot u=f(u)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The module $U$ is finitely generated over the PID $k[t]$.


::: pf-proof

The hypothesis says that the iterates $f^d(u_i)$ span $U$ as a $k$-vector space. Equivalently, the finitely many $u_i$ generate $U$ as a $k[t]$-module. Since $k[t]$ is Euclidean, it is a PID.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There exists a decomposition $U=V\oplus W$ into $f$-invariant subspaces with $V$ possessing the prescribed orbit basis and $W$ finite-dimensional over $k$.


::: pf-proof

By the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} yields
\[
U\cong k[t]^n\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^s k[t]/(p_j(t)),
\]
where each $p_j$ is nonzero. Take $V$ to be the image of the free summand and $W$ the torsion summand under such an isomorphism. Both are $k[t]$-submodules, hence $f$-invariant. If $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ are the images of its standard free generators, then $f^d(v_i)=t^dv_i$ form a $k$-basis of $V$ as $i$ and $d\geq0$ vary. Also
\[
\dim_k W=\sum_{j=1}^s\deg p_j<\infty.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

In every such decomposition the finite-dimensional summand is the torsion submodule of $U$.


::: pf-proof

An $f$-invariant subspace is a $k[t]$-submodule. Every element of a finite-dimensional $k[t]$-module is annihilated by a nonzero polynomial, by linear dependence of its iterates, so that module is torsion. If $V'$ is $f$-invariant with $k$-basis $v_i, f^d(v_i)$, then $V'$ is a free $k[t]$-module on $v_1,\ldots,v_n$, and a free $k[t]$-module has no nonzero torsion elements. Hence in any $k[t]$-module direct-sum decomposition into a free summand and a finite-dimensional summand, the latter coincides with the torsion submodule of $U$. It is therefore determined intrinsically.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves the existence assertion, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows $W'=W$ for any other decomposition, so in particular $W'\cong W$.

:::
