::: {.problem}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $k$ and $T:V\to V$ a linear transformation.

a. Provide a definition for the *minimal polynomial* in $k[x]$ for $T$.

b. Define the *characteristic polynomial* for $T$.

c. Prove the Cayley-Hamilton theorem: the linear transformation $T$ satisfies its characteristic polynomial.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #part-a-min-poly-def}
Part (a): Definition of the minimal polynomial:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
The evaluation homomorphism $\operatorname{ev}_T: k[x] \to \operatorname{End}_k(V)$ given by $p(x) \mapsto p(T)$ is a ring homomorphism.
Its kernel $\operatorname{Ann}(T) = \{p(x) \in k[x] \mid p(T) = 0\}$ is a non-zero ideal of $k[x]$ (since $\dim_k \operatorname{End}_k(V) = n^2 < \infty$).

::: pf-proof
finite-dimensionality of endomorphism ring.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since $k[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, $\operatorname{Ann}(T)$ is generated by a unique monic polynomial.
The **minimal polynomial** $m_T(x) \in k[x]$ is this unique monic generator of lowest positive degree that annihilates $T$.

::: pf-proof
PID property of polynomial rings over fields.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b-char-poly-def}
Part (b): Definition of the characteristic polynomial:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Choose an ordered basis $\mathcal{B}$ for $V$ and let $A = [T]_\mathcal{B} \in M_n(k)$ be the matrix representation of $T$, where $n = \dim_k V$.
The **characteristic polynomial** $p_T(x) \in k[x]$ is defined by:
\[
p_T(x) = \det(x I_n - A).
\]

::: pf-proof
definition of characteristic polynomial.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $\mathcal{B}'$ is another basis with change-of-basis matrix $P$, then $[T]_{\mathcal{B}'} = P A P^{-1}$, and:
\[
\det(x I_n - P A P^{-1}) = \det(P(x I_n - A)P^{-1}) = \det(P)\det(x I_n - A)\det(P)^{-1} = \det(x I_n - A).
\]
Thus $p_T(x)$ is independent of the choice of basis.

::: pf-proof
multiplicativity of determinant.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-c-cayley-hamilton}
Part (c): Proof of the Cayley–Hamilton Theorem ($p_T(T) = 0$):

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Working over the commutative polynomial ring $R = k[x]$, the classical adjugate formula gives:
\[
\operatorname{adj}(x I_n - A) \cdot (x I_n - A) = \det(x I_n - A) \cdot I_n = p_T(x) I_n.
\]

::: pf-proof
Cramer’s Rule / adjugate property over commutative rings.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Each entry of $\operatorname{adj}(x I_n - A)$ is an $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ cofactor of $x I_n - A$, which is a polynomial in $x$ of degree at most $n-1$.
Thus $\operatorname{adj}(x I_n - A)$ can be expressed uniquely as a matrix polynomial:
\[
\operatorname{adj}(x I_n - A) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} x^j C_j = C_0 + x C_1 + \cdots + x^{n-1} C_{n-1},
\]
for matrices $C_0, C_1, \ldots, C_{n-1} \in M_n(k)$.

::: pf-proof
collecting powers of $x$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Writing $p_T(x) = \sum_{j=0}^n a_j x^j$ (where $a_n = 1$), the identity becomes:
\[
\left( \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} x^j C_j \right) (x I_n - A) = \sum_{j=0}^n a_j x^j I_n.
\]
Equating coefficients of each power $x^j$ in $M_n(k)$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
x^0 &: -C_0 A = a_0 I_n, \\
x^1 &: C_0 - C_1 A = a_1 I_n, \\
x^2 &: C_1 - C_2 A = a_2 I_n, \\
&\;\,\vdots \\
x^{n-1} &: C_{n-2} - C_{n-1} A = a_{n-1} I_n, \\
x^n &: C_{n-1} = a_n I_n.
\end{aligned}
\]

::: pf-proof
equality of polynomial coefficients in $M_n(k)[x]$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Multiplying the $j$-th equation on the right by $A^j$ and summing from $j = 0$ to $n$:
\[
p_T(A) = \sum_{j=0}^n a_j A^j = (-C_0 A) + (C_0 A - C_1 A^2) + (C_1 A^2 - C_2 A^3) + \cdots + (C_{n-2} A^{n-1} - C_{n-1} A^n) + C_{n-1} A^n.
\]
The right-hand side telescopes completely to $0$.
Therefore $p_T(A) = 0$, which implies $p_T(T) = 0$.

::: pf-proof
telescoping sum of matrices.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Conclusion:
$T$ satisfies its characteristic polynomial: $p_T(T) = 0$. Q.E.D.
