::: {.problem}
Let $f(x) = x^7 - 3\in \QQ[x]$ and $E/\QQ$ be a splitting field of $f$ with $\alpha \in E$ a root of $f$.

a. Show that $E$ contains a primitive 7th root of unity.

b. Show that $E\neq \QQ(\alpha)$.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

The splitting field $E$ contains a primitive seventh root of unity.


::: pf-proof

The polynomial $x^7-3$ is separable over $\mathbb Q$, so its splitting field contains distinct roots $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Their quotient $\zeta=\beta/\gamma$ is defined because every root is nonzero. It satisfies $\zeta^7=\beta^7/\gamma^7=1$, but $\zeta\neq1$. Since $7$ is prime, $\zeta$ has multiplicative order seven and is primitive.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The field $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ has degree seven over $\mathbb Q$.


::: pf-proof

Eisenstein's criterion at $3$ applies to $x^7-3$, so this polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. It is therefore the minimal polynomial of the root $\alpha$, giving $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=7$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The splitting field strictly contains $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$:
\[
\boxed{E\neq\mathbb Q(\alpha)}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Suppose $E=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. By step <1>1, a primitive seventh root of unity $\zeta$ belongs to $E$, so $\mathbb Q(\zeta)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Its cyclotomic degree is $\varphi(7)=6$. The tower law would imply $6\mid[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=7$, contradicting step <1>2.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>1 and <1>3 prove parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::

:::
