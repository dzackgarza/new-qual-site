::: {.problem}
Let $f(x) = x^7 - 3\in \QQ[x]$ and $E/\QQ$ be a splitting field of $f$ with $\alpha \in E$ a root of $f$.

a. Show that $E$ contains a primitive 7th root of unity.

b. Show that $E\neq \QQ(\alpha)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The splitting field $E$ contains a primitive seventh root of unity.


::: pf-proof

The polynomial $x^7-3$ is separable over $\QQ$, so its splitting field contains distinct roots $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Their quotient $\zeta=\beta/\gamma$ is defined because every root is nonzero. It satisfies $\zeta^7=\beta^7/\gamma^7=1$, but $\zeta\neq1$. Since $7$ is prime, $\zeta$ has multiplicative order seven and is primitive.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The field $\QQ(\alpha)$ has degree seven over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Eisenstein's criterion at $3$ applies to $x^7-3$, so this polynomial is irreducible over $\QQ$. It is therefore the minimal polynomial of the root $\alpha$, giving $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=7$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The splitting field strictly contains $\QQ(\alpha)$:
\[
\boxed{E\neq\QQ(\alpha)}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Suppose $E=\QQ(\alpha)$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, a primitive seventh root of unity $\zeta$ belongs to $E$, so $\QQ(\zeta)\subseteq\QQ(\alpha)$. Its cyclotomic degree is $\varphi(7)=6$. The tower law would imply $6\mid[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=7$, contradicting step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} prove parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::
