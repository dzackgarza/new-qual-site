::: problem
Let $f(x) = x^7 - 3\in \QQ[x]$ and $E/\QQ$ be a splitting field of $f$ with $\alpha \in E$ a root of $f$.

a. Show that $E$ contains a primitive 7th root of unity.

b. Show that $E\neq \QQ(\alpha)$.
:::

::: solution
The roots of $x^7-3$ in a splitting field are
\[
\alpha_0,\alpha_0\zeta,\ldots,\alpha_0\zeta^6,
\]
where $\alpha_0^7=3$ and $\zeta$ is a primitive seventh root of unity.

For (a), because $E$ is a splitting field, it contains two distinct roots $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Their quotient satisfies
\[
(\beta/\gamma)^7=1
\]
and is not $1$. Since $7$ is prime, every nontrivial seventh root of unity has order $7$. Thus $E$ contains a primitive seventh root of unity.

For (b), Eisenstein's criterion at $3$ shows that $x^7-3$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. Hence
\[
[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=7.
\]
If $E=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$, then by part (a) $E$ contains a primitive seventh root $\zeta$, so
\[
\mathbb Q(\zeta)\subseteq E.
\]
But $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]=\varphi(7)=6$, and the tower law would force $6$ to divide $[E:\mathbb Q]=7$, impossible. Therefore
\[
E\ne\mathbb Q(\alpha).
\]
:::
