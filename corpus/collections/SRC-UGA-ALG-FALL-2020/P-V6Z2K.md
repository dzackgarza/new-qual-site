::: problem
Let $A\in \Mat(n\times n, \CC)$ such that the group generated by $A$ under multiplication is finite.
Show that
\[
\Tr(A\inv) =\conj{\Tr(A) }
,\]
where $\conj{(\wait)}$ denotes taking the complex conjugate and $\Tr(\wait)$ is the trace.
:::

::: solution
Because the multiplicative subgroup generated by $A$ is finite, there exists $m\ge1$ such that
\[
A^m=I.
\]
Hence $A$ is invertible and is annihilated by $x^m-1$. Over $\mathbb C$, the polynomial $x^m-1$ has distinct roots, so $A$ is diagonalizable. Let its eigenvalues be $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$. Each satisfies $\lambda_i^m=1$, hence $|\lambda_i|=1$ and therefore
\[
\lambda_i^{-1}=\overline{\lambda_i}.
\]
The eigenvalues of $A^{-1}$ are $\lambda_1^{-1},\ldots,\lambda_n^{-1}$, so
\[
\Tr(A^{-1})
=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i^{-1}
=\sum_{i=1}^n\overline{\lambda_i}
=\overline{\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i}
=\overline{\Tr(A)}.
\]
:::
