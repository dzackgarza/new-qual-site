::: {.problem}
Let $P\in M_n(\mathbb R)$.
Show that $P$ is the matrix of an orthogonal projection onto a subspace $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ if and only if
\[
P^T=P,\qquad P^2=P.
\]
What is the matrix of the orthogonal projection onto $E^\perp$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ be the linear map $T(x)=Px$.

<1>1. If $T$ is the orthogonal projection onto $E$, then
\[
P^2=P.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every $x\in\mathbb R^n$ has a unique orthogonal decomposition
\[
x=e+u,
\qquad
e\in E,
\quad
u\in E^\perp.
\]
Since $T(e)=e$ and $T(u)=0$, we have $T(x)=e$.  Applying $T$ once more gives
\[
T^2(x)=T(e)=e=T(x).
\]
Thus $T^2=T$, which in the standard basis is $P^2=P$.
:::

<1>2. If $T$ is the orthogonal projection onto $E$, then
\[
P^T=P.
\]
::: {.proof}
For
\[
x=e+u,
\qquad
y=f+v,
\]
with $e,f\in E$ and $u,v\in E^\perp$, we have
\[
\langle T(x),y\rangle
=\langle e,f+v\rangle
=\langle e,f\rangle
=\langle e+u,f\rangle
=\langle x,T(y)\rangle.
\]
Hence $T$ is self-adjoint.  In the standard orthonormal basis, the matrix of the adjoint is $P^T$, so $P^T=P$.
:::

<1>3. Conversely, suppose
\[
P^T=P,
\qquad
P^2=P,
\]
and set
\[
E=\operatorname{im}P.
\]
Then $T$ is the identity on $E$.
::: {.proof}
If $e\in E$, then $e=Px$ for some $x$.  Therefore
\[
T(e)=Pe=P^2x=Px=e.
\]
:::

<1>4. Under the assumptions of <1>3,
\[
\ker P=E^\perp.
\]
::: {.proof}
First let $u\in\ker P$ and $e\in E$.  Write $e=Px$.  Since $P^T=P$,
\[
\langle u,e\rangle
=\langle u,Px\rangle
=\langle Pu,x\rangle
=0.
\]
Thus $u\in E^\perp$, so $\ker P\subseteq E^\perp$.

Conversely, let $u\in E^\perp$.  Since $Pu\in E$,
\[
\lVert Pu\rVert^2
=\langle Pu,Pu\rangle
=\langle u,P^TPu\rangle
=\langle u,P^2u\rangle
=\langle u,Pu\rangle
=0.
\]
Hence $Pu=0$, so $u\in\ker P$.  Therefore $E^\perp\subseteq\ker P$ as well.
:::

<1>5. A symmetric idempotent matrix $P$ is the matrix of the orthogonal projection onto $E=\operatorname{im}P$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, $T$ is the identity on $E$.  By <1>4, $T$ vanishes on $E^\perp$.  These two properties characterize the orthogonal projection onto $E$.
:::

<1>6. The matrix of the orthogonal projection onto $E^\perp$ is
\[
\boxed{I-P}.
\]
::: {.proof}
If $x=e+u$ with $e\in E$ and $u\in E^\perp$, then
\[
(I-P)x=e+u-e=u.
\]
Thus $I-P$ fixes $E^\perp$ and vanishes on $E$, so it is the orthogonal projection onto $E^\perp$.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 prove the necessary conditions, steps <1>3--<1>5 prove their sufficiency, and step <1>6 identifies the complementary projection.
:::
