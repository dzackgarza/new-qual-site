::: {.problem}
Prove the Cartan-calculus identities displayed immediately before this exercise in Section 6.9 of the source.
The source suggests first checking that the left-hand sides satisfy the graded product rule for wedge products and then reducing to functions $f$ and exact $1$-forms $df$.
:::

::: {.solution}
We use the basic formulas
\[
\iota_X f=0,
\qquad
\iota_X(df)=X(f),
\qquad
L_Xf=X(f),
\qquad
L_X(df)=d(Xf),
\qquad
d(df)=0.
\]

<1>1. For each identity below, the difference between the two sides is a graded derivation of the appropriate degree.  Hence it is enough to check that difference on functions $f$ and exact one-forms $df$.
::: {.proof}
The operators $d$ and $\iota_X$ are odd derivations for the wedge product, while $L_X$ is an even derivation.  Expanding the graded commutator of two graded derivations by the product rules shows that the mixed terms cancel, leaving another graded derivation.

Locally, in coordinates $u_1,\ldots,u_m$, every differential form is a finite sum of terms
\[
f\,du_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge du_{i_k}.
\]
Thus the algebra of forms is locally generated under wedge products by functions and exact one-forms.  A graded derivation vanishing on those generators vanishes on all forms.  Since all the operators involved are local, the local conclusion is global.
:::

<1>2. One has
\[
\boxed{d\circ d=0.}
\]
::: {.proof}
On a function $f$,
\[
d(df)=0
\]
by the defining property of the exterior differential.  On an exact one-form $df$,
\[
d(d(df))=0.
\]
By <1>1 this proves $d^2=0$ on all forms.
:::

<1>3. One has
\[
\boxed{L_XL_Y-L_YL_X=L_{[X,Y]}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
On a function $f$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(L_XL_Y-L_YL_X)f
&=X(Yf)-Y(Xf)\\
&=[X,Y]f\\
&=L_{[X,Y]}f.
\end{aligned}
\]
On an exact one-form $df$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(L_XL_Y-L_YL_X)(df)
&=L_X(d(Yf))-L_Y(d(Xf))\\
&=d(X(Yf))-d(Y(Xf))\\
&=d([X,Y]f)\\
&=L_{[X,Y]}(df).
\end{aligned}
\]
Apply <1>1.
:::

<1>4. One has
\[
\boxed{\iota_X\iota_Y+\iota_Y\iota_X=0.}
\]
::: {.proof}
On functions both terms vanish.  On an exact one-form $df$,
\[
\iota_X\iota_Y(df)+\iota_Y\iota_X(df)
=\iota_X(Yf)+\iota_Y(Xf)=0,
\]
because contraction annihilates functions.  By <1>1 the identity holds on all forms.
:::

<1>5. One has
\[
\boxed{dL_X-L_Xd=0.}
\]
::: {.proof}
On a function $f$,
\[
d(L_Xf)-L_X(df)
=d(Xf)-d(Xf)=0.
\]
On $df$,
\[
d(L_Xdf)-L_X(d(df))
=d(d(Xf))-0=0
\]
by <1>2.  Apply <1>1.
:::

<1>6. One has
\[
\boxed{L_X\iota_Y-\iota_YL_X=\iota_{[X,Y]}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
On functions, all contractions vanish, so both sides are zero.  On $df$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(L_X\iota_Y-\iota_YL_X)(df)
&=L_X(Yf)-\iota_Y(d(Xf))\\
&=X(Yf)-Y(Xf)\\
&=[X,Y]f\\
&=\iota_{[X,Y]}(df).
\end{aligned}
\]
Apply <1>1.
:::

<1>7. One has the Cartan formula
\[
\boxed{\iota_Xd+d\iota_X=L_X.}
\]
::: {.proof}
On a function $f$,
\[
(\iota_Xd+d\iota_X)f
=\iota_X(df)
=Xf
=L_Xf.
\]
On $df$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\iota_Xd+d\iota_X)(df)
&=\iota_X(d(df))+d(\iota_Xdf)\\
&=0+d(Xf)\\
&=L_X(df).
\end{aligned}
\]
Apply <1>1.  This is also the formula used by the source to construct $L_X$.
:::

<1>8. Thus the Cartan-calculus relations are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
d^2=0,\\
[L_X,L_Y]=L_{[X,Y]},\\
\iota_X\iota_Y+\iota_Y\iota_X=0,\\
dL_X-L_Xd=0,\\
L_X\iota_Y-\iota_YL_X=\iota_{[X,Y]},\\
\iota_Xd+d\iota_X=L_X.
\end{gathered}
}
\]
::: {.proof}
These are exactly <1>2--<1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the collection of identities requested in the exercise.
