::: {.problem}
Show that for every $X\in M_n(\mathbb R)$,
\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\det(I+tX)=\operatorname{tr}(X).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the columns of $X$ as $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ and the standard basis of $\mathbb R^n$ as $e_1,\ldots,e_n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #leading-order-determinant-expansion}
By multilinearity of the determinant,
\[
\det(I+tX)
=1
+t\sum_{j=1}^n
\det(e_1,\ldots,e_{j-1},X_j,e_{j+1},\ldots,e_n)
+O(t^2).
\]

::: pf-proof
The $j$th column of $I+tX$ is
\[
e_j+tX_j.
\]
Expanding the determinant multilinearly in all columns, the term of degree zero in $t$ is
\[
\det(e_1,\ldots,e_n)=1.
\]
The terms of degree one are exactly those in which $tX_j$ is chosen from one column and $e_i$ from every other column.  Every remaining term contains at least two factors of $t$, hence contributes $O(t^2)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #diagonal-cofactor-value}
For every $j$,
\[
\det(e_1,\ldots,e_{j-1},X_j,e_{j+1},\ldots,e_n)=x_{jj}.
\]

::: pf-proof
Write
\[
X_j=\sum_{i=1}^n x_{ij}e_i.
\]
By linearity in the $j$th column,
\[
\det(e_1,\ldots,X_j,\ldots,e_n)
=\sum_{i=1}^n x_{ij}
\det(e_1,\ldots,e_i,\ldots,e_n),
\]
where $e_i$ occupies the $j$th slot.  If $i\ne j$, then the determinant has two equal columns and vanishes.  The sole surviving term is $i=j$, for which the determinant is $1$.  Thus the value is $x_{jj}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #trace-appears-in-expansion}
Therefore
\[
\det(I+tX)=1+t\operatorname{tr}(X)+O(t^2).
\]

::: pf-proof
Substitute step [](#diagonal-cofactor-value){.pf-ref} into step [](#leading-order-determinant-expansion){.pf-ref}:
\[
\sum_{j=1}^n x_{jj}=\operatorname{tr}(X).
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boxed-derivative-identity}
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\det(I+tX)=\operatorname{tr}(X).
}
\]

::: pf-proof
The derivative at $0$ is the coefficient of $t$ in the polynomial $\det(I+tX)$, which is $\operatorname{tr}(X)$ by step [](#trace-appears-in-expansion){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#boxed-derivative-identity){.pf-ref} is the desired identity.
:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark}
The source hint states that every real matrix is similar over $\mathbb R$ to an upper-triangular matrix.  That is false: for example,
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
has no real eigenvalue and hence cannot be similar over $\mathbb R$ to a real upper-triangular matrix.  The hint can be repaired by complexifying first, since every complex matrix is triangularizable; the multilinearity argument above works directly over $\mathbb R$.
:::
