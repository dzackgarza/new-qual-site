::: {.problem}
Let
\[
\alpha=y^2e^x\,dx+2ye^x\,dy
\]
on $\mathbb R^2$ and let
\[
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^2,\qquad \gamma(t)=(\sin(\pi t/2),t^3).
\]
Find $\int_\gamma\alpha$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
The one-form $\alpha$ is exact:
\[
\alpha=d f,
\qquad
f(x,y)=y^2e^x.
\]

::: pf-proof
Differentiate $f$:
\[
d f
=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dx
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\,dy
=y^2e^x\,dx+2ye^x\,dy
=\alpha.
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #endpoints-of-gamma}
The endpoints of $\gamma$ are
\[
\gamma(0)=(0,0),
\qquad
\gamma(1)=(1,1).
\]

::: pf-proof
Substitute $t=0$ and $t=1$ into
\[
\gamma(t)=\left(\sin\frac{\pi t}{2},t^3\right).
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boxed-integral-value}
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\int_\gamma\alpha=e.}
\]

::: pf-proof
The fundamental theorem for exact one-forms gives
\[
\int_\gamma\alpha
=\int_\gamma df
=f(\gamma(1))-f(\gamma(0)).
\]
By step [](#endpoints-of-gamma){.pf-ref},
\[
f(1,1)=1^2e^1=e,
\qquad
f(0,0)=0^2e^0=0.
\]
Hence the integral equals $e$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#boxed-integral-value){.pf-ref} is the required value.
:::
