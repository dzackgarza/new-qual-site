::: {.problem}
(a) Show that $T_p(\operatorname{id}_M)=\operatorname{id}_{T_pM}$.

(b) If $F:M\to N$ is a diffeomorphism, prove that $T_pF$ is a linear isomorphism and identify its inverse as the tangent map of $F^{-1}$ at $F(p)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $v\in T_pM$,
\[
T_p(\operatorname{id}_M)(v)=v.
\]

::: pf-proof

For every $g\in C^\infty(M)$, the definition of the tangent map gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(T_p(\operatorname{id}_M)(v)\bigr)(g)
&=v(g\circ\operatorname{id}_M)\\
&=v(g).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the two tangent vectors agree on every smooth function, so they are equal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Hence
\[
\boxed{T_p(\operatorname{id}_M)=\operatorname{id}_{T_pM}.}
\]

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} says that the two linear maps have the same value on every $v\in T_pM$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Suppose $F:M\to N$ is a diffeomorphism and put $q=F(p)$.  Then
\[
T_q(F^{-1})\circ T_pF=\operatorname{id}_{T_pM}.
\]

::: pf-proof

Since
\[
F^{-1}\circ F=\operatorname{id}_M,
\]
the chain rule from the source gives
\[
T_p(F^{-1}\circ F)
=T_{F(p)}(F^{-1})\circ T_pF
=T_q(F^{-1})\circ T_pF.
\]
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the left-hand side is $\operatorname{id}_{T_pM}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One also has
\[
T_pF\circ T_q(F^{-1})=\operatorname{id}_{T_qN}.
\]

::: pf-proof

Apply the chain rule at $q\in N$ to
\[
F\circ F^{-1}=\operatorname{id}_N.
\]
Since $F^{-1}(q)=p$, this gives
\[
T_q(F\circ F^{-1})
=T_pF\circ T_q(F^{-1}).
\]
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref} applied to $N$, the left-hand side is $\operatorname{id}_{T_qN}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore $T_pF$ is a linear isomorphism with
\[
\boxed{(T_pF)^{-1}=T_{F(p)}(F^{-1}).}
\]

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} show that $T_q(F^{-1})$ is respectively a left and right inverse of $T_pF$.  Hence $T_pF$ is invertible and its inverse is $T_q(F^{-1})$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s5){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
