::: {.problem}
For the unit sphere $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$, let $U_+=S^2\setminus\{(0,0,-1)\}$ and $U_-=S^2\setminus\{(0,0,1)\}$.
Verify directly that stereographic projection to the plane $z=0$ is given by
\[
\phi_+(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x}{1+z},\frac{y}{1+z}\right),\qquad
\phi_-(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x}{1-z},\frac{y}{1-z}\right).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $p=(x,y,z)\in S^2$.

For $\phi_+$, stereographic projection is taken from the south pole $s=(0,0,-1)$.
The affine line through $s$ and $p$ is
\[
s+t(p-s)=(tx,ty,-1+t(z+1)).
\]
Its intersection with the plane $z=0$ occurs when
\[
-1+t(z+1)=0,
\]
so, since $p\ne s$,
\[
t=\frac1{1+z}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\phi_+(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x}{1+z},\frac{y}{1+z}\right).
\]

Similarly, for $\phi_-$ we project from the north pole $n=(0,0,1)$.
The line through $n$ and $p$ is
\[
n+t(p-n)=(tx,ty,1+t(z-1)).
\]
Setting the third coordinate equal to zero gives
\[
1+t(z-1)=0,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
t=\frac1{1-z},
\]
because $p\ne n$.
Thus
\[
\phi_-(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x}{1-z},\frac{y}{1-z}\right).
\]
:::
