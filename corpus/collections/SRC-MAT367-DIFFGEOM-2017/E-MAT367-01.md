::: {.problem}
For the unit sphere $S^2\subset\RR^3$, let $U_+=S^2\setminus\{(0,0,-1)\}$ and $U_-=S^2\setminus\{(0,0,1)\}$.
Verify directly that stereographic projection to the plane $z=0$ is given by
$$
\phi_+(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x}{1+z},\frac{y}{1+z}\right),\qquad
\phi_-(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x}{1-z},\frac{y}{1-z}\right).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $p = (x,y,z) \in S^2$, and let $s = (0,0,-1)$ and $n = (0,0,1)$ be the south and north poles.
Stereographic projection from a pole sends $p$ to the point where the line through the pole and $p$ meets the plane $z = 0$.

::: pf

::: pf-step
For $p \in U_+$, projection from $s$ sends $p$ to $\phi_+(x,y,z) = \left(\frac{x}{1+z}, \frac{y}{1+z}\right)$.

::: pf-proof
The line through $s$ and $p$ is $s + t(p - s) = (tx, ty, -1 + t(1 + z))$.
On $S^2$, $z = -1$ forces $x = y = 0$, so $p \neq s$ gives $z \neq -1$.
The third coordinate vanishes exactly when $t = 1/(1+z)$, which gives the stated point.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
For $p \in U_-$, projection from $n$ sends $p$ to $\phi_-(x,y,z) = \left(\frac{x}{1-z}, \frac{y}{1-z}\right)$.

::: pf-proof
The line through $n$ and $p$ is $n + t(p - n) = (tx, ty, 1 + t(z - 1))$.
On $S^2$, $z = 1$ forces $x = y = 0$, so $p \neq n$ gives $z \neq 1$.
The third coordinate vanishes exactly when $t = 1/(1-z)$, which gives the stated point.
:::
