::: {.problem}
Derive a formula for the exterior derivative of a $2$-form analogous to the source's formula
\[
(d\alpha)(X,Y)=L_X(\alpha(Y))-L_Y(\alpha(X))-\alpha([X,Y])
\]
for a $1$-form.
Then generalize the formula to arbitrary $k$-forms.
:::

::: {.solution}
We use the Cartan identities proved in the preceding exercise,
\[
\iota_Xd=L_X-d\iota_X,
\qquad
L_X\iota_Y-\iota_YL_X=\iota_{[X,Y]}.
\]

<1>1. If $\alpha\in\Omega^2(M)$ and $X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak X(M)$, then
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
(d\alpha)(X,Y,Z)
={}&L_X\bigl(\alpha(Y,Z)\bigr)
-L_Y\bigl(\alpha(X,Z)\bigr)
+L_Z\bigl(\alpha(X,Y)\bigr)\\
&-\alpha([X,Y],Z)
+\alpha([X,Z],Y)
-\alpha([Y,Z],X).
\end{aligned}
}
\]
::: {.proof}
By definition of contraction,
\[
(d\alpha)(X,Y,Z)=\iota_Z\iota_Y\iota_X(d\alpha).
\]
Cartan's formula gives
\[
\iota_Xd\alpha=L_X\alpha-d(\iota_X\alpha),
\]
so
\[
(d\alpha)(X,Y,Z)
=\iota_Z\iota_YL_X\alpha
-\iota_Z\iota_Yd(\iota_X\alpha).
\]

For the first term, commute $L_X$ successively through the contractions:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\iota_Z\iota_YL_X\alpha
&=\iota_Z\bigl(L_X\iota_Y\alpha-\iota_{[X,Y]}\alpha\bigr)\\
&=L_X\iota_Z\iota_Y\alpha
-\iota_{[X,Z]}\iota_Y\alpha
-\iota_Z\iota_{[X,Y]}\alpha\\
&=L_X\bigl(\alpha(Y,Z)\bigr)
+\alpha([X,Z],Y)
-\alpha([X,Y],Z).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here skew-symmetry gives
\[
-\iota_{[X,Z]}\iota_Y\alpha
=-\alpha(Y,[X,Z])
=\alpha([X,Z],Y).
\]

Now $\iota_X\alpha$ is a $1$-form.  Applying the source's $1$-form formula to it gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\iota_Z\iota_Yd(\iota_X\alpha)
={}&L_Y\bigl(\alpha(X,Z)\bigr)
-L_Z\bigl(\alpha(X,Y)\bigr)
-(\iota_X\alpha)([Y,Z])\\
={}&L_Y\bigl(\alpha(X,Z)\bigr)
-L_Z\bigl(\alpha(X,Y)\bigr)
-\alpha(X,[Y,Z]).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $\alpha(X,[Y,Z])=-\alpha([Y,Z],X)$, subtracting this expression gives the displayed six-term formula.
:::

<1>2. More generally, for $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$ and vector fields $X_0,\ldots,X_k$,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
(d\alpha)(X_0,\ldots,X_k)
={}&\sum_{i=0}^k(-1)^i
L_{X_i}\!\left(
\alpha(X_0,\ldots,\widehat{X_i},\ldots,X_k)
\right)\\
&+\sum_{0\le i<j\le k}(-1)^{i+j}
\alpha\!\left(
[X_i,X_j],
X_0,\ldots,\widehat{X_i},\ldots,\widehat{X_j},\ldots,X_k
\right).
\end{aligned}
}
\]
The hats indicate omitted arguments, and the bracket is placed in the first argument of $\alpha$.
::: {.proof}
We prove the formula by induction on $k$.  For $k=0$, a $0$-form is a smooth function $f$, and the formula is
\[
(df)(X_0)=L_{X_0}f=X_0(f),
\]
which is the definition of $df$.

Assume the formula holds for $(k-1)$-forms, and let $\alpha$ be a $k$-form.  Since
\[
(d\alpha)(X_0,\ldots,X_k)
=\iota_{X_k}\cdots\iota_{X_1}\iota_{X_0}d\alpha,
\]
Cartan's formula gives
\[
(d\alpha)(X_0,\ldots,X_k)
=\iota_{X_k}\cdots\iota_{X_1}L_{X_0}\alpha
-d(\iota_{X_0}\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,X_k).
\]

Repeatedly using
\[
\iota_YL_{X_0}=L_{X_0}\iota_Y-\iota_{[X_0,Y]}
\]
and then using skew-symmetry of $\alpha$ yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\iota_{X_k}\cdots\iota_{X_1}L_{X_0}\alpha
={}&L_{X_0}\bigl(\alpha(X_1,\ldots,X_k)\bigr)\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^j
\alpha([X_0,X_j],X_1,\ldots,\widehat{X_j},\ldots,X_k).
\end{aligned}
\]
These are exactly the $i=0$ terms in the claimed formula.

Set $\beta=\iota_{X_0}\alpha\in\Omega^{k-1}(M)$.  By the induction hypothesis,
\[
\begin{aligned}
-d\beta(X_1,\ldots,X_k)
={}&\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^i
L_{X_i}\bigl(\alpha(X_0,X_1,\ldots,\widehat{X_i},\ldots,X_k)\bigr)\\
&+\sum_{1\le i<j\le k}(-1)^{i+j}
\alpha([X_i,X_j],X_0,X_1,\ldots,\widehat{X_i},\ldots,\widehat{X_j},\ldots,X_k).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the second sum, before this rewriting one has $\alpha(X_0,[X_i,X_j],\ldots)$ with coefficient $(-1)^{i+j-1}$; moving the bracket past $X_0$ into the first slot contributes one more minus sign and gives the displayed coefficient $(-1)^{i+j}$.  Thus these are exactly all remaining $i\ge1$ derivative terms and all remaining bracket terms in the claimed formula.  This completes the induction.
:::

<1>3. The formula in <1>1 is the special case $k=2$ of <1>2.
::: {.proof}
Substituting $k=2$ and $(X_0,X_1,X_2)=(X,Y,Z)$ into <1>2 gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(d\alpha)(X,Y,Z)
={}&L_X\alpha(Y,Z)-L_Y\alpha(X,Z)+L_Z\alpha(X,Y)\\
&-\alpha([X,Y],Z)+\alpha([X,Z],Y)-\alpha([Y,Z],X),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is precisely <1>1.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the requested $2$-form identity, and step <1>2 gives its generalization to arbitrary degree.
:::
