::: {.problem}
Show that
\[
x^2+y=0,\qquad x^2+y^2+z^3+w^4+y=1
\]
define a two-dimensional submanifold $S\subseteq\mathbb R^4$, and find the equations of the tangent space at $(-1,-1,-1,-1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
\[
F:\mathbb R^4\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
F(x,y,z,w)
=\bigl(x^2+y,\ x^2+y^2+z^3+w^4+y\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
S=F^{-1}(0,1).
\]

<1>1. The derivative of $F$ at $(x,y,z,w)$ is
\[
D F_{(x,y,z,w)}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2x&1&0&0\\
2x&2y+1&3z^2&4w^3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Differentiate the two coordinate functions of $F$ with respect to $x,y,z,w$.
:::

<1>2. The matrix in <1>1 has rank $2$ at every point of $S$.
::: {.proof}
Its first row
\[
(2x,1,0,0)
\]
is always nonzero, so rank can fail to be $2$ only if the second row is a scalar multiple of the first.

Suppose first that $x\ne0$.  Comparing the first coordinates of the two rows forces the scalar multiple to be $1$.  Comparing the remaining coordinates then gives
\[
2y+1=1,
\qquad
3z^2=0,
\qquad
4w^3=0,
\]
so $y=z=w=0$.  But a point of $S$ must satisfy $x^2+y=0$, which would give $x=0$, a contradiction.

Suppose instead that $x=0$.  The first defining equation of $S$ gives $y=0$.  The two rows can then be dependent only if
\[
z=w=0.
\]
At $(0,0,0,0)$ the second coordinate of $F$ is $0$, not $1$, so this point is not in $S$.  Thus the rows are independent at every point of $S$.
:::

<1>3. The set $S$ is a smooth submanifold of $\mathbb R^4$ of dimension $2$.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 says that $(0,1)\in\mathbb R^2$ is a regular value of $F$.  The regular-level-set theorem therefore makes
\[
S=F^{-1}(0,1)
\]
a submanifold of codimension $2$, hence of dimension
\[
4-2=2.
\]
:::

<1>4. The point
\[
p=(-1,-1,-1,-1)
\]
lies in $S$, and
\[
D F_p
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&1&0&0\\
-2&-1&3&-4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The defining equations give
\[
(-1)^2+(-1)=0
\]
and
\[
(-1)^2+(-1)^2+(-1)^3+(-1)^4+(-1)=1.
\]
Substituting $p$ into the matrix from <1>1 gives the displayed derivative.
:::

<1>5. The tangent space at $p$ is
\[
\boxed{
T_pS
=\{(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb R^4:
-2a+b=0,
\ -2a-b+3c-4d=0\}.
}
\]
::: {.proof}
For a regular level set, the source proves
\[
T_pS=\ker D F_p.
\]
Thus $(a,b,c,d)$ is tangent exactly when
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&1&0&0\\
-2&-1&3&-4
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\\c\\d\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is precisely the pair of displayed linear equations.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves the submanifold statement, and step <1>5 gives the requested tangent-space equations.
:::
