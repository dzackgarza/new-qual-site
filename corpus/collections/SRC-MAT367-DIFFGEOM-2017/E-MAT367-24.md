::: {.problem}
Find all critical points of
\[
f:S^2\to\mathbb R,\qquad f(x,y,z)=xy.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
h:\mathbb R^3\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\qquad
h(x,y,z)=xy,
\]
so that $f=h|_{S^2}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #criticality-iff-kernel-contains-tangent}
A point $p\in S^2$ is critical for $f$ if and only if
\[
T_pS^2\subseteq\ker D_ph.
\]

::: pf-proof
If $i:S^2\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^3$ is the inclusion, then $f=h\circ i$.  By the chain rule,
\[
T_pf=D_ph\circ T_pi.
\]
The image of $T_pi$ is the tangent plane $T_pS^2\subseteq\mathbb R^3$.  Hence $T_pf=0$ exactly when $D_ph$ vanishes on $T_pS^2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #derivative-and-tangent-plane-formulas}
For $p=(x,y,z)$,
\[
D_ph(a,b,c)=ya+xb,
\qquad
T_pS^2=p^\perp.
\]

::: pf-proof
The first formula is the ordinary derivative of $h(x,y,z)=xy$.  The second is the standard tangent-space description of the unit sphere, obtained by differentiating $x^2+y^2+z^2=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #poles-are-critical}
If $x=y=0$, then $p=(0,0,\pm1)$ and $p$ is critical.

::: pf-proof
When $x=y=0$, step [](#derivative-and-tangent-plane-formulas){.pf-ref} gives $D_ph=0$ on all of $\mathbb R^3$, so in particular it vanishes on $T_pS^2$.  Step [](#criticality-iff-kernel-contains-tangent){.pf-ref} therefore makes both poles critical.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #kernel-basis-away-from-origin}
Suppose $(x,y)\ne(0,0)$.  Then
\[
\ker D_ph
=\operatorname{span}\{(0,0,1),(x,-y,0)\}.
\]

::: pf-proof
Both displayed vectors lie in the kernel because
\[
D_ph(0,0,1)=0,
\qquad
D_ph(x,-y,0)=yx-xy=0.
\]
Since $D_ph\ne0$, its kernel has dimension two, and these two vectors are linearly independent.  They therefore form a basis of the kernel.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #criticality-condition-away-from-origin}
Under the hypothesis of step [](#kernel-basis-away-from-origin){.pf-ref}, the point $p$ is critical if and only if
\[
z=0,
\qquad
x^2-y^2=0.
\]

::: pf-proof
Both $T_pS^2=p^\perp$ and $\ker D_ph$ are two-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb R^3$.  By step [](#criticality-iff-kernel-contains-tangent){.pf-ref}, criticality is therefore equivalent to their equality, which is equivalent to both basis vectors from step [](#kernel-basis-away-from-origin){.pf-ref} lying in $p^\perp$.

Their dot products with $p=(x,y,z)$ are
\[
p\cdot(0,0,1)=z,
\qquad
p\cdot(x,-y,0)=x^2-y^2.
\]
Thus the required conditions are exactly $z=0$ and $x^2=y^2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #critical-points-away-from-origin}
The critical points in the case $(x,y)\ne(0,0)$ are
\[
\left(\pm\frac1{\sqrt2},\pm\frac1{\sqrt2},0\right),
\]
with the two signs chosen independently.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#criticality-condition-away-from-origin){.pf-ref}, $z=0$ and $x^2=y^2$.  Since $p\in S^2$,
\[
x^2+y^2=1.
\]
Therefore $2x^2=2y^2=1$, so $|x|=|y|=1/\sqrt2$.  Every independent choice of signs satisfies the conditions of step [](#criticality-condition-away-from-origin){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #critical-set-boxed}
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Crit}(f)
=\{(0,0,1),(0,0,-1)\}
\cup
\left\{
\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt2},\frac{\delta}{\sqrt2},0\right):
\varepsilon,\delta\in\{\pm1\}
\right\}.
}
\]

::: pf-proof
Step [](#poles-are-critical){.pf-ref} gives the two pole critical points.  Steps [](#kernel-basis-away-from-origin){.pf-ref}, [](#criticality-condition-away-from-origin){.pf-ref}, and [](#critical-points-away-from-origin){.pf-ref} exhaust the complementary case and give four more points.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#critical-set-boxed){.pf-ref} lists all six critical points.
:::
