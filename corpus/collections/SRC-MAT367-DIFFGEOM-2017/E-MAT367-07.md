::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal A$ be a maximal atlas on a manifold $M$, let $U\subseteq M$ be open, and let $\mathcal A_U$ be the atlas obtained by restricting every chart in $\mathcal A$ to $U$.
Verify that $\mathcal A_U$ is maximal.
If $\mathcal A$ satisfies the countability and Hausdorff conditions, show that $\mathcal A_U$ does also.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

The restricted family $\mathcal A_U$ is an atlas on $U$.

::: pf-proof

Write
\[
\mathcal A=\{(V_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)\}_\alpha.
\]
Since $U$ is open in $M$, each
\[
\varphi_\alpha(U\cap V_\alpha)
\]
is open in $\mathbb R^m$, so
\[
(U\cap V_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha|_{U\cap V_\alpha})
\]
is a chart whenever $U\cap V_\alpha\ne\varnothing$.  These domains cover $U$.

If two restricted charts overlap, their transition map is simply the restriction of the transition map between the original charts in $\mathcal A$ to an open subset.  Hence it is a diffeomorphism.  Therefore the restricted charts form an atlas on $U$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The atlas $\mathcal A_U$ is maximal.

::: pf-proof

Let $(W,\psi)$ be a chart on $U$ compatible with every chart in $\mathcal A_U$.  Because $W$ is open in $U$ and $U$ is open in $M$, the set $W$ is open in $M$; thus $(W,\psi)$ is also a chart on $M$.

For any $(V_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)\in\mathcal A$, the corresponding restricted chart
\[
(U\cap V_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha|_{U\cap V_\alpha})
\]
lies in $\mathcal A_U$.  Since $W\subseteq U$, its overlap with this restricted chart is
\[
W\cap(U\cap V_\alpha)=W\cap V_\alpha.
\]
Hence compatibility of $(W,\psi)$ with the restricted chart is exactly compatibility with $(V_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)$ on their full overlap.  Thus $(W,\psi)$ is compatible with every chart in $\mathcal A$.

Maximality of $\mathcal A$ implies
\[
(W,\psi)\in\mathcal A.
\]
Restricting this chart to $U$ changes nothing because $W\subseteq U$, so $(W,\psi)\in\mathcal A_U$.  Therefore no compatible chart can be added to $\mathcal A_U$, and $\mathcal A_U$ is maximal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $\mathcal A$ satisfies the countability condition, then $\mathcal A_U$ does also.

::: pf-proof

Choose countably many charts
\[
(V_1,\varphi_1),(V_2,\varphi_2),\ldots
\]
from $\mathcal A$ whose domains cover $M$.  The nonempty sets
\[
U\cap V_1,U\cap V_2,\ldots
\]
are domains of charts in $\mathcal A_U$, and
\[
U=\bigcup_{j\ge1}(U\cap V_j).
\]
After discarding empty restrictions, this is a countable chart cover of $U$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

If $\mathcal A$ satisfies the Hausdorff condition, then $\mathcal A_U$ does also.

::: pf-proof

Let $p,q\in U$ be distinct.  By the Hausdorff condition for $\mathcal A$, there are charts
\[
(V_p,\varphi_p),(V_q,\varphi_q)\in\mathcal A
\]
such that
\[
p\in V_p,
\qquad
q\in V_q,
\qquad
V_p\cap V_q=\varnothing.
\]
Their restrictions to $U$ belong to $\mathcal A_U$, contain $p$ and $q$, respectively, and have disjoint domains because
\[
(U\cap V_p)\cap(U\cap V_q)=U\cap(V_p\cap V_q)=\varnothing.
\]
Thus $\mathcal A_U$ satisfies the Hausdorff condition.

:::
