::: {.problem}
Let $S\subseteq M$ be a submanifold with inclusion $i:S\hookrightarrow M$, and let $F:Q\to S$ be a map from a manifold $Q$.
Prove that $F$ is smooth if and only if $i\circ F:Q\to M$ is smooth.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $F:Q\to S$ is smooth, then $i\circ F:Q\to M$ is smooth.
::: {.proof}
The source proves immediately before this exercise that the inclusion
\[
i:S\hookrightarrow M
\]
of a submanifold is smooth.  Therefore $i\circ F$ is a composition of smooth maps and is smooth.
:::

<1>2. Conversely, suppose $i\circ F:Q\to M$ is smooth.  Fix $q\in Q$, and choose a submanifold chart $(V,\psi)$ of $M$ around $F(q)$ such that, after identifying
\[
\mathbb R^{\dim M}=\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^{\dim M-k},
\qquad k=\dim S,
\]
one has
\[
\psi(V\cap S)=\psi(V)\cap(\mathbb R^k\times\{0\}).
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the defining local normal form of a submanifold.  Since $F(q)\in S$, a submanifold chart exists around $F(q)$.
:::

<1>3. There is a coordinate chart $(U,\phi)$ of $Q$ around $q$ such that
\[
(i\circ F)(U)\subseteq V.
\]
::: {.proof}
The map $i\circ F$ is smooth by hypothesis.  Applying the local-coordinate shrinking result proved earlier in the source to the target chart $(V,\psi)$ gives a chart $(U,\phi)$ around $q$ with the stated containment.
:::

<1>4. In the induced submanifold chart on $V\cap S$, the coordinate expression of $F$ is smooth.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
\pi:\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^{\dim M-k}\longrightarrow\mathbb R^k
\]
be projection onto the first factor.  The induced chart on $V\cap S$ is
\[
\psi_S=\pi\circ\psi|_{V\cap S}.
\]
Since $i\circ F$ is smooth, its coordinate expression
\[
G=\psi\circ i\circ F\circ\phi^{-1}:\phi(U)\longrightarrow\psi(V)
\]
is smooth.  Because $F(U)\subseteq S$, the map $G$ takes values in
\[
\mathbb R^k\times\{0\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\psi_S\circ F\circ\phi^{-1}
=\pi\circ G
\]
is smooth as a composition of smooth Euclidean maps.
:::

<1>5. The map $F:Q\to S$ is smooth.
::: {.proof}
The point $q\in Q$ was arbitrary.  Step <1>4 gives a smooth coordinate expression for $F$ in a chart around every $q$, so $F$ is smooth at every point of $Q$.
:::

<1>6. Hence
\[
\boxed{
F:Q\to S\text{ is smooth}
\iff
i\circ F:Q\to M\text{ is smooth}.
}
\]
::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves the forward implication, and steps <1>2--<1>5 prove the reverse implication.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required equivalence.
:::
