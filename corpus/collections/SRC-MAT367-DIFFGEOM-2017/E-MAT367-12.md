::: {.problem}
Verify that
\[
\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)\longrightarrow M_n(\mathbb R),\qquad E\longmapsto P_E,
\]
where $P_E$ is orthogonal projection onto $E$, is smooth and injective.
Deduce that $\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$ is Hausdorff.
Discuss the analogous map for the complex Grassmannian.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix a Grassmannian coordinate chart determined by an orthogonal decomposition
\[
\mathbb R^n=F\oplus F^\perp,
\qquad \dim F=k.
\]
Every subspace in this chart is the graph
\[
E=\{x+Ax:x\in F\}
\]
of a unique linear map $A:F\to F^\perp$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

In these coordinates the orthogonal projection onto $E=\operatorname{graph}(A)$ is
\[
\boxed{
P_E=
\begin{pmatrix}I\\ A\end{pmatrix}
(I+A^\top A)^{-1}
\begin{pmatrix}I&A^\top\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

::: pf-proof

Let
\[
B=
\begin{pmatrix}I\\ A\end{pmatrix}:F\longrightarrow F\oplus F^\perp.
\]
Then $B$ is injective and $\operatorname{im}B=E$.  Moreover
\[
B^\top B=I+A^\top A.
\]
For $x\ne0$ in $F$,
\[
\langle (I+A^\top A)x,x\rangle
=\|x\|^2+\|Ax\|^2>0,
\]
so $B^\top B$ is invertible.

Set
\[
Q=B(B^\top B)^{-1}B^\top.
\]
Then $Qx\in E$ for every $x\in\mathbb R^n$.  If $y=Bc\in E$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle x-Qx,y\rangle
&=c^\top B^\top\bigl(x-B(B^\top B)^{-1}B^\top x\bigr)\\
&=c^\top\bigl(B^\top x-B^\top x\bigr)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $x-Qx\in E^\perp$, so $Q$ is the orthogonal projection onto $E$.  Substituting $B$ gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The map
\[
E\longmapsto P_E
\]
is smooth on every Grassmannian coordinate chart and hence smooth on $\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$.

::: pf-proof

In the chart above, $E$ is represented by the matrix entries of $A$.  By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, every entry of $P_E$ is obtained from the entries of $A$ by matrix addition, multiplication, transpose, and inversion of
\[
I+A^\top A.
\]
The latter matrix is positive definite for every $A$, hence always invertible.  Matrix inversion is smooth on the open set of invertible matrices, so the displayed formula is a smooth function of the entries of $A$.  Since this holds in every Grassmannian chart, the global map is smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The map $E\mapsto P_E$ is injective.

::: pf-proof

The image of the orthogonal projection $P_E$ is exactly $E$.  Hence
\[
P_E=P_{E'}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E=\operatorname{im}P_E
=\operatorname{im}P_{E'}=E'.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The Grassmannian $\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$ is Hausdorff.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} there is a smooth injective map
\[
\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)\longrightarrow M_n(\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^{n^2}.
\]
The Hausdorff criterion proved immediately before this exercise in the source says that a manifold admitting a smooth injective map into Euclidean space is Hausdorff.  Therefore $\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$ is Hausdorff.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

For the complex Grassmannian, Hermitian orthogonal projection gives the analogous smooth injective map
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,n)\longrightarrow M_n(\mathbb C),
\qquad E\longmapsto P_E.
}
\]
In a complex Grassmannian chart $E=\operatorname{graph}(A)$,
\[
P_E=
\begin{pmatrix}I\\ A\end{pmatrix}
(I+A^\dagger A)^{-1}
\begin{pmatrix}I&A^\dagger\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $A^\dagger$ is the conjugate transpose.

::: pf-proof

The proof of step [](#s1){.pf-ref} applies verbatim with the Euclidean inner product replaced by the Hermitian inner product and transpose replaced by conjugate transpose.  The matrix $I+A^\dagger A$ is Hermitian positive definite, hence invertible.  As a map of real manifolds, conjugation and matrix inversion are smooth, so the formula depends smoothly on $A$.  Its image determines $E$, so the map is injective.  Consequently $\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,n)$ is Hausdorff as well, after identifying $M_n(\mathbb C)$ with $\mathbb R^{2n^2}$.

This projection embedding is generally not holomorphic with respect to the complex-manifold structure.  For example, on the standard chart of $\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(1,2)$ with $A=z\in\mathbb C$, the formula becomes
\[
P_z=\frac1{1+|z|^2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\overline z\\
z&|z|^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its $(1,1)$ entry is $(1+|z|^2)^{-1}$.  Since
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline z}\frac1{1+|z|^2}
=-\frac{z}{(1+|z|^2)^2},
\]
this entry is not holomorphic near any nonzero point.  Hence the smooth projection embedding is not a holomorphic embedding in general.

:::
