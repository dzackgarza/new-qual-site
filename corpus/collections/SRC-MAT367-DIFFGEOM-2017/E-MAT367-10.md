::: {.problem}
For the non-Hausdorff manifold constructed in Example 2.6 of the source, find a compact subset that is not closed.
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the notation of Example 2.6.  Thus $M$ has two coordinate charts
\[
\phi:U\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\qquad
\psi:V\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\]
whose negative halves are identified, while the two origins
\[
p=\phi^{-1}(0),
\qquad
q=\psi^{-1}(0)
\]
are distinct.

Define
\[
A=\phi^{-1}([-1,0])\subseteq U.
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The subset $A$ is compact in $M$.

::: pf-proof

The set $A$ lies in the coordinate domain $U$, and
\[
\phi(A)=[-1,0]
\]
is compact in $\mathbb R$.  Compactness is preserved under a coordinate chart for subsets contained in its domain, so $A$ is compact in $M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The point $q$ belongs to the closure of $A$.

::: pf-proof

Let $W\subseteq M$ be any open neighborhood of $q$.  Since $q\in V$,
\[
\psi(W\cap V)
\]
is an open subset of $\mathbb R$ containing $0$.  Hence it contains some number
\[
t\in(-1,0).
\]
For $t<0$, the two copies of the real line are identified in Example 2.6, so
\[
\psi^{-1}(t)=\phi^{-1}(t).
\]
This common point lies in $W$, and because $t\in[-1,0]$, it also lies in $A$.  Thus every open neighborhood of $q$ meets $A$, which means
\[
q\in\overline A.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The subset $A$ is not closed.

::: pf-proof

By construction $q\notin U$ at the origin, so $q\notin A$.  But step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives $q\in\overline A$.  Therefore
\[
A\ne\overline A,
\]
and $A$ is not closed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Hence
\[
\boxed{A=\phi^{-1}([-1,0])}
\]
is a compact subset of the non-Hausdorff manifold $M$ that is not closed.

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives the requested example.

:::
