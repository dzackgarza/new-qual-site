::: {.problem}
Using the tangent-map definitions, prove that a composition of two submersions is a submersion and a composition of two immersions is an immersion.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
F:M\longrightarrow N,
\qquad
G:N\longrightarrow Q
\]
be smooth maps.  For every $p\in M$, the chain rule gives
\[
T_p(G\circ F)=T_{F(p)}G\circ T_pF.
\]

<1>1. If $F$ and $G$ are submersions, then $G\circ F$ is a submersion.
::: {.proof}
Fix $p\in M$.  Since $F$ is a submersion,
\[
T_pF:T_pM\longrightarrow T_{F(p)}N
\]
is surjective.  Since $G$ is a submersion,
\[
T_{F(p)}G:T_{F(p)}N\longrightarrow T_{G(F(p))}Q
\]
is surjective.

The composition of two surjective linear maps is surjective.  Therefore
\[
T_p(G\circ F)
=T_{F(p)}G\circ T_pF
\]
is surjective.  Since $p$ was arbitrary, $G\circ F$ is a submersion.
:::

<1>2. If $F$ and $G$ are immersions, then $G\circ F$ is an immersion.
::: {.proof}
Fix $p\in M$.  Since $F$ is an immersion, $T_pF$ is injective, and since $G$ is an immersion, $T_{F(p)}G$ is injective.

The composition of two injective linear maps is injective.  Hence
\[
T_p(G\circ F)
=T_{F(p)}G\circ T_pF
\]
is injective.  Since $p$ was arbitrary, $G\circ F$ is an immersion.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves the submersion statement, and step <1>2 proves the immersion statement.
:::
:::
