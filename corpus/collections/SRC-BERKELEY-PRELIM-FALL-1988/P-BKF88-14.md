::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be generated by two elements $a,b$, both of order $2$.
Prove that $G$ has a subgroup of index $2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
c=ab
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H=\langle c\rangle.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Conjugation by $a$ sends $c$ to $c^{-1}$:
$$
aca=c^{-1}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Using $a^2=b^2=1$,
$$
aca
=
a(ab)a
=
ba
=
(ab)^{-1}
=
c^{-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Every element of $G$ belongs to either $H$ or $aH$.

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
b=ac,
$$
the group $G=\langle a,b\rangle$ is also generated by $a$ and $c$. Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
ac^k=c^{-k}a
$$
for every integer $k$. Therefore any word in $a$ and $c$ can be reduced, using $a^2=1$, to one of the forms
$$
c^m
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
ac^m.
$$
The first lies in $H$ and the second in $aH$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The element $a$ does not belong to $H$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $a\in H$. Then
$$
b=ac\in H,
$$
so $G=\langle a,b\rangle=H$ would be cyclic. A cyclic group has at most one element of order $2$, so the two order-$2$ elements $a$ and $b$ would have to be equal. But then
$$
c=ab=a^2=1,
$$
so $H=\{1\}$, contradicting $a\in H$ and $|a|=2$. Hence $a\notin H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The subgroup
$$
\boxed{H=\langle ab\rangle}
$$
has index $2$ in $G$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} shows that $G$ is the union of the two cosets $H$ and $aH$. Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that these cosets are distinct. Hence
$$
[G:H]=2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} supplies the required subgroup.

:::
