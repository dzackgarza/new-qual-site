::: {.problem}
Let $g$ be a continuous real-valued function on $[0,1]$.
Prove that there exists a continuous real-valued function $f$ on $[0,1]$ satisfying
$$
f(x)-\int_0^x f(x-t)e^{-t^2}\,dt=g(x).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
X=C([0,1],\RR)
$$
with the supremum norm, and define
$$
(Th)(x)=\int_0^x h(x-t)e^{-t^2}\,dt.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The operator $T$ maps $X$ linearly into $X$ and satisfies
$$
\norm{ Th}_\infty\leq\norm{ h}_\infty.
$$

::: pf-proof

After the substitution $s=x-t$,
$$
(Th)(x)
=
\int_0^x h(s)e^{-(x-s)^2}\,ds.
$$
The integrand is continuous in $(x,s)$ on the triangular region
$$
0\leq s\leq x\leq1,
$$
so $Th$ is continuous. Linearity is immediate. Also,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{(Th)(x)}
&\leq
\int_0^x \abs{h(x-t)}e^{-t^2}\,dt\\
&\leq
\norm{ h}_\infty x\\
&\leq
\norm{ h}_\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the supremum over $x\in[0,1]$ proves the bound.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every integer $m\geq0$, every $h\in X$, and every $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
\abs{(T^m h)(x)}
\leq
\norm{ h}_\infty\frac{x^m}{m!}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The case $m=0$ is the definition of the supremum norm. Suppose the estimate holds for $m$. Since $e^{-t^2}\leq1$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{(T^{m+1}h)(x)}
&\leq
\int_0^x \abs{(T^m h)(x-t)}\,dt\\
&\leq
\frac{\norm{ h}_\infty}{m!}
\int_0^x(x-t)^m\,dt\\
&=
\norm{ h}_\infty\frac{x^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Induction proves the estimate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The series
$$
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}T^m g
$$
converges uniformly on $[0,1]$ to a continuous real-valued function $f$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\norm{ T^m g}_\infty
\leq
\frac{\norm{ g}_\infty}{m!}.
$$
Since
$$
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\norm{ g}_\infty}{m!}
$$
converges, the Weierstrass $M$-test gives uniform convergence. Each $T^m g$ is continuous by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, so the uniform limit $f$ is continuous and real-valued.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The function $f$ from step [](#s3){.pf-ref} satisfies
$$
f-Tf=g.
$$

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
f_N=\sum_{m=0}^{N}T^m g.
$$
Then the finite sum telescopes:
$$
(I-T)f_N
=
g-T^{N+1}g.
$$
By step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
f_N\longrightarrow f
$$
uniformly. Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} shows that $T$ is continuous in the supremum norm, so
$$
(I-T)f_N\longrightarrow(I-T)f.
$$
On the other hand, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\norm{ T^{N+1}g}_\infty
\leq
\frac{\norm{ g}_\infty}{(N+1)!}
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Taking limits in the telescoping identity yields
$$
(I-T)f=g.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore there exists a continuous real-valued $f$ on $[0,1]$ satisfying
$$
\boxed{
f(x)-\int_0^x f(x-t)e^{-t^2}\,dt=g(x)
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} constructs a continuous real-valued $f$, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives exactly the displayed integral equation.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required existence statement.
