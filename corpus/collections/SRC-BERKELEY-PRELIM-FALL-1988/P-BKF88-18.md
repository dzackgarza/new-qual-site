::: {.problem}
Let $g$ be a continuous real-valued function on $[0,1]$.
Prove that there exists a continuous real-valued function $f$ on $[0,1]$ satisfying
\[
f(x)-\int_0^x f(x-t)e^{-t^2}\,dt=g(x).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
X=C([0,1],\RR)
$$
with the supremum norm, and define
$$
(Th)(x)=\int_0^x h(x-t)e^{-t^2}\,dt.
$$

<1>1. The operator $T$ maps $X$ linearly into $X$ and satisfies
$$
\lVert Th\rVert_\infty\leq\lVert h\rVert_\infty.
$$

::: {.proof}
After the substitution $s=x-t$,
$$
(Th)(x)
=
\int_0^x h(s)e^{-(x-s)^2}\,ds.
$$
The integrand is continuous in $(x,s)$ on the triangular region
$$
0\leq s\leq x\leq1,
$$
so $Th$ is continuous. Linearity is immediate. Also,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|(Th)(x)|
&\leq
\int_0^x |h(x-t)|e^{-t^2}\,dt\\
&\leq
\lVert h\rVert_\infty x\\
&\leq
\lVert h\rVert_\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the supremum over $x\in[0,1]$ proves the bound.
:::

<1>2. For every integer $m\geq0$, every $h\in X$, and every $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
|(T^m h)(x)|
\leq
\lVert h\rVert_\infty\frac{x^m}{m!}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The case $m=0$ is the definition of the supremum norm. Suppose the estimate holds for $m$. Since $e^{-t^2}\leq1$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|(T^{m+1}h)(x)|
&\leq
\int_0^x |(T^m h)(x-t)|\,dt\\
&\leq
\frac{\lVert h\rVert_\infty}{m!}
\int_0^x(x-t)^m\,dt\\
&=
\lVert h\rVert_\infty\frac{x^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Induction proves the estimate.
:::

<1>3. The series
$$
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}T^m g
$$
converges uniformly on $[0,1]$ to a continuous real-valued function $f$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
\lVert T^m g\rVert_\infty
\leq
\frac{\lVert g\rVert_\infty}{m!}.
$$
Since
$$
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lVert g\rVert_\infty}{m!}
$$
converges, the Weierstrass $M$-test gives uniform convergence. Each $T^m g$ is continuous by step <1>1, so the uniform limit $f$ is continuous and real-valued.
:::

<1>4. The function $f$ from step <1>3 satisfies
$$
f-Tf=g.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
f_N=\sum_{m=0}^{N}T^m g.
$$
Then the finite sum telescopes:
$$
(I-T)f_N
=
g-T^{N+1}g.
$$
By step <1>3,
$$
f_N\longrightarrow f
$$
uniformly. Step <1>1 shows that $T$ is continuous in the supremum norm, so
$$
(I-T)f_N\longrightarrow(I-T)f.
$$
On the other hand, step <1>2 gives
$$
\lVert T^{N+1}g\rVert_\infty
\leq
\frac{\lVert g\rVert_\infty}{(N+1)!}
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Taking limits in the telescoping identity yields
$$
(I-T)f=g.
$$
:::

<1>5. Therefore there exists a continuous real-valued $f$ on $[0,1]$ satisfying
$$
\boxed{
f(x)-\int_0^x f(x-t)e^{-t^2}\,dt=g(x)
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 constructs a continuous real-valued $f$, and step <1>4 gives exactly the displayed integral equation.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required existence statement.
:::
