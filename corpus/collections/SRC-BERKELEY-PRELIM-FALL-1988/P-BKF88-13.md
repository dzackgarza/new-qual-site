::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a complex $n\times n$ matrix, and let
\[
C(A)=\{B\in M_n(\mathbb C):AB=BA\}
\]
be its commutant.
Prove that
\[
\dim C(A)\ge n.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Similar matrices have commutants of the same dimension.

::: {.proof}
If
$$
J=P^{-1}AP,
$$
then
$$
\Phi:C(A)\longrightarrow C(J),
\qquad
\Phi(B)=P^{-1}BP,
$$
is a complex-linear isomorphism. Indeed,
$$
AB=BA
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
J(P^{-1}BP)=(P^{-1}BP)J,
$$
and the inverse map is $D\mapsto PDP^{-1}$. Hence
$$
\dim C(A)=\dim C(J).
$$
:::

<1>2. Put $A$ into Jordan form
$$
J
=
J_{m_1}(\lambda_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{m_r}(\lambda_r),
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r m_i=n.
$$
For each block write
$$
J_{m_i}(\lambda_i)=\lambda_i I_{m_i}+N_i,
$$
where $N_i$ is the standard nilpotent Jordan block.

::: {.proof}
Every complex matrix has a Jordan canonical form. The block sizes partition the total dimension, so their sum is $n$.
:::

<1>3. For each $i$ and each
$$
0\leq k<m_i,
$$
let $E_{i,k}$ be the block-diagonal matrix whose $i$th block is $N_i^k$ and whose other blocks are zero. Then
$$
E_{i,k}\in C(J).
$$

::: {.proof}
On the $i$th block,
$$
N_i^kJ_{m_i}(\lambda_i)
=
N_i^k(\lambda_iI+N_i)
=
(\lambda_iI+N_i)N_i^k
=
J_{m_i}(\lambda_i)N_i^k.
$$
On every other block $E_{i,k}$ is zero. Thus $E_{i,k}$ commutes with the whole block-diagonal matrix $J$.
:::

<1>4. The matrices
$$
\{E_{i,k}:1\leq i\leq r,\ 0\leq k<m_i\}
$$
are linearly independent.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
\sum_{i=1}^r\sum_{k=0}^{m_i-1}c_{i,k}E_{i,k}=0.
$$
Looking at the $i$th diagonal block gives
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{m_i-1}c_{i,k}N_i^k=0.
$$
For the standard nilpotent Jordan block, $N_i^k$ has nonzero entries exactly on its $k$th superdiagonal. These superdiagonals are distinct for
$$
k=0,\ldots,m_i-1,
$$
so the matrices
$$
I,N_i,\ldots,N_i^{m_i-1}
$$
are linearly independent. Hence every $c_{i,k}=0$.
:::

<1>5. The commutant $C(J)$ contains at least $n$ linearly independent matrices.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>3 and <1>4, the displayed family lies in $C(J)$ and is linearly independent. Its cardinality is
$$
\sum_{i=1}^r m_i=n.
$$
Therefore
$$
\dim C(J)\geq n.
$$
:::

<1>6. Consequently,
$$
\boxed{\dim C(A)\geq n}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine step <1>1 with step <1>5:
$$
\dim C(A)=\dim C(J)\geq n.
$$
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required lower bound.
:::
