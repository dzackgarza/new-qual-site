::: {.problem}
Prove that a real-valued $C^3$ function $f$ on $\mathbb R^2$ whose Laplacian
\[
\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}
\]
is everywhere positive cannot have a local maximum.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ has a local maximum at
$$
(x_0,y_0).
$$
Then
$$
f_{xx}(x_0,y_0)\leq0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Consider the one-variable function
$$
\phi(t)=f(x_0+t,y_0).
$$
Since $(x_0,y_0)$ is a local maximum of $f$, the point $t=0$ is a local maximum of $\phi$. The one-variable second derivative test therefore gives
$$
\phi''(0)\leq0.
$$
But
$$
\phi''(0)=f_{xx}(x_0,y_0),
$$
which proves the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

At the same point,
$$
f_{yy}(x_0,y_0)\leq0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply the argument of step [](#s1){.pf-ref} to
$$
\psi(t)=f(x_0,y_0+t).
$$
Again $t=0$ is a local maximum, so
$$
\psi''(0)=f_{yy}(x_0,y_0)\leq0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Hence
$$
\Delta f(x_0,y_0)\leq0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Adding steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\Delta f(x_0,y_0)
=
f_{xx}(x_0,y_0)+f_{yy}(x_0,y_0)
\leq0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

This contradicts the hypothesis that $\Delta f$ is everywhere positive.

::: pf-proof

The hypothesis gives
$$
\Delta f(x_0,y_0)>0,
$$
whereas step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives the opposite inequality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{f\text{ has no local maximum on }\RR^2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The assumption of a local maximum led to the contradiction in step [](#s4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::

:::

:::
