::: {.problem}
Let $p\ge2$ be prime.
Find, up to isomorphism, all groups of order $2p$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $p=2$, the groups of order $2p=4$ are
$$
\boxed{C_4\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_2\times C_2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Let $G$ have order $4$. If $G$ contains an element of order $4$, that element generates $G$, so $G\cong C_4$.

Otherwise every nonidentity element has order $2$. Choose distinct nonidentity elements $a,b$. Then $ab$ is the third nonidentity element and also has order $2$. Hence
$$
ab=(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}=ba.
$$
Thus $G$ is abelian and every nonidentity element has order $2$, so
$$
G\cong C_2\times C_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Suppose $p$ is odd. Then every group $G$ of order $2p$ has a unique subgroup
$$
P\cong C_p
$$
of order $p$, and $P$ is normal.

::: pf-proof

Let $n_p$ be the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups. Sylow's theorems give
$$
n_p\equiv1\pmod p
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n_p\mid2.
$$
Since $p$ is odd, the only possibility is $n_p=1$. Thus the Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ is unique and hence normal. Every group of prime order is cyclic, so $P\cong C_p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For odd $p$, choose an element $s\in G$ of order $2$ and a generator $r$ of $P$. Then
$$
G=\langle r,s\rangle
$$
and
$$
srs^{-1}=r^a
$$
for some $a\in(\ZZ/p\ZZ)^\times$ satisfying
$$
a^2\equiv1\pmod p.
$$

::: pf-proof

Cauchy's theorem gives an element $s$ of order $2$. Since $P$ has odd order,
$$
P\cap\langle s\rangle=\{1\}.
$$
Because $P$ is normal by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the product $P\langle s\rangle$ is a subgroup of order
$$
\frac{|P|\,|\langle s\rangle|}{|P\cap\langle s\rangle|}
=
2p,
$$
so it is all of $G$.

Conjugation by $s$ preserves the normal subgroup $P=\langle r\rangle$, hence
$$
srs^{-1}=r^a
$$
for some $a\not\equiv0\pmod p$. Since $s^2=1$,
$$
r
=
s^2rs^{-2}
=
r^{a^2},
$$
so $a^2\equiv1\pmod p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The congruence in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} forces
$$
a\equiv1\pmod p
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
a\equiv-1\pmod p.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $p$ is prime,
$$
a^2-1=(a-1)(a+1)\equiv0\pmod p
$$
implies $a\equiv\pm1\pmod p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

If $a\equiv1\pmod p$, then
$$
\boxed{G\cong C_{2p}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

In this case $srs^{-1}=r$, so $r$ and $s$ commute. Therefore
$$
G=P\times\langle s\rangle
\cong
C_p\times C_2.
$$
Since $p$ and $2$ are coprime, this direct product is cyclic of order $2p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

If $a\equiv-1\pmod p$, then $G$ is the dihedral group of order $2p$:
$$
\boxed{
G\cong
\left\langle r,s\ \middle|\ r^p=s^2=1,\ srs^{-1}=r^{-1}\right\rangle.
}
$$

::: pf-proof

The generators $r,s$ from step [](#s3){.pf-ref} satisfy precisely the displayed relations. Those relations reduce every word to one of
$$
1,r,\ldots,r^{p-1},
\qquad
s,rs,\ldots,r^{p-1}s,
$$
so the presented group has at most $2p$ elements. It maps surjectively to $G$, which has exactly $2p$ elements; hence the map is an isomorphism. This is the symmetry group of a regular $p$-gon.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

Therefore the complete classification is:
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
p=2: & C_4,\ C_2\times C_2,\\[2mm]
p\text{ odd}: & C_{2p},\ \text{the dihedral group of order }2p.
\end{array}}
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} handles $p=2$. For odd $p$, steps [](#s2){.pf-ref}, [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} show that the conjugation action has only the two cases treated in steps [](#s5){.pf-ref} and [](#s6){.pf-ref}, and both groups have order $2p$.

:::

:::
