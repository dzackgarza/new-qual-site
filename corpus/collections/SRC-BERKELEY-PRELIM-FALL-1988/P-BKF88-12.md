::: {.problem}
Let $n$ be positive and let $f\in\mathbb R[x]$ have degree $n$.
Prove that there are real numbers $a_0,\ldots,a_n$, not all zero, such that
\[
\sum_{i=0}^n a_i x^{2^i}
\]
is divisible by $f$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The quotient
$$
V=\RR[x]/(f)
$$
is an $n$-dimensional real vector space.

::: pf-proof

Every polynomial has a unique remainder of degree less than $n$ upon division by the degree-$n$ polynomial $f$. Hence every class in $V$ has a unique representative in
$$
\operatorname{span}_{\RR}\{1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}\}.
$$
Thus the residue classes of
$$
1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}
$$
form a basis of $V$, so $\dim_{\RR}V=n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The $n+1$ residue classes
$$
[x],[x^2],[x^{2^2}],\ldots,[x^{2^n}]
$$
are linearly dependent in $V$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives $\dim_{\RR}V=n$. Any collection of $n+1$ vectors in an $n$-dimensional vector space is linearly dependent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Therefore there are real numbers
$$
a_0,\ldots,a_n,
$$
not all zero, such that
$$
\boxed{\sum_{i=0}^n a_i x^{2^i}}
$$
is divisible by $f$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, there are coefficients $a_0,\ldots,a_n$, not all zero, with
$$
\sum_{i=0}^n a_i[x^{2^i}]=0
$$
in $V=\RR[x]/(f)$. Equivalently,
$$
\sum_{i=0}^n a_i x^{2^i}\in(f),
$$
which means exactly that $f$ divides the displayed polynomial.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} is the required assertion.

:::
