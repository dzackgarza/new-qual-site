::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic on a disk $D$ centered at $z_0$.
Assume
\[
|f'(z)-f'(z_0)|<|f'(z_0)|
\]
for every $z\in D$.
Prove that $f$ is one-to-one on $D$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. One has
$$
f'(z_0)\neq0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Substituting $z=z_0$ into the hypothesis gives
$$
0
=
|f'(z_0)-f'(z_0)|
<
|f'(z_0)|.
$$
Thus $|f'(z_0)|>0$.
:::

<1>2. For distinct $z_1,z_2\in D$,
$$
\left|
f(z_2)-f(z_1)-f'(z_0)(z_2-z_1)
\right|
<
|f'(z_0)|\,|z_2-z_1|.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because a disk is convex, the line segment
$$
\gamma(t)=z_1+t(z_2-z_1),
\qquad
0\leq t\leq1,
$$
lies in $D$. By the fundamental theorem for complex line integrals,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(z_2)-f(z_1)
&=
\int_0^1 f'(\gamma(t))(z_2-z_1)\,dt\\
&=
f'(z_0)(z_2-z_1)
+
(z_2-z_1)
\int_0^1\bigl(f'(\gamma(t))-f'(z_0)\bigr)\,dt.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|
f(z_2)-f(z_1)-f'(z_0)(z_2-z_1)
\right|
&\leq
|z_2-z_1|
\int_0^1
\left|f'(\gamma(t))-f'(z_0)\right|\,dt\\
&<
|z_2-z_1|\,|f'(z_0)|.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last inequality is strict because the hypothesis is strict at every point of the segment.
:::

<1>3. If $z_1\neq z_2$ in $D$, then
$$
f(z_1)\neq f(z_2).
$$

::: {.proof}
By the reverse triangle inequality and step <1>2,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|f(z_2)-f(z_1)|
&\geq
|f'(z_0)|\,|z_2-z_1|
-
\left|
f(z_2)-f(z_1)-f'(z_0)(z_2-z_1)
\right|\\
&>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus distinct points have distinct images.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\boxed{f\text{ is one-to-one on }D}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is exactly injectivity.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
