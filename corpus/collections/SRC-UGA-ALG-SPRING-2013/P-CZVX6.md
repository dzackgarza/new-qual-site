::: {.problem}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $F$ and let $T: V \to V$ be a linear operator with characteristic polynomial $f(x) = \det(x I - T) \in F[x]$.

(a) Show that $f(x)$ is irreducible in $F[x]$ if and only if there are no proper non-zero $T$-invariant subspaces of $V$ (i.e. $W \le V$ with $T(W) \subseteq W$ implies $W = \{0\}$ or $W = V$).

(b) If $f(x)$ is irreducible in $F[x]$ and $\operatorname{char}(F) = 0$, show that $T$ is diagonalizable over the algebraic closure $\overline{F}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $f$ is irreducible, then $V$ has no nonzero proper $T$-invariant subspace.

::: pf-proof

If $0\neq W\subsetneq V$ were invariant, extend a basis of $W$ to a basis of $V$. The matrix of $T$ would be block upper triangular with diagonal blocks representing $T\restrict_W$ and the induced operator on $V/W$. Its characteristic polynomial would therefore be the product of their characteristic polynomials, both of positive degree, contradicting irreducibility of $f$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $V$ has no nonzero proper $T$-invariant subspace, then $f$ is irreducible.

::: pf-proof

Choose $0\neq v\in V$. The cyclic subspace $F[T]v$ is nonzero and invariant, so it equals $V$. Thus the annihilator of $v$ in $F[x]$ is generated by a monic polynomial $m$ with $\deg m=\dim_F V$. The polynomial $m$ annihilates all of $V$ because $v$ generates it as an $F[x]$-module. By Cayley–Hamilton, $m$ divides $f$, and both are monic of degree $\dim_F V$, so $m=f$.

Suppose $f=gh$ with $\deg g,\deg h>0$. Because $\deg g<\deg m$, the vector $g(T)v$ is nonzero. But $h(T)g(T)v=f(T)v=0$, so the cyclic invariant subspace $F[T]g(T)v$ has dimension at most $\deg h<\dim_F V$. This contradicts the hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $\operatorname{char}(F)=0$ and $f$ is irreducible, then $T$ becomes diagonalizable over $\overline F$.

::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial of $T$ equals $f$: it is nonconstant and divides the irreducible characteristic polynomial by Cayley–Hamilton. In characteristic zero, irreducible polynomials are separable: $f'\neq0$ and $\deg f'<\deg f$ imply $\gcd(f,f')=1$. Hence $f$ splits over $\overline F$ into distinct linear factors. The minimal polynomial of the scalar extension of $T$ divides this square-free polynomial, so the scalar extension is diagonalizable.

:::
