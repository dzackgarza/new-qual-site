::: {.problem}
Let $P$ be a finite $p$-group. Prove that every non-trivial normal subgroup of $P$ intersects the center $Z(P)$ non-trivially (that is, $N \trianglelefteq P$ with $N \ne \{e\}$ implies $N \cap Z(P) \ne \{e\}$).
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Conjugation by $P$ defines an action on the normal subgroup $N$, whose fixed-point set is $N\cap Z(P)$.

::: {.proof}
Normality makes $g\cdot x=gxg^{-1}$ an element of $N$ for all $g\in P$ and $x\in N$. An element $x$ is fixed by this action precisely when $gxg^{-1}=x$ for every $g\in P$, equivalently when $x\in Z(P)$.
:::

<1>2. The cardinality $|N\cap Z(P)|$ is divisible by $p$.

::: {.proof}
Partition $N$ into the orbits of the action from step <1>1. By orbit–stabilizer, the orbit of any $x\in N$ has size $[P:C_P(x)]$, a power of $p$. Any orbit that is not a singleton therefore has cardinality divisible by $p$. Since $N$ is a nontrivial subgroup of the finite $p$-group $P$, its order is a positive power of $p$. The class equation yields
\[
|N|=|N\cap Z(P)|+\sum_{\mathcal O:\,|\mathcal O|>1}|\mathcal O|.
\]
Both the left side and every summand are divisible by $p$, so $p\mid |N\cap Z(P)|$.
:::

<1>3. The intersection $N\cap Z(P)$ is nontrivial.

::: {.proof}
It contains the identity and hence is nonempty. By step <1>2 its cardinality is a positive multiple of $p$, so it has at least $p>1$ elements.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives the required nonidentity central element of $N$.
:::
:::
