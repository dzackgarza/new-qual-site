::: {.problem}
Let $P$ be a finite $p$-group. Prove that every non-trivial normal subgroup of $P$ intersects the center $Z(P)$ non-trivially (that is, $N \trianglelefteq P$ with $N \ne \{e\}$ implies $N \cap Z(P) \ne \{e\}$).
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Conjugation by $P$ defines an action on the normal subgroup $N$, whose fixed-point set is $N\cap Z(P)$.

::: pf-proof

Normality makes $g\cdot x=gxg^{-1}$ an element of $N$ for all $g\in P$ and $x\in N$. An element $x$ is fixed by this action precisely when $gxg^{-1}=x$ for every $g\in P$, equivalently when $x\in Z(P)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The cardinality $|N\cap Z(P)|$ is divisible by $p$.

::: pf-proof

Partition $N$ into the orbits of the action from step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. By orbit–stabilizer, the orbit of any $x\in N$ has size $[P:C_P(x)]$, a power of $p$. Any orbit that is not a singleton therefore has cardinality divisible by $p$. Since $N$ is a nontrivial subgroup of the finite $p$-group $P$, its order is a positive power of $p$. The class equation yields
$$
|N|=|N\cap Z(P)|+\sum_{\mathcal O:\,|\mathcal O|>1}|\mathcal O|.
$$
Both the left side and every summand are divisible by $p$, so $p\mid |N\cap Z(P)|$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The intersection $N\cap Z(P)$ is nontrivial.

::: pf-proof

It contains the identity and hence is nonempty. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref} its cardinality is a positive multiple of $p$, so it has at least $p>1$ elements.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives the required nonidentity central element of $N$.

:::
