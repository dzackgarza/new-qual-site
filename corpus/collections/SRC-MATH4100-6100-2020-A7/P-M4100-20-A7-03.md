::: {.problem}
(a) Let $f : [ 0 , 1 ] \to [ 0 , 1 ]$ be continuous.
Prove that f must have a fixed point; that is, show that there must exist $x \in [ 0 , 1 ]$ with the property that $f ( x ) = x$

(b) Let $f : [ 0 , 1 ] \to \mathbb { R }$ be continuous with $f ( 0 ) = f ( 1 )$

Show that there must exist $x \in [ 0 , 1 / 2 ]$ with the property that $f ( x ) = f ( x + 1 / 2 )$
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

For part (a), define
$$
g(x)=f(x)-x,
\qquad
x\in[0,1].
$$
Then $g$ is continuous.


::: pf-proof

The function $f$ is continuous by hypothesis and the identity function
$x\mapsto x$ is continuous. Therefore their difference is continuous.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
g(0)\geq0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
g(1)\leq0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
f([0,1])\subseteq[0,1],
$$
one has
$$
f(0)\geq0
$$
and
$$
f(1)\leq1.
$$
Hence
$$
g(0)=f(0)\geq0
$$
and
$$
g(1)=f(1)-1\leq0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists $x\in[0,1]$ such that
$$
g(x)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

If either endpoint value is zero, take that endpoint. Otherwise
$$
g(0)>0>g(1),
$$
and the Intermediate Value Theorem gives some $x\in(0,1)$ with $g(x)=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The function $f$ has a fixed point.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>3,
$$
0=g(x)=f(x)-x,
$$
so
$$
f(x)=x.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step <1>4.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For part (b), define
$$
h(x)=f(x)-f\left(x+\frac12\right),
\qquad
x\in\left[0,\frac12\right].
$$
Then $h$ is continuous.


::: pf-proof

The functions
$$
x\longmapsto f(x)
$$
and
$$
x\longmapsto f\left(x+\frac12\right)
$$
are continuous on $[0,1/2]$. Their difference is therefore continuous.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The endpoint values of $h$ satisfy
$$
h\left(\frac12\right)=-h(0).
$$


::: pf-proof

Using the hypothesis $f(0)=f(1)$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
h(0)
&=
f(0)-f\left(\frac12\right),\\
h\left(\frac12\right)
&=
f\left(\frac12\right)-f(1)\\
&=
f\left(\frac12\right)-f(0)\\
&=
-h(0).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists
$$
x\in\left[0,\frac12\right]
$$
such that
$$
h(x)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $h(0)=0$, take $x=0$. Otherwise step <1>7 shows that the endpoint values
$h(0)$ and $h(1/2)$ have opposite signs. The Intermediate Value Theorem then
gives a zero of $h$ in $(0,1/2)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists
$$
x\in\left[0,\frac12\right]
$$
such that
$$
f(x)=f\left(x+\frac12\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>8,
$$
0=h(x)
=
f(x)-f\left(x+\frac12\right).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step <1>9.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>5 and <1>10 establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.
