::: {.problem}
(a) If A is a non-empty subset of R, we define the distance from $x \in \mathbb { R }$ to A by

$$
\rho _ { A } ( x ) = \operatorname* { i n f } _ { y \in A } | x - y | .
$$

i. Prove that $\rho _ { A } ( x ) = 0$ if and only if $x \in { \overline { { A } } }$

ii.
Prove that $\rho _ { A }$ is uniformly continuous on R, by showing that

$$
| \rho _ { A } ( x ) - \rho _ { A } ( y ) | \leq | x - y |
$$

for all $x , y \in \mathbb { R } .$

(b) Let A and B be disjoint non-empty closed subsets of R, and define

$$
f ( x ) : = \frac { \rho _ { A } ( x ) } { \rho _ { A } ( x ) + \rho _ { B } ( x ) }
$$

for each $x \in \mathbb { R }$ . Show that f is a continuous function from R into [0, 1] such that $f ( x ) = 0$ for all $x \in A$, and $f ( x ) = 1$ for all $x \in B$

Note that this in particular establishes a converse to Q1b: Every closed set in R is $Z ( f )$ for some continuous function $f : \mathbb { R } \to \mathbb { R }$ . It also gives a proof (but bot the easiest one) that R cannot be written as a disjoint union of two non-empty closed subsets.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $x\in\overline A$, then
$$
\rho_A(x)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $x\in\overline A$, every neighborhood of $x$
meets $A$. Hence there exists $y\in A$ such that
$$
|x-y|<\varepsilon.
$$
By definition of infimum,
$$
0\leq\rho_A(x)\leq|x-y|<\varepsilon.
$$
Since this holds for every $\varepsilon>0$, one has $\rho_A(x)=0$.
:::

<1>2. If
$$
\rho_A(x)=0,
$$
then $x\in\overline A$.

::: {.proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since
$$
\inf_{y\in A}|x-y|=0,
$$
there exists $y\in A$ such that
$$
|x-y|<\varepsilon.
$$
Thus every open interval centered at $x$ meets $A$, which means
$$
x\in\overline A.
$$
:::

<1>3. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\rho_A(x)=0
\iff
x\in\overline A.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 prove the two implications.
:::

<1>4. For every $x,y\in\RR$,
$$
\rho_A(x)
\leq
|x-y|+\rho_A(y).
$$

::: {.proof}
For every $z\in A$, the triangle inequality gives
$$
|x-z|
\leq
|x-y|+|y-z|.
$$
Taking the infimum over $z\in A$ yields
$$
\inf_{z\in A}|x-z|
\leq
|x-y|
+
\inf_{z\in A}|y-z|,
$$
which is the claimed inequality.
:::

<1>5. For every $x,y\in\RR$,
$$
\boxed{
|\rho_A(x)-\rho_A(y)|
\leq
|x-y|.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 gives
$$
\rho_A(x)-\rho_A(y)
\leq
|x-y|.
$$
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives
$$
\rho_A(y)-\rho_A(x)
\leq
|x-y|.
$$
Together these are equivalent to the absolute-value inequality.
:::

<1>6. The function $\rho_A$ is uniformly continuous on $\RR$.

::: {.proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
$$
\delta=\varepsilon.
$$
Then step <1>5 gives
$$
|x-y|<\delta
\implies
|\rho_A(x)-\rho_A(y)|
\leq
|x-y|
<
\varepsilon.
$$
The same $\delta$ works for all $x,y$.
:::

<1>7. Part (a) follows.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves part (a)(i), and steps <1>5--<1>6 prove part (a)(ii).
:::

<1>8. Suppose $A$ and $B$ are disjoint nonempty closed subsets of $\RR$.
Then for every $x\in\RR$,
$$
\rho_A(x)+\rho_B(x)>0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $A$ and $B$ are closed,
$$
\overline A=A,
\qquad
\overline B=B.
$$
If
$$
\rho_A(x)+\rho_B(x)=0,
$$
then both nonnegative terms must vanish. By step <1>3,
$$
x\in A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x\in B,
$$
contradicting $A\cap B=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>9. The function
$$
f(x)
=
\frac{\rho_A(x)}
{\rho_A(x)+\rho_B(x)}
$$
is continuous on $\RR$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6, both $\rho_A$ and $\rho_B$ are continuous. Their sum is
continuous and never zero by step <1>8. Therefore the quotient is continuous.
:::

<1>10. For every $x\in\RR$,
$$
0\leq f(x)\leq1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Both distance functions are nonnegative, and the denominator is positive by
step <1>8. Hence
$$
0
\leq
\rho_A(x)
\leq
\rho_A(x)+\rho_B(x).
$$
Dividing by the positive denominator gives the claim.
:::

<1>11. If $x\in A$, then
$$
f(x)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $A$ is closed and $x\in A$, step <1>3 gives
$$
\rho_A(x)=0.
$$
Because $A$ and $B$ are disjoint,
$$
x\notin B,
$$
so step <1>3 gives
$$
\rho_B(x)>0.
$$
Therefore
$$
f(x)=0.
$$
:::

<1>12. If $x\in B$, then
$$
f(x)=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Now
$$
\rho_B(x)=0
$$
and, because $x\notin A$,
$$
\rho_A(x)>0.
$$
Hence
$$
f(x)
=
\frac{\rho_A(x)}{\rho_A(x)}
=1.
$$
:::

<1>13. Thus
$$
\boxed{
f:\RR\to[0,1]
}
$$
is continuous, equals $0$ on $A$, and equals $1$ on $B$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>9 gives continuity, step <1>10 gives the stated codomain, and
steps <1>11--<1>12 give the prescribed values.
:::

<1>14. Part (b) follows.

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>13.
:::

<1>15. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>7 and <1>14 establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.
:::
