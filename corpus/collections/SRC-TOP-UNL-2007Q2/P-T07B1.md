::: {.problem}
Let $X = T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$ and $X = U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_n$ where each $U_i$ is open and simply-connected.
Show that there is an $i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}$ for which
\[
V_i := U_i \cap (U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_{i-1})
\]
is either empty or not path-connected.
(Hint: argue by contradiction.)
:::

::: {.solution}
For $i=1,\ldots,n$, set
\[
W_i=U_1\cup\cdots\cup U_i.
\]

<1>1. We must have $n\ge2$.
::: {.proof}
If $n=1$, then
\[
T^2=X=U_1.
\]
But $U_1$ is simply connected by hypothesis, whereas
\[
\pi_1(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z^2\ne0.
\]
This is impossible.
:::

<1>2. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that for every $i=2,\ldots,n$, the set
\[
V_i=U_i\cap W_{i-1}
\]
is nonempty and path-connected.
Then every $W_i$ is simply connected.
::: {.proof}
We argue by induction on $i$.

For $i=1$,
\[
W_1=U_1
\]
is simply connected by hypothesis.

Now let $i\ge2$ and assume $W_{i-1}$ is simply connected.
Both $W_{i-1}$ and $U_i$ are open in $X$, and both are path-connected because they are simply connected.
By the contradiction hypothesis,
\[
W_{i-1}\cap U_i=V_i
\]
is nonempty and path-connected.
Hence
\[
W_i=W_{i-1}\cup U_i
\]
is path-connected.

Choose a basepoint in $V_i$.
The Seifert--van Kampen theorem gives
\[
\pi_1(W_i)
\cong
\pi_1(W_{i-1})*_{\pi_1(V_i)}\pi_1(U_i).
\]
Both outer groups are trivial, since $W_{i-1}$ and $U_i$ are simply connected.
Therefore
\[
\pi_1(W_i)=0.
\]
Together with path-connectedness, this says that $W_i$ is simply connected.

The induction is complete.
:::

<1>3. The assumption in <1>2 is impossible.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2,
\[
W_n
\]
is simply connected.
But the $U_i$ cover $X$, so
\[
W_n=X=T^2.
\]
Thus $T^2$ would be simply connected, contradicting
\[
\pi_1(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>4. Consequently, for some $i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}$, the set $V_i$ is empty or is not path-connected.
::: {.proof}
This is the negation of the impossible assumption in <1>2.
:::
