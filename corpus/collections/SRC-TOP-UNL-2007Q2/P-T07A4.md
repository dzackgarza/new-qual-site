::: {.problem}
A space $(X,\mathcal{T})$ is called *locally compact* if, for every $x \in X$ and $x \in U \in \mathcal{T}$, there is a compact set $C \subseteq U$ containing an open neighborhood of $x$.
Show that the Cartesian product of two locally compact spaces is locally compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Choose basic open neighborhood in the product:
<2>1. Let $(X, \mathcal{T}_X)$ and $(Y, \mathcal{T}_Y)$ be locally compact topological spaces.
Let $(x, y) \in X \times Y$ and let $W \subseteq X \times Y$ be an open neighborhood containing $(x, y)$.
::: {.proof}
setup.
:::
<2>2. By the definition of the product topology, there exist open sets $U \in \mathcal{T}_X$ and $V \in \mathcal{T}_Y$ such that:
\[
(x, y) \in U \times V \subseteq W.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of standard basis for product topology.
:::

<1>2. Apply local compactness to each factor:
<2>1. Since $X$ is locally compact and $x \in U \in \mathcal{T}_X$, there exists a compact set $K \subseteq U$ and an open neighborhood $U' \subseteq X$ such that:
\[
x \in U' \subseteq K \subseteq U.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of local compactness of $X$.
:::
<2>2. Symmetrically, since $Y$ is locally compact and $y \in V \in \mathcal{T}_Y$, there exists a compact set $L \subseteq V$ and an open neighborhood $V' \subseteq Y$ such that:
\[
y \in V' \subseteq L \subseteq V.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of local compactness of $Y$.
:::

<1>3. Construct the compact set in the product:
<2>1. The set $U' \times V'$ is open in $X \times Y$ and contains $(x, y)$.
::: {.proof}
product of open sets is open.
:::
<2>2. By Tychonoff’s Theorem (or the finite product theorem for compact spaces), $C = K \times L$ is a compact subset of $X \times Y$.
::: {.proof}
product of compact spaces is compact.
:::
<2>3. By construction:
\[
(x, y) \in U' \times V' \subseteq K \times L \subseteq U \times V \subseteq W.
\]
Thus $C = K \times L$ is a compact subset of $W$ containing the open neighborhood $U' \times V'$ of $(x, y)$.
::: {.proof}
Cartesian product of inclusions.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
The Cartesian product $X \times Y$ is locally compact. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>3.
:::
:::
