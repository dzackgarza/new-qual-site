::: {.problem}
A space $(X,\mathcal{T})$ is called *locally compact* if, for every $x \in X$ and $x \in U \in \mathcal{T}$, there is a compact set $C \subseteq U$ containing an open neighborhood of $x$.
Show that the Cartesian product of two locally compact spaces is locally compact.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Show $X\times Y$ is locally compact when $X$ and $Y$ are locally compact.

<1> Fix a point $(x,y)\in X\times Y$ and an open set $W\ni (x,y)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Choose a basic neighborhood $U\times V\subseteq W$ with $x\in U$, $y\in V$.

<1> Use local compactness of each factor.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $X$ is locally compact, there is compact $K\subseteq U$ and open $U'\ni x$ with $U'\subseteq K\subseteq U$.
    <2>2. Since $Y$ is locally compact, there is compact $L\subseteq V$ and open $V'\ni y$ with $V'\subseteq L\subseteq V$.
    <2>3. Then $U'\times V'$ is an open neighborhood of $(x,y)$.

<1> Build the compact neighborhood inside $W$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The set $K\times L$ is compact by the finite-product theorem for compact spaces.
    <2>2. We have $U'\times V'\subseteq K\times L\subseteq U\times V\subseteq W$.
    <2>3. Therefore $(x,y)$ has an open neighborhood with compact closure inside $W$.

<1> Conclusion.
    <2>1. By definition, $X\times Y$ is locally compact.
