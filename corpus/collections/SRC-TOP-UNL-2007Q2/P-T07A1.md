::: {.problem}
A space $(X,\mathcal{T})$ is called *extremally disconnected* if the closure of every open set is open; that is, whenever $U \in \mathcal{T}$ we have $\operatorname{cl}(U) \in \mathcal{T}$.
Show that in an extremally disconnected space, if $U,V \in \mathcal{T}$ and $U \cap V = \varnothing$, then $$\operatorname{cl}(U) \cap \operatorname{cl}(V) = \varnothing.$$

*(Hint: first show that $U \cap \operatorname{cl}(V) = \varnothing$.)*
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $U,V\subseteq X$ be open and suppose
\[
U\cap V=\varnothing.
\]

<1>1. We have
\[
U\cap\operatorname{cl}(V)=\varnothing.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $x\in U$.
Because $U$ is an open neighborhood of $x$ and
\[
U\cap V=\varnothing,
\]
there is a neighborhood of $x$ that misses $V$.
By the neighborhood characterization of closure,
\[
x\notin\operatorname{cl}(V).
\]
Since this holds for every $x\in U$,
\[
U\cap\operatorname{cl}(V)=\varnothing.
\]
:::

<1>2. The set
\[
X\setminus\operatorname{cl}(V)
\]
is closed and contains $U$.
::: {.proof}
By extremal disconnectedness, the closure of the open set $V$ is itself open.
Therefore
\[
X\setminus\operatorname{cl}(V)
\]
is closed.
By <1>1,
\[
U\subseteq X\setminus\operatorname{cl}(V).
\]
:::

<1>3. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{cl}(U)\cap\operatorname{cl}(V)=\varnothing.
\]
::: {.proof}
The closure $\operatorname{cl}(U)$ is the smallest closed subset of $X$ containing $U$.
By <1>2, the closed set
\[
X\setminus\operatorname{cl}(V)
\]
contains $U$.
Hence
\[
\operatorname{cl}(U)
\subseteq
X\setminus\operatorname{cl}(V).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\operatorname{cl}(U)\cap\operatorname{cl}(V)=\varnothing.
\]
:::
:::
