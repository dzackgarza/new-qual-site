::: {.problem}
Let $p : (\widetilde{X},y_0) \to (X,x_0)$ be a covering space projection, $x_0 \in A \subseteq X$, and $\iota : A \to X$ the inclusion map.
Show that $$q = p|_{p^{-1}(A)} : (p^{-1}(A),y_0) \to (A,x_0)$$ is also a covering space, and $$\ker(\iota_*) \subseteq \operatorname{im}(q_*) \subseteq \pi_1(A,x_0).$$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The restriction
\[
q=p|_{p^{-1}(A)}:p^{-1}(A)\longrightarrow A
\]
is a covering map.
::: {.proof}
Let $a\in A$.
Since $p:\widetilde X\to X$ is a covering map, there is an open neighborhood $U\subseteq X$ of $a$ such that
\[
p^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_\lambda,
\]
where each $U_\lambda$ is open in $\widetilde X$ and
\[
p|_{U_\lambda}:U_\lambda\longrightarrow U
\]
is a homeomorphism.

Set
\[
V=U\cap A.
\]
Then $V$ is an open neighborhood of $a$ in the subspace $A$.
Moreover,
\[
q^{-1}(V)
=
p^{-1}(U\cap A)
=
\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
\left(U_\lambda\cap p^{-1}(A)\right).
\]
For each $\lambda$,
\[
U_\lambda\cap p^{-1}(A)
=
(p|_{U_\lambda})^{-1}(V),
\]
so it is open in the subspace $p^{-1}(A)$, and restriction of the sheet homeomorphism gives a homeomorphism
\[
q|_{U_\lambda\cap p^{-1}(A)}:
U_\lambda\cap p^{-1}(A)
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
V.
\]
Thus $V$ is evenly covered by $q$.
Since $a$ was arbitrary, $q$ is a covering map.
:::

<1>2. Let
\[
[\gamma]\in\ker(\iota_*),
\]
where $\gamma:I\to A$ is a loop based at $x_0$.
The lift of $\gamma$ through $p$ starting at $y_0$ is a loop.
::: {.proof}
The condition
\[
[\gamma]\in\ker(\iota_*)
\]
means that the loop
\[
\iota\circ\gamma:I\longrightarrow X
\]
is nullhomotopic relative to its basepoint $x_0$.

Let
\[
\widetilde\gamma:I\longrightarrow\widetilde X
\]
be the unique $p$-lift of $\iota\circ\gamma$ satisfying
\[
\widetilde\gamma(0)=y_0.
\]
Choose a based homotopy in $X$ from $\iota\circ\gamma$ to the constant loop at $x_0$.
The covering homotopy property lifts this homotopy starting from $\widetilde\gamma$.
The endpoints of the lifted paths remain fixed during the lifted homotopy because they lift the constant basepoint path.
At the other end of the homotopy, the lift starting at $y_0$ of the constant loop at $x_0$ is the constant loop at $y_0$.
Therefore
\[
\widetilde\gamma(1)=y_0.
\]
Hence $\widetilde\gamma$ is a loop based at $y_0$.
:::

<1>3. The lift $\widetilde\gamma$ from <1>2 lies entirely in $p^{-1}(A)$ and is a $q$-lift of $\gamma$.
::: {.proof}
For every $t\in I$,
\[
p(\widetilde\gamma(t))
=
\iota(\gamma(t))
=
\gamma(t)
\in A.
\]
Thus
\[
\widetilde\gamma(t)\in p^{-1}(A)
\]
for all $t$.
On this subspace $q$ is the restriction of $p$, so
\[
q\circ\widetilde\gamma=\gamma.
\]
By <1>2, $\widetilde\gamma$ is a loop based at $y_0$.
:::

<1>4. We have
\[
\ker(\iota_*)\subseteq\operatorname{im}(q_*).
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $[\gamma]\in\ker(\iota_*)$.
By <1>3, there is a loop $\widetilde\gamma$ in $p^{-1}(A)$ based at $y_0$ such that
\[
q\circ\widetilde\gamma=\gamma.
\]
Therefore
\[
q_*([\widetilde\gamma])=[\gamma],
\]
so $[\gamma]\in\operatorname{im}(q_*)$.
:::

<1>5. Finally,
\[
\operatorname{im}(q_*)\subseteq\pi_1(A,x_0),
\]
and hence
\[
\ker(\iota_*)
\subseteq
\operatorname{im}(q_*)
\subseteq
\pi_1(A,x_0).
\]
::: {.proof}
By definition,
\[
q_*:
\pi_1(p^{-1}(A),y_0)
\longrightarrow
\pi_1(A,x_0).
\]
Thus its image is a subgroup of $\pi_1(A,x_0)$.
Combine this with <1>4.
:::
