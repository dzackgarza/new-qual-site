::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the surface of genus $2$ shown below and let $Y \supseteq X$ be the region of $\mathbb{R}^3$ that it encloses.
Show that there is no retraction $r : Y \to X$ (that is, no continuous map for which $r(x) = x$ for all $x \in X$).

![](../../assets/Topology/figures/2007Q2-topology-B3.png)
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
i:X\hookrightarrow Y
\]
be the inclusion.

<1>1. If a retraction
\[
r:Y\longrightarrow X
\]
existed, then the induced homomorphism
\[
i_*:H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)
\]
would be injective.
::: {.proof}
A retraction satisfies
\[
r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_X.
\]
Functoriality of singular homology gives
\[
r_*\circ i_*=(r\circ i)_*=\operatorname{id}_{H_1(X;\mathbb Z)}.
\]
Thus $i_*$ has a left inverse, so $i_*$ is injective.
:::

<1>2. The genus-$2$ surface $X$ has
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4.
\]
::: {.proof}
A closed orientable surface of genus $g$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, $2g$ $1$-cells, and one $2$-cell whose attaching word is the product of the $g$ commutators.
After abelianization, the commutator attaching word gives zero boundary in degree $1$.
For $g=2$ this yields
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{2g}=\mathbb Z^4.
\]
:::

<1>3. The region $Y$ bounded by $X$ has
\[
H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
The supplied figure shows $Y$ as the standard genus-$2$ handlebody.
Collapse each solid handle to its core circle and the remaining central $3$-ball to the common basepoint.
This gives a deformation retraction
\[
Y\simeq S^1\vee S^1.
\]
Therefore
\[
H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(S^1\vee S^1;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
:::

<1>4. No retraction $r:Y\to X$ exists.
::: {.proof}
If a retraction existed, <1>1--<1>3 would give an injective homomorphism
\[
\mathbb Z^4\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Tensoring with $\mathbb Q$ preserves injectivity because $\mathbb Q$ is a flat $\mathbb Z$-module, so this would induce an injection
\[
\mathbb Q^4\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q^2,
\]
which is impossible by dimension.
Hence the assumed retraction cannot exist.
:::
:::
