::: {.problem}
Suppose that $X$ is compact, $Y$ is Hausdorff, $f : X \to Y$ is a continuous surjective map, and $g : Y \to Z$ is a function.
Show that if $g \circ f : X \to Z$ is continuous, then $g$ is continuous.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that if $f: X \to Y$ is a continuous surjection from a compact space $X$ to a Hausdorff space $Y$, and $g \circ f: X \to Z$ is continuous, then $g: Y \to Z$ is continuous.

<1>1. The map $f: X \to Y$ is a closed map:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $A \subseteq X$ be any closed subset of $X$.
    <2>2. Since $X$ is compact, the closed subset $A$ is compact.
    <2>3. Because $f$ is continuous, the image $f(A) \subseteq Y$ is a compact subset of $Y$.
    <2>4. Since $Y$ is Hausdorff, every compact subset of $Y$ is closed.
    <2>5. Therefore $f(A)$ is closed in $Y$, proving that $f$ maps closed sets to closed sets (i.e. $f$ is a closed map).

<1>2. The map $f$ is a quotient map:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Any continuous, surjective, and closed map is a quotient map.
    <2>2. Indeed, if $V \subseteq Y$ has $f^{-1}(V)$ closed in $X$, then $V = f(f^{-1}(V))$ (by surjectivity of $f$) is the image of a closed set under a closed map, so $V$ is closed in $Y$.

<1>3. Continuity of $g$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $F \subseteq Z$ be any closed subset of $Z$.
    <2>2. By the continuity of the composite map $h = g \circ f: X \to Z$, the preimage
    $$h^{-1}(F) = (g \circ f)^{-1}(F) = f^{-1}(g^{-1}(F))$$
    is a closed subset of $X$.
    <2>3. Applying $f$ to both sides and using the surjectivity of $f$:
    $$f\left(f^{-1}(g^{-1}(F))\right) = g^{-1}(F).$$
    <2>4. Since $f$ is a closed map (<1>1) and $f^{-1}(g^{-1}(F))$ is closed in $X$, its image $g^{-1}(F)$ is closed in $Y$.
    <2>5. Since the preimage of every closed set in $Z$ under $g$ is closed in $Y$, $g$ is continuous.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    *Proof:*
    The map $g: Y \to Z$ is continuous.
:::
