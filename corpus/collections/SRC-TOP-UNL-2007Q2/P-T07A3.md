::: {.problem}
Suppose that $X$ is compact, $Y$ is Hausdorff, $f : X \to Y$ is a continuous surjective map, and $g : Y \to Z$ is a function.
Show that if $g \circ f : X \to Z$ is continuous, then $g$ is continuous.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that $g$ is continuous.

<1> Show $f$ is a closed map.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $C\subseteq Y$ be closed.
    <2>2. Then $f^{-1}(C)\subseteq X$ is compact (as a closed subset of compact $X$).
    <2>3. $f(f^{-1}(C))=C$ by surjectivity.
    <2>4. Since $f(f^{-1}(C))$ is the continuous image of a compact set into Hausdorff $Y$, $C$ is compact and therefore closed.

<1> Preimages of closed sets in $Z$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $F\subseteq Z$ be closed.
    <2>2. $(g\circ f)^{-1}(F)=f^{-1}(g^{-1}(F))$ is closed in $X$ by continuity of $g\circ f$.
    <2>3. Apply step <2.1> with $C=g^{-1}(F)$ to get $g^{-1}(F)$ closed in $Y$.
    <2>4. Therefore preimages of closed sets under $g$ are closed.

<1> Conclude continuity.
    *Proof:* A map is continuous iff preimages of closed sets are closed, so $g$ is continuous.
