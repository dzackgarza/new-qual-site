::: {.problem}
Suppose that $X$ is compact, $Y$ is Hausdorff, $f : X \to Y$ is a continuous surjective map, and $g : Y \to Z$ is a function.
Show that if $g \circ f : X \to Z$ is continuous, then $g$ is continuous.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The map
\[
f:X\longrightarrow Y
\]
is closed.
::: {.proof}
Let $A\subseteq X$ be closed.
Since $X$ is compact, $A$ is compact.
Continuity of $f$ implies that
\[
f(A)
\]
is compact in $Y$.
Because $Y$ is Hausdorff, compact subsets of $Y$ are closed.
Hence $f(A)$ is closed, and therefore $f$ is a closed map.
:::

<1>2. The map $f$ is a quotient map.
::: {.proof}
The map $f$ is continuous and surjective by hypothesis.
It remains to verify the quotient condition.

Let $V\subseteq Y$ and suppose
\[
f^{-1}(V)
\]
is closed in $X$.
By <1>1,
\[
f\bigl(f^{-1}(V)\bigr)
\]
is closed in $Y$.
Surjectivity gives
\[
f\bigl(f^{-1}(V)\bigr)=V.
\]
Thus $V$ is closed.
Equivalently, a subset of $Y$ is open exactly when its inverse image under $f$ is open, so $f$ is a quotient map.
:::

<1>3. For every closed set $F\subseteq Z$, the inverse image
\[
g^{-1}(F)
\]
is closed in $Y$.
::: {.proof}
Let $F\subseteq Z$ be closed.
Since $g\circ f$ is continuous,
\[
(g\circ f)^{-1}(F)
=
f^{-1}\bigl(g^{-1}(F)\bigr)
\]
is closed in $X$.
By <1>1, its image under $f$ is closed in $Y$.
Using surjectivity of $f$,
\[
f\left(f^{-1}\bigl(g^{-1}(F)\bigr)\right)
=
g^{-1}(F).
\]
Hence $g^{-1}(F)$ is closed.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
\[
g:Y\longrightarrow Z
\]
is continuous.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, the inverse image under $g$ of every closed subset of $Z$ is closed in $Y$.
This is the closed-set characterization of continuity.
:::
:::
