::: {.problem}
Use $n$th roots of unity to show that
$$
2^{n-1} \sin\qty{\pi \over n} \sin\qty{2\pi \over n} \cdots \sin\qty{(n-1)\pi \over n} = n
.$$

> Hint:
$$
1 - \cos(2\theta) &= 2\sin^2(\theta) \\
2 \sin(2\theta) &= 2\sin(\theta) \cos(\theta)
.$$
:::

::: {.concept}
\envlist

- $\zeta_n \definedas e^{2\pi i \over n}$
- $P_n(z) \definedas \prod_{1\leq j \leq n-1}(z-\zeta_n^j)$
- $P_n(1) = n$, since $P_n(z) = {z^n-1\over z-1} = \sum_{0\leq j\leq n-1} z^j$.
- $\sin(z) = \qty{e^{iz} - e^{-iz} \over 2i}$.
- $\prod_k \exp(c_k) = \exp\qty{\sum_k c_k}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume $n\geq2$ and put $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The product of the nontrivial differences from unity is
$$
\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-\zeta^k)=n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The factorization $z^n-1=(z-1)\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(z-\zeta^k)$ gives the polynomial identity
$$
\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(z-\zeta^k)=1+z+\cdots+z^{n-1}.
$$
Evaluating at $z=1$ proves the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The requested sine product satisfies
$$
\boxed{2^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{n}\right)=n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Euler's formula implies, for $1\leq k<n$,
$$
2i\sin(k\pi/n)=e^{ik\pi/n}(1-\zeta^{-k}).
$$
Multiplying these equations and using $\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}k=n(n-1)/2$ gives
$$
(2i)^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\sin(k\pi/n)
=e^{i\pi(n-1)/2}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-\zeta^{-k})
=i^{n-1}n.
$$
The last equality uses step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and the permutation $k\mapsto n-k$ of $\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$. Division by $i^{n-1}$ proves the formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} establishes the required identity.

:::
