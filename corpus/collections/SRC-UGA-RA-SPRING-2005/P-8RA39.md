::: problem
If $0 < p \leq 1$, prove that $\frac{\sin x}{x^{p}}$ is not integrable on $(0,\infty)$, but
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x^{p}}\, dx = \lim_{r \rightarrow \infty}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\sin x}{x^{p}}\, dx
\]
exists.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. Show that the function is not Lebesgue integrable.
::: proof
For every integer $k\ge1$,
\[
\int_{k\pi}^{(k+1)\pi}\frac{|\sin x|}{x^p}\,dx
\ge \frac{1}{((k+1)\pi)^p}
\int_{k\pi}^{(k+1)\pi}|\sin x|\,dx
=\frac{2}{\pi^p(k+1)^p}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_\pi^{(N+1)\pi}\frac{|\sin x|}{x^p}\,dx
\ge \frac{2}{\pi^p}\sum_{k=1}^N\frac1{(k+1)^p}.
\]
Since $0<p\le1$, the series on the right diverges. Hence
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{|\sin x|}{x^p}\,dx=\infty,
\]
so $\sin x/x^p\notin L^1(0,\infty)$.
:::

<1>2. Prove convergence near the origin.
::: proof
For $0<x\le1$,
\[
\left|\frac{\sin x}{x^p}\right|\le x^{1-p}.
\]
Because $1-p\ge0$, the right-hand side is integrable on $(0,1)$. Thus
\[
\int_0^1\frac{\sin x}{x^p}\,dx
\]
converges absolutely.
:::

<1>3. Prove convergence at infinity.
::: proof
For $r>1$, integration by parts gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^r\frac{\sin x}{x^p}\,dx
&=\left[-\frac{\cos x}{x^p}\right]_1^r
-p\int_1^r\frac{\cos x}{x^{p+1}}\,dx\\
&=\cos1-\frac{\cos r}{r^p}
-p\int_1^r\frac{\cos x}{x^{p+1}}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\]
Now
\[
\frac{\cos r}{r^p}\to0
\]
because $p>0$, and
\[
\int_1^\infty\left|\frac{\cos x}{x^{p+1}}\right|dx
\le \int_1^\infty x^{-p-1}\,dx<\infty.
\]
Hence the limit
\[
\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_1^r\frac{\sin x}{x^p}\,dx
\]
exists. Together with Step 2, this proves that
\[
\boxed{\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_0^r\frac{\sin x}{x^p}\,dx\text{ exists}.}
\]
Thus the improper integral converges, although the function is not Lebesgue integrable.
:::
:::
