::: {.problem}
Suppose $A$ is an uncountable subset of $\RR$ and $A'$ is the set of limit points of $A$.
Prove $A \cap A'$ is uncountable.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

Recall that $x$ is a limit point of $A$ when every neighbourhood of $x$ meets $A\setminus\{x\}$, and that $A'$ is the set of limit points of $A$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #isolated}
$A\setminus A'$ is the set of points $x\in A$ for which there is an open interval $I_x$ with $I_x\cap A=\{x\}$.

::: pf-proof
If $x\in A\setminus A'$ then $x$ is not a limit point of $A$, so some neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ satisfies $(U\setminus\{x\})\cap A=\varnothing$; taking an open interval $I_x$ with $x\in I_x\subseteq U$ gives $I_x\cap A\subseteq\{x\}$, and $x\in A$ gives $I_x\cap A=\{x\}$. Conversely, if $I_x\cap A=\{x\}$ then $(I_x\setminus\{x\})\cap A=\varnothing$, so $x$ is not a limit point of $A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rational}
There is an injective map from $A\setminus A'$ into the set of pairs of rationals.

::: pf-proof
For each $x\in A\setminus A'$ choose $I_x$ as in step [](#isolated){.pf-ref}; since the intervals with rational endpoints form a base for the topology of $\RR$, choose rationals $p_x<q_x$ with $x\in(p_x,q_x)\subseteq I_x$. If $x\ne y$ in $A\setminus A'$ had $(p_x,q_x)=(p_y,q_y)=:(p,q)$, then $y\in(p,q)\subseteq I_x$ with $y\in A$, contradicting $I_x\cap A=\{x\}$. So $x\mapsto(p_x,q_x)$ is injective.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #countable}
$A\setminus A'$ is at most countable.

::: pf-proof
$\QQ\times\QQ$ is countable, being a product of two countable sets, and by step [](#rational){.pf-ref} $A\setminus A'$ injects into it.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
$A\cap A'$ is uncountable.

::: pf-proof
$A=(A\cap A')\cup(A\setminus A')$ and the two parts are disjoint. If $A\cap A'$ were at most countable, then $A$ would be the union of two at most countable sets, hence at most countable, contradicting the hypothesis that $A$ is uncountable. So $A\cap A'$ is uncountable.
:::
