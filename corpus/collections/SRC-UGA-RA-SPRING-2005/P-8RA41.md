::: {.problem}
If $\mu$ is Lebesgue measure on $\RR$ and $f \in L(\RR,d\mu)$, prove that $F$ is absolutely continuous where
$$
F(x) = \int_{(-\infty,x]} f\, d\mu.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #integral-continuity}
For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that every measurable $E$ with $m(E)<\delta$ satisfies $\int_E\abs{f}\,dm<\varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof
This is the absolute continuity of the Lebesgue integral for a fixed $f\in L^1(\RR)$: the measure $E\mapsto\int_E\abs{f}\,dm$ is finite and vanishes on $m$-null sets, hence is absolutely continuous with respect to $m$, which is exactly the stated property.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #finite}
$F(x)=\int_{(-\infty,x]}f\,dm$ is a real number for every $x$.

::: pf-proof
$\abs{\int_{(-\infty,x]}f\,dm}\le\int_{(-\infty,x]}\abs{f}\,dm\le\norm{f}_{L^1}<\infty$, so the integral converges absolutely.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #difference}
For $a<b$, $F(b)-F(a)=\int_{(a,b]}f\,dm$.

::: pf-proof
$(-\infty,b]=(-\infty,a]\cup(a,b]$ is a disjoint union, so additivity of the integral over measurable sets gives $F(b)=F(a)+\int_{(a,b]}f\,dm$; both terms are finite by step [](#finite){.pf-ref}, so the subtraction is legitimate.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #estimate}
If $(a_1,b_1),\ldots,(a_N,b_N)$ are pairwise disjoint with $a_j<b_j$ and $\sum_j(b_j-a_j)<\delta$, then $\sum_j\abs{F(b_j)-F(a_j)}<\varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#difference){.pf-ref} and the triangle inequality,
$$
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\abs{F(b_j)-F(a_j)}\le\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{(a_j,b_j]}\abs{f}\,dm .
$$
The intervals $(a_j,b_j]$ are pairwise disjoint, so the sum of their integrals is the integral over their union, and $m\left(\bigcup_j(a_j,b_j]\right)=\sum_j(b_j-a_j)<\delta$; step [](#integral-continuity){.pf-ref} then bounds that integral by $\varepsilon$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$F$ is absolutely continuous on $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
The definition of absolute continuity requires exactly what steps [](#integral-continuity){.pf-ref} and [](#estimate){.pf-ref} supply: for every $\varepsilon>0$ a $\delta>0$ such that every finite family of pairwise disjoint intervals of total length below $\delta$ has the sum of the absolute increments of $F$ below $\varepsilon$. Hence $\boxed{F \text{ is absolutely continuous}}$, and in particular $F$ is of bounded variation on each compact interval.
:::
