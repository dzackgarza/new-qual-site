::: problem
Let $X$ be a space obtained by attaching two 2-cells to the torus $S^1 \times S^1$, one along a simple closed curve $\theset{x} \times S^1$ and the other along $\theset{y} \times S^1$ for two points $x \neq y$ in $S^1$ .

Draw an embedding of $X$ in $\RR^3$ and calculate its fundamental group.

Calculate the homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $X$ is the torus with two $2$-cells attached along two distinct meridians $\{x\} \times S^1$ and $\{y\} \times S^1$.
Proof: setup.

<1>2. The $1$-skeleton is the torus $T^2$, with $\pi_1(T^2) = \langle a, b \mid [a, b] = 1 \rangle$ (where $a$ is the meridian and $b$ the longitude).
Proof: fundamental group of the torus.

<1>3. Attaching a $2$-cell along a meridian $\{x\} \times S^1$ kills the class $a$ (the meridian).
Proof: attaching a $2$-cell along a loop kills that loop in $\pi_1$.

<1>4. Attaching the two $2$-cells along the two meridians kills $a$ (both meridians represent the same class $a$).
Proof: <1>3 (both attaching curves are meridians, representing the same class $a$).

<1>5. Hence $\pi_1(X) = \langle a, b \mid [a, b] = 1, a = 1 \rangle = \langle b \rangle \cong \ZZ$.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>4 (killing $a$ leaves the free abelian group on $b$).

<1>6. For homology: $H_0(X) = \ZZ$ (connected).
Proof: $X$ is path-connected.

<1>7. $H_1(X) = \pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}} = \ZZ$.
Proof: <1>5 (Hurewicz).

<1>8. $H_2(X)$: the cellular chain complex is $0 \to \ZZ^2 \xrightarrow{d_2} \ZZ^2 \xrightarrow{d_1} \ZZ \to 0$ (two $2$-cells, two $1$-cells, one $0$-cell).
Proof: cell structure.

<1>9. $d_2$ sends each $2$-cell to the meridian $a$ (both $2$-cells are attached along meridians), so $d_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (in the basis $\{a, b\}$).
Proof: <1>3 (each $2$-cell's boundary is the meridian $a$).

<1>10. Hence $H_2(X) = \ker d_2 = \{(x, y) : x + y = 0\} \cong \ZZ$ (generated by $(1, -1)$).
Proof: <1>8 and <1>9.

<1>11. $H_n(X) = 0$ for $n \ge 3$.
Proof: $X$ is a $2$-dimensional complex.

<1>12. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>5, <1>6, <1>7, <1>10, <1>11.
:::
