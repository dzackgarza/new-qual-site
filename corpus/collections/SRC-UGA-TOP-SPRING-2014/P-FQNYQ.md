::: problem
Let $X$ be a space obtained by attaching two 2-cells to the torus $S^1 \times S^1$, one along a simple closed curve $\theset{x} \times S^1$ and the other along $\theset{y} \times S^1$ for two points $x \neq y$ in $S^1$ .

Draw an embedding of $X$ in $\RR^3$ and calculate its fundamental group.

Calculate the homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The requested embedding is obtained from the boundary of a solid torus by adjoining two disjoint meridian disks in its interior.
::: {.proof}
Write a solid torus as
\[
D^2\times S^1
\subset\mathbb R^3.
\]
Identify the given torus $S^1\times S^1$ with its boundary by interchanging the factors:
\[
(u,v)\longmapsto(v,u)
\in \partial D^2\times S^1.
\]
Under this identification, for each fixed $u\in S^1$ the curve
\[
\{u\}\times S^1
\]
is the meridian
\[
\partial D^2\times\{u\}.
\]
It bounds the embedded disk
\[
D^2\times\{u\}
\]
inside the solid torus.

Because $x\ne y$, the two disks
\[
D^2\times\{x\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D^2\times\{y\}
\]
are disjoint.
Therefore
\[
\partial(D^2\times S^1)
\cup
(D^2\times\{x\})
\cup
(D^2\times\{y\})
\subset\mathbb R^3
\]
is an embedded copy of $X$.
This is the drawing geometrically: a standard torus together with two disjoint cross-sectional meridian disks.
:::

<1>2. Let
\[
a=S^1\times\{1\},
\qquad
b=\{1\}\times S^1.
\]
Then
\[
\pi_1(T^2)\cong\langle a,b\mid[a,b]=1\rangle,
\]
and both attaching curves represent the element $b$.
::: {.proof}
The standard product generators give the displayed presentation of the torus group.
For any $u\in S^1$, the loop $\{u\}\times S^1$ is freely homotopic to $\{1\}\times S^1$ through the product torus, hence represents the same conjugacy class as $b$.
Since $\pi_1(T^2)$ is abelian, it represents $b$ itself after choosing connecting paths to the basepoint.
Thus both $2$-cell attaching maps impose the same relation $b=1$.
:::

<1>3. Van Kampen's theorem gives
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid[a,b],b,b\rangle
\cong
\langle a\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
Attaching a $2$-cell along a loop quotients the fundamental group by the normal closure of that loop.
Starting from <1>2, the first disk kills $b$ and the second imposes the same relation again.
The remaining generator $a$ has no nontrivial relation, so the quotient is infinite cyclic.
:::

<1>4. For the pair $(X,T^2)$,
\[
H_n(X,T^2;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^2,&n=2,\\
0,&n\ne2.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
The space $X$ is obtained from $T^2$ by attaching exactly two $2$-cells.
Collapsing $T^2$ to a point therefore gives
\[
X/T^2\cong S^2\vee S^2.
\]
For a CW pair,
\[
H_n(X,T^2)\cong\widetilde H_n(X/T^2),
\]
and the reduced homology of $S^2\vee S^2$ is $\mathbb Z^2$ in degree $2$ and zero otherwise.
:::

<1>5. In the long exact sequence of the pair, the boundary map
\[
\partial:H_2(X,T^2)\cong\mathbb Z^2
\longrightarrow
H_1(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z^2
\]
sends both standard generators to the class $[b]$.
Hence
\[
\operatorname{im}\partial=\mathbb Z[b]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\ker\partial\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
For a relative $2$-cell, the connecting homomorphism sends its relative fundamental class to the homology class of its attaching loop.
By <1>2 both attaching loops are homologous to $b$.
Thus, after orienting the two disks compatibly,
\[
\partial(m,n)=(m+n)[b].
\]
Its image is the rank-one subgroup generated by $[b]$, and its kernel is generated by $(1,-1)$.
:::

<1>6. The first homology group is
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
The relevant part of the long exact sequence is
\[
H_2(X,T^2)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
H_1(T^2)
\longrightarrow
H_1(X)
\longrightarrow
H_1(X,T^2)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
H_1(X)
\cong
H_1(T^2)/\operatorname{im}\partial
\cong
\mathbb Z[a]\oplus\mathbb Z[b]\big/\mathbb Z[b]
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
This agrees with the abelianization of <1>3.
:::

<1>7. The second homology group is
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
The long exact sequence gives
\[
0=H_3(X,T^2)
\longrightarrow
H_2(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow
H_2(X)
\longrightarrow
H_2(X,T^2)\cong\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial}
H_1(T^2).
\]
Hence there is a short exact sequence
\[
0
\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow
H_2(X)
\longrightarrow
\ker\partial
\longrightarrow
0.
\]
By <1>5,
\[
\ker\partial\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Since $\mathbb Z$ is free abelian, the short exact sequence splits, giving
\[
H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z.
\]
The first summand contains the original torus fundamental class; the second comes from the difference of the two attached relative disk classes.
:::

<1>8. The remaining homology groups are
\[
H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_n(X;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(n\ge3).
\]
::: {.proof}
The space $X$ is path connected, so $H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.

For $n\ge4$, both $H_n(T^2)$ and $H_n(X,T^2)$ vanish, so the long exact sequence gives $H_n(X)=0$.
For $n=3$, it gives
\[
H_3(T^2)=0
\longrightarrow
H_3(X)
\longrightarrow
H_3(X,T^2)=0,
\]
so $H_3(X)=0$ as well.
:::

<1>9. Thus
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
H_n(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&n=0,1,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&n=2,\\
0,&n\ge3.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>3 and <1>6--<1>8.
:::
