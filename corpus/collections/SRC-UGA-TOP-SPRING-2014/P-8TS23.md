::: problem
Prove that a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous bijection, where $X$ is compact and $Y$ is Hausdorff.
Then $f$ is a closed map.
::: {.proof}
Let $C\subseteq X$ be closed.
Since $X$ is compact, the closed subspace $C$ is compact.
Continuity of $f$ implies that
\[
f(C)
\]
is compact.
Every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed, so $f(C)$ is closed in $Y$.
Thus $f$ sends every closed subset of $X$ to a closed subset of $Y$.
:::

<1>2. The inverse map $f^{-1}:Y\to X$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
Because $f$ is bijective, $f^{-1}$ is a well-defined function.
For every closed set $C\subseteq X$,
\[
(f^{-1})^{-1}(C)=f(C).
\]
By <1>1, the right-hand side is closed in $Y$.
Therefore the preimage under $f^{-1}$ of every closed subset of $X$ is closed, so $f^{-1}$ is continuous.
:::

<1>3. Hence $f$ is a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
The map $f$ is continuous and bijective by hypothesis, and its inverse is continuous by <1>2. This is precisely the definition of a homeomorphism.
:::
:::
