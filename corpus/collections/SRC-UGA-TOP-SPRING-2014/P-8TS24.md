::: problem
(a) Give the definition of a covering space $\widehat{X}$ (and covering map $p:\widehat{X}\to X$) for a topological space $X$.

(b) State the homotopy lifting property of covering spaces.
Use it to show that a covering map induces an injection on fundamental groups.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A covering map is a continuous surjection $p:\widehat X\to X$ that is locally a disjoint union of homeomorphisms onto the base.
::: {.proof}
Explicitly, for every $x\in X$ there is an open neighborhood $U\subseteq X$ such that
\[
p^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{\alpha\in A}V_\alpha,
\]
where every $V_\alpha$ is open in $\widehat X$ and every restriction
\[
p|_{V_\alpha}:V_\alpha\to U
\]
is a homeomorphism.
Such a neighborhood $U$ is called evenly covered, $p$ is a covering map, and the pair $(\widehat X,p)$ is a covering space of $X$.
:::

<1>2. The homotopy lifting property for covering maps has the following form.
::: {.proof}
Let $Z$ be a topological space, let
\[
F:Z\times[0,1]\to X
\]
be a homotopy, and suppose
\[
\widetilde F_0:Z\to\widehat X
\]
is a lift of its initial map, so
\[
p\circ\widetilde F_0=F(-,0).
\]
Then there is a unique continuous map
\[
\widetilde F:Z\times[0,1]\to\widehat X
\]
such that
\[
p\circ\widetilde F=F
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\widetilde F(-,0)=\widetilde F_0.
\]
This is the homotopy lifting property for covering spaces.
:::

<1>3. Fix $\widehat x_0\in\widehat X$ and put $x_0=p(\widehat x_0)$.
To prove
\[
p_*:\pi_1(\widehat X,\widehat x_0)\to\pi_1(X,x_0)
\]
is injective, it suffices to show that every based loop whose projection is null-homotopic is itself null-homotopic.
::: {.proof}
The kernel of $p_*$ consists exactly of the based homotopy classes $[\gamma]$ satisfying
\[
[p\circ\gamma]=1
\]
in $\pi_1(X,x_0)$.
Thus triviality of every such $[\gamma]$ is equivalent to triviality of the kernel.
:::

<1>4. Let $\gamma:[0,1]\to\widehat X$ be a loop at $\widehat x_0$ such that $p\circ\gamma$ is null-homotopic as a based loop.
Choose a based null-homotopy
\[
F:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to X
\]
with
\[
F(s,0)=p(\gamma(s)),
\qquad
F(s,1)=x_0,
\]
and
\[
F(0,t)=F(1,t)=x_0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The hypothesis that $[p\circ\gamma]$ is the identity element of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ means precisely that $p\circ\gamma$ is homotopic to the constant loop at $x_0$ through loops based at $x_0$.
Writing that based homotopy as $F$ gives the displayed boundary conditions.
:::

<1>5. Lift $F$ through $p$ using the initial lift $\gamma$.
::: {.proof}
Apply <1>2 with $Z=[0,1]$ and
\[
\widetilde F_0(s)=\gamma(s).
\]
Since
\[
p\circ\gamma=F(-,0),
\]
there is a lift
\[
\widetilde F:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to\widehat X
\]
satisfying
\[
p\circ\widetilde F=F
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\widetilde F(s,0)=\gamma(s).
\]
:::

<1>6. The two side edges of $\widetilde F$ are constantly $\widehat x_0$.
::: {.proof}
The path
\[
t\longmapsto\widetilde F(0,t)
\]
projects, by <1>4 and <1>5, to the constant path $t\mapsto x_0$ and starts at
\[
\widetilde F(0,0)=\gamma(0)=\widehat x_0.
\]
The constant path $t\mapsto\widehat x_0$ is another lift of the same constant path with the same initial point.
Uniqueness of path lifting therefore gives
\[
\widetilde F(0,t)=\widehat x_0
\]
for all $t$.

The same argument, using
\[
\widetilde F(1,0)=\gamma(1)=\widehat x_0,
\]
gives
\[
\widetilde F(1,t)=\widehat x_0
\]
for all $t$.
:::

<1>7. The top edge of $\widetilde F$ is also constantly $\widehat x_0$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4,
\[
F(s,1)=x_0
\]
for every $s$, so the path
\[
s\longmapsto\widetilde F(s,1)
\]
is a lift of the constant path at $x_0$.
By <1>6 it starts at
\[
\widetilde F(0,1)=\widehat x_0.
\]
Uniqueness of path lifting again implies
\[
\widetilde F(s,1)=\widehat x_0
\]
for all $s$.
:::

<1>8. Hence $\gamma$ is null-homotopic as a based loop in $\widehat X$, so $p_*$ is injective.
::: {.proof}
By <1>5, the bottom edge of $\widetilde F$ is $\gamma$.
By <1>6, both side edges remain at the basepoint $\widehat x_0$.
By <1>7, the top edge is the constant loop at $\widehat x_0$.
Thus $\widetilde F$ is a based null-homotopy of $\gamma$.
Therefore
\[
\ker p_*=\{1\},
\]
and $p_*$ is injective.
:::
