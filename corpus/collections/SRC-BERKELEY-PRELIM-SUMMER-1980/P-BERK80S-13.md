::: {.problem}
Let $J$ be the ideal in $\mathbb Z[x]$ generated by $x-5$ and $14$. Find $n\in\mathbb Z$ such that $0\le n\le13$ and
\[
(x^3+2x+1)^{50}-n\in J.
\]
:::


::: {.solution}
The required integer is
\[
\boxed{n=2}.
\]

<1>1. Reduction modulo $J$ is evaluation at $x=5$ followed by reduction modulo $14$.
::: {.proof}
Because
\[
J=(x-5,14),
\]
the quotient satisfies
\[
\mathbb Z[x]/J
\cong (\mathbb Z/14\mathbb Z)[x]/(x-5)
\cong \mathbb Z/14\mathbb Z.
\]
Under this isomorphism, the class of a polynomial $f(x)$ is the residue class of $f(5)$ modulo $14$.
Thus we need the residue modulo $14$ of
\[
(5^3+2\cdot5+1)^{50}=136^{50}.
\]
:::

<1>2. Compute the residue modulo $14$.
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
136\equiv10\pmod{14},
\]
it remains to compute $10^{50}$ modulo $14$.
Using the Chinese remainder theorem,
\[
10^{50}\equiv0\pmod2.
\]
Modulo $7$,
\[
10^{50}\equiv3^{50}.
\]
Since $3^6\equiv1\pmod7$ and $50\equiv2\pmod6$,
\[
3^{50}\equiv3^2\equiv9\equiv2\pmod7.
\]
The unique residue modulo $14$ that is $0$ modulo $2$ and $2$ modulo $7$ is $2$. Hence
\[
136^{50}\equiv2\pmod{14}.
\]
Therefore
\[
(x^3+2x+1)^{50}-2\in J,
\]
and $n=2$ satisfies $0\le n\le13$.
:::
:::
