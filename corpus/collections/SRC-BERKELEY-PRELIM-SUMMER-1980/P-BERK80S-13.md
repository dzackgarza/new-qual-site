::: {.problem}
Let $J$ be the ideal in $\mathbb Z[x]$ generated by $x-5$ and $14$.
Find $n\in\mathbb Z$ such that $0\le n\le13$ and
\[
(x^3+2x+1)^{50}-n\in J.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For $f\in\ZZ[x]$ and $m\in\ZZ$, one has $f-m\in J$ if and only if
$f(5)\equiv m\pmod{14}$.

::: {.proof}
Because $J=(x-5,14)$, the quotient satisfies
$$
\ZZ[x]/J
\cong (\ZZ/14\ZZ)[x]/(x-5)
\cong \ZZ/14\ZZ,
$$
and under this isomorphism the class of $f$ is the residue class of
$f(5)$ modulo $14$.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
136^{50}\equiv2\pmod{14}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
136\equiv10\pmod{14},
$$
it suffices to compute $10^{50}$ modulo $14$. Modulo $2$,
$$
10^{50}\equiv0\pmod2.
$$
Modulo $7$,
$$
10^{50}\equiv3^{50}.
$$
Since $3^6\equiv1\pmod7$ and $50\equiv2\pmod6$,
$$
3^{50}\equiv3^2\equiv9\equiv2\pmod7.
$$
By the Chinese remainder theorem, the unique residue modulo $14$ that is
$0$ modulo $2$ and $2$ modulo $7$ is $2$.
:::

<1>3. The required integer is
$$
\boxed{n=2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
With $f=x^3+2x+1$, one has $f(5)=136$, so $f(5)^{50}=136^{50}\equiv2\pmod{14}$
by step <1>2. Step <1>1 gives
$$
(x^3+2x+1)^{50}-2\in J,
$$
and $0\le2\le13$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives the requested $n$.
:::
