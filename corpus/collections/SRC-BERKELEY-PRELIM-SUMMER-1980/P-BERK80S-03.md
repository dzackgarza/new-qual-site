::: {.problem}
Let $T:V\to V$ be an invertible linear transformation of a vector space $V$. Denote by G the group of all maps $f _ { k , a } : V \to V$ where $k \in \mathbb { Z } , a \in V$ , and for $x \in V$

$$
f _ { k , a } ( x ) = T ^ { k } x + a \quad ( x \in V ) .
$$

Prove that the commutator subgroup $G ^ { \prime }$ of G is isomorphic to the additive group of the vector space $( T - I ) V$ , the image of $T - I$ . (G0 is generated by all $g h g ^ { - 1 } h ^ { - 1 }$ , g and h in G.)
:::


::: {.solution}
Write $f_{k,a}$ as the pair $(k,a)$. Composition gives
\[
(k,a)(\ell,b)=(k+\ell,\ a+T^k b),
\]
and therefore
\[
(k,a)^{-1}=(-k,-T^{-k}a).
\]
Let
\[
W=(T-I)V.
\]
We prove that $G'$ is exactly the subgroup of translations
\[
N:=\{(0,w):w\in W\}.
\]
Since $(0,w)(0,w')=(0,w+w')$, the map $w\mapsto(0,w)$ then identifies $G'$ with the additive group of $W$.

<1>1. Every commutator belongs to $N$.
::: {.proof}
For $g=(k,a)$ and $h=(\ell,b)$, direct multiplication gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
[g,h]
&=(k,a)(\ell,b)(k,a)^{-1}(\ell,b)^{-1}\\
&=\bigl(0,(I-T^\ell)a+(T^k-I)b\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
It remains to see that its translation vector lies in $W$.
For every integer $n$, one has
\[
(T^n-I)V\subseteq (T-I)V.
\]
Indeed, for $n>0$,
\[
T^n-I=(T-I)(I+T+\cdots+T^{n-1}),
\]
while for $n<0$,
\[
T^n-I=-T^n(T^{-n}-I),
\]
and the positive-power factorization applies to $T^{-n}-I$; all powers of $T$ commute with $T-I$.
Thus both $(I-T^\ell)a$ and $(T^k-I)b$ lie in $W$, so every commutator lies in $N$.
Since $N$ is a subgroup, this proves
\[
G'\subseteq N.
\]
:::

<1>2. Every translation by an element of $W$ is a commutator.
::: {.proof}
Let $w\in W$. Then $w=(T-I)v$ for some $v\in V$.
Take
\[
g=(1,0),\qquad h=(0,v).
\]
The commutator formula from <1>1 gives
\[
[g,h]=(0,(T-I)v)=(0,w).
\]
Hence every element of $N$ lies in $G'$, so
\[
N\subseteq G'.
\]
:::

<1>3. The derived subgroup is isomorphic to the additive group of $(T-I)V$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and <1>2,
\[
G'=N=\{f_{0,w}:w\in (T-I)V\}.
\]
Define
\[
\Phi:(T-I)V\longrightarrow G',\qquad \Phi(w)=f_{0,w}.
\]
Since
\[
f_{0,w}\circ f_{0,w'}=f_{0,w+w'},
\]
$\Phi$ is a homomorphism from the additive group of $(T-I)V$ to $G'$. It is injective because distinct translation vectors give distinct maps, and it is surjective by the description of $G'$ above. Therefore
\[
\boxed{G'\cong ((T-I)V,+)}.
\]
:::
:::
