::: {.problem}
Let $T:V\to V$ be an invertible linear transformation of a vector space $V$.
Denote by $G$ the group of all maps $f_{k,a}:V\to V$ where $k\in\ZZ$, $a\in V$, and for $x\in V$

$$
f_{k,a}(x)=T^kx+a\quad(x\in V).
$$

Prove that the commutator subgroup $G'$ of $G$ is isomorphic to the additive group of the vector space $(T-I)V$, the image of $T-I$. ($G'$ is generated by all $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, $g$ and $h$ in $G$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $f_{k,a}$ as the pair $(k,a)$. Composition gives
$$
(k,a)(\ell,b)=(k+\ell,\ a+T^k b),
$$
and therefore
$$
(k,a)^{-1}=(-k,-T^{-k}a).
$$
Let
$$
W=(T-I)V
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
N=\{(0,w):w\in W\}.
$$
Since $(0,w)(0,w')=(0,w+w')$ and $W$ is a subspace, $N$ is a subgroup of
$G$.

<1>1. $G'\subseteq N$.

::: {.proof}
For $g=(k,a)$ and $h=(\ell,b)$, direct multiplication gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
[g,h]
&=(k,a)(\ell,b)(k,a)^{-1}(\ell,b)^{-1}\\
&=\bigl(0,(I-T^\ell)a+(T^k-I)b\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
For every integer $n$, one has
$$
(T^n-I)V\subseteq (T-I)V.
$$
Indeed, for $n>0$,
$$
T^n-I=(T-I)(I+T+\cdots+T^{n-1}),
$$
while for $n<0$,
$$
T^n-I=-T^n(T^{-n}-I),
$$
and the positive-power factorization applies to $T^{-n}-I$; all powers of
$T$ commute with $T-I$. Thus both $(I-T^\ell)a$ and $(T^k-I)b$ lie in $W$,
so every commutator lies in $N$. Since $N$ is a subgroup, $G'\subseteq N$.
:::

<1>2. $N\subseteq G'$.

::: {.proof}
Let $w\in W$. Then $w=(T-I)v$ for some $v\in V$. Take
$$
g=(1,0),\qquad h=(0,v).
$$
The commutator formula in the proof of step <1>1 gives
$$
[g,h]=(0,(T-I)v)=(0,w).
$$
Hence every element of $N$ is a commutator and lies in $G'$.
:::

<1>3. The map
$$
\Phi:(T-I)V\longrightarrow G',\qquad \Phi(w)=f_{0,w},
$$
is an isomorphism from the additive group of $(T-I)V$ onto $G'$, so
$$
\boxed{G'\cong ((T-I)V,+)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 give
$$
G'=N=\{f_{0,w}:w\in (T-I)V\},
$$
so $\Phi$ takes values in $G'$ and is surjective. Since
$$
f_{0,w}\circ f_{0,w'}=f_{0,w+w'},
$$
$\Phi$ is a homomorphism. It is injective because $f_{0,w}(0)=w$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the required isomorphism.
:::
