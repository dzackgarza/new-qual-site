::: {.exercise}

Show that if $\mathcal{A}$ is a basis for a topology on $X$, then the topology generated by $\mathcal{A}$ equals the intersection of all topologies on $X$ that contain $\mathcal{A}$.
Prove the same if $\mathcal{A}$ is a subbasis.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\mathcal T$ be the topology generated by a basis $\mathcal A$, and let
\[
\mathcal S=\bigcap\{\mathcal U:\mathcal U\text{ is a topology on }X\text{ and }\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal U\}.
\]
Since $\mathcal T$ itself is one of the topologies in the intersection, $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal T$. Conversely, if $U\in\mathcal T$, then $U$ is a union of elements of $\mathcal A$. Every topology containing $\mathcal A$ therefore contains $U$, hence $U\in\mathcal S$. So $\mathcal T=\mathcal S$.

If $\mathcal A$ is a subbasis, the same first inclusion holds. The basis generated by $\mathcal A$ consists of finite intersections of elements of $\mathcal A$, and every topology containing $\mathcal A$ contains all such finite intersections and all their unions. Therefore every open set in the topology generated by the subbasis lies in every topology containing $\mathcal A$. Again the two collections are equal.
:::
