::: {.exercise}

Show that if $\mathcal{A}$ is a basis for a topology on $X$, then the topology generated by $\mathcal{A}$ equals the intersection of all topologies on $X$ that contain $\mathcal{A}$.
Prove the same if $\mathcal{A}$ is a subbasis.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained published solution, **Dan Whitman, *Topology, Second Edition by James Munkres: Solutions Manual* (30 November 2019), Exercise 13.5** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-whitman.pdf`), restructured into steps; the subbasis case is argued directly instead of by an appeal to the basis case at the end.

Throughout, $\mathcal{T}$ is the topology generated by $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{C}$ is the collection of all topologies on $X$ containing $\mathcal{A}$; $\mathcal{C}$ is nonempty because the discrete topology contains every collection of subsets.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis}
Suppose $\mathcal{A}$ is a basis for a topology on $X$; then $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #basis-in}
$\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}'$ for every $\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}$ and $U\in\mathcal{T}$. By Lemma 13.1, $U$ is a union of members of $\mathcal{A}$, each of which lies in $\mathcal{T}'$ because $\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{T}'$; a topology is closed under unions, so $U\in\mathcal{T}'$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis-out}
$\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'\subset\mathcal{T}$.

::: pf-proof
The topology $\mathcal{T}$ contains $\mathcal{A}$, since a basis is contained in the topology it generates, so $\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{C}$; an intersection is contained in each of its members, hence in $\mathcal{T}$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subbasis}
Suppose $\mathcal{A}$ is a subbasis for a topology on $X$; then $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #sub-in}
$\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}'$ for every $\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the collection of all finite intersections of members of $\mathcal{A}$; by the definition of the topology generated by a subbasis, $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis and $\mathcal{T}$ is the topology generated by $\mathcal{B}$. If $\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}$, then $\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{T}'$, and $\mathcal{T}'$ is closed under finite intersections, so $\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{T}'$; then every union of members of $\mathcal{B}$ lies in $\mathcal{T}'$, that is $\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}'$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sub-out}
$\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'\subset\mathcal{T}$, and therefore the two sides are equal.

::: pf-proof
Each member of $\mathcal{A}$ is a finite intersection of members of $\mathcal{A}$, namely of the one-term family consisting of itself, so $\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{C}$. An intersection is contained in each of its members, so the intersection lies in $\mathcal{T}$. With step [](#sub-in){.pf-ref} this gives equality, which is the subbasis case.
:::
