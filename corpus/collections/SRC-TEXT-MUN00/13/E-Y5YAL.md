::: {.exercise}

Show that if $\mathcal{A}$ is a basis for a topology on $X$, then the topology generated by $\mathcal{A}$ equals the intersection of all topologies on $X$ that contain $\mathcal{A}$.
Prove the same if $\mathcal{A}$ is a subbasis.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **Dan Whitman, *Topology, Second Edition by James Munkres: Solutions Manual* (30 November 2019), Exercise 13.5, printed pp. 144--145** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-whitman.pdf`); both cases are his.

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a basis for a topology on $X$, let $\mathcal{T}$ be the topology it generates, and let $\mathcal{C}$ be the collection of all topologies on $X$ that contain $\mathcal{A}$ as a subset. We show $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'$.

First, $\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}'$ for every $\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}$. If $U\in\mathcal{T}$ then by Lemma 13.1 $U$ is a union of elements of $\mathcal{A}$, and each of those lies in $\mathcal{T}'$ because $\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{T}'$; a topology is closed under unions, so $U\in\mathcal{T}'$.

Second, $\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'\subset\mathcal{T}$. The topology $\mathcal{T}$ itself contains $\mathcal{A}$, so $\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{C}$; an intersection is contained in each of its factors, so it is contained in $\mathcal{T}$.

Hence $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'$.

Now let $\mathcal{A}$ be a subbasis for a topology on $X$, with $\mathcal{T}$ the topology it generates and $\mathcal{C}$ as before. Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the collection of all finite intersections of elements of $\mathcal{A}$; by the definition of the topology generated by a subbasis, $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis and $\mathcal{T}$ is the topology generated by the basis $\mathcal{B}$.

If $\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}$ then every element of $\mathcal{A}$ lies in $\mathcal{T}'$, so every finite intersection of elements of $\mathcal{A}$ lies in $\mathcal{T}'$; that is, $\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{T}'$. Since this holds for every $\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}$, and $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis generating $\mathcal{T}$, the basis case just proved (applied to $\mathcal{B}$) gives $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'$.
:::
