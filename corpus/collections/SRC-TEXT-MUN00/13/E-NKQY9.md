::: {.exercise}

(a) Apply Lemma 13.2 to show that the countable collection

$$
\mathcal{B} = \theset{(a, b) \mid a < b, \ a \text{ and } b \text{ rational}}
$$

is a basis that generates the standard topology on $\RR$.

(b) Show that the collection

$$
\mathcal{C} = \theset{[a, b) \mid a < b, \ a \text{ and } b \text{ rational}}
$$

is a basis that generates a topology different from the lower limit topology on $\RR$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The retained published solution is **Dan Whitman, *Topology, Second Edition by James Munkres: Solutions Manual* (30 November 2019), Exercise 13.8** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-whitman.pdf`); both parts are his, with the two basis conditions checked explicitly in (a). Write $\mathcal{T}$ for the standard topology of $\RR$ and $\mathcal{T}_\ell$ for the lower limit topology.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis-rational-open}
(a) $\mathcal{B}=\{(a,b)\mid a<b,\ a,b\in\QQ\}$ satisfies the two conditions of Lemma 13.2.

::: pf-proof
First, for $x\in\RR$ choose rationals $a<x<b$, which exist because $\QQ$ is dense in $\RR$; then $x\in(a,b)\in\mathcal{B}$. Second, if $B_1=(a_1,b_1),B_2=(a_2,b_2)\in\mathcal{B}$ and $x\in B_1\cap B_2$, put $a=\max(a_1,a_2)$ and $b=\min(b_1,b_2)$, which are rational; then $a\le x<b$, so $x\in(a,b)\in\mathcal{B}$, and $(a,b)\subseteq B_1\cap B_2$, since $a\ge a_i$ and $b\le b_i$ for $i=1,2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #generates-standard}
(a) The topology $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}$ generated by $\mathcal{B}$ is $\mathcal{T}$.

::: pf-proof
Every element of $\mathcal{B}$ is an open interval, hence open in $\mathcal{T}$, so $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}$. Conversely let $U\in\mathcal{T}$ and $x\in U$; by the definition of the standard topology there is a basis element $(a_0,b_0)$ with $x\in(a_0,b_0)\subseteq U$, and by density of $\QQ$ in $\RR$ there are rationals $a,b$ with $a_0<a<x<b<b_0$. Then $x\in(a,b)\in\mathcal{B}$ and $(a,b)\subseteq(a_0,b_0)\subseteq U$, so every point of $U$ lies in a member of $\mathcal{B}$ contained in $U$, giving $U\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}$. Hence $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}=\mathcal{T}$, which is the assertion of Lemma 13.2.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis-rational-half-open}
(b) $\mathcal{C}=\{[a,b)\mid a<b,\ a,b\in\QQ\}$ is a basis for a topology $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$.

::: pf-proof
For $x\in\RR$ choose rationals $a\le x<b$, for instance $a$ rational with $a<x$ and $b$ rational with $b>x$; then $x\in[a,b)\in\mathcal{C}$. If $C_1=[a_1,b_1),C_2=[a_2,b_2)\in\mathcal{C}$ and $x\in C_1\cap C_2$, put $a=\max(a_1,a_2)$ and $b=\min(b_1,b_2)$, which are rational with $a\le x<b$; then $C=[a,b)\in\mathcal{C}$ satisfies $x\in C\subseteq C_1\cap C_2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(b) $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}\ne\mathcal{T}_\ell$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x$ be irrational and $U=[x,x+1)$, which is a basis element of the lower limit topology, so $U\in\mathcal{T}_\ell$. Let $C=[a,b)\in\mathcal{C}$ contain $x$; then $a$ is rational and $a\le x$, and $a\ne x$ because $x$ is irrational, so $a<x$ and $a\in C$ while $a\notin[x,x+1)=U$; hence $C\not\subseteq U$. Thus no basis element of $\mathcal{C}$ containing $x$ lies inside $U$, so by Lemma 13.1 the set $U$ is not in $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$. Steps [](#basis-rational-half-open){.pf-ref} and this argument show that $\mathcal{C}$ generates a topology, and that this topology is different from $\mathcal{T}_\ell$; by steps [](#basis-rational-open){.pf-ref} and [](#generates-standard){.pf-ref} the corresponding collection of open intervals generates $\mathcal{T}$, which completes both parts.
:::
