::: {.exercise}

(a) Apply Lemma 13.2 to show that the countable collection

$$
\mathcal{B} = \ts{(a, b) \mid a < b, \ a \text{ and } b \text{ rational}}
$$

is a basis that generates the standard topology on $\mathbb{R}$.

(b) Show that the collection

$$
\mathcal{C} = \ts{[a, b) \mid a < b, \ a \text{ and } b \text{ rational}}
$$

is a basis that generates a topology different from the lower limit topology on $\mathbb{R}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) The family
\[
\mathcal B=\{(a,b):a,b\in\mathbb Q,\ a<b\}
\]
is countable because $\mathbb Q^2$ is countable. Given an ordinary open set $U$ and $x\in U$, choose an interval $(r,s)$ with
\[
x\in(r,s)\subseteq U.
\]
By density of $\mathbb Q$, choose rationals $a,b$ with
\[
r<a<x<b<s.
\]
Then $x\in(a,b)\subseteq U$. By the basis criterion, $\mathcal B$ generates exactly the standard topology.

(b) The family
\[
\mathcal C=\{[a,b):a,b\in\mathbb Q,\ a<b\}
\]
covers $\mathbb R$. If $x\in[a,b)\cap[c,d)$, put $r=\max(a,c)$. If $x=r$, take $e=r$; otherwise choose rational $e$ with $r<e<x$. In either case choose rational $f$ with
\[
x<f<\min(b,d).
\]
Then $x\in[e,f)\subseteq[a,b)\cap[c,d)$, so $\mathcal C$ is a basis.

Every member of $\mathcal C$ is lower-limit open, so the topology generated by $\mathcal C$ is no finer than $\mathbb R_\ell$. It is strictly coarser: for irrational $\alpha$, the lower-limit open set $[\alpha,\alpha+1)$ is not $\mathcal C$-open. If a basic set $[a,b)\in\mathcal C$ contained $\alpha$ and were contained in $[\alpha,\alpha+1)$, then $a\le\alpha$ and containment would force $a\ge\alpha$, hence $a=\alpha$, impossible because $a$ is rational.
:::
