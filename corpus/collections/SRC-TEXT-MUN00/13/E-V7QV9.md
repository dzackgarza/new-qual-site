::: {.exercise}

Consider the nine topologies on the set $X = \theset{a, b, c}$ indicated in Example 1 of §12. Compare them; that is, for each pair of topologies, determine whether they are comparable, and if so, which is the finer.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **Dan Whitman, *Topology, Second Edition by James Munkres: Solutions Manual* (30 November 2019), Exercise 13.2, printed pp. 141--142** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-whitman.pdf`); the answer is his. He indexes the nine topologies by the row and column of their place in Munkres's Figure 12.1, the upper left corner being $(1,1)$ and the row index coming first, and prints a $9\times 9$ matrix whose entries name the finer of each pair or read "Inc" for incomparable. Below the same nine topologies are written out and the comparison is recorded in the numbering of that list; the two agree, and the book's own remark on printed p. 77 (that the upper right-hand topology is strictly finer than the three in the first column, strictly coarser than the other two in the third column, and incomparable with those in the second) is the corresponding row of it.

The nine topologies of Figure 12.1 are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T_1&=\{\varnothing,X\}, &
\mathcal T_2&=\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{a,b\},X\}, &
\mathcal T_3&=\{\varnothing,\{b\},\{a,b\},\{b,c\},X\},\\
\mathcal T_4&=\{\varnothing,\{b\},X\}, &
\mathcal T_5&=\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{b,c\},X\}, &
\mathcal T_6&=\{\varnothing,\{b\},\{c\},\{a,b\},\{b,c\},X\},\\
\mathcal T_7&=\{\varnothing,\{a,b\},X\}, &
\mathcal T_8&=\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\},X\}, &
\mathcal T_9&=\mathcal P(X),
\end{aligned}
$$
which in the figure stand in the nine positions, by row,
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathcal T_1 & \mathcal T_2 & \mathcal T_3\\
\mathcal T_4 & \mathcal T_5 & \mathcal T_6\\
\mathcal T_7 & \mathcal T_8 & \mathcal T_9 .
\end{array}
$$
Write $\mathcal T<\mathcal T'$ for $\mathcal T\subsetneq\mathcal T'$. The strict inclusions are generated by transitivity from
$$
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_4<\mathcal T_3<\mathcal T_6<\mathcal T_9,\qquad
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_4<\mathcal T_8<\mathcal T_9,\qquad
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_7<\mathcal T_2<\mathcal T_8<\mathcal T_9,
$$
$$
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_5<\mathcal T_9,\qquad
\mathcal T_7<\mathcal T_3 ;
$$
each of these is read off the lists above, since a topology is finer than another exactly when it has more open sets. These twelve cover relations generate exactly $24$ comparable pairs, which with the $12$ incomparable pairs below accounts for all $\binom{9}{2}=36$ pairs; so nothing further is comparable.

For each incomparable pair the table gives a set in the first topology only and a set in the second topology only:

| Pair | In the first only | In the second only |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathcal T_2,\mathcal T_3$ | $\{a\}$ | $\{b\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_2,\mathcal T_4$ | $\{a\}$ | $\{b\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_2,\mathcal T_5$ | $\{a,b\}$ | $\{b,c\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_2,\mathcal T_6$ | $\{a\}$ | $\{b\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_3,\mathcal T_5$ | $\{b\}$ | $\{a\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_3,\mathcal T_8$ | $\{b,c\}$ | $\{a\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_4,\mathcal T_5$ | $\{b\}$ | $\{a\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_4,\mathcal T_7$ | $\{b\}$ | $\{a,b\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_5,\mathcal T_6$ | $\{a\}$ | $\{b\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_5,\mathcal T_7$ | $\{a\}$ | $\{a,b\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_5,\mathcal T_8$ | $\{b,c\}$ | $\{b\}$ |
| $\mathcal T_6,\mathcal T_8$ | $\{c\}$ | $\{a\}$ |

So $\mathcal T_1$ is the coarsest topology and $\mathcal T_9$ the finest, and the order of the nine is the partial order displayed; every pair not listed there is incomparable. As Whitman adds, the maximal simply ordered subcollections are
$$
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_5<\mathcal T_9,\quad
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_7<\mathcal T_2<\mathcal T_8<\mathcal T_9,\quad
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_7<\mathcal T_3<\mathcal T_6<\mathcal T_9,
$$
$$
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_4<\mathcal T_3<\mathcal T_6<\mathcal T_9,\quad
\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_4<\mathcal T_8<\mathcal T_9 .
$$
