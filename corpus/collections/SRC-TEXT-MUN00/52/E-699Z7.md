::: {.exercise}

Let $A \subset X$; suppose $r: X \to A$ is a continuous map such that $r(a) = a$ for each $a \in A$.
(The map $r$ is called a retraction of $X$ onto $A$.)
If $a_0 \in A$, show that

$$
r_*: \pi_1(X, a_0) \to \pi_1(A, a_0)
$$

is surjective.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that if $r: X \to A$ is a retraction onto $A \subset X$ with basepoint $a_0 \in A$, then the induced homomorphism $r_*: \pi_1(X, a_0) \to \pi_1(A, a_0)$ is surjective.

<1>1. Categorical setup and inclusion map:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $i: (A, a_0) \hookrightarrow (X, a_0)$ denote the canonical inclusion map.
    <2>2. By definition of a retraction, $r(a) = a$ for all $a \in A$, which means that the composition satisfies:
        $$r \circ i = \operatorname{id}_A: A \to A.$$
    <2>3. Since $r(a_0) = a_0$ and $i(a_0) = a_0$, both $i$ and $r$ are basepoint-preserving continuous maps.

<1>2. Functoriality of the fundamental group:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The fundamental group operator $\pi_1$ is a functor from pointed topological spaces to groups.
    <2>2. Applying functoriality to the identity $r \circ i = \operatorname{id}_A$ yields:
        $$r_* \circ i_* = (r \circ i)_* = (\operatorname{id}_A)_* = \operatorname{id}_{\pi_1(A, a_0)},$$
        where $i_*: \pi_1(A, a_0) \to \pi_1(X, a_0)$ and $r_*: \pi_1(X, a_0) \to \pi_1(A, a_0)$.

<1>3. Surjectivity of $r_*$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\gamma \in \pi_1(A, a_0)$ be an arbitrary element.
    <2>2. Define $\beta = i_*(\gamma) \in \pi_1(X, a_0)$.
    <2>3. Applying $r_*$ to $\beta$:
        $$r_*(\beta) = r_*(i_*(\gamma)) = (r_* \circ i_*)(\gamma) = \operatorname{id}_{\pi_1(A, a_0)}(\gamma) = \gamma.$$
    <2>4. Thus, every element of $\pi_1(A, a_0)$ is in the image of $r_*$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    The induced homomorphism $r_*: \pi_1(X, a_0) \to \pi_1(A, a_0)$ is surjective (and $i_*$ is injective). Q.E.D.
:::
