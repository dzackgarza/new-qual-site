::: {.exercise}

Theorem.
Let $U$ be a simply connected open set in $\RR^2$.
If $C$ is a simple closed curve lying in $U$, then each bounded component of $\RR^2 - C$ also lies in $U$.

(This condition actually characterizes the simply connected open sets of $\RR^2$.
See [RW]. The space $\RR^2 - C$ has, of course, only one bounded component, as we shall prove in the next section.)
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $U$ be a simply connected open set in $\RR^2$ and let $C=g(S^1)$ be a simple closed curve in $U$, where $g:S^1\to C$ is a homeomorphism. Let $x$ be a point of $\RR^2-C$ that does not lie in $U$; it is enough to show that $x$ lies in the unbounded component of $\RR^2-C$, since then no point outside $U$ lies in a bounded component.

Because $U$ is simply connected, the loop $g$ in $U$ is path homotopic in $U$ to a constant loop, so it is nullhomotopic. Since $g$ is a homeomorphism onto $C$, the inclusion $i:C\to U$ is nullhomotopic as well, being the composite of the nullhomotopic loop $g$ with $g^{-1}:C\to S^1$. Composing a nullhomotopy of $i$ with the inclusion of $U$ in $\RR^2-\theset{x}$, which contains $U$ because $x\notin U$, exhibits $i$ as a nullhomotopic map into the complement of $x$.

Identify $\RR^2$ with $S^2-\theset{\infty}$. Then $\RR^2-\theset{x}=S^2-\theset{x,\infty}$, and $i:C\to S^2-\theset{x,\infty}$ is continuous and injective with compact domain and is nullhomotopic. The Borsuk lemma, Lemma 62.2 (printed p. 382), therefore says that $x$ and $\infty$ lie in the same component of $S^2-C$, that is, $x$ lies in the unbounded component of $\RR^2-C$.

So every point of $\RR^2-C$ outside $U$ lies in the unbounded component: each bounded component of $\RR^2-C$ is contained in $U$.
:::
