::: {.exercise}

Theorem.
Let $U$ be a simply connected open set in $\RR^2$.
If $C$ is a simple closed curve lying in $U$, then each bounded component of $\RR^2 - C$ also lies in $U$.

(This condition actually characterizes the simply connected open sets of $\RR^2$.
See [RW]. The space $\RR^2 - C$ has, of course, only one bounded component, as we shall prove in the next section.)
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained published solution of Munkres, *Topology*, §62 Exercise 2 was located: the sets searched are listed in the `COMPLAINTS.md` entry for §51--§63 of `SRC-TEXT-MUN00`, and no retained set reaches §62. The proof is the card's own, checked against the numbered statements it cites.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #reduction}
It is enough to show that every $x\in\RR^2-C$ with $x\notin U$ lies in the unbounded component of $\RR^2-C$.

::: pf-proof
$\RR^2-C$ falls into its components, exactly one of which is unbounded; a bounded component is one containing no point of the form $\lambda y$ for $\lambda\to\infty$, so if every point outside $U$ lies in the unbounded component then no bounded component meets $\RR^2-U$, that is, each bounded component lies in $U$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nullhomotopic-inclusion}
The inclusion $i:C\to U$ is nullhomotopic.

::: pf-proof
Let $C=g(S^1)$ with $g:S^1\to C$ a homeomorphism. $U$ is simply connected, so the class of the loop $g$ in $\pi_1(U)$ is trivial and $g$ is nullhomotopic as a map into $U$ (Munkres, Lemma 55.3). Composing a nullhomotopy of $g$ with $g^{-1}:C\to S^1$ gives a nullhomotopy of $g\circ g^{-1}$, which is the inclusion $i$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #into-complement}
With $x\in\RR^2-U$, the map $i:C\to\RR^2-\{x\}$ is continuous, injective, has compact domain, and is nullhomotopic.

::: pf-proof
$U\subseteq\RR^2-\{x\}$ because $x\notin U$, so the inclusion $U\to\RR^2-\{x\}$ is defined, and composing it with the nullhomotopy of step [#nullhomotopic-inclusion]{.pf-ref} gives a nullhomotopy of $i$ as a map into $\RR^2-\{x\}$. $C$ is compact, being a homeomorphic copy of $S^1$, and $i$ is injective because $g$ is.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #borsuk}
Under the identification $\RR^2=S^2-\{\infty\}$, the points $x$ and $\infty$ lie in the same component of $S^2-C$.

::: pf-proof
$\RR^2-\{x\}=S^2-\{x,\infty\}$, so step [#into-complement]{.pf-ref} exhibits $i$ as a continuous injective nullhomotopic map from the compact space $C$ into $S^2-\{x,\infty\}$; the Borsuk lemma (Munkres, Lemma 62.2) then puts $x$ and $\infty$ in the same component of $S^2-C$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #unbounded}
That component, minus $\infty$, is the unbounded component of $\RR^2-C$; hence $x$ lies in it and the theorem follows.

::: pf-proof
Let $W$ be the component of $x$ and $\infty$ in $S^2-C$. $C$ is compact and $\infty\notin C$, so $C$ is closed and bounded in $\RR^2$ and the identity is a homeomorphism of $S^2-\{\infty\}$ with $\RR^2$; Munkres, Lemma 61.1 applied with $b=\infty$ says that $W-\{\infty\}$ is the unbounded component of $\RR^2-C$. Since $x\ne\infty$ lies in $W$, it lies in $W-\{\infty\}$, the unbounded component. By step [#reduction]{.pf-ref} every bounded component of $\RR^2-C$ lies in $U$.
:::
