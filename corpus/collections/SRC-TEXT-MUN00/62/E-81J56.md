::: {.exercise}

Theorem.
Let $U$ be a simply connected open set in $\RR^2$.
If $C$ is a simple closed curve lying in $U$, then each bounded component of $\RR^2 - C$ also lies in $U$.

(This condition actually characterizes the simply connected open sets of $\RR^2$.
See [RW]. The space $\RR^2 - C$ has, of course, only one bounded component, as we shall prove in the next section.)
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $B$ be a bounded component of $\RR^2-C$ and suppose that $B\not\subseteq U$; choose $p\in B\setminus U$. Fix $x_0\in C$ and let $\gamma:S^1\to C$ be a homeomorphism with $\gamma(1)=x_0$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The loop $\gamma$ is not nullhomotopic in $\RR^2-\theset{p}$.

::: pf-proof

Identify $\RR^2$ with $S^2-\theset{\infty}$; the points $p$ and $\infty$ lie in different components of $S^2-C$, since $p\in B$ is bounded and $\infty$ is in the unbounded component. The inclusion $C\to S^2-\theset{p,\infty}=\RR^2-\theset{p}$ is continuous and injective with compact domain. If it were nullhomotopic, Lemma 62.2 (the Borsuk lemma, printed p. 382) would put $p$ and $\infty$ in the same component of $S^2-C$. So it is not nullhomotopic, and neither is $\gamma$, its composition with the homeomorphism $S^1\to C$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The loop $\gamma$ is nullhomotopic in $\RR^2-\theset{p}$.

::: pf-proof

Its image $C$ lies in $U$, and $p\notin U$, so $\gamma$ is a loop in the simply connected space $U$, hence nullhomotopic there; composing the nullhomotopy with the inclusion $U\to\RR^2-\theset{p}$ gives a nullhomotopy in $\RR^2-\theset{p}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Contradiction, hence $B\subseteq U$.

::: pf-proof

Steps 1 and 2 contradict each other. As $B$ was an arbitrary bounded component, every bounded component of $\RR^2-C$ lies in $U$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Each bounded component of $\RR^2-C$ lies in the simply connected open set $U$.

:::
