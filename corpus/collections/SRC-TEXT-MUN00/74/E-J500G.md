::: {.exercise}

Consider the space $X$ obtained from a seven-sided polygonal region by means of the labelling scheme $abaaab^{-1}a^{-1}$.
Show that the fundamental group of $X$ is the free product of two cyclic groups.
[Hint: See Theorem 68.7.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The relator computation is that of **WWK, Math Stack Exchange answer 1104612** (posted 2015-01-14 to question 1103462, "Practice Problem Fundamental Group of 7-figured polygon"). WWK begins at the normal subgroup the scheme generates and never checks the hypothesis of Theorem 74.2, that the pasting identifies all seven vertices of the region, which the book leaves to the reader (printed p. 451); that check is the second step below and is this card's own.

Write $v_{0},\dots,v_{6}$ for the successive vertices of the seven-sided region, so that the boundary traverses edges labelled $a,b,a,a,a,b^{-1},a^{-1}$ starting and ending at $v_{0}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #arrows}
The $a$-arrows are $v_{0}\to v_{1}$, $v_{2}\to v_{3}$, $v_{3}\to v_{4}$, $v_{4}\to v_{5}$ and $v_{0}\to v_{6}$; the $b$-arrows are $v_{1}\to v_{2}$ and $v_{6}\to v_{5}$.

::: pf-proof
The $i$-th edge of the boundary runs from $v_{i-1}$ to $v_{i}$. The edges labelled $a$ are the first, third, fourth and fifth, giving $v_{0}\to v_{1}$, $v_{2}\to v_{3}$, $v_{3}\to v_{4}$, $v_{4}\to v_{5}$; the seventh edge is labelled $a^{-1}$ and runs from $v_{6}$ to $v_{0}$, so the $a$-arrow on it points from $v_{0}$ to $v_{6}$. The second and sixth edges are labelled $b$ and $b^{-1}$ and run from $v_{1}$ to $v_{2}$ and from $v_{5}$ to $v_{6}$, so the $b$-arrows are $v_{1}\to v_{2}$ and $v_{6}\to v_{5}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #vertices}
The pasting identifies all seven vertices of the region in one class.

::: pf-proof
Edges carrying the same label are pasted preserving the arrow, so the initial points of the $a$-arrows are identified with one another and the terminal points with one another. By step [](#arrows){.pf-ref} the initial points are $v_{0},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}$ and the terminal points are $v_{1},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},v_{6}$. Their union is $\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},v_{6}\}$, all seven vertices, and the two sets meet in $v_{3}$ and $v_{4}$; so the identifications put all seven vertices in a single class. This is the hypothesis of Theorem 74.2 (printed p. 451), which the cited source leaves unchecked.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #presentation}
Theorem 74.2 gives $\pi_{1}(X)\cong\langle a,b\mid abaaab^{-1}a^{-1}=1\rangle$.

::: pf-proof
$X$ is obtained from the seven-sided region by the labelling scheme $abaaab^{-1}a^{-1}$, each of the two labels occurs at least once, and by step [](#vertices){.pf-ref} the pasting identifies all vertices of the region in one class. Under these hypotheses Theorem 74.2 presents $\pi_{1}(X)$ with one generator for each label and the scheme read as a relator.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conjugate}
$w=abaaab^{-1}a^{-1}$ is a conjugate of $a^{3}$, namely $w=(ab)\,a^{3}\,(ab)^{-1}$.

::: pf-proof
In the free group on $a,b$ one has $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}$, so
$$
(ab)\,a^{3}\,(ab)^{-1}=ab\,aaa\,b^{-1}a^{-1}=abaaab^{-1}a^{-1}=w .
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #normal-closure}
The least normal subgroup $N$ of $F(a,b)$ containing $w$ is the least normal subgroup containing $a^{3}$.

::: pf-proof
A normal subgroup contains an element exactly when it contains every conjugate of it; so a normal subgroup contains $w=(ab)a^{3}(ab)^{-1}$ exactly when it contains $a^{3}$. Hence the family of normal subgroups containing $w$ is the family containing $a^{3}$, and the two least members coincide.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cyclic-free-product}
$\pi_{1}(X)\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ*\ZZ$, the free product of two cyclic groups.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#presentation){.pf-ref} and [](#normal-closure){.pf-ref}, $\pi_{1}(X)\cong F(a,b)/N\cong\langle a,b\mid a^{3}=1\rangle$. Now $\langle a,b\mid\ \rangle$ is the free product $G_{1}*G_{2}$ with $G_{1}=\langle a\rangle\cong\ZZ$ and $G_{2}=\langle b\rangle\cong\ZZ$, and $\langle a,b\mid a^{3}\rangle$ is this free product modulo the least normal subgroup generated by the normal subgroup $N_{1}=3\ZZ$ of $G_{1}$ together with the trivial subgroup of $G_{2}$. Theorem 68.7, the theorem named in the printed hint, identifies this quotient as $(\ZZ/3\ZZ)*(\ZZ/\{0\})=\ZZ/3\ZZ*\ZZ$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\pi_{1}(X)$ is the free product of two cyclic groups.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#cyclic-free-product){.pf-ref} exhibits it as the free product of the cyclic group of order $3$ and an infinite cyclic group.
:::
