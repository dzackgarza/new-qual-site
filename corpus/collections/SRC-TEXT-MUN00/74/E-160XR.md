::: {.exercise}

The Klein bottle $K$ is the space obtained from a square by means of the labelling scheme $aba^{-1}b$.
Figure 74.11 indicates how $K$ can be pictured as an immersed surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

(a) Find a presentation for the fundamental group of $K$.

(b) Find a double covering map $p: T \to K$, where $T$ is the torus.
Describe the induced homomorphism of fundamental groups.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Compute the fundamental group of the Klein bottle $K$ and construct its 2-sheeted orientable torus covering $p: T \to K$.

<1>1. Part (a): Presentation of $\pi_1(K)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. In the square $P = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ with labelling scheme $a b a^{-1} b$, the four vertices are identified to a single equivalence class $x_0 \in K$.
    <2>2. The 1-skeleton $K^1$ is a wedge of two circles $S^1 \vee S^1$ represented by the loops $a$ and $b$, with $\pi_1(K^1, x_0) \cong \langle a, b \rangle$.
    <2>3. The single 2-cell is attached along the boundary loop $a b a^{-1} b$.
    <2>4. By the Seifert-van Kampen theorem / cellular presentation of fundamental groups,
        $$\pi_1(K, x_0) \cong \langle a, b \mid a b a^{-1} b = 1 \rangle \cong \langle a, b \mid a b a^{-1} = b^{-1} \rangle.$$

<1>2. Part (b): Construction of the 2-fold covering $p: T \to K$ and the induced map $p_*$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Universal covering action: The group $G = \pi_1(K)$ acts properly discontinuously on the universal cover $\mathbb{R}^2$ by affine isometries:
        $$\tau_a(x, y) = (x + 1, -y), \qquad \tau_b(x, y) = (x, y + 1).$$
        Note that $\tau_a \circ \tau_b \circ \tau_a^{-1}(x, y) = (x, y - 1) = \tau_b^{-1}(x, y)$, satisfying the relation $a b a^{-1} = b^{-1}$.
    <2>2. The orientation-preserving subgroup: Consider the subgroup $H \le G$ of words containing an even exponent sum in $a$:
        $$H = \langle a^2, b \rangle \triangleleft G.$$
    <2>3. Commutativity in $H$:
        $$a^2 b a^{-2} = a (a b a^{-1}) a^{-1} = a b^{-1} a^{-1} = (a b a^{-1})^{-1} = (b^{-1})^{-1} = b.$$
        Thus $a^2 b = b a^2$, and $H \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ is abelian of rank 2.
    <2>4. Identification of the quotient $\mathbb{R}^2 / H$: The translations $\tau_a^2(x, y) = (x + 2, y)$ and $\tau_b(x, y) = (x, y + 1)$ generate a standard 2-dimensional lattice $2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$. The quotient space $\mathbb{R}^2 / H$ is homeomorphic to the torus $T = S^1 \times S^1$.
    <2>5. The double cover $p: T \to K$: Since $[G : H] = 2$, the canonical projection $p: \mathbb{R}^2 / H \to \mathbb{R}^2 / G$ is a 2-sheeted covering map from the torus $T$ to the Klein bottle $K$.
    <2>6. Induced homomorphism $p_*$: Identifying $\pi_1(T) \cong \langle \alpha, \beta \mid \alpha\beta = \beta\alpha \rangle$, the induced homomorphism $p_*: \pi_1(T) \hookrightarrow \pi_1(K)$ is the inclusion of the index-2 subgroup $H$, given by:
        $$p_*(\alpha) = a^2, \qquad p_*(\beta) = b.$$
    Q.E.D.
:::
