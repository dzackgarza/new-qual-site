::: {.exercise}

Let $A$ be a set; let $\theset{X_\alpha}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of spaces; and let $\theset{f_\alpha}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of functions $f_\alpha: A \to X_\alpha$.

(a) Show there is a unique coarsest topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $A$ relative to which each of the functions $f_\alpha$ is continuous.

(b) Let

$$
\mathcal{S}_\beta = \theset{f_\beta^{-1}(U_\beta) \mid U_\beta \text{ is open in } X_\beta},
$$

and let $S = \bigcup \mathcal{S}_\beta$.
Show that $S$ is a subbasis for $\mathcal{T}$.

(c) Show that a map $g: Y \to A$ is continuous relative to $\mathcal{T}$ if and only if each map $f_\alpha \circ g$ is continuous.

(d) Let $f: A \to \prod X_\alpha$ be defined by the equation

$$
f(a) = (f_\alpha(a))_{\alpha \in J};
$$

let $Z$ denote the subspace $f(A)$ of the product space $\prod X_\alpha$.
Show that the image under $f$ of each element of $\mathcal{T}$ is an open set of $Z$.
:::

::: {.solution}

All four parts are **Dan Whitman, *Topology, Second Edition by James Munkres: Solutions Manual* (30 November 2019), Exercise 19.10, printed pp. 197--198** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-whitman.pdf`): the intersection of the topologies making the maps continuous is the coarsest and unique; the union of the preimages of open sets is a subbasis for it by Exercise 13.5; a map out of it is continuous exactly when its composites with the $f_{\alpha}$ are; and a finite-intersection basis element is carried to a product basis element in the image. The case the earlier prose ran together is the last one when the finite index set is empty, which is separated out at step [](#image-inside){.pf-ref}.

The statement was checked against Munkres, *Topology* (2000), §19 Exercise 10, with Exercise 13.4 on printed p. 83, Exercise 13.5 on printed p. 83 and the definition of the product topology on printed p. 114. Throughout, $A$ is a set, $\theset{X_{\alpha}}_{\alpha\in J}$ a family of spaces and $f_{\alpha}\colon A\to X_{\alpha}$ a function for each $\alpha$; $\mathcal{C}$ is the collection of topologies on $A$ relative to which every $f_{\alpha}$ is continuous; $f\colon A\to\prod_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}$ is the evaluation $f(a)\coloneqq(f_{\alpha}(a))_{\alpha\in J}$; $Z$ is the subspace $f(A)$; and a *product basis element* is a product $\prod_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}$ of open sets with $V_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}$ for all but finitely many $\alpha$ (printed p. 114). The empty intersection of subbasis elements is taken to be $A$ itself, which is how the basis generated by a subbasis is defined.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a}

(a) There is a unique coarsest topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $A$ making every $f_{\alpha}$ continuous.

:::: pf-proof

The first part is settled by intersecting every topology that makes all the maps continuous.

::: {.pf-step #intersection}

$\mathcal{C}$ is nonempty and $\mathcal{T}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$ is a topology on $A$.

::: pf-proof

The discrete topology is in $\mathcal{C}$, every map out of a discrete space being continuous, so $\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing$ and the intersection is taken over a nonempty family. An intersection of topologies on a set is a topology (Exercise 13.4(a)), so $\mathcal{T}$ is one.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #continuous}

Every $f_{\alpha}$ is continuous with respect to $\mathcal{T}$.

::: pf-proof

Let $U_{\alpha}$ be open in $X_{\alpha}$. Since each $\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}$ makes $f_{\alpha}$ continuous, $f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$ for every such $\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$, hence $f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})\in\bigcap\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{T}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coarsest}

$\mathcal{T}$ is the coarsest element of $\mathcal{C}$, and it is the only one.

::: pf-proof

If $\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{C}$ then $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}$, so no topology in $\mathcal{C}$ is strictly coarser than $\mathcal{T}$, and $\mathcal{T}$ is in $\mathcal{C}$ by step [](#continuous){.pf-ref}. If $\mathcal{T}_{1}$ and $\mathcal{T}_{2}$ were both coarsest, each would contain the other, so they are equal.

:::

:::

::::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}

(b) With $\mathcal{S}_{\beta}\coloneqq\theset{f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})\mid U_{\beta}\text{ open in }X_{\beta}}$ and $\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\beta\in J}\mathcal{S}_{\beta}$, the collection $\mathcal{S}$ is a subbasis for $\mathcal{T}$.

::: pf-proof

For a topology $\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$ on $A$, every $f_{\alpha}$ is continuous with respect to $\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$ if and only if $\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$: each $f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})$ is in $\mathcal{S}$, and conversely every member of $\mathcal{S}$ is some $f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})$, which must be open if $f_{\beta}$ is continuous. So the elements of $\mathcal{C}$ are exactly the topologies on $A$ containing $\mathcal{S}$, and by Exercise 13.5 the intersection of all topologies containing a collection is the topology that collection generates. Hence $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap\mathcal{C}$ is the topology generated by the subbasis $\mathcal{S}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c}

(c) A map $g\colon Y\to A$ is continuous with respect to $\mathcal{T}$ if and only if each $f_{\alpha}\circ g$ is continuous.

:::: pf-proof

One direction is immediate, the other uses that $\mathcal{S}$ is a subbasis.

::: {.pf-step #c-forward}

If $g$ is continuous then each $f_{\alpha}\circ g$ is continuous.

::: pf-proof

$f_{\alpha}$ is continuous by step [](#continuous){.pf-ref}, and a composite of continuous maps is continuous (Theorem 18.2(c)).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-converse}

If each $f_{\alpha}\circ g$ is continuous then $g$ is continuous.

::: pf-proof

Let $U\in\mathcal{T}$. By step [](#b){.pf-ref} the collection $\mathcal{S}$ is a subbasis for $\mathcal{T}$, so $U$ is a union of finite intersections of members of $\mathcal{S}$. The preimage under $g$ of a member $f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})$ of $\mathcal{S}$ is $(f_{\alpha}\circ g)^{-1}(U_{\alpha})$, open in $Y$ by hypothesis; preimages preserve unions and intersections, so $g^{-1}(U)$ is a union of finite intersections of open sets of $Y$, hence open. Therefore $g$ is continuous.

:::

:::

::::

:::

::: {.pf-step #d}

(d) For $U\in\mathcal{T}$ the image $f(U)$ is open in the subspace $Z=f(A)$.

:::: pf-proof

Fix $U\in\mathcal{T}$ and a point $f(a)\in f(U)$ with $a\in U$.

::: {.pf-step #basis-element}

For $a\in U$ there is a basis element $B_{A}$ of $\mathcal{T}$ with $a\in B_{A}\subset U$, and $B_{A}=\bigcap_{\beta\in I}f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})$ for a finite $I\subset J$ and open $U_{\beta}\subset X_{\beta}$.

::: pf-proof

The basis generated by the subbasis $\mathcal{S}$ consists of the finite intersections of members of $\mathcal{S}$ (with the empty intersection equal to $A$), so $U$, being open, contains such a basis element about $a$. This is the only place the empty $I$ can occur, and then $B_{A}=A$ and the inclusion $B_{A}\subset U$ reads $A\subset U$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product-element}

$B_{P}\coloneqq\prod_{\alpha\in J}V_{\alpha}$, with $V_{\alpha}\coloneqq U_{\alpha}$ for $\alpha\in I$ and $V_{\alpha}\coloneqq X_{\alpha}$ otherwise, is a product basis element, $B_{Z}\coloneqq Z\cap B_{P}$ is a basis element of $Z$, and $f(a)\in B_{Z}$.

::: pf-proof

Each $V_{\alpha}$ is open, and $V_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}$ for every $\alpha$ outside the finite set $I$, so $B_{P}$ is a product basis element (printed p. 114); it is the whole product when $I$ is empty, which is a basis element too. Its trace on the subspace $Z$ is a basis element of $Z$. Finally $f_{\beta}(a)\in U_{\beta}=V_{\beta}$ for $\beta\in I$ because $a\in f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})$, and $f_{\alpha}(a)\in X_{\alpha}=V_{\alpha}$ for $\alpha\notin I$; so $f(a)\in B_{P}$, and $f(a)\in Z$ by definition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #image-inside}

$B_{Z}\subset f(U)$.

::: pf-proof

Let $z\in B_{Z}$. Since $z\in Z=f(A)$ there is $x\in A$ with $f(x)=z$, and $z_{\beta}\in V_{\beta}=U_{\beta}$ for every $\beta\in I$. Hence $f_{\beta}(x)=z_{\beta}\in U_{\beta}$, that is $x\in f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})$ for each $\beta\in I$, so $x\in B_{A}\subset U$ --- and when $I$ is empty this reads $x\in B_{A}=A$ with $B_{A}\subset U$ as at step [](#basis-element){.pf-ref}. Therefore $z=f(x)\in f(U)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$f(U)$ is open in $Z$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#product-element){.pf-ref} and [](#image-inside){.pf-ref} exhibit, about an arbitrary point $f(a)$ of $f(U)$, a basis element $B_{Z}$ of $Z$ with $f(a)\in B_{Z}\subset f(U)$. So every point of $f(U)$ is interior to it in $Z$, which is what it means for $f(U)$ to be open in $Z$.

:::

:::

::::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the four assertions of the exercise hold.

::: pf-proof

Parts [](#a){.pf-ref}, [](#b){.pf-ref}, [](#c){.pf-ref} and [](#d){.pf-ref} establish respectively the existence and uniqueness of the coarsest topology, that $\mathcal{S}$ is a subbasis for it, the characterisation of continuity of maps into $A$, and the openness of $f(U)$ in $f(A)$; those are exactly the four parts of the exercise.

:::
