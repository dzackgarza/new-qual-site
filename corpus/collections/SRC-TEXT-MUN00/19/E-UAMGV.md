::: {.exercise}

Let $A$ be a set; let $\theset{X_\alpha}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of spaces; and let $\theset{f_\alpha}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of functions $f_\alpha: A \to X_\alpha$.

(a) Show there is a unique coarsest topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $A$ relative to which each of the functions $f_\alpha$ is continuous.

(b) Let

$$
\mathcal{S}_\beta = \theset{f_\beta^{-1}(U_\beta) \mid U_\beta \text{ is open in } X_\beta},
$$

and let $S = \bigcup \mathcal{S}_\beta$.
Show that $S$ is a subbasis for $\mathcal{T}$.

(c) Show that a map $g: Y \to A$ is continuous relative to $\mathcal{T}$ if and only if each map $f_\alpha \circ g$ is continuous.

(d) Let $f: A \to \prod X_\alpha$ be defined by the equation

$$
f(a) = (f_\alpha(a))_{\alpha \in J};
$$

let $Z$ denote the subspace $f(A)$ of the product space $\prod X_\alpha$.
Show that the image under $f$ of each element of $\mathcal{T}$ is an open set of $Z$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **Dan Whitman, *Topology, Second Edition by James Munkres: Solutions Manual* (30 November 2019), Exercise 19.10, printed pp. 197--198** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-whitman.pdf`); all four parts are his, with the reference to Exercise 13.5 for the topology generated by a subbasis.

Write $\mathcal{C}$ for the collection of topologies on $A$ relative to which every $f_{\alpha}$ is continuous.

(a) $\mathcal{C}$ is nonempty, since the discrete topology is in it. Put $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_{\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{T}'$; by Exercise 13.4(a) (printed p. 83) an intersection of topologies is a topology. Each $f_{\alpha}$ is continuous with respect to $\mathcal{T}$, because for open $U_{\alpha}\subset X_{\alpha}$ the set $f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})$ belongs to every $\mathcal{T}'\in\mathcal{C}$, hence to $\mathcal{T}$. And if $\mathcal{T}''\in\mathcal{C}$ then $\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}''$; so $\mathcal{T}$ is the coarsest member of $\mathcal{C}$, and it is unique because two coarsest members contain each other.

(b) Let $\mathcal{S}_{\beta}=\{f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})\mid U_{\beta}\ \text{open in}\ X_{\beta}\}$ and $\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{\beta\in J}\mathcal{S}_{\beta}$. For a topology $\mathcal{T}'$ on $A$, every $f_{\alpha}$ is continuous relative to $\mathcal{T}'$ if and only if $\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{T}'$: each $f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})$ is in $\mathcal{S}$, and conversely every member of $\mathcal{S}$ is some $f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})$ and so must be open if $f_{\beta}$ is continuous. Hence the topologies of $\mathcal{C}$ are exactly the topologies on $A$ containing $\mathcal{S}$. By Exercise 13.5 an intersection of all topologies containing a collection is the topology that collection generates, so $\mathcal{T}$ is the topology generated by the subbasis $\mathcal{S}$.

(c) If $g:Y\to A$ is continuous and $U_{\alpha}$ is open in $X_{\alpha}$, then $f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{T}$ and $(f_{\alpha}\circ g)^{-1}(U_{\alpha})=g^{-1}(f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha}))$ is open in $Y$. Conversely, suppose every $f_{\alpha}\circ g$ is continuous and let $U\in\mathcal{T}$. By (b) the set $U$ is a union of finite intersections of members of $\mathcal{S}$, so $g^{-1}(U)$ is a union of finite intersections of the sets $g^{-1}(f_{\alpha}^{-1}(U_{\alpha}))=(f_{\alpha}\circ g)^{-1}(U_{\alpha})$, each open in $Y$: hence $g^{-1}(U)$ is open and $g$ is continuous.

(d) Let $U\in\mathcal{T}$ and let $y\in f(U)$, say $y=f(a)$ with $a\in U$. Since $U$ is open there is a basis element $B_{A}\ni a$ with $B_{A}\subset U$, and by (b) a basis element is a finite intersection of subbasis elements: $B_{A}=\bigcap_{\beta\in I}f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})$ with $I\subset J$ finite and $U_{\beta}$ open in $X_{\beta}$. Put $V_{\alpha}=U_{\alpha}$ for $\alpha\in I$ and $V_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}$ otherwise; then $B_{P}=\prod_{\alpha\in J}V_{\alpha}$ is a product basis element, because $V_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}$ off the finite set $I$, and $B_{Z}=Z\cap B_{P}$ is a basis element of the subspace $Z=f(A)$.

Now $y=f(a)\in B_{P}$, since $f_{\beta}(a)\in U_{\beta}$ for $\beta\in I$ (as $a\in f_{\beta}^{-1}(U_{\beta})$) and $f_{\alpha}(a)\in X_{\alpha}=V_{\alpha}$ otherwise; so $y\in B_{Z}$. Conversely let $z\in B_{Z}$, say $z=f(x)$ with $x\in A$ and $z_{\alpha}\in V_{\alpha}$ for all $\alpha$. Then $f_{\beta}(x)=z_{\beta}\in U_{\beta}$ for every $\beta\in I$, so $x\in B_{A}\subset U$ and $z=f(x)\in f(U)$. Hence $B_{Z}\subset f(U)$, and $B_{Z}$ is a basis element of $Z$ containing $y$. So every point of $f(U)$ is interior to $f(U)$ in $Z$, that is, $f(U)$ is open in $Z$.
:::
