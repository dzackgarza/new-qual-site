::: {.exercise}

Let $A$ be a set; let $\ts{X_\alpha}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of spaces; and let $\ts{f_\alpha}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of functions $f_\alpha: A \to X_\alpha$.

(a) Show there is a unique coarsest topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $A$ relative to which each of the functions $f_\alpha$ is continuous.

(b) Let

$$
\mathcal{S}_\beta = \ts{f_\beta^{-1}(U_\beta) \mid U_\beta \text{ is open in } X_\beta},
$$

and let $S = \bigcup \mathcal{S}_\beta$.
Show that $S$ is a subbasis for $\mathcal{T}$.

(c) Show that a map $g: Y \to A$ is continuous relative to $\mathcal{T}$ if and only if each map $f_\alpha \circ g$ is continuous.

(d) Let $f: A \to \prod X_\alpha$ be defined by the equation

$$
f(a) = (f_\alpha(a))_{\alpha \in J};
$$

let $Z$ denote the subspace $f(A)$ of the product space $\prod X_\alpha$.
Show that the image under $f$ of each element of $\mathcal{T}$ is an open set of $Z$.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) Let
\[
\mathcal S=\bigcup_{\beta\in J}\{f_\beta^{-1}(U):U\subseteq X_\beta\text{ open}\}.
\]
Let $\mathcal T$ be the topology generated by the subbasis $\mathcal S$. Then every $f_\beta$ is continuous by construction. If $\mathcal U$ is any topology on $A$ making every $f_\beta$ continuous, every member of $\mathcal S$ belongs to $\mathcal U$, hence the topology generated by $\mathcal S$ satisfies
\[
\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal U.
\]
Therefore $\mathcal T$ is the unique coarsest such topology.

(b) This is exactly the construction in part (a): $\mathcal S$ is a subbasis for $\mathcal T$.

(c) If $g:Y\to A$ is continuous, then each $f_\alpha\circ g$ is continuous as a composition. Conversely, suppose every $f_\alpha\circ g$ is continuous. For a subbasic set $f_\alpha^{-1}(U)$ of $A$,
\[
g^{-1}(f_\alpha^{-1}(U))=(f_\alpha\circ g)^{-1}(U),
\]
which is open in $Y$. Therefore $g$ is continuous.

(d) Define
\[
f:A\to\prod_\alpha X_\alpha,\qquad f(a)=(f_\alpha(a))_\alpha,
\]
and let $Z=f(A)$. The product topology on $\prod X_\alpha$ has subbasis $\pi_\alpha^{-1}(U)$, and
\[
f^{-1}(\pi_\alpha^{-1}(U))=f_\alpha^{-1}(U).
\]
Thus $\mathcal T$ is exactly the topology generated by inverse images under $f$ of open sets of the product.

Every $\mathcal T$-open set is therefore of the form
\[
V=f^{-1}(W)
\]
for some product-open $W$: this follows first for finite intersections of subbasic sets and then for arbitrary unions. Such $V$ is saturated on fibers of $f$, and
\[
f(V)=f(A)\cap W=Z\cap W.
\]
Hence $f(V)$ is open in the subspace $Z$.
:::
