::: {.exercise}

Prove Theorem 19.4: if each space $X_\alpha$ is a Hausdorff space, then $\prod X_\alpha$ is a Hausdorff space in both the box and product topologies.
:::

::: {.solution}

The complete published solution is **Dan Whitman, *Topology, Second Edition by James Munkres: Solutions Manual* (30 November 2019), Exercise 19.3** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-whitman.pdf`); the proof is his, separated at the index where the two points differ. The statement was checked against Munkres, *Topology* (2000), §19 Exercise 3, which asks for Theorem 19.4 on printed p. 116; the box topology and the product topology on $\prod_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}$ are as defined in §19 , and a product of open sets that differs from the whole product at only finitely many indices is a basis element of the product topology.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #separate}

There is an index $\beta$ at which $x$ and $y$ differ, and in $X_{\beta}$ there are disjoint open sets $W_{x}\ni x_{\beta}$ and $W_{y}\ni y_{\beta}$.

::: pf-proof

Since $x\neq y$ the functions $\alpha\mapsto x_{\alpha}$ and $\alpha\mapsto y_{\alpha}$ differ somewhere, say at $\beta$. The space $X_{\beta}$ is Hausdorff, so the distinct points $x_{\beta}$ and $y_{\beta}$ have disjoint open neighbourhoods $W_{x}$ and $W_{y}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis-element}

For each $\alpha$ put $U_{\alpha}\coloneqq W_{x}$ if $\alpha=\beta$ and $U_{\alpha}\coloneqq X_{\alpha}$ otherwise, and $V_{\alpha}\coloneqq W_{y}$ if $\alpha=\beta$ and $V_{\alpha}\coloneqq X_{\alpha}$ otherwise. Then $U\coloneqq\prod_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}$ and $V\coloneqq\prod_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}$ are open in both topologies on $\prod_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}$, and $x\in U$ and $y\in V$.

::: pf-proof

Each $U_{\alpha}$ is open in $X_{\alpha}$, and $U_{\alpha}\neq X_{\alpha}$ for the single index $\alpha=\beta$; so $U$ is a basis element of the product topology (§19), hence open in it. The box topology contains every open set of the product topology, since it is generated by all products of open sets and every product basis element of the product topology is one of those; so $U$ is open in the box topology too. The same argument applies to $V$ with $W_{y}$ in place of $W_{x}$. Finally $x_{\beta}\in W_{x}$ and $x_{\alpha}\in X_{\alpha}$ for $\alpha\neq\beta$ give $x\in U$, and likewise $y\in V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #disjoint}

$U\cap V=\varnothing$.

::: pf-proof

A point $z$ of both would satisfy $z_{\beta}\in U_{\beta}=W_{x}$ and $z_{\beta}\in V_{\beta}=W_{y}$, contradicting $W_{x}\cap W_{y}=\varnothing$ by step [](#separate){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $\prod_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}$ is Hausdorff in the box topology and in the product topology, which is Theorem 19.4.

::: pf-proof

Let $x\neq y$ in the product. Steps [](#basis-element){.pf-ref} and [](#disjoint){.pf-ref} exhibit, in each of the two topologies, open sets $U\ni x$ and $V\ni y$ with $U\cap V=\varnothing$. A space is Hausdorff when distinct points have disjoint neighbourhoods, so the product is Hausdorff in both topologies.

:::
