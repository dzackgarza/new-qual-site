::: {.exercise}

(a) In $\RR^n$, define

$$
d'(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \abs{x_1 - y_1} + \dots + \abs{x_n - y_n}.
$$

Show that $d'$ is a metric that induces the usual topology of $\RR^n$.
Sketch the basis elements under $d'$ when $n = 2$.

(b) More generally, given $p \geq 1$, define

$$
d'(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} \abs{x_i - y_i}^p \right]^{1/p}
$$

for $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \in \RR^n$.
Assume that $d'$ is a metric.
Show that it induces the usual topology on $\RR^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 20.1, PDF p. 17** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`); both parts are theirs.

Let $\rho$ be the square metric $\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\max_{i}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}$ on $\RR^{n}$, which induces the usual topology (Theorem 20.3, printed p. 122).

(a) For $\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\RR^{n}$,
$$
\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\max_{i}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}\le\sum_{i=1}^{n}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}=d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\le n\max_{i}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}=n\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).
$$
$d'$ is a metric: it is a sum of nonnegative terms, vanishes exactly when every term does, is symmetric, and satisfies the triangle inequality term by term. The two inequalities give, for every $\mathbf{x}$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
$$
B_{\rho}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon/n)\subset B_{d'}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon)\subset B_{\rho}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon),
$$
so by Lemma 20.2 (printed p. 121) the metrics $d'$ and $\rho$ induce the same topology; since $\rho$ induces the usual topology, so does $d'$. For $n=2$ the balls of $d'$ are open diamonds with vertices on the axes at distance $\varepsilon$ from the centre.

(b) For $p\ge1$ and $\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\RR^{n}$,
$$
\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{p}=\max_{i}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}^{p}\le\sum_{i=1}^{n}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}^{p}\le n\max_{i}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}^{p}=n\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{p},
$$
so taking $p$-th roots,
$$
\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\le d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\le n^{1/p}\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).
$$
Hence $B_{\rho}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon/n^{1/p})\subset B_{d'}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon)\subset B_{\rho}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon)$, and $d'$ induces the usual topology by Lemma 20.2 again.
:::
