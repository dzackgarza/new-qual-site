::: {.exercise}

(a) In $\mathbb{R}^n$, define

$$
d'(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \abs{x_1 - y_1} + \dots + \abs{x_n - y_n}.
$$

Show that $d'$ is a metric that induces the usual topology of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
Sketch the basis elements under $d'$ when $n = 2$.

(b) More generally, given $p \geq 1$, define

$$
d'(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} \abs{x_i - y_i}^p \right]^{1/p}
$$

for $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$.
Assume that $d'$ is a metric.
Show that it induces the usual topology on $\mathbb{R}^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\rho(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=\max_i\abs{x_i-y_i}$ be the square metric, which induces the usual topology of $\RR^n$, and write $d_p$ for the metric of (b), so that $d_1$ is the metric $d'$ of (a).

<1>1. $d_1$ is a metric.

::: {.proof}
$d_1(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\ge0$, with equality if and only if $x_i=y_i$ for every $i$, and $d_1$ is symmetric.
Summing $\abs{x_i-z_i}\le\abs{x_i-y_i}+\abs{y_i-z_i}$ over $i$ gives the triangle inequality.
:::

<1>2. For $p\ge1$, $\rho\le d_p\le n^{1/p}\rho$.

::: {.proof}
For each $j$, $\abs{x_j-y_j}=(\abs{x_j-y_j}^p)^{1/p}\le d_p(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$; take the maximum over $j$.
Also $d_p(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\le\bigl(n\,\rho(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)^p\bigr)^{1/p}=n^{1/p}\rho(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$.
:::

<1>3. For $p\ge1$, $d_p$ induces the usual topology of $\RR^n$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $B_\rho(\mathbf x,\varepsilon/n^{1/p})\subseteq B_{d_p}(\mathbf x,\varepsilon)\subseteq B_\rho(\mathbf x,\varepsilon)$ for every $\mathbf x$ and $\varepsilon>0$.
So every $d_p$-ball about a point contains a $\rho$-ball about it and conversely, and the two metrics have the same open sets.
:::

<1>4. For $n=2$, the $d_1$-ball $B_{d_1}((x_0,y_0),r)$ is the open square with vertices $(x_0\pm r,y_0)$ and $(x_0,y_0\pm r)$, whose sides make angles of $45^\circ$ with the axes.

::: {.proof}
The inequality $\abs{x-x_0}+\abs{y-y_0}<r$ describes, in each quadrant about $(x_0,y_0)$, the open half-plane bounded by the line through two adjacent vertices.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1, <1>3 with $p=1$, and <1>4 answer (a); step <1>3 answers (b).
:::
:::
