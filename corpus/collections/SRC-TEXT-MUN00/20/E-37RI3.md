::: {.exercise}

(a) In $\RR^n$, define

$$
d'(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \abs{x_1 - y_1} + \dots + \abs{x_n - y_n}.
$$

Show that $d'$ is a metric that induces the usual topology of $\RR^n$.
Sketch the basis elements under $d'$ when $n = 2$.

(b) More generally, given $p \geq 1$, define

$$
d'(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} \abs{x_i - y_i}^p \right]^{1/p}
$$

for $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \in \RR^n$.
Assume that $d'$ is a metric.
Show that it induces the usual topology on $\RR^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The comparison inequalities and the two ball inclusions are those of **"positron0802"**, *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 20.1, PDF p. 17 (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`); that solution states the metric axioms of $d'$ in (a) and the diamond picture not at all, so those are supplied here. The statement was checked against Munkres, *Topology* (2000), §20 Exercise 1, printed p. 126, with Lemma 20.2 on printed p. 122 and Theorem 20.3 on printed p. 123; the card's earlier pages for those two, p. 121 and p. 122, are corrected.

Throughout, $\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\coloneqq\max_{i}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}$ is the square metric on $\RR^{n}$, and for $p\geq1$
$$
d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\coloneqq\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}^{p}\Big]^{1/p}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #square-metric}
$\rho$ is a metric on $\RR^{n}$ whose topology is the usual one.

::: pf-proof
$\rho$ is the square metric of §20 and Theorem 20.3 (printed p. 123) says that the topologies induced by the euclidean metric and by $\rho$ on $\RR^{n}$ are both the product topology; the product topology on $\RR^{n}$ is the usual topology of $\RR^{n}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #l1-metric}
(a) $d'$ for $p=1$ is a metric on $\RR^{n}$.

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #positivity}
$d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\geq0$, with equality exactly when $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}$.

::: pf-proof
$d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$ is a sum of the nonnegative numbers $\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}$, so it is nonnegative, and a sum of nonnegative terms vanishes exactly when every term does; $\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}=0$ for all $i$ means $x_{i}=y_{i}$ for all $i$, that is $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #symmetry-triangle}
$d'$ is symmetric and satisfies the triangle inequality.

::: pf-proof
$\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}=\abs{y_{i}-x_{i}}$ gives symmetry. For the triangle inequality let $\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}\in\RR^{n}$; then
$$
d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})=\sum_{i}\abs{x_{i}-z_{i}}\leq\sum_{i}\big(\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}+\abs{y_{i}-z_{i}}\big)=d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+d'(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}),
$$
using the triangle inequality of $\abs{\cdot}$ in $\RR$ in each summand.
:::

:::

::::

:::

::: {.pf-step #l1-comparison}
(a) For all $\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\RR^{n}$,
$$
\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq n\,\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).
$$

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #lower}
$\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$.

::: pf-proof
$\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$ is the largest of the $n$ nonnegative numbers $\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}$, hence at most their sum, which is $d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #upper}
$d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq n\,\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$.

::: pf-proof
Each $\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}$ is at most $\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$, so the sum of the $n$ terms is at most $n\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$.
:::

:::

::::

:::

::: {.pf-step #l1-same-topology}
(a) $d'$ induces the usual topology of $\RR^{n}$.

::: pf-proof
By Lemma 20.2 (printed p. 122) two metrics induce the same topology when each ball of the one about a point contains a ball of the other about that point. Step [](#l1-comparison){.pf-ref} gives, for every $\mathbf{x}$ and every $\varepsilon>0$,
$$
B_{\rho}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon/n)\subset B_{d'}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon)\subset B_{\rho}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon):
$$
the second inclusion because $\rho\leq d'$, the first because $d'\leq n\rho$ turns $d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})<\varepsilon$ into $\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})<\varepsilon/n$. So $d'$ and $\rho$ induce the same topology, which is the usual one by step [](#square-metric){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #diamonds}
(a) For $n=2$ the basis elements under $d'$ are the open diamonds
$$
B_{d'}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon)=\theset{\mathbf{y}\in\RR^{2}\mid\abs{y_{1}-x_{1}}+\abs{y_{2}-x_{2}}<\varepsilon},
$$
with vertices at $x_{1}\pm\varepsilon$ and $x_{2}\pm\varepsilon$ along the coordinate directions.

::: pf-proof
This is the definition of the ball for $p=1$ and $n=2$: the set of points of the plane whose coordinate deviations from $\mathbf{x}$ add to less than $\varepsilon$. Its boundary is the locus $\abs{y_{1}-x_{1}}+\abs{y_{2}-x_{2}}=\varepsilon$, whose four vertices are the points $(x_{1}+\varepsilon,x_{2})$, $(x_{1},x_{2}+\varepsilon)$, $(x_{1}-\varepsilon,x_{2})$ and $(x_{1},x_{2}-\varepsilon)$, joined in that order by the four segments on which one deviation increases while the other decreases; so the ball is the open diamond with those vertices, each at distance $\varepsilon$ from $\mathbf{x}$ along one of the coordinate directions.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lp-comparison}
(b) For $p\geq1$ and all $\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\RR^{n}$,
$$
\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq d'(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq n^{1/p}\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).
$$

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #powers}
$\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{p}\leq\sum_{i}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}^{p}\leq n\,\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{p}$.

::: pf-proof
$\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{p}=\max_{i}\abs{x_{i}-y_{i}}^{p}$ is the largest of the $n$ nonnegative summands, hence at most their sum, and each summand is at most $\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{p}$, so their sum is at most $n\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{p}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #roots}
The comparison follows by taking $p$-th roots.

::: pf-proof
The map $t\mapsto t^{1/p}$ is defined and increasing on $[0,\infty)$ for $p\geq1$, so it preserves the two inequalities of step [](#powers){.pf-ref}; $\big(\rho^{p}\big)^{1/p}=\rho$ and $\big(n\rho^{p}\big)^{1/p}=n^{1/p}\rho$ for $\rho\geq0$, and the middle term becomes $d'$ by its definition.
:::

:::

::::

:::

::: pf-step
(b) Hence $d'$ for any $p\geq1$ induces the usual topology on $\RR^{n}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#lp-comparison){.pf-ref}, for every $\mathbf{x}$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
$$
B_{\rho}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon/n^{1/p})\subset B_{d'}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon)\subset B_{\rho}(\mathbf{x},\varepsilon),
$$
so $d'$ and $\rho$ induce the same topology by Lemma 20.2 (printed p. 122), and that topology is the usual one by step [](#square-metric){.pf-ref}. Together with steps [](#l1-metric){.pf-ref}, [](#l1-same-topology){.pf-ref} and [](#diamonds){.pf-ref} this answers both parts.
:::
