::: {.exercise}
Let $P$ be the group of bijections of the set $\{0, 1, 2\}$ with itself.
For $i = 1, 2$, let $\pi_i \in P$ be the bijection with $\pi_i(i) = i - 1$, $\pi_i(i-1) = i$, and $\pi_i(j) = j$ otherwise, and let $G_i$ be the subgroup generated by $\pi_i$.
Check that each $G_i$ has order $2$, that $G_1$ and $G_2$ generate $P$, and that $P$ is not the free product of $G_1$ and $G_2$: for instance, the reduced words $(\pi_1, \pi_2, \pi_1)$ and $(\pi_2, \pi_1, \pi_2)$ represent the same element of $P$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write a bijection of $\theset{0,1,2}$ as a product of cycles, so that $\pi_1=(0\,1)$ and $\pi_2=(1\,2)$ in the notation where $(a\,b)$ interchanges $a$ and $b$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

$P$ has six elements, and each $G_i$ has order $2$.

::: pf-proof

A bijection of a three-element set is determined by the images of $0,1,2$, so $P$ has $3!=6$ elements. The map $\pi_i$ interchanges the two elements $i-1$ and $i$ and fixes the remaining one, so $\pi_i\neq\mathrm{id}$ while $\pi_i^2=\mathrm{id}$; hence $G_i=\theset{\mathrm{id},\pi_i}$ has order $2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$G_1$ and $G_2$ generate $P$.

::: pf-proof

By direct computation, $\pi_1\pi_2=(0\,1\,2)$ and $\pi_1\pi_2\pi_1=(0\,2)$. The subgroup $H=\langle\pi_1,\pi_2\rangle$ therefore contains $(0\,1)$, $(1\,2)$, $(0\,2)$ and the $3$-cycle $(0\,1\,2)$ with its powers. Since every element of $P$ is either the identity, one of the three transpositions, or one of the two $3$-cycles, $H=P$ has order $6$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$P$ is not the free product of $G_1$ and $G_2$.

::: pf-proof

By the extension condition of Lemma 68.3 (printed p. 418) the assignments $\pi_i\mapsto\pi_i$ extend to a homomorphism $\phi:G_1*G_2\to P$. In the free product the words $(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_1)$ and $(\pi_2,\pi_1,\pi_2)$ are reduced and distinct, because by Theorem 68.2 (printed p. 415) each element of a free product has a unique reduced representative. But in $P$,

$$
\pi_1\pi_2\pi_1=(0\,2)=\pi_2\pi_1\pi_2 ,
$$

the middle computation by $\pi_2\pi_1=(0\,2\,1)$ and $(0\,2\,1)(1\,2)=(0\,2)$. So $\phi$ is not injective, and $P$ is not the free product of $G_1$ and $G_2$.

:::
