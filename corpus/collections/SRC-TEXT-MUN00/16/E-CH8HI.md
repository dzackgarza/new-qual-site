::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ and $X'$ denote a single set in the topologies $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}'$, respectively; let $Y$ and $Y'$ denote a single set in the topologies $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{U}'$, respectively.
Assume these sets are nonempty.

(a) Show that if $\mathcal{T}' \supset \mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{U}' \supset \mathcal{U}$, then the product topology on $X' \times Y'$ is finer than the product topology on $X \times Y$.

(b) Does the converse of (a) hold?
Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 16.5** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`); part (a) and the answer to (b) are the set's. Part (b) is answered **no** by Whitman in his Exercise 16.5(b), and that answer is not usable: his counterexample passes to the subspace topologies on the two-point set in each factor, which coincide, so the hypothesis of the converse ($\mathcal{T}'\not\supset\mathcal{T}$) is not met; the defect is recorded on this card rather than followed.

Throughout, the product topology on $X\times Y$ is the one generated by the basis $\{U\times V: U\in\mathcal{T},\, V\in\mathcal{U}\}$ (§16), and likewise for $X'\times Y'$ with the primed topologies.

::: pf

::: pf-step
(a) If $\mathcal{T}'\supset\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{U}'\supset\mathcal{U}$, then the product topology on $X'\times Y'$ is finer than the product topology on $X\times Y$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #a-basis}
Every basis element of $X\times Y$ is a basis element of $X'\times Y'$.

::: pf-proof
Let $W=U\times V$ with $U\in\mathcal{T}$ and $V\in\mathcal{U}$. By hypothesis $U\in\mathcal{T}'$ and $V\in\mathcal{U}'$, so $W$ belongs to the basis of $X'\times Y'$ given above.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-union}
Every open set of $X\times Y$ is open in $X'\times Y'$.

::: pf-proof
An open set of $X\times Y$ is a union of basis elements of $X\times Y$ (Lemma 13.1), each of which is open in $X'\times Y'$ by step [](#a-basis){.pf-ref}, and a union of open sets is open (§12).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
A topology $\mathcal{V}'$ is finer than $\mathcal{V}$ exactly when $\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{V}'$ (§13), which is what step [](#a-union){.pf-ref} asserts of the two product topologies.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
(b) **The converse holds**: if the product topology on $X'\times Y'$ is finer than the product topology on $X\times Y$, then $\mathcal{T}'\supset\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{U}'\supset\mathcal{U}$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #b-neigh}
For $U\in\mathcal{T}$ and $x\in U$ there is $U'\in\mathcal{T}'$ with $x\in U'\subset U$; hence $U\in\mathcal{T}'$.

::: pf-proof
If $U=\varnothing$ then $U\in\mathcal{T}'$. Otherwise choose $x\in U$ and, since $Y\ne\varnothing$, a point $y\in Y$. The set $U\times Y$ is a basis element of $X\times Y$, hence open in $X\times Y$, hence open in $X'\times Y'$ by the hypothesis. Since the sets $U'\times V'$ with $U'\in\mathcal{T}'$, $V'\in\mathcal{U}'$ form a basis of $X'\times Y'$, there are such $U',V'$ with
$$
(x,y)\in U'\times V'\subset U\times Y .
$$
Then $x\in U'$ and $U'\subset U$, so $U$ is a neighbourhood of each of its points in the topology $\mathcal{T}'$, that is, $U\in\mathcal{T}'$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-factors}
$\mathcal{T}'\supset\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{U}'\supset\mathcal{U}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#b-neigh){.pf-ref} gives $\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}'$, since $\varnothing\in\mathcal{T}'$ as well. The same argument with the two factors exchanged, which uses $X\ne\varnothing$ in place of $Y\ne\varnothing$, gives $\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}'$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-essential}
The hypothesis that both sets are nonempty is essential to the converse.

::: pf-proof
Take $Y=\varnothing$ and let $X$ carry two topologies with $\mathcal{T}\not\subset\mathcal{T}'$. Then $X\times Y=\varnothing$, whose only topology is $\{\varnothing\}$; so the product topologies on $X'\times Y'$ and on $X\times Y$ are both $\{\varnothing\}$, and the first is finer than the second, while $\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}'$ fails.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
By steps [](#b-factors){.pf-ref} and [](#b-essential){.pf-ref}, the converse of (a) holds when both sets are nonempty and can fail when a factor is empty. So the answer to (b) is yes, under the hypothesis of the exercise.
:::
