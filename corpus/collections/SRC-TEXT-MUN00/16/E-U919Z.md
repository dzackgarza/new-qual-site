::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be an ordered set.
If $Y$ is a proper subset of $X$ that is convex in $X$, does it follow that $Y$ is an interval or a ray in $X$?
:::

::: {.solution}
No. Take $X=\mathbb Q$ with its usual order and
\[
Y=\{q\in\mathbb Q:-\sqrt2<q<\sqrt2\}.
\]
This is convex in $\mathbb Q$: if $p<q<r$ with $p,r\in Y$, then necessarily $q\in Y$. It is a proper subset of $\mathbb Q$.

However, $Y$ is not an interval with endpoints in $\mathbb Q$, since its two cuts are at the irrational numbers $\pm\sqrt2$, and it is not a ray since it is bounded on both sides in $\mathbb Q$. Thus a proper convex subset of an ordered set need not be one of the intervals or rays whose endpoints belong to the ambient ordered set.
:::
