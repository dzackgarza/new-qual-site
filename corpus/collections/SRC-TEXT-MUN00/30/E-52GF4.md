::: {.exercise}

Let $A$ be a closed subspace of $X$.
Show that if $X$ is Lindelöf, then $A$ is Lindelöf.
Show by example that if $X$ has a countable dense subset, $A$ need not have a countable dense subset.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that the Lindelöf property is inherited by closed subspaces, and provide a standard counterexample showing that separability (having a countable dense subset) is not hereditary to closed subspaces.

<1>1. Closed subspaces of Lindelöf spaces are Lindelöf:
    Let $X$ be Lindelöf and $A \subseteq X$ be a closed subspace. Then $A$ is Lindelöf.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\mathcal{U} = \{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an open cover of the subspace $A$.
    <2>2. By definition of the subspace topology, for each $\alpha \in J$ there exists an open set $V_\alpha \subseteq X$ such that $U_\alpha = V_\alpha \cap A$.
    <2>3. Since $A \subseteq \bigcup_{\alpha \in J} V_\alpha$ and $A$ is closed in $X$, the complement $X \setminus A$ is open in $X$.
    <2>4. The family $\mathcal{V} = \{V_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in J} \cup \{X \setminus A\}$ is an open cover of the entire space $X$.
    <2>5. Because $X$ is Lindelöf, there exists a countable subcover of $X$:
        $$\mathcal{V}' = \{V_{\alpha_n}\}_{n=1}^\infty \cup \{X \setminus A\}.$$
    <2>6. Intersecting with $A$, since $(X \setminus A) \cap A = \varnothing$:
        $$A = A \cap \left[ (X \setminus A) \cup \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty V_{\alpha_n} \right] = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty (V_{\alpha_n} \cap A) = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty U_{\alpha_n}.$$
    <2>7. Thus $\{U_{\alpha_n}\}_{n=1}^\infty$ is a countable subcover of $A$, proving $A$ is Lindelöf.

<1>2. Counterexample: Separability is not hereditary to closed subspaces.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Consider the Sorgenfrey plane $X = \mathbb{R}_l \times \mathbb{R}_l$, where $\mathbb{R}_l$ is the lower limit topology (Sorgenfrey line).
    <2>2. The countable subset $D = \mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $X$ because every basic open rectangle $[a, b) \times [c, d)$ contains points of $\mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Q}$. Thus $X$ is separable.
    <2>3. Consider the "anti-diagonal" line $L = \{(x, -x) \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\} \subset X$.
    <2>4. The subspace $L$ is closed in $X$: for any $(x, y) \notin L$, $x + y \neq 0$, and one can easily find a basic rectangle around $(x, y)$ disjoint from $L$.
    <2>5. The subspace topology induced on $L$ is discrete: for any $p = (x, -x) \in L$, the basic open set $U = [x, x+1) \times [-x, -x+1)$ in $X$ satisfies $U \cap L = \{(x, -x)\} = \{p\}$.
    <2>6. In an uncountable discrete space, the only dense subset is the entire space itself, which is uncountable.
    <2>7. Therefore, the closed subspace $L$ is not separable. Q.E.D.
:::
