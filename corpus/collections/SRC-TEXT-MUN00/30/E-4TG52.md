::: {.exercise}

Show that every compact metrizable space $X$ has a countable basis.
[Hint: Let $\mathcal{A}_n$ be a finite covering of $X$ by $1/n$-balls.]
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that every compact metrizable space $X$ is second-countable (admits a countable topological basis).

<1>1. Construction of candidate basis $\mathcal{B}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $d$ be a compatible metric on $X$.
    <2>2. For each positive integer $n \in \mathbb{Z}_+$, the family of open balls $\{B_d(x, 1/n)\}_{x \in X}$ is an open cover of $X$.
    <2>3. Because $X$ is compact, there exists a finite subcover:
        $$\mathcal{A}_n = \left\{ B_d\left(x_{n, 1}, \frac{1}{n}\right), \dots, B_d\left(x_{n, k_n}, \frac{1}{n}\right) \right\}.$$
    <2>4. Define $\mathcal{B} = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \mathcal{A}_n$.
    <2>5. As a countable union of finite sets, $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable collection of open subsets of $X$.

<1>2. Verification that $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis for the topology of $X$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $U \subseteq X$ be an open set and let $x \in U$.
    <2>2. Since $U$ is metric-open, there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ such that the metric ball $B_d(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$.
    <2>3. Choose an integer $n \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ sufficiently large such that $\frac{1}{n} < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
    <2>4. Because $\mathcal{A}_n$ covers $X$, there exists an index $i \in \{1, \dots, k_n\}$ such that $x \in B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n})$.
    <2>5. We claim that $B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n}) \subseteq B_d(x, \varepsilon)$:
        - For any $y \in B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n})$, the triangle inequality gives:
          $$d(y, x) \le d(y, x_{n, i}) + d(x_{n, i}, x) < \frac{1}{n} + \frac{1}{n} = \frac{2}{n} < \varepsilon.$$
        - Thus $y \in B_d(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$.
    <2>6. Hence $x \in B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n}) \subseteq U$, with $B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n}) \in \mathcal{B}$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable basis for $X$, so $X$ is second-countable. Q.E.D.
:::
