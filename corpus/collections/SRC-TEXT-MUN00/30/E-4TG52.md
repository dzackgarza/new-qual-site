::: {.exercise}

Show that every compact metrizable space $X$ has a countable basis.
[Hint: Let $\mathcal{A}_n$ be a finite covering of $X$ by $1/n$-balls.]
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Prove that every compact metrizable space $X$ is second-countable (admits a countable topological basis).

::: pf

::: pf-step
Construction of candidate basis $\mathcal{B}$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Let $d$ be a compatible metric on $X$.
:::

::: pf-step
For each positive integer $n \in \mathbb{Z}_+$, the family of open balls $\{B_d(x, 1/n)\}_{x \in X}$ is an open cover of $X$.
:::

::: pf-step
Because $X$ is compact, there exists a finite subcover:
$$\mathcal{A}_n = \left\{ B_d\left(x_{n, 1}, \frac{1}{n}\right), \dots, B_d\left(x_{n, k_n}, \frac{1}{n}\right) \right\}.$$
:::

::: pf-step
Define $\mathcal{B} = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \mathcal{A}_n$.
:::

::: pf-step
As a countable union of finite sets, $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable collection of open subsets of $X$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Verification that $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis for the topology of $X$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Let $U \subseteq X$ be an open set and let $x \in U$.
:::

::: pf-step
Since $U$ is metric-open, there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ such that the metric ball $B_d(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$.
:::

::: pf-step
Choose an integer $n \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ sufficiently large such that $\frac{1}{n} < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
:::

::: pf-step
Because $\mathcal{A}_n$ covers $X$, there exists an index $i \in \{1, \dots, k_n\}$ such that $x \in B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n})$.
:::

::: pf-step
We claim that $B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n}) \subseteq B_d(x, \varepsilon)$:
- For any $y \in B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n})$, the triangle inequality gives:
  $$d(y, x) \le d(y, x_{n, i}) + d(x_{n, i}, x) < \frac{1}{n} + \frac{1}{n} = \frac{2}{n} < \varepsilon.$$
- Thus $y \in B_d(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$.
:::

::: pf-step
Hence $x \in B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n}) \subseteq U$, with $B_d(x_{n, i}, \frac{1}{n}) \in \mathcal{B}$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Conclusion:
$\mathcal{B}$ is a countable basis for $X$, so $X$ is second-countable. Q.E.D.
:::
