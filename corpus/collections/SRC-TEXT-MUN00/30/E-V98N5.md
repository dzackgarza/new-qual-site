::: {.exercise}

Let $G$ be a first-countable topological group.
Show that if $G$ has a countable dense subset, or is Lindelöf, then $G$ has a countable basis.
[Hint: Let $\theset{B_n}$ be a countable basis at $e$. If $D$ is a countable dense subset of $G$, show the sets $dB_n$, for $d \in D$, form a basis for $G$. If $G$ is Lindelöf, choose for each $n$ a countable set $C_n$ such that the sets $cB_n$, for $c \in C_n$, cover $G$. Show that as $n$ ranges over $\ZZ_+$, these sets form a basis for $G$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $B_1,B_2,\dots$ be a countable local basis at the identity $e$ consisting of neighbourhoods of $e$. Replacing $B_n$ by $B_1\cap\dots\cap B_n$ if necessary we may take the basis decreasing, and replacing each $B_n$ by $B_n\cap B_n^{-1}$ we may take each of them symmetric, since inversion is a homeomorphism and the intersection of two neighbourhoods of $e$ is a neighbourhood of $e$.

Assume first that $G$ has a countable dense subset $D$. We claim the countable family
$$
\{dB_n:d\in D,\ n\in\ZZ_+\}
$$
is a basis. Let $x\in U$ with $U$ open. The map $(a,b)\mapsto ab^{-1}$ of $G\times G$ to $G$ is continuous and sends $(e,e)$ to $x$, so there is a neighbourhood $W$ of $e$ with
$$
xWW^{-1}\subset U.
$$
Choose $n$ with $B_n\subset W$. Since $D$ is dense, the open set $xB_n^{-1}$ meets $D$; choose $d=xb^{-1}$ with $b\in B_n$. Then
$$
x=db\in dB_n,
$$
and
$$
dB_n=xb^{-1}B_n\subset xB_n^{-1}B_n\subset xW^{-1}W\subset U,
$$
using $b\in B_n$ and $B_n\subset W$. Thus the displayed family is a countable basis.

Now assume $G$ is Lindelöf, with the decreasing symmetric local basis fixed above. For each $n$, the translates $\{gB_n:g\in G\}$ cover $G$. Lindelöfness gives a countable set $C_n\subset G$ such that
$$
G=\bigcup_{c\in C_n}cB_n.
$$
The union over $n$ of all $cB_n$ is countable. To see it is a basis, let $x\in U$ open. The multiplication map $G\times G\to G$ sends $(e,e)$ to $x$, so there is a neighbourhood $N$ of $e$ with $xN\subset U$; because the local basis is decreasing, $B_nB_n\subset N$ for $n$ large enough, so that
$$
xB_nB_n\subset U.
$$
Pick $c\in C_n$ with $x\in cB_n$. Then $c=xb^{-1}$ for some $b\in B_n$, and therefore
$$
cB_n\subset xB_nB_n\subset U.
$$
Hence $G$ is second countable.
:::
