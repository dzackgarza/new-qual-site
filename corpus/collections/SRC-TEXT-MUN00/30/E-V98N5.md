::: {.exercise}

Let $G$ be a first-countable topological group.
Show that if $G$ has a countable dense subset, or is Lindelöf, then $G$ has a countable basis.
[Hint: Let $\theset{B_n}$ be a countable basis at $e$. If $D$ is a countable dense subset of $G$, show the sets $dB_n$, for $d \in D$, form a basis for $G$. If $G$ is Lindelöf, choose for each $n$ a countable set $C_n$ such that the sets $cB_n$, for $c \in C_n$, cover $G$. Show that as $n$ ranges over $\ZZ_+$, these sets form a basis for $G$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The retained published solution is dbFin, *Section 30: Problem 18 Solution* (`https://dbfin.com/topology/munkres/chapter-4/section-30-the-countability-axioms/problem-18-solution/`, read 2026-11-10, kept at `tmp/sources/dbfin/section-30-the-countability-axioms/problem-18-solution.txt`): its two halves take a symmetric neighbourhood $V$ of $e$ with $V\cdot V\subseteq x^{-1}U$, choose $B_n\subseteq V$ and $d\in x\cdot B_n^{-1}$, and in the Lindelöf case subdivide the cover $\{xB_n:x\in G\}$; the card states the same argument with the continuity steps made explicit. Let $B_1,B_2,\ldots$ be a countable local base at the identity $e$ consisting of neighbourhoods of $e$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #normalise}
There is a countable local base $B_1\supseteq B_2\supseteq\cdots$ at $e$ with each $B_n$ symmetric, that is $B_n^{-1}=B_n$.

::: pf-proof
Replace $B_n$ by $B_1\cap\cdots\cap B_n$, a neighbourhood of $e$ contained in $B_n$; the result is a decreasing local base. Then replace each of these by $B_n\cap B_n^{-1}$, which contains $e$, is contained in $B_n$, and is symmetric because inversion is a homeomorphism of $G$ with itself and $(B_n^{-1})^{-1}=B_n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #separable-basis}
If $G$ has a countable dense subset $D$, then $\{dB_n:d\in D,\ n\in\ZZ_+\}$ is a countable basis for the topology.

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #three-fold}
For $x\in G$ and an open $U$ with $x\in U$ there is a neighbourhood $W$ of $e$ with $xW^{-1}W\subseteq U$.

::: pf-proof
The map $\varphi\colon G\times G\times G\to G$, $\varphi(g,h,k)=gh^{-1}k$, is continuous, being built from the continuous multiplication and inversion; and $\varphi(x,e,e)=x\in U$. Hence there are neighbourhoods $V$ of $x$ and $W$ of $e$ with $\varphi(V\times W\times W)\subseteq U$, and $x\in V$ gives $xW^{-1}W\subseteq U$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #one-basic-set}
For $x\in U$ open there are $d\in D$ and $n$ with $x\in dB_n\subseteq U$.

::: pf-proof
Take $W$ from step [](#three-fold){.pf-ref} and then $n$ with $B_n\subseteq W$, which exists because the $B_n$ form a local base at $e$. The set $xB_n^{-1}$ is a neighbourhood of $x$, since $x=xe\in xB_n^{-1}$, so by density it meets $D$; choose $d=xb^{-1}$ with $b\in B_n$. Then $b^{-1}\in B_n$ by symmetry, so $x=db^{-1}\in dB_n$. Moreover $dB_n=xb^{-1}B_n\subseteq xB_n^{-1}B_n\subseteq xW^{-1}W\subseteq U$, using $b^{-1}\in B_n$ and $B_n\subseteq W$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
With $D$ countable dense, $G$ is second countable.

::: pf-proof
The family $\{dB_n\}$ is countable, being indexed by $D\times\ZZ_+$, and step [](#one-basic-set){.pf-ref} shows that each point of each open set lies in a member of the family contained in that set; so the family is a countable basis.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lindelof-basis}
If $G$ is Lindelöf, then $G$ is second countable.

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #countable-covers}
For each $n$ there is a countable $C_n\subseteq G$ with $G=\bigcup_{c\in C_n}cB_n$.

::: pf-proof
The translates $\{gB_n:g\in G\}$ cover $G$, because $g\in gB_n$ as $e\in B_n$, and each is open. Lindelöfness extracts a countable subcover, whose index set is the required $C_n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #two-neighbourhoods}
For $x\in U$ open there are a neighbourhood $N$ of $e$ with $xN\subseteq U$ and a neighbourhood $M$ of $e$ with $MM\subseteq N$.

::: pf-proof
Multiplication $G\times G\to G$ is continuous at $(x,e)$, where it takes the value $x\in U$, so there are neighbourhoods $V$ of $x$ and $N$ of $e$ with $VN\subseteq U$; then $xN\subseteq U$. Continuity at $(e,e)$, where multiplication takes the value $e\in N$, gives neighbourhoods $M_1,M_2$ of $e$ with $M_1M_2\subseteq N$, and $M=M_1\cap M_2$ satisfies $MM\subseteq N$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
With $G$ Lindelöf, the countable family $\{cB_n:c\in C_n,\ n\in\ZZ_+\}$ is a basis.

::: pf-proof
Let $x\in U$ open. Take $N$ and $M$ from step [](#two-neighbourhoods){.pf-ref}; since the $B_n$ are a decreasing local base at $e$, choose $n$ with $B_n\subseteq M$. Then $B_nB_n\subseteq MM\subseteq N$ and therefore $xB_nB_n\subseteq xN\subseteq U$. By step [](#countable-covers){.pf-ref} choose $c\in C_n$ with $x\in cB_n$; then $c=xb^{-1}$ for some $b\in B_n$, and $cB_n=xb^{-1}B_n\subseteq xB_nB_n\subseteq U$ by symmetry of $B_n$. So $x\in cB_n\subseteq U$ with $cB_n$ in the family, which is therefore a countable basis and $G$ is second countable.
:::
