::: {.exercise}

Let $G$ be a first-countable topological group.
Show that if $G$ has a countable dense subset, or is Lindelöf, then $G$ has a countable basis.
[Hint: Let $\ts{B_n}$ be a countable basis at $e$. If $D$ is a countable dense subset of $G$, show the sets $dB_n$, for $d \in D$, form a basis for $G$. If $G$ is Lindelöf, choose for each $n$ a countable set $C_n$ such that the sets $cB_n$, for $c \in C_n$, cover $G$. Show that as $n$ ranges over $\mathbb{Z}_+$, these sets form a basis for $G$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let \(B_1,B_2,\dots\) be a countable local basis at the identity \(e\).

Assume first that \(G\) has a countable dense subset \(D\). We claim the countable family
\[
\{dB_n:d\in D,\ n\in\mathbb Z_+\}
\]
is a basis. Let \(x\in U\) with \(U\) open. Choose a neighborhood \(W\) of \(e\) such that
\[
xWW^{-1}\subset U.
\]
Choose \(n\) with \(B_n\subset W\). Since \(D\) is dense, the open set \(xB_n^{-1}\) meets \(D\); choose \(d=xb^{-1}\) with \(b\in B_n\). Then
\[
x=db\in dB_n,
\]
and
\[
dB_n=xb^{-1}B_n\subset xB_n^{-1}B_n\subset xW^{-1}W\subset U
\]
after choosing \(W\) symmetric, or equivalently replacing it by a smaller symmetric neighborhood. Thus the displayed family is a countable basis.

Now assume \(G\) is Lindelöf. Shrink the local basis if necessary so each \(B_n\) is symmetric. For each \(n\), the translates \(\{gB_n:g\in G\}\) cover \(G\). Lindelöfness gives a countable set \(C_n\subset G\) such that
\[
G=\bigcup_{c\in C_n}cB_n.
\]
The union over \(n\) of all \(cB_n\) is countable. To see it is a basis, let \(x\in U\) open. Choose \(n\) so small that
\[
xB_nB_n\subset U.
\]
Pick \(c\in C_n\) with \(x\in cB_n\). Then \(c=xb^{-1}\) for some \(b\in B_n\), and therefore
\[
cB_n\subset xB_nB_n\subset U.
\]
Hence \(G\) is second countable.
:::
