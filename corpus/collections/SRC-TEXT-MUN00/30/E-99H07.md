::: {.exercise}

(a) Show that every metrizable space with a countable dense subset has a countable basis.

(b) Show that every metrizable Lindelöf space has a countable basis.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that for any metrizable topological space $(X, d)$, separability implies second-countability, and the Lindelöf property implies second-countability.

<1>1. Part (a): Separability implies second-countability for metrizable spaces.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $D = \{x_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a countable dense subset of $(X, d)$.
    <2>2. Define the collection of open metric balls:
        $$\mathcal{B} = \{B(x_n, r) \mid x_n \in D, \, r \in \mathbb{Q}_+\}.$$
    <2>3. Since $D$ and $\mathbb{Q}_+$ are countable, $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable collection of open sets in $X$.
    <2>4. Let $U \subseteq X$ be an arbitrary open set, and let $x \in U$.
    <2>5. Because $U$ is open, there is $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $B(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$.
    <2>6. Choose a rational radius $r \in \mathbb{Q}_+$ such that $r < \varepsilon / 3$.
    <2>7. Because $D$ is dense in $X$, there exists $x_n \in D$ such that $d(x, x_n) < r$.
    <2>8. The ball $B(x_n, r)$ contains $x$ because $d(x, x_n) < r$.
    <2>9. For any $y \in B(x_n, r)$, by the triangle inequality:
        $$d(y, x) \le d(y, x_n) + d(x_n, x) < r + r = 2r < \frac{2\varepsilon}{3} < \varepsilon.$$
    <2>10. Thus $x \in B(x_n, r) \subseteq B(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$, proving $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable basis for $X$.

<1>2. Part (b): Lindelöf property implies second-countability for metrizable spaces.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For each integer $n \ge 1$, the collection of open balls $\mathcal{U}_n = \{B(x, 1/n) \mid x \in X\}$ covers $X$.
    <2>2. Since $X$ is Lindelöf, there exists a countable subcollection $\mathcal{C}_n \subseteq \mathcal{U}_n$ covering $X$, say:
        $$\mathcal{C}_n = \{B(x_{n, k}, 1/n) \mid k \in \mathbb{Z}_+\}.$$
    <2>3. Define $\mathcal{B} = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \mathcal{C}_n = \{B(x_{n, k}, 1/n) \mid n, k \in \mathbb{Z}_+\}$.
    <2>4. As a countable union of countable sets, $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable collection of open sets.
    <2>5. Let $U \subseteq X$ be an open set and $x \in U$. Choose $n \ge 1$ such that $B(x, 2/n) \subseteq U$.
    <2>6. Since $\mathcal{C}_n$ covers $X$, there is some $k \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ such that $x \in B(x_{n, k}, 1/n)$.
    <2>7. For any $y \in B(x_{n, k}, 1/n)$, the triangle inequality gives:
        $$d(y, x) \le d(y, x_{n, k}) + d(x_{n, k}, x) < \frac{1}{n} + \frac{1}{n} = \frac{2}{n}.$$
    <2>8. Thus $x \in B(x_{n, k}, 1/n) \subseteq B(x, 2/n) \subseteq U$, proving $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable basis for $X$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    In any metrizable space, separability, Lindelöfness, and second-countability are all equivalent. Q.E.D.
:::
