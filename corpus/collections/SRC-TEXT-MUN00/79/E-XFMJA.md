::: {.exercise}

Let $T = S^1 \times S^1$, the torus.
There is an isomorphism of $\pi_1(T, b_0 \times b_0)$ with $\ZZ \times \ZZ$ induced by projections of $T$ onto its two factors.

(a) Find a covering space of $T$ corresponding to the subgroup of $\ZZ \times \ZZ$ generated by the element $m \times 0$, where $m$ is a positive integer.

(b) Find a covering space of $T$ corresponding to the trivial subgroup of $\ZZ \times \ZZ$.

(c) Find a covering space of $T$ corresponding to the subgroup of $\ZZ \times \ZZ$ generated by $m \times 0$ and $0 \times n$, where $m$ and $n$ are positive integers.
:::

::: {.solution}

The covering of part (a), and the computation of each of the three images in $\pi_{1}(T)$, are the card's own. Paul Frost's answer 4590100, cited at the foot of this solution, states the correspondence for $e_{n}$ and $e_{\infty}$ without computing the induced maps, and gives no covering for part (a).

Write $q:\RR\to S^{1}$ for $q(t)=e^{2\pi i t}$ and $T=S^{1}\times S^{1}$, and identify $\pi_{1}(T,b_{0}\times b_{0})$ with $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ through the two projections, so that the loop $t\mapsto(q(t),b_{0})$ represents $(1,0)$ and $t\mapsto(b_{0},q(t))$ represents $(0,1)$; the exercise states this isomorphism. For a covering $p:(E,e_{0})\to(T,b_{0})$ the corresponding subgroup is $p_{*}\big(\pi_{1}(E,e_{0})\big)$ (Theorem 54.5, and §80).

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a}
(a) The covering $p_{m}(z,t)=\big(z^{m},q(t)\big)$ of $S^{1}\times\RR$ onto $T$ corresponds to $\langle(m,0)\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #a-covering}
$p_{m}:S^{1}\times\RR\to T$ is a covering map.

::: pf-proof
The map $z\mapsto z^{m}$ of $S^{1}$ is an $m$-fold covering of $S^{1}$ (Theorem 53.3 and Example 1 of §53), the map $q:\RR\to S^{1}$ is a covering (Example 1 of §53), and a product of two covering maps is a covering map (Theorem 53.3 applies to each factor separately; the sets $U\times V$ with $U$ evenly covered by the first and $V$ by the second are evenly covered by the product).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-image}
$p_{m*}\big(\pi_{1}(S^{1}\times\RR)\big)=\langle(m,0)\rangle$.

::: pf-proof
$\pi_{1}(S^{1}\times\RR)\cong\pi_{1}(S^{1})\oplus\pi_{1}(\RR)\cong\ZZ\oplus0$ (Theorem 60.1), generated by the class of the loop $t\mapsto(q(t),0)$, which is $z\mapsto z$ in the first factor and a point in the second. Its image under $p_{m}$ is the loop $t\mapsto\big(q(t)^{m},q(0)\big)=t\mapsto\big(q(mt),b_{0}\big)$, which winds $m$ times about the first factor and does not wind about the second; so its class is $(m,0)$ and the image, the infinite cyclic group generated by it, is $\langle(m,0)\rangle$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
The total space $S^{1}\times\RR$ is connected, so by the classification of coverings the covering $p_{m}$ corresponds to a subgroup of $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$, namely $p_{m*}(\pi_{1})$ (Theorem 54.5), which step [](#a-image){.pf-ref} identifies as $\langle(m,0)\rangle$. That is the required covering.
:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
(b) The universal covering $p(s,t)=\big(q(s),q(t)\big)$ of $\RR^{2}$ onto $T$ corresponds to the trivial subgroup.

::: pf-proof
$p$ is the product of two copies of the covering $\RR\to S^{1}$, hence a covering map. $\RR^{2}$ is contractible, in the sense that its identity map is nullhomotopic (§51, Exercise 3): a nullhomotopy of the identity carries every loop to a constant loop, so $\pi_{1}(\RR^{2})=0$, and $\RR^{2}$ is path connected, so it is simply connected. The implication is not Lemma 52.3, which runs the other way, from simple connectedness to path homotopy of paths with the same endpoints; it is the form Munkres uses in §70, where $\RR^{2}$ minus two points is simply connected because it is contractible. The image of the trivial group is the trivial subgroup, so $p$ corresponds to it.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c}
(c) The covering $p_{m,n}(z,w)=\big(z^{m},w^{n}\big)$ of $T$ onto $T$ corresponds to $\langle(m,0),(0,n)\rangle$.

::: pf-proof
The map $p_{m,n}$ is a covering map, being the product of the $m$-fold covering $z\mapsto z^{m}$ with the $n$-fold covering $w\mapsto w^{n}$. With $\pi_{1}(T)$ identified with $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ by the coordinate loops, the induced homomorphism is
$$
(p_{m,n})_{*}:\ZZ\oplus\ZZ\to\ZZ\oplus\ZZ,\qquad (r,s)\mapsto(mr,ns),
$$
since the loop representing $(r,s)$ is carried to one winding $mr$ times about the first factor and $ns$ times about the second. This homomorphism is injective, and its image is the set of pairs with first entry divisible by $m$ and second entry divisible by $n$, namely the subgroup generated by $(m,0)$ and $(0,n)$.
:::

The three answers are stated, for the subgroups $m\ZZ\oplus n\ZZ$ and the trivial group, by Paul Frost, answer 4590100 (https://math.stackexchange.com/a/4590100), posted 2022-12-02 to Math Stack Exchange question 4590058, "About covering spaces of the torus" (https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/4590058, asked by Lam18373 on the same day): the maps $e_{n}:S^{1}\to S^{1}$, $e_{n}(z)=z^{n}$, correspond to the subgroups $n\ZZ$, the map $e_{\infty}:\RR\to S^{1}$ corresponds to the trivial subgroup, and therefore $e_{2}\times e_{3}$ corresponds to $2\ZZ\oplus3\ZZ$ and $e_{\infty}\times e_{\infty}$ to the trivial subgroup. Each of those correspondences is asserted there and not computed, and the covering of part (a) is not given in the thread; the computations of the three images in $\pi_{1}(T)$, and the covering of part (a), are the card's.
