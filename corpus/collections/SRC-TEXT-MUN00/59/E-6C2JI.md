::: {.exercise}

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem 59.1.

(a) What can you say about the fundamental group of $X$ if $j_*$ is the trivial homomorphism?
If both $i_*$ and $j_*$ are trivial?

(b) Give an example where $i_*$ and $j_*$ are trivial but neither $U$ nor $V$ have trivial fundamental groups.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
General Seifert-van Kampen relation structure:
Theorem 59.1 itself gives only that the images of $i_*$ and $j_*$ generate $\pi_1(X,x_0)$: a loop of $X$ splits into loops lying in $U$ or in $V$, so if $j_*$ is trivial the group is generated by $i_*(\pi_1(U,x_0))$ and if both are trivial it is trivial. The sharper description used below is the Seifert-van Kampen theorem, Theorem 70.1, which the book proves in Chapter 11: under the same hypotheses the fundamental group is the amalgamated free product:
$$\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong (\pi_1(U, x_0) * \pi_1(V, x_0)) / N,$$
where $N$ is the normal closure in $\pi_1(U) * \pi_1(V)$ of the subset:
$$R = \{ i_*(g) j_*(g)^{-1} \mid g \in \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0) \}.$$
:::

::: pf-step
Analysis of Part (a):

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
**Case 1 ($j_*$ is trivial):**
- If $j_*(g) = 1$ for all $g \in \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)$, then $i_*(g) j_*(g)^{-1} = i_*(g) \in \pi_1(U, x_0)$.
- The relators lie in the factor $\pi_1(U, x_0)$. Quotient that factor by the normal closure of $i_*(\pi_1(U \cap V, x_0))$ before taking the free product.
- Factoring out $N$ affects only the first factor, yielding:
  $$\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong (\pi_1(U, x_0) / \langle\langle i_*(\pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)) \rangle\rangle) * \pi_1(V, x_0).$$
:::

::: pf-step
**Case 2 (Both $i_*$ and $j_*$ are trivial):**
- If $i_*(g) = 1$ and $j_*(g) = 1$ for all $g \in \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)$, then $i_*(g) j_*(g)^{-1} = 1$.
- Thus the set of relations is $R = \{1\}$, so the normal subgroup $N$ is trivial ($N = \{1\}$).
- Therefore, the amalgamated product reduces directly to the free product:
  $$\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \pi_1(U, x_0) * \pi_1(V, x_0).$$
:::

::: pf-step
Construction for Part (b):

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Let $X = S^1 \vee S^1 = A \vee B$ be the wedge sum of two circles with common basepoint $x_0$.
:::

::: pf-step
Choose points $p \in A \setminus \{x_0\}$ and $q \in B \setminus \{x_0\}$.
:::

::: pf-step
Define open subsets:
$$U = X \setminus \{q\} = A \vee (B \setminus \{q\}) \quad \text{and} \quad V = X \setminus \{p\} = (A \setminus \{p\}) \vee B.$$
:::

::: pf-step
The open arc $B \setminus \{q\}$ deformation retracts to $x_0$, so $U \simeq A \cong S^1$ and $\pi_1(U, x_0) \cong \ZZ \neq \{1\}$.
:::

::: pf-step
Symmetrically, $V \simeq B \cong S^1$ and $\pi_1(V, x_0) \cong \ZZ \neq \{1\}$.
:::

::: pf-step
The intersection $U \cap V = (A \setminus \{p\}) \vee (B \setminus \{q\})$ is the wedge of two contractible open arcs, hence contractible:
$$\pi_1(U \cap V, x_0) = \{1\}.$$
:::

::: pf-step
Since $\pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)$ is the trivial group, both homomorphisms $i_*$ and $j_*$ are trivial, while $\pi_1(U, x_0) \cong \ZZ$ and $\pi_1(V, x_0) \cong \ZZ$ are non-trivial.
:::
