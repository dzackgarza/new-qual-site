::: {.exercise}

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem 59.1.

(a) What can you say about the fundamental group of $X$ if $j_*$ is the trivial homomorphism?
If both $i_*$ and $j_*$ are trivial?

(b) Give an example where $i_*$ and $j_*$ are trivial but neither $U$ nor $V$ have trivial fundamental groups.
:::

::: {.solution}
The exercise is §59 Exercise 4, printed p. 370, under the hypotheses of Theorem
59.1 (printed p. 368): $X=U\cup V$ with $U$ and $V$ open in $X$, $U\cap V$ path
connected and containing $x_0$, and $i:U\to X$, $j:V\to X$ the inclusions.

Part (b) is transcribed from Pearse, *Topology (2nd ed.) — James R. Munkres*,
solutions, Cornell 2005, §59 Exercise 4, his p. 25, read in
`tmp/sources/cornell-erin-munkres.md`, and so is the first half of (a), that both
homomorphisms trivial forces $\pi_1(X)$ trivial. The source's second half of (a)
is defective, and the card does not follow it there: for the case that only $j_*$
is trivial, the source answers that "there's not much you can say" and offers
$U=\theset{x\in S^2:-1<x<0}$ and $V$ equal to $S^2$ punctured at two points of
positive $x$-coordinate. There $U\subseteq V$, so $X=U\cup V=V$ and $j$ is an
identity map, whose induced homomorphism is an isomorphism and not the trivial
homomorphism; the example does not exhibit the case it is offered for. That case
does have a consequence, which Theorem 59.1 supplies: a trivial $j_*$ makes $i_*$
surjective, so $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is a quotient of $\pi_1(U,x_0)$, as step
[](#surjective){.pf-ref} shows. What the hypothesis does not give is triviality of
$\pi_1(X,x_0)$, as step [](#one-trivial){.pf-ref} shows.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #surjective}
(a) If $j_*$ is the trivial homomorphism, then $i_*$ is surjective and
$\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong\pi_1(U,x_0)/\ker i_*$; if $i_*$ and $j_*$ are both trivial then
$\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is trivial.

::: pf-proof
By Theorem 59.1 the images of $i_*$ and $j_*$ generate $\pi_1(X,x_0)$. If $j_*$ is
trivial its image is the one-element subgroup, so the subgroup generated by the
two images is the image of $i_*$; that subgroup is the whole group, so $i_*$ is
surjective and $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is isomorphic to $\pi_1(U,x_0)/\ker i_*$. If $i_*$
is trivial as well, its image is the one-element subgroup and
$\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is the one-element subgroup.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #one-trivial}
An example with $j_*$ trivial and $\pi_1(U)\cong\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ$:
$X=U=S^1$ and $V=S^1-\theset p$ for a point $p\ne x_0$.

::: pf-proof
Take $X=S^1$, $x_0=(1,0)$, $p=(-1,0)$, $U=X$ and $V=X-\theset p$. Then $U$ and
$V$ are open in $X$ and $U\cup V=X$, while $U\cap V=V$ is an open arc containing
$x_0$, hence path connected; so the hypotheses of Theorem 59.1 hold.

The space $V$ is homeomorphic to an open interval, hence contractible, so
$\pi_1(V,x_0)$ is trivial and $j_*:\pi_1(V,x_0)\to\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is the trivial
homomorphism. Since $U=X$, the homomorphism $i_*$ is induced by the identity map
of $S^1$ and is an isomorphism, and $\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong\ZZ$ by Theorem 54.5
(printed p. 345). So a trivial $j_*$ does not force $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ to be trivial:
what Theorem 59.1 gives here is the surjectivity of $i_*$, and $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ can
be all of $\pi_1(U,x_0)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(b) An example in which $i_*$ and $j_*$ are trivial but neither $U$ nor $V$ has
trivial fundamental group: $U$ and $V$ the complements in $S^2$ of two points
with negative, respectively positive, $x$-coordinate.

::: pf-proof
Let $a_1,a_2$ be distinct points of $S^2$ with negative $x$-coordinate and
$b_1,b_2$ distinct points with positive $x$-coordinate, and put
$U=S^2-\theset{a_1,a_2}$, $V=S^2-\theset{b_1,b_2}$, $X=S^2$. Then $U$ and $V$
are open in $X$ and $U\cup V=X$. Also $U\cap V=S^2-\theset{a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2}$ is
path connected: it is $S^2$ minus four points, and any two of its points can be
joined by a polygonal path avoiding four points, four points not separating the
sphere. So the hypotheses of Theorem 59.1 hold.

Each of $U$ and $V$ is homeomorphic to $\RR^2$ minus a point: stereographic
projection from one of its two deleted points is a homeomorphism of the punctured
sphere onto $\RR^2$ carrying the other deleted point to a point $q$, and
$\RR^2-\theset q$ has the circle of radius $1$ about $q$ as a deformation retract
by $x\mapsto q+(x-q)/\norm{x-q}$, so
$\pi_1(U)\cong\pi_1(V)\cong\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ$ by Theorem 58.3 (printed p. 361),
Theorem 58.7 (printed p. 364) and Theorem 54.5. Neither is trivial.

Finally $X=S^2$ is simply connected by Theorem 59.3 (printed p. 369), so
$\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is trivial; a homomorphism into a one-element group is trivial, so
$i_*$ and $j_*$ are both trivial. That is the example required.
:::
