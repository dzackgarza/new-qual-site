::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be a space that is written as the union of finitely many arcs $A_1, \ldots, A_n$, each pair of which intersect in at most a common end point.

(a) Show that $X$ is Hausdorff if and only if each arc $A_i$ is closed in $X$.

(b) Give an example to show that $X$ need not be Hausdorff.
[Hint: See [[E-29WP0]].]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
Part (a): $X$ is Hausdorff $\iff$ each $A_i$ is closed in $X$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
**Forward direction ($\implies$):**
- Suppose $X$ is Hausdorff.
- Each arc $A_i$ is homeomorphic to the unit interval $[0, 1]$, which is compact.
- Since compact subsets of a Hausdorff space are closed, each $A_i$ is closed in $X$.
:::

::: pf-step
**Reverse direction ($\impliedby$):**
- Suppose each $A_i$ is closed in $X$.
- Since $X = \bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i$ is a finite union of closed sets, the topology of $X$ is coherent with $\{A_1, \dots, A_n\}$: a set $C \subseteq X$ is closed if and only if $C \cap A_i$ is closed in $A_i$ for all $i=1,\dots,n$.
- For each point $x \in X$, $\{x\} \cap A_i$ is either empty or a singleton, hence closed in $A_i$, so $\{x\}$ is closed in $X$ ($X$ is $T_1$).
- Let $x, y \in X$ be distinct points.
- For each $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, since $A_i \cong [0, 1]$ is a compact metric space, it is normal ($T_4$).
- We can construct a continuous function $f_i: A_i \to [0, 1]$ such that $f_i(x) = 0$ (if $x \in A_i$) and $f_i(y) = 1$ (if $y \in A_i$), with matching values at the finite set of intersection endpoints $\bigcup_{j \neq k} (A_j \cap A_k)$.
- By the Pasting Lemma for finite closed covers, the piecewise defined map $f: X \to [0, 1]$ is continuous on $X$, with $f(x) = 0$ and $f(y) = 1$.
- The preimages $U = f^{-1}([0, 1/3))$ and $V = f^{-1}((2/3, 1])$ are disjoint open neighborhoods of $x$ and $y$ in $X$.
- Thus $X$ is Hausdorff.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Part (b): Counterexample where $X$ is not Hausdorff.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Take the line with two origins: its points are the nonzero real numbers and two distinct origins $p,q$. A neighborhood of either origin contains both sufficiently small positive and sufficiently small negative nonzero numbers. Give
$$
X=[-1,0)\cup\{p,q\}\cup(0,1]
$$
the subspace topology, and set
$$
A_1=[-1,0)\cup\{p\},\qquad A_2=\{q\}\cup(0,1].
$$
Each $A_i$ has the usual interval topology, so each is an arc.
:::

::: pf-step
The space $X = A_1 \cup A_2$ has $A_1 \cap A_2 = \emptyset$, so the intersection condition is vacuously satisfied.
:::

::: pf-step
However, every neighborhood of $p$ and every neighborhood of $q$ contain a common punctured interval around $0$. Thus $p$ and $q$ cannot be separated by disjoint open sets.
:::

::: pf-step
Also $q \in \overline{A_1} \setminus A_1$, as part (a) predicts.
:::
