::: {.exercise}

Prove the following.

Theorem.
If $A$ is a free abelian group of rank $n$, then any subgroup $B$ of $A$ is a free abelian group of rank at most $n$.

Proof.
We can assume $A = \mathbb{Z}^n$, the $n$-fold cartesian product of $\mathbb{Z}$ with itself.
Let $\pi_i: \mathbb{Z}^n \to \mathbb{Z}$ be projection on the $i$th coordinate.
Given $m \leq n$, let $B_m$ consist of all elements $\mathbf{x}$ of $B$ such that $\pi_i(\mathbf{x}) = 0$ for $i > m$.
Then $B_m$ is a subgroup of $B$.

Consider the subgroup $\pi_m(B_m)$ of $\mathbb{Z}$.
If this subgroup is nontrivial, choose $\mathbf{x}_m \in B_m$ so that $\pi_m(\mathbf{x}_m)$ is a generator of this subgroup.
Otherwise, set $\mathbf{x}_m = \mathbf{0}$.

(a) Show $\ts{\mathbf{x}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_m}$ generates $B_m$, for each $m$.

(b) Show the nonzero elements of $\ts{\mathbf{x}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_m}$ form a basis for $B_m$, for each $m$.

(c) Show that $B_n = B$ is free abelian with rank at most $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove (a) and (b) simultaneously by induction on \(m\).

For \(m=0\), \(B_0=0\), so there is nothing to prove. Assume the statements hold for \(m-1\), and take \(b\in B_m\). By construction, \(\pi_m(B_m)\) is either zero or the cyclic subgroup generated by \(\pi_m(x_m)\).

If \(\pi_m(B_m)=0\), then \(B_m=B_{m-1}\) and \(x_m=0\), so the induction hypothesis gives both assertions.

Otherwise there is an integer \(q\) such that
\[
\pi_m(b)=q\pi_m(x_m).
\]
Then
\[
b-qx_m\in B_m
\]
has zero \(m\)-th coordinate, hence belongs to \(B_{m-1}\). By induction it is generated by \(x_1,\dots,x_{m-1}\). Therefore \(b\) is generated by \(x_1,\dots,x_m\). This proves (a).

For linear independence, suppose
\[
a_1x_1+\cdots+a_mx_m=0.
\]
If \(x_m\ne0\), then \(\pi_m(x_m)\ne0\), while \(\pi_m(x_i)=0\) for \(i<m\). Applying \(\pi_m\) gives
\[
a_m\pi_m(x_m)=0,
\]
so \(a_m=0\). The induction hypothesis then gives \(a_i=0\) for every nonzero \(x_i\) with \(i<m\). If \(x_m=0\), the induction hypothesis applies immediately. Hence the nonzero members of \(\{x_1,\dots,x_m\}\) are linearly independent and, by (a), generate \(B_m\). They form a basis, proving (b).

Taking \(m=n\), we have \(B_n=B\). Its basis is a subset of the \(n\) elements \(x_1,\dots,x_n\), so \(B\) is free abelian of rank at most \(n\). This proves (c).
:::
