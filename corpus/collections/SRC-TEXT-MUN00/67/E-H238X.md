::: {.exercise}

Prove the following.

Theorem.
If $A$ is a free abelian group of rank $n$, then any subgroup $B$ of $A$ is a free abelian group of rank at most $n$.

Proof.
We can assume $A = \ZZ^n$, the $n$-fold cartesian product of $\ZZ$ with itself.
Let $\pi_i: \ZZ^n \to \ZZ$ be projection on the $i$th coordinate.
Given $m \leq n$, let $B_m$ consist of all elements $\mathbf{x}$ of $B$ such that $\pi_i(\mathbf{x}) = 0$ for $i > m$.
Then $B_m$ is a subgroup of $B$.

Consider the subgroup $\pi_m(B_m)$ of $\ZZ$.
If this subgroup is nontrivial, choose $\mathbf{x}_m \in B_m$ so that $\pi_m(\mathbf{x}_m)$ is a generator of this subgroup.
Otherwise, set $\mathbf{x}_m = \mathbf{0}$.

(a) Show $\theset{\mathbf{x}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_m}$ generates $B_m$, for each $m$.

(b) Show the nonzero elements of $\theset{\mathbf{x}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_m}$ form a basis for $B_m$, for each $m$.

(c) Show that $B_n = B$ is free abelian with rank at most $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

A free abelian group of rank $n$ is isomorphic to $\ZZ^n$ by Theorem 67.8 (printed p. 411), and a subgroup isomorphism carries subgroups to subgroups, so it suffices to treat $A=\ZZ^n$. Only the abstract statement is at issue, so write $B_0=\theset{\mathbf{0}}$ as well; then $B_0\subseteq B_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq B_n=B$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

A subgroup of $\ZZ$ is trivial or infinite cyclic, so $\pi_m(B_m)$ is generated by one element.

::: pf-proof

If $S\le\ZZ$ is nonzero, let $d$ be the least positive element of $S$; dividing any $s\in S$ by $d$ with remainder gives $s=qd+r$ with $0\le r<d$ and $r\in S$, so $r=0$ and $S=\ZZ d$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(a) For each $m$, $\theset{\mathbf{x}_1,\dots,\mathbf{x}_m}$ generates $B_m$.

::: pf-proof

Induction on $m$, the case $m=0$ being $B_0=\theset{\mathbf{0}}$. Let $\mathbf{b}\in B_m$. If $\pi_m(B_m)=0$ then $\mathbf{x}_m=\mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{b}\in B_{m-1}$, so the induction hypothesis applies. Otherwise $\pi_m(\mathbf{x}_m)$ generates $\pi_m(B_m)$, so $\pi_m(\mathbf{b})=q\,\pi_m(\mathbf{x}_m)$ for some $q\in\ZZ$, and

$$
\pi_m(\mathbf{b}-q\mathbf{x}_m)=0,\qquad \pi_i(\mathbf{b}-q\mathbf{x}_m)=0\ (i>m),
$$

so $\mathbf{b}-q\mathbf{x}_m\in B_{m-1}$, hence is an integer combination of $\mathbf{x}_1,\dots,\mathbf{x}_{m-1}$; adding $q\mathbf{x}_m$ gives $\mathbf{b}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) The nonzero elements of $\theset{\mathbf{x}_1,\dots,\mathbf{x}_m}$ are linearly independent over $\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

Again by induction on $m$. Suppose $\sum_{i\le m}a_i\mathbf{x}_i=\mathbf{0}$. For $i<m$ we have $\mathbf{x}_i\in B_{m-1}$, so $\pi_m(\mathbf{x}_i)=0$. Applying $\pi_m$ gives $a_m\pi_m(\mathbf{x}_m)=0$. If $\pi_m(B_m)\ne0$ then $\pi_m(\mathbf{x}_m)$ generates a nontrivial subgroup of $\ZZ$, hence $\pi_m(\mathbf{x}_m)\ne0$, and $a_m=0$; then the induction hypothesis gives $a_i=0$ for the nonzero $\mathbf{x}_i$ with $i<m$. If $\pi_m(B_m)=0$ then $\mathbf{x}_m=\mathbf{0}$ and the induction hypothesis applies directly. Together with (a), the nonzero $\mathbf{x}_i$ form a basis of $B_m$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(c) $B=B_n$ is free abelian of rank at most $n$.

::: pf-proof

By steps 2 and 3 the nonzero elements of $\theset{\mathbf{x}_1,\dots,\mathbf{x}_n}$ are a basis of $B_n$, a set of at most $n$ elements; a free abelian group with a finite basis has rank equal to its cardinality by Theorem 67.8 (printed p. 411). Hence $B$ is free abelian of rank $\le n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Parts (a), (b) and (c) are steps 2, 3 and 4.
