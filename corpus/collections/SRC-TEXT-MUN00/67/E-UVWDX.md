::: {.exercise}

If $G$ is free abelian with basis $\ts{x, y}$, show that $\ts{2x + 3y, x - y}$ is also a basis for $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
u=2x+3y,\qquad v=x-y.
\]
Relative to the basis \((x,y)\), the coordinate matrix with columns \(u,v\) is
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\3&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det M=-5.
\]
Thus, as written, \(\{2x+3y,x-y\}\) is **not** a basis of the free abelian group \(G\cong\mathbb Z^2\): a change-of-basis matrix over \(\mathbb Z\) must have determinant \(\pm1\). Indeed the subgroup generated by \(u,v\) has index \(5\) in \(G\).

For example, solving for \(x,y\) gives
\[
x=\frac{u+3v}{5},\qquad y=\frac{u-2v}{5},
\]
which need not be integral combinations of \(u,v\). Hence the exercise statement is false as written.
:::
