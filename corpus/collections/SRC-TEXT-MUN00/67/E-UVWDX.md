::: {.exercise}

If $G$ is free abelian with basis $\theset{x, y}$, show that $\theset{2x + 3y, x + 2y}$ is also a basis for $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
u=2x+3y,\qquad v=x+2y.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The elements $x$ and $y$ lie in the subgroup generated by $u$ and $v$.

::: pf-proof

Direct computation gives
$$
2u-3v=(4x+6y)-(3x+6y)=x,
\qquad
2v-u=(2x+4y)-(2x+3y)=y.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The elements $u$ and $v$ generate $G$.

::: pf-proof

The subgroup $\langle u,v\rangle$ contains $x$ and $y$ by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, and $x,y$ generate $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The elements $u$ and $v$ are linearly independent over $\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $au+bv=0$ with $a,b\in\ZZ$. Then
$$
(2a+b)x+(3a+2b)y=0,
$$
and since $\{x,y\}$ is a basis, $2a+b=0$ and $3a+2b=0$. Subtracting twice the first equation from the second gives $-a=0$, so $a=0$ and then $b=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $\{2x+3y,\,x+2y\}$ is a basis for $G$.

:::
