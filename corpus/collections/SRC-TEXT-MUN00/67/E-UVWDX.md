::: {.exercise}

If $G$ is free abelian with basis $\theset{x, y}$, show that $\theset{2x + 3y, x - y}$ is also a basis for $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

As printed, the assertion is false, and the failure is the matrix of coefficients. Put $u=2x+3y$ and $v=x-y$; a relation $au+bv=0$ with $a,b\in\ZZ$ means

$$
(2a+b)x+(3a-b)y=0,
$$

so $2a+b=0$ and $3a-b=0$, hence $5a=0$ and $a=b=0$. Thus $\{u,v\}$ is linearly independent over $\ZZ$ and is a basis of the subgroup $H=\langle u,v\rangle$; but $H\neq G$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The coordinate matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}2 & 1\\ 3 & -1\end{pmatrix}$ has $\det A=-5$.

::: pf-proof

$\det A=2\cdot(-1)-3\cdot1=-5$, computed from the coordinates of $u,v$ in the basis $x,y$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$H$ has index $5$ in $G$, so $H\neq G$.

::: pf-proof

Write $G=\ZZ x\oplus \ZZ y$ and $H=\ZZ u+\ZZ v$. Solving $x=au+bv$, $y=cu+dv$ means solving $A^{\mathsf T}\binom{a}{b}=\binom{1}{0}$ and $A^{\mathsf T}\binom{c}{d}=\binom{0}{1}$; by Cramer's rule the unique rational solutions have entries $a=1/5$, so $x\notin H$ because a solution in integers would give $5a=1$. Since $2a+b=1$ and $3a-b=0$ forces $a=1/5$, the element $x$ lies in no integer combination of $u$ and $v$. Hence $G/H$ is nontrivial, in fact cyclic of order $5$: the inclusion $\ZZ x\oplus\ZZ y\supset H$ presents $G/H$ as generated by the image of $x,y$ subject to $2\bar x+\bar y=0$, $3\bar x-\bar y=0$, and $5\bar x=0$ with $\bar x\ne0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The printed claim fails, and the two repairs the page admits are bases.

::: pf-proof

Taking $v'=x+2y$ gives the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}2 & 1\\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ of determinant $1$, and then $2u-3v'=x$ and $2v'-u=y$, so $\{u,v'\}$ spans $G$; independence is as above since $\det=1\neq0$. Taking $v''=x+y$ gives determinant $-1$ and $u-v''=y$, $3v''-2u=x$, so $\{u,v''\}$ is a basis as well. In each case the integer solutions exhibit $x$ and $y$ as combinations of the pair, which the printed pair does not admit.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

The printed pair generates the proper subgroup of index $5$; the pairs $\{2x+3y,\,x+2y\}$ and $\{2x+3y,\,x+y\}$ are bases.

:::
