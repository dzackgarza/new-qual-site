::: {.exercise}

The order of an element $a$ of an abelian group $G$ is the smallest positive integer $m$ such that $ma = 0$, if such exists; otherwise, the order of $a$ is said to be infinite.
The order of $a$ thus equals the order of the subgroup generated by $a$.

(a) Show the elements of finite order in $G$ form a subgroup of $G$, called its torsion subgroup.

(b) Show that if $G$ is free abelian, it has no elements of finite order.

(c) Show the additive group of rationals has no elements of finite order, but is not free abelian.
[Hint: If $\theset{a_\alpha}$ is a basis, express $\frac{1}{2}a_\alpha$ in terms of this basis.]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

(a) The elements of finite order form a subgroup.

::: pf-proof

Let $T=\theset{g\in G : mg=0 \text{ for some } m\ge1}$. It is nonempty, since $0\in T$. If $a,b\in T$ with $ma=0$ and $nb=0$, then

$$
mn(a+b)=n(ma)+m(nb)=0+0=0
$$

and $mn(-a)=-n(ma)=0$, so $a+b\in T$ and $-a\in T$. Hence $T\le G$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) A free abelian group has no nonzero element of finite order.

::: pf-proof

Let $\theset{a_\alpha}$ be a basis, so that by the definition on p. 410 the subgroups $\ZZ a_\alpha$ are infinite cyclic and $G=\bigoplus_\alpha \ZZ a_\alpha$. A nonzero $g\in G$ has a unique expression $g=\sum_\alpha k_\alpha a_\alpha$ with $k_\alpha\in\ZZ$, finitely many nonzero; some $k_\beta\neq0$. If $mg=0$ with $m\ge1$, then $\sum_\alpha m k_\alpha a_\alpha=0=\sum_\alpha 0\cdot a_\alpha$, and uniqueness of coordinates gives $mk_\beta=0$ in $\ZZ$, so $k_\beta=0$, a contradiction. Hence $g$ has infinite order.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(c) $\QQ$ is torsion-free but not free abelian.

::: pf-proof

If $mq=0$ in $\QQ$ with $m\ge1$, then $q=0$, so $\QQ$ has no nonzero element of finite order. Suppose $\QQ$ were free abelian with basis $\theset{a_\alpha}$, and fix a basis element $a=a_\beta$. By the hint, write $\tfrac12 a=\sum_\alpha k_\alpha a_\alpha$ with $k_\alpha\in\ZZ$; multiplying by $2$,

$$
a=\sum_\alpha 2k_\alpha a_\alpha .
$$

But also $a=1\cdot a_\beta=\sum_\alpha \delta_{\alpha\beta}a_\alpha$, so uniqueness of coordinates gives $2k_\beta=1$ in $\ZZ$, which is impossible. Hence $\QQ$ is not free abelian.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Parts (a), (b) and (c) are steps 1, 2 and 3.
