::: {.exercise}

Let $p: X \to Y$ be a closed continuous surjective map such that $p^{-1}(\theset{y})$ is compact, for each $y \in Y$.
(Such a map is called a perfect map.)
Show that if $Y$ is compact, then $X$ is compact.
[Hint: If $U$ is an open set containing $p^{-1}(\theset{y})$, there is a neighborhood $W$ of $y$ such that $p^{-1}(W)$ is contained in $U$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 26.12, PDF pp. 14--15** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); the argument follows the hint as they use it.

Let $p:X\to Y$ be a closed continuous surjection with compact fibres, and let $Y$ be compact; we show $X$ compact.

*Step 1.* If $U\supset p^{-1}(\{y\})$ is open, there is a neighbourhood $W$ of $y$ with $p^{-1}(W)\subset U$. Indeed $X-U$ is closed, so $p(X-U)$ is closed and does not contain $y$; put $W=Y-p(X-U)$, a neighbourhood of $y$. Since $X-U\subset p^{-1}(p(X-U))$, we get $p^{-1}(W)=X-p^{-1}(p(X-U))\subset U$.

*Step 2.* Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an open cover of $X$. For each $y\in Y$, the compact fibre $p^{-1}(\{y\})$ is covered by finitely many members of $\mathcal{A}$, whose union is an open set containing it; by Step 1 there is a neighbourhood $W_{y}$ of $y$ with $p^{-1}(W_{y})$ contained in that finite union. The $W_{y}$ cover the compact $Y$, so $W_{y_{1}},\dots,W_{y_{k}}$ do, and then the finitely many members of $\mathcal{A}$ used at those $k$ points cover
$$
X=p^{-1}(Y)=\bigcup_{j\le k}p^{-1}(W_{y_{j}}).
$$
So every open cover has a finite subcover and $X$ is compact.
:::
