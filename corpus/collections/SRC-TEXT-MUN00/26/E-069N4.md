::: {.exercise}

Theorem.
Let $f: X \to Y$; let $Y$ be compact Hausdorff.
Then $f$ is continuous if and only if the graph of $f$,

$$
G_f = \theset{x \times f(x) \mid x \in X},
$$

is closed in $X \times Y$.
[Hint: If $G_f$ is closed and $V$ is a neighborhood of $f(x_0)$, then the intersection of $G_f$ and $X \times (Y - V)$ is closed. Apply Exercise 7.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 26.8, PDF p. 13** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); both directions are theirs.

Let $G_{f}=\{x\times f(x)\}\subset X\times Y$ be the graph of $f$, with $Y$ compact Hausdorff.

*If $f$ is continuous then $G_{f}$ is closed.* Let $x_{0}\times y_{0}\notin G_{f}$, so $f(x_{0})\ne y_{0}$; since $Y$ is Hausdorff there are disjoint open $U\ni f(x_{0})$ and $V\ni y_{0}$. Then $f^{-1}(U)\times V$ is a neighbourhood of $x_{0}\times y_{0}$ meeting $G_{f}$ in nothing: a point $x\times y$ of it has $f(x)\in U$ and $y\in V$, and $U\cap V=\varnothing$.

*If $G_{f}$ is closed then $f$ is continuous.* Let $x_{0}\in X$ and let $V$ be a neighbourhood of $f(x_{0})$; then $C=G_{f}\cap(X\times(Y-V))$ is closed in $X\times Y$, so by Exercise 26.7 (with $Y$ compact) $\pi_{1}(C)$ is closed in $X$ and $W=X-\pi_{1}(C)$ is a neighbourhood of $x_{0}$. For $x\in W$, if $f(x)\notin V$ then $x\times f(x)\in C$ and $x\in\pi_{1}(C)$, a contradiction; so $f(W)\subset V$ and $f$ is continuous at $x_{0}$.
:::
