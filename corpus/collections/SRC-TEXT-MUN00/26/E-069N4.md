::: {.exercise}

Theorem.
Let $f: X \to Y$; let $Y$ be compact Hausdorff.
Then $f$ is continuous if and only if the graph of $f$,

$$
G_f = \ts{x \times f(x) \mid x \in X},
$$

is closed in $X \times Y$.
[Hint: If $G_f$ is closed and $V$ is a neighborhood of $f(x_0)$, then the intersection of $G_f$ and $X \times (Y - V)$ is closed. Apply Exercise 7.]
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove the Closed Graph Theorem for topological spaces: if $Y$ is compact Hausdorff, then $f: X \to Y$ is continuous if and only if its graph $G_f \subseteq X \times Y$ is closed.

<1>1. Direct implication ($\implies$): If $f$ is continuous and $Y$ is Hausdorff, then $G_f$ is closed in $X \times Y$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $(x_0, y_0) \in (X \times Y) \setminus G_f$, so $y_0 \neq f(x_0)$.
    <2>2. Since $Y$ is Hausdorff, there exist disjoint open sets $V, W \subseteq Y$ with $f(x_0) \in V$, $y_0 \in W$, and $V \cap W = \emptyset$.
    <2>3. Since $f$ is continuous, $U = f^{-1}(V)$ is an open neighborhood of $x_0$ in $X$.
    <2>4. The set $U \times W$ is an open neighborhood of $(x_0, y_0)$ in $X \times Y$.
    <2>5. For any $(x, y) \in U \times W$, $x \in U \implies f(x) \in V$, while $y \in W$. Since $V \cap W = \emptyset$, $y \neq f(x)$, so $(x, y) \notin G_f$.
    <2>6. Thus $(U \times W) \cap G_f = \emptyset$, proving that $(X \times Y) \setminus G_f$ is open. Hence $G_f$ is closed.

<1>2. Projection along a compact factor is a closed map:
    If $Y$ is compact, the projection map $\pi_1: X \times Y \to X$ defined by $\pi_1(x, y) = x$ is a closed map.
    *Proof (Tube Lemma):*
    <2>1. Let $C \subseteq X \times Y$ be closed. We show that $X \setminus \pi_1(C)$ is open in $X$.
    <2>2. Let $x_0 \in X \setminus \pi_1(C)$, so $(\{x_0\} \times Y) \cap C = \emptyset$, meaning $\{x_0\} \times Y \subseteq (X \times Y) \setminus C$.
    <2>3. Since $Y$ is compact and $(X \times Y) \setminus C$ is open, the Tube Lemma guarantees the existence of an open neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$ in $X$ such that $U \times Y \subseteq (X \times Y) \setminus C$.
    <2>4. Then $(U \times Y) \cap C = \emptyset$, so $U \cap \pi_1(C) = \emptyset$, which means $U \subseteq X \setminus \pi_1(C)$.
    <2>5. Thus $X \setminus \pi_1(C)$ is open, so $\pi_1(C)$ is closed in $X$.

<1>3. Reverse implication ($\impliedby$): If $G_f$ is closed in $X \times Y$ and $Y$ is compact Hausdorff, then $f$ is continuous.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $x_0 \in X$ and let $V \subseteq Y$ be an open neighborhood of $f(x_0)$.
    <2>2. The complement $Y \setminus V$ is closed in $Y$, and since $Y$ is compact, $Y \setminus V$ is compact.
    <2>3. The cylinder $X \times (Y \setminus V)$ is closed in $X \times Y$.
    <2>4. The intersection $C = G_f \cap (X \times (Y \setminus V))$ is closed in $X \times Y$.
    <2>5. By <1>2, $\pi_1(C)$ is closed in $X$.
    <2>6. We identify the set $\pi_1(C)$:
        $$\pi_1(C) = \{x \in X : (x, f(x)) \in X \times (Y \setminus V)\} = \{x \in X : f(x) \in Y \setminus V\} = X \setminus f^{-1}(V).$$
    <2>7. Since $\pi_1(C) = X \setminus f^{-1}(V)$ is closed in $X$, its complement $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X$.
    <2>8. Moreover, $f(x_0) \in V \implies x_0 \in f^{-1}(V)$, so $f^{-1}(V)$ is an open neighborhood of $x_0$.
    <2>9. Thus $f$ is continuous at $x_0$. Since $x_0 \in X$ was arbitrary, $f$ is continuous on $X$. Q.E.D.
:::
