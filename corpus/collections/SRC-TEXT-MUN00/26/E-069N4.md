::: {.exercise}

Theorem.
Let $f: X \to Y$; let $Y$ be compact Hausdorff.
Then $f$ is continuous if and only if the graph of $f$,

$$
G_f = \theset{x \times f(x) \mid x \in X},
$$

is closed in $X \times Y$.
[Hint: If $G_f$ is closed and $V$ is a neighborhood of $f(x_0)$, then the intersection of $G_f$ and $X \times (Y - V)$ is closed. Apply Exercise 7.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 26.8** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); both directions are theirs.

Let $G_{f}=\{x\times f(x)\mid x\in X\}\subset X\times Y$ and let $Y$ be compact Hausdorff.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #continuous-closed}
If $f$ is continuous, then $G_{f}$ is closed in $X\times Y$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x_{0}\times y_{0}\in(X\times Y)-G_{f}$, so $f(x_{0})\ne y_{0}$. Since $Y$ is Hausdorff there are disjoint open sets $U\ni f(x_{0})$ and $V\ni y_{0}$. Then $f^{-1}(U)\times V$ is a basis element of $X\times Y$ containing $x_{0}\times y_{0}$, and it meets $G_{f}$ in nothing: a point $x\times y$ of it has $f(x)\in U$ and $y\in V$, while $U\cap V=\varnothing$. So every point of the complement of $G_{f}$ has a neighbourhood inside that complement, which is therefore open.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #closed-continuous}
If $G_{f}$ is closed, then $f$ is continuous.

::: pf-proof
Let $x_{0}\in X$ and let $V$ be a neighbourhood of $f(x_{0})$; it suffices to find a neighbourhood $W$ of $x_{0}$ with $f(W)\subset V$. The set $C=G_{f}\cap\big(X\times(Y-V)\big)$ is closed in $X\times Y$, as an intersection of the closed set $G_{f}$ and the closed set $X\times(Y-V)$. By Munkres §26 Exercise 7, with $Y$ compact, the projection of a closed subset of $X\times Y$ to $X$ is closed, so $\pi_{1}(C)$ is closed in $X$ and $W=X-\pi_{1}(C)$ is open. If $x\in W$ and $f(x)\notin V$ then $x\times f(x)\in C$, so $x\in\pi_{1}(C)$, contrary to $x\in W$; hence $f(W)\subset V$. Finally $x_{0}\in W$: if $x_{0}\in\pi_{1}(C)$ then some $y$ has $x_{0}\times y\in G_{f}\cap\big(X\times(Y-V)\big)$, that is $y=f(x_{0})\notin V$, contrary to $V$ containing $f(x_{0})$. So $W$ is a neighbourhood of $x_{0}$ mapped into $V$, and $f$ is continuous at $x_{0}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The equivalence holds.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#continuous-closed){.pf-ref} and [](#closed-continuous){.pf-ref} are the two implications.
:::
