::: {.exercise}

Let $G$ be a group; let $x$ be an element of $G$; let $N$ be the least normal subgroup of $G$ containing $x$.
Show that if there is a normal, path-connected space whose fundamental group is isomorphic to $G$, then there is a normal, path-connected space whose fundamental group is isomorphic to $G/N$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $X$ be a normal path-connected space with $\pi_1(X,a_0)\cong G$; fix an isomorphism and let $\gamma$ be a loop in $X$ at $a_0$ whose class corresponds to $x$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

There is a loop $\gamma:I\to X$ based at $a_0$ whose class is the element corresponding to $x$.

::: pf-proof

The class of a loop depends only on the path-homotopy class, and by Theorem 52.1 (printed p. 332) the fundamental group is the set of those classes with concatenation as multiplication; so any element of $\pi_1(X,a_0)$ is the class of a loop.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let $f:S^1\to X$ be the map induced by $\gamma$, that is, $f\circ p=\gamma$ for the standard quotient map $p:I\to S^1$; it exists because $\gamma$ is a loop. Let $Y$ be the adjunction space of $X$ and $B^2$ along $f$. Then $Y$ is normal, path connected, and $\pi_1(Y,\ast)\cong G/N$.

::: pf-proof

$Y$ is the adjunction space of Exercise 2 with $A=X$. It is normal: $X\sqcup B^2$ is normal, $X$ being normal by hypothesis and $B^2$ compact Hausdorff hence normal (Theorem 32.3, printed p. 202), the quotient map $q:X\sqcup B^2\to Y$ is closed by step 1 of [[E-2B91X]], whose saturation of a closed set carries the two preimages along $f$, and a closed quotient of a normal space is normal, Lemma 73.3 (printed p. 443). It is path connected: $q(X)$ and $q(B^2)$ are path connected, being continuous images of the path connected spaces $X$ and the convex $B^2$, and they meet, since $q(S^1)=f(S^1)$ contains $f(p(0))=\gamma(0)=a_0$; a union of two path connected subspaces with a point in common is path connected, a path to that point followed by a path from it joining any two of its points. The hypotheses of Theorem 72.1 hold by Exercise 2 (printed p. 441). Theorem 72.1 (printed p. 439) then says the inclusion $X\hookrightarrow Y$ induces an epimorphism $\pi_1(X,\ast)\to\pi_1(Y,\ast)$ whose kernel is the least normal subgroup containing the image of $k_\ast:\pi_1(S^1,p)\to\pi_1(X,\ast)$, where $k=f$; the image of $k_\ast$ is generated by the class of $f$, which is the class of $\gamma$, that is $x$. So the kernel is $N$ and $\pi_1(Y,\ast)\cong G/N$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

$Y$ is a normal path-connected space with fundamental group $G/N$.
