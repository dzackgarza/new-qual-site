::: {.exercise}

Let $G$ be a group; let $x$ be an element of $G$; let $N$ be the least normal subgroup of $G$ containing $x$.
Show that if there is a normal, path-connected space whose fundamental group is isomorphic to $G$, then there is a normal, path-connected space whose fundamental group is isomorphic to $G/N$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that attaching a 2-cell $e^2$ to a normal path-connected space $X$ along a loop representing $x \in \pi_1(X, p) \cong G$ produces a normal path-connected space $Y$ with fundamental group isomorphic to $G/N$, where $N = \langle\langle x \rangle\rangle$.

<1>1. Construction of the space $Y$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $X$ be a normal, path-connected topological space with $\pi_1(X, p) \cong G$.
    <2>2. Choose a representative loop $\alpha: [0, 1] \to X$ based at $p$ such that $[\alpha] = x \in \pi_1(X, p)$.
    <2>3. Let $D^2 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid |z| \le 1\}$ be the standard closed 2-disk with boundary $S^1 = \partial D^2$.
    <2>4. Define the attaching map $f: S^1 \to X$ by $f(e^{2\pi i t}) = \alpha(t)$ for $t \in [0, 1]$.
    <2>5. Form the adjunction space $Y = X \cup_f D^2 = (X \sqcup D^2) / \sim$, where $z \sim f(z)$ for $z \in S^1$.

<1>2. Topological properties of $Y$ (path-connected and normal):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Path-connectedness:** $X$ and $D^2$ are path-connected, and the gluing identifies the non-empty connected set $S^1 \subset D^2$ with $f(S^1) \subset X$. Thus $Y$ is path-connected.
    <2>2. **Normality:** Adjoining a cell along a closed subspace of a compact metric space ($S^1 \subset D^2$) to a normal space $X$ preserves normality. (Any closed disjoint subsets in $Y$ pull back to closed disjoint sets in $X$ and $D^2$, which are separated by continuous Urysohn functions compatible along $S^1$.)

<1>3. Computation of $\pi_1(Y, p)$ via Seifert-van Kampen:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\mathbf{0} \in D^2$ be the center of the attached disk.
    <2>2. Define open subsets of $Y$:
        $$U = Y \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\} \quad \text{and} \quad V = \operatorname{Int}(D^2) \subset Y.$$
    <2>3. We have $U \cup V = Y$.
    <2>4. The open set $V$ is homeomorphic to the open disk, so $V$ is contractible: $\pi_1(V) = \{1\}$.
    <2>5. The set $U$ deformation retracts onto $X$ along the radial lines of the punctured disk $D^2 \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}$. Hence the inclusion induces an isomorphism $\pi_1(U, p) \cong \pi_1(X, p) \cong G$.
    <2>6. The intersection $U \cap V = \operatorname{Int}(D^2) \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\} \cong S^1 \times (0, 1)$ is path-connected with $\pi_1(U \cap V) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by a standard loop $\gamma$ around the origin.
    <2>7. Under the inclusion $U \cap V \hookrightarrow U$, the generator $[\gamma]$ maps to $[\alpha] = x \in G$.
    <2>8. By the Seifert-van Kampen Theorem:
        $$\pi_1(Y, p) \cong \pi_1(U, p) *_{\pi_1(U \cap V, p)} \pi_1(V, p) \cong G *_{\mathbb{Z}} \{1\} \cong G / \langle\langle x \rangle\rangle = G/N.$$

<1>4. Conclusion:
    The space $Y = X \cup_f D^2$ is normal, path-connected, and has $\pi_1(Y, p) \cong G/N$. Q.E.D.
:::
