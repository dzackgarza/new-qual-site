::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be the adjunction space formed from the disjoint union of the normal, path-connected space $A$ and the unit ball $B^2$ by means of a continuous map $f: S^1 \to A$.
(See [[E-Z964O]].) Show that $X$ satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 72.1. Where do you use the fact that $A$ is normal?
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $Z=A\sqcup B^2$ be the topological sum and let $q:Z\to X$ be the quotient map that identifies $x\in S^1$ with $f(x)\in A$; put $h=q\restrict_{B^2}$ and write $A$ again for its image $q(A)$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

$Z$ is normal and $q$ is a closed map.

::: pf-proof

A topological sum of two normal spaces is normal: disjoint closed sets $C,D$ of $Z$ have closed disjoint traces $C\cap A$, $D\cap A$ and $C\cap B^2$, $D\cap B^2$, separated by disjoint open sets $U_A,V_A$ in $A$ and $U,V$ in $B^2$; then $U_A\cup U$ and $V_A\cup V$ are disjoint open sets of $Z$ containing $C$ and $D$. So $Z$ is normal, since $A$ is normal by hypothesis and $B^2$ is compact Hausdorff, hence normal (Theorem 32.3, printed p. 202). For closedness, let $C\subseteq Z$ be closed. Its saturation is
$$
q^{-1}q(C)=C\cup f(C\cap S^1)\cup f^{-1}\bigl(f(C\cap S^1)\bigr)\cup f^{-1}(C\cap A):
$$
a point of $A$ lies in it when it lies in $C$ or is $f(x)$ for some $x\in C\cap S^1$; a point $x$ of $S^1$ lies in it when $x\in C$, when $f(x)\in C\cap A$, or when $f(x)=f(x')$ for some $x'\in C\cap S^1$; and a point of $\operatorname{Int}B^2$ lies in it only when it lies in $C$. Each of the four pieces is closed in $Z$: $C$ by hypothesis; $C\cap S^1$ is compact, being a closed subset of the compact space $S^1$, so $f(C\cap S^1)$ is compact (Theorem 26.5, printed p. 166), hence closed in the Hausdorff space $A$ (Theorem 26.3, printed p. 165), and closed in $Z$; and the two preimages are closed in $S^1$, hence in $Z$, because $f$ is continuous and $f(C\cap S^1)$ and $C\cap A$ are closed in $A$. So the saturation is closed, i.e. $q(C)$ is closed in $X$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$X$ is Hausdorff and $A$ is a closed subspace of $X$.

::: pf-proof

$X$ is normal by Lemma 73.3 (printed p. 443, the same statement as Exercise 6 of §31, printed p. 199), applied to the closed surjection $q$; a normal space is Hausdorff, since one-point sets are closed and two points are disjoint closed sets (the definition, printed p. 195). $A$ is closed in $X$ because $A$ is closed in $Z$ and $q$ is closed.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$A$ is path connected and $h(S^1)\subseteq A$.

::: pf-proof

Path connectedness of $A$ is a hypothesis. For $x\in S^1$ the point $x$ is identified with $f(x)$, so $h(x)=q(x)=q(f(x))\in q(A)=A$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$h$ maps $\operatorname{Int}B^2$ bijectively onto $X-A$.

::: pf-proof

The only identifications made by $q$ are between a point $x\in S^1\subseteq B^2$ and the point $f(x)\in A$, so $q$ is one-to-one on $B^2-S^1=\operatorname{Int}B^2$; hence $h$ is one-to-one there. Its image is $q(\operatorname{Int}B^2)$, and $X$ is the disjoint union of $q(\operatorname{Int}B^2)$ and $A$: every point of $X$ is the class of a point of $B^2$ or of $A$; the class of a point $x$ of $S^1$ is the class of $f(x)$, a point of $A$; and the class of a point of $\operatorname{Int}B^2$ is that point alone, so it misses $A$. Hence $q(\operatorname{Int}B^2)=X-A$. That set equals $X-q(S^1)$ only when $f(S^1)=A$, since $q(S^1)=f(S^1)$ is contained in $A$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Where the normality of $A$ is used.

::: pf-proof

In step 1, for the sum: normality of $A$ makes the sum $Z$ normal, with $B^2$ compact Hausdorff and hence normal (Theorem 32.3, printed p. 202), and it is normality of $Z$, not of $A$ alone, that Lemma 73.3 needs in step 2 to conclude that $X$ is normal and hence Hausdorff. The closedness of $q$ itself is cheaper: it uses only that $A$ is Hausdorff, which normality supplies, since a compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed. Hausdorffness of $X$ and closedness of $A$ are two of the hypotheses of Theorem 72.1; the others, path connectedness of $A$ and the behaviour of $h$ on $S^1$ and $\operatorname{Int}B^2$, use no separation axiom.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps 1--4 verify the hypotheses of Theorem 72.1 (printed p. 439) and step 5 answers the question.
