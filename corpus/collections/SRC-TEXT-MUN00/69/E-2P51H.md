::: {.exercise}

If $G = G_1 * G_2$, show that

$$
G/[G, G] \cong (G_1/[G_1, G_1]) \oplus (G_2/[G_2, G_2]).
$$

[Hint: Use the extension condition for direct sums and free products to define homomorphisms

$$
G/[G, G] \to (G_1/[G_1, G_1]) \oplus (G_2/[G_2, G_2])
$$

that are inverse to each other.]
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $\mathrm{Ab}(H)=H/[H,H]$ and let $\alpha_i:G_i\to\mathrm{Ab}(G_i)$ be the quotient homomorphisms. By Theorem 69.3 (printed p. 422), $\mathrm{Ab}(H)$ is abelian and every homomorphism of $H$ into an abelian group factors uniquely through $\alpha_H$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

There is a homomorphism $\theta:G_1*G_2\to \mathrm{Ab}(G_1)\oplus\mathrm{Ab}(G_2)$ with $\theta(g_1)=(\alpha_1(g_1),0)$ and $\theta(g_2)=(0,\alpha_2(g_2))$.

::: pf-proof

The two assignments are homomorphisms $G_i\to\mathrm{Ab}(G_1)\oplus\mathrm{Ab}(G_2)$, so the extension condition for free products (Lemma 68.3, printed p. 418) produces $\theta$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\theta$ induces $\phi:\mathrm{Ab}(G_1*G_2)\to\mathrm{Ab}(G_1)\oplus\mathrm{Ab}(G_2)$.

::: pf-proof

The target of $\theta$ is abelian, so $[G_1*G_2,G_1*G_2]\subseteq\ker\theta$ and $\theta$ factors through the quotient $\alpha_G$: there is $\phi$ with $\phi\circ\alpha_G=\theta$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There is a homomorphism $\psi:\mathrm{Ab}(G_1)\oplus\mathrm{Ab}(G_2)\to\mathrm{Ab}(G_1*G_2)$ whose composites with the two summands are the abelianizations $J_i$ of the inclusions.

::: pf-proof

Let $J_i:\mathrm{Ab}(G_i)\to\mathrm{Ab}(G_1*G_2)$ be induced by the inclusion $G_i\hookrightarrow G_1*G_2$, which carries $[G_i,G_i]$ into the commutator subgroup. The extension condition for direct sums (Lemma 67.1, printed p. 408) applied to $J_1,J_2$ gives $\psi$ with $\psi(u,0)=J_1(u)$ and $\psi(0,v)=J_2(v)$, that is $\psi(u,v)=J_1(u)+J_2(v)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\phi\circ\psi=\mathrm{id}$ and $\psi\circ\phi=\mathrm{id}$.

::: pf-proof

On the first summand, with $u=\alpha_1(g_1)$,

$$
\phi\psi(u,0)=\phi J_1\alpha_1(g_1)=\phi\alpha_G(g_1)=\theta(g_1)=(\alpha_1(g_1),0)=(u,0),
$$

and symmetrically $\phi\psi(0,v)=(0,v)$; so $\phi\psi=\mathrm{id}$. Hence $\phi$ is onto and $\psi$ is one-to-one. For the other composite, $\mathrm{Ab}(G_1*G_2)$ is generated by the images of $J_1$ and $J_2$: the group $G_1*G_2$ is generated by $G_1\cup G_2$, so its abelian quotient is generated by the images of $\mathrm{Ab}(G_1)$ and $\mathrm{Ab}(G_2)$. Thus $\psi$ is onto as well, hence bijective with inverse $\phi$, and $\psi\phi=\mathrm{id}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

$\phi$ is an isomorphism $\mathrm{Ab}(G_1*G_2)\cong\mathrm{Ab}(G_1)\oplus\mathrm{Ab}(G_2)$ by steps 2--4.

:::
