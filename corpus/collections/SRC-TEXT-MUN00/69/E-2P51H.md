::: {.exercise}

If $G = G_1 * G_2$, show that

$$
G/[G, G] \cong (G_1/[G_1, G_1]) \oplus (G_2/[G_2, G_2]).
$$

[Hint: Use the extension condition for direct sums and free products to define homomorphisms

$$
G/[G, G] \to (G_1/[G_1, G_1]) \oplus (G_2/[G_2, G_2])
$$

that are inverse to each other.]
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that the abelianization of a free product of groups $G = G_1 * G_2$ is canonically isomorphic to the direct sum (product) of the individual abelianizations: $G_{\text{ab}} \cong G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$.

<1>1. Universal property setup:
    Let $\pi_i: G_i \to G_i^{\text{ab}} = G_i/[G_i, G_i]$ ($i=1, 2$) and $\pi_G: G \to G_{\text{ab}} = G/[G, G]$ denote the canonical abelianization projections.
    Let $\iota_i: G_i \to G_1 * G_2$ and $j_i: G_i^{\text{ab}} \to G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$ denote the canonical inclusions.

<1>2. Construction of the forward homomorphism $\Phi: G_{\text{ab}} \to G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For $i = 1, 2$, the composite map $\phi_i = j_i \circ \pi_i: G_i \to G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$ is a group homomorphism into the abelian group $G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$.
    <2>2. By the universal property of the free product $G = G_1 * G_2$, there exists a unique homomorphism $\phi: G \to G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$ such that $\phi \circ \iota_i = \phi_i$ for $i = 1, 2$.
    <2>3. Since the target group $G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$ is abelian, the commutator subgroup $[G, G] \subseteq \ker \phi$.
    <2>4. By the universal property of quotient groups (abelianization), $\phi$ factors uniquely to a homomorphism:
        $$\Phi: G_{\text{ab}} \to G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}, \quad \Phi(\pi_G(\iota_i(g_i))) = j_i(\pi_i(g_i)).$$

<1>3. Construction of the reverse homomorphism $\Psi: G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}} \to G_{\text{ab}}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For $i = 1, 2$, the composite map $\psi_i = \pi_G \circ \iota_i: G_i \to G_{\text{ab}}$ maps into the abelian group $G_{\text{ab}}$.
    <2>2. By the universal property of abelianization of $G_i$, each $\psi_i$ factors uniquely through $G_i^{\text{ab}}$ to yield a homomorphism $\bar{\psi}_i: G_i^{\text{ab}} \to G_{\text{ab}}$ satisfying $\bar{\psi}_i(\pi_i(g_i)) = \pi_G(\iota_i(g_i))$.
    <2>3. By the universal property of the direct sum of abelian groups, the pair $(\bar{\psi}_1, \bar{\psi}_2)$ induces a unique homomorphism:
        $$\Psi: G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}} \to G_{\text{ab}}, \quad \Psi(x_1, x_2) = \bar{\psi}_1(x_1) \cdot \bar{\psi}_2(x_2).$$

<1>4. Verification that $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are mutual inverses:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. On $G_{\text{ab}}$: Every element of $G_{\text{ab}}$ is a product of cosets $\pi_G(\iota_1(g_1))$ and $\pi_G(\iota_2(g_2))$.
        $$(\Psi \circ \Phi)(\pi_G(\iota_1(g_1))) = \Psi(\pi_1(g_1), 0) = \bar{\psi}_1(\pi_1(g_1)) = \pi_G(\iota_1(g_1)).$$
        Similarly for elements from $G_2$. Since these generators span $G_{\text{ab}}$, $\Psi \circ \Phi = \operatorname{id}_{G_{\text{ab}}}$.
    <2>2. On $G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$: For any $(x_1, 0) \in G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus \{0\}$, choose $g_1 \in G_1$ with $\pi_1(g_1) = x_1$.
        $$(\Phi \circ \Psi)(x_1, 0) = \Phi(\bar{\psi}_1(\pi_1(g_1))) = \Phi(\pi_G(\iota_1(g_1))) = (\pi_1(g_1), 0) = (x_1, 0).$$
        Similarly for $(0, x_2)$. Since these generate $G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}$, $\Phi \circ \Psi = \operatorname{id}_{G_1^{\text{ab}} \oplus G_2^{\text{ab}}}$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $\Phi$ is an isomorphism, establishing $G/[G, G] \cong (G_1/[G_1, G_1]) \oplus (G_2/[G_2, G_2])$. Q.E.D.
:::
