::: {.exercise}

Let $\{G_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in J}$ be an arbitrary family of groups and let $G=\bigast_{\alpha\in J}G_\alpha$.
Show that
$$
G/[G,G]\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha\in J}G_\alpha/[G_\alpha,G_\alpha].
$$
This generalizes [[E-2P51H]], the case of two groups.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $\mathrm{Ab}(H)=H/[H,H]$ and $\alpha_\alpha:G_\alpha\to\mathrm{Ab}(G_\alpha)$ for the quotient homomorphisms.

::: pf

::: pf-step

There is a homomorphism $\theta:G\to\bigoplus_\alpha\mathrm{Ab}(G_\alpha)$ with $\theta\circ i_\alpha=\alpha_\alpha$ followed by the inclusion of the $\alpha$th summand.

::: pf-proof

By the extension condition for free products (Lemma 68.3, printed p. 418) applied to the family of homomorphisms $G_\alpha\to\bigoplus_\beta\mathrm{Ab}(G_\beta)$, $g\mapsto$ the tuple with $\alpha_\alpha(g)$ in coordinate $\alpha$ and $0$ elsewhere.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\theta$ induces $\phi:\mathrm{Ab}(G)\to\bigoplus_\alpha\mathrm{Ab}(G_\alpha)$.

::: pf-proof

The direct sum is abelian, so $[G,G]\subseteq\ker\theta$ and $\theta=\phi\circ\alpha_G$ for the abelianization $\alpha_G:G\to\mathrm{Ab}(G)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There is a homomorphism $\psi:\bigoplus_\alpha\mathrm{Ab}(G_\alpha)\to\mathrm{Ab}(G)$ whose restriction to the $\alpha$th summand is the map $J_\alpha$ induced by the inclusion $G_\alpha\hookrightarrow G$.

::: pf-proof

Each inclusion induces $J_\alpha:\mathrm{Ab}(G_\alpha)\to\mathrm{Ab}(G)$, since $[G_\alpha,G_\alpha]$ maps into $[G,G]$. The extension condition for direct sums (Lemma 67.1, printed p. 408), which applies to an arbitrary index set because a direct sum consists of finite tuples, gives $\psi$ with $\psi$ equal to $J_\alpha$ on the $\alpha$th summand.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\phi\psi=\mathrm{id}$ and $\psi\phi=\mathrm{id}$.

::: pf-proof

On the $\alpha$th summand, for $u=\alpha_\alpha(g)$,

$$
\phi\psi(u)=\phi J_\alpha\alpha_\alpha(g)=\phi\alpha_G(i_\alpha(g))=\theta(i_\alpha(g))=u,
$$

so $\phi\psi=\mathrm{id}$ and $\phi$ is onto, $\psi$ one-to-one. Conversely $\mathrm{Ab}(G)$ is generated by the images of the $J_\alpha$, because $G$ is generated by $\bigcup_\alpha i_\alpha(G_\alpha)$; so $\psi$ is onto, hence bijective, and $\psi\phi=\mathrm{id}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

$\mathrm{Ab}(G)\cong\bigoplus_\alpha\mathrm{Ab}(G_\alpha)$ by steps 2--4.

:::
