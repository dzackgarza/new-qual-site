::: {.exercise}

Generalize the result of Exercise 1 to arbitrary free products.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Prove that for an arbitrary indexed family of groups $\{G_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in J}$, the abelianization of their free product is isomorphic to the direct sum of their individual abelianizations: $$\left( \bigast_{\alpha \in J} G_\alpha \right)_{\text{ab}} \cong \bigoplus_{\alpha \in J} (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}}.$$

::: pf

::: pf-step
Notation and universal maps:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Let $G = \bigast_{\alpha \in J} G_\alpha$, and let $i_\alpha: G_\alpha \hookrightarrow G$ be the canonical inclusion homomorphisms into the free product.
:::

::: pf-step
For each $\alpha \in J$, let $\pi_\alpha: G_\alpha \to (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}} = G_\alpha / [G_\alpha, G_\alpha]$ be the canonical quotient projection onto the abelianization.
:::

::: pf-step
Let $A = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in J} (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}}$, and let $j_\alpha: (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}} \hookrightarrow A$ be the canonical injection into the direct sum.
:::

::: pf-step
Let $\pi: G \to G_{\text{ab}} = G / [G, G]$ be the quotient homomorphism onto the abelianization of $G$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Construction of the homomorphism $\Phi: G_{\text{ab}} \to \bigoplus_{\alpha \in J} (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}}$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
For each $\alpha \in J$, define the composite homomorphism $\phi_\alpha = j_\alpha \circ \pi_\alpha: G_\alpha \to A$.
:::

::: pf-step
By the universal property of the free product, there exists a unique group homomorphism $\phi: G \to A$ such that $\phi \circ i_\alpha = \phi_\alpha$ for all $\alpha \in J$.
:::

::: pf-step
Because the codomain $A$ is abelian, the commutator subgroup $[G, G] \subseteq \ker(\phi)$.
:::

::: pf-step
By the universal property of quotient groups (abelianization), $\phi$ induces a unique homomorphism $\Phi: G_{\text{ab}} \to A$ satisfying $\Phi(\pi(g)) = \phi(g)$ for all $g \in G$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Construction of the inverse homomorphism $\Psi: \bigoplus_{\alpha \in J} (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}} \to G_{\text{ab}}$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
For each $\alpha \in J$, consider the composite homomorphism $\psi_\alpha = \pi \circ i_\alpha: G_\alpha \to G_{\text{ab}}$.
:::

::: pf-step
Since $G_{\text{ab}}$ is abelian, $[G_\alpha, G_\alpha] \subseteq \ker(\psi_\alpha)$, so $\psi_\alpha$ factors uniquely through $(G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}}$ to yield a homomorphism $\bar{\psi}_\alpha: (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}} \to G_{\text{ab}}$ satisfying $\bar{\psi}_\alpha \circ \pi_\alpha = \psi_\alpha$.
:::

::: pf-step
By the universal property of the direct sum of abelian groups (coproduct in $\mathbf{Ab}$), the family $\{\bar{\psi}_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in J}$ induces a unique homomorphism $\Psi: A \to G_{\text{ab}}$ such that $\Psi \circ j_\alpha = \bar{\psi}_\alpha$ for all $\alpha \in J$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Verification that $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are mutually inverse:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
**$\Phi \circ \Psi = \operatorname{id}_A$:** For each $\alpha \in J$ and each $x \in (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}}$, choose $g \in G_\alpha$ with $\pi_\alpha(g) = x$.
Then: $$\Phi(\Psi(j_\alpha(x))) = \Phi(\bar{\psi}_\alpha(\pi_\alpha(g))) = \Phi(\pi(i_\alpha(g))) = \phi(i_\alpha(g)) = j_\alpha(\pi_\alpha(g)) = j_\alpha(x).$$ Since the images $j_\alpha((G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}})$ generate $A$, $\Phi \circ \Psi = \operatorname{id}_A$.
:::

::: pf-step
**$\Psi \circ \Phi = \operatorname{id}_{G_{\text{ab}}}$:** For each generator $\pi(i_\alpha(g)) \in G_{\text{ab}}$ ($g \in G_\alpha$): $$\Psi(\Phi(\pi(i_\alpha(g)))) = \Psi(j_\alpha(\pi_\alpha(g))) = \bar{\psi}_\alpha(\pi_\alpha(g)) = \pi(i_\alpha(g)).$$ Since the elements $\pi(i_\alpha(G_\alpha))$ generate $G_{\text{ab}}$, $\Psi \circ \Phi = \operatorname{id}_{G_{\text{ab}}}$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Conclusion: $\Phi$ is an isomorphism, so $\left( \bigast_{\alpha \in J} G_\alpha \right)_{\text{ab}} \cong \bigoplus_{\alpha \in J} (G_\alpha)_{\text{ab}}$.
:::
