::: {.exercise}

Let $p: E \to X$ be a covering map such that $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is countable.
Show that if $X$ is regular with a countable basis, so is $E$.
[Hint: Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a countable basis for $X$ consisting of path-connected sets. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the collection of path components of $p^{-1}(B)$, for $B \in \mathcal{B}$. Compare Exercise 6 of §53.]

:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own, following the book's hint; no retained solution set covers this exercise, which is Munkres §82 Exercise 1. Throughout, $E$ and $X$ are path connected and locally path connected by the Convention of Chapter 13 , and a slice over an evenly covered open $U$ is a member of a partition of $p^{-1}(U)$ into open sets mapped homeomorphically onto $U$ (Definition, §53).

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis-good}
$X$ has a countable basis $\mathcal{B}$ consisting of path connected evenly covered open sets.

::: pf-proof
Take a countable basis and replace each member by its path components, which are open because $X$ is locally path connected (§25) and path connected by construction; the result is a countable basis of path connected sets. An open set contained in an evenly covered set is evenly covered, so discarding the members that are not evenly covered leaves a countable basis with the required properties.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fibres}
For each $B\in\mathcal{B}$ the collection of slices over $B$ is at most countable.

::: pf-proof
Since $B$ is connected, the components of $p^{-1}(B)$ are precisely the slices over $B$, each mapped homeomorphically onto $B$. Fix $b\in B$ and $e_{b}\in p^{-1}(b)$; each slice contains exactly one point of $p^{-1}(b)$, so the slices over $B$ correspond bijectively to the fibre $p^{-1}(b)$. That fibre is at most countable: the lifting correspondence $\Phi:\pi_{1}(X,b)/p_{*}\pi_{1}(E,e_{b})\to p^{-1}(b)$ is a bijection (Theorem 54.6), and the quotient of the countable group $\pi_{1}(X,b)$ by a subgroup is countable. Any other fibre $p^{-1}(b')$ is equinumerous with $p^{-1}(b)$, since a path from $b$ to $b'$ lifts to a path from $e_{b}$ to a point of $p^{-1}(b')$ and the reverse lifting takes it back (Lemma 54.1).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #second-countable}
$E$ is second countable.

::: pf-proof
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the collection of all slices over all $B\in\mathcal{B}$; it is a countable union of at most countable families, hence countable, and each of its members is open in $E$. It is a basis: given $e\in E$ and an open $O\ni e$, choose an evenly covered $U\ni p(e)$ whose slice $V$ containing $e$ lies in $O$; then choose $B\in\mathcal{B}$ with $p(e)\in B\subset U$, and the slice over $B$ containing $e$ lies in $V\subset O$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #regular}
$E$ is regular.

::: pf-proof
Let $e\in E$ and let $O$ be an open neighbourhood of $e$. Choose an evenly covered open $U$ whose slice $V$ satisfies $e\in V\subset O$, and, $X$ being regular, an open $W$ with $p(e)\in W$ and $\overline{W}\subset U$. Put $V_{W}=(p\restrict_{V})^{-1}(W)$, an open subset of $V$ containing $e$. Then $p\big(\overline{V_{W}}\big)\subset\overline{p(V_{W})}\subset\overline{W}\subset U$ by continuity, so $\overline{V_{W}}\subset p^{-1}(U)$. Now $p^{-1}(U)$ is the disjoint union of the open slices over $U$, so each slice is also closed in $p^{-1}(U)$; in particular $V$ is closed in $p^{-1}(U)$, and $V_{W}\subset V$ gives $\overline{V_{W}}\cap p^{-1}(U)\subset V$. Since $\overline{V_{W}}\subset p^{-1}(U)$, the closure of $V_{W}$ in $E$ equals its closure in the subspace $p^{-1}(U)$, so $\overline{V_{W}}\subset V\subset O$. Thus every neighbourhood of every point contains a closed neighbourhood, and $E$ is regular.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$E$ is regular with a countable basis.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#second-countable){.pf-ref} gives the countable basis and step [](#regular){.pf-ref} the regularity.
:::
