::: {.exercise}

Let $p: E \to X$ be a covering map such that $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is countable.
Show that if $X$ is regular with a countable basis, so is $E$.
[Hint: Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a countable basis for $X$ consisting of path-connected sets. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the collection of path components of $p^{-1}(B)$, for $B \in \mathcal{B}$. Compare Exercise 6 of §53.]

:::

::: {.solution}

Throughout, $E$ and $X$ are path connected and locally path connected by the Convention of Chapter 13 (printed p. 477), and a slice means a member of a partition of $p^{-1}(U)$ into sets mapped homeomorphically onto $U$ (Definition, printed p. 336).

Choose a countable basis $\mathcal B$ for $X$ consisting of path-connected evenly covered open sets. Since $X$ is locally path connected, the path components of the elements of a countable basis form a countable basis of path-connected sets; discarding those that are not evenly covered, which is legitimate because an open set contained in an evenly covered set is evenly covered, leaves a countable basis with the required properties.

For $B\in\mathcal B$, the components of $p^{-1}(B)$ are precisely the slices over $B$, because $B$ is connected and each slice is mapped homeomorphically onto it. Fix $b\in B$. Each slice contains exactly one point of the fibre $p^{-1}(b)$. That fibre is at most countable: the lifting correspondence $\Phi:\pi_1(X,b)/p_*\pi_1(E,e_b)\to p^{-1}(b)$ is a bijection (Theorem 54.6, printed p. 346) for a suitable $e_b\in p^{-1}(b)$, and any two fibres of $p$ are equinumerous, because a path in $X$ from $b$ to $b'$ lifts to a path in $E$ from $e_b$ to a point of $p^{-1}(b')$ and the lifting of the reversed path is its inverse (Lemma 54.1, printed p. 342). Hence every $p^{-1}(B)$ has at most countably many sheets.

Let $\mathcal C$ be the collection of all these sheets as $B$ ranges over $\mathcal B$. It is a countable union of countable families, hence countable. It is a basis for $E$: each member is open, and given $e\in E$ and an open $O\ni e$, choose an evenly covered open set $U\ni p(e)$ whose slice $V$ containing $e$ lies in $O$, then $B\in\mathcal B$ with $p(e)\in B\subseteq U$, and the slice over $B$ containing $e$, which lies in $V\subseteq O$. Thus $E$ is second countable.

It remains to prove regularity. Let $e\in E$ and let $O$ be an open neighbourhood of $e$. Choose an evenly covered open set $U\subset X$ such that the slice $V$ containing $e$ satisfies
$$
e\in V\subset O.
$$
Because $X$ is regular (Definition, printed p. 196), choose an open neighbourhood $W$ of $p(e)$ with
$$
\overline W\subset U.
$$
Let
$$
V_W=(p|_V)^{-1}(W).
$$
Then $V_W$ is open and contains $e$. Its closure lies in $p^{-1}(\overline W)\subset p^{-1}(U)$, because the closure of a preimage lies in the preimage of the closure (Theorem 18.1, printed p. 104), and $p^{-1}(U)$ is the disjoint union of open sets of which $V$ is one, so $V$ is both open and closed in $p^{-1}(U)$. Hence the closure of $V_W$ is contained in the closure of $V_W$ taken in $p^{-1}(U)$, which lies in $V$. Since $p|_V$ is a homeomorphism,
$$
\overline{V_W}\subset (p|_V)^{-1}(\overline W)\subset V\subset O.
$$
Therefore $E$ is regular. Hence $E$ is regular with a countable basis.
