::: {.exercise}

Let $\mathcal{C}'(X, Y)$ denote the set $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ in some topology $\mathcal{T}$.
Show that if the evaluation map

$$
e: X \times \mathcal{C}'(X, Y) \to Y
$$

is continuous, then $\mathcal{T}$ contains the compact-open topology.
[Hint: The induced map $E: \mathcal{C}'(X, Y) \to \mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ is continuous.]
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that if the evaluation map $e: X \times \mathcal{C}'(X, Y) \to Y$ is continuous, then the topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $\mathcal{C}'(X, Y)$ contains the compact-open topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{co}}$.

<1>1. Subbasis for the compact-open topology:
    The compact-open topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{co}}$ on $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ is generated by the subbasis of sets of the form:
    $$S(C, U) = \{f \in \mathcal{C}(X, Y) : f(C) \subseteq U\}$$
    where $C \subseteq X$ is compact and $U \subseteq Y$ is open.
    To show $\mathcal{T}_{\text{co}} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$, it suffices to show that each subbasis element $S(C, U)$ is open in $(\mathcal{C}(X, Y), \mathcal{T})$.

<1>2. Preimage under evaluation:
    Let $C \subseteq X$ be compact, $U \subseteq Y$ open, and let $f_0 \in S(C, U)$.
    The set $e^{-1}(U) = \{(x, f) \in X \times \mathcal{C}'(X, Y) : f(x) \in U\}$ is open in $X \times \mathcal{C}'(X, Y)$ by continuity of $e$.
    *Proof:* For every $x \in C$, $e(x, f_0) = f_0(x) \in f_0(C) \subseteq U$. Thus $C \times \{f_0\} \subseteq e^{-1}(U)$.

<1>3. Application of the Tube Lemma:
    There exists an open neighborhood $W \in \mathcal{T}$ of $f_0$ in $\mathcal{C}'(X, Y)$ such that $C \times W \subseteq e^{-1}(U)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $C$ is compact in $X$.
    <2>2. $e^{-1}(U)$ is an open subset of the product space $X \times \mathcal{C}'(X, Y)$ containing the slice $C \times \{f_0\}$.
    <2>3. By the Tube Lemma, there exists an open set $W \subseteq \mathcal{C}'(X, Y)$ (with $W \in \mathcal{T}$) such that $f_0 \in W$ and $C \times W \subseteq e^{-1}(U)$.

<1>4. Inclusion of the neighborhood $W$ in $S(C, U)$:
    $W \subseteq S(C, U)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $f \in W$. For any $x \in C$, $(x, f) \in C \times W \subseteq e^{-1}(U)$.
    <2>2. Hence $e(x, f) = f(x) \in U$ for all $x \in C$, which means $f(C) \subseteq U$.
    <2>3. By definition of $S(C, U)$, this shows $f \in S(C, U)$.
    <2>4. Thus $f_0 \in W \subseteq S(C, U)$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    Since every point $f_0 \in S(C, U)$ has an open neighborhood $W \in \mathcal{T}$ contained in $S(C, U)$, $S(C, U)$ is open in $\mathcal{T}$. Therefore $\mathcal{T}$ contains the compact-open topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{co}}$. Q.E.D.
:::
