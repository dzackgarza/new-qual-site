::: {.exercise}

Show that the sets $B_C(f, \epsilon)$ form a basis for a topology on $Y^X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For compact $C\subset X$, $f\in Y^X$, and $\varepsilon>0$, let
$$
B_C(f,\varepsilon)=\{g:\sup_{x\in C}d(f(x),g(x))<\varepsilon\}.
$$
For $C=\varnothing$, set the supremum to $0$; then $B_C(f,\varepsilon)=Y^X$.
Every $f$ belongs to such a set (take any compact $C$, including a singleton when $X\ne\varnothing$). If
$$
h\in B_C(f,\varepsilon)\cap B_D(g,\delta),
$$
set
$$
r=\min\{\varepsilon-d_C(f,h),\delta-d_D(g,h)\}>0,
$$
where $d_C(u,v)=\sup_C d(u,v)$. Since $C\cup D$ is compact, $B_{C\cup D}(h,r)$ is defined, and the triangle inequality gives
$$
B_{C\cup D}(h,r)\subset B_C(f,\varepsilon)\cap B_D(g,\delta).
$$
Thus these sets satisfy the basis intersection axiom and form a basis for the topology of compact convergence.
:::
