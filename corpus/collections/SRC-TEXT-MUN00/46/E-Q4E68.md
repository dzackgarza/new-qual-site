::: {.exercise}

Consider the sequence of continuous functions $f_n: \RR \to \RR$ defined by

$$
f_n(x) = x/n.
$$

In which of the three topologies of Theorem 46.7 does this sequence converge?
Answer the same question for the sequence given in [[E-BTI7W]].
:::

::: {.solution}
Theorem 46.7 compares pointwise, compact-convergence, and uniform topologies.

For $f_n(x)=x/n$, we have pointwise convergence to $0$. On every compact $C\subset\RR$, $M=\sup_{x\in C}\abs{x}<\infty$, so
$$
\sup_C\abs{f_n}=M/n\to0;
$$
hence convergence is compact-uniform. It is not uniform on all of $\RR$, since for every $n$, $\sup_x\abs{x}/n=\infty$.

For the sequence of [[E-BTI7W]],
$$
f_n(x)=\frac1{n^3(x-1/n)^2+1},
$$
$f_n(x)\to0$ pointwise by [[E-BTI7W]]. It does not converge uniformly because $f_n(1/n)=1$. Nor does it converge in the compact-convergence topology: the compact set $[0,1]$ contains $1/n$ for all $n$, so
$$
\sup_{[0,1]}f_n=1.
$$
Thus the first sequence converges pointwise and compact-uniformly but not uniformly; the second converges only pointwise.
:::
