::: {.exercise}

Let $f: S^1 \to \RR$ be a continuous map.
Show there exists a point $x$ of $S^1$ such that $f(x) = f(-x)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 24.2, PDF p. 4** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); the argument is theirs.

Let $f:S^{1}\to\RR$ be continuous and put $g(x)=f(x)-f(-x)$, which is continuous on $S^{1}$ and satisfies $g(-x)=-g(x)$.

If $g(x)=0$ for some $x$ we are done. Otherwise fix $x$ with $g(x)\ne0$; say $g(x)>0$, so $g(-x)<0$. Since $S^{1}$ is connected (Example 5 of §24: it is the continuous image of the path-connected space $\RR^{2}-\{0\}$, or the image of $[0,1]$ under $t\mapsto e^{2\pi it}$) and $g$ is continuous, the intermediate value theorem (Theorem 24.3) applied to the points $x,-x$ gives $y\in S^{1}$ with $g(y)=0$, that is $f(y)=f(-y)$. The case $g(x)<0$ is the same with the roles of $x$ and $-x$ exchanged.
:::
