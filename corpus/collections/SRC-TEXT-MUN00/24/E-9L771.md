::: {.exercise}

Let $f: S^1 \to \RR$ be a continuous map.
Show there exists a point $x$ of $S^1$ such that $f(x) = f(-x)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained published solution, **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 24.2** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`), restructured into steps.

Throughout, $S^1$ is the unit circle in $\RR^2$ and $-x$ is the antipode of $x$ on it.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #odd}
The map $g(x)=f(x)-f(-x)$ is continuous and satisfies $g(-x)=-g(x)$ for every $x\in S^1$.

::: pf-proof
The antipodal map $x\mapsto -x$ is the restriction to $S^1$ of the linear map $z\mapsto -z$ of $\RR^2$, hence continuous, and $f$ is continuous by hypothesis; so both $f(x)$ and $f(-x)$ depend continuously on $x$, and so does their difference. Finally $g(-x)=f(-x)-f(x)=-g(x)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #zero}
Some $x\in S^1$ satisfies $f(x)=f(-x)$.

::: pf-proof
Suppose that $g$ has no zero, and choose $x\in S^1$. Then $g(x)\ne0$, and replacing $x$ by $-x$ if necessary and using step [](#odd){.pf-ref} we may assume $g(x)>0$ and hence $g(-x)=-g(x)<0$. The circle $S^1$ is the image of the connected space $[0,1]$ under the continuous map $t\mapsto(\cos 2\pi t,\sin 2\pi t)$, hence is connected by Theorem 23.5, and $g$ is a continuous real-valued function on it. The intermediate value theorem (Theorem 24.3) applied to the two points $x$ and $-x$ of $S^1$, between whose $g$-values $0$ lies, produces $y\in S^1$ with $g(y)=0$, contradicting the supposition. So $g$ has a zero $y$, and then $f(y)-f(-y)=g(y)=0$, that is $f(y)=f(-y)$.
:::
