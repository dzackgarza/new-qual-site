::: {.exercise}

Let $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous map.
Show there exists a point $x$ of $S^1$ such that $f(x) = f(-x)$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove the 1-dimensional Borsuk-Ulam theorem: for any continuous map $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{R}$, there exists a pair of antipodal points $x, -x \in S^1$ with $f(x) = f(-x)$.

<1>1. Definition and properties of the difference function $g$:
    Define $g: S^1 \to \mathbb{R}$ by:
    $$g(x) = f(x) - f(-x).$$
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The antipodal map $a: S^1 \to S^1$ given by $a(x) = -x$ is continuous.
    <2>2. As the difference of the continuous maps $f$ and $f \circ a$, $g$ is continuous.
    <2>3. For every $x \in S^1$, $g(-x) = f(-x) - f(x) = -g(x)$.

<1>2. Existence of a zero via the Intermediate Value Theorem:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Choose an arbitrary point $x_0 \in S^1$.
    <2>2. If $g(x_0) = 0$, then $f(x_0) = f(-x_0)$, and $x = x_0$ satisfies the requirement.
    <2>3. If $g(x_0) \neq 0$, then $g(-x_0) = -g(x_0)$ has the opposite sign to $g(x_0)$.
    <2>4. Let $A \subset S^1$ be a connected semicircular arc connecting $x_0$ and $-x_0$.
    <2>5. The image $g(A) \subset \mathbb{R}$ is connected, hence an interval containing the values $g(x_0)$ and $-g(x_0)$.
    <2>6. Since $0$ lies between $g(x_0)$ and $-g(x_0)$, by the Intermediate Value Theorem there exists a point $x \in A$ such that $g(x) = 0$.
    <2>7. Thus $f(x) - f(-x) = 0 \implies f(x) = f(-x)$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    There exists $x \in S^1$ such that $f(x) = f(-x)$. Q.E.D.
:::
