::: {.exercise}

Let $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous map.
Show there exists a point $x$ of $S^1$ such that $f(x) = f(-x)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $g\colon S^1\to\mathbb R$, $g(x)=f(x)-f(-x)$.

::: pf

::: pf-step
$g$ is continuous and $g(-x)=-g(x)$.

::: pf-proof
The antipodal map $x\mapsto-x$ is continuous, so $g$ is a difference of continuous maps.
The identity is immediate from the definition.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Fix $x_0\in S^1$.
If $g(x_0)=0$, take $x=x_0$.
Otherwise $g(x_0)$ and $g(-x_0)=-g(x_0)$ have opposite signs.
A semicircular arc $A\subseteq S^1$ from $x_0$ to $-x_0$ is connected, so $g(A)$ is an interval containing both values, hence $0$.
A point $x\in A$ with $g(x)=0$ satisfies $f(x)=f(-x)$.
:::
