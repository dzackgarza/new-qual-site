::: {.exercise}

Show that if $U$ is an open connected subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$, then $U$ is path connected.
[Hint: Show that given $x_0 \in U$, the set of points that can be joined to $x_0$ by a path in $U$ is both open and closed in $U$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
If $U=\varnothing$ there is nothing to join. Otherwise fix $x_0\in U$ and let $C$ be the set of $x\in U$ joined to $x_0$ by a path in $U$; the constant path shows $x_0\in C$.

<1>1. If $x\in U$ and $B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq U$, then either $B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq C$ or $B(x,\varepsilon)\cap C=\varnothing$.

::: {.proof}
The ball is convex, so any two of its points are joined by a line segment in $B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq U$.
If some $w\in B(x,\varepsilon)$ lies in $C$, concatenating a path in $U$ from $x_0$ to $w$ with the segment from $w$ to any $y\in B(x,\varepsilon)$ gives a path in $U$ from $x_0$ to $y$, so $B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq C$.
:::

<1>2. $C$ is open and closed in $U$.

::: {.proof}
Every $x\in U$ has a ball $B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq U$ because $U$ is open.
By step <1>1, if $x\in C$ the ball lies in $C$, and if $x\notin C$ the ball lies in $U-C$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
$C$ is nonempty, and by step <1>2 it is open and closed in the connected space $U$, so $C=U$.
Hence every point of $U$ is joined to $x_0$, and any two points of $U$ are joined through $x_0$.
:::
:::
