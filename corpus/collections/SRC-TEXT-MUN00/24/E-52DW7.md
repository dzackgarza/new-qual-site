::: {.exercise}

Show that if $U$ is an open connected subspace of $\RR^2$, then $U$ is path connected.
[Hint: Show that given $x_0 \in U$, the set of points that can be joined to $x_0$ by a path in $U$ is both open and closed in $U$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 24.10, PDF p. 6** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); the argument is theirs, following the hint.

Let $U\subset\RR^{2}$ be open and connected. For $x\in U$ let $C_{x}$ be the set of points of $U$ that can be joined to $x$ by a path in $U$; it is nonempty since $x\in C_{x}$.

$C_{x}$ is open: if $y\in C_{x}$, the openness of $U$ gives a ball $B(y,r)\subset U$, and every $z\in B(y,r)$ is joined to $y$ by the straight segment in the convex set $B(y,r)$, hence to $x$ by concatenation.

$U-C_{x}$ is open: if $z\in U-C_{x}$, choose a ball $B(z,r)\subset U$; no point $w$ of it lies in $C_{x}$, for then $z$ would be joined to $w$ inside $B(z,r)$ and hence to $x$, contradicting $z\notin C_{x}$.

So $C_{x}$ is a nonempty subset of the connected space $U$ that is both open and closed; hence $C_{x}=U$, and every point of $U$ is joined to $x$. As $x$ was arbitrary, $U$ is path connected.
:::
