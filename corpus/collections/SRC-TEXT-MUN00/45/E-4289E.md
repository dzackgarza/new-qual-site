::: {.exercise}

Prove the following.

Theorem (Arzela's theorem).
Let $X$ be compact; let $f_n \in \mathcal{C}(X, \RR^k)$.
If the collection $\theset{f_n}$ is pointwise bounded and equicontinuous, then the sequence $f_n$ has a uniformly convergent subsequence.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
$\overline{\theset{f_n}}$ is a compact subset of $\mathcal{C}(X, \RR^k)$ in the uniform metric.

::: pf-proof
$X$ is compact, the family $\theset{f_n}$ is equicontinuous by hypothesis and pointwise bounded by hypothesis, so Ascoli's theorem (Theorem 45.4) says that the closure of $\theset{f_n}$ in $\mathcal{C}(X, \RR^k)$ under the uniform metric is compact.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
A compact metric space is sequentially compact, so $(f_n)$ has a subsequence converging in the uniform metric.

::: pf-proof
A compact subset of a metric space is sequentially compact (Theorem 28.2), applied to the compact metric space $\overline{\theset{f_n}}$ with the uniform metric. The sequence $(f_n)$ lies in it, so some subsequence $(f_{n_i})$ converges to a point $f$ of $\overline{\theset{f_n}}$, that is, in the uniform metric; completeness of the ambient space is not used for this step, only compactness.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
That subsequence converges uniformly, and Arzelà's theorem follows. $\square$

::: pf-proof
Convergence in the uniform metric on $\mathcal{C}(X,\RR^k)$ is uniform convergence, so $(f_{n_i})$ converges uniformly on $X$; this is the subsequence the theorem asks for.
:::
