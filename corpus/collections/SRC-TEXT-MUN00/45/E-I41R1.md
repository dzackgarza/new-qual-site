::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be a space.
A subset $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{C}(X, \RR)$ is said to vanish uniformly at infinity if given $\epsilon > 0$, there is a compact subspace $C$ of $X$ such that $\abs{f(x)} < \epsilon$ for $x \in X - C$ and $f \in \mathcal{F}$.
If $\mathcal{F}$ consists of a single function $f$, we say simply that $f$ vanishes at infinity.
Let $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \RR)$ denote the set of continuous functions $f: X \to \RR$ that vanish at infinity.

Theorem.
Let $X$ be locally compact Hausdorff; give $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \RR)$ the uniform topology.
A subset $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \RR)$ has compact closure if and only if it is pointwise bounded, equicontinuous, and vanishes uniformly at infinity.

[Hint: Let $Y$ denote the one-point compactification of $X$. Show that $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \RR)$ is isometric with a closed subspace of $\mathcal{C}(Y, \RR)$ if both are given the sup metric.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $Y=X\cup\{\infty\}$ be the one-point compactification, which is compact Hausdorff because $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff (Theorem 29.1). For $f\in C_0(X,\RR)$ define
$$
Tf(x)=f(x)\ (x\in X),\qquad Tf(\infty)=0.
$$
The condition that $f$ vanish at infinity is exactly continuity of $Tf$ at $\infty$: a neighbourhood of $\infty$ in $Y$ is $Y-C$ for a compact $C\subset X$, so continuity at $\infty$ says that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a compact $C$ with $\abs{f(x)}<\varepsilon$ for $x\in X-C$, which is the definition in the exercise. Hence $T$ is an isometry from $C_0(X,\RR)$, with the sup metric, onto
$$
E=\{F\in C(Y,\RR):F(\infty)=0\}.
$$
An element of $C_0(X,\RR)$ is bounded, since it is bounded by $1$ off a compact set and continuous on it, so the sup metric is defined, and the sup over $Y$ of two elements of $E$ is their sup over $X$. Evaluation at $\infty$ is continuous on $C(Y,\RR)$ under the uniform metric, so $E$ is closed.

Apply Ascoli's theorem (Theorem 45.4) on the compact space $Y$ with the target $\RR$. For a family $\mathcal F\subset C_0(X,\RR)$, pointwise boundedness of $T\mathcal F$ on $Y$ is exactly pointwise boundedness on $X$ (all values at $\infty$ are $0$). Since $X$ is open in $Y$, equicontinuity of $T\mathcal F$ at points of $X$ is the stated equicontinuity. Equicontinuity at $\infty$ says: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a neighbourhood $Y-C$ of $\infty$, with $C\subset X$ compact, such that
$$
\abs{f(x)}=\abs{Tf(x)-Tf(\infty)}<\varepsilon
$$
for all $x\notin C$ and all $f\in\mathcal F$; this is precisely uniform vanishing at infinity. Therefore Ascoli on $Y$ says that $T\mathcal F$ has compact closure in $C(Y,\RR)$ iff the three stated conditions hold. Since $T$ is an isometry onto the closed subspace $E$, a subset $A$ of $E$ has compact closure in $E$ iff it has compact closure in $C(Y,\RR)$: the closure in the larger space meets $E$ in the closure in $E$, and that set is closed in $C(Y,\RR)$ because $E$ is. So $\overline{\mathcal F}$ is compact in $C_0(X,\RR)$ iff the three conditions hold.
