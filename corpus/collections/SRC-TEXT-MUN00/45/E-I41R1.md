::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be a space.
A subset $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{C}(X, \RR)$ is said to vanish uniformly at infinity if given $\epsilon > 0$, there is a compact subspace $C$ of $X$ such that $\abs{f(x)} < \epsilon$ for $x \in X - C$ and $f \in \mathcal{F}$.
If $\mathcal{F}$ consists of a single function $f$, we say simply that $f$ vanishes at infinity.
Let $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \RR)$ denote the set of continuous functions $f: X \to \RR$ that vanish at infinity.

Theorem.
Let $X$ be locally compact Hausdorff; give $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \RR)$ the uniform topology.
A subset $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \RR)$ has compact closure if and only if it is pointwise bounded, equicontinuous, and vanishes uniformly at infinity.

[Hint: Let $Y$ denote the one-point compactification of $X$. Show that $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \RR)$ is isometric with a closed subspace of $\mathcal{C}(Y, \RR)$ if both are given the sup metric.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own: no retained set proves this exercise. Math StackExchange question 2274433 with M A Pelto's answer 2277508 gives only the direction from compact closure to uniform vanishing at infinity, and the AoPS "Day 16" answer to §45 has its formulas lost in extraction, so neither supplies the proof below. What is used is Munkres: the one-point compactification and the description of its topology (Theorem 29.1, printed p. 183, Step 2), and Ascoli's theorem in its classical form for a compact domain (Theorem 45.4, printed p. 278).

Throughout, $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff, $Y=X\cup\{\infty\}$ is its one-point compactification, $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$ and $\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$ carry the sup metrics, and a family $\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$ is given.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #Y}
$Y$ is compact Hausdorff, $X$ is an open subspace of $Y$, and the neighbourhoods of $\infty$ in $Y$ are the sets containing $Y-C$ for some compact $C\subset X$.

::: pf-proof
Theorem 29.1 (printed p. 183) applies to a locally compact Hausdorff space $X$ and gives a space $Y$ with $X$ a subspace, $Y-X$ a single point, and $Y$ compact Hausdorff. In the construction of that theorem, Step 2, the open sets of $Y$ are the open sets of $X$ together with the sets $Y-C$ with $C$ compact in $X$; hence $X$ is open in $Y$, and a set containing $\infty$ is a neighbourhood of $\infty$ exactly when it contains such a $Y-C$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #T}
For $f\in\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$ the map $Tf$ equal to $f$ on $X$ and to $0$ at $\infty$ is continuous, so $T$ is a map $\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)\to\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$.

::: pf-proof
On $X$ the map $Tf$ agrees with $f$ and $X$ is open in $Y$ by step [](#Y){.pf-ref}, so $Tf$ is continuous at every point of $X$. At $\infty$ we use the description of the neighbourhoods there: $Tf$ is continuous at $\infty$ with value $0$ if and only if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a compact $C\subset X$ with $\abs{Tf(y)}<\varepsilon$ for $y\in Y-C$, that is with $\abs{f(x)}<\varepsilon$ for $x\in X-C$; and that is exactly the statement that $f$ vanishes at infinity, which holds because $f\in\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #isometry}
$T$ is a bijection of $\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$ onto $E=\{F\in\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR) \mid F(\infty)=0\}$ and preserves the sup metric, so $\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$ is isometric to $E$.

::: pf-proof
An element $f\in\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$ is bounded: vanishing at infinity gives a compact $C$ with $\abs{f}<1$ off $C$, and $f$ is continuous, hence bounded, on the compact set $C$. So the sup metric is defined on $\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$, and likewise on $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$ because $Y$ is compact (Theorem 45.4, printed p. 278). For $f,g\in\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$ the values of $Tf-Tg$ at $\infty$ are both $0$, so $\sup_Y\abs{Tf-Tg}=\sup_X\abs{f-g}$, which is the isometry. If $Tf=Tg$ then $f=g$ on $X$, so $T$ is injective. For surjectivity let $F\in E$ and put $f=F$ on $X$; then $f$ is continuous, and for $\varepsilon>0$ continuity of $F$ at $\infty$ with value $0$ gives a compact $C$ with $\abs{F}<\varepsilon$ on $Y-C$, so $f$ vanishes at infinity and $Tf=F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #closed}
$E$ is closed in $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$.

::: pf-proof
$E$ is the preimage of $\{0\}$ under evaluation at $\infty$, and evaluation $\mathrm{ev}_\infty:\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)\to\RR$ is continuous for the uniform topology because $\abs{F(\infty)-G(\infty)}\le\sup_Y\abs{F-G}$. A preimage of a closed set under a continuous map is closed.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conditions}
The family $\mathcal{F}$ is pointwise bounded, equicontinuous and vanishes uniformly at infinity if and only if $T\mathcal{F}$ is pointwise bounded and equicontinuous on $Y$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #bounded}
$T\mathcal{F}$ is pointwise bounded on $Y$ if and only if $\mathcal{F}$ is pointwise bounded on $X$.

::: pf-proof
Every element of $T\mathcal{F}$ takes the value $0$ at $\infty$, so a bound at $\infty$ is automatic, and at a point $x\in X$ the values of $T\mathcal{F}$ are those of $\mathcal{F}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equi-X}
$T\mathcal{F}$ is equicontinuous at each point of $X$ if and only if $\mathcal{F}$ is equicontinuous.

::: pf-proof
At $x\in X$ and $\varepsilon>0$, equicontinuity of $T\mathcal{F}$ asks for a neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ in $Y$ with $\abs{Tf(x)-Tf(y)}<\varepsilon$ for all $y\in V$ and all $f$. Since $X$ is open in $Y$, a neighbourhood of $x$ in $Y$ may be taken inside $X$, where $Tf$ agrees with $f$; so the condition is that for every $\varepsilon$ there is a neighbourhood of $x$ in $X$ on which all $f\in\mathcal{F}$ move by less than $\varepsilon$, which is equicontinuity of $\mathcal{F}$ at $x$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equi-inf}
$T\mathcal{F}$ is equicontinuous at $\infty$ if and only if $\mathcal{F}$ vanishes uniformly at infinity.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#Y){.pf-ref}, equicontinuity of $T\mathcal{F}$ at $\infty$ says that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a compact $C\subset X$ such that $\abs{Tf(y)-Tf(\infty)}=\abs{Tf(y)}<\varepsilon$ for all $y\in Y-C$ and all $f\in\mathcal{F}$. On $X$ this reads $\abs{f(x)}<\varepsilon$ for all $x\in X-C$ and all $f\in\mathcal{F}$, which is the definition of uniform vanishing at infinity; and the two statements are the same one, since $Tf(\infty)=0$ for every $f$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\mathcal{F}$ has compact closure in $\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$ if and only if it is pointwise bounded, equicontinuous and vanishes uniformly at infinity.

::: pf-proof
$Y$ is compact by step [](#Y){.pf-ref}, so Ascoli's theorem in its classical form (Theorem 45.4, printed p. 278, with $n=1$, the euclidean and square metrics giving the same topology on $\RR$) applies to subspaces of $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$ with the uniform topology: $T\mathcal{F}$ has compact closure in $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$ if and only if $T\mathcal{F}$ is equicontinuous and pointwise bounded, which by step [](#conditions){.pf-ref} is the three stated conditions on $\mathcal{F}$. It remains to pass between $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$ and its closed subspace $E$: for $A\subset E$ with $E$ closed, the closure of $A$ in $E$ is $E\cap\overline{A}$, and that set is closed in $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$; and a set is compact as a subspace of $E$ exactly when it is compact as a subspace of $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$, since compactness is tested by covers by open sets of the ambient space. So $T\mathcal{F}$ has compact closure in $E$ if and only if it has compact closure in $\mathcal{C}(Y,\RR)$, and by the isometry of step [](#isometry){.pf-ref} the same holds for $\mathcal{F}$ in $\mathcal{C}_0(X,\RR)$.
:::
