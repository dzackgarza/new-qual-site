::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be a space.
A subset $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{C}(X, \mathbb{R})$ is said to vanish uniformly at infinity if given $\epsilon > 0$, there is a compact subspace $C$ of $X$ such that $\abs{f(x)} < \epsilon$ for $x \in X - C$ and $f \in \mathcal{F}$.
If $\mathcal{F}$ consists of a single function $f$, we say simply that $f$ vanishes at infinity.
Let $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \mathbb{R})$ denote the set of continuous functions $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ that vanish at infinity.

Theorem.
Let $X$ be locally compact Hausdorff; give $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \mathbb{R})$ the uniform topology.
A subset $\mathcal{F}$ of $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \mathbb{R})$ has compact closure if and only if it is pointwise bounded, equicontinuous, and vanishes uniformly at infinity.

[Hint: Let $Y$ denote the one-point compactification of $X$. Show that $\mathcal{C}_0(X, \mathbb{R})$ is isometric with a closed subspace of $\mathcal{C}(Y, \mathbb{R})$ if both are given the sup metric.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $Y=X\cup\{\infty\}$ be the one-point compactification. For $f\in C_0(X,\mathbb R)$ define
\[
Tf(x)=f(x)\ (x\in X),\qquad Tf(\infty)=0.
\]
The condition that $f$ vanish at infinity is exactly continuity of $Tf$ at $\infty$. Hence $T$ is an isometry from $C_0(X,\mathbb R)$, with the sup metric, onto
\[
E=\{F\in C(Y,\mathbb R):F(\infty)=0\}.
\]
Evaluation at $\infty$ is continuous, so $E$ is closed in $C(Y,\mathbb R)$.

Apply Ascoli's theorem on the compact Hausdorff space $Y$. For a family $\mathcal F\subset C_0(X,\mathbb R)$, pointwise boundedness of $T\mathcal F$ on $Y$ is exactly pointwise boundedness on $X$ (all values at $\infty$ are $0$). Equicontinuity at points of $X$ is the stated equicontinuity. Equicontinuity at $\infty$ says: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a neighborhood $Y-C$ of $\infty$, with $C\subset X$ compact, such that
\[
|f(x)|=|Tf(x)-Tf(\infty)|<\varepsilon
\]
for all $x\notin C$ and all $f\in\mathcal F$; this is precisely uniform vanishing at infinity. Therefore Ascoli on $Y$ says that $T\mathcal F$ has compact closure iff the three stated conditions hold. Since $T$ is an isometry onto the closed subspace $E$, this is equivalent to compactness of $\overline{\mathcal F}$ in $C_0(X,\mathbb R)$.
:::
