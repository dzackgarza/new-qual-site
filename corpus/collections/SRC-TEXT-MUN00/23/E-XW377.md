::: {.exercise}

Let $Y \subset X$; let $X$ and $Y$ be connected.
Show that if $A$ and $B$ form a separation of $X - Y$, then $Y \cup A$ and $Y \cup B$ are connected.
:::

::: {.solution}

The strategy is **"positron0802"**, *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 23.12, PDF p. 3 (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); the openness and closedness of the one remaining piece in $X$ are written out below. The statement was checked against Munkres, *Topology* (2000), §23 Exercise 12, printed p. 152, with Lemma 23.2 on printed p. 149.

Throughout, $Y\subset X$ with $X$ and $Y$ connected, and $A,B$ form a separation of the subspace $X-Y$: they are disjoint, nonempty, open in $X-Y$, and $A\cup B=X-Y$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a-clopen}
$A$ is both open and closed in $X-Y$, and there are $U$ open in $X$ and $F$ closed in $X$ with $A=(X-Y)\cap U=(X-Y)\cap F$.

::: pf-proof
In a separation both members are open in the subspace; and a subset of $X-Y$ that is open in $X-Y$ is the intersection with $X-Y$ of an open set of $X$, and one that is closed in $X-Y$ is such an intersection with a closed set of $X$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #outside}
$U\subset A\cup Y$ and $F\subset A\cup Y$.

::: pf-proof
$X-Y$ and $Y$ partition $X$, so $A\cup Y=(X-Y)\cup Y$ contains $U\cap(X-Y)=A$ together with all of $Y$, that is $A\cup Y$; and $U=(U\cap(X-Y))\cup(U\cap Y)\subset A\cup Y$. The same computation with $F$ in place of $U$ gives $F\subset A\cup Y$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #suppose}
Suppose that $Y\cup A=C\cup D$ is a separation of $Y\cup A$. Then, after relabelling, $Y\subset C$ and $D\subset A$.

::: pf-proof
$Y$ is a connected subspace of $Y\cup A$ and $C,D$ form a separation of $Y\cup A$, so Lemma 23.2 (printed p. 149) puts $Y$ inside $C$ or inside $D$; exchange the names $C,D$ if necessary. Then $D\subset(Y\cup A)-Y\subset A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #D-clopen-in-X}
$D$ is a nonempty subset of $X$ that is both open and closed in $X$, and $D\neq X$.

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #D-open}
$D$ is open in $X$.

::: pf-proof
$D$ is open in $Y\cup A$, so $D=(Y\cup A)\cap G$ for some $G$ open in $X$. By step [](#suppose){.pf-ref} $D\subset A\subset U$ with $U$ from step [](#a-clopen){.pf-ref}, so $D\subset G\cap U$; conversely if $z\in G\cap U$ then step [](#outside){.pf-ref} gives $z\in U\subset A\cup Y$, so $z\in(Y\cup A)\cap G=D$. Hence $D=G\cap U$, an intersection of open sets of $X$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #D-closed}
$D$ is closed in $X$.

::: pf-proof
$D$ is closed in $Y\cup A$, so $D=(Y\cup A)\cap F'$ for some $F'$ closed in $X$. By step [](#suppose){.pf-ref} $D\subset A\subset F$ with $F$ from step [](#a-clopen){.pf-ref}, so $D\subset F'\cap F$; conversely if $z\in F'\cap F$ then step [](#outside){.pf-ref} gives $z\in F\subset A\cup Y$, so $z\in D$. Hence $D=F'\cap F$, an intersection of closed sets of $X$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #D-proper}
$D\neq\varnothing$ and $D\neq X$.

::: pf-proof
$D$ is a member of a separation, hence nonempty, and $C$ is nonempty with $C\cap D=\varnothing$, so $D$ omits the points of $C$.
:::

:::

::::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore $Y\cup A$ and, symmetrically, $Y\cup B$ are connected.

::: pf-proof
If $Y\cup A$ admitted a separation, step [](#D-clopen-in-X){.pf-ref} would exhibit $D$ as a nonempty proper subset of $X$ that is both open and closed, equivalently a separation $X=D\cup(X-D)$ of $X$, contradicting the connectedness of $X$. So $Y\cup A$ is connected, and the same argument with $A$ and $B$ interchanged gives $Y\cup B$.
:::
