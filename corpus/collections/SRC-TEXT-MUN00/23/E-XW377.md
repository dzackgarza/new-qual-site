::: {.exercise}

Let $Y \subset X$; let $X$ and $Y$ be connected.
Show that if $A$ and $B$ form a separation of $X - Y$, then $Y \cup A$ and $Y \cup B$ are connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
Because $A$ and $B$ form a separation of $X-Y$, they are both open and closed in the subspace $X-Y$. Hence
\[
\overline A^{\,X}\cap B=\varnothing,
\qquad
\overline B^{\,X}\cap A=\varnothing.
\tag{1}
\]

We prove $Y\cup A$ is connected. Suppose instead that
\[
Y\cup A=P\cup Q
\]
is a separation. Since $Y$ is connected, it lies in one side, say $Y\subseteq P$. Then $Q\subseteq A$.

The set $Q$ is closed in $Y\cup A$, so
\[
\overline Q^{\,X}\cap(Y\cup A)\subseteq Q.
\]
Also $Q\subseteq A$, so by (1), $\overline Q^{\,X}\cap B=\varnothing$. Since
\[
X=(Y\cup A)\cup B,
\]
it follows that $\overline Q^{\,X}\subseteq Q$; hence $Q$ is closed in $X$.

Similarly, $P$ is closed in $Y\cup A$, while (1) gives $\overline B^{\,X}\cap A=\varnothing$. Therefore
\[
\overline{P\cup B}^{\,X}\subseteq P\cup B,
\]
so $P\cup B$ is closed in $X$. Thus $Q=X-(P\cup B)$ is also open in $X$.

Hence $Q$ is a nonempty proper clopen subset of connected $X$, a contradiction. So $Y\cup A$ is connected. Interchanging $A$ and $B$ proves that $Y\cup B$ is connected as well.
:::
