::: {.exercise}

Let $Y \subset X$; let $X$ and $Y$ be connected.
Show that if $A$ and $B$ form a separation of $X - Y$, then $Y \cup A$ and $Y \cup B$ are connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 23.12, PDF p. 3** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); the strategy is theirs, the last step written out.

Let $Y\subset X$ with $X$ and $Y$ connected, and let $A,B$ be a separation of $X-Y$ (so $A,B$ are disjoint nonempty open subsets of the subspace $X-Y$ with union $X-Y$). We show $Y\cup A$ is connected; the other case is symmetric.

Suppose $Y\cup A=C\cup D$ is a separation. $Y$ is connected and contained in $C\cup D$, so $Y\subset C$ or $Y\subset D$; say $Y\subset C$. Then $D\subset A$.

Since $A$ is open and closed in $X-Y$, there are $U$ open and $F$ closed in $X$ with $A=(X-Y)\cap U=U-Y$ and $A=(X-Y)\cap F=F-Y$; as $A\subset U$ and $A\subset F$ we get $U\subset A\cup Y$ and $F\subset A\cup Y$.

$D$ is open in $Y\cup A$, so $D=(Y\cup A)\cap G$ with $G$ open in $X$; then
$$
D=(Y\cup A)\cap(G\cap U)=G\cap U,
$$
the first equality because $D\subset A\subset U$ and $(Y\cup A)\cap G=D$, so $D$ is open in $X$. Similarly $D=(Y\cup A)\cap(G\cap F)=G\cap F$ is closed in $X$, using $D\subset A\subset F$ and $F\subset A\cup Y$.

So $D$ is a nonempty subset of $X$, open and closed in $X$, and $D\ne X$ because $C\ne\varnothing$ and $C\cap D=\varnothing$. This contradicts the connectedness of $X$. Hence $Y\cup A$ is connected.
:::
