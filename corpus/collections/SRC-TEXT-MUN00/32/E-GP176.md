::: {.exercise}

Is $\RR^\omega$ normal in the product topology?
In the uniform topology?

It is not known whether $\RR^\omega$ is normal in the box topology.
Mary-Ellen Rudin has shown that the answer is affirmative if one assumes the continuum hypothesis [RM]. In fact, she shows it satisfies a stronger condition called paracompactness.
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this exercise: the card's own argument follows, with the two metric statements cited where the book supplies them.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #product-metrizable}
$\RR^{\omega}$ in the product topology is metrizable.

::: pf-proof
The function $D(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sup_{n\ge1}\bar{d}(x_{n},y_{n})/n$, with $\bar{d}(a,b)=\min\theset{\abs{a-b},1}$, is a metric on $\RR^{\omega}$ and induces the product topology (Theorem 20.5, printed p. 125).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #uniform-metrizable}
$\RR^{\omega}$ in the uniform topology is metrizable.

::: pf-proof
The uniform topology is by definition the topology induced by the uniform metric $\bar\rho(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sup_{n}\bar{d}(x_{n},y_{n})$ (§20, printed p. 122).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #normal}
Every metrizable space is normal.

::: pf-proof
This is Theorem 32.2 (printed p. 202).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
By steps [](#product-metrizable){.pf-ref} and [](#uniform-metrizable){.pf-ref} both topologies on $\RR^{\omega}$ are metrizable, and by step [](#normal){.pf-ref} both are therefore normal. The box topology is not decided here: as the exercise records, its normality is independent of ZFC modulo Mary-Ellen Rudin's result under the continuum hypothesis.
:::
