::: {.exercise}

Show that the following are directed sets:

(a) Any nonempty simply ordered set, under the relation $\leq$.

(b) The collection of all subsets of a set $S$, partially ordered by inclusion (that is, $A \preceq B$ if $A \subset B$).

(c) A nonempty collection $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $S$ that is closed under finite intersections, partially ordered by reverse inclusion (that is, $A \preceq B$ if $A \supset B$).

(d) The collection of all closed subsets of a space $X$, partially ordered by inclusion.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
(a) A nonempty simply ordered set $J$ is a directed set under $\leq$.

::: pf-proof
The order of a simply ordered set is a partial order: it is reflexive, transitive, and $a \le b$ with $b \le a$ forces $a = b$. Given $a, b \in J$, simplicity of the order gives $a \le b$ or $b \le a$; in the first case $b$ satisfies $a \le b$ and $b \le b$, and in the second $a$ does, so each pair has an upper bound in $J$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
(b) The collection of all subsets of $S$, partially ordered by inclusion, is a directed set.

::: pf-proof
Inclusion is a partial order on any collection of sets. For subsets $A, B$ of $S$ the union $A \cup B$ is a subset of $S$ with $A \subset A \cup B$ and $B \subset A \cup B$, so it is an upper bound of the pair.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
(c) A nonempty collection $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $S$, closed under finite intersections and partially ordered by reverse inclusion, is a directed set.

::: pf-proof
Reverse inclusion is a partial order because inclusion is: $A \supset A$, and $A \supset B$ with $B \supset A$ gives $A = B$, and $A \supset B$ with $B \supset C$ gives $A \supset C$. For $A, B \in \mathcal{A}$ the intersection $A \cap B$ is the intersection of a finite nonempty subcollection, hence lies in $\mathcal{A}$, and $A \supset A \cap B$ and $B \supset A \cap B$; so $A \preceq A \cap B$ and $B \preceq A \cap B$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
(d) The collection of all closed subsets of a space $X$, partially ordered by inclusion, is a directed set.

::: pf-proof
Inclusion is a partial order. For closed $A, B \subset X$ the union $A \cup B$ is closed, being a finite union of closed sets, and it contains both $A$ and $B$; so $A \cup B$ is an upper bound of the pair.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The four collections of (a)--(d) are directed sets. $\square$

::: pf-proof
Steps 1--4 verify, for each of the four, the two things the definition asks: that the relation is a partial order and that each pair of elements has an upper bound in the collection.
:::
