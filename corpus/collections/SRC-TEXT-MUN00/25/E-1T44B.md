::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be a space.
Let us define $x \sim y$ if there is no separation $X = A \cup B$ of $X$ into disjoint open sets such that $x \in A$ and $y \in B$.

(a) Show this relation is an equivalence relation.
The equivalence classes are called the quasicomponents of $X$.

(b) Show that each component of $X$ lies in a quasicomponent of $X$, and that the components and quasicomponents of $X$ are the same if $X$ is locally connected.

(c) Let $K$ denote the set $\theset{1/n \mid n \in \ZZ_+}$ and let $-K$ denote the set $\theset{-1/n \mid n \in \ZZ_+}$.
Determine the components, path components, and quasicomponents of the following subspaces of $\RR^2$:

$$
A = (K \times [0, 1]) \cup \theset{0 \times 0} \cup \theset{0 \times 1},
$$

$$
B = A \cup ([0, 1] \times \theset{0}),
$$

$$
C = (K \times [0, 1]) \cup (-K \times [-1, 0]) \cup ([0, 1] \times -K) \cup ([-1, 0] \times K).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 25.10, PDF pp. 10--12** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); all three parts are theirs.

(a) Write $x\sim y$ when no separation $X=A\cup B$ has $x\in A$, $y\in B$. The relation is reflexive and symmetric (interchange the two sides); if $x\sim y$ and $y\sim z$ but not $x\sim z$, a separation with $x\in A$, $z\in B$ puts $y$ in $A$, contradicting $y\sim z$, or in $B$, contradicting $x\sim y$. So $\sim$ is an equivalence relation, and its classes are the quasicomponents.

(b) If $C$ is a component and $x,y\in C$ with $x\not\sim y$, a separation $X=A\cup B$ with $x\in A$, $y\in B$ would separate $C$ into $C\cap A$ and $C\cap B$. So each component lies in a quasicomponent. If $X$ is locally connected then components are open (Theorem 25.3) and closed, so for a quasicomponent $Q$ and $x\in Q$ with component $C\ni x$, the sets $C$ and $X-C$ would separate $x$ from any $y\notin C$, so $Q\subset C$; with $C\subset Q$ from above, $Q=C$. Hence components and quasicomponents coincide.

(c) With $K=\{1/n\}$ and $-K=\{-1/n\}$:

- $A=(K\times[0,1])\cup\{0\times0\}\cup\{0\times1\}$: the components and path components are $\{0\times0\}$, $\{0\times1\}$ and the segments $V_{n}=\{1/n\}\times[0,1]$; the quasicomponents are the $V_{n}$ and the two-point set $\{0\times0,\,0\times1\}$.
- $B=A\cup([0,1]\times\{0\})$: one component and one quasicomponent, namely $B$; the path components are $B-\{0\times1\}$ and $\{0\times1\}$.
- $C=(K\times[0,1])\cup(-K\times[-1,0])\cup([0,1]\times-K)\cup([-1,0]\times K)$: one component and one quasicomponent, namely $C$; the path components are the segments $U_{n}=[-1,0]\times\{1/n\}$, $V_{n}=\{1/n\}\times[0,1]$, $W_{n}=[0,1]\times\{-1/n\}$ and $Z_{n}=\{-1/n\}\times[-1,0]$.

The arguments are as follows. In $A$, a vertical line $x=t$ with $1/(m+1)<t<1/m$ separates the segments $V_{n}$ with $n\le m$ from those with $n>m$ inside $A$ by two disjoint open sets, but no such vertical line separates $0\times0$ from $0\times1$, since any separation of $A$ one side of which contains $0\times0$ and the other $0\times1$ would have to cut some segment $V_{n}$ (that segment being connected and meeting both sides), and the pieces would give a separation of $V_{n}$. In $B$, the set $B-\{0\times1\}$ is path connected — it is the union of the path-connected sets $V_{n}\cup([0,1]\times\{0\})$ sharing $0\times0$ — and its closure is $B$, so $B$ is connected; but $0\times1$ cannot be joined to $0\times0$ by a path, for such a path $f$ would have a first time $t_{0}$ at which its first coordinate reaches $1/2$, and the restriction of $f$ to $[t_{0},1]$ would join $f(t_{0})$ to $0\times1$ inside $B\cap(\RR\times[1/2,1])-\{0\times1\}\subset A-\{0\times1\}$, in which $0\times1$ is a path component of $A$. In $C$ the same "cut by a vertical or horizontal line" argument separates the individual segments, while any separation of $C$ would have to split some segment, since the segments accumulate at the origin along each axis.
