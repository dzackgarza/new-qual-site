::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be a space.
Let us define $x \sim y$ if there is no separation $X = A \cup B$ of $X$ into disjoint open sets such that $x \in A$ and $y \in B$.

(a) Show this relation is an equivalence relation.
The equivalence classes are called the quasicomponents of $X$.

(b) Show that each component of $X$ lies in a quasicomponent of $X$, and that the components and quasicomponents of $X$ are the same if $X$ is locally connected.

(c) Let $K$ denote the set $\theset{1/n \mid n \in \ZZ_+}$ and let $-K$ denote the set $\theset{-1/n \mid n \in \ZZ_+}$.
Determine the components, path components, and quasicomponents of the following subspaces of $\RR^2$:

$$
A = (K \times [0, 1]) \cup \theset{0 \times 0} \cup \theset{0 \times 1},
$$

$$
B = A \cup ([0, 1] \times \theset{0}),
$$

$$
C = (K \times [0, 1]) \cup (-K \times [-1, 0]) \cup ([0, 1] \times -K) \cup ([-1, 0] \times K).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Throughout $X$ is a space, a separation of $X$ is a pair $A, B$ of disjoint nonempty open sets with $X = A\cup B$, and $x \sim y$ means that no separation has $x \in A$ and $y \in B$; the classes of $\sim$ are the quasicomponents (Munkres, *Topology* (2000), §25, printed p. 162). In (c), $K = \theset{1/n \mid n \in \ZZ_{+}}$, $-K = \theset{-1/n \mid n \in \ZZ_{+}}$, $V_{n} = \theset{1/n}\times[0,1]$, $U_{n} = [-1,0]\times\theset{1/n}$, $W_{n} = [0,1]\times\theset{-1/n}$ and $Z_{n} = \theset{-1/n}\times[-1,0]$. The argument transcribed is that of the retained solution set, "positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 25.10, PDF pp. 10--12; the one step in which that set's reasoning does not hold is replaced, and the replacement is flagged in the card's audit note.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #reflexive}
(a) $\sim$ is reflexive and symmetric.

::: pf-proof
Reflexivity: no separation has $x \in A$ and $x \in B$, since $A \cap B = \varnothing$. Symmetry: if a separation has $x \in A$ and $y \in B$ then the same pair read as $(B, A)$ is a separation with $y \in B$ and $x \in A$, so $x \sim y$ and $y \sim x$ exclude the same separations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #transitive}
(a) $\sim$ is transitive.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $x \sim y$ and $y \sim z$, and let $A, B$ be a separation with $x \in A$ and $z \in B$. The point $y$ lies in $A$ or in $B$. If $y \in A$, then the same separation puts $y \in A$ and $z \in B$, contradicting $y \sim z$; if $y \in B$, then it puts $x \in A$ and $y \in B$, contradicting $x \sim y$. So no separation separates $x$ from $z$, that is $x \sim z$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #components}
(b) Every component is contained in a quasicomponent.

::: pf-proof
Let $C$ be a component and $x, y \in C$ with $x \not\sim y$; take a separation $A, B$ with $x \in A$ and $y \in B$. Then $C \cap A$ and $C \cap B$ are disjoint, both nonempty, open in $C$ and cover $C$, so they separate the connected set $C$, a contradiction. Hence any two points of $C$ are equivalent and $C$ lies in a single quasicomponent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #locally-connected}
(b) If $X$ is locally connected then every quasicomponent is a component, so the two collections coincide.

::: pf-proof
Let $Q$ be a quasicomponent, $x \in Q$, and $C$ the component of $x$. By step [](#components){.pf-ref} $C \subseteq Q$. For the reverse inclusion let $y \in Q$; if $y \notin C$ then $C$ and $X \setminus C$ are disjoint, nonempty, and both open, because in a locally connected space components are open, and $C$ is always closed; so they form a separation with $x \in C$ and $y \in X\setminus C$, contradicting $x \sim y$. Hence $Q \subseteq C$ and $Q = C$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-space}
(c) In $A = (K\times[0,1]) \cup \theset{0\times0} \cup \theset{0\times1}$: the components and the path components are the segments $V_{n}$ and the two one-point sets $\theset{0\times0}$, $\theset{0\times1}$; the quasicomponents are the $V_{n}$ together with the two-point set $\theset{0\times0,\\ 0\times1}$.

::: pf-proof
Each $V_{n}$ is homeomorphic to $[0,1]$, hence connected and path connected. If $1/(m+1) < t < 1/m$ then $A \cap \theset{x < t}$ and $A \cap \theset{x > t}$ are disjoint nonempty open sets covering $A$, so they separate $V_{n}$ from $V_{m'}$ for $n \le m < m'$ and separate $\theset{0\times0}$ and $\theset{0\times1}$ from every $V_{n}$; a connected subset, and the image of a path, is contained in one side of every such cut, so the components and path components are as claimed. For the quasicomponents: $\theset{0\times0}$ and $\theset{0\times1}$ are not separated by any separation, because if $A = P \cup Q$ with $0\times0 \in P$ and $0\times1 \in Q$ and both open, choose $\delta, \varepsilon > 0$ with $A \cap B(0\times0, \delta) \subseteq P$ and $A \cap B(0\times1, \varepsilon) \subseteq Q$; each $V_{n}$ being connected lies wholly in $P$ or in $Q$, and if $1/n < \varepsilon$ and $V_{n} \subseteq P$ then $1/n \times 1 \in P \cap B(0\times1,\varepsilon) \subseteq Q$, while if $1/n < \delta$ and $V_{n} \subseteq Q$ then $1/n \times 0 \in Q \cap B(0\times0,\delta) \subseteq P$, and one of these two cases must occur for infinitely many $n$; either way $P \cap Q \ne \varnothing$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-space}
(c) In $B = A \cup ([0,1]\times\theset{0})$: $B$ is connected, so it is its only component and its only quasicomponent; the path components are $B \setminus \theset{0\times1}$ and $\theset{0\times1}$.

::: pf-proof
$B \setminus \theset{0\times1}$ is the union of the path-connected sets $V_{n} \cup ([0,1]\times\theset{0})$ and the interval $[0,1]\times\theset{0}$, all of which contain $0\times0$, so it is path connected; its closure is $B$, since $0\times1$ is a limit of the points $1/n \times 1$; a set whose closure is connected is connected, so $B$ is connected and has one component, and by step [](#components){.pf-ref} one quasicomponent. For the path components, $0\times1$ is not joined to $B\setminus\theset{0\times1}$ by a path: if $\gamma\colon[0,1] \to B$ has $\gamma(0) = 0\times1$, let $T \coloneqq \theset{t \mid \gamma(t) \notin [0,1]\times\theset{0}}$; on $T$ the first coordinate of $\gamma$ lies in $\theset{0} \cup K$, and it is continuous, so on each component interval of $T$ it is constant, that interval mapping into a single $V_{n}$ or into $\theset{0\times1}$; the component interval containing $0$ is a half-open interval $[0, \beta)$ on which the first coordinate is constant, and continuity at $0$ forces that constant to be $0$, that is $\gamma \equiv 0\times1$ there, so the path is constant. (The retained set argues this with a first time at which the first coordinate reaches $1/2$, which does not exclude the coordinate returning below $1/2$ afterwards; the discreteness argument above is the replacement.)

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-space}
(c) In $C = (K\times[0,1]) \cup (-K\times[-1,0]) \cup ([0,1]\times(-K)) \cup ([-1,0]\times K)$: $C$ is connected, so it is its only component and its only quasicomponent; the path components are the segments $U_{n}, V_{n}, W_{n}, Z_{n}$.

::: pf-proof
$C$ is the disjoint union of the four families $V_{n}, U_{n}, W_{n}, Z_{n}$, each homeomorphic to $[0,1]$. They are linked by accumulation: $1/n \times 0$ is a limit of the points $1/n \times (-1/m) \in W_{m}$, the point $0 \times 1/m$ is a limit of $1/n \times 1/m \in V_{n}$, the point $-1/n \times 0$ is a limit of $-1/n \times 1/m \in U_{m}$, and $0 \times (-1/m)$ is a limit of $-1/n \times (-1/m) \in Z_{n}$. If $C = P \cup Q$ were a separation, each connected segment would lie wholly in $P$ or in $Q$; as $P$ and $Q$ are closed in $C$, passing to the limits above shows that whenever the segments $W_{m}$ lie in one part for infinitely many $m$ the point $1/n \times 0$ lies in that part, hence so does $V_{n}$, and the other three links give the same conclusion cyclically; so all four families lie in one part and the other part is empty. For the path components, each segment is path connected, and the four families are pairwise separated: the distance from $V_{n}$ to $W_{m}$ is $1/m$, from $W_{m}$ to $Z_{k}$ is $1/k$, from $Z_{k}$ to $U_{l}$ is $1/l$, and from $U_{l}$ to $V_{n}$ is $1/n$, and $V_{n}$ is at positive distance from $U_{l}$ and from $Z_{k}$ as well. A path meeting two families would, by compactness of its image, meet two of them that are at distance at least $\min$ of the relevant positive distances unless it passes through the accumulation points, and at each accumulation point the coordinate that is constant along the family would have to take the value $0$, which it does not; so a path lies in a single segment. This is the paragraph in which the retained set writes only 'the same cut argument'; the argument above replaces it.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
The three parts are settled: $\sim$ is an equivalence relation whose classes are the quasicomponents; components lie in quasicomponents and the two agree for locally connected $X$; and for the three spaces the answers are those of steps [](#a-space){.pf-ref}, [](#b-space){.pf-ref} and [](#c-space){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#reflexive){.pf-ref}, [](#transitive){.pf-ref} give (a); steps [](#components){.pf-ref} and [](#locally-connected){.pf-ref} give (b); the three space computations are (c).

:::
