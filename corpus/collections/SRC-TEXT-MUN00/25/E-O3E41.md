::: {.exercise}

A space $X$ is said to be weakly locally connected at $x$ if for every neighborhood $U$ of $x$, there is a connected subspace of $X$ contained in $U$ that contains a neighborhood of $x$.
Show that if $X$ is weakly locally connected at each of its points, then $X$ is locally connected.
[Hint: Show that components of open sets are open.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 25.6, PDF p. 9** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`); the argument follows the book's hint as they use it. One slip in the printed conclusion is corrected: the last sentence should say that $X$ is locally connected.

Let $U$ be open in $X$ and $C$ a component of $U$. For $x\in C$ weak local connectedness gives a connected subspace $C_{x}\subset U$ containing a neighbourhood $V_{x}$ of $x$. Since $C_{x}$ is connected and meets $C$, it lies in the component $C$ of $U$ (Lemma 25.1); hence $x\in V_{x}\subset C_{x}\subset C$. So $C=\bigcup_{x\in C}V_{x}$ is open. As $U$ and then $C$ were arbitrary, every component of every open set is open, and by Theorem 25.3 $X$ is locally connected.
:::
