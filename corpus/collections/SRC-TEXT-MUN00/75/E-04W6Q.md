::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be the quotient space obtained from an 8-sided polygonal region $P$ by means of the labelling scheme $abcdad^{-1}cb^{-1}$.
Let $\pi: P \to X$ be the quotient map.

(a) Show that $\pi$ does not map all the vertices of $P$ to the same point of $X$.

(b) Determine the space $A = \pi(\operatorname{Bd} P)$ and calculate its fundamental group.

(c) Calculate $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ and $H_1(X)$.

(d) Assuming $X$ is homeomorphic to one of the surfaces given in Theorem 75.5, which surface is it?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Analyze the quotient surface $X$ determined by the boundary word $w = abcdad^{-1}cb^{-1}$ on an octagon $P$.

<1>1. Part (a): Vertex equivalence classes under $\pi$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Label the vertices of the octagon $P$ in cyclic order as $v_0, v_1, \dots, v_7$, with directed edges $v_0 \xrightarrow{a} v_1$, $v_1 \xrightarrow{b} v_2$, $v_2 \xrightarrow{c} v_3$, $v_3 \xrightarrow{d} v_4$, $v_4 \xrightarrow{a} v_5$, $v_6 \xrightarrow{d} v_5$, $v_6 \xrightarrow{c} v_7$, $v_0 \xrightarrow{b} v_7$.
    <2>2. The identifications induced by the edge labels are:
        - Label $a$: $v_0 \sim v_4$ and $v_1 \sim v_5$.
        - Label $b$: $v_1 \sim v_0$ and $v_2 \sim v_7$.
        - Label $c$: $v_2 \sim v_6$ and $v_3 \sim v_7$.
        - Label $d$: $v_3 \sim v_6$ and $v_4 \sim v_5$.
    <2>3. Tracing equivalence classes yields:
        - Class 1: $\{v_0, v_1, v_4, v_5\}$ (since $v_0 \sim v_1 \sim v_5 \sim v_4$).
        - Class 2: $\{v_2, v_3, v_6, v_7\}$ (since $v_2 \sim v_7 \sim v_3 \sim v_6$).
    <2>4. There are exactly 2 equivalence classes. Thus $\pi$ maps the vertices of $P$ to two distinct points $P_1, P_2 \in X$.

<1>2. Part (b): Topology of the 1-skeleton $A = \pi(\operatorname{Bd} P)$ and $\pi_1(A)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The 1-complex $A$ has 2 vertices $P_1, P_2$ and 4 directed edges $a, b, c, d$.
    <2>2. $a$ is a self-loop at $P_1$; $c$ is a self-loop at $P_2$; $b$ is an edge from $P_1$ to $P_2$; $d$ is an edge from $P_2$ to $P_1$.
    <2>3. Choosing the spanning tree $T = \{b\}$ and collapsing it to a point yields a wedge sum of $4 - 1 = 3$ circles: $A \simeq S^1 \vee S^1 \vee S^1$.
    <2>4. Therefore $\pi_1(A, P_1) \cong F_3$, the free group of rank 3, with basis $\alpha = a$, $\beta = bcb^{-1}$, and $\gamma = bd$.

<1>3. Part (c): Computation of $\pi_1(X)$ and $H_1(X)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $X$ is obtained from $A$ by attaching a 2-cell along the boundary word $w = abcdad^{-1}cb^{-1}$.
    <2>2. Rewriting $w$ in terms of the generators of $\pi_1(A, P_1)$ based at $P_1$:
        $$w = a \cdot (bcb^{-1}) \cdot (bd) \cdot a \cdot (d^{-1}b^{-1}) \cdot (bcb^{-1}) = \alpha \beta \gamma \alpha \gamma^{-1} \beta.$$
    <2>3. By Seifert-van Kampen theorem,
        $$\pi_1(X, P_1) \cong \langle \alpha, \beta, \gamma \mid \alpha \beta \gamma \alpha \gamma^{-1} \beta = 1 \rangle.$$
    <2>4. Abelianizing gives $H_1(X) \cong \pi_1(X)_{\text{ab}}$: in additive notation,
        $$\alpha + \beta + \gamma + \alpha - \gamma + \beta = 2\alpha + 2\beta = 2(\alpha + \beta) = 0.$$
    <2>5. Setting $u = \alpha + \beta$, the abelian group presentation is $\langle u, \alpha, \gamma \mid 2u = 0 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}$.
    <2>6. Thus $H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_2$.

<1>4. Part (d): Surface classification.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $X$ is a compact, connected 2-manifold without boundary.
    <2>2. The Euler characteristic is $\chi(X) = V - E + F = 2 - 4 + 1 = -1$.
    <2>3. Since $H_1(X)$ contains non-trivial 2-torsion $\mathbb{Z}_2$, $X$ is non-orientable.
    <2>4. The non-orientable surface of genus $k$, $N_k = \#^k \mathbb{R}P^2$, has Euler characteristic $\chi(N_k) = 2 - k$.
    <2>5. Setting $2 - k = -1$ gives $k = 3$.
    <2>6. Therefore, $X$ is homeomorphic to the connected sum of three projective planes:
        $$X \cong \mathbb{R}P^2 \# \mathbb{R}P^2 \# \mathbb{R}P^2 \quad (\text{or } K \# \mathbb{R}P^2).$$
    Q.E.D.
