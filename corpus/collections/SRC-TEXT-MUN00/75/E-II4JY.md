::: {.exercise}

If $K$ is the Klein bottle, calculate $H_1(K)$ directly.
:::

::: {.solution}

The square model of $K$ carries the labelling scheme $aba^{-1}b$, and the pasting map identifies its four vertices: the two $a$-edges join $v_0$ to $v_1$ and $v_2$ to $v_3$, the two $b$-edges join $v_1$ to $v_2$ and $v_3$ to $v_0$, so all four are one class. Theorem 74.2 (printed p. 451) therefore gives
$$
\pi_1(K)\cong\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b=1\rangle .
$$
By Corollary 75.2 (printed p. 456), with $F$ free on $a,b$ and $x=aba^{-1}b$, the group $H_1(K)=\pi_1/[\pi_1,\pi_1]$ is the quotient of the free abelian group on $[a],[b]$ by the subgroup generated by the image of $x$; written additively that image is
$$
[a]+[b]-[a]+[b]=2[b].
$$
Hence
$$
H_1(K)\cong\bigl(\ZZ[a]\oplus\ZZ[b]\bigr)/\langle 2[b]\rangle\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2 .
$$

A second computation of the same group, in a different model, is that of Paul VanKoughnett, answer 7339 (https://math.stackexchange.com/a/7339), posted 2010-10-20 to Math StackExchange question 7334, "Homology of the Klein bottle" (https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/7334, asked by NebulousReveal on 2010-10-20). Take a $\Delta$-complex model with one vertex, edges $a$, $b$, $c$ and triangles $U$, $L$; then the $1$-cycles are free abelian on $a,b,c$, and $\partial U=a+b-c$, $\partial L=c+a-b$, so
$$
H_1=\langle a,b,c\rangle/\langle a+b-c,\ a-b+c\rangle
=\langle b,c\rangle/\langle 2b-2c\rangle\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2 .
$$
That answer computes the singular homology of a $\Delta$-complex, an invariant the book does not develop; its agreement with the $H_1$ of §75 is the Hurewicz theorem, which the book states without proof (printed p. 455). The computation above uses only Theorem 74.2 and Corollary 75.2.
:::
