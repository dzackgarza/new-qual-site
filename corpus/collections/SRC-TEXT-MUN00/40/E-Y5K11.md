::: {.exercise}

Many spaces have countable bases; but no $T_1$ space has a locally finite basis unless it is discrete.
Prove this fact.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X$ be a $T_1$ space with a locally finite basis $\mathcal B$, and fix $x \in X$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #finitely-many-basis-elements}
Only finitely many members $B_1, \ldots, B_k$ of $\mathcal B$ contain $x$, and $k \ge 1$.

::: pf-proof
By local finiteness, some neighborhood $U$ of $x$ meets only finitely many members of $\mathcal B$. Every member of $\mathcal B$ that contains $x$ meets $U$. Since $\mathcal B$ is a basis and $X$ is open, some member of $\mathcal B$ contains $x$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #each-bi-excludes-some-y}
For each $y \neq x$, some $B_i$ does not contain $y$.

::: pf-proof
Since $X$ is $T_1$, $\{y\}$ is closed, so $X \setminus \{y\}$ is an open set containing $x$. It contains a member $B$ of $\mathcal B$ with $x \in B$, and $B$ is one of $B_1, \ldots, B_k$ by step [](#finitely-many-basis-elements){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #singleton-is-intersection}
$\{x\} = B_1 \cap \cdots \cap B_k$, so $\{x\}$ is open.

::: pf-proof
Each $B_i$ contains $x$, and by step [](#each-bi-excludes-some-y){.pf-ref} no $y \neq x$ lies in every $B_i$. A finite intersection of open sets is open.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
By step [](#singleton-is-intersection){.pf-ref}, every singleton of $X$ is open, so $X$ is discrete.
:::
