::: {.exercise}

Many spaces have countable bases; but no $T_1$ space has a locally finite basis unless it is discrete.
Prove this fact.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $X$ be a $T_1$ space with a locally finite basis $\mathcal B$, and let $x \in X$.
::: {.proof}
take an arbitrary point.
:::

<1>2. Since $\mathcal B$ is locally finite, there is a neighborhood $U$ of $x$ meeting only finitely many members of $\mathcal B$.
::: {.proof}
definition of local finiteness.
:::

<1>3. Let $B_1, \ldots, B_k$ be the finitely many basis elements meeting $U$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2.
:::

<1>4. For each $y \neq x$, since $X$ is $T_1$, there is a basis element $B_y \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B_y$ and $y \notin B_y$.
::: {.proof}
in a $T_1$ space, $\{x\}$ is closed, so $X \setminus \{y\}$ is an open neighborhood of $x$; it contains a basis element $B_y$ with $x \in B_y \subseteq X \setminus \{y\}$.
:::

<1>5. Each such $B_y$ meets $U$ (since $x \in B_y \cap U$), so $B_y \in \{B_1, \ldots, B_k\}$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Hence $\{x\} = B_1 \cap \cdots \cap B_k$ (intersecting the finitely many basis elements containing $x$).
::: {.proof}
$x$ lies in each $B_i$; and for any $y \neq x$, <1>4 gives a $B_y$ (one of the $B_i$) not containing $y$, so $y$ is excluded from the intersection.
:::

<1>7. Therefore $\{x\}$ is open.
::: {.proof}
<1>6 expresses $\{x\}$ as a finite intersection of open sets.
:::

<1>8. Since every singleton is open, $X$ is discrete.
::: {.proof}
<1>7 holds for every $x \in X$.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>8.
:::
:::
