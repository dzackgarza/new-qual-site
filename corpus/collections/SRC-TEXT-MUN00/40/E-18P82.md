::: {.exercise}

A collection $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $X$ is said to be locally discrete if each point of $X$ has a neighborhood that intersects at most one element of $\mathcal{A}$.
A collection $\mathcal{B}$ is countably locally discrete (or "$\sigma$-locally discrete") if it equals a countable union of locally discrete collections.
Prove the following.

Theorem (Bing metrization theorem).
A space $X$ is metrizable if and only if it is regular and has a basis that is countably locally discrete.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Sufficiency ($\impliedby$): Regular spaces with $\sigma$-locally discrete bases are metrizable.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. A collection $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $X$ is locally finite if every point of $X$ has an open neighborhood intersecting only finitely many elements of $\mathcal{A}$.
    <2>2. If $\mathcal{A}$ is locally discrete, every point has a neighborhood intersecting at most one element of $\mathcal{A}$, so $\mathcal{A}$ is locally finite.
    <2>3. If $\mathcal{B} = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \mathcal{B}_n$ is a countably locally discrete basis, each $\mathcal{B}_n$ is locally discrete, hence locally finite. Thus $\mathcal{B}$ is a countably locally finite ($\sigma$-locally finite) basis.
    <2>4. By the Nagata-Smirnov Metrization Theorem, any regular space with a countably locally finite basis is metrizable.
    <2>5. Therefore $X$ is metrizable.

<1>2. Necessity ($\implies$): Metrizable spaces possess a $\sigma$-locally discrete basis.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space. Metric spaces are $T_4$, hence regular.
    <2>2. Every open cover $\mathcal{A}$ of $X$ has an open refinement $\mathcal{E} = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \mathcal{E}_n$ that covers $X$, with each $\mathcal{E}_n$ locally discrete.
        *Proof:* Well-order $\mathcal{A}$ by $<$. For $U \in \mathcal{A}$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ put
        $$S_n(U) = \{x \in X : B_d(x, \tfrac1n) \subseteq U\}, \qquad T_n(U) = S_n(U) \setminus \bigcup_{V < U} V,$$
        and let $E_n(U) = \bigcup_{x \in T_n(U)} B_d(x, \frac{1}{3n})$, an open set. Then $E_n(U) \subseteq U$, because a point within $\frac{1}{3n}$ of a point $x \in S_n(U)$ lies in $B_d(x, \frac1n) \subseteq U$.
        If $V < W$ in $\mathcal{A}$, $x \in T_n(V)$ and $y \in T_n(W)$, then $B_d(x, \frac1n) \subseteq V$ and $y \notin V$, so $d(x, y) \ge \frac1n$. Hence points of $E_n(V)$ and $E_n(W)$ are at distance at least $\frac1n - \frac{2}{3n} = \frac{1}{3n}$, and a ball of radius $\frac{1}{6n}$ meets at most one member of $\mathcal{E}_n = \{E_n(U) : U \in \mathcal{A}\}$. So $\mathcal{E}_n$ is locally discrete.
        For $x \in X$, let $U$ be the first element of $\mathcal{A}$ containing $x$, and choose $n$ with $B_d(x, \frac1n) \subseteq U$. Then $x \in T_n(U) \subseteq E_n(U)$. So $\mathcal{E}$ covers $X$.
    <2>3. For $m \in \mathbb{Z}_+$, apply step <2>2 to the cover $\mathcal{A}_m = \{B_d(x, \frac1m) : x \in X\}$ to get an open refinement $\mathcal{C}_m$ that covers $X$ and is countably locally discrete. Each member of $\mathcal{C}_m$ lies in a ball of radius $\frac1m$, so it has diameter at most $\frac2m$.
    <2>4. $\mathcal{B} = \bigcup_{m=1}^\infty \mathcal{C}_m$ is countably locally discrete, as a countable union of countably locally discrete collections.
    <2>5. $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis. For an open set $U \subseteq X$ and $x \in U$, choose $m$ with $B_d(x, \frac{3}{m}) \subseteq U$. Some $C \in \mathcal{C}_m$ contains $x$, and $\operatorname{diam} C \le \frac2m$, so $x \in C \subseteq B_d(x, \frac3m) \subseteq U$.
    <2>6. Q.E.D. Steps <2>1, <2>4, and <2>5 show that $X$ is regular and has a countably locally discrete basis.
:::
