::: {.exercise}

Find a nondiscrete space that has a countably locally finite basis but does not have a countable basis.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $X$ be the set $\RR$ with the topology where every point $x \neq 0$ is isolated, and $0$ has its usual Euclidean neighborhoods.
Proof: this is a nondiscrete space (the point $0$ is not isolated).

<1>2. $X$ has a countably locally finite basis.
<2>1. A basis is $\mathcal B = \{\{x\} : x \neq 0\} \cup \{(1/n, 1/n) : n \ge 1\}$ (the singletons of the isolated points, together with a countable local basis at $0$).
Proof: definition.
<2>2. The family of singletons $\{\{x\} : x \neq 0\}$ is locally finite.
Proof: each point $x \neq 0$ has the neighborhood $\{x\}$ meeting only the singleton $\{x\}$, and $0$ has a neighborhood (e.g. $(-1,1)$) meeting no singleton (since $0$ is not in any singleton).
<2>3. Hence $\mathcal B$ is a countable union of locally finite families (the singletons form one locally finite family, and the countable local basis at $0$ is countable), so $\mathcal B$ is countably locally finite.
Proof: <2>2.

<1>3. $X$ does not have a countable basis.
Proof: $X$ has uncountably many isolated points ($\RR \setminus \{0\}$), and any basis must contain the singleton $\{x\}$ for each isolated point $x$ (since $\{x\}$ is open and is the only open set contained in itself containing $x$), so any basis is uncountable.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>3.
:::
