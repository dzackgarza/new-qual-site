::: {.exercise}

Compute the fundamental groups of the "solid torus" $S^1 \times B^2$ and the product space $S^1 \times S^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The exercise is §60 Exercise 1, printed p. 375. The proof is transcribed from
Pearse, *Topology (2nd ed.) — James R. Munkres*, solutions, Cornell 2005, §60
Exercise 1, his p. 26, read in `tmp/sources/cornell-erin-munkres.md`, and it is
one application of Theorem 60.1 (printed p. 371) to each product, with the
fundamental groups of $S^1$, of $B^2$ and of $S^2$ known: $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ$ by
Theorem 54.5 (printed p. 345), $\pi_1(B^2)$ trivial because the ball is convex,
and $\pi_1(S^2)$ trivial by Theorem 59.3 (printed p. 369).

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ball}
$\pi_1(B^2,b)$ is trivial.

::: pf-proof
$B^2$ is convex and contains its centre, so $H(x,t)=(1-t)x+t\,\mathbf 0$ is a
deformation retraction of $B^2$ onto $\theset{\mathbf 0}$, and Theorem 58.3
(printed p. 361) gives $\pi_1(B^2,b)\cong\pi_1(\theset{\mathbf 0},\mathbf 0)$,
which is trivial: a loop in a one-point space is constant.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #torus-product}
$\pi_1(S^1\times B^2,(s_0,\mathbf 0))\cong\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
By Theorem 60.1 (printed p. 371), the homomorphism
$\Phi([f])=[p\circ f]\times[q\circ f]$ is an isomorphism of
$\pi_1(S^1\times B^2)$ onto $\pi_1(S^1)\times\pi_1(B^2)$. The second factor is
trivial by step [](#ball){.pf-ref}, so $\Phi$ is an isomorphism onto
$\pi_1(S^1)\times 1\cong\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ$ by Theorem 54.5.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\boxed{\pi_1(S^1\times S^2,(s_0,s_0'))\cong\ZZ}$.

::: pf-proof
Again by Theorem 60.1, $\pi_1(S^1\times S^2)\cong\pi_1(S^1)\times\pi_1(S^2)$, and
$\pi_1(S^2)$ is trivial by Theorem 59.3 with $n=2$; so the group is isomorphic to
$\pi_1(S^1)\times1\cong\ZZ$ by Theorem 54.5. Both spaces therefore have infinite
cyclic fundamental group, and by Theorem 58.7 (printed p. 364) any space homotopy
equivalent to either has the same group.
:::
