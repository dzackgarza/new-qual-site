::: {.exercise}

Let $A$, $B$, and $A_\alpha$ denote subsets of a space $X$.
Determine whether the following equations hold; if an equality fails, determine whether one of the inclusions $\supset$ or $\subset$ holds.

(a) $\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$.

(b) $\overline{\bigcap A_\alpha} = \bigcap \overline{A}_\alpha$.

(c) $\overline{A - B} = \overline{A} - \overline{B}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

(a) $\overline{A\cap B}\subseteq\overline A\cap\overline B$, and equality can fail.

::: pf-proof

Since $A\cap B\subseteq A$ and $A\cap B\subseteq B$, monotonicity of closure gives $\overline{A\cap B}\subseteq\overline A$ and $\overline{A\cap B}\subseteq\overline B$.
In $\mathbb R$ let $A=(0,1)$ and $B=(1,2)$.
Then $\overline{A\cap B}=\varnothing$, while $\overline A\cap\overline B=[0,1]\cap[1,2]=\{1\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

(b) $\overline{\bigcap A_\alpha}\subseteq\bigcap\overline{A_\alpha}$, and equality can fail.

::: pf-proof

For each index $\beta$, $\bigcap_\alpha A_\alpha\subseteq A_\beta$, so $\overline{\bigcap_\alpha A_\alpha}\subseteq\overline{A_\beta}$; intersect over $\beta$.
In $\mathbb R$ let $A_n=(0,\frac1n)$ for $n\in\mathbb Z_+$.
Then $\bigcap_nA_n=\varnothing$, while $\bigcap_n\overline{A_n}=\bigcap_n[0,\frac1n]=\{0\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

(c) $\overline A-\overline B\subseteq\overline{A-B}$, and equality can fail.

::: pf-proof

Let $x\in\overline A-\overline B$.
Since $x\notin\overline B$, some open $U\ni x$ satisfies $U\cap B=\varnothing$.
Let $V$ be an open neighborhood of $x$.
Since $x\in\overline A$, the neighborhood $U\cap V$ contains a point $y\in A$; then $y\notin B$ because $y\in U$, so $y\in V\cap(A-B)$.
Hence every neighborhood of $x$ meets $A-B$, and $x\in\overline{A-B}$.

In $\mathbb R$ let $A=[0,1]$ and $B=(0,1)$.
Then $\overline{A-B}=\{0,1\}$, while $\overline A-\overline B=[0,1]-[0,1]=\varnothing$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref}, [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} decide (a), (b), and (c): in each, the inclusion stated in the step holds and the reverse inclusion fails.

:::
