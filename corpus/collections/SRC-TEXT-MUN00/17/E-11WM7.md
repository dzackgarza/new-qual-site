::: {.exercise}

Let $A$, $B$, and $A_\alpha$ denote subsets of a space $X$.
Determine whether the following equations hold; if an equality fails, determine whether one of the inclusions $\supset$ or $\subset$ holds.

(a) $\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$.

(b) $\overline{\bigcap A_\alpha} = \bigcap \overline{A}_\alpha$.

(c) $\overline{A - B} = \overline{A} - \overline{B}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 17.8, PDF p. 7** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`) for the inclusions and the counterexample to (a); the counterexample to (c) is Whitman's, printed p. 163.

(a) *$\overline{A\cap B}\subset\overline{A}\cap\overline{B}$ holds, and equality fails.* Since $A\cap B\subset A$ and $A\cap B\subset B$, Exercise 17.6(a) gives $\overline{A\cap B}\subset\overline{A}$ and $\overline{A\cap B}\subset\overline{B}$; hence the inclusion. It is not an equality: in $\RR$, $\overline{\QQ}=\RR=\overline{\RR-\QQ}$ while $\QQ\cap(\RR-\QQ)=\varnothing$ and $\overline{\varnothing}=\varnothing$. (The reverse inclusion fails too, as this example shows: $\overline{A}\cap\overline{B}=\RR\not\subset\varnothing$.)

(b) *$\overline{\bigcap_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}}\subset\bigcap_{\alpha}\overline{A_{\alpha}}$ holds, and equality fails.* The intersection of the closed sets $\overline{A_{\alpha}}$ is closed, and it contains $\bigcap_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}$, so it contains the closure of that intersection. The example of (a), with the two sets $A_{1}=\QQ$ and $A_{2}=\RR-\QQ$, shows that equality can fail: $\overline{A_{1}\cap A_{2}}=\varnothing$ while $\overline{A_{1}}\cap\overline{A_{2}}=\RR$.

(c) *$\overline{A-B}\supset\overline{A}-\overline{B}$ holds, and equality fails.* Let $x\in\overline{A}-\overline{B}$. Since $x\notin\overline{B}$ there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ with $U\cap B=\varnothing$; since $x\in\overline{A}$, every neighbourhood of $x$ meets $A$, so $V\cap A\ne\varnothing$ for each neighbourhood $V$ of $x$. Then $U\cap V$ is a neighbourhood of $x$, and any $y\in(U\cap V)\cap A$ satisfies $y\in A$ and $y\in U$, so $y\notin B$; hence $U\cap V$ meets $A-B$, and $x\in\overline{A-B}$. Equality fails for $A=[0,2]$ and $B=(1,3]$ in $\RR$: $A-B=[0,1]$ so $\overline{A-B}=[0,1]$, while $\overline{A}-\overline{B}=[0,2]-[1,3]=[0,1)$. (Here the reverse inclusion also fails: $1\in[0,1]$ but $1\notin\overline{A}-\overline{B}$.)
:::
