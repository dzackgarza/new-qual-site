::: {.exercise}

Consider the lower limit topology on $\RR$ and the topology given by the basis $\mathcal{C}$ of Exercise 8 of §13. Determine the closures of the intervals $A = (0, \sqrt{2})$ and $B = (\sqrt{2}, 3)$ in these two topologies.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **Dan Whitman, *Topology, Second Edition by James Munkres: Solutions Manual* (30 November 2019), Exercise 17.17, printed pp. 170--171** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-whitman.pdf`); the two lemmas and the answers are his. "positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2*, Exercise 17.17, PDF p. 9, gives the same answers.

Let $\mathcal{T}_{\ell}$ be the lower limit topology and $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$ the topology generated by $\mathcal{C}=\{[a,b)\mid a<b,\ a,b\in\QQ\}$ (Exercise 13.8). Recall that a point $x$ lies in the closure of $S$ exactly when every basis element containing $x$ meets $S$ (Theorem 17.5, printed p. 97).

**Lemma 1.** *In $\mathcal{T}_{\ell}$ the closure of an open interval $(a,b)$ is $[a,b)$.*

If $x\in(a,b)$ then $x\in\overline{(a,b)}$. If $x=a$ and $[c,d)$ is a basis element with $a\in[c,d)$, then $c\le a<d$; since $a<b$, the element $e=\min(b,d)$ satisfies $e>a$, and any $y$ with $a<y<e$ lies in $[c,d)$ and in $(a,b)$. So $a\in\overline{(a,b)}$. Conversely, if $x\notin[a,b)$ then either $x<a$, and $[x,a)$ is a basis element containing $x$ and meeting $(a,b)$ in nothing, or $x\ge b$, and $[b,x+1)$ is such a basis element.

**Lemma 2.** *In $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$ the closure of $(a,b)$ is $[a,b)$ if $b$ is rational and $[a,b]$ if $b$ is irrational.*

Let $x\in\RR$ and let $[p,q)$ with $p,q$ rational be a basis element containing $x$.

If $x<a$: choose rationals $p,q$ with $p<x<q<a$; then $[p,q)$ contains $x$ and misses $(a,b)$.

If $x=a$: for any basis element $[p,q)\ni a$ we have $p\le a<q$, and $e=\min(b,q)>a$; any $y$ with $a<y<e$ lies in $[p,q)$ and in $(a,b)$. So $a\in\overline{(a,b)}$.

If $a<x<b$: $x$ lies in the open interval.

If $x=b$ and $b$ is rational: $[b,q)$ with $q>b$ rational is a basis element containing $b$ and meeting $(a,b)$ in nothing, so $b\notin\overline{(a,b)}$.

If $x=b$ and $b$ is irrational: a basis element $[p,q)$ containing $b$ has rational $p\le b<q$, and $p\ne b$, so $p<b<q$; with $c=\max(p,a)<b$ any $y$ with $c<y<b$ lies in $[p,q)$ and in $(a,b)$, so $b\in\overline{(a,b)}$.

If $x>b$: choose rationals $p,q$ with $b<p<x<q$; then $[p,q)\ni x$ misses $(a,b)$.

These cases are exhaustive, and they give the two assertions.

**The answers.** By Lemma 1, in $\mathcal{T}_{\ell}$
$$
\overline{A}=[0,\sqrt2),\qquad \overline{B}=[\sqrt2,3)
$$
for $A=(0,\sqrt2)$ and $B=(\sqrt2,3)$. By Lemma 2, in $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$
$$
\overline{A}=[0,\sqrt2],\qquad \overline{B}=[\sqrt2,3),
$$
since $\sqrt2$ is irrational and $3$ is rational.

As Whitman notes, $A$ and $B$ are themselves basis elements of $\mathcal{T}_{\ell}$ and are closed there too, so in the lower limit topology every basis element is both open and closed.
