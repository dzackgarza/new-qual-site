::: {.exercise}

Consider the collection of all subsets $A$ of the topological space $X$.
The operations of closure $A \to \overline{A}$ and complementation $A \to X - A$ are functions from this collection to itself.

(a) Show that starting with a given set $A$, one can form no more than 14 distinct sets by applying these two operations successively.

(b) Find a subset $A$ of $\mathbb{R}$ (in its usual topology) for which the maximum of 14 is obtained.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $k$ denote closure, $i$ interior, and $c$ complementation. Since
\[
i=ckc,
\]
the monoid generated by $k$ and $c$ is the same as that generated by $k,i,c$. The identities
\[
k^2=k,\qquad i^2=i,\qquad c^2=1,
\]
and
\[
ic=ck,\qquad kc=ci
\]
allow every complement symbol to be moved to the far left. Hence it suffices to bound the number of distinct words in $k$ and $i$.

After removing consecutive repetitions, such a word alternates. Moreover
\[
kiki=ki,\qquad ikik=ik.
\]
For example, if $U=iA$ is open, then
\[
U\subseteq ikU\subseteq kU,
\]
so taking closures gives $kikU=kU$, hence $kikiA=kiA$; the other identity follows by complementation. Thus every word in $k,i$ reduces to one of
\[
1,\ k,\ i,\ ki,\ ik,\ kik,\ iki.
\]
There are at most seven such sets, and possibly their seven complements, for a total of at most $14$.

The bound is sharp in $\mathbb R$. Let
\[
A=\{0\}\cup(1,2)\cup(2,3)\cup(\mathbb Q\cap(4,5)).
\]
The seven sets obtained from the seven reduced $k,i$ words are
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=\{0\}\cup(1,2)\cup(2,3)\cup(\mathbb Q\cap(4,5)),\\
iA&=(1,2)\cup(2,3),\\
kA&=\{0\}\cup[1,3]\cup[4,5],\\
kiA&=[1,3],\\
ikA&=(1,3)\cup(4,5),\\
kikA&=[1,3]\cup[4,5],\\
ikiA&=(1,3).
\end{aligned}
\]
They are pairwise distinct. Each is bounded, whereas each complement is unbounded, so none of these seven equals the complement of another. Their complements are also pairwise distinct. Thus exactly $14$ distinct sets occur.
:::
