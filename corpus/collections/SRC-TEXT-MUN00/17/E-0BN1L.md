::: {.exercise}

Show that every order topology is Hausdorff.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that any linearly ordered set $(X, <)$ equipped with the order topology is a Hausdorff ($T_2$) space.

<1>1. Setup:
    Let $x, y \in X$ with $x \neq y$. Without loss of generality, assume $x < y$. The order topology on $X$ is generated by the subbasis of open rays $(-\infty, a) = \{z \in X : z < a\}$ and $(b, \infty) = \{z \in X : z > b\}$ for all $a, b \in X$.

<1>2. Case 1: The open interval $(x, y) = \{z \in X : x < z < y\}$ is nonempty.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $(x, y) \neq \emptyset$, choose an element $c \in X$ such that $x < c < y$.
    <2>2. Define $U = (-\infty, c) = \{z \in X : z < c\}$ and $V = (c, \infty) = \{z \in X : z > c\}$.
    <2>3. The sets $U$ and $V$ are open in $X$ as standard open rays.
    <2>4. $x \in U$ because $x < c$, and $y \in V$ because $c < y$.
    <2>5. $U \cap V = \{z \in X : z < c \text{ and } z > c\} = \emptyset$.
    <2>6. Thus $U$ and $V$ are disjoint open neighborhoods separating $x$ and $y$.

<1>3. Case 2: The open interval $(x, y)$ is empty ($x$ and $y$ are adjacent).
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define $U = (-\infty, y) = \{z \in X : z < y\}$ and $V = (x, \infty) = \{z \in X : z > x\}$.
    <2>2. $U$ and $V$ are open in $X$ as open rays.
    <2>3. $x \in U$ because $x < y$, and $y \in V$ because $x < y$.
    <2>4. If $z \in U \cap V$, then $x < z < y$, so $z \in (x, y) = \emptyset$, a contradiction.
    <2>5. Thus $U \cap V = \emptyset$.
    <2>6. Thus $U$ and $V$ are disjoint open neighborhoods separating $x$ and $y$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    In all cases, distinct points $x, y \in X$ can be separated by disjoint open sets. Therefore, $(X, <)$ is Hausdorff. Q.E.D.
:::
