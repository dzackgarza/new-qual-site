::: {.exercise}

In the finite complement topology on $\RR$, to what point or points does the sequence $x_n = 1/n$ converge?
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 17.14, PDF p. 8** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`); the argument is theirs. Whitman, printed p. 166, argues by contradiction from the definition of convergence and adds the remark that the same holds for any sequence with infinite image.

In the finite complement topology on $\RR$ the sequence $x_{n}=1/n$ converges to **every** point of $\RR$.

Let $a\in\RR$ and let $U$ be a neighbourhood of $a$. Since $U$ is open and nonempty, $\RR-U$ is finite (it cannot be all of $\RR$, as $a\in U$). The terms $x_{n}$ are pairwise distinct, so only finitely many indices $n$ satisfy $x_{n}\in\RR-U$; hence there is $N\in\ZZ_{+}$ with $x_{n}\in U$ for every $n\ge N$. As $U$ was an arbitrary neighbourhood of $a$, the sequence converges to $a$. Since $a$ was arbitrary, it converges to every point.

The same argument applies to any sequence whose set of values is infinite, so in this topology limits are not unique; dually, the topology is not Hausdorff (Theorem 17.10).
:::
