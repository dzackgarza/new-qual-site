::: {.exercise}

In the finite complement topology on $\mathbb{R}$, to what point or points does the sequence $x_n = 1/n$ converge?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. In the finite complement topology, the open sets are $\varnothing$ and the complements of finite sets.
::: {.proof}
definition of the finite complement (cofinite) topology.
:::

<1>2. A sequence $x_n$ converges to $x$ iff every open neighborhood of $x$ contains all but finitely many of the $x_n$.
::: {.proof}
definition of convergence.
:::

<1>3. The sequence $x_n = 1/n$ converges to every point $x \in \RR$.
<2>1. Let $x \in \RR$ and let $U$ be an open neighborhood of $x$.
::: {.proof}
take an arbitrary point and neighborhood.
:::
<2>2. $U$ is the complement of a finite set, so $U$ contains all but finitely many points of $\RR$.
::: {.proof}
<1>1.
:::
<2>3. The sequence $1/n$ takes infinitely many distinct values, but only finitely many of them can lie outside $U$ (since $U$ misses only finitely many points).
::: {.proof}
<2>2.
:::
<2>4. Hence $U$ contains all but finitely many of the $1/n$, so $1/n \to x$.
::: {.proof}
<2>3 and <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
the sequence converges to every point of $\RR$ (<1>3).
:::
:::
