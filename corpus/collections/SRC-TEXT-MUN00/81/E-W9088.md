::: {.exercise}

Let $p: X \to B$ be a covering map (not necessarily regular); let $G$ be its group of covering transformations.

(a) Show that the action of $G$ on $X$ is properly discontinuous.

(b) Let $\pi: X \to X/G$ be the projection map.
Show there is a covering map $k: X/G \to B$ such that $k \circ \pi = p$.

:::

::: {.solution}

By the Convention of Chapter 13 (printed p. 477), $X$ and $B$ are path connected and locally path connected; a covering transformation is an equivalence of $p$ with itself, that is, a homeomorphism $g:X\to X$ with $p\circ g=p$, and these form the group $G=\mathcal C(X,p,B)$ (printed p. 487).

(a) Fix $x\in X$, choose an open neighbourhood $U$ of $p(x)$ evenly covered by $p$ (Definition of a covering map, printed p. 336), and let $V$ be the slice of $p^{-1}(U)$ containing $x$. Suppose $g\in G$ and
$$
g(V)\cap V\ne\varnothing .
$$
Because $p\circ g=p$, the set $g(V)$ is again a slice of $p^{-1}(U)$, so $g(V)=V$. Then for $y\in V$ the point $g(y)$ lies in $V$ and satisfies $p(g(y))=p(y)$, and $p|_V$ is injective, so $g(y)=y$. Thus $g$ and the identity agree at the point $y$, and both are lifts of $p$; by the uniqueness part of Lemma 79.1 (printed p. 478), $g$ is the identity. Hence
$$
g(V)\cap V=\varnothing\qquad(g\ne 1),
$$
which is the definition of a properly discontinuous action (printed p. 490).

(b) Define
$$
k:X/G\to B,\qquad k([x])=p(x).
$$
This is well defined because every $g\in G$ satisfies $p\circ g=p$. With $\pi:X\to X/G$ the orbit projection, $k\circ\pi=p$. Since $\pi$ is a quotient map and $p$ is continuous, $k$ is continuous.

Let $U\subset B$ be evenly covered by $p$, say
$$
p^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{i\in I}V_i .
$$
Each $g\in G$ carries the slice $V_i$ onto a slice of $p^{-1}(U)$, so $G$ permutes the slices. Choose one representative $V_j$ from each $G$-orbit of slices. By part (a), no nonidentity element of $G$ preserves a slice. Therefore $\pi|_{V_j}$ is injective, and
$$
k:\pi(V_j)\longrightarrow U
$$
is a bijection with inverse $\pi\circ(p|_{V_j})^{-1}$, continuous in both directions, so a homeomorphism.

Moreover,
$$
k^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{[j]}\pi(V_j),
$$
where $[j]$ ranges over the $G$-orbits of slices. These sets are open and pairwise disjoint: their inverse images under the quotient map $\pi$ are unions of slices, which are open in $X$, and distinct orbits of slices give disjoint unions. Hence every point of $B$ has a neighbourhood evenly covered by $k$, and $k$ is a covering map by the Definition (printed p. 336). Thus
$$
\boxed{k:X/G\to B\text{ is a covering map and }k\pi=p.}
$$

:::
