::: {.exercise}

Let $p: X \to B$ be a covering map (not necessarily regular); let $G$ be its group of covering transformations.

(a) Show that the action of $G$ on $X$ is properly discontinuous.

(b) Let $\pi: X \to X/G$ be the projection map.
Show there is a covering map $k: X/G \to B$ such that $k \circ \pi = p$.

:::

::: {.solution}
Part (a) is **David Yang, *MATH104: Topology, Fall 2023*, Homework 11**, `HW/hw11.tex` lines 102--124 of `dyang5/MATH104-Topology` at commit `12686a815d58f29aeaadb90a30449ae85c0a613f` (2024-05-04): a slice over an evenly covered neighbourhood meets its image under $g\ne e$ in nothing, since a point of the intersection would give two points of the slice with the same image. Two sentences of his are slips rather than steps, and the fixed-point-freeness he uses is a consequence of the uniqueness of lifts, as step [](#injective-slice){.pf-ref} shows. Part (b) is commented out in his file and is the card's own.

By the Convention of Chapter 13 (printed p. 477), $X$ and $B$ are path connected and locally path connected; a covering transformation is a homeomorphism $g:X\to X$ with $p\circ g=p$, and these form the group $G=\mathcal{C}(X,p,B)$ (printed p. 487).

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #slice-image}
For $g\in G$ and a slice $V$ of $p^{-1}(U)$ over an evenly covered $U$, the set $g(V)$ is again a slice of $p^{-1}(U)$.

::: pf-proof
$g$ is a homeomorphism, so $g(V)$ is connected and $p\restrict_{g(V)}=\big(p\circ g\big)\restrict_{V}=p\restrict_{V}$ composed with $g^{-1}$, which is a homeomorphism from $g(V)$ onto $V$; hence $p$ maps $g(V)$ homeomorphically onto $U$. Also $g(V)\subset p^{-1}(U)$, since $p\big(g(V)\big)=p(V)=U$. So $g(V)$ is a slice.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equal-slice}
If $g(V)\cap V\ne\varnothing$ then $g(V)=V$.

::: pf-proof
Two slices of $p^{-1}(U)$ are either disjoint or equal: if they meet, they are components of $p^{-1}(U)$ meeting, hence equal. By step [](#slice-image){.pf-ref} both $g(V)$ and $V$ are slices, so the nonempty intersection forces equality.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #injective-slice}
If $g\ne\mathsf{l}_{X}$ then $g(V)\cap V=\varnothing$.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $g(V)=V$ and pick $y\in V$; then $g(y)\in V$ and $p\big(g(y)\big)=p(y)$ because $p\circ g=p$, while $p\restrict_{V}$ is injective, so $g(y)=y$. Thus $g$ and the identity are two lifts of $p$ that agree at the point $y$ of the connected space $X$, and the uniqueness part of Lemma 79.1 (printed p. 478) gives $g=\mathsf{l}_{X}$. So a nonidentity element moves $V$ off itself, and by step [](#equal-slice){.pf-ref} the intersection is empty.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) The action of $G$ on $X$ is properly discontinuous.

::: pf-proof
Let $x\in X$ and let $V$ be the slice containing $x$ of the inverse image of an evenly covered neighbourhood of $p(x)$. By step [](#injective-slice){.pf-ref}, $g(V)\cap V=\varnothing$ for every $g\ne\mathsf{l}_{X}$; that is the definition of a properly discontinuous action (printed p. 490).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #k-well-defined}
(b) $k:X/G\to B$, $k([x])=p(x)$, is well defined and continuous with $k\circ\pi=p$.

::: pf-proof
If $[x]=[x']$ then $x'=g(x)$ for some $g\in G$, so $p(x')=p(g(x))=p(x)$; hence $k$ is well defined. With $\pi:X\to X/G$ the orbit projection, $k\circ\pi=p$ by definition, and $p$ is continuous, so $k$ is continuous by the defining property of the quotient map $\pi$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #slices-permuted}
$G$ permutes the slices of $p^{-1}(U)$, and for a set $\{V_{j}\}$ of representatives of the $G$-orbits of slices the restriction $\pi\restrict_{V_{j}}$ is injective.

::: pf-proof
Each $g\in G$ carries slices to slices by step [](#slice-image){.pf-ref}, and $g\mapsto g(V)$ is an action on the set of slices, so the slices fall into orbits. If $\pi(x)=\pi(x')$ for $x,x'\in V_{j}$, then $x'=g(x)$ for some $g\in G$, so $g(V_{j})$ meets $V_{j}$ and hence equals it by step [](#equal-slice){.pf-ref}; step [](#injective-slice){.pf-ref} then gives $g=\mathsf{l}_{X}$ and $x'=x$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #local-homeo}
For each orbit representative $V_{j}$, the map $k:\pi(V_{j})\to U$ is a homeomorphism.

::: pf-proof
It is a bijection: injective because $\pi\restrict_{V_{j}}$ is injective by step [](#slices-permuted){.pf-ref}, and onto $U$ because every slice is $g(V_{j})$ for some $g$, so every $u\in U$ is $p(v)$ for a $v\in V_{j}$. Its inverse is $\pi\circ(p\restrict_{V_{j}})^{-1}$, which is continuous as a composite of continuous maps, since $p\restrict_{V_{j}}$ is a homeomorphism onto $U$; and $k$ is continuous by step [](#k-well-defined){.pf-ref}. A continuous bijection with continuous inverse is a homeomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #evenly-covered}
$k^{-1}(U)$ is the disjoint union of the open sets $\pi(V_{j})$, one for each $G$-orbit of slices.

::: pf-proof
Every slice lies in the orbit of exactly one representative, so $p^{-1}(U)$, which is the union of the slices, is the union of the sets $\pi^{-1}\big(\pi(V_{j})\big)$; hence $k^{-1}(U)=\bigcup_{j}\pi(V_{j})$. Each $\pi(V_{j})$ is open in $X/G$ because $\pi^{-1}\big(\pi(V_{j})\big)=\bigcup_{g\in G}g(V_{j})$ is a union of open slices, so it is open in $X$ and $\pi$ is a quotient map. Two of them are disjoint: if $\pi(x)=\pi(x')$ with $x\in V_{j}$ and $x'\in V_{j'}$, then $x'=g(x)\in g(V_{j})$, a slice in the orbit of $V_{j}$, so $V_{j'}$ lies in that orbit and $j'=j$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b}
(b) $k:X/G\to B$ is a covering map with $k\circ\pi=p$.

::: pf-proof
Let $u\in B$ and let $U$ be an evenly covered neighbourhood of $u$; such a $U$ exists for every $u$ by the definition of a covering map (printed p. 336). Steps [](#local-homeo){.pf-ref} and [](#evenly-covered){.pf-ref} show that $k^{-1}(U)$ is a disjoint union of open sets, each mapped homeomorphically onto $U$ by $k$; so $U$ is evenly covered by $k$, and $k$ is a covering map. The identity $k\circ\pi=p$ is step [](#k-well-defined){.pf-ref}.
:::
