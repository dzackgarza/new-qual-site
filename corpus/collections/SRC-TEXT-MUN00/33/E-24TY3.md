::: {.exercise}

Give a direct proof of the Urysohn lemma for a metric space $(X, d)$ by setting

$$
f(x) = \frac{d(x, A)}{d(x, A) + d(x, B)}
$$

for disjoint nonempty closed sets $A$ and $B$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Throughout $(X,d)$ is a metric space, $A, B \subseteq X$ are nonempty, closed and disjoint, $d(x,S) \coloneqq \inf_{s \in S}d(x,s)$ for $\emptyset \ne S \subseteq X$, and $f(x) \coloneqq \frac{d(x,A)}{d(x,A) + d(x,B)}$. The exercise is Munkres, *Topology* (2000), §33.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #distance}
For every nonempty $S \subseteq X$ the function $d_{S}$ is $1$-Lipschitz, and $d(x,S) = 0$ exactly when $x \in \overline{S}$.

::: pf-proof
For $x, y \in X$ and $s \in S$ the triangle inequality gives $d(x,s) \le d(x,y) + d(y,s)$; taking the infimum over $s$ gives $d(x,S) \le d(x,y) + d(y,S)$, and interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives $\abs{d(x,S) - d(y,S)} \le d(x,y)$. For the second claim, $d(x,S) = 0$ means that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $s \in S$ with $d(x,s) < \varepsilon$, that is $B(x,\varepsilon) \cap S \ne \emptyset$ for every $\varepsilon$, which is exactly $x \in \overline{S}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #well-defined}
$f$ is well defined and takes values in $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof
The denominator $d(x,A) + d(x,B)$ is a sum of two nonnegative numbers, so it suffices that it never vanishes. If $d(x,A) = d(x,B) = 0$ then $x \in \overline{A} = A$ and $x \in \overline{B} = B$ by step [](#distance){.pf-ref} and the closedness of $A$ and $B$, contradicting their disjointness; so the denominator is positive for every $x$. Finally $0 \le d(x,A) \le d(x,A) + d(x,B)$ gives $0 \le f(x) \le 1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #continuous}
$f$ is continuous.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#distance){.pf-ref} the functions $d(\cdot,A)$ and $d(\cdot,B)$ are $1$-Lipschitz, hence continuous; their sum is continuous and, by step [](#well-defined){.pf-ref}, nowhere zero. A quotient of continuous real-valued functions with nonvanishing denominator is continuous, since inversion is continuous on $\RR \setminus \theset{0}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
$f$ is continuous, $f(A) = \theset{0}$ and $f(B) = \theset{1}$: the Urysohn lemma for a metric space.

::: pf-proof
For $x \in A$ one has $d(x,A) = 0$ and $d(x,B) > 0$ because $x \notin B = \overline{B}$, so $f(x) = 0$; for $x \in B$ one has $d(x,B) = 0$ and $d(x,A) > 0$, so $f(x) = 1$. Continuity is step [](#continuous){.pf-ref}, so $f$ separates the two closed sets, which is the assertion of the lemma for metric spaces.

:::
