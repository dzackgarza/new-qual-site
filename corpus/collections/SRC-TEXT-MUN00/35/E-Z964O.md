::: {.exercise}

Prove the following.

Theorem.
Let $Y$ be a normal space.
Then $Y$ is an absolute retract if and only if $Y$ has the universal extension property.

[Hint: If $X$ and $Y$ are disjoint normal spaces, $A$ is closed in $X$, and $f: A \to Y$ is a continuous map, define the adjunction space $Z_f$ to be the quotient space obtained from $X \cup Y$ by identifying each point $a$ of $A$ with the point $f(a)$ and with all the points of $f^{-1}(\theset{f(a)})$. Using the Tietze theorem, show that $Z_f$ is normal. If $p: X \cup Y \to Z_f$ is the quotient map, show that $p \mid Y$ is a homeomorphism of $Y$ with a closed subspace of $Z_f$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
If $Y$ has the universal extension property, the argument of the preceding exercise shows immediately that $Y$ is an absolute retract: extend a homeomorphism from a closed copy $Y_0\subset Z$ to $Z$, then compose with its inverse to obtain a retraction.

Conversely, assume $Y$ is an absolute retract. Let $X$ be normal, let $A\subset X$ be closed, and let $f:A\to Y$ be continuous. Form the adjunction space
$$
Z_f=(X\sqcup Y)/\sim,
$$
where each $a\in A$ is identified with $f(a)$, so points of the same fiber of $f$ are identified through their common image. Let
$$
p:X\sqcup Y\to Z_f
$$
be the quotient map.

We first show $Z_f$ is $T_1$. A point represented by $x\in X\setminus A$ has singleton preimage $\{x\}$, closed in $X\sqcup Y$. A point represented by $y\in Y$ has preimage $f^{-1}(\{y\})\sqcup\{y\}$, closed because $A$ is closed in $X$, $f$ is continuous, and $X,Y$ are $T_1$. The quotient criterion makes each singleton of $Z_f$ closed.

Now let $C,D\subset Z_f$ be disjoint closed sets. Write
$$
C_X=p^{-1}(C)\cap X,\quad C_Y=p^{-1}(C)\cap Y,
$$
and similarly for $D$. These are closed, and saturation gives
$$
f(A\cap C_X)\subset C_Y,\qquad f(A\cap D_X)\subset D_Y.
$$
Since $Y$ is normal, Urysohn's lemma gives a continuous $g:Y\to[0,1]$ with
$$
g(C_Y)=0,\qquad g(D_Y)=1.
$$
On the closed subset
$$
A\cup C_X\cup D_X\subset X
$$
define $h_0$ by
$$
h_0\restrict_A=g\circ f,\qquad h_0\restrict_{C_X}=0,\qquad h_0\restrict_{D_X}=1.
$$
These prescriptions agree on overlaps by saturation, so the finite pasting lemma makes $h_0$ continuous. Tietze extends it to a continuous $h:X\to[0,1]$.

Because $h\restrict_A=g\circ f$, the function on $X\sqcup Y$ equal to $h$ on $X$ and $g$ on $Y$ is constant on equivalence classes. Hence it descends to a continuous
$$
H:Z_f\to[0,1]
$$
with $H(C)=0$ and $H(D)=1$. The disjoint open sets $H^{-1}([0,1/3))$ and $H^{-1}((2/3,1])$ separate $C$ and $D$. Thus $Z_f$ is normal.

Next, $p\restrict_Y:Y\to p(Y)$ is a homeomorphism onto a closed subspace. It is a continuous bijection. The set $p(Y)$ is closed because
$$
p^{-1}(p(Y))=A\sqcup Y
$$
is closed in $X\sqcup Y$. Moreover, if $F\subset Y$ is closed, then
$$
p^{-1}(p(F))=f^{-1}(F)\sqcup F
$$
is closed, so $p(F)$ is closed; hence $p\restrict_Y$ is a closed bijection and therefore a homeomorphism.

Since $Y$ is an absolute retract and $p(Y)$ is a closed copy of $Y$ in the normal space $Z_f$, there is a retraction
$$
r:Z_f\to p(Y).
$$
The composite
$$
X\xrightarrow{p}Z_f\xrightarrow{r}p(Y)\xrightarrow{(p\restrict_Y)^{-1}}Y
$$
is continuous. For $a\in A$, $p(a)=p(f(a))\in p(Y)$, so the retraction fixes this point and the composite equals $f(a)$. Thus every $f:A\to Y$ extends to $X$. Hence $Y$ has the universal extension property.
:::
