::: {.exercise}

Let $D$ be a closed connected subspace of $S^2$ that separates $S^2$ into $n$ components.

(a) If $A$ is an arc in $S^2$ whose intersection with $D$ consists of one of its end points, show that $D \cup A$ separates $S^2$ into $n$ components.

(b) If $A$ is an arc in $S^2$ whose intersection with $D$ consists of its end points, show that $D \cup A$ separates $S^2$ into $n + 1$ components.

(c) If $C$ is a simple closed curve in $S^2$ that intersects $D$ in a single point, show $D \cup C$ separates $S^2$ into $n + 1$ components.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Homological characterization of component count:
    For any closed subset $K \subset S^2$, the number of path components of the open set $S^2 \setminus K$ is $1 + \operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus K))$. In particular, $S^2 \setminus D$ having $n$ components means $\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus D) \cong \ZZ^{n-1}$.

::: pf-proof
For a space with path components $\{P_\alpha\}$, the group $H_0$ is the free abelian group on the set of those components, and the augmentation $H_0\to\ZZ$ sends each component to $1$; its kernel $\widetilde H_0$ is therefore free on the set of components with one relation, of rank one less than the number of components. The set $S^2\setminus K$ is open in the sphere, hence locally path connected, so its components and path components coincide. Hence the rank of $\widetilde H_0$ is one less than the number of components, which is the displayed formula; with $n$ components the group is $\ZZ^{n-1}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Mayer-Vietoris framework:
    Let $U = S^2 \setminus D$ and let $V = S^2 \setminus K'$ (where $K'$ is $A$ or $C$). Then $U \cap V = S^2 \setminus (D \cup K')$ and $U \cup V = S^2 \setminus (D \cap K')$.
    Since $U, V$ are open subsets of $S^2$, the Mayer-Vietoris sequence in reduced homology is exact:
    $$\widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) \xrightarrow{\partial} \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \xrightarrow{(i_U, i_V)} \widetilde{H}_0(U) \oplus \widetilde{H}_0(V) \xrightarrow{j_U - j_V} \widetilde{H}_0(U \cup V) \to 0.$$

::: pf-proof
$D$ is closed by hypothesis and the arc $A$ and the circle $C$ are compact, hence closed in the Hausdorff sphere, so $U$ and $V$ are open. The two set identities are De Morgan's laws: the complement of $D\cup K'$ is the intersection of the complements, and the complement of $D\cap K'$ is the union. Mayer-Vietoris applies to a pair of open subsets, and in reduced homology its tail is exact as displayed, the last term being $\widetilde H_0(U\cup V)$ because the sequence continues to $0$ at the end of the $n=0$ row.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$D \cap A = \{p\}$ (one endpoint of arc $A$).

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Since $A \cong [0, 1]$ is an arc, $S^2 \setminus A$ is connected and simply connected, so $\widetilde{H}_0(V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(V) = 0$.

::: pf-proof
An arc in $S^2$ does not separate it (Theorem 63.2), so $V=S^2\setminus A$ is connected and $\widetilde H_0(V)=0$. Moreover $S^2$ minus an arc is homeomorphic to $\RR^2$ — collapse the arc to a point of a disk containing it and use that the complement of a point of the sphere is a plane — and $\RR^2$ is contractible, so $\widetilde H_1(V)=0$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

$D \cap A = \{p\}$, so $U \cup V = S^2 \setminus \{p\} \cong \RR^2$, which is contractible. Thus $\widetilde{H}_0(U \cup V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) = 0$.

::: pf-proof
By step 2, $U\cup V=S^2\setminus(D\cap A)=S^2\setminus\{p\}$, and the complement of a point of $S^2$ is homeomorphic to $\RR^2$ by stereographic projection. A contractible space has vanishing reduced homology in every degree.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Mayer-Vietoris sequence reduces to:
        $$0 \to \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \xrightarrow{\cong} \widetilde{H}_0(U) \oplus 0 \to 0.$$

::: pf-proof
Substituting the vanishing of steps 3.1 and 3.2 into the sequence of step 2 leaves $\widetilde H_1(U\cup V)=0$ at the left and $\widetilde H_0(U\cup V)=0$ at the right, so the only surviving terms are $\widetilde H_0(U\cap V)\to\widetilde H_0(U)\oplus\widetilde H_0(V)=\widetilde H_0(U)$, and exactness at both ends makes this map an isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)) \cong \widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus D) \cong \ZZ^{n-1}$.

::: pf-proof
The first group is $\widetilde H_0(U\cap V)$ by step 2, and step 3.3 identifies it with $\widetilde H_0(U)=\widetilde H_0(S^2\setminus D)$, which is $\ZZ^{n-1}$ by step 1 applied to the closed set $D$ with its $n$ complementary components.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore, $S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)$ has $1 + (n - 1) = n$ components.

::: pf-proof
$D\cup A$ is closed, so step 1 applies to it: the number of components is $1+\operatorname{rank}\widetilde H_0(S^2\setminus(D\cup A))=1+(n-1)=n$ by step 3.4.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$D \cap A = \{p, q\}$ with $p \neq q$ (both endpoints of arc $A$).

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

As in step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $V = S^2 \setminus A$ satisfies $\widetilde{H}_0(V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(V) = 0$.

::: pf-proof
The argument is the one of step 3.1 and does not use where the endpoints of $A$ lie: an arc separates nothing, so $V$ is connected with $\widetilde H_0(V)=0$, and $S^2$ minus an arc is a plane, so $\widetilde H_1(V)=0$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

$D \cap A = \{p, q\}$, so $U \cup V = S^2 \setminus \{p, q\} \simeq S^1$.

::: pf-proof
By step 2, $U\cup V=S^2\setminus(D\cap A)=S^2\setminus\{p,q\}$, and the sphere with two points removed deformation retracts onto an equator separating them, which is a circle; equivalently it is homeomorphic to $\RR^2\setminus\{0\}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus $\widetilde{H}_0(U \cup V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) \cong \ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
$S^2\setminus\{p,q\}$ is connected, so $\widetilde H_0=0$; it is homotopy equivalent to $S^1$ by step 4.2, and $\widetilde H_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $\widetilde H_1(U)\to\widetilde H_1(S^2-\{p,q\})$ is zero. Indeed, choose a point of $U$ outside a given loop as the point at infinity. The loop then has a winding number about each point of $D$. Winding number is locally constant on $D$, hence constant because $D$ is connected. Its winding numbers about $p$ and $q$ are equal, so its class in $H_1(S^2-\{p,q\})\cong\ZZ$ vanishes. Also $\widetilde H_1(V)=0$. Therefore the preceding map in the Mayer-Vietoris sequence has zero image, and the sequence gives the short exact sequence:
        $$0 \to \widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) \xrightarrow{\partial} \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \to \widetilde{H}_0(U) \oplus 0 \to 0$$
        $$0 \to \ZZ \to \widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)) \to \ZZ^{n-1} \to 0.$$

::: pf-proof
Let $\gamma$ be a loop in $U=S^2\setminus D$ and let $a\notin\gamma$ be a point of $D$, which exists because $\gamma$ avoids $D$. In $S^2\setminus\{a\}\cong\RR^2$ the loop has a winding number $w_a$ about $a$, and $a\mapsto w_a$ is constant on each component of $S^2$ minus the image of $\gamma$; since $D$ is connected and disjoint from that image, $D$ lies in one component and $w$ is constant on $D$, in particular $w_p=w_q=k$. The group $H_1(S^2\setminus\{p,q\})\cong\ZZ$ is generated by a small circle about $p$, and the class of a loop in it is $w_p-w_q$: this is the identification $S^2\setminus\{p,q\}\cong S^1\times\RR$, under which $w_p-w_q$ is the winding about the circle factor. Hence the class is $k-k=0$, so the induced map is zero. With $\widetilde H_1(V)=0$ by step 4.1, the group $\widetilde H_1(U)\oplus\widetilde H_1(V)$ maps to zero in $\widetilde H_1(U\cup V)$, so exactness identifies $\widetilde H_1(U\cup V)$ with the kernel of the next map, giving the displayed short exact sequences after substituting steps 4.1 and 4.3.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $\ZZ^{n-1}$ is free abelian, the sequence splits, giving:
        $$\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)) \cong \ZZ^{n-1} \oplus \ZZ \cong \ZZ^n.$$

::: pf-proof
A short exact sequence $0\to A\to B\to C\to 0$ of abelian groups with $C$ free splits: choosing a preimage of each basis element of $C$ gives a homomorphism $C\to B$ splitting the projection, so $B\cong A\oplus C$. Here $A=\ZZ$ and $C=\ZZ^{n-1}$ by step 4.4.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore, $S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)$ has $1 + n = n + 1$ components.

::: pf-proof
$D\cup A$ is closed, so step 1 gives $1+\operatorname{rank}\widetilde H_0(S^2\setminus(D\cup A))=1+n$ by step 4.5.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$C$ is a simple closed curve and $D \cap C = \{p\}$ (a single point).

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Since $C \cong S^1$ is a simple closed curve, by the Jordan Curve Theorem $V = S^2 \setminus C$ has two components, so $\widetilde{H}_0(V) \cong \ZZ$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(V) = 0$.

::: pf-proof
A simple closed curve in the sphere separates it into exactly two components, by the Jordan curve theorem (Theorem 61.3), so $V$ has two components and $\widetilde H_0(V)\cong\ZZ^{2-1}=\ZZ$ by step 1. Each of the two complementary domains is homeomorphic to an open disk — the Jordan--Schoenflies theorem for the sphere, which Theorem 63.4 states for the disk — and an open disk is contractible, so each domain has vanishing reduced homology and hence $\widetilde H_1(V)=0$, being the direct sum over the two components.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

$D \cap C = \{p\}$, so $U \cup V = S^2 \setminus \{p\} \cong \RR^2$, which has $\widetilde{H}_0(U \cup V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) = 0$.

::: pf-proof
By step 2, $U\cup V=S^2\setminus(D\cap C)=S^2\setminus\{p\}$, which is a plane by stereographic projection and is contractible, so all its reduced homology vanishes.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Mayer-Vietoris sequence reduces to:
        $$0 \to \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \xrightarrow{\cong} \widetilde{H}_0(U) \oplus \widetilde{H}_0(V) \to 0.$$

::: pf-proof
Insert the vanishing of step 5.2 and the vanishing of $\widetilde H_1(V)$ from step 5.1 into the sequence of step 2: the terms $\widetilde H_1(U\cup V)$, $\widetilde H_1(V)$ and $\widetilde H_0(U\cup V)$ are all zero, so the only surviving arrow is $\widetilde H_0(U\cap V)\to\widetilde H_0(U)\oplus\widetilde H_0(V)$, and exactness at both ends makes it an isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus (D \cup C)) \cong \widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus D) \oplus \widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus C) \cong \ZZ^{n-1} \oplus \ZZ \cong \ZZ^n$.

::: pf-proof
The left group is $\widetilde H_0(U\cap V)$ by step 2 and the right one is $\widetilde H_0(U)\oplus\widetilde H_0(V)$ with $\widetilde H_0(U)=\widetilde H_0(S^2\setminus D)$ and $\widetilde H_0(V)=\widetilde H_0(S^2\setminus C)$; step 5.3 gives the isomorphism between them, and steps 1 and 5.1 give the two values $\ZZ^{n-1}$ and $\ZZ$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore, $S^2 \setminus (D \cup C)$ has $1 + n = n + 1$ components. Q.E.D.

::: pf-proof
$D\cup C$ is closed, so step 1 gives $1+\operatorname{rank}\widetilde H_0(S^2\setminus(D\cup C))=1+n$ by step 5.4.
:::
