::: {.exercise}

Let $D$ be a closed connected subspace of $S^2$ that separates $S^2$ into $n$ components.

(a) If $A$ is an arc in $S^2$ whose intersection with $D$ consists of one of its end points, show that $D \cup A$ separates $S^2$ into $n$ components.

(b) If $A$ is an arc in $S^2$ whose intersection with $D$ consists of its end points, show that $D \cup A$ separates $S^2$ into $n + 1$ components.

(c) If $C$ is a simple closed curve in $S^2$ that intersects $D$ in a single point, show $D \cup C$ separates $S^2$ into $n + 1$ components.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Determine the number of connected components of $S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)$ and $S^2 \setminus (D \cup C)$ using the Mayer-Vietoris sequence in reduced homology.

<1>1. Homological characterization of component count:
    For any closed subset $K \subset S^2$, the number of path components of the open set $S^2 \setminus K$ is $1 + \operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus K))$. In particular, $S^2 \setminus D$ having $n$ components means $\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus D) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$.

<1>2. Mayer-Vietoris framework:
    Let $U = S^2 \setminus D$ and let $V = S^2 \setminus K'$ (where $K'$ is $A$ or $C$). Then $U \cap V = S^2 \setminus (D \cup K')$ and $U \cup V = S^2 \setminus (D \cap K')$.
    Since $U, V$ are open subsets of $S^2$, the Mayer-Vietoris sequence in reduced homology is exact:
    $$\widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) \xrightarrow{\partial} \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \xrightarrow{(i_U, i_V)} \widetilde{H}_0(U) \oplus \widetilde{H}_0(V) \xrightarrow{j_U - j_V} \widetilde{H}_0(U \cup V) \to 0.$$

<1>3. Part (a): $D \cap A = \{p\}$ (one endpoint of arc $A$).
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $A \cong [0, 1]$ is an arc, $S^2 \setminus A$ is connected and simply connected, so $\widetilde{H}_0(V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(V) = 0$.
    <2>2. $D \cap A = \{p\}$, so $U \cup V = S^2 \setminus \{p\} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$, which is contractible. Thus $\widetilde{H}_0(U \cup V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) = 0$.
    <2>3. The Mayer-Vietoris sequence reduces to:
        $$0 \to \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \xrightarrow{\cong} \widetilde{H}_0(U) \oplus 0 \to 0.$$
    <2>4. Hence $\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)) \cong \widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus D) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$.
    <2>5. Therefore, $S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)$ has $1 + (n - 1) = n$ components.

<1>4. Part (b): $D \cap A = \{p, q\}$ with $p \neq q$ (both endpoints of arc $A$).
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. As in <1>3, $V = S^2 \setminus A$ satisfies $\widetilde{H}_0(V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(V) = 0$.
    <2>2. $D \cap A = \{p, q\}$, so $U \cup V = S^2 \setminus \{p, q\} \simeq S^1$.
    <2>3. Thus $\widetilde{H}_0(U \cup V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
    <2>4. The Mayer-Vietoris sequence gives the short exact sequence:
        $$0 \to \widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) \xrightarrow{\partial} \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \to \widetilde{H}_0(U) \oplus 0 \to 0$$
        $$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)) \to \mathbb{Z}^{n-1} \to 0.$$
    <2>5. Since $\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$ is free abelian, the sequence splits, giving:
        $$\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{n-1} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}^n.$$
    <2>6. Therefore, $S^2 \setminus (D \cup A)$ has $1 + n = n + 1$ components.

<1>5. Part (c): $C$ is a simple closed curve and $D \cap C = \{p\}$ (a single point).
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $C \cong S^1$ is a simple closed curve, by the Jordan Curve Theorem $V = S^2 \setminus C$ has two components, so $\widetilde{H}_0(V) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(V) = 0$.
    <2>2. $D \cap C = \{p\}$, so $U \cup V = S^2 \setminus \{p\} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$, which has $\widetilde{H}_0(U \cup V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_1(U \cup V) = 0$.
    <2>3. The Mayer-Vietoris sequence reduces to:
        $$0 \to \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \xrightarrow{\cong} \widetilde{H}_0(U) \oplus \widetilde{H}_0(V) \to 0.$$
    <2>4. Hence $\widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus (D \cup C)) \cong \widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus D) \oplus \widetilde{H}_0(S^2 \setminus C) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{n-1} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$.
    <2>5. Therefore, $S^2 \setminus (D \cup C)$ has $1 + n = n + 1$ components. Q.E.D.
