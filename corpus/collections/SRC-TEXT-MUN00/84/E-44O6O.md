::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be the wedge of two circles; let $p: E \to X$ be a covering map.
The fundamental group of $E$ maps isomorphically under $p_*$ onto a subgroup $H$ of the fundamental group of $X$; the latter is free on two generators $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

(a) For each of the four covering spaces $E$ given in [[E-79XXG]], determine the cardinality of a system of free generators for the fundamental group of $E$.

(b) For each of these covering spaces, find, in terms of $\alpha$ and $\beta$, a system of free generators for the subgroup $H$ of the fundamental group of $X$.

:::

::: {.solution}

Let $X=A\vee B$ be the wedge of the circles $A$ and $B$ at $x_0$; by Theorem 71.1 (printed p. 434) the group $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is free on the class $\alpha$ of a loop in $A$ and the class $\beta$ of a loop in $B$, and by Theorem 54.6 (printed p. 346) the map $p_*$ is injective, so $\pi_1(E,e_0)\cong H$.

For each figure $E$ is a graph whose vertices form the fibre $p^{-1}(x_0)$ and whose edges are labelled $A$ or $B$; $p$ maps an edge onto $A$ or $B$ in the direction of its arrow. Every vertex has exactly one incoming and one outgoing edge of each label, so $E$ contains exactly as many edges as vertices times two minus the loops. In the three finite cases the number of vertices is the number $d$ of sheets, so $E$ has $d$ vertices and $2d$ edges.

If $T$ is a maximal tree in the connected graph $E$, then by Theorem 84.4 (printed p. 509) it contains every vertex, so it has $V-1$ edges, and by Theorem 84.7 (printed p. 511) the classes $[g_A]$, for $A$ an edge of $E$ not in $T$, freely generate $\pi_1(E,e_0)$, where $g_A$ is the edge followed by tree paths to and from the base point. The number of free generators is therefore
$$
E-(V-1)=E-V+1 .
$$

(a) In Figures 81.4, 81.5 and 81.6 the covering has $d=2,3,4$ sheets, so $V=d$ and $E=2d$, and the free rank is $2d-d+1=d+1$, that is $\boxed{3, 4, 5}$ respectively. In Figure 81.7 the graph is infinite, and the count is the cardinality of the set of edges outside a maximal tree, computed in (b) below: it is countably infinite.

(b) In each case let the base vertex be $e_0$ and, for a vertex $v$, let $\gamma_v$ be the tree path from $e_0$ to $v$; the loop of an edge $A$ from $x$ to $y$ not in $T$ projects to the word $\gamma_x\cdot(\text{label of }A)\cdot\gamma_y^{-1}$, where a label means $\alpha$ or $\beta$ according to the letter.

Figure 81.4, with vertices $e_0$ and $v$, edges $A_1$ from $e_0$ to $v$, $A_2$ from $v$ to $e_0$, and loops $B_1$ at $v$, $B_2$ at $e_0$. Take $T=\{A_1\}$, so $\gamma_v=\alpha$. The edges outside $T$ give
$$
\boxed{\alpha^2,\ \beta,\ \alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}}.
$$

Figure 81.5, with vertices $u,e_0,w$, edges $A_1$ from $e_0$ to $u$, $A_2$ from $u$ to $e_0$, $B_2$ from $e_0$ to $w$, $B_1$ from $w$ to $e_0$, and loops $B_3$ at $u$, $A_3$ at $w$. Take $T=\{A_1,B_2\}$, so $\gamma_u=\alpha$ and $\gamma_w=\beta$. The edges outside $T$ give
$$
\boxed{\alpha^2,\ \beta^2,\ \alpha\beta\alpha^{-1},\ \beta\alpha\beta^{-1}}.
$$

Figure 81.6, with vertices $u$ (carrying the loop $B_3$), $m$, $e_0$ and $r$ (carrying the loop $B_4$), and edges $A_1\colon m\to u$, $A_2\colon u\to m$, $B_1\colon e_0\to m$, $B_2\colon m\to e_0$, $A_3\colon r\to e_0$, $A_4\colon e_0\to r$. Take
$$
T=\{A_2,\,B_2,\,A_4\},
$$
which spans the four vertices; then $\gamma_m=\beta^{-1}$ (the reverse of $B_2$), $\gamma_u=\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}$ (then the reverse of $A_2$) and $\gamma_r=\alpha$. The five edges outside $T$ give
$$
\boxed{\beta^{-1}\alpha^2\beta,\quad \beta^2,\quad \alpha^2,\quad \beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha\beta,\quad \alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}},
$$
from $A_1$, $B_1$, $A_3$, $B_3$ and $B_4$ respectively.

Figure 81.7, with vertices $v_n$, $n\in\ZZ$, edges $A_n$ from $v_n$ to $v_{n+1}$ and loops $B_n$ at $v_n$. The set of all $A_n$ is a maximal tree: it is connected, contains every vertex, and has no closed reduced edge path; the edges outside it are exactly the loops $B_n$, one for each integer. Since $\gamma_{v_n}=\alpha^n$, the loop of $B_n$ projects to
$$
\boxed{\{\alpha^n\beta\alpha^{-n}\mid n\in\ZZ\}},
$$
a system of free generators for $H$ of countably infinite cardinality.
