::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be the wedge of two circles; let $p: E \to X$ be a covering map.
The fundamental group of $E$ maps isomorphically under $p_*$ onto a subgroup $H$ of the fundamental group of $X$; the latter is free on two generators $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

(a) For each of the four covering spaces $E$ given in [[E-79XXG]], determine the cardinality of a system of free generators for the fundamental group of $E$.

(b) For each of these covering spaces, find, in terms of $\alpha$ and $\beta$, a system of free generators for the subgroup $H$ of the fundamental group of $X$.

:::

::: {.solution}
No complete published solution of this exercise was located; the argument is the card's own, checked step by step against Munkres §84 Exercise 3, and the numbered theorems cited below. One sentence of the earlier prose is repaired: the count of edges is $E=2V$, not "vertices times two minus the loops", because a loop is counted among the edges that leave its vertex.

The four coverings are those of Munkres Figures 81.4--81.7, whose vertices and labelled edges are recorded on [[E-79XXG]]: in Figure 81.4 the vertices are $e_{0},v$ with $A_{1}\colon e_{0}\to v$, $A_{2}\colon v\to e_{0}$ and loops $B_{1}$ at $v$, $B_{2}$ at $e_{0}$; in Figure 81.5 they are $u$ (loop $B_{3}$), $e_{0}$, $w$ (loop $A_{3}$) with $A_{1}\colon e_{0}\to u$, $A_{2}\colon u\to e_{0}$, $B_{2}\colon e_{0}\to w$, $B_{1}\colon w\to e_{0}$; in Figure 81.6 they are $u$ (loop $B_{3}$), $m$, $e_{0}$, $r$ (loop $B_{4}$) with $A_{1}\colon m\to u$, $A_{2}\colon u\to m$, $B_{1}\colon e_{0}\to m$, $B_{2}\colon m\to e_{0}$, $A_{3}\colon r\to e_{0}$, $A_{4}\colon e_{0}\to r$; in Figure 81.7 they are $v_{n}$ for $n\in\ZZ$ with $A_{n}\colon v_{n}\to v_{n+1}$ and a loop $B_{n}$ at $v_{n}$. In every figure $p$ maps an $A$-labelled edge onto $A$ and a $B$-labelled edge onto $B$ in the direction of its arrow.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #group}
$\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})$ is free on the classes $\alpha$ of a loop in $A$ and $\beta$ of a loop in $B$, and $p_{*}$ injects $\pi_{1}(E,e_{0})$ onto $H$.

::: pf-proof
$X=A\vee B$ is a graph, and its fundamental group is free on the classes of the loops obtained by deleting the edges of a maximal tree; taking as maximal tree the edge class joining the wedge point to itself, that is the point $x_{0}$ alone, the two circles give the free generators $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (Theorem 71.1, is the computation for the wedge). A covering map induces an injection of fundamental groups (Theorem 54.6), so $p_{*}$ carries $\pi_{1}(E,e_{0})$ isomorphically onto its image $H$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #edges}
In each of the four figures every vertex is the tail of exactly one edge of each label, so $E$ has $V$ edges labelled $A$, $V$ labelled $B$, and $E=2V$.

::: pf-proof
In each figure the arrows give, at every vertex, exactly one outgoing edge labelled $A$ and exactly one outgoing edge labelled $B$; a loop counts as outgoing at its vertex and is a single edge, not two. Each $A$-labelled edge has exactly one tail, so the $A$-labelled edges and the vertices are in bijection and there are $V$ of them; likewise $V$ edges labelled $B$. Hence $E=2V$. In Figures 81.4, 81.5 and 81.6 this reads $E=4,6,8$ for $V=2,3,4$, checked against the edge lists above, and in Figure 81.7 the count is infinite.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rank}
If $E$ is connected, $T$ a maximal tree in $E$ and $e_{0}$ a vertex, then the classes of the edges outside $T$ freely generate $\pi_{1}(E,e_{0})$, so the number of free generators is $E-V+1$.

::: pf-proof
A maximal tree in a connected graph contains every vertex (Theorem 84.4), so $T$ has $V-1$ edges; the edges outside $T$ therefore number $E-(V-1)=E-V+1$. For each such edge $A$, oriented from $x$ to $y$, the loop $g_{A}=\gamma_{x}*(f_{A}*\bar\gamma_{y})$ has class $[\gamma_{x}]\,[\text{label of }A]\,[\gamma_{y}]^{-1}$, where $\gamma_{v}$ is the path in $T$ from $e_{0}$ to $v$ and $f_{A}$ traverses $A$; and the classes $[g_{A}]$ form a system of free generators for $\pi_{1}(E,e_{0})$ (Theorem 84.7, whose proof is exactly this construction).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) The numbers of free generators are $3$, $4$, $5$ and countably infinitely many, for Figures 81.4, 81.5, 81.6 and 81.7 respectively.

::: pf-proof
In Figures 81.4, 81.5 and 81.6 the covering has $d=2,3,4$ sheets, so $V=d$ and $E=2d$ by step [](#edges){.pf-ref}; the rank $E-V+1$ of step [](#rank){.pf-ref} is then $2d-d+1=d+1$, that is $3$, $4$, $5$. In Figure 81.7 the graph is infinite and the set of edges outside the maximal tree described in step [](#fig817){.pf-ref} is countably infinite, so the rank is countably infinite.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #recipe}
(b) In each figure, the loop of an edge outside the chosen tree, oriented from $x$ to $y$, projects to the word $[\gamma_{x}]\,L\,[\gamma_{y}]^{-1}$, where $L$ is $\alpha$ or $\beta$ according to the label, and these words freely generate $H$.

::: pf-proof
Both the projection of the loop and the word of step [](#rank){.pf-ref} are the class of a loop based at $e_{0}$, and by step [](#group){.pf-ref} the projection $p_{*}$ is injective with image $H$. So the images of a free basis of $\pi_{1}(E,e_{0})$ freely generate $H$, and step [](#rank){.pf-ref} says that basis is the set of these loops.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fig814}
For Figure 81.4 the words are $\boxed{\alpha^{2},\ \beta,\ \alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}}$.

::: pf-proof
Take $T=\{A_{1}\}$, a maximal tree by step [](#rank){.pf-ref} since it spans $e_{0}$, $v$; then $\gamma_{e_{0}}$ is the constant path and $\gamma_{v}$ has class $\alpha$. The edges outside $T$ are $A_{2}\colon v\to e_{0}$, the loop $B_{1}$ at $v$ and the loop $B_{2}$ at $e_{0}$, giving
$$
[\gamma_{v}]\,\alpha\,[\gamma_{e_{0}}]^{-1}=\alpha\alpha=\alpha^{2},
\qquad
[\gamma_{v}]\,\beta\,[\gamma_{v}]^{-1}=\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1},
\qquad
[\gamma_{e_{0}}]\,\beta\,[\gamma_{e_{0}}]^{-1}=\beta,
$$
by step [](#recipe){.pf-ref}. There are $4-2+1=3$ of them, matching step [](#part-a){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fig815}
For Figure 81.5 the words are $\boxed{\alpha^{2},\ \beta^{2},\ \alpha\beta\alpha^{-1},\ \beta\alpha\beta^{-1}}$.

::: pf-proof
Take $T=\{A_{1},B_{2}\}$, spanning $e_{0}$, $u$ and $w$; then $\gamma_{e_{0}}$ is constant, $\gamma_{u}$ has class $\alpha$ (the edge $A_{1}$) and $\gamma_{w}$ has class $\beta$ (the edge $B_{2}$). The edges outside $T$ are $A_{2}\colon u\to e_{0}$, $B_{1}\colon w\to e_{0}$, the loop $B_{3}$ at $u$ and the loop $A_{3}$ at $w$:
$$
[\gamma_{u}]\alpha[\gamma_{e_{0}}]^{-1}=\alpha^{2},\quad
[\gamma_{w}]\beta[\gamma_{e_{0}}]^{-1}=\beta^{2},\quad
[\gamma_{u}]\beta[\gamma_{u}]^{-1}=\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1},\quad
[\gamma_{w}]\alpha[\gamma_{w}]^{-1}=\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}.
$$
There are $6-3+1=4$ of them, matching step [](#part-a){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fig816}
For Figure 81.6, with $T=\{A_{2},B_{2},A_{4}\}$, the words are $\boxed{\beta^{-1}\alpha^{2}\beta,\ \beta^{2},\ \alpha^{2},\ \beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha\beta,\ \alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}}$.

::: pf-proof
$T$ has three edges and spans $u,m,e_{0},r$: $B_{2}$ joins $m$ to $e_{0}$, $A_{2}$ joins $u$ to $m$, $A_{4}$ joins $e_{0}$ to $r$. So $\gamma_{e_{0}}$ is constant, $\gamma_{m}=\beta^{-1}$ (the reverse of $B_{2}$), $\gamma_{u}=\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}$ (then the reverse of $A_{2}$) and $\gamma_{r}=\alpha$ (the edge $A_{4}$). The edges outside $T$ are $A_{1}\colon m\to u$, $B_{1}\colon e_{0}\to m$, $A_{3}\colon r\to e_{0}$ and the loops $B_{3}$ at $u$, $B_{4}$ at $r$; step [](#recipe){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{1}:&\quad [\gamma_{m}]\,\alpha\,[\gamma_{u}]^{-1}=\beta^{-1}\alpha(\alpha\beta)=\beta^{-1}\alpha^{2}\beta,\\
B_{1}:&\quad [\gamma_{e_{0}}]\,\beta\,[\gamma_{m}]^{-1}=\beta(\beta^{-1})^{-1}=\beta^{2},\\
A_{3}:&\quad [\gamma_{r}]\,\alpha\,[\gamma_{e_{0}}]^{-1}=\alpha^{2},\\
B_{3}:&\quad [\gamma_{u}]\,\beta\,[\gamma_{u}]^{-1}=\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha\beta,\\
B_{4}:&\quad [\gamma_{r}]\,\beta\,[\gamma_{r}]^{-1}=\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1},
\end{aligned}
$$
using $(\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1})^{-1}=\alpha\beta$. There are $8-4+1=5$ of them, matching step [](#part-a){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fig817}
For Figure 81.7 the words are $\boxed{\{\alpha^{n}\beta\alpha^{-n}\mid n\in\ZZ\}}$, countably many.

::: pf-proof
The set $T=\{A_{n}\mid n\in\ZZ\}$ is a maximal tree: it contains every vertex $v_{n}$, it is connected along the line, and it contains no closed reduced edge path, since a reduced path in it moves monotonically in the index and so cannot return to its start. The edges outside $T$ are exactly the loops $B_{n}$, one for each integer. The tree path from $v_{0}$ to $v_{n}$ runs along $A_{0},\dots,A_{n-1}$ (or backwards along $A_{-1},\dots,A_{-n}$ for $n<0$), so $[\gamma_{v_{n}}]=\alpha^{n}$, and the loop of $B_{n}$ at $v_{n}$ has class $\alpha^{n}\beta\alpha^{-n}$ by step [](#recipe){.pf-ref}. As $n$ ranges over $\ZZ$ these are countably many distinct words, freely generating $H$ by step [](#rank){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Both parts hold.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#part-a){.pf-ref} answers (a) with the ranks $3,4,5$ and countably infinite, and steps [](#fig814){.pf-ref}, [](#fig815){.pf-ref}, [](#fig816){.pf-ref} and [](#fig817){.pf-ref} answer (b) with a free basis for each of the four coverings.
:::
