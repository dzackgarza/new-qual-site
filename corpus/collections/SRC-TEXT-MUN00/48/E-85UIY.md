::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ be a topological space; let $Y$ be a complete metric space.
Show that $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ is a Baire space in the fine topology (see [[E-0GM3H]]). [Hint: Given basis elements $B(f_i, \delta_i)$ such that $\delta_1 \leq 1$ and $\delta_{i+1} \leq \delta_i/3$ and $f_{i+1} \in B(f_i, \delta_i/3)$, show that

$$
\bigcap B(f_i, \delta_i) \neq \varnothing.]
$$
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that if $(Y, d)$ is a complete metric space and $X$ is an arbitrary topological space, the function space $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ equipped with the fine topology is a Baire space.

<1>1. Setting up the Baire characterization:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. A topological space is a Baire space if and only if given any non-empty open set $U_0$ and any countable family of dense open sets $\{D_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$, the intersection $U_0 \cap \bigcap_{n=1}^\infty D_n$ is non-empty.
    <2>2. The fine topology on $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ is generated by basic open sets of the form:
        $$B(f, \delta) = \{g \in \mathcal{C}(X, Y) \mid \forall x \in X, \, d(f(x), g(x)) < \delta(x)\},$$
        where $f \in \mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ and $\delta: X \to (0, \infty)$ is a continuous strictly positive function.

<1>2. Inductive construction of nested balls:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $D_1$ is dense and open, $U_0 \cap D_1$ is non-empty and open. Choose $f_1 \in \mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ and continuous $\delta_1: X \to (0, 1]$ such that $B(f_1, \delta_1) \subseteq U_0 \cap D_1$.
    <2>2. Assume $f_n$ and continuous $\delta_n: X \to (0, \infty)$ have been chosen.
    <2>3. Because $D_{n+1}$ is dense and open, the open set $B(f_n, \delta_n/3) \cap D_{n+1}$ is non-empty.
    <2>4. Choose $f_{n+1} \in \mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ and continuous $\delta_{n+1}: X \to (0, \infty)$ such that:
        $$\delta_{n+1}(x) \le \frac{\delta_n(x)}{3} \quad \text{and} \quad B(f_{n+1}, \delta_{n+1}) \subseteq B(f_n, \delta_n/3) \cap D_{n+1}.$$
    <2>5. For any $g \in B(f_{n+1}, \delta_{n+1})$ and any $x \in X$:
        $$d(g(x), f_n(x)) \le d(g(x), f_{n+1}(x)) + d(f_{n+1}(x), f_n(x)) < \delta_{n+1}(x) + \frac{\delta_n(x)}{3} \le \frac{2}{3}\delta_n(x) < \delta_n(x).$$
        Thus $\overline{B}(f_{n+1}, \delta_{n+1}) \subseteq B(f_n, \delta_n)$.

<1>3. Pointwise convergence and limit definition:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For each fixed $x \in X$, the sequence $(f_n(x))_{n=1}^\infty$ in $Y$ satisfies:
        $$d(f_{n+1}(x), f_n(x)) < \frac{\delta_n(x)}{3} \le \frac{\delta_1(x)}{3^n} \le \frac{1}{3^n}.$$
    <2>2. For $m > n$:
        $$d(f_m(x), f_n(x)) \le \sum_{k=n}^{m-1} d(f_{k+1}(x), f_k(x)) < \delta_n(x) \sum_{j=1}^\infty 3^{-j} = \frac{\delta_n(x)}{2}.$$
    <2>3. Since $(Y, d)$ is complete, $(f_n(x))_{n=1}^\infty$ is Cauchy and converges to a unique point $f(x) = \lim_{n \to \infty} f_n(x) \in Y$.
    <2>4. Taking $m \to \infty$ yields $d(f(x), f_n(x)) \le \frac{1}{2}\delta_n(x) < \delta_n(x)$ for all $x \in X$.

<1>4. Continuity of the limit function $f$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $x_0 \in X$ and $\varepsilon > 0$.
    <2>2. Choose $N$ large enough so that $\delta_N(x_0) < \varepsilon/3$.
    <2>3. By continuity of $\delta_N$ and $f_N$, there is an open neighborhood $W$ of $x_0$ such that $\delta_N(x) < \varepsilon/3$ and $d(f_N(x), f_N(x_0)) < \varepsilon/3$ for all $x \in W$.
    <2>4. For all $x \in W$:
        $$d(f(x), f(x_0)) \le d(f(x), f_N(x)) + d(f_N(x), f_N(x_0)) + d(f_N(x_0), f(x_0)) < \frac{\delta_N(x)}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{3} + \frac{\delta_N(x_0)}{2} < \varepsilon.$$
    <2>5. Thus $f: X \to Y$ is continuous, so $f \in \mathcal{C}(X, Y)$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $f \in \bigcap_{n=1}^\infty B(f_n, \delta_n) \subseteq U_0 \cap \bigcap_{n=1}^\infty D_n \neq \varnothing$.
    Thus $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ is a Baire space in the fine topology. Q.E.D.
:::
