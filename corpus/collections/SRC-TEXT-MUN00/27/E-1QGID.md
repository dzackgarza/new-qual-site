::: {.exercise}

Recall that $\RR_K$ denotes $\RR$ in the $K$-topology.

(a) Show that $[0, 1]$ is not compact as a subspace of $\RR_K$.

(b) Show that $\RR_K$ is connected.
[Hint: $(-\infty, 0)$ and $(0, \infty)$ inherit their usual topologies as subspaces of $\RR_K$.]

(c) Show that $\RR_K$ is not path connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained published solution, **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 3* (1 January 2021), Exercise 27.3, PDF pp. 16--17** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch3.pdf`), restructured into steps; the citation in their part (b) is repaired, where $\overline K$ there means the closure of $K$ in the usual topology.

Throughout, $K=\{1/n \mid n\in\ZZ_+\}$ and $\RR_K$ is $\RR$ with the $K$-topology, generated by the open intervals together with the sets $(a,b)-K$ (Munkres, *Topology* (2000), §13); $[0,1]$ and the rays carry their subspace topologies.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a}
(a) $[0,1]$ is not compact as a subspace of $\RR_K$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #a-finer}
The topology that $[0,1]$ inherits from $\RR_K$ is strictly finer than the one it inherits from $\RR$, and under the inherited usual topology $[0,1]$ is compact and Hausdorff.

::: pf-proof
Every open interval is a basis element of the $K$-topology, so the usual topology is contained in it, and intersecting with $[0,1]$ preserves the containment. The containment is strict: $(-1,2)-K$ is a basis element of $\RR_K$, so $[0,1]-K=[0,1]\cap((-1,2)-K)$ is open in the inherited $K$-topology, and it is not open in the inherited usual topology, for it contains $0$ and every usual neighbourhood of $0$ in $[0,1]$ contains an interval $[0,\varepsilon)$, which meets $K$. Under the inherited usual topology $[0,1]$ is closed and bounded in $\RR$, hence compact (Theorem 27.3), and it is Hausdorff because that topology is metrizable.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-contradiction}
If $[0,1]$ were compact as a subspace of $\RR_K$, the two inherited topologies would coincide.

::: pf-proof
Under that supposition $[0,1]$ would be compact and Hausdorff under both inherited topologies, and the two are comparable, the inherited $K$-topology being the finer. Exercise 26.1(b) says that a set compact Hausdorff under two comparable topologies carries equal topologies; here that is the identity map from the finer to the coarser, a continuous bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space, which is a homeomorphism (Theorem 26.6). So the inherited topologies would be equal, which step [](#a-finer){.pf-ref} denies.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
(b) $\RR_K$ is connected.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #b-positive}
$(0,\infty)$ inherits the usual topology from $\RR_K$ and is connected.

::: pf-proof
A basis element of $\RR_K$ intersected with $(0,\infty)$ is $(a,b)\cap(0,\infty)$, or $((a,b)-K)\cap(0,\infty)=(a,b)\cap(0,\infty)-K$ because $K\subset(0,\infty)$. Both are open in the usual topology of $(0,\infty)$: the first is an interval, and the second is a usual open set minus $K$, which is closed in $(0,\infty)$ in the usual topology because its only limit point in $\RR$ is $0$, a point outside $(0,\infty)$. Conversely every usual open subset of $(0,\infty)$ is a union of the intervals $(a,b)\cap(0,\infty)$, which are open in the inherited $K$-topology. So the inherited topology is the usual one, and $(0,\infty)$ is connected in it (Corollary 24.2).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-negative}
$(-\infty,0)$ inherits the usual topology from $\RR_K$ and is connected.

::: pf-proof
Here $K\cap(-\infty,0)$ is empty, so a basis element of $\RR_K$ intersected with $(-\infty,0)$ is $(a,b)\cap(-\infty,0)$, and conversely every such interval is a basis element; the inherited topology is the usual one, in which the ray is connected (Corollary 24.2).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-closures}
$[0,\infty)$ and $(-\infty,0]$ are connected subspaces of $\RR_K$.

::: pf-proof
The point $0$ lies in the closure of $(0,\infty)$ in $\RR_K$: a basis element containing $0$ is an interval $(a,b)$ with $a<0<b$, or $(a,b)-K$ with $a<0<b$, and each contains positive reals. Hence $(0,\infty)\subset[0,\infty)\subset\closure{(0,\infty)}$, so $[0,\infty)$ is connected by Theorem 23.4, using step [](#b-positive){.pf-ref}. The same argument with $(-\infty,0)$ in place of $(0,\infty)$ makes $(-\infty,0]$ connected, using step [](#b-negative){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-union}
$\RR_K$ is connected.

::: pf-proof
$\RR_K=[0,\infty)\cup(-\infty,0]$ is a union of two connected subspaces containing the common point $0$, hence is connected by Theorem 23.3, the connectedness of both pieces being step [](#b-closures){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c}
(c) $\RR_K$ is not path connected.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #c-image}
If $f:[0,1]\to\RR_K$ is continuous with $f(0)=0$ and $f(1)=1$, then $[0,1]\subset f([0,1])$.

::: pf-proof
The usual topology is contained in the $K$-topology, so $f$ is continuous as a map into $\RR$. The intermediate value theorem (Theorem 24.3) applied to the connected space $[0,1]$ and the points $0$ and $1$ then says that every real between $f(0)=0$ and $f(1)=1$ is a value of $f$, which is the asserted containment.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-closed}
$[0,1]$ is closed in $\RR_K$.

::: pf-proof
Its complement $\RR-[0,1]=(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)$ is open in $\RR_K$: the first ray is the union of the basis elements $(-n,0)$ for $n\in\ZZ_+$, and the second is the union of the basis elements $(1,n)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-contradiction}
No such path $f$ exists, and $\RR_K$ is connected but not path connected.

::: pf-proof
If $f$ were such a path, $f([0,1])$ would be compact in $\RR_K$ as the image of a compact space under a continuous map (Theorem 26.5), and $[0,1]=[0,1]\cap f([0,1])$ would be a closed subset of it by step [](#c-closed){.pf-ref}, hence compact in $\RR_K$ (Theorem 26.2). That contradicts part (a), step [](#a){.pf-ref}. So there is no path in $\RR_K$ from $0$ to $1$, and $\RR_K$ is not path connected, while it is connected by part (b), step [](#b){.pf-ref}.
:::
