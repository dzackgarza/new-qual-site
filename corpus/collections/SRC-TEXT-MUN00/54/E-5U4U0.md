::: {.exercise}

Consider the covering map $p \times p: \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \to S^1 \times S^1$ of Example 4 of §53. Consider the path

$$
f(t) = (\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t) \times (\cos 4\pi t, \sin 4\pi t)
$$

in $S^1 \times S^1$.
Sketch what $f$ looks like when $S^1 \times S^1$ is identified with the doughnut surface $D$.
Find a lifting $\overline{f}$ of $f$ to $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$, and sketch it.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Explicitly determine the lifting of the loop $f(t) = (\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t, \cos 4\pi t, \sin 4\pi t)$ on the torus $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$ under the universal covering map $p \times p: \mathbb{R}^2 \to T^2$, and describe its geometric trajectory.

<1>1. Geometric description on the torus surface $D$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Under the standard identification of $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$ with the surface of a doughnut in $\mathbb{R}^3$, the first factor parameterizes the longitudinal direction (going once around the central donut hole) and the second factor parameterizes the meridional direction (going around the circular cross-section of the tube).
    <2>2. As $t$ increases from $0$ to $1$, the first coordinate $p_1(f(t)) = (\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t)$ completes $1$ full revolution around the longitudinal circle.
    <2>3. Concurrently, the second coordinate $p_2(f(t)) = (\cos 4\pi t, \sin 4\pi t)$ completes $2$ full revolutions around the meridional circle.
    <2>4. Thus, the loop $f$ is a $(1, 2)$-curve on the torus, winding once around the central hole while wrapping twice through the tube.

<1>2. Explicit formula for the path lifting $\overline{f}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The standard covering map $p: \mathbb{R} \to S^1$ is given by $p(x) = (\cos 2\pi x, \sin 2\pi x)$, so the product covering map is $(p \times p)(x, y) = (p(x), p(y))$.
    <2>2. Let $\overline{f}: [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be defined by:
        $$\overline{f}(t) = (t, 2t) \quad \text{for } t \in [0, 1].$$
    <2>3. **Continuity:** The components $\overline{f}_1(t) = t$ and $\overline{f}_2(t) = 2t$ are linear, hence $\overline{f}$ is continuous.
    <2>4. **Lifting property:** For every $t \in [0, 1]$:
        $$(p \times p)(\overline{f}(t)) = (p(t), p(2t)) = (\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t) \times (\cos 4\pi t, \sin 4\pi t) = f(t).$$
    <2>5. **Initial point:** $\overline{f}(0) = (0, 0)$.
    <2>6. By the Path Lifting Lemma (Munkres Lemma 54.1), this is the unique lifting of $f$ starting at the origin $(0, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
    <2>7. (Any other lifting starting at an arbitrary integer lattice point $(n, m) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ is given by $\overline{f}_{(n, m)}(t) = (n + t, m + 2t)$.)

<1>3. Geometric description in the universal covering plane $\mathbb{R}^2$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The lifting $\overline{f}(t) = (t, 2t)$ is the straight line segment in the Cartesian plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ connecting the origin $(0, 0)$ to the lattice point $(1, 2)$.
    <2>2. The endpoint $\overline{f}(1) = (1, 2)$ reflects the winding numbers $(1, 2) \in \pi_1(S^1 \times S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$. Q.E.D.
:::
