::: {.exercise}

Consider the covering map $p \times p: \RR \times \RR \to S^1 \times S^1$ of Example 4 of §53. Consider the path

$$
f(t) = (\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t) \times (\cos 4\pi t, \sin 4\pi t)
$$

in $S^1 \times S^1$.
Sketch what $f$ looks like when $S^1 \times S^1$ is identified with the doughnut surface $D$.
Find a lifting $\overline{f}$ of $f$ to $\RR \times \RR$, and sketch it.
:::

::: {.solution}
The lift is the one the published solution of dbFin, "Section 54: Problem 5 Solution", gives, $\bar f(t)=(t+z,2t+z')$ with $(z,z')\in\ZZ^2$. The exercise is Munkres, *Topology* (2000), Section 54, Exercise 5, printed p. 348; the covering is Example 4 of §53, printed p. 339, the uniqueness of lifts is Lemma 54.1, printed p. 342, and the degree of a loop in $S^1$ is the displacement of its lift to $\RR$, Theorem 54.5, printed p. 345.

Throughout, $p(x)=(\cos2\pi x,\sin2\pi x)$, the covering is $p\times p\colon\RR^2\to S^1\times S^1$, and $f(t)=p(t)\times p(2t)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #lift}
$\bar f(t)=(t,2t)$ is the lift of $f$ with $\bar f(0)=(0,0)$, and for every $(n,m)\in\ZZ^2$ the lift with $\bar f_{(n,m)}(0)=(n,m)$ is $\bar f_{(n,m)}(t)=(n+t,m+2t)$.

::: pf-proof
$(p\times p)(n+t,m+2t)=(p(n+t),p(m+2t))=((\cos2\pi t,\sin2\pi t),(\cos4\pi t,\sin4\pi t))=f(t)$, because $\cos$ and $\sin$ have period $1$, and the value at $t=0$ is $(n,m)$. A lift with a prescribed initial point is unique (Lemma 54.1, printed p. 342).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #winding}
$f$ winds once around the first factor and twice around the second, and $\bar f(1)-\bar f(0)=(1,2)$ records those two degrees.

::: pf-proof
The first component of $f$ is the loop $p(t)$ in $S^1$, whose lift to $\RR$ under $p$ is $t\mapsto t$, with displacement $1$; the second is $p(2t)$, whose lift is $t\mapsto 2t$, with displacement $2$. By Theorem 54.5, printed p. 345, the degree of a loop in $S^1$ is the displacement of its lift, so the degrees are $1$ and $2$, and the two entries of $\bar f(1)-\bar f(0)$ are those displacements by step [](#lift){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark title="The sketches"}
On the doughnut surface the loop $f$ is the $(1,2)$-curve: it runs once around the central hole while wrapping twice around the tube, so it closes up after one passage round the hole. Under the covering $p\times p$ it lifts to the straight segment in $\RR^2$ from $(0,0)$ to $(1,2)$, and the other lifts are the translates of that segment by the integer lattice; the segment meets no other lattice point on the way, which is why $f$ is not null-homotopic.
:::
