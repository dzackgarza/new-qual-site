::: {.exercise}

Let $p: E \to B$ be a covering map.
Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be paths in $B$ with $\alpha(1) = \beta(0)$; let $\tilde{\alpha}$ and $\tilde{\beta}$ be liftings of them such that $\tilde{\alpha}(1) = \tilde{\beta}(0)$.
Show that $\tilde{\alpha} * \tilde{\beta}$ is a lifting of $\alpha * \beta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The computation is that of the published solution of dbFin, "Section 54: Problem 3 Solution": the projection of the concatenation is checked on the two halves of the interval, and the pasting lemma is what makes the concatenation continuous. The exercise is Munkres, *Topology* (2000), Section 54, Exercise 3, printed p. 347; the pasting lemma is Theorem 18.3, printed p. 108, and a covering map is defined on printed p. 336.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
$\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta}$ is a continuous path in $E$, with $(\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta})(0)=\tilde{\alpha}(0)$ and $(\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta})(1)=\tilde{\beta}(1)$.

::: pf-proof
By the definition of the path product, $(\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta})(t)=\tilde{\alpha}(2t)$ for $0\le t\le\frac12$ and $(\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta})(t)=\tilde{\beta}(2t-1)$ for $\frac12\le t\le1$. The two formulas agree at $t=\frac12$, because $\tilde{\alpha}(1)=\tilde{\beta}(0)$, and the two closed intervals cover $[0,1]$, so the pasting lemma (Munkres, Theorem 18.3, printed p. 108) makes the concatenation continuous; the values at $0$ and at $1$ are those of $\tilde{\alpha}$ and of $\tilde{\beta}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}
$p\circ(\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta})=\alpha*\beta$.

::: pf-proof
For $0\le t\le\frac12$, $p((\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta})(t))=p(\tilde{\alpha}(2t))=\alpha(2t)=(\alpha*\beta)(t)$, because $p\circ\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha$; for $\frac12\le t\le1$, $p((\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta})(t))=p(\tilde{\beta}(2t-1))=\beta(2t-1)=(\alpha*\beta)(t)$, because $p\circ\tilde{\beta}=\beta$. The two halves cover $[0,1]$, so the two maps agree there.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref} exhibit a continuous map of $[0,1]$ into $E$ that projects to $\alpha*\beta$, which is what it means for $\tilde{\alpha}*\tilde{\beta}$ to be a lifting of $\alpha*\beta$.
:::
