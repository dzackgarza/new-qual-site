::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ and $X'$ denote a single set in the two topologies $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}'$, respectively.
Let $i: X' \to X$ be the identity function.

(a) Show that $i$ is continuous if and only if $\mathcal{T}'$ is finer than $\mathcal{T}$.

(b) Show that $i$ is a homeomorphism if and only if $\mathcal{T}' = \mathcal{T}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) The identity $i:X'\to X$ is continuous exactly when every set open in $X$ has open preimage in $X'$. But $i^{-1}(U)=U$, so this says precisely
\[
\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T'.
\]
Thus $i$ is continuous iff $\mathcal T'$ is finer than $\mathcal T$.

(b) The identity is a homeomorphism iff both $i:X'\to X$ and its inverse identity $i^{-1}:X\to X'$ are continuous. By part (a), these two conditions are
\[
\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T'\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal T'\subseteq\mathcal T.
\]
Hence $i$ is a homeomorphism iff $\mathcal T=\mathcal T'$.
:::
