::: {.exercise}

Let $X$ and $X'$ denote a single set in the two topologies $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}'$, respectively.
Let $i: X' \to X$ be the identity function.

(a) Show that $i$ is continuous if and only if $\mathcal{T}'$ is finer than $\mathcal{T}$.

(b) Show that $i$ is a homeomorphism if and only if $\mathcal{T}' = \mathcal{T}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 18.3, PDF p. 11** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`); both parts are theirs. Whitman, printed p. 180, gives the same.

Let $i:X'\to X$ be the identity; it is a bijection with $i^{-1}=i$ as a map of sets, and $i^{-1}(V)=V$ for every $V\subset X$.

(a) $i$ is continuous if and only if for every $V\in\mathcal{T}$ the set $i^{-1}(V)=V$ lies in $\mathcal{T}'$, that is if and only if $\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}'$: $\mathcal{T}'$ is finer than $\mathcal{T}$.

(b) $i$ is a homeomorphism if and only if $i$ and $i^{-1}$ are both continuous, which by (a) applied to $i$ and to $i^{-1}$ is the conjunction of $\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}'$ and $\mathcal{T}'\subset\mathcal{T}$, that is $\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}'$. (Here $i^{-1}$ is the identity $X\to X'$, so applying (a) to it reverses the two topologies.)
:::
