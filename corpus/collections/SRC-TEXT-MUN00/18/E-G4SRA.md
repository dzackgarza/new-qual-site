::: {.exercise}

Let $f: A \to B$ and $g: C \to D$ be continuous functions.
Let us define a map $f \times g: A \times C \to B \times D$ by the equation

$$
(f \times g)(a \times c) = f(a) \times g(c).
$$

Show that $f \times g$ is continuous.
:::

::: {.solution}

The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 18.10, PDF p. 13** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`); the computation of the inverse image is theirs. Dan Whitman, *Solutions Manual*, printed p. 187, proves the same statement pointwise through Theorem 18.1.

Let $f:A\to B$ and $g:C\to D$ be continuous and $(f\times g)(a,c)=\big(f(a),g(c)\big)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis}
The sets $U\times V$ with $U$ open in $B$ and $V$ open in $D$ form a basis for the topology of $B\times D$.

::: pf-proof
The product topology on $B\times D$ is defined as the topology generated by the collection of all sets $U\times V$ with $U$ open in $B$ and $V$ open in $D$; those sets are the basis elements of $B\times D$ (§16, printed p. 87).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #preimage}
$\big(f\times g\big)^{-1}(U\times V)=f^{-1}(U)\times g^{-1}(V)$ for $U\subseteq B$, $V\subseteq D$.

::: pf-proof
For $a\in A$, $c\in C$ one has $(f\times g)(a,c)\in U\times V$ exactly when $f(a)\in U$ and $g(c)\in V$, that is exactly when $a\in f^{-1}(U)$ and $c\in g^{-1}(V)$. So the two sets contain the same pairs $(a,c)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #preimage-open}
$\big(f\times g\big)^{-1}(U\times V)$ is open in $A\times C$ for open $U$, $V$.

::: pf-proof
$f^{-1}(U)$ is open in $A$ and $g^{-1}(V)$ is open in $C$ because $f$ and $g$ are continuous, so $f^{-1}(U)\times g^{-1}(V)$ is a basis element of $A\times C$, hence open; by step [](#preimage){.pf-ref} that set is $\big(f\times g\big)^{-1}(U\times V)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
The preimage under $f\times g$ of every basis element of $B\times D$ is open in $A\times C$ by steps [](#basis){.pf-ref} and [](#preimage-open){.pf-ref}. A map is continuous when the preimage of every basis element of the target is open, since an open set is a union of basis elements and preimages commute with unions (Theorem 18.1(1)$\Leftrightarrow$(3), printed p. 104). So $f\times g$ is continuous.
:::
