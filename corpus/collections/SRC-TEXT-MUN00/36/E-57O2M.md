::: {.exercise}

Consider the seventeen properties listed in Exercise 1 of the Supplementary Exercises (Review of the Basics).

Which of these properties are preserved under finite products?
Countable products?
Arbitrary products?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Classify the preservation behavior of the 17 fundamental topological properties under finite, countable, and arbitrary Cartesian products equipped with the product topology.

<1>1. Complete classification table:
    - **Preserved under arbitrary products (7):**
      1. $T_1$
      2. Hausdorff ($T_2$)
      3. Regular ($T_3$)
      4. Completely regular ($T_{3\frac{1}{2}}$)
      5. Compact (by Tychonoff's Theorem)
      6. Connected
      7. Path-connected
    - **Preserved under countable products, but NOT arbitrary (5):**
      8. First-countable
      9. Second-countable
      10. Separable (by Hewitt-Marczewski-Pondiczery theorem up to cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$)
      11. Metrizable
      12. Sequentially compact
    - **Preserved under finite products, but NOT countable (2):**
      13. Locally compact (an infinite product is locally compact $\iff$ almost all factors are compact)
      14. Locally connected (an infinite product is locally connected $\iff$ almost all factors are connected)
    - **NOT preserved even under finite products (3):**
      15. Normal ($T_4$) (e.g. Sorgenfrey plane $\mathbb{R}_l \times \mathbb{R}_l$)
      16. Lindelöf (e.g. $\mathbb{R}_l \times \mathbb{R}_l$)
      17. Limit point compact

<1>2. Proof outlines for arbitrary products:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Separation axioms ($T_1, T_2, T_3, T_{3\frac{1}{2}}$):** Points and coordinates can be separated in individual factor spaces; taking cylinder open sets $\pi_\alpha^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ transfers separation to $\prod X_\alpha$.
    <2>2. **Compactness:** Proved by Tychonoff's Theorem.
    <2>3. **Connectedness & Path-connectedness:** A product is path-connected (resp. connected) by mapping coordinate paths $\gamma_\alpha(t)$ (resp. using dense unions of finite cylinder sets).

<1>3. Proof outlines and counterexamples for countable/finite/failing properties:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Countability and Metrizability (Countable YES, Arbitrary NO):** A countable product of metric spaces is metrizable via $D(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^{-n} \bar{d}_n(x_n, y_n)$. Uncountable products such as $\{0, 1\}^{\mathbb{R}}$ fail to be first-countable at any point.
    <2>2. **Local Compactness & Local Connectedness (Finite YES, Infinite NO):** For $\mathbb{R}^\omega = \prod_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{R}$, no basic open set has compact closure because basic open sets equal $\mathbb{R}$ in all but finitely many coordinates.
    <2>3. **Normality & Lindelöf (Finite NO):** The Sorgenfrey line $\mathbb{R}_l$ is normal and Lindelöf, but the Sorgenfrey plane $\mathbb{R}_l \times \mathbb{R}_l$ contains the closed discrete anti-diagonal $L = \{(x, -x)\}$, rendering it neither Lindelöf nor normal. Q.E.D.
:::
