::: {.exercise}

Prove that every manifold is regular and hence metrizable.
Where do you use the Hausdorff condition?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Definition of topological manifold:
<2>1. An $m$-dimensional topological manifold $M$ is a Hausdorff space with a countable basis that is locally Euclidean of dimension $m$ (every point $x \in M$ has an open neighborhood homeomorphic to an open ball in $\mathbb{R}^m$).
Proof: definition of topological manifold.

<1>2. Proof that $M$ is regular:
<2>1. A $T_1$ space is regular if and only if for every point $x \in M$ and every open neighborhood $V$ of $x$, there exists an open set $W$ containing $x$ such that $\overline{W} \subseteq V$.
Proof: characterization of regular spaces.
<2>2. Let $x \in M$ and let $V$ be an open neighborhood of $x$.
Since $M$ is locally Euclidean, there is an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ and a homeomorphism $\phi: U \to B(0, 1) \subset \mathbb{R}^m$ such that $\phi(x) = 0$.
The set $\phi(U \cap V)$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^m$ and contains $0$.
Proof: definition of locally Euclidean space.
<2>3. Choose $r \in (0, 1)$ sufficiently small so that the closed Euclidean ball $\overline{B(0, r)} \subset \phi(U \cap V)$.
Let $W = \phi^{-1}(B(0, r))$ and $K = \phi^{-1}(\overline{B(0, r)})$.
Since $\overline{B(0, r)}$ is compact in $\mathbb{R}^m$ and $\phi^{-1}$ is continuous, $K$ is a compact subset of $M$.
Proof: continuous image of a compact set is compact.
<2>4. **Role of the Hausdorff condition:** Because $M$ is Hausdorff, every compact subset of $M$ is closed in $M$.
Therefore $K$ is closed in $M$.
Since $W \subseteq K$ and $K$ is closed, the closure of $W$ in $M$ satisfies:
\[
\overline{W} \subseteq K \subseteq U \cap V \subseteq V.
\]
Thus $M$ is regular.
Proof: compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed.

<1>3. Proof of metrizability via Urysohn's Metrization Theorem:
<2>1. By definition, a manifold $M$ has a countable basis (second-countable).
By <1>2, $M$ is regular.
Proof: definition of manifold and <1>2.
<2>2. By the Urysohn Metrization Theorem, every regular, second-countable space is metrizable.
Therefore $M$ is metrizable.
Proof: Urysohn's Metrization Theorem.

<1>4. Discussion of where Hausdorff is used:
<2>1. The Hausdorff condition was used crucially in <2>4 to ensure that the compact set $K = \phi^{-1}(\overline{B(0, r)})$ is closed in $M$, ensuring $\overline{W} \subseteq K \subseteq V$.
Without the Hausdorff condition, locally Euclidean second-countable spaces (such as the line with two origins) fail to be Hausdorff, fail to be regular (the two origins cannot be separated), and are not metrizable.
Proof: counterexample (line with two origins).

<1>5. Conclusion:
Manifolds are regular and metrizable, with the Hausdorff condition guaranteeing that compact Euclidean neighborhoods are closed. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 through <1>4.
:::
