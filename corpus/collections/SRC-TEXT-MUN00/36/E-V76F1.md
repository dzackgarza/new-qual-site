::: {.exercise}

Prove that every manifold is regular and hence metrizable.
Where do you use the Hausdorff condition?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #m-is-regular}
$M$ is regular.

::: pf-proof
Let $x \in M$ and let $V$ be an open neighbourhood of $x$. By the definition of a manifold, $M$ is locally Euclidean, so there is an open $U \ni x$ and a homeomorphism $\phi \colon U \to B(0,1) \subseteq \RR^m$ with $\phi(x) = 0$; the set $\phi(U \cap V)$ is open in $\RR^m$ and contains $0$. Choose $r \in (0,1)$ with the closed ball $\overline{B(0,r)} \subseteq \phi(U \cap V)$, possible because $\phi(U \cap V)$ is open, and put $W = \phi^{-1}(B(0,r))$ and $K = \phi^{-1}(\overline{B(0,r)})$. The closed ball is compact and $\phi^{-1}$ is continuous, so $K$ is compact; and $M$ is Hausdorff by definition, so the compact set $K$ is closed in $M$. Since $W \subseteq K$ and $K$ is closed, the closure of $W$ lies in $K$, so
$$\overline{W} \subseteq K \subseteq U \cap V \subseteq V.$$
A $T_1$ space is regular exactly when every point and every open neighbourhood of it admit such a $W$, and $M$ is $T_1$ because it is Hausdorff; hence $M$ is regular.
:::

:::

::: pf-step

$M$ is metrizable.

::: pf-proof
A manifold is second countable and Hausdorff, hence regular by step [](#m-is-regular){.pf-ref}; the Urysohn metrization theorem says that a second-countable regular space is metrizable, so $M$ admits a metric inducing its topology.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hausdorff-role}
The Hausdorff condition is used exactly where a compact set is concluded to be closed.

::: pf-proof
In the regularity step the set $K = \phi^{-1}(\overline{B(0,r)})$ is compact, and it is closed because compact subsets of a Hausdorff space are closed; that closedness is what gives $\overline{W} \subseteq K \subseteq V$. Without a Hausdorff hypothesis the implication fails: the line with two origins is locally Euclidean and second countable, its two origins have no disjoint neighbourhoods, so it is not regular, and it is not metrizable either, a metric space being Hausdorff.
:::
