::: {.exercise}

The Hausdorff condition is an essential part of the definition of a manifold; it is not implied by the other parts of the definition.
Consider the following space.
Let $X$ be the union of the set $\mathbb{R} - \ts{0}$ and the two-point set $\ts{p, q}$.
Topologize $X$ by taking as basis the collection of all open intervals in $\mathbb{R}$ that do not contain 0, along with all sets of the form $(-a, 0) \cup \ts{p} \cup (0, a)$ and all sets of the form $(-a, 0) \cup \ts{q} \cup (0, a)$, for $a > 0$.
The space $X$ is called the line with two origins.

(a) Check that this is a basis for a topology.

(b) Show that each of the spaces $X - \ts{p}$ and $X - \ts{q}$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$.

(c) Show that $X$ satisfies the $T_1$ axiom, but is not Hausdorff.

(d) Show that $X$ satisfies all the conditions for a 1-manifold except for the Hausdorff condition.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove the topological properties of the line with two origins $X = (\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}) \cup \{p, q\}$.

<1>1. Part (a): Basis verification.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Covering: For any $x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$, $x \in (x - \frac{|x|}{2}, x + \frac{|x|}{2}) \subset \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$. For $p$ and $q$, $p \in (-1, 0) \cup \{p\} \cup (0, 1)$ and $q \in (-1, 0) \cup \{q\} \cup (0, 1)$.
    <2>2. Intersections:
        - The intersection of two intervals in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ is an interval in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ or empty.
        - The intersection of an interval in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ with a set $U_a(p) = (-a, 0) \cup \{p\} \cup (0, a)$ is open in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$.
        - $U_a(p) \cap U_b(p) = U_{\min\{a, b\}}(p)$, and similarly $U_a(q) \cap U_b(q) = U_{\min\{a, b\}}(q)$.
        - $U_a(p) \cap U_b(q) = (-\min\{a, b\}, 0) \cup (0, \min\{a, b\})$, which is a union of two basic open intervals in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$.
    <2>3. Hence the collection forms a basis for a topology on $X$.

<1>2. Part (b): Homeomorphisms with $\mathbb{R}$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define $f: X \setminus \{q\} \to \mathbb{R}$ by $f(x) = x$ for $x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ and $f(p) = 0$.
    <2>2. $f$ is a bijection.
    <2>3. $f$ maps basic intervals $(a, b) \subset \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ to the identical intervals in $\mathbb{R}$, and maps $U_a(p)$ to $(-a, a)$.
    <2>4. Since open intervals not containing $0$ together with the symmetric intervals $(-a, a)$ generate the standard Euclidean topology on $\mathbb{R}$, $f$ is a homeomorphism.
    <2>5. By the identical argument with $g(x) = x$ for $x \neq 0$ and $g(q) = 0$, $X \setminus \{p\} \cong \mathbb{R}$.

<1>3. Part (c): $X$ is $T_1$ but not Hausdorff.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $T_1$ axiom: For any $x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$, $\{x\}$ is closed because its complement is open. For $p$, $X \setminus \{p\} = X \setminus \{q\} \setminus \{p\} \cup (X \setminus \{p\})$ is open (since $X \setminus \{p\} \cong \mathbb{R}$ is open in $X$), so $\{p\}$ is closed. Similarly $\{q\}$ is closed. Thus $X$ is $T_1$.
    <2>2. Not Hausdorff: Let $U$ be any open neighborhood of $p$ and $V$ any open neighborhood of $q$.
    <2>3. $U$ contains a basic neighborhood $U_a(p)$ and $V$ contains a basic neighborhood $U_b(q)$ for some $a, b > 0$.
    <2>4. The intersection $U \cap V \supseteq U_a(p) \cap U_b(q) = (-\min\{a, b\}, 0) \cup (0, \min\{a, b\}) \neq \emptyset$.
    <2>5. Thus $p$ and $q$ cannot be separated by disjoint open sets, so $X$ is not Hausdorff.

<1>4. Part (d): $X$ is a non-Hausdorff 1-manifold.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Locally 1-Euclidean: By <1>2, every point $x \in X \setminus \{q\}$ has the open neighborhood $X \setminus \{q\} \cong \mathbb{R}$, and $q$ has the open neighborhood $X \setminus \{p\} \cong \mathbb{R}$. Thus every point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^1$.
    <2>2. Second-countable: The countable collection of intervals $(a, b)$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $0 \notin [a, b]$, together with $U_{1/n}(p)$ and $U_{1/n}(q)$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}_+$, forms a countable basis for $X$.
    <2>3. Therefore $X$ is locally 1-Euclidean and second-countable, satisfying all axioms of a 1-manifold except the Hausdorff condition. Q.E.D.
:::
