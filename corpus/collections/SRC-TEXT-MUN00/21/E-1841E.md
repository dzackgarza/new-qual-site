::: {.exercise}

Prove the following standard facts about infinite series:

(a) Show that if $(s_n)$ is a bounded sequence of real numbers and $s_n \leq s_{n+1}$ for each $n$, then $(s_n)$ converges.

(b) Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence of real numbers; define

$$
s_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i.
$$

If $s_n \to s$, we say that the infinite series

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i
$$

converges to $s$ also.
Show that if $\sum a_i$ converges to $s$ and $\sum b_i$ converges to $t$, then $\sum (ca_i + b_i)$ converges to $cs + t$.

(c) Prove the comparison test for infinite series: if $\abs{a_i} \leq b_i$ for each $i$, and if the series $\sum b_i$ converges, then the series $\sum a_i$ converges.
[Hint: Show that the series $\sum \abs{a_i}$ and $\sum c_i$ converge, where $c_i = \abs{a_i} + a_i$.]

(d) Given a sequence of functions $f_n: X \to \RR$, let

$$
s_n(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(x).
$$

Prove the Weierstrass $M$-test for uniform convergence: if $\abs{f_i(x)} \leq M_i$ for all $x \in X$ and all $i$, and if the series $\sum M_i$ converges, then the sequence $(s_n)$ converges uniformly to a function $s$.
[Hint: Let $r_n = \sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty} M_i$. Show that if $k > n$, then $\abs{s_k(x) - s_n(x)} \leq r_n$; conclude that $\abs{s(x) - s_n(x)} \leq r_n$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 21.11, PDF p. 26** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`); the four parts are theirs, following the book's hints.

(a) Let $(s_{n})$ be bounded and nondecreasing, and put $s=\sup_{n}s_{n}$, which exists by the least upper bound property of $\RR$. Given $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N$ with $s-\varepsilon<s_{N}\le s$; then for $n\ge N$, $s-\varepsilon<s_{N}\le s_{n}\le s$, so $\abs{s_{n}-s}<\varepsilon$. Hence $s_{n}\to s$, that is, an increasing bounded sequence converges.

(b) Let $s_{n}=\sum_{i\le n}a_{i}\to s$ and $t_{n}=\sum_{i\le n}b_{i}\to t$. Then $\sum_{i\le n}(ca_{i}+b_{i})=cs_{n}+t_{n}$, and $cs_{n}+t_{n}\to cs+t$ by the arithmetic of convergent sequences (Exercise 21.5). So $\sum(ca_{i}+b_{i})$ converges to $cs+t$.

(c) Let $\abs{a_{i}}\le b_{i}$ for all $i$ and let $\sum b_{i}$ converge, say to $t$. Since $b_{i}\ge0$, the partial sums $t_{n}$ are nondecreasing and bounded by $t$, so the partial sums $r_{n}=\sum_{i\le n}\abs{a_{i}}$ are nondecreasing and bounded by $t$; by (a), $\sum\abs{a_{i}}$ converges. Next $c_{i}=\abs{a_{i}}+a_{i}$ satisfies $0\le c_{i}\le2\abs{a_{i}}$, so its partial sums are nondecreasing and bounded by $2\sum\abs{a_{i}}$, and $\sum c_{i}$ converges by (a). Then by (b),
$$
a_{i}=c_{i}-\abs{a_{i}}\quad\text{gives}\quad \sum a_{i}=\sum c_{i}-\sum\abs{a_{i}},
$$
so $\sum a_{i}$ converges.

(d) Let $\abs{f_{i}(x)}\le M_{i}$ for all $x\in X$ and all $i$, with $\sum M_{i}$ convergent, and let $s_{n}=\sum_{i\le n}f_{i}$. For each fixed $x$ the comparison test (c) shows that $\sum f_{i}(x)$ converges; let $s(x)$ be its sum, so $s_{n}\to s$ pointwise. Put $r_{n}=\sum_{i>n}M_{i}$, which tends to $0$ because $\sum M_{i}$ converges. For $k>n$ and any $x\in X$,
$$
\abs{s_{k}(x)-s_{n}(x)}=\abs{\sum_{i=n+1}^{k}f_{i}(x)}\le\sum_{i=n+1}^{k}\abs{f_{i}(x)}\le\sum_{i=n+1}^{k}M_{i}\le r_{n}.
$$
Letting $k\to\infty$ with $n$ fixed gives $\abs{s(x)-s_{n}(x)}\le r_{n}$ for every $x\in X$, since $s_{k}(x)\to s(x)$. So $\sup_{x}\abs{s(x)-s_{n}(x)}\le r_{n}\to0$ and $s_{n}\to s$ uniformly.
:::
