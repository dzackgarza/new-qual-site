::: {.exercise}

Prove the following standard facts about infinite series:

(a) Show that if $(s_n)$ is a bounded sequence of real numbers and $s_n \leq s_{n+1}$ for each $n$, then $(s_n)$ converges.

(b) Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence of real numbers; define

$$
s_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i.
$$

If $s_n \to s$, we say that the infinite series

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i
$$

converges to $s$ also.
Show that if $\sum a_i$ converges to $s$ and $\sum b_i$ converges to $t$, then $\sum (ca_i + b_i)$ converges to $cs + t$.

(c) Prove the comparison test for infinite series: if $\abs{a_i} \leq b_i$ for each $i$, and if the series $\sum b_i$ converges, then the series $\sum a_i$ converges.
[Hint: Show that the series $\sum \abs{a_i}$ and $\sum c_i$ converge, where $c_i = \abs{a_i} + a_i$.]

(d) Given a sequence of functions $f_n: X \to \mathbb{R}$, let

$$
s_n(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(x).
$$

Prove the Weierstrass $M$-test for uniform convergence: if $\abs{f_i(x)} \leq M_i$ for all $x \in X$ and all $i$, and if the series $\sum M_i$ converges, then the sequence $(s_n)$ converges uniformly to a function $s$.
[Hint: Let $r_n = \sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty} M_i$. Show that if $k > n$, then $\abs{s_k(x) - s_n(x)} \leq r_n$; conclude that $\abs{s(x) - s_n(x)} \leq r_n$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) A bounded nondecreasing sequence $(s_n)$ converges to $s=\sup_ns_n$.

::: pf-proof
The supremum exists by the least upper bound property of $\mathbb R$.
Given $\varepsilon>0$, $s-\varepsilon$ is not an upper bound, so $s_N>s-\varepsilon$ for some $N$.
For $n\ge N$, $s-\varepsilon<s_N\le s_n\le s$, so $\abs{s_n-s}<\varepsilon$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b}
(b) If $\sum a_i=s$ and $\sum b_i=t$, then $\sum(ca_i+b_i)=cs+t$.

::: pf-proof
With $s_n=\sum_{i\le n}a_i$ and $t_n=\sum_{i\le n}b_i$, the $n$-th partial sum of $\sum(ca_i+b_i)$ is $cs_n+t_n$, which converges to $cs+t$ by the limit laws for sequences.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-c}
(c) If $\abs{a_i}\le b_i$ and $\sum b_i$ converges, then $\sum a_i$ converges.

::: pf-proof
Let $B=\sum_ib_i$ and $c_i=\abs{a_i}+a_i$, so $0\le c_i\le2\abs{a_i}\le2b_i$.
The partial sums of $\sum\abs{a_i}$ and $\sum c_i$ are nondecreasing and bounded by $B$ and $2B$, so both series converge by step [](#part-a){.pf-ref}.
Since $a_i=c_i-\abs{a_i}$, step [](#part-b){.pf-ref} with $c=-1$ shows that $\sum a_i$ converges.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-d}
(d) If $\abs{f_i(x)}\le M_i$ for all $x$ and $i$ and $\sum M_i$ converges, then $(s_n)$ converges uniformly on $X$.

::: pf-proof
For each $x$, step [](#part-c){.pf-ref} shows that $\sum_if_i(x)$ converges; call its sum $s(x)$.
Let $r_n=\sum_{i>n}M_i$, so $r_n\to0$.
For $k>n$ and $x\in X$,
$$
\abs{s_k(x)-s_n(x)}\le\sum_{i=n+1}^k\abs{f_i(x)}\le\sum_{i=n+1}^kM_i\le r_n,
$$
and letting $k\to\infty$ gives $\abs{s(x)-s_n(x)}\le r_n$.
Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ with $r_N<\varepsilon$; then $\abs{s(x)-s_n(x)}\le r_n\le r_N<\varepsilon$ for all $n\ge N$ and all $x\in X$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Steps [](#part-a){.pf-ref} through [](#part-d){.pf-ref} prove (a) through (d).
:::
