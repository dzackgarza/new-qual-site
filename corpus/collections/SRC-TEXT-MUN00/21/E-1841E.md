::: {.exercise}

Prove the following standard facts about infinite series:

(a) Show that if $(s_n)$ is a bounded sequence of real numbers and $s_n \leq s_{n+1}$ for each $n$, then $(s_n)$ converges.

(b) Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence of real numbers; define

$$
s_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i.
$$

If $s_n \to s$, we say that the infinite series

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i
$$

converges to $s$ also.
Show that if $\sum a_i$ converges to $s$ and $\sum b_i$ converges to $t$, then $\sum (ca_i + b_i)$ converges to $cs + t$.

(c) Prove the comparison test for infinite series: if $\abs{a_i} \leq b_i$ for each $i$, and if the series $\sum b_i$ converges, then the series $\sum a_i$ converges.
[Hint: Show that the series $\sum \abs{a_i}$ and $\sum c_i$ converge, where $c_i = \abs{a_i} + a_i$.]

(d) Given a sequence of functions $f_n: X \to \mathbb{R}$, let

$$
s_n(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(x).
$$

Prove the Weierstrass $M$-test for uniform convergence: if $\abs{f_i(x)} \leq M_i$ for all $x \in X$ and all $i$, and if the series $\sum M_i$ converges, then the sequence $(s_n)$ converges uniformly to a function $s$.
[Hint: Let $r_n = \sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty} M_i$. Show that if $k > n$, then $\abs{s_k(x) - s_n(x)} \leq r_n$; conclude that $\abs{s(x) - s_n(x)} \leq r_n$.]
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove the Monotone Convergence Theorem for sequences, linearity of infinite series, the comparison test, and the Weierstrass $M$-test.

<1>1. Part (a): Monotone Convergence Theorem for sequences.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The set $S = \{s_n : n \in \mathbb{Z}_+\} \subset \mathbb{R}$ is non-empty and bounded above.
    <2>2. By the least-upper-bound property of $\mathbb{R}$, $s = \sup S$ exists.
    <2>3. For any $\varepsilon > 0$, $s - \varepsilon < s$, so $s - \varepsilon$ is not an upper bound for $S$. Thus there exists $N \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ such that $s_N > s - \varepsilon$.
    <2>4. Since $(s_n)$ is non-decreasing, for all $n \ge N$:
        $$s - \varepsilon < s_N \le s_n \le s < s + \varepsilon \implies |s_n - s| < \varepsilon.$$
    <2>5. Therefore $\lim_{n \to \infty} s_n = s$.

<1>2. Part (b): Linearity of series summation.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $s_n = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i$ and $t_n = \sum_{i=1}^n b_i$. By hypothesis, $\lim s_n = s$ and $\lim t_n = t$.
    <2>2. The $n$-th partial sum of the series $\sum (c a_i + b_i)$ is:
        $$u_n = \sum_{i=1}^n (c a_i + b_i) = c \sum_{i=1}^n a_i + \sum_{i=1}^n b_i = c s_n + t_n.$$
    <2>3. By the algebraic limit laws for sequences in $\mathbb{R}$, $\lim_{n \to \infty} u_n = c \lim s_n + \lim t_n = cs + t$.

<1>3. Part (c): Comparison test for series.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define $c_i = |a_i| + a_i$. Since $-|a_i| \le a_i \le |a_i|$, we have $0 \le c_i \le 2|a_i| \le 2b_i$.
    <2>2. The partial sums $C_n = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i$ are non-decreasing ($c_i \ge 0$) and bounded above by $2 \sum_{i=1}^\infty b_i$. By <1>1, $\sum c_i$ converges to some $C \in \mathbb{R}$.
    <2>3. The partial sums $A_n^* = \sum_{i=1}^n |a_i|$ are non-decreasing and bounded above by $\sum_{i=1}^\infty b_i$, so by <1>1, $\sum |a_i|$ converges to some $A^* \in \mathbb{R}$.
    <2>4. Since $a_i = c_i - |a_i|$, by <1>2 the series $\sum a_i = \sum (c_i - |a_i|)$ converges to $C - A^*$.

<1>4. Part (d): Weierstrass $M$-test for uniform convergence.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Pointwise convergence: For each fixed $x \in X$, $|f_i(x)| \le M_i$ and $\sum M_i < \infty$. By <1>3, the series $\sum f_i(x)$ converges in $\mathbb{R}$ to a limit $s(x) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty f_i(x)$.
    <2>2. Tail bound: Let $r_n = \sum_{i=n+1}^\infty M_i$. Since $\sum M_i$ converges, $\lim_{n \to \infty} r_n = 0$.
    <2>3. For any $k > n$ and any $x \in X$:
        $$|s_k(x) - s_n(x)| = \left|\sum_{i=n+1}^k f_i(x)\right| \le \sum_{i=n+1}^k |f_i(x)| \le \sum_{i=n+1}^k M_i \le r_n.$$
    <2>4. Taking $k \to \infty$, continuity of the absolute value yields $|s(x) - s_n(x)| \le r_n$ for all $x \in X$.
    <2>5. Uniform convergence: Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $N \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ such that $r_N < \varepsilon$.
    <2>6. For all $n \ge N$ and all $x \in X$, $|s(x) - s_n(x)| \le r_n \le r_N < \varepsilon$.
    <2>7. Thus $(s_n)$ converges uniformly to $s$ on $X$. Q.E.D.
:::
