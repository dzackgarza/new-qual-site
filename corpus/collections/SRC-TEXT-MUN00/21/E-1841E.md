::: {.exercise}

Prove the following standard facts about infinite series:

(a) Show that if $(s_n)$ is a bounded sequence of real numbers and $s_n \leq s_{n+1}$ for each $n$, then $(s_n)$ converges.

(b) Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence of real numbers; define

$$
s_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i.
$$

If $s_n \to s$, we say that the infinite series

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i
$$

converges to $s$ also.
Show that if $\sum a_i$ converges to $s$ and $\sum b_i$ converges to $t$, then $\sum (ca_i + b_i)$ converges to $cs + t$.

(c) Prove the comparison test for infinite series: if $\abs{a_i} \leq b_i$ for each $i$, and if the series $\sum b_i$ converges, then the series $\sum a_i$ converges.
[Hint: Show that the series $\sum \abs{a_i}$ and $\sum c_i$ converge, where $c_i = \abs{a_i} + a_i$.]

(d) Given a sequence of functions $f_n: X \to \RR$, let

$$
s_n(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(x).
$$

Prove the Weierstrass $M$-test for uniform convergence: if $\abs{f_i(x)} \leq M_i$ for all $x \in X$ and all $i$, and if the series $\sum M_i$ converges, then the sequence $(s_n)$ converges uniformly to a function $s$.
[Hint: Let $r_n = \sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty} M_i$. Show that if $k > n$, then $\abs{s_k(x) - s_n(x)} \leq r_n$; conclude that $\abs{s(x) - s_n(x)} \leq r_n$.]
:::

::: {.solution}

The complete published solution is **"positron0802", *Solutions to Topology, Chapter 2* (1 January 2021), Exercise 21.11, PDF p. 26** (`tmp/sources/munkres-solutions-positron-ch2.pdf`); the four parts are theirs and follow the book's hints.

Throughout, $s_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}$ and $t_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}$ are the partial sums, so that $\sum a_{i}$ converges to $s$ means $s_{n}\to s$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #arith}
(a) A nondecreasing bounded sequence of reals converges, to its supremum.

::: pf-proof
Let $(s_{n})$ be nondecreasing with $s_{n}\le M$ for all $n$, and put $s=\sup_{n}s_{n}$, which exists and is finite because the set $\theset{s_{n}}$ is nonempty and bounded above (least upper bound property of $\RR$). Given $\varepsilon>0$, the element $s-\varepsilon$ is not an upper bound, so there is $N$ with $s-\varepsilon<s_{N}$; for $n\ge N$ monotonicity gives $s-\varepsilon<s_{N}\le s_{n}\le s$, hence $\abs{s_{n}-s}<\varepsilon$. So $s_{n}\to s$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #linear}
(b) If $\sum a_{i}=s$ and $\sum b_{i}=t$ then $\sum(ca_{i}+b_{i})=cs+t$.

::: pf-proof
For every $n$, $\sum_{i=1}^{n}(ca_{i}+b_{i})=c\,s_{n}+t_{n}$ by finite distributivity and commutativity of addition in $\RR$. Since $s_{n}\to s$ and $t_{n}\to t$, the rules for limits give $c\,s_{n}+t_{n}\to cs+t$ (Exercise 21.5, printed p. 134), which is the assertion.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #comparison}
(c) If $\abs{a_{i}}\le b_{i}$ for every $i$ and $\sum b_{i}$ converges, then $\sum a_{i}$ converges.

:::: {.pf-proof}

::: {.pf-step #abs-converges}
$\sum\abs{a_{i}}$ converges.

::: pf-proof
Let $t_{n}\to t$. Each $b_{i}\ge\abs{a_{i}}\ge0$, so $(t_{n})$ is nondecreasing and $t_{n}\le t$ for all $n$: a nondecreasing sequence bounded above by $t$ has all its terms $\le t$. Hence the partial sums $r_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\abs{a_{i}}$ satisfy $r_{n}\le t_{n}\le t$ and are nondecreasing, so $\sum\abs{a_{i}}$ converges by step [](#arith){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-series}
With $c_{i}=\abs{a_{i}}+a_{i}$, the series $\sum c_{i}$ converges.

::: pf-proof
Each $c_{i}\ge0$, and $c_{i}\le2\abs{a_{i}}$ since $a_{i}\le\abs{a_{i}}$. So the partial sums of $\sum c_{i}$ are nondecreasing and bounded by $2L$, where $L=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\abs{a_{i}}$ is the finite sum of step [](#abs-converges){.pf-ref}, and step [](#arith){.pf-ref} gives convergence.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #split}
$\sum a_{i}$ converges, to $\sum c_{i}-\sum\abs{a_{i}}$.

::: pf-proof
Since $a_{i}=c_{i}-\abs{a_{i}}=(-1)\abs{a_{i}}+c_{i}$, step [](#linear){.pf-ref} applied to the two convergent series of steps [](#abs-converges){.pf-ref} and [](#c-series){.pf-ref}, with $c=-1$, gives that $\sum a_{i}$ converges.
:::

:::

::::

:::

::: {.pf-step #m-test}
(d) If $\abs{f_{i}(x)}\le M_{i}$ for all $x\in X$ and all $i$ and $\sum M_{i}$ converges, then $s_{n}=\sum_{i\le n}f_{i}$ converges uniformly.

:::: {.pf-proof}

::: {.pf-step #pointwise}
For each $x\in X$ the series $\sum f_{i}(x)$ converges; let $s(x)$ be its sum, so $s_{n}\to s$ pointwise.

::: pf-proof
For fixed $x$ the hypothesis gives $\abs{f_{i}(x)}\le M_{i}$ for all $i$, so the comparison test of step [](#comparison){.pf-ref} gives convergence of $\sum f_{i}(x)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #majorant-tail}
With $r_{n}=\sum_{i>n}M_{i}$ one has $r_{n}\to0$, and $\abs{s_{k}(x)-s_{n}(x)}\le r_{n}$ for all $k>n$ and all $x\in X$.

::: pf-proof
The series $\sum M_{i}$ converges, so its tail $r_{n}=M-\sum_{i\le n}M_{i}$ tends to $0$. For $k>n$ and $x\in X$, the triangle inequality and the hypothesis give $\abs{s_{k}(x)-s_{n}(x)}=\abs{\sum_{i=n+1}^{k}f_{i}(x)}\le\sum_{i=n+1}^{k}\abs{f_{i}(x)}\le\sum_{i=n+1}^{k}M_{i}\le r_{n}$, all terms of the last sum being $\ge0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #uniform}
$\abs{s(x)-s_{n}(x)}\le r_{n}$ for every $x\in X$, hence $s_{n}\to s$ uniformly.

::: pf-proof
Fix $n$ and $x$. By step [](#majorant-tail){.pf-ref}, $s_{k}(x)$ lies in the closed interval $[s_{n}(x)-r_{n},\,s_{n}(x)+r_{n}]$ for every $k>n$, and $s_{k}(x)\to s(x)$ by step [](#pointwise){.pf-ref}; a limit of points of a closed interval lies in it, so $\abs{s(x)-s_{n}(x)}\le r_{n}$. As $x$ was arbitrary, $\sup_{x\in X}\abs{s(x)-s_{n}(x)}\le r_{n}$, and $r_{n}\to0$ by step [](#majorant-tail){.pf-ref}: given $\varepsilon>0$ choose $N$ with $r_{n}<\varepsilon$ for $n\ge N$, and then the uniform distance of $s_{n}$ from $s$ is less than $\varepsilon$.
:::
