::: {.exercise}

If $X$ and $Y$ are spaces, define

$$
e: X \times \mathcal{C}(X, Y) \to Y
$$

by the equation $e(x, f) = f(x)$; the map $e$ is called the evaluation map.
Show that if $d$ is a metric for $Y$ and $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ has the corresponding uniform topology, then $e$ is continuous.
We shall generalize this result in §46.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that the evaluation map $e: X \times \mathcal{C}(X, Y) \to Y$, defined by $e(x, f) = f(x)$, is continuous when $Y$ is a metric space $(Y, d)$ and $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ is endowed with the uniform metric $\bar{\rho}(f, g) = \sup_{x \in X} \bar{d}(f(x), g(x))$.

<1>1. Setting and goal:
    Let $(x_0, f_0) \in X \times \mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ and let $\varepsilon > 0$. We may assume without loss of generality that $\varepsilon < 1$.
    We must find an open neighborhood $U \subseteq X$ of $x_0$ and an open neighborhood $V \subseteq \mathcal{C}(X, Y)$ of $f_0$ such that $e(U \times V) \subseteq B_d(f_0(x_0), \varepsilon)$.

<1>2. Construction of neighborhoods:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $f_0: X \to Y$ is continuous at $x_0$, there exists an open neighborhood $U \subseteq X$ of $x_0$ such that:
        $$d(f_0(x), f_0(x_0)) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \quad \text{for all } x \in U.$$
    <2>2. In the uniform metric space $\mathcal{C}(X, Y)$, define the open ball $V = B_{\bar{\rho}}(f_0, \frac{\varepsilon}{2})$.
    <2>3. For any $f \in V$, the uniform bound holds:
        $$\bar{\rho}(f, f_0) = \sup_{x \in X} \bar{d}(f(x), f_0(x)) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.$$
    <2>4. Since $\frac{\varepsilon}{2} < 1$, $\bar{d}(f(x), f_0(x)) = d(f(x), f_0(x))$, which implies:
        $$d(f(x), f_0(x)) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \quad \text{for all } x \in X.$$

<1>3. Verification of continuity:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $(x, f) \in U \times V$.
    <2>2. By the triangle inequality in the metric space $(Y, d)$:
        $$d(e(x, f), e(x_0, f_0)) = d(f(x), f_0(x_0)) \le d(f(x), f_0(x)) + d(f_0(x), f_0(x_0)).$$
    <2>3. Since $f \in V$, $d(f(x), f_0(x)) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
    <2>4. Since $x \in U$, $d(f_0(x), f_0(x_0)) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
    <2>5. Summing these bounds gives:
        $$d(e(x, f), e(x_0, f_0)) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.$$
    <2>6. Thus $e(U \times V) \subseteq B_d(f_0(x_0), \varepsilon)$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    The evaluation map $e$ is continuous at every point $(x_0, f_0) \in X \times \mathcal{C}(X, Y)$. Q.E.D.
:::
