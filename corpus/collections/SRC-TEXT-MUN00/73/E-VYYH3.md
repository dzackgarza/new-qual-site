::: {.exercise}

Prove the following.

Theorem.
If $G$ is a finitely presented group, then there is a compact Hausdorff space $X$ whose fundamental group is isomorphic to $G$.

Proof.
Suppose $G$ has a presentation consisting of $n$ generators and $m$ relations.
Let $A$ be the wedge of $n$ circles; form an adjunction space $X$ from the union of $A$ and $m$ copies $B_1, \ldots, B_m$ of the unit ball by means of a continuous map $f: \bigcup \operatorname{Bd} B_i \to A$.

(a) Show that $X$ is Hausdorff.

(b) Prove the theorem in the case $m = 1$.

(c) Proceed by induction on $m$, using the algebraic result stated in the following exercise.

The construction outlined in this exercise is a standard one in algebraic topology; the space $X$ is called a two-dimensional CW complex.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix a finite presentation $G\cong\langle a_1,\dots,a_n\mid r_1,\dots,r_m\rangle$, and let $A$ be the wedge of $n$ circles with Wedge point $a_0$, so that $\pi_1(A,a_0)$ is free on $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ by Theorem 71.1 (printed p. 434). Let $R_k$ be the least normal subgroup of $\pi_1(A,a_0)$ generated by the classes $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_k$ of the loops in $A$ spelling the relations $r_1,\dots,r_k$. Write $E=A\sqcup B_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup B_m$ and let $q:E\to X$ be the quotient whose attaching map on $\operatorname{Bd}B_i$ spells $\rho_i$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

(a) $X$ is compact Hausdorff.

::: pf-proof

$A$ is the union of $n$ circles in the plane (printed p. 434), hence compact metrizable, so $A$ is normal (Theorem 32.2, printed p. 202). Applying Exercise 2 of §72 (printed p. 441) $m$ times — each time adjoining one two-cell to the normal, path-connected space built so far — gives that $X$ is normal, hence Hausdorff, and compact as the continuous image of the compact space $E$ (Theorem 26.5, printed p. 166).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) If $m=1$ then $\pi_1(X)\cong\langle a_1,\dots,a_n\mid r_1\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

By Theorem 72.1 (printed p. 439) the inclusion $A\hookrightarrow X$ induces an epimorphism $\pi_1(A,a_0)\to\pi_1(X,a_0)$ with kernel the least normal subgroup containing the image of $\pi_1(\operatorname{Bd}B_1)$, which is generated by $\rho_1$. So $\pi_1(X,a_0)\cong F_n/R_1$, the group presented by $n$ generators and the single relation $r_1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(c) For $1\le k\le m$, $\pi_1(X_k,a_0)\cong F_n/R_k$, where $X_k$ is the space obtained by attaching the first $k$ two-cells.

::: pf-proof

Induction on $k$. For $k=0$, $X_0=A$ and $\pi_1(A,a_0)$ is free on $n$ generators. Suppose $\pi_1(X_{k-1},a_0)\cong F_n/R_{k-1}$, and obtain $X_k$ by attaching $B_k$ to $X_{k-1}$ along the loop spelling $\rho_k$. By Theorem 72.1, $\pi_1(X_k,a_0)\cong\pi_1(X_{k-1},a_0)/N$ where $N$ is the least normal subgroup containing the class of that loop. That is exactly the hypothesis of Exercise 3 of this section (printed p. 445) with $G'=F_n$ the free group, $f$ the quotient onto $H=\pi_1(X_{k-1},a_0)$, $g$ the quotient onto $\pi_1(X_k,a_0)$ and $x_0$ the word $\rho_k$: the lemma gives $\pi_1(X_k,a_0)\cong F_n/R_k$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\pi_1(X,a_0)\cong G$.

::: pf-proof

$X=X_m$, so step 3 gives $\pi_1(X,a_0)\cong F_n/R_m=\langle a_1,\dots,a_n\mid r_1,\dots,r_m\rangle\cong G$; and $X$ is compact Hausdorff by step 1.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

$X$ is a compact Hausdorff space with $\pi_1(X)\cong G$, which proves the theorem.

:::
