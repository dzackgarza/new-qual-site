::: {.exercise}

Theorem.
Let $J$ and $C$ be well-ordered sets; assume that there is no surjective function mapping a section of $J$ onto $C$ . Then there exists a unique function $h \cdot J \to C$ satisfying the equation

(\*)

$$
h (x) = \text { smallest } [ C - h (S _ {x}) ]
$$

for each $x \in J$, where $S_x$ is the section of $J$ by $x$ .

(a) If $h$ and $k$ map sections of $J$, or all of $J$, into $C$ and satisfy (\*) for all $x$ in their respective domains, show that $h(x) = k(x)$ for all $x$ in both domains.

(b) If there exists a function $h: S_{\alpha} \to C$ satisfying (\*), show that there exists a function $k \cdot S_{\alpha} \cup \{\alpha\} \to C$ satisfying (\*).

(c) If $K \subset J$ and for all $\alpha \in K$ there exists a function $h_{\alpha} \cdot S_{\alpha} \to C$ satisfying (\*), show that there exists a function

$$
k: \bigcup_ {\alpha \in K} S _ {\alpha} \longrightarrow C
$$

satisfying (\*).

(d) Show by transfinite induction that for every $\beta \in J$, there exists a function $h_{\beta}: S_{\beta} \to C$ satisfying (\*). [Hint: If $\beta$ has an immediate predecessor $\alpha$, then $S_{\beta} = S_{\alpha} \cup \{\alpha\}$ . If not, $S_{\beta}$ is the union of all $S_{\alpha}$ with $\alpha < \beta$ .]

(e) Prove the theorem.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) Let \(D_h,D_k\) be the respective domains. Suppose \(h\) and \(k\) disagree somewhere in \(D_h\cap D_k\), and let \(x\) be the smallest point of disagreement. For every \(y<x\) in the common domain, \(h(y)=k(y)\). Since the domains are sections (or all of \(J\)), this gives
\[
h(S_x)=k(S_x).
\]
Therefore the recursion formula yields
\[
h(x)=\min(C-h(S_x))=\min(C-k(S_x))=k(x),
\]
a contradiction. Thus the two partial solutions agree wherever both are defined.

(b) Suppose \(h:S_\alpha\to C\) satisfies (*). By hypothesis no section of \(J\) maps surjectively onto \(C\), so \(h(S_\alpha)\ne C\). Hence
\[
c=\min(C-h(S_\alpha))
\]
exists. Define \(k\) on \(S_\alpha\cup\{\alpha\}\) by \(k|_{S_\alpha}=h\) and \(k(\alpha)=c\). For \(x<\alpha\), the recursion formula is unchanged, and at \(\alpha\) it holds by definition. Thus \(k\) satisfies (*).

(c) For \(x\in\bigcup_{\alpha\in K}S_\alpha\), choose any \(\alpha\in K\) with \(x\in S_\alpha\) and set
\[
k(x)=h_\alpha(x).
\]
Part (a) shows this value is independent of the choice of \(\alpha\), so \(k\) is well-defined. For a fixed \(x\), choose \(\alpha\in K\) with \(x<\alpha\). Then all of \(S_x\cup\{x\}\) lies in \(S_\alpha\), and \(k\) agrees there with \(h_\alpha\). Hence \(k\) satisfies (*) at \(x\). Therefore it satisfies (*) throughout its domain.

(d) Let \(J_0\) be the set of all \(\beta\in J\) for which there exists \(h_\beta:S_\beta\to C\) satisfying (*). We show \(J_0\) is inductive.

Assume \(S_\beta\subset J_0\). If \(S_\beta=\varnothing\), the empty function works. If \(\beta\) has an immediate predecessor \(\alpha\), then
\[
S_\beta=S_\alpha\cup\{\alpha\},
\]
and part (b) extends \(h_\alpha\) to \(S_\beta\). If \(\beta\) has no immediate predecessor, then
\[
S_\beta=\bigcup_{\alpha<\beta}S_\alpha,
\]
and part (c), applied to \(K=S_\beta\), glues the functions \(h_\alpha\) to a solution on \(S_\beta\). Thus \(\beta\in J_0\) in every case. By transfinite induction, \(J_0=J\).

(e) Part (d) gives a compatible partial solution on every section. If \(J\) has no largest element, part (c) with \(K=J\) glues them to a function \(h:J\to C\). If \(J\) has largest element \(\beta\), part (d) gives a solution on \(S_\beta\), and part (b) extends it to \(J=S_\beta\cup\{\beta\}\). Thus a global solution exists. Uniqueness follows from part (a).
:::
