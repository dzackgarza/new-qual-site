::: {.problem}
Find the degree (over $\QQ$) of the splitting field of

(a) $x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1$;

(b) $x^4-2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For a polynomial $f\in\QQ[x]$ the splitting field is the subfield of $\CC$ generated by all roots of $f$, and its degree over $\QQ$ is the order of its Galois group, because a splitting field over a field of characteristic zero is Galois.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cyclotomic}
(a) The splitting field of $x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1$ has degree $\boxed{4}$ over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
Since $(x-1)(x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1)=x^5-1$, the roots of $f$ are the primitive fifth roots of unity $\zeta_5,\zeta_5^2,\zeta_5^3,\zeta_5^4$, and the splitting field is $\QQ(\zeta_5)$.
The polynomial $x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1=\Phi_5(x)$ is irreducible over $\QQ$: the substitution $x\mapsto x+1$ turns it into
$$
\frac{(x+1)^5-1}{x}=x^4+5x^3+10x^2+10x+5,
$$
which is Eisenstein at the prime $5$, and Eisenstein at $5$ for the shifted polynomial is equivalent to irreducibility of $\Phi_5$ over $\QQ$.
Hence $\Phi_5$ is the minimal polynomial of $\zeta_5$ and $[\QQ(\zeta_5):\QQ]=4$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #quartic-radical}
(b) The splitting field of $x^4-2$ has degree $\boxed{8}$ over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
The roots of $x^4-2$ are $\rho,\rho i,\rho i^2,\rho i^3$, where $\rho=2^{1/4}$ is the positive real fourth root; the splitting field is $K=\QQ(\rho,i)$.
The polynomial $x^4-2$ is Eisenstein at $2$ and the polynomial $x^2+1$ is irreducible over $\QQ$, so $[\QQ(\rho):\QQ]=4$ and $[\QQ(i):\QQ]=2$.
Now $i\notin\QQ(\rho)$, because $\QQ(\rho)\subseteq\RR$ while $i\notin\RR$; hence $[K:\QQ(\rho)]=2$ and the tower law gives $[K:\QQ]=4\cdot2=8$.
:::
