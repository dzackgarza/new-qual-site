::: {.problem}
Find all subgroups of order $4$ in $\ZZ_{4} \oplus \ZZ_{4}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
In the seventh edition this is Chapter 8 Exercise 28, printed p. 168, where the printed
words are the statement below.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #count}

The elements of order $4$ in $\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_4$ are the pairs $(a,b)$ with
$\operatorname{lcm}(\elementorder{a},\elementorder{b})=4$, and there are twelve of them.

::: pf-proof

An element of $\ZZ_4$ has order at most $2$ exactly when it lies in $\{0,2\}$. So the pairs
with both coordinates of order at most $2$ number $2\cdot 2=4$, and each of the remaining
$16-4=12$ pairs has a coordinate of order $4$, hence has order
$\operatorname{lcm}(\elementorder{a},\elementorder{b})=4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cyclic}

The cyclic subgroups of order $4$ are six:
$$
\langle(1,0)\rangle=\langle(3,0)\rangle,\quad
\langle(1,1)\rangle=\langle(3,3)\rangle,\quad
\langle(1,2)\rangle=\langle(3,2)\rangle,
$$
$$
\langle(1,3)\rangle=\langle(3,1)\rangle,\quad
\langle(0,1)\rangle=\langle(0,3)\rangle,\quad
\langle(2,1)\rangle=\langle(2,3)\rangle.
$$

::: pf-proof

Each element of order $4$ generates a cyclic subgroup of order $4$, and a cyclic group of
order $4$ has exactly two generators, $g$ and $g^{-1}=g^{3}$, so the twelve elements of
order $4$ found in step [](#count){.pf-ref} fall into six pairs. The six pairs are the ones
displayed: $(1,0)$ pairs with $(3,0)$, $(1,1)$ with $(3,3)$, $(1,2)$ with $(3,2)$, $(1,3)$
with $(3,1)$, $(0,1)$ with $(0,3)$ and $(2,1)$ with $(2,3)$, since each second entry is the
cube of the first in $\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_4$. No two of the six subgroups coincide, because their
nonidentity elements are distinct.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #klein}

The only subgroup of order $4$ that is not cyclic is
$\{0,2\}\oplus\{0,2\}=\{(0,0),(0,2),(2,0),(2,2)\}$.

::: pf-proof

A group of order $4$ is either cyclic or $\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2$, and in $\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2$ every
element has order $2$. The elements of $\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_4$ of order at most $2$ are exactly
the four in $\{0,2\}\oplus\{0,2\}$, so a subgroup of order $4$ with no element of order $4$
must be contained in that four-element set and hence equal to it. That set is closed under
addition coordinatewise, so it is a subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}

There are exactly seven subgroups of order $4$ in $\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_4$.

::: pf-proof

Six cyclic ones by step [](#cyclic){.pf-ref} and one non-cyclic one by step
[](#klein){.pf-ref}, and every subgroup of order $4$ is one or the other by the
classification of groups of order $4$.

:::
