::: {.problem}
Show that $\ZZ_{2} \oplus \ZZ_{2} \oplus \ZZ_{2}$ has seven subgroups of order $2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $G=\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2$, an abelian group of order $8$ written
additively, whose elements are the triples of bits.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #order2}

Every nonidentity element of $G$ has order $2$, and there are seven of them.

::: pf-proof

For $x=(a,b,c)\in G$ with $a,b,c\in\{0,1\}$ and $x\ne0$, one computes
$2x=(2a,2b,2c)=(0,0,0)$, so the order of $x$ divides $2$ and, $x$ being
nonzero, equals $2$. The group has $2^3=8$ elements, one of which is $0$, so
exactly seven are nonidentity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subgroups}

The subgroups of order $2$ of $G$ are exactly the sets $\{0,x\}$ with $x$ of
order $2$, and distinct such $x$ give distinct subgroups.

::: pf-proof

A subgroup of order $2$ is cyclic of prime order, so it is $\{0,x\}$ for any
$x\ne0$ in it, and its nonidentity element has order $2$. Conversely, if $x$
has order $2$ then $\{0,x\}$ is closed under addition and inverses and so is a
subgroup. If $x\ne y$ then $\{0,x\}\ne\{0,y\}$, since $y$ is not in the first
set.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #seven}

$G$ has exactly seven subgroups of order $2$, namely
$$
\langle(1,0,0)\rangle,\ \langle(0,1,0)\rangle,\ \langle(0,0,1)\rangle,\ \langle(1,1,0)\rangle,\ \langle(1,0,1)\rangle,\ \langle(0,1,1)\rangle,\ \langle(1,1,1)\rangle.
$$

::: pf-proof

The seven listed triples are exactly the nonzero elements of $G$, as their
first nonzero coordinate runs over the three positions and the rest over the
four possibilities. By steps [](#order2){.pf-ref} and [](#subgroups){.pf-ref}
each generates a subgroup of order $2$, the map $x\mapsto\{0,x\}$ from nonzero
elements to order-$2$ subgroups is a bijection, and there are seven nonzero
elements. So there are seven, listed above.

:::
