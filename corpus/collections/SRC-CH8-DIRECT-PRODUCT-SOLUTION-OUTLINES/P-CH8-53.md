::: {.problem}
Is $\ZZ_{10} \oplus \ZZ_{12} \oplus \ZZ_{6} \approx \ZZ_{15} \oplus \ZZ_{4} \oplus \ZZ_{12}$?
:::

::: {.solution}

In the seventh edition this is Chapter 8 Exercise 45, where the printed
words are the statement below.

The argument is that of the retained solution outline for Chapter 8, Exercise 53 (`assets/attachments/Ch8Sltns.pdf`), restructured into numbered steps, with the outline's list of four elements of order $2$ replaced by the exact count. Here $\ZZ_n$ is the additive group of residues and $\oplus$ the external direct product; by the Chinese remainder theorem $\ZZ_{mn}\cong\ZZ_m\oplus\ZZ_n$ when $\gcd(m,n)=1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #first}

$\ZZ_{10}\oplus\ZZ_{12}\oplus\ZZ_{6}\cong\ZZ_5\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2$.

::: pf-proof

$\ZZ_{10}\cong\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_5$, $\ZZ_{12}\cong\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_3$ and $\ZZ_6\cong\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_3$, by the Chinese remainder theorem applied to $2\cdot5$, $4\cdot3$ and $2\cdot3$; substituting and collecting the factors gives the right-hand side, and the isomorphism class of a finite direct product of cyclic groups depends only on its factors.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #second}

$\ZZ_{15}\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_{12}\cong\ZZ_5\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_3$.

::: pf-proof

$\ZZ_{15}\cong\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_5$ and $\ZZ_{12}\cong\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_3$ by the same theorem, since $\gcd(3,5)=1$ and $\gcd(4,3)=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #count}

$\ZZ_{10}\oplus\ZZ_{12}\oplus\ZZ_6$ has exactly $8$ elements $x$ with $2x=0$; $\ZZ_{15}\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_{12}$ has exactly $4$.

::: pf-proof

In a direct product, $2x=0$ holds coordinatewise, and in $\ZZ_n$ the solutions of $2x=0$ are $x=0$ and, when $n$ is even, $x=n/2$; so $\ZZ_n$ contributes $\gcd(2,n)$ solutions. The first group contributes $2\cdot2\cdot2=8$ and the second $1\cdot2\cdot2=4$. The outline's four elements $(5,6,3),(0,6,3),(5,6,0),(5,0,3)$ are a partial list of the first eight.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #notiso}

The two groups are not isomorphic.

::: pf-proof

An isomorphism carries the set $\theset{x\mid2x=0}$ onto itself as a set, so isomorphic groups have the same number of such elements; by step [](#count){.pf-ref} the two numbers $8$ and $4$ differ.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$\ZZ_{10}\oplus\ZZ_{12}\oplus\ZZ_6\not\approx\ZZ_{15}\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_{12}$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#notiso){.pf-ref}. The decompositions of steps [](#first){.pf-ref} and [](#second){.pf-ref} also differ, the first having two factors $\ZZ_2$ and no $\ZZ_4$, the second two factors $\ZZ_4$, which is the outline's primary argument.

:::
