::: {.problem}
Is $\ZZ_{10} \oplus \ZZ_{12} \oplus \ZZ_{6} \approx \ZZ_{60} \oplus \ZZ_{6} \oplus \ZZ_{2}$?
:::

::: {.solution}

Yes: both sides decompose into the same multiset of prime-power cyclic factors.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #decompose}

$\ZZ_{10}\oplus\ZZ_{12}\oplus\ZZ_{6}\approx\ZZ_{5}\oplus\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{3}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{3}$.

::: pf-proof

$\ZZ_{mn}\approx\ZZ_{m}\oplus\ZZ_{n}$ when $\gcd(m,n)=1$, since $1\mapsto(1,1)$ has order $mn$ in the product. Applying this to $10=2\cdot5$, $12=4\cdot3$ and $6=2\cdot3$ and regrouping the direct summands gives the displayed decomposition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #recombine}

$\ZZ_{5}\oplus\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{3}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{3}\approx\ZZ_{60}\oplus\ZZ_{6}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$.

::: pf-proof

The same splitting theorem, read right to left, gives $\ZZ_{5}\oplus\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{3}\approx\ZZ_{60}$ because $5,4,3$ are pairwise coprime, and $\ZZ_{3}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\approx\ZZ_{6}$; the remaining $\ZZ_{2}$ stays. Both sides are therefore the direct sum of the cyclic groups of orders $2,4,3,2,3,5$, up to the order of the summands.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}

$\ZZ_{10}\oplus\ZZ_{12}\oplus\ZZ_{6}\approx\ZZ_{60}\oplus\ZZ_{6}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#decompose){.pf-ref} and [](#recombine){.pf-ref} chain the two decompositions:
$$
\boxed{\ZZ_{10}\oplus\ZZ_{12}\oplus\ZZ_{6}\approx\ZZ_{60}\oplus\ZZ_{6}\oplus\ZZ_{2}.}
$$

:::
