::: {.problem}
Let $(a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{n}) \in G_{1} \oplus G_{2} \oplus \cdots \oplus G_{n}$. Give a necessary and sufficient condition for $\elementorder{(a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{n})} = \infty$.
:::

::: {.solution}
In the seventh edition this is Chapter 8 Exercise 36, printed p. 169, where the printed
words are the statement below.

The argument is the card's own, checked against Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra*, seventh edition, Chapter 8, exercise 40; the retained Chapter 8 outline gives the criterion in one sentence, and the two implications are separated and proved here.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #lcm}
For $(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in G_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus G_{n}$ the order of the tuple is the least common multiple of the orders of the coordinates, in the sense that the tuple has finite order $L$ exactly when every $\elementorder{a_{i}}$ is finite with $L=\operatorname{lcm}(\elementorder{a_{1}},\dots,\elementorder{a_{n}})$.

::: pf-proof
$m(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=(ma_{1},\dots,ma_{n})$ in the direct product, and this is the identity exactly when $ma_{i}=e_{i}$ for every $i$, that is when $\elementorder{a_{i}}$ divides $m$ for every $i$. Such an $m$ exists precisely when all the orders are finite, and the least one is their least common multiple.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sufficient}
If some coordinate has infinite order then the tuple has infinite order.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $\elementorder{a_{j}}=\infty$ and the tuple had finite order $m$; then $ma_{j}=e_{j}$ by step [](#lcm){.pf-ref}, so $\elementorder{a_{j}}$ divides $m$, contradicting infiniteness. Hence no finite $m$ works.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #necessary}
If every coordinate has finite order then the tuple has finite order.

::: pf-proof
Let $L=\operatorname{lcm}(\elementorder{a_{1}},\dots,\elementorder{a_{n}})$, which exists because the list is finite; then $La_{i}=e_{i}$ for each $i$, so $L(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})$ is the identity and the tuple has finite order.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The tuple has infinite order if and only if some coordinate has infinite order.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#sufficient){.pf-ref} and [](#necessary){.pf-ref} give
$$
\boxed{\elementorder{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}=\infty\iff\elementorder{a_{i}}=\infty\text{ for some }i.}
$$
Finiteness of the index set is used in step [](#necessary){.pf-ref}, through the existence of the least common multiple of finitely many numbers; over an infinite family a tuple whose every coordinate has finite order can have infinite order.
:::
