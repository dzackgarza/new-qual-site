::: {.problem}
Let $H = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & a & b \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \,\st\, a, b \in \ZZ_3 \right\}$.
Show that $H$ is an Abelian group of order 9.
Is $H$ isomorphic to $\ZZ_9$ or to $\ZZ_3 \oplus \ZZ_3$?
:::

::: {.solution}
In the seventh edition this is Chapter 8 Exercise 34, printed p. 168, where the printed
words are the statement below.

The argument is the card's own: the collection's provenance is a set of solution outlines. Let
$$
H=\Bigl\{\begin{bmatrix}1&a&b\\ 0&1&0\\ 0&0&1\end{bmatrix}:a,b\in\ZZ_3\Bigr\}\subseteq M_3(\ZZ_3).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #group}
$H$ is a subgroup of $M_3(\ZZ_3)$, and the map $(a,b)\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}1&a&b\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}$ is an isomorphism of groups from $(\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_3,+)$ onto $H$.

::: pf-proof
Multiplying out,
$$
\begin{bmatrix}1&a&b\\ 0&1&0\\ 0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&c&d\\ 0&1&0\\ 0&0&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&a+c&b+d\\ 0&1&0\\ 0&0&1\end{bmatrix},
$$
the $(1,3)$ entry being $1\cdot d+a\cdot0+b\cdot1=b+d$; so the product of two elements of $H$ is again of the displayed form, the identity corresponds to $(0,0)$, and the inverse of $(a,b)$ is $(-a,-b)$. The map is therefore a bijection onto $H$ preserving the operation, since the product corresponds to the coordinatewise sum; hence $H$ is a group of order $9$, and it is abelian because coordinatewise addition in $\ZZ_3^2$ is.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not_cyclic}
$H\cong\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_3$, not $\ZZ_9$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#group){.pf-ref} $H\cong\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_3$, which has order $9$. In $\ZZ_3\oplus\ZZ_3$ every element satisfies $3(a,b)=(3a,3b)=(0,0)$, so every element has order $1$ or $3$ and there is no element of order $9$. The cyclic group $\ZZ_9$ has an element of order $9$ — a generator — so the two groups of order $9$ are not isomorphic and $H$ is the elementary abelian one.
:::
