::: {.problem}
Express $U(165)$ as an external direct product of $U$-groups in four different ways.
:::

::: {.solution}

In the seventh edition this is Chapter 8 Exercise 57, where the printed
words are the statement below.

The argument is that of the retained solution outline for Chapter 8, Exercise 67, PDF p. 5 of `assets/attachments/Ch8Sltns.pdf`, restructured into numbered steps. Here $U(n)$ is the group of units of $\ZZ_n$, and the criterion used is $U(mn)\cong U(m)\oplus U(n)$ for $\gcd(m,n)=1$, which follows from the Chinese remainder theorem applied to the rings.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factor}

$165=3\cdot5\cdot11$ with the three factors pairwise coprime.

::: pf-proof

The displayed factorization is the prime factorization, and distinct primes are pairwise coprime.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #crt}

For pairwise coprime $m,n$, $U(mn)\cong U(m)\oplus U(n)$.

::: pf-proof

The Chinese remainder theorem gives a ring isomorphism $\ZZ_{mn}\to\ZZ_m\times\ZZ_n$; a ring isomorphism carries units to units, and an element of a product of rings is a unit exactly when both coordinates are.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #one}

$U(165)\cong U(3)\oplus U(55)$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#crt){.pf-ref} with $m=3$, $n=55=5\cdot11$, using $\gcd(3,55)=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #two}

$U(165)\cong U(5)\oplus U(33)$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#crt){.pf-ref} with $m=5$, $n=33=3\cdot11$, using $\gcd(5,33)=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #three}

$U(165)\cong U(11)\oplus U(15)$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#crt){.pf-ref} with $m=11$, $n=15=3\cdot5$, using $\gcd(11,15)=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #four}

$U(165)\cong U(3)\oplus U(5)\oplus U(11)$.

::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#crt){.pf-ref} twice: $U(165)\cong U(3)\oplus U(55)$ by step [](#one){.pf-ref}, and $U(55)\cong U(5)\oplus U(11)$ by step [](#crt){.pf-ref} with $m=5$, $n=11$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$U(165)$ is an external direct product of $U$-groups in the four displayed ways.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#one){.pf-ref} to [](#four){.pf-ref}; they are different groupings of the three prime factors $3,5,11$ into two or three blocks.

:::
