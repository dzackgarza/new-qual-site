::: {.problem}
Find a subgroup of $\ZZ_{12} \oplus \ZZ_{4} \oplus \ZZ_{15}$ that has order $9$.
:::

::: {.solution}
In the seventh edition this is Chapter 8 Exercise 32, where the printed
words are the statement below.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factors}

In $\ZZ_{12}$ the element $4$ has order $3$; in $\ZZ_{15}$ the element $5$ has order $3$;
and $0$ is the identity of $\ZZ_4$.

::: pf-proof

The multiples of $4$ in $\ZZ_{12}$ are $0,4,8$, and $4+4+4=12\equiv0$ with no smaller
positive multiple vanishing, so $\elementorder{4}=12/\gcd(12,4)=3$. The multiples of $5$ in
$\ZZ_{15}$ are $0,5,10$, and $5+5+5=15\equiv0$, so $\elementorder{5}=15/\gcd(15,5)=3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order}

$H=\langle 4\rangle\oplus\{0\}\oplus\langle 5\rangle$ is a subgroup of
$\ZZ_{12}\oplus\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_{15}$ of order $3\cdot 1\cdot 3=9$.

::: pf-proof

A direct product of subgroups is a subgroup of the direct product, and the order of a direct
product of finite groups is the product of the orders of the factors. The factors have orders
$3$, $1$ and $3$ by step [](#factors){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #list}

$H$ consists of the nine triples $(4i,0,5j)$ with $0\le i,j\le 2$, that is
$$
(0,0,0),(4,0,0),(8,0,0),(0,0,5),(0,0,10),(4,0,5),(4,0,10),(8,0,5),(8,0,10).
$$

::: pf-proof

An element of $\langle4\rangle\oplus\{0\}\oplus\langle5\rangle$ is a triple $(4i,0,5j)$
with $i,j\in\ZZ$, and the classes of $i$ and $j$ modulo $3$ give the same triple, so the nine
listed triples exhaust $H$; they are distinct because $4i$ determines $i$ modulo $3$ in
$\ZZ_{12}$ and $5j$ determines $j$ modulo $3$ in $\ZZ_{15}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #distinct}

$H$ is not the cyclic subgroup generated by $(4,0,5)$.

::: pf-proof

$\langle(4,0,5)\rangle=\{(0,0,0),(4,0,5),(8,0,10)\}$ has order $3$, while $H$ has order $9$
by step [](#order){.pf-ref}. The exercise asks only for a subgroup of order $9$, and $H$ is
one.

:::

:::

:::
