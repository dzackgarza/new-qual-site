::: {.problem}
The group $S_{3} \oplus \ZZ_{2}$ is isomorphic to one of the following groups: $\ZZ_{12}$, $\ZZ_{6} \oplus \ZZ_{2}$, $A_{4}$, $D_{6}$. Determine which one by elimination.
:::

::: {.solution}

In the seventh edition this is Chapter 8 Exercise 24, printed p. 168, where the printed
words are the statement below.

The argument is the card's own: eliminate the three abelian or order-deficient candidates and identify the fourth.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #elimab}

$S_{3}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$ is not abelian, so it is isomorphic to neither $\ZZ_{12}$ nor $\ZZ_{6}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$.

::: pf-proof

$(a,b)(a',b')=(aa',bb')$ while $(a',b')(a,b)=(a'a,b'b)$, so the direct product is abelian exactly when both factors are. $S_{3}$ is not abelian: $(12)(23)\ne(23)(12)$ in cycle notation. A direct product with a non-abelian factor is non-abelian, whereas $\ZZ_{12}$ and $\ZZ_{6}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$ are abelian, being products of abelian groups. Isomorphic groups are simultaneously abelian or not, so the two abelian candidates are excluded.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #elimA4}

$S_{3}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$ has an element of order $6$, so it is not isomorphic to $A_{4}$.

::: pf-proof

The element $\bigl((123),1\bigr)$ has order $\operatorname{lcm}(3,2)=6$, since $(123)$ has order $3$ in $S_{3}$ and $1$ has order $2$ in $\ZZ_{2}$. The group $A_{4}$ has order $12$ and its elements have orders $1$, $2$ or $3$: a $3$-cycle has order $3$, a product of two disjoint transpositions has order $2$, and the identity has order $1$, and these are all the even permutations of $S_{4}$. No element of $A_{4}$ has order $6$, and an isomorphism preserves element orders.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #identify}

$S_{3}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\cong D_{6}$.

::: pf-proof

$D_{6}$ denotes the dihedral group of order $12$, the symmetry group of a regular hexagon, and it is isomorphic to $D_{3}\times\ZZ_{2}$: writing the hexagon's rotations as $r$ with $r^{6}=1$, the element $r$ has order $6$ and $r^{3}$ is the half-turn, so the symmetry group of the hexagon is the direct product of the symmetry group of the triangle generated by $r^{2}$ and $s$ with the central subgroup $\{1,r^{3}\}$. Since $D_{3}\cong S_{3}$, this gives $D_{6}\cong S_{3}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$. By steps [](#elimab){.pf-ref} and [](#elimA4){.pf-ref} this is the only surviving candidate among those listed:
$$
\boxed{S_{3}\oplus\ZZ_{2}\cong D_{6}.}
$$

:::
