::: {.problem}
Without doing any calculations in $\Aut(\ZZ_{20})$, determine how many elements of $\Aut(\ZZ_{20})$ have order $4$. How many have order $2$?
:::

::: {.solution}
In the seventh edition this is Chapter 8 Exercise 58, printed p. 170, where the printed
words are the statement below.

The argument is the retained solution outline for this chapter, **Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra*, seventh edition, Chapter 8 solution outlines, exercise 68**, restructured into steps; the identifications of $U(4)$ and $U(5)$ with cyclic groups are proved rather than asserted, and the two counts are re-derived from the criterion for the order of an element of a direct product.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #aut}
$\operatorname{Aut}(\ZZ_{20})\cong U(20)\cong U(4)\oplus U(5)$.

::: pf-proof
An endomorphism of $\ZZ_{20}$ is determined by the image of $1$ and is a bijection exactly when that image is a unit modulo $20$; so $\operatorname{Aut}(\ZZ_{20})\cong U(20)$ under $k\mapsto(x\mapsto kx)$. By the Chinese remainder theorem $\ZZ_{20}\cong\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{5}$, and the units of a product of rings with coprime moduli are the product of the units, so $U(20)\cong U(4)\oplus U(5)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cyclic}
$U(4)\cong\ZZ_{2}$ and $U(5)\cong\ZZ_{4}$, so $\operatorname{Aut}(\ZZ_{20})\cong\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{4}$.

::: pf-proof
$U(4)=\{1,3\}$ with $3^{2}=1$, hence cyclic of order $2$. $U(5)=\{1,2,3,4\}$ with $2^{1}=2$, $2^{2}=4$, $2^{3}=3$, $2^{4}=1$, so $2$ generates and $U(5)$ is cyclic of order $4$. Combining with step [](#aut){.pf-ref} gives the stated isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order4}
Exactly four elements of $\operatorname{Aut}(\ZZ_{20})$ have order $4$.

::: pf-proof
In $\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{4}$ the order of $(a,b)$ is $\operatorname{lcm}(\elementorder{a},\elementorder{b})$. It equals $4$ exactly when $\elementorder{b}=4$ and $\elementorder{a}\in\{1,2\}$: $b\in\{1,3\}$ are the two elements of order $4$, and $a\in\{0,1\}$ are the two of order at most $2$. That is $2\cdot2=4$ elements, and no others, since $b$ of order $1$ or $2$ makes the lcm at most $2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order2}
Exactly three elements have order $2$.

::: pf-proof
The elements killed by $2$ are those with $2a=0$ and $2b=0$, namely $a\in\{0,1\}$ and $b\in\{0,2\}$: four elements. The identity has order $1$, and the other three have order exactly $2$, since an element killed by $2$ but not the identity has order $2$. They are $(1,0)$, $(0,2)$ and $(1,2)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The counts are $4$ and $3$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#order4){.pf-ref} and [](#order2){.pf-ref} give
$$
\boxed{\cardinality{\{g\in\operatorname{Aut}(\ZZ_{20}):\elementorder{g}=4\}}=4,\qquad \cardinality{\{g:\elementorder{g}=2\}}=3.}
$$
Equivalently, among the units $\{1,3,7,9,11,13,17,19\}$ modulo $20$, four have multiplicative order $4$ and three have order $2$; no computation in $\operatorname{Aut}(\ZZ_{20})$ itself is needed, which is what the exercise asks.
:::
