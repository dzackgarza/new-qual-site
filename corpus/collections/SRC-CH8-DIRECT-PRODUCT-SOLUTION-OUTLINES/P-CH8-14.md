::: {.problem}
The dihedral group $D_{n}$ of order $2n$ ($n \geq 3$) has a subgroup of $n$ rotations and a subgroup of order $2$. Explain why $D_{n}$ cannot be isomorphic to the external direct product of two such groups.
:::

::: {.solution}

The exercise is Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra* (7th ed.), Chapter 8 exercise 12,
printed p. 167, and is item 14 of the retained Chapter 8 solution outline
(`assets/attachments/Ch8Sltns.pdf`). Its elimination is kept, and both of its
premises are proved below rather than asserted.

"Two such groups" means a group of the $n$ rotations and a group of order $2$: the rotation
subgroup $\langle r\rangle$ is cyclic of order $n$, hence is $\ZZ_{n}$, and a group of order
$2$ is $\ZZ_{2}$ because its nonidentity element generates it. So the claim to be disproved
is $D_{n}\cong\ZZ_{n}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$ for $n\ge3$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #product-abelian}

$\ZZ_{n}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$ is abelian.

::: pf-proof

Its operation is componentwise, $(a,b)+(a',b')=(a+a',b+b')$, and both $\ZZ_{n}$ and
$\ZZ_{2}$ are abelian under addition, so $(a,b)+(a',b')=(a+a',b+b')=(a'+a,b'+b)
=(a',b')+(a,b)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dihedral-nonabelian}

$D_{n}$ is not abelian for $n\ge3$.

::: pf-proof

Place the regular $n$-gon in the plane with centre at the origin and a vertex on the
$x$-axis, let $r$ be the rotation by $\theta=2\pi/n$ and $s$ the reflection in the $x$-axis,
so $r,s\in D_{n}$. On a point $(x,y)$:
$rs(x,y)=r(x,-y)=(x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta,\ x\sin\theta-y\cos\theta)$, while
$sr(x,y)=s(x\cos\theta-y\sin\theta,\ x\sin\theta+y\cos\theta)
=(x\cos\theta-y\sin\theta,\ -x\sin\theta-y\cos\theta)$. These differ whenever
$\sin\theta\ne0$, since the first coordinates differ by $2y\sin\theta$ and the second by
$2x\sin\theta$; for $n\ge3$ we have $0<\theta=2\pi/n\le2\pi/3<\pi$, so $\sin\theta\ne0$.
Hence $rs\ne sr$ and $D_{n}$ is nonabelian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #no-isomorphism}

There is no isomorphism $D_{n}\to\ZZ_{n}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$.

::: pf-proof

An isomorphism preserves the commutation of elements: if $rs\ne sr$ in $D_{n}$ and
$\phi$ were an isomorphism, then $\phi(r)\phi(s)=\phi(rs)\ne\phi(sr)=\phi(s)\phi(r)$, so the
images would not commute either, contradicting step [](#product-abelian){.pf-ref}. By step
[](#dihedral-nonabelian){.pf-ref} there are such $r,s$ when $n\ge3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}

$D_{n}$ is not isomorphic to $\ZZ_{n}\oplus\ZZ_{2}$, hence not to the external direct
product of its rotation subgroup and a subgroup of order $2$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#no-isomorphism){.pf-ref}, and by the identification of the two factors at the
head of the solution: every group of order $2$ is $\ZZ_{2}$, and the rotation subgroup of
$D_{n}$ is cyclic of order $n$, hence $\ZZ_{n}$.

:::
