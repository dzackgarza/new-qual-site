::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite group and $s, t\in G$ be two distinct elements of order 2.
Show that subgroup of $G$ generated by $s$ and $t$ is a dihedral group.

> Recall that the dihedral groups of order $2m$ for $m\geq 2$ are of the form
\[
D_{2m} = \gens{\sigma, \tau \suchthat \sigma^m = 1 = \tau^2, \tau \sigma = \sigma\inv \tau}
.\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $H=\langle s,t\rangle$, put $r=st$, and let $m$ be the order of $r$ in the finite group $H$.

<1>1. The order $m$ is at least two, and $trt=r^{-1}$.

::: {.proof}
If $r=1$, then $st=1$ and $s=t^{-1}=t$, contradicting the distinctness of $s,t$. Thus $m\geq2$. Since $s^2=t^2=1$, one has
\[
trt=t(st)t=ts=(st)^{-1}=r^{-1}.
\]
:::

<1>2. The involution $t$ does not lie in the cyclic subgroup $\langle r\rangle$.

::: {.proof}
If $t\in\langle r\rangle$, then $s=rt\in\langle r\rangle$ also. A cyclic group contains at most one element of order exactly two: such an element must be the unique element $r^{m/2}$ when $m$ is even. This contradicts the fact that $s$ and $t$ are distinct elements of order two.
:::

<1>3. The subgroup $H$ consists of exactly the $2m$ elements
\[
H=\{r^j,\;r^jt:0\leq j<m\}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since $s=rt$, the elements $r,t$ generate $H$. The relations $t^2=1$ and $tr=r^{-1}t$ from step <1>1 reduce every word in $r,t$ to either $r^j$ or $r^jt$, with $0\leq j<m$. Powers of $r$ are pairwise distinct by the definition of its order. Likewise the $r^jt$ are pairwise distinct, and equality $r^i=r^jt$ would imply $t\in\langle r\rangle$, contrary to step <1>2. Thus the displayed $2m$ elements are distinct and exhaust $H$.
:::

<1>4. The subgroup generated by $s,t$ is
\[
\boxed{H\cong D_{2m}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the assignment $\sigma\mapsto r$, $\tau\mapsto t$ respects the defining relations $\sigma^m=\tau^2=1$ and $\tau\sigma=\sigma^{-1}\tau$. It therefore determines a homomorphism $D_{2m}\to H$, which is surjective because $r,t$ generate $H$. The standard dihedral presentation has order $2m$, and step <1>3 shows that $H$ also has order $2m$. The surjective homomorphism is consequently an isomorphism.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
