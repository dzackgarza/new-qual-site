::: {.problem}
Let R be a commutative ring with identity and let U be maximal among non-principal ideals of R. Prove U is a prime ideal.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The ideal $U$ is proper.


::: pf-proof

The whole ring is the principal ideal
$$
R=(1).
$$
Since $U$ is not principal,
$$
U\ne R.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Suppose
$$
ab\in U
$$
with
$$
a\notin U,
\qquad
b\notin U.
$$
Then
$$
U+(a)
$$
is a principal ideal.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
a\notin U,
$$
the ideal
$$
U+(a)
$$
properly contains $U$. By maximality of $U$ among non-principal ideals, every ideal properly containing $U$ is principal. Hence
$$
U+(a)=(c)
$$
for some
$$
c\in R.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

There exist
$$
u\in U,
\qquad
r\in R
$$
such that
$$
c=u+ra.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
c\in(c)
=
U+(a),
$$
one can write
$$
c=u+ra
$$
with
$$
u\in U
$$
and
$$
r\in R.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Define
$$
V
\coloneqq
\{x\in R:cx\in U\}.
$$
Then $V$ is an ideal of $R$ containing $U$.


::: pf-proof

The set $V$ is the colon ideal
$$
(U:c).
$$
Indeed, if
$$
x,y\in V,
$$
then
$$
cx,cy\in U,
$$
so
$$
c(x-y)=cx-cy\in U,
$$
hence
$$
x-y\in V.
$$
If
$$
s\in R
$$
and
$$
x\in V,
$$
then
$$
c(sx)=s(cx)\in U,
$$
so
$$
sx\in V.
$$
Thus $V$ is an ideal.

If
$$
x\in U,
$$
then
$$
cx\in U
$$
because $U$ is an ideal. Hence
$$
U\subseteq V.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The element
$$
b
$$
belongs to
$$
V\setminus U.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
c=u+ra.
$$
Multiply by $b$:
$$
cb
=
ub+r(ab).
$$
Since
$$
u\in U
$$
and
$$
ab\in U,
$$
both terms on the right lie in $U$. Therefore
$$
cb\in U,
$$
which means
$$
b\in V.
$$
By the standing assumption,
$$
b\notin U.
$$
Thus
$$
b\in V\setminus U.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The ideal $V$ is principal.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
U\subsetneq V.
$$
By maximality of $U$ among non-principal ideals, $V$ must be principal:
$$
V=(d)
$$
for some
$$
d\in R.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

One has
$$
U\subseteq(cd).
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
x\in U.
$$
Since
$$
U\subseteq U+(a)=(c),
$$
there exists
$$
y\in R
$$
such that
$$
x=cy.
$$
Because
$$
x\in U,
$$
this equality implies
$$
cy\in U,
$$
so
$$
y\in V.
$$
By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
V=(d),
$$
so
$$
y=dz
$$
for some
$$
z\in R.
$$
Therefore
$$
x
=
c(dz)
=
(cd)z
\in
(cd).
$$
Hence
$$
U\subseteq(cd).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

One has
$$
(cd)\subseteq U.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
d\in V=(U:c),
$$
one has
$$
cd\in U.
$$
Therefore the principal ideal generated by $cd$ is contained in $U$:
$$
(cd)\subseteq U.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Thus
$$
U=(cd),
$$
contradicting the assumption that $U$ is non-principal.


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore $U$ is prime.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows $U$ is proper. If
$$
ab\in U
$$
and neither
$$
a
$$
nor
$$
b
$$
lay in $U$, steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} would produce a contradiction. Hence
$$
ab\in U
\implies
a\in U
\text{ or }
b\in U.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{U\text{ is prime}.}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
