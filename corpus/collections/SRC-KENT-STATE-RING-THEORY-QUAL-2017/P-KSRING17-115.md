::: {.problem}
Let p be a prime.
A ring S is called a p-ring if the characteristic of S is a power of p. Show that if R is a ring with identity of finite characteristic, then R is isomorphic to a finite direct product of p-rings for distinct primes.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\operatorname{char}R
=
n
>0.
$$
Write the prime factorization
$$
n
=
\prod_{i=1}^{r}
p_i^{e_i},
$$
where the
$$
p_i
$$
are distinct primes and
$$
e_i\ge1.
$$
For each
$$
i,
$$
set
$$
I_i
\coloneqq
p_i^{e_i}R.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For
$$
i\ne j,
$$
the ideals
$$
I_i
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
I_j
$$
are comaximal.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
\gcd(p_i^{e_i},p_j^{e_j})=1,
$$
Bézout's identity gives integers
$$
a,b
$$
such that
$$
a p_i^{e_i}
+
b p_j^{e_j}
=
1.
$$
Multiplying by
$$
1_R
$$
gives
$$
1_R
\in
p_i^{e_i}R
+
p_j^{e_j}R
=
I_i+I_j.
$$
Hence
$$
I_i+I_j=R.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For pairwise comaximal ideals,
$$
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}I_i
=
\prod_{i=1}^{r}I_i.
$$


::: pf-proof

For two comaximal ideals
$$
I+J=R,
$$
choose
$$
a\in I,
\qquad
b\in J
$$
with
$$
a+b=1.
$$
If
$$
x\in I\cap J,
$$
then
$$
x
=
x(a+b)
=
xa+xb.
$$
Since
$$
x\in J
$$
and
$$
a\in I,
$$
one has
$$
xa\in IJ.
$$
Similarly,
$$
xb\in IJ.
$$
Thus
$$
I\cap J\subseteq IJ.
$$
The reverse inclusion always holds, so
$$
I\cap J=IJ.
$$

Apply this repeatedly to the pairwise comaximal family
$$
I_1,\ldots,I_r.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
\prod_{i=1}^{r}I_i
=
nR.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
I_i
=
p_i^{e_i}R.
$$
Products of principal ideals generated by central integer multiples satisfy
$$
\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_i^{e_i}R
=
\left(
\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_i^{e_i}
\right)R
=
nR.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Since
$$
\operatorname{char}R=n,
$$
one has
$$
nR=(0).
$$


::: pf-proof

The characteristic condition means
$$
n1_R=0.
$$
For every
$$
r\in R,
$$
$$
nr
=
(n1_R)r
=
0.
$$
Thus
$$
nR=(0).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}I_i
=(0).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The canonical homomorphism
$$
\Phi:
R
\longrightarrow
\prod_{i=1}^{r}R/I_i,
\qquad
r
\longmapsto
(r+I_1,\ldots,r+I_r)
$$
is surjective.


::: pf-proof

The ideals
$$
I_1,\ldots,I_r
$$
are pairwise comaximal by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
The Chinese remainder theorem therefore gives surjectivity of the canonical map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The kernel of $\Phi$ is zero.


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
\ker\Phi
=
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}I_i.
$$
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\ker\Phi=(0).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
R
\cong
\prod_{i=1}^{r}
R/p_i^{e_i}R.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} show that
$$
\Phi
$$
is a bijective ring homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For each $i$, the quotient
$$
R_i
\coloneqq
R/p_i^{e_i}R
$$
is nonzero.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
R_i=0.
$$
Then
$$
1_R
\in
p_i^{e_i}R,
$$
so
$$
1_R
=
p_i^{e_i}r
$$
for some
$$
r\in R.
$$
Let
$$
m_i
\coloneqq
\frac{n}{p_i^{e_i}}.
$$
Multiplying the displayed identity by
$$
m_i
$$
gives
$$
m_i1_R
=
nr
=
0.
$$
But
$$
0<m_i<n,
$$
contradicting minimality of
$$
n=\operatorname{char}R.
$$
Thus
$$
R_i\ne0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The characteristic of $R_i$ divides
$$
p_i^{e_i}.
$$


::: pf-proof

In the quotient,
$$
p_i^{e_i}(1_R+I_i)
=
p_i^{e_i}1_R+I_i
=
0+I_i,
$$
because
$$
p_i^{e_i}1_R
\in
I_i.
$$
Therefore the characteristic of
$$
R_i
$$
divides
$$
p_i^{e_i}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The characteristic of $R_i$ is a positive power of $p_i$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
R_i
\ne
0,
$$
so its characteristic is a positive integer greater than $1$ or possibly $1$ only for the zero ring, which has been excluded.
By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, it divides
$$
p_i^{e_i}.
$$
Every positive divisor greater than $1$ of a prime power
$$
p_i^{e_i}
$$
is itself a power
$$
p_i^{f_i}
$$
with
$$
1\le f_i\le e_i.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{char}R_i
=
p_i^{f_i}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Each factor $R_i$ is a $p_i$-ring.


::: pf-proof

This is exactly step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} together with the definition of a $p$-ring.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
R
\text{ is isomorphic to a finite direct product of }
p_i\text{-rings for distinct primes }p_i.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the finite direct-product decomposition, and step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} identifies the $i$th factor as a $p_i$-ring.
The primes
$$
p_1,\ldots,p_r
$$
are distinct by construction.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
