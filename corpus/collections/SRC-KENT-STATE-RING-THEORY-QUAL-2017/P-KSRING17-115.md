::: {.problem}
Let p be a prime.
A ring S is called a p-ring if the characteristic of S is a power of p. Show that if R is a ring with identity of finite characteristic, then R is isomorphic to a finite direct product of p-rings for distinct primes.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\operatorname{char}R
=
n
>0.
$$
Write the prime factorization
$$
n
=
\prod_{i=1}^{r}
p_i^{e_i},
$$
where the
$$
p_i
$$
are distinct primes and
$$
e_i\ge1.
$$
For each
$$
i,
$$
set
$$
I_i
\coloneqq
p_i^{e_i}R.
$$

<1>1. For
$$
i\ne j,
$$
the ideals
$$
I_i
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
I_j
$$
are comaximal.

::: {.proof}
Because
$$
\gcd(p_i^{e_i},p_j^{e_j})=1,
$$
Bézout's identity gives integers
$$
a,b
$$
such that
$$
a p_i^{e_i}
+
b p_j^{e_j}
=
1.
$$
Multiplying by
$$
1_R
$$
gives
$$
1_R
\in
p_i^{e_i}R
+
p_j^{e_j}R
=
I_i+I_j.
$$
Hence
$$
I_i+I_j=R.
$$
:::

<1>2. For pairwise comaximal ideals,
$$
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}I_i
=
\prod_{i=1}^{r}I_i.
$$

::: {.proof}
For two comaximal ideals
$$
I+J=R,
$$
choose
$$
a\in I,
\qquad
b\in J
$$
with
$$
a+b=1.
$$
If
$$
x\in I\cap J,
$$
then
$$
x
=
x(a+b)
=
xa+xb.
$$
Since
$$
x\in J
$$
and
$$
a\in I,
$$
one has
$$
xa\in IJ.
$$
Similarly,
$$
xb\in IJ.
$$
Thus
$$
I\cap J\subseteq IJ.
$$
The reverse inclusion always holds, so
$$
I\cap J=IJ.
$$

Apply this repeatedly to the pairwise comaximal family
$$
I_1,\ldots,I_r.
$$
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\prod_{i=1}^{r}I_i
=
nR.
$$

::: {.proof}
By definition,
$$
I_i
=
p_i^{e_i}R.
$$
Products of principal ideals generated by central integer multiples satisfy
$$
\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_i^{e_i}R
=
\left(
\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_i^{e_i}
\right)R
=
nR.
$$
:::

<1>4. Since
$$
\operatorname{char}R=n,
$$
one has
$$
nR=(0).
$$

::: {.proof}
The characteristic condition means
$$
n1_R=0.
$$
For every
$$
r\in R,
$$
$$
nr
=
(n1_R)r
=
0.
$$
Thus
$$
nR=(0).
$$
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}I_i
=(0).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>2--<1>4.
:::

<1>6. The canonical homomorphism
$$
\Phi:
R
\longrightarrow
\prod_{i=1}^{r}R/I_i,
\qquad
r
\longmapsto
(r+I_1,\ldots,r+I_r)
$$
is surjective.

::: {.proof}
The ideals
$$
I_1,\ldots,I_r
$$
are pairwise comaximal by step <1>1.
The Chinese remainder theorem therefore gives surjectivity of the canonical map.
:::

<1>7. The kernel of $\Phi$ is zero.

::: {.proof}
By definition,
$$
\ker\Phi
=
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}I_i.
$$
Step <1>5 gives
$$
\ker\Phi=(0).
$$
:::

<1>8. Hence
$$
\boxed{
R
\cong
\prod_{i=1}^{r}
R/p_i^{e_i}R.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>6--<1>7 show that
$$
\Phi
$$
is a bijective ring homomorphism.
:::

<1>9. For each $i$, the quotient
$$
R_i
\coloneqq
R/p_i^{e_i}R
$$
is nonzero.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
R_i=0.
$$
Then
$$
1_R
\in
p_i^{e_i}R,
$$
so
$$
1_R
=
p_i^{e_i}r
$$
for some
$$
r\in R.
$$
Let
$$
m_i
\coloneqq
\frac{n}{p_i^{e_i}}.
$$
Multiplying the displayed identity by
$$
m_i
$$
gives
$$
m_i1_R
=
nr
=
0.
$$
But
$$
0<m_i<n,
$$
contradicting minimality of
$$
n=\operatorname{char}R.
$$
Thus
$$
R_i\ne0.
$$
:::

<1>10. The characteristic of $R_i$ divides
$$
p_i^{e_i}.
$$

::: {.proof}
In the quotient,
$$
p_i^{e_i}(1_R+I_i)
=
p_i^{e_i}1_R+I_i
=
0+I_i,
$$
because
$$
p_i^{e_i}1_R
\in
I_i.
$$
Therefore the characteristic of
$$
R_i
$$
divides
$$
p_i^{e_i}.
$$
:::

<1>11. The characteristic of $R_i$ is a positive power of $p_i$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>9,
$$
R_i
\ne
0,
$$
so its characteristic is a positive integer greater than $1$ or possibly $1$ only for the zero ring, which has been excluded.
By step <1>10, it divides
$$
p_i^{e_i}.
$$
Every positive divisor greater than $1$ of a prime power
$$
p_i^{e_i}
$$
is itself a power
$$
p_i^{f_i}
$$
with
$$
1\le f_i\le e_i.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{char}R_i
=
p_i^{f_i}.
$$
:::

<1>12. Each factor $R_i$ is a $p_i$-ring.

::: {.proof}
This is exactly step <1>11 together with the definition of a $p$-ring.
:::

<1>13. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
R
\text{ is isomorphic to a finite direct product of }
p_i\text{-rings for distinct primes }p_i.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 gives the finite direct-product decomposition, and step <1>12 identifies the $i$th factor as a $p_i$-ring.
The primes
$$
p_1,\ldots,p_r
$$
are distinct by construction.
:::

<1>14. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>13 is the required conclusion.
:::
