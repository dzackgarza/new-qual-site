::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a Noetherian integral domain in which every 2-generated ideal is principal.
Prove that $R$ is a Principal Ideal Domain.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated.

::: pf-proof

The ring $R$ is Noetherian.
By definition, every ideal in a Noetherian ring is finitely generated.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every ideal generated by one element is principal.

::: pf-proof

An ideal generated by one element $a$ is, by definition,
$$
(a).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Suppose every ideal generated by at most $n-1$ elements is principal, where
$$
n\ge2.
$$
Let
$$
I
\coloneqq
(a_1,\ldots,a_n).
$$
Then
$$
J
\coloneqq
(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})
$$
is principal.

::: pf-proof

The ideal $J$ is generated by
$$
n-1
$$
elements.
The induction hypothesis therefore gives
$$
J=(d)
$$
for some
$$
d\in R.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
I
=
(d,a_n).
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
I
&=
(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1},a_n)\\
&=
(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})+(a_n)\\
&=
(d)+(a_n)\\
&=
(d,a_n).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The ideal $I$ is principal.

::: pf-proof

By hypothesis, every ideal generated by two elements is principal.
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} expresses $I$ as the two-generated ideal
$$
(d,a_n).
$$
Hence $I$ is principal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Every finitely generated ideal of $R$ is principal.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives the base case.
Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} give the induction step from
$$
n-1
$$
generators to
$$
n
$$
generators.
Therefore every ideal generated by finitely many elements is principal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Every ideal of $R$ is principal.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, every ideal is finitely generated.
By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, every finitely generated ideal is principal.
Thus every ideal is principal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
R
\text{ is a principal ideal domain}.
}
$$

::: pf-proof

By hypothesis, $R$ is an integral domain.
Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} shows that every ideal of $R$ is principal.
These are exactly the defining conditions of a PID.

:::
