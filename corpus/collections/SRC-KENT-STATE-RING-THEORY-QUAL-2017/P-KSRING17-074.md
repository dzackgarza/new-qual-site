::: {.problem}
Let R be a commutative ring with identity such that not every ideal is a principal ideal.

(a) Show that there is an ideal I maximal with respect to the property that I is not a principal ideal.

(b) If I is the ideal of part (a), show that $R / I$ is a principal ideal ring.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\mathcal P
\coloneqq
\{
J\trianglelefteq R:
J\text{ is not principal}
\},
$$
ordered by inclusion.

<1>1. The poset $\mathcal P$ is nonempty.

::: {.proof}
By hypothesis, not every ideal of $R$ is principal.
Hence at least one non-principal ideal exists, so
$$
\mathcal P\ne\varnothing.
$$
:::

<1>2. Let
$$
\mathcal C
\subseteq
\mathcal P
$$
be a chain and set
$$
U
\coloneqq
\bigcup_{J\in\mathcal C}J.
$$
Then $U$ is an ideal of $R$.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
x,y\in U.
$$
Then
$$
x\in J_1,
\qquad
y\in J_2
$$
for some
$$
J_1,J_2\in\mathcal C.
$$
Since $\mathcal C$ is a chain, one of these ideals contains the other, so $x$ and $y$ lie in a common member of the chain.
Hence
$$
x-y\in U.
$$

If
$$
r\in R
$$
and
$$
x\in U,
$$
choose
$$
J\in\mathcal C
$$
with
$$
x\in J.
$$
Then
$$
rx\in J\subseteq U.
$$
Thus $U$ is an ideal.
:::

<1>3. The ideal $U$ from step <1>2 is not principal.

::: {.proof}
Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
U=(u)
$$
for some
$$
u\in R.
$$
Since
$$
u\in U,
$$
there exists
$$
J_0\in\mathcal C
$$
such that
$$
u\in J_0.
$$
Then
$$
(u)\subseteq J_0.
$$
But
$$
J_0\subseteq U=(u),
$$
so
$$
J_0=(u)=U.
$$
This makes
$$
J_0
$$
principal, contradicting
$$
J_0\in\mathcal P.
$$
Therefore
$$
U
$$
is non-principal.
:::

<1>4. Every chain in $\mathcal P$ has an upper bound in $\mathcal P$.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>2--<1>3, the union
$$
U
$$
of any chain is a non-principal ideal, hence
$$
U\in\mathcal P.
$$
It contains every member of the chain, so it is an upper bound.
:::

<1>5. There exists an ideal
$$
I
$$
maximal with respect to being non-principal.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $\mathcal P$ is nonempty, and by step <1>4 every chain has an upper bound in $\mathcal P$.
Zorn's lemma therefore yields a maximal element
$$
I\in\mathcal P.
$$
Thus $I$ is non-principal, and every ideal properly containing $I$ is principal.
:::

<1>6. Let
$$
\overline J
\trianglelefteq
R/I
$$
be an ideal of the quotient.
Then there exists a unique ideal
$$
J\trianglelefteq R
$$
with
$$
I\subseteq J
$$
such that
$$
\overline J
=
J/I.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the correspondence theorem for ideals under the quotient map
$$
\pi:R\to R/I.
$$
Ideals of $R/I$ are in bijection with ideals of $R$ containing
$$
\ker\pi=I.
$$
:::

<1>7. If
$$
J=I,
$$
then
$$
J/I
$$
is principal.

::: {.proof}
In this case
$$
J/I=(0),
$$
the zero ideal of $R/I$, which is generated by
$$
0+I.
$$
:::

<1>8. If
$$
J\supsetneq I,
$$
then
$$
J
$$
is principal.

::: {.proof}
The ideal $I$ is maximal among non-principal ideals by step <1>5.
Therefore every ideal properly containing $I$ must be principal.
Thus
$$
J=(a)
$$
for some
$$
a\in R.
$$
:::

<1>9. Under the hypothesis of step <1>8,
$$
J/I
$$
is the principal ideal generated by
$$
a+I
$$
in $R/I$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
J=(a)
$$
and
$$
I\subseteq J,
$$
one has
$$
\begin{aligned}
J/I
&=
\{ra+I:r\in R\}\\
&=
\{(r+I)(a+I):r\in R\}\\
&=
(a+I)
\end{aligned}
$$
as an ideal of
$$
R/I.
$$
:::

<1>10. Every ideal of
$$
R/I
$$
is principal.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\overline J
\trianglelefteq
R/I.
$$
By step <1>6, write
$$
\overline J=J/I
$$
for an ideal
$$
J\supseteq I.
$$
If
$$
J=I,
$$
step <1>7 gives a principal ideal.
If
$$
J\supsetneq I,
$$
steps <1>8--<1>9 give a principal ideal.
Thus every ideal of the quotient is principal.
:::

<1>11. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
R/I
\text{ is a principal ideal ring}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>10 is exactly the defining property of a principal ideal ring.
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 proves part (a), and step <1>11 proves part (b).
:::
:::
