::: {.problem}
(a) How many units does the ring $\ZZ/60\ZZ$ have?
Explain your answer.

(b) How many ideals does the ring $\ZZ/60\ZZ$ have?
Explain your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

The units of $\ZZ/60\ZZ$ are exactly the residue classes represented by integers relatively prime to $60$.


::: pf-proof

A class
$$
\overline a\in\ZZ/60\ZZ
$$
is invertible if and only if there exists $b\in\ZZ$ such that
$$
ab\equiv1\pmod{60}.
$$
By Bézout's identity, this occurs exactly when
$$
\gcd(a,60)=1.
$$
Thus the number of units is Euler's totient
$$
\varphi(60).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The number of units is
$$
\boxed{16}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
60
=
2^2\cdot3\cdot5,
$$
Euler's product formula gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\varphi(60)
&=
60
\left(1-\frac12\right)
\left(1-\frac13\right)
\left(1-\frac15\right)\\
&=
60\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac23\cdot\frac45\\
&=
16.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Every ideal of $\ZZ/60\ZZ$ is of the form
$$
(\overline d)
$$
for a unique positive divisor $d$ of $60$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\pi:\ZZ\to\ZZ/60\ZZ
$$
be the quotient map. By the correspondence theorem, ideals of $\ZZ/60\ZZ$ are in bijection with ideals $J\subseteq\ZZ$ containing
$$
(60).
$$
Every ideal of $\ZZ$ has the form
$$
(d)
$$
for a unique integer $d\ge0$, and
$$
(60)\subseteq(d)
$$
holds exactly when
$$
d\mid60.
$$
Under the quotient map, $(d)$ corresponds to the ideal generated by
$$
\overline d.
$$
Thus ideals of $\ZZ/60\ZZ$ are in bijection with positive divisors of $60$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The number of ideals is
$$
\boxed{12}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For
$$
60=2^2\cdot3^1\cdot5^1,
$$
a positive divisor has the form
$$
2^a3^b5^c
$$
with
$$
0\le a\le2,
\qquad
0\le b\le1,
\qquad
0\le c\le1.
$$
Hence the number of positive divisors is
$$
(2+1)(1+1)(1+1)
=
12.
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, this is exactly the number of ideals of $\ZZ/60\ZZ$.

:::
