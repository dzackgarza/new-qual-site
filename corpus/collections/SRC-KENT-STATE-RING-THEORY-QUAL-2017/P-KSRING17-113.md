::: {.problem}
(a) Let $\ZZ\left[\frac{1}{2}\right] = \left\{\frac{a}{2^{n}} \st a, n \in \ZZ,\ n \geqslant 0\right\}$, the smallest subring of $\QQ$ containing $\ZZ$ and $\frac{1}{2}$. Let $(2x - 1)$ be the ideal of $\ZZ[x]$ generated by the polynomial $2x - 1$. Show that $\ZZ[x]/(2x - 1) \cong \ZZ\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]$.

(b) Find an ideal $I$ of $\ZZ[x]$ such that $(2x - 1) \subsetneq I \subsetneq \ZZ[x]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Define
$$
\varphi:\ZZ[x]\to\ZZ\left[\frac12\right]
$$
by
$$
\varphi(f(x))
\coloneqq
f\left(\frac12\right).
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map $\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism.


::: pf-proof

Evaluation at a fixed element of a commutative ring preserves addition, multiplication, and the identity:
$$
\varphi(f+g)
=
f\left(\frac12\right)
+
g\left(\frac12\right),
$$
and
$$
\varphi(fg)
=
f\left(\frac12\right)
g\left(\frac12\right).
$$
Also
$$
\varphi(1)=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map $\varphi$ is surjective.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\frac{a}{2^n}
\in
\ZZ\left[\frac12\right].
$$
Then
$$
\varphi(ax^n)
=
a\left(\frac12\right)^n
=
\frac{a}{2^n}.
$$
Thus every element of
$$
\ZZ[1/2]
$$
lies in the image.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
(2x-1)
\subseteq
\ker\varphi.
$$


::: pf-proof

Evaluate the generator:
$$
\varphi(2x-1)
=
2\cdot\frac12-1
=
0.
$$
Therefore every multiple of
$$
2x-1
$$
lies in the kernel.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Let
$$
f(x)\in\ker\varphi.
$$
Then
$$
x-\frac12
$$
divides $f(x)$ in
$$
\QQ[x].
$$


::: pf-proof

The condition
$$
f\in\ker\varphi
$$
means
$$
f\left(\frac12\right)=0.
$$
By the factor theorem over the field $\QQ$,
$$
x-\frac12
$$
divides $f$ in $\QQ[x]$.
Equivalently,
$$
2x-1
$$
divides $f$ in $\QQ[x]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The polynomial
$$
2x-1
$$
is primitive in
$$
\ZZ[x].
$$


::: pf-proof

Its coefficients are
$$
2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
-1,
$$
whose greatest common divisor is $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The divisibility in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} already holds in
$$
\ZZ[x].
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
2x-1
$$
is primitive.
Gauss's lemma says that if a primitive polynomial in
$$
\ZZ[x]
$$
divides another polynomial from
$$
\ZZ[x]
$$
in
$$
\QQ[x],
$$
then it divides that polynomial in
$$
\ZZ[x].
$$
Hence
$$
2x-1
\mid
f(x)
$$
in
$$
\ZZ[x].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
\ker\varphi
=
(2x-1).
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives one inclusion.
Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} show that every element of the kernel is divisible by
$$
2x-1,
$$
giving the reverse inclusion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\ZZ[x]/(2x-1)
\cong
\ZZ\left[\frac12\right].
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, $\varphi$ is surjective with kernel
$$
(2x-1).
$$
The first isomorphism theorem gives the result.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For part (b), set
$$
\boxed{
I
\coloneqq
(2x-1,3).
}
$$
Then
$$
(2x-1)
\subsetneq
I.
$$


::: pf-proof

The containment is immediate from the definition.
It is strict because
$$
3\in I,
$$
but
$$
3\notin(2x-1).
$$
Indeed, if
$$
3=(2x-1)g(x),
$$
then evaluating at
$$
x=\frac12
$$
would give
$$
3=0,
$$
impossible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The ideal $I$ is proper.


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
\psi:\ZZ[x]\to\FF_3
$$
by reducing coefficients modulo $3$ and evaluating at
$$
x=2.
$$
Then
$$
\psi(3)=0,
$$
and
$$
\psi(2x-1)
=
2\cdot2-1
=
3
=
0
$$
in $\FF_3$.
Thus
$$
I
\subseteq
\ker\psi.
$$
But
$$
\psi(1)=1\ne0,
$$
so
$$
\ker\psi
\subsetneq
\ZZ[x].
$$
Hence
$$
I
\subsetneq
\ZZ[x].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(2x-1)
\subsetneq
(2x-1,3)
\subsetneq
\ZZ[x].
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
