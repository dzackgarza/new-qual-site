::: {.problem}
Let D be an integral domain and let c be an irreducible element in D. Show that the ideal $( x , c )$ generated by x and c in the polynomial ring $D [ x ]$ is not a principal ideal.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
(x,c)
$$
is principal.
Write
$$
(x,c)=(f)
$$
for some
$$
f\in D[x].
$$

<1>1. The polynomial $f$ divides the constant polynomial $c$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
c\in(x,c)=(f),
$$
there exists
$$
g\in D[x]
$$
such that
$$
c=fg.
$$
Thus
$$
f\mid c.
$$
:::

<1>2. The polynomial $f$ is constant.

::: {.proof}
The element
$$
c
$$
is irreducible, hence nonzero.
Therefore the equality
$$
c=fg
$$
is an equality of nonzero polynomials.
Because $D$ is an integral domain,
$$
\deg(fg)
=
\deg f+\deg g.
$$
The left-hand side has degree $0$.
Hence
$$
\deg f=0.
$$
Thus
$$
f=d
$$
for some nonzero
$$
d\in D.
$$
:::

<1>3. The element $d$ divides $x$ in $D[x]$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
x\in(x,c)=(d),
$$
there exists
$$
h(x)\in D[x]
$$
such that
$$
x=d\,h(x).
$$
Thus $d$ divides $x$ in the polynomial ring.
:::

<1>4. The element $d$ is a unit of $D$.

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
h(x)
=
h_0+h_1x+\cdots+h_mx^m.
$$
From
$$
x=d\,h(x),
$$
comparison of the coefficient of $x$ gives
$$
1=dh_1.
$$
Hence
$$
d
$$
has inverse
$$
h_1\in D.
$$
Thus $d$ is a unit.
:::

<1>5. If $(x,c)$ were principal, then
$$
(x,c)=D[x].
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4,
$$
d
$$
is a unit.
Therefore
$$
(d)=D[x].
$$
Since
$$
(x,c)=(d),
$$
the conclusion follows.
:::

<1>6. The ideal $(x,c)$ is proper.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
1\in(x,c).
$$
Then there exist
$$
g(x),h(x)\in D[x]
$$
such that
$$
1
=
xg(x)+ch(x).
$$
Evaluate at
$$
x=0.
$$
This gives
$$
1
=
c\,h(0).
$$
Hence $c$ is a unit of $D$, contradicting the hypothesis that $c$ is irreducible.
Therefore
$$
1\notin(x,c),
$$
so
$$
(x,c)\subsetneq D[x].
$$
:::

<1>7. The assumption that $(x,c)$ is principal is impossible.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives
$$
(x,c)=D[x],
$$
while step <1>6 gives
$$
(x,c)\ne D[x].
$$
This is a contradiction.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(x,c)
\text{ is not a principal ideal of }D[x].
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 rules out the existence of a single generator.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the required conclusion.
:::
