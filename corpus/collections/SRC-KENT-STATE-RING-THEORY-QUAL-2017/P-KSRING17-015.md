::: {.problem}
The Jacobson Radical $J ( R )$ of a ring R is defined to be the intersection of all maximal ideals of R.

Let R be a commutative ring with 1 and let $x \in R$ . Show that $x \in J ( R )$ if and only if $1 - x y$ is a unit for all y in R.
:::

Recall that
$$
J(R)
\coloneqq
\bigcap_{\mathfrak m\in\operatorname{MaxSpec}R}\mathfrak m.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Assume
$$
x\in J(R).
$$
Then for every
$$
y\in R,
$$
the element
$$
1-xy
$$
is a unit.


::: pf-proof

Fix
$$
y\in R.
$$
Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
1-xy
$$
is not a unit.

In a commutative ring with identity, every nonunit belongs to some maximal ideal. Hence there exists a maximal ideal
$$
\mathfrak m
$$
such that
$$
1-xy\in\mathfrak m.
$$

Since
$$
x\in J(R),
$$
one has
$$
x\in\mathfrak m.
$$
Because $\mathfrak m$ is an ideal,
$$
xy\in\mathfrak m.
$$
Therefore
$$
1
=
(1-xy)+xy
\in
\mathfrak m,
$$
contradicting the fact that a maximal ideal is proper.

Thus
$$
1-xy
$$
must be a unit.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Assume now that
$$
1-xy
$$
is a unit for every
$$
y\in R.
$$
Then
$$
x\in J(R).
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
x\notin J(R).
$$
Then there exists a maximal ideal
$$
\mathfrak m
$$
such that
$$
x\notin\mathfrak m.
$$

Because $\mathfrak m$ is maximal and
$$
x\notin\mathfrak m,
$$
the ideal generated by $\mathfrak m$ and $x$ is the whole ring:
$$
\mathfrak m+(x)
=
R.
$$
Hence there exist
$$
m\in\mathfrak m,
\qquad
y\in R
$$
such that
$$
m+xy=1.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
1-xy
=
m
\in
\mathfrak m.
$$

No element of a proper ideal can be a unit. Therefore
$$
1-xy
$$
is not a unit, contradicting the hypothesis.

Thus
$$
x\in J(R).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
x\in J(R)
\iff
1-xy\in R^\times
\text{ for every }y\in R.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>1 proves the forward implication, and step <1>2 proves the converse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>3 is the required equivalence.

:::

::: pf-step

Assume
$$
x\in J(R).
$$
Then
$$
1-xy
$$
is a unit for every $y\in R$.


::: pf-proof

Fix
$$
y\in R.
$$
Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
1-xy
$$
is not a unit. In a commutative ring with identity, every nonunit is contained in a maximal ideal. Hence there exists a maximal ideal
$$
M
$$
such that
$$
1-xy\in M.
$$

Since
$$
x\in J(R),
$$
and $J(R)$ is the intersection of all maximal ideals,
$$
x\in M.
$$
Therefore
$$
xy\in M.
$$
Adding the two elements in $M$ gives
$$
1
=
(1-xy)+xy
\in M,
$$
contradicting the fact that a maximal ideal is proper. Thus $1-xy$ must be a unit.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Assume
$$
1-xy
$$
is a unit for every $y\in R$. Then
$$
x\in J(R).
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
x\notin J(R).
$$
Then there is a maximal ideal
$$
M
$$
such that
$$
x\notin M.
$$
Since $M$ is maximal and the ideal
$$
M+(x)
$$
properly contains $M$, one has
$$
M+(x)=R.
$$
Hence there exist
$$
m\in M,
\qquad
y\in R
$$
such that
$$
1=m+xy.
$$
Thus
$$
1-xy=m\in M.
$$
But no proper ideal can contain a unit. This contradicts the hypothesis that
$$
1-xy
$$
is a unit for every $y\in R$.

Therefore $x$ lies in every maximal ideal, so
$$
x\in J(R).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
x\in J(R)
\iff
1-xy\in U(R)
\text{ for every }y\in R.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>1 proves the forward implication, and step <1>2 proves the reverse implication.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>3 is the required characterization.
