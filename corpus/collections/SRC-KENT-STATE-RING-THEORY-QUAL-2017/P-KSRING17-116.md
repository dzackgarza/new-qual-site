::: {.problem}
Let R be a ring.

(a) Show that there is a unique smallest (with respect to inclusion) ideal A such that $R / A$ is a commutative ring.

(b) Give an example of a ring R such that for every proper ideal $I , R / I$ is not commutative.
Verify your example.

(c) For the ring $R = \left\{ { \left[ \begin{array} { l l } { a } & { b } \\ { 0 } & { c } \end{array} \right] } \mid a , b , c \in \mathbb { Z } \right\}$ with the usual matrix operations, find the ideal A of part (a).
:::

::: {.solution}
For
$$
r,s\in R,
$$
write
$$
[r,s]
\coloneqq
rs-sr.
$$
Let
$$
C
\coloneqq
\left(
[r,s]:
r,s\in R
\right),
$$
the two-sided ideal generated by all commutators.

<1>1. The quotient
$$
R/C
$$
is commutative.

::: {.proof}
For any
$$
r,s\in R,
$$
their images in the quotient satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
(r+C)(s+C)
-
(s+C)(r+C)
&=
rs-sr+C\\
&=
[r,s]+C\\
&=
C.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
(r+C)(s+C)
=
(s+C)(r+C)
$$
for all $r,s$, so
$$
R/C
$$
is commutative.
:::

<1>2. If
$$
I\trianglelefteq R
$$
and
$$
R/I
$$
is commutative, then every commutator lies in $I$.

::: {.proof}
Commutativity in the quotient gives
$$
(r+I)(s+I)
=
(s+I)(r+I).
$$
Therefore
$$
rs-sr
\in
I.
$$
Thus
$$
[r,s]\in I
$$
for every
$$
r,s\in R.
$$
:::

<1>3. Under the hypotheses of step <1>2,
$$
C\subseteq I.
$$

::: {.proof}
The ideal $C$ is generated by all commutators.
Step <1>2 shows every generator belongs to $I$.
Since $I$ is an ideal,
$$
C\subseteq I.
$$
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
C
\text{ is the unique smallest ideal }A
\text{ such that }R/A\text{ is commutative}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 shows $C$ has the required property.
Step <1>3 shows $C$ is contained in every ideal with the required property.
Hence it is the unique smallest such ideal.
:::

<1>5. For part (b), let
$$
\boxed{
R=M_2(F)
}
$$
for any field $F$.
Then $R$ is noncommutative and simple.

::: {.proof}
Noncommutativity follows from the matrix units:
$$
E_{12}E_{21}
=
E_{11}
\ne
E_{22}
=
E_{21}E_{12}.
$$

To prove simplicity, let
$$
0\ne J\trianglelefteq M_2(F).
$$
Choose
$$
A=(a_{ij})\in J
$$
with some
$$
a_{ij}\ne0.
$$
For any
$$
k,\ell,
$$
the ideal property gives
$$
E_{ki}AE_{j\ell}
=
a_{ij}E_{k\ell}
\in J.
$$
Since
$$
a_{ij}\in F^\times,
$$
scalar multiplication gives
$$
E_{k\ell}\in J.
$$
Thus every matrix unit lies in $J$, and therefore
$$
J=M_2(F).
$$
Hence the only two-sided ideals are
$$
(0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
R.
$$
:::

<1>6. Every proper ideal $I$ of the ring in step <1>5 satisfies
$$
R/I
\text{ noncommutative}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because $R$ is simple, the only proper ideal is
$$
I=(0).
$$
Then
$$
R/I
\cong
R,
$$
which is noncommutative by step <1>5.
Thus this ring has the required property.
:::

<1>7. Now let
$$
R
=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&c
\end{pmatrix}
:
a,b,c\in\ZZ
\right\}.
$$
For
$$
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&c
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Y=
\begin{pmatrix}
d&e\\
0&f
\end{pmatrix},
$$
one has
$$
[X,Y]
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&
ae+bf-db-ec\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Compute
$$
XY
=
\begin{pmatrix}
ad&ae+bf\\
0&cf
\end{pmatrix}
$$
and
$$
YX
=
\begin{pmatrix}
da&db+ec\\
0&fc
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Since integers commute,
$$
ad-da=0
$$
and
$$
cf-fc=0.
$$
Subtracting yields the formula.
:::

<1>8. Every commutator in this ring is strictly upper triangular.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 shows
$$
[X,Y]
\in
N
\coloneqq
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
0&m\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}
:
m\in\ZZ
\right\}.
$$
Thus all commutators lie in $N$.
:::

<1>9. Every element of $N$ is itself a commutator.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
m\in\ZZ.
$$
Set
$$
E
\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U_m
\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
0&m\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then
$$
EU_m
=
U_m,
$$
while
$$
U_mE
=
0.
$$
Therefore
$$
[E,U_m]
=
U_m.
$$
Thus every element of $N$ is a commutator.
:::

<1>10. The set $N$ is a two-sided ideal of $R$.

::: {.proof}
It is clearly an additive subgroup.
For
$$
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&c
\end{pmatrix}
$$
and
$$
U_m=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&m\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
one has
$$
XU_m
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&am\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}
\in
N
$$
and
$$
U_mX
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&cm\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}
\in
N.
$$
Thus $N$ is a two-sided ideal.
:::

<1>11. The commutator ideal of this upper-triangular ring is exactly
$$
\boxed{
N
=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
0&m\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}
:
m\in\ZZ
\right\}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 shows all commutators lie in $N$, so the ideal they generate is contained in $N$ because $N$ is an ideal by step <1>10.
Step <1>9 shows every element of $N$ is itself a commutator, so $N$ is contained in the commutator ideal.
Hence equality holds.
:::

<1>12. The quotient
$$
R/N
$$
is commutative.

::: {.proof}
Modulo $N$, every matrix is represented by its diagonal part:
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&c
\end{pmatrix}
+
N
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&0\\
0&c
\end{pmatrix}
+
N.
$$
Thus
$$
R/N
\cong
\ZZ\times\ZZ,
$$
which is commutative.
:::

<1>13. Therefore the ideal $A$ requested in part (c) is
$$
\boxed{
A
=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
0&m\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}
:
m\in\ZZ
\right\}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>11 this is the commutator ideal, and by part (a), proved in step <1>4, the commutator ideal is exactly the smallest ideal with commutative quotient.
:::

<1>14. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves part (a), step <1>6 proves part (b), and step <1>13 proves part (c).
