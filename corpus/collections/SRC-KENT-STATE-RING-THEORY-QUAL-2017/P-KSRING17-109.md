::: {.problem}
Let $F [ x , y ]$ be the polynomial ring over a field F in two indeterminates $x , y$ . Show that the ideal generated by $\{ x , y \}$ is not a principal ideal.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
R
\coloneqq
F[x,y],
$$
and suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
(x,y)
$$
is principal.
Write
$$
(x,y)=(f)
$$
for some
$$
f\in R.
$$

<1>1. The element $f$ divides both $x$ and $y$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
x\in(x,y)=(f),
$$
there exists
$$
g\in R
$$
such that
$$
x=fg.
$$
Hence
$$
f\mid x.
$$
Similarly,
$$
y\in(f)
$$
implies
$$
f\mid y.
$$
:::

<1>2. The polynomials $x$ and $y$ are irreducible in $R$.

::: {.proof}
The polynomial ring
$$
F[x,y]
$$
is a UFD because it is obtained by adjoining indeterminates to the field $F$.
Each of
$$
x
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y
$$
has total degree $1$.
If, for example,
$$
x=ab
$$
with nonunits $a,b$, then
$$
1
=
\deg x
=
\deg a+\deg b,
$$
so one of $a,b$ has degree $0$ and is therefore a nonzero element of the field $F$, hence a unit.
Thus $x$ is irreducible.
The same argument proves irreducibility of $y$.
:::

<1>3. The irreducibles $x$ and $y$ are not associates.

::: {.proof}
If they were associates, there would exist
$$
u\in R^\times=F^\times
$$
such that
$$
x=uy.
$$
But the coefficient of the monomial $x$ on the right is $0$, whereas on the left it is $1$.
This is impossible.
:::

<1>4. The common divisor $f$ from step <1>1 must be a unit.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
f\mid x
$$
and $x$ is irreducible, either $f$ is a unit or $f$ is associate to $x$.
If $f$ were associate to $x$, then because
$$
f\mid y,
$$
one would have
$$
x\mid y.
$$
Since $y$ is irreducible, this would make $x$ and $y$ associates, contradicting step <1>3.
Therefore
$$
f
$$
is a unit.
:::

<1>5. If $f$ is a unit, then
$$
(x,y)=R.
$$

::: {.proof}
A principal ideal generated by a unit is the whole ring:
$$
(f)=R.
$$
Since
$$
(x,y)=(f),
$$
the conclusion follows.
:::

<1>6. The ideal $(x,y)$ is proper.

::: {.proof}
Consider evaluation at the origin:
$$
\operatorname{ev}_{(0,0)}:
F[x,y]\to F,
\qquad
h(x,y)\longmapsto h(0,0).
$$
Every element of
$$
(x,y)
$$
has zero constant term, so lies in the kernel.
In particular,
$$
1\notin(x,y),
$$
because
$$
1(0,0)=1.
$$
Hence
$$
(x,y)\ne R.
$$
:::

<1>7. The assumption that $(x,y)$ is principal is impossible.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives
$$
(x,y)=R,
$$
while step <1>6 gives
$$
(x,y)\ne R.
$$
This contradiction proves that no generator $f$ can exist.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(x,y)
\text{ is not a principal ideal of }F[x,y].
}
$$

::: {.proof}
This is exactly the conclusion of step <1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the required conclusion.
:::
