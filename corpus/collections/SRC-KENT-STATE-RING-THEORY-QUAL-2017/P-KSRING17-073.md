::: {.problem}
Let R be a commutative ring with 1. Show that the sum of any two principal ideals of R is principal if and only if every finitely generated ideal of R is principal.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

Assume every finitely generated ideal of $R$ is principal.
Then the sum of any two principal ideals is principal.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
(a)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(b)
$$
be principal ideals.
Their sum is
$$
(a)+(b)
=
(a,b),
$$
the ideal generated by the two elements $a$ and $b$.
This ideal is finitely generated, so by hypothesis it is principal.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Conversely, assume that the sum of any two principal ideals is principal.
Then every ideal generated by one element is principal.


::: pf-proof

This is immediate: an ideal generated by one element $a$ is exactly
$$
(a).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Suppose for some
$$
n\ge2
$$
that every ideal generated by at most
$$
n-1
$$
elements is principal.
Let
$$
I
\coloneqq
(a_1,\ldots,a_n).
$$
Then
$$
J
\coloneqq
(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})
$$
is principal.


::: pf-proof

The ideal $J$ is generated by
$$
n-1
$$
elements, so the induction hypothesis applies.
Hence there exists
$$
d\in R
$$
such that
$$
J=(d).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The ideal $I$ is the sum of two principal ideals:
$$
I
=
(d)+(a_n).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>3,
$$
(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})
=
(d).
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
I
&=
(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1},a_n)\\
&=
(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})+(a_n)\\
&=
(d)+(a_n).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The ideal $I$ is principal.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>4,
$$
I
=
(d)+(a_n).
$$
By the standing hypothesis of the converse direction, the sum of any two principal ideals is principal.
Hence
$$
I
$$
is principal.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore every finitely generated ideal of $R$ is principal.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>2 gives the base case.
Steps <1>3--<1>5 give the induction step from
$$
n-1
$$
generators to
$$
n
$$
generators.
Thus every ideal generated by finitely many elements is principal.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\text{every sum of two principal ideals is principal}
\iff
\text{every finitely generated ideal is principal}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>1 proves one implication and step <1>6 proves the converse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>7 is the required equivalence.

:::

:::
