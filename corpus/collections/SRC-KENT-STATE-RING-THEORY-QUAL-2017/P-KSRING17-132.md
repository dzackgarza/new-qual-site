::: {.problem}
Define Noetherian ring and prove that if R is Noetherian, then R[x] is Noetherian.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

A commutative ring $R$ is \dfn{Noetherian} if every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated.


::: pf-proof

Equivalently, $R$ is Noetherian if it satisfies the ascending chain condition on ideals.
For this proof we use the finitely-generated-ideal formulation.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Assume $R$ is Noetherian and let
$$
I\trianglelefteq R[x].
$$
For each integer
$$
n\ge0,
$$
define
$$
A_n
\coloneqq
\left\{
a\in R:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there exists }f\in I\text{ with }\deg f\le n\\
\text{whose coefficient of }x^n\text{ is }a
\end{array}
\right\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\deg f<n,
$$
the coefficient of
$$
x^n
$$
is understood to be $0$.
Thus the displayed set is well-defined for every $n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Each $A_n$ is an ideal of $R$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
a,b\in A_n.
$$
Choose
$$
f,g\in I
$$
of degree at most $n$ whose $x^n$-coefficients are $a$ and $b$.
Then
$$
f-g\in I
$$
has degree at most $n$ and $x^n$-coefficient
$$
a-b.
$$
Hence
$$
a-b\in A_n.
$$

If
$$
r\in R
$$
and
$$
a\in A_n,
$$
choose $f$ as above.
Then
$$
rf\in I
$$
has degree at most $n$ and $x^n$-coefficient
$$
ra.
$$
Thus
$$
ra\in A_n.
$$
Therefore $A_n$ is an ideal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The ideals $A_n$ form an ascending chain:
$$
A_0
\subseteq
A_1
\subseteq
A_2
\subseteq
\cdots.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
a\in A_n.
$$
Choose
$$
f\in I
$$
of degree at most $n$ with $x^n$-coefficient $a$.
Then
$$
xf\in I
$$
has degree at most
$$
n+1
$$
and its coefficient of
$$
x^{n+1}
$$
is again $a$.
Hence
$$
a\in A_{n+1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Since $R$ is Noetherian, there exists
$$
N\ge0
$$
such that
$$
A_N
=
A_{N+1}
=
A_{N+2}
=
\cdots.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the $A_n$ form an ascending chain of ideals of $R$.
A Noetherian ring satisfies the ascending chain condition.
Therefore the chain stabilizes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For every
$$
0\le n\le N,
$$
choose generators
$$
A_n
=
(a_{n1},\ldots,a_{nr_n})
$$
and choose polynomials
$$
f_{nj}\in I
$$
of degree at most $n$ whose coefficient of $x^n$ is $a_{nj}$.


::: pf-proof

Because $R$ is Noetherian, every ideal $A_n$ is finitely generated.
By the definition of $A_n$, each chosen generator
$$
a_{nj}
$$
occurs as the coefficient of $x^n$ of some polynomial
$$
f_{nj}\in I
$$
of degree at most $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Let
$$
J
\coloneqq
\left(
f_{nj}:
0\le n\le N,\ 1\le j\le r_n
\right)
\subseteq
R[x].
$$
Then
$$
J\subseteq I.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every generator
$$
f_{nj}
$$
was chosen inside $I$ in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.
Since $I$ is an ideal, it contains the ideal generated by them.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Let
$$
0\ne f\in I
$$
and set
$$
m
\coloneqq
\deg f.
$$
If
$$
m\le N,
$$
then the leading coefficient
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)
$$
belongs to $A_m$.


::: pf-proof

The polynomial $f$ has degree at most $m$ and its coefficient of $x^m$ is its leading coefficient.
Thus the definition of $A_m$ gives
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)\in A_m.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

If
$$
m>N,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)\in A_N.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)\in A_m.
$$
Since
$$
m\ge N
$$
and the chain has stabilized by step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
A_m=A_N.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)\in A_N.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

If
$$
0<m\le N,
$$
there exists
$$
g\in J
$$
with
$$
\deg(f-g)<m.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)\in A_m.
$$
Write
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{r_m}
c_j a_{mj}
$$
with
$$
c_j\in R.
$$
Set
$$
g
\coloneqq
\sum_{j=1}^{r_m}
c_j f_{mj}.
$$
Then
$$
g\in J.
$$
Each
$$
f_{mj}
$$
has degree at most $m$, and the coefficient of $x^m$ in $g$ is exactly
$$
\sum_j c_j a_{mj}
=
\operatorname{lc}(f).
$$
Therefore the $x^m$ terms cancel in
$$
f-g,
$$
so
$$
\deg(f-g)<m
$$
unless $f-g=0$, in which case the conclusion is even stronger.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

If
$$
m>N,
$$
there exists
$$
g\in J
$$
with
$$
\deg(f-g)<m.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)\in A_N.
$$
Write
$$
\operatorname{lc}(f)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{r_N}
c_j a_{Nj}.
$$
Set
$$
g
\coloneqq
\sum_{j=1}^{r_N}
c_j x^{m-N}f_{Nj}.
$$
Because
$$
f_{Nj}\in J
$$
and $J$ is an ideal,
$$
g\in J.
$$
Each polynomial
$$
x^{m-N}f_{Nj}
$$
has degree at most $m$, and its coefficient of $x^m$ is
$$
a_{Nj}.
$$
Hence the coefficient of $x^m$ in $g$ is
$$
\sum_j c_j a_{Nj}
=
\operatorname{lc}(f).
$$
Therefore the leading term cancels in
$$
f-g,
$$
and
$$
\deg(f-g)<m
$$
unless the difference is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Every polynomial
$$
f\in I
$$
belongs to $J$.


::: pf-proof

We argue by induction on
$$
\deg f
$$
for nonzero $f$.

If
$$
\deg f=0,
$$
then
$$
f\in A_0.
$$
Write
$$
f
=
\sum_{j=1}^{r_0}
c_j a_{0j}.
$$
The corresponding polynomials
$$
f_{0j}
$$
are constants equal to
$$
a_{0j},
$$
because they have degree at most $0$.
Thus
$$
f
=
\sum_j c_j f_{0j}
\in
J.
$$

Now suppose
$$
\deg f=m>0.
$$
If
$$
m\le N,
$$
step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives
$$
g\in J
$$
with
$$
\deg(f-g)<m
$$
or
$$
f-g=0.
$$
If
$$
m>N,
$$
step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives the same conclusion.

Because
$$
f\in I
$$
and
$$
g\in J\subseteq I
$$
by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
f-g\in I.
$$
If it is zero, then
$$
f=g\in J.
$$
Otherwise its degree is smaller than $m$, so the induction hypothesis gives
$$
f-g\in J.
$$
Hence
$$
f
=
(f-g)+g
\in
J.
$$
The zero polynomial also lies in $J$.
Therefore
$$
I\subseteq J.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Hence
$$
I=J,
$$
so $I$ is finitely generated.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives
$$
J\subseteq I,
$$
and step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} gives
$$
I\subseteq J.
$$
Thus
$$
I
=
\left(
f_{nj}:
0\le n\le N,\ 1\le j\le r_n
\right),
$$
a finite generating set.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Every ideal of
$$
R[x]
$$
is finitely generated.


::: pf-proof

The ideal
$$
I\trianglelefteq R[x]
$$
was arbitrary.
Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} shows that it is finitely generated.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
R\text{ Noetherian}
\implies
R[x]\text{ Noetherian}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} defines Noetherianity by finite generation of ideals.
Step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} verifies this property for
$$
R[x].
$$
This is Hilbert's basis theorem.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
