::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a commutative Noetherian ring with identity and let $I$ be an ideal in $R$. Let $J = \operatorname{Rad}(I)$. Prove that there exists a positive integer $n$ such that $j^{n} \in I$ for all $j \in J$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
J
\coloneqq
\sqrt I
=
\{r\in R:r^m\in I\text{ for some }m\ge1\}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The ideal $J$ is finitely generated.


::: pf-proof

The radical
$$
\sqrt I
$$
is an ideal of the commutative ring $R$.
Because $R$ is Noetherian, every ideal is finitely generated.
Thus there exist
$$
x_1,\ldots,x_r\in J
$$
such that
$$
J=(x_1,\ldots,x_r).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For each
$$
i=1,\ldots,r,
$$
there exists a positive integer
$$
n_i
$$
such that
$$
x_i^{n_i}\in I.
$$


::: pf-proof

Each
$$
x_i
$$
belongs to
$$
J=\sqrt I.
$$
By definition of the radical, some positive power of $x_i$ lies in $I$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Define
$$
N
\coloneqq
1+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_i-1).
$$
Then every monomial
$$
x_1^{e_1}\cdots x_r^{e_r}
$$
of total degree
$$
e_1+\cdots+e_r=N
$$
is divisible by
$$
x_i^{n_i}
$$
for some $i$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose instead that
$$
e_i<n_i
$$
for every $i$.
Then
$$
e_i\le n_i-1
$$
for every $i$, so
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{r}e_i
\le
\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_i-1)
=
N-1.
$$
This contradicts
$$
e_1+\cdots+e_r=N.
$$
Hence
$$
e_i\ge n_i
$$
for at least one index $i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Every degree-$N$ monomial in
$$
x_1,\ldots,x_r
$$
belongs to $I$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, such a monomial is divisible by
$$
x_i^{n_i}
$$
for some $i$.
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
x_i^{n_i}\in I.
$$
Since $I$ is an ideal, every multiple of
$$
x_i^{n_i}
$$
also lies in $I$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

One has
$$
\boxed{
J^N\subseteq I.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
J=(x_1,\ldots,x_r),
$$
the ideal
$$
J^N
$$
is generated by the monomials
$$
x_1^{e_1}\cdots x_r^{e_r}
$$
with
$$
e_1+\cdots+e_r=N.
$$
Every such generator lies in $I$ by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.
Therefore
$$
J^N\subseteq I.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For every
$$
j\in J,
$$
one has
$$
j^N\in I.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
j\in J,
$$
then
$$
j^N\in J^N.
$$
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
J^N\subseteq I.
$$
Hence
$$
j^N\in I.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore there exists a single positive integer $N$ such that
$$
\boxed{
j^N\in I
\quad
\text{for every }j\in\sqrt I.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Take the integer $N$ defined in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and apply step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
