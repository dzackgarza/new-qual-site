::: {.problem}
Let R be a commutative Noetherian ring with identity and let I be an ideal in R. Let $J = { \mathrm { R a d } } ( I )$ . Prove that there exists a positive integer n such that $j ^ { n } \in I$ for all $j \in J$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
J
\coloneqq
\sqrt I
=
\{r\in R:r^m\in I\text{ for some }m\ge1\}.
$$

<1>1. The ideal $J$ is finitely generated.

::: {.proof}
The radical
$$
\sqrt I
$$
is an ideal of the commutative ring $R$.
Because $R$ is Noetherian, every ideal is finitely generated.
Thus there exist
$$
x_1,\ldots,x_r\in J
$$
such that
$$
J=(x_1,\ldots,x_r).
$$
:::

<1>2. For each
$$
i=1,\ldots,r,
$$
there exists a positive integer
$$
n_i
$$
such that
$$
x_i^{n_i}\in I.
$$

::: {.proof}
Each
$$
x_i
$$
belongs to
$$
J=\sqrt I.
$$
By definition of the radical, some positive power of $x_i$ lies in $I$.
:::

<1>3. Define
$$
N
\coloneqq
1+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_i-1).
$$
Then every monomial
$$
x_1^{e_1}\cdots x_r^{e_r}
$$
of total degree
$$
e_1+\cdots+e_r=N
$$
is divisible by
$$
x_i^{n_i}
$$
for some $i$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that
$$
e_i<n_i
$$
for every $i$.
Then
$$
e_i\le n_i-1
$$
for every $i$, so
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{r}e_i
\le
\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_i-1)
=
N-1.
$$
This contradicts
$$
e_1+\cdots+e_r=N.
$$
Hence
$$
e_i\ge n_i
$$
for at least one index $i$.
:::

<1>4. Every degree-$N$ monomial in
$$
x_1,\ldots,x_r
$$
belongs to $I$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, such a monomial is divisible by
$$
x_i^{n_i}
$$
for some $i$.
By step <1>2,
$$
x_i^{n_i}\in I.
$$
Since $I$ is an ideal, every multiple of
$$
x_i^{n_i}
$$
also lies in $I$.
:::

<1>5. One has
$$
\boxed{
J^N\subseteq I.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Because
$$
J=(x_1,\ldots,x_r),
$$
the ideal
$$
J^N
$$
is generated by the monomials
$$
x_1^{e_1}\cdots x_r^{e_r}
$$
with
$$
e_1+\cdots+e_r=N.
$$
Every such generator lies in $I$ by step <1>4.
Therefore
$$
J^N\subseteq I.
$$
:::

<1>6. For every
$$
j\in J,
$$
one has
$$
j^N\in I.
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
j\in J,
$$
then
$$
j^N\in J^N.
$$
Step <1>5 gives
$$
J^N\subseteq I.
$$
Hence
$$
j^N\in I.
$$
:::

<1>7. Therefore there exists a single positive integer $N$ such that
$$
\boxed{
j^N\in I
\quad
\text{for every }j\in\sqrt I.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Take the integer $N$ defined in step <1>3 and apply step <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
