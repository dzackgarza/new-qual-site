::: {.problem}
[NEW] Prove that if D is a Euclidean Domain, then D is a Principal Ideal Domain.
:::

Let
$$
\delta:D\setminus\{0\}\to\NN
$$
be a Euclidean function on the Euclidean domain $D$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The zero ideal of $D$ is principal.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
(0)=0D.
$$
Thus the zero ideal is generated by $0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
I\trianglelefteq D
$$
be a nonzero ideal.
Choose a nonzero element
$$
d\in I
$$
for which
$$
\delta(d)
$$
is minimal among the values
$$
\{\delta(x):0\ne x\in I\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $I$ is nonzero, the displayed set of Euclidean values is nonempty.
It is a subset of the natural numbers, so by well-ordering it has a least element.
Choose $d\in I$ realizing that minimum.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For any
$$
a\in I,
$$
Euclidean division by $d$ gives
$$
a=qd+r
$$
with either
$$
r=0
$$
or
$$
\delta(r)<\delta(d).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the Euclidean-division property, applied to
$$
a
$$
and the nonzero divisor
$$
d.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The remainder
$$
r
$$
from step <1>3 belongs to $I$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
a\in I
$$
and
$$
d\in I,
$$
the multiple
$$
qd
$$
lies in $I$.
Hence
$$
r
=
a-qd
\in
I.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The remainder in step <1>3 must be zero.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
r\ne0,
$$
then step <1>4 gives
$$
0\ne r\in I,
$$
while step <1>3 gives
$$
\delta(r)<\delta(d).
$$
This contradicts the minimal choice of $d$ in step <1>2.
Therefore
$$
r=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every element of $I$ is divisible by $d$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
a\in I.
$$
By steps <1>3 and <1>5,
$$
a=qd
$$
for some
$$
q\in D.
$$
Hence
$$
a\in(d).
$$
Thus
$$
I\subseteq(d).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
I=(d).
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>6 gives
$$
I\subseteq(d).
$$
Since
$$
d\in I
$$
and $I$ is an ideal,
$$
(d)\subseteq I.
$$
Therefore
$$
I=(d).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every ideal of $D$ is principal.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>1 treats the zero ideal.
Steps <1>2--<1>7 show that every nonzero ideal is principal.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
D\text{ is a principal ideal domain}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

A Euclidean domain is, by definition, an integral domain.
By step <1>8, every ideal is principal.
This is exactly the definition of a principal ideal domain.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>9 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
