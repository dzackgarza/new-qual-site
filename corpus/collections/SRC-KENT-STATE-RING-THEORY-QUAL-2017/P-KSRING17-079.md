::: {.problem}
Prove that if D is a Euclidean Domain, then D is a Principal Ideal Domain.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\delta:D\setminus\{0\}\to\NN
$$
be a Euclidean function on the Euclidean domain $D$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The zero ideal of $D$ is principal.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
(0)=0D.
$$
Thus the zero ideal is generated by $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Let
$$
I\trianglelefteq D
$$
be a nonzero ideal.
Choose a nonzero element
$$
d\in I
$$
for which
$$
\delta(d)
$$
is minimal among the values
$$
\{\delta(x):0\ne x\in I\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $I$ is nonzero, the displayed set of Euclidean values is nonempty.
It is a subset of the natural numbers, so by well-ordering it has a least element.
Choose $d\in I$ realizing that minimum.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For any
$$
a\in I,
$$
Euclidean division by $d$ gives
$$
a=qd+r
$$
with either
$$
r=0
$$
or
$$
\delta(r)<\delta(d).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the Euclidean-division property, applied to
$$
a
$$
and the nonzero divisor
$$
d.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The remainder
$$
r
$$
from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} belongs to $I$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
a\in I
$$
and
$$
d\in I,
$$
the multiple
$$
qd
$$
lies in $I$.
Hence
$$
r
=
a-qd
\in
I.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The remainder in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} must be zero.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
r\ne0,
$$
then step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
0\ne r\in I,
$$
while step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\delta(r)<\delta(d).
$$
This contradicts the minimal choice of $d$ in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
Therefore
$$
r=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Every element of $I$ is divisible by $d$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
a\in I.
$$
By steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
a=qd
$$
for some
$$
q\in D.
$$
Hence
$$
a\in(d).
$$
Thus
$$
I\subseteq(d).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

One has
$$
I=(d).
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
I\subseteq(d).
$$
Since
$$
d\in I
$$
and $I$ is an ideal,
$$
(d)\subseteq I.
$$
Therefore
$$
I=(d).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Every ideal of $D$ is principal.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} treats the zero ideal.
Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} show that every nonzero ideal is principal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
D\text{ is a principal ideal domain}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

A Euclidean domain is, by definition, an integral domain.
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, every ideal is principal.
This is exactly the definition of a principal ideal domain.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
