::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring with $1$. We say $R$ satisfies the ascending chain condition if whenever $I_{1} \subseteq I_{2} \subseteq I_{3} \subseteq \cdots$ is an ascending chain of ideals, there is an integer $N$ such that $I_{k} = I_{N}$ for all $k \geqslant N$. Show that $R$ satisfies the ascending chain condition if and only if every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Assume $R$ satisfies the ascending chain condition, and let
$$
I\trianglelefteq R.
$$
If $I$ is not finitely generated, then $I\ne(0)$.


::: pf-proof

The zero ideal is generated by the single element
$$
0.
$$
Thus a non-finitely-generated ideal cannot be zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Under the assumption that $I$ is not finitely generated, choose
$$
a_1\in I.
$$
Then
$$
(a_1)\subsetneq I.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the ideal $I$ contains a nonzero element; choose one and call it $a_1$.
If
$$
(a_1)=I,
$$
then $I$ would be generated by one element, contrary to the assumption.
Hence the containment is strict.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Suppose
$$
a_1,\ldots,a_n\in I
$$
have been chosen and
$$
I_n
\coloneqq
(a_1,\ldots,a_n).
$$
Then
$$
I_n\subsetneq I.
$$


::: pf-proof

The ideal $I_n$ is finitely generated.
If
$$
I_n=I,
$$
then $I$ would be finitely generated, contrary to the standing assumption.
Therefore
$$
I_n\subsetneq I.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Under the hypotheses of step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, choose
$$
a_{n+1}\in I\setminus I_n.
$$
Then
$$
I_n
\subsetneq
I_{n+1}
\coloneqq
(a_1,\ldots,a_n,a_{n+1}).
$$


::: pf-proof

Strict containment
$$
I_n\subsetneq I
$$
from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives an element
$$
a_{n+1}\in I\setminus I_n.
$$
Clearly
$$
I_n\subseteq I_{n+1}.
$$
Since
$$
a_{n+1}\in I_{n+1}
$$
but
$$
a_{n+1}\notin I_n,
$$
the containment is strict.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $I$ were not finitely generated, there would be a strictly increasing chain
$$
I_1
\subsetneq
I_2
\subsetneq
I_3
\subsetneq
\cdots
$$
of ideals of $R$.


::: pf-proof

Start with step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
Given
$$
I_n,
$$
steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} construct
$$
I_{n+1}
$$
strictly containing it.
Thus the construction continues indefinitely and yields the displayed chain.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore, if $R$ satisfies the ascending chain condition, every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated.


::: pf-proof

The chain in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} never stabilizes because every containment is strict.
This contradicts the ascending chain condition.
Hence no non-finitely-generated ideal can exist.
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\text{ACC}
\implies
\text{every ideal is finitely generated}.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Conversely, assume every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated.
Let
$$
I_1
\subseteq
I_2
\subseteq
I_3
\subseteq
\cdots
$$
be an ascending chain of ideals.
Define
$$
I
\coloneqq
\bigcup_{n\ge1}I_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

This introduces the union of the chain.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The set $I$ is an ideal of $R$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
x,y\in I.
$$
Then
$$
x\in I_m,
\qquad
y\in I_n
$$
for some
$$
m,n.
$$
Assume without loss of generality that
$$
m\le n.
$$
Since the chain is ascending,
$$
I_m\subseteq I_n.
$$
Thus
$$
x,y\in I_n,
$$
so
$$
x-y\in I_n\subseteq I.
$$

If
$$
r\in R
$$
and
$$
x\in I,
$$
choose
$$
n
$$
with
$$
x\in I_n.
$$
Then
$$
rx\in I_n\subseteq I.
$$
Hence $I$ is an ideal.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The ideal $I$ is finitely generated:
$$
I
=
(a_1,\ldots,a_t)
$$
for some
$$
a_1,\ldots,a_t\in I.
$$


::: pf-proof

This follows from the standing hypothesis that every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated, applied to the ideal $I$ from step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

For each
$$
j=1,\ldots,t,
$$
choose
$$
n_j
$$
such that
$$
a_j\in I_{n_j}.
$$
Let
$$
N
\coloneqq
\max\{n_1,\ldots,n_t\}.
$$
Then every generator $a_j$ lies in $I_N$.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
a_j\in I
=
\bigcup_{n\ge1}I_n,
$$
some index $n_j$ exists.
Since
$$
n_j\le N
$$
and the chain is ascending,
$$
I_{n_j}\subseteq I_N.
$$
Therefore
$$
a_j\in I_N
$$
for every $j$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

One has
$$
I=I_N.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, all generators
$$
a_1,\ldots,a_t
$$
of $I$ lie in $I_N$.
Therefore
$$
I
=(a_1,\ldots,a_t)
\subseteq
I_N.
$$
Conversely,
$$
I_N
\subseteq
\bigcup_{n\ge1}I_n
=
I.
$$
Thus
$$
I=I_N.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

For every
$$
k\ge N,
$$
one has
$$
I_k=I_N.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because the chain is ascending,
$$
I_N\subseteq I_k.
$$
Also
$$
I_k\subseteq I
=
I_N
$$
by step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.
Therefore
$$
I_k=I_N.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Hence, if every ideal is finitely generated, $R$ satisfies the ascending chain condition.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} shows that every ascending chain stabilizes.
Thus
$$
\boxed{
\text{every ideal finitely generated}
\implies
\text{ACC}.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
R\text{ satisfies ACC}
\iff
\text{every ideal of }R\text{ is finitely generated}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves the forward implication, and step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} proves the converse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} is the required equivalence.

:::
