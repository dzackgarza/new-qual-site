::: {.problem}
Let D be a principal ideal domain.
Prove that every nonzero prime ideal of D is a maximal ideal.
:::

Let
$$
P
$$
be a nonzero prime ideal of the PID $D$.
Since $D$ is principal, write
$$
P=(p)
$$
with
$$
p\ne0.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The element $p$ is irreducible.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
p=ab.
$$
Then
$$
ab\in(p).
$$
Since
$$
(p)
$$
is prime,
$$
a\in(p)
\quad\text{or}\quad
b\in(p).
$$

Suppose
$$
a\in(p).
$$
Then
$$
a=pc
$$
for some
$$
c\in D.
$$
Substituting into
$$
p=ab
$$
gives
$$
p=pcb.
$$
Because $D$ is an integral domain and
$$
p\ne0,
$$
cancellation gives
$$
1=cb.
$$
Thus $b$ is a unit.

The case
$$
b\in(p)
$$
similarly implies that $a$ is a unit.
Hence every factorization of $p$ has a unit factor, so $p$ is irreducible.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
I
$$
be an ideal satisfying
$$
(p)\subseteq I\subseteq D.
$$
Write
$$
I=(a).
$$


::: pf-proof

Every ideal of a PID is principal, so such an
$$
a\in D
$$
exists.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The inclusion
$$
(p)\subseteq(a)
$$
implies
$$
p=ar
$$
for some
$$
r\in D.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
p\in(p)\subseteq(a),
$$
the element $p$ lies in the principal ideal generated by $a$.
Thus
$$
p=ar
$$
for some
$$
r\in D.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Either
$$
I=D
$$
or
$$
I=(p).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>3,
$$
p=ar.
$$
Since $p$ is irreducible by step <1>1, either
$$
a
$$
is a unit or
$$
r
$$
is a unit.

If $a$ is a unit, then
$$
(a)=D,
$$
so
$$
I=D.
$$

If $r$ is a unit, then
$$
a
$$
is associate to $p$, so
$$
(a)=(p).
$$
Thus
$$
I=(p).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{P\text{ is maximal}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>4 shows that there is no proper ideal strictly between
$$
P=(p)
$$
and
$$
D.
$$
Since $P$ is prime, it is proper. Hence it is maximal.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Since $P$ was an arbitrary nonzero prime ideal, step <1>5 proves the theorem.

:::

:::
