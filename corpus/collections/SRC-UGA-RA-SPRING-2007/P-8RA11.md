::: problem
Let $I$ denote the unit interval, equipped with Lebesgue measure.
Suppose $f\in L^2(I)$ and $F\in L^2(I\times I)$.
For each $x\in I$ at which the integral exists, set
\[
g(x)=\int_I f(y)F(x,y)\,dy.
\]
Show that $g(x)$ exists almost everywhere, and that $\|g\|_2\le\|f\|_2\|F\|_2$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Identify the almost-everywhere $L^2$ slices of the kernel.
::: proof
Since $F\in L^2(I\times I)$, Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
\int_I\left(\int_I |F(x,y)|^2\,dy\right)dx
=\|F\|_2^2<\infty.
\]
Hence for almost every $x\in I$,
\[
\int_I|F(x,y)|^2\,dy<\infty,
\]
so the slice $F(x,\cdot)$ belongs to $L^2(I)$.
:::

<1>2. Prove existence of $g(x)$ and the pointwise estimate.
::: proof
Fix an $x$ for which $F(x,\cdot)\in L^2(I)$. Since $f\in L^2(I)$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\int_I |f(y)F(x,y)|\,dy
\le \|f\|_2
\left(\int_I|F(x,y)|^2\,dy\right)^{1/2}<\infty.
\]
Thus $g(x)$ exists for almost every $x$. Moreover,
\[
|g(x)|^2
\le \|f\|_2^2\int_I|F(x,y)|^2\,dy.
\]
:::

<1>3. Integrate the pointwise estimate.
::: proof
Integrating over $x$ and applying Tonelli again,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|g\|_2^2
&=\int_I|g(x)|^2\,dx\\
&\le \|f\|_2^2
\int_I\int_I|F(x,y)|^2\,dy\,dx\\
&=\|f\|_2^2\|F\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking square roots yields
\[
\boxed{\|g\|_2\le\|f\|_2\|F\|_2.}
\]
:::
:::
