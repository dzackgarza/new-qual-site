::: {.problem}
Let $f:\RR\to\RR$ be a continuously differentiable function.
Let $g:\RR^2\to\RR$ be defined by
$$
g(x,y)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y} & x\neq y,\\
f'(x) & x=y.
\end{cases}
$$
Prove that $g$ is continuous.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no solution set for this examination is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed, and the card's existing solution is restructured into numbered steps. Here $f:\RR\to\RR$ is continuously differentiable and $g$ is the difference quotient off the diagonal, extended by $f'(x)$ on it; the criterion is that a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, applied to the parameter integral.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #quotient}

For $x\neq y$, $\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}=\int_0^1f'(y+t(x-y))\,dt$.

::: pf-proof

The fundamental theorem of calculus gives $f(x)-f(y)=\int_y^xf'(s)\,ds$, and the substitution $s=y+t(x-y)$, $ds=(x-y)\,dt$, turns this into $(x-y)\int_0^1f'(y+t(x-y))\,dt$; divide by $x-y\neq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #diag}

For $x=y$, $\int_0^1f'(x)\,dt=f'(x)$.

::: pf-proof

The integrand is constant in $t$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #formula}

$g(x,y)=\int_0^1f'(y+t(x-y))\,dt$ for all $(x,y)\in\RR^{2}$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#quotient){.pf-ref} and [](#diag){.pf-ref} cover the two cases and agree with the definition of $g$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cont}

$g$ is continuous at each $(x_0,y_0)$.

::: pf-proof

Let $K$ be a compact interval containing the points $y+t(x-y)$ for $0\leq t\leq1$ and $(x,y)$ in a small neighbourhood of $(x_0,y_0)$ — for instance the interval spanned by $x_0,y_0$ enlarged by the diameter of that neighbourhood. On $K$ the continuous function $f'$ is uniformly continuous. Hence for $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that $\abs{f'(u)-f'(v)}<\varepsilon$ whenever $u,v\in K$ with $\abs{u-v}<\delta$; taking $(x,y)$ within $\delta$ of $(x_0,y_0)$ gives $\abs{(y+t(x-y))-(y_0+t(x_0-y_0))}<\delta$ uniformly in $t\in[0,1]$, so $\abs{g(x,y)-g(x_0,y_0)}\leq\int_0^1\abs{f'(y+t(x-y))-f'(y_0+t(x_0-y_0))}\,dt<\varepsilon$ by step [](#formula){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$g\in C(\RR^{2})$.

::: pf-proof
