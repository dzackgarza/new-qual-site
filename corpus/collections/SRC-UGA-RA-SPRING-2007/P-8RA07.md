::: problem
Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be a continuously differentiable function.
Let $g:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ be defined by
\[
g(x,y)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y} & x\neq y,\\
f'(x) & x=y.
\end{cases}
\]
Prove that $g$ is continuous.
:::

::: solution
For $x\neq y$, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus gives
\[
f(x)-f(y)
=\int_y^x f'(s)\,ds.
\]
With the change of variables
\[
s=y+t(x-y),
\qquad 0\le t\le1,
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}
=\int_0^1 f'(y+t(x-y))\,dt.
\]
When $x=y$, the same formula gives
\[
\int_0^1 f'(x)\,dt=f'(x).
\]
Thus for every $(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2$,
\[
g(x,y)=\int_0^1 f'(y+t(x-y))\,dt.
\]

Fix $(x_0,y_0)$. For $(x,y)$ in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $(x_0,y_0)$, all points
\[
y+t(x-y),\qquad 0\le t\le1,
\]
lie in one compact interval $K$. Since $f'$ is continuous, it is uniformly continuous on $K$. Therefore, as $(x,y)\to(x_0,y_0)$,
\[
f'(y+t(x-y))
\longrightarrow
f'(y_0+t(x_0-y_0))
\]
uniformly in $t\in[0,1]$. Hence
\[
g(x,y)
\longrightarrow
\int_0^1 f'(y_0+t(x_0-y_0))\,dt
=g(x_0,y_0).
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{g\in C(\mathbb R^2).}
\]
:::
