::: problem
Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{M},\mu)$ be a measure space.
A set $\Phi\subset L^1(\Omega)$ is said to be uniformly integrable if, for each $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that whenever $f\in\Phi$ and $E\subset\Omega$ is measurable with $\mu(E)<\delta$, we have $\int_E|f|\,d\mu<\varepsilon$.

Assume $\mu(\Omega)=1$ and suppose $\{f_n\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is a uniformly integrable sequence of functions such that $f_n\to 0$ almost everywhere.
Prove that
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_\Omega|f_n|\,d\mu=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Uniform integrability condition:
<2>1. Let $\varepsilon > 0$ be given.
Since $\{f_n\}$ is uniformly integrable, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that for every measurable set $E \subset \Omega$ with $\mu(E) < \delta$:
\[
\int_E |f_n|\, d\mu < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \quad \text{for all } n \in \mathbb{N}.
\]
Proof: definition of uniform integrability.

<1>2. Application of Egorov’s Theorem:
<2>1. Because $\mu(\Omega) = 1 < \infty$ and $f_n \to 0$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$, by Egorov’s Theorem there exists a measurable set $E \subset \Omega$ such that:
1. $\mu(E) < \delta$,
2. $f_n \to 0$ uniformly on $E^c = \Omega \setminus E$.
Proof: Egorov’s Theorem for finite measure spaces.
<2>2. Uniform convergence on $E^c$ implies there exists an integer $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $n \ge N$:
\[
|f_n(x)| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \quad \text{for all } x \in E^c.
\]
Proof: definition of uniform convergence.

<1>3. Splitting the integral over $\Omega = E^c \cup E$:
<2>1. For every $n \ge N$, decompose the integral:
\[
\int_\Omega |f_n|\, d\mu = \int_{E^c} |f_n|\, d\mu + \int_E |f_n|\, d\mu.
\]
Proof: additivity of the Lebesgue integral over disjoint measurable sets.
<2>2. Bound the integral over $E^c$:
\[
\int_{E^c} |f_n|\, d\mu \le \left( \sup_{x \in E^c} |f_n(x)| \right) \mu(E^c) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \cdot \mu(\Omega) = \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
Proof: uniform bound on $E^c$ and $\mu(E^c) \le \mu(\Omega) = 1$.
<2>3. Bound the integral over $E$:
Since $\mu(E) < \delta$, by uniform integrability in <1>1:
\[
\int_E |f_n|\, d\mu < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
Proof: <1>1.
<2>4. Combining the two bounds:
\[
\int_\Omega |f_n|\, d\mu < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } n \ge N.
\]
Proof: sum of inequalities.

<1>4. Conclusion:
Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, $\lim_{n\to\infty} \int_\Omega |f_n|\, d\mu = 0$. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 through <1>3.
:::
