::: {.problem}
For what triples of real numbers $(a, b, c)$ with $a \neq 0$ is the function defined by

$$f(x) = \begin{cases} x, & \text{if } x \leq 1 \\ ax^2 + bx + c, & \text{if } x > 1 \end{cases}$$

differentiable at all real $x$?

(A) $\{(a, 1 - 2a, a) \mid a \text{ is a nonzero real number}\}$
(B) $\{(a, 1 - 2a, c) \mid a, c \text{ are real numbers and } a \neq 0\}$
(C) $\{(a, b, c) \mid a, b, c \text{ are real numbers, } a \neq 0, \text{ and } a + b + c = 1\}$
(D) $\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}, 0, 0\right)\right\}$
(E) $\{(a, 1 - 2a, 0) \mid a \text{ is a nonzero real number}\}$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Continuity at $x=1$ requires
$$
a+b+c=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
The left-hand value is $f(1)=1$, while the right-hand limit is
$a+b+c$. Differentiability at $1$ implies continuity there, so these must be
equal.
:::

<1>2. Equality of the one-sided derivatives at $x=1$ requires
$$
2a+b=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $x<1$, one has $f'(x)=1$. For $x>1$,
$$
f'(x)=2ax+b,
$$
whose right-hand limit at $1$ is $2a+b$. Differentiability at $1$ therefore
requires $2a+b=1$.
:::

<1>3. The conditions in steps <1>1 and <1>2 are equivalent to
$$
b=1-2a,
\qquad
c=a.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives $b=1-2a$. Substituting into step <1>1 yields
$$
c=1-a-(1-2a)=a.
$$
Conversely these formulas satisfy both equations.
:::

<1>4. The set of admissible triples is
$$
\boxed{\{(a,1-2a,a):a\in\RR\setminus\{0\}\}},
$$
so the answer is (A).

::: {.proof}
Away from $x=1$, each branch is a polynomial and hence differentiable. At
$x=1$, steps <1>1--<1>3 give exactly the conditions for continuity and matching
one-sided derivatives. The problem additionally imposes $a\neq0$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 gives exactly the requested triples.
:::
:::
