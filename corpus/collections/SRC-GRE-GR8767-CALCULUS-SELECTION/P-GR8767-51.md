::: {.problem}
Let $x_1 = 1$ and $x_{n+1} = \sqrt{3 + 2x_n}$ for all positive integers $n$. If it is assumed that $\{x_n\}$ converges, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n =$

(A) $-1$
(B) $0$
(C) $\sqrt{5}$
(D) $e$
(E) $3$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If
$$
L=\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n,
$$
then $L\geq0$ and
$$
L=\sqrt{3+2L}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every term from $x_2$ onward is a principal square root, hence is nonnegative;
therefore the assumed limit is nonnegative. Since the square-root function is
continuous on $[0,\infty)$, taking limits in
$x_{n+1}=\sqrt{3+2x_n}$ gives the displayed fixed-point equation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The only possible nonnegative value of $L$ is $3$.


::: pf-proof

Squaring the equation from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
L^2=3+2L,
$$
so
$$
(L-3)(L+1)=0.
$$
Thus $L=3$ or $L=-1$. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} excludes the negative root.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n=3},
$$
so the answer is (E).


::: pf-proof

The problem assumes convergence, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} determines its only possible
limit.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the requested limit and choice.

:::
