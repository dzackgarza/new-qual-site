::: {.problem}
If $c > 0$ and $f(x) = e^x - cx$ for all real numbers $x$, then the minimum value of $f$ is

(A) $f(c)$
(B) $f(e^c)$
(C) $f\left(\frac{1}{c}\right)$
(D) $f(\log c)$
(E) nonexistent
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The unique critical point of $f$ is $x=\log c$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
f'(x)=e^x-c,
$$
one has $f'(x)=0$ exactly when $e^x=c$, equivalently $x=\log c$ because
$c>0$.
:::

<1>2. The point $x=\log c$ is the global minimizer of $f$.

::: {.proof}
The second derivative is
$$
f''(x)=e^x>0
$$
for every real $x$. Hence $f'$ is strictly increasing. By step <1>1,
$f'(x)<0$ for $x<\log c$ and $f'(x)>0$ for $x>\log c$, so $f$ decreases up to
$\log c$ and increases thereafter.
:::

<1>3. The minimum value is
$$
\boxed{f(\log c)},
$$
so the answer is (D).

::: {.proof}
This is the value of $f$ at the global minimizer from step <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives the requested minimum value and choice.
:::
:::
