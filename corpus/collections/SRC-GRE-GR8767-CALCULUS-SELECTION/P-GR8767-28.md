::: {.problem}
Questions 28–30 are based on the following information. Let $f$ be a function such that the graph of $f$ is a semicircle $S$ with end points $(a, 0)$ and $(b, 0)$ where $a < b$.

$\abs{\int_a^b f(x) \, dx} =$

(A) $f(b) - f(a)$
(B) $\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$
(C) $(b - a)\frac{\pi}{4}$
(D) $(b - a)^2 \pi$
(E) $(b - a)^2 \frac{\pi}{8}$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The radius of the semicircle is
$$
r=\frac{b-a}{2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The endpoints $(a,0)$ and $(b,0)$ are the endpoints of a diameter, whose
length is $b-a$.
:::

<1>2. The absolute value of the integral is the area of that semicircle.

::: {.proof}
The graph is one semicircle lying entirely on one side of the $x$-axis. Thus
$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is its signed area, and taking the absolute value removes
the sign.
:::

<1>3. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\abs{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx}=\frac{\pi(b-a)^2}{8}},
$$
so the answer is (E).

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1 and <1>2, the required quantity is
$$
\frac12\pi r^2
=\frac12\pi\left(\frac{b-a}{2}\right)^2
=\frac{\pi(b-a)^2}{8}.
$$
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives the requested value and choice.
:::
:::
