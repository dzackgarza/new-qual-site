::: {.problem}
$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{n+1} =$

(A) $\frac{1}{e}$
(B) $\log 2$
(C) $1$
(D) $e$
(E) $+\infty$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $n\geq1$,
$$
\frac{n}{n+1}\geq\frac12.
$$


::: pf-proof

The inequality is equivalent to $2n\geq n+1$, which holds for $n\geq1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The partial sums satisfy
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{n}{n+1}\geq\frac N2,
$$
and hence tend to $+\infty$.


::: pf-proof

Summing the estimate from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} over $1\leq n\leq N$ gives the displayed
lower bound. Since $N/2\to+\infty$, the partial sums are unbounded above; they
are also increasing because every summand is positive. Therefore they tend to
$+\infty$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The answer is $\boxed{\text{(E)}}$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that the series diverges to $+\infty$, which is option (E).

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} identifies the required choice.

:::
