::: {.problem}
Which of the following is true of the behavior of $f(x) = \frac{x^3 + 8}{x^2 - 4}$ as $x \to 2$?

(A) The limit is $0$.
(B) The limit is $1$.
(C) The limit is $4$.
(D) The graph of the function has a vertical asymptote at $2$.
(E) The function has unequal, finite left-hand and right-hand limits.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Near $x=2$,
$$
f(x)=\frac{x^3+8}{(x-2)(x+2)},
$$
where the numerator and the factor $x+2$ are positive and bounded away from
$0$.


::: pf-proof

At $x=2$,
$$
x^3+8=16>0,
\qquad
x+2=4>0.
$$
By continuity, both quantities remain positive near $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The one-sided limits are
$$
\lim_{x\to2^-}f(x)=-\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{x\to2^+}f(x)=+\infty.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the sign and divergence are controlled by the factor
$1/(x-2)$: it tends to $-\infty$ from the left and $+\infty$ from the right,
while $(x^3+8)/(x+2)$ tends to $4>0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The answer is $\boxed{\text{(D)}}$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} says precisely that $x=2$ is a vertical asymptote. The one-sided
limits are not finite, so option (E) does not apply.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} identifies the stated behavior.

:::
