::: {.problem}
For $x \geq 0$, $\frac{d}{dx}(x^e \cdot e^x) =$

(A) $x^e \cdot e^x + x^{e-1} \cdot e^{x+1}$
(B) $x^e \cdot e^x + x^{e+1} \cdot e^{x-1}$
(C) $x^e \cdot e^x$
(D) $x^{e-1} \cdot e^{x+1}$
(E) $x^{e+1} \cdot e^{x-1}$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For $x>0$,
$$
\frac{d}{dx}\left(x^e e^x\right)
=e x^{e-1}e^x+x^e e^x.
$$


::: pf-proof

The product rule gives
$$
\frac{d}{dx}\left(x^e e^x\right)
=\frac{d}{dx}(x^e)e^x+x^e\frac{d}{dx}(e^x)
=e x^{e-1}e^x+x^e e^x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

At the endpoint $x=0$, the right derivative is $0$, which is also the
value of the expression in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} at $x=0$.


::: pf-proof

Since $f(0)=0$,
$$
\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{f(h)-f(0)}h
=\lim_{h\downarrow0}h^{e-1}e^h
=0
$$
because $e-1>0$. Both terms
$x^e e^x$ and $x^{e-1}e^{x+1}$ vanish at $x=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The answer is $\boxed{\text{(A)}}$.


::: pf-proof

Since $e\,e^x=e^{x+1}$, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} becomes
$$
x^e e^x+x^{e-1}e^{x+1},
$$
which is option (A), and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} verifies the endpoint.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} identifies the required choice.

:::
