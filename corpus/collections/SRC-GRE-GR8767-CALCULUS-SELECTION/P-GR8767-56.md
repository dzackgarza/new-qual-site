::: {.problem}
The polynomial $p(x) = 1 + \frac{1}{2}(x - 1) - \frac{1}{8}(x - 1)^2$ is used to approximate $\sqrt{1.01}$. Which of the following most closely approximates the error $\sqrt{1.01} - p(1.01)$?

(A) $\left(\frac{1}{16}\right) \times 10^{-6}$
(B) $\left(\frac{1}{48}\right) \times 10^{-8}$
(C) $\left(\frac{3}{8}\right) \times 10^{-10}$
(D) $-\left(\frac{3}{8}\right) \times 10^{-10}$
(E) $-\left(\frac{1}{16}\right) \times 10^{-6}$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ and $h=0.01$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The polynomial $p$ is the degree-$2$ Taylor polynomial of $f$ at $x=1$.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
f(1)=1,
\qquad
f'(1)=\frac12,
\qquad
f''(1)=-\frac14.
$$
Therefore
$$
f(1)+f'(1)(x-1)+\frac{f''(1)}{2}(x-1)^2
=1+\frac12(x-1)-\frac18(x-1)^2
=p(x).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For some $\xi\in(1,1.01)$,
$$
\sqrt{1.01}-p(1.01)
=\frac1{16}\xi^{-5/2}\times10^{-6}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The third derivative is
$$
f'''(x)=\frac38x^{-5/2}.
$$
Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder gives
$$
f(1+h)-p(1+h)
=\frac{f'''(\xi)}{3!}h^3
=\frac{(3/8)\xi^{-5/2}}6(10^{-2})^3
=\frac1{16}\xi^{-5/2}\times10^{-6}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The closest listed approximation is
$$
\boxed{\frac1{16}\times10^{-6}},
$$
so the answer is (A).


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} has the positive factor $\xi^{-5/2}$ with
$$
1.01^{-5/2}<\xi^{-5/2}<1.
$$
Thus the exact error is positive and differs from
$(1/16)\times10^{-6}$ only by the multiplicative factor
$\xi^{-5/2}$, which is close to $1$. Among the listed values, option (A) is the
corresponding third-order Taylor error.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} identifies the requested approximation and choice.

:::
