::: {.problem}
Questions 28–30 are based on the following information. Let $f$ be a function such that the graph of $f$ is a semicircle $S$ with end points $(a, 0)$ and $(b, 0)$ where $a < b$.

The improper integral $\int_a^b f(x) f'(x) \, dx$ is

(A) necessarily zero
(B) possibly zero but not necessarily
(C) necessarily nonexistent
(D) possibly nonexistent but not necessarily
(E) none of the above
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
h=\frac{a+b}{2},
\qquad
r=\frac{b-a}{2}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $x\in(a,b)$,
$$
f(x)^2=r^2-(x-h)^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The graph is one of the two semicircles of the circle with center $(h,0)$ and
radius $r$. Thus each point $(x,f(x))$ satisfies
$(x-h)^2+f(x)^2=r^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $x\in(a,b)$,
$$
f(x)f'(x)=-(x-h).
$$


::: pf-proof

On the open interval $(a,b)$ the semicircle is differentiable. Differentiating
the identity from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
2f(x)f'(x)=-2(x-h).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The improper integral exists and equals
$$
\boxed{\int_a^b f(x)f'(x)\,dx=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the integrand equals the continuous function $-(x-h)$ throughout
$(a,b)$, so the endpoint improper limits exist and
$$
\int_a^b f(x)f'(x)\,dx
=-\int_a^b(x-h)\,dx
=-\left[\frac{(x-h)^2}{2}\right]_a^b
=0,
$$
because $a-h=-r$ and $b-h=r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The answer is $\boxed{\text{(A)}}$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that the integral is necessarily zero for either semicircle.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} identifies the requested choice.

:::

:::

:::
