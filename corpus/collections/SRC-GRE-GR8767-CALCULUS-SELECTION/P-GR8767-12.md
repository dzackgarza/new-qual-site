::: {.problem}
$\int_0^\pi e^{\sin^2 x} e^{\cos^2 x} \, dx =$

(A) $\pi$
(B) $e\pi$
(C) $e^\pi$
(D) $e^{\sin^2 \pi}$
(E) $e^\pi - 1$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every real $x$,
$$
e^{\sin^2x}e^{\cos^2x}=e.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$,
$$
e^{\sin^2x}e^{\cos^2x}
=e^{\sin^2x+\cos^2x}
=e.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The integral equals
$$
\boxed{e\pi},
$$
so the answer is (B).


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\int_0^\pi e^{\sin^2x}e^{\cos^2x}\,dx
=\int_0^\pi e\,dx
=e\pi.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives the requested value and choice.

:::
