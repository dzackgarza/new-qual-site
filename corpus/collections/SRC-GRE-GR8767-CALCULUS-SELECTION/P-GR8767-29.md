::: {.problem}
Questions 28–30 are based on the following information. Let $f$ be a function such that the graph of $f$ is a semicircle $S$ with end points $(a, 0)$ and $(b, 0)$ where $a < b$.

The graph of $y = 3f(x)$ is a

(A) translation of $S$
(B) semicircle with radius three times that of $S$
(C) subset of an ellipse
(D) subset of a parabola
(E) subset of a hyperbola
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
h=\frac{a+b}{2},
\qquad
r=\frac{b-a}{2}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Points $(x,y)$ on the semicircle $S$ satisfy
$$
(x-h)^2+y^2=r^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The endpoints $(a,0)$ and $(b,0)$ form a horizontal diameter, so the circle has
center $(h,0)$ and radius $r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $Y=3f(x)$, then every point $(x,Y)$ on the new graph satisfies
$$
\frac{(x-h)^2}{r^2}+\frac{Y^2}{9r^2}=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a point of the original graph, put $y=f(x)$, so $Y=3y$ and $y=Y/3$.
Substituting into step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
(x-h)^2+\frac{Y^2}{9}=r^2,
$$
which is the displayed equation after division by $r^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The answer is $\boxed{\text{(C)}}$.


::: pf-proof

The equation in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} is an ellipse with semiaxes $r$ and $3r$. Since the
original graph is only one semicircle, its vertical stretch is the corresponding
half of that ellipse, hence a subset of an ellipse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} identifies the requested geometric type.

:::
