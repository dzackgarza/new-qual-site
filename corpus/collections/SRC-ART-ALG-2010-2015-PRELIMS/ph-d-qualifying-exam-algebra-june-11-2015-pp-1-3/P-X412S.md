::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $X^4 - 12$ over $\QQ$.

(a) Give an explicit description of $K$ and compute its degree over $\QQ$.

(b) What is the Galois group of $K$ over $\QQ$?
You should identify the group as well as its action on the roots of $X^4 - 12$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\alpha=12^{1/4}>0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The roots of $X^4-12$ are
$$
\alpha,\quad i\alpha,\quad -\alpha,\quad -i\alpha.
$$

::: pf-proof

If $z^4=12=\alpha^4$, then $(z/\alpha)^4=1$. The fourth roots of unity
are $1,i,-1,-i$, which gives exactly the four displayed roots.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The splitting field is
$$
K=\QQ(\alpha,i).
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, every root lies in $\QQ(\alpha,i)$, so the splitting field
is contained in this field. Conversely, a splitting field contains both
$\alpha$ and $i\alpha$, hence also
$$
i=\frac{i\alpha}{\alpha}.
$$
Therefore it contains $\QQ(\alpha,i)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

One has $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4$.

::: pf-proof

The polynomial $X^4-12$ is Eisenstein at $3$: every nonleading
coefficient is divisible by $3$, and the constant term $-12$ is not
divisible by $9$. Hence $X^4-12$ is irreducible over $\QQ$, so it is the
minimal polynomial of $\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The degree of the splitting field is
$$
[K:\QQ]=\boxed{8}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Because $\alpha$ is real, $\QQ(\alpha)\subset\RR$, so
$i\notin\QQ(\alpha)$. Since $i$ satisfies $T^2+1$, it follows that
$$
[K:\QQ(\alpha)]=2.
$$
Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} and the tower law therefore give
$$
[K:\QQ]=[K:\QQ(\alpha)]\,[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=2\cdot4=8.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The Galois group is
$$
\Gal(K/\QQ)\cong\boxed{D_4},
$$
the dihedral group of order $8$.

::: pf-proof

Order the roots as
$$
(1,2,3,4)=(\alpha,i\alpha,-\alpha,-i\alpha).
$$
Since $[K:\QQ(i)]=4$, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ(i)$
still has degree $4$ and is $X^4-12$. Hence the $\QQ(i)$-embedding
sending $\alpha$ to the conjugate root $i\alpha$ extends to an
automorphism $\sigma$ of $K$ with
$$
\sigma(\alpha)=i\alpha,
\qquad
\sigma(i)=i.
$$
Thus $\sigma$ acts on the roots as
$$
(1\,2\,3\,4).
$$

Complex conjugation gives another automorphism $\tau$, with
$$
\tau(\alpha)=\alpha,
\qquad
\tau(i)=-i,
$$
so on the roots
$$
\tau=(2\,4).
$$
Therefore
$$
\sigma^4=\tau^2=1,
\qquad
\tau\sigma\tau=\sigma^{-1}.
$$
The subgroup generated by $\sigma$ and $\tau$ has the eight distinct
elements
$$
1,\sigma,\sigma^2,\sigma^3,
\tau,\tau\sigma,\tau\sigma^2,\tau\sigma^3.
$$
By step [](#s4){.pf-ref} the full Galois group has order $8$, so these automorphisms
constitute the whole group. Their presentation is that of the dihedral
group of order $8$.

:::

:::
