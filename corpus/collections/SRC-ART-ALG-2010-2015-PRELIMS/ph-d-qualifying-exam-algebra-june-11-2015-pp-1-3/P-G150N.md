::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite group with $|G| = 150$.
Prove that for at least one $k \in \{5, 10, 15, 25\}$ there exists a normal subgroup $N \trianglelefteq G$ of order $k$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A Sylow $5$-subgroup $P$ has order $25$ and index $6$.

::: {.proof}
The factorization is $150=2\cdot3\cdot5^2$. Sylow's existence
theorem [@DF04] supplies $P\leq G$ of order $5^2=25$.
The index is $[G:P]=150/25=6$.
:::

<1>2. The action on the six left cosets gives a normal subgroup
$N\lhd G$ contained in $P$.

::: {.proof}
Left multiplication defines a homomorphism
$$
\rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}(G/P)\cong S_6,
\qquad \rho(g)(aP)=gaP.
$$
It is well-defined because multiplication preserves equality
of left cosets, and the inverse permutation is induced by
$g^{-1}$. Put $N=\ker\rho$, which is normal in $G$.
An element of $N$ fixes the coset $P$, so it lies in its
stabilizer $P$. Thus $N\subseteq P$ and
$$
|N|\in\{1,5,25\}.
$$
:::

<1>3. The kernel is nontrivial, giving an allowed order.

::: {.proof}
If $N=1$, the homomorphism $\rho$ would embed $G$ in $S_6$.
Lagrange's theorem would then imply
$$
150\mid |S_6|=6!=720=2^4\cdot3^2\cdot5.
$$
This is impossible because $25\mid150$ but $25\nmid720$.
Therefore step <1>2 leaves $|N|=5$ or $25$, both members
of the required set. The normal subgroup $N$ proves the claim.
:::
:::
