::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $x^3 + 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

a. What is the dimension of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$?

b. What is the Galois group of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$?
(Identify the isomorphism type of the group, and also indicate how its elements act as permutations of the roots of $x^3 + 2$.)

c. Show that $K$ contains a primitive third root of unity $\zeta_3$.
What is the Galois group of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$?
What is the Galois group of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$?
Again identify these groups as permutation groups and indicate their isomorphism type.

d. How are the three groups discussed in this problem related?
:::

::: solution
Let $a=-\sqrt[3]{2}$ be the real root of $x^3+2$, and choose
$\zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}$. Label the three roots by
$a_j=\zeta^ja$, with indices in $\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$.

<1>1. The splitting field is $K=\mathbb Q(a,\zeta)$, and
$[K:\mathbb Q]=6$.

::: proof
The roots $a,\zeta a,\zeta^2a$ lie in $\mathbb Q(a,\zeta)$.
Conversely, the field generated by them contains $a$ and
$\zeta=(\zeta a)/a$, so it is precisely $K$.
Eisenstein's criterion at $2$ makes $x^3+2$ irreducible over
$\mathbb Q$, giving $[\mathbb Q(a):\mathbb Q]=3$ [@DF04].
The field $\mathbb Q(a)$ is real, whereas $\zeta$ is nonreal
and satisfies $\zeta^2+\zeta+1=0$. Thus adjoining $\zeta$
has degree $2$, and the tower law gives $[K:\mathbb Q]=6$.
As a splitting field in characteristic zero, this is a Galois
extension [@DF04].
:::

<1>2. The full Galois group is $S_3$ acting on the three roots,
with generators
$$
r(a)=\zeta a,\quad r(\zeta)=\zeta,
\qquad s(a)=a,\quad s(\zeta)=\zeta^{-1}.
$$

::: proof
The polynomial $x^2+x+1$ is the minimal polynomial of $\zeta$
over $\mathbb Q$, since its roots are nonreal. The tower law
therefore gives $[K:\mathbb Q(\zeta)]=3$. Hence $x^3+2$
remains the minimal polynomial of $a$ over $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$.
Sending $a$ to $\zeta a$ defines an embedding of $K$ into
itself fixing $\zeta$. Its image contains $a$ as well, so it
is the automorphism $r$. Complex conjugation restricts to $s$.

Their actions on the root indices are
$$
r:j\longmapsto j+1,\qquad s:j\longmapsto-j.
$$
Thus $r$ is the cycle $(0\,1\,2)$ and $s$ the transposition
$(1\,2)$. Their six products $r^us^v$, with $0\leq u<3$
and $v\in\{0,1\}$, act by $j\mapsto u+(-1)^vj$ and are
distinct permutations. They exhaust the Galois group, whose
order is $6$. Equivalently, these are all permutations of the
three roots, since the roots generate $K$ and the action is
faithful. The relations are
$r^3=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$.
:::

<1>3. The other two groups are $C_3$ and $C_2$, linked to the
full group by a split exact sequence.

::: proof
An automorphism $r^us^v$ fixes $\zeta$ exactly when $v=0$.
Consequently
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q(\zeta))=\langle r\rangle
\cong C_3,
$$
acting on $a_0,a_1,a_2$ by cyclic rotations.
The extension $\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q$ is the splitting
field of $x^2+x+1$ and has degree $2$. Its nonidentity
automorphism $\bar s$ interchanges the roots $\zeta,\zeta^2$.
Thus its Galois group is the full two-letter permutation group
$S_2\cong C_2$.

Restriction to $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ sends $r$ to the identity
and $s$ to $\bar s$, so is onto and has kernel $\langle r\rangle$.
This gives
$$
1\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q(\zeta))
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}}
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow1.
$$
The map sending $\bar s$ to $s$ is a homomorphic section,
because both have order $2$. Thus $G\cong C_3\rtimes C_2$,
where the nonidentity element of $C_2$ acts on $C_3$ by
inversion, as the relation $srs=r^{-1}$ explicitly shows.
This is not a direct product, since $r\ne r^{-1}$.
:::
:::
