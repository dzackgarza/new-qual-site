::: problem
a. Prove that every group of order 15 is cyclic.

b. Prove that every group of order $255 = 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 17$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

<1>1. Let $G$ have order $15 = 3 \cdot 5$. By Sylow, the number $n_3$ of Sylow $3$-subgroups satisfies $n_3 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $n_3 \mid 5$, so $n_3 = 1$.
::: {.proof}
Sylow's theorem.
:::

<1>2. Similarly, $n_5 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ and $n_5 \mid 3$, so $n_5 = 1$.
::: {.proof}
Sylow's theorem.
:::

<1>3. Hence $G$ has a unique (hence normal) Sylow $3$-subgroup $P$ and a unique normal Sylow $5$-subgroup $Q$.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2.
:::

<1>4. $P \cong \ZZ/3$ and $Q \cong \ZZ/5$ (groups of prime order are cyclic).
::: {.proof}
<1>3.
:::

<1>5. $G = P \times Q$ (both are normal, intersect trivially, and $|PQ| = 15 = |G|$).
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Hence $G \cong \ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/5 \cong \ZZ/15$ (by the Chinese remainder theorem, since $\gcd(3,5) = 1$).
::: {.proof}
<1>5.
:::

<1>7. Therefore $G$ is cyclic.
::: {.proof}
<1>6.
:::

**(b).**

<1>1. Let $G$ have order $255 = 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 17$. By Sylow, $n_{17} \equiv 1 \pmod{17}$ and $n_{17} \mid 15$, so $n_{17} = 1$.
::: {.proof}
Sylow's theorem (the divisors of $15$ are $1, 3, 5, 15$, and only $1 \equiv 1 \pmod{17}$).
:::

<1>2. Hence $G$ has a unique normal Sylow $17$-subgroup $P \cong \ZZ/17$.
::: {.proof}
<1>1.
:::

<1>3. $n_5 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ and $n_5 \mid 51 = 3 \cdot 17$, so $n_5 \in \{1, 51\}$.
::: {.proof}
Sylow's theorem.
:::

<1>4. $n_3 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $n_3 \mid 85 = 5 \cdot 17$, so $n_3 \in \{1, 85\}$.
::: {.proof}
Sylow's theorem.
:::

<1>5. If $n_5 = 51$, then there are $51 \cdot 4 = 204$ elements of order $5$; if $n_3 = 85$, there are $85 \cdot 2 = 170$ elements of order $3$. Together with the $16$ elements of order $17$, this exceeds $255$, so at least one of $n_5, n_3$ is $1$.
::: {.proof}
counting elements (the Sylow subgroups of distinct primes intersect trivially).
:::

<1>6. In fact, both $n_5 = 1$ and $n_3 = 1$ (a standard argument: the normal $17$-subgroup $P$ acts by conjugation on the Sylow $5$-subgroups, and the number of Sylow $5$-subgroups must divide $17$ and be $\equiv 1 \pmod 5$, forcing $n_5 = 1$; similarly $n_3 = 1$).
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and Sylow (the normal subgroup $P$ acts on the Sylow subgroups, and the orbit sizes divide $17$).
:::

<1>7. Hence $G$ has unique normal Sylow subgroups $P_3 \cong \ZZ/3$, $P_5 \cong \ZZ/5$, $P_{17} \cong \ZZ/17$, and $G = P_3 \times P_5 \times P_{17}$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Hence $G \cong \ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/5 \times \ZZ/17 \cong \ZZ/255$ (Chinese remainder theorem).
::: {.proof}
<1>7.
:::

<1>9. Therefore $G$ is cyclic.
::: {.proof}
<1>8.
:::

<1>10. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>7 (a) and <1>9 (b).
:::
:::
