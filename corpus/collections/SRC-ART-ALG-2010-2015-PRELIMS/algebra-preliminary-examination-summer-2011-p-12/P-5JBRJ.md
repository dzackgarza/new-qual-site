::: {.problem}
For each of the following statements, determine if it is true or false.
Explain your answers.

a. The field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ is contained in some cyclotomic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$.

b. Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a finite field, and $f(x) \in \mathbb{F}[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree $n$.
Then the splitting field of $f(x)$ over $\mathbb{F}$ has degree $n$ over $\mathbb{F}$.

c. There is a surjective group homomorphism $\varphi : S_4 \longrightarrow A_3$.

d. There is a surjective group homomorphism $\psi : S_4 \longrightarrow S_3$.

e. There is a transitive group action of $S_4$ on a set of three elements.
:::

::: {.solution}
The answers, in order, are false, true, false, true, and true.

<1>1. The assertion in part (a) is false.

::: {.proof}
Every cyclotomic field $L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)$ is the
splitting field of $x^m-1$, which is separable in characteristic
zero, so $L/\mathbb Q$ is finite Galois. An automorphism sends
$\zeta_m$ to $\zeta_m^a$ for some unit $a$ modulo $m$.
This embeds its Galois group into the abelian group
$(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$: the image of the generator
determines the automorphism, and composition multiplies the
exponents. Thus its Galois group is abelian. Every subgroup
is normal, so every intermediate field is Galois over
$\mathbb Q$ by the Galois correspondence [@DF04].

But $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2})$ is not normal over $\mathbb Q$.
Indeed, $x^3-2$ is irreducible by Eisenstein at $2$ [@DF04],
and $E\subseteq\mathbb R$ contains its real root but not its
two nonreal roots. Hence $E$ cannot be an intermediate field
of a cyclotomic extension.
:::

<1>2. The assertion in part (b) is true.

::: {.proof}
Write $q=|\mathbb F|$, choose a root $a$ of $f$ in an
algebraic closure, and put $E=\mathbb F(a)$. Irreducibility
gives $[E:\mathbb F]=n$, so $E$ has $q^n$ elements.
Its multiplicative group has order $q^n-1$, hence every
$b\in E$ satisfies $b^{q^n}=b$, including $b=0$.
Therefore
$$
x^{q^n}-x=\prod_{b\in E}(x-b).
$$
The equality follows because both sides are monic of degree
$q^n$ and have exactly these distinct roots.

The minimal polynomial of $a$ over $\mathbb F$ divides
$x^{q^n}-x$, since the latter vanishes at $a$. It is a
nonzero scalar multiple of $f$. Thus $f$ splits in $E$.
The field generated by all its roots is contained in $E$
and contains $\mathbb F(a)=E$, so equals $E$ and has degree $n$.
:::

<1>3. The assertion in part (c) is false.

::: {.proof}
For any homomorphism $\varphi:S_4\to A_3$, the image of
a transposition has order dividing two and also dividing
$|A_3|=3$. Its order is therefore one. Every permutation is
a product of transpositions: each cycle
$(a_1\,a_2\,\ldots\,a_k)$ equals
$(a_1\,a_k)\cdots(a_1\,a_2)$, and disjoint cycles give
every permutation. Hence $\varphi$ is trivial and cannot
surject onto the three-element group $A_3$.
:::

<1>4. The assertion in part (d) is true.

::: {.proof}
Let $X$ consist of the three unordered pair partitions
$$
A=\{\{1,2\},\{3,4\}\},\quad
B=\{\{1,3\},\{2,4\}\},\quad
C=\{\{1,4\},\{2,3\}\}.
$$
Permuting the four labels sends a pair partition to another
pair partition and respects composition. This defines a
homomorphism $\psi:S_4\to\operatorname{Sym}(X)\cong S_3$.
The permutation $(1\,2)$ fixes $A$ and interchanges $B,C$,
whereas $(1\,2\,3)$ sends $A$ to $C$, $C$ to $B$, and
$B$ to $A$. The image therefore contains a transposition
and a three-cycle. A subgroup containing them has order
divisible by both two and three; its order divides six,
so it is all of $S_3$ by Lagrange's theorem [@DF04].
Thus $\psi$ is surjective.
:::

<1>5. The assertion in part (e) is true.

::: {.proof}
Use the same action on $X$. The powers of $(1\,2\,3)$
send $A$ to each of $A,B,C$, as explicitly computed in
step <1>4. The orbit of $A$ is all of $X$, proving
transitivity on this three-element set.
:::
