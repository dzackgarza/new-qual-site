::: {.problem}
Suppose $K$ is a field.
Let $K(x)$ denote the field of rational functions in one variable $x$ over $K$, and let $G$ be the subgroup of the automorphism group of $K(x)$ over $K$ generated by $\sigma$, where $\sigma(x) = 1 - (1/x)$.
Find a specific $u \in K(x)$ such that the fixed field of $G$ is $K(u)$, and find the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $K(u)$.
:::

::: solution
One may take
$$
\boxed{u=\frac{x^3-3x+1}{x^2-x}}.
$$
Then $K(x)^G=K(u)$, and the minimal polynomial is
$$
\boxed{T^3-uT^2+(u-3)T+1\in K(u)[T]}.
$$
All integers in these formulas are interpreted in $K$.

<1>1. The automorphism $\sigma$ has order $3$.

::: proof
Direct substitution gives
$$
\sigma(x)=\frac{x-1}{x},\qquad
\sigma^2(x)=1-\frac{x}{x-1}=\frac1{1-x},\qquad
\sigma^3(x)=1-(1-x)=x.
$$
The rational functions used here have nonzero denominators
because $x$ is transcendental. Also $\sigma(x)\ne x$:
equality would imply $x^2-x+1=0$, a nonzero polynomial
relation over $K$. Since the automorphism fixes $K$ and
$K(x)$ is generated by $x$, it follows that $\sigma^3=1$
and $\sigma\ne1$. Its order is therefore exactly $3$.
In particular $x,\sigma(x),\sigma^2(x)$ are distinct.
:::

<1>2. The element $u$ is fixed by $G$, and $x$ satisfies
the displayed cubic over $K(u)$.

::: proof
The sum of the three iterates is fixed by $\sigma$, which
cyclically permutes its summands. Combining the fractions gives
$$
x+\frac{x-1}{x}+\frac1{1-x}
=\frac{x^2(x-1)+(x-1)^2-x}{x(x-1)}
=\frac{x^3-3x+1}{x^2-x}=u.
$$
Thus $K(u)\subseteq K(x)^G$. Multiplying the formula for
$u$ by $x^2-x$ and rearranging gives
$$
x^3-ux^2+(u-3)x+1=0.
$$
This is a monic cubic relation. Hence
$[K(x):K(u)]\leq3$.
:::

<1>3. The fixed field is exactly $K(u)$, and the cubic is
the minimal polynomial of $x$.

::: proof
Put $E=K(x)^G$. Since $K(u)\subseteq E\subseteq K(x)$,
step <1>2 makes $K(x)/E$ finite of degree at most $3$.
Let $m(T)$ be the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $E$.
Every element of $G$ fixes its coefficients, so applying
$1,\sigma,\sigma^2$ to $m(x)=0$ shows that
$x,\sigma(x),\sigma^2(x)$ are roots of $m$.
Step <1>1 shows that they are distinct; consequently
$\deg m\geq3$. As $E$ contains $K$, one has $E(x)=K(x)$,
and therefore $[K(x):E]=3$.

The tower law now gives
$$
3\geq[K(x):K(u)]=[K(x):E][E:K(u)]=3[E:K(u)].
$$
Thus $[E:K(u)]=1$, so $E=K(u)$ and
$[K(x):K(u)]=3$. The monic cubic from step <1>2 must be
the minimal polynomial. Its roots are the three distinct
iterates above, so it is separable as well. The argument
never divides by $2$ or $3$ and applies in every characteristic.
:::
