::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a finite abelian group of order $n$.
Prove that $A$ is cyclic if and only if $A$ has a unique subgroup of each order $k$ such that $k$ divides $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A cyclic group of order $n$ has exactly one subgroup
of order $k$ for every positive divisor $k$ of $n$.

::: {.proof}
Write $A=\langle g\rangle$. The element $g^{n/k}$ has
order $k$: its $j$th power is one exactly when
$n\mid jn/k$, equivalently $k\mid j$. Thus the subgroup
$\langle g^{n/k}\rangle$ has the required order.

For uniqueness, let $H\leq A$ and choose the least positive
integer $t$ with $g^t\in H$. Such an integer exists since
$g^n=1\in H$. If $g^j\in H$, divide $j$ by $t$ to write
$j=qt+r$ with $0\leq r<t$. Then $g^r=g^j(g^t)^{-q}\in H$,
and minimality forces $r=0$. Applying this to $j=n$ gives
$t\mid n$, and applying it to every element of $H$ gives
$H=\langle g^t\rangle$. This subgroup has order $n/t$.
If that order is $k$, then $t=n/k$, proving uniqueness.
:::

<1>2. Under the uniqueness hypothesis, the primary
decomposition of $A$ has at most one cyclic factor for each prime.

::: {.proof}
The structure theorem for finite abelian groups expresses
$A$ as a product of cyclic groups of prime-power order
[@DF04]. Suppose two factors belonging to the same prime
$p$ occur, say $C_{p^a}$ and $C_{p^b}$ with $a,b\geq1$.
Use additive notation on the product. In the first factor,
the class of $p^{a-1}$ has order $p$, so the element with
that coordinate and all other coordinates zero generates
a subgroup of order $p$. The element with coordinate
$p^{b-1}$ in the second factor and zero elsewhere generates
another subgroup of order $p$.

These two subgroups are distinct: their nonzero elements
have nonzero coordinates in different factors. Their images
under the decomposition isomorphism remain distinct
subgroups of $A$. Since $p\mid n$, this contradicts the
assumed uniqueness for the divisor $k=p$.
:::

<1>3. The group $A$ is cyclic.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2 its primary decomposition has the form
$A\cong\prod_{p\mid n}C_{p^{a_p}}$. Choose a generator
in each factor. The resulting tuple has order equal to
the least common multiple of the orders of its coordinates:
a power of the tuple is one exactly when that power is
divisible by every coordinate order. Since these orders
are pairwise coprime, their least common multiple is
$\prod_{p\mid n}p^{a_p}=n$. The tuple therefore generates
the entire order-$n$ product, and its image generates $A$.
For $n=1$, the group is trivial and generated by its identity,
and its unique subgroup is itself. This also satisfies both
directions of the assertion.
:::
:::
