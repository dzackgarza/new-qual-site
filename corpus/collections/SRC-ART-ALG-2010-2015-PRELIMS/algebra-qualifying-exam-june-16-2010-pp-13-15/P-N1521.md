::: {.problem}
For each of the three orders $n = 15, 20, 21$, either find a non-abelian group of order $n$ or prove that all groups of order $n$ are abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every group of order $15$ is cyclic, hence abelian.

::: {.proof}
Let $|G|=15$. Sylow's theorems give
$n_5\mid3$, $n_5\equiv1\pmod5$, and
$n_3\mid5$, $n_3\equiv1\pmod3$ [@DF04].
The first pair forces $n_5=1$; the second forces $n_3=1$
because $5\not\equiv1\pmod3$. Thus the Sylow subgroups
$P$ and $Q$ of orders $3$ and $5$ are both normal.
Their intersection is trivial by Lagrange's theorem.

For $u\in P$, $v\in Q$, the commutator $uvu^{-1}v^{-1}$
lies in both $P$ and $Q$ by their normality, so is one.
Hence the multiplication map $P\times Q\to G$ is a
homomorphism. Its kernel is trivial, since $uv=1$ implies
$u=v^{-1}\in P\cap Q$. Domain and codomain both have
order $15$, so this is an isomorphism. Both prime-order
factors are cyclic, and a pair of generators has order
$\operatorname{lcm}(3,5)=15$. Therefore $G$ is cyclic.
:::

<1>2. There is a nonabelian group of order $20$.

::: {.proof}
On $\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z$, consider the permutations
$$
f_{\varepsilon,b}(x)=\varepsilon x+b,
\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\},\quad
b\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z.
$$
They form a group under composition, because
$$
f_{\varepsilon,b}\circ f_{\delta,c}
=f_{\varepsilon\delta,\,b+\varepsilon c},
\qquad
f_{\varepsilon,b}^{-1}=f_{\varepsilon,\,-\varepsilon b},
$$
and $f_{1,0}$ is the identity. Associativity is inherited
from composition of functions. Evaluation at $0$ determines
$b$, and evaluation at $1$ then determines $\varepsilon$,
since $1\ne-1$ modulo $10$. Thus these are exactly twenty
distinct permutations.

Let $r(x)=x+1$ and $s(x)=-x$. Then
$srs^{-1}(x)=x-1$, which differs from $r(x)=x+1$
modulo $10$. Hence $r,s$ do not commute. This gives
the required nonabelian group, the dihedral group of order $20$.
:::

<1>3. There is a nonabelian group of order $21$.

::: {.proof}
The subset $U=\{1,2,4\}\subseteq\mathbb F_7^\times$
is a subgroup: it consists of the powers of $2$, with
$2^3=1$ and $2,2^2\ne1$. On $\mathbb F_7$ take the maps
$$
g_{a,b}(x)=ax+b,
\qquad a\in U,\quad b\in\mathbb F_7.
$$
Composition and inverse are
$$
g_{a,b}\circ g_{c,d}=g_{ac,\,b+ad},
\qquad g_{a,b}^{-1}=g_{a^{-1},\,-a^{-1}b}.
$$
These formulas and the identity $g_{1,0}$ prove that the
maps form a group. They are distinct because evaluation
at $0$ recovers $b$, and evaluation at $1$ then recovers $a$.
The group therefore has $3\cdot7=21$ elements.

For $r(x)=x+1$ and $t(x)=2x$, one has
$trt^{-1}(x)=x+2\ne x+1=r(x)$. Thus the group is
nonabelian. This proves existence for the final order.
:::
