::: {.problem}
Let $\FF_p$ be the finite field with $p$ elements, and let $f(x) \in \FF_p[x]$ be a polynomial of degree 4.

a. Show that $f(x)$ is irreducible if and only if the polynomials $f(x)$ and $x^{p^2} - x$ are relatively prime.

b. Assuming $f(x)$ is irreducible over $\FF_p$, describe the Galois group of $f(x)$ over $\FF_p$.
Include a generating set for the Galois group in your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The polynomial $X^{p^2}-X$ is the product of all monic
irreducible polynomials over $\FF_p$ whose degrees are $1$ or $2$.

::: pf-proof

Every element $a\in\FF_{p^2}$ satisfies $a^{p^2}=a$, so
$$
X^{p^2}-X=\prod_{a\in\FF_{p^2}}(X-a).
$$
Its derivative is $-1$, so these roots are distinct.

If $a\in\FF_{p^2}$, then
$$
[\FF_p(a):\FF_p]\mid[\FF_{p^2}:\FF_p]=2,
$$
so its minimal polynomial over $\FF_p$ has degree $1$ or $2$.
Conversely, if $g\in\FF_p[X]$ is monic irreducible of degree $1$ or
$2$, then a root $a$ generates a field with $p$ or $p^2$ elements,
hence embeds in $\FF_{p^2}$. Thus every such $g$ divides
$X^{p^2}-X$, and these are exactly its irreducible factors.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The polynomial $f$ is irreducible if and only if
$$
\gcd(f,X^{p^2}-X)=1.
$$

::: pf-proof

Suppose first that $f$ is irreducible of degree $4$. If it had a
nonconstant common divisor with $X^{p^2}-X$, step [](#s1){.pf-ref} would give an
irreducible factor of $f$ of degree $1$ or $2$, contradicting
irreducibility of the degree-$4$ polynomial $f$. Hence the gcd is $1$.

Conversely, suppose that $f$ is reducible. In any nontrivial
factorization of a degree-$4$ polynomial, at least one factor has degree
at most $2$. Taking an irreducible factor of that factor gives an
irreducible divisor $g$ of $f$ of degree $1$ or $2$. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$g\mid X^{p^2}-X$. Therefore
$$
\gcd(f,X^{p^2}-X)\ne1.
$$
This proves the contrapositive of the reverse implication.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $f$ is irreducible, its splitting field is $\FF_{p^4}$.

::: pf-proof

Let $\alpha$ be a root. Irreducibility gives
$$
[\FF_p(\alpha):\FF_p]=4,
$$
so $\FF_p(\alpha)$ has $p^4$ elements and is therefore isomorphic to
$\FF_{p^4}$. Every irreducible polynomial over a finite field is
separable, and all conjugates of $\alpha$ are
$$
\alpha,\alpha^p,\alpha^{p^2},\alpha^{p^3},
$$
which lie in this same field. Hence adjoining one root already gives the
splitting field.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The Galois group in part (b) is
$$
\boxed{\Gal(\FF_{p^4}/\FF_p)\cong\ZZ/4\ZZ},
$$
generated by the Frobenius automorphism
$$
\sigma(a)=a^p.
$$

::: pf-proof

The Frobenius map fixes $\FF_p$ and is an automorphism of the finite
field $\FF_{p^4}$. Its fourth power is the identity because
$a^{p^4}=a$ for every element. If $\sigma^d=1$ for
$1\le d<4$, then every element of $\FF_{p^4}$ would be a root of
$X^{p^d}-X$, a nonzero polynomial of degree $p^d<p^4$, which is
impossible. Thus $\sigma$ has order $4$.

The extension has degree $4$, so its Galois group has order $4$.
Therefore the cyclic subgroup generated by $\sigma$ is the whole Galois
group.

:::
