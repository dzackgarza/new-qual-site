::: {.problem}
Let $\mathbb{F}_p$ be the finite field with $p$ elements, and let $f(x) \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ be a polynomial of degree 4.

a. Show that $f(x)$ is irreducible if and only if the polynomials $f(x)$ and $x^{p^2} - x$ are relatively prime.

b. Assuming $f(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$, describe the Galois group of $f(x)$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
Include a generating set for the Galois group in your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** For $f \in \FF_p[x]$ of degree $4$, characterize irreducibility via $\gcd(f, x^{p^2}-x)$, and describe the Galois group when $f$ is irreducible.

<1>1. $x^{p^2} - x$ is the product of all monic irreducibles of degree $1$ and $2$ over $\FF_p$.
<2>1. $x^{p^2} - x = \prod_{\alpha \in \FF_{p^2}} (x - \alpha)$.
::: {.proof}
$\FF_{p^2}$ is the splitting field of $x^{p^2} - x$; every element of $\FF_{p^2}$ is a root, and there are $p^2$ of them.
:::
<2>2. The minimal polynomial of $\alpha \in \FF_{p^2} \sm \FF_p$ has degree $2$.
::: {.proof}
$[\FF_p(\alpha) : \FF_p] = 2$ since $\alpha \notin \FF_p$ and $\alpha \in \FF_{p^2}$.
:::
<2>3. $x^{p^2} - x$ factors as the product of all monic irreducibles of degree $1$ and $2$.
::: {.proof}
each root's minimal polynomial is irreducible of degree $1$ or $2$, and each such irreducible splits in $\FF_{p^2}$.
:::

<1>2. $f$ is irreducible iff $\gcd(f, x^{p^2}-x) = 1$.
<2>1. ($\Rightarrow$) If $f$ is irreducible of degree $4$, no root of $f$ lies in $\FF_{p^2}$.
::: {.proof}
a root $\alpha$ of $f$ has $[\FF_p(\alpha):\FF_p] = 4$, so $\alpha \notin \FF_{p^2}$ (which has degree $2$).
:::
<2>2. Hence $f$ shares no root with $x^{p^2}-x$, so $\gcd(f, x^{p^2}-x) = 1$.
::: {.proof}
by <1>1.1, roots of $x^{p^2}-x$ are exactly $\FF_{p^2}$; $f$ has none of them.
:::
<2>3. ($\Leftarrow$) If $f$ is reducible, then $f$ has an irreducible factor of degree $1$ or $2$.
::: {.proof}
$f$ has degree $4$; any nontrivial factorization has a factor of degree $1$ or $2$.
:::
<2>4. Such a factor divides $x^{p^2}-x$, so $\gcd(f, x^{p^2}-x) \neq 1$.
::: {.proof}
by <1>2.3, every irreducible of degree $1$ or $2$ divides $x^{p^2}-x$.
:::

<1>3. If $f$ is irreducible, its Galois group is $\Gal(\FF_{p^4}/\FF_p) \cong \ZZ/4$, generated by the Frobenius $x \mapsto x^p$.
<2>1. The splitting field of $f$ is $\FF_{p^4}$.
::: {.proof}
$f$ is irreducible of degree $4$, so adjoining one root gives $\FF_{p^4}$, which is the splitting field (finite fields are perfect, so $f$ is separable and all roots lie in $\FF_{p^4}$).
:::
<2>2. $\Gal(\FF_{p^4}/\FF_p) \cong \ZZ/4$.
::: {.proof}
the Galois group of a finite extension of finite fields is cyclic, generated by Frobenius, of order $[\FF_{p^4}:\FF_p] = 4$.
:::
<2>3. A generating set is $\theset{\sigma}$ where $\sigma(x) = x^p$.
::: {.proof}
the Frobenius automorphism generates the cyclic group of order $4$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 is part (a); <1>3 is part (b).
:::
