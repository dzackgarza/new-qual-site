::: problem
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f(x)=x^3 - 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

a. What is the dimension of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$?

b. What is the Galois group of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$?
(Identify the isomorphism type of the Galois group, and also indicate how its elements act as permutations of the roots of $f$.)

c. What are all the subfields of $K$?
Be sure to explain how you know you have included all of them.

d. Which of these subfields are Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$?
:::

::: solution
Put $a=\sqrt[3]{5}>0$, choose $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}$, and
write $a_j=\zeta^ja$ for $j\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$.

<1>1. One has $K=\mathbb Q(a,\zeta)$ and $[K:\mathbb Q]=6$.

::: proof
The roots are $a_0,a_1,a_2$. The field they generate contains
$a=a_0$ and $\zeta=a_1/a_0$, and conversely all the roots
belong to $\mathbb Q(a,\zeta)$. This proves the field identity.
The polynomial $x^3-5$ is Eisenstein at $5$, hence irreducible
over $\mathbb Q$ [@DF04]. Thus $[\mathbb Q(a):\mathbb Q]=3$.
Since $\mathbb Q(a)\subset\mathbb R$ and $\zeta$ is nonreal
with $\zeta^2+\zeta+1=0$, adjoining $\zeta$ has degree two.
The tower law gives the asserted degree six.
The polynomial has distinct roots, and its splitting field
is a finite Galois extension [@DF04].
:::

<1>2. The Galois group is $S_3$, acting as all permutations
of $a_0,a_1,a_2$.

::: proof
An automorphism permutes the roots; that action is faithful
because they generate $K$. Since the Galois group has order
$[K:\mathbb Q]=6=|S_3|$, its image is all of $S_3$.
Explicit generators are
$$
r(a)=\zeta a,\quad r(\zeta)=\zeta,
\qquad s(a)=a,\quad s(\zeta)=\zeta^{-1}.
$$
To verify existence of $r$, first note that
$[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]=2$, since $x^2+x+1$
has no rational root. Therefore $[K:\mathbb Q(\zeta)]=3$,
and $x^3-5$ is the minimal polynomial of $a$ over
$\mathbb Q(\zeta)$. Sending $a$ to its root $\zeta a$
defines an embedding fixing that field; its image contains
$a=\zeta^{-1}(\zeta a)$, so the embedding is onto.
Complex conjugation restricts to the automorphism $s$.

On indices, $r$ is $j\mapsto j+1$ and $s$ is $j\mapsto-j$.
Thus $r=(0\,1\,2)$ and $s=(1\,2)$, and the six maps
$r^us^v$ act by $j\mapsto u+(-1)^v j$, with
$u\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$ and $v\in\{0,1\}$.
These specify every automorphism and its root permutation.
:::

<1>3. The complete subfield list is
$$
\mathbb Q,\qquad \mathbb Q(\zeta)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3}),
\qquad \mathbb Q(a_0),\quad\mathbb Q(a_1),\quad\mathbb Q(a_2),
\qquad K.
$$
Their degrees over $\mathbb Q$ are respectively $1,2,3,3,3,6$.

::: proof
By the Galois correspondence, intermediate fields correspond
bijectively to subgroups $H\leq S_3$, and the fixed field
has degree $[S_3:H]$ [@DF04]. Lagrange's theorem restricts
subgroup orders to $1,2,3,6$. There is just the identity
subgroup of order one and the whole group of order six.
A subgroup of order two is generated by an involution;
the three transpositions are exactly the involutions of $S_3$.
There is exactly one subgroup of order three, namely
$\langle r\rangle=A_3$, because the only elements of order
three are $r$ and $r^2$, which generate the same subgroup.
This proves that the six subgroups just described are exhaustive.

The whole group and identity subgroup fix $\mathbb Q$ and $K$.
The subgroup $\langle r\rangle$ fixes $\zeta$, and its
fixed field has degree two, so it is precisely $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$.
The formula $\zeta=(-1+\sqrt{-3})/2$ identifies that field.
For each $j$, the stabilizer of $a_j$ is the order-two
subgroup generated by the transposition of the other two
roots. Its fixed field contains $\mathbb Q(a_j)$.
Each $a_j$ has minimal polynomial $x^3-5$, and both this
field and the fixed field have degree three. They are
therefore equal. The three stabilizers are different, so
the three cubic fields are different. Every subfield of
$K$ contains its prime field $\mathbb Q$, so no additional
subfields lie outside this correspondence.
:::

<1>4. Exactly $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$, and $K$
are Galois over $\mathbb Q$.

::: proof
A fixed field $K^H$ is Galois over $\mathbb Q$ exactly
when $H$ is normal in $S_3$ [@DF04]. The identity subgroup
and the whole group are normal. The subgroup $A_3$ has
index two and is normal, giving the quadratic Galois field.
Conjugation by $r$ cyclically permutes the three transpositions,
so none of their order-two subgroups is normal.
Thus none of the three cubic fields is Galois, and the
exhaustive list in step <1>3 proves that these decisions
cover every subfield.
:::
