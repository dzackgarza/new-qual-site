::: {.problem}
Give an example of each of the following or explain why no such examples exist.
One or two sentence answers will suffice; you don't need to give formal proofs.

a. A Galois extension $K/F$ and an intermediate field $E$ ($F \subset E \subset K$) such that $F/E$ is not Galois.

b. A Galois extension $K/F$ and an intermediate field $E$ ($F \subset E \subset K$) such that $E/K$ is not Galois.

c. A finite field of size 8.

d. A subfield $E$ of the splitting field of $x^5 + x - 1$ with $[E:\mathbb{Q}] = 7$.

e. A field extension $K/F$ which is finite dimensional but not separable.

f. An irreducible fifth degree polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ which is solvable by radicals.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Part (a): Intermediate field $E/F$ not Galois:
<2>1. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}$ and $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega)$ (where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$), which is the splitting field of $x^3 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, with Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$.
Take the intermediate field $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$.
Then $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois, but $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is not Galois because $E$ is not normal over $\mathbb{Q}$ (it contains only one of the three complex roots of the irreducible polynomial $x^3 - 2$).
Proof: $E$ corresponds to the non-normal subgroup $\langle (2\,3) \rangle \le S_3$.

<1>2. Part (b): Intermediate field with $K/E$ not Galois:
<2>1. **No such example exists.**
If $K/F$ is a Galois extension, then $K/F$ is normal and separable.
For any intermediate field $E$ with $F \subseteq E \subseteq K$, $K$ is the splitting field over $E$ of the same separable polynomial, so $K/E$ is automatically normal and separable, hence $K/E$ is always Galois.
Proof: intermediate extensions of Galois extensions are always Galois over the intermediate field.

<1>3. Part (c): Finite field of size 8:
<2>1. The polynomial $p(x) = x^3 + x + 1 \in \mathbb{F}_2[x]$ has degree 3 and no roots in $\mathbb{F}_2$ ($p(0) = 1, p(1) = 1$), hence is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
The quotient ring:
\[
\mathbb{F}_8 = \mathbb{F}_2[x] / \langle x^3 + x + 1 \rangle
\]
is a field of cardinality $2^3 = 8$.
Proof: quotient of polynomial ring by maximal ideal generated by an irreducible polynomial.

<1>4. Part (d): Subfield $E$ of splitting field of $x^5 + x - 1$ with $[E : \mathbb{Q}] = 7$:
<2>1. **No such example exists.**
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f(x) = x^5 + x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
The Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ embeds into the symmetric group $S_5$, so $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = |\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})|$ divides $|S_5| = 120$.
By the Tower Law, if $E \subseteq K$ is a subfield, then $[E : \mathbb{Q}]$ must divide $[K : \mathbb{Q}]$.
Since $7 \nmid 120$, no intermediate subfield of degree 7 over $\mathbb{Q}$ can exist.
Proof: Tower Law for field extension degrees.

<1>5. Part (e): Finite-dimensional non-separable field extension:
<2>1. Let $F = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ be the rational function field over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and let $K = F(t^{1/p}) = \mathbb{F}_p(t^{1/p})$.
The minimal polynomial of $t^{1/p}$ over $F$ is $x^p - t = (x - t^{1/p})^p$.
It is irreducible over $F$ (by Eisenstein’s Criterion at the prime element $t \in \mathbb{F}_p[t]$), but has a root of multiplicity $p$.
Thus $[K : F] = p < \infty$, but $K/F$ is purely inseparable.
Proof: irreducible polynomial with vanishing formal derivative over characteristic $p$.

<1>6. Part (f): Irreducible 5th degree polynomial solvable by radicals:
<2>1. Let $f(x) = x^5 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$.
$f(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein’s Criterion at $p = 2$.
Its splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[5]{2}, \zeta_5)$, where $\zeta_5 = e^{2\pi i/5}$.
The Galois group is $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_5 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_4$ (the Frobenius group of order 20), which is solvable because its derived series terminates in $\{1\}$.
Thus $f(x)$ is solvable by radicals.
Proof: radical extension tower $\mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5, \sqrt[5]{2})$.

<1>7. Conclusion:
Examples and non-existence proofs for parts (a)-(f) are fully established. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 through <1>6.
