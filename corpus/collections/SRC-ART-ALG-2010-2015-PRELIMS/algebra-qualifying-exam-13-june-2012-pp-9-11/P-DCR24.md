::: {.problem}
a. Find Sylow subgroups $P_2, P_3$, and $P_5$ for the three primes $2, 3, 5$ dividing the order of $A_5$.
(It suffices to find generators of each of these groups.)

b. Find the normalizers in $A_5$ of each of the three groups you found in part (a).

c. For each of the divisors $d = 6, 10, 15$ of 60, either find a subgroup of $A_5$ of order $d$ or explain why none exists for that choice of $d$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Part (a).**

<1>1. $|A_5| = 60 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$.
Proof: $|A_5| = 5!/2 = 60$.

<1>2. $P_2 = \langle (12)(34), (13)(24) \rangle \cong \ZZ_2 \times \ZZ_2$ (the Klein four-group).
Proof: the double transpositions form a Sylow $2$-subgroup of order $4$.

<1>3. $P_3 = \langle (123) \rangle \cong \ZZ_3$.
Proof: a $3$-cycle generates a Sylow $3$-subgroup of order $3$.

<1>4. $P_5 = \langle (12345) \rangle \cong \ZZ_5$.
Proof: a $5$-cycle generates a Sylow $5$-subgroup of order $5$.

**Part (b).**

<1>1. $N_{A_5}(P_2) = A_4$.
Proof: the normalizer of a Sylow $2$-subgroup $V_4$ in $A_5$ is $A_4$ (the subgroup fixing one of the five points, of order $12$).

<1>2. $N_{A_5}(P_3) \cong S_3$ (of order $6$).
Proof: the normalizer of a $3$-cycle in $A_5$ has order $6$ (it is the subgroup of $A_5$ fixing the two points not moved by the $3$-cycle, isomorphic to $S_3$).

<1>3. $N_{A_5}(P_5) \cong D_5$ (the dihedral group of order $10$).
Proof: the normalizer of a $5$-cycle in $A_5$ has order $10$ (it is the dihedral group $D_5$, generated by the $5$-cycle and a suitable double transposition).

**Part (c).**

<1>1. $d = 6$: $A_5$ has a subgroup of order $6$ (e.g. $N_{A_5}(P_3) \cong S_3$).
Proof: <1>2 of part (b).

<1>2. $d = 10$: $A_5$ has a subgroup of order $10$ (e.g. $N_{A_5}(P_5) \cong D_5$).
Proof: <1>3 of part (b).

<1>3. $d = 15$: $A_5$ has no subgroup of order $15$.
<2>1. A subgroup of order $15$ would be cyclic (since $15 = 3 \cdot 5$ with both Sylow subgroups normal).
Proof: a group of order $15$ is cyclic.
<2>2. But $A_5$ has no element of order $15$.
Proof: the elements of $A_5$ have orders $1, 2, 3, 5$ (cycle types $1^5, 2^2 1, 3 \cdot 1^2, 5$), and none has order $15$.
<2>3. Hence no subgroup of order $15$ exists.
Proof: <2>1 and <2>2.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>2–<1>4 (a), <1>1–<1>3 (b), and <1>1–<1>3 (c).
:::
