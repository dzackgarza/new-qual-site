::: {.problem}
Let $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ denote the space of two-dimensional vector subspaces of $\RR^n$.

(a) Let $\mathcal P\in\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$.
Show that any two bases $\{v_1,v_2\}$ and $\{w_1,w_2\}$ for $\mathcal P$ are related by
$$
w_i=\sum_{j=1}^2 a_i^jv_j,\qquad i=1,2,
$$
for some $(a_i^j)\in GL(2)$, and conclude that $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ is the set of equivalence classes of linearly independent pairs under this relation.

(b) Let $GL(2)$ act by left multiplication on the space $\mathcal T$ of $2\times n$ matrices of rank $2$.
Use part (a) to identify $\mathcal T/GL(2)$ with $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$.

(c) Give $\mathcal T$ the subspace topology as an open subset of $\RR^{2n}$ and $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ the quotient topology.
Show that the projection
$$
\pi_{Gr}:\mathcal T\to\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)
$$
is open and that $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ is Hausdorff.

(d) Let $\mathcal T(i,j)$ be the matrices whose minor in columns $i,j$ is invertible.
Show that $GL(2)$ preserves $\mathcal T(i,j)$ and every orbit in $\mathcal T(i,j)$ contains a unique matrix whose $(i,j)$-minor is the identity.

(e) Conclude that every plane represented by $\mathcal T(i,j)$ is determined uniquely by an $(n-2)\times2$ matrix.
For example, when $n=4$, $i=1$, and $j=3$, the matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
$$
corresponds to the plane with basis
$$
v_1=(1,a,0,b),\qquad v_2=(0,c,1,d).
$$

(f) Show that each
$$
G_{ij}=\pi_{Gr}(\mathcal T(i,j))\subset\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)
$$
is open, and use part (e) to define a coordinate map
$$
\varphi_{ij}:G_{ij}\longrightarrow\RR^{2n-4}
$$
which is a homeomorphism.

(g) Compute a representative sample of transition functions and conclude that $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ is a differentiable manifold.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) If $v_1,v_2$ and $w_1,w_2$ are two ordered bases of the same plane $\mathcal P$, then there is a unique matrix
$$
A=(a_i^j)\in GL(2,\RR)
$$
such that
$$
w_i=\sum_{j=1}^2a_i^jv_j.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $v_1,v_2$ is a basis, each $w_i$ has a unique expansion in that basis, producing a unique $2\times2$ coefficient matrix $A$. If $A$ were singular, the vectors $w_1,w_2$ would be linearly dependent, contradicting that they form a basis. Thus $A\in GL(2)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Therefore $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ is the set of equivalence classes of linearly independent ordered pairs under change of basis by $GL(2)$.


::: pf-proof

Two linearly independent pairs determine the same plane exactly when each is a basis of that plane. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} says this is equivalent to one pair being obtained from the other by an invertible $2\times2$ matrix.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

(b) Let $\mathcal T\subset\Mat_{2\times n}(\RR)$ be the rank-two matrices, with $GL(2)$ acting by left multiplication. The orbit of $M\in\mathcal T$ is determined exactly by the row space of $M$.


::: pf-proof

The two rows of $M$ are linearly independent and span a $2$-plane in $\RR^n$. Left multiplication by $A\in GL(2)$ replaces these rows by another basis of the same row space. Conversely, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that any two matrices with the same row space differ by a unique left multiplication by an element of $GL(2)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\mathcal T/GL(2)\cong\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Send the orbit of a rank-two matrix to its row space. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves that this map is well defined and bijective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

(c) The set $\mathcal T$ is open in $\RR^{2n}$.


::: pf-proof

A $2\times n$ matrix has rank $2$ exactly when at least one of its $2\times2$ minors is nonzero. For each pair $i<j$, nonvanishing of that minor is an open condition. Thus $\mathcal T$ is the union of finitely many open sets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The quotient projection
$$
\pi_{Gr}:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T/GL(2)
$$
is open.


::: pf-proof

If $O\subset\mathcal T$ is open, then its saturation is
$$
\pi_{Gr}^{-1}(\pi_{Gr}(O))
=\bigcup_{A\in GL(2)}AO.
$$
For fixed $A$, left multiplication is a homeomorphism of $\mathcal T$, so $AO$ is open. Hence the saturation is open, and therefore $\pi_{Gr}(O)$ is open by the quotient topology.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Define
$$
Q(M)=M^T(MM^T)^{-1}M\in\Mat_n(\RR).
$$
Then $Q$ is continuous, invariant under the $GL(2)$-action, and $Q(M)$ is the orthogonal projection onto the row space of $M$.


::: pf-proof

Since $M$ has rank $2$, the $2\times2$ matrix $MM^T$ is positive definite and invertible. All operations in the formula are continuous on $\mathcal T$.

For $A\in GL(2)$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(AM)
&=M^TA^T(AMM^TA^T)^{-1}AM\\
&=M^TA^T A^{-T}(MM^T)^{-1}A^{-1}AM\\
&=Q(M).
\end{aligned}
$$
Also $Q(M)$ is symmetric and idempotent, its image is contained in the row space of $M$, and for every row-space vector $M^Tc$,
$$
Q(M)M^Tc=M^T(MM^T)^{-1}MM^Tc=M^Tc.
$$
Thus it is the orthogonal projection onto the row space.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The quotient $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ is Hausdorff.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, $Q$ descends to a continuous map
$$
\overline Q:\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)\to\operatorname{Sym}(n).
$$
It is injective because an orthogonal projection determines its image, hence the underlying plane. If $P_1\ne P_2$, then $\overline Q(P_1)\ne\overline Q(P_2)$. Since the Euclidean space $\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ is Hausdorff, choose disjoint open neighborhoods of these two projection matrices. Their inverse images under $\overline Q$ are disjoint open neighborhoods of $P_1,P_2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

(d) For $i<j$, let $M_{ij}$ denote the $2\times2$ minor formed by columns $i,j$. The set
$$
\mathcal T(i,j)=\{M\in\mathcal T:\det M_{ij}\ne0\}
$$
is preserved by the $GL(2)$-action.


::: pf-proof

For $A\in GL(2)$,
$$
(AM)_{ij}=AM_{ij}.
$$
Hence
$$
\det((AM)_{ij})=\det(A)\det(M_{ij})\ne0
$$
exactly when $\det(M_{ij})\ne0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Every orbit in $\mathcal T(i,j)$ contains a unique representative whose $(i,j)$-minor is the identity matrix.


::: pf-proof

For $M\in\mathcal T(i,j)$, set
$$
\widetilde M=M_{ij}^{-1}M.
$$
Then
$$
\widetilde M_{ij}=I_2.
$$
If $AM$ also has identity $(i,j)$-minor, then
$$
AM_{ij}=I_2,
$$
so necessarily
$$
A=M_{ij}^{-1}.
$$
Thus the normalized representative is unique.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

(e) A plane in $G_{ij}=\pi_{Gr}(\mathcal T(i,j))$ is uniquely determined by the entries in the $n-2$ columns of its normalized representative other than columns $i,j$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, every orbit has a unique normalized representative with columns $i,j$ equal to the standard basis vectors of $\RR^2$. All remaining entries are unconstrained real numbers. Listing each remaining column as a row gives an $(n-2)\times2$ array, equivalently a point of $\RR^{2n-4}$. Uniqueness of the normalized representative gives uniqueness of this array.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

In the example $n=4$, $i=1$, $j=3$, the coordinate array
$$
\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\ b&d\end{pmatrix}
$$
corresponds to the normalized matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&a&0&b\\
0&c&1&d
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose rows are the basis vectors stated in the problem.


::: pf-proof

Columns $1$ and $3$ form the identity, while the remaining columns are $(a,c)^T$ and $(b,d)^T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

(f) Each $G_{ij}$ is open in $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$.


::: pf-proof

The set $\mathcal T(i,j)$ is open in $\mathcal T$ because it is defined by nonvanishing of a determinant. By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, its image
$$
G_{ij}=\pi_{Gr}(\mathcal T(i,j))
$$
is open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Define
$$
\varphi_{ij}:G_{ij}\to\RR^{2n-4}
$$
by taking the non-$(i,j)$ entries of the unique normalized representative from step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}. Then $\varphi_{ij}$ is a homeomorphism.


::: pf-proof

Define on $\mathcal T(i,j)$
$$
N_{ij}(M)=M_{ij}^{-1}M.
$$
This depends continuously on $M$ and is invariant under left multiplication:
$$
N_{ij}(AM)=(AM_{ij})^{-1}AM=M_{ij}^{-1}M.
$$
Taking the non-$(i,j)$ entries therefore gives a continuous orbit-invariant map, which descends through the quotient to $\varphi_{ij}$.

Conversely, from any point of $\RR^{2n-4}$, insert the corresponding entries into the unique $2\times n$ matrix whose $(i,j)$-minor is $I_2$. This is a continuous map into $\mathcal T(i,j)$; composing with $\pi_{Gr}$ gives a continuous inverse to $\varphi_{ij}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

(g) On an overlap $G_{ij}\cap G_{k\ell}$, let $M(C)$ be the $(i,j)$-normalized matrix determined by coordinates $C=\varphi_{ij}(P)$. If
$$
B(C)=M(C)_{k\ell},
$$
then
$$
\varphi_{k\ell}(P)
$$
is obtained from the non-$(k,\ell)$ entries of
$$
B(C)^{-1}M(C).
$$


::: pf-proof

The matrix $B(C)$ is invertible exactly on the overlap $G_{ij}\cap G_{k\ell}$. Multiplication by $B(C)^{-1}$ is precisely the unique normalization making columns $k,\ell$ equal to the identity, by step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Every transition map $\varphi_{k\ell}\circ\varphi_{ij}^{-1}$ is smooth.


::: pf-proof

The entries of $M(C)$ depend linearly on $C$. The entries of
$$
B(C)^{-1}
$$
are rational functions of the entries of $B(C)$ with denominator $\det B(C)$; this denominator is nonzero on the chart overlap. Therefore every entry of
$$
B(C)^{-1}M(C)
$$
is a smooth rational function of $C$ on the overlap. Selecting the non-$(k,\ell)$ entries preserves smoothness.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

For example, when $n=4$, the $G_{12}$-normalized representative
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&a&b\\
0&1&c&d
\end{pmatrix}
$$
lies in $G_{13}$ exactly when $c\ne0$. On this overlap, the $G_{13}$-normalized representative is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-a/c&0&b-ad/c\\
0&1/c&1&d/c
\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The $(1,3)$-minor is
$$
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&c\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is invertible exactly when $c\ne0$, with
$$
B^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-a/c\\0&1/c\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Multiplying $B^{-1}M$ gives the displayed matrix. This is a representative sample of the rational transition maps described in step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Therefore $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ is a smooth manifold of dimension
$$
\boxed{2n-4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}, the finitely many open sets $G_{ij}$ cover the Grassmannian and are homeomorphic to $\RR^{2n-4}$. Each chart has a countable base, so the finite union of the transported bases is a countable base for the Grassmannian. Step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} shows that all transition maps are smooth. Together with Hausdorffness from step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, these charts define a differentiable-manifold structure of dimension $2n-4$.

:::
