::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a differentiable manifold.

(a) State the $n$-submanifold property for a subset $N\subset M$.

(b) Let $F:M\to P$ be a smooth map of constant rank $k$.
Starting from the rank theorem, show that for any $p\in P$ the level set
\[
N_p=\{q\in M:F(q)=p\}
\]
satisfies the submanifold property, provided it is nonempty.

(c) Show that the three-dimensional unit sphere
\[
S^3=\{(x,y,z,u)\in\mathbb R^4:x^2+y^2+z^2+u^2=1\}
\]
is an embedded submanifold of $\mathbb R^4$.

(d) Show that the subgroup $SL(n)\subset GL(n)$ of matrices of determinant $1$ is an embedded submanifold of $GL(n)$.

(e) Show that the group $SO(n)\subset GL(n)$ of orthogonal matrices of determinant $1$ is an embedded submanifold of $GL(n)$.

(f) Let $M,N$ be differentiable manifolds and $b\in N$.
Show that
\[
i_b:M\longrightarrow M\times N,\qquad i_b(p)=(p,b),
\]
is an embedding.
:::

Let $m=\dim M$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

(a) A subset $N\subset M$ has the $n$-submanifold property if for every $q\in N$ there is a chart
$$
(U,x^1,\ldots,x^m)
$$
of $M$ about $q$ such that
$$
N\cap U
=
\{p\in U:x^{n+1}(p)=\cdots=x^m(p)=0\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the local-slice definition of an embedded $n$-dimensional submanifold.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) Let $F:M^m\to P^r$ have constant rank $k$, and let $q_0\in F^{-1}(p)$. Then there are local coordinates around $q_0$ and $p$ in which
$$
F(x^1,\ldots,x^m)
=
(x^1,\ldots,x^k,0,\ldots,0).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the constant-rank theorem, after translating the target coordinates so that the coordinate of $p=F(q_0)$ is the origin.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In the coordinates of step <1>2,
$$
F^{-1}(p)
=
\{x^1=\cdots=x^k=0\}.
$$
Hence every nonempty level set $F^{-1}(p)$ is an embedded submanifold of dimension $m-k$.


::: pf-proof

In the normal form of step <1>2, the equality $F(x)=p$ is exactly the system
$$
x^1=\cdots=x^k=0.
$$
The remaining $m-k$ coordinates are free. Reordering coordinates puts the level set into the slice form of step <1>1.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(c) The sphere $S^3$ is an embedded $3$-dimensional submanifold of $\RR^4$.


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
F:\RR^4\to\RR,
\qquad
F(x,y,z,u)=x^2+y^2+z^2+u^2.
$$
Then
$$
S^3=F^{-1}(1).
$$
At a point $q=(x,y,z,u)\in S^3$,
$$
DF_q(v)=2\langle q,v\rangle.
$$
Since $q\ne0$, this linear functional is nonzero, hence has rank $1$. Since $q\ne0$, a sufficiently small neighborhood of $q$ avoids the origin; on that neighborhood $DF$ has rank $1$. The local conclusion of step <1>3 therefore gives dimension
$$
4-1=3.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(d) The group $SL(n)$ is an embedded submanifold of $GL(n)$ of dimension $n^2-1$.


::: pf-proof

View $GL(n)$ as the open subset of $\mathbb M_n(\RR)\cong\RR^{n^2}$ consisting of invertible matrices. The determinant map
$$
\det:GL(n)\to\RR
$$
is smooth and
$$
SL(n)=\det^{-1}(1).
$$
Its differential at $A\in GL(n)$ is
$$
D(\det)_A(H)=\det(A)\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}H).
$$
This is surjective: choosing $H=A$ gives
$$
D(\det)_A(A)=n\det(A)\ne0.
$$
Hence the determinant has rank $1$ everywhere on $GL(n)$. Step <1>3 gives the claim and the dimension $n^2-1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
G:GL(n)\to\operatorname{Sym}(n),
\qquad
G(A)=A^TA.
$$
Then $G$ is a submersion.


::: pf-proof

The differential is
$$
DG_A(H)=A^TH+H^TA.
$$
Let $S\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)$. Set
$$
H=\frac12A^{-T}S.
$$
Then
$$
A^TH=\frac12S
$$
and, since $S^T=S$,
$$
H^TA=\frac12S.
$$
Thus
$$
DG_A(H)=S.
$$
So $DG_A$ is surjective for every $A\in GL(n)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The orthogonal group
$$
O(n)=\{A\in GL(n):A^TA=I\}
$$
is an embedded submanifold of dimension $n(n-1)/2$.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>6,
$$
O(n)=G^{-1}(I)
$$
is a level set of a submersion. Since
$$
\dim\operatorname{Sym}(n)=\frac{n(n+1)}2,
$$
step <1>3 gives
$$
\dim O(n)
=n^2-\frac{n(n+1)}2
=\frac{n(n-1)}2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(e) The special orthogonal group $SO(n)$ is an embedded submanifold of $GL(n)$ of dimension $n(n-1)/2$.


::: pf-proof

For $A\in O(n)$,
$$
\det(A)^2=\det(A^TA)=1,
$$
so $\det A\in\{-1,1\}$. Hence
$$
SO(n)
=O(n)\cap\det^{-1}((0,\infty)).
$$
Because $\det^{-1}((0,\infty))$ is open in $GL(n)$, $SO(n)$ is an open subset of the embedded submanifold $O(n)$. Every open subset of an embedded submanifold is an embedded submanifold of the same dimension. Apply step <1>7.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(f) The map
$$
i_b:M\to M\times N,
\qquad
i_b(p)=(p,b),
$$
is smooth and injective.


::: pf-proof

It is injective because equality
$$
(p,b)=(q,b)
$$
forces $p=q$. In product coordinates its coordinate representation is
$$
x\longmapsto(x,c),
$$
where $c$ is the fixed coordinate vector of $b$, hence it is smooth.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The differential of $i_b$ is injective at every point.


::: pf-proof

Under the canonical identification
$$
T_{(p,b)}(M\times N)\cong T_pM\oplus T_bN,
$$
one has
$$
(i_b)_{*p}(v)=(v,0).
$$
This linear map is injective.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $i_b$ is a homeomorphism from $M$ onto its image
$$
M\times\{b\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The inverse on the image is the restriction of the first projection
$$
\pi_1:M\times N\to M,
$$
which is continuous. Since $i_b$ is continuous and bijective onto its image, this proves the homeomorphism statement.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $i_b$ is an embedding.


::: pf-proof

By steps <1>9--<1>10, $i_b$ is an injective immersion. By step <1>11, it is a homeomorphism onto its image. This is the definition of a smooth embedding.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>1, <1>3, <1>4, <1>5, <1>8, and <1>12 prove parts (a)--(f), respectively.
