::: {.problem}
(a) Let $F:M\to N$ be smooth and suppose
\[
F_*:T_pM\longrightarrow T_{F(p)}N
\]
is an isomorphism for every $p\in M$.
Prove that $F$ is an open map.
Show in addition that $F$ is surjective if $M$ is compact and $N$ is connected.

(b) Suppose $F:M\to N$ has constant rank and is surjective.
Prove that $F$ is a smooth submersion.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

(a) For every $p\in M$, there are open neighborhoods $U_p\subset M$ of $p$ and $V_p\subset N$ of $F(p)$ such that
$$
F|_{U_p}:U_p\to V_p
$$
is a diffeomorphism.


::: pf-proof

The differential
$$
F_{*p}:T_pM\to T_{F(p)}N
$$
is an isomorphism. In particular, $\dim M=\dim N$. The inverse function theorem therefore applies at $p$ and gives the stated neighborhoods.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $F$ is open.


::: pf-proof

Let $O\subset M$ be open. For every $p\in O$, shrink the neighborhood $U_p$ from step <1>1 so that
$$
U_p\subset O.
$$
Then
$$
F(U_p)
$$
is open in $N$, because $F|_{U_p}$ is a diffeomorphism onto an open set. Since
$$
F(O)=\bigcup_{p\in O}F(U_p),
$$
the image $F(O)$ is open.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $M$ is compact, then $F(M)$ is closed in $N$.


::: pf-proof

The image $F(M)$ is compact because $F$ is continuous. A manifold is Hausdorff, and compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed. Hence $F(M)$ is closed in $N$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $M$ is nonempty and compact and $N$ is connected, then
$$
\boxed{F(M)=N}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>2, $F(M)$ is open in $N$, because $M$ is open in itself. By step <1>3, it is also closed. Since $M$ is nonempty, $F(M)$ is nonempty. A connected space has no nonempty proper subset that is both open and closed. Therefore $F(M)=N$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) Let
$$
r=\operatorname{rank}F
$$
be the constant rank, and put $n=\dim N$. If $r<n$, then every point $p\in M$ has an open neighborhood $U_p$ with compact closure contained in a constant-rank coordinate neighborhood such that
$$
F(\overline{U_p})
$$
is a closed nowhere-dense subset of $N$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $p\in M$. By the constant-rank theorem, after choosing source and target coordinates near $p$ and $F(p)$, the map has the form
$$
(x^1,\ldots,x^m)
\longmapsto
(x^1,\ldots,x^r,0,\ldots,0).
$$
Shrink the source coordinate neighborhood and choose an open set $U_p$ whose closure is compact and contained in it, with $F(\overline{U_p})$ contained in the target coordinate neighborhood.

Since $\overline{U_p}$ is compact, $F(\overline{U_p})$ is compact and hence closed in the Hausdorff manifold $N$. In the target coordinates it is contained in
$$
\{y^{r+1}=\cdots=y^n=0\}.
$$
When $r<n$, this coordinate slice has empty interior. Thus the closed set $F(\overline{U_p})$ has empty interior and is nowhere dense.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There is a countable family of neighborhoods
$$
U_1,U_2,\ldots
$$
of the kind constructed in step <1>5 that covers $M$.


::: pf-proof

The neighborhoods from step <1>5 form an open cover of $M$. Smooth manifolds are second countable, hence Lindelöf: every open cover has a countable subcover. Choose such a subcover.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The inequality $r<n$ contradicts surjectivity of $F$.


::: pf-proof

If $F$ is surjective, then using step <1>6,
$$
N=F(M)
\subseteq
\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}F(\overline{U_j}).
$$
The reverse inclusion is automatic, so equality holds. By step <1>5, every set $F(\overline{U_j})$ is closed and nowhere dense in $N$.

A manifold is locally compact Hausdorff, hence a Baire space. A nonempty Baire space cannot be a countable union of closed nowhere-dense subsets. This contradiction shows that $r<n$ is impossible.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since the rank of a map into an $n$-manifold cannot exceed $n$, one has
$$
r=n.
$$
Therefore every differential
$$
F_{*p}:T_pM\to T_{F(p)}N
$$
is surjective.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>7 gives $r\ge n$, while linear algebra gives
$$
r\le\dim T_{F(p)}N=n.
$$
Thus $r=n$. A linear map whose rank equals the dimension of its codomain is surjective.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
\boxed{F\text{ is a smooth submersion}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The map $F$ is smooth by hypothesis, and step <1>8 shows that its differential is surjective at every point. This is exactly the definition of a submersion.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>1--<1>4 prove part (a), and steps <1>5--<1>9 prove part (b).
