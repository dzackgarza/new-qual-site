::: {.problem}
(a) Let
$$
X=x^2y\,\partial_y-z\,\partial_z,
\qquad
Y=xy\,\partial_x+y\,\partial_y-z^2\,\partial_z
$$
be vector fields on $\RR^3$.
Compute $[X,Y]$.

(b) Let $(\mathcal U,\phi)$ and $(\mathcal U,\psi)$ be coordinate systems on a two-dimensional manifold with transition function
$$
(u^1,u^2)=\psi\circ\phi^{-1}(x^1,x^2)
=\bigl((x^1)^2-(x^2)^2,\,2x^1x^2\bigr).
$$
If $X=x^2\partial_{x^1}$ in the $\phi$-coordinates, find its coordinate expression in the $\psi$-coordinates.

(c) Express $X=y\partial_x$ in polar coordinates.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) The $x$-component of $[X,Y]$ is $x^3y$.


::: pf-proof

Since $X^x=0$ and $Y^x=xy$,
$$
[X,Y]^x=X(xy)-Y(0).
$$
Now
$$
X(xy)=x^2y\,\partial_y(xy)-z\,\partial_z(xy)=x^3y.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The $y$-component of $[X,Y]$ is $-2x^2y^2$.


::: pf-proof

Here $X^y=x^2y$ and $Y^y=y$, so
$$
[X,Y]^y=X(y)-Y(x^2y).
$$
One has
$$
X(y)=x^2y,
$$
while
$$
\begin{aligned}
Y(x^2y)
&=xy\,\partial_x(x^2y)+y\,\partial_y(x^2y)-z^2\,\partial_z(x^2y)\\
&=2x^2y^2+x^2y.
\end{aligned}
$$
Subtracting gives $-2x^2y^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The $z$-component of $[X,Y]$ is $z^2$.


::: pf-proof

Since $X^z=-z$ and $Y^z=-z^2$,
$$
[X,Y]^z=X(-z^2)-Y(-z).
$$
Now
$$
X(-z^2)=(-z)\partial_z(-z^2)=2z^2,
$$
and
$$
Y(-z)=(-z^2)\partial_z(-z)=z^2.
$$
Thus the difference is $z^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
[X,Y]
=x^3y\,\partial_x
-2x^2y^2\,\partial_y
+z^2\,\partial_z
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

(b) In the $u$-coordinates,
$$
X(u^1)=u^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
u^1=(x^1)^2-(x^2)^2
$$
and
$$
X=x^2\partial_{x^1},
$$
one has
$$
X(u^1)=x^2(2x^1)=2x^1x^2=u^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

One also has
$$
X(u^2)
=\sqrt{(u^1)^2+(u^2)^2}-u^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

First,
$$
X(u^2)
=x^2\partial_{x^1}(2x^1x^2)
=2(x^2)^2.
$$
Also
$$
(u^1)^2+(u^2)^2
=\bigl((x^1)^2+(x^2)^2\bigr)^2.
$$
Hence
$$
\sqrt{(u^1)^2+(u^2)^2}
=(x^1)^2+(x^2)^2,
$$
so
$$
\sqrt{(u^1)^2+(u^2)^2}-u^1
=2(x^2)^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Thus the coordinate expression in the $\psi$-chart is
$$
\boxed{
X
=u^2\frac{\partial}{\partial u^1}
+\left(\sqrt{(u^1)^2+(u^2)^2}-u^1\right)
\frac{\partial}{\partial u^2}
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a vector field, the coefficient of $\partial/\partial u^i$ is $X(u^i)$. Apply steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

(c) In polar coordinates
$$
x=r\cos\theta,
\qquad
y=r\sin\theta,
$$
one has
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
=\cos\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
$$
for $r>0$.


::: pf-proof

The coordinate functions satisfy
$$
\frac{\partial r}{\partial x}=\frac xr=\cos\theta,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x}=-\frac y{r^2}=-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}.
$$
Apply the chain rule to $\partial_x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
=r\sin\theta\cos\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
-\sin^2\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Multiply the identity in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} by
$$
y=r\sin\theta.
$$

:::
