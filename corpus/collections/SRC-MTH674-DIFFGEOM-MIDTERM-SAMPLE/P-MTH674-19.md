::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal U\subset\mathbb R^2$ be open.
A proper coordinate patch is a one-to-one immersion
\[
x:\mathcal U\longrightarrow\mathbb R^3
\]
such that
\[
x^{-1}:x(\mathcal U)\longrightarrow\mathcal U
\]
is continuous for the subspace topology on $x(\mathcal U)$.
A surface is a subset $S\subset\mathbb R^3$ with the subspace topology such that each $p\in S$ has a neighborhood contained in the image of a proper coordinate patch.
Show that a surface is a differentiable manifold.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The statement is false under the literal definition printed in the problem.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
S=\{(x,0,0):x\in\RR\}\cup\{(0,y,0):y\in\RR\},
$$
the union of the two coordinate axes in the plane $z=0$.

Consider the proper coordinate patch
$$
x:\RR^2\to\RR^3,
\qquad
x(u,v)=(u,v,0).
$$
It is a one-to-one immersion and a homeomorphism onto the plane $z=0$. Its image contains all of $S$. Hence every point of $S$ has a neighborhood in $S$ contained in the image of this proper patch. Thus $S$ satisfies the literal definition in the problem.
:::

<1>2. The set $S$ in step <1>1 is not a topological manifold, so in particular it is not a differentiable manifold.

::: {.proof}
The space $S$ is connected. Every point of $S$ other than the origin has a neighborhood in $S$ homeomorphic to an open interval, so any manifold structure compatible with this topology would have dimension $1$ on the connected space $S$.

At the origin, however, every sufficiently small connected neighborhood is a cross. Removing the origin leaves four connected components. If a neighborhood of the origin were homeomorphic to an open interval, removing the image of the origin would leave exactly two connected components. This contradiction shows that the origin has no $1$-manifold neighborhood.
:::

<1>3. The standard intended statement becomes true if the missing condition is imposed: for every $p\in S$, there is a proper coordinate patch
$$
x:U\to\RR^3
$$
whose image $x(U)$ is an open neighborhood of $p$ in $S$.

::: {.proof}
This is the usual local parametrization condition in the definition of a regular surface. The remaining steps prove that this corrected condition produces a smooth $2$-manifold.
:::

<1>4. Under the corrected condition of step <1>3, each inverse map
$$
x^{-1}:x(U)\to U
$$
is a topological chart on $S$.

::: {.proof}
By definition of a proper coordinate patch, $x$ is one-to-one and its inverse on $x(U)$ is continuous. Since $x$ is smooth, it is continuous. Therefore
$$
x:U\to x(U)
$$
is a homeomorphism. By the corrected condition, $x(U)$ is open in $S$, and $U$ is open in $\RR^2$. Thus $x^{-1}$ is a coordinate chart.
:::

<1>5. Let
$$
x:U\to S,
\qquad
y:V\to S
$$
be two such patches with overlapping images. Their transition map
$$
y^{-1}\circ x
$$
is smooth.

::: {.proof}
Fix $u_0\in U$ with
$$
x(u_0)=y(v_0)
$$
for some $v_0\in V$. Since $y$ is an immersion, the $3\times2$ derivative matrix $Dy_{v_0}$ has rank $2$. Hence some $2\times2$ minor is nonzero. Let
$$
\pi:\RR^3\to\RR^2
$$
be projection onto the corresponding two ambient coordinates. Then
$$
D(\pi\circ y)_{v_0}
$$
is invertible.

By the inverse function theorem, after shrinking around $v_0$,
$$
\pi\circ y
$$
is a diffeomorphism onto an open subset of $\RR^2$. After also shrinking around $u_0$ so that the images lie in this common surface neighborhood,
$$
y^{-1}\circ x
=(\pi\circ y)^{-1}\circ\pi\circ x.
$$
The right-hand side is smooth. Since $u_0$ was arbitrary, the full transition map is smooth.
:::

<1>6. Under the corrected condition, the proper patches form a smooth atlas on $S$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4, their inverse maps are topological charts covering $S$. By step <1>5, all transition maps are smooth.
:::

<1>7. Under the corrected condition, $S$ is Hausdorff and second countable.

::: {.proof}
The topology on $S$ is the subspace topology inherited from $\RR^3$. Subspaces of Hausdorff spaces are Hausdorff, and subspaces of second-countable spaces are second countable. Since $\RR^3$ has both properties, so does $S$.
:::

<1>8. Thus the standard corrected surface definition makes $S$ a differentiable $2$-manifold.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 supplies a smooth atlas with coordinate images open in $\RR^2$, and step <1>7 supplies the Hausdorff and second-countable axioms.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>2 disprove the statement under the literal retained wording. Steps <1>3--<1>8 prove the intended theorem once the standard missing condition—that the patch image itself is an open neighborhood in $S$—is restored.
:::
:::
