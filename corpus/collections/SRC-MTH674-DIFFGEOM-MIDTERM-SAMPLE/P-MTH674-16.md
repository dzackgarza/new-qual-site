::: {.problem}
(a) Show that
\[
[fX,gY]=fg[X,Y]+f(Xg)Y-g(Yf)X
\]
for smooth functions $f,g$ and vector fields $X,Y\in\mathcal X(M)$.

(b) Let $F:M\to N$ be smooth.
Suppose vector fields $X,Y\in\mathcal X(M)$ are $F$-related to $\widehat X,\widehat Y\in\mathcal X(N)$.
Show that $[X,Y]$ is $F$-related to $[\widehat X,\widehat Y]$.

(c) Let $F:M\to N$ and $G:N\to M$ be smooth maps satisfying $G\circ F=\operatorname{id}_M$.
Given $Y\in\mathcal X(N)$, define
\[
X_p=G_*(Y_{F(p)}).
\]
Show that $p\mapsto X_p$ is a smooth vector field on $M$.

(d) With $F,G$ as in part (c), show that $F(M)\subset N$ is an embedded submanifold.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. (a) For every $h\in C^\infty(M)$,
$$
[fX,gY](h)
=fg[X,Y](h)+f(Xg)Y(h)-g(Yf)X(h).
$$

::: {.proof}
Using the definition of the Lie bracket and the Leibniz rule,
$$
\begin{aligned}
[fX,gY](h)
&=fX(gYh)-gY(fXh)\\
&=f(Xg)Yh+fgX(Yh)-g(Yf)Xh-fgY(Xh)\\
&=fg[X,Y](h)+f(Xg)Y(h)-g(Yf)X(h).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since two vector fields are equal when they act equally on every smooth function, the required identity follows.
:::

<1>2. (b) If $X$ is $F$-related to $\widehat X$, then for every $h\in C^\infty(N)$,
$$
X(h\circ F)=(\widehat Xh)\circ F.
$$
The analogous identity holds for $Y$ and $\widehat Y$.

::: {.proof}
At $p\in M$,
$$
X_p(h\circ F)
=(F_{*p}X_p)(h).
$$
Because the fields are $F$-related,
$$
F_{*p}X_p=\widehat X_{F(p)}.
$$
Thus
$$
X_p(h\circ F)=\widehat X_{F(p)}h.
$$
:::

<1>3. For every $h\in C^\infty(N)$,
$$
[X,Y](h\circ F)
=([\widehat X,\widehat Y]h)\circ F.
$$

::: {.proof}
Using step <1>2 twice,
$$
\begin{aligned}
[X,Y](h\circ F)
&=X(Y(h\circ F))-Y(X(h\circ F))\\
&=X((\widehat Yh)\circ F)-Y((\widehat Xh)\circ F)\\
&=(\widehat X(\widehat Yh))\circ F-(\widehat Y(\widehat Xh))\circ F\\
&=([\widehat X,\widehat Y]h)\circ F.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>4. Hence $[X,Y]$ is $F$-related to $[\widehat X,\widehat Y]$.

::: {.proof}
The defining condition that vector fields $Z$ on $M$ and $\widehat Z$ on $N$ are $F$-related is equivalent to
$$
Z(h\circ F)=(\widehat Zh)\circ F
$$
for every smooth $h$ on $N$. Apply step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. (c) The assignment
$$
X_p=G_{*F(p)}(Y_{F(p)})
$$
defines a tangent vector in $T_pM$ for every $p\in M$.

::: {.proof}
Because $G\circ F=\operatorname{id}_M$,
$$
G(F(p))=p.
$$
Thus
$$
G_{*F(p)}:T_{F(p)}N\to T_pM,
$$
so the displayed vector lies in the correct tangent space.
:::

<1>6. The vector field $X$ from step <1>5 is smooth.

::: {.proof}
The tangent map
$$
TG:TN\to TM
$$
is smooth, and a smooth vector field $Y$ is a smooth map
$$
Y:N\to TN.
$$
Therefore
$$
X=TG\circ Y\circ F:M\to TM
$$
is smooth. Step <1>5 shows that this map is a section of $TM\to M$, so it is a smooth vector field.
:::

<1>7. (d) The map $F:M\to N$ is injective.

::: {.proof}
If $F(p)=F(q)$, then applying $G$ gives
$$
p=G(F(p))=G(F(q))=q.
$$
:::

<1>8. The differential $F_{*p}$ is injective for every $p\in M$.

::: {.proof}
Differentiate
$$
G\circ F=\operatorname{id}_M
$$
at $p$. The chain rule gives
$$
G_{*F(p)}\circ F_{*p}=\operatorname{id}_{T_pM}.
$$
A linear map admitting a left inverse is injective. Hence $F_{*p}$ is injective.
:::

<1>9. The map $F$ is a homeomorphism from $M$ onto $F(M)$ with the subspace topology.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>7, $F$ is a continuous bijection from $M$ to its image. The inverse map on $F(M)$ is
$$
F^{-1}=G|_{F(M)},
$$
because $G(F(p))=p$. Since $G$ is continuous, its restriction is continuous. Thus $F$ is a homeomorphism onto its image.
:::

<1>10. Therefore
$$
\boxed{F(M)\subset N\text{ is an embedded submanifold}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>7--<1>8, $F$ is an injective immersion. By step <1>9, it is a homeomorphism onto its image. Hence $F$ is an embedding, and its image is an embedded submanifold of $N$.
:::

<1>11. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves part (a), steps <1>2--<1>4 prove part (b), steps <1>5--<1>6 prove part (c), and steps <1>7--<1>10 prove part (d).
:::
