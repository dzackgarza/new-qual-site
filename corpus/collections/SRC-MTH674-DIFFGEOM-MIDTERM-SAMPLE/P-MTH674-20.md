::: {.problem}
(a) Show that the set of all lines in $\RR^2$ can be identified with an open subset of $\RP^2$.

(b) Show that the set of all planes in $\RR^3$ can be identified with an open subset of $\RP^3$.

(c) Show that the space of all oriented lines in $\RR^3$ can be identified with $TS^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) Every affine line in $\RR^2$ can be written
$$
a x+b y+c=0
$$
with $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, and the coefficient triple $(a,b,c)$ is unique up to multiplication by a nonzero real scalar.


::: pf-proof

A line is the level set of a nonzero affine linear functional. Multiplying its defining equation by $\lambda\in\RR^*$ does not change the zero set. Conversely, two nonzero affine linear equations with the same line as zero set have proportional normal vectors and then proportional constant terms, so their coefficient triples differ by a nonzero scalar.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Hence the space of affine lines in $\RR^2$ is identified with
$$
\boxed{\RP^2\setminus\{[0:0:1]\}}.
$$
This is an open subset of $\RP^2$.


::: pf-proof

Send the line $ax+by+c=0$ to $[a:b:c]$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} this is a well-defined bijection onto the projective points with $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$. The only excluded projective point is $[0:0:1]$. Since $\RP^2$ is Hausdorff, a singleton is closed, so its complement is open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

(b) Every affine plane in $\RR^3$ has an equation
$$
a x+b y+c z+d=0
$$
with $(a,b,c)\ne(0,0,0)$, unique up to multiplication of all four coefficients by a nonzero scalar.


::: pf-proof

An affine plane is the zero set of a nonconstant affine linear functional. The same proportionality argument as in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives uniqueness up to nonzero scalar.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore the space of affine planes in $\RR^3$ is identified with
$$
\boxed{\RP^3\setminus\{[0:0:0:1]\}},
$$
which is open in $\RP^3$.


::: pf-proof

Send the plane to its homogeneous coefficient vector $[a:b:c:d]$. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives a bijection onto the complement of the one projective point with zero normal vector. The complement of a point is open in the Hausdorff space $\RP^3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

(c) An oriented affine line in $\RR^3$ has a unique unit direction vector $u\in S^2$ compatible with its orientation.


::: pf-proof

An unoriented line has two unit tangent directions $u$ and $-u$. Choosing an orientation selects exactly one of them.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If an oriented line with unit direction $u$ is written
$$
L=x_0+\RR u,
$$
then
$$
v=x_0-\langle x_0,u\rangle u
$$
is independent of the chosen point $x_0\in L$, lies on $L$, and satisfies
$$
\langle v,u\rangle=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $x_1=x_0+tu$ is another point of the line, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1-\langle x_1,u\rangle u
&=x_0+tu-(\langle x_0,u\rangle+t)u\\
&=x_0-\langle x_0,u\rangle u.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $v$ is independent of the representative. It differs from $x_0$ by a multiple of $u$, so $v\in L$, and
$$
\langle v,u\rangle
=\langle x_0,u\rangle-\langle x_0,u\rangle\norm{u}^2
=0.
$$
Geometrically, $v$ is the point of $L$ closest to the origin.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Since
$$
T_uS^2=\{v\in\RR^3:\langle u,v\rangle=0\},
$$
the assignment
$$
\Phi(L)=(u,v)
$$
defines a map from oriented lines in $\RR^3$ to $TS^2$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} supplies the unit direction $u$, and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} supplies the uniquely determined perpendicular vector $v$. The displayed description of $T_uS^2$ follows because $S^2$ is the level set $\norm{x}^2=1$, whose tangent space is the kernel of $w\mapsto2\langle u,w\rangle$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Conversely, for $(u,v)\in TS^2$, define
$$
\Psi(u,v)=\{v+tu:t\in\RR\},
$$
with orientation determined by increasing $t$. Then $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are inverse bijections.


::: pf-proof

Since $(u,v)\in TS^2$, one has $\norm u=1$ and $v\perp u$. Thus $v$ is already the closest point of the line $v+\RR u$ to the origin, so applying $\Phi$ returns $(u,v)$. Conversely, starting with an oriented line, step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} writes it uniquely as $v+\RR u$ with $v\perp u$, so applying $\Psi\circ\Phi$ returns the same oriented line.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The identification in steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is smooth.


::: pf-proof

Model oriented lines as equivalence classes of pairs
$$
(x,u)\in\RR^3\times S^2,
$$
where
$$
(x,u)\sim(x+tu,u).
$$
The map to the unique perpendicular representative is
$$
(x,u)\longmapsto\bigl(u,x-\langle x,u\rangle u\bigr),
$$
which is smooth and constant on equivalence classes. Its inverse sends $(u,v)\in TS^2$ to the class of $(v,u)$, also smoothly. Hence the bijection is a diffeomorphic identification with $TS^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\{\text{oriented affine lines in }\RR^3\}\cong TS^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the smooth bijection established in steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::
