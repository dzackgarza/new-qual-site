::: {.problem}
Let
\[
M=\{(x,y,z)\in\RR^3:z=x^2+y^2\}
\]
with the subspace topology.

(a) Let $\varphi:M\to\RR^2$ be given by $\varphi(x,y,z)=(x,y)$.
Show that $\varphi$ is a global coordinate map for $M$.

(b) Let
\[
F:M\longrightarrow S^2,
\qquad
F(x,y,z)=\frac{(-2x,-2y,1)}{\sqrt{1+4x^2+4y^2}}.
\]
Is $F$ smooth?

(c) Let
\[
v=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^1}\right|_{(0,1,1)}.
\]
Compute a coordinate expression for $F_*(v)$.

(d) Let $f:M\to\RR$ be $f(x,y,z)=xyz^2$.
Compute
\[
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^1}\right|_{(1,-1,2)}f.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) The map
$$
\varphi:M\to\RR^2,
\qquad
\varphi(x,y,z)=(x,y),
$$
is bijective with inverse
$$
\varphi^{-1}(u,v)=(u,v,u^2+v^2).
$$


::: pf-proof

Every point of $M$ has the form
$$
(x,y,x^2+y^2),
$$
so its first two coordinates determine it uniquely. Conversely, every $(u,v)\in\RR^2$ gives a point
$$
(u,v,u^2+v^2)\in M.
$$
Thus the displayed maps are inverse bijections.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map $\varphi$ is a homeomorphism, hence a global coordinate map for $M$.


::: pf-proof

The map $\varphi$ is the restriction to $M$ of the continuous coordinate projection
$$
\RR^3\to\RR^2.
$$
Its inverse from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} is polynomial and therefore continuous. Since its image is all of the open set $\RR^2$, it is a global chart.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

(b) The map $F:M\to S^2$ is smooth.


::: pf-proof

Use the global coordinates $(u,v)=\varphi(x,y,z)$. Then
$$
F\circ\varphi^{-1}(u,v)
=
\frac{(-2u,-2v,1)}{\sqrt{1+4u^2+4v^2}}.
$$
The denominator is strictly positive, so all three component functions are smooth.

Moreover, the third component is positive, so the image lies in the upper hemisphere
$$
H=\{(a,b,c)\in S^2:c>0\}.
$$
Use the smooth chart
$$
\psi:H\to\{(a,b):a^2+b^2<1\},
\qquad
\psi(a,b,c)=(a,b).
$$
Its inverse is
$$
(a,b)\mapsto(a,b,\sqrt{1-a^2-b^2}).
$$
The coordinate representation
$$
\psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}(u,v)
=
\left(
\frac{-2u}{\sqrt{1+4u^2+4v^2}},
\frac{-2v}{\sqrt{1+4u^2+4v^2}}
\right)
$$
is smooth. Therefore $F$ is smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

(c) Let
$$
p=(0,1,1).
$$
Then
$$
F(p)=\left(0,-\frac2{\sqrt5},\frac1{\sqrt5}\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute $x=0$ and $y=1$ into the defining formula for $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

In the upper-hemisphere coordinates $\psi$ from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
F_*\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^1}\right|_p\right)
=
\boxed{-\frac2{\sqrt5}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^1}\right|_{F(p)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
D(u,v)=\sqrt{1+4u^2+4v^2}.
$$
The coordinate representation of $F$ is
$$
G(u,v)=\left(-\frac{2u}{D(u,v)},-\frac{2v}{D(u,v)}\right).
$$
The vector
$$
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^1}\right|_p
$$
corresponds to $\partial/\partial u$ at $(u,v)=(0,1)$. Differentiate:
$$
\left.\frac{\partial G^1}{\partial u}\right|_{(0,1)}
=-\frac2{\sqrt5},
$$
while
$$
\left.\frac{\partial G^2}{\partial u}\right|_{(0,1)}=0.
$$
These are exactly the coefficients of the pushed-forward tangent vector in the $\psi$-coordinate basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

(d) In the global coordinates $(u,v)=\varphi(x,y,z)$,
$$
f\circ\varphi^{-1}(u,v)
=uv(u^2+v^2)^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

On $M$, one has
$$
z=u^2+v^2.
$$
Substitute this into
$$
f(x,y,z)=xyz^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^1}\right|_{(1,-1,2)}f=-12
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate the coordinate expression from step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}:
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial u}
\bigl(uv(u^2+v^2)^2\bigr)
=
v(u^2+v^2)^2+4u^2v(u^2+v^2).
$$
At $(u,v)=(1,-1)$, one has $u^2+v^2=2$, so
$$
-1\cdot4+4\cdot1\cdot(-1)\cdot2
=-4-8
=-12.
$$

:::
