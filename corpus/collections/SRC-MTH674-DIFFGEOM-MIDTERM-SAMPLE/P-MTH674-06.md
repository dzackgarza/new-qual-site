::: {.problem}
Let $D$ be a derivation at $p\in M$.

(a) Suppose $f:M\to\mathbb R$ vanishes on some neighborhood of $p$.
Show that $Df=0$; that is, $D$ is a local operator.

(b) Let $M=\mathbb R^n$ with coordinates $x^1,\ldots,x^n$, and let $D$ be a derivation at $0$.
Show that $D$ is a linear combination of the coordinate derivations $\partial/\partial x^i$, $i=1,\ldots,n$.

You may assume without proof that if $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ is smooth and $f(0)=0$, then there are smooth functions $g_i:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ such that
\[
f(x^1,\ldots,x^n)=\sum_{i=1}^n x^i g_i(x^1,\ldots,x^n).
\]
:::
