::: {.problem}
(a) Let $M_1,M_2$ be smooth manifolds.
Show that $M_1\times M_2$ is diffeomorphic to $M_2\times M_1$ in the standard product manifold structures.

(b) Let
\[
F:\mathbb{RP}^2\longrightarrow \operatorname{Gr}(3,2)
\]
map a line in $\mathbb R^3$ to the plane perpendicular to it.
Show that $F$ is a diffeomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The map
$$
S:M_1\times M_2\to M_2\times M_1,
\qquad
S(p,q)=(q,p),
$$
is a bijection and satisfies $S^{-1}=S$.

::: {.proof}
Applying $S$ twice gives
$$
S(S(p,q))=(p,q),
$$
so $S$ is its own inverse.
:::

<1>2. The map $S$ is smooth, and hence is a diffeomorphism.

::: {.proof}
Let $(U,x)$ be a chart on $M_1$ and $(V,y)$ a chart on $M_2$. The corresponding product charts are
$$
(U\times V,x\times y)
$$
and
$$
(V\times U,y\times x).
$$
In these coordinates the map $S$ is
$$
\RR^{m+n}\to\RR^{n+m},
\qquad
(u,v)\mapsto(v,u),
$$
which is linear and therefore smooth. Since $S^{-1}=S$, the inverse is smooth as well.
:::

<1>3. For part (b), the map
$$
F:\RP^2\to\operatorname{Gr}(3,2),
\qquad
F(L)=L^\perp,
$$
is bijective, with inverse
$$
G(P)=P^\perp.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $L\subset\RR^3$ is a line, then $L^\perp$ is a $2$-plane. If $P\subset\RR^3$ is a $2$-plane, then $P^\perp$ is a line. In finite-dimensional Euclidean space,
$$
(L^\perp)^\perp=L
$$
and
$$
(P^\perp)^\perp=P.
$$
Therefore $G\circ F$ and $F\circ G$ are identity maps.
:::

<1>4. In standard local coordinates, $F$ is smooth.

::: {.proof}
Consider the projective chart
$$
U_3=\{[X:Y:Z]\in\RP^2:Z\ne0\}
$$
with coordinates
$$
[x:y:1]\longleftrightarrow(x,y).
$$
Let $L$ be represented by the vector
$$
(x,y,1).
$$
Then
$$
F(L)=L^\perp
=\{(u,v,w):xu+yv+w=0\}.
$$
Hence
$$
F(L)
=\{(u,v,-xu-yv):(u,v)\in\RR^2\}.
$$
This is the graph of the linear map
$$
\RR^2\to\RR,
\qquad
(u,v)\mapsto-xu-yv.
$$

The standard Grassmannian chart consisting of $2$-planes whose projection onto
$$
\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2)
$$
is an isomorphism identifies such a plane with the two coefficients $(a,b)$ of its graph
$$
w=au+bv.
$$
In these coordinates, $F$ is therefore
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto(-x,-y),
$$
which is smooth.

The analogous projective charts $U_1,U_2$ give the same conclusion near lines whose first or second homogeneous coordinate is nonzero. These three charts cover $\RP^2$, so $F$ is smooth everywhere.
:::

<1>5. The inverse map $G(P)=P^\perp$ is smooth.

::: {.proof}
In the Grassmannian chart used in step <1>4, write
$$
P=\{(u,v,au+bv):(u,v)\in\RR^2\}.
$$
A vector orthogonal to both
$$
(1,0,a)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(0,1,b)
$$
is
$$
(-a,-b,1).
$$
Thus
$$
G(P)=[-a:-b:1].
$$
In the corresponding projective chart, the coordinate representation of $G$ is
$$
(a,b)\longmapsto(-a,-b),
$$
which is smooth. The corresponding argument in the other standard Grassmannian charts covers all of $\operatorname{Gr}(3,2)$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{F:\RP^2\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gr}(3,2)}
$$
is a diffeomorphism.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves bijectivity. Steps <1>4--<1>5 prove that $F$ and its inverse are smooth.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves part (a), and step <1>6 proves part (b).
:::
