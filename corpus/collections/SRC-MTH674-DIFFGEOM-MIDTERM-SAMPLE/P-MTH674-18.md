::: {.problem}
(a) Define
\[
F:\RR^m\longrightarrow\RP^m,
\qquad
F(x^1,\ldots,x^m)=[x^1,\ldots,x^m,1].
\]
Show that $F$ is smooth onto a dense open subset of $\RP^m$.

(b) Define similarly
\[
G:\CC^m\longrightarrow\CP^m,
\qquad
G(z^1,\ldots,z^m)=[z^1,\ldots,z^m,1].
\]
Show that $G$ is a diffeomorphism onto a dense open subset of $\CP^m$.

(c) Let $\widehat p(z)$ be a complex polynomial in one variable and let
\[
G:\CC\longrightarrow\mathcal U\subset\CP^1
\]
be as in part (b), with image $\mathcal U$.
Define $p:\mathcal U\to\mathcal U$ by
\[
p\circ G=G\circ\widehat p.
\]
Show that $p$ extends to a smooth map on all of $\CP^1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) The image of
$$
F:\RR^m\to\RP^m,
\qquad
F(x^1,\ldots,x^m)=[x^1:\cdots:x^m:1],
$$
is the standard affine chart
$$
U_{m+1}=\{[X^1:\cdots:X^{m+1}]:X^{m+1}\ne0\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every point in the image has last homogeneous coordinate nonzero. Conversely, if $X^{m+1}\ne0$, then
$$
[X^1:\cdots:X^{m+1}]
=
\left[\frac{X^1}{X^{m+1}}:\cdots:\frac{X^m}{X^{m+1}}:1\right],
$$
so the point lies in the image of $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map $F$ is a diffeomorphism from $\RR^m$ onto $U_{m+1}$; in particular it is smooth.


::: pf-proof

The standard projective coordinate map
$$
\varphi_{m+1}:U_{m+1}\to\RR^m,
\qquad
[X^1:\cdots:X^{m+1}]
\mapsto
\left(\frac{X^1}{X^{m+1}},\ldots,\frac{X^m}{X^{m+1}}\right)
$$
is the inverse of $F$. Thus $F$ is the inverse of a standard smooth chart.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The subset $U_{m+1}$ is open and dense in $\RP^m$.


::: pf-proof

It is open because it is a standard projective chart. If
$$
q=[X^1:\cdots:X^m:0]
$$
lies in its complement, then for $t\ne0$,
$$
q_t=[X^1:\cdots:X^m:t]
$$
belongs to $U_{m+1}$ and tends to $q$ as $t\to0$. Hence every point of the complement lies in the closure of $U_{m+1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

(b) The image of
$$
G:\CC^m\to\CP^m,
\qquad
G(z^1,\ldots,z^m)=[z^1:\cdots:z^m:1],
$$
is the standard affine chart
$$
\mathcal U_{m+1}=\{[Z^1:\cdots:Z^{m+1}]:Z^{m+1}\ne0\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The same normalization argument as in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} works over $\CC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The map $G$ is a diffeomorphism from $\CC^m$ onto the dense open subset $\mathcal U_{m+1}\subset\CP^m$.


::: pf-proof

Its inverse is the standard complex projective chart
$$
[Z^1:\cdots:Z^{m+1}]
\mapsto
\left(\frac{Z^1}{Z^{m+1}},\ldots,\frac{Z^m}{Z^{m+1}}\right).
$$
Both maps are holomorphic and hence smooth. Openness and density follow as in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

(c) Suppose first that $\widehat p$ is a nonconstant polynomial of degree $d\ge1$,
$$
\widehat p(z)=a_dz^d+a_{d-1}z^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0,
\qquad a_d\ne0.
$$
Define
$$
P_h(Z,W)=a_dZ^d+a_{d-1}Z^{d-1}W+\cdots+a_0W^d.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every term of $P_h$ has total degree $d$, and for $W\ne0$,
$$
P_h(Z,W)=W^d\widehat p(Z/W).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Define
$$
\widetilde p:\CP^1\to\CP^1,
\qquad
\widetilde p([Z:W])=[P_h(Z,W):W^d].
$$
This is well defined.


::: pf-proof

Replacing $(Z,W)$ by $(\lambda Z,\lambda W)$ multiplies both homogeneous coordinates by $\lambda^d$, so the projective point is unchanged. The two coordinates cannot vanish simultaneously: if $W\ne0$, then $W^d\ne0$; if $W=0$, then $Z\ne0$ and $P_h(Z,0)=a_dZ^d\ne0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

On the affine chart $\mathcal U=G(\CC)$,
$$
\widetilde p\circ G=G\circ\widehat p.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $z\in\CC$,
$$
\widetilde p([z:1])=[P_h(z,1):1]=[\widehat p(z):1].
$$
This is exactly $G(\widehat p(z))$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The extension $\widetilde p$ is smooth at the point at infinity $[1:0]$.


::: pf-proof

Use the source coordinate
$$
\eta=\frac WZ
$$
near $[1:0]$. Since
$$
P_h(1,0)=a_d\ne0,
$$
the first homogeneous coordinate of the image is nonzero for sufficiently small $\eta$. In the corresponding target chart the coordinate expression is
$$
\eta\longmapsto
\frac{\eta^d}{P_h(1,\eta)}.
$$
The denominator is nonzero near $0$, so this is smooth at $\eta=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Hence the map $p$ extends smoothly to all of $\CP^1$ when $\deg\widehat p\ge1$.


::: pf-proof

On the affine chart, step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} identifies the extension with the polynomial $\widehat p$, so it is smooth there. Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} proves smoothness at the only missing point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

If $\widehat p$ is constant, then $p$ extends to the same constant map on all of $\CP^1$.


::: pf-proof

If $\widehat p(z)=c$, define
$$
\widetilde p([Z:W])=[c:1]
$$
for all $[Z:W]\in\CP^1$. This is smooth and agrees with $p$ on $\mathcal U$.

:::

:::
