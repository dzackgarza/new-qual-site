::: {.problem}
The equations
\[
x=\rho\cos\theta\sin\phi,
\qquad
y=\rho\sin\theta\sin\phi,
\qquad z=\rho\cos\phi
\]
define spherical coordinates $(\rho,\theta,\phi)$ on $\RR^3$.

(a) Let $V=\partial/\partial\phi$ and $W=\partial/\partial\rho$.
Express $V$ and $W$ in Cartesian coordinates $(x,y,z)$.

(b) Let
\[
\pi_P:\RR^3\setminus\{0\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{RP}^2
\]
be the projection.
Find an expression for $(\pi_P)_*(V)$ in suitable coordinates on $\mathbb{RP}^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) The coordinate vector field $V=\partial/\partial\phi$ is
$$
\boxed{
V
=\rho\cos\theta\cos\phi\,\partial_x
+\rho\sin\theta\cos\phi\,\partial_y
-\rho\sin\phi\,\partial_z
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate the coordinate transformation with respect to $\phi$, holding $\rho$ and $\theta$ fixed:
$$
\frac{\partial x}{\partial\phi}=\rho\cos\theta\cos\phi,
\qquad
\frac{\partial y}{\partial\phi}=\rho\sin\theta\cos\phi,
$$
and
$$
\frac{\partial z}{\partial\phi}=-\rho\sin\phi.
$$
The coordinate-change formula for tangent vectors gives the result.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The coordinate vector field $W=\partial/\partial\rho$ is
$$
\boxed{
W
=\cos\theta\sin\phi\,\partial_x
+\sin\theta\sin\phi\,\partial_y
+\cos\phi\,\partial_z
}.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
W=\frac{x}{\rho}\partial_x+\frac{y}{\rho}\partial_y+\frac{z}{\rho}\partial_z.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate the coordinate transformation with respect to $\rho$:
$$
\frac{\partial x}{\partial\rho}=\cos\theta\sin\phi,
\quad
\frac{\partial y}{\partial\rho}=\sin\theta\sin\phi,
\quad
\frac{\partial z}{\partial\rho}=\cos\phi.
$$
The second formula follows from the defining equations for $x,y,z$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For part (b), restrict to a spherical-coordinate region with $z>0$, so that $0<\phi<\pi/2$, and use the standard projective chart
$$
U_z=\{[x:y:z]:z\ne0\}
$$
with coordinates
$$
(u,v)=\left(\frac{x}{z},\frac{y}{z}\right).
$$
Then
$$
u=\cos\theta\tan\phi,
\qquad
v=\sin\theta\tan\phi.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute the spherical-coordinate expressions for $x,y,z$ and cancel the common factor $\rho$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

On this local branch,
$$
V(u)=\cos\theta\sec^2\phi,
\qquad
V(v)=\sin\theta\sec^2\phi.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $V=\partial/\partial\phi$, differentiate the formulas in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} with respect to $\phi$ while keeping $\theta$ fixed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Put
$$
r=\sqrt{u^2+v^2}.
$$
On the region $z>0$, one has
$$
r=\tan\phi,
\qquad
\cos\theta=\frac ur,
\qquad
\sin\theta=\frac vr.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
(\pi_P)_*(V)
=
\frac{1+r^2}{r}
\left(
 u\frac{\partial}{\partial u}
+v\frac{\partial}{\partial v}
\right)
}
$$
where the spherical coordinates are defined and $r>0$.


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
u^2+v^2=\tan^2\phi.
$$
On $0<\phi<\pi/2$, $r=\tan\phi>0$. Also
$$
\sec^2\phi=1+\tan^2\phi=1+r^2.
$$
Substitute these relations into step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. The coefficients of $\partial_u$ and $\partial_v$ are precisely $V(u)$ and $V(v)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Equivalently, if the local coordinates on $\RP^2$ are transported directly from the chosen hemisphere using the angular coordinates $(\theta,\phi)$, then
$$
\boxed{(\pi_P)_*(V)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

On a hemisphere, $\pi_P$ is a local diffeomorphism. Using the same angular coordinates on the image makes its coordinate representation the identity in the angular variables, so the differential carries $\partial_\phi$ to $\partial_\phi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The field $V$ does not descend to a single globally defined vector field on $\RP^2$.


::: pf-proof

Let $a(p)=-p$ be the antipodal map. Since
$$
\pi_P\circ a=\pi_P,
$$
a vector field descends through the quotient only if its values at antipodal points are related by $da$. In spherical coordinates the antipodal transformation is
$$
(\rho,\theta,\phi)
\longmapsto
(\rho,\theta+\pi,\pi-\phi),
$$
so
$$
da\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right)
=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
$$
when expressed at the antipodal point. Thus the two local pushforwards from opposite hemisphere branches differ by sign. The expression in steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is therefore correctly local, as requested by choosing suitable coordinates.

:::

:::
