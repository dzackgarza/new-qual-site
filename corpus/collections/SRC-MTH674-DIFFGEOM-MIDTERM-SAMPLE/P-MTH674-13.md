::: {.problem}
Let $\CP^n$ and $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ denote the complex projective space and Grassmann manifold constructed in Problems XI and XII.

(a) Let $S^3$ be the unit sphere in $\CC^2\cong\RR^4$, and let the Hopf map
$$
\pi_H:S^3\longrightarrow S^2
$$
be the restriction of the projective projection to $S^3$.
Describe $\pi_H^{-1}(p)$ for $p\in S^2$.

(b) Show that
$$
\pi_{Gr}:\mathcal T\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)
$$
is differentiable.

(c) Show that $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ is compact.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) Let $p\in S^2\cong\CP^1$ correspond to a complex line $L\subset\CC^2$. Then
$$
\pi_H^{-1}(p)=L\cap S^3.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Hopf map is the restriction to $S^3$ of the projective projection
$$
\pi_P:\CC^2\setminus\{0\}\to\CP^1,
$$
which sends a nonzero vector to the complex line it spans. Hence a unit vector $z\in S^3$ lies over $p=[L]$ exactly when $z\in L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $v\in L$ is any unit vector, then
$$
\boxed{\pi_H^{-1}(p)=\{e^{i\theta}v:\theta\in\RR\}\cong S^1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every nonzero vector in the one-dimensional complex vector space $L$ has the form $\lambda v$ for a unique $\lambda\in\CC$. Such a vector has norm $1$ exactly when
$$
1=\norm{\lambda v}=|\lambda|\norm v=|\lambda|.
$$
Thus $\lambda$ lies on the unit circle and can be written $e^{i\theta}$. Conversely, every $e^{i\theta}v$ is a unit vector in $L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

(b) Fix $i<j$. On the open set
$$
\mathcal T(i,j)=\{M\in\mathcal T:\det M_{ij}\ne0\},
$$
the coordinate representation of $\pi_{Gr}$ in the chart $\varphi_{ij}$ of Problem XII is obtained from
$$
N_{ij}(M)=M_{ij}^{-1}M
$$
by deleting columns $i,j$.


::: pf-proof

Problem XII constructs $\varphi_{ij}$ by choosing the unique representative of the $GL(2)$-orbit whose $(i,j)$-minor is $I_2$. For $M\in\mathcal T(i,j)$, multiplying by $M_{ij}^{-1}$ produces exactly this representative. The chart coordinates are its remaining $2n-4$ entries.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The map
$$
\varphi_{ij}\circ\pi_{Gr}:\mathcal T(i,j)\to\RR^{2n-4}
$$
is smooth.


::: pf-proof

The entries of $M_{ij}^{-1}$ are rational functions of the four entries of $M_{ij}$ with denominator $\det M_{ij}$. This denominator is nonzero on $\mathcal T(i,j)$. Therefore every entry of
$$
M_{ij}^{-1}M
$$
is a smooth function of the entries of $M$ on this open set. Deleting two columns is linear, hence smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\pi_{Gr}:\mathcal T\to\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)\text{ is smooth}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The sets $\mathcal T(i,j)$ cover $\mathcal T$, because every rank-two matrix has at least one nonzero $2\times2$ minor. Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that the coordinate representation of $\pi_{Gr}$ is smooth on each member of this open cover. Hence $\pi_{Gr}$ is smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

(c) Let
$$
V_2(\RR^n)=\{(v_1,v_2)\in\RR^n\times\RR^n:\norm{v_1}=\norm{v_2}=1,\ \langle v_1,v_2\rangle=0\}
$$
be the Stiefel space of ordered orthonormal $2$-frames. Then $V_2(\RR^n)$ is compact.


::: pf-proof

It is the common zero set of the continuous functions
$$
\norm{v_1}^2-1,
\qquad
\norm{v_2}^2-1,
\qquad
\langle v_1,v_2\rangle,
$$
so it is closed in $\RR^{2n}$. It is bounded because both vectors have norm $1$. By Heine--Borel, it is compact.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The map
$$
q:V_2(\RR^n)\to\operatorname{Gr}(n,2),
\qquad
q(v_1,v_2)=\operatorname{span}\{v_1,v_2\},
$$
is continuous and surjective.


::: pf-proof

Identify $(v_1,v_2)$ with the $2\times n$ matrix whose rows are $v_1,v_2$. Then $V_2(\RR^n)\subset\mathcal T$, and $q$ is the restriction of the quotient projection $\pi_{Gr}$, which is continuous by the definition of the quotient topology. Thus $q$ is continuous.

Every $2$-plane in $\RR^n$ has an ordered orthonormal basis by Gram--Schmidt. Therefore every element of $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$ lies in the image of $q$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)\text{ is compact}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, $V_2(\RR^n)$ is compact. By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, $q$ is a continuous surjection onto $\operatorname{Gr}(n,2)$. A continuous image of a compact space is compact.

:::
