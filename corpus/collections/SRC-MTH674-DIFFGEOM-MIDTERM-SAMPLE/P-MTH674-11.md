::: {.problem}
Let $\mathbb{CP}^1$ be the set of one-dimensional complex subspaces of $\CC^2$, equivalently the orbit space of the scalar-multiplication action of $\CC^*=\CC\setminus\{0\}$ on $\CC^2\setminus\{0\}$.
Write
\[
\pi_P:\CC^2\setminus\{0\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^1
\]
for the projection, and give $\mathbb{CP}^1$ the quotient topology.

(a) Explain why $\mathbb{CP}^1$ is second countable and Hausdorff, and show that $\pi_P$ is open.

(b) Define an atlas of coordinate charts on $\mathbb{CP}^1$ analogous to the standard charts on $\mathbb{RP}^1$, prove that the coordinate maps are homeomorphisms, and identify their images.

(c) Find the transition functions for the atlas from part (b).

(d) Compute the rank of $\pi_P$ at every point of $\CC^2\setminus\{0\}$.

(e) Use part (c) to show that $\mathbb{CP}^1$ is diffeomorphic to $S^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write homogeneous coordinates on $\CP^1$ as $[z_0:z_1]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) The quotient projection
$$
\pi_P:\CC^2\setminus\{0\}\to\CP^1
$$
is an open map.


::: pf-proof

Let $O\subset\CC^2\setminus\{0\}$ be open. Its saturation under the scalar action is
$$
\pi_P^{-1}(\pi_P(O))
=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\CC^*}\lambda O.
$$
For each $\lambda\ne0$, scalar multiplication by $\lambda$ is a homeomorphism, so $\lambda O$ is open. Hence the saturation is open. By the definition of the quotient topology, $\pi_P(O)$ is open in $\CP^1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

(b) Define
$$
U_0=\{[z_0:z_1]:z_0\ne0\},
\qquad
U_1=\{[z_0:z_1]:z_1\ne0\}.
$$
These are open and cover $\CP^1$.


::: pf-proof

Their inverse images are
$$
\{(z_0,z_1):z_0\ne0\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{(z_0,z_1):z_1\ne0\},
$$
which are open in $\CC^2\setminus\{0\}$. Thus $U_0,U_1$ are open by the quotient topology. Every nonzero vector has at least one nonzero coordinate, so the two sets cover.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Define coordinate maps
$$
\varphi_0:U_0\to\CC,
\qquad
\varphi_0([z_0:z_1])=\frac{z_1}{z_0},
$$
and
$$
\varphi_1:U_1\to\CC,
\qquad
\varphi_1([z_0:z_1])=\frac{z_0}{z_1}.
$$
Each is a homeomorphism onto $\CC$.


::: pf-proof

The ratios are invariant under multiplication of $(z_0,z_1)$ by a nonzero complex scalar, so the maps are well defined. Their inverses are
$$
\varphi_0^{-1}(\zeta)=[1:\zeta],
\qquad
\varphi_1^{-1}(\eta)=[\eta:1].
$$
These inverse maps are continuous because they are compositions of the continuous sections
$$
\zeta\mapsto(1,\zeta),
\qquad
\eta\mapsto(\eta,1)
$$
with $\pi_P$.

For continuity of $\varphi_0$, let $W\subset\CC$ be open. Then
$$
\pi_P^{-1}(\varphi_0^{-1}(W))
=\{(z_0,z_1):z_0\ne0,\ z_1/z_0\in W\},
$$
which is open. Hence $\varphi_0^{-1}(W)$ is open in the quotient topology. The same argument applies to $\varphi_1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The space $\CP^1$ is second countable.


::: pf-proof

Each $U_j$ is homeomorphic to $\CC\cong\RR^2$, which has a countable base. The union of the two transported countable bases is a countable base for $\CP^1$, since $U_0\cup U_1=\CP^1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The space $\CP^1$ is Hausdorff.


::: pf-proof

Let $p\ne q$ in $\CP^1$. If both points lie in one $U_j$, separate them by disjoint open sets using the homeomorphism $U_j\cong\CC$.

The only case in which no standard chart contains both points is
$$
p=[1:0],
\qquad
q=[0:1].
$$
In $U_0$, take
$$
V_0=\{[1:\zeta]:|\zeta|<1/2\},
$$
and in $U_1$ take
$$
V_1=\{[\eta:1]:|\eta|<1/2\}.
$$
These are open neighborhoods of $p$ and $q$. If a point belonged to both, then on $U_0\cap U_1$ one would have
$$
\eta=\frac1\zeta,
$$
which is impossible when both $|\zeta|<1/2$ and $|\eta|<1/2$. Thus $V_0$ and $V_1$ are disjoint.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

(c) On $U_0\cap U_1$, the transition maps are
$$
\boxed{\varphi_1\circ\varphi_0^{-1}(\zeta)=\frac1\zeta},
\qquad
\boxed{\varphi_0\circ\varphi_1^{-1}(\eta)=\frac1\eta},
$$
with domain $\CC^*$.


::: pf-proof

For $\zeta\ne0$,
$$
\varphi_0^{-1}(\zeta)=[1:\zeta]
=[\zeta^{-1}:1],
$$
so applying $\varphi_1$ gives $1/\zeta$. The other formula is symmetric.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The transition maps in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} are smooth as maps of real $2$-manifolds, so the two charts define the standard smooth structure on $\CP^1$.


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
\zeta\mapsto\frac1\zeta
$$
is holomorphic on $\CC^*$, hence smooth as a map $\RR^2\setminus\{0\}\to\RR^2\setminus\{0\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

(d) The projection $\pi_P$ has real rank $2$ at every point of $\CC^2\setminus\{0\}$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose $z_0\ne0$. In the target chart $\varphi_0$, the coordinate representation is
$$
(z_0,z_1)\longmapsto\frac{z_1}{z_0}.
$$
Its differential at $(z_0,z_1)$ is the complex-linear map
$$
(h_0,h_1)
\longmapsto
\frac{z_0h_1-z_1h_0}{z_0^2}.
$$
This map is surjective onto $\CC$: for any $w\in\CC$, choose
$$
h_0=0,
\qquad
h_1=z_0w.
$$
Thus its complex rank is $1$, equivalently its real rank is $2$. If $z_1\ne0$, use the chart $\varphi_1$ instead. Since every nonzero vector lies in one of these cases, the rank is $2$ everywhere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

(e) Define
$$
\Phi:\CP^1\to S^2
$$
by
$$
\Phi([z_0:z_1])
=
\frac{1}{|z_0|^2+|z_1|^2}
\left(
2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{z_0}z_1),
2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{z_0}z_1),
|z_1|^2-|z_0|^2
\right).
$$
This map is well defined and takes values in $S^2$.


::: pf-proof

Multiplying $(z_0,z_1)$ by $\lambda\in\CC^*$ multiplies every numerator component and the denominator by $|\lambda|^2$, so the value is unchanged.

Let
$$
a=|z_0|^2,
\qquad
b=|z_1|^2.
$$
The squared norm of the numerator is
$$
4ab+(b-a)^2=(a+b)^2,
$$
so after division by $(a+b)^2$ the image has norm $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

In the chart $U_0$, writing $\zeta=x+iy=z_1/z_0$, one has
$$
\Phi([1:\zeta])
=
\left(
\frac{2x}{1+|\zeta|^2},
\frac{2y}{1+|\zeta|^2},
\frac{|\zeta|^2-1}{1+|\zeta|^2}
\right).
$$
This is the inverse stereographic parametrization of $S^2\setminus\{(0,0,1)\}$.


::: pf-proof

Substitute $z_0=1$ and $z_1=\zeta$ into step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}. The displayed formula is the standard inverse of stereographic projection from the north pole.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The map $\Phi$ is a diffeomorphism $\CP^1\to S^2$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}, $\Phi$ restricts to a diffeomorphism
$$
U_0\to S^2\setminus\{N\},
\qquad N=(0,0,1).
$$
The missing point of $U_0$ is $[0:1]$, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\Phi([0:1])=N.
$$
Thus $\Phi$ is bijective.

Smoothness on $U_0$ follows from the formula in step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}. On $U_1$, write $\eta=z_0/z_1$. Substitution into step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\Phi([\eta:1])
=
\left(
\frac{2\operatorname{Re}\eta}{1+|\eta|^2},
\frac{-2\operatorname{Im}\eta}{1+|\eta|^2},
\frac{1-|\eta|^2}{1+|\eta|^2}
\right),
$$
which is smooth at $\eta=0$, so $\Phi$ is smooth at $[0:1]$ as well.

The inverse is smooth on $S^2\setminus\{N\}$ by stereographic projection and on $S^2\setminus\{S\}$, $S=(0,0,-1)$, by the corresponding second formula. Hence $\Phi$ is a diffeomorphism.

:::
