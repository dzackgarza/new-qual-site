::: {.problem}
Define in detail:

(a) a topological manifold $M$;

(b) a differentiable manifold $M$;

(c) transition functions;

(d) an atlas of coordinate charts;

(e) the real and complex projective spaces $\RP^n$ and $\CP^n$;

(f) the Grassmannians $\operatorname{Gr}(m,k)$;

(g) the product manifold $M_1\times M_2$ of two differentiable manifolds $M_1,M_2$;

(h) a coordinate representation of a map $F:M_1\to M_2$ between topological manifolds;

(i) a smooth map $F:M_1\to M_2$ between differentiable manifolds;

(j) a diffeomorphism $F:M_1\to M_2$;

(k) a bump function;

(l) a partition of unity subordinate to a cover;

(m) a derivation at a point $p\in M$;

(n) the tangent space $T_pM$;

(o) the pushforward $F_*(v)$ of $v\in T_pM$ under a map $F:M\to N$;

(p) the coordinate vectors $\partial/\partial x^i$ for a coordinate chart $(\mathcal U,x=(x^1,\ldots,x^m))$;

(q) a smooth vector field $X$ on a differentiable manifold $M$;

(r) the rank of a map $F:M_1\to M_2$;

(s) the Lie bracket $[X,Y]$ of two smooth vector fields;

(t) $F$-related vector fields on $M_1,M_2$, where $F:M_1\to M_2$ is smooth;

(u) quotient topology;

(v) immersion and submersion;

(w) immersed and embedded submanifold;

(x) slice coordinates;

(y) Lie group and the Lie algebra of a Lie group;

(z) the Einstein summation convention.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) An $n$-dimensional topological manifold is a Hausdorff, second-countable topological space $M$ such that every $p\in M$ has an open neighborhood $U$ homeomorphic to an open subset of $\RR^n$.


::: pf-proof

This is the definition. A homeomorphism $x:U\to x(U)\subset\RR^n$ is a coordinate chart.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

(b) A differentiable manifold is a topological manifold equipped with a maximal smooth atlas.


::: pf-proof

A smooth atlas is a collection of coordinate charts covering $M$ whose transition maps are smooth wherever defined. Maximal means it contains every chart smoothly compatible with all charts already in the atlas.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

(c) If $(U,x)$ and $(V,y)$ are charts with $U\cap V\ne\varnothing$, their transition functions are
$$
y\circ x^{-1}:x(U\cap V)\to y(U\cap V)
$$
and
$$
x\circ y^{-1}:y(U\cap V)\to x(U\cap V).
$$


::: pf-proof

These are the coordinate changes between the two Euclidean representations of points in $U\cap V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

(d) An atlas of coordinate charts is a collection $\{(U_\alpha,x_\alpha)\}$ of charts such that
$$
M=\bigcup_\alpha U_\alpha.
$$
A smooth atlas additionally requires every transition map to be smooth.


::: pf-proof

This is the definition of an atlas and of smooth compatibility.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

(e) The real projective space $\RP^n$ is the set of one-dimensional real linear subspaces of $\RR^{n+1}$, equivalently
$$
\RP^n=(\RR^{n+1}\setminus\{0\})/\sim,
\qquad
x\sim \lambda x\quad(\lambda\in\RR^\times).
$$
Similarly,
$$
\CP^n=(\CC^{n+1}\setminus\{0\})/\sim,
\qquad
z\sim\lambda z\quad(\lambda\in\CC^\times).
$$


::: pf-proof

These quotient descriptions identify nonzero vectors exactly when they span the same one-dimensional subspace.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

(f) The Grassmannian $\operatorname{Gr}(m,k)$ is the set of $k$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\RR^m$; over $\CC$ the same notation may denote the complex Grassmannian of $k$-planes in $\CC^m$.


::: pf-proof

This is the defining moduli description of the Grassmannian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

(g) If $M_1$ and $M_2$ are smooth manifolds of dimensions $m$ and $n$, their product manifold is the set $M_1\times M_2$ with the product topology and the smooth atlas consisting of charts
$$
(U_1\times U_2,\,x_1\times x_2),
$$
where
$$
(x_1\times x_2)(p,q)=(x_1(p),x_2(q))\in\RR^{m+n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Transition functions split as products of the transition functions on the two factors and are therefore smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

(h) If $F:M_1\to M_2$, $(U,x)$ is a chart on $M_1$, and $(V,y)$ is a chart on $M_2$ with $F(U)\subseteq V$, the coordinate representation of $F$ is
$$
y\circ F\circ x^{-1}:x(U)\to y(V).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the expression of $F$ in Euclidean coordinates.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

(i) A map $F:M_1\to M_2$ between smooth manifolds is smooth if it is continuous and every coordinate representation
$$
y\circ F\circ x^{-1}
$$
is a smooth map between open subsets of Euclidean spaces.


::: pf-proof

Equivalently, it suffices to check one pair of compatible charts around each point and its image.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

(j) A diffeomorphism $F:M_1\to M_2$ is a bijection such that both $F$ and $F^{-1}$ are smooth.


::: pf-proof

This is the definition of isomorphism in the category of smooth manifolds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

(k) A bump function on a smooth manifold is a smooth function $\phi:M\to[0,1]$ with compact support, typically chosen to equal $1$ on a prescribed closed set or neighborhood and to vanish outside a prescribed larger open set.


::: pf-proof

The essential defining feature is smoothness together with compact support; the additional support/value conditions specify the bump adapted to a given pair of sets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

(l) A partition of unity subordinate to an open cover $\{U_\alpha\}$ is a family of smooth functions
$$
\{\phi_\alpha:M\to[0,1]\}
$$
such that the supports are locally finite,
$$
\operatorname{supp}(\phi_\alpha)\subseteq U_\alpha,
$$
and
$$
\sum_\alpha\phi_\alpha(p)=1
$$
for every $p\in M$.


::: pf-proof

Local finiteness makes the pointwise sum locally finite and therefore well defined and smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

(m) A derivation at $p\in M$ is an $\RR$-linear map
$$
v:C^\infty(M)\to\RR
$$
satisfying the Leibniz rule
$$
v(fg)=f(p)v(g)+g(p)v(f).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the algebraic definition of a tangent vector at $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

(n) The tangent space $T_pM$ is the real vector space of derivations at $p$.


::: pf-proof

Addition and scalar multiplication are defined pointwise on derivations, and the Leibniz rule is preserved.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

(o) If $F:M\to N$ is smooth and $v\in T_pM$, the pushforward
$$
F_{*p}(v)\in T_{F(p)}N
$$
is defined by
$$
(F_{*p}v)(g)=v(g\circ F)
$$
for every $g\in C^\infty(N)$.


::: pf-proof

Composition with $F$ carries smooth functions on $N$ to smooth functions on $M$, and the Leibniz rule for $v$ implies the Leibniz rule for $F_{*p}v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

(p) For a chart $(U,x)$ with coordinates $x=(x^1,\ldots,x^m)$, the coordinate vector
$$
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p
$$
is the derivation
$$
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p(f)
=
\frac{\partial(f\circ x^{-1})}{\partial u^i}(x(p)).
$$
The coordinate vectors form a basis of $T_pM$.


::: pf-proof

This is the coordinate realization of tangent derivations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

(q) A smooth vector field on $M$ is a map
$$
X:M\to TM
$$
such that $X(p)\in T_pM$ for every $p$ and such that, in every local chart,
$$
X=\sum_i X^i\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}
$$
with smooth coefficient functions $X^i$.


::: pf-proof

This is the definition of a smooth section of the tangent bundle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

(r) The rank of a smooth map $F:M_1\to M_2$ at $p\in M_1$ is
$$
\operatorname{rank}_pF
=
\operatorname{rank}(F_{*p}:T_pM_1\to T_{F(p)}M_2).
$$
If this rank is independent of $p$, that common value is called the rank of $F$.


::: pf-proof

In coordinates this equals the matrix rank of the Jacobian of a coordinate representation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

(s) The Lie bracket of smooth vector fields $X,Y$ is the vector field
$$
[X,Y](f)=X(Yf)-Y(Xf)
$$
for every $f\in C^\infty(M)$.


::: pf-proof

The commutator of the two derivations is again a derivation and therefore defines a vector field.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

(t) Vector fields $X$ on $M_1$ and $Y$ on $M_2$ are $F$-related for a smooth map $F:M_1\to M_2$ if
$$
F_{*p}(X_p)=Y_{F(p)}
$$
for every $p\in M_1$.


::: pf-proof

This is the definition of compatibility of the two fields with the differential of $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

(u) Given a surjective map $q:X\to Y$ from a topological space $X$, the quotient topology on $Y$ is defined by
$$
U\subseteq Y\text{ open}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q^{-1}(U)\subseteq X\text{ open}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the finest topology on $Y$ for which $q$ is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

(v) A smooth map $F:M\to N$ is an immersion if every differential
$$
F_{*p}:T_pM\to T_{F(p)}N
$$
is injective, and a submersion if every $F_{*p}$ is surjective.


::: pf-proof

These are the definitions of immersion and submersion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-23}

(w) An immersed submanifold of $N$ is a manifold $M$ together with an injective immersion
$$
i:M\to N.
$$
It is an embedded submanifold if, in addition, $i$ is a homeomorphism from $M$ onto $i(M)$ with the subspace topology of $N$.


::: pf-proof

Thus embedded submanifolds carry exactly the topology induced from the ambient manifold, while immersed submanifolds need not.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-24}

(x) Slice coordinates for a $k$-dimensional embedded submanifold $S\subset M^n$ near $p\in S$ are local coordinates
$$
(x^1,\ldots,x^n)
$$
on a neighborhood $U$ of $p$ such that
$$
S\cap U
=
\{x^{k+1}=\cdots=x^n=0\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

In such coordinates the submanifold is represented by a coordinate slice of $\RR^n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-25}

(y) A Lie group is a smooth manifold $G$ equipped with a group structure for which multiplication
$$
G\times G\to G,
\qquad
(g,h)\mapsto gh,
$$
and inversion
$$
G\to G,
\qquad
g\mapsto g^{-1},
$$
are smooth. Its Lie algebra is
$$
\mathfrak g=T_eG
$$
with Lie bracket induced by the bracket of the corresponding left-invariant vector fields.


::: pf-proof

Every $v\in T_eG$ extends uniquely to a left-invariant vector field, and the bracket of left-invariant fields is again left-invariant, yielding the bracket on $T_eG$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-26}

(z) The Einstein summation convention suppresses an explicit summation sign over any index repeated once upstairs and once downstairs in a monomial; for example,
$$
X^i\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}
$$
means
$$
\sum_i X^i\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard index-notation convention requested in the problem.

:::
