::: {.problem}
(a) Let $M_1=\RR$ with coordinate map $\phi(x)=x$, and let $M_2=\RR$ with coordinate map $\psi(x)=x^{1/3}$.
Show that $M_1$ and $M_2$ are not equal as manifolds but are diffeomorphic as manifolds.

(b) The figure eight is the image of
$$
F:(-\pi,\pi)\longrightarrow\RR^2,
\qquad
F(t)=\left(2\cos\left(t-\frac\pi2\right),\sin\left(2\left(t-\frac\pi2\right)\right)\right).
$$
Show that $F$ is an injective immersion but not an embedding.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The identity map
$$
\operatorname{id}:M_1\to M_2
$$
is not smooth at $0$.


::: pf-proof

The coordinate representation of the identity map in the given global coordinates is
$$
\psi\circ\operatorname{id}\circ\phi^{-1}(u)
=u^{1/3}.
$$
This function is not differentiable at $u=0$, since
$$
\frac{h^{1/3}-0}{h}=h^{-2/3}
$$
does not have a finite limit as $h\to0$. Thus the two smooth structures are not the same.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map
$$
G:M_1\to M_2,
\qquad
G(x)=x^3,
$$
is a diffeomorphism.


::: pf-proof

The map $G$ is bijective with inverse
$$
G^{-1}(y)=y^{1/3}.
$$
Its coordinate representation is
$$
\psi\circ G\circ\phi^{-1}(u)
=(u^3)^{1/3}
=u,
$$
which is smooth. For the inverse,
$$
\phi\circ G^{-1}\circ\psi^{-1}(v)
=\phi\bigl((v^3)^{1/3}\bigr)
=v,
$$
which is also smooth. Hence $G$ is a diffeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For part (b), the map can be written as
$$
F(t)=(2\sin t,-\sin 2t).
$$


::: pf-proof

The identities
$$
\cos\left(t-\frac\pi2\right)=\sin t
$$
and
$$
\sin\left(2t-\pi\right)=-\sin2t
$$
give the stated form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The map $F:(-\pi,\pi)\to\RR^2$ is injective.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
F(t)=F(s).
$$
Then
$$
\sin t=\sin s
$$
and
$$
\sin2t=\sin2s.
$$
If $\sin t=0$, then $t=0$ because $t\in(-\pi,\pi)$; likewise $s=0$, so $t=s$.

Assume now that $\sin t\ne0$. Since
$$
\sin2t=2\sin t\cos t,
$$
and the same for $s$, equality of $\sin t$ and $\sin s$ together with equality of $\sin2t$ and $\sin2s$ gives
$$
\cos t=\cos s.
$$
Thus
$$
e^{it}=\cos t+i\sin t
=
\cos s+i\sin s
=e^{is}.
$$
Hence $t-s\in2\pi\ZZ$. Since both $t$ and $s$ lie in $(-\pi,\pi)$, this forces $t=s$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The map $F$ is an immersion.


::: pf-proof

Differentiate:
$$
F'(t)=(2\cos t,-2\cos2t).
$$
If $F'(t)=0$, then
$$
\cos t=0.
$$
Thus $t=\pm\pi/2$, but then
$$
\cos2t=\cos(\pm\pi)=-1\ne0,
$$
a contradiction. Therefore $F'(t)\ne0$ for every $t$, so the differential has rank $1$ everywhere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The map $F$ is not an embedding.


::: pf-proof

An injective immersion is an embedding only if it is a homeomorphism onto its image with the subspace topology.

Let
$$
t_n=\pi-\frac1n.
$$
Then $t_n\in(-\pi,\pi)$ and
$$
t_n\longrightarrow\pi,
$$
so in particular $t_n$ does not converge to $0$ in the domain. On the other hand,
$$
F(t_n)\longrightarrow(0,0)=F(0),
$$
because $\sin t_n\to0$ and $\sin2t_n\to0$.

If the inverse
$$
F^{-1}:F((-\pi,\pi))\to(-\pi,\pi)
$$
were continuous at $F(0)$, then from $F(t_n)\to F(0)$ one would obtain
$$
t_n=F^{-1}(F(t_n))\to F^{-1}(F(0))=0,
$$
contradicting $t_n\to\pi$. Thus the inverse is not continuous, so $F$ is not an embedding.

:::
