::: {.problem}
(a) Let $\varphi_3$ denote the standard coordinates on
\[
\mathcal U_3=\{[x,y,z]\in\RP^2:z\ne0\},
\qquad
(u^1,u^2)=\varphi_3([x,y,z])=(x/z,y/z).
\]
The coordinate tangent vector $v=\partial/\partial u^1$ is represented by a curve $\alpha$ in $\RP^2$, hence by a one-parameter family of lines in $\RR^3$.
Describe this family for a curve $\alpha$ of your choice.

(b) Let $\varphi_{34}$ denote the coordinates on $\operatorname{Gr}(4,2)$ from Problem XII, with coordinate matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}u_1^1&u_1^2\\u_2^1&u_2^2\end{pmatrix}=\varphi_{34}(\mathcal P).
\]
The coordinate tangent vector $v=\partial/\partial u_2^2$ is represented by a curve $\alpha$ in $\operatorname{Gr}(4,2)$, hence by a one-parameter family of planes in $\RR^4$.
Describe this family for a curve $\alpha$ of your choice.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) Let
$$
p=[0:0:1]\in\mathcal U_3,
$$
so that
$$
\varphi_3(p)=(0,0).
$$
Define
$$
\alpha(t)=\varphi_3^{-1}(t,0)=[t:0:1]
$$
for $t$ near $0$.


::: pf-proof

By construction,
$$
(\varphi_3\circ\alpha)(t)=(t,0).
$$
Therefore
$$
\left.\frac d{dt}\right|_{t=0}(\varphi_3\circ\alpha)(t)
=(1,0),
$$
which is exactly the coordinate tangent vector
$$
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial u^1}\right|_p.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The curve from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} represents the one-parameter family of lines
$$
\boxed{
L_t=\operatorname{span}\{(t,0,1)\}\subset\RR^3
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The projective point $[t:0:1]$ is, by definition, the one-dimensional vector subspace of $\RR^3$ spanned by $(t,0,1)$. At $t=0$ the family passes through the $z$-axis, and its derivative in the $u^1$ coordinate is the prescribed tangent vector.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

(b) Use the $G_{34}$ chart from Problem XII in which every plane has the unique normalized row-matrix representative
$$
M(U)=
\begin{pmatrix}
 u_1^1&u_1^2&1&0\\
 u_2^1&u_2^2&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Let $P_0=\operatorname{span}\{e_3,e_4\}$, whose chart coordinates are zero, and define
$$
\alpha(t)=
\varphi_{34}^{-1}
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\
0&t
\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The coordinate curve is
$$
(\varphi_{34}\circ\alpha)(t)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\
0&t
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its derivative at $t=0$ has only the $u_2^2$ component equal to $1$. Hence
$$
\alpha'(0)
=
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial u_2^2}\right|_{P_0}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

In terms of planes in $\RR^4$, the curve in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is
$$
\boxed{
P_t
=
\operatorname{span}\{(0,0,1,0),(0,t,0,1)\}
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substituting the coordinate matrix from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} into the normalized representative gives
$$
M(t)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&1&0\\
0&t&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The plane represented by a rank-two matrix is its row space, so its spanning vectors are precisely the two displayed rows.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives an explicit one-parameter family of lines representing $\partial/\partial u^1$, and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives an explicit one-parameter family of planes representing $\partial/\partial u_2^2$.

:::
