::: {.problem}
(a) Let
\[
\pi_P:S^2\longrightarrow\RP^2,
\qquad
\pi_P(x,y,z)=[x,y,z],
\]
be the natural projection.
Show that the open subsets of $\RP^2$ are precisely the images under $\pi_P$ of open subsets of $S^2$.

(b) Show that $\pi_P:S^2\to\RP^2$ is smooth and has rank $2$ everywhere.

(c) Let
\[
F:\RR^2\longrightarrow\RP^2,
\qquad F(x,y)=[x,y,1],
\]
and let $X=x\partial_x-y\partial_y$ on $\RR^2$.
Prove that there is a smooth vector field on $\RP^2$ that is $F$-related to $X$, and find its coordinate expressions in the standard charts on $\RP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

(a) If $U\subset S^2$ is open, then $\pi_P(U)$ is open in $\RP^2$.


::: pf-proof

The projective plane is the quotient of $S^2$ by the antipodal relation $p\sim -p$. Therefore
$$
\pi_P^{-1}(\pi_P(U))=U\cup(-U).
$$
The antipodal map is a homeomorphism, so $-U$ is open. Hence $U\cup(-U)$ is open in $S^2$. By the quotient topology, $\pi_P(U)$ is open in $\RP^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Conversely, every open subset of $\RP^2$ is the image under $\pi_P$ of an open subset of $S^2$.


::: pf-proof

If $V\subset\RP^2$ is open, then $\pi_P^{-1}(V)$ is open in $S^2$. Since $\pi_P$ is surjective,
$$
V=\pi_P(\pi_P^{-1}(V)).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

(b) Around every point of $S^2$, the map $\pi_P$ is a diffeomorphism onto an open subset of $\RP^2$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $p=(x,y,z)\in S^2$. Suppose first that $z\ne0$. On the hemisphere where the third coordinate has the same sign as $z$, the image is
$$
U_z=\{[X:Y:Z]:Z\ne0\}.
$$
With standard coordinates
$$
\psi_z([X:Y:Z])=\left(\frac XZ,\frac YZ\right),
$$
one has
$$
\psi_z\circ\pi_P(a,b,c)=\left(\frac ac,\frac bc\right).
$$
Its inverse is
$$
(u,v)\longmapsto
\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(z)}{\sqrt{1+u^2+v^2}}(u,v,1),
$$
which is smooth. The cases $x\ne0$ and $y\ne0$ are identical using the other standard charts.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore $\pi_P:S^2\to\RP^2$ is smooth and has rank $2$ everywhere.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $\pi_P$ is locally a diffeomorphism between $2$-manifolds. Thus its differential is an isomorphism at every point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

(c) Define
$$
\Phi_t([X:Y:Z])=[e^tX:e^{-t}Y:Z].
$$
This is a smooth one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms of $\RP^2$; let $Y$ be its infinitesimal generator.


::: pf-proof

The diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(e^t,e^{-t},1)$ is invertible and depends smoothly on $t$, so it induces the stated projective diffeomorphism. The infinitesimal generator
$$
Y_q=\left.\frac d{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Phi_t(q)
$$
is therefore a smooth vector field.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The vector field $Y$ is $F$-related to
$$
X=x\partial_x-y\partial_y.
$$


::: pf-proof

The flow of $X$ is
$$
\varphi_t(x,y)=(e^tx,e^{-t}y).
$$
Since $F(x,y)=[x:y:1]$,
$$
F(\varphi_t(x,y))=[e^tx:e^{-t}y:1]=\Phi_t(F(x,y)).
$$
Differentiating at $t=0$ gives $F_*X=Y\circ F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

In the standard chart
$$
U_z=\{[X:Y:Z]:Z\ne0\},
\qquad
(w^1,w^2)=\left(\frac XZ,\frac YZ\right),
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{Y=w^1\frac{\partial}{\partial w^1}-w^2\frac{\partial}{\partial w^2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

In these coordinates,
$$
\Phi_t(w^1,w^2)=(e^tw^1,e^{-t}w^2).
$$
Differentiating at $t=0$ gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

In the standard chart
$$
U_x=\{[X:Y:Z]:X\ne0\},
\qquad
(u^1,u^2)=\left(\frac YX,\frac ZX\right),
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{Y=-2u^1\frac{\partial}{\partial u^1}-u^2\frac{\partial}{\partial u^2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Under $\Phi_t$,
$$
u^1\longmapsto e^{-2t}u^1,
\qquad
u^2\longmapsto e^{-t}u^2.
$$
Differentiate at $t=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

In the standard chart
$$
U_y=\{[X:Y:Z]:Y\ne0\},
\qquad
(v^1,v^2)=\left(\frac XY,\frac ZY\right),
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{Y=2v^1\frac{\partial}{\partial v^1}+v^2\frac{\partial}{\partial v^2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Under $\Phi_t$,
$$
v^1\longmapsto e^{2t}v^1,
\qquad
v^2\longmapsto e^tv^2.
$$
Differentiate at $t=0$.

:::
