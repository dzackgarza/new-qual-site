::: {.problem}
Give Laplace's equation in polar coordinates.
In other words, if $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, write down a relation equivalent to
$$
\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}=0
$$
in terms of $r$, $\theta$, and the partial derivatives of $f$ with respect to $r$ and $\theta$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $f$ be of class $C^2$, and write $f_x,f_{xy},\ldots$ for partial derivatives in Cartesian coordinates.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #r}

$f_r=\cos\theta\,f_x+\sin\theta\,f_y$ and $f_{rr}=\cos^2\theta\,f_{xx}+2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,f_{xy}+\sin^2\theta\,f_{yy}$.

::: pf-proof

The chain rule with $x_r=\cos\theta$ and $y_r=\sin\theta$, applied twice. The coefficients $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$ do not depend on $r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #t}

$f_{\theta\theta}=r^2\bigl(\sin^2\theta\,f_{xx}-2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,f_{xy}+\cos^2\theta\,f_{yy}\bigr)-rf_r$.

::: pf-proof

With $x_\theta=-r\sin\theta$ and $y_\theta=r\cos\theta$, the chain rule gives $f_\theta=-r\sin\theta\,f_x+r\cos\theta\,f_y$. Differentiate again in $\theta$ with the product rule:
$$
f_{\theta\theta}=r^2\sin^2\theta\,f_{xx}-2r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,f_{xy}+r^2\cos^2\theta\,f_{yy}-r\cos\theta\,f_x-r\sin\theta\,f_y,
$$
and the last two terms are $-rf_r$ by Step [](#r){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sum}

For $r>0$, $f_{rr}+\dfrac1rf_r+\dfrac1{r^2}f_{\theta\theta}=f_{xx}+f_{yy}$.

::: pf-proof

Divide Step [](#t){.pf-ref} by $r^2$ and add Step [](#r){.pf-ref}. The $f_{xy}$ terms cancel, $\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$, and the $\frac1rf_r$ terms cancel.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By Step [](#sum){.pf-ref}, Laplace's equation in polar coordinates is
$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial r^2}+\frac1r\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}+\frac1{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial\theta^2}=0,\qquad r>0.}
$$

:::
