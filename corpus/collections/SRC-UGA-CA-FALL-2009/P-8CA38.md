::: {.problem}
Give the Laurent series for the following functions, and characterize their singularities at zero (essential, removable, or pole):

(a) $\dfrac{\sin^2 z}{z}$

(b) $ze^{-1/z^2}$

(c) $\dfrac{1}{z(4-z)}$
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a)** Using $\sin^2z=(1-\cos 2z)/2$,
\[
\frac{\sin^2z}{z}
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
(-1)^{k+1}\frac{2^{2k-1}}{(2k)!}z^{2k-1}
=z-\frac{z^3}{3}+\frac{2z^5}{45}-\cdots.
\]
There are no negative powers, so the singularity at $0$ is removable; the
holomorphic extension takes the value $0$ there.

**(b)**
\[
ze^{-1/z^2}
=z\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!z^{2k}}
=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}z^{1-2k}
=z-\frac1z+\frac1{2z^3}-\frac1{6z^5}+\cdots.
\]
The Laurent series has infinitely many negative-power terms, so $0$ is an
essential singularity.

**(c)** For $0<|z|<4$,
\[
\frac1{z(4-z)}
=\frac1{4z}\frac1{1-z/4}
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n-1}}{4^{n+1}}
=\frac1{4z}+\frac1{16}+\frac{z}{64}+\cdots.
\]
Thus $0$ is a simple pole.
:::
