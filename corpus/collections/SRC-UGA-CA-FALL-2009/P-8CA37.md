::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic on the unit disk.
Is it possible that $f\bigl(\frac{1}{n}\bigr)$ takes the following values for $n=2,3,4,\ldots$?
Why or why not?

(a) $(-1)^n$

(b) $e^{-n}$ for $n$ even, and $0$ for $n$ odd

(c) $\dfrac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor}$ (here $\lfloor x\rfloor$ is the greatest integer not greater than $x$)

(d) $\dfrac{n-2}{n-1}$
:::

::: {.solution}
Since $1/n\to0$, continuity and the identity theorem impose strong constraints.

**(a)** No. The values $(-1)^n$ do not converge as $n\to\infty$, whereas
continuity at $0$ would force
\[
f(1/n)\longrightarrow f(0).
\]

**(b)** No. For every odd $n$ we have $f(1/n)=0$, and those zeros accumulate
at the interior point $0$. By the identity theorem, $f\equiv0$, contradicting
$f(1/n)=e^{-n}\ne0$ for even $n$.

**(c)** No. The prescribed values tend to $0$, so necessarily $f(0)=0$. If
$f$ were not identically zero, let $m\ge1$ be the order of its zero at $0$.
Then
\[
f(z)=z^m g(z),\qquad g(0)\ne0,
\]
so
\[
n^m f(1/n)=g(1/n)\longrightarrow g(0).
\]
But the prescribed values give
\[
n^m f(1/n)=\frac{n^m}{\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
a contradiction.

**(d)** Yes. The function
\[
f(z)=\frac{1-2z}{1-z}
\]
is holomorphic on the unit disk and satisfies
\[
f(1/n)=\frac{1-2/n}{1-1/n}=\frac{n-2}{n-1}.
\]
:::
