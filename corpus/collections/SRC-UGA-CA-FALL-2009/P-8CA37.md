::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic on the unit disk.
Is it possible that $f\bigl(\frac{1}{n}\bigr)$ takes the following values for $n=2,3,4,\ldots$?
Why or why not?

(a) $(-1)^n$

(b) $e^{-n}$ for $n$ even, and $0$ for $n$ odd

(c) $\dfrac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor}$ (here $\lfloor x\rfloor$ is the greatest integer not greater than $x$)

(d) $\dfrac{n-2}{n-1}$
:::

::: {.solution}
This is the first problem of the University of Georgia complex-analysis qualifying examination of Fall 2009 (`assets/attachments/Complex_Analysis_Fall_2009.docx`). No published solution set is transcribed: the argument is the card's own, and it turns on continuity at $0$ and the identity theorem.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #parta}
(a) No: there is no $f$ analytic on $\DD$ with $f(1/n)=(-1)^{n}$ for all $n\geq2$.

::: pf-proof
$1/n\to0$ and $0\in\DD$, so continuity of $f$ at $0$ gives $f(1/n)\to f(0)$. The sequence $(-1)^{n}$ does not converge, so the values $f(1/n)$ do not converge, a contradiction.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #partb}
(b) No: there is no $f$ analytic on $\DD$ with $f(1/n)=e^{-n}$ for even $n$ and $f(1/n)=0$ for odd $n$.

::: pf-proof
The set $\theset{1/n\mid n\text{ odd}}$ has the limit point $0$ in the domain $\DD$, and $f$ vanishes on it, so the identity theorem gives $f\equiv0$ on $\DD$. But $f(1/n)=e^{-n}\neq0$ for even $n$, a contradiction.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #partc}
(c) No: there is no $f$ analytic on $\DD$ with $f(1/n)=1/\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor$ for all $n\geq2$.

::: pf-proof
The prescribed values tend to $0$, so continuity at $0$ forces $f(0)=0$. Were $f$ not identically zero, its zero at $0$ would have a finite order $m\geq1$, with $f(z)=z^{m}g(z)$ and $g(0)\neq0$; then
$$n^{m}f(1/n)=g(1/n)\longrightarrow g(0)\neq0,$$
by continuity of $g$ at $0$. But the prescribed values give $n^{m}f(1/n)=n^{m}/\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor$, which tends to $+\infty$ because $\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor\leq\sqrt{n}$ and $n^{m}/\sqrt{n}=n^{m-1/2}\to\infty$. This contradiction shows that $f\equiv0$, which does not take the prescribed values $1/\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$ either; so neither alternative is possible.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #partd}
(d) Yes: $f(z)=\frac{1-2z}{1-z}$ is analytic on $\DD$ and $f(1/n)=\frac{n-2}{n-1}$.

::: pf-proof
The denominator does not vanish on $\DD$, so $f$ is analytic there, and
$$f(1/n)=\frac{1-2/n}{1-1/n}=\frac{(n-2)/n}{(n-1)/n}=\frac{n-2}{n-1}$$
for every $n\geq2$.
:::
