::: {.problem}
Let $f\in L^1(0,\infty)$.
For $x>0$, define $$g(x)=\int_0^\infty f(t)e^{-tx}\,dt.$$ Prove that $g(x)$ is differentiable for $x>0$ with derivative $$g'(x)=\int_0^\infty -tf(t)e^{-tx}\,dt.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $x_0>0$.

<1>1. The proposed derivative is absolutely integrable at $x_0$.
::: {.proof}
For $t\ge0$, the function $t\mapsto t e^{-x_0t}$ is bounded; indeed its maximum is
\[
\frac1{e x_0}.
\]
Hence
\[
\int_0^\infty t|f(t)|e^{-x_0t}\,dt
\le
\frac1{e x_0}\int_0^\infty |f(t)|\,dt
<\infty.
\]
Therefore
\[
t\longmapsto -t f(t)e^{-x_0t}
\]
belongs to $L^1(0,\infty)$.
:::

<1>2. For every $t\ge0$, the difference quotient converges pointwise to the proposed derivative integrand.
::: {.proof}
For $h\ne0$ with $x_0+h>0$,
\[
\frac{f(t)e^{-t(x_0+h)}-f(t)e^{-tx_0}}{h}
=
f(t)e^{-tx_0}\frac{e^{-th}-1}{h}.
\]
Since
\[
\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^{-th}-1}{h}=-t,
\]
the quotient converges, for each fixed $t$, to
\[
-t f(t)e^{-tx_0}.
\]
:::

<1>3. The difference quotients admit an $L^1$ majorant independent of sufficiently small $h$.
::: {.proof}
Restrict to
\[
0<|h|<\frac{x_0}{2}.
\]
By the mean value theorem applied to $s\mapsto e^{-ts}$, there is a number $\theta$ between $0$ and $h$ such that
\[
\frac{e^{-th}-1}{h}=-t e^{-t\theta}.
\]
Thus
\[
\left|
f(t)e^{-tx_0}\frac{e^{-th}-1}{h}
\right|
=
t|f(t)|e^{-t(x_0+\theta)}.
\]
Because $|\theta|\le |h|<x_0/2$,
\[
x_0+\theta\ge \frac{x_0}{2},
\]
so
\[
\left|
f(t)e^{-tx_0}\frac{e^{-th}-1}{h}
\right|
\le
t|f(t)|e^{-tx_0/2}.
\]
The scalar factor $t e^{-tx_0/2}$ is bounded on $[0,\infty)$, with maximum
\[
\frac{2}{e x_0}.
\]
Consequently
\[
t|f(t)|e^{-tx_0/2}
\le
\frac{2}{e x_0}|f(t)|,
\]
and the right-hand side is integrable because $f\in L^1(0,\infty)$.
:::

<1>4. $g$ is differentiable at $x_0$ with the asserted derivative.
::: {.proof}
For $0<|h|<x_0/2$,
\[
\frac{g(x_0+h)-g(x_0)}{h}
=
\int_0^\infty
f(t)e^{-tx_0}\frac{e^{-th}-1}{h}\,dt.
\]
By <1>2 and <1>3, the dominated convergence theorem gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
g'(x_0)
&=
\lim_{h\to0}\frac{g(x_0+h)-g(x_0)}{h}\\
&=
\int_0^\infty
\lim_{h\to0}
f(t)e^{-tx_0}\frac{e^{-th}-1}{h}\,dt\\
&=
\int_0^\infty -t f(t)e^{-tx_0}\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $x_0>0$ was arbitrary, the formula holds for every $x>0$.
:::
