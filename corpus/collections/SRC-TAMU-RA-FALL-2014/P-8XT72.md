::: problem
For $n\in\mathbb{N}$, let $f_n:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuous, and assume that for every $x\in[0,1]$ the sequence $(f_n(x))$ is decreasing.
Suppose that $f_n$ converges pointwise to a continuous function $f$.
Show that this convergence is uniform.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $g_n = f_n - f$; then $g_n$ is continuous, $g_n \ge 0$, and $g_n \downarrow 0$ pointwise.
Proof: $f_n \downarrow f$ pointwise, so $g_n = f_n - f \ge 0$ and $g_n \downarrow 0$.

<1>2. It suffices to show $g_n \to 0$ uniformly.
Proof: uniform convergence of $f_n$ to $f$ is equivalent to uniform convergence of $g_n$ to $0$.

<1>3. Suppose for contradiction that $g_n$ does not converge uniformly to $0$.
Proof: assume the contrary.

<1>4. Then there is $\epsilon > 0$ and a sequence $x_{n_k}$ with $g_{n_k}(x_{n_k}) \ge \epsilon$.
Proof: negation of uniform convergence.

<1>5. Since $[0,1]$ is compact, $\{x_{n_k}\}$ has a convergent subsequence $x_{n_k} \to x_0 \in [0,1]$.
Proof: Bolzano–Weierstrass.

<1>6. For any fixed $m$, since $g_n \downarrow 0$ pointwise and $g_{n_k} \ge g_m$ for $n_k \ge m$ (monotone decreasing), we have $g_m(x_{n_k}) \ge g_{n_k}(x_{n_k}) \ge \epsilon$ for all $n_k \ge m$.
Proof: the sequence $g_n$ is decreasing pointwise, so $g_m \ge g_{n_k}$ when $m \le n_k$.

<1>7. Letting $k \to \infty$ and using continuity of $g_m$, we get $g_m(x_0) \ge \epsilon$ for all $m$.
Proof: <1>5 and <1>6.

<1>8. But $g_m(x_0) \to 0$ as $m \to \infty$ (pointwise convergence), contradicting $g_m(x_0) \ge \epsilon$ for all $m$.
Proof: <1>7 and pointwise convergence.

<1>9. Hence $g_n \to 0$ uniformly, so $f_n \to f$ uniformly.
Proof: <1>3–<1>8.

<1>10. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>9.
:::
