::: {.problem}
Let $C^1[0,1]$ be the space of functions $f\in C[0,1]$ such that $f'$ exists and is continuous on $[0,1]$.
The space $C^1[0,1]$ is given the supremum norm.
Define $T:C^1[0,1]\to C[0,1]$ by $Tf=f'$ for $f\in C^1[0,1]$.
Show that $T$ has a closed graph and that $T$ is not bounded.
Decide if $C^1[0,1]$ (together with the supremum norm) is a Banach space or not.
(Explain your answer.)
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. The graph of $T$ is closed in $C^1[0,1]\times C[0,1]$ when both spaces carry the supremum norm.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
f_n\longrightarrow f
\qquad\text{in }\|\cdot\|_\infty
\]
with $f_n,f\in C^1[0,1]$, and suppose also
\[
Tf_n=f_n'\longrightarrow g
\qquad\text{in }\|\cdot\|_\infty
\]
for some $g\in C[0,1]$.
For every $n$ and every $x\in[0,1]$, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
f_n(x)=f_n(0)+\int_0^x f_n'(t)\,dt.
\]
Because $f_n\to f$ uniformly,
\[
f_n(0)\longrightarrow f(0).
\]
Because $f_n'\to g$ uniformly, for each $x\in[0,1]$,
\[
\left|\int_0^x(f_n'(t)-g(t))\,dt\right|
\le x\,\|f_n'-g\|_\infty
\le\|f_n'-g\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.
\]
Taking limits in the identity for $f_n(x)$ yields
\[
f(x)=f(0)+\int_0^x g(t)\,dt
\qquad(0\le x\le1).
\]
Since $g$ is continuous, the fundamental theorem of calculus implies
\[
f'(x)=g(x)
\qquad(0\le x\le1).
\]
Thus $Tf=g$, so every convergent sequence in the graph has its limit in the graph.
Since these normed spaces are metric, the graph is closed.
:::

<1>2. $T$ is not bounded.
::: {.proof}
For $n\ge1$, let
\[
f_n(x)=x^n.
\]
Then
\[
\|f_n\|_\infty=1,
\]
whereas
\[
Tf_n(x)=n x^{n-1}
\]
and hence
\[
\|Tf_n\|_\infty=n.
\]
If $T$ were bounded, there would be a constant $C$ such that
\[
\|Tf\|_\infty\le C\|f\|_\infty
\qquad(f\in C^1[0,1]),
\]
which applied to $f_n$ would give $n\le C$ for every $n$, a contradiction.
:::

<1>3. $C^1[0,1]$ with the supremum norm is not a Banach space.
::: {.proof}
The codomain $C[0,1]$ with the supremum norm is Banach.
Suppose, for contradiction, that $C^1[0,1]$ with the same norm were also Banach.
Then $T:C^1[0,1]\to C[0,1]$ would be a linear operator between Banach spaces with closed graph by <1>1.
The closed graph theorem would therefore imply that $T$ is bounded.
This contradicts <1>2.
Hence $C^1[0,1]$ is not complete in the supremum norm.
:::
