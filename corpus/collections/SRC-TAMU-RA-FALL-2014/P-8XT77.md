::: {.problem}
(a) Define the $w^*$-topology on the dual $X^*$ of a Banach space $X$.

(b) Let $X$ be an infinite dimensional Banach space.
What is the $w^*$-closure of $$S_{X^*}=\{x^*\in X^*:\norm{x^*}=1\}?$$ (As usual, prove your answer.)
:::

::: {.solution}
We claim that $\overline{S_{X^*}}$ in the $w^*$ topology is $\text{Ball}_{X^*}$.
Indeed, at first, if $\norm{x^*}>1$, then there is a $x\in X$ such that $\norm{x}=1$ and $\abs{x^*(x)}>1$.
Then, there is an $\epsilon$ such that the nbhd of $x^*$, $A=\{y^*:\abs{x^*(x)-y^*(x)}<\epsilon\}$ does not intersect with $\text{Ball}_{X^*}$.
To see this, for all $y^*\in A$, $\abs{y^*(x)}>1$ and thus $\norm{y^*}>1$.

Then, fix a $x^*$ with $\norm{x^*}\le 1$.
Consider a general nbhd of $x^*$, say $O=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\{y^*:\abs{x^*(x_i)-y^*(x_i)}<\epsilon\}$.
Let $M=\text{span}\{x_i:i=1,\dots,n\}$.
To simplify the notation, we denote $\phi$ for $x^*$.
Let $H_\phi=\{f\in X^*:f\restrict_M=\phi\}$.
We claim that $H_\psi$ is weak*-closed, convex and nonempty set.

Indeed, any Hahn-Banach extension of $\phi$, say, $f$ with $\norm{f}=\norm{\psi}\le 1$ implies that $H_\phi$ is nonempty.
For all $f_1,f_2\in H_\phi$, $0<\lambda<1$, $\lambda f_1+(1-\lambda)f_2\restrict_M=\phi$ and $\norm{\lambda f_1+(1-\lambda)f_2}\le 1$.
Thus, $H_\phi$ is convex.
Now, let $f_\mu(x)\to f(x)$ for all $x\in X$, where $f_\mu\in H_\phi$.
Then since $f_\mu\restrict_M=\phi$, $f\restrict_M=\phi$.
In addition, for all $x$ is of norm 1, $\abs{f_\mu(x)}\le\norm{f_\mu}\norm{x}\le 1$.
This implies that $\abs{f(x)}\le 1$ and thus $\norm{f}\le 1$, which implies that $H_\phi$ is $w^*$-closed.

Then, by Krein-Milman theorem, $\text{Ext}(H_\phi)\ne\varnothing$.
Let $f\in\text{Ext}(H_\phi)$, we claim that $\norm{f}=1$.
Indeed, suppose $\norm{f}<1$.
Let $g$ be a linear functional, such that $g\restrict_M=0$ but $\norm{g}=1$.
Then define $f_1=f+(1-\norm{f})g$ and $f_2=f-(1-\norm{f})g$.
Then $f_1\restrict_M = f\restrict_M + (1-\norm{f})g\restrict_M = \phi$ since $g\restrict_M = 0$, and by the triangle inequality $\norm{f_1} \le \norm{f} + (1-\norm{f})\norm{g} = \norm{f} + (1-\norm{f}) = 1$; the same identities with the sign reversed give $f_2\restrict_M = \phi$ and $\norm{f_2} \le 1$.
However, $f=(f_1+f_2)/2$.
This contradicts to $f\in\text{Ext}(H_\phi)$.
Thus, $f\in S_{X^*}\cap O$.
We are done.
:::
