::: {.problem}
(a) Define the $w^*$-topology on the dual $X^*$ of a Banach space $X$.

(b) Let $X$ be an infinite dimensional Banach space.
What is the $w^*$-closure of $$S_{X^*}=\{x^*\in X^*:\norm{x^*}=1\}?$$ (As usual, prove your answer.)
:::

::: {.solution}
This is Problem 6 of the Texas A\&M real-analysis qualifying examination of Fall 2014 (`assets/attachments/a14.pdf`). No published solution set is transcribed: the argument is the card's own, by Krein--Milman in the $w^{*}$-topology, and it was rewritten in this pass from a rough transcription of the same argument.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #definition}
(a) The $w^{*}$-topology on $X^{*}$ is the topology of pointwise convergence on $X$: the initial topology for the maps $x^{*}\mapsto x^{*}(x)$ with $x\in X$, so that a local base at $x^{*}_{0}$ is formed by the sets
$$N(x^{*}_{0};x_{1},\dots,x_{n};\varepsilon)=\theset{x^{*}\in X^{*}\mid\abs{x^{*}(x_{i})-x^{*}_{0}(x_{i})}<\varepsilon,\ i=1,\dots,n}$$
with $x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in X$ and $\varepsilon>0$.

::: pf-proof
This is the definition: the $w^{*}$-topology is the weakest topology making every evaluation map $x^{*}\mapsto x^{*}(x)$ continuous, and the displayed family is closed under finite intersections and is a neighbourhood base at $x^{*}_{0}$ by the definition of an initial topology.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #outside}
(b) If $\norm{x^{*}}>1$ then $x^{*}$ is not in the $w^{*}$-closure of $S_{X^{*}}$.

::: pf-proof
Choose $x\in X$ with $\norm{x}=1$ and $\abs{x^{*}(x)}>1$, possible because $\norm{x^{*}}=\sup_{\norm{x}=1}\abs{x^{*}(x)}$, and put $\varepsilon=\abs{x^{*}(x)}-1>0$. The $w^{*}$-neighbourhood $N=\theset{y^{*}\in X^{*}\mid\abs{y^{*}(x)-x^{*}(x)}<\varepsilon}$ of $x^{*}$ contains no element of $S_{X^{*}}$: any $y^{*}\in N$ has $\abs{y^{*}(x)}>1$, hence $\norm{y^{*}}>1$. So $x^{*}$ is not a $w^{*}$-limit point of $S_{X^{*}}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #inside}
(b) Every $\varphi\in X^{*}$ with $\norm{\varphi}\leq1$ lies in the $w^{*}$-closure of $S_{X^{*}}$, so that closure is exactly $\theset{x^{*}\in X^{*}\mid\norm{x^{*}}\leq1}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $O=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\theset{y^{*}\in X^{*}\mid\abs{y^{*}(x_{i})-\varphi(x_{i})}<\varepsilon}$ be a basic $w^{*}$-neighbourhood of $\varphi$, and let $M=\operatorname{span}\theset{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}$, a finite dimensional subspace. Let
$$H=\theset{f\in X^{*}\mid f\restrictionof_{M}=\varphi\restrictionof_{M},\ \norm{f}\leq1}.$$
$H$ is nonempty by Hahn--Banach, since $\varphi\restrictionof_{M}$ has norm at most $\norm{\varphi}\leq1$ and extends to a functional of the same norm; it is convex, because both conditions defining it are convex; and it is $w^{*}$-closed and $w^{*}$-compact, being the intersection of the $w^{*}$-closed unit ball, compact by Banach--Alaoglu, with the $w^{*}$-closed set of functionals agreeing with $\varphi$ on the finite set $\theset{x_{i}}$. By the Krein--Milman theorem the nonempty convex compact set $H$ has an extreme point $f$.

We claim $\norm{f}=1$. If $\norm{f}<1$, then $M\neq X$, because $M$ is finite dimensional and $X$ is not, so there is $g\in X^{*}$ with $g\restrictionof_{M}=0$ and $\norm{g}=1$: take $g_{0}\in X^{*}$ vanishing on $M$ and not identically zero, which Hahn--Banach supplies since $M\neq X$, and scale it. Then $f_{1}=f+(1-\norm{f})g$ and $f_{2}=f-(1-\norm{f})g$ both restrict to $\varphi$ on $M$ and have norm at most $\norm{f}+(1-\norm{f})=1$, while $f=(f_{1}+f_{2})/2$ with $f_{1}\neq f_{2}$, contradicting the extremality of $f$. So $\norm{f}=1$, that is $f\in S_{X^{*}}$. Since $f\in H$ agrees with $\varphi$ on $M$, hence on each $x_{i}$, one has $f\in O$. Thus every basic neighbourhood of $\varphi$ meets $S_{X^{*}}$, and $\varphi$ lies in the $w^{*}$-closure by step [](#outside){.pf-ref}'s companion statement: no functional of norm greater than $1$ lies in that closure, so the closure is exactly $\theset{x^{*}\in X^{*}\mid\norm{x^{*}}\leq1}$.
:::
