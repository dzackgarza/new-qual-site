::: {.problem}
Let $\Sigma_{g, n}$ denote the compact oriented surface of genus $g$ with $n$ boundary components.

a. Show that $\Sigma_{0, 3}$ and $\Sigma_{1, 1}$ are both homotopy equivalent to $S^1 \vee S^1$.

b. Give a complete classification of pairs $(g, n)$ and $(g', n')$ for which $\Sigma_{g, n}$ is homotopy equivalent to $\Sigma_{g', n'}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $n>0$, then
\[
\Sigma_{g,n}\simeq\bigvee^{\,2g+n-1}S^1.
\]
::: {.proof}
A compact connected orientable surface with nonempty boundary has a handle decomposition with one $0$-handle, no $2$-handles, and
\[
2g+n-1
\]
$1$-handles.
Indeed, start with a disk and attach bands to obtain the standard genus-$g$ surface with $n$ boundary components.
If $k$ bands are attached, the resulting surface has Euler characteristic
\[
1-k.
\]
Since
\[
\chi(\Sigma_{g,n})=2-2g-n,
\]
one has
\[
k=1-(2-2g-n)=2g+n-1.
\]

Shrink the $0$-handle to a point and each $1$-handle to its core interval.
This gives a deformation retraction onto a graph with one vertex and $2g+n-1$ loop-edges, hence onto
\[
\bigvee^{\,2g+n-1}S^1.
\]
:::

<1>2. In particular,
\[
\Sigma_{0,3}\simeq S^1\vee S^1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Sigma_{1,1}\simeq S^1\vee S^1.
\]
::: {.proof}
Both surfaces have nonempty boundary, so <1>1 applies.
For the pair of pants,
\[
2g+n-1=0+3-1=2,
\]
while for the once-punctured torus,
\[
2g+n-1=2+1-1=2.
\]
Thus each deformation retracts onto a wedge of two circles.
This proves part (a).
:::

<1>3. If $n>0$ and $n'>0$, then
\[
\Sigma_{g,n}\simeq\Sigma_{g',n'}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2g+n=2g'+n'.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
\Sigma_{g,n}\simeq\bigvee^{\,r}S^1,
\qquad
r=2g+n-1,
\]
and similarly
\[
\Sigma_{g',n'}\simeq\bigvee^{\,r'}S^1,
\qquad
r'=2g'+n'-1.
\]
If $r=r'$, the two surfaces are homotopy equivalent by transitivity of homotopy equivalence.

Conversely, homotopy equivalent spaces have isomorphic first homology groups, while
\[
H_1\left(\bigvee^{\,r}S^1;\ZZ\right)\cong\ZZ^r.
\]
Hence a homotopy equivalence forces $r=r'$.
This is exactly
\[
2g+n=2g'+n'.
\]
:::

<1>4. A closed oriented surface is not homotopy equivalent to an oriented surface with nonempty boundary.
::: {.proof}
For a connected closed oriented surface,
\[
H_2(\Sigma_{g,0};\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\]
generated by its fundamental class.
If $n'>0$, then by <1>1 the surface $\Sigma_{g',n'}$ is homotopy equivalent to a graph, so
\[
H_2(\Sigma_{g',n'};\ZZ)=0.
\]
Since homotopy equivalence preserves homology, the two spaces cannot be homotopy equivalent.
The same argument applies with the two surfaces interchanged.
:::

<1>5. Two closed oriented surfaces are homotopy equivalent exactly when their genera are equal.
::: {.proof}
The standard CW structure on $\Sigma_{g,0}$ has one $0$-cell, $2g$ $1$-cells, and one $2$-cell whose attaching word is
\[
[a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_g,b_g].
\]
The cellular boundary $d_1$ is zero because there is one $0$-cell.
The cellular boundary $d_2$ is also zero: the exponent sum of every $1$-cell generator in the attaching word is zero.
Therefore
\[
H_1(\Sigma_{g,0};\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^{2g}.
\]
If
\[
\Sigma_{g,0}\simeq\Sigma_{g',0},
\]
then their first homology groups are isomorphic, so
\[
2g=2g'
\]
and hence $g=g'$.
Conversely, if $g=g'$, the two surfaces have the same topological type and are homeomorphic, hence homotopy equivalent.
:::

<1>6. Therefore the complete classification is
\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_{g,n}\simeq\Sigma_{g',n'}
\iff
\begin{cases}
n=n'=0\text{ and }g=g',&\text{or}\\
n>0,\ n'>0\text{ and }2g+n=2g'+n'.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
The nonempty-boundary case is <1>3, the mixed closed/boundary case is excluded by <1>4, and the closed case is <1>5.
:::
:::
