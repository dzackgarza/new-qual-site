::: {.problem}
Identify five mutually non-homeomorphic connected spaces $X$ for which there is a covering map $p:X\to K$ where $K$ is the Klein bottle.
Give an example of the covering in each case.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K$ be represented as the orbit space of the standard Klein-bottle action on $\RR^2$.

<1>1. Define homeomorphisms $u,v:\RR^2\to\RR^2$ by
\[
u(x,y)=(x+1,y),
\qquad
v(x,y)=(-x,y+1),
\]
and let
\[
G=\langle u,v\rangle.
\]
Then
\[
K\cong\RR^2/G.
\]
::: {.proof}
The transformation $u$ identifies the two vertical sides of a unit-width strip by translation, while $v$ identifies horizontal sides with a reflection.
Thus a fundamental square has the usual Klein-bottle edge identifications.
The action is free and properly discontinuous, so the orbit projection
\[
q:\RR^2\longrightarrow\RR^2/G\cong K
\]
is the universal covering map.
Also
\[
v^2(x,y)=(x,y+2).
\]
:::

<1>2. Every subgroup $H\le G$ gives a connected covering
\[
p_H:\RR^2/H\longrightarrow\RR^2/G\cong K,
\qquad
p_H([z]_H)=[z]_G.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because the $G$-action is a covering-space action, its restriction to every subgroup $H$ is also a covering-space action.
The quotient map $q$ factors as
\[
\RR^2\longrightarrow\RR^2/H\xrightarrow{p_H}\RR^2/G.
\]
An evenly covered neighborhood for the $G$-action is also evenly covered by this intermediate quotient, so $p_H$ is a covering map.
Since $\RR^2$ is connected and the quotient map onto $\RR^2/H$ is continuous and surjective, $\RR^2/H$ is connected.
:::

<1>3. The choice $H_1=G$ gives the Klein bottle itself.
::: {.proof}
Here
\[
\RR^2/H_1=\RR^2/G\cong K,
\]
and $p_{H_1}$ is the identity covering of $K$.
:::

<1>4. The subgroup
\[
H_2=\langle u,v^2\rangle
\]
gives a torus cover.
::: {.proof}
The generators act by the independent translations
\[
u(x,y)=(x+1,y),
\qquad
v^2(x,y)=(x,y+2).
\]
Hence
\[
\RR^2/H_2\cong\RR^2/(\ZZ(1,0)\oplus\ZZ(0,2))\cong T^2.
\]
The subgroup $H_2$ has index two in $G$, so this is the standard two-sheeted orientation cover
\[
T^2\longrightarrow K.
\]
:::

<1>5. The subgroup
\[
H_3=\langle u\rangle
\]
gives the open cylinder.
::: {.proof}
Only the first coordinate is identified by integer translation, so
\[
\RR^2/H_3
\cong
(\RR/\ZZ)\times\RR
\cong
S^1\times\RR.
\]
Thus <1>2 gives a connected covering
\[
S^1\times\RR\longrightarrow K.
\]
:::

<1>6. The subgroup
\[
H_4=\langle v\rangle
\]
gives the open Möbius band.
::: {.proof}
A fundamental region for the cyclic action generated by $v$ is the strip
\[
\RR\times[0,1]
\]
with boundary identification
\[
(x,0)\sim(-x,1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\RR^2/H_4
\cong
\bigl(\RR\times[0,1]\bigr)/((x,0)\sim(-x,1)),
\]
the total space of the nontrivial real line bundle over $S^1$.
This is homeomorphic to the interior $M^\circ$ of a Möbius band.
Indeed, any odd homeomorphism $\RR\to(-1,1)$ intertwines $x\mapsto-x$ on the two fibers, and therefore descends to a homeomorphism with
\[
\bigl((-1,1)\times[0,1]\bigr)/((x,0)\sim(-x,1)),
\]
which is the interior of the usual compact Möbius band.
Hence <1>2 gives a connected covering
\[
M^\circ\longrightarrow K.
\]
:::

<1>7. The trivial subgroup
\[
H_5=\{e\}
\]
gives the plane.
::: {.proof}
We have
\[
\RR^2/H_5=\RR^2,
\]
and the corresponding map is the universal covering
\[
\RR^2\longrightarrow K.
\]
:::

<1>8. The five total spaces are pairwise non-homeomorphic.
::: {.proof}
They are
\[
K,
\qquad
T^2,
\qquad
S^1\times\RR,
\qquad
M^\circ,
\qquad
\RR^2.
\]
The first two are compact and the last three are noncompact.
Among the compact pair, $T^2$ is orientable while $K$ is nonorientable, so they are not homeomorphic.

Among the noncompact three,
\[
\pi_1(\RR^2)=0,
\qquad
\pi_1(S^1\times\RR)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
\pi_1(M^\circ)\cong\ZZ.
\]
The last isomorphism follows by contracting each fiber of the Möbius line bundle to its zero section, so $M^\circ$ deformation retracts onto $S^1$.
Thus the plane is not homeomorphic to either of the other two.
Finally, $S^1\times\RR$ is orientable, whereas the fiber-reversing gluing $(x,0)\sim(-x,1)$ makes $M^\circ$ nonorientable.
Thus those two are not homeomorphic.
Hence all five covering spaces are mutually non-homeomorphic.
:::
