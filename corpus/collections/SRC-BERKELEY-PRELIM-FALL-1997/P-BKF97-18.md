::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group such that for every positive integer $n$ the set
\[
\{x\in G:x^n=1\}
\]
has at most $n$ elements.
Prove that $G$ is cyclic, and deduce that the multiplicative group of every finite field is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
e
\coloneqq
\operatorname{lcm}\{\operatorname{ord}(x):x\in G\}
$$
be the exponent of $G$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #every-element-satisfies-exponent}
Every element of $G$ satisfies
$$
x^e=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
For each $x\in G$, its order divides the least common multiple $e$.
Therefore $x^e=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #group-order-at-most-exponent}
One has
$$
\abs G\leq e.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#every-element-satisfies-exponent){.pf-ref},
$$
G
=
\{x\in G:x^e=1\}.
$$
Apply the hypothesis with $n=e$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #exponent-divides-group-order}
One has
$$
e\mid\abs G.
$$

::: pf-proof
By Lagrange's theorem, the order of every element of $G$ divides
$\abs G$. The least common multiple of a collection of divisors of
$\abs G$ also divides $\abs G$. That least common multiple is $e$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #element-of-order-exponent}
The group $G$ contains an element of order $e$.

::: pf-proof
Write
$$
e=\prod_p p^{a_p}.
$$
For every prime $p$ with $a_p>0$, the definition of the least common
multiple gives an element $g_p\in G$ whose order is divisible by
$p^{a_p}$. Write
$$
\operatorname{ord}(g_p)
=
p^{a_p}m_p
$$
with $p\nmid m_p$, and define
$$
h_p\coloneqq g_p^{m_p}.
$$
Then $h_p$ has order $p^{a_p}$.

Because $G$ is abelian, the elements $h_p$ commute. Their orders are
pairwise coprime, so
$$
h\coloneqq\prod_p h_p
$$
has order
$$
\prod_p p^{a_p}
=
e.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #exponent-equals-group-order}
One has
$$
e=\abs G.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#group-order-at-most-exponent){.pf-ref} gives $\abs G\leq e$. Step [](#exponent-divides-group-order){.pf-ref} gives
$$
e\leq\abs G.
$$
Hence equality holds.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #G-is-cyclic}
The group $G$ is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
Let $h$ be the element from step [](#element-of-order-exponent){.pf-ref}. By step [](#exponent-equals-group-order){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{ord}(h)
=
e
=
\abs G.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{\langle h\rangle}
=
\abs G,
$$
so $\langle h\rangle=G$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #finite-field-root-count}
Let $\FF_q$ be a finite field. For every positive integer $n$,
the equation
$$
x^n=1
$$
has at most $n$ solutions in $\FF_q^\times$.

::: pf-proof
Its solutions are roots of the nonzero polynomial
$$
X^n-1\in\FF_q[X],
$$
which has degree $n$. A nonzero polynomial over a field has at most as many
roots as its degree.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #finite-field-mult-group-cyclic}
The multiplicative group of every finite field is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
The group
$$
\FF_q^\times
$$
is finite and abelian. Step [](#finite-field-root-count){.pf-ref} shows that it satisfies the root-count
hypothesis of the problem. Apply step [](#G-is-cyclic){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#G-is-cyclic){.pf-ref} proves the first assertion, and step [](#finite-field-mult-group-cyclic){.pf-ref} proves the finite-field
consequence.
:::
