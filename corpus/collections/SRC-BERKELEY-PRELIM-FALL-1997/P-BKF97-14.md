::: {.problem}
Let $k\ge0$.
Evaluate
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(kx)}{1+x+x^2}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
a\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
$$

<1>1. Under the substitution
$$
y=x+\frac12,
$$
one has
$$
1+x+x^2
=
y^2+a^2
$$
and
$$
\cos(kx)
=
\cos(ky)\cos\frac{k}{2}
+
\sin(ky)\sin\frac{k}{2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Completing the square gives
$$
x^2+x+1
=
\left(x+\frac12\right)^2+\frac34
=
y^2+a^2.
$$
Also
$$
kx
=
ky-\frac{k}{2},
$$
so the cosine subtraction formula gives the second identity.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin(ky)}{y^2+a^2}\,dy
=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The denominator is even in $y$, while $\sin(ky)$ is odd. The integrand is
therefore odd and absolutely integrable, so its integral over the real line
vanishes.
:::

<1>3. For $k>0$,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{e^{iky}}{y^2+a^2}\,dy
=
\frac{\pi}{a}e^{-ak}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Integrate
$$
F(z)
\coloneqq
\frac{e^{ikz}}{z^2+a^2}
$$
over the upper semicircle of radius $R$. The only pole in the upper
half-plane is $z=ia$, and it is simple, with residue
$$
\Res_{z=ia}F
=
\frac{e^{ik(ia)}}{2ia}
=
\frac{e^{-ak}}{2ia}.
$$
On the upper semicircle,
$$
\abs{e^{ikz}}
=
e^{-k\Im z}
\leq1.
$$
Jordan's lemma, applied to the rational factor
$1/(z^2+a^2)$, makes the arc integral tend to zero as $R\to\infty$.
The residue theorem therefore gives
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{e^{iky}}{y^2+a^2}\,dy
=
2\pi i
\frac{e^{-ak}}{2ia}
=
\frac{\pi}{a}e^{-ak}.
$$
:::

<1>4. For every $k\geq0$,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\cos(ky)}{y^2+a^2}\,dy
=
\frac{\pi}{a}e^{-ak}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $k>0$, take real parts in step <1>3. For $k=0$,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{dy}{y^2+a^2}
=
\frac1a
\left[
\arctan\frac{y}{a}
\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}
=
\frac{\pi}{a},
$$
which is the same formula with $e^{-ak}=1$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\cos(kx)}{1+x+x^2}\,dx
=
\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}
e^{-\sqrt3 k/2}
\cos\frac{k}{2}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1 and <1>2, the original integral is
$$
\cos\frac{k}{2}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\cos(ky)}{y^2+a^2}\,dy.
$$
Apply step <1>4 and substitute
$$
a=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the requested value for every $k\geq0$.
:::
