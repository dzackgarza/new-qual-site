::: {.problem}
A map $f:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n$ is **proper** if it is continuous and $f^{-1}(B)$ is compact for every compact subset $B\subseteq\mathbb R^n$.
It is **closed** if it is continuous and $f(A)$ is closed for every closed subset $A\subseteq\mathbb R^m$.

1. Prove that every proper map $f:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n$ is closed.

2. Prove that every one-to-one closed map $f:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n$ is proper.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #image-closed-iff-sequentially-closed}
Let
$$
f:\RR^m\longrightarrow\RR^n
$$
be proper, and let $A\subseteq\RR^m$ be closed. To prove that $f(A)$ is
closed, it is enough to prove that it contains the limit of every convergent
sequence of its points.

::: pf-proof
Euclidean space is a metric space, and in a metric space a set is closed
if and only if it is sequentially closed.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #compact-set-containing-sequence}
Suppose
$$
y_j\in f(A)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y_j\longrightarrow y.
$$
Choose
$$
x_j\in A
$$
with
$$
f(x_j)=y_j.
$$
Then the set
$$
K\coloneqq\{y\}\cup\{y_j:j\geq1\}
$$
is compact.

::: pf-proof
A convergent sequence together with its limit is compact in $\RR^n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subsequence-converges-in-A}
Some subsequence of $(x_j)$ converges to a point
$$
x\in A.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#compact-set-containing-sequence){.pf-ref},
$$
x_j\in f^{-1}(K)
$$
for every $j$. Properness makes $f^{-1}(K)$ compact, so $(x_j)$ has a
convergent subsequence
$$
x_{j_\ell}\longrightarrow x\in f^{-1}(K).
$$
Since every $x_{j_\ell}$ belongs to the closed set $A$, its limit $x$ also
belongs to $A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #limit-in-image}
One has
$$
y\in f(A).
$$

::: pf-proof
Continuity of a proper map is part of the definition. Hence step [](#subsequence-converges-in-A){.pf-ref} gives
$$
f(x)
=
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}f(x_{j_\ell})
=
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}y_{j_\ell}
=
y.
$$
Since $x\in A$, this gives $y\in f(A)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #proper-implies-closed}
Every proper map
$$
f:\RR^m\to\RR^n
$$
is closed.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#image-closed-iff-sequentially-closed){.pf-ref}, [](#compact-set-containing-sequence){.pf-ref}, [](#subsequence-converges-in-A){.pf-ref} and [](#limit-in-image){.pf-ref} show that the image of every closed set is closed.
Continuity is already included in the definition of properness.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #preimage-of-compact-closed}
Now suppose
$$
f:\RR^m\longrightarrow\RR^n
$$
is one-to-one and closed. For every compact set
$$
K\subseteq\RR^n,
$$
the set $f^{-1}(K)$ is closed.

::: pf-proof
A closed map is continuous by the definition in the problem. Since compact
sets in Euclidean space are closed,
$$
f^{-1}(K)
$$
is closed by continuity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #preimage-of-compact-bounded}
The set $f^{-1}(K)$ is bounded.

::: pf-proof
Suppose instead that $f^{-1}(K)$ is unbounded. Choose a sequence
$$
x_j\in f^{-1}(K)
$$
such that
$$
\norm{x_j}\geq j.
$$
Since
$$
f(x_j)\in K
$$
and $K$ is compact, after passing to a subsequence there is
$$
y\in K
$$
such that
$$
f(x_j)\longrightarrow y.
$$

At most one term of the sequence can satisfy $f(x_j)=y$, because $f$ is
one-to-one. Discard that term if it occurs, and still denote the remaining
sequence by $(x_j)$. Then
$$
f(x_j)\neq y
$$
for every $j$, while $f(x_j)\to y$.

The set
$$
A\coloneqq\{x_j:j\geq1\}
$$
is closed in $\RR^m$: every bounded subset of $\RR^m$ contains only
finitely many of the $x_j$, because $\norm{x_j}\geq j$, so $A$ has no
finite accumulation point outside itself. Since $f$ is closed,
$$
f(A)
$$
must be closed. But
$$
y\in\overline{f(A)}
$$
because $f(x_j)\to y$, while
$$
y\notin f(A)
$$
by construction. This is a contradiction.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #preimage-of-compact-compact}
The set $f^{-1}(K)$ is compact.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#preimage-of-compact-closed){.pf-ref} it is closed, and by step [](#preimage-of-compact-bounded){.pf-ref} it is bounded. The
Heine--Borel theorem therefore makes it compact in $\RR^m$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #injective-closed-implies-proper}
Every one-to-one closed map
$$
f:\RR^m\to\RR^n
$$
is proper.

::: pf-proof
Continuity is part of the definition of a closed map in the problem, and
step [](#preimage-of-compact-compact){.pf-ref} shows that the preimage of every compact subset of $\RR^n$ is
compact.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#proper-implies-closed){.pf-ref} proves part 1, and step [](#injective-closed-implies-proper){.pf-ref} proves part 2.
:::
