::: {.problem}
Conformally map the region inside the disk
\[
\{z\in\mathbb C:|z-1|\le1\}
\]
and outside the disk
\[
\left\{z\in\mathbb C:\left|z-\frac12\right|\le\frac12\right\}
\]
onto the upper half-plane.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\Omega
\coloneqq
\left\{
z\in\CC:
\abs{z-1}<1,
\quad
\abs{z-\frac12}>\frac12
\right\}
$$
be the interior of the stated region.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #inversion-of-larger-circle}
Under the inversion
$$
w=\frac1z,
$$
the circle
$$
\abs{z-1}=1
$$
is mapped to the line
$$
\Re w=\frac12.
$$

::: pf-proof
The circle equation is
$$
\abs z^2=z+\overline z.
$$
For $z\neq0$, divide by $\abs z^2$:
$$
1
=
\frac1z+\frac1{\overline z}
=
w+\overline w
=
2\Re w.
$$
Hence its image is the stated vertical line.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inversion-of-smaller-circle}
Under the same inversion, the circle
$$
\abs{z-\frac12}=\frac12
$$
is mapped to the line
$$
\Re w=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
The circle equation is
$$
\abs z^2
=
\frac{z+\overline z}{2}.
$$
Dividing by $\abs z^2$ gives
$$
1
=
\frac12
\left(
\frac1z+\frac1{\overline z}
\right)
=
\Re w.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inversion-maps-lune-to-strip}
The inversion maps $\Omega$ biholomorphically onto the vertical
strip
$$
S
\coloneqq
\left\{
w\in\CC:
\frac12<\Re w<1
\right\}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The point $0$ lies only on the boundary of the two disks, so inversion is
holomorphic and one-to-one throughout $\Omega$.

For the larger disk,
$$
\abs{z-1}<1
$$
is equivalent to
$$
\abs z^2<z+\overline z.
$$
After division by $\abs z^2$ this becomes
$$
1<2\Re w,
$$
or
$$
\Re w>\frac12.
$$

For the smaller disk,
$$
\abs{z-\frac12}>\frac12
$$
is equivalent to
$$
\abs z^2>\frac{z+\overline z}{2}.
$$
After division by $\abs z^2$ this becomes
$$
1>\Re w.
$$
Thus the two inequalities are exactly
$$
\frac12<\Re w<1.
$$
The inverse map is $z=1/w$, so the correspondence is biholomorphic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #exp-map-into-upper-half-plane}
The function
$$
E(w)
\coloneqq
\exp\left(
2\pi i\left(w-\frac12\right)
\right)
$$
maps $S$ into the upper half-plane.

::: pf-proof
Write
$$
w=u+iv.
$$
For $w\in S$,
$$
0<u-\frac12<\frac12,
$$
so the argument of $E(w)$ is
$$
2\pi\left(u-\frac12\right)\in(0,\pi).
$$
Therefore
$$
\Im E(w)>0.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #exp-map-strip-to-upper-half-plane}
The map
$$
E:S\longrightarrow\{\,\zeta\in\CC:\Im\zeta>0\,\}
$$
is one-to-one and onto.

::: pf-proof
If
$$
E(w_1)=E(w_2),
$$
then
$$
w_1-w_2\in\ZZ.
$$
But both real parts lie in the interval $(1/2,1)$, so
$$
\Re(w_1-w_2)\in(-1/2,1/2).
$$
The only integer in this interval is $0$, hence $w_1=w_2$.

Conversely, let
$$
\zeta=re^{i\theta}
$$
with
$$
r>0,
\qquad
0<\theta<\pi.
$$
Set
$$
w
=
\frac12+\frac{\theta}{2\pi}
-\frac{i}{2\pi}\log r.
$$
Then
$$
\frac12<\Re w<1
$$
and direct substitution gives
$$
E(w)=\zeta.
$$
Thus $E$ is bijective. Its derivative never vanishes, so it is a
biholomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #composed-conformal-map}
A conformal map from the lune onto the upper half-plane is
$$
\boxed{
\Phi(z)
=
\exp\left(
2\pi i\left(\frac1z-\frac12\right)
\right)
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#inversion-maps-lune-to-strip){.pf-ref}, inversion maps $\Omega$ biholomorphically onto $S$, and by
step [](#exp-map-strip-to-upper-half-plane){.pf-ref}, $E$ maps $S$ biholomorphically onto the upper half-plane.
Their composition is exactly the displayed function.
:::
