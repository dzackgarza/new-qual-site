::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a $2\times2$ matrix over the integers.
Suppose
\[
A^n=I
\]
for some positive integer $n$.
Prove that
\[
A^{12}=I.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #det-is-plus-minus-one}
The matrix $A$ is invertible and
$$
\det A\in\{1,-1\}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The equation
$$
A^n=I
$$
shows directly that $A$ is invertible. Taking determinants gives
$$
(\det A)^n=1.
$$
Since $A$ has integer entries,
$$
\det A\in\ZZ.
$$
The only integer real roots of unity are $1$ and $-1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #A-diagonalizable}
The matrix $A$ is diagonalizable over $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
The minimal polynomial $m_A(t)$ divides
$$
t^n-1.
$$
Over characteristic zero,
$$
\gcd(t^n-1,nt^{n-1})=1,
$$
so $t^n-1$ has no repeated roots. Hence $m_A$ splits over $\CC$ into
distinct linear factors. Therefore $A$ is diagonalizable over $\CC$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eigenvalue-modulus-one}
Every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $A$ is a root of unity, so
$$
\abs\lambda=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
If
$$
Av=\lambda v
$$
with $v\neq0$, then
$$
v
=
A^nv
=
\lambda^n v.
$$
Thus $\lambda^n=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #det-minus-one-eigenvalues}
If
$$
\det A=-1,
$$
then the two eigenvalues of $A$ are $1$ and $-1$.

::: pf-proof
The characteristic polynomial has real coefficients. If its two
eigenvalues were nonreal, they would be complex conjugates. By step [](#eigenvalue-modulus-one){.pf-ref},
their product would then be
$$
\lambda\overline\lambda
=
\abs\lambda^2
=1,
$$
contradicting $\det A=-1$.

Hence both eigenvalues are real. The only real roots of unity are $\pm1$,
and their product is $-1$, so the eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #det-one-eigenvalue-orders}
If
$$
\det A=1,
$$
then every eigenvalue has order among
$$
1,2,3,4,6.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let the eigenvalues be $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$. Since their product is $1$,
$$
\lambda_2=\lambda_1^{-1}.
$$
By step [](#eigenvalue-modulus-one){.pf-ref},
$$
\lambda_1^{-1}=\overline{\lambda_1}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{tr}A
=
\lambda_1+\lambda_1^{-1}
=
2\Re\lambda_1.
$$
The trace is an integer because $A$ has integer entries, while
$$
-2\leq2\Re\lambda_1\leq2.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{tr}A
\in
\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}.
$$

The characteristic polynomial is
$$
t^2-(\operatorname{tr}A)t+1.
$$
For the five possible traces, its roots are respectively:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\operatorname{tr}A&\text{roots}\\
\hline
2&1,1\\
-2&-1,-1\\
1&e^{i\pi/3},e^{-i\pi/3}\\
-1&e^{2\pi i/3},e^{-2\pi i/3}\\
0&i,-i.
\end{array}
$$
Their orders are respectively $1,2,6,3,4$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eigenvalue-twelfth-power-one}
Every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $A$ satisfies
$$
\lambda^{12}=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
If $\det A=-1$, step [](#det-minus-one-eigenvalues){.pf-ref} gives eigenvalue orders at most $2$. If
$\det A=1$, step [](#det-one-eigenvalue-orders){.pf-ref} gives orders among $1,2,3,4,6$. Each of these orders
divides $12$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #A-twelfth-power-identity}
One has
$$
\boxed{A^{12}=I}.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#A-diagonalizable){.pf-ref}, choose an invertible complex matrix $P$ such that
$$
P^{-1}AP
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda_1&0\\
0&\lambda_2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Step [](#eigenvalue-twelfth-power-one){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\lambda_1^{12}
=
\lambda_2^{12}
=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
P^{-1}A^{12}P
=
I,
$$
so $A^{12}=I$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#A-twelfth-power-identity){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
:::
