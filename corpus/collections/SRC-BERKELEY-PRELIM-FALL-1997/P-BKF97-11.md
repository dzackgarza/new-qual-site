::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be twice differentiable and suppose
\[
|f(x)|\le1,
\qquad
|f''(x)|\le1
\]
for every $x\in\mathbb R$.
Prove that
\[
|f'(x)|\le2
\]
for every $x\in\mathbb R$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix
$$
x\in\RR.
$$

<1>1. If
$$
f'(x)=0,
$$
then the desired inequality holds.

::: {.proof}
In this case
$$
\abs{f'(x)}=0\leq2.
$$
:::

<1>2. Suppose
$$
f'(x)\neq0.
$$
Set
$$
s\coloneqq\frac{f'(x)}{\abs{f'(x)}}\in\{1,-1\}
$$
and
$$
h\coloneqq2s.
$$
Then there is a point $\xi$ between $x$ and $x+h$ such that
$$
f(x+h)-f(x)
=
2\abs{f'(x)}
+2f''(\xi).
$$

::: {.proof}
Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder gives
$$
f(x+h)
=
f(x)+hf'(x)+\frac{h^2}{2}f''(\xi)
$$
for some $\xi$ between $x$ and $x+h$. Since
$$
h=2s,
\qquad
sf'(x)=\abs{f'(x)},
\qquad
\frac{h^2}{2}=2,
$$
the displayed identity follows.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
f(x+h)-f(x)\leq2.
$$

::: {.proof}
The hypothesis $\abs{f(t)}\leq1$ gives
$$
f(x+h)\leq1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f(x)\geq-1.
$$
Subtracting gives the claim.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
f(x+h)-f(x)
\geq
2\abs{f'(x)}-2.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
f(x+h)-f(x)
=
2\abs{f'(x)}
+2f''(\xi).
$$
The hypothesis $\abs{f''(\xi)}\leq1$ gives
$$
f''(\xi)\geq-1.
$$
Substitution yields the displayed lower bound.
:::

<1>5. One has
$$
\abs{f'(x)}\leq2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>3 and <1>4:
$$
2\abs{f'(x)}-2
\leq
2.
$$
Hence
$$
2\abs{f'(x)}\leq4,
$$
so $\abs{f'(x)}\leq2$.
:::

<1>6. The inequality holds for every real $x$.

::: {.proof}
The point $x$ was arbitrary. Step <1>1 handles the case $f'(x)=0$, and
step <1>5 handles the case $f'(x)\neq0$.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required conclusion.
:::
