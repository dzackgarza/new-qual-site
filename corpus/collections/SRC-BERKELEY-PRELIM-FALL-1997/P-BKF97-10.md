::: {.problem}
Prove that for every $x>0$,
\[
\sin x>x-\frac{x^3}{6}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
g(x)
\coloneqq
\sin x-x+\frac{x^3}{6}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #initial-derivatives-vanish}
One has
$$
g(0)=g'(0)=g''(0)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Differentiate:
$$
g'(x)
=
\cos x-1+\frac{x^2}{2},
$$
and
$$
g''(x)
=
-\sin x+x.
$$
Substitution of $x=0$ gives all three equalities.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #third-derivative-nonneg}
For every real $x$,
$$
g'''(x)
=
1-\cos x
\geq0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Differentiate the formula for $g''$ from step [](#initial-derivatives-vanish){.pf-ref}:
$$
g'''(x)=1-\cos x.
$$
Since $\cos x\leq1$, the derivative is nonnegative.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #second-derivative-positive}
For every $x>0$,
$$
g''(x)>0.
$$

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#initial-derivatives-vanish){.pf-ref} and [](#third-derivative-nonneg){.pf-ref},
$$
g''(x)
=
\int_0^x(1-\cos t)\,dt.
$$
The integrand is continuous and nonnegative. It is not identically zero on
any interval $(0,x)$, because $\cos t=1$ only at isolated points
$2\pi\ZZ$. Hence the integral is strictly positive.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #first-derivative-positive}
For every $x>0$,
$$
g'(x)>0.
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#initial-derivatives-vanish){.pf-ref},
$$
g'(x)
=
\int_0^x g''(t)\,dt.
$$
Step [](#second-derivative-positive){.pf-ref} makes the integrand strictly positive for every $t>0$, so the
integral is positive.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #g-positive}
For every $x>0$,
$$
g(x)>0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Again by step [](#initial-derivatives-vanish){.pf-ref},
$$
g(x)
=
\int_0^x g'(t)\,dt.
$$
Step [](#first-derivative-positive){.pf-ref} makes the integrand strictly positive for every $t>0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boxed-inequality}
Therefore, for every $x>0$,
$$
\boxed{
\sin x>x-\frac{x^3}{6}
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#g-positive){.pf-ref} says
$$
\sin x-x+\frac{x^3}{6}>0.
$$
Rearrange.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#boxed-inequality){.pf-ref} is the required inequality.
:::
