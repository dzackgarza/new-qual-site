::: {.problem}
Prove that for every $x>0$,
\[
\sin x>x-\frac{x^3}{6}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
g(x)
\coloneqq
\sin x-x+\frac{x^3}{6}.
$$

<1>1. One has
$$
g(0)=g'(0)=g''(0)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Differentiate:
$$
g'(x)
=
\cos x-1+\frac{x^2}{2},
$$
and
$$
g''(x)
=
-\sin x+x.
$$
Substitution of $x=0$ gives all three equalities.
:::

<1>2. For every real $x$,
$$
g'''(x)
=
1-\cos x
\geq0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Differentiate the formula for $g''$ from step <1>1:
$$
g'''(x)=1-\cos x.
$$
Since $\cos x\leq1$, the derivative is nonnegative.
:::

<1>3. For every $x>0$,
$$
g''(x)>0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1 and <1>2,
$$
g''(x)
=
\int_0^x(1-\cos t)\,dt.
$$
The integrand is continuous and nonnegative. It is not identically zero on
any interval $(0,x)$, because $\cos t=1$ only at isolated points
$2\pi\ZZ$. Hence the integral is strictly positive.
:::

<1>4. For every $x>0$,
$$
g'(x)>0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
g'(x)
=
\int_0^x g''(t)\,dt.
$$
Step <1>3 makes the integrand strictly positive for every $t>0$, so the
integral is positive.
:::

<1>5. For every $x>0$,
$$
g(x)>0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Again by step <1>1,
$$
g(x)
=
\int_0^x g'(t)\,dt.
$$
Step <1>4 makes the integrand strictly positive for every $t>0$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore, for every $x>0$,
$$
\boxed{
\sin x>x-\frac{x^3}{6}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 says
$$
\sin x-x+\frac{x^3}{6}>0.
$$
Rearrange.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required inequality.
:::
:::
