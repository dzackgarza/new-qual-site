::: {.problem}
Let $M_{n\times n}(K)$ be the vector space of $n\times n$ matrices over a field $K$.
Find the dimension of the subspace spanned by
\[
\{XY-YX:X,Y\in M_{n\times n}(K)\}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
V
\coloneqq
\operatorname{span}
\{XY-YX:X,Y\in M_n(K)\}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #commutators-trace-zero}
Every matrix in $V$ has trace zero.

::: pf-proof
For arbitrary $X,Y\in M_n(K)$,
$$
\operatorname{tr}(XY)
=
\operatorname{tr}(YX).
$$
Indeed,
$$
\operatorname{tr}(XY)
=
\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}y_{ji}
=
\sum_{j,i}y_{ji}x_{ij}
=
\operatorname{tr}(YX),
$$
using commutativity of the field $K$. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{tr}(XY-YX)=0.
$$
Since trace is linear, every linear combination of commutators also has
trace zero.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix-unit-in-V}
If $i\neq j$, then the matrix unit $E_{ij}$ belongs to $V$.

::: pf-proof
For $i\neq j$,
$$
E_{ii}E_{ij}=E_{ij}
$$
and
$$
E_{ij}E_{ii}=0.
$$
Hence
$$
[E_{ii},E_{ij}]
\coloneqq
E_{ii}E_{ij}-E_{ij}E_{ii}
=
E_{ij}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #diagonal-difference-in-V}
If $i\neq j$, then
$$
E_{ii}-E_{jj}\in V.
$$

::: pf-proof
Matrix-unit multiplication gives
$$
E_{ij}E_{ji}=E_{ii}
$$
and
$$
E_{ji}E_{ij}=E_{jj}.
$$
Thus
$$
[E_{ij},E_{ji}]
=
E_{ii}-E_{jj}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #trace-zero-matrix-in-V}
If $n\geq2$, every trace-zero matrix belongs to $V$.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
A=(a_{ij})
$$
have trace zero. Its off-diagonal part
$$
\sum_{i\neq j}a_{ij}E_{ij}
$$
lies in $V$ by step [](#matrix-unit-in-V){.pf-ref}.

For the diagonal part, trace zero means
$$
a_{nn}
=
-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{ii}.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{diag}(a_{11},\ldots,a_{nn})
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}
a_{ii}(E_{ii}-E_{nn}),
$$
which lies in $V$ by step [](#diagonal-difference-in-V){.pf-ref}. Therefore $A\in V$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #V-equals-trace-zero-hyperplane}
For every $n\geq1$,
$$
V
=
\{A\in M_n(K):\operatorname{tr}A=0\}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For $n\geq2$, step [](#commutators-trace-zero){.pf-ref} gives one inclusion and step [](#trace-zero-matrix-in-V){.pf-ref} gives the other.
For $n=1$, every commutator is zero, and the only trace-zero $1\times1$
matrix is also zero.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dimension-of-V}
The dimension of $V$ is
$$
\boxed{n^2-1}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The trace map
$$
\operatorname{tr}:M_n(K)\longrightarrow K
$$
is a nonzero linear functional, since
$$
\operatorname{tr}(E_{11})=1.
$$
Therefore its kernel has codimension $1$ in the $n^2$-dimensional vector
space $M_n(K)$. By step [](#V-equals-trace-zero-hyperplane){.pf-ref}, that kernel is $V$, so
$$
\dim V=n^2-1.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#dimension-of-V){.pf-ref} gives the requested dimension.
:::
