::: problem
Let $\Omega$ be a star-shaped domain of $\mathbb{C}$.
Let $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a sequence of injective analytic functions on $\Omega$ that converges uniformly on every compact subset of $\Omega$ to a function $f$.
Prove that:

(a) $f$ is analytic on $\Omega$, and

(b) $f$ is either injective or a constant.
:::

::: solution
(a) Since $f_n\to f$ uniformly on compact subsets, the locally uniform limit
theorem for holomorphic functions implies that $f$ is holomorphic. For
completeness, if $T\Subset\Omega$ is any triangle, then
\[
\int_{\partial T}f(z)\,dz
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\partial T}f_n(z)\,dz=0,
\]
so Morera's theorem gives holomorphicity.

(b) Assume that $f$ is not constant. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
$f(a)=f(b)$ for distinct $a,b\in\Omega$. Since $\Omega$ is star-shaped it is
connected, and since $f$ is nonconstant, the zeros of
\[
g(z)=f(z)-f(a)
\]
are isolated. Choose a small closed disk $D$ around $b$ whose closure lies in
$\Omega$, which does not contain $a$, and such that $b$ is the only zero of
$g$ in $D$.

Set
\[
g_n(z)=f_n(z)-f_n(a).
\]
Then $g_n\to g$ uniformly on $D$. By Hurwitz's theorem, for all sufficiently
large $n$, $g_n$ has a zero $b_n$ in $D$. But
\[
g_n(b_n)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f_n(b_n)=f_n(a).
\]
Injectivity of $f_n$ forces $b_n=a$, impossible because $a\notin D$. Hence
$f$ is injective. Therefore the limit is either injective or constant.
:::
