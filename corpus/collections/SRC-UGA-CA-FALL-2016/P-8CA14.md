::: problem
Prove that the map
\[
w=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
\]
maps the first quadrant in the plane conformally onto the upper open unit disk
\[
\Delta^+=\{w=u+iv:\ u^2+v^2<1\text{ and }v>0\}.
\]

(a) Is the intersection of $\Delta^+$ with the first quadrant conformally equivalent to the upper half-plane $\{z=x+iy:\ y>0\}$?
:::

::: solution
Let
\[
T(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}.
\]
For $z=x+iy$ with $x>0$ and $y>0$,
\[
|T(z)|^2=\frac{(x-1)^2+y^2}{(x+1)^2+y^2}<1
\]
because $x>0$, while
\[
\operatorname{Im}T(z)=\frac{2y}{|z+1|^2}>0.
\]
Thus the first quadrant maps into $\Delta^+$.

Conversely,
\[
T^{-1}(w)=\frac{1+w}{1-w}.
\]
If $|w|<1$, then
\[
\operatorname{Re}T^{-1}(w)=\frac{1-|w|^2}{|1-w|^2}>0,
\]
and if $\operatorname{Im}w>0$, then
\[
\operatorname{Im}T^{-1}(w)=\frac{2\operatorname{Im}w}{|1-w|^2}>0.
\]
Hence $T^{-1}$ maps $\Delta^+$ into the first quadrant. Therefore $T$ is a
conformal bijection from the first quadrant onto $\Delta^+$.

For (a), the intersection of $\Delta^+$ with the first quadrant is the quarter
disk
\[
Q=\{w:|w|<1,\ 0<\arg w<\pi/2\}.
\]
It is a nonempty simply connected proper domain, so by the Riemann mapping
theorem it is conformally equivalent to $\mathbb D$, and hence to the upper
half-plane via a Cayley transform. Thus the answer is yes.
:::
