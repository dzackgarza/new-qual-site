::: {.problem}
Prove that the map
$$
w=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
$$
maps the first quadrant in the plane conformally onto the upper open unit disk
$$
\Delta^+=\{w=u+iv:\ u^2+v^2<1\text{ and }v>0\}.
$$

(a) Is the intersection of $\Delta^+$ with the first quadrant conformally equivalent to the upper half-plane $\{z=x+iy:\ y>0\}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on it. Write $Q_{1}=\{z:x>0,\ y>0\}$ for the first quadrant and $\Delta^{+}=\{w:\abs{w}<1,\ \operatorname{Im}w>0\}$ for the upper open unit disk.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #map}
$T(z)=\dfrac{z-1}{z+1}$ is holomorphic on $\CC-\{-1\}$, with $T'(z)=\dfrac{2}{(z+1)^{2}}\ne0$ there, and its inverse is $T^{-1}(w)=\dfrac{1+w}{1-w}$, holomorphic off $w=1$.

::: pf-proof
$T$ is a quotient of polynomials whose denominator does not vanish on $\CC-\{-1\}$; the quotient rule gives $T'(z)=\big((z+1)-(z-1)\big)/(z+1)^{2}=2/(z+1)^{2}$, which is nonzero there. Solving $w=(z-1)/(z+1)$ gives $wz+w=z-1$, hence $z(w-1)=-1-w$ and $z=(1+w)/(1-w)$, so $T^{-1}$ is as displayed, holomorphic where $1-w\ne0$, and it is the two-sided inverse of $T$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #forward}
$T$ maps $Q_{1}$ into $\Delta^{+}$.

::: pf-proof
For $z=x+iy$ with $x,y>0$,
$$
\abs{T(z)}^{2}=\frac{\abs{z-1}^{2}}{\abs{z+1}^{2}}=\frac{(x-1)^{2}+y^{2}}{(x+1)^{2}+y^{2}}<1,
$$
the inequality being equivalent to $(x-1)^{2}<(x+1)^{2}$, that is to $x>0$. And
$$
\operatorname{Im}T(z)=\frac{\operatorname{Im}\big((z-1)(\bar z+1)\big)}{\abs{z+1}^{2}}=\frac{\operatorname{Im}\big(\abs{z}^{2}+2iy-1\big)}{\abs{z+1}^{2}}=\frac{2y}{\abs{z+1}^{2}}>0
$$
because $y>0$. So $T(Q_{1})\subset\Delta^{+}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #backward}
$T^{-1}$ maps $\Delta^{+}$ into $Q_{1}$.

::: pf-proof
For $w=u+iv$ with $\abs{w}<1$ and $v>0$,
$$
\operatorname{Re}T^{-1}(w)=\frac{\operatorname{Re}\big((1+w)(1-\bar w)\big)}{\abs{1-w}^{2}}=\frac{1-\abs{w}^{2}}{\abs{1-w}^{2}}>0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Im}T^{-1}(w)=\frac{\operatorname{Im}\big((1+w)(1-\bar w)\big)}{\abs{1-w}^{2}}=\frac{2v}{\abs{1-w}^{2}}>0,
$$
using $(1+w)(1-\bar w)=1-\bar w+w-\abs{w}^{2}$ and $w-\bar w=2iv$. So $T^{-1}(\Delta^{+})\subset Q_{1}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conformal}
$T$ is a conformal bijection of $Q_{1}$ onto $\Delta^{+}$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#forward){.pf-ref} and [](#backward){.pf-ref} show that $T$ and $T^{-1}$ map these regions into each other, and by step [](#map){.pf-ref} they are mutually inverse, so $T$ is a bijection. It is holomorphic with nonzero derivative on $Q_{1}$ by step [](#map){.pf-ref}, which is conformality at every point, and its inverse is holomorphic as well.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) Yes: $\Delta^{+}\cap Q_{1}$ is conformally equivalent to the upper half-plane.

::: pf-proof
$\Delta^{+}\cap Q_{1}=\{w:\abs{w}<1,\ \operatorname{Re}w>0,\ \operatorname{Im}w>0\}$, the open quarter disk of radius $1$: a nonempty proper subdomain of $\CC$ that is convex and therefore simply connected. By the Riemann mapping theorem it is conformally equivalent to the open unit disk, and the Cayley transform $w\mapsto i(1+w)/(1-w)$ is a conformal bijection of $\DD$ onto the upper half-plane. Composing gives the required equivalence.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Both parts hold.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#conformal){.pf-ref} is the first assertion, and step [](#part-a){.pf-ref} answers (a) in the affirmative.
:::
