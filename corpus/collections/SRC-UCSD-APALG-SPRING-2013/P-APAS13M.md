::: {.problem}
Let
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

(a) Show that $A$ generates a matrix group $G$ of order three.

(b) Find a set of homogeneous $G$-invariant polynomials which generate $\CC[x,y]^G$.

(c) Compute the Hilbert series of $\CC[x,y]^G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this exercise: the card's own diagonalisation is completed, with the order of $A$, the invariant monomials and the Hilbert series as separate steps.

Let $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, $u=x+\omega y$, $v=x+\omega^{2}y$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #order}
$A^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$, $A^{3}=I$ and $A\ne I$, so $G=\theset{I,A,A^{2}}$ has order $3$.

::: pf-proof
Direct multiplication gives $A^{2}$ as displayed and $A^{3}=A^{2}A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$; $A\ne I$ and $A^{2}\ne I$ since $A^{2}$ has $-1$ in its first row, so the cyclic subgroup generated by $A$ has exactly three elements.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #diagonalise}
The change of variables $(x,y)\mapsto(u,v)$ is invertible and diagonalises the action: $u\mapsto\omega u$ and $v\mapsto\omega^{2}v$ under $A$.

::: pf-proof
$u,v$ are related to $x,y$ by the invertible matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega\\1&\omega^{2}\end{pmatrix}$ (determinant $\omega^{2}-\omega\ne0$), so $\CC[x,y]=\CC[u,v]$. Substituting $(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x-y)$, the image of $u$ is $-y+\omega(x-y)=\omega x-(1+\omega)y=\omega x+\omega^{2}y=\omega u$ using $1+\omega=-\omega^{2}$, and similarly $v\mapsto\omega^{2}v$; so $A$ acts by $u\mapsto\omega u$, $v\mapsto\omega^{2}v$, and $A^{2}$ by $\omega^{2},\omega$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #invariants}
A monomial $u^{a}v^{b}$ is $G$-invariant exactly when $a+2b\equiv0\pmod 3$, and then
$$u^{a}v^{b}=\begin{cases}(uv)^{b}\big(u^{3}\big)^{(a-b)/3},&a\ge b,\\ (uv)^{a}\big(v^{3}\big)^{(b-a)/3},&b\ge a.\end{cases}$$
Hence $\CC[x,y]^{G}=\CC[u^{3},uv,v^{3}]$.

::: pf-proof
$A$ sends $u^{a}v^{b}$ to $\omega^{a+2b}u^{a}v^{b}$, so invariance is $\omega^{a+2b}=1$, that is $3\mid a+2b$. If $a\ge b$ then $a+2b=3b+(a-b)$, so the congruence forces $3\mid a-b$ and the displayed factorisation is integral; the case $b\ge a$ is symmetric with $a+2b=3a+(b-a)$. So every invariant monomial lies in $\CC[u^{3},uv,v^{3}]$, and $u^{3}$, $uv$, $v^{3}$ are themselves invariant; a polynomial is invariant iff all its monomials are, since the action is diagonal.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hilbert}
The Hilbert series of $\CC[x,y]^{G}$ is $\dfrac{1}{(1-t^{2})(1-t^{3})^{2}}$.

::: pf-proof
$\CC[u^{3},uv,v^{3}]$ is a polynomial ring in the three algebraically independent invariants $u^{3}$ (degree $3$), $uv$ (degree $2$) and $v^{3}$ (degree $3$): independence holds because the monomials $(u^{3})^{i}(uv)^{j}(v^{3})^{k}=u^{3i+j}v^{3k+j}$ are distinct for distinct $(i,j,k)$, since $u$- and $v$-exponents determine $j$ and then $i,k$. So the invariant monomials form a basis and the Hilbert series is the product $\frac{1}{1-t^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-t^{3}}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
(a) is step [](#order){.pf-ref}; (b) the generators are $u^{3}=(x+\omega y)^{3}$, $uv=x^{2}-xy+y^{2}$ and $v^{3}=(x+\omega^{2}y)^{3}$, by steps [](#diagonalise){.pf-ref} and [](#invariants){.pf-ref}; (c) the Hilbert series is $\frac{1}{(1-t^{2})(1-t^{3})^{2}}$ by step [](#hilbert){.pf-ref}.
:::
