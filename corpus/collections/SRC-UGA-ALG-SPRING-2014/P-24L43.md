::: {.problem}
Let $G = \GL(3, \QQ[x])$ be the group of invertible $3\times 3$ matrices over $\QQ[x]$.
For each $f\in \QQ[x]$, let $S_f$ be the set of $3\times 3$ matrices $A$ over $\QQ[x]$ such that $\det(A) = c f(x)$ for some nonzero constant $c\in \QQ$.

a. Show that for $(P, Q) \in G\cross G$ and $A\in S_f$, the formula
\[
(P, Q)\cdot A \definedas PAQ\inv
\]
gives a well defined map $G\cross G \cross S_f \to S_f$ and show that this map gives a group action of $G\cross G$ on $S_f$.

b. For $f(x) = x^3(x^2+1)^2$, give one representative from each orbit of the group action in (a), and justify your assertion.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $R=\mathbb Q[x]$ and $q=x^2+1$.

<1>1. The formula $(P,Q)\cdot A=PAQ^{-1}$ defines an action of $\mathrm{GL}_3(R)\times\mathrm{GL}_3(R)$ on $S_f$.

::: {.proof}
For $P,Q$ invertible, their determinants are units in $R$, namely nonzero rational constants. Thus $\det(PAQ^{-1})=\det(P)\det(A)\det(Q)^{-1}$ is a nonzero constant multiple of $f$ whenever $\det(A)$ is. The identity pair acts trivially, and
\[
(P_1,Q_1)\cdot((P_2,Q_2)\cdot A)
=P_1P_2AQ_2^{-1}Q_1^{-1}
=(P_1P_2,Q_1Q_2)\cdot A.
\]
The action is therefore well defined and satisfies the group action axioms.
:::

<1>2. Every orbit in $S_{x^3q^2}$ contains exactly one diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2,d_3)$ with monic entries $d_1\mid d_2\mid d_3$ and $d_1d_2d_3=x^3q^2$.

::: {.proof}
The ring $R$ is Euclidean. Smith normal form over a PID gives invertible row and column transformations taking any matrix to such a diagonal matrix, uniquely after choosing its nonzero diagonal entries monic. Its determinant differs from the original by a unit, and the monic product is $x^3q^2$. Conversely two matrices are in the same orbit precisely when their Smith normal forms coincide.
:::

<1>3. There are exactly six orbits, represented by
\[
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{diag}(1,1,x^3q^2),\quad
\operatorname{diag}(1,q,x^3q),\\
\operatorname{diag}(1,x,x^2q^2),\quad
\operatorname{diag}(1,xq,x^2q),\\
\operatorname{diag}(x,x,xq^2),\quad
\operatorname{diag}(x,xq,xq).
\end{gathered}}
\]

::: {.proof}
The irreducible polynomials dividing $x^3q^2$ are $x$ and $q$. The divisibility condition on the Smith entries requires the exponent triples of each irreducible to be nondecreasing. For $x^3$, the possibilities are $(0,0,3)$, $(0,1,2)$, and $(1,1,1)$; for $q^2$, they are $(0,0,2)$ and $(0,1,1)$. Their six pairwise combinations yield exactly the displayed diagonal matrices. Each has the prescribed determinant, and step <1>2 gives both existence and uniqueness of the representative in each orbit.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves part (a); steps <1>2 and <1>3 prove part (b).
:::
