::: {.problem}
Let $G = \GL(3, \QQ[x])$ be the group of invertible $3\times 3$ matrices over $\QQ[x]$.
For each $f\in \QQ[x]$, let $S_f$ be the set of $3\times 3$ matrices $A$ over $\QQ[x]$ such that $\det(A) = c f(x)$ for some nonzero constant $c\in \QQ$.

a. Show that for $(P, Q) \in G\cross G$ and $A\in S_f$, the formula
\[
(P, Q)\cdot A \definedas PAQ\inv
\]
gives a well defined map $G\cross G \cross S_f \to S_f$ and show that this map gives a group action of $G\cross G$ on $S_f$.

b. For $f(x) = x^3(x^2+1)^2$, give one representative from each orbit of the group action in (a), and justify your assertion.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $R=\mathbb Q[x]$. Its units are exactly the nonzero rational constants.

For (a), if $A\in S_f$ and $(P,Q)\in G\times G$, then
\[
\det(PAQ^{-1})=\det(P)\det(A)\det(Q)^{-1}.
\]
Both $\det(P)$ and $\det(Q)$ are units of $R$, hence nonzero rational constants. Therefore $\det(PAQ^{-1})$ is again a nonzero rational constant times $f$, so $PAQ^{-1}\in S_f$. The identity pair acts trivially, and
\[
(P_1,Q_1)\cdot((P_2,Q_2)\cdot A)
=P_1P_2A Q_2^{-1}Q_1^{-1}
=(P_1P_2,Q_1Q_2)\cdot A,
\]
so this is a group action.

For (b), two matrices over the PID $R$ are in the same left-right $G\times G$ orbit exactly when they have the same Smith normal form. Thus every orbit contains a unique diagonal representative
\[
\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2,d_3),
\qquad
d_1\mid d_2\mid d_3,
\]
with the $d_i$ chosen monic, and with product
\[
d_1d_2d_3=x^3(x^2+1)^2.
\]
The polynomial $q=x^2+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. For the exponent $3$ of $x$, the nondecreasing triples of exponents are
\[
(0,0,3),\qquad(0,1,2),\qquad(1,1,1),
\]
and for the exponent $2$ of $q$ they are
\[
(0,0,2),\qquad(0,1,1).
\]
Combining these independently gives exactly six Smith forms, hence exactly six orbits. Representatives are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{diag}(1,1,x^3q^2),
&&\operatorname{diag}(1,q,x^3q),\\
&\operatorname{diag}(1,x,x^2q^2),
&&\operatorname{diag}(1,xq,x^2q),\\
&\operatorname{diag}(x,x,xq^2),
&&\operatorname{diag}(x,xq,xq),
\end{aligned}
\qquad q=x^2+1.
\]
Each has determinant exactly $x^3q^2$, so lies in $S_f$, and uniqueness of Smith normal form proves that no two listed matrices lie in the same orbit and that every orbit is represented.
:::
