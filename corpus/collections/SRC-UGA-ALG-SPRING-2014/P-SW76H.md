::: {.problem}
Let $F\subset C$ be a field extension with $C$ algebraically closed.

a. Prove that the intermediate field $C_{\text{alg}} \subset C$ consisting of elements algebraic over $F$ is algebraically closed.

b. Prove that if $F\to E$ is an algebraic extension, there exists a homomorphism $E\to C$ that is the identity on $F$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Set $C_{\mathrm{alg}}=\{z\in C:z\text{ is algebraic over }F\}$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The subset $C_{\mathrm{alg}}$ is an intermediate field between $F$ and $C$.


::: pf-proof

If $a,b$ are algebraic over $F$, the finite extension $F(a,b)/F$ contains $a\pm b$, $ab$, and $a^{-1}$ when $a\neq0$. These elements are therefore algebraic over $F$, giving closure under the field operations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The field $C_{\mathrm{alg}}$ is algebraically closed.


::: pf-proof

Let $g\in C_{\mathrm{alg}}[x]$ be nonconstant and let $L$ be the extension of $F$ generated by its finitely many coefficients. Since these coefficients are algebraic over $F$, the extension $L/F$ is finite. Algebraic closedness of $C$ gives a root $\alpha\in C$ of $g$. The root $\alpha$ is algebraic over $L$, hence algebraic over $F$ by transitivity. Therefore $\alpha\in C_{\mathrm{alg}}$, so every nonconstant polynomial over $C_{\mathrm{alg}}$ has a root there.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Every algebraic extension $E/F$ admits an $F$-embedding
$$
\boxed{E\hookrightarrow C}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Partially order pairs $(L,\varphi)$, where $F\subseteq L\subseteq E$ and $\varphi:L\hookrightarrow C$ is an $F$-embedding, by extension of embeddings. The set is nonempty because it contains $(F,\mathrm{id}_F)$. The union of a chain of intermediate fields, together with the compatible union of its embeddings, supplies an upper bound. By Zorn's lemma choose a maximal pair $(L,\varphi)$. If $L\neq E$, choose $\alpha\in E\setminus L$. Its minimal polynomial $m_\alpha\in L[x]$ exists because $E/F$ is algebraic. Applying $\varphi$ to the coefficients yields a polynomial in $C[x]$ with a root $\beta\in C$. The map $L[x]/(m_\alpha)\to C$ extending $\varphi$ and sending the class of $x$ to $\beta$ is an embedding of fields, since $m_\alpha$ is irreducible. Hence $\varphi$ extends to $L(\alpha)$, contradicting maximality. Therefore $L=E$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
