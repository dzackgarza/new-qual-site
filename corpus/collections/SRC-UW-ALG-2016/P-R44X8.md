::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be a field of positive characteristic $p$, $\ZZ/p$ a cyclic group of order $p$, and $k\ZZ/p$ the group algebra of $\ZZ/p$ over $k$.

(a) Let $\sigma$ be a generator of $\ZZ/p$ and let $t=\sigma-1$.
Show that there is an isomorphism $k\ZZ/p\simeq k[t]/t^p$.

(b) Let $M$ be a finite dimensional projective $k\ZZ/p$-module and $\Sigma$ the linear operator on $M$ induced by the action of $\sigma$.
Show that

(i) $\dim_k M$ is divisible by $p$; and

(ii) $\operatorname{rk}(\Sigma-\operatorname{Id}_M)=\frac{p-1}{p}\dim_k M$.

(c) Let $M$ be a finite dimensional $k\ZZ/p$-module, and assume that
$$
\operatorname{rk}(\Sigma-\operatorname{Id}_M)=\frac{p-1}{p}\dim_k M.
$$
Prove that then $M$ is projective.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p>0$, $G=\ZZ/p=\langle\sigma\rangle$, and $t=\sigma-1\in kG$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Part (a): $k\ZZ/p\cong k[t]/(t^p)$, via $t\mapsto\sigma-1$.

::: pf-proof

In $k\ZZ/p$ one has $\sigma^p=1$, so
$$
t^p=(\sigma-1)^p=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\binom{p}{j}\sigma^j(-1)^{p-j}=\sigma^p+(-1)^p=\sigma^p-1=0,
$$
because $\binom{p}{j}\equiv0 \pmod p$ for $0<j<p$ and $(-1)^p=-1$ in characteristic $p$.

Hence there is a well-defined $k$-algebra homomorphism $\Phi:k[t]/(t^p)\to k\ZZ/p$ with $\Phi(t)=\sigma-1$. It is surjective, because $\Phi(t+1)=\sigma$ generates $k\ZZ/p$ as an algebra. Its source and target both have $k$-dimension $p$: the source because $1,t,\dots,t^{p-1}$ is a $k$-basis, the target because $\sigma^0,\dots,\sigma^{p-1}$ is. A surjection between $k$-vector spaces of the same finite dimension is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$k[t]/(t^p)$ is a local ring with residue field $k$; hence every projective module over it is free.

::: pf-proof

The ideal $(t)$ is the unique maximal ideal: it consists of all non-units, since an element $a_0+a_1t+\cdots$ with $a_0\ne0$ has inverse $a_0^{-1}(1-a_0^{-1}(a_1t+\cdots))$ because $(t)^p=0$, so the ring is local with residue field $k[t]/(t)\cong k$.

A finitely generated projective module over a local ring is free: if $P$ is finitely generated projective and $P/\mathfrak mP\cong k^r$, the chosen lift $R^r\to P$ is split by projectivity and its kernel $K$ satisfies $K=\mathfrak mK$, so $K=0$ by Nakayama, whence $P\cong R^r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Part (b): if $M$ is a finite dimensional projective $k\ZZ/p$-module with $r=\dim_k M/p$, then $\dim_kM=pr$ and $\operatorname{rk}(\Sigma-\mathrm{Id}_M)=r(p-1)=\frac{p-1}{p}\dim_kM$.

::: pf-proof

By parts [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $M\cong(k[t]/t^p)^r$ for some $r\ge0$, so $\dim_kM=pr$, which is divisible by $p$: this is (i).

The operator $\Sigma$ is multiplication by $\sigma=1+t$, so $\Sigma-\mathrm{Id}_M$ is multiplication by $t$. On $k[t]/t^p$ the multiplication map $t\cdot$ has kernel spanned by $t^{p-1}$ (dimension $1$) and image $(t)$, of dimension $p-1$; hence its rank is $p-1$. On the $r$-fold direct sum the rank is $r(p-1)$, and
$$
r(p-1)=\frac{p-1}{p}\cdot pr=\frac{p-1}{p}\dim_kM,
$$
which is (ii).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Part (c): a finite dimensional $k\ZZ/p$-module $M$ with $\operatorname{rk}(\Sigma-\mathrm{Id}_M)=\frac{p-1}{p}\dim_kM$ is projective.

::: pf-proof

Regard $M$ as a $k[t]/(t^p)$-module; then $\Sigma-\mathrm{Id}_M$ is multiplication by $t$, a nilpotent operator with $t^p=0$. By the structure theory of modules over $k[t]/(t^p)$ — equivalently, the decomposition of a nilpotent operator into Jordan blocks — there are positive integers $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_s\le p$ with
$$
M\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}k[t]\big/(t^{\lambda_j}).
$$
On $k[t]/(t^{\lambda})$ multiplication by $t$ has kernel of dimension $1$ and rank $\lambda-1$. Hence
$$
\dim_kM=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\lambda_j,\qquad
\operatorname{rk}(\Sigma-\mathrm{Id}_M)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}(\lambda_j-1)=\dim_kM-s .
$$
The hypothesis gives $\dim_kM-s=\frac{p-1}{p}\dim_kM$, so
$$
s=\dim_kM-\frac{p-1}{p}\dim_kM=\frac{\dim_kM}{p},\qquad\text{and}\qquad \sum_{j=1}^s\lambda_j=\dim_kM=ps .
$$
Since $\lambda_j\le p$ for every $j$, a sum of $s$ terms each at most $p$ equals $ps$ only if every $\lambda_j=p$. Therefore
$$
M\cong\bigl(k[t]/(t^p)\bigr)^{s},
$$
which is free, hence projective, by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::
