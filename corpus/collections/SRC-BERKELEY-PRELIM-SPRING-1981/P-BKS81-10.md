::: {.problem}
1. Give an example of $C^1$ functions
   \[
   f_k:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R,
   \qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,
   \]
   such that $f_k(0)=0$ for all $k$ and
   \[
   f_k'(x)\to f_0'(x)
   \]
   for every $x$, but $f_k(x)$ does not converge to $f_0(x)$ for every $x$.
2. State an additional condition that would imply $f_k(x)\to f_0(x)$ for every $x$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define a continuous function $\phi:[0,\infty)\to\RR$ by
$$
\phi(u)=
\begin{cases}
4(u-1),&1\le u\le\frac32,\\
4(2-u),&\frac32\le u\le2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
Then
$$
\int_0^\infty\phi(u)\,du=1.
$$

<1>1. Put
$$
f_0(x)\coloneqq0
$$
and, for $k\ge1$, define
$$
f_k(x)
\coloneqq
\int_0^x k\phi(kt)\,dt.
$$
Then every $f_k$ is $C^1$ and $f_k(0)=0$.

::: {.proof}
The function $t\mapsto k\phi(kt)$ is continuous, so the fundamental theorem
of calculus gives
$$
f_k'(x)=k\phi(kx).
$$
The defining integral is zero at $x=0$. The same statements are immediate
for $f_0=0$.
:::

<1>2. For every fixed $x\ge0$,
$$
f_k'(x)\longrightarrow f_0'(x)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
At $x=0$ one has
$$
f_k'(0)=k\phi(0)=0.
$$
Fix $x>0$. Once $k>2/x$, one has $kx>2$, hence
$$
\phi(kx)=0.
$$
Therefore $f_k'(x)=0$ for all sufficiently large $k$.
:::

<1>3. Nevertheless, for every fixed $x>0$,
$$
f_k(x)\longrightarrow1\ne0=f_0(x).
$$

::: {.proof}
The substitution $u=kt$ gives
$$
f_k(x)=\int_0^{kx}\phi(u)\,du.
$$
For all sufficiently large $k$, one has $kx\ge2$, so the support of $\phi$
is contained in $[0,kx]$. Hence
$$
f_k(x)
=
\int_0^\infty\phi(u)\,du
=
1.
$$
Thus the sequence fails to converge to $f_0$ at every $x>0$.
:::

<1>4. One sufficient additional condition is that
$$
f_k'\longrightarrow f_0'
$$
uniformly on every bounded interval.

::: {.proof}
Fix $x\ge0$. Since $f_k(0)=f_0(0)=0$, the fundamental theorem of calculus
gives
$$
f_k(x)-f_0(x)
=
\int_0^x\bigl(f_k'(t)-f_0'(t)\bigr)\,dt.
$$
Therefore
$$
\abs{f_k(x)-f_0(x)}
\le
x\sup_{0\le t\le x}
\abs{f_k'(t)-f_0'(t)}.
$$
Uniform convergence of the derivatives on $[0,x]$ makes the right-hand
side tend to zero.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 provide the requested counterexample, and step <1>4 gives
a sufficient extra condition for pointwise convergence of the functions.
:::
