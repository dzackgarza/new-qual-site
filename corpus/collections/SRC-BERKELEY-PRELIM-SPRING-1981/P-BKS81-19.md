::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite group. For $a\in G$, let
\[
C(a)=\{bab^{-1}:b\in G\}
\]
be its conjugacy class.

1. Prove that $|C(a)|$ divides $|G|$.
2. Must all conjugacy classes have the same number of elements?
3. If $G$ has only two conjugacy classes, prove that $|G|=2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #class-size-divides-order}
For every $a\in G$,
$$
\boxed{\abs{C(a)}\mid\abs{G}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $G$ act on itself by conjugation:
$$
b\cdot a=bab^{-1}.
$$
The orbit of $a$ is exactly $C(a)$, while its stabilizer is the centralizer
$$
C_G(a)
\coloneqq
\{b\in G:ba=ab\}.
$$
The orbit-stabilizer theorem therefore gives
$$
\abs{C(a)}
=
\frac{\abs{G}}{\abs{C_G(a)}}.
$$
Hence $\abs{C(a)}$ divides $\abs{G}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sizes-need-not-match}
Conjugacy classes need not all have the same size.

::: pf-proof
In $S_3$, the conjugacy classes are
$$
\{e\},
\qquad
\{(12),(13),(23)\},
\qquad
\{(123),(132)\}.
$$
Their sizes are $1$, $3$, and $2$, respectively.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #two-classes-implies-order-two}
If $G$ has exactly two conjugacy classes, then
$$
\boxed{\abs{G}=2}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The identity is conjugate only to itself, so one conjugacy class is
$$
\{e\}.
$$
If there are exactly two classes, the other contains every nonidentity
element and therefore has size
$$
\abs{G}-1.
$$
By step [](#class-size-divides-order){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{G}-1\mid\abs{G}.
$$
It follows that
$$
\abs{G}-1\mid
\bigl(\abs{G}-(\abs{G}-1)\bigr)
=
1.
$$
Since $\abs{G}-1$ is a positive integer, it equals $1$, and hence
$\abs{G}=2$.
:::

:::
