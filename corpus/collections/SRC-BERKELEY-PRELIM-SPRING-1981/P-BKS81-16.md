::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be real-valued for $x\ge1$, with
\[
f(1)=1,
\qquad
f'(x)=\frac{1}{x^2+f(x)^2}.
\]
Prove that
\[
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)
\]
exists and is less than
\[
1+\frac\pi4.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function $f$ is strictly increasing on $[1,\infty)$, and
$$
f(x)\ge1
$$
for every $x\ge1$.

::: {.proof}
The differential equation gives
$$
f'(x)=\frac1{x^2+f(x)^2}>0.
$$
Hence $f$ is strictly increasing. Since $f(1)=1$, the stated lower bound
follows.
:::

<1>2. For every $x\ge1$,
$$
f(x)
\le
1+\int_1^x\frac{dt}{1+t^2}
<
1+\frac\pi4.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
f(t)^2\ge1,
$$
so
$$
0<f'(t)
=
\frac1{t^2+f(t)^2}
\le
\frac1{t^2+1}.
$$
Integrating from $1$ to $x$ gives
$$
f(x)-1
\le
\arctan x-\frac\pi4
<
\frac\pi2-\frac\pi4
=
\frac\pi4.
$$
:::

<1>3. The limit
$$
L\coloneqq\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)
$$
exists and is finite.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 shows that $f$ is increasing, while step <1>2 gives the uniform
upper bound
$$
f(x)<1+\frac\pi4.
$$
Every increasing real-valued function that is bounded above has a finite
limit at infinity.
:::

<1>4. The limiting bound is strict:
$$
L<1+\frac\pi4.
$$

::: {.proof}
For every $t>1$, strict monotonicity gives $f(t)>1$, and hence
$$
\frac1{t^2+f(t)^2}
<
\frac1{t^2+1}.
$$
The difference
$$
h(t)
\coloneqq
\frac1{t^2+1}
-
\frac1{t^2+f(t)^2}
$$
is continuous and strictly positive on, for example, $[2,3]$. Therefore
$$
\int_1^\infty h(t)\,dt>0.
$$
Taking $x\to\infty$ in
$$
f(x)-1
=
\int_1^x\frac{dt}{t^2+f(t)^2}
$$
and using the comparison with $1/(1+t^2)$ yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
L-1
&=
\int_1^\infty\frac{dt}{t^2+f(t)^2}\\
&=
\int_1^\infty\frac{dt}{1+t^2}
-
\int_1^\infty h(t)\,dt\\
&<
\frac\pi4.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $L<1+\pi/4$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)
\text{ exists and is less than }
1+\frac\pi4
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Existence is step <1>3 and the strict bound is step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
