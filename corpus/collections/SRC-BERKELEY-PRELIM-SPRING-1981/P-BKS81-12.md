::: {.problem}
For $x\in\mathbb R$, let
\[
A_x=\begin{pmatrix}
x&1&1&1\\
1&x&1&1\\
1&1&x&1\\
1&1&1&x
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

1. Prove that
   \[
   \det A_x=(x-1)^3(x+3).
   \]
2. Prove that if $x\ne1,-3$, then
   \[
   A_x^{-1}=-\frac{1}{(x-1)(x+3)}A_{-x-2}.
   \]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $J$ denote the $4\times4$ matrix all of whose entries are $1$. Then
$$
A_x=(x-1)I+J.
$$

<1>1. The eigenvalues of $J$ are $4$ with multiplicity $1$ and $0$ with
multiplicity $3$.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\mathbf 1=(1,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}.
$$
Then
$$
J\mathbf 1=4\mathbf 1.
$$
If $v$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf 1$, the sum of its coordinates is zero,
so every coordinate of $Jv$ is zero. Thus $J$ vanishes on the
three-dimensional space $\mathbf 1^\perp$ and acts by $4$ on
$\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
\boxed{\det A_x=(x-1)^3(x+3)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1 and the identity $A_x=(x-1)I+J$, the eigenvalue of $A_x$ on
$\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$ is
$$
(x-1)+4=x+3,
$$
while its eigenvalue on $\mathbf 1^\perp$ is $x-1$ with multiplicity $3$.
The determinant is the product of the four eigenvalues.
:::

<1>3. The matrices $A_x$ and $A_{-x-2}$ satisfy
$$
A_xA_{-x-2}
=
-(x-1)(x+3)I.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $J^2=4J$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_xA_{-x-2}
&=
\bigl((x-1)I+J\bigr)
\bigl((-x-3)I+J\bigr)\\
&=
-(x-1)(x+3)I
+\bigl((x-1)+(-x-3)\bigr)J
+J^2\\
&=
-(x-1)(x+3)I-4J+4J\\
&=
-(x-1)(x+3)I.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>4. If $x\ne1,-3$, then
$$
\boxed{
A_x^{-1}
=
-\frac1{(x-1)(x+3)}A_{-x-2}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For these values of $x$, the scalar $(x-1)(x+3)$ is nonzero. Divide the
identity in step <1>3 by its negative to obtain a right inverse of $A_x$.
For square matrices a right inverse is the inverse, giving the displayed
formula.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves part (1), and step <1>4 proves part (2).
:::
:::
