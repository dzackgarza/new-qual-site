::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ be continuous with $f(0)=0$.
Show that there is a continuous concave function $g:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ such that
\[
g(0)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
g(x)\ge f(x)
\]
for every $x\in[0,1]$.

Here concavity means that
\[
g(tx+(1-t)y)\ge t g(x)+(1-t)g(y)
\]
for all $x,y\in[0,1]$ and $0\le t\le1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
f_+(x)\coloneqq\max\{f(x),0\},
\qquad
M\coloneqq\max_{0\le x\le1}f_+(x).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #trivial-case-m-zero}
If $M=0$, then $g=0$ has all the required properties.

::: pf-proof
The equality $M=0$ means that $f(x)\le0$ for every $x$. Hence the constant
function $g=0$ is continuous and concave, satisfies $g(0)=0$, and obeys
$g\ge f$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #scale-sequence}
Suppose $M>0$. There is a decreasing sequence
$$
1=\delta_0>\delta_1>\delta_2>\cdots>0
$$
with $\delta_n\to0$ and
$$
f_+(x)\le M4^{-n}
\qquad
\text{for }0\le x\le\delta_n.
$$

::: pf-proof
Because $f_+$ is continuous and $f_+(0)=0$, for every $n\ge1$ there is
$\eta_n>0$ such that
$$
0\le x\le\eta_n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f_+(x)\le M4^{-n}.
$$
Choose recursively
$$
0<\delta_n<
\min\left\{\eta_n,\frac{\delta_{n-1}}2\right\}.
$$
Then the displayed bound holds and
$\delta_n<2^{-n}$, so $\delta_n\to0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cap-functions}
For $n\ge1$, define
$$
a_n\coloneqq M4^{-(n-1)}
$$
and
$$
\phi_n(x)
\coloneqq
\min\left\{1,\frac{x}{\delta_n}\right\}.
$$
Each $\phi_n$ is continuous, concave, nonnegative, and vanishes at $0$.

::: pf-proof
The function $\phi_n$ is linear with slope $1/\delta_n$ on
$[0,\delta_n]$ and constant with slope $0$ on $[\delta_n,1]$. Its slope
therefore only decreases, so it is concave. The other assertions are
immediate from the definition.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #series-defines-g}
The series
$$
g(x)
\coloneqq
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\phi_n(x)
$$
converges uniformly on $[0,1]$ and defines a continuous concave function
with $g(0)=0$.

::: pf-proof
Since $0\le\phi_n\le1$,
$$
0\le a_n\phi_n(x)\le a_n
$$
for every $x$, while
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n
=
M\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}4^{-(n-1)}
=
\frac{4M}{3}.
$$
The Weierstrass test gives uniform convergence, and hence continuity of
$g$.

Every partial sum is a positive linear combination of the concave
functions $\phi_n$, hence is concave. Passing to the pointwise limit in
the concavity inequality shows that $g$ is concave. Finally,
$\phi_n(0)=0$ for every $n$, so $g(0)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #g-majorizes-f}
The function $g$ majorizes $f$ on $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof
At $x=0$ one has
$$
g(0)=f(0)=0.
$$
Fix $x\in(0,1]$. Since $\delta_n\downarrow0$ and $\delta_0=1$, there is an
$n\ge1$ such that
$$
\delta_n\le x\le\delta_{n-1}.
$$
Because $x\ge\delta_n$, one has $\phi_n(x)=1$, and therefore
$$
g(x)\ge a_n=M4^{-(n-1)}.
$$
If $n=1$, the definition of $M$ gives
$$
f_+(x)\le M=a_1.
$$
If $n\ge2$, step [](#scale-sequence){.pf-ref} applied with $n-1$ and
$x\le\delta_{n-1}$ gives
$$
f_+(x)\le M4^{-(n-1)}=a_n.
$$
Thus in every case
$$
g(x)\ge f_+(x)\ge f(x).
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion-boxed}
Therefore there is a continuous concave function
$g:[0,1]\to\RR$ satisfying
$$
\boxed{g(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad g(x)\ge f(x)\text{ for all }x\in[0,1].}
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#trivial-case-m-zero){.pf-ref} handles $M=0$. For $M>0$, step [](#series-defines-g){.pf-ref} gives continuity,
concavity, and the value at the origin, while step [](#g-majorizes-f){.pf-ref} gives the required
majorization.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#conclusion-boxed){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
:::
