::: {.problem}
Determine which of the following series converge:

1. \[
   \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2n)!(3n)!}{n!(4n)!}.
   \]
2. \[
   \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1+1/n}}.
   \]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2n)!(3n)!}{n!(4n)!}
$$
converges.

::: pf-proof

Put
$$
a_n\coloneqq\frac{(2n)!(3n)!}{n!(4n)!}.
$$
Then
$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}
=
\frac{
(2n+2)(2n+1)
(3n+3)(3n+2)(3n+1)
}{
(n+1)
(4n+4)(4n+3)(4n+2)(4n+1)
}.
$$
Dividing numerator and denominator by $n^5$ gives
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}
=
\frac{(2)(2)(3)(3)(3)}
{(1)(4)(4)(4)(4)}
=
\frac{27}{64}
<1.
$$
The ratio test therefore gives convergence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1+1/n}}
$$
diverges.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
b_n\coloneqq\frac{1}{n^{1+1/n}}.
$$
Comparing with the harmonic term $1/n$,
$$
\frac{b_n}{1/n}
=
n^{-1/n}
=
\exp\left(-\frac{\log n}{n}\right)
\longrightarrow1.
$$
Hence the limit comparison test with the divergent harmonic series shows
that $\sum b_n$ diverges.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\text{series (1) converges, while series (2) diverges}.}
$$

::: pf-proof

This is exactly the combination of steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} answers both parts.
