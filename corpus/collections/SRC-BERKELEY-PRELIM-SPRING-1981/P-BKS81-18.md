::: {.problem}
Describe the two regions in $(a,b)$-space for which the restriction of
\[
f_{a,b}(x,y)=ay^2+bx
\]
to the unit circle
\[
x^2+y^2=1
\]
has exactly two critical points and exactly four critical points, respectively.
:::

::: {.solution}
Parameterize the unit circle by
$$
(x,y)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta).
$$
Then the restricted function is
$$
F_{a,b}(\theta)
=
a\sin^2\theta+b\cos\theta.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #derivative-formula}
Its derivative is
$$
F_{a,b}'(\theta)
=
\sin\theta\bigl(2a\cos\theta-b\bigr).
$$

::: pf-proof
Differentiate directly:
$$
\frac{d}{d\theta}
\left(a\sin^2\theta+b\cos\theta\right)
=
2a\sin\theta\cos\theta-b\sin\theta.
$$
Factoring out $\sin\theta$ gives the formula.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #poles-always-critical}
The points
$$
(1,0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(-1,0)
$$
are always critical.

::: pf-proof
They correspond to $\theta=0$ and $\theta=\pi$, where
$\sin\theta=0$. Step [](#derivative-formula){.pf-ref} therefore makes the derivative vanish at both
points for every $(a,b)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #four-critical-points-case}
If
$$
\abs{b}<2\abs{a},
$$
there are exactly two additional critical points, hence exactly four in
total.

::: pf-proof
The strict inequality forces $a\ne0$. The second factor in step [](#derivative-formula){.pf-ref}
vanishes precisely when
$$
\cos\theta=\frac{b}{2a}.
$$
Because
$$
\abs{\frac{b}{2a}}<1,
$$
this equation has exactly two solutions modulo $2\pi$, and neither has
$\sin\theta=0$. They therefore give two critical points distinct from those
in step [](#poles-always-critical){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #two-critical-points-case}
If
$$
\abs{b}\ge2\abs{a}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(a,b)\ne(0,0),
$$
there are exactly two critical points.

::: pf-proof
If $a=0$, then $b\ne0$ and
$$
F_{0,b}'(\theta)=-b\sin\theta,
$$
so only the two points in step [](#poles-always-critical){.pf-ref} are critical.

Suppose $a\ne0$. If $\abs{b}>2\abs{a}$, then
$$
\abs{\frac{b}{2a}}>1,
$$
so the equation $2a\cos\theta=b$ has no solution. If
$\abs{b}=2\abs{a}$, its only possible solutions have
$\cos\theta=\pm1$, hence are already the two points from step [](#poles-always-critical){.pf-ref}. Thus
there are exactly two distinct critical points in either case.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #origin-everything-critical}
At $(a,b)=(0,0)$, every point of the circle is critical.

::: pf-proof
Here $f_{0,0}$ is identically zero on the circle.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #regions-boxed}
Therefore the requested parameter sets are
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{exactly four critical points:}\quad
&\abs{b}<2\abs{a},\\
\text{exactly two critical points:}\quad
&\abs{b}\ge2\abs{a},\quad (a,b)\ne(0,0).
\end{aligned}
}
$$

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#four-critical-points-case){.pf-ref} and [](#two-critical-points-case){.pf-ref} give the two cases, while step [](#origin-everything-critical){.pf-ref} records the unique
parameter value belonging to neither case.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#regions-boxed){.pf-ref} is the required description of the two regions.
:::
