::: {.problem}
Let $b$ be a nonzero real $n\times1$ column vector and set
\[
M=bb^T.
\]

1. Prove that there is an orthogonal matrix $Q$ such that $QMQ^{-1}=D$ is diagonal, and find $D$.
2. Describe geometrically the linear transformation $M:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
u\coloneqq\frac{b}{\norm{b}}.
$$

<1>1. The vector $u$ is an eigenvector of $M$ with eigenvalue
$$
\norm{b}^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $M=bb^{\mathsf T}$,
$$
Mu
=
b\,b^{\mathsf T}\frac{b}{\norm{b}}
=
b\frac{\norm{b}^2}{\norm{b}}
=
\norm{b}^2u.
$$
:::

<1>2. Every vector in $b^\perp$ is an eigenvector of $M$ with eigenvalue
$0$.

::: {.proof}
If $v\in b^\perp$, then
$$
b^{\mathsf T}v=0.
$$
Hence
$$
Mv=b(b^{\mathsf T}v)=0.
$$
:::

<1>3. There is an orthogonal matrix $Q$ such that
$$
\boxed{
QMQ^{-1}
=
D
\coloneqq
\operatorname{diag}(\norm{b}^2,0,\ldots,0)
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Extend the unit vector $u$ to an orthonormal basis
$$
u,u_2,\ldots,u_n
$$
with $u_2,\ldots,u_n\in b^\perp$. Let $P$ be the orthogonal matrix whose
columns are these basis vectors and put
$$
Q\coloneqq P^{\mathsf T}.
$$
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 show that the matrix of $M$ in this orthonormal basis is
$D$. Therefore
$$
QMQ^{-1}
=
P^{\mathsf T}MP
=
D.
$$
:::

<1>4. Geometrically,
$$
\boxed{
M=\norm{b}^2P_{\operatorname{span}\{b\}},
}
$$
where $P_{\operatorname{span}\{b\}}$ is orthogonal projection onto the
line spanned by $b$.

::: {.proof}
For $x\in\RR^n$, orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{span}\{b\}$ is
$$
P_{\operatorname{span}\{b\}}(x)
=
\frac{b^{\mathsf T}x}{\norm{b}^2}b.
$$
Multiplying by $\norm{b}^2$ gives
$$
\norm{b}^2P_{\operatorname{span}\{b\}}(x)
=
b(b^{\mathsf T}x)
=
Mx.
$$
Thus $M$ kills the hyperplane $b^\perp$ and multiplies the component along
$b$ by $\norm{b}^2$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves part (1), and step <1>4 gives the geometric description in
part (2).
:::
:::
