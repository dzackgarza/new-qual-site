::: {.problem}
Evaluate
$$
\int_C\frac{e^z-1}{z^2(z-1)}\,dz,
$$
The depicted curve winds twice clockwise around $0$ and twice
counterclockwise around $1$. Thus
$$
\operatorname{Ind}_C(0)=-2,\qquad
\operatorname{Ind}_C(1)=2.
$$

The figure appears in [de Souza and Silva, *Berkeley Problems in
Mathematics*, Problem 5.11.18 (Sp81), pp. 81–82](https://hoclieuxanh.com/download-file/61e72d2e-93b4-4bce-ba30-e7a6c03ac0eb/Barkaley%20Problems%20%28chap%201%20_%207%29.pdf#page=87).
The retained exam PDF lacks it.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For
$$
F(z)\coloneqq\frac{e^z-1}{z^2(z-1)},
$$
the only singularities are simple poles at $0$ and $1$, with
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}F=-1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{Res}_{z=1}F=e-1.
$$

::: pf-proof

At $z=0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}F
&=
\lim_{z\to0}
\frac{e^z-1}{z(z-1)}\\
&=
\left(\lim_{z\to0}\frac{e^z-1}{z}\right)
\left(\lim_{z\to0}\frac1{z-1}\right)\\
&=
1\cdot(-1)
=
-1.
\end{aligned}
$$
At $z=1$,
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=1}F
=
\lim_{z\to1}\frac{e^z-1}{z^2}
=
e-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Reading the arrow and the two nested turns at each marked pole gives
$\operatorname{Ind}_C(0)=-2$ and $\operatorname{Ind}_C(1)=2$. Hence
$$
\boxed{
\int_C\frac{e^z-1}{z^2(z-1)}\,dz
=
4\pi i e
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The residue theorem and step [](#s1){.pf-ref} give
$$
\int_C F(z)\,dz
=2\pi i\left((-2)(-1)+2(e-1)\right)
=4\pi i e.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

The integral is $4\pi i e$.
