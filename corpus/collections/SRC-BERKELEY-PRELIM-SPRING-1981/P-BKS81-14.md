::: {.problem}
The real symmetric $3\times3$ matrices form a six-dimensional real vector space, hence may be identified with $\mathbb R^6$.
Show that the subset consisting of matrices of signature $(2,1)$ is open and connected in the usual topology on $\mathbb R^6$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\Sigma_{2,1}
$$
denote the set of real symmetric $3\times3$ matrices of signature $(2,1)$,
and put
$$
J\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1).
$$

<1>1. The set $\Sigma_{2,1}$ is open.

::: {.proof}
Fix $A\in\Sigma_{2,1}$. By the spectral theorem and rescaling the three
eigenvectors, there is an invertible matrix $S$ such that
$$
A=SJS^{\mathsf T}.
$$
For a symmetric matrix $B$ near $A$, write
$$
S^{-1}BS^{-\mathsf T}
=
J+E.
$$
The matrix $E$ is symmetric and tends to $0$ as $B\to A$. Choose the
neighborhood of $A$ so that
$$
\norm{E}_{\mathrm{op}}<1.
$$

If $v$ lies in
$$
P\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\},
$$
then
$$
v^{\mathsf T}(J+E)v
\ge
\norm{v}^2-\norm{E}_{\mathrm{op}}\norm{v}^2
>0
$$
for $v\ne0$. Thus $J+E$ has a positive-definite subspace of dimension $2$.
On the other hand,
$$
e_3^{\mathsf T}(J+E)e_3
\le
-1+\norm{E}_{\mathrm{op}}
<0,
$$
so it has a negative-definite subspace of dimension at least $1$. Since the
ambient dimension is $3$, its inertia is exactly $(2,1)$ and it has no zero
eigenvalues. Congruence preserves inertia, so $B$ also has signature
$(2,1)$. Hence every $A\in\Sigma_{2,1}$ has a neighborhood contained in
$\Sigma_{2,1}$.
:::

<1>2. Every matrix in $\Sigma_{2,1}$ can be joined to $J$ by a path lying
entirely in $\Sigma_{2,1}$.

<2>1. Let $A\in\Sigma_{2,1}$. There is an orthogonal matrix
$Q\in SO(3)$ and numbers
$$
\lambda_1,\lambda_2>0,
\qquad
\lambda_3<0,
$$
such that
$$
A
=
Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)Q^{\mathsf T}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The spectral theorem gives such a diagonalization with $Q\in O(3)$. If
$\det Q=-1$, multiply one column of $Q$ by $-1$. This does not change the
diagonalization and changes the determinant of $Q$ to $1$.
:::

<2>2. The path
$$
D_t
\coloneqq
\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(
(1-t)\lambda_1+t,\,
(1-t)\lambda_2+t,\,
(1-t)\lambda_3-t
\bigr),
\qquad
0\le t\le1,
$$
has signature $(2,1)$ for every $t$ and joins the diagonal matrix of
eigenvalues to $J$.

::: {.proof}
The first two diagonal entries are convex combinations of positive numbers
and are therefore positive. The third is a convex combination of the
negative numbers $\lambda_3$ and $-1$, hence is negative. Thus the
signature remains $(2,1)$ throughout.
:::

<2>3. There is a path from $QJQ^{\mathsf T}$ to $J$ inside
$\Sigma_{2,1}$.

::: {.proof}
The group $SO(3)$ is path connected: every element is a rotation about some
axis, and continuously scaling its rotation angle to zero gives a path to
the identity. Choose such a path $Q_t$ from $Q$ to $I$. Then
$$
Q_tJQ_t^{\mathsf T}
$$
is a continuous path of matrices congruent to $J$, hence every member has
signature $(2,1)$. Its endpoints are $QJQ^{\mathsf T}$ and $J$.
:::

<2>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
First use the path $QD_tQ^{\mathsf T}$ from step <2>2 to join $A$ to
$QJQ^{\mathsf T}$, then use the path from step <2>3 to join that matrix to
$J$.
:::

<1>3. The set $\Sigma_{2,1}$ is connected.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, every point of $\Sigma_{2,1}$ can be joined to the same point
$J$ by a path in $\Sigma_{2,1}$. Hence $\Sigma_{2,1}$ is path connected,
and therefore connected.
:::

<1>4. Thus the signature-$(2,1)$ locus is open and connected in
$\RR^6$.

::: {.proof}
Openness is step <1>1 and connectedness is step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
