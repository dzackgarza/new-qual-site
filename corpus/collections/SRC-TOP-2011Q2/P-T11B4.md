::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained from the sphere $S^2$ by joining the North and South poles together with a straight line segment.

(i) Describe a structure of a CW complex on $X$.

(ii) Compute the homology of $X$ using cellular homology, with decomposition from (i).
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $N,S$ be the north and south poles.
Choose two distinct meridian arcs $a,b$ on $S^2$ from $S$ to $N$, so that $a\cup b$ is a simple closed curve, and let $c$ be the added segment from $S$ to $N$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cw-structure}
(i) $X$ is a CW complex with $0$-cells $N,S$, $1$-cells $a,b,c$, and $2$-cells $u,v$, the two components of $S^2\setminus(a\cup b)$.

::: pf-proof
Each of $a,b,c$ is an arc with endpoints in the $0$-skeleton $\{N,S\}$, and their interiors are disjoint.
The closures of the two components of $S^2\setminus(a\cup b)$ are discs attached along the circle $a\cup b$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #chain-complex}
Orient $a,b,c$ from $S$ to $N$ and $u,v$ so that $\partial_2(u)=a-b$ and $\partial_2(v)=b-a$. In the bases $(u,v)$, $(a,b,c)$, and $(S,N)$, the cellular chain complex is
$$0\to\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{\partial_2}\ZZ^3\xrightarrow{\partial_1}\ZZ^2\to0,\qquad
[\partial_2]=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
[\partial_1]=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1&-1\\1&1&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

::: pf-proof
The two $2$-cells have the same boundary circle $a\cup b$, with opposite induced orientations.
Each $1$-cell runs from $S$ to $N$, so its cellular boundary is $N-S$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #homology-groups}
$H_0(X)\cong\ZZ$, $H_1(X)\cong\ZZ$, and $H_2(X)\cong\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#chain-complex){.pf-ref}, $\operatorname{im}\partial_1=\ZZ(N-S)$, so $H_0(X)=\ZZ^2/\langle N-S\rangle\cong\ZZ$.

$\ker\partial_1=\{(x,y,z)\in\ZZ^3:x+y+z=0\}$ is free of rank $2$, and $\operatorname{im}\partial_2=\ZZ(1,-1,0)$ is generated by a primitive vector of it, which extends to the basis $(1,-1,0),(-1,0,1)$.
Hence $H_1(X)\cong\ZZ$, generated by the class of $c-a$.

There is no $3$-cell, so $H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2$.
Since $\partial_2(m,n)=(m-n)(1,-1,0)$, the kernel is $\ZZ(u+v)\cong\ZZ$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#cw-structure){.pf-ref} answers (i).
For (ii), step [](#homology-groups){.pf-ref} and the absence of cells above dimension $2$ give
$$H_k(X)\cong\begin{cases}\ZZ,&k=0,1,2,\\0,&k\ge3.\end{cases}$$
:::
