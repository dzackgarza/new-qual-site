::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained from the sphere $S^2$ by joining the North and South poles together with a straight line segment.

(i) Describe a structure of a CW complex on $X$.

(ii) Compute the homology of $X$ using cellular homology, with decomposition from (i).
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $N,S$ be the north and south poles. Choose two distinct meridian arcs $a,b$ on $S^2$, each running from $S$ to $N$, so that $a\cup b$ is a simple closed curve. Let $c$ be the added straight line segment from $S$ to $N$.
::: {.proof}
The two meridians divide $S^2$ into two open discs. Together with the added chord, they give the desired $1$-skeleton.
:::

<1>2. A CW structure on $X$ has two $0$-cells $N,S$, three $1$-cells $a,b,c$, and two $2$-cells $u,v$, the two components of $S^2\setminus(a\cup b)$.
::: {.proof}
Each of $a,b,c$ has endpoints in the $0$-skeleton and otherwise disjoint interior. The closures of the two complementary components of $S^2\setminus(a\cup b)$ are discs attached along the circle $a\cup b$. No cells of dimension greater than $2$ are needed.
:::

<1>3. Orient $a,b,c$ from $S$ to $N$, and orient $u,v$ so that
$$
\partial_2(u)=a-b,
\qquad
\partial_2(v)=b-a.
$$
::: {.proof}
The two $2$-cells have the same boundary circle with opposite induced orientations.
:::

<1>4. The cellular chain complex is
$$
0\longrightarrow \mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}\mathbb Z^3
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}\mathbb Z^2
\longrightarrow0,
$$
where, in the bases $(u,v)$, $(a,b,c)$, and $(S,N)$,
$$
[\partial_2]=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1\\
-1&1\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
[\partial_1]=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&-1&-1\\
1&1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
::: {.proof}
The formula for $\partial_2$ is <1>3. Since each $1$-cell is oriented from $S$ to $N$, its cellular boundary is $N-S$.
:::

<1>5. The zeroth homology is $H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
The image of $\partial_1$ is the rank-one subgroup generated by $N-S$, so
$$
H_0(X)=\mathbb Z^2/\langle N-S\rangle\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
:::

<1>6. The first homology is $H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4,
$$
\ker\partial_1=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z^3:x+y+z=0\},
$$
a free abelian group of rank $2$, while
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_2=\mathbb Z(1,-1,0)
$$
is a primitive rank-one subgroup of this kernel. Hence
$$
H_1(X)=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
For example, the class of $c-a$ generates the quotient.
:::

<1>7. The second homology is $H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
There is no $3$-cell, so $H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2$. From <1>4,
$$
\partial_2(m,n)=(m-n)(1,-1,0),
$$
so the kernel is $\mathbb Z(1,1)\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>8. Thus
$$
H_k(X)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,1,2,\\
0,&k\ge3.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>5--<1>7 with the absence of cells above dimension $2$.
:::
:::
