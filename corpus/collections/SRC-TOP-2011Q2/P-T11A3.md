::: {.problem}
Prove that every metrizable space is normal Hausdorff (aka $T_4$).
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $d$ be a metric inducing the topology of $X$.

<1>1. $X$ is Hausdorff.

::: {.proof}
If $x\neq y$, let $r=d(x,y)/3$.
A point $z\in B(x,r)\cap B(y,r)$ would give $d(x,y)\le d(x,z)+d(z,y)<2r=\frac23d(x,y)$, a contradiction; so $B(x,r)$ and $B(y,r)$ are disjoint.
:::

<1>2. Let $A,B\subseteq X$ be disjoint nonempty closed sets. Then $d(a,B)>0$ for every $a\in A$ and $d(b,A)>0$ for every $b\in B$.

::: {.proof}
Because $B$ is closed and $a\notin B$, the open set $X\setminus B$ contains a ball $B(a,\varepsilon)$, so $d(a,B)\ge\varepsilon>0$.
The same argument with $A$ and $B$ interchanged gives $d(b,A)>0$.
:::

<1>3. With $A$ and $B$ as in step <1>2, the sets
$$U=\bigcup_{a\in A}B\!\left(a,\frac{d(a,B)}3\right),
\qquad
V=\bigcup_{b\in B}B\!\left(b,\frac{d(b,A)}3\right)$$
are disjoint open sets with $A\subseteq U$ and $B\subseteq V$.

::: {.proof}
They are unions of open balls, and each center lies in its ball because the radius is positive by step <1>2.
Suppose $z\in U\cap V$, and choose $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ with $d(z,a)<d(a,B)/3$ and $d(z,b)<d(b,A)/3$.
Since $d(a,B)\le d(a,b)$ and $d(b,A)\le d(a,b)$,
$$d(a,b)\le d(a,z)+d(z,b)<\frac{d(a,B)+d(b,A)}3\le\frac23d(a,b),$$
which is impossible because $d(a,b)>0$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the Hausdorff property.
If $A$ or $B$ is empty, the open sets $X$ and $\varnothing$ separate them; otherwise step <1>3 separates them.
Hence $X$ is normal and Hausdorff, that is, $T_4$.
:::
:::
