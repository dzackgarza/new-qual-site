::: {.problem}
Prove that every metrizable space is normal Hausdorff (aka $T_4$).
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $d$ be a metric inducing the topology on $X$.
::: {.proof}
It suffices to prove that the metric topology is Hausdorff and normal.
:::

<1>2. The space $X$ is Hausdorff.
::: {.proof}
If $x\neq y$, let $r=d(x,y)/3$. Then $B(x,r)$ and $B(y,r)$ are disjoint: otherwise a point $z$ in their intersection would give
$$
d(x,y)\le d(x,z)+d(z,y)<2r=\frac23d(x,y),
$$
a contradiction.
:::

<1>3. Let $A,B\subseteq X$ be disjoint closed sets. If either is empty, they are separated by the open sets $X$ and $\varnothing$; hence assume both are nonempty.
::: {.proof}
This removes the degenerate case before distances to the opposite set are used.
:::

<1>4. For every $a\in A$ and $b\in B$, the numbers $d(a,B)$ and $d(b,A)$ are positive.
::: {.proof}
Because $B$ is closed and $a\notin B$, the open set $X\setminus B$ contains a ball $B(a,\varepsilon)$, so $d(a,B)\ge\varepsilon>0$. The same argument with $A$ and $B$ interchanged gives $d(b,A)>0$.
:::

<1>5. Define
$$
U=\bigcup_{a\in A}B\!\left(a,\frac{d(a,B)}3\right),
\qquad
V=\bigcup_{b\in B}B\!\left(b,\frac{d(b,A)}3\right).
$$
Then $U$ and $V$ are open, with $A\subseteq U$ and $B\subseteq V$.
::: {.proof}
They are unions of open balls, and each center belongs to its corresponding ball because the radii are positive by <1>4.
:::

<1>6. The open sets $U$ and $V$ are disjoint.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $z\in U\cap V$. Choose $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ such that
$$
d(z,a)<\frac{d(a,B)}3,
\qquad
d(z,b)<\frac{d(b,A)}3.
$$
Since $b\in B$ and $a\in A$,
$$
d(a,B)\le d(a,b),\qquad d(b,A)\le d(a,b).
$$
Thus
$$
d(a,b)\le d(a,z)+d(z,b)
<\frac{d(a,B)+d(b,A)}3
\le\frac23d(a,b),
$$
which is impossible.
:::

<1>7. Therefore $X$ is normal Hausdorff, hence $T_4$.
::: {.proof}
Hausdorffness is <1>2. By <1>3--<1>6, every pair of disjoint closed subsets has disjoint open neighborhoods, so $X$ is normal.
:::
:::
