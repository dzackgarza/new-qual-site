::: {.problem}
Let $p:X\to Y$ be a quotient map.
Show that if $Y$ is connected and moreover each set $p^{-1}(\{y\})$ is connected, then $X$ is connected.
:::

::: solution
**Theorem.**  
For quotient $p:X\to Y$, if $Y$ is connected and every fiber $p^{-1}(\{y\})$ is connected, then $X$ is connected.

*Proof.*

1. Assume for contradiction that $X=U\sqcup V$ with $U,V$ non-empty, disjoint, clopen subsets.
2. Define $A=p(U)$ and $B=p(V)$.
   By surjectivity, $A\cup B=Y$ and $A\cap B=\varnothing$.
3. For $y\in A$, choose $x\in U\cap p^{-1}(y)$.
   The fiber $p^{-1}(y)$ is connected and meets the clopen set $U$, so
   $p^{-1}(y)\subseteq U$.
   Hence $p^{-1}(A)=U$.
4. Similarly, $p^{-1}(B)=V$.
5. As $p$ is quotient, a subset $E\subseteq Y$ is open (resp. closed)
   exactly when $p^{-1}(E)$ is open (resp. closed).
   Therefore $A$ and $B$ are both open and closed in $Y$.
6. Since $Y=A\sqcup B$ with $A,B\ne\varnothing$, this contradicts connectedness of $Y$.

Hence such $U,V$ do not exist and $X$ is connected. ∎
:::
