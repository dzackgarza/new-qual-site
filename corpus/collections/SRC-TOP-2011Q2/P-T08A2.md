::: {.problem}
Let $p:X\to Y$ be a quotient map.
Show that if $Y$ is connected and moreover each set $p^{-1}(\{y\})$ is connected, then $X$ is connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose $X=U\sqcup V$ with $U$ and $V$ disjoint open subsets of $X$.
It suffices to show that $U$ or $V$ is empty.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #fiber-in-one-side}
Every fiber $p^{-1}(\{y\})$ lies entirely in $U$ or entirely in $V$.

::: pf-proof
Fix $y\in Y$.
The fiber $p^{-1}(\{y\})$ is the disjoint union of $p^{-1}(\{y\})\cap U$ and $p^{-1}(\{y\})\cap V$, both open in the subspace topology on the fiber.
Since the fiber is connected, one of them is empty.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #preimage-saturated}
$p^{-1}(p(U))=U$ and $p^{-1}(p(V))=V$.

::: pf-proof
Clearly $U\subseteq p^{-1}(p(U))$.
Conversely, if $x\in p^{-1}(p(U))$, then $p(x)=p(u)$ for some $u\in U$, so $x$ and $u$ lie in the same fiber.
By step [](#fiber-in-one-side){.pf-ref} that fiber, since it meets $U$, lies entirely in $U$, so $x\in U$.
The same argument applies to $V$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #images-separate-y}
$p(U)$ and $p(V)$ are disjoint open subsets of $Y$ with $Y=p(U)\cup p(V)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#preimage-saturated){.pf-ref}, $p^{-1}(p(U))=U$ is open, so $p(U)$ is open in $Y$ because $p$ is a quotient map; likewise $p(V)$ is open.
A quotient map is surjective, so $Y=p(U)\cup p(V)$.
If $y\in p(U)\cap p(V)$, then the fiber over $y$ meets both $U$ and $V$, contrary to step [](#fiber-in-one-side){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Since $Y$ is connected, step [](#images-separate-y){.pf-ref} gives $p(U)=\varnothing$ or $p(V)=\varnothing$, hence $U=\varnothing$ or $V=\varnothing$.
Therefore $X$ is connected.
:::
