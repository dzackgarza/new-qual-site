::: {.problem}
Prove that if $f:X\to Y$ is continuous and $C$ is a compact subset of $X$, then $f(C)$ is a compact subset of $Y$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $\mathcal U$ be an arbitrary open cover of $f(C)$ by open subsets of $Y$.
::: {.proof}
To prove that $f(C)$ is compact, it suffices to show that every such cover has a finite subcover.
:::

<1>2. The family $\{f^{-1}(U):U\in\mathcal U\}$ is an open cover of $C$.
::: {.proof}
Each $f^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$ because $f$ is continuous. If $x\in C$, then $f(x)\in f(C)$, so some $U\in\mathcal U$ contains $f(x)$; hence $x\in f^{-1}(U)$.
:::

<1>3. There exist $U_1,\ldots,U_n\in\mathcal U$ such that
$$
C\subseteq f^{-1}(U_1)\cup\cdots\cup f^{-1}(U_n).
$$
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, the inverse images form an open cover of the compact space $C$, so compactness of $C$ gives a finite subcover.
:::

<1>4. Then $f(C)\subseteq U_1\cup\cdots\cup U_n$.
::: {.proof}
If $y\in f(C)$, write $y=f(x)$ for some $x\in C$. By <1>3, $x\in f^{-1}(U_i)$ for some $i$, so $y=f(x)\in U_i$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore $f(C)$ is compact.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, the arbitrary open cover $\mathcal U$ has a finite subcover. This is the definition of compactness.
:::
:::
