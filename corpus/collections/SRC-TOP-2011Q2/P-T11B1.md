::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the space obtained by deleting three distinct points from $\mathbb R^2$.
Compute $\pi_1(X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let the deleted points be $p_1,p_2,p_3$, and choose pairwise disjoint closed discs $D_i$ centered at $p_i$.
::: {.proof}
The points are distinct, so sufficiently small closed discs around them are pairwise disjoint.
:::

<1>2. The punctured plane $X=\mathbb R^2\setminus\{p_1,p_2,p_3\}$ deformation retracts onto a graph $G$ consisting of the three circles $\partial D_i$ joined to one common basepoint by pairwise disjoint arcs.
::: {.proof}
Choose a large closed disc $D$ containing the $D_i$ in its interior. Radial contraction outside $D$ retracts $X$ onto $D\setminus\{p_1,p_2,p_3\}$. Replacing each puncture by the boundary circle $\partial D_i$ gives a deformation retract onto the compact planar surface
$$
D\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^3\operatorname{int}(D_i).
$$
Choose three pairwise disjoint arcs from one point of $\partial D$ to the three inner boundary circles. Cutting the surface along these arcs yields a disc, so collapsing that disc to the union of the arcs and boundary circles gives a deformation retract onto $G$.
:::

<1>3. The graph $G$ is homotopy equivalent to the wedge $S^1\vee S^1\vee S^1$.
::: {.proof}
The joining arcs form a tree. Collapsing that tree to the common basepoint is a homotopy equivalence of graphs and leaves the three boundary circles as three wedge summands.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\pi_1(X)\cong \pi_1(S^1\vee S^1\vee S^1)\cong F_3,
$$
the free group on three generators.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>3, $X$ is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of three circles. Van Kampen's theorem identifies the fundamental group of a wedge of three circles with the free product $\mathbb Z*\mathbb Z*\mathbb Z=F_3$.
:::
:::
