::: {.problem}
Suppose $A,B$ are disjoint, compact subspaces of the Hausdorff topological space $X$.
Prove there are open subsets $U,V$ of $X$ such that $A\subseteq U$, $B\subseteq V$, and $U\cap V=\varnothing$.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $A=\varnothing$, take $U=\varnothing$ and $V=X$; if $B=\varnothing$, take $U=X$ and $V=\varnothing$.
Assume from now on that $A$ and $B$ are nonempty.
For every $(a,b)\in A\times B$ we have $a\neq b$, so since $X$ is Hausdorff we may choose disjoint open sets $U_{a,b},V_{a,b}\subseteq X$ with $a\in U_{a,b}$ and $b\in V_{a,b}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #separate-a-from-b}
For each $a\in A$ there are disjoint open sets $U_a\ni a$ and $V_a\supseteq B$.

::: pf-proof
The family $\{V_{a,b}:b\in B\}$ is an open cover of the compact space $B$, so there are $b_1,\ldots,b_n\in B$ with $B\subseteq V_{a,b_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{a,b_n}$.
Put $U_a=\bigcap_{i=1}^n U_{a,b_i}$ and $V_a=\bigcup_{i=1}^n V_{a,b_i}$.
These are open as a finite intersection and a finite union of open sets, $a\in U_a$, and $B\subseteq V_a$.
If $x\in U_a\cap V_a$, then $x\in V_{a,b_i}$ for some $i$ while $x\in U_a\subseteq U_{a,b_i}$, contradicting $U_{a,b_i}\cap V_{a,b_i}=\varnothing$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #finite-cover-of-a}
There exist $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in A$ with $A\subseteq U_{a_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{a_m}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#separate-a-from-b){.pf-ref}, $\{U_a:a\in A\}$ is an open cover of the compact space $A$, so it has a finite subcover.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Set $U=\bigcup_{j=1}^m U_{a_j}$ and $V=\bigcap_{j=1}^m V_{a_j}$.
Both are open because the union and the intersection are finite.
By step [](#finite-cover-of-a){.pf-ref}, $A\subseteq U$, and by step [](#separate-a-from-b){.pf-ref}, $B\subseteq V_{a_j}$ for every $j$, so $B\subseteq V$.
If $x\in U\cap V$, then $x\in U_{a_j}\cap V_{a_j}$ for some $j$, contradicting step [](#separate-a-from-b){.pf-ref}.
Hence $U$ and $V$ are disjoint.
:::
