::: {.problem}
Suppose $A,B$ are disjoint, compact subspaces of the Hausdorff topological space $X$.
Prove there are open subsets $U,V$ of $X$ such that $A\subseteq U$, $B\subseteq V$, and $U\cap V=\varnothing$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $A=\varnothing$ or $B=\varnothing$, the result is immediate; hence assume both are nonempty.
::: {.proof}
If, for example, $A=\varnothing$, take $U=\varnothing$ and $V=X$; the other case is symmetric.
:::

<1>2. For every $(a,b)\in A\times B$, choose disjoint open sets $U_{a,b},V_{a,b}\subseteq X$ with
$$
a\in U_{a,b},\qquad b\in V_{a,b}.
$$
::: {.proof}
This is possible because $X$ is Hausdorff and $a\neq b$ since $A\cap B=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>3. Fix $a\in A$. There exist $b_1,\ldots,b_n\in B$ such that
$$
B\subseteq V_{a,b_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{a,b_n}.
$$
::: {.proof}
The family $\{V_{a,b}:b\in B\}$ is an open cover of the compact space $B$, so it has a finite subcover.
:::

<1>4. Define
$$
U_a=\bigcap_{i=1}^n U_{a,b_i},
\qquad
V_a=\bigcup_{i=1}^n V_{a,b_i}.
$$
Then $U_a,V_a$ are open, $a\in U_a$, $B\subseteq V_a$, and $U_a\cap V_a=\varnothing$.
::: {.proof}
Finite intersections and finite unions of open sets are open. The containment statements follow from <1>2 and <1>3. If $x\in U_a\cap V_a$, then $x\in V_{a,b_i}$ for some $i$ while $x\in U_a\subseteq U_{a,b_i}$, contradicting $U_{a,b_i}\cap V_{a,b_i}=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>5. There exist $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in A$ such that
$$
A\subseteq U_{a_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{a_m}.
$$
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, the family $\{U_a:a\in A\}$ is an open cover of the compact space $A$, so it has a finite subcover.
:::

<1>6. Set
$$
U=\bigcup_{j=1}^m U_{a_j},
\qquad
V=\bigcap_{j=1}^m V_{a_j}.
$$
Then $U,V$ are open, $A\subseteq U$, and $B\subseteq V$.
::: {.proof}
The sets are open because the unions and intersections are finite. The first containment is <1>5. By <1>4, $B\subseteq V_{a_j}$ for every $j$, hence $B\subseteq V$.
:::

<1>7. The sets $U$ and $V$ are disjoint.
::: {.proof}
If $x\in U\cap V$, then $x\in U_{a_j}$ for some $j$. Since $x\in V$, we also have $x\in V_{a_j}$, contradicting <1>4.
:::

<1>8. Therefore $A$ and $B$ have disjoint open neighborhoods in $X$.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>6 and <1>7.
:::
:::
