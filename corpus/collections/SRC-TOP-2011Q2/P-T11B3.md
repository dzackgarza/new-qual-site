::: {.problem}
(i) Describe a way of identifying pairs of faces of $\Delta^3$ (the standard three simplex) to produce a $\Delta$-complex structure on $S^3$ having a single 3-simplex.

(ii) Write down the chain complex corresponding to the $\Delta$-complex in (i). Be sure to include the differentials of the complex, but you do not need to compute the homology.
:::

::: {.solution}
Label the vertices of $\Delta^3$ by $v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3$, and write
$$F_0=[v_1,v_2,v_3],\quad F_1=[v_0,v_2,v_3],\quad F_2=[v_0,v_1,v_3],\quad F_3=[v_0,v_1,v_2].$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #identification-scheme}
(i) Identify $F_0$ with $F_1$ by the order-preserving affine map $v_1\mapsto v_0$, $v_2\mapsto v_2$, $v_3\mapsto v_3$, and $F_2$ with $F_3$ by $v_0\mapsto v_0$, $v_1\mapsto v_1$, $v_3\mapsto v_2$. The quotient is a $\Delta$-complex with one $3$-simplex, homeomorphic to $S^3$.

::: pf-proof
Both maps preserve vertex order, so they are admissible $\Delta$-complex face identifications, and they pair the four faces of the single $3$-simplex.
Regard the tetrahedron as the join $\Delta^3=[v_0,v_1]*[v_2,v_3]$.
Since $F_0=\{v_1\}*[v_2,v_3]$ and $F_1=\{v_0\}*[v_2,v_3]$, the first pairing identifies the two endpoints of $[v_0,v_1]$ and fixes the coordinate in $[v_2,v_3]$; likewise the second pairing identifies the two endpoints of $[v_2,v_3]$ and fixes the coordinate in $[v_0,v_1]$.
Hence the quotient is $([v_0,v_1]/\partial)*([v_2,v_3]/\partial)\cong S^1*S^1\cong S^3$, by the join homeomorphism $S^p*S^q\cong S^{p+q+1}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cells-of-quotient}
The quotient has two vertices $A=[v_0]=[v_1]$ and $B=[v_2]=[v_3]$, three edges $e=[v_0,v_1]$, $f=[v_2,v_3]$, and $c=[v_0,v_2]=[v_0,v_3]=[v_1,v_2]=[v_1,v_3]$, two $2$-simplices $P=F_0=F_1$ and $Q=F_2=F_3$, and one $3$-simplex $\sigma=[v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3]$.

::: pf-proof
The first pairing identifies $v_1$ with $v_0$, $[v_1,v_2]$ with $[v_0,v_2]$, and $[v_1,v_3]$ with $[v_0,v_3]$.
The second identifies $v_3$ with $v_2$, $[v_0,v_3]$ with $[v_0,v_2]$, and $[v_1,v_3]$ with $[v_1,v_2]$.
The edges $[v_0,v_1]$ and $[v_2,v_3]$ are identified with nothing else.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boundary-maps}
$\partial_3(\sigma)=0$, $\partial_2(P)=f$, $\partial_2(Q)=e$, $\partial_1(e)=\partial_1(f)=0$, and $\partial_1(c)=B-A$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#cells-of-quotient){.pf-ref}, $\partial\sigma=F_0-F_1+F_2-F_3=P-P+Q-Q=0$.
With $P=[v_1,v_2,v_3]$ and $Q=[v_0,v_1,v_3]$,
$$\partial P=[v_2,v_3]-[v_1,v_3]+[v_1,v_2]=f-c+c=f,\qquad \partial Q=[v_1,v_3]-[v_0,v_3]+[v_0,v_1]=c-c+e=e.$$
The edges $e$ and $f$ are loops at $A$ and $B$, and $c$ runs from $A$ to $B$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#identification-scheme){.pf-ref} answers (i).
For (ii), in the bases $\sigma$ of $C_3$, $(P,Q)$ of $C_2$, $(e,f,c)$ of $C_1$, and $(A,B)$ of $C_0$, step [](#boundary-maps){.pf-ref} gives the chain complex
$$0\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{0}\ZZ^2
\xrightarrow{\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\\0&0\end{psmallmatrix}}\ZZ^3
\xrightarrow{\begin{psmallmatrix}0&0&-1\\0&0&1\end{psmallmatrix}}\ZZ^2\to0.$$
:::
