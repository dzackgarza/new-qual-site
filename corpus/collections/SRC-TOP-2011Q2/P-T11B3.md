::: {.problem}
(i) Describe a way of identifying pairs of faces of $\Delta^3$ (the standard three simplex) to produce a $\Delta$-complex structure on $S^3$ having a single 3-simplex.

(ii) Write down the chain complex corresponding to the $\Delta$-complex in (i). Be sure to include the differentials of the complex, but you do not need to compute the homology.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Label the vertices of $\Delta^3$ by $v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3$, and write
$$
F_0=[v_1,v_2,v_3],\quad F_1=[v_0,v_2,v_3],\quad
F_2=[v_0,v_1,v_3],\quad F_3=[v_0,v_1,v_2].
$$
Identify $F_0$ with $F_1$ by the affine map
$$
v_1\mapsto v_0,\qquad v_2\mapsto v_2,\qquad v_3\mapsto v_3,
$$
and identify $F_2$ with $F_3$ by
$$
v_0\mapsto v_0,\qquad v_1\mapsto v_1,\qquad v_3\mapsto v_2.
$$
::: {.proof}
These are affine identifications of the four boundary faces in two pairs, so they define a $\Delta$-complex quotient with one $3$-simplex.
:::

<1>2. The resulting quotient is homeomorphic to $S^3$.
::: {.proof}
Regard the tetrahedron as the join
$$
\Delta^3=\Delta^1_{01}*\Delta^1_{23},
$$
where $\Delta^1_{01}=[v_0,v_1]$ and $\Delta^1_{23}=[v_2,v_3]$. The first face pairing identifies the two endpoints $v_0,v_1$ of the first interval while leaving the second interval coordinate unchanged; the second pairing identifies the two endpoints $v_2,v_3$ of the second interval while leaving the first coordinate unchanged. Hence the quotient is
$$
(\Delta^1_{01}/\partial\Delta^1_{01})*(\Delta^1_{23}/\partial\Delta^1_{23})
\cong S^1*S^1.
$$
The standard join homeomorphism $S^p*S^q\cong S^{p+q+1}$ gives $S^1*S^1\cong S^3$.
:::

<1>3. In the quotient there are two vertices
$$
A=[v_0]=[v_1],\qquad B=[v_2]=[v_3],
$$
three edges
$$
e=[v_0,v_1],\qquad f=[v_2,v_3],\qquad c=[v_0,v_2]=[v_0,v_3]=[v_1,v_2]=[v_1,v_3],
$$
and two $2$-simplices
$$
P=F_0=F_1,\qquad Q=F_2=F_3.
$$
::: {.proof}
These are exactly the vertex, edge, and face identifications induced by <1>1.
:::

<1>4. Thus the simplicial chain groups are
$$
0\longrightarrow C_3\cong\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\partial_3}C_2\cong\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}C_1\cong\mathbb Z^3
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}C_0\cong\mathbb Z^2
\longrightarrow0.
$$
::: {.proof}
Use the bases $\sigma=[v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3]$ for $C_3$, $(P,Q)$ for $C_2$, $(e,f,c)$ for $C_1$, and $(A,B)$ for $C_0$.
:::

<1>5. With these orientations,
$$
\partial_3(\sigma)=0.
$$
::: {.proof}
The oriented boundary is
$$
\partial\sigma=F_0-F_1+F_2-F_3=P-P+Q-Q=0.
$$
:::

<1>6. The degree-two differential is
$$
\partial_2(P)=f,\qquad \partial_2(Q)=e.
$$
::: {.proof}
Using $P=[v_1,v_2,v_3]$ and $Q=[v_0,v_1,v_3]$,
$$
\partial P=[v_2,v_3]-[v_1,v_3]+[v_1,v_2]=f-c+c=f,
$$
and
$$
\partial Q=[v_1,v_3]-[v_0,v_3]+[v_0,v_1]=c-c+e=e.
$$
:::

<1>7. The degree-one differential is
$$
\partial_1(e)=0,\qquad \partial_1(f)=0,\qquad \partial_1(c)=B-A.
$$
::: {.proof}
The edges $e$ and $f$ are loops at $A$ and $B$, respectively, while $c$ is oriented from $A$ to $B$.
:::

<1>8. Therefore, in the displayed bases, the chain complex is
$$
0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\\0&0\end{psmallmatrix}}\mathbb Z^3
\xrightarrow{\begin{psmallmatrix}0&0&-1\\0&0&1\end{psmallmatrix}}\mathbb Z^2\to0.
$$
::: {.proof}
This is the matrix form of <1>5--<1>7.
:::
:::
