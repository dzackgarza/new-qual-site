::: {.problem}
On $[0,\pi]$, solve
$$
u_{tt}=9u_{xx}+\sin(1000t)
$$
with initial position
$$
u(x,0)=2\sin(17x)+5\sin(10x)
$$
and initial velocity
$$
u_t(x,0)=5\sin x-2\sin(3x).
$$

(a) Find a special solution independent of $x$.

(b) Find a solution of the homogeneous equation with the given initial data.

(c) Write the final solution.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

A particular solution independent of $x$ and with zero initial position and velocity is
$$
\boxed{u_p(t)=\frac{t}{1000}-\frac{\sin(1000t)}{1000^2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $u_p$ is independent of $x$, then $(u_p)_{xx}=0$, so the forced equation reduces to
$$
u_p''(t)=\sin(1000t).
$$
Integrating twice gives
$$
u_p(t)=-\frac{\sin(1000t)}{1000^2}+c_1t+c_2.
$$
The conditions $u_p(0)=0$ and $u_p'(0)=0$ give $c_2=0$ and $c_1=1/1000$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For the homogeneous equation $u_{tt}=9u_{xx}$, a sine mode $\sin(nx)$ has temporal frequency $3n$.


::: pf-proof

For $u(x,t)=\sin(nx)T(t)$, substitution into $u_{tt}=9u_{xx}$ gives
$$
T''(t)=-9n^2T(t),
$$
so
$$
T(t)=A\cos(3nt)+B\sin(3nt).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The homogeneous solution with the prescribed initial data is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u_h(x,t)={}&2\sin(17x)\cos(51t)
+5\sin(10x)\cos(30t)\\
&+\frac53\sin x\sin(3t)
-\frac29\sin(3x)\sin(9t).
\end{aligned}}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the two initial-position modes evolve as
$$
2\sin(17x)\cos(51t),
\qquad
5\sin(10x)\cos(30t).
$$
For a pure initial-velocity mode $b_n\sin(nx)$, the term
$$
\frac{b_n}{3n}\sin(nx)\sin(3nt)
$$
has zero initial position and initial velocity $b_n\sin(nx)$. Applying this with $b_1=5$ and $b_3=-2$ gives the remaining two terms. Consequently
$$
u_h(x,0)=2\sin(17x)+5\sin(10x)
$$
and
$$
(u_h)_t(x,0)=5\sin x-2\sin(3x).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The requested solution of the forced problem is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u(x,t)={}&2\sin(17x)\cos(51t)
+5\sin(10x)\cos(30t)\\
&+\frac53\sin x\sin(3t)
-\frac29\sin(3x)\sin(9t)\\
&+\frac{t}{1000}-\frac{\sin(1000t)}{1000^2}.
\end{aligned}}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $u_p$ satisfies the forced equation and has zero initial data. By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $u_h$ satisfies the homogeneous equation and has exactly the prescribed initial data. Linearity therefore implies that $u=u_h+u_p$ satisfies the forced equation and both initial conditions.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (b), and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves part (c).

:::
