::: {.problem}

(a) Match the four discrete systems $x(t+1)=A_ix(t)$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_1&=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\0&2\end{pmatrix},&
A_2&=\begin{pmatrix}\cos1&-\sin1\\\sin1&\cos1\end{pmatrix},\\
A_3&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},&
A_4&=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\0&\tfrac12\end{pmatrix},
\end{aligned}
$$
to phase portraits a--d.

(b) Match the four continuous systems $x'=B_ix$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_1&=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},&
B_2&=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\\
B_3&=\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\0&-2\end{pmatrix},&
B_4&=\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\0&2\end{pmatrix},
\end{aligned}
$$
to the same phase portraits.

The four source portraits are: (a) a saddle, (b) counterclockwise circles about the origin, (c) a repelling improper node, and (d) vertical trajectories moving away from the $x$-axis.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For the discrete systems $A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4$, respectively, the matching portraits are
$$
\boxed{\mathrm c,\ \mathrm b,\ \mathrm d,\ \mathrm a}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $A_1$, the only eigenvalue is $2$, with a nontrivial Jordan block. Since $\abs{2}>1$, every nonzero orbit is repelled from the origin, and the Jordan block produces the sheared-node geometry of portrait c. The matrix $A_2$ is rotation through one radian, so its orbits lie on circles and rotate counterclockwise, giving portrait b. For $A_3$, the recurrence is
$$
x_{n+1}=x_n,
\qquad
y_{n+1}=2y_n,
$$
so trajectories are vertical lines moving away from the $x$-axis, which is portrait d. Finally, $A_4$ has eigenvalues $2$ and $1/2$; one eigendirection expands and the other contracts, giving the saddle portrait a.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For the continuous systems $B_1,B_2,B_3,B_4$, respectively, the matching portraits are
$$
\boxed{\mathrm b,\ \mathrm d,\ \mathrm a,\ \mathrm c}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $B_1$ one has $x'=-y$ and $y'=x$, so
$$
\frac{d}{dt}(x^2+y^2)=2xx'+2yy'=0,
$$
and the vector field points counterclockwise; this is portrait b. For $B_2$, the equations are $x'=0$ and $y'=y$, hence trajectories are vertical lines moving away from the $x$-axis, which is portrait d. The eigenvalues of $B_3$ are $2$ and $-2$, so the origin is a saddle, giving portrait a. The matrix $B_4$ has the repeated positive eigenvalue $2$ and a nontrivial Jordan block; indeed
$$
e^{tB_4}=e^{2t}\begin{pmatrix}1&2t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$
so nonzero trajectories are repelled with the sheared-node geometry of portrait c.

:::
