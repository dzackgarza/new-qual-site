::: {.problem}
(a) Among the five matrices
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
B=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
C=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
D=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
E=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$
connect pairs that are similar and have the same eigenvalues.

(b) Match the four initial-value problems
$$
f''+f=2\sin t,
\quad
f''+f=6\sin(2t),
\quad
f''-f=2\sin t,
\quad
f''-f=6\sin(2t),
$$
all with $f(0)=1$, $f'(0)=0$, to the four candidate solutions
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm A&:\quad f(t)=\cos t+\sin t-t\cos t,\\
\mathrm B&:\quad f(t)=-0.7e^{-t}+1.7e^t-1.2\sin(2t),\\
\mathrm C&:\quad f(t)=e^t-\sin t,\\
\mathrm D&:\quad f(t)=\cos t+4\sin t-2\sin(2t).
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The similarity classes in part (a) are
$$
\boxed{\{A,B,E\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{C,D\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The matrices $A$, $B$, and $E$ each have characteristic polynomial
$$
(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1).
$$
Because the two eigenvalues are distinct, each matrix is diagonalizable and hence similar to $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. Thus every pair among $A,B,E$ is similar. Likewise, both $C$ and $D$ have characteristic polynomial $\lambda(\lambda-2)$, so both are diagonalizable and similar to $\operatorname{diag}(2,0)$. The two classes cannot mix because similar matrices have the same eigenvalues.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

In the order in which the four initial-value problems are listed, the matching candidates are
$$
\boxed{\mathrm A,\ \mathrm D,\ \mathrm C,\ \mathrm B}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Direct differentiation gives the following identities for the four candidate functions:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{candidate}&f(0)&f'(0)&\text{differential equation}\\ \hline
\mathrm A&1&0&f''+f=2\sin t\\
\mathrm D&1&0&f''+f=6\sin(2t)\\
\mathrm C&1&0&f''-f=2\sin t\\
\mathrm B&1&0&f''-f=6\sin(2t).
\end{array}
$$
For example, the resonant term in candidate A satisfies
$$
\left(\frac{d^2}{dt^2}+1\right)(-t\cos t)=2\sin t,
$$
while its $\cos t+\sin t$ terms solve the homogeneous equation. For candidate B, the exponential terms solve $f''-f=0$, and
$$
\left(\frac{d^2}{dt^2}-1\right)(-1.2\sin(2t))=6\sin(2t).
$$
The remaining two rows follow immediately from differentiating their displayed formulas. Hence each candidate satisfies exactly the listed initial-value problem.

:::
