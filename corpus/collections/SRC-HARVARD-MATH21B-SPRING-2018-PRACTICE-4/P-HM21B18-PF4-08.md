::: {.problem}
Let
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}4&3\\2&5\end{pmatrix}.
$$

(a) Compute $A^2$ and $A^{-1}$.

(b) Find the QR decomposition of $A$.

(c) Find the characteristic polynomial of $A$.

(d) If $A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix $D$, find $D$; otherwise explain why none exists.

(e) Find a $2\times2$ matrix $X$ satisfying
$$
AX=A+A^2.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\boxed{
A^2=\begin{pmatrix}22&27\\18&31\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A^{-1}=\frac1{14}\begin{pmatrix}5&-3\\-2&4\end{pmatrix}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives the displayed $A^2$. Also
$$
\det A=4\cdot5-3\cdot2=14\ne0,
$$
so the $2\times2$ inverse formula gives the displayed $A^{-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

A QR decomposition of $A$ is
$$
\boxed{
A=QR,
\quad
Q=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
\quad
R=\begin{pmatrix}2\sqrt5&11/\sqrt5\\0&7/\sqrt5\end{pmatrix}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $v_1=(4,2)^T$ and $v_2=(3,5)^T$ be the columns of $A$. Then
$$
q_1=\frac{v_1}{\norm{v_1}}=\frac1{\sqrt5}\binom21.
$$
Furthermore,
$$
v_2-\langle q_1,v_2\rangle q_1
=\binom35-\frac{11}{5}\binom21
=\frac75\binom{-1}{2},
$$
so $q_2=(-1,2)^T/\sqrt5$. Hence $Q=(q_1\ q_2)$ is orthogonal, and
$$
R=Q^TA
=\begin{pmatrix}2\sqrt5&11/\sqrt5\\0&7/\sqrt5\end{pmatrix}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The characteristic polynomial is
$$
\boxed{\chi_A(\lambda)=\lambda^2-9\lambda+14=(\lambda-2)(\lambda-7)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the trace-determinant formula for a $2\times2$ matrix,
$$
\chi_A(\lambda)=\lambda^2-(\operatorname{tr}A)\lambda+\det A
=\lambda^2-9\lambda+14.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The matrix $A$ is similar to
$$
\boxed{D=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&7\end{pmatrix}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that $A$ has the two distinct real eigenvalues $2$ and $7$. Eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent, so $A$ is diagonalizable, with a diagonal form whose diagonal entries are $2$ and $7$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

A matrix satisfying $AX=A+A^2$ is
$$
\boxed{X=I_2+A=\begin{pmatrix}5&3\\2&6\end{pmatrix}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Distributivity gives
$$
A(I_2+A)=A+A^2,
$$
so the displayed $X$ satisfies the required equation.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} prove parts (a)--(e), respectively.
