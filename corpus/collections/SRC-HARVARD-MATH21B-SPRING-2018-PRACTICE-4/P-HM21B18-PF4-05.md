::: {.problem}
Using least squares, find the best parabola
$$
y=ax+bx^2
$$
fitting
$$
(1,2),(0,1),(2,8),(-1,1).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
0&0\\
2&4\\
-1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
c=\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\8\\1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The least-squares coefficients satisfy the normal equations
$$
M^TM\,\beta=M^Tc.
$$


::: pf-proof

For each data point $(x_i,y_i)$, the model value is $ax_i+bx_i^2$, so the residual vector is $M\beta-c$. Minimizing its squared Euclidean norm gives the normal equations $M^T(M\beta-c)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The normal equations are
$$
\begin{pmatrix}6&8\\8&18\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}17\\35\end{pmatrix},
$$
and their unique solution is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}13/22\\37/22\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives
$$
M^TM=\begin{pmatrix}6&8\\8&18\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M^Tc=\begin{pmatrix}17\\35\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Since
$$
\det(M^TM)=6\cdot18-8^2=44\ne0,
$$
the solution is unique, and
$$
(M^TM)^{-1}=\frac1{44}\begin{pmatrix}18&-8\\-8&6\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Multiplying by $M^Tc$ yields
$$
a=\frac{18\cdot17-8\cdot35}{44}=\frac{13}{22},
\qquad
b=\frac{-8\cdot17+6\cdot35}{44}=\frac{37}{22}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The best-fitting parabola is
$$
\boxed{y=\frac{13}{22}x+\frac{37}{22}x^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute the coefficients from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} into the prescribed model $y=ax+bx^2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the requested least-squares fit.

:::
