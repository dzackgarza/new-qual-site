::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
\dot x=x-y^3,
\qquad
\dot y=x^3+y.
$$

(a) Find the nullclines and all equilibrium points.

(b) Analyze the stability of the equilibrium point or points.

(c) Choose which of the source-displayed phase portraits A--D belongs to the system.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The nullclines are
$$
\boxed{x=y^3\quad\text{and}\quad y=-x^3},
$$
and their unique intersection, hence the unique equilibrium, is
$$
\boxed{(0,0)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The $x$-nullcline is determined by $x-y^3=0$, and the $y$-nullcline by $x^3+y=0$. At an intersection, $x=y^3$ and therefore
$$
y=-x^3=-y^9,
$$
so $y(y^8+1)=0$. Over the reals this forces $y=0$, and then $x=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The equilibrium $(0,0)$ is unstable; in fact it is locally repelling.


::: pf-proof

The Jacobian is
$$
J(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-3y^2\\3x^2&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
J(0,0)=I_2,
$$
whose two eigenvalues are both $1>0$. Thus the linearization already identifies the origin as a hyperbolic source.

Independently, let $V=x^2+y^2$. Along a trajectory,
$$
\dot V
=2x(x-y^3)+2y(x^3+y)
=2V+2xy(x^2-y^2).
$$
Since $|2xy|\le V$ and $|x^2-y^2|\le V$,
$$
\dot V\ge2V-V^2\ge V
$$
whenever $0<V\le1$. Hence every nonzero trajectory starting sufficiently near the origin has strictly increasing $V$ and must leave any sufficiently small neighborhood in forward time. This proves instability directly.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Every nonconstant trajectory rotates counterclockwise about the origin wherever polar angle is defined.


::: pf-proof

For $V=x^2+y^2>0$,
$$
\dot\theta
=\frac{x\dot y-y\dot x}{x^2+y^2}
=\frac{x(x^3+y)-y(x-y^3)}{x^2+y^2}
=\frac{x^4+y^4}{x^2+y^2}>0.
$$
Thus the local phase portrait at the unique equilibrium is an outward counterclockwise spiral.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For part (c), the source's accepted exam choice is $\boxed{\mathrm B}$, but none of the four displayed candidates is an exact phase portrait of the system.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that an exact portrait must have exactly one equilibrium. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that this equilibrium is repelling, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that nearby trajectories rotate counterclockwise while moving outward. Comparing these properties with the four source figures leaves no exact match. The source's worked solution explicitly records this defect and states that portrait B was nevertheless counted as correct because it supplies the intended unstable-equilibrium choice.

:::
