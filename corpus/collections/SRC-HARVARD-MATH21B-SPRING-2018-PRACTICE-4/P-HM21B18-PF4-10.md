::: {.problem}
Solve
$$
f''+9f=e^t,
\qquad
f(0)=3,\quad f'(0)=0,
$$
and
$$
f''-5f'+6f=2,
\qquad
f(0)=3,\quad f'(0)=-4.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The general solution of $f''+9f=e^t$ is
$$
f(t)=c_1\cos(3t)+c_2\sin(3t)+\frac1{10}e^t.
$$


::: pf-proof

The characteristic equation of the homogeneous equation is $r^2+9=0$, with roots $\pm3i$, so the homogeneous solution is $c_1\cos(3t)+c_2\sin(3t)$. For a particular solution $f_p=Ce^t$, substitution gives
$$
(1+9)Ce^t=e^t,
$$
hence $C=1/10$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The solution of the first initial-value problem is
$$
\boxed{f(t)=\frac{29}{10}\cos(3t)-\frac1{30}\sin(3t)+\frac1{10}e^t}.
$$


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
f(0)=c_1+\frac1{10}=3,
$$
so $c_1=29/10$. Also
$$
f'(0)=3c_2+\frac1{10}=0,
$$
so $c_2=-1/30$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The general solution of $f''-5f'+6f=2$ is
$$
f(t)=c_1e^{2t}+c_2e^{3t}+\frac13.
$$


::: pf-proof

The characteristic polynomial is
$$
r^2-5r+6=(r-2)(r-3),
$$
so the homogeneous solution is $c_1e^{2t}+c_2e^{3t}$. A constant particular solution $f_p=C$ must satisfy $6C=2$, hence $C=1/3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The solution of the second initial-value problem is
$$
\boxed{f(t)=12e^{2t}-\frac{28}{3}e^{3t}+\frac13}.
$$


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the initial conditions give
$$
c_1+c_2+\frac13=3,
\qquad
2c_1+3c_2=-4.
$$
Thus $c_1+c_2=8/3$. Subtracting twice this equation from the second gives $c_2=-28/3$, and then $c_1=12$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} give the two requested solutions.

:::
