::: {.problem}
For $I_{n,j}$ as in the dyadic decomposition of $[0,1]$, define the Haar function $h_{n,j}=2^{n/2}(\chi_{I_{n+1,2j}}-\chi_{I_{n+1,2j+1}})$.

(a) Carefully draw $I_{2,1}$ and graph $h_{2,1}$.

(b) Prove that if $f\in L^2(I)$ with respect to Lebesgue measure and $\int_I f\,dt=0$, then $$\int_I |f(x)|^2\,dx=\sum_{n,j}\left|\int f(t)h_{n,j}(t)\,dt\right|^2.$$

(c) Prove that if $f\in L^1(I)$ and $\int_I f(t)\,dt=0$, then almost everywhere on $I$, $$f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\Bigl(\int f(t)h_{n,j}(t)\,dt\Bigr)h_{n,j}(x).$$ Hint: Compare the $n$th partial sum to the dyadic averages $E_n f$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove the orthonormality of the Haar basis and establish the corresponding $L^2$ and $L^1$ expansions for mean-zero functions on $I = [0, 1)$.

<1>1. Part (a): Graph of $h_{2,1}$ and dyadic interval $I_{2,1}$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. With the standard dyadic partition $I_{n,j} = [j 2^{-n}, (j+1) 2^{-n})$ for $0 \le j < 2^n$:
    $$I_{2,1} = [1 \cdot 2^{-2}, 2 \cdot 2^{-2}) = [1/4, 1/2).$$
    <2>2. The two halves of $I_{2,1}$ at level 3 are
    $$I_{3,2} = [2 \cdot 2^{-3}, 3 \cdot 2^{-3}) = [1/4, 3/8), \qquad I_{3,3} = [3 \cdot 2^{-3}, 4 \cdot 2^{-3}) = [3/8, 1/2).$$
    <2>3. By definition, $h_{2,1} = 2^{2/2}(\chi_{I_{3,2}} - \chi_{I_{3,3}}) = 2(\chi_{[1/4, 3/8)} - \chi_{[3/8, 1/2)})$.
    <2>4. Thus $h_{2,1}(x) = 2$ on $[1/4, 3/8)$, $-2$ on $[3/8, 1/2)$, and $0$ elsewhere on $[0, 1)$.

<1>2. Part (b): Parseval identity in $L^2(I)$ for mean-zero functions.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Orthonormality: For each $(n,j)$,
    $$\|h_{n,j}\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^1 |h_{n,j}(t)|^2\,dt = (2^{n/2})^2 \cdot m(I_{n+1,2j}) + (-2^{n/2})^2 \cdot m(I_{n+1,2j+1}) = 2^n \cdot 2^{-(n+1)} + 2^n \cdot 2^{-(n+1)} = 1.$$
    <2>2. If $(n, j) \neq (n', j')$ with $n = n'$, their supports $I_{n,j}$ and $I_{n,j'}$ are disjoint, so $\langle h_{n,j}, h_{n,j'} \rangle = 0$.
    <2>3. If $n < n'$, then $I_{n',j'}$ is either disjoint from $I_{n,j}$ or completely contained in one of the dyadic halves $I_{n+1,2j}$ or $I_{n+1,2j+1}$ on which $h_{n,j}$ is constant. Since $\int_{I_{n',j'}} h_{n',j'}(t)\,dt = 0$,
    $$\langle h_{n,j}, h_{n',j'} \rangle = \pm 2^{n/2} \int_{I_{n',j'}} h_{n',j'}(t)\,dt = 0.$$
    Thus the family $\{h_{n,j}\}_{n \ge 0, 0 \le j < 2^n}$ is orthonormal.
    <2>4. Let $V_N$ be the subspace of $L^2(I)$ consisting of functions constant on each dyadic interval $I_{N,j}$ of length $2^{-N}$. Then $\dim V_N = 2^N$.
    <2>5. The spaces form a nested filtration $V_0 \subset V_1 \subset V_2 \subset \cdots \subset L^2(I)$, where $V_0 = \operatorname{span}\{\chi_{[0,1)}\}$, and
    $$V_{N+1} = V_N \oplus \operatorname{span}\{h_{N,j} : 0 \le j < 2^N\}.$$
    <2>6. For $f \in L^2(I)$, let $f_N = P_{V_N} f$ denote the orthogonal projection. Since $\bigcup_{N \ge 0} V_N$ is dense in $L^2(I)$ (the step functions on dyadic intervals are dense in $L^2$), $\|f - f_N\|_{L^2} \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$.
    <2>7. Since $\int_I f\,dt = 0$, the projection $f_0 = P_{V_0} f = \left(\int_I f\,dt\right)\chi_I = 0$.
    <2>8. By Pythagoras' theorem on the orthogonal decomposition $V_N = V_0 \oplus \bigoplus_{n=0}^{N-1} \operatorname{span}\{h_{n,j}\}$:
    $$\|f_N\|_{L^2}^2 = \|f_0\|_{L^2}^2 + \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1} |\langle f, h_{n,j} \rangle|^2 = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1} \left| \int_I f(t) h_{n,j}(t)\,dt \right|^2.$$
    <2>9. Taking $N \to \infty$ yields $\int_I |f(x)|^2\,dx = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1} \left| \int_I f(t) h_{n,j}(t)\,dt \right|^2$.

<1>3. Part (c): Almost-everywhere convergence in $L^1(I)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For $f \in L^1(I)$, define the dyadic average operator $E_N f$:
    $$E_N f(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{2^N-1} \left( \frac{1}{|I_{N,j}|} \int_{I_{N,j}} f(t)\,dt \right) \chi_{I_{N,j}}(x).$$
    <2>2. By direct computation on each interval $I_{n,j} = I_{n+1,2j} \cup I_{n+1,2j+1}$, the difference between successive dyadic averages is
    $$E_{n+1} f(x) - E_n f(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1} \langle f, h_{n,j} \rangle h_{n,j}(x).$$
    <2>3. Telescoping the sum from $n = 0$ to $N - 1$:
    $$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1} \langle f, h_{n,j} \rangle h_{n,j}(x) = E_N f(x) - E_0 f(x).$$
    <2>4. Since $\int_I f(t)\,dt = 0$, the base average is $E_0 f(x) = \int_I f(t)\,dt = 0$ for all $x \in I$.
    <2>5. By Lebesgue's differentiation theorem for shrinking dyadic cubes (or Doob's martingale convergence theorem for the filtration $\sigma(I_{N,j})$), for any $f \in L^1(I)$,
    $$\lim_{N \to \infty} E_N f(x) = f(x) \quad \text{for almost every } x \in I.$$
    <2>6. Therefore, almost everywhere on $I$:
    $$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1} \left( \int_I f(t) h_{n,j}(t)\,dt \right) h_{n,j}(x).$$
:::
