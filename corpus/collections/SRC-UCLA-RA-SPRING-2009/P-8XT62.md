::: {.problem}
For $I_{n,j}$ as in the dyadic decomposition of $[0,1]$, define the Haar function $h_{n,j}=2^{n/2}(\chi_{I_{n+1,2j}}-\chi_{I_{n+1,2j+1}})$.

(a) Carefully draw $I_{2,1}$ and graph $h_{2,1}$.

(b) Prove that if $f\in L^2(I)$ with respect to Lebesgue measure and $\int_I f\,dt=0$, then $$\int_I |f(x)|^2\,dx=\sum_{n,j}\left|\int f(t)h_{n,j}(t)\,dt\right|^2.$$

(c) Prove that if $f\in L^1(I)$ and $\int_I f(t)\,dt=0$, then almost everywhere on $I$, $$f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\Bigl(\int f(t)h_{n,j}(t)\,dt\Bigr)h_{n,j}(x).$$ Hint: Compare the $n$th partial sum to the dyadic averages $E_n f$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove orthonormality of the nonconstant Haar family and its expansion results in
$L^2$ and $L^1$.

<1>1. Part (a): Description of $I_{2,1}$ and $h_{2,1}$.
    <2>1. $I_{2,1}=[1/4,1/2)$.
    <2>2. $I_{3,2}=[1/4,3/8)$ and $I_{3,3}=[3/8,1/2)$, so $I_{2,1}=I_{3,2}\cup I_{3,3}$.
    <2>3. By definition,
    $$h_{2,1}(x)=2(\chi_{I_{3,2}}(x)-\chi_{I_{3,3}}(x)),$$
    hence $h_{2,1}=2$ on $I_{3,2}$, $-2$ on $I_{3,3}$, and $0$ elsewhere.

<1>2. Part (b): Parseval identity on mean-zero $L^2(I)$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For each $(n,j)$, $h_{n,j}$ is supported on $I_{n,j}$ with values $\pm 2^{n/2}$ on two dyadic halves, hence
    $$\|h_{n,j}\|_2^2=2^{n}\cdot 2\cdot 2^{-(n+1)}=1.$$
    <2>2. If $(n,j)\neq(n',j')$, either the supports are disjoint or one support is nested in the other and the integral of the fine function over the coarse dyadic parent is zero. In each case
    $$\langle h_{n,j},h_{n',j'}\rangle=0.$$
    <2>3. Let
    $$d_n f := \sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\langle f,h_{n,j}\rangle h_{n,j}, \qquad n\ge1.$$
    By the orthonormality above, for any finite set $F\subset\mathbb N\times\mathbb N$,
    $$\left\|\sum_{(n,j)\in F} \langle f,h_{n,j}\rangle h_{n,j}\right\|_2^2
      =\sum_{(n,j)\in F}\left|\langle f,h_{n,j}\rangle\right|^2.$$
    <2>4. The spaces $V_n$ of functions constant on dyadic intervals of length $2^{-n}$ satisfy
    $$V_{n+1}=V_n\oplus \operatorname{span}\{h_{n,j}:0\le j<2^n\}, \quad f_n:=P_{V_n}f,$$
    and
    $$f_{n+1}=f_n+d_n f,$$
    where $P_{V_n}$ is the orthogonal projection onto $V_n$.
    <2>5. Orthogonality gives the martingale identity
    $$\|f_{n+1}\|_2^2=\|f_n\|_2^2+\|d_n f\|_2^2.$$
    <2>6. For mean-zero $f$, $f_0=\int_I f\,dt=0$ and $(f_n)$ is Cauchy in $L^2$ with $\|f-f_n\|_2\to0$.
    <2>7. Summing step 5 from $n=0$ to $N-1$ and letting $N\to\infty$ gives
    $$\|f\|_2^2=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\left|\langle f,h_{n,j}\rangle\right|^2.$$

<1>3. Part (c): Almost-everywhere expansion for mean-zero $L^1$ functions.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define dyadic conditional means
    $$E_n f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\left(\frac{1}{|I_{n,j}|}\int_{I_{n,j}}f\right)\chi_{I_{n,j}}(x).$$
    <2>2. The same decomposition on each level gives
    $$E_{n+1}f-E_n f=\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\langle f,h_{n,j}\rangle h_{n,j}.$$
    <2>3. Therefore for each $N\ge1$,
    $$\sum_{n=1}^N\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\langle f,h_{n,j}\rangle h_{n,j}=E_N f-E_1 f.$$
    <2>4. Since $\int_I f=0$, $E_1 f=0$ almost everywhere, hence
    $$S_N:=\sum_{n=1}^N\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\langle f,h_{n,j}\rangle h_{n,j}=E_N f.$$
    <2>5. By the dyadic differentiation theorem (equivalently, $L^1$ martingale convergence), $E_N f(x)\to f(x)$ for almost every $x\in I$.
    <2>6. Conclude
    $$f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\Bigl(\int f(t)h_{n,j}(t)\,dt\Bigr)h_{n,j}(x)$$
    for almost every $x$, as required.
:::
