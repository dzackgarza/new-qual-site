::: {.problem}
Let $I=I_{0,0}=[0,1]$ be the unit interval, and for $n=0,1,2,\ldots$ and $0\le j\le 2^n-1$ let $$I_{n,j}=[j2^{-n},(j+1)2^{-n}].$$ For $f\in L^1(I,dx)$ define $$E_n f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}\Bigl(2^n\int_{I_{n,j}}f\,dt\Bigr)\chi_{I_{n,j}}.$$ Prove that if $f\in L^1(I,dx)$ then $\lim_{n\to\infty}E_n f(x)=f(x)$ almost everywhere on $I$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Extend $f$ by $0$ outside $[0,1]$ and denote the extension by $\widetilde f\in L^1(\mathbb R)$.
Let
\[
D=\left\{\frac{k}{2^m}:m\ge0,\ 0\le k\le2^m\right\}.
\]
The set $D$ is countable, hence has Lebesgue measure zero.

<1>1. If $x\in[0,1]\setminus D$, then for every $n\ge0$ there is a unique $j_n$ such that
\[
x\in I_{n,j_n},
\]
and
\[
E_n f(x)=2^n\int_{I_{n,j_n}}f(t)\,dt.
\]
::: {.proof}
The only points that belong to two adjacent closed dyadic intervals of level $n$ are their common endpoints, all of which lie in $D$.
Thus for $x\notin D$ exactly one indicator $\chi_{I_{n,j}}(x)$ equals $1$, namely the one corresponding to $j=j_n$.
Substituting this into the definition of $E_n f$ gives the displayed formula.
:::

<1>2. For $x\in[0,1]\setminus D$, if $h_n=2^{-n}$ and $J_n=I_{n,j_n}$, then
\[
J_n\subseteq[x-h_n,x+h_n]
\]
and
\[
\left|E_n f(x)-f(x)\right|
\le
2\,\frac1{2h_n}
\int_{x-h_n}^{x+h_n}
\left|\widetilde f(t)-\widetilde f(x)\right|\,dt.
\]
::: {.proof}
The interval $J_n$ has length $h_n$ and contains $x$, hence every $t\in J_n$ satisfies $|t-x|\le h_n$.
Therefore
\[
J_n\subseteq[x-h_n,x+h_n].
\]
By <1>1 and $|J_n|=h_n$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|E_n f(x)-f(x)|
&=
\left|\frac1{h_n}\int_{J_n}(f(t)-f(x))\,dt\right|\\
&\le
\frac1{h_n}\int_{J_n}|f(t)-f(x)|\,dt\\
&\le
\frac1{h_n}\int_{x-h_n}^{x+h_n}
|\widetilde f(t)-\widetilde f(x)|\,dt\\
&=
2\,\frac1{2h_n}\int_{x-h_n}^{x+h_n}
|\widetilde f(t)-\widetilde f(x)|\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>3. For almost every $x\in[0,1]$,
\[
E_n f(x)\longrightarrow f(x).
\]
::: {.proof}
By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, almost every $x\in\mathbb R$ is a Lebesgue point of $\widetilde f$; equivalently,
\[
\lim_{h\downarrow0}
\frac1{2h}
\int_{x-h}^{x+h}
|\widetilde f(t)-\widetilde f(x)|\,dt
=0
\]
for almost every $x$.

Remove from $[0,1]$ the null set of non-Lebesgue points of $\widetilde f$ and also the countable null set $D$.
For every remaining $x$, apply <1>2 with $h_n=2^{-n}\to0$.
The right-hand side tends to zero, so
\[
|E_n f(x)-f(x)|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $E_n f(x)\to f(x)$ for almost every $x\in[0,1]$.
:::
