::: {.problem}
(a) Let $f(z)$ be complex analytic in the disc $|z| < \pi$.
Assume that the only zero of $f$ in the closed unit disc $\overline{D} = \{z : |z| \leq 1\}$ is a simple zero at the origin.
Let $C$ be the unit circle, oriented counterclockwise.
Evaluate
\[
\int_C \frac{dz}{f(z)},
\]
in the sense that no integration symbols should appear in the answer.

(b) Let $f$ be as in part (a), except assume that $f$ has a 2nd-order (i.e., double) zero at the origin.
Verify or give a counterexample:
\[
\text{Assertion:}\quad \int_C \frac{dz}{f(z)} = 0.
\]
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. In part (a), the integral is $2\pi i/f'(0)$.
::: {.proof}
Because the zero at the origin is simple, write
$$
f(z)=z g(z)
$$
with $g$ holomorphic near the closed unit disk and $g(0)=f'(0)\ne0$.
The hypothesis that zero is the only zero of $f$ in the closed unit disk
implies that $g$ has no zero there. Hence $1/f$ is meromorphic inside and on
$C$, with its only pole at zero, and that pole is simple. Its residue is
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{0}\frac1{f(z)}
=\lim_{z\to0}\frac{z}{f(z)}
=\frac1{g(0)}=\frac1{f'(0)}.
$$
Since $C$ is counterclockwise, the residue theorem gives
$$
\boxed{\int_C\frac{dz}{f(z)}=\frac{2\pi i}{f'(0)}}.
$$
:::

<1>2. The assertion in part (b) is false.
::: {.proof}
Take
$$
f(z)=z^2e^z.
$$
This function is entire and has exactly one zero, a double zero at the origin,
so it satisfies the hypotheses of part (b). Its reciprocal has Laurent
expansion
$$
\frac1{f(z)}=\frac{e^{-z}}{z^2}
=\frac1{z^2}-\frac1z+\frac12+\cdots.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{0}\frac1f=-1.
$$
There are no other poles inside the unit circle, so the residue theorem yields
$$
\int_C\frac{dz}{f(z)}=-2\pi i\ne0.
$$
Therefore the stated assertion is false.
:::
:::
