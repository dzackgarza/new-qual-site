::: {.problem}
For $x\in\mathbb R$, let
$$
f_1(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{e^{2\pi i x\xi}}{1+\xi^2}\,d\xi,
$$
calculate $f_1(x)$ by the method of residues.
:::


::: {.solution}
For every real $x$,
$$
\boxed{f_1(x)=\pi e^{-2\pi|x|}}.
$$

The integral converges absolutely: its absolute integrand
is $(1+\xi^2)^{-1}$, bounded on $[-1,1]$ and at most
$\xi^{-2}$ outside that interval. Therefore limits of
the truncated real integrals below give the ordinary integral.

<1>1. For $x>0$, close the contour in the upper half-plane.
::: {.proof}
Set
$$
F_x(z)=\frac{e^{2\pi i xz}}{1+z^2}.
$$
On the upper semicircle $z=Re^{i\theta}$,
$$
|e^{2\pi i xz}|=e^{-2\pi xR\sin\theta}\le1,
\qquad |1+z^2|\ge R^2-1.
$$
Hence the arc integral has modulus at most
$\pi R/(R^2-1)$, which tends to zero. The only pole in the
upper half-plane is $z=i$, and
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=i}F_x
=\frac{e^{2\pi i x i}}{2i}
=\frac{e^{-2\pi x}}{2i}.
$$
The residue theorem on the counterclockwise upper contour therefore gives [@SS03]
$$
f_1(x)=2\pi i\frac{e^{-2\pi x}}{2i}
=\pi e^{-2\pi x}.
$$
:::

<1>2. For $x<0$, close in the lower half-plane; the case $x=0$ is direct.
::: {.proof}
When $x<0$, the same estimate holds on the lower semicircle because
$\operatorname{Im}z\le0$ and $x\operatorname{Im}z\ge0$.
The contour is clockwise and encloses only $z=-i$. Its residue is
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i}F_x
=\frac{e^{2\pi i x(-i)}}{-2i}
=-\frac{e^{2\pi x}}{2i}.
$$
The residue theorem with clockwise orientation gives [@SS03]
$$
f_1(x)=-2\pi i\left(-\frac{e^{2\pi x}}{2i}\right)
=\pi e^{2\pi x}=\pi e^{-2\pi|x|}.
$$
For $x=0$,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\xi}{1+\xi^2}=\pi,
$$
either from the same upper contour or from the elementary antiderivative.
This matches the displayed formula.
:::
:::
