::: problem
Let $\alpha > 1/2$ and consider
\[
f_\alpha(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} (1 + \xi^2)^{-\alpha} e^{2\pi i x\xi}\,d\xi.
\]
Without doing the integration, determine, for each $\alpha$, which of the following properties holds for $f_\alpha$, and prove that your answer is correct:

(a) (i) $\lim_{|x|\to\infty} |f_\alpha(x)| = 0$, (ii) $f_\alpha \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$

(b) Without appealing to the properties of the Fourier transform, show that (i) $f_\alpha \in C(\mathbb{R})$, (ii) $f_\alpha$ is bounded on $\mathbb{R}$.
:::
