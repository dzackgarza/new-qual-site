::: problem
Determine a one-to-one complex analytic mapping $f$, other than $f(z) = z$, that takes $D = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ onto itself and satisfies $f(1/3) = 1/3$.
:::


::: solution
One example is
$$
\boxed{f(z)=\frac{3-5z}{5-3z}}.
$$

<1>1. Conjugating a nontrivial rotation gives a disk automorphism fixing $1/3$.
::: proof
Let $a=1/3$ and define
$$
\phi_a(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-az}.
$$
For $|z|<1$,
$$
1-|\phi_a(z)|^2
=\frac{(1-a^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-az|^2}>0,
$$
so $\phi_a$ maps the unit disk into itself. Direct substitution shows that
$$
\phi_a^{-1}(w)=\frac{w+a}{1+aw},
$$
which also maps the disk into itself. Hence $\phi_a$ is a biholomorphic disk
automorphism and $\phi_a(a)=0$.

Now let $R(w)=-w$, a nonidentity disk automorphism fixing zero, and set
$$
f=\phi_a^{-1}\circ R\circ\phi_a.
$$
Then $f$ is a one-to-one holomorphic map of $D$ onto itself and
$$
f(a)=\phi_a^{-1}(0)=a.
$$
Because conjugation preserves whether a map is the identity and $R$ is not the
identity, $f$ is not the identity.
:::

<1>2. The conjugated map has the displayed explicit formula.
::: proof
Using $a=1/3$,
$$
f(z)=\frac{a-\phi_a(z)}{1-a\phi_a(z)}
=\frac{2a-(1+a^2)z}{1+a^2-2az}
=\frac{3-5z}{5-3z}.
$$
Its denominator cannot vanish in the unit disk because its only zero is
$z=5/3$. The formula also gives directly
$$
f(1/3)=1/3,
$$
and, for example, $f(0)=3/5\ne0$, so the map is visibly nonidentity.
:::
:::
