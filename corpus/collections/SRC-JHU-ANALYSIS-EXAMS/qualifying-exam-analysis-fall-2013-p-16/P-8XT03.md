::: {.problem}
Let $f : \RR \to \RR$ be a measurable function.
For $M>0$, prove that $\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\leq M\abs{x-y}$ for every
$x,y\in\RR$ if and only if $f$ is absolutely continuous
and $\abs{f'}\leq M$ almost everywhere.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The Lipschitz bound implies absolute continuity and the derivative bound.

::: pf-proof

Assume the stated Lipschitz inequality. For every finite
collection of pairwise disjoint intervals $(a_j,b_j)$,
$$
\sum_j\abs{f(b_j)-f(a_j)}\leq M\sum_j(b_j-a_j).
$$
Given $\varepsilon>0$, taking $\delta=\varepsilon/M$
proves absolute continuity, with a single $\delta$ valid
on the whole line. In particular $f$ is absolutely
continuous on each $[-n,n]$, hence differentiable almost
everywhere there [@Fol99]. The countable union of the
exceptional sets is null, so $f$ is differentiable almost
everywhere on $\RR$.

At any differentiability point $x$, every nonzero $h$
satisfies $\abs{(f(x+h)-f(x))/h}\leq M$. Passing to the
limit gives $\abs{f'(x)}\leq M$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Absolute continuity and the almost-everywhere bound imply the Lipschitz inequality.

::: pf-proof

Fix $x<y$. The fundamental theorem for absolutely
continuous functions on $[x,y]$ gives [@Fol99]
$$
f(y)-f(x)=\int_x^y f'(t)\,dt.
$$
Consequently
$$
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}\leq\int_x^y\abs{f'(t)}\,dt\leq M(y-x).
$$
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ covers $x>y$, and the equality
case is immediate. This proves the global Lipschitz bound.
This step uses absolute continuity only on each compact
interval. With step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, the equivalence therefore also
holds when absolute continuity on $\RR$ means
absolute continuity on every compact interval.

:::
