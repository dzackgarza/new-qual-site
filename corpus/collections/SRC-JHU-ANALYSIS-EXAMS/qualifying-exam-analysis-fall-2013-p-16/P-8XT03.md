Let $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a measurable function.
Prove the following statement: There exists $M > 0$ such that $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq M|x - y|$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ if and only if $f$ is absolutely continuous and $|f'| \le M$.
:::

::: solution
**Theorem.**  
For measurable $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$,  
$f$ is $M$-Lipschitz iff $f$ is absolutely continuous and $|f'|\le M$ a.e.

1. *(Lipschitz $\Rightarrow$ AC and derivative bound).*  
Assume $|f(x)-f(y)|\le M|x-y|$.
Then for any disjoint intervals $\{(a_i,b_i)\}$ in $\mathbb R$ with $\sum|b_i-a_i|<\delta$,
\[
\sum |f(b_i)-f(a_i)|\le M\sum|b_i-a_i|<M\delta.
\]
Choose $\delta=\varepsilon/M$ to get absolute continuity.
By Rademacher’s theorem, a Lipschitz function is differentiable a.e. with
$|f'|\le M$ a.e.

2. *(AC and $|f'|\le M$ a.e. $\Rightarrow$ Lipschitz).*  
Assume $f$ is absolutely continuous and $|f'|\le M$ a.e.
For $x<y$, absolute continuity gives
\[
f(y)-f(x)=\int_x^y f'(t)\,dt.
\]
Taking absolute values and using the bound yields
\[
|f(y)-f(x)|\le \int_x^y |f'(t)|\,dt\le M(y-x)=M|x-y|.
\]

Hence $f$ is $M$-Lipschitz.
:::
