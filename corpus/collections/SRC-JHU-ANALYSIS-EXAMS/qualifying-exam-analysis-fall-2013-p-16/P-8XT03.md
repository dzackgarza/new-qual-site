::: problem
Let $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a measurable function.
For $M>0$, prove that $|f(x)-f(y)|\leq M|x-y|$ for every
$x,y\in\mathbb R$ if and only if $f$ is absolutely continuous
and $|f'|\leq M$ almost everywhere.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. The Lipschitz bound implies absolute continuity and the derivative bound.

::: proof
Assume the stated Lipschitz inequality. For every finite
collection of pairwise disjoint intervals $(a_j,b_j)$,
$$
\sum_j|f(b_j)-f(a_j)|\leq M\sum_j(b_j-a_j).
$$
Given $\varepsilon>0$, taking $\delta=\varepsilon/M$
proves absolute continuity, with a single $\delta$ valid
on the whole line. In particular $f$ is absolutely
continuous on each $[-n,n]$, hence differentiable almost
everywhere there [@Fol13]. The countable union of the
exceptional sets is null, so $f$ is differentiable almost
everywhere on $\mathbb R$.

At any differentiability point $x$, every nonzero $h$
satisfies $|(f(x+h)-f(x))/h|\leq M$. Passing to the
limit gives $|f'(x)|\leq M$.
:::

<1>2. Absolute continuity and the almost-everywhere bound imply the Lipschitz inequality.

::: proof
Fix $x<y$. The fundamental theorem for absolutely
continuous functions on $[x,y]$ gives [@Fol13]
$$
f(y)-f(x)=\int_x^y f'(t)\,dt.
$$
Consequently
$$
|f(y)-f(x)|\leq\int_x^y|f'(t)|\,dt\leq M(y-x).
$$
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ covers $x>y$, and the equality
case is immediate. This proves the global Lipschitz bound.
The converse proof only requires absolute continuity on
each compact interval; combined with step <1>1, it also
establishes the equivalence under that convention on the line.
:::
:::
