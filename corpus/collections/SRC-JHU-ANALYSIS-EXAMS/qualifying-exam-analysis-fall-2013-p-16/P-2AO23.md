Let $U \subset \mathbb{C}$ be an open set, $f : U \to \mathbb{C}$ be a holomorphic function and $z_0 \in U$.
Prove that if $f'(z_0) = 0$, then $f$ is not one-to-one in any neighborhood of $z_0$.

::: solution
Let $V$ be any neighborhood of $z_0$ contained in $U$.
If $f$ is constant on a disk about $z_0$, its restriction
to a smaller disk in $V$ is already noninjective.
Otherwise its Taylor series at $z_0$ has a first nonconstant
term of some order $m\geq2$, since $f'(z_0)=0$.

<1>1. A small disk in $V$ has only the central zero of $f-f(z_0)$ and only the central critical point.

::: proof
The Taylor expansion gives
$$
f(z)-f(z_0)=(z-z_0)^m h(z),\qquad h(z_0)\ne0,
$$
with $h$ holomorphic near $z_0$ [@SS03]. Differentiating,
$$
f'(z)=(z-z_0)^{m-1}\bigl(mh(z)+(z-z_0)h'(z)\bigr).
$$
Both $h$ and the bracketed factor are nonzero at $z_0$.
Choose $r>0$ so that $\overline{D(z_0,r)}\subset V$ and
both factors are holomorphic and nonzero on a neighborhood
of this closed disk. Then $f-f(z_0)$ has exactly $m$
zeros in the disk counted with multiplicity, all at $z_0$,
and $f'$ has no zero in the punctured disk.
:::

<1>2. A nearby noncentral value has at least two distinct preimages in $V$.

::: proof
On the boundary circle let
$\delta=\min_{|z-z_0|=r}|f(z)-f(z_0)|>0$.
Choose $w\ne f(z_0)$ with $|w-f(z_0)|<\delta$.
The difference between $f-w$ and $f-f(z_0)$ is the
constant $f(z_0)-w$, whose modulus is strictly smaller
than $|f-f(z_0)|$ on that circle. Rouché's theorem gives
exactly $m$ zeros of $f-w$ in $D(z_0,r)$, with multiplicity
[@SS03]. None is $z_0$ since $w\ne f(z_0)$, and each
has nonzero derivative by step <1>1. Thus all are simple,
so they are $m\geq2$ distinct points with the same image $w$.
The restriction to $V$ is not injective. Since $V$ was
arbitrary, the conclusion holds in every neighborhood.
:::
:::
