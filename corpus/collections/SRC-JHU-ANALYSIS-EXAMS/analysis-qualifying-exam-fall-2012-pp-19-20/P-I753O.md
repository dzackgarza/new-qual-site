::: problem
Let $T$ be a linear transformation on $C_0(\mathbb{R})$ (the space of continuous functions of compact support) that satisfies:
$$
\|Tf\|_{L^\infty} \le \|f\|_{L^\infty}, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \left| \{x \in \mathbb{R} : |Tf(x)| > \lambda\} \right| \le \frac{\|f\|_{L^1}}{\lambda}
$$
for all $\lambda > 0$. Prove that there exists a fixed constant $C > 0$ such that
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} |Tf(x)|^2\,dx \le C \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} |f(x)|^2\,dx
$$
for all $f \in C_0(\mathbb{R})$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Use the distribution-function identity.
::: proof
For any measurable $h$,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|h(x)|^2\,dx
=2\int_0^\infty \lambda\,m\{|h|>\lambda\}\,d\lambda.
\]
This is the layer-cake formula applied to $|h|^2$. Hence
\[
\|Tf\|_2^2
=2\int_0^\infty \lambda\,m\{|Tf|>\lambda\}\,d\lambda.
\]
:::

<1>2. Decompose $f$ by continuous truncation at each level.
::: proof
Fix $\lambda>0$. Define
\[
f_{2,\lambda}(x)=
\begin{cases}
f(x),&|f(x)|\le \lambda/2,\\
\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\dfrac{f(x)}{|f(x)|},&|f(x)|>\lambda/2,
\end{cases}
\]
and put
\[
f_{1,\lambda}=f-f_{2,\lambda}.
\]
The radial truncation map
\[
z\longmapsto
\begin{cases}
z,&|z|\le\lambda/2,\\
(\lambda/2)z/|z|,&|z|>\lambda/2
\end{cases}
\]
is continuous and sends $0$ to $0$. Since $f\in C_c(\mathbb R)$, both $f_{1,\lambda}$ and $f_{2,\lambda}$ therefore belong to $C_c(\mathbb R)$, so $T$ is defined on both of them.

Moreover,
\[
\|f_{2,\lambda}\|_\infty\le\lambda/2
\]
and
\[
|f_{1,\lambda}(x)|
=(|f(x)|-\lambda/2)_+
\le |f(x)|\mathbf1_{\{|f(x)|>\lambda/2\}}.
\]
:::

<1>3. Estimate the distribution function of $Tf$.
::: proof
By the strong $(\infty,\infty)$ hypothesis,
\[
\|Tf_{2,\lambda}\|_\infty
\le\|f_{2,\lambda}\|_\infty
\le\lambda/2.
\]
Since $Tf=Tf_{1,\lambda}+Tf_{2,\lambda}$, the implication
\[
|Tf(x)|>\lambda
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|Tf_{1,\lambda}(x)|>\lambda/2
\]
holds. Therefore the weak $(1,1)$ hypothesis gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
m\{|Tf|>\lambda\}
&\le m\{|Tf_{1,\lambda}|>\lambda/2\}\\
&\le \frac{2}{\lambda}\|f_{1,\lambda}\|_1\\
&\le \frac{2}{\lambda}
\int_{\{|f(t)|>\lambda/2\}}|f(t)|\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>4. Integrate the distribution estimate.
::: proof
Substituting Step 3 into Step 1 and applying Tonelli to the nonnegative integrand,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|Tf\|_2^2
&\le 4\int_0^\infty
\int_{\mathbb R}|f(t)|
\mathbf1_{\{\lambda<2|f(t)|\}}\,dt\,d\lambda\\
&=4\int_{\mathbb R}|f(t)|
\left(\int_0^{2|f(t)|}d\lambda\right)dt\\
&=8\int_{\mathbb R}|f(t)|^2\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{\int_{\mathbb R}|Tf|^2\le8\int_{\mathbb R}|f|^2.}
\]
So the required estimate holds with $C=8$.
:::
:::
