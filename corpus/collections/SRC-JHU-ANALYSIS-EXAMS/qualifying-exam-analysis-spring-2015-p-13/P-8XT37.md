Assume $f : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ is a non-constant entire function.
Prove $f(\mathbb{C})$ is dense in $\mathbb{C}$.

::: solution
Suppose $f(\mathbb C)$ were not dense. Then some open disk $B(a,r)$ would be
disjoint from the image, so
\[
|f(z)-a|\ge r
\qquad(z\in\mathbb C).
\]
Hence
\[
g(z)=\frac1{f(z)-a}
\]
is an entire function satisfying $|g(z)|\le1/r$ for all $z$. Liouville's
theorem makes $g$ constant, and therefore $f$ constant, a contradiction.
Thus $f(\mathbb C)$ is dense in $\mathbb C$.
:::
