::: {.problem}
Let $U \subset \mathbb{C}$ be an open set.
Assume $f, g : U \to \mathbb{C}$ are holomorphic functions such that $\bar{f} g$ is holomorphic.
Prove that on each connected component of $U$, either $f$ is constant or $g$ is identically zero. In particular, if $U$ is connected, prove the global dichotomy.
:::

::: {.remark}
Connectedness is necessary for the global conclusion. On
$U=D(0,1)\cup D(3,1)$, let $f$ equal zero on the first disk
and one on the second, and let $g=1$ throughout $U$.
Then $f$, $g$, and $\overline f g$ are holomorphic, although
$f$ is not constant on $U$ and $g$ is not identically zero.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The Cauchy–Riemann equations give $\overline{f'}g=0$ on $U$.

::: {.proof}
For a continuously differentiable function $h$, set
$\partial_{\bar z}h=(h_x+ih_y)/2$. A holomorphic function
has $\partial_{\bar z}h=0$; moreover
$\partial_{\bar z}\overline f=\overline{f'}$ for
holomorphic $f$, directly from its Cauchy–Riemann equations
[@SS03]. The product rule therefore gives
$$
0=\partial_{\bar z}(\overline f g)
=\overline{f'}g+\overline f\,\partial_{\bar z}g
=\overline{f'}g.
$$
The differentiability needed here follows from holomorphy
of $f$ and $g$.
:::

<1>2. The dichotomy holds on each connected component.

::: {.proof}
Fix a component $V$ of $U$, which is open. If $g|_V=0$,
one alternative already holds. Otherwise choose $a\in V$
with $g(a)\ne0$. Continuity gives a disk about $a$ on
which $g$ is nonzero. Step <1>1 then implies $f'=0$
on that disk. The holomorphic function $f'$ vanishes
on a nonempty open subset of the connected domain $V$,
so the identity theorem gives $f'|_V=0$ [@SS03].
A holomorphic function with zero derivative is locally
constant, by its Taylor series; connectedness makes this
constant the same throughout $V$. Thus $f|_V$ is constant.
When $U$ is connected, take $V=U$ to obtain the stated
global alternative. The empty open set causes no exception
to the componentwise statement.
:::
:::
