::: {.problem}
Assume $f, f_j \subset L^2(\RR^n)$ for $j = 1, 2, \ldots$, $f_j \to f$ a.e. and $\int f_j^2 \, dx \to \int f^2 \, dx$.
Prove $\int \abs{f_j - f}^2 \, dx \to 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

The sequence is uniformly bounded in $L^2$.

::: pf-proof

By hypothesis,
$$
\norm{f_j}_2^2\longrightarrow\norm{f}_2^2.
$$
Hence there is $M<\infty$ such that
$$
\sup_j\norm{f_j}_2\le M.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Show that $f_j\rightharpoonup f$ weakly in $L^2$.

::: pf-proof

Fix $h\in L^2(\RR^n)$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Choose a bounded compactly supported function $\phi$ such that
$$
\norm{h-\phi}_2<\varepsilon.
$$
Then
$$
\abs{\int (f_j-f)(h-\phi)}
\le (M+\norm{f}_2)\varepsilon.
$$

It remains to prove
$$
\int (f_j-f)\phi\to0.
$$
Let $K$ be a finite-measure set containing the support of $\phi$. Since $f_j\to f$ almost everywhere on $K$, Egorov's theorem gives, for every $\delta>0$, a measurable set $A\subset K$ with $m(A)<\delta$ such that $f_j\to f$ uniformly on $K\setminus A$.

On $K\setminus A$,
$$
\abs{\int_{K\setminus A}(f_j-f)\phi}
\le \norm{\phi}_\infty m(K)
\sup_{K\setminus A}\abs{f_j-f}\longrightarrow0.
$$
On $A$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
\abs{\int_A(f_j-f)\phi}
\le \norm{\phi}_\infty
(\norm{f_j}_2+\norm{f}_2)m(A)^{1/2}
\le \norm{\phi}_\infty(M+\norm{f}_2)\delta^{1/2}.
$$
First choose $\delta$ small and then $j$ large. Thus
$$
\int (f_j-f)\phi\to0.
$$
Since $\varepsilon$ was arbitrary,
$$
\int f_jh\to\int fh
$$
for every $h\in L^2$, so $f_j\rightharpoonup f$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Combine weak convergence with convergence of norms.

::: pf-proof

Using the Hilbert-space identity,
$$
\norm{f_j-f}_2^2
=\norm{f_j}_2^2+\norm{f}_2^2
-2\operatorname{Re}\langle f_j,f\rangle.
$$
By Step 2,
$$
\langle f_j,f\rangle\to\norm{f}_2^2,
$$
while the hypothesis gives
$$
\norm{f_j}_2^2\to\norm{f}_2^2.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\norm{f_j-f}_2^2\longrightarrow0.}
$$

:::
