Assume $f, f_j \subset L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for $j = 1, 2, \ldots$, $f_j \to f$ a.e. and $\int f_j^2 \, dx \to \int f^2 \, dx$.
Prove $\int |f_j - f|^2 \, dx \to 0$.

::: solution
<1>1. The sequence is uniformly bounded in $L^2$.
::: proof
By hypothesis,
\[
\|f_j\|_2^2\longrightarrow\|f\|_2^2.
\]
Hence there is $M<\infty$ such that
\[
\sup_j\|f_j\|_2\le M.
\]
:::

<1>2. Show that $f_j\rightharpoonup f$ weakly in $L^2$.
::: proof
Fix $h\in L^2(\mathbb R^n)$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Choose a bounded compactly supported function $\phi$ such that
\[
\|h-\phi\|_2<\varepsilon.
\]
Then
\[
\left|\int (f_j-f)(h-\phi)\right|
\le (M+\|f\|_2)\varepsilon.
\]

It remains to prove
\[
\int (f_j-f)\phi\to0.
\]
Let $K$ be a finite-measure set containing the support of $\phi$. Since $f_j\to f$ almost everywhere on $K$, Egorov's theorem gives, for every $\delta>0$, a measurable set $A\subset K$ with $m(A)<\delta$ such that $f_j\to f$ uniformly on $K\setminus A$.

On $K\setminus A$,
\[
\left|\int_{K\setminus A}(f_j-f)\phi\right|
\le \|\phi\|_\infty m(K)
\sup_{K\setminus A}|f_j-f|\longrightarrow0.
\]
On $A$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\left|\int_A(f_j-f)\phi\right|
\le \|\phi\|_\infty
(\|f_j\|_2+\|f\|_2)m(A)^{1/2}
\le \|\phi\|_\infty(M+\|f\|_2)\delta^{1/2}.
\]
First choose $\delta$ small and then $j$ large. Thus
\[
\int (f_j-f)\phi\to0.
\]
Since $\varepsilon$ was arbitrary,
\[
\int f_jh\to\int fh
\]
for every $h\in L^2$, so $f_j\rightharpoonup f$.
:::

<1>3. Combine weak convergence with convergence of norms.
::: proof
Using the Hilbert-space identity,
\[
\|f_j-f\|_2^2
=\|f_j\|_2^2+\|f\|_2^2
-2\operatorname{Re}\langle f_j,f\rangle.
\]
By Step 2,
\[
\langle f_j,f\rangle\to\|f\|_2^2,
\]
while the hypothesis gives
\[
\|f_j\|_2^2\to\|f\|_2^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\|f_j-f\|_2^2\longrightarrow0.}
\]
:::
:::
