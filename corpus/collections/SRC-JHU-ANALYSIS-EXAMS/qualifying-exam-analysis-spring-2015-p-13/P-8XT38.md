::: {.problem}
Prove that $z^5 + 3z^3 + 7$ has all its zeros in the disk $D(0,2) = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 2\}$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. The polynomial has five zeros in $|z|<2$, counted with multiplicity.

::: proof
On $|z|=2$,
$$
|3z^3+7|\leq3\cdot2^3+7=31<32=|z^5|.
$$
Both summands are entire. Rouché's theorem therefore
gives the same number of zeros in this circle for $z^5$
and $z^5+3z^3+7$, namely five with multiplicity
[@SS03, Chapter 3, Theorem 4.3]. The strict estimate also
gives $|z^5+3z^3+7|\geq1$ on $|z|=2$.
:::

<1>2. There are no further zeros on or outside the circle.

::: proof
A nonzero degree-five polynomial has at most five zeros
counted with multiplicity: each zero of multiplicity $m$
contributes a factor $(z-a)^m$, and division by these factors
cannot remove more than the total degree [@DF04]. Step <1>1
already accounts for five inside the disk. No additional
zero can occur elsewhere, proving the assertion.
:::
:::
