::: {.problem}
Prove that if $f$ is an entire function and $|f(z)|\le a+b|z|^k$ for all $z\in\mathbb{C}$ where $a$, $b$ and $k$ are positive real numbers, then $f$ is a polynomial.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Power series expansion of $f$: <2>1. Since $f$ is an entire function, it has a global Taylor series representation centered at $0$ with infinite radius of convergence:
\[
f(z) = \sum_{m=0}^\infty c_m z^m, \quad \text{where } c_m = \frac{f^{(m)}(0)}{m!}.
\]
Proof: Taylor's Theorem for entire functions.
<2>2. By Cauchy’s Integral Formula for derivatives:
\[
c_m = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{|z| = R} \frac{f(z)}{z^{m+1}}\,dz \quad \text{for any } R > 0.
\]
Proof: Cauchy's differentiation formula along the circle $|z| = R$.

<1>2. Apply Cauchy’s Estimates: <2>1. On the circle $|z| = R$, the hypothesis gives $|f(z)| \le a + b R^k$.
Proof: hypothesis $|f(z)| \le a + b|z|^k$.
<2>2. By the $ML$-inequality along the circular path of length $2\pi R$:
\[
|c_m| \le \frac{1}{2\pi} \frac{a + b R^k}{R^{m+1}} (2\pi R) = \frac{a + b R^k}{R^m} = \frac{a}{R^m} + b R^{k - m}.
\]
Proof: Cauchy estimates on $|z| = R$.

<1>3. Show that $c_m = 0$ for all integers $m > k$: <2>1. Let $m$ be an integer strictly greater than $k$, so $k - m < 0$.
Proof: definition of $m > k$.
<2>2. The coefficient $c_m$ is independent of the choice of radius $R > 0$.
Proof: Cauchy's Integral Theorem (contour deformation).
<2>3. Taking the limit as $R \to \infty$ in <2>2 of <1>2:
\[
|c_m| \le \lim_{R \to \infty} \left(\frac{a}{R^m} + b R^{k - m}\right) = 0 + 0 = 0.
\]
Proof: $m > 0$ and $k - m < 0$.
<2>4. Thus $c_m = 0$ for all $m > k$.
Proof: $|c_m| \le 0 \implies c_m = 0$.

<1>4. Conclusion: The power series terminates after degree $d = \lfloor k \rfloor$:
\[
f(z) = \sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor k \rfloor} c_m z^m.
\]
Hence $f(z)$ is a polynomial of degree at most $\lfloor k \rfloor$.
Q.E.D. Proof: <1>1 and <1>3.
:::
