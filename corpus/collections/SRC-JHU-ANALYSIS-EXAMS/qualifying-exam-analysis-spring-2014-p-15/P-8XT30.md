::: {.problem}
Determine the number of distinct solutions of the equation

$$e^{z^2} = 5z^5$$

in the unit disk $\{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| \leq 1\}$.
:::

::: solution
There are exactly $\boxed{5}$ distinct solutions.
Let $F(z)=e^{z^2}-5z^5$.

<1>1. There are five zeros inside the unit circle counted with multiplicity, and none on the circle.

::: proof
For $|z|=1$,
$$
|e^{z^2}|=e^{\operatorname{Re}(z^2)}\leq e<5=|5z^5|.
$$
For example, the exponential series and $n!\geq2^{n-1}$
for $n\geq2$, with strict inequality for $n\geq3$, give
$e=2+\sum_{n\geq2}1/n!<3<5$.
Rouché's theorem therefore gives the same interior
zero count for $F$ and $-5z^5$, namely five with
multiplicity [@SS03]. The strict inequality also shows
that $F$ is nonzero on $|z|=1$.
:::

<1>2. Every zero in the disk is simple.

::: proof
A zero $z$ of $F$ is nonzero since $F(0)=1$. Substituting
$e^{z^2}=5z^5$ into the derivative yields
$$
F'(z)=2z e^{z^2}-25z^4
=5z^4(2z^2-5).
$$
For $|z|\leq1$ one has $|2z^2|\leq2<5$, so this
derivative is nonzero. Thus no counted zero has multiplicity
greater than one, by its Taylor expansion [@SS03].
The five zeros in step <1>1 are therefore distinct and
give all the solutions in the requested closed disk.
:::
:::
