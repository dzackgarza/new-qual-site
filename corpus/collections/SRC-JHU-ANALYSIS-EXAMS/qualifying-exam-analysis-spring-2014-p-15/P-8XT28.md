::: {.problem}
Prove that smooth compactly supported functions are dense in $L^2(\RR^n)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own, the standard truncate-and-mollify proof; no retained solution set is cited on it. Write $\tau_{y}g(x)=g(x-y)$ for the translate of $g$ by $y$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #translation}
For every $g\in L^{2}(\RR^{n})$, $\ \norm{\tau_{y}g-g}_{2}\to0$ as $y\to0$.

::: pf-proof
It suffices to prove this for characteristic functions of measurable sets of finite measure, since every $g\in L^{2}$ is an $L^{2}$-limit of finite linear combinations of such functions (bounded measurable functions of compact support are such limits by the construction of the integral, and those are dense in $L^{2}$). For $g=\mathbf{1}_{E}$ with $m(E)<\infty$,
$$
\norm{\tau_{y}g-g}_{2}^{2}=\int\abs{\mathbf{1}_{E}(x-y)-\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)}^{2}dx=m\big(E\triangle(E+y)\big),
$$
and $m\big(E\triangle(E+y)\big)\to0$ as $y\to0$: for a bounded measurable $E$ this is continuity of Lebesgue measure under translation on a fixed large ball, and a general $E$ of finite measure is approximated in measure by $E\cap B(0,R)$. The general case follows because $\norm{\tau_{y}g-g}_{2}\le\norm{\tau_{y}g-\tau_{y}g_{k}}_{2}+\norm{\tau_{y}g_{k}-g_{k}}_{2}+\norm{g_{k}-g}_{2}=2\norm{g-g_{k}}_{2}+\norm{\tau_{y}g_{k}-g_{k}}_{2}$, translations preserving the $L^{2}$ norm.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #truncate}
For $f\in L^{2}(\RR^{n})$ and $N>0$ put $f_{N}=f\,\mathbf{1}_{B(0,N)}\mathbf{1}_{\{\abs{f}\le N\}}$. Then $f_{N}$ is bounded with support in $B(0,N)$, so $f_{N}\in L^{1}\cap L^{2}$, and $\norm{f-f_{N}}_{2}\to0$.

::: pf-proof
$\abs{f-f_{N}}^{2}\le2\abs{f}^{2}\in L^{1}$, and $\abs{f-f_{N}}^{2}\to0$ pointwise: where $\abs{f(x)}\le N$ and $x\in B(0,N)$ the value is $0$, and the set where either fails shrinks as $N$ grows, its measure tending to $0$ because $\abs{f}<\infty$ a.e. and $\RR^{n}$ is exhausted by the balls. Domination plus pointwise convergence gives $\int\abs{f-f_{N}}^{2}\to0$ by the dominated convergence theorem. Clearly $\abs{f_{N}}\le N$ and $f_{N}$ vanishes off $B(0,N)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #mollify}
Let $\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\RR^{n})$ with $\rho\ge0$, $\int\rho=1$, let $\rho_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{-n}\rho(x/\delta)$ and $\varphi_{\delta}=f_{N}*\rho_{\delta}$. Then $\varphi_{\delta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\RR^{n})$ and
$$
\norm{\varphi_{\delta}-f_{N}}_{2}\le\int\rho_{\delta}(y)\,\norm{\tau_{y}f_{N}-f_{N}}_{2}\,dy .
$$

::: pf-proof
$\varphi_{\delta}$ is smooth because $\rho_{\delta}$ is, and compactly supported because $\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{\delta}\subset\overline{\operatorname{supp}f_{N}+B(0,\delta)}$, a bounded closed set. Since $\int\rho_{\delta}=1$,
$$
\varphi_{\delta}(x)-f_{N}(x)=\int\rho_{\delta}(y)\big(f_{N}(x-y)-f_{N}(x)\big)dy=\int\rho_{\delta}(y)\big(\tau_{y}f_{N}-f_{N}\big)(x)\,dy,
$$
and taking $L^{2}$ norms and applying the triangle inequality for the Bochner integral (Minkowski's integral inequality) gives the displayed bound.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #small-delta}
The right-hand side tends to $0$ as $\delta\to0$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#translation){.pf-ref}, for $\eta>0$ there is $\delta_{0}>0$ with $\norm{\tau_{y}f_{N}-f_{N}}_{2}<\eta$ whenever $\abs{y}<\delta_{0}$. For $\delta<\delta_{0}$ the integrand is supported in $\abs{y}\le\delta$, so by step [](#mollify){.pf-ref}
$$
\norm{\varphi_{\delta}-f_{N}}_{2}\le\int\rho_{\delta}(y)\,\eta\,dy=\eta\int\rho=\eta .
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #density}
$C_{c}^{\infty}(\RR^{n})$ is dense in $L^{2}(\RR^{n})$.

::: pf-proof
Let $f\in L^{2}$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By step [](#truncate){.pf-ref} choose $N$ with $\norm{f-f_{N}}_{2}<\varepsilon/2$, and by step [](#small-delta){.pf-ref} choose $\delta>0$ with $\norm{f_{N}-\varphi_{\delta}}_{2}<\varepsilon/2$. Then $\varphi_{\delta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}$ and
$$
\norm{f-\varphi_{\delta}}_{2}\le\norm{f-f_{N}}_{2}+\norm{f_{N}-\varphi_{\delta}}_{2}<\varepsilon .
$$
:::
