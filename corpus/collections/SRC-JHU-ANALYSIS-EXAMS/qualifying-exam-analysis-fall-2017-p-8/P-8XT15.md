::: {.problem}
1. Let $f_{n}$ be a sequence of continuous functions on $\RR$ satisfying $0 \leq f_{n} \leq f_{n+1} \leq 1$ for all $x \in \RR$ and $n \in \ZZ^{+}$. Let $f(x) = \lim_{n \to \infty} f_{n}(x)$. Show that if $f$ is continuous at $x$, then for any $\epsilon > 0$ there exist $\delta$ and $N$ such that $\abs{f_{n}(y) - f_{n}(x)} < \epsilon$ whenever $\abs{y - x} < \delta$ and $n > N$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Choose a sufficiently late index at the base point.

::: pf-proof

Fix $x\in\RR$ at which $f$ is continuous, and let $\varepsilon>0$. Since
$$
f_n(x)\uparrow f(x),
$$
choose $N$ so large that
$$
0\le f(x)-f_N(x)<\frac\varepsilon3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Choose a common neighborhood using continuity of $f$ and $f_N$.

::: pf-proof

Because both $f$ and $f_N$ are continuous at $x$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that whenever $\abs{y-x}<\delta$,
$$
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}<\frac\varepsilon3
$$
and
$$
\abs{f_N(y)-f_N(x)}<\frac\varepsilon3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Bound both one-sided differences uniformly for all later indices.

::: pf-proof

Let $n\ge N$ and $\abs{y-x}<\delta$. Since
$$
f_N\le f_n\le f,
$$
we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_n(y)-f_n(x)
&\le f(y)-f_N(x)\\
&=[f(y)-f(x)]+[f(x)-f_N(x)]\\
&<\frac{2\varepsilon}{3},
\end{aligned}
$$
while
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_n(x)-f_n(y)
&\le f(x)-f_N(y)\\
&=[f(x)-f_N(x)]+[f_N(x)-f_N(y)]\\
&<\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{f_n(y)-f_n(x)}<\varepsilon
$$
for every $n\ge N$ and every $y$ with $\abs{y-x}<\delta$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref}, [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} produce $N$ and $\delta$ with the required estimate for every $n\ge N$.

:::
