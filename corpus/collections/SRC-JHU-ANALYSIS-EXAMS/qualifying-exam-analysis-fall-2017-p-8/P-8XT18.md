::: {.problem}
5. Let $f$ be an entire function $| R e ( f ( z ) ) | \leq C ( 1 + | z | ) ^ { p }$ for some $p > 0 , C > 0$ . Show that $f$ is a polynomial.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $u=\Re f$. Then $u$ is harmonic on $\mathbb C$ and
$$
|u(z)| \le C(1+|z|)^p.
$$

<1>1. Every partial derivative of $u$ of order $m>p$ vanishes identically.

::: {.proof}
Fix $z_0\in\mathbb C$ and $r>0$. The interior derivative
estimate for harmonic functions gives a constant $C_m$,
depending only on $m$, such that for every multi-index
$\alpha$ with $|\alpha|=m$,
$$
|\partial^\alpha u(z_0)|
\le\frac{C_m}{r^m}\sup_{|z-z_0|\le r}|u(z)|
\le C_m\frac{C(1+|z_0|+r)^p}{r^m}.
$$
If $m>p$, the right-hand side tends to zero as
$r\to\infty$. Hence $\partial^\alpha u(z_0)=0$ for every
$z_0$.
:::

<1>2. Every complex derivative of $f$ of order $k>p$ vanishes identically.

::: {.proof}
Write $f=u+iv$. The Cauchy--Riemann equations give
$f'=u_x-iu_y$. Since $f^{(k)}=\partial_x^{k-1}f'$, the
real and imaginary parts of $f^{(k)}$ are
$\partial_x^{k}u$ and $-\partial_x^{k-1}\partial_yu$,
which are partial derivatives of $u$ of order $k$.
Step <1>1 makes both vanish when $k>p$.
:::

<1>3. $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most $\lfloor p\rfloor$.

::: {.proof}
The Taylor series of $f$ at $0$ represents $f$ on all of
$\mathbb C$ [@SS03]. By step <1>2 its coefficients
$f^{(k)}(0)/k!$ vanish for $k>p$, so
$$
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}z^k.
$$
:::
:::
