::: problem
Let $u$ be a subharmonic function on $\mathbb C$, and put
$M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}u(z)$. For $0<r_1<r_2$, prove that

$$
u(z)\leq
\frac{\log r_2-\log|z|}{\log r_2-\log r_1}M(r_1)
+\frac{\log|z|-\log r_1}{\log r_2-\log r_1}M(r_2)
$$

for $r_1\leq|z|\leq r_2$.
:::

::: remark
The displayed fractions require $r_1<r_2$. If the radii
coincide, the annulus is a circle and the corresponding
bound is simply $u(z)\leq M(r_1)$. For extended-valued
subharmonic functions, interpret the boundary bounds directly
as $u(z)\leq M(r_j)$, so no product $0\cdot(-\infty)$ is needed.
:::

::: solution
We use upper semicontinuity and the submean inequality
that define subharmonicity. First suppose $u$ is finite-valued.

<1>1. The right-hand side is a harmonic function on the open annulus.

::: proof
Upper semicontinuity implies that $u$ attains a finite
maximum on each compact circle. Let $h(z)$ be the displayed
linear interpolation of $M(r_1)$ and $M(r_2)$. It has
the form $a\log|z|+b$ for real constants $a,b$. The radial
Laplacian of $\log|z|$ for $z\ne0$ is
$-1/|z|^2+(1/|z|)(1/|z|)=0$, so $h$ is harmonic.
It is continuous on the closed annulus and equals
$M(r_j)$ on its $j$th boundary circle. Consequently
$v=u-h$ is upper semicontinuous, satisfies the submean
inequality, and is nonpositive on the boundary. The
submean inequality follows by subtracting the mean-value
equality for the harmonic function $h$ [@SS03].
:::

<1>2. The comparison $u\leq h$ holds throughout the closed annulus.

::: proof
For $\varepsilon>0$, put
$$
v_\varepsilon(z)=v(z)+\varepsilon(|z|^2-r_2^2).
$$
It is nonpositive on both boundary circles. If it were
positive somewhere, upper semicontinuity and compactness
would give a positive global maximum at an interior point $a$.
Choose a small circle $a+\rho e^{it}$ whose disk lies
in the open annulus. The submean inequality for $v$ and
the identity
$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|a+\rho e^{it}|^2\,dt
=|a|^2+\rho^2
$$
give
$$
v_\varepsilon(a)
\leq\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}v_\varepsilon(a+\rho e^{it})\,dt
-\varepsilon\rho^2
\leq v_\varepsilon(a)-\varepsilon\rho^2,
$$
a contradiction. Thus $v_\varepsilon\leq0$. Letting
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves $v\leq0$, or $u\leq h$.
:::

<1>3. The argument also covers the value $-\infty$.

::: proof
For an extended-valued subharmonic $u$, set
$u_N=\max(u,-N)$ for positive integers $N$.
This is finite-valued and upper semicontinuous. It satisfies
the submean inequality: at a center where $u_N=u$, use
the inequality for $u$ and $u\leq u_N$ on the circle;
at a center where $u_N=-N$, use $u_N\geq-N$ on the circle.
Its circular maximum is $M_N(r)=\max(M(r),-N)$.

Apply steps <1>1–<1>2 to $u_N$. At an interior point of
the annulus both interpolation coefficients are positive.
Letting $N\to\infty$ gives the required inequality in
the extended real numbers, since $u_N\downarrow u$ and
$M_N(r_j)\downarrow M(r_j)$. On a boundary circle the
bound is the defining maximum inequality. Thus all the
stated cases are covered.
:::
:::
