::: problem
1. Let $k\geq0$ and let $f$ be entire with
   $$
   \max_{|z|=R}|f(z)|\leq AR^k+B
   $$
   for positive constants $A,B$ and all $R>1000$.
   Show that $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k$.
2. Define a normal family of holomorphic functions on an open,
   possibly unbounded, domain $U$.
3. If $\mathcal F$ is normal on the open unit disk $D$, prove
   that $\mathcal F'=\{f':f\in\mathcal F\}$ is normal on $D$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. The growth bound makes every Taylor coefficient of order
greater than $k$ vanish.

::: proof
Write $f(z)=\sum_{m\geq0}a_mz^m$, the Taylor expansion
valid on the entire plane. Cauchy's coefficient formula
and the length of the circle give, for $R>1000$,
$$
|a_m|=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\int_{|\zeta|=R}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta^{m+1}}\,d\zeta\right|
\leq AR^{k-m}+BR^{-m}
$$
[@SS03]. For an integer $m>k\geq0$, both terms tend
to zero as $R\to\infty$. Hence $a_m=0$. All terms
above $\lfloor k\rfloor$ vanish, proving the required
degree bound, including constant functions when $k=0$.
The zero polynomial satisfies the same assertion.

Nonnegativity matters for the stated degree bound. If
$k<0$, the same estimate kills every coefficient with
$m\geq1$, so $f$ is constant, but need not be zero:
$f=1$, $A=B=1$ satisfy the bound for every negative $k$.
:::

<1>2. Normality requires a locally uniformly convergent subsequence
with finite holomorphic limit from every sequence in the family.

::: proof
Precisely, a family $\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal O(U)$
is normal if every sequence $(f_n)$ in it has a subsequence
$(f_{n_j})$ converging uniformly on every compact subset
of $U$ to a holomorphic function $f:U\to\mathbb C$.
The limit need not belong to $\mathcal F$. This definition
does not require $U$ to be bounded, and does not include
convergence to infinity [@SS03].
:::

<1>3. The family of derivatives is normal.

::: proof
Given a sequence $(g_n)$ in $\mathcal F'$, choose
$f_n\in\mathcal F$ with $g_n=f_n'$. Normality supplies
a subsequence $f_{n_j}\to f$ locally uniformly on $D$,
where $f$ is holomorphic. Let $K\subset D$ be a nonempty
compact set. Choose $\delta>0$ such that the compact set
$$
L=\{z+w:z\in K,\ |w|\leq\delta\}
$$
is contained in $D$. For instance, take
$\delta=(1-\max_{z\in K}|z|)/2$.
For $z\in K$, the differentiated Cauchy formula gives
$$
g_{n_j}(z)-f'(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\int_{|\zeta-z|=\delta}
\frac{f_{n_j}(\zeta)-f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^2}\,d\zeta.
$$
Consequently
$$
\sup_{z\in K}|g_{n_j}(z)-f'(z)|
\leq\delta^{-1}\sup_{\zeta\in L}|f_{n_j}(\zeta)-f(\zeta)|
\longrightarrow0.
$$
The empty compact set needs no estimate. Thus the same
subsequence converges locally uniformly to the holomorphic
function $f'$, exactly as required by step <1>2.
:::
:::
