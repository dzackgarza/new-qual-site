Prove that if a sequence $f_1, f_2, \ldots$ of functions in $L^1(\mathbb{R})$ converges to $f$ in $L^1(\mathbb{R})$, then there exists a subsequence $f_{n_1}, f_{n_2}, \ldots$ that converges a.e. to $f$.

::: solution
<1>1. Choose a subsequence with summable $L^1$ errors.
::: proof
Since
\[
\|f_n-f\|_1\longrightarrow0,
\]
we can choose
\[
n_1<n_2<\cdots
\]
so that
\[
\|f_{n_k}-f\|_1<2^{-k}
\]
for every $k\ge1$.
:::

<1>2. Apply Tonelli to the sum of the pointwise errors.
::: proof
Because the summands are nonnegative, Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}\sum_{k=1}^\infty
|f_{n_k}(x)-f(x)|\,dx
&=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\|f_{n_k}-f\|_1\\
&<\sum_{k=1}^\infty2^{-k}<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{k=1}^\infty|f_{n_k}(x)-f(x)|<\infty
\]
for almost every $x\in\mathbb R$. In particular, for almost every $x$,
\[
|f_{n_k}(x)-f(x)|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{f_{n_k}(x)\to f(x)\text{ for almost every }x\in\mathbb R.}
\]
:::
:::
