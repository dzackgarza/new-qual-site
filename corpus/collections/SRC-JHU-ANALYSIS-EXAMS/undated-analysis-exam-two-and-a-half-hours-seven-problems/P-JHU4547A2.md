::: {.problem}
(a) Let $f$ be integrable over a measurable set $A$ and suppose
\[
A=\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n,
\]
where the $A_n$ are pairwise disjoint. Show that
\[
\int_A f=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\int_{A_n}f
\]
and that the series on the right is absolutely convergent.

(b) Let $\mu$ be Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R^2$ and let $f\in L^1(\mathbb R^2)$. Show that
\[
\lambda(E):=\int_E f\,d\mu
\]
defines a finite signed Borel measure on $\mathbb R^2$. In particular, if $f\ge0$ almost everywhere, then $\lambda$ is a finite positive Borel measure and $d\lambda=f\,d\mu$.

(c) For $f(x,y)=x^2+y^2$ and $D$ the unit disc, compute $\lambda(D)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The integral is countably additive over the disjoint partition.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
B_N=\bigcup_{n=1}^N A_n.
\]
Then $\mathbf 1_{B_N}f\to \mathbf 1_Af$ pointwise and
\[
|\mathbf 1_{B_N}f|\le \mathbf 1_A|f|\in L^1.
\]
By dominated convergence,
\[
\int_A f
=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{B_N}f
=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^N\int_{A_n}f
=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\int_{A_n}f.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left|\int_{A_n}f\right|
\le
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\int_{A_n}|f|
=\int_A|f|<\infty.
\]
Hence the series is absolutely convergent.
:::

<1>2. The density defines a finite signed Borel measure.
::: {.proof}
For every Borel set $E\subseteq\mathbb R^2$, define
\[
\lambda(E)=\int_E f\,d\mu.
\]
Clearly $\lambda(\varnothing)=0$. If $(E_n)$ are pairwise disjoint Borel sets, then applying <1>1 to $f$ on $\bigcup_nE_n$ gives
\[
\lambda\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n\right)
=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\lambda(E_n),
\]
and the series is absolutely convergent. Thus $\lambda$ is countably additive.

It is finite in total variation because
\[
|\lambda(E)|\le\int_E|f|\,d\mu\le\|f\|_1,
\]
and in fact $|\lambda|(\mathbb R^2)=\int_{\mathbb R^2}|f|\,d\mu<\infty$. Therefore $\lambda$ is a finite signed Borel measure. If $f\ge0$ almost everywhere, then $\lambda(E)\ge0$ for every Borel $E$, so $\lambda$ is a finite positive Borel measure.
:::

<1>3. Compute the mass of the unit disc.
::: {.proof}
For $f(x,y)=x^2+y^2$, the density is nonnegative, so $\lambda$ is a positive measure. In polar coordinates,
\[
\lambda(D)
=\int_D(x^2+y^2)\,dx\,dy
=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1 r^2\,r\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
Hence
\[
\lambda(D)
=2\pi\left[\frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^1
=\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
:::
:::
