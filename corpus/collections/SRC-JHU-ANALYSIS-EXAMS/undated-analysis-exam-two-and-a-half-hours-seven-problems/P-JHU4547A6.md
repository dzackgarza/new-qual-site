::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be an entire function with $\abs{f(z)} \leq 3\log\abs{z}$ when $\abs{z} > 2$.
Either verify that $f$ must be constant, or give a counterexample.
:::

::: {.solution}
$f$ must be constant. The admissible functions are exactly
the constants $c$ with $\abs{c}\leq3\log2$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Every Taylor coefficient of $f$ of order $n\geq1$ vanishes.

::: pf-proof

Write $f(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}a_nz^n$ on $\CC$. For $R>2$,
Cauchy's estimate on the circle $\abs{z}=R$ gives
$$
\abs{a_n}\leq\frac{3\log R}{R^n}
$$
[@SS03]. For $n\geq1$ the right-hand side tends to zero as
$R\to\infty$, so $a_n=0$. Hence $f\equiv a_0$ is constant.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

A constant $c$ satisfies the bound exactly when $\abs{c}\leq3\log2$.

::: pf-proof

The bound requires $\abs{c}\leq3\log\abs{z}$ for every $\abs{z}>2$.
The infimum of the right-hand side over $\abs{z}>2$ is $3\log2$,
approached as $\abs{z}\downarrow2$. Thus the bound holds exactly
when $\abs{c}\leq3\log2$.

:::
