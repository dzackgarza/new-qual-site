::: {.problem}
(a) Let $C([0,1])$ denote the space of continuous functions on $[0,1]$, endowed with the supremum norm. Show that $C([0,1])$ is a Banach space.

(b) Let $B_p=L^p([0,1])$, with $1<p<\infty$. Define weak and strong convergence in $B_p$. Then show that
\[
f_n(x)=\sin(nx)
\]
converges weakly to $0$, but not strongly to $0$, in $B_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $C([0,1])$ is complete in the supremum norm.
::: {.proof}
Let $(u_n)$ be Cauchy in $\|\cdot\|_\infty$. For each $x\in[0,1]$, the scalar sequence $(u_n(x))$ is Cauchy because
\[
|u_n(x)-u_m(x)|\le \|u_n-u_m\|_\infty.
\]
Define
\[
u(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_n(x).
\]
Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ such that
\[
\|u_n-u_m\|_\infty<\varepsilon
\qquad(n,m\ge N).
\]
Fix $n\ge N$ and let $m\to\infty$. Then for every $x$,
\[
|u_n(x)-u(x)|\le\varepsilon,
\]
so
\[
\|u_n-u\|_\infty\le\varepsilon.
\]
Thus $u_n\to u$ uniformly. A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, hence $u\in C([0,1])$. Therefore $C([0,1])$ is complete.
:::

<1>2. Strong and weak convergence in $L^p$.
::: {.proof}
Strong convergence $v_n\to v$ in $L^p([0,1])$ means
\[
\|v_n-v\|_p\longrightarrow0.
\]
If $1<p<\infty$ and $q$ is the conjugate exponent, $1/p+1/q=1$, weak convergence $v_n\rightharpoonup v$ means
\[
\int_0^1 v_n(x)g(x)\,dx
\longrightarrow
\int_0^1 v(x)g(x)\,dx
\qquad(g\in L^q([0,1])).
\]
For $p=2$, this is convergence against every element of $L^2([0,1])$ under the Hilbert-space inner product.
:::

<1>3. $\sin(nx)\rightharpoonup0$ in $L^2([0,1])$.
::: {.proof}
Let $g\in L^2([0,1])$. Since $[0,1]$ has finite measure, Hölder's inequality gives $g\in L^1([0,1])$. By the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma,
\[
\int_0^1 g(x)e^{inx}\,dx\longrightarrow0.
\]
Taking imaginary parts,
\[
\int_0^1 g(x)\sin(nx)\,dx\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence $f_n\rightharpoonup0$ in $L^2([0,1])$.
:::

<1>4. The convergence is not strong.
::: {.proof}
We have
\[
\|f_n\|_2^2
=\int_0^1\sin^2(nx)\,dx
=\frac12-\frac{\sin(2n)}{4n}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|f_n\|_2^2\longrightarrow\frac12,
\]
so $\|f_n\|_2$ does not tend to $0$. Thus $f_n$ does not converge strongly to $0$ in $L^2([0,1])$.
:::
