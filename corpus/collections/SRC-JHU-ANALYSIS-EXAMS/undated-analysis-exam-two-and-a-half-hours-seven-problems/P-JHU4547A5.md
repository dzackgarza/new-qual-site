::: problem
Determine whether the equation $z^3 + z^4 = 2$ in the complex variable $z$ has any non-real solutions with $|z| < 2$.
:::

::: solution
Yes. There are exactly two nonreal solutions, and both satisfy
$|z|<\sqrt2<2$.

<1>1. Factoring off the root $1$ leaves a cubic with exactly one real root.
::: proof
The polynomial factors as
$$
z^4+z^3-2=(z-1)q(z),\qquad q(z)=z^3+2z^2+2z+2.
$$
For real $x$,
$$
q'(x)=3x^2+4x+2
=3\left(x+\frac23\right)^2+\frac23>0.
$$
Hence $q$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb R$. Since
$$
q(-2)=-2<0<1=q(-1),
$$
the intermediate value theorem gives a root $r\in(-2,-1)$,
and strict monotonicity makes it the unique real root of $q$.
Because $q$ has real coefficients, its remaining two roots are a
nonreal conjugate pair $\alpha,\overline\alpha$.
:::

<1>2. Vieta's formula places the conjugate pair strictly inside the radius-two disk.
::: proof
For the monic cubic $q$, the product of its three roots is $-2$. Thus
$$
r\alpha\overline\alpha=-2,
\qquad |\alpha|^2=-\frac2r.
$$
Since $-2<r<-1$,
$$
1<-\frac2r<2.
$$
Therefore
$$
|\alpha|=|\overline\alpha|<\sqrt2<2.
$$
Thus the original quartic has exactly two nonreal roots and both lie in the
specified disk.
:::
:::
