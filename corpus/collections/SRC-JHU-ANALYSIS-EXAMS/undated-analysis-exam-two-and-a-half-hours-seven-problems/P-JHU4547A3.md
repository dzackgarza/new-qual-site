::: {.problem}
Let $f\in L^1(\mathbb R)$. Show directly, without citing general properties of the Fourier transform, that
\[
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx
\]
is uniformly continuous and satisfies
\[
\widehat f(\xi)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(|\xi|\to\infty).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $\widehat f$ is uniformly continuous.
::: {.proof}
For $h,\xi\in\mathbb R$,
\[
\widehat f(\xi+h)-\widehat f(\xi)
=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ix\xi}(e^{-ixh}-1)f(x)\,dx.
\]
Therefore
\[
|\widehat f(\xi+h)-\widehat f(\xi)|
\le
\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{-ixh}-1|\,|f(x)|\,dx.
\]
The right-hand side is independent of $\xi$. For each fixed $x$,
\[
|e^{-ixh}-1|\to0
\qquad(h\to0),
\]
and
\[
|e^{-ixh}-1|\,|f(x)|\le2|f(x)|\in L^1(\mathbb R).
\]
By dominated convergence,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{-ixh}-1|\,|f(x)|\,dx\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence
\[
\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb R}|\widehat f(\xi+h)-\widehat f(\xi)|\longrightarrow0,
\]
which is uniform continuity.
:::

<1>2. Interval indicators have transforms tending to zero.
::: {.proof}
If $a<b$, then for $\xi\ne0$,
\[
\widehat{\mathbf1_{[a,b]}}(\xi)
=\int_a^b e^{-ix\xi}\,dx
=\frac{e^{-ia\xi}-e^{-ib\xi}}{i\xi}.
\]
Thus
\[
\left|\widehat{\mathbf1_{[a,b]}}(\xi)\right|
\le\frac{2}{|\xi|}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(|\xi|\to\infty).
\]
The same conclusion holds for every finite linear combination of indicators of bounded intervals.
:::

<1>3. Approximate an arbitrary $L^1$ function.
::: {.proof}
Finite linear combinations of indicators of bounded intervals are dense in $L^1(\mathbb R)$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose such a function $s$ with
\[
\|f-s\|_1<\varepsilon.
\]
For every $\xi$,
\[
|\widehat f(\xi)-\widehat s(\xi)|
\le\int_{\mathbb R}|f-s|
=\|f-s\|_1<\varepsilon.
\]
By <1>2, there is $R>0$ such that
\[
|\widehat s(\xi)|<\varepsilon
\qquad(|\xi|>R).
\]
Hence, for $|\xi|>R$,
\[
|\widehat f(\xi)|
\le|\widehat f(\xi)-\widehat s(\xi)|+|\widehat s(\xi)|
<2\varepsilon.
\]
Therefore $\widehat f(\xi)\to0$ as $|\xi|\to\infty$.
:::
