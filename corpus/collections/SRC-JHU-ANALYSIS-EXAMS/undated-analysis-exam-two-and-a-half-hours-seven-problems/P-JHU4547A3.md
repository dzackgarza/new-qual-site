::: {.problem}
Let $f\in L^1(\RR)$. Show directly, without citing general properties of the Fourier transform, that
$$
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\RR}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx
$$
is uniformly continuous and satisfies
$$
\widehat f(\xi)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(\abs{\xi}\to\infty).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

$\widehat f$ is uniformly continuous.

::: pf-proof

For $h,\xi\in\RR$,
$$
\widehat f(\xi+h)-\widehat f(\xi)
=\int_{\RR}e^{-ix\xi}(e^{-ixh}-1)f(x)\,dx.
$$
Therefore
$$
\abs{\widehat f(\xi+h)-\widehat f(\xi)}
\le
\int_{\RR}\abs{e^{-ixh}-1}\,\abs{f(x)}\,dx.
$$
The right-hand side is independent of $\xi$. For each fixed $x$,
$$
\abs{e^{-ixh}-1}\to0
\qquad(h\to0),
$$
and
$$
\abs{e^{-ixh}-1}\,\abs{f(x)}\le2\abs{f(x)}\in L^1(\RR).
$$
By dominated convergence,
$$
\int_{\RR}\abs{e^{-ixh}-1}\,\abs{f(x)}\,dx\longrightarrow0.
$$
Hence
$$
\sup_{\xi\in\RR}\abs{\widehat f(\xi+h)-\widehat f(\xi)}\longrightarrow0,
$$
which is uniform continuity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Interval indicators have transforms tending to zero.

::: pf-proof

If $a<b$, then for $\xi\ne0$,
$$
\widehat{\mathbf1_{[a,b]}}(\xi)
=\int_a^b e^{-ix\xi}\,dx
=\frac{e^{-ia\xi}-e^{-ib\xi}}{i\xi}.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{\widehat{\mathbf1_{[a,b]}}(\xi)}
\le\frac{2}{\abs{\xi}}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(\abs{\xi}\to\infty).
$$
The same conclusion holds for every finite linear combination of indicators of bounded intervals.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Approximate an arbitrary $L^1$ function.

::: pf-proof

Finite linear combinations of indicators of bounded intervals are dense in $L^1(\RR)$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose such a function $s$ with
$$
\norm{f-s}_1<\varepsilon.
$$
For every $\xi$,
$$
\abs{\widehat f(\xi)-\widehat s(\xi)}
\le\int_{\RR}\abs{f-s}
=\norm{f-s}_1<\varepsilon.
$$
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, there is $R>0$ such that
$$
\abs{\widehat s(\xi)}<\varepsilon
\qquad(\abs{\xi}>R).
$$
Hence, for $\abs{\xi}>R$,
$$
\abs{\widehat f(\xi)}
\le\abs{\widehat f(\xi)-\widehat s(\xi)}+\abs{\widehat s(\xi)}
<2\varepsilon.
$$
Therefore $\widehat f(\xi)\to0$ as $\abs{\xi}\to\infty$.

:::
