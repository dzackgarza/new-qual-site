::: {.problem}
Suppose that $f\in C^1([0,\pi])$ and $f(0)=f(\pi)=0$. Prove that
\[
\int_0^\pi f(x)^2\,dx\le\int_0^\pi f'(x)^2\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For $0<\varepsilon<\pi/2$, expand the nonnegative integral
\[
0\le \int_\varepsilon^{\pi-\varepsilon}
\bigl(f'(x)-f(x)\cot x\bigr)^2\,dx.
\]
We have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_\varepsilon^{\pi-\varepsilon}
\bigl(f'-f\cot x\bigr)^2
&=\int_\varepsilon^{\pi-\varepsilon}(f')^2
 +\int_\varepsilon^{\pi-\varepsilon}f^2\cot^2x
 -\int_\varepsilon^{\pi-\varepsilon}(f^2)'\cot x\\
&=\int_\varepsilon^{\pi-\varepsilon}(f')^2
 -\int_\varepsilon^{\pi-\varepsilon}f^2
 -\bigl[f(x)^2\cot x\bigr]_{\varepsilon}^{\pi-\varepsilon},
\end{aligned}
\]
because $(\cot x)'=-\csc^2x$ and $\cot^2x-\csc^2x=-1$.

It remains to pass to the endpoints. Since $f\in C^1$ and $f(0)=0$,
\[
f(x)=O(x)\qquad(x\downarrow0),
\]
so $f(x)^2\cot x=O(x)\to0$. Similarly, $f(x)^2\cot x\to0$ as $x\uparrow\pi$. Therefore, letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives
\[
0\le\int_0^\pi (f')^2-\int_0^\pi f^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\int_0^\pi f^2\le\int_0^\pi(f')^2}.
\]
Equality holds, for example, for scalar multiples of $\sin x$, so the constant is sharp.
:::
