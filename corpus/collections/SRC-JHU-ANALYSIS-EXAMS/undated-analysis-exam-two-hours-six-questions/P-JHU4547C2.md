::: {.problem}
Suppose that $f\in C^1([0,\pi])$ and $f(0)=f(\pi)=0$. Prove that
$$
\int_0^\pi f(x)^2\,dx\le\int_0^\pi f'(x)^2\,dx.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this exercise: the card's own nonnegative-square argument follows, with the integration by parts, the vanishing boundary term and the limiting step as separate steps.

Let $f\in C^{1}([0,\pi])$ with $f(0)=f(\pi)=0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #square}
For $0<\varepsilon<\pi/2$,
$$\int_{\varepsilon}^{\pi-\varepsilon}\big(f'-f\cot x\big)^{2}dx=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\pi-\varepsilon}(f')^{2}dx-\int_{\varepsilon}^{\pi-\varepsilon}f^{2}dx-\big[f^{2}\cot x\big]_{\varepsilon}^{\pi-\varepsilon}.$$

::: pf-proof
Expanding the square, $\int(f')^{2}+\int f^{2}\cot^{2}-\int 2ff'\cot$, and $2ff'\cot x=(f^{2})'\cot x$. Integrating the last term by parts with $(\cot x)'=-\csc^{2}x$ gives $-\big[f^{2}\cot x\big]_{\varepsilon}^{\pi-\varepsilon}-\int f^{2}\csc^{2}x$, and $\cot^{2}-\csc^{2}=-1$, which yields the displayed identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boundary}
$\big[f^{2}\cot x\big]_{\varepsilon}^{\pi-\varepsilon}\to0$ as $\varepsilon\to0$.

::: pf-proof
$\cot(\pi-s)=-\cot s$ and $\cot s=1/\tan s$, so the bracket is $f(\pi-\varepsilon)^{2}\cot(\pi-\varepsilon)-f(\varepsilon)^{2}\cot\varepsilon$. By the mean value theorem $f(\varepsilon)^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}f'(\xi_{\varepsilon})^{2}$ with $\xi_\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon)$, and $\varepsilon\cot\varepsilon\to1$; so $f(\varepsilon)^{2}\cot\varepsilon\to0$. The term at $\pi-\varepsilon$ is treated the same way with $\pi-x$ in place of $x$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #limit}
$\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(f')^{2}dx-\int_{0}^{\pi}f^{2}dx\ge0$.

::: pf-proof
For $0<\varepsilon<\pi/2$ the right side of the identity in step [](#square){.pf-ref} is nonnegative, being the integral of a square. As $\varepsilon\to0$ the sets $[\varepsilon,\pi-\varepsilon]$ increase to $(0,\pi)$ and the continuous functions $(f')^{2}$ and $f^{2}$ are integrable there, so $\int_{\varepsilon}^{\pi-\varepsilon}(f')^{2}\to\int_{0}^{\pi}(f')^{2}$ and likewise for $f^{2}$; the bracket tends to $0$ by step [](#boundary){.pf-ref}. Hence the limit of the right side, which is $\int_{0}^{\pi}(f')^{2}-\int_{0}^{\pi}f^{2}$, is a limit of nonnegative numbers.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
$\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}f^{2}dx\le\int_{0}^{\pi}(f')^{2}dx$ by step [](#limit){.pf-ref}.
:::
