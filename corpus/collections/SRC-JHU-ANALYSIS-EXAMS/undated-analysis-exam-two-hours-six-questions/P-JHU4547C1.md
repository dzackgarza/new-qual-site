::: {.problem}
Suppose that $f\in L^1(\mathbb R)$. Prove that for every $\epsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that
\[
\int_A |f|<\epsilon
\]
for every measurable set $A$ with $m(A)<\delta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $\epsilon>0$. Since $|f|\in L^1(\mathbb R)$, the truncations
\[
|f|\mathbf 1_{\{|f|\le M\}}
\]
increase pointwise to $|f|$ as $M\to\infty$. Hence, equivalently by monotone convergence applied to the complementary tails,
\[
\int_{\{|f|>M\}}|f|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Choose $M>0$ so large that
\[
\int_{\{|f|>M\}}|f|<\frac\epsilon2.
\]
Set
\[
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{2M}.
\]
If $A$ is measurable and $m(A)<\delta$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_A|f|
&=\int_{A\cap\{|f|\le M\}}|f|+\int_{A\cap\{|f|>M\}}|f|\\
&\le M m(A)+\int_{\{|f|>M\}}|f|\\
&<M\delta+\frac\epsilon2
=\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the Lebesgue integral of an $L^1$ function is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure in the stated sense.
:::
