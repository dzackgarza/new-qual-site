::: {.problem}
Suppose that $f\in L^1(\RR)$. Prove that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\int_A \abs{f}<\varepsilon
$$
for every measurable set $A$ with $m(A)<\delta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper, so no retained solution set covers the problem and the argument is the card's own: split the integral where $\abs f$ is bounded, and use that the tail where it is not is small.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #tail}
$\int_{\{\abs f>M\}}\abs f\to0$ as $M\to\infty$.

::: pf-proof
The functions $\abs f\mathbf 1_{\{\abs f\le M\}}$ increase pointwise to $\abs f$ as $M\to\infty$ and are dominated by $\abs f\in L^1$. Monotone convergence gives $\int\abs f\mathbf 1_{\{\abs f\le M\}}\to\int\abs f<\infty$, and $\int_{\{\abs f>M\}}\abs f=\int\abs f-\int_{\{\abs f\le M\}}\abs f$, which therefore tends to $0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #choice}
For $\varepsilon>0$ there are $M>0$ and $\delta>0$ with $\int_{\{\abs f>M\}}\abs f<\varepsilon/2$ and $\delta=\varepsilon/(2M)$.

::: pf-proof
Choose $M$ by step [](#tail){.pf-ref}, which is possible because the integrals tend to $0$; $M>0$ can be assumed, and then $\delta=\varepsilon/(2M)$ is positive.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #bound}
For every measurable $A$ with $m(A)<\delta$, $\int_A\abs f<\varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof
Split $A$ into $A\cap\{\abs f\le M\}$ and $A\cap\{\abs f>M\}$. On the first the integrand is at most $M$, so that part is at most $M\,m(A)<M\delta=\varepsilon/2$; the second part is at most $\int_{\{\abs f>M\}}\abs f<\varepsilon/2$ because it is an integral over a smaller set. Adding gives $\int_A\abs f<\varepsilon$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\boxed{\text{For every }\varepsilon>0\text{ there is }\delta>0\text{ with }\textstyle\int_A\abs f<\varepsilon\text{ whenever }m(A)<\delta}$.

::: pf-proof
Given $\varepsilon>0$, the $\delta$ of step [](#choice){.pf-ref} works by step [](#bound){.pf-ref}. This is the absolute continuity of the integral $\int\abs f\,dm$ with respect to Lebesgue measure $m$.
:::
