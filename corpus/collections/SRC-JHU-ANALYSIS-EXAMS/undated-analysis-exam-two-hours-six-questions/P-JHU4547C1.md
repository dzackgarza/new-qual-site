::: {.problem}
Suppose that $f\in L^1(\RR)$. Prove that for every $\epsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\int_A \abs{f}<\epsilon
$$
for every measurable set $A$ with $m(A)<\delta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $\epsilon>0$. Since $\abs{f}\in L^1(\RR)$, the truncations
$$
\abs{f}\mathbf 1_{\{\abs{f}\le M\}}
$$
increase pointwise to $\abs{f}$ as $M\to\infty$. Monotone convergence gives $\int\abs{f}\mathbf 1_{\{\abs{f}\le M\}}\to\int\abs{f}<\infty$, hence
$$
\int_{\{\abs{f}>M\}}\abs{f}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Choose $M>0$ so large that
$$
\int_{\{\abs{f}>M\}}\abs{f}<\frac\epsilon2.
$$
Set
$$
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{2M}.
$$
If $A$ is measurable and $m(A)<\delta$, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_A\abs{f}
&=\int_{A\cap\{\abs{f}\le M\}}\abs{f}+\int_{A\cap\{\abs{f}>M\}}\abs{f}\\
&\le M m(A)+\int_{\{\abs{f}>M\}}\abs{f}\\
&<M\delta+\frac\epsilon2
=\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the Lebesgue integral of an $L^1$ function is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure in the stated sense.
:::
