::: {.problem}
Suppose that $1<p<\infty$ and define
\[
Tf(x)=x^{-1/p}\int_0^x f(t)\,dt,
\qquad x>0.
\]
Show that $T$ is a bounded map from $L^q((0,\infty))$ to $C^0((0,\infty))$, where $q$ is conjugate to $p$:
\[
\frac1p+\frac1q=1.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $Tf$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$.
::: {.proof}
If $f\in L^q((0,\infty))$, then $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}((0,\infty))$ by Hölder's inequality. Hence
\[
F(x):=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt
\]
is absolutely continuous, and therefore continuous, on every compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$. Since $x\mapsto x^{-1/p}$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$, the product
\[
Tf(x)=x^{-1/p}F(x)
\]
is continuous on $(0,\infty)$.
:::

<1>2. $T$ is bounded, with operator norm at most $1$ for the supremum norm.
::: {.proof}
For every $x>0$, Hölder's inequality on $(0,x)$ gives
\[
\left|\int_0^x f(t)\,dt\right|
\le
\|f\|_{L^q(0,x)}\,\|1\|_{L^p(0,x)}
=x^{1/p}\|f\|_{L^q(0,x)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
|Tf(x)|
\le
\|f\|_{L^q(0,x)}
\le
\|f\|_{L^q(0,\infty)}.
\]
Taking the supremum over $x>0$ yields
\[
\|Tf\|_\infty\le\|f\|_q.
\]
Thus $T:L^q((0,\infty))\to C_b((0,\infty))\subset C^0((0,\infty))$ is a bounded linear map.
:::
:::
