::: {.problem}
Let $D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, let $U$ be an open set
containing $\overline D$, and let $f:U\to\mathbb C$ be
holomorphic and nonconstant on the connected component of
$U$ containing $\overline D$. If $|f(z)|=1$ for every
$z\in\partial D$, prove that $D\subset f(\overline D)$.
:::

::: {.remark}
Nonconstancy on all of a disconnected $U$ is insufficient.
On $U=D(0,2)\cup D(4,1)$, set $f=1$ on the first component
and $f(z)=z$ on the second. This function is holomorphic
and nonconstant on $U$, but $f(\overline D)=\{1\}$.
The component qualification excludes precisely this obstruction.
:::

::: {.solution}
In fact $f(D)=D$.

<1>1. The function has at least one zero in $D$ and satisfies $|f|<1$ there.

::: {.proof}
The maximum modulus principle applied on the closed disk
gives $|f|\leq1$ in $D$ [@SS03]. If equality held at
an interior point, $f$ would be constant on $D$ and hence,
by the identity theorem, on its component in $U$. This
contradicts the stated nonconstancy. Thus $|f|<1$ in $D$.

Suppose $f$ had no zero in $D$. It has none on the boundary
either, since the boundary modulus is one. Consequently
$1/f$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline D$.
Applying the maximum modulus principle to $1/f$ would give
$|1/f|\leq1$ in $D$, contradicting $|f|<1$. Thus $f$
has an interior zero. Its number $N$ of zeros in $D$,
counting multiplicities, is finite and positive: otherwise
compactness of $\overline D$ would give an accumulation
point in $U$, forcing $f$ to vanish identically on its
component by the identity theorem [@SS03].
:::

<1>2. Every point of $D$ is attained inside $D$.

::: {.proof}
Fix $w$ with $|w|<1$. On $\partial D$,
$$
|(f-w)-f|=|w|<1=|f|.
$$
Rouche's theorem says that $f-w$ and $f$ have the same
number $N\geq1$ of zeros in $D$, counting multiplicity
[@SS03]. Hence $f(z)=w$ for some $z\in D$.
This proves $D\subset f(D)$, while step <1>1 gives
$f(D)\subset D$. Therefore $f(D)=D$ and, in particular,
$D\subset f(\overline D)$ as required.
:::
:::
