7. Compute the integral

$$
\int _ { 0 } ^ { \infty } { \frac { ( \log x ) ^ { 2 } } { 1 + x ^ { 2 } } } d x .
$$

::: solution
The value is $\boxed{\pi^3/8}$.

<1>1. The improper integral converges.

::: proof
On $(0,1]$ the integrand is at most $(\log x)^2$, and
on $[1,\infty)$ it is at most $(\log x)^2/x^2$.
Substituting $x=e^{-t}$ in the first comparison integral
and $x=e^t$ in the second makes each equal to
$\int_0^\infty t^2e^{-t}\,dt=2$, by two integrations
by parts. Thus the requested nonnegative integral is finite.
:::

<1>2. An indented upper semicircle gives a residue identity.

::: proof
Choose $\operatorname{Log}z=\log|z|+i\arg z$ with
$-\pi/2<\arg z<3\pi/2$. Its cut is the nonpositive
imaginary axis, so it is holomorphic near the upper
half-plane away from zero [@SS03]. For $0<\varepsilon<1<R$,
use the contour consisting of $[-R,-\varepsilon]$, the
clockwise upper arc of radius $\varepsilon$, $[\varepsilon,R]$,
and the counterclockwise upper arc of radius $R$.

For $F(z)=(\operatorname{Log}z)^2/(1+z^2)$ the only pole
inside is $i$, with
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=i}F=\frac{(i\pi/2)^2}{2i}
=\frac{i\pi^2}{8}.
$$
The residue theorem therefore gives total contour integral
$-\pi^3/4$ [@SS03]. Since
$\operatorname{Log}(-x)=\log x+i\pi$ for $x>0$, the
two real segments contribute
$$
\int_\varepsilon^R
\frac{(\log x+i\pi)^2+(\log x)^2}{1+x^2}\,dx.
$$
Its real part is
$$
2\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{(\log x)^2}{1+x^2}\,dx
-\pi^2\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{dx}{1+x^2}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Both arcs vanish, determining the integral.

::: proof
On the outer arc, $|\operatorname{Log}z|\leq\log R+\pi$
and $|1+z^2|\geq R^2-1$. Its integral has modulus at most
$$
\frac{\pi R(\log R+\pi)^2}{R^2-1}\longrightarrow0.
$$
The inner arc integral has modulus at most
$$
\frac{\pi\varepsilon(|\log\varepsilon|+\pi)^2}
{1-\varepsilon^2}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Taking real parts in step <1>2 and then letting
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and $R\to\infty$ is justified
by these bounds and step <1>1. Since
$\int_0^\infty dx/(1+x^2)=\pi/2$ by the arctangent primitive,
the resulting equation is
$$
2I-\frac{\pi^3}{2}=-\frac{\pi^3}{4}.
$$
Hence $I=\pi^3/8$.
:::
:::
