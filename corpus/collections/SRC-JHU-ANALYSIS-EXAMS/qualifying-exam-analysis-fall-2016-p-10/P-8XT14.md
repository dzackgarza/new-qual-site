::: {.problem}
4. Prove that if a sequence of harmonic functions on the open disk converges uniformly on compact subset of the disk, then the limit is harmonic.
:::

::: solution
Let $(u_n)$ converge uniformly on compact subsets of the
open disk $D$ to $u$. First take the harmonic functions
to be real-valued.

<1>1. On each smaller disk, the limit is the Poisson integral of its boundary values.

::: proof
Fix $a\in D$ and $r>0$ with $\overline{D(a,r)}\subset D$.
Uniform convergence on this closed disk makes $u$ continuous
there. For $|z-a|<r$, the Poisson formula for $u_n$ gives
$$
u_n(z)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{r^2-|z-a|^2}{|re^{it}-(z-a)|^2}
u_n(a+re^{it})\,dt
$$
[@SS03]. The kernel is nonnegative and has integral
$2\pi$. Therefore replacing the boundary values of $u_n$
by those of $u$ changes this integral by at most
$\sup_{|w-a|=r}|u_n(w)-u(w)|$, which tends to zero.
Taking limits gives the same formula for $u$.
:::

<1>2. This Poisson integral is harmonic in the smaller disk.

::: proof
Define
$$
H(z)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{re^{it}+(z-a)}{re^{it}-(z-a)}
u(a+re^{it})\,dt,
\qquad |z-a|<r.
$$
The integrand is holomorphic in $z$. On any compact
subdisk $|z-a|\leq s<r$, the denominator has modulus at
least $r-s$, and both the integrand and its $z$ derivative
are uniformly bounded in $t$. The continuous boundary
values of $u$ are bounded. Differentiation under the
integral is consequently justified, so $H$ is holomorphic.

For $\xi=re^{it}$ and $w=z-a$, multiplication by
$\overline\xi-\overline w$ shows that
$$
\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi+w}{\xi-w}
=\frac{r^2-|w|^2}{|\xi-w|^2}.
$$
Step <1>1 therefore says $u=\operatorname{Re}H$ on
$D(a,r)$. The real part of a holomorphic function is
harmonic [@SS03]. Since $a$ was arbitrary, $u$ is
harmonic throughout $D$.

For complex-valued harmonic functions, apply the same
argument separately to the real and imaginary parts;
both inherit compact-uniform convergence.
:::
:::
