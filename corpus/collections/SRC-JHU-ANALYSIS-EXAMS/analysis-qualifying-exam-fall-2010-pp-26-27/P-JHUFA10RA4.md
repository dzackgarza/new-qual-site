Define $U : L^2(\mathbb{R}) \to L^2(\mathbb{R})$ by

$$Uf(x) = f(x - 1).$$

Show that if $f \in L^2$ satisfies $Uf = \lambda f$, for some $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ (i.e., $f$ is an eigenvector of $U$) then $f$ must be the zero element, i.e., $f = 0$ almost everywhere.
