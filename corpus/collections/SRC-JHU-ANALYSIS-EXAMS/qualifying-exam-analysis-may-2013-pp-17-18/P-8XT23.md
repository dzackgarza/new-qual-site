::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on the unit disc $\{z : |z| < 1\}$ satisfying $f(0) = 0$ and $\mathrm{Re}\, f(z) \leq A$ for some positive number $A > 0$.
Prove:

$$|f(z)| \leq \frac{2A|z|}{1 - |z|}.$$
:::

::: solution
<1>1. A fractional transformation of $f$ satisfies the Schwarz estimate.

::: proof
Put $D=\{z:|z|<1\}$ and
$$
h(z)=\frac{f(z)}{2A-f(z)}.
$$
The denominator cannot vanish: $f(z)=2A$ would contradict
$\operatorname{Re}f(z)\leq A$ and $A>0$. Thus $h$ is
holomorphic on $D$ and $h(0)=0$. Moreover,
$$
|2A-f(z)|^2-|f(z)|^2=4A\bigl(A-\operatorname{Re}f(z)\bigr)\geq0,
$$
so $|h(z)|\leq1$.

For completeness, the non-strict bound still gives
$|h(z)|\leq|z|$. The quotient $H(z)=h(z)/z$ extends
holomorphically to zero by the Taylor expansion of $h$.
For $0<\rho<1$, it satisfies $|H|\leq1/\rho$ on
$|z|=\rho$. The maximum modulus principle gives this
bound throughout that closed disk [@SS03]. For fixed
$z\in D$, let $\rho\uparrow1$ with $\rho>|z|$ to obtain
$|H(z)|\leq1$. Therefore $|h(z)|\leq|z|$ on $D$.
:::

<1>2. Inverting the transformation yields the required modulus bound.

::: proof
The identity defining $h$ rearranges to
$f(1+h)=2Ah$. Step <1>1 gives $|h(z)|\leq|z|<1$,
so $1+h(z)$ is nonzero and
$$
|f(z)|=\frac{2A|h(z)|}{|1+h(z)|}
\leq\frac{2A|z|}{1-|z|}.
$$
Here the denominator estimate is
$|1+h(z)|\geq1-|h(z)|\geq1-|z|>0$.
This also covers $z=0$, where both sides vanish.
:::
:::
