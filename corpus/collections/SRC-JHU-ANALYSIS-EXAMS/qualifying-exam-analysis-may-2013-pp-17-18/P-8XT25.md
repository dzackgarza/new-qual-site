Suppose that $E$ and $F$ are Lebesgue measurable sets of $\mathbb{R}$, and their Lebesgue measures $m(E) > 0$, $m(F) > 0$.
Prove that

$$E + F = \{x + y : x \in E, y \in F\}$$

contains a nonempty open interval.

::: {.solution}
Choose $R>0$ so large that
\[
E_0:=E\cap[-R,R],
\qquad
F_0:=F\cap[-R,R]
\]
both have positive measure. Then $E_0,F_0$ have finite positive measure and
\[
E_0+F_0\subseteq E+F.
\]

Define
\[
G(x):=(\mathbf1_{E_0}*\mathbf1_{F_0})(x)
=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_{E_0}(x-y)\mathbf1_{F_0}(y)\,dy.
\]
Since $\mathbf1_{E_0}\in L^1(\mathbb R)$ and translations are continuous in $L^1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|G(x+h)-G(x)|
&\le \int_{\mathbb R}
|\mathbf1_{E_0}(x+h-y)-\mathbf1_{E_0}(x-y)|
\mathbf1_{F_0}(y)\,dy\\
&\le \|\tau_h\mathbf1_{E_0}-\mathbf1_{E_0}\|_1
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $G$ is continuous.

Moreover, Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}G(x)\,dx
=m(E_0)m(F_0)>0.
\]
Hence $G(x_0)>0$ for some $x_0$. By continuity there is $\delta>0$ such that
\[
G(x)>0
\qquad\text{whenever }|x-x_0|<\delta.
\]

If $G(x)>0$, then the set of $y$ for which
\[
y\in F_0,
\qquad
x-y\in E_0
\]
has positive measure, hence is nonempty. Therefore $x\in E_0+F_0$.

Consequently
\[
(x_0-\delta,x_0+\delta)
\subseteq E_0+F_0
\subseteq E+F.
\]
Thus $E+F$ contains a nonempty open interval.
:::
