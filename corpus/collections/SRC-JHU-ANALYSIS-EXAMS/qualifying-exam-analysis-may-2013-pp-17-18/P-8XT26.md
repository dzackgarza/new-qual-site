If the sequence of Lebesgue measurable functions $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ satisfying that

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} |f_n(x)|^2 \, dx = 0,$$

show that there exists a subsequence of functions $\{f_{n_j}\}_{j=1}^\infty$ such that

$$f_{n_j}(x) \to 0 \quad \text{a.e. } x.$$

::: {.solution}
Since
\[
\|f_n\|_2^2=\int_{\mathbb R^d}|f_n(x)|^2\,dx\longrightarrow0,
\]
we may choose a subsequence $(f_{n_j})$ such that
\[
\|f_{n_j}\|_2^2\le 2^{-j}
\qquad(j\ge1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty \int_{\mathbb R^d}|f_{n_j}(x)|^2\,dx
\le \sum_{j=1}^\infty 2^{-j}<\infty.
\]
By Tonelli's theorem,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}\sum_{j=1}^\infty |f_{n_j}(x)|^2\,dx
=\sum_{j=1}^\infty\|f_{n_j}\|_2^2<\infty.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty |f_{n_j}(x)|^2<\infty
\]
for almost every $x$. For every such $x$, the terms of this convergent nonnegative series tend to zero, so
\[
|f_{n_j}(x)|^2\to0.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{f_{n_j}(x)\to0\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb R^d.}
\]
:::
