::: {.problem}
Let $U \subset \mathbb{C}$ be an open set and let $f$ be a continuous function on $U$.
If $f^2$ is holomorphic on $U$, prove that $f$ is holomorphic on $U$.
:::

::: {.solution}
It suffices to prove that $f$ is holomorphic on each
connected component $W$ of $U$. Put $A=f^2$.

<1>1. If $A\equiv0$ on $W$, then $f\equiv0$ on $W$.

::: {.proof}
$f(z)^2=0$ forces $f(z)=0$ at every point of $W$, and
the zero function is holomorphic.
:::

<1>2. $f$ is holomorphic on the open set $V=\{z\in W:f(z)\neq0\}$.

::: {.proof}
$V$ is open because $f$ is continuous. Fix $a\in V$ and
a disk $\Delta\subset V$ about $a$. The holomorphic function
$A$ has no zero on the simply connected disk $\Delta$, so it
has a holomorphic square root $g$ there, with $g^2=A$
[@SS03]. On $\Delta$ the quotient $f/g$ is continuous,
and $(f/g)^2=1$. It therefore takes values in $\{1,-1\}$,
and connectedness of $\Delta$ makes it constant. Hence
$f=\pm g$ is holomorphic on $\Delta$.
:::

<1>3. If $A\not\equiv0$ on $W$, then $f$ is holomorphic at every zero of $f$ in $W$.

::: {.proof}
Let $f(a)=0$. Then $A(a)=0$, and the zeros of $A$ in the
connected set $W$ are isolated [@SS03]. Choose a disk
$\Delta$ about $a$ with $A\neq0$ on $\Delta\setminus\{a\}$.
By step <1>2, $f$ is holomorphic on the punctured disk.
It is bounded near $a$, being continuous at $a$. The
removable-singularity theorem gives a holomorphic function
on $\Delta$ that agrees with $f$ off $a$ [@SS03]. Its value
at $a$ is $\lim_{z\to a}f(z)=f(a)$ by continuity of $f$.
Thus $f$ itself is holomorphic on $\Delta$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
On a component where $A\equiv0$, step <1>1 applies.
On any other component, every point is either a point
of $V$ or a zero of $f$, and steps <1>2 and <1>3 cover
both cases.
:::
:::
