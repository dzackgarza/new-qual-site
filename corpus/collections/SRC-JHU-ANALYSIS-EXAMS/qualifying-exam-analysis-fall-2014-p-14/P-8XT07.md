::: {.problem}
Let $D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$. Prove that every conformal
bijection of $D$ onto $D$ is a linear fractional transformation.
:::

::: {.solution}
The maps are exactly
$$
f(z)=\lambda\frac{z-a}{1-\overline a z},
\qquad a\in D,\quad |\lambda|=1.
$$

<1>1. A disk automorphism reduces the problem to a bijection fixing zero.

::: {.proof}
Surjectivity gives $a\in D$ with $f(a)=0$. Define
$$
\phi_a(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-\overline a z},
\qquad \phi_a^{-1}(w)=\frac{w+a}{1+\overline a w}.
$$
Their denominators are nonzero on $D$, and direct substitution
shows that they are inverse. Moreover
$$
1-|\phi_a(z)|^2
=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-\overline a z|^2}>0
\qquad(z\in D).
$$
The same identity with $-a$ proves that the inverse maps
$D$ into $D$. Thus $H=f\circ\phi_a^{-1}$ is a conformal
bijection of $D$ onto $D$ and $H(0)=0$. Its inverse is
holomorphic by the holomorphic inverse function theorem
[@SS03], and also fixes zero.
:::

<1>2. The normalized map is a rotation, giving the full classification.

::: {.proof}
Schwarz's lemma applied to $H$ and $H^{-1}$ gives
$$
|H(z)|\leq|z|=|H^{-1}(H(z))|\leq|H(z)|.
$$
Hence $|H(z)|=|z|$. The quotient $H(z)/z$ extends
holomorphically across zero and has modulus at most one.
It has modulus one at every nonzero interior point, so
the maximum modulus principle makes it a constant $\lambda$
of modulus one [@SS03]. Therefore $H(z)=\lambda z$ and
$f=H\circ\phi_a$ has the displayed form. Conversely, every
displayed map is the composition of $\phi_a$ with a rotation,
so is a conformal bijection of $D$ onto $D$.
:::
:::

::: {.remark}
An injective holomorphic map merely into $D$ need not be
linear fractional. For example, $q(z)=z/2+z^2/8$ satisfies
$|q(z)|<5/8<1$ on $D$, and
$$
q(z)-q(w)=(z-w)\left(\frac12+\frac{z+w}{8}\right).
$$
The second factor has modulus greater than $1/4$ for
$z,w\in D$, proving injectivity. If $q(z)=(Az+B)/(Cz+E)$
with $AE-BC\ne0$, multiplication gives a polynomial identity.
Its cubic coefficient forces $C=0$, and its quadratic
coefficient then forces $E=0$, contradicting the determinant
condition. Thus it is not linear fractional; surjectivity
is essential to the classification.
:::
