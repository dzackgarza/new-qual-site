::: {.problem}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C$ be a bounded region and let
$(f_n)$ be continuous on $\overline\Omega$ and holomorphic
in $\Omega$. If $(f_n)$ converges uniformly on
$\partial\Omega$, prove that it converges uniformly on $\Omega$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Boundary differences control differences on the entire closure.

::: {.proof}
For each $n,m$, the function $h=f_n-f_m$ is continuous
on the compact set $\overline\Omega$ and holomorphic on
$\Omega$. Its modulus attains a maximum. If that maximum
is attained in $\Omega$, the maximum modulus principle
makes $h$ constant on the region, and continuity gives
the same value on its boundary [@SS03]. Otherwise the
maximum is already a boundary value. Thus in every case
$$
\sup_{\overline\Omega}|f_n-f_m|
\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}|f_n-f_m|.
$$
No regularity of the boundary is required for this argument.
:::

<1>2. The sequence has a uniform limit on $\overline\Omega$.

::: {.proof}
Uniform convergence on $\partial\Omega$ makes the sequence
uniformly Cauchy there. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$
so that the right-hand side of step <1>1 is less than
$\varepsilon$ for $n,m\geq N$. The same estimate holds
on $\overline\Omega$. Completeness of $\mathbb C$ gives
a pointwise limit $F(z)$ for every $z\in\overline\Omega$.
For fixed $n\geq N$, let $m\to\infty$ in the estimate
to obtain $|f_n(z)-F(z)|\leq\varepsilon$ simultaneously
for all $z\in\overline\Omega$. This proves uniform convergence
on the closure, hence also on $\Omega$.

The limit is continuous on $\overline\Omega$ as a uniform
limit of continuous functions. It is holomorphic on
$\Omega$ by the local uniform limit theorem [@SS03].
:::
:::
