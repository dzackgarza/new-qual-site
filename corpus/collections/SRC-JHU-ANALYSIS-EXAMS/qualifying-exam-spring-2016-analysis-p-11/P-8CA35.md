::: {.problem}
Let $f : D \to D$ be a holomorphic function where $D = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ is the unit disk.
Prove that if $f$ has at least 2 fixed points then $f$ is the identity map.
(Note: A point $a$ is said to be a fixed point of $f$ if $f(a) = a$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose distinct fixed points $a,b\in D$.

<1>1. Conjugation moves the two fixed points to zero and a nonzero point.

::: {.proof}
Set
$$
\phi_a(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-\overline a z},
\qquad \phi_a^{-1}(w)=\frac{w+a}{1+\overline a w}.
$$
The denominators do not vanish on $D$. Direct substitution
verifies that these maps are inverse, and
$$
1-|\phi_a(z)|^2
=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-\overline a z|^2}>0
$$
for $z\in D$. Applying the same formula to $-a$ shows
that the inverse also maps $D$ into itself. Thus $\phi_a$
is an automorphism of $D$ with $\phi_a(a)=0$.

The function $F=\phi_a\circ f\circ\phi_a^{-1}$ maps $D$
holomorphically into $D$. It fixes zero and also
$\beta=\phi_a(b)$, which is nonzero because $b\ne a$.
:::

<1>2. The second fixed point forces $F$, and hence $f$, to be the identity.

::: {.proof}
Schwarz's lemma gives $|F(z)|\leq|z|$ [@SS03]. The
quotient $H(z)=F(z)/z$ extends holomorphically to zero
with value $F'(0)$, by the Taylor expansion of $F$.
It satisfies $|H|\leq1$ on $D$, including zero by
continuity. At the nonzero fixed point $\beta$,
$H(\beta)=F(\beta)/\beta=1$. The maximum modulus
principle makes $H$ constant, and its value there makes
that constant one [@SS03]. Thus $F(z)=z$ throughout $D$.
Undoing the conjugation gives
$f=\phi_a^{-1}\circ\operatorname{id}_D\circ\phi_a
=\operatorname{id}_D$.
:::
:::
