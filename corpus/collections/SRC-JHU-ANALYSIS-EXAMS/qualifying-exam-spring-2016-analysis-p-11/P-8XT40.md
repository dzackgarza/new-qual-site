Let $f : [0,1] \to [0,1]$ be a continuous function and $\phi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a $C^1$ function with $\phi(0) = 0$.
Prove

$$
\int_0^1 \phi(f(x))\,dx
=\int_0^\infty m(\{x\in[0,1]:f(x)>t\})\phi'(t)\,dt.
$$

::: solution
<1>1. Write the weighted value pointwise as an integral over its level parameter.
::: proof
For every $x\in[0,1]$, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and $\phi(0)=0$ give
\[
\phi(f(x))
=\int_0^{f(x)}\phi'(t)\,dt
=\int_0^\infty
\mathbf1_{\{t<f(x)\}}\phi'(t)\,dt.
\]
Because $0\le f(x)\le1$, the integrand vanishes for $t>1$.
:::

<1>2. Justify interchanging the integrals.
::: proof
Since $\phi'$ is continuous, it is bounded on $[0,1]$. Thus
\[
\int_0^1\int_0^\infty
\left|\mathbf1_{\{t<f(x)\}}\phi'(t)\right|\,dt\,dx
\le \|\phi'\|_{L^\infty([0,1])}<\infty.
\]
Therefore Fubini's theorem applies. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\phi(f(x))\,dx
&=\int_0^1\int_0^\infty
\mathbf1_{\{t<f(x)\}}\phi'(t)\,dt\,dx\\
&=\int_0^\infty
\left(\int_0^1\mathbf1_{\{f(x)>t\}}\,dx\right)
\phi'(t)\,dt\\
&=\int_0^\infty
m(\{x\in[0,1]:f(x)>t\})\phi'(t)\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the stated identity.
:::
:::
