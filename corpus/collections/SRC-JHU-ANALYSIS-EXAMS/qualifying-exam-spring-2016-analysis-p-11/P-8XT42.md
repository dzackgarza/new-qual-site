::: {.problem}
Determine the number of zeroes of the polynomial

$$2z^5 - 6z^2 + z + 1 = 0$$

in the annulus $1 \leq |z| \leq 2$.
:::

::: solution
There are exactly $\boxed{3}$ zeros in the closed annulus.
They are distinct. Put $p(z)=2z^5-6z^2+z+1$.

<1>1. There are two zeros inside the unit circle and none on it.

::: proof
On $|z|=1$,
$$
|2z^5+z+1|\leq2+1+1=4<6=|-6z^2|.
$$
Rouche's theorem says that $p$ and $-6z^2$ have the same
number of zeros in $|z|<1$, namely two counted with
multiplicity [@SS03]. The strict inequality also gives
$|p(z)|\geq6-4>0$ on the circle, excluding boundary zeros.
:::

<1>2. There are five zeros inside the radius-two circle and none on it.

::: proof
On $|z|=2$,
$$
|-6z^2+z+1|\leq24+2+1=27<64=|2z^5|.
$$
Rouche's theorem gives five zeros in $|z|<2$, counted
with multiplicity, and the same strict inequality
excludes zeros on $|z|=2$ [@SS03]. Subtracting the two
zeros in $|z|<1$ leaves $5-2=3$ in $1<|z|<2$.
Neither boundary has a zero, so this is also the count
in the requested closed annulus.
:::

<1>3. All zeros of $p$ are simple.

::: proof
The derivative is $p'(z)=10z^4-12z+1$. Reducing these
two integer polynomials modulo three gives
$$
\overline p=2z^5+z+1,\qquad \overline{p'}=z^4+1.
$$
Their Euclidean remainders satisfy
$$
\overline p-2z\,\overline{p'}=1-z,
\qquad \overline{p'}(1)=2\ne0
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_3.
$$
Hence their gcd in $\mathbb F_3[z]$ is one.

If $p$ and $p'$ had a nonconstant common factor over
$\mathbb Q$, Gauss's lemma would give a primitive common
factor $h\in\mathbb Z[z]$ [@DF04]. Since $h$ divides
$p$ in $\mathbb Z[z]$, its leading coefficient divides
$2$, and so is nonzero modulo three. Its reduction
would therefore be a nonconstant common factor of
$\overline p$ and $\overline{p'}$, a contradiction.
Thus $p$ and $p'$ are coprime over $\mathbb Q$, hence
over $\mathbb C$ by their Bezout identity. A repeated
zero would be common to both, so none exists. The
three zeros counted in step <1>2 are consequently distinct.
:::
:::
