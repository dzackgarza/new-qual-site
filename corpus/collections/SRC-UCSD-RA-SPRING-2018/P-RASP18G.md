::: problem
Let $(X, d)$ be a compact metric space.

1. Prove that $X$ is a separable metric space, i.e. there exists a countable dense subset $\{x_n\}_{n \geq 1} \subset X$.

2. Let $\{f_n\}_{n \geq 1} \subset C(X, \mathbb{R})$ be the sequence of functions defined by $f_n(x) = d(x, x_n)$ for every $n \geq 1$ and $x \in X$.
   Prove that this sequence $\{f_n\}_{n \geq 1}$ separates points in $X$.

3. Prove that $C(X, \mathbb{R})$ is separable when endowed with the uniform norm.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. Prove that \(X\) is separable.
::: proof
For each \(m\ge1\), compactness of \(X\) gives a finite \(1/m\)-net
\[
F_m\subset X.
\]
Set
\[
D:=\bigcup_{m=1}^\infty F_m.
\]
This is countable. Given \(x\in X\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(m\) with \(1/m<\varepsilon\). Since \(F_m\) is a \(1/m\)-net, some \(y\in F_m\subset D\) satisfies
\[
d(x,y)<\varepsilon.
\]
Thus \(D\) is dense, so \(X\) is separable.
:::

<1>2. Show that the distance functions separate points.
::: proof
Let \(x\ne y\) and put \(\delta=d(x,y)>0\). Since \(\{x_n\}\) is dense, choose \(n\) with
\[
d(x,x_n)<\delta/3.
\]
Then
\[
f_n(x)=d(x,x_n)<\delta/3,
\]
while the triangle inequality gives
\[
f_n(y)=d(y,x_n)
\ge d(x,y)-d(x,x_n)
>2\delta/3.
\]
Hence
\[
f_n(x)\ne f_n(y).
\]
Therefore \(\{f_n\}\) separates points of \(X\).
:::

<1>3. Prove that \(C(X,\mathbb R)\) is separable.
::: proof
Let \(\mathcal A\) be the real algebra generated by the constants and the functions \(f_n\). By Step 2, \(\mathcal A\) separates points and contains the constants. The real Stone--Weierstrass theorem therefore gives
\[
\overline{\mathcal A}^{\,\|\cdot\|_\infty}=C(X,\mathbb R).
\]

Now let \(\mathcal A_{\mathbb Q}\) be the set of all polynomials with rational coefficients in finitely many of the functions \(f_n\). There are only countably many such expressions, so \(\mathcal A_{\mathbb Q}\) is countable. Approximating the finitely many real coefficients of any element of \(\mathcal A\) by rationals shows that \(\mathcal A_{\mathbb Q}\) is dense in \(\mathcal A\) in the uniform norm. Consequently
\[
\overline{\mathcal A_{\mathbb Q}}^{\,\|\cdot\|_\infty}
=C(X,\mathbb R).
\]
Thus \(C(X,\mathbb R)\) has a countable dense subset and is separable.
:::
:::
