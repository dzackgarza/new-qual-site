::: {.problem}
Construct a bounded set of real numbers having exactly three limit points.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

Define
$$
E=
\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}
\cup
\left\{1+\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}
\cup
\left\{2+\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}.
$$
Then $E$ is bounded.


::: pf-proof

Every element of the first family lies in $(0,1]$, every element of the second
lies in $(1,2]$, and every element of the third lies in $(2,3]$. Hence
$$
E\subseteq(0,3].
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The numbers $0$, $1$, and $2$ are limit points of $E$.


::: pf-proof

The three sequences
$$
\frac1n,
\qquad
1+\frac1n,
\qquad
2+\frac1n
$$
consist of points of $E$ and converge respectively to
$$
0,\qquad1,\qquad2.
$$
Thus each of these three numbers is a limit point.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every sequence of distinct points of $E$ has a subsequence lying
entirely in one of the three displayed families.


::: pf-proof

Partition $E$ according to the three families in step <1>1. Given infinitely
many distinct points of $E$, at least one of the three families contains
infinitely many of them. Selecting those terms gives the required subsequence.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every limit point of $E$ belongs to
$$
\{0,1,2\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $x$ be a limit point of $E$. Choose a sequence of distinct points
$$
e_j\in E
$$
with
$$
e_j\longrightarrow x.
$$
By step <1>3, some subsequence lies entirely in one of the three families.

If it lies in
$$
\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\},
$$
then, because its points are distinct, the corresponding indices tend to
infinity along a further subsequence, so its limit is $0$.

The same argument gives limit $1$ for the second family and limit $2$ for the
third. Since every subsequence of $(e_j)$ converges to the same limit $x$, one
has
$$
x\in\{0,1,2\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The set of limit points of $E$ is exactly
$$
\boxed{\{0,1,2\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>2 shows that all three displayed numbers are limit points, and
step <1>4 excludes every other real number.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>1 and <1>5 show that the constructed set is bounded and has exactly
three limit points.

:::

:::
