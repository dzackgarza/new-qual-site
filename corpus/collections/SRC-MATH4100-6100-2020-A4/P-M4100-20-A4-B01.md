::: {.problem}
Prove directly from the open-cover definition, without using sequential compactness, that every compact subset of $\mathbb R$ is closed and bounded.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K\subseteq\RR$ be compact.

<1>1. The family
$$
\mathcal U=\{(-n,n):n\in\NN\}
$$
is an open cover of $K$.

::: {.proof}
It is an open cover of all of $\RR$: for every $x\in\RR$, the Archimedean
property gives $n\in\NN$ with
$$
n>|x|,
$$
and then $x\in(-n,n)$. Hence it covers the subset $K$.
:::

<1>2. The set $K$ is bounded.

::: {.proof}
By compactness, the cover in step <1>1 has a finite subcover
$$
(-n_1,n_1),\ldots,(-n_r,n_r).
$$
Let
$$
N=\max\{n_1,\ldots,n_r\}.
$$
Then every member of the finite subcover is contained in $(-N,N)$, so
$$
K\subseteq(-N,N).
$$
Thus $K$ is bounded.
:::

<1>3. Fix $x\in\RR\setminus K$. For every $y\in K$, there are disjoint
open intervals $V_y$ containing $y$ and $W_y$ containing $x$.

::: {.proof}
Since $x\neq y$, put
$$
r_y=\frac{|x-y|}{3}>0
$$
and define
$$
V_y=(y-r_y,y+r_y),
\qquad
W_y=(x-r_y,x+r_y).
$$
If a point $z$ belonged to both intervals, then
$$
|x-y|
\leq
|x-z|+|z-y|
<
2r_y
=
\frac{2}{3}|x-y|,
$$
a contradiction. Hence $V_y\cap W_y=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>4. There exist finitely many points $y_1,\ldots,y_m\in K$ such that
$$
K\subseteq V_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{y_m}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The family
$$
\{V_y:y\in K\}
$$
is an open cover of $K$. Compactness gives a finite subcover.
:::

<1>5. The set
$$
W=W_{y_1}\cap\cdots\cap W_{y_m}
$$
is an open neighborhood of $x$ disjoint from $K$.

::: {.proof}
Each $W_{y_i}$ is open and contains $x$, so their finite intersection is open
and contains $x$.

If $z\in K$, step <1>4 gives some $i$ with $z\in V_{y_i}$. But
$$
V_{y_i}\cap W_{y_i}=\varnothing
$$
by step <1>3, while
$$
W\subseteq W_{y_i}.
$$
Thus $z\notin W$. Hence
$$
W\cap K=\varnothing.
$$
:::

<1>6. The complement $\RR\setminus K$ is open.

::: {.proof}
For every $x\in\RR\setminus K$, step <1>5 provides an open neighborhood
$W$ of $x$ with
$$
W\subseteq\RR\setminus K.
$$
This is exactly the definition of openness.
:::

<1>7. The set $K$ is closed.

::: {.proof}
A subset of $\RR$ is closed exactly when its complement is open. Apply
step <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Therefore every compact subset of $\RR$ is closed and bounded.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves boundedness directly from an open cover, and step <1>7 proves
closedness directly from compactness.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
