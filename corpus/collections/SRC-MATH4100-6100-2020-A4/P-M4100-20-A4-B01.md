::: {.problem}
Prove directly from the open-cover definition, without using sequential compactness, that every compact subset of $\RR$ is closed and bounded.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $K\subseteq\RR$ be compact.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The family
$$
\mathcal U=\{(-n,n):n\in\NN\}
$$
is an open cover of $K$.


::: pf-proof

It is an open cover of all of $\RR$: for every $x\in\RR$, the Archimedean
property gives $n\in\NN$ with
$$
n>|x|,
$$
and then $x\in(-n,n)$. Hence it covers the subset $K$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The set $K$ is bounded.


::: pf-proof

By compactness, the cover in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} has a finite subcover
$$
(-n_1,n_1),\ldots,(-n_r,n_r).
$$
Let
$$
N=\max\{n_1,\ldots,n_r\}.
$$
Then every member of the finite subcover is contained in $(-N,N)$, so
$$
K\subseteq(-N,N).
$$
Thus $K$ is bounded.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Fix $x\in\RR\setminus K$. For every $y\in K$, there are disjoint
open intervals $V_y$ containing $y$ and $W_y$ containing $x$.


::: pf-proof

Since $x\neq y$, put
$$
r_y=\frac{|x-y|}{3}>0
$$
and define
$$
V_y=(y-r_y,y+r_y),
\qquad
W_y=(x-r_y,x+r_y).
$$
If a point $z$ belonged to both intervals, then
$$
|x-y|
\leq
|x-z|+|z-y|
<
2r_y
=
\frac{2}{3}|x-y|,
$$
a contradiction. Hence $V_y\cap W_y=\varnothing$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

There exist finitely many points $y_1,\ldots,y_m\in K$ such that
$$
K\subseteq V_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{y_m}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The family
$$
\{V_y:y\in K\}
$$
is an open cover of $K$. Compactness gives a finite subcover.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The set
$$
W=W_{y_1}\cap\cdots\cap W_{y_m}
$$
is an open neighborhood of $x$ disjoint from $K$.


::: pf-proof

Each $W_{y_i}$ is open and contains $x$, so their finite intersection is open
and contains $x$.

If $z\in K$, step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives some $i$ with $z\in V_{y_i}$. But
$$
V_{y_i}\cap W_{y_i}=\varnothing
$$
by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, while
$$
W\subseteq W_{y_i}.
$$
Thus $z\notin W$. Hence
$$
W\cap K=\varnothing.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The complement $\RR\setminus K$ is open.


::: pf-proof

For every $x\in\RR\setminus K$, step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} provides an open neighborhood
$W$ of $x$ with
$$
W\subseteq\RR\setminus K.
$$
This is exactly the definition of openness.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The set $K$ is closed.


::: pf-proof

A subset of $\RR$ is closed exactly when its complement is open. Apply
step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore every compact subset of $\RR$ is closed and bounded.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves boundedness directly from an open cover, and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves
closedness directly from compactness.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
