::: {.problem}
1. Let $u$ be differentiable and suppose
\[
u'(t)\le a\,u(t),\qquad t\ge0,
\qquad
u(0)\le b,
\]
where $a,b>0$. Starting from first principles, derive an upper bound for $u(t)$ for $t>0$.

2. Let $x:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^n$ satisfy
\[
x'(t)=f(x(t)),
\]
where $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ is continuous and
\[
\langle f(y),y\rangle\le\|y\|^2
\]
for every $y\in\mathbb R^n$. Derive an inequality showing that $\|x(t)\|$ grows at most exponentially.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

In Part (1),
$$
\boxed{u(t)\le b e^{at}}
\qquad
(t\ge0).
$$

::: pf-proof

Define
$$
v(t)\coloneqq e^{-at}u(t).
$$
By the product rule,
$$
\begin{aligned}
v'(t)
&=
-ae^{-at}u(t)+e^{-at}u'(t)\\
&=
e^{-at}\bigl(u'(t)-au(t)\bigr)
\le0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence $v$ is nonincreasing on $[0,\infty)$, so
$$
e^{-at}u(t)
=
v(t)
\le
v(0)
=
u(0)
\le b.
$$
Multiplying by $e^{at}>0$ gives
$$
u(t)\le be^{at}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For the solution in Part (2), the squared norm
$$
U(t)\coloneqq\norm{x(t)}^2
$$
satisfies
$$
U'(t)\le2U(t).
$$

::: pf-proof

Using the differential equation,
$$
\begin{aligned}
U'(t)
&=
2\inner{x'(t)}{x(t)}\\
&=
2\inner{f(x(t))}{x(t)}\\
&\le
2\norm{x(t)}^2\\
&=
2U(t),
\end{aligned}
$$
by the assumed inequality for $f$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every $t\ge0$,
$$
\boxed{
\norm{x(t)}
\le
e^t\norm{x(0)}
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1){.pf-ref} to $U$ with
$$
a=2,
\qquad
b=U(0)=\norm{x(0)}^2.
$$
This gives
$$
\norm{x(t)}^2
=
U(t)
\le
U(0)e^{2t}
=
\norm{x(0)}^2e^{2t}.
$$
Both sides are nonnegative, so taking square roots yields
$$
\norm{x(t)}
\le
e^t\norm{x(0)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives the scalar exponential upper bound, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives
the requested exponential-growth inequality for the vector solution.

:::
