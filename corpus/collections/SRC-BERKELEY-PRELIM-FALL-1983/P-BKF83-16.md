::: {.problem}
Let $F(t)$ be an $n\times n$ matrix whose entries are continuously differentiable real functions of $t$, and define
\[
u(t)=\operatorname{tr}(F(t)^3).
\]
Show that $u$ is differentiable and that
\[
u'(t)=3\operatorname{tr}(F(t)^2F'(t)).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The matrix-valued function $F^3$ is differentiable and
$$
(F^3)'
=
F'F^2+FF'F+F^2F'.
$$

::: {.proof}
Each entry of a product of two matrix-valued functions is a finite sum of
products of scalar functions. Hence the ordinary product rule applied
entrywise gives
$$
(AB)'=A'B+AB'
$$
whenever $A$ and $B$ are differentiable matrix-valued functions. Applying
this twice,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(F^3)'
&=
(F^2)'F+F^2F'\\
&=
(F'F+FF')F+F^2F'\\
&=
F'F^2+FF'F+F^2F'.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. The function $u$ is differentiable and
$$
\boxed{
u'(t)=3\operatorname{tr}\bigl(F(t)^2F'(t)\bigr)
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The trace is a finite sum of diagonal entries, so step <1>1 implies
$$
\begin{aligned}
u'
&=
\operatorname{tr}\bigl((F^3)'\bigr)\\
&=
\operatorname{tr}(F'F^2)
+\operatorname{tr}(FF'F)
+\operatorname{tr}(F^2F').
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the cyclic identity
$$
\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA),
$$
one obtains
$$
\operatorname{tr}(F'F^2)
=
\operatorname{tr}(F^2F')
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{tr}(FF'F)
=
\operatorname{tr}(F^2F').
$$
Thus all three terms are equal, giving the claimed formula.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves both the differentiability of $u$ and the required
derivative formula.
:::
:::
