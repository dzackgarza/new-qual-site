::: {.problem}
Let $f,g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be smooth, with
\[
f(0)=0,
\qquad
f'(0)\ne0.
\]
Consider
\[
f(x)=t\,g(x).
\]

1. Show that for $|t|<\delta$, for some $\delta>0$, there is a unique continuous solution $x(t)$ with $x(0)=0$.
2. Find the first-order Taylor expansion of $x(t)$ at $t=0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
H(x,t)\coloneqq f(x)-t g(x).
$$

<1>1. There are $r,\delta>0$ such that, for every $\abs{t}<\delta$, the
equation $H(x,t)=0$ has exactly one solution in $(-r,r)$.

::: {.proof}
Because $f'(0)\neq0$, choose $r>0$ so small that $f'$ has a constant
nonzero sign on $[-r,r]$. In particular,
$$
f(-r)f(r)<0.
$$
Since
$$
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(x,t)
=
f'(x)-t g'(x),
$$
continuity on the compact interval $[-r,r]$ allows $\delta>0$ to be chosen
so small that
$$
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(x,t)
$$
has the same nonzero sign as $f'(x)$ whenever
$\abs{x}\leq r$ and $\abs{t}<\delta$. Shrinking $\delta$ once more, the
two numbers
$$
H(-r,t)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H(r,t)
$$
retain the opposite signs of $f(-r)$ and $f(r)$.

For each $\abs{t}<\delta$, the intermediate value theorem therefore gives
a root of the function $x\mapsto H(x,t)$ in $(-r,r)$, while the nonvanishing
derivative with respect to $x$ makes this function strictly monotone there.
The root is consequently unique.
:::

<1>2. These roots define the unique continuous solution $x(t)$ with
$x(0)=0$ on $\abs{t}<\delta$, and in fact $x$ is smooth.

::: {.proof}
At every root found in step <1>1,
$$
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\neq0.
$$
The implicit function theorem therefore makes the unique root a smooth
function of $t$ locally. The local functions agree on overlaps by uniqueness
in step <1>1, so they form a smooth function
$$
x:(-\delta,\delta)\longrightarrow(-r,r)
$$
satisfying
$$
f(x(t))=t g(x(t)).
$$
At $t=0$, the unique root in $(-r,r)$ is $x=0$, so $x(0)=0$.

If $y$ is any continuous solution on $(-\delta,\delta)$ with $y(0)=0$,
then $y$ cannot leave $(-r,r)$: otherwise continuity would give some
$t_0$ with $y(t_0)=r$ or $y(t_0)=-r$, contradicting the fact from step
<1>1 that neither boundary point is a root for $\abs{t}<\delta$.
Thus $y(t)\in(-r,r)$ for every $t$, and uniqueness in step <1>1 gives
$y=x$.
:::

<1>3. The first-order Taylor expansion is
$$
\boxed{
x(t)=\frac{g(0)}{f'(0)}t+O(t^2)
}
\qquad
(t\to0).
$$

::: {.proof}
Differentiate
$$
f(x(t))=t g(x(t)).
$$
The chain and product rules give
$$
f'(x(t))x'(t)
=
g(x(t))+t g'(x(t))x'(t).
$$
Evaluating at $t=0$ and using $x(0)=0$ yields
$$
f'(0)x'(0)=g(0),
$$
so
$$
x'(0)=\frac{g(0)}{f'(0)}.
$$
Since $x$ is smooth, Taylor's theorem gives
$$
x(t)
=
x(0)+x'(0)t+O(t^2)
=
\frac{g(0)}{f'(0)}t+O(t^2).
$$
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves Part (1), and step <1>3 gives the expansion required in
Part (2).
