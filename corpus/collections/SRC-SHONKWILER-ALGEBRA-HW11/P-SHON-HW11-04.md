::: {.problem}
1. Prove that every polynomial $f(x)\in\QQ[x]$ of degree less than $5$ is solvable by radicals.

2. Find an algebraic number $\alpha$ whose irreducible polynomial over $\QQ$ has degree $5$ and is solvable by radicals.
:::

::: {.solution}

## Part 1

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

It is enough to prove that every irreducible polynomial in
$$
\QQ[x]
$$
of degree $n<5$ is solvable by radicals.


::: pf-proof

Factor an arbitrary polynomial over $\QQ$ into irreducible factors. Its roots are obtained by adjoining the roots of those factors. If every irreducible factor is solvable by radicals, then adjoining all of their radical towers gives all roots of the original polynomial.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
f(x)\in\QQ[x]
$$
be irreducible of degree
$$
n<5,
$$
and let $L$ be its splitting field.


::: pf-proof

This fixes one irreducible factor for the reduction in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Galois group
$$
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\QQ)
$$
embeds in
$$
S_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group $G$ acts faithfully on the $n$ distinct roots of $f$. Distinctness holds because $\operatorname{char}\QQ=0$, so $f$ is separable. Faithfulness follows because $L$ is generated by the roots.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The groups $S_1$ and $S_2$ are solvable.


::: pf-proof

They are respectively the trivial group and the cyclic group $C_2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The group $S_3$ is solvable.


::: pf-proof

The subgroup
$$
A_3=\langle(123)\rangle\cong C_3
$$
is normal of index $2$, so
$$
1\triangleleft A_3\triangleleft S_3
$$
has abelian quotients
$$
C_3,\ C_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Let
$$
V_4=
\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}
\le S_4.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{V_4\triangleleft S_4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Conjugation preserves cycle type. Thus conjugating a double transposition produces another double transposition, so the displayed set is invariant under conjugation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

One has
$$
\boxed{
V_4\cong C_2\times C_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every nonidentity element has order $2$, and the product of any two distinct nonidentity elements is the third.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Since
$$
A_4/V_4
$$
has order $3$,
$$
\boxed{
A_4/V_4\cong C_3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The subgroup $V_4$ is contained in $A_4$, and
$$
|A_4/V_4|=\frac{12}{4}=3.
$$
Every group of prime order is cyclic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Since
$$
S_4/A_4\cong C_2,
$$
the chain
$$
1\triangleleft C_2\triangleleft V_4\triangleleft A_4\triangleleft S_4
$$
has abelian quotients.


::: pf-proof

Choose any order-$2$ subgroup inside $V_4$. The quotient of $V_4\cong C_2\times C_2$ by it is $C_2$. Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the next quotient $C_3$, and the sign homomorphism gives
$$
S_4/A_4\cong C_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{S_n\text{ is solvable for }n\le4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Use steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Every subgroup of a solvable group is solvable.


::: pf-proof

Intersect a subnormal series with the subgroup; the resulting successive quotients embed in the corresponding abelian quotients.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Hence the Galois group $G$ from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is solvable.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
n<5,
$$
step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} says $S_n$ is solvable. Then apply step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} to
$$
G\le S_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore $f$ is solvable by radicals.


::: pf-proof

Over a characteristic-zero field containing the required roots of unity after a radical extension, a polynomial is solvable by radicals if and only if its splitting-field Galois group is solvable. Apply step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\text{every polynomial in }\QQ[x]\text{ of degree }<5
\text{ is solvable by radicals}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} to every irreducible factor as in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The cyclotomic field
$$
\QQ(\zeta)
$$
is Galois over $\QQ$ of degree $10$.


::: pf-proof

The $11$th cyclotomic polynomial has degree
$$
\varphi(11)=10,
$$
and cyclotomic extensions are Galois.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Complex conjugation acts by
$$
\zeta\longmapsto\zeta^{-1}
$$
and fixes $\alpha$.


::: pf-proof

$$
\overline\zeta=\zeta^{-1},
$$
so
$$
\overline\alpha
=
\zeta^{-1}+\zeta
=
\alpha.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The fixed field of complex conjugation is
$$
\boxed{
\QQ(\alpha)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The element $\alpha$ is fixed, so
$$
\QQ(\alpha)
$$
lies in the fixed field. Conversely, $\zeta$ satisfies
$$
x^2-\alpha x+1=0
$$
over $\QQ(\alpha)$, so
$$
[\QQ(\zeta):\QQ(\alpha)]\le2.
$$
The extension is not trivial because $\zeta$ is nonreal while $\alpha$ is real. Hence the degree is exactly $2$, so $\QQ(\alpha)$ is the index-$2$ fixed field.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=5}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the tower law,
$$
10
=
[\QQ(\zeta):\QQ]
=
[\QQ(\zeta):\QQ(\alpha)]
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]
=
2[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

Hence the irreducible polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ has degree $5$.


::: pf-proof

For any algebraic element,
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]
=
\deg m_\alpha.
$$
Use step [](#s1-18){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Galois group of
$$
\QQ(\alpha)/\QQ
$$
is cyclic of order $5$.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta)/\QQ)
\cong
(\ZZ/11\ZZ)^\times
\cong
C_{10}.
$$
The fixed subgroup for the real subfield is generated by complex conjugation, corresponding to $-1$, and has order $2$. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\alpha)/\QQ)
\cong
C_{10}/C_2
\cong
C_5.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

Therefore the irreducible polynomial of $\alpha$ is solvable by radicals.


::: pf-proof

The Galois group $C_5$ is abelian, hence solvable. Apply the solvability-by-radicals criterion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
\alpha=\zeta_{11}+\zeta_{11}^{-1}}
$$
is an algebraic number of degree $5$ over $\QQ$ whose irreducible polynomial is solvable by radicals.


::: pf-proof

Degree $5$ is step [](#s1-19){.pf-ref}, and solvability by radicals is step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} proves part 1, and step [](#s1-22){.pf-ref} supplies the requested degree-$5$ example in part 2.
