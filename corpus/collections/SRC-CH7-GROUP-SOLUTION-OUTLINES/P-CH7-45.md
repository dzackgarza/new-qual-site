::: {.problem}
Let $G = \{(1), (12)(34), (1234)(56), (13)(24), (1432)(56), (56)(13), (14)(23), (24)(56)\}$.

(a) Find the stabilizer of $1$ and the orbit of $1$.

(b) Find the stabilizer of $3$ and the orbit of $3$.

(c) Find the stabilizer of $5$ and the orbit of $5$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $a=(1234)(56)$ and $b=(12)(34)$, and compose right to left. The eight listed elements are exactly $a^ib^j$ for $0\le i<4$, $0\le j<2$, so $G$ is a group of order $8$; each stabilizer is found by testing those eight elements on the given point, and the orbit is the complementary count $\abs G=\abs{\operatorname{stab}}\cdot\abs{\operatorname{orb}}$. The argument is this card's own: no retained solution set in the checkout covers this chapter of Gallian.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #group}
$G=\{(1),(12)(34),(1234)(56),(13)(24),(1432)(56),(13)(56),(14)(23),(24)(56)\}$ equals $\{a^ib^j:0\le i<4,\ 0\le j<2\}$ with $a=(1234)(56)$ and $b=(12)(34)$, and it is a subgroup of $S_6$ of order $8$.

::: pf-proof
The powers of $a$ are $a^2=(13)(24)(5)(6)=(13)(24)$, $a^3=(1432)(56)$ and $a^4=(1)$, and $b^2=(1)$. The products with $b$ are computed right to left: $ab(1)=a(b(1))=a(2)=3$ and $ab(3)=a(b(3))=a(4)=1$, while $ab(5)=a(5)=6$ and $ab(6)=a(6)=5$, so $ab=(13)(56)$; next $a^2b=(13)(24)(12)(34)=(14)(23)$ and $a^3b=(1432)(56)(12)(34)=(24)(56)$. These eight permutations are distinct, and they are exactly the eight elements listed, so $G=\{a^ib^j\}$. Since $bab^{-1}=a^{-1}$ (both sides send $1\mapsto4\mapsto3\mapsto2\mapsto1$ and swap $5,6$), one has $a^ib^j\cdot a^{i'}b^{j'}=a^{i+(-1)^ji'}b^{j+j'}$, so $G$ is closed under multiplication, contains the identity, and is finite; hence it is a subgroup of order $8$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #staba}
(a) $\operatorname{stab}_G(1)=\{(1),(24)(56)\}$ and $\operatorname{orb}_G(1)=\{1,2,3,4\}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#group){.pf-ref} the eight elements are $(1)$, $(12)(34)$, $a=(1234)(56)$, $a^2=(13)(24)$, $a^3=(1432)(56)$, $ab=(13)(56)$, $a^2b=(14)(23)$ and $a^3b=(24)(56)$. Exactly $(1)$ and $(24)(56)$ fix $1$: the other six send $1$ to $2$ ($(12)(34)$ and $a$), to $3$ ($(13)(24)$ and $(13)(56)$) or to $4$ ($(1432)(56)$ and $(14)(23)$). So the stabilizer has two elements, and applying the eight elements to $1$ gives $\{1,2,3,4\}$, in agreement with $\abs G=8=2\cdot4$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #stabb}
(b) $\operatorname{stab}_G(3)=\{(1),(24)(56)\}$ and $\operatorname{orb}_G(3)=\{1,2,3,4\}$.

::: pf-proof
$(24)(56)$ has support $\{2,4,5,6\}$ and fixes $1$ and $3$. The other six move $3$: $(13)(24)$ and $(13)(56)$ send $3\mapsto1$; $(12)(34)$ and $(1234)(56)$ send $3\mapsto4$; $(14)(23)$ and $(1432)(56)$ send $3\mapsto2$. So the stabilizer is $\{(1),(24)(56)\}$ and the orbit is $\{3,4,1,2\}=\{1,2,3,4\}$, again with $8=2\cdot4$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #stabc}
(c) $\operatorname{stab}_G(5)=\{(1),(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}$ and $\operatorname{orb}_G(5)=\{5,6\}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#group){.pf-ref} the elements containing the factor $(56)$ are $(1234)(56)$, $(1432)(56)$, $(13)(56)$ and $(24)(56)$, and each of them exchanges $5$ and $6$. The remaining four — $(1)$, $(12)(34)$, $(13)(24)$ and $(14)(23)$ — fix both $5$ and $6$. Hence the stabilizer of $5$ is the four-element set displayed and the orbit is $\{5,6\}$, with $8=4\cdot2$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
The three parts are as displayed in the statement, each consistent with $\abs G=\abs{\operatorname{stab}}\abs{\operatorname{orb}}=8$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#staba){.pf-ref}, [](#stabb){.pf-ref} and [](#stabc){.pf-ref} give the three stabilizers and orbits, and each satisfies the orbit-stabilizer identity for a group of order $8$, which is the check that no element of $G$ was overlooked.
:::
