::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group with $\cardinality{G} = pq$, where $p$ and $q$ are prime. Prove that every proper subgroup of $G$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's `provenance` is the textbook's Google Books record and no solutions sheet is retained, so the argument below is the card's own, structured from the outline already on the card.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #order-divides}
For a proper subgroup $H$ of $G$, $\cardinality{H}$ divides $pq$ and $\cardinality{H} \ne pq$.

::: pf-proof
Lagrange's theorem gives that $\cardinality{H}$ divides $\cardinality{G} = pq$, and $\cardinality{H} = pq$ would give $H = G$, which properness excludes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #divisors}
$\cardinality{H}$ is $1$, $p$ or $q$; if $p = q$ it is $1$ or $p$.

::: pf-proof
When $p \ne q$ the divisors of $pq$ are $1, p, q, pq$, and $pq$ is excluded by step [](#order-divides){.pf-ref}. When $p = q$ one has $\cardinality{G} = p^{2}$, whose divisors are $1, p, p^{2}$, and $p^{2}$ is excluded by the same step.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #trivial}
If $\cardinality{H} = 1$ then $H = \theset{e} = \generatedby{e}$ is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
A subgroup contains the identity and here has one element, so it is $\theset{e}$, the cyclic group generated by $e$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #prime-order}
If $\cardinality{H}$ is prime then $H$ is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
In this case $\cardinality{H} > 1$, so $H$ contains some $h \ne e$. The cyclic subgroup $\generatedby{h}$ has order $\elementorder{h} > 1$, and $\elementorder{h}$ divides $\cardinality{H}$ by Lagrange's theorem; since $\cardinality{H}$ is prime, $\elementorder{h} = \cardinality{H}$, so $\generatedby{h} = H$ and $H$ is cyclic.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Every proper subgroup of $G$ is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#divisors){.pf-ref} a proper subgroup has order $1$, $p$ or $q$; step [](#trivial){.pf-ref} disposes of order $1$, and step [](#prime-order){.pf-ref} of the prime orders $p$ and $q$.

:::
