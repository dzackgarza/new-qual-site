::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the group of rotations of a plane about a point $P$ in the plane. Thinking of $G$ as a group of permutations of the plane, describe the orbit of a point $Q$ in the plane. (This is the motivation for the name “orbit”.)
:::

::: {.solution}

If $Q\neq P$ the orbit of $Q$ is the circle with centre $P$ passing through $Q$;
if $Q=P$ the orbit is $\{P\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #inside}

Every element of the orbit of $Q$ lies on that circle.

::: pf-proof

An element of $G$ is a rotation about $P$, so it fixes $P$ and preserves all
distances. Hence for a rotation $\rho$ the distance from $P$ to $\rho(Q)$ equals
the distance from $\rho(P)=P$ to $\rho(Q)$, which equals $|PQ|$. So every point
of the orbit is at distance $|PQ|$ from $P$, that is, it lies on the circle with
centre $P$ and radius $|PQ|$. When $Q=P$ this radius is $0$ and the orbit,
containing only points at distance $0$ from $P$, is $\{P\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #onto}

Every point of the circle occurs in the orbit of $Q$, for $Q\neq P$.

::: pf-proof

Let $R$ be a point with $|PR|=|PQ|$ and $Q\neq P$. If $R=Q$ then $R$ is the
image of $Q$ under the identity rotation. Otherwise $R$ and $Q$ determine two
rays from $P$ making an angle $\theta$, and the rotation about $P$ through
$-\theta$ (or $+\theta$, depending on orientation) carries the ray $PQ$ to the
ray $PR$; since both points are at the same distance from $P$, that rotation
carries $Q$ to $R$. So $R$ lies in the orbit.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #orbit}

The orbit is the circle, or the single point $P$ when $Q=P$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#inside){.pf-ref} and [](#onto){.pf-ref} give equality of the orbit of
$Q\neq P$ with the circle of radius $|PQ|$ about $P$; the degenerate case is
settled in step [](#inside){.pf-ref}. This is why a stabilizer is described as
fixing a point and an orbit as the set of positions it can be carried to: the
rotations about $P$ move $Q$ around that circle and nowhere else.

:::
