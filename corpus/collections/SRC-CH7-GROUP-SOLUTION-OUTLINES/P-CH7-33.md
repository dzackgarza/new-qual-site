::: {.problem}
Let $H$ and $K$ be subgroups of a finite group $G$ with $H \subseteq K \subseteq G$. Prove that $\subgroupindex{G : H} = \subgroupindex{G : K} \subgroupindex{K : H}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is that of the retained solution outline for Chapter 7, Exercise 33, PDF p. 3 of `assets/attachments/Ch7Sltns.pdf`, restructured into numbered steps. Here $H\subseteq K\subseteq G$ are subgroups of a finite group, and $\subgroupindex{G:H}$ is the number of left cosets of $H$ in $G$, so that $\cardinality{G}=\subgroupindex{G:H}\cardinality{H}$ by Lagrange's theorem.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #index}

$\subgroupindex{G:K}=\cardinality{G}/\cardinality{K}$ and $\subgroupindex{K:H}=\cardinality{K}/\cardinality{H}$.

::: pf-proof

$K$ is a subgroup of $G$ and $H$ is a subgroup of $K$, and Lagrange's theorem gives $\cardinality{G}=\subgroupindex{G:K}\cardinality{K}$ and $\cardinality{K}=\subgroupindex{K:H}\cardinality{H}$; divide.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #indexg}

$\subgroupindex{G:H}=\cardinality{G}/\cardinality{H}$.

::: pf-proof

The same theorem applied to the subgroup $H$ of $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$\subgroupindex{G:H}=\subgroupindex{G:K}\subgroupindex{K:H}$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#index){.pf-ref} and [](#indexg){.pf-ref}, $\cardinality{G}/\cardinality{H}=(\cardinality{G}/\cardinality{K})(\cardinality{K}/\cardinality{H})$, the multiplication being legitimate because $\cardinality{H},\cardinality{K}$ are nonzero.

:::
