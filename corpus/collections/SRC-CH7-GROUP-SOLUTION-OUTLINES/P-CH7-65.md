::: {.problem}
If $G$ is a finite group with fewer than $100$ elements and $G$ has subgroups of orders $10$ and $25$, what is the order of $G$?
:::

::: {.solution}

$|G|=50$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #divisible}

$10$ and $25$ both divide $|G|$, hence $50\mid |G|$.

::: pf-proof

By Lagrange's theorem the order of a subgroup divides the order of the group, so
$10\mid |G|$ and $25\mid |G|$. An integer divisible by $10=2\cdot 5$ and by
$25=5^{2}$ is divisible by $2\cdot 5^{2}=50$: the exponent of $2$ in $|G|$ is at
least $1$ and the exponent of $5$ is at least $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #bound}

The only multiple of $50$ that is less than $100$ is $50$ itself.

::: pf-proof

A multiple of $50$ is $50k$ with $k\ge 1$ an integer, and $50k<100$ gives $k<2$,
so $k=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #consistent}

So $|G|=50$, and this is consistent: groups of order $50$ with subgroups of
orders $10$ and $25$ exist.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#divisible){.pf-ref} and [](#bound){.pf-ref} force $|G|=50$. For
consistency, $\ZZ_{50}$ is cyclic of order $50$ with a unique subgroup of each
order dividing $50$, in particular of orders $10$ and $25$, so the conditions
are simultaneously satisfiable and $50$ is attained.

:::
