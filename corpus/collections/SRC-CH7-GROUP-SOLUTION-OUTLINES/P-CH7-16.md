::: {.problem}
Suppose that $K$ is a proper subgroup of $H$ and $H$ is a proper subgroup of $G$. If $\cardinality{K} = 42$ and $\cardinality{G} = 420$, what are the possible orders of $H$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection retains its solution outline as `assets/attachments/Ch7Sltns.pdf`, and entry "# 16" of that outline covers Gallian 7e Chapter 7 Exercise 14. The outline applies Lagrange's theorem to obtain the candidates $42,84,210,420$, then excludes $42$ and $420$ by properness. The argument below retains those steps and proves the divisor enumeration explicitly.

Write $h=\cardinality{H}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #candidates}
The order $h$ is one of $42,84,210,420$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #divisibility}
The integer $h$ satisfies $42\mid h$ and $h\mid420$.

::: pf-proof
Since $K$ is a subgroup of the finite group $H$, Lagrange's theorem gives $\cardinality{K}\mid h$, that is, $42\mid h$. Since $H$ is a subgroup of the finite group $G$, it likewise gives $h\mid\cardinality{G}=420$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #enumeration}
Writing $h=42d$, the quotient $d$ is a positive divisor of $10$, so $d\in\theset{1,2,5,10}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#divisibility){.pf-ref} supplies a positive integer $d$ with $h=42d$. Substitution into $h\mid420$ gives $42d\mid42\cdot10$, so $d\mid10$. Conversely, every positive divisor $d$ of $10$ gives $42d\mid420$. The positive divisors of $10$ are exactly $1,2,5,10$; multiplying them by $42$ gives the four displayed candidates.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #properness}
Neither $h=42$ nor $h=420$ is compatible with the proper inclusions.

::: pf-proof
If $h=42=\cardinality{K}$, then the finite subset $K$ of $H$ has the same cardinality as $H$, so $K=H$, contrary to $K$ being a proper subgroup. If $h=420=\cardinality{G}$, then similarly $H=G$, contrary to $H$ being a proper subgroup. Thus the first and last candidates from step [](#candidates){.pf-ref} are impossible.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The possible orders of $H$ are $\boxed{84\text{ and }210}$.

::: pf-proof
The two remaining candidates from step [](#candidates){.pf-ref} are $h=42\cdot2=84$ and $h=42\cdot5=210$. Neither $K=H$ nor $H=G$ follows in either case, since $42<84,210<420$. Thus these are precisely the orders left by Lagrange's theorem and the two proper-inclusion hypotheses.
:::
