::: {.problem}
Let $X_f$ be the mapping cylinder of a continuous map $f:X\to Y$, and let
$$
j:Y\to X_f,\qquad j(y)=[y]
$$
denote the canonical inclusion.
Write $\overline Y=j(Y)$.

1. Define
   $$
   r:X_f\to\overline Y,
   \qquad
   r([(x,s)])=[f(x)],\quad r([y])=[y].
   $$
   Show that $r$ is a retraction.
   Show that
   $$
   F_t([(x,s)])=[(x,t+(1-t)s)],\qquad F_t([y])=[y]
   $$
   defines a deformation retraction of $X_f$ onto $\overline Y$.

2. Show that $j$ is an embedding and conclude that $X_f$ is homotopy equivalent to $Y$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
q:
(X\times I)\sqcup Y
\longrightarrow
X_f
$$
for the quotient map, where
$$
(x,1)\sim f(x).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Define
$$
p:X_f\to Y
$$
by
$$
p([(x,s)])=f(x),
\qquad
p([y])=y.
$$
Then $p$ is well defined and continuous.


::: pf-proof

On the disjoint union
$$
(X\times I)\sqcup Y,
$$
define
$$
\widetilde p(x,s)=f(x),
\qquad
\widetilde p(y)=y.
$$
This map is continuous on each summand, hence continuous on the disjoint
union.

The only identifications used to form $X_f$ are
$$
(x,1)\sim f(x),
$$
and
$$
\widetilde p(x,1)
=
f(x)
=
\widetilde p(f(x)).
$$
Thus $\widetilde p$ is constant on equivalence classes.
By the universal property of the quotient, it descends uniquely to a
continuous map
$$
p:X_f\to Y.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The canonical inclusion
$$
j:Y\to X_f,
\qquad
j(y)=[y],
$$
is injective and satisfies
$$
p\circ j
=
\operatorname{id}_Y.
$$


::: pf-proof

For
$$
y\in Y,
$$
$$
(p\circ j)(y)
=
p([y])
=
y.
$$
Hence
$$
p\circ j
=
\operatorname{id}_Y.
$$
A map with a left inverse is injective, so $j$ is injective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Define
$$
r:X_f\to\overline Y
$$
by
$$
r([(x,s)])=[f(x)],
\qquad
r([y])=[y].
$$
Then
$$
r=j\circ p.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a cylinder point,
$$
(j\circ p)([(x,s)])
=
j(f(x))
=
[f(x)].
$$
For a target point,
$$
(j\circ p)([y])
=
j(y)
=
[y].
$$
These are exactly the formulas defining $r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The map $r$ is continuous.


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
p:X_f\to Y
$$
is continuous by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
The canonical map
$$
j:Y\to X_f
$$
is continuous because it is the restriction of the quotient map $q$ to the
$Y$ summand, followed by corestriction to its image.
Therefore
$$
r=j\circ p
$$
is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The map $r$ is a retraction onto
$$
\overline Y=j(Y).
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
z\in\overline Y.
$$
Then
$$
z=[y]
$$
for some
$$
y\in Y.
$$
By definition,
$$
r(z)
=
r([y])
=
[y]
=
z.
$$
Hence
$$
r|_{\overline Y}
=
\operatorname{id}_{\overline Y}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For
$$
t\in I,
$$
define
$$
F_t([(x,s)])
=
[(x,t+(1-t)s)],
\qquad
F_t([y])=[y].
$$
The formula is well defined.


::: pf-proof

The only issue is compatibility with the mapping-cylinder identification
$$
(x,1)\sim f(x).
$$
For such a point,
$$
t+(1-t)\cdot1
=
1,
$$
so
$$
F_t([(x,1)])
=
[(x,1)]
=
[f(x)].
$$
On the other representative,
$$
F_t([f(x)])
=
[f(x)].
$$
Thus equivalent representatives have the same image.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The map
$$
F:X_f\times I\to X_f,
\qquad
F(z,t)=F_t(z),
$$
is continuous.


::: pf-proof

Define on
$$
((X\times I)\sqcup Y)\times I
$$
the map
$$
\widetilde F((x,s),t)
=
q(x,t+(1-t)s)
$$
and
$$
\widetilde F(y,t)
=
q(y).
$$
This map is continuous.

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} it is constant on the equivalence classes induced by
$$
(x,1,t)\sim(f(x),t).
$$
The product
$$
q\times\operatorname{id}_I
:
((X\times I)\sqcup Y)\times I
\to
X_f\times I
$$
is a quotient map because $I$ is locally compact Hausdorff.
Hence the quotient universal property gives a unique continuous map
$$
F:X_f\times I\to X_f
$$
with the displayed formulas.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

One has
$$
F_0
=
\operatorname{id}_{X_f}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a cylinder point,
$$
F_0([(x,s)])
=
[(x,0+(1-0)s)]
=
[(x,s)].
$$
For
$$
[y]\in\overline Y,
$$
the point is fixed by definition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

One has
$$
F_1=r.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a cylinder point,
$$
F_1([(x,s)])
=
[(x,1)]
=
[f(x)]
=
r([(x,s)]).
$$
For
$$
[y],
$$
both maps fix the point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The homotopy fixes
$$
\overline Y
$$
pointwise:
$$
F_t(z)=z
\qquad
(z\in\overline Y, t\in I).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is part of the definition:
$$
F_t([y])=[y].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore $F$ is a deformation retraction of $X_f$ onto
$$
\overline Y.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the identity at time $0$.
Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives the retraction at time $1$.
Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} fixes the retract throughout the homotopy.
These are precisely the defining conditions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The canonical map
$$
j:Y\to X_f
$$
is an embedding.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $j$ is a continuous injection.
Its image is
$$
\overline Y.
$$
The inverse map
$$
j(Y)\to Y
$$
is the restriction
$$
p|_{\overline Y},
$$
because
$$
p([y])=y.
$$
This restriction is continuous since $p$ is continuous.
Thus
$$
j:Y\xrightarrow{\cong}\overline Y
$$
is a homeomorphism, so $j$ is an embedding.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The maps
$$
j:Y\to X_f
$$
and
$$
p:X_f\to Y
$$
are homotopy inverses.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
p\circ j
=
\operatorname{id}_Y.
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
j\circ p
=
r.
$$
Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives
$$
r
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_{X_f}.
$$
Hence $j$ and $p$ are homotopy inverses.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Consequently
$$
\boxed{
X_f\simeq Y.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} is exactly the definition of a homotopy equivalence.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} prove part (1), and steps [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} prove part (2).

:::
