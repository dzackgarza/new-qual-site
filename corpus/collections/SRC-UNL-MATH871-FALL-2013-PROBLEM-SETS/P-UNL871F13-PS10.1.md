::: {.problem}
Identify a familiar space homeomorphic to each of the following constructions, and prove your answer geometrically.

1. The mapping cylinder of
   \[
   f:I\to S^1,\qquad f(t)=(\cos(4\pi t),\sin(4\pi t)).
   \]

2. The mapping torus of
   \[
   f:S^1\to S^1,\qquad f(x,y)=(x,-y).
   \]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
\[
f:I\to S^1,
\qquad
f(t)=e^{4\pi i t}.
\]
Its mapping cylinder is
\[
M_f
=
\bigl(
(I\times I)\sqcup S^1
\bigr)/{\sim},
\]
where
\[
(t,0)\sim f(t).
\]


::: pf-proof

This is the definition of the mapping cylinder, using the second interval
coordinate as the cylinder direction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The mapping cylinder $M_f$ is a compact connected surface with one
boundary component.


::: pf-proof

Compactness and connectedness follow immediately from the quotient
construction.

Away from the target circle
\[
S^1\subset M_f,
\]
the local models come from the rectangle
\[
I\times I.
\]

At a generic point of the target circle, the map
\[
t\longmapsto e^{4\pi i t}
\]
has two local inverse branches. The corresponding two half-strips along
\[
I\times\{0\}
\]
glue along their boundary intervals to form an ordinary disk neighborhood.

At the special point
\[
f(0)=f(1/2)=f(1),
\]
the two endpoint half-neighborhoods at
\[
t=0
\quad\text{and}\quad
t=1
\]
together form one local branch, while the interior point
\[
t=1/2
\]
forms the other. Again the quotient neighborhood is a disk.
Thus the target circle consists of interior points of the surface.

The remaining boundary consists of the top edge
\[
I\times\{1\}
\]
together with the two vertical sides. Their lower endpoints are identified
at the same target point, so these three arcs form a single boundary circle.
Hence $M_f$ is a compact connected surface with exactly one boundary
component.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The mapping cylinder deformation retracts onto the target circle.


::: pf-proof

Every mapping cylinder deformation retracts onto its target:
slide
\[
(t,s)
\]
down the cylinder coordinate toward
\[
(t,0)\sim f(t),
\]
while fixing the target copy of $S^1$.
Therefore
\[
M_f\simeq S^1.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently
\[
\chi(M_f)=0.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
\[
M_f\simeq S^1.
\]
Euler characteristic is a homotopy invariant for finite CW complexes, and
\[
\chi(S^1)=0.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The surface $M_f$ is homeomorphic to the Möbius band.


::: pf-proof

A compact connected orientable surface of genus $g$ with one boundary
component has
\[
\chi
=
2-2g-1
=
1-2g,
\]
which can never equal $0$.

A compact connected nonorientable surface with $k$ crosscaps and one
boundary component has
\[
\chi
=
2-k-1
=
1-k.
\]
The equation
\[
1-k=0
\]
gives
\[
k=1.
\]
The nonorientable genus-one surface with one boundary component is the
Möbius band.

Thus, by the classification of compact surfaces,
\[
\boxed{
M_f\cong\text{Möbius band}.
}
\]
Geometrically, the two traversals of the target circle become the two local
sides of the band and exchange after one circuit, which is exactly the
Möbius twist.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For part (2), parameterize
\[
S^1
\cong
\RR/\ZZ
\]
by
\[
t\longmapsto
(\cos2\pi t,\sin2\pi t).
\]
Under this parameterization, the reflection
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(x,-y)
\]
is
\[
t\longmapsto -t
\pmod1.
\]


::: pf-proof

Indeed,
\[
(\cos2\pi(-t),\sin2\pi(-t))
=
(\cos2\pi t,-\sin2\pi t).
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The mapping torus is the quotient of the square
\[
[0,1]\times[0,1]
\]
by the identifications
\[
(0,s)\sim(1,s)
\]
and
\[
(t,1)\sim(1-t,0).
\]


::: pf-proof

The first identification expresses the circle coordinate
\[
t\in\RR/\ZZ.
\]
The mapping-torus relation identifies
\[
(t,1)
\]
with
\[
(f(t),0),
\]
and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
\[
f(t)=1-t
\pmod1.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The quotient in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is the standard fundamental polygon for
the Klein bottle.


::: pf-proof

One pair of opposite sides is glued preserving orientation:
\[
(0,s)\sim(1,s).
\]
The other pair is glued reversing orientation:
\[
(t,1)\sim(1-t,0).
\]
This is exactly the usual square construction of the Klein bottle.
Hence
\[
\boxed{
T_f\cong K,
}
\]
where $K$ is the Klein bottle.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} identifies the mapping cylinder as the Möbius band, and step
[](#s1-8){.pf-ref} identifies the mapping torus as the Klein bottle.

:::
