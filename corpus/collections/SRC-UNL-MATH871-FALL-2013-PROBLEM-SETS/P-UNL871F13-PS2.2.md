::: {.problem}
For a set $X$, define
$$
\mathcal B:=\{U\subseteq X:X\setminus U\text{ is infinite}\}\cup\{X\}.
$$

1. Show that $\mathcal B$ is a basis for a topology on $X$.

2. Identify the topology $\mathcal T(\mathcal B)$ for every set $X$, and prove your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The family $\mathcal B$ covers $X$.


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
X\in\mathcal B.
$$
Hence every point of $X$ belongs to at least one basis candidate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If
$$
U,V\in\mathcal B
$$
and
$$
x\in U\cap V,
$$
then there exists
$$
W\in\mathcal B
$$
such that
$$
x\in W\subseteq U\cap V.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
U=V=X,
$$
take
$$
W=X.
$$

Otherwise at least one of $U,V$ is not $X$.
Suppose, without loss of generality,
$$
U\ne X.
$$
Then
$$
X\setminus U
$$
is infinite.
Now
$$
X\setminus(U\cap V)
=
(X\setminus U)\cup(X\setminus V),
$$
which contains the infinite set
$$
X\setminus U.
$$
Hence
$$
X\setminus(U\cap V)
$$
is infinite.
Therefore
$$
U\cap V\in\mathcal B.
$$
Take
$$
W=U\cap V.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal B
\text{ is a basis for a topology on }X.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The two basis axioms are exactly steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Suppose $X$ is finite.
Then
$$
\mathcal B=\{X\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
U\subseteq X,
$$
then
$$
X\setminus U
$$
is finite because $X$ is finite.
Thus no proper subset $U$ satisfies the condition that its complement be
infinite.
The only basis element supplied separately by the definition is $X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $X$ is finite, then
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal T(\mathcal B)
=
\{\varnothing,X\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

A topology generated by the one-element basis
$$
\{X\}
$$
consists of unions of basis elements.
The only such unions are
$$
\varnothing
$$
and
$$
X.
$$
Thus the topology is indiscrete.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Suppose $X$ is infinite.
Then for every
$$
x\in X,
$$
the singleton
$$
\{x\}
$$
belongs to $\mathcal B$.


::: pf-proof

Since $X$ is infinite,
$$
X\setminus\{x\}
$$
is still infinite.
Therefore
$$
\{x\}\in\mathcal B.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If $X$ is infinite, then every subset
$$
A\subseteq X
$$
is open in
$$
\mathcal T(\mathcal B).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
\{x\}\in\mathcal B
$$
for every
$$
x\in A.
$$
Hence
$$
A
=
\bigcup_{x\in A}\{x\}
$$
is a union of basis elements.
Therefore $A$ is open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Thus if $X$ is infinite,
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal T(\mathcal B)
=
\mathcal P(X),
}
$$
the discrete topology.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} shows every subset of $X$ is open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The complete answer is
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal T(\mathcal B)
=
\begin{cases}
\{\varnothing,X\},
&X\text{ finite},\\[1mm]
\mathcal P(X),
&X\text{ infinite}.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves that $\mathcal B$ is a basis, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} identifies
the generated topology in every case.

:::
