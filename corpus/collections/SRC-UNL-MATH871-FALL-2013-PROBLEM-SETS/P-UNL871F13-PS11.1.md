::: {.problem}
1. Let $h:(X,x_0)\to(Y,y_0)$ be continuous.
   Prove that the induced map
   $$
   h_*:\pi_1(X,x_0)\to\pi_1(Y,y_0)
   $$
   is a well-defined group homomorphism.

2. For composable pointed maps $h$ and $k$, prove
   $$
   (k\circ h)_*=k_*\circ h_*.
   $$

3. Prove that the identity map induces the identity homomorphism on $\pi_1$.

4. Let $f:X\to Y$ be continuous, let $x_0,x_1\in X$, and let $\alpha$ be a path from $x_0$ to $x_1$.
   If $\beta_\alpha$ denotes change of basepoint along $\alpha$, prove
   $$
   \beta_{f\circ\alpha}\circ f_{*,x_0}
   =f_{*,x_1}\circ\beta_\alpha.
   $$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For a based loop
$$
\gamma:I\to X
$$
at $x_0$, define
$$
h_*([\gamma])
\coloneqq
[h\circ\gamma].
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
h(x_0)=y_0,
$$
one has
$$
(h\circ\gamma)(0)
=
h(x_0)
=
y_0
$$
and similarly
$$
(h\circ\gamma)(1)=y_0.
$$
Thus
$$
h\circ\gamma
$$
is a loop based at $y_0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map
$$
h_*:
\pi_1(X,x_0)
\to
\pi_1(Y,y_0)
$$
is well defined.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\gamma_0
\simeq
\gamma_1
$$
rel endpoints.
Let
$$
H:I\times I\to X
$$
be such a homotopy.
Then
$$
h\circ H:
I\times I\to Y
$$
is continuous and satisfies
$$
(h\circ H)(s,0)
=
h(\gamma_0(s)),
$$
$$
(h\circ H)(s,1)
=
h(\gamma_1(s)).
$$
Since the original homotopy fixes the endpoints at $x_0$, the composed
homotopy fixes them at
$$
h(x_0)=y_0.
$$
Hence
$$
h\circ\gamma_0
\simeq
h\circ\gamma_1
$$
rel endpoints.
Thus the value of $h_*([\gamma])$ depends only on the homotopy class.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The induced map $h_*$ is a group homomorphism.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
[\gamma],[\lambda]
\in
\pi_1(X,x_0).
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_*([\gamma][\lambda])
&=
h_*([\gamma*\lambda])\\
&=
[h\circ(\gamma*\lambda)].
\end{aligned}
$$
Composition with $h$ commutes with path concatenation:
$$
h\circ(\gamma*\lambda)
=
(h\circ\gamma)*(h\circ\lambda).
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_*([\gamma][\lambda])
&=
[(h\circ\gamma)*(h\circ\lambda)]\\
&=
[h\circ\gamma],[h\circ\lambda]\\
&=
h_*([\gamma])h_*([\lambda]).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For pointed maps
$$
h:(X,x_0)\to(Y,y_0),
\qquad
k:(Y,y_0)\to(Z,z_0),
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{
(k\circ h)_*
=
k_*\circ h_*.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For
$$
[\gamma]\in\pi_1(X,x_0),
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(k\circ h)_*([\gamma])
&=
[(k\circ h)\circ\gamma]\\
&=
[k\circ(h\circ\gamma)]\\
&=
k_*([h\circ\gamma])\\
&=
(k_*\circ h_*)([\gamma]).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the homomorphisms agree on every element.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The identity map induces the identity homomorphism:
$$
\boxed{
(\operatorname{id}_X)_*
=
\operatorname{id}_{\pi_1(X,x_0)}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For every based loop $\gamma$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname{id}_X)_*([\gamma])
&=
[\operatorname{id}_X\circ\gamma]\\
&=
[\gamma].
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Let
$$
\alpha:I\to X
$$
be a path from $x_0$ to $x_1$.
Define the change-of-basepoint isomorphism
$$
\beta_\alpha:
\pi_1(X,x_0)
\to
\pi_1(X,x_1)
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\beta_\alpha([\gamma])
=
[\bar\alpha*\gamma*\alpha].
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The reverse path
$$
\bar\alpha
$$
runs from $x_1$ to $x_0$.
Thus
$$
\bar\alpha*\gamma*\alpha
$$
starts and ends at $x_1$, so it is a loop based at $x_1$.

This construction is the standard change-of-basepoint isomorphism; its
inverse is
$$
\beta_{\bar\alpha}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The path
$$
f\circ\alpha
$$
runs from
$$
f(x_0)
$$
to
$$
f(x_1),
$$
and
$$
\overline{f\circ\alpha}
=
f\circ\bar\alpha.
$$


::: pf-proof

The endpoint statement follows directly from composition.
For
$$
t\in I,
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{f\circ\alpha}(t)
&=
(f\circ\alpha)(1-t)\\
&=
f(\alpha(1-t))\\
&=
f(\bar\alpha(t))\\
&=
(f\circ\bar\alpha)(t).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

For every
$$
[\gamma]
\in
\pi_1(X,x_0),
$$
$$
\bigl(
\beta_{f\circ\alpha}
\circ
f_{*,x_0}
\bigr)([\gamma])
=
[f\circ\bar\alpha
*
f\circ\gamma
*
f\circ\alpha].
$$


::: pf-proof

First,
$$
f_{*,x_0}([\gamma])
=
[f\circ\gamma].
$$
Apply the change-of-basepoint formula in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} using the path
$$
f\circ\alpha.
$$
Then use step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} to identify its reverse.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

On the other hand,
$$
\bigl(
f_{*,x_1}
\circ
\beta_\alpha
\bigr)([\gamma])
=
[f\circ\bar\alpha
*
f\circ\gamma
*
f\circ\alpha].
$$


::: pf-proof

First,
$$
\beta_\alpha([\gamma])
=
[\bar\alpha*\gamma*\alpha].
$$
Applying
$$
f_{*,x_1}
$$
gives
$$
[f\circ(\bar\alpha*\gamma*\alpha)].
$$
Composition with $f$ preserves concatenation, so this equals
$$
[f\circ\bar\alpha
*
f\circ\gamma
*
f\circ\alpha].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\beta_{f\circ\alpha}\circ f_{*,x_0}
=
f_{*,x_1}\circ\beta_\alpha.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} show that both homomorphisms take every
$$
[\gamma]\in\pi_1(X,x_0)
$$
to the same element.

:::
