::: {.problem}
1. Show that the composition of homotopy equivalences $X\to Y$ and $Y\to Z$ is a homotopy equivalence $X\to Z$.
   Deduce that homotopy equivalence is an equivalence relation.

2. Show that homotopy among maps $X\to Y$ is an equivalence relation.

3. Show that a map homotopic to a homotopy equivalence is itself a homotopy equivalence.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If
$$
f_0,f_1:X\to Y
$$
are homotopic and
$$
u:W\to X,
\qquad
v:Y\to Z
$$
are continuous, then
$$
f_0\circ u
\simeq
f_1\circ u
$$
and
$$
v\circ f_0
\simeq
v\circ f_1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
H:X\times I\to Y
$$
be a homotopy from $f_0$ to $f_1$.
Then
$$
(w,t)
\longmapsto
H(u(w),t)
$$
is a homotopy from
$$
f_0\circ u
$$
to
$$
f_1\circ u.
$$

Similarly,
$$
(x,t)
\longmapsto
v(H(x,t))
$$
is a homotopy from
$$
v\circ f_0
$$
to
$$
v\circ f_1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Suppose
$$
f:X\to Y
$$
and
$$
g:Y\to Z
$$
are homotopy equivalences.
Choose homotopy inverses
$$
f':Y\to X
$$
and
$$
g':Z\to Y.
$$
Then
$$
f'\circ g':Z\to X
$$
is a homotopy inverse for
$$
g\circ f:X\to Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $f'$ is a homotopy inverse for $f$,
$$
f'\circ f
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X
$$
and
$$
f\circ f'
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Y.
$$
Similarly,
$$
g'\circ g
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Y
$$
and
$$
g\circ g'
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Z.
$$

Now
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f'\circ g')\circ(g\circ f)
&=
f'\circ(g'\circ g)\circ f\\
&\simeq
f'\circ\operatorname{id}_Y\circ f\\
&=
f'\circ f\\
&\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X,
\end{aligned}
$$
using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} to compose homotopies.

Likewise,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)\circ(f'\circ g')
&=
g\circ(f\circ f')\circ g'\\
&\simeq
g\circ\operatorname{id}_Y\circ g'\\
&=
g\circ g'\\
&\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
f'\circ g'
$$
is a homotopy inverse of
$$
g\circ f.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Homotopy equivalence of spaces is reflexive.


::: pf-proof

For every space $X$,
$$
\operatorname{id}_X:X\to X
$$
is its own homotopy inverse.
Indeed,
$$
\operatorname{id}_X\circ\operatorname{id}_X
=
\operatorname{id}_X.
$$
Thus
$$
X\simeq X.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Homotopy equivalence of spaces is symmetric.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
f:X\to Y
$$
is a homotopy equivalence with homotopy inverse
$$
g:Y\to X.
$$
Then
$$
g\circ f
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X
$$
and
$$
f\circ g
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Y.
$$
These same equations say that $f$ is a homotopy inverse for $g$.
Hence
$$
Y\simeq X.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Homotopy equivalence of spaces is transitive.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
X\simeq Y
$$
and
$$
Y\simeq Z,
$$
choose homotopy equivalences
$$
f:X\to Y,
\qquad
g:Y\to Z.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
g\circ f:X\to Z
$$
is a homotopy equivalence.
Thus
$$
X\simeq Z.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore homotopy equivalence is an equivalence relation on spaces.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} prove reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Homotopy among maps
$$
X\to Y
$$
is reflexive.


::: pf-proof

For a map
$$
f:X\to Y,
$$
define
$$
H(x,t)=f(x).
$$
Then $H$ is a homotopy from $f$ to itself.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Homotopy among maps
$$
X\to Y
$$
is symmetric.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
H:X\times I\to Y
$$
is a homotopy from $f$ to $g$.
Define
$$
\overline H(x,t)
=
H(x,1-t).
$$
Then
$$
\overline H(x,0)
=
H(x,1)
=
g(x)
$$
and
$$
\overline H(x,1)
=
H(x,0)
=
f(x).
$$
Thus
$$
g\simeq f.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Homotopy among maps
$$
X\to Y
$$
is transitive.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
H:X\times I\to Y
$$
is a homotopy from $f$ to $g$, and
$$
K:X\times I\to Y
$$
is a homotopy from $g$ to $h$.
Define
$$
L(x,t)
=
\begin{cases}
H(x,2t),
&0\le t\le\frac12,\\[1mm]
K(x,2t-1),
&\frac12\le t\le1.
\end{cases}
$$
At
$$
t=\frac12,
$$
the two formulas agree because
$$
H(x,1)
=
g(x)
=
K(x,0).
$$
The pasting lemma therefore gives continuity.
Also
$$
L(x,0)=f(x),
\qquad
L(x,1)=h(x).
$$
Hence
$$
f\simeq h.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore homotopy is an equivalence relation on the set of maps
$$
X\to Y.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} establish reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Suppose
$$
f,g:X\to Y
$$
satisfy
$$
f\simeq g,
$$
and suppose $g$ is a homotopy equivalence.
Choose a homotopy inverse
$$
h:Y\to X
$$
for $g$.
Then $h$ is also a homotopy inverse for $f$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
f\simeq g,
$$
step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
h\circ f
\simeq
h\circ g.
$$
Because $h$ is a homotopy inverse for $g$,
$$
h\circ g
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X.
$$
By transitivity of homotopy,
$$
h\circ f
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X.
$$

Similarly,
$$
f\circ h
\simeq
g\circ h
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Y.
$$
Hence $h$ is a homotopy inverse for $f$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore any map homotopic to a homotopy equivalence is itself a
homotopy equivalence.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} explicitly supplies a homotopy inverse.

:::
