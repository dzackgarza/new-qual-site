::: {.problem}
1. Show that the composition of homotopy equivalences $X\to Y$ and $Y\to Z$ is a homotopy equivalence $X\to Z$.
   Deduce that homotopy equivalence is an equivalence relation.

2. Show that homotopy among maps $X\to Y$ is an equivalence relation.

3. Show that a map homotopic to a homotopy equivalence is itself a homotopy equivalence.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If
\[
f_0,f_1:X\to Y
\]
are homotopic and
\[
u:W\to X,
\qquad
v:Y\to Z
\]
are continuous, then
\[
f_0\circ u
\simeq
f_1\circ u
\]
and
\[
v\circ f_0
\simeq
v\circ f_1.
\]

::: {.proof}
Let
\[
H:X\times I\to Y
\]
be a homotopy from $f_0$ to $f_1$.
Then
\[
(w,t)
\longmapsto
H(u(w),t)
\]
is a homotopy from
\[
f_0\circ u
\]
to
\[
f_1\circ u.
\]

Similarly,
\[
(x,t)
\longmapsto
v(H(x,t))
\]
is a homotopy from
\[
v\circ f_0
\]
to
\[
v\circ f_1.
\]
:::

<1>2. Suppose
\[
f:X\to Y
\]
and
\[
g:Y\to Z
\]
are homotopy equivalences.
Choose homotopy inverses
\[
f':Y\to X
\]
and
\[
g':Z\to Y.
\]
Then
\[
f'\circ g':Z\to X
\]
is a homotopy inverse for
\[
g\circ f:X\to Z.
\]

::: {.proof}
Because $f'$ is a homotopy inverse for $f$,
\[
f'\circ f
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X
\]
and
\[
f\circ f'
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Y.
\]
Similarly,
\[
g'\circ g
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Y
\]
and
\[
g\circ g'
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Z.
\]

Now
\[
\begin{aligned}
(f'\circ g')\circ(g\circ f)
&=
f'\circ(g'\circ g)\circ f\\
&\simeq
f'\circ\operatorname{id}_Y\circ f\\
&=
f'\circ f\\
&\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X,
\end{aligned}
\]
using step <1>1 to compose homotopies.

Likewise,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)\circ(f'\circ g')
&=
g\circ(f\circ f')\circ g'\\
&\simeq
g\circ\operatorname{id}_Y\circ g'\\
&=
g\circ g'\\
&\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Z.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
f'\circ g'
\]
is a homotopy inverse of
\[
g\circ f.
\]
:::

<1>3. Homotopy equivalence of spaces is reflexive.

::: {.proof}
For every space $X$,
\[
\operatorname{id}_X:X\to X
\]
is its own homotopy inverse.
Indeed,
\[
\operatorname{id}_X\circ\operatorname{id}_X
=
\operatorname{id}_X.
\]
Thus
\[
X\simeq X.
\]
:::

<1>4. Homotopy equivalence of spaces is symmetric.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
f:X\to Y
\]
is a homotopy equivalence with homotopy inverse
\[
g:Y\to X.
\]
Then
\[
g\circ f
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X
\]
and
\[
f\circ g
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Y.
\]
These same equations say that $f$ is a homotopy inverse for $g$.
Hence
\[
Y\simeq X.
\]
:::

<1>5. Homotopy equivalence of spaces is transitive.

::: {.proof}
If
\[
X\simeq Y
\]
and
\[
Y\simeq Z,
\]
choose homotopy equivalences
\[
f:X\to Y,
\qquad
g:Y\to Z.
\]
By step <1>2,
\[
g\circ f:X\to Z
\]
is a homotopy equivalence.
Thus
\[
X\simeq Z.
\]
:::

<1>6. Therefore homotopy equivalence is an equivalence relation on spaces.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3--<1>5 prove reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity.
:::

<1>7. Homotopy among maps
\[
X\to Y
\]
is reflexive.

::: {.proof}
For a map
\[
f:X\to Y,
\]
define
\[
H(x,t)=f(x).
\]
Then $H$ is a homotopy from $f$ to itself.
:::

<1>8. Homotopy among maps
\[
X\to Y
\]
is symmetric.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
H:X\times I\to Y
\]
is a homotopy from $f$ to $g$.
Define
\[
\overline H(x,t)
=
H(x,1-t).
\]
Then
\[
\overline H(x,0)
=
H(x,1)
=
g(x)
\]
and
\[
\overline H(x,1)
=
H(x,0)
=
f(x).
\]
Thus
\[
g\simeq f.
\]
:::

<1>9. Homotopy among maps
\[
X\to Y
\]
is transitive.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
H:X\times I\to Y
\]
is a homotopy from $f$ to $g$, and
\[
K:X\times I\to Y
\]
is a homotopy from $g$ to $h$.
Define
\[
L(x,t)
=
\begin{cases}
H(x,2t),
&0\le t\le\frac12,\\[1mm]
K(x,2t-1),
&\frac12\le t\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
At
\[
t=\frac12,
\]
the two formulas agree because
\[
H(x,1)
=
g(x)
=
K(x,0).
\]
The pasting lemma therefore gives continuity.
Also
\[
L(x,0)=f(x),
\qquad
L(x,1)=h(x).
\]
Hence
\[
f\simeq h.
\]
:::

<1>10. Therefore homotopy is an equivalence relation on the set of maps
\[
X\to Y.
\]

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>7--<1>9 establish reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity.
:::

<1>11. Suppose
\[
f,g:X\to Y
\]
satisfy
\[
f\simeq g,
\]
and suppose $g$ is a homotopy equivalence.
Choose a homotopy inverse
\[
h:Y\to X
\]
for $g$.
Then $h$ is also a homotopy inverse for $f$.

::: {.proof}
Since
\[
f\simeq g,
\]
step <1>1 gives
\[
h\circ f
\simeq
h\circ g.
\]
Because $h$ is a homotopy inverse for $g$,
\[
h\circ g
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X.
\]
By transitivity of homotopy,
\[
h\circ f
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_X.
\]

Similarly,
\[
f\circ h
\simeq
g\circ h
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_Y.
\]
Hence $h$ is a homotopy inverse for $f$.
:::

<1>12. Therefore any map homotopic to a homotopy equivalence is itself a
homotopy equivalence.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>11 explicitly supplies a homotopy inverse.
:::

<1>13. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves closure under composition.
Step <1>6 proves that homotopy equivalence is an equivalence relation.
Step <1>10 proves that homotopy of maps is an equivalence relation.
Step <1>12 proves the final stability statement.
