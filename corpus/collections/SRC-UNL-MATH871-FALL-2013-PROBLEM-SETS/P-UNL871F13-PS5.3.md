::: {.problem}
For each space and equivalence relation below, identify a familiar space homeomorphic to the quotient and prove your answer.

1. Let $X=\RR^2$ and define
   $$
   (x_0,y_0)\sim(x_1,y_1)
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   x_0+y_0^2=x_1+y_1^2.
   $$

2. Let $X=\RR^2$ and define
   $$
   (x_0,y_0)\sim(x_1,y_1)
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   x_0^2+y_0^2=x_1^2+y_1^2.
   $$

3. Let $X=[-1,1]$ and let $\sim$ be the smallest equivalence relation satisfying $-p\sim p$ for every $p\in X$.

4. Let $X=\RR$ and let $\sim$ be the smallest equivalence relation satisfying $x\sim x+n$ for every $x\in\RR$ and $n\in\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For part (1), define
$$
q_1:\RR^2\to\RR,
\qquad
q_1(x,y)=x+y^2.
$$
Then the fibers of $q_1$ are exactly the equivalence classes.


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
(x_0,y_0)\sim(x_1,y_1)
$$
if and only if
$$
x_0+y_0^2
=
x_1+y_1^2.
$$
This is precisely
$$
q_1(x_0,y_0)
=
q_1(x_1,y_1).
$$
Thus two points are equivalent exactly when they lie in the same fiber.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map $q_1$ is a continuous open surjection.


::: pf-proof

Continuity is immediate.
Surjectivity follows from
$$
q_1(t,0)=t.
$$

To prove openness, write
$$
q_1
=
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ H,
$$
where
$$
H:\RR^2\to\RR^2,
\qquad
H(x,y)=(x+y^2,y).
$$
The map $H$ is a homeomorphism with inverse
$$
H^{-1}(u,v)=(u-v^2,v).
$$
The coordinate projection
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1:\RR^2\to\RR
$$
is open.
Hence $q_1$ is open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\RR^2/{\sim}
\cong
\RR.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\pi:\RR^2\to\RR^2/{\sim}
$$
be the quotient map.
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $q_1$ is constant exactly on the equivalence classes, so it
factors uniquely through a bijection
$$
\bar q_1:
\RR^2/{\sim}
\to
\RR
$$
with
$$
q_1=\bar q_1\circ\pi.
$$

Because $q_1$ is a quotient map by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the induced bijection
$\bar q_1$ is a homeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For part (2), define
$$
q_2:\RR^2\to[0,\infty),
\qquad
q_2(x,y)=x^2+y^2.
$$
Again, its fibers are exactly the equivalence classes.


::: pf-proof

The stated relation is
$$
(x_0,y_0)\sim(x_1,y_1)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x_0^2+y_0^2
=
x_1^2+y_1^2,
$$
which is exactly equality of $q_2$-values.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The map $q_2$ is a continuous open surjection onto
$$
[0,\infty)
$$
with its usual subspace topology.


::: pf-proof

Continuity is clear, and
$$
q_2(\sqrt t,0)=t
$$
for
$$
t\ge0,
$$
so it is surjective.

Let
$$
U\subseteq\RR^2
$$
be open and let
$$
r\in q_2(U).
$$
Choose
$$
p=(x,y)\in U
$$
with
$$
x^2+y^2=r.
$$

If
$$
r>0,
$$
move $p$ slightly in the radial direction.
Because $U$ is open, all sufficiently small radial perturbations remain in
$U$, and their squared radii fill an open interval around $r$.

If
$$
r=0,
$$
then
$$
p=(0,0).
$$
Some Euclidean ball
$$
B(0,\varepsilon)
$$
lies in $U$, and
$$
q_2(B(0,\varepsilon))
\supseteq
[0,\varepsilon^2),
$$
which is open in the subspace
$$
[0,\infty).
$$

Thus every point of $q_2(U)$ is interior relative to $[0,\infty)$, so
$q_2$ is open.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\RR^2/{\sim}
\cong
[0,\infty).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

As in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the map $q_2$ factors through a bijection from the quotient
onto $[0,\infty)$.
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} says $q_2$ is a quotient map, so the induced bijection is a
homeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For part (3), define
$$
q_3:[-1,1]\to[0,1],
\qquad
q_3(x)=|x|.
$$
Its fibers are exactly the classes of the relation generated by
$$
-p\sim p.
$$


::: pf-proof

The smallest equivalence relation containing
$$
-p\sim p
$$
identifies exactly
$$
p
\quad\text{with}\quad
-p.
$$
Thus
$$
x\sim y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|x|=|y|.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
[-1,1]/{\sim}
\cong
[0,1].
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The continuous map $q_3$ is surjective and constant exactly on the
equivalence classes.
Hence it induces a continuous bijection
$$
\bar q_3:
[-1,1]/{\sim}
\to
[0,1].
$$

The quotient
$$
[-1,1]/{\sim}
$$
is compact because it is the continuous image of the compact interval
under the quotient map.
The target $[0,1]$ is Hausdorff.
A continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a
homeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For part (4), define
$$
q_4:\RR\to S^1,
\qquad
q_4(x)=e^{2\pi i x}.
$$
Its fibers are exactly the equivalence classes.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
e^{2\pi i x}
=
e^{2\pi i y}
$$
if and only if
$$
x-y\in\ZZ.
$$
Thus
$$
q_4(x)=q_4(y)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\sim y.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The map $q_4$ is an open quotient map.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
U\subseteq\RR
$$
be open.
For each
$$
x\in U,
$$
choose an interval
$$
(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)
\subseteq U
$$
with
$$
\varepsilon<\frac12.
$$
On such an interval, the exponential map
$$
t\mapsto e^{2\pi i t}
$$
is a homeomorphism onto an open arc of $S^1$.
Therefore $q_4(U)$ is a union of open arcs and is open.

Since $q_4$ is also surjective, it is a quotient map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Consequently
$$
\boxed{
\RR/{\ZZ}
\cong
S^1.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, $q_4$ factors through a bijection
$$
\bar q_4:
\RR/{\sim}
\to
S^1.
$$
Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} says $q_4$ is quotient, so $\bar q_4$ is a homeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The four quotient identifications are therefore
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
1&\RR\\
2&[0,\infty)\\
3&[0,1]\\
4&S^1
\end{array}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Collect steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} gives all four requested quotient spaces, with the preceding
steps proving the homeomorphisms.
