::: {.problem}
Let
\[
\mathbb{Q} = K_0 \subset K_1 \subset K_2 \subset \cdots \subset K_n,
\]
be a sequence of field extensions such that $K_{i+1}/K_i$ is Galois of order $3$ for all $0 \leq i < n$.
Show that $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ is not contained in $K_n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
\[
\alpha:=\sqrt[3]{2}.
\]
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\[
\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subseteq K_n.
\]

<1>1. There is a least $m\ge1$ such that $\alpha\in K_m$, and then
\[
K_m=K_{m-1}(\alpha).
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\alpha\notin\mathbb Q=K_0$ but $\alpha\in K_n$, choose the least $m\ge1$ such that
\[
\alpha\in K_m.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha\notin K_{m-1}.
\]
We have an intermediate tower
\[
K_{m-1}\subsetneq K_{m-1}(\alpha)\subseteq K_m.
\]
Since
\[
[K_m:K_{m-1}]=3
\]
is prime, there is no proper intermediate degree.
Therefore
\[
K_{m-1}(\alpha)=K_m.
\]
:::

<1>2. The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K_{m-1}$ is $x^3-2$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
[K_{m-1}(\alpha):K_{m-1}]=3.
\]
Hence the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K_{m-1}$ has degree $3$.
It divides
\[
x^3-2,
\]
which also has degree $3$ and has $\alpha$ as a root.
Thus the minimal polynomial is exactly
\[
x^3-2.
\]
:::

<1>3. The field $K_m$ contains a primitive cube root of unity $\zeta_3$.
::: {.proof}
By hypothesis,
\[
K_m/K_{m-1}
\]
is Galois.
By <1>1 and <1>2 it is generated by a root $\alpha$ of the separable polynomial
\[
x^3-2.
\]
Since a Galois extension is normal, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ splits over $K_m$.
Its roots are
\[
\alpha,
\qquad
\zeta_3\alpha,
\qquad
\zeta_3^2\alpha,
\]
where $\zeta_3$ is a primitive cube root of unity.
Thus
\[
\zeta_3\alpha\in K_m.
\]
Since $\alpha\neq0$ and $\alpha\in K_m$,
\[
\zeta_3=\frac{\zeta_3\alpha}{\alpha}\in K_m.
\]
:::

<1>4. This contradicts the degree of $K_m/\mathbb Q$.
::: {.proof}
By the tower law,
\[
[K_m:\mathbb Q]
=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}[K_{i+1}:K_i]
=3^m.
\]
On the other hand, <1>3 gives
\[
\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\subseteq K_m.
\]
Since
\[
[\mathbb Q(\zeta_3):\mathbb Q]=2,
\]
the tower law would force $2$ to divide $3^m$, which is impossible.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2})\not\subseteq K_n.
\]
::: {.proof}
The assumption of containment led to the contradiction in <1>4.
:::
