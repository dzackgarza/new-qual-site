::: problem
There are five nonisomorphic groups of order 8. For each of those groups $G$, find the smallest positive integer n such that there is an injective homomorphism $\varphi: G\rightarrow S_n$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. For the cyclic group $C_8$,
\[
\mu(C_8)=8.
\]
::: {.proof}
An $8$-cycle gives an embedding
\[
C_8\hookrightarrow S_8,
\]
so $\mu(C_8)\le8$.

Conversely, if $C_8$ embeds in $S_n$, then $S_n$ contains an element of order $8$. The order of a permutation is the least common multiple of its cycle lengths. For this least common multiple to be divisible by $8$, at least one cycle length must be divisible by $8$. Hence $n\ge8$.
:::

<1>2. For $C_4\times C_2$,
\[
\mu(C_4\times C_2)=6.
\]
::: {.proof}
The commuting permutations
\[
a=(1\,2\,3\,4),
\qquad
b=(5\,6)
\]
generate a subgroup of $S_6$ isomorphic to $C_4\times C_2$, so the minimal degree is at most $6$.

Suppose instead that $C_4\times C_2$ embedded in $S_n$ for some $n\le5$. Its element of order $4$ would map to a permutation of order $4$. Such a permutation on at most five points must contain a $4$-cycle and therefore is a $4$-cycle together with at most one fixed point. The centralizer of a $4$-cycle in $S_4$ or $S_5$ is precisely the cyclic group generated by that $4$-cycle, of order $4$. Since the image of the abelian group $C_4\times C_2$ must lie in the centralizer of its order-$4$ element, its image could have order at most $4$, contradicting injectivity. Thus $n\ge6$.
:::

<1>3. For the elementary abelian group $E=C_2^3$,
\[
\mu(E)=6.
\]
::: {.proof}
The three disjoint transpositions
\[
(1\,2),\qquad(3\,4),\qquad(5\,6)
\]
generate a subgroup of $S_6$ isomorphic to $C_2^3$, so $\mu(E)\le6$.

Now suppose $E$ acts faithfully on a set $X$ with $|X|\le5$. Since $E$ is a $2$-group, every orbit has size $1$, $2$, or $4$. Because $E$ is abelian, for a transitive orbit $E/H$ the kernel of the action on that orbit is exactly $H$.

If there is an orbit of size $4$, its stabilizer has order $2$. Since at most one point remains, every other orbit is fixed, whose action kernel is all of $E$. Hence the kernel of the full action contains that order-$2$ stabilizer and is nontrivial.

If there is no orbit of size $4$, then there are at most two orbits of size $2$. Their stabilizers $H_1,H_2$ each have order $4$. Their intersection satisfies
\[
|H_1\cap H_2|
=\frac{|H_1||H_2|}{|H_1H_2|}
\ge \frac{4\cdot4}{8}=2.
\]
Fixed points do not shrink the kernel. Thus again the full action has nontrivial kernel. Therefore no faithful action has degree at most $5$, so $\mu(E)=6$.
:::

<1>4. For the dihedral group $D_8$ of order $8$,
\[
\mu(D_8)=4.
\]
::: {.proof}
The symmetry action of a square on its four vertices gives a faithful embedding
\[
D_8\hookrightarrow S_4.
\]
Thus $\mu(D_8)\le4$. An embedding into $S_n$ with $n\le3$ is impossible because an injective homomorphism would identify $D_8$ with a subgroup of $S_n$, forcing
\[
8=|D_8|\mid |S_n|\le6,
\]
which is impossible. Hence $\mu(D_8)=4$.
:::

<1>5. For the quaternion group $Q_8$,
\[
\mu(Q_8)=8.
\]
::: {.proof}
The regular action gives a faithful embedding
\[
Q_8\hookrightarrow S_8,
\]
so $\mu(Q_8)\le8$.

Suppose $Q_8$ acted faithfully on a set of cardinality less than $8$. Decompose the action into transitive orbits. Each orbit has the form $Q_8/H$. Since its size is less than $8$, the stabilizer $H$ is nontrivial. Every subgroup of $Q_8$ is normal, so the kernel of the action on $Q_8/H$ is exactly $H$. Moreover, every nontrivial subgroup of $Q_8$ contains the central element $-1$: the unique subgroup of order $2$ is $\{1,-1\}$, and every subgroup of order $4$ also contains $-1$.

Therefore $-1$ lies in the kernel of the action on every orbit, hence in the kernel of the whole action. This contradicts faithfulness. Thus every faithful permutation action of $Q_8$ has degree at least $8$, and so $\mu(Q_8)=8$.
:::

<1>6. In summary,
\[
\boxed{
\mu(C_8)=8,\quad
\mu(C_4\times C_2)=6,\quad
\mu(C_2^3)=6,\quad
\mu(D_8)=4,\quad
\mu(Q_8)=8.
}
\]
