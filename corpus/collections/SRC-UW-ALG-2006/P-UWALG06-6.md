::: {.problem}
Let $R=\ZZ[x]$, the polynomial ring in a variable $x$ with coefficients in the integers.

(a) Let $M$ be a maximal ideal of $R$.
Show that $R/M$ is a finite field.

(b) Suppose that $k$ is any finite field.
Prove that there exists at least one and no more than a finite number of maximal ideals $M$ such that $R/M\simeq k$.

(c) Prove that no maximal ideal of $R$ is principal, but that all nonmaximal prime ideals of $R$ are principal.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $R=\ZZ[x]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Part (a): if $M$ is maximal then $R/M$ is a finite field.

::: pf-proof

$R/M$ is a field, and it is a finitely generated $\ZZ$-algebra (generated by the image of $x$). A field that is a finitely generated $\ZZ$-algebra has a characteristic, which is $0$ or a prime.

If the characteristic were $0$, then $\QQ\subseteq R/M$ and $R/M$ is a finitely generated $\QQ$-algebra that is a field, hence a finite extension of $\QQ$ by Zariski's lemma; then $R/M=\ZZ[\alpha]\subseteq\mathcal O_F[1/D]$ for the image $\alpha$ of $x$ and a suitable $D$, while $1/p\in R/M$ for a prime $p\nmid D$ — a contradiction, exactly as in the argument recorded on `P-AKYLF`.

So the characteristic is a prime $p$, so $\FF_p\subseteq R/M$ and $R/M$ is a finitely generated $\FF_p$-algebra that is a field; by Zariski's lemma it is a finite extension of $\FF_p$, hence finite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Part (b): for a finite field $k$ of characteristic $p$ with $\abs k=q$, the maximal ideals with $R/M\cong k$ are in bijection with the elements $\alpha\in k$ that generate $k$ over $\FF_p$; there are at least one and at most $q$ of them.

::: pf-proof

A maximal ideal $M$ with $R/M\cong k$ is exactly the kernel of a surjective ring homomorphism $\varphi:\ZZ[x]\to k$. Such a $\varphi$ is determined by $\alpha=\varphi(x)\in k$ together with the map $\ZZ\to k$, which is surjective onto the prime field only if the characteristic of the composite is $p$; and $\varphi$ is surjective if and only if $\FF_p(\alpha)=k$.

*Existence.* $\ZZ\to\FF_p\subseteq k$ and $k=\FF_p(\alpha)$ for the generator $\alpha$ of the cyclic group $k^\times$ (which exists because $k^\times$ is cyclic and finite); the resulting $\varphi$ is onto with kernel a maximal ideal $M$ and $R/M\cong k$.

*Finiteness.* Any such $\varphi$ is determined by $\alpha\in k$, and $k$ is a finite set, so there are at most $\abs k=q$ of them; distinct $\alpha$ give distinct $\varphi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Part (c): no maximal ideal of $R$ is principal.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $(f)=M$ is maximal. Then $R/(f)$ is a field, hence finite by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, so it has a prime characteristic $p$ and the composite $\ZZ\to R/(f)$ has kernel $(p)$. Hence $(p)\subseteq(f)$, that is, $p\in(f)$, so $p=fg$ for some $g$. If $\deg f\ge1$ then $fg=p\ne0$ forces $\deg g<0$, impossible; thus $f$ is a constant, $f=\pm p$ (or $\pm1$, in which case $(f)=R$ is not maximal).

But $(p)$ is not maximal: $R/(p)=\FF_p[x]\cong\FF_p[\bar x]$ is a polynomial ring in one variable over a field, hence an integral domain that is not a field. This contradicts the minimality of the maximal ideal $(p)\subseteq(f)$ when $f=\pm p$; and $f$ cannot be a nonconstant. So no maximal ideal is principal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Part (c), second half: every nonmaximal prime ideal of $R$ is principal.

::: pf-proof

Let $P$ be a prime ideal of $R$ that is not maximal. Write $P_0=P\cap\ZZ$, a prime ideal of $\ZZ$.

*Case $P_0=(0)$.* Then $P\neq0$ (the zero ideal is not maximal but is principal, generated by $0$, which handles that case separately), and $P$ has height $1$: $R$ is a UFD and $\dim R=2$. In a UFD every height-one prime ideal is principal — generated by an irreducible element — and here that generator is a primitive polynomial $f$ (primitive because $P\cap\ZZ=0$). So $P=(f)$.

*Case $P_0=(p)$ for a prime $p$.* Then $P\supseteq(p)$, and $P/(p)$ is a prime ideal of $R/(p)=\FF_p[x]$. Since $\FF_p[x]$ is a PID, $P/(p)=(\bar g)$ for some $\bar g$, so $P=(p,g)$ where $g$ is a lift of $\bar g$. If $\bar g=0$ then $P=(p)$, which is principal. If $\bar g\ne0$, then $\bar g$ is irreducible or $0$; if $\bar g$ is a nonconstant irreducible of $\FF_p[x]$, then $\FF_p[x]/(\bar g)$ is a finite field, so $R/P\cong\FF_p[x]/(\bar g)$ is a field and $P$ is maximal, contrary to hypothesis. So in the nonmaximal case $\bar g=0$ and $P=(p)$.

In every case a nonmaximal prime ideal is principal: $(0)$, $(p)$, or $(f)$ for a primitive irreducible $f$.

:::
