::: {.problem}
Let $X=S^1\times S^1$, also thought of as the standard quotient of the unit square $[0,1]\times[0,1]$, and let $A=\{(x,x):x\in S^1\}$ be the diagonal of $X$.
Show that $A$ is a retract of $X$, but not a deformation retract of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
i:A\hookrightarrow X
\]
denote the inclusion.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map
\[
r:X=S^1\times S^1\longrightarrow A,
\qquad
r(z,w)=(z,z),
\]
is continuous.

::: pf-proof

Let
\[
\pi_1:S^1\times S^1\longrightarrow S^1,
\qquad
\pi_1(z,w)=z
\]
be the first-coordinate projection, and let
\[
d:S^1\longrightarrow A,
\qquad
d(z)=(z,z).
\]
The projection $\pi_1$ is continuous by the product topology, and $d$ is continuous because its two coordinate maps are both the identity on $S^1$.
Since
\[
r=d\circ\pi_1,
\]
the map $r$ is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The map $r$ is a retraction of $X$ onto $A$.

::: pf-proof

For every point $(z,z)\in A$,
\[
r(z,z)=(z,z).
\]
Thus
\[
r|_A=\operatorname{id}_A,
\]
which is exactly the defining property of a retraction.

Fix the basepoint
\[
a_0=(1,1)\in A.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

There are natural identifications
\[
\pi_1(A,a_0)\cong\mathbb Z
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\pi_1(X,a_0)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]

::: pf-proof

The diagonal map
\[
d:S^1\longrightarrow A,
\qquad
z\longmapsto(z,z)
\]
is a homeomorphism, with inverse given by either coordinate projection restricted to $A$.
Hence
\[
\pi_1(A,a_0)\cong\pi_1(S^1,1)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

For the torus, the standard product theorem for fundamental groups gives
\[
\pi_1(S^1\times S^1,(1,1))
\cong
\pi_1(S^1,1)\times\pi_1(S^1,1)
\cong
\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Under the identifications in step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the homomorphism induced by the inclusion is
\[
i_*:\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
n\longmapsto(n,n).
\]

::: pf-proof

A positive generator of $\pi_1(A,a_0)$ is represented by the diagonal loop
\[
\delta(t)=\bigl(e^{2\pi it},e^{2\pi it}\bigr).
\]
Viewed as a loop in $X=S^1\times S^1$, both coordinate projections of $\delta$ wind once positively around $S^1$.
Therefore its class corresponds to
\[
(1,1)\in\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Since $i_*$ is a homomorphism, it sends $n$ to $(n,n)$ for every $n\in\mathbb Z$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The homomorphism $i_*$ is not surjective.

::: pf-proof

Its image is the diagonal subgroup
\[
\{(n,n):n\in\mathbb Z\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^2.
\]
For example,
\[
(1,0)
\]
does not lie in this subgroup.
Thus $i_*$ is not surjective and hence not an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

If $A$ were a deformation retract of $X$, then $i_*:\pi_1(A,a_0)\to\pi_1(X,a_0)$ would be an isomorphism.

::: pf-proof

A deformation retraction supplies a retraction
\[
r:X\to A
\]
with
\[
r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_A
\]
and a homotopy from
\[
i\circ r
\]
to $\operatorname{id}_X$ that fixes $A$ pointwise.
In particular the homotopy fixes the basepoint $a_0$, so the induced maps on fundamental groups satisfy
\[
r_*\circ i_*=\operatorname{id}_{\pi_1(A,a_0)}
\]
and
\[
i_*\circ r_*=\operatorname{id}_{\pi_1(X,a_0)}.
\]
Hence $i_*$ and $r_*$ would be inverse isomorphisms.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $A$ is not a deformation retract of $X$.

::: pf-proof

If it were, step [](#s6){.pf-ref} would make $i_*$ an isomorphism, contradicting step [](#s5){.pf-ref}. Together with step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, this proves that $A$ is a retract but not a deformation retract of the torus.

:::
