::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a connected space and $A,B\subseteq X$ be closed subsets of $X$ with $X=A\cup B$ and $A\cap B$ a connected subset of $X$.
Show that both $A$ and $B$ are connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $A$ is disconnected.
Then there are nonempty disjoint subsets $U,V\subseteq A$ such that
\[
A=U\cup V
\]
and $U,V$ are both open and closed in the subspace $A$.
::: {.proof}
By definition, disconnectedness means that $A$ admits a separation into two disjoint nonempty relatively open subsets $U,V$ whose union is $A$.
Because
\[
U=A\setminus V,
\qquad
V=A\setminus U,
\]
each is also closed in $A$.
:::

<1>2. The subsets $U$ and $V$ are closed in $X$.
::: {.proof}
Since $U$ is closed in the subspace $A$, there is a closed set $F\subseteq X$ such that
\[
U=A\cap F.
\]
The set $A$ is closed in $X$ by hypothesis, so $U$ is an intersection of two closed subsets of $X$ and is therefore closed in $X$.
The same argument applies to $V$.
:::

<1>3. The connected subset $A\cap B$ is contained entirely in $U$ or entirely in $V$.
::: {.proof}
Inside the subspace $A\cap B$ we have the disjoint union
\[
A\cap B
=
\bigl((A\cap B)\cap U\bigr)
\cup
\bigl((A\cap B)\cap V\bigr).
\]
Because $U$ and $V$ are open in $A$, the two displayed pieces are open in the subspace $A\cap B$.
If both were nonempty, they would form a separation of $A\cap B$, contradicting its connectedness.
Thus one is empty, so $A\cap B$ lies in the other member of the separation.
This also covers the case $A\cap B=\varnothing$, since then it is contained in both.
:::

<1>4. After interchanging $U$ and $V$ if necessary, we may assume
\[
A\cap B\subseteq U,
\]
and then
\[
V\cap B=\varnothing.
\]
::: {.proof}
The first assertion follows from <1>3 and the symmetry of the names $U,V$.
For the second, any point of $V\cap B$ would lie in
\[
A\cap B
\]
because $V\subseteq A$, hence would lie in $U$ by the assumed inclusion.
This contradicts $U\cap V=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>5. We have a disjoint decomposition
\[
X=V\cup(U\cup B)
\]
into two nonempty closed subsets of $X$.
::: {.proof}
Using $X=A\cup B$ and $A=U\cup V$,
\[
X=U\cup V\cup B=V\cup(U\cup B).
\]
The union is disjoint because $V\cap U=\varnothing$ by <1>1 and $V\cap B=\varnothing$ by <1>4.

Both sets are nonempty since $U$ and $V$ are nonempty.
The set $V$ is closed in $X$ by <1>2, and $U\cup B$ is closed because $U$ is closed by <1>2 and $B$ is closed by hypothesis.
:::

<1>6. The decomposition in <1>5 contradicts connectedness of $X$; therefore $A$ is connected.
::: {.proof}
If a space is the disjoint union of two nonempty closed subsets, then each is also open because it is the complement of the other.
Thus the two sets in <1>5 would form a separation of $X$.
This contradicts the hypothesis that $X$ is connected.
Hence the assumption in <1>1 was false, and $A$ is connected.
:::

<1>7. The subset $B$ is connected as well.
::: {.proof}
The hypotheses are symmetric in $A$ and $B$: both are closed, their union is $X$, and their intersection is connected.
Applying the argument of <1>1--<1>6 with $A$ and $B$ interchanged proves that $B$ is connected.
:::
