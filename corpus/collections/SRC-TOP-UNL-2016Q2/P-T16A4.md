::: {.problem}
Suppose that the space $X$ has the fixed point property (that is, for any continuous function $f:X\to X$ there is a point $p\in X$ with $f(p)=p$). Suppose also that $A\subseteq X$ is a subspace admitting a retraction $r:X\to A$.
Show that $A$ also has the fixed point property.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Setup maps and construction:
<2>1. Let $g: A \to A$ be an arbitrary continuous function on $A$.
Proof: setup.
<2>2. Let $i: A \hookrightarrow X$ denote the canonical inclusion map, which is continuous.
Proof: subspace topology definition.
<2>3. By definition of a retraction, $r: X \to A$ is continuous and satisfies $r(a) = a$ for all $a \in A$ (so $r \circ i = \operatorname{id}_A$).
Proof: definition of retract.
<2>4. Define the map $F: X \to X$ as the composition:
\[
F = i \circ g \circ r: X \xrightarrow{r} A \xrightarrow{g} A \xrightarrow{i} X.
\]
Proof: composition of maps.
<2>5. Since $r, g,$ and $i$ are continuous, $F: X \to X$ is continuous.
Proof: composition of continuous functions is continuous.

<1>2. Existence and properties of the fixed point:
<2>1. Since $X$ has the fixed point property and $F: X \to X$ is continuous, there exists a point $p \in X$ such that $F(p) = p$.
Proof: hypothesis that $X$ has the fixed point property.
<2>2. Since the codomain of $i$ restricted to $A$ is $A$, we have $p = F(p) = i(g(r(p))) \in A$.
Proof: image of $i$ is $A$.
<2>3. Since $p \in A$, the retraction property implies $r(p) = p$.
Proof: $r|_A = \operatorname{id}_A$.
<2>4. Substituting $r(p) = p$ into the fixed point equation yields:
\[
p = F(p) = i(g(r(p))) = i(g(p)) = g(p).
\]
Proof: <2>2 and <2>3.
<2>5. Thus $p \in A$ satisfies $g(p) = p$.
Proof: <2>4.

<1>3. Conclusion:
Every continuous map $g: A \to A$ has a fixed point in $A$, so $A$ has the fixed point property. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.
:::
