::: {.problem}
Suppose that the space $X$ has the fixed point property (that is, for any continuous function $f:X\to X$ there is a point $p\in X$ with $f(p)=p$). Suppose also that $A\subseteq X$ is a subspace admitting a retraction $r:X\to A$.
Show that $A$ also has the fixed point property.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
g:A\longrightarrow A
\]
be an arbitrary continuous map, and let
\[
i:A\hookrightarrow X
\]
be the inclusion.

<1>1. The composite
\[
F=i\circ g\circ r:X\longrightarrow X
\]
is continuous.
::: {.proof}
The retraction $r:X\to A$ is continuous by hypothesis, $g$ is continuous by choice, and the inclusion of a subspace is continuous.
Therefore their composite is continuous.
:::

<1>2. There exists $p\in X$ such that
\[
F(p)=p.
\]
::: {.proof}
The space $X$ has the fixed point property and $F:X\to X$ is continuous by <1>1.
Apply the defining fixed point property to $F$.
:::

<1>3. The point $p$ from <1>2 lies in $A$.
::: {.proof}
The image of $F=i\circ g\circ r$ is contained in the image $i(A)$, which is the subspace $A\subseteq X$.
Since
\[
p=F(p),
\]
the point $p$ belongs to $A$.
:::

<1>4. The point $p$ is a fixed point of $g$.
::: {.proof}
Because $r$ is a retraction,
\[
r|_A=\operatorname{id}_A.
\]
By <1>3, $p\in A$, so
\[
r(p)=p.
\]
Using <1>2,
\[
p
=F(p)
=i(g(r(p)))
=i(g(p)).
\]
Both $p$ and $g(p)$ are points of the subspace $A$, and the inclusion $i$ is injective.
Thus
\[
g(p)=p.
\]
:::

<1>5. Therefore $A$ has the fixed point property.
::: {.proof}
The continuous map $g:A\to A$ was arbitrary.
By <1>4 it has a fixed point in $A$.
Hence every continuous self-map of $A$ has a fixed point, which is exactly the fixed point property.
:::
:::
