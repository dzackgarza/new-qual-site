::: {.problem}
Construct a $\Delta$-complex structure and use it to compute the simplicial homology groups for the connected sum of two projective planes.
:::

::: {.solution}
Represent
\[
\mathbb{RP}^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2
\]
by the standard four-sided polygon with boundary word
\[
aabb.
\]
Concretely, let the vertices of a square be $A,B,C,D$ in counterclockwise order.
Identify the oriented edges
\[
A\to B
\quad\text{and}\quad
B\to C
\]
with the same oriented edge $a$, and identify
\[
C\to D
\quad\text{and}\quad
D\to A
\]
with the same oriented edge $b$.
Triangulate the square by the diagonal $AC$, oriented from $A$ to $C$ and denoted by $c$.

<1>1. The polygon quotient with boundary word $aabb$ is a model for
\[
\mathbb{RP}^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
A projective plane has the standard polygon model with boundary word
\[
aa:
\]
it is a disk whose boundary is split into two arcs that are identified in the same boundary direction.

For connected sums of polygonal surface models, delete a small open disk from the interior of each polygon and identify the two new boundary circles by an orientation-reversing homeomorphism.
Cutting the resulting annular joining region along an arc from the joined circle to the original polygon boundaries turns the connected sum back into a single polygon, and its boundary word is the concatenation of the two original words.
Thus the connected sum of two copies with words $aa$ and $bb$ has polygon word
\[
aabb.
\]
The square above realizes exactly this word.
:::

<1>2. All four vertices $A,B,C,D$ have one common image $v$ in the quotient.
::: {.proof}
The two $a$-edges are identified in their boundary directions, so their initial and terminal vertices satisfy
\[
A\sim B,
\qquad
B\sim C.
\]
The two $b$-edges similarly give
\[
C\sim D,
\qquad
D\sim A.
\]
Hence
\[
A\sim B\sim C\sim D.
\]
:::

<1>3. The quotient triangulation is a $\Delta$-complex with one $0$-simplex $v$, three $1$-simplices $a,b,c$, and two $2$-simplices
\[
\sigma=[A,B,C],
\qquad
\tau=[A,C,D].
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 all vertices descend to the single vertex $v$.
The four boundary edges descend to the two edge classes $a,b$, while the diagonal gives the third edge $c$.
The interiors of the two triangles are disjoint and no boundary identification identifies interior points of either triangle.
Their characteristic maps therefore descend to two open $2$-simplices whose faces are the listed $1$-simplices.
This is precisely a $\Delta$-complex structure on the polygon quotient from <1>1.
:::

<1>4. The simplicial chain groups are
\[
C_2\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
C_1\cong\mathbb Z^3,
\qquad
C_0\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
and
\[
\partial_1=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The ranks follow directly from the numbers of simplices in <1>3. Every $1$-simplex begins and ends at the unique vertex $v$, so for each oriented edge $e$,
\[
\partial_1(e)=v-v=0.
\]
Hence $\partial_1$ is the zero map.
:::

<1>5. With the chosen orientations,
\[
\partial_2(\sigma)=2a-c
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\partial_2(\tau)=2b+c.
\]
::: {.proof}
For
\[
\sigma=[A,B,C],
\]
the simplicial boundary formula gives
\[
\partial_2\sigma
=[B,C]-[A,C]+[A,B].
\]
Both $[A,B]$ and $[B,C]$ represent the oriented edge $a$, while $[A,C]=c$.
Thus
\[
\partial_2\sigma=a-c+a=2a-c.
\]

For
\[
\tau=[A,C,D],
\]
we have
\[
\partial_2\tau
=[C,D]-[A,D]+[A,C].
\]
The edge $[C,D]$ is $b$.
The boundary edge identified with it is oriented $D\to A$, so the edge $A\to D$ represents $-b$.
Hence
\[
\partial_2\tau
=b-(-b)+c
=2b+c.
\]
:::

<1>6. The boundary map
\[
\partial_2:\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^3
\]
is injective.
::: {.proof}
In the ordered basis $(a,b,c)$ of $C_1$, its two columns from <1>5 are
\[
(2,0,-1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(0,2,1).
\]
Suppose
\[
m(2,0,-1)+n(0,2,1)=0.
\]
The $a$-coordinate gives
\[
2m=0,
\]
so $m=0$.
The $b$-coordinate gives
\[
2n=0,
\]
so $n=0$.
Thus the kernel is trivial.
:::

<1>7. We have
\[
H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
There are no $3$-simplices, so
\[
H_2=\ker\partial_2.
\]
This kernel is zero by <1>6.
:::

<1>8. We have
\[
H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\partial_1=0$, <1>5 gives
\[
H_1
\cong
\mathbb Z\langle a,b,c\rangle
\big/
\langle 2a-c,\ 2b+c\rangle.
\]
The first relation eliminates $c$:
\[
c=2a.
\]
Substituting into the second relation gives
\[
2b+2a=2(a+b)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
H_1
\cong
\mathbb Z\langle a,b\rangle/\langle2(a+b)\rangle.
\]
Make the unimodular change of basis
\[
u=a,
\qquad
v=a+b.
\]
Its inverse is
\[
a=u,
\qquad
b=v-u,
\]
so $(u,v)$ is again a basis of the free abelian group $\mathbb Z^2$.
In this basis the sole relation is
\[
2v=0.
\]
Hence
\[
H_1\cong\mathbb Z\langle u\rangle\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)\langle v\rangle.
\]
:::

<1>9. We have
\[
H_0(\mathbb{RP}^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
and
\[
H_i(\mathbb{RP}^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2)=0
\qquad(i\ge3).
\]
::: {.proof}
The $\Delta$-complex has one vertex and is connected, and <1>4 gives
\[
H_0=C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1
=
\mathbb Z/0
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
There are no simplices in dimensions at least $3$, so all higher chain groups and homology groups vanish.
:::

<1>10. Thus the simplicial homology groups are
\[
\boxed{
H_i(\mathbb{RP}^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & i=0,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z, & i=1,\\
0, & i\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>7--<1>9.
:::
