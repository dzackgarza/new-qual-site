::: {.problem}
A group $G$ is called *residually finite* if for every $g\in G$ with $g\ne 1$, there is a finite group $H$ and a (surjective) homomorphism $\varphi:G\to H$ with $\varphi(g)\ne 1$.
Let $G$ be a residually finite group and let $X$ be the presentation complex for a presentation of $G$, with vertex $x_0$.
Show that for any loop $\gamma:I\to X$ at $x_0$ with $1\ne[\gamma]\in\pi_1(X,x_0)$, there is a finite-sheeted covering space $p:\widetilde X\to X$ and basepoint $\widetilde x_0\in p^{-1}(\{x_0\})$ such that $\gamma$ does not lift to a loop at $\widetilde x_0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the presentation-complex identification
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong G,
\]
and put
\[
g=[\gamma]\in G.
\]
By hypothesis, $g\ne1$.

<1>1. There is a finite group $H$ and a surjective homomorphism
\[
\varphi:G\longrightarrow H
\]
such that
\[
\varphi(g)\ne1.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the residual-finiteness hypothesis applied to the nonidentity element $g\in G$.
:::

<1>2. If
\[
K=\ker\varphi,
\]
then $K$ has finite index in $G$ and
\[
g\notin K.
\]
::: {.proof}
By the first isomorphism theorem and surjectivity of $\varphi$,
\[
G/K\cong H.
\]
Since $H$ is finite,
\[
[G:K]=|H|<\infty.
\]
Moreover
\[
g\in K
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\varphi(g)=1,
\]
which is false by <1>1.
:::

<1>3. There is a connected based covering
\[
p:(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\longrightarrow(X,x_0)
\]
whose associated subgroup is $K$:
\[
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)=K.
\]
::: {.proof}
A presentation complex is a connected CW complex.
In particular it is locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected, so the classification theorem for connected based covering spaces applies.
Under that theorem, the subgroup
\[
K\le\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong G
\]
determines a connected based covering with the displayed associated subgroup.
:::

<1>4. The covering in <1>3 is finite-sheeted.
::: {.proof}
For a connected based covering, the cardinality of the fiber over $x_0$ equals the index of the associated subgroup:
\[
|p^{-1}(x_0)|
=
[\pi_1(X,x_0):p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)].
\]
Using <1>2--<1>3,
\[
|p^{-1}(x_0)|=[G:K]=|H|<\infty.
\]
Hence $p$ has finitely many sheets.
:::

<1>5. A loop $\alpha$ in $X$ based at $x_0$ lifts, starting at $\widetilde x_0$, to a loop at $\widetilde x_0$ if and only if
\[
[\alpha]\in p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0).
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $\widetilde\alpha$ be the unique lift of $\alpha$ beginning at $\widetilde x_0$.

If $\widetilde\alpha$ is a loop, then
\[
[\alpha]
=
p_*[\widetilde\alpha]
\in
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0).
\]

Conversely, suppose
\[
[\alpha]=p_*[\widetilde\beta]
\]
for some loop $\widetilde\beta$ at $\widetilde x_0$.
Then the based loops $\alpha$ and $p\circ\widetilde\beta$ represent the same element of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$, so they are homotopic relative to the basepoint.
Lift such a based homotopy with initial lift $\widetilde\beta$.
The endpoint track of the lifted homotopy lies in the discrete fiber $p^{-1}(x_0)$ and is therefore constant.
Consequently the lift of $\alpha$ beginning at $\widetilde x_0$ has the same terminal point as $\widetilde\beta$, namely $\widetilde x_0$.
Thus it is a loop.
:::

<1>6. The loop $\gamma$ does not lift to a loop at $\widetilde x_0$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2--<1>3,
\[
[\gamma]=g\notin K
=
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0).
\]
Apply the criterion in <1>5. The unique lift of $\gamma$ beginning at $\widetilde x_0$ therefore ends at a different point of the fiber and is not a loop.
Together with <1>4, this is the required finite-sheeted covering.
:::
:::
