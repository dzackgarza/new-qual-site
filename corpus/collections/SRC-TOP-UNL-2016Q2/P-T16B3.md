::: {.problem}
Let $p:\widetilde X\to X$ and $q:\widetilde Y\to Y$ be covering spaces of path-connected, locally path-connected spaces $X$ and $Y$ with $\widetilde X$ and $\widetilde Y$ locally path-connected and simply-connected.
Show that if $X$ and $Y$ are homeomorphic, then $\widetilde X$ and $\widetilde Y$ are homeomorphic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
h:X\longrightarrow Y
\]
be a homeomorphism.
Choose
\[
\widetilde x_0\in\widetilde X,
\qquad
x_0=p(\widetilde x_0),
\qquad
y_0=h(x_0),
\]
and choose
\[
\widetilde y_0\in q^{-1}(y_0).
\]

<1>1. There is a continuous lift
\[
F:\widetilde X\longrightarrow\widetilde Y
\]
of $h\circ p$ such that
\[
F(\widetilde x_0)=\widetilde y_0;
\]
that is,
\[
q\circ F=h\circ p.
\]
::: {.proof}
The space $\widetilde X$ is simply connected, so
\[
\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)=1.
\]
Hence
\[
(h\circ p)_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)=1
\subseteq
q_*\pi_1(\widetilde Y,\widetilde y_0).
\]
The domain $\widetilde X$ is path-connected and locally path-connected by the hypotheses on the total space and the meaning of simple connectedness.
Therefore the covering-space lifting criterion applies to
\[
h\circ p:\widetilde X\to Y
\]
and the covering $q:\widetilde Y\to Y$, producing a lift $F$ with the prescribed value at $\widetilde x_0$.
:::

<1>2. There is a continuous lift
\[
G:\widetilde Y\longrightarrow\widetilde X
\]
of $h^{-1}\circ q$ such that
\[
G(\widetilde y_0)=\widetilde x_0;
\]
that is,
\[
p\circ G=h^{-1}\circ q.
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply the same lifting criterion with the roles reversed.
Since $\widetilde Y$ is simply connected,
\[
(h^{-1}\circ q)_*\pi_1(\widetilde Y,\widetilde y_0)=1
\subseteq
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0).
\]
The chosen basepoints satisfy
\[
p(\widetilde x_0)=x_0=h^{-1}(y_0)=h^{-1}(q(\widetilde y_0)),
\]
so there is a lift $G$ with $G(\widetilde y_0)=\widetilde x_0$.
:::

<1>3. The maps
\[
G\circ F
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{id}_{\widetilde X}
\]
are two lifts of the same map
\[
p:\widetilde X\longrightarrow X
\]
through the covering $p:\widetilde X\to X$.
::: {.proof}
Using <1>1--<1>2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p\circ G\circ F
&=h^{-1}\circ q\circ F\\
&=h^{-1}\circ h\circ p\\
&=p.
\end{aligned}
\]
Also
\[
p\circ\operatorname{id}_{\widetilde X}=p.
\]
Thus both maps from $\widetilde X$ to $\widetilde X$ lift the same map $p$ through the covering $p$.
:::

<1>4. We have
\[
G\circ F=\operatorname{id}_{\widetilde X}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By construction,
\[
(G\circ F)(\widetilde x_0)
=
G(\widetilde y_0)
=
\widetilde x_0
=
\operatorname{id}_{\widetilde X}(\widetilde x_0).
\]
The domain $\widetilde X$ is connected.
Two lifts through a covering map of the same map from a connected domain that agree at one point agree everywhere.
Apply this uniqueness-of-lifts theorem to the two lifts in <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Similarly,
\[
F\circ G=\operatorname{id}_{\widetilde Y}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using <1>1--<1>2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
q\circ F\circ G
&=h\circ p\circ G\\
&=h\circ h^{-1}\circ q\\
&=q.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $F\circ G$ and $\operatorname{id}_{\widetilde Y}$ are lifts of $q:\widetilde Y\to Y$ through the covering $q$.
They agree at $\widetilde y_0$ because
\[
(F\circ G)(\widetilde y_0)
=
F(\widetilde x_0)
=
\widetilde y_0.
\]
Since $\widetilde Y$ is connected, uniqueness of lifts gives the claimed equality.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $F:\widetilde X\to\widetilde Y$ is a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
The maps $F$ and $G$ are continuous by <1>1--<1>2 and are mutual inverses by <1>4--<1>5. Hence $F$ is a homeomorphism with inverse $G$.
:::
:::
