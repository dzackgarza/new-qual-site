::: {.problem}
Which of the following matrices are similar over $\mathbb R$?
\[
(a)\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
(b)\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&1&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
(c)\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
(d)\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
(e)\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
(f)\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\0&1&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Denote the six matrices by $A_a,A_b,A_c,A_d,A_e,A_f$.

<1>1. The characteristic polynomials are
$$
\chi_{A_a}
=
\chi_{A_c}
=
\chi_{A_d}
=
\chi_{A_e}
=
(t-1)^3
$$
and
$$
\chi_{A_b}
=
\chi_{A_f}
=
(t-1)^2(t+1).
$$

::: {.proof}
The matrices $A_a,A_c,A_d,A_e$ are triangular with all diagonal
entries equal to $1$, giving the first identity.

For $A_b$,
$$
A_b
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}z\\y\\x\end{pmatrix},
$$
so it fixes the two-dimensional subspace $x=z$ and acts by $-1$ on
the line spanned by $(1,0,-1)^T$. Hence its characteristic
polynomial is $(t-1)^2(t+1)$.

Finally,
$$
\det(tI-A_f)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
t&-1&-1\\
0&t-1&0\\
-1&0&t
\end{pmatrix}
=
(t-1)(t^2-1)
=
(t-1)^2(t+1).
$$
:::

<1>2. The minimal polynomials of $A_a,A_c,A_d,A_e$ are respectively
$$
t-1,
\qquad
(t-1)^2,
\qquad
(t-1)^3,
\qquad
(t-1)^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $A_a=I$, the minimal polynomial is $t-1$.

Set $N_c=A_c-I$ and $N_e=A_e-I$. Each is nonzero and has exactly
one nonzero entry, so
$$
N_c^2=0=N_e^2.
$$
Thus both minimal polynomials are $(t-1)^2$.

For
$$
N_d=A_d-I
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
one has
$$
N_d^2
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0
\end{pmatrix}
\neq0,
\qquad
N_d^3=0.
$$
Hence the minimal polynomial of $A_d$ is $(t-1)^3$.
:::

<1>3. Among $A_a,A_c,A_d,A_e$, precisely $A_c$ and $A_e$ are
similar.

::: {.proof}
Similar matrices have the same minimal polynomial, so step <1>2
excludes every pair except $A_c,A_e$.

For both $A_c$ and $A_e$, the nilpotent matrix $A-I$ is nonzero,
has rank $1$, and has square $0$. Therefore each has Jordan form
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus $A_c$ and $A_e$ are similar over $\RR$.
:::

<1>4. The matrix $A_b$ is diagonalizable over $\RR$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 already exhibited a two-dimensional eigenspace for
eigenvalue $1$ and a one-dimensional eigenspace for eigenvalue
$-1$. These eigenspaces have total dimension $3$, so $A_b$ is
diagonalizable.
:::

<1>5. The matrix $A_f$ is not diagonalizable.

::: {.proof}
The equation
$$
(A_f-I)
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{pmatrix}
=
0
$$
is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
-x+y+z\\
0\\
x-z
\end{pmatrix}
=
0.
$$
Thus $z=x$ and $y=0$, so
$$
\ker(A_f-I)
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}
\right\}
$$
has dimension $1$. But step <1>1 shows that the algebraic
multiplicity of eigenvalue $1$ is $2$. Therefore $A_f$ is not
diagonalizable.
:::

<1>6. The matrices $A_b$ and $A_f$ are not similar.

::: {.proof}
Diagonalizability is invariant under similarity. Step <1>4 shows
that $A_b$ is diagonalizable, while step <1>5 shows that $A_f$ is
not.
:::

<1>7. The complete similarity classification is
$$
\boxed{(c)\sim(e),\quad\text{and no other distinct pair is similar}.}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 separates the four matrices
$A_a,A_c,A_d,A_e$ from $A_b,A_f$ by characteristic polynomial.
Step <1>3 classifies the first group, and step <1>6 separates the
two matrices in the second group.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 answers the classification question completely.
:::
