::: {.problem}
Prove that every finite subgroup of the multiplicative group $\mathbb C^\times$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G\leq\CC^\times$ be finite, set $n=\abs{G}$, and write
$$
\mu_n
=
\left\{
z\in\CC^\times:z^n=1
\right\},
\qquad
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #g-subset-mu-n}
$G\subseteq\mu_n$.

::: pf-proof
For every $g\in G$, Lagrange's theorem gives
$$
g^n=1,
$$
because the order of $g$ divides $n=\abs{G}$. Hence every element of
$G$ lies in $\mu_n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #mu-n-is-cyclic}
The group $\mu_n$ has exactly $n$ elements and
$$
\mu_n=\generators{\zeta}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The $n$ numbers
$$
1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}.
$$
all satisfy $z^n=1$. They are distinct: if
$0\leq j<k\leq n-1$ and $\zeta^j=\zeta^k$, then
$e^{2\pi i(k-j)/n}=1$, so $n$ divides $k-j$, which is impossible
because $0<k-j<n$.

Conversely, if $z^n=1$, then $\abs{z}^n=1$, so $\abs{z}=1$. Writing
$z=e^{i\theta}$, we have $e^{in\theta}=1$, hence
$n\theta\in2\pi\ZZ$. Therefore $z=\zeta^k$ for some integer $k$.
Thus these are exactly the elements of $\mu_n$, and
$\mu_n=\generators{\zeta}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #g-equals-mu-n}
$G=\mu_n$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#g-subset-mu-n){.pf-ref}, $G\subseteq\mu_n$. Step [](#mu-n-is-cyclic){.pf-ref} gives
$\abs{\mu_n}=n=\abs{G}$. An inclusion between finite sets of equal
cardinality is equality.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #g-cyclic}
$G$ is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#g-equals-mu-n){.pf-ref} and step [](#mu-n-is-cyclic){.pf-ref},
$$
G=\mu_n=\generators{\zeta}.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#g-cyclic){.pf-ref} proves the required conclusion.
:::
