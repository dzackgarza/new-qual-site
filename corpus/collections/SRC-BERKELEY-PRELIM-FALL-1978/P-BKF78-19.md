::: {.problem}
Let
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Express $A^{-1}$ as a polynomial in $A$ with real coefficients.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The matrix $A$ satisfies
$$
A^2=3I.
$$

::: {.proof}
Direct multiplication gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
A^2
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&0\\
0&3
\end{pmatrix}
=
3I.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. The requested polynomial expression is
$$
\boxed{A^{-1}=\frac13A}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1,
$$
A\left(\frac13A\right)
=
\frac13A^2
=
I.
$$
Since $A$ commutes with every polynomial in $A$, the same computation
also gives
$$
\left(\frac13A\right)A=I.
$$
Thus $A^{-1}=\frac13A$, corresponding to the real polynomial
$p(t)=t/3$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives the required polynomial expression.
:::
:::
