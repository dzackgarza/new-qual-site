::: {.problem}
Let $M_{n\times n}$ be the vector space of real $n\times n$ matrices, identified with $\mathbb R^{n^2}$, and let $X\subset M_{n\times n}$ be compact.
Let $S\subset\mathbb C$ be the set of all numbers that occur as eigenvalues of at least one matrix in $X$.
Prove that $S$ is compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $X$ is empty, then $S$ is empty and hence compact. Assume
henceforth that $X$ is nonempty, and regard each real matrix in $X$
as acting on $\CC^n$.

<1>1. There is a constant $M\ge0$ such that
$$
\norm{A}_{\operatorname{op}}\le M
$$
for every $A\in X$.

::: {.proof}
The operator norm is a continuous function of the matrix entries.
Since $X$ is compact, it attains a maximum there. Let
$$
M
=
\max_{A\in X}\norm{A}_{\operatorname{op}}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Every $\lambda\in S$ satisfies
$$
\abs{\lambda}\le M.
$$

::: {.proof}
Choose $A\in X$ and a nonzero eigenvector $v\in\CC^n$ such that
$$
Av=\lambda v.
$$
After replacing $v$ by $v/\norm{v}$, assume $\norm{v}=1$. Then
step <1>1 gives
$$
\abs{\lambda}
=
\norm{\lambda v}
=
\norm{Av}
\le
\norm{A}_{\operatorname{op}}
\le
M.
$$
:::

<1>3. Define
$$
K
=
\left\{
(A,\lambda,v):
\begin{array}{l}
A\in X,\quad \abs{\lambda}\le M,\quad \norm{v}=1,\\
Av=\lambda v
\end{array}
\right\}.
$$
Then $K$ is compact.

::: {.proof}
The product
$$
X
\times
\{\lambda\in\CC:\abs{\lambda}\le M\}
\times
\{v\in\CC^n:\norm{v}=1\}
$$
is compact. The map
$$
(A,\lambda,v)
\longmapsto
Av-\lambda v
$$
is continuous, and $K$ is its zero set inside that compact product.
Thus $K$ is closed in a compact space and therefore compact.
:::

<1>4. The projection of $K$ onto its $\lambda$-coordinate is exactly
$S$.

::: {.proof}
If $(A,\lambda,v)\in K$, then $\norm{v}=1$ and
$Av=\lambda v$, so $v\neq0$ and $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of
$A\in X$. Hence $\lambda\in S$.

Conversely, if $\lambda\in S$, choose $A\in X$ and a nonzero
eigenvector $v$ with $Av=\lambda v$. Normalize $v$ to have norm
$1$. Step <1>2 gives $\abs{\lambda}\le M$, so
$(A,\lambda,v)\in K$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{S\text{ is compact in }\CC}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, $K$ is compact. The coordinate projection
$$
(A,\lambda,v)\longmapsto\lambda
$$
is continuous, and its image is $S$ by step <1>4. A continuous image
of a compact space is compact.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
