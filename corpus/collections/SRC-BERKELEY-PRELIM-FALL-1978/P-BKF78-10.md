::: {.problem}
How many homomorphisms are there from
\[
\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2
\]
to the symmetric group on three objects?
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
V=C_2\times C_2
=
\gens{a,b},
$$
where
$$
a^2=b^2=e,
\qquad
ab=ba.
$$

<1>1. A homomorphism $\varphi\colon V\to S_3$ is determined uniquely
by an ordered pair $(x,y)$ of commuting elements of $S_3$ satisfying
$$
x^2=y^2=e.
$$

::: {.proof}
The images
$$
x=\varphi(a),
\qquad
y=\varphi(b)
$$
must satisfy the defining relations of $V$, so
$x^2=y^2=e$ and $xy=yx$. Conversely, any ordered pair satisfying
these relations determines a unique homomorphism from the displayed
presentation of $V$.
:::

<1>2. The elements of $S_3$ whose square is the identity are the
identity and the three transpositions.

::: {.proof}
The elements of $S_3$ have cycle types
$$
1,\qquad (12),\qquad (123).
$$
The identity has order $1$, the three transpositions have order $2$,
and the two $3$-cycles have order $3$. Thus precisely the identity
and the three transpositions square to the identity.
:::

<1>3. There are exactly $10$ ordered commuting pairs among the four
elements from step <1>2.

::: {.proof}
If $x=e$, then $y$ may be any of the four elements from step <1>2,
giving $4$ choices.

If $x$ is a transposition, then among those four elements it commutes
only with $e$ and itself. Indeed, two distinct transpositions in
$S_3$ share one letter; after relabeling they are $(12)$ and $(13)$,
and
$$
(12)(13)=(132)\neq(123)=(13)(12).
$$
Thus each of the three possible transpositions for $x$ gives $2$
choices for $y$. The total number is therefore
$$
4+3\cdot2=10.
$$
:::

<1>4. The requested number of homomorphisms is
$$
\boxed{10}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, homomorphisms are in bijection with the ordered pairs
counted in step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required count.
:::
:::
