::: {.problem}
(a) Let $A$ be a set and $\mathcal P(A)$ its power set. Prove that there is no surjective function
\[
f:A\to\mathcal P(A).
\]
You may argue by considering
\[
B=\{a\in A:a\notin f(a)\}.
\]

(b) Prove that the set of all infinite subsets of $\mathbb N$ is uncountable.
Hint: first show directly that the set of all finite subsets of $\mathbb N$ is countable.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
f:A\to\mathcal P(A)
$$
is surjective, and define
$$
B=\{a\in A:a\notin f(a)\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By construction, $B\subseteq A$, so
$$
B\in\mathcal P(A).
$$
Surjectivity of $f$ therefore gives some $b\in A$ such that
$$
f(b)=B.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The element $b$ from step <1>1 yields a contradiction.


::: pf-proof

By definition of $B$,
$$
b\in B
\iff
b\notin f(b).
$$
Since $f(b)=B$, this becomes
$$
b\in B
\iff
b\notin B,
$$
which is impossible.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore no function
$$
A\to\mathcal P(A)
$$
is surjective.


::: pf-proof

The assumption of surjectivity led to the contradiction in step <1>2.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step <1>3.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For each $k\geq1$, the set $F_k$ of all $k$-element subsets of $\NN$
is countable.


::: pf-proof

Every $E\in F_k$ has a unique increasing enumeration
$$
E=\{n_1<\cdots<n_k\}.
$$
The map
$$
E\longmapsto(n_1,\ldots,n_k)
$$
injects $F_k$ into $\NN^k$.  Finite Cartesian powers of a countable set are
countable, so $\NN^k$ is countable. Hence $F_k$ is countable.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The set $F$ of all finite subsets of $\NN$ is countable.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
F
=
\{\varnothing\}
\cup
\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}F_k.
$$
By step <1>5, every $F_k$ is countable. Therefore $F$ is a countable union of
countable sets, together with one additional point, and is countable.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The power set $\mathcal P(\NN)$ is uncountable.


::: pf-proof

If $\mathcal P(\NN)$ were countable, then there would be a surjection
$$
\NN\to\mathcal P(\NN).
$$
This contradicts part (a), applied to $A=\NN$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The set $I$ of all infinite subsets of $\NN$ is uncountable.


::: pf-proof

The power set decomposes as
$$
\mathcal P(\NN)=F\cup I,
$$
where $F$ is the set of finite subsets and $I$ is the set of infinite subsets.
By step <1>6, $F$ is countable. If $I$ were also countable, then
$\mathcal P(\NN)$ would be a union of two countable sets and therefore
countable, contradicting step <1>7.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>8 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>4 and <1>9 establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::

:::
