::: {.problem}
Assume there are injective maps
\[
f:X\to Y,
\qquad
g:Y\to X.
\]
Follow the construction below to prove that there is a bijection $h:X\to Y$.

(a) Explain why it suffices to partition
\[
X=A\sqcup A',
\qquad
Y=B\sqcup B'
\]
so that $f$ maps $A$ bijectively onto $B$ and $g$ maps $B'$ bijectively onto $A'$.

(b) Set
\[
A_1=X\setminus g(Y),
\qquad
A_{n+1}=g(f(A_n)).
\]
Show that the sets $A_n$ are pairwise disjoint and that the sets $f(A_n)$ are pairwise disjoint.

(c) Let
\[
A=\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n,
\qquad
B=\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty f(A_n).
\]
Show that $f$ maps $A$ onto $B$.

(d) Let $A'=X\setminus A$ and $B'=Y\setminus B$. Show that $g$ maps $B'$ onto $A'$ and conclude the theorem.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If
$$
X=A\sqcup A',
\qquad
Y=B\sqcup B',
$$
with $f:A\to B$ bijective and $g:B'\to A'$ bijective, then there is a
bijection $h:X\to Y$.

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
h(x)=
\begin{cases}
f(x),&x\in A,\\
(g|_{B'})^{-1}(x),&x\in A'.
\end{cases}
$$
The two cases are well-defined because $A\cap A'=\varnothing$ and
$A\cup A'=X$.  The first branch maps $A$ bijectively onto $B$, and the
second maps $A'$ bijectively onto $B'$. Since
$$
B\cap B'=\varnothing,
\qquad
B\cup B'=Y,
$$
the combined map $h$ is bijective.
:::

<1>2. The set
$$
A_1=X\setminus g(Y)
$$
is disjoint from every $A_n$ with $n\geq2$.

::: {.proof}
For $n\geq2$, the recursive definition gives
$$
A_n=g(f(A_{n-1}))\subseteq g(Y).
$$
But
$$
A_1=X\setminus g(Y).
$$
Hence
$$
A_1\cap A_n=\varnothing
$$
for every $n\geq2$.
:::

<1>3. The map
$$
T=g\circ f:X\to X
$$
is injective, and
$$
A_{n+1}=T(A_n)
$$
for every $n\geq1$.

::: {.proof}
Both $f$ and $g$ are injective, so their composition is injective. The second
statement is exactly the recursive definition of the sets $A_n$.
:::

<1>4. The sets $A_n$ are pairwise disjoint.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
A_m\cap A_n\neq\varnothing
$$
for some $m<n$. If $m=1$, this contradicts step <1>2. Thus suppose $m\geq2$.
By step <1>3,
$$
A_m=T(A_{m-1}),
\qquad
A_n=T(A_{n-1}).
$$
If these images intersect, injectivity of $T$ implies
$$
A_{m-1}\cap A_{n-1}\neq\varnothing.
$$
Repeating this argument $m-1$ times gives
$$
A_1\cap A_{n-m+1}\neq\varnothing.
$$
Since $n-m+1\geq2$, this contradicts step <1>2. Therefore all $A_n$ are
pairwise disjoint.
:::

<1>5. The sets $f(A_n)$ are pairwise disjoint.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
f(A_m)\cap f(A_n)\neq\varnothing.
$$
Then there exist $x\in A_m$ and $y\in A_n$ with
$$
f(x)=f(y).
$$
Since $f$ is injective, $x=y$. Hence
$$
A_m\cap A_n\neq\varnothing.
$$
By step <1>4 this forces $m=n$. Thus the sets $f(A_n)$ are pairwise
disjoint.
:::

<1>6. Part (b) follows.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4 and <1>5 establish the two required pairwise-disjointness
statements.
:::

<1>7. Let
$$
A=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n,
\qquad
B=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}f(A_n).
$$
Then
$$
f(A)=B.
$$

::: {.proof}
For any function $f$ and any family of subsets,
$$
f\left(\bigcup_n A_n\right)
=
\bigcup_n f(A_n).
$$
Applying this to the displayed definition of $A$ gives $f(A)=B$.
:::

<1>8. The restriction
$$
f|_A:A\to B
$$
is a bijection.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 shows surjectivity onto $B$. Injectivity follows from injectivity of
$f$ on all of $X$.
:::

<1>9. Part (c) follows.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is exactly the required assertion.
:::

<1>10. If $y\in B'$, then
$$
g(y)\in A'.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $g(y)\in g(Y)$, one has
$$
g(y)\notin A_1=X\setminus g(Y).
$$
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $g(y)\in A$. Then
$$
g(y)\in A_{n+1}
$$
for some $n\geq1$. By definition of $A_{n+1}$, there exists $x\in A_n$ such
that
$$
g(y)=g(f(x)).
$$
Injectivity of $g$ gives
$$
y=f(x)\in f(A_n)\subseteq B,
$$
contradicting $y\in B'=Y\setminus B$. Hence $g(y)\notin A$, so
$g(y)\in A'$.
:::

<1>11. Every $x\in A'$ lies in $g(B')$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
A_1=X\setminus g(Y)
$$
and $A_1\subseteq A$, the condition $x\in A'=X\setminus A$ implies
$$
x\notin A_1.
$$
Hence $x\in g(Y)$, so
$$
x=g(y)
$$
for some $y\in Y$.

Suppose $y\in B$. Then by definition of $B$ there are $n\geq1$ and
$z\in A_n$ such that
$$
y=f(z).
$$
Therefore
$$
x=g(y)=g(f(z))\in A_{n+1}\subseteq A,
$$
contradicting $x\in A'$. Hence $y\notin B$, so $y\in B'$. Thus
$x=g(y)\in g(B')$.
:::

<1>12. One has
$$
g(B')=A'.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>10 gives
$$
g(B')\subseteq A',
$$
and step <1>11 gives the reverse inclusion.
:::

<1>13. The restriction
$$
g|_{B'}:B'\to A'
$$
is a bijection.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>12 gives surjectivity. Injectivity follows from injectivity of $g$ on
all of $Y$.
:::

<1>14. There exists a bijection
$$
\boxed{h:X\to Y}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>8 and <1>13, the partitions
$$
X=A\sqcup A',
\qquad
Y=B\sqcup B'
$$
satisfy exactly the hypotheses of step <1>1. The map defined there is
therefore a bijection from $X$ to $Y$.
:::

<1>15. Part (d) and the Schröder--Bernstein theorem follow.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>12--<1>14 prove the required statement and construct the desired
bijection.
:::

<1>16. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves part (a), step <1>6 proves part (b), step <1>9 proves
part (c), and step <1>15 proves part (d).
:::
