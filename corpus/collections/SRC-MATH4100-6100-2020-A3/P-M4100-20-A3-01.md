::: {.problem}
(a) Give an example of a countable collection of pairwise disjoint open intervals in $\mathbb R$.

(b) Give an example of an uncountable collection of pairwise disjoint open intervals in $\mathbb R$, or prove that no such collection exists.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The collection
$$
\mathcal I
=
\left\{
\left(n,n+\frac12\right):n\in\NN
\right\}
$$
is a countable collection of pairwise disjoint open intervals.

::: {.proof}
The map
$$
n\longmapsto\left(n,n+\frac12\right)
$$
is a bijection from $\NN$ onto $\mathcal I$, so the collection is countable.
If $m<n$, then
$$
m+\frac12<n,
$$
so
$$
\left(m,m+\frac12\right)
\cap
\left(n,n+\frac12\right)
=\varnothing.
$$
Thus the intervals are pairwise disjoint.
:::

<1>2. Part (a) follows.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the requested example.
:::

<1>3. Every nonempty open interval in $\RR$ contains a rational number.

::: {.proof}
This is the density of $\QQ$ in $\RR$.
:::

<1>4. Let $\mathcal U$ be any collection of pairwise disjoint nonempty open
intervals in $\RR$. Then there is an injection
$$
\mathcal U\hookrightarrow\QQ.
$$

::: {.proof}
Fix an enumeration
$$
\QQ=\{q_1,q_2,q_3,\ldots\}.
$$
For each interval $U\in\mathcal U$, step <1>3 shows that the set
$$
\{j\in\NN:q_j\in U\}
$$
is nonempty. Let $j(U)$ be its least element and define
$$
\Phi(U)=q_{j(U)}.
$$
Then $\Phi(U)\in U$.

If $U,V\in\mathcal U$ are distinct and
$$
\Phi(U)=\Phi(V),
$$
then that rational number belongs to both $U$ and $V$, so
$$
U\cap V\neq\varnothing,
$$
contradicting pairwise disjointness. Hence $\Phi$ is injective.
:::

<1>5. Every collection of pairwise disjoint nonempty open intervals in
$\RR$ is at most countable.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4, such a collection injects into the countable set $\QQ$.
Therefore it is at most countable.
:::

<1>6. No uncountable collection of pairwise disjoint nonempty open intervals
in $\RR$ exists.

::: {.proof}
This is exactly the contrapositive form of step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Part (b) follows.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 proves that the requested uncountable example cannot exist.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2 and <1>7 establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.
:::
:::
