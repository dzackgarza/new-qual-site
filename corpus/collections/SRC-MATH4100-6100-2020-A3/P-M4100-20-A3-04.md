::: {.problem}
Let $C\subseteq[0,1]$ be uncountable.

(a) Show that there exists $a\in(0,1)$ such that
\[
C\cap[a,1]
\]
is uncountable.

(b) Let
\[
A=\{a\in(0,1):C\cap[a,1]\text{ is uncountable}\}
\]
and set $\alpha=\sup A$. Must $C\cap[\alpha,1]$ be uncountable?

(c) Does the assertion in part (a) remain true if “uncountable” is replaced by “infinite”?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. One has
$$
(0,1]
=
\bigcup_{n=2}^{\infty}\left[\frac1n,1\right].
$$

::: {.proof}
If $x\in(0,1]$, choose $n\geq2$ so large that
$$
\frac1n\leq x.
$$
Then
$$
x\in\left[\frac1n,1\right].
$$
The reverse inclusion is immediate.
:::

<1>2. There exists $n\geq2$ such that
$$
C\cap\left[\frac1n,1\right]
$$
is uncountable.

::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that every set
$$
C\cap\left[\frac1n,1\right]
$$
were countable for $n\geq2$. By step <1>1,
$$
C\cap(0,1]
=
\bigcup_{n=2}^{\infty}
\left(
C\cap\left[\frac1n,1\right]
\right),
$$
which would be a countable union of countable sets and therefore countable.
Then
$$
C
\subseteq
\{0\}\cup(C\cap(0,1])
$$
would also be countable, contradicting the hypothesis.
:::

<1>3. Part (a) follows.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
a=\frac1n
$$
for the integer $n$ from step <1>2. Since $n\geq2$, one has $a\in(0,1)$,
and the required tail is uncountable.
:::

<1>4. In part (b), $C\cap[\alpha,1]$ need not be uncountable.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
C=\left[0,\frac12\right).
$$
This set is uncountable. For every
$$
0<a<\frac12,
$$
one has
$$
C\cap[a,1]
=
\left[a,\frac12\right),
$$
which is uncountable. If
$$
a\geq\frac12,
$$
then
$$
C\cap[a,1]=\varnothing.
$$
Therefore
$$
A=\left(0,\frac12\right),
$$
so
$$
\alpha=\sup A=\frac12.
$$
But
$$
C\cap[\alpha,1]
=
C\cap\left[\frac12,1\right]
=
\varnothing,
$$
which is countable.
:::

<1>5. Part (b) follows.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 gives a counterexample, so the answer is no.
:::

<1>6. The assertion in part (a) becomes false if “uncountable” is replaced by
“infinite.”

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
C=\left\{\frac1n:n\in\NN\right\}.
$$
This set is infinite. Fix any $a\in(0,1)$. If
$$
\frac1n\geq a,
$$
then
$$
n\leq\frac1a.
$$
Only finitely many positive integers satisfy this inequality. Hence
$$
C\cap[a,1]
$$
is finite for every $a>0$.
:::

<1>7. Part (c) follows.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 provides an infinite set for which no positive tail is infinite.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3, <1>5, and <1>7 establish parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
:::
