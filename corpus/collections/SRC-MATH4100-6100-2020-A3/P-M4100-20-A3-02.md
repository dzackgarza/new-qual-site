::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be countable and let $A_n$ be the set of all $n$-tuples
$$
(a_1,\ldots,a_n),
\qquad a_j\in A.
$$
Prove that $A_n$ is countable for every $n\in\NN$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $B$ and $C$ are countable sets, then $B\times C$ is countable.


::: pf-proof

Since $B$ and $C$ are countable, there are injections
$$
i:B\hookrightarrow\NN,
\qquad
j:C\hookrightarrow\NN.
$$
Define
$$
\Phi:B\times C\longrightarrow\NN,
\qquad
\Phi(b,c)=2^{i(b)}3^{j(c)}.
$$
Suppose
$$
\Phi(b,c)=\Phi(b',c').
$$
Unique factorization in $\NN$ gives
$$
i(b)=i(b')
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
j(c)=j(c').
$$
Since $i$ and $j$ are injective,
$$
b=b',
\qquad
c=c'.
$$
Thus $\Phi$ is injective, so $B\times C$ is countable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The set $A_1$ is countable.


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
A\longrightarrow A_1,
\qquad
a\longmapsto(a)
$$
is a bijection. Since $A$ is countable, so is $A_1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $A_n$ is countable, then $A_{n+1}$ is countable.


::: pf-proof

There is a natural bijection
$$
A_{n+1}
\longrightarrow
A_n\times A,
$$
given by
$$
(a_1,\ldots,a_n,a_{n+1})
\longmapsto
((a_1,\ldots,a_n),a_{n+1}).
$$
By the induction hypothesis, $A_n$ is countable, and $A$ is countable by
hypothesis. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} therefore shows that $A_n\times A$ is countable, hence
$A_{n+1}$ is countable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every positive integer $n$,
$$
\boxed{A_n\text{ is countable}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} is the base case. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the induction step. The result follows
by induction on $n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
