::: {.problem}
(a) Let $R$ be an integral domain, let $M$ be a torsion $R$-module, and let $N$ be a torsion-free $R$-module.
Show that $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M, N) = \langle 0 \rangle$.

(b) If $n = km$, then show that $\operatorname{Hom}_{\ZZ_n}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ_n) \cong \ZZ_m$.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) Let $f \in \operatorname{Hom}_R(M, N)$ and let $m \in M$.
Since $M$ is a torsion module, there is a nonzero $r \in R$ with $rm = 0$.
Then
$$
0 = f(0) = f(rm) = rf(m).
$$
Since $r \ne 0$ and $N$ is torsion-free, $f(m) = 0$.
Since $m \in M$ is arbitrary, $f = 0$.

(b) Since $m \mid n$, $\ZZ_m$ is a $\ZZ_n$-module.
Define
$$
\varphi : \operatorname{Hom}_{\ZZ_n}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ_n) \to \ZZ_n, \qquad \varphi(f) = f(1).
$$
This is a $\ZZ_n$-module homomorphism by the definitions of the sum and scalar multiple of homomorphisms.
If $f(1) = 0$, then $f(r) = rf(1) = 0$ for all $r \in \ZZ_m$, so $\ker\varphi = \langle 0 \rangle$.
If $t = f(1)$ is in the image of $\varphi$, then $mt = mf(1) = f(m) = f(0) = 0$, so the order of $t$ divides $m$.
Conversely, if $t \in \ZZ_n$ satisfies $mt = 0$, then $r \mapsto rt$ is a well-defined $\ZZ_n$-module homomorphism $\ZZ_m \to \ZZ_n$ that sends $1$ to $t$.
Thus the image of $\varphi$ is the set of elements of $\ZZ_n$ whose order divides $m$, which is the cyclic subgroup $\langle k \rangle$ of order $m$.
Hence
$$
\operatorname{Hom}_{\ZZ_n}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ_n) \cong \langle k \rangle \cong \ZZ_m.
$$
:::
