::: {.problem}
Let $u = (a, b) \in \ZZ^2$.

(a) Show that there is a basis of $\ZZ^2$ containing $u$ if and only if $a$ and $b$ are relatively prime.

(b) Suppose that $u = (5, 12)$.
Find a $v \in \ZZ^2$ such that $\{u, v\}$ is a basis of $\ZZ^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) Suppose that $u$ and $v = (c, d)$ form a basis of $\ZZ^2$.
Then there are integers $k, l, m, n$ with
$$
ku + lv = (1, 0), \qquad mu + nv = (0, 1),
$$
which is the matrix equation
$$
\begin{bmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} k & m \\ l & n \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Taking determinants gives $(ad - bc)(kn - ml) = 1$ in $\ZZ$, so $ad - bc = \pm 1$, and hence $a$ and $b$ are relatively prime.

Conversely, suppose that $a$ and $b$ are relatively prime, and write $ra + sb = 1$ with $r, s \in \ZZ$.
We claim that $u = (a, b)$ and $v = (-s, r)$ form a basis of $\ZZ^2$.
For $(\alpha, \beta) \in \ZZ^2$, the equation $xu + yv = (\alpha, \beta)$ is
$$
\begin{bmatrix} a & -s \\ b & r \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} \alpha \\ \beta \end{bmatrix}.
$$
The coefficient matrix has determinant $ra + sb = 1$ and inverse $\begin{bmatrix} r & s \\ -b & a \end{bmatrix}$, so the equation has the unique solution
$$
\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} r & s \\ -b & a \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \alpha \\ \beta \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} r\alpha + s\beta \\ -b\alpha + a\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$
in $\ZZ^2$.
Thus $u$ and $v$ form a linearly independent generating set of $\ZZ^2$, that is, a basis.

(b) Since $5 \cdot 5 + (-2) \cdot 12 = 1$, part (a) with $r = 5$ and $s = -2$ gives $v = (2, 5)$.
:::
