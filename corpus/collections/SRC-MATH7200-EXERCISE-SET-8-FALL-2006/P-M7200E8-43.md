::: {.problem}
Suppose $R$ is a PID and $M = R\langle x \rangle$ is a cyclic $R$-module with $\operatorname{Ann}(x) = \langle a \rangle \neq \langle 0 \rangle$.
Show that if $N$ is a submodule of $M$, then $N$ is cyclic with $\operatorname{Ann} N = \langle b \rangle$ where $b$ is a divisor of $a$.
Conversely, show that $M$ has a unique submodule $N$ with annihilator $\langle b \rangle$ for each divisor $b$ of $a$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define an $R$-module homomorphism $\varphi : R \to M$ by $\varphi(r) = rx$.
Since $M$ is cyclic with generator $x$, $\varphi$ is surjective, and $\ker\varphi = \operatorname{Ann}(x) = \langle a \rangle$.
By the correspondence theorem, $N \mapsto \varphi^{-1}(N)$ is a bijection from the submodules of $M$ onto the submodules of $R$ that contain $\langle a \rangle$, with inverse $I \mapsto \varphi(I)$.
Since $R$ is a PID, the submodules of $R$ are the principal ideals.
So $\varphi^{-1}(N) = \langle c \rangle \supseteq \langle a \rangle$ for some divisor $c$ of $a$, and
$$
N = \varphi(\langle c \rangle) = \{r(cx) : r \in R\}
$$
is cyclic with generator $cx$.
Its annihilator is
$$
\operatorname{Ann}(N) = \{r \in R : rcx = 0\} = \{r \in R : a \divides rc\} = \left\langle \frac{a}{c} \right\rangle,
$$
so $\operatorname{Ann}(N)$ is generated by the divisor $b = a/c$ of $a$.

Conversely, let $b$ be a divisor of $a$.
The submodule $N = \langle (a/b)x \rangle$ has $\operatorname{Ann}(N) = \langle b \rangle$ by the computation above with $c = a/b$.
If $N'$ is any submodule with $\operatorname{Ann}(N') = \langle b \rangle$, write $\varphi^{-1}(N') = \langle c' \rangle$; then $\langle a/c' \rangle = \langle b \rangle$, so $c'$ and $a/b$ are associates, $\varphi^{-1}(N') = \langle a/b \rangle$, and $N' = N$.
Hence $M$ has exactly one submodule with annihilator $\langle b \rangle$ for each divisor $b$ of $a$.
:::
