::: {.problem}
Show that $\QQ$ is a torsion-free $\ZZ$-module that is not free.
:::

::: {.solution}
The module $\QQ$ is torsion-free over $\ZZ$: if $m \in \ZZ$ is nonzero and $r \in \QQ$ satisfies $mr = 0$, then $m$ is invertible in the field $\QQ$, so $r = (1/m)(mr) = 0$.

Suppose that $S \subseteq \QQ$ is a basis of $\QQ$ over $\ZZ$.
Since $\QQ \ne 0$, $S$ is nonempty, and $0 \notin S$.
If $S$ contains two distinct elements $r/s$ and $t/u$, with $r, s, t, u \in \ZZ$ all nonzero, then
$$
(st)\frac{r}{s} - (ur)\frac{t}{u} = 0
$$
is a $\ZZ$-linear dependence relation with nonzero coefficients, which contradicts linear independence.
Hence $S = \{r/s\}$ with $r, s$ nonzero integers.
But then $1/(2s)$ is not an integer multiple of $r/s$: an equation $1/(2s) = m(r/s)$ with $m \in \ZZ$ gives $1 = 2mr$ in $\ZZ$, which is impossible.
So $S$ does not generate $\QQ$, a contradiction.
Therefore $\QQ$ is not a free $\ZZ$-module.
:::
