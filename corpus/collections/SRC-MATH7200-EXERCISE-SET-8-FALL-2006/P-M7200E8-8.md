::: {.problem}
Show that $\QQ$ is a torsion-free $\ZZ$-module that is not free.
:::

::: {.solution}

The statement is exercise 8 of the Fall 2006 Math 7200 exercise set as this collection
records it. The argument below is the card's own — no retained solution set for the exercise
was found — restated as numbered steps.

$\QQ$ is a $\ZZ$-module by $n\cdot r=nr$ for $n\in\ZZ$, $r\in\QQ$, and it is nonzero.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #torsion-free}

$\QQ$ is torsion-free as a $\ZZ$-module.

::: pf-proof

Let $0\ne m\in\ZZ$ and $r\in\QQ$ with $m\cdot r=mr=0$. Since $m\ne0$, $m$ has an inverse
$1/m$ in the field $\QQ$, so $r=(1/m)(mr)=(1/m)\cdot0=0$. Hence no nonzero integer annihilates
a nonzero element, which is torsion-freeness.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis-not-two}

No basis of $\QQ$ over $\ZZ$ has two distinct elements.

::: pf-proof

Let $S$ be a basis and let $r/s,t/u\in S$ with $r,s,t,u$ nonzero integers and $r/s\ne t/u$.
Then
$(st)\cdot(r/s)-(ur)\cdot(t/u)=rt-rt=0$, and the coefficients $st$ and $ur$ are nonzero
integers, so $S$ is linearly dependent over $\ZZ$, contradicting that a basis is linearly
independent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis-not-one}

No basis of $\QQ$ over $\ZZ$ has exactly one element.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $S=\theset{w}$ with $w\ne0$. A basis spans, so there is $m\in\ZZ$ with
$1/(2s)=m\,w$ whenever $w$ is written in lowest terms as $r/s$ with $s>0$: writing $w=r/s$
with $r,s$ nonzero integers, the equation $1/(2s)=m(r/s)$ gives $2mr=1$ after multiplying by
$2s$, which is impossible for integers $m,r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not-free}

$\QQ$ is not a free $\ZZ$-module.

::: pf-proof

A free module has a basis. By step [](#basis-not-two){.pf-ref} a basis of $\QQ$ has at most
one element, and it is nonempty because a basis generates $\QQ\ne0$ and $0$ belongs to no
linearly independent set; so a basis would have exactly one element, which step
[](#basis-not-one){.pf-ref} excludes. Hence $\QQ$ has no basis over $\ZZ$ and is not free.

:::
