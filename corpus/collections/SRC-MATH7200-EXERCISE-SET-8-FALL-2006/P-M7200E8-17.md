::: {.problem}
Give examples of short exact sequences of $R$-modules
$$
0 \longrightarrow M_1 \xrightarrow{\phi} M \xrightarrow{\phi'} M_2 \longrightarrow 0
$$
and
$$
0 \longrightarrow N_1 \xrightarrow{\psi} N \xrightarrow{\psi'} N_2 \longrightarrow 0
$$
such that

(a) $M_1 \cong N_1$, $M \cong N$, $M_2 \not\cong N_2$;

(b) $M_1 \cong N_1$, $M \not\cong N$, $M_2 \cong N_2$;

(c) $M_1 \not\cong N_1$, $M \cong N$, $M_2 \cong N_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A short exact sequence $0 \to M_{1} \xrightarrow{\phi} M \xrightarrow{\phi'} M_{2} \to 0$ of $R$-modules means that $\phi$ is injective, $\phi'$ is surjective and $\ker\phi' = \operatorname{im}\phi$; throughout $R = \ZZ$, $\ZZ_{n} = \ZZ/n\ZZ$, and $\ZZ_{2}^{n}$ is the direct sum of $n$ copies of $\ZZ_{2}$. The collection's `provenance` is the Fall 2006 exercise set, with no solutions sheet, so the examples below are the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a-first}
(a) For every $n \ge 1$ the sequence $0 \to \ZZ \xrightarrow{\phi_{n}} \ZZ \xrightarrow{\pi} \ZZ_{n} \to 0$ with $\phi_{n}(x) = nx$ is exact.

::: pf-proof
$\phi_{n}$ is injective because $nx = 0$ forces $x = 0$ in $\ZZ$; $\pi$ is the canonical projection onto the quotient, hence surjective with kernel $n\ZZ = \operatorname{im}\phi_{n}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-second}
(a) For $n \ne m$ the two sequences have isomorphic first and middle terms and $\ZZ_{n} \not\cong \ZZ_{m}$.

::: pf-proof
The first two terms are $\ZZ$ and $\ZZ$ in both cases. A group isomorphism preserves the number of elements, and $\ZZ_{n}$ has $n$ elements while $\ZZ_{m}$ has $m$, so for $n \ne m$ the third terms are not isomorphic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-first}
(b) Both $0 \to \ZZ_{p} \xrightarrow{\phi} \ZZ_{p^{2}} \xrightarrow{\pi} \ZZ_{p} \to 0$ with $\phi(u) = pu$ and $0 \to \ZZ_{p} \xrightarrow{\iota} \ZZ_{p}\oplus\ZZ_{p} \xrightarrow{\rho} \ZZ_{p} \to 0$ with $\iota$ the inclusion of the first summand and $\rho$ the projection onto the second are exact.

::: pf-proof
In the first, $\phi$ is injective since $pu = 0$ in $\ZZ_{p^{2}}$ forces $p \mid u$, and the kernel of the reduction $\pi$ is $p\ZZ_{p^{2}} = \operatorname{im}\phi$, of order $p$. In the second, $\iota$ is injective and $\rho$ is surjective, with kernel the first summand, which is $\operatorname{im}\iota$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-second}
(b) $\ZZ_{p^{2}} \not\cong \ZZ_{p} \oplus \ZZ_{p}$, so the middle terms differ while the outer terms agree.

::: pf-proof
Both outer terms are $\ZZ_{p}$ in the two sequences. In $\ZZ_{p^{2}}$ the element $1$ has order $p^{2}$, whereas every element of $\ZZ_{p}\oplus\ZZ_{p}$ is killed by $p$, so no isomorphism between the two groups exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-map}
(c) For the module $M$ of all sequences in $\ZZ_{2}$ and $\psi_{n}(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots) = (a_{n},a_{n+1},\ldots)$, the map $\psi_{n}$ is a surjective homomorphism with kernel $\operatorname{im}\phi_{n} \cong \ZZ_{2}^{n}$, where $\phi_{n}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}) = (a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1},0,\ldots)$.

::: pf-proof
$\psi_{n}$ is $\ZZ$-linear because each coordinate of the image is a coordinate of the input; it is surjective because any sequence $(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)$ is the image of $(0,\ldots,0,b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)$ with $n$ leading zeros. Its kernel consists of the sequences vanishing from position $n$ on, which are exactly the images of $\phi_{n}$, and $\phi_{n}$ is injective, so the kernel is isomorphic to $\ZZ_{2}^{n}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-second}
(c) Hence $0 \to \ZZ_{2}^{n} \xrightarrow{\phi_{n}} M \xrightarrow{\psi_{n}} M \to 0$ is exact, with the same middle and last terms for every $n$ and first terms $\ZZ_{2}^{n}$ pairwise non-isomorphic.

::: pf-proof
Exactness is step [](#c-map){.pf-ref}: $\phi_{n}$ injective, $\psi_{n}$ surjective with image of $\phi_{n}$ as kernel. The middle and last terms are $M$ and $M$ independently of $n$, while $\ZZ_{2}^{n}$ has $2^{n}$ elements, so $\ZZ_{2}^{n} \not\cong \ZZ_{2}^{m}$ for $n \ne m$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
The three requested example pairs are supplied, with (a) $M_{1} \cong N_{1}$, $M \cong N$ and $M_{2} \not\cong N_{2}$, (b) $M_{1} \cong N_{1}$, $M \not\cong N$ and $M_{2} \cong N_{2}$, and (c) $M_{1} \not\cong N_{1}$, $M \cong N$ and $M_{2} \cong N_{2}$.

::: pf-proof
(a) is steps [](#a-first){.pf-ref} and [](#a-second){.pf-ref} with the sequences of different $n$; (b) is steps [](#b-first){.pf-ref} and [](#b-second){.pf-ref}; (c) is steps [](#c-map){.pf-ref} and [](#c-second){.pf-ref} with the sequences of different $n$. Each pair consists of genuine short exact sequences, by those steps.

:::
