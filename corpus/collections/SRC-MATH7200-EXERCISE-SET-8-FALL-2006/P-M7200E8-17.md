::: {.problem}
Give examples of short exact sequences of $R$-modules
$$
0 \longrightarrow M_1 \xrightarrow{\phi} M \xrightarrow{\phi'} M_2 \longrightarrow 0
$$
and
$$
0 \longrightarrow N_1 \xrightarrow{\psi} N \xrightarrow{\psi'} N_2 \longrightarrow 0
$$
such that

(a) $M_1 \cong N_1$, $M \cong N$, $M_2 \not\cong N_2$;

(b) $M_1 \cong N_1$, $M \not\cong N$, $M_2 \cong N_2$;

(c) $M_1 \not\cong N_1$, $M \cong N$, $M_2 \cong N_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) For $n \in \NN$, consider the short exact sequence
$$
0 \longrightarrow \ZZ \xrightarrow{\phi_n} \ZZ \xrightarrow{\pi} \ZZ_n \longrightarrow 0, \tag{$*_n$}
$$
where $\phi_n(x) = nx$ and $\pi$ is the canonical projection.
For any two natural numbers $n \ne m$, the sequences $(*_n)$ and $(*_m)$ have the same first two terms and third terms $\ZZ_n \not\cong \ZZ_m$.

(b) Let $p$ be a prime, and take the sequences
$$
0 \longrightarrow \ZZ_p \xrightarrow{\phi} \ZZ_{p^2} \xrightarrow{\pi} \ZZ_p \longrightarrow 0
\quad\text{and}\quad
0 \longrightarrow \ZZ_p \xrightarrow{\iota} \ZZ_p \oplus \ZZ_p \xrightarrow{\rho} \ZZ_p \longrightarrow 0,
$$
where $\phi(m) = pm$, $\pi$ is the canonical projection, $\iota$ is the inclusion of the first summand and $\rho$ is the projection onto the second summand.
Both sequences are exact and their outer terms agree, but $\ZZ_{p^2} \not\cong \ZZ_p \oplus \ZZ_p$ because $\ZZ_{p^2}$ has an element of order $p^2$.

(c) Let $M$ be the $\ZZ$-module of sequences of elements of the field $\ZZ_2$:
$$
M = \{(a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots) : a_j \in \ZZ_2\}.
$$
For each $n \in \NN$ define $\psi_n : M \to M$ by
$$
\psi_n(a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots) = (a_n, a_{n+1}, a_{n+2}, \ldots).
$$
Then $\psi_n$ is a surjective $\ZZ$-module homomorphism, and
$$
\ker\psi_n = \{(a_0, \ldots, a_{n-1}, 0, 0, \ldots) : a_j \in \ZZ_2\} \cong \ZZ_2^n.
$$
Thus for each $n \in \NN$ there is a short exact sequence
$$
0 \longrightarrow \ZZ_2^n \xrightarrow{\phi_n} M \xrightarrow{\psi_n} M \longrightarrow 0,
$$
where
$$
\phi_n(a_0, \ldots, a_{n-1}) = (a_0, \ldots, a_{n-1}, 0, 0, \ldots).
$$
Since $\ZZ_2^n$ has $2^n$ elements, $\ZZ_2^n \not\cong \ZZ_2^m$ for $m \ne n$, and the sequences for $n$ and $m$ give the required example.
:::
