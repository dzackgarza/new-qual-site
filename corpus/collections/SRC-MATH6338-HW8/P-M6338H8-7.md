::: {.problem}
Compute the Fourier transform of
$$
g(x)=e^{-2\pi\norm{x}},\qquad x\in\RR^n,
$$
using the following steps.

(a) Let $f\in L^1(\RR)$ and show that
$$
\int_{\RR}f(x)\,dx=\int_{\RR}f\left(x-\frac1x\right)dx.
$$

(b) Use part (a) with $f(x)=e^{-tx^2}$ and $t>0$ to obtain the identity
$$
e^{-2t}=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-y-t^2/y}\,\frac{dy}{\sqrt y}.
$$

(c) Set $t=\pi\norm{x}$ and integrate with respect to $e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\,dx$ to obtain
$$
\widehat g(\xi)
=\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}
\frac1{(1+\norm{\xi}^2)^{(n+1)/2}}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) First let $f\ge0$ be measurable. The map $x\mapsto -1/x$ is a $C^1$ bijection of $(0,\infty)$ onto $(-\infty,0)$ with Jacobian $1/x^2$, and it leaves $x-1/x$ unchanged. Hence
$$
\int_{-\infty}^0 f\left(x-\frac1x\right)dx=\int_0^\infty f\left(x-\frac1x\right)\frac{dx}{x^2}.
$$
Adding $\int_0^\infty f(x-1/x)\,dx$ to both sides gives
$$
\int_{\RR}f\left(x-\frac1x\right)dx=\int_0^\infty f\left(x-\frac1x\right)\left(1+\frac1{x^2}\right)dx=\int_{\RR}f(u)\,du,
$$
because $u=x-1/x$ is an increasing $C^1$ bijection of $(0,\infty)$ onto $\RR$ with $du=(1+1/x^2)\,dx$.
For $f\in L^1(\RR)$, the case $\abs f$ shows that $f(x-1/x)$ is integrable, and applying the nonnegative case to the positive and negative parts of $\operatorname{Re}f$ and $\operatorname{Im}f$ gives the identity.

(b) For $f(x)=e^{-tx^2}$, $\int_{\RR}f=\sqrt{\pi/t}$. Since $(x-1/x)^2=x^2-2+1/x^2$, part (a) gives
$$
\sqrt{\frac\pi t}=e^{2t}\int_{\RR}e^{-tx^2-t/x^2}\,dx=2e^{2t}\int_0^\infty e^{-tx^2-t/x^2}\,dx.
$$
Substitute $y=tx^2$, so that $dx=dy/(2\sqrt{ty})$ and $t/x^2=t^2/y$:
$$
\sqrt{\frac\pi t}=\frac{e^{2t}}{\sqrt t}\int_0^\infty e^{-y-t^2/y}\,\frac{dy}{\sqrt y}.
$$
Multiplying by $e^{-2t}\sqrt{t/\pi}$ gives the identity.

(c) Part (b) with $t=\pi\norm{x}$ gives
$$
e^{-2\pi\norm{x}}
=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty
e^{-y-\pi^2\norm{x}^2/y}\,\frac{dy}{\sqrt y}.
$$
For $y>0$, the Gaussian-transform formula gives
$$
\int_{\RR^n}e^{-\pi^2\norm{x}^2/y}e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,dx
=\left(\frac{y}{\pi}\right)^{n/2}e^{-y\norm{\xi}^2}.
$$
Taking $\xi=0$ shows that
$$
\int_0^\infty\int_{\RR^n}e^{-y-\pi^2\norm{x}^2/y}\,dx\,\frac{dy}{\sqrt y}=\pi^{-n/2}\int_0^\infty y^{(n-1)/2}e^{-y}\,dy<\infty,
$$
so Fubini applies. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat g(\xi)
&=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}
\int_0^\infty y^{-1/2}e^{-y}
\left(\frac{y}{\pi}\right)^{n/2}e^{-y\norm{\xi}^2}\,dy\\
&=\pi^{-(n+1)/2}
\int_0^\infty y^{(n+1)/2-1}e^{-(1+\norm{\xi}^2)y}\,dy\\
&=\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}
(1+\norm{\xi}^2)^{-(n+1)/2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
