::: problem
Let
\[
g(x)=e^{-2\pi|x|},\qquad x\in\mathbb R^n.
\]
Compute its Fourier transform and prove
\[
\widehat g(\xi)
=\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}
\frac1{(1+|\xi|^2)^{(n+1)/2}}.
\]
:::

::: solution
Use the Gamma-integral identity
\[
r^{-\alpha}=\frac1{\Gamma(\alpha)}
\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}e^{-rt}\,dt
\qquad(r>0,\ \alpha>0)
\]
in the equivalent Gaussian-mixture form
\[
e^{-2\pi|x|}
=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty
s^{-1/2}e^{-s-\pi^2|x|^2/s}\,ds.
\]
(The identity follows from the standard integral
$\int_0^\infty s^{-1/2}e^{-s-a^2/s}\,ds=\sqrt\pi e^{-2a}$.)
The integrand is nonnegative before the Fourier phase is inserted, so Tonelli justifies the mixture, and absolute integrability justifies Fubini in the Fourier calculation.

For $s>0$, the Gaussian-transform formula gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-\pi^2|x|^2/s}e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,dx
=\left(\frac{s}{\pi}\right)^{n/2}e^{-s|\xi|^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat g(\xi)
&=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}
\int_0^\infty s^{-1/2}e^{-s}
\left(\frac{s}{\pi}\right)^{n/2}e^{-s|\xi|^2}\,ds\\
&=\pi^{-(n+1)/2}
\int_0^\infty s^{(n+1)/2-1}e^{-(1+|\xi|^2)s}\,ds\\
&=\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}
(1+|\xi|^2)^{-(n+1)/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the required formula.
:::
