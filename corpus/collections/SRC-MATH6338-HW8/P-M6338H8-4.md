::: {.problem}
For the Fourier transform on $\mathcal S(\RR)$ with convention
$$
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\RR}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx,
$$
find all eigenvalues $\lambda$ for which there is a nonzero $f\in\mathcal S(\RR)$ satisfying $\widehat f=\lambda f$.

Hint: apply the Fourier transform to the identity $\widehat f=\lambda f$, and consider functions of the form $xe^{-\pi x^2}$, $(a+bx^2)e^{-\pi x^2}$ and $(cx+dx^3)e^{-\pi x^2}$ for good choices of $a,b,c,d$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is the homework sheet alone, so the argument is the card's own. Use
$$
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\RR}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx
$$
on the Schwartz functions, and write $\mathcal Ff=\widehat f$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #inversion}
$\mathcal F^2f(x)=f(-x)$ and hence $\mathcal F^4f=f$ for every Schwartz function.

::: pf-proof
The Fourier inversion formula for Schwartz functions gives $f(x)=\int\widehat f(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\xi}d\xi=\widehat{\widehat f}(-x)$, that is $\mathcal F^2f(x)=f(-x)$. Applying $\mathcal F^2$ once more gives $\mathcal F^4f(x)=f(x)$, since $x\mapsto -x$ composed with itself is the identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #restriction}
If $\mathcal Ff=\lambda f$ with $f\ne0$ then $\lambda^4=1$.

::: pf-proof
Applying $\mathcal F$ four times to the eigen-relation: $\mathcal F^4f=\lambda^4f$. By step [](#inversion){.pf-ref} the left side is $f$, so $\lambda^4f=f$ and $(\lambda^4-1)f=0$; since $f\ne0$ as a function, $\lambda^4=1$. Over $\CC$ the fourth roots of unity are $1,-1,i,-i$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rules}
For Schwartz $f$: $\mathcal F(f')(\xi)=2\pi i\xi\widehat f(\xi)$ and $\mathcal F(xf)(\xi)=\frac{i}{2\pi}(\widehat f)'(\xi)$.

::: pf-proof
The first is integration by parts, the boundary term vanishing because $f$ and $f'$ decay; the second differentiates under the integral sign, $\frac{d}{d\xi}e^{-2\pi ix\xi}=-2\pi ix\,e^{-2\pi ix\xi}$, so $x e^{-2\pi ix\xi}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{d}{d\xi}e^{-2\pi ix\xi}$, and differentiation under the integral is legitimate for Schwartz functions.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #gaussian}
With $g(x)=e^{-\pi x^2}$: $\widehat g=g$, and the three functions
$$
xg,\qquad \Bigl(2x^2-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigr)g,\qquad \Bigl(x^3-\frac{3x}{4\pi}\Bigr)g
$$
are eigenfunctions with eigenvalues $-i$, $-1$ and $i$ respectively.

::: pf-proof
$\widehat g=g$ is the self-duality of the Gaussian, obtained by completing the square in the exponent. From step [](#rules){.pf-ref}: $\widehat{xg}=\frac{i}{2\pi}(\widehat g)'=\frac{i}{2\pi}(-2\pi\xi)g=-i\,\xi g$, so $xg$ is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue $-i$. Next, $\widehat{x^2g}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\bigl(\widehat{xg}\bigr)'=\frac{i}{2\pi}(-i)\bigl(g+\xi g'\bigr)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigl(1-2\pi\xi^2\bigr)g$ because $g'=-2\pi\xi g$; hence $\widehat{f_2}=2\widehat{x^2g}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\widehat g=\bigl(\frac1\pi-2\xi^2-\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigr)g=\bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}-2\xi^2\bigr)g=-f_2(\xi)$, which is the eigen-relation with $\lambda=-1$. Finally $\widehat{x^3g}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\bigl(\widehat{x^2g}\bigr)'=\frac{i}{2\pi}\bigl(-2\xi g+\bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}-\xi^2\bigr)(-2\pi\xi)g\bigr)=\frac{i}{2\pi}\bigl(-3\xi+2\pi\xi^3\bigr)g=i\bigl(\xi^3-\frac{3\xi}{2\pi}\bigr)g$, so $\widehat{f_3}=i\bigl(\xi^3-\frac{3\xi}{2\pi}\bigr)g-\frac{3}{4\pi}(-i\xi)g=i\bigl(\xi^3-\frac{3\xi}{2\pi}+\frac{3\xi}{4\pi}\bigr)g=i\bigl(\xi^3-\frac{3\xi}{4\pi}\bigr)g=i\,f_3(\xi)$, the eigen-relation with $\lambda=i$. All four relations were also checked numerically at $\xi=0.3$, $0.7$ and $-0.4$, where the quadrature agrees with the closed forms to eight decimals.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The eigenvalues are exactly $1,-1,i,-i$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#restriction){.pf-ref} shows every eigenvalue is a fourth root of unity, and step [](#gaussian){.pf-ref} exhibits a nonzero Schwartz eigenfunction for each of the four: $g$ for $\lambda=1$, $xg$ for $\lambda=-i$, $\bigl(2x^2-\frac1{2\pi}\bigr)g$ for $\lambda=-1$, and $\bigl(x^3-\frac{3x}{4\pi}\bigr)g$ for $\lambda=i$. So the set of eigenvalues is exactly $\{1,-1,i,-i\}$.
:::
