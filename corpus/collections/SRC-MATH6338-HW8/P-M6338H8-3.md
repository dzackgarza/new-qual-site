::: problem
If $f_k,f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$ and $f_k\to f$ in the Schwartz topology, prove that
\[
\widehat f_k\to\widehat f
\quad\text{and}\quad
\check f_k\to\check f
\]
in $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$.
:::

::: solution
It suffices by linearity to show that $u_k\to0$ in $\mathcal S$ implies $\widehat u_k\to0$ in $\mathcal S$.
For multi-indices $\alpha,\beta$, the Fourier identities give
\[
\xi^\alpha\partial_\xi^\beta\widehat u_k(\xi)
=C_{\alpha,\beta}\,
\widehat{\partial^\alpha(x^\beta u_k)}(\xi)
\]
for a constant $C_{\alpha,\beta}$. Hence
\[
\sup_\xi|\xi^\alpha\partial^\beta\widehat u_k(\xi)|
\le C_{\alpha,\beta}\,
\|\partial^\alpha(x^\beta u_k)\|_1.
\]
Choose an integer $N>n$. For every Schwartz function $v$,
\[
\|v\|_1
\le \int_{|x|\le1}\|v\|_\infty\,dx
+\int_{|x|>1}|x|^{-N}\sup_z|z|^N|v(z)|\,dx,
\]
so the $L^1$ norm is bounded by finitely many Schwartz seminorms. Applying this to $v=\partial^\alpha(x^\beta u_k)$ shows the right side tends to $0$ because $u_k\to0$ in $\mathcal S$.
Thus every Schwartz seminorm of $\widehat u_k$ tends to zero.

The inverse transform differs from the forward transform only by reflection (with the chosen normalization), and reflection is continuous on $\mathcal S$. Hence $\check f_k\to\check f$ as well.
:::
