::: {.problem}
If $f_k,f\in\mathcal S(\RR^n)$ and $f_k\to f$ in the Schwartz topology, prove that
$$
\widehat f_k\to\widehat f
\quad\text{and}\quad
\check f_k\to\check f
$$
in $\mathcal S(\RR^n)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
This is Problem 3 of the retained Homework 8 of MATH 6338 (`assets/attachments/math6338_hw8.pdf`), whose statement is "If $f_{k},f\in\mathcal{S}(\RR^{n})$ and $f_{k}\to f$ in $\mathcal{S}(\RR^{n})$, then $\widehat{f_{k}}\to\widehat{f}$ and $\check{f_{k}}\to\check{f}$ in $\mathcal{S}(\RR^{n})$". No published solution set is transcribed: the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #seminorms}
The Schwartz topology is given by the seminorms $\norm{v}_{(\alpha,\beta)}=\sup_{x}\abs{x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}v(x)}$, and $u_{k}\to0$ in $\mathcal{S}$ means $\norm{u_{k}}_{(\alpha,\beta)}\to0$ for every pair of multi-indices. It suffices to prove that $u_{k}\to0$ implies $\widehat{u_{k}}\to0$ and $\check{u_{k}}\to0$.

::: pf-proof
By definition of the Schwartz topology. For the reduction: the Fourier transform is linear, so with $u_{k}=f_{k}-f$ one has $\widehat{f_{k}}-\widehat{f}=\widehat{u_{k}}$ and $\check{f_{k}}-\check{f}=\check{u_{k}}$, and $f_{k}\to f$ means exactly $u_{k}\to0$ in $\mathcal{S}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #identity}
For multi-indices $\alpha,\beta$ there is a constant $C_{\alpha,\beta}\neq0$ with $\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{u}(\xi)=C_{\alpha,\beta}\,\widehat{\partial^{\alpha}(x^{\beta}u)}(\xi)$.

::: pf-proof
Differentiating under the integral, $\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{u}(\xi)=(-2\pi i)^{\abs{\beta}}\widehat{x^{\beta}u}(\xi)$. Integrating by parts, $\widehat{\partial^{\alpha}u}(\xi)=(2\pi i)^{\abs{\alpha}}\xi^{\alpha}\widehat{u}(\xi)$, since $u$ and all its derivatives decay faster than any polynomial. Combining the two gives the identity with $C_{\alpha,\beta}=(-2\pi i)^{\abs{\beta}}(2\pi i)^{-\abs{\alpha}}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #sup}
$\norm{\widehat{u_{k}}}_{(\alpha,\beta)}\leq\abs{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\norm{\partial^{\alpha}(x^{\beta}u_{k})}_{1}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#identity){.pf-ref}, $\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{u_{k}}(\xi)=C_{\alpha,\beta}\widehat{\partial^{\alpha}(x^{\beta}u_{k})}(\xi)$, and the Fourier transform of an $L^{1}$ function is bounded by its $L^{1}$ norm; taking the supremum over $\xi$ gives the bound.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #l1}
There are $N$ and a constant $D$ with $\norm{v}_{1}\leq D\sum_{\abs{\gamma}\leq N}\left(\norm{v}_{(0,0)}+\norm{v}_{(\gamma,0)}\right)$ for every $v\in\mathcal{S}(\RR^{n})$.

::: pf-proof
Choose an integer $N>n$. On $\abs{x}\leq1$, $\abs{v(x)}\leq\norm{v}_{(0,0)}$, so that part of the integral is at most $\omega_{n}\norm{v}_{(0,0)}$ with $\omega_{n}$ the volume of the unit ball. On $\abs{x}>1$,
$$\int_{\abs{x}>1}\abs{v(x)}\,dx=\int_{\abs{x}>1}\abs{x}^{-N}\abs{x}^{N}\abs{v(x)}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\abs{x}>1}\abs{x}^{-N}dx\right)\sup_{x}\abs{x}^{N}\abs{v(x)},$$
and the first factor is finite because $N>n$. Finally $\abs{x}^{N}\leq D_{N}\sum_{\abs{\gamma}\leq N}\abs{x^{\gamma}}$ for a constant $D_{N}$, so $\sup_{x}\abs{x}^{N}\abs{v(x)}\leq D_{N}\sum_{\abs{\gamma}\leq N}\norm{v}_{(\gamma,0)}$. Adding the two estimates gives the bound.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #forward}
$\norm{\widehat{u_{k}}}_{(\alpha,\beta)}\to0$ for every pair of multi-indices, so $\widehat{u_{k}}\to0$ in $\mathcal{S}(\RR^{n})$.

::: pf-proof
$v=\partial^{\alpha}(x^{\beta}u_{k})$ is a Schwartz function, since multiplication by $x^{\beta}$ and differentiation preserve $\mathcal{S}$, so step [](#l1){.pf-ref} applies to it. Every seminorm of $v$ is a finite combination of seminorms of $u_{k}$ of the form $\norm{u_{k}}_{(\gamma,\delta)}$: the derivative $\partial^{\alpha}$ and the factor $x^{\beta}$ shift the indices in a way independent of $k$. Since $\norm{u_{k}}_{(\gamma,\delta)}\to0$ for all pairs by hypothesis, step [](#sup){.pf-ref} gives $\norm{\widehat{u_{k}}}_{(\alpha,\beta)}\to0$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #inverse}
$\check{u_{k}}\to0$ in $\mathcal{S}(\RR^{n})$ as well.

::: pf-proof
With the normalization used above, the inverse transform is $\check{v}(x)=\widehat{v}(-x)$, so $\check{v}=\widehat{v}\circ R$ with $R(x)=-x$. The reflection $R$ is a linear homeomorphism of $\mathcal{S}(\RR^{n})$: it permutes the seminorms, $\norm{v\circ R}_{(\alpha,\beta)}=\norm{v}_{(\alpha,\beta)}$ up to a sign. Composition of the continuous map $v\mapsto\widehat{v}$ with the continuous reflection gives $\norm{\check{u_{k}}}_{(\alpha,\beta)}\to0$, and the conclusion for $f_{k}\to f$ follows from the reduction in step [](#seminorms){.pf-ref}.
:::
