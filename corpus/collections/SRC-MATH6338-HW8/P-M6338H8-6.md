::: problem
Suppose $f\in L^1(\mathbb R^n)$ and $f>0$ almost everywhere. Prove that
\[
|\widehat f(y)|<\widehat f(0)
\qquad(y\ne0).
\]
:::

::: solution
Since $f\ge0$,
\[
|\widehat f(y)|
\le\int f(x)\,dx=\widehat f(0).
\]
Suppose equality holds for some $y\ne0$. Equality in the triangle inequality for the integral implies that the unit complex numbers $e^{-2\pi i x\cdot y}$ have a common phase for $f(x)\,dx$-almost every $x$. Equivalently, for some $\eta\in\mathbb C$ with $|\eta|=1$,
\[
e^{-2\pi i x\cdot y}=\eta
\]
for $f(x)\,dx$-almost every $x$.
Because $f>0$ almost everywhere, this holds for Lebesgue-almost every $x$.
But for fixed $y\ne0$ and fixed $\eta$, the level set
\[
\{x:e^{-2\pi i x\cdot y}=\eta\}
\]
is a countable union of affine hyperplanes and therefore has Lebesgue measure zero. This is impossible. Hence equality cannot occur for $y\ne0$, proving the strict inequality.
:::
