::: {.problem}
For $c>0$, let $f_c(x)=e^{-cx^2}$ on $\RR$.

(a) Compute $\widehat f_c$ as follows: let $\varphi=\widehat f_c$, show that $4\pi^2t\varphi(t)+2c\varphi'(t)=0$, and solve the resulting differential equation.

(b) Show that there is one and only one value of $c$ for which $f_c=\widehat f_c$.

(c) Show that $f_a*f_b=\gamma f_d$ for constants $\gamma=\gamma(a,b)$ and $d=d(a,b)$, and find them.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) Let $\varphi(t)=\widehat f_c(t)$. Since $x e^{-cx^2}$ is integrable, differentiation under the integral and integration by parts give
$$
\varphi'(t)=-2\pi i\int_{\RR} x e^{-cx^2}e^{-2\pi ixt}\,dx
=\frac{\pi i}{c}\int_{\RR}\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(e^{-cx^2}\bigr)e^{-2\pi ixt}\,dx
=-\frac{2\pi^2}{c}t\varphi(t).
$$
Thus
$$
4\pi^2t\varphi(t)+2c\varphi'(t)=0,
$$
so
$$
\varphi(t)=K e^{-\pi^2t^2/c}.
$$
At $t=0$,
$$
K=\int_{\RR}e^{-cx^2}\,dx=\sqrt{\frac\pi c}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\widehat f_c(t)=\sqrt{\frac\pi c}\,e^{-\pi^2t^2/c}.}
$$

(b) Equality $f_c=\widehat f_c$ at $t=0$ forces $\sqrt{\pi/c}=1$, hence $c=\pi$. Conversely, for $c=\pi$ the exponents agree, since $\pi^2/c=c$. So $c=\pi$ is the unique value.

(c) By the convolution theorem and part (a),
$$
\widehat{f_a*f_b}(t)
=\widehat f_a(t)\widehat f_b(t)
=\frac\pi{\sqrt{ab}}
 e^{-\pi^2(a+b)t^2/(ab)}.
$$
Set
$$
d=\frac{ab}{a+b}.
$$
Since
$$
\widehat f_d(t)=\sqrt{\frac\pi d}\,e^{-\pi^2t^2/d}
=\sqrt{\frac{\pi(a+b)}{ab}}\,e^{-\pi^2(a+b)t^2/(ab)},
$$
comparison gives
$$
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac\pi{a+b}}.
$$
Both sides are continuous and integrable, so Fourier inversion gives
$$
\boxed{f_a*f_b=\sqrt{\frac\pi{a+b}}\,f_{ab/(a+b)}.}
$$
:::
