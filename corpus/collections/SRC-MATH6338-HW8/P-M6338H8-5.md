::: {.problem}
For $c>0$, let $f_c(x)=e^{-cx^2}$ on $\RR$.

(a) Compute $\widehat f_c$ as follows: let $\varphi=\widehat f_c$, show that $4\pi^2t\varphi(t)+2c\varphi'(t)=0$, and solve the resulting differential equation.

(b) Show that there is one and only one value of $c$ for which $f_c=\widehat f_c$.

(c) Show that $f_a*f_b=\gamma f_d$ for constants $\gamma=\gamma(a,b)$ and $d=d(a,b)$, and find them.
:::

::: {.solution}
Part (a) is the differential equation the exercise suggests: differentiating $\widehat f_c$ brings down a factor $x$, and integrating by parts turns it into a multiple of $\widehat f_c$; the solution of $\varphi'=-\frac{2\pi^2}{c}t\varphi$ is the Gaussian, normalised by the value at $t=0$. Part (b) compares values at $t=0$. Part (c) multiplies the two transforms, and the product is again of the form $\widehat f_d$, so inversion identifies the convolution. The argument is this card's own: the checkout retains no solution set for this course.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #diff}
(a) $\varphi(t)=\widehat f_c(t)$ is differentiable with $\varphi'(t)=-\frac{2\pi^2}{c}t\varphi(t)$, hence $4\pi^2t\varphi(t)+2c\varphi'(t)=0$.

::: pf-proof
$\varphi(t)=\int_\RR e^{-cx^2}e^{-2\pi ixt}\dd x$. The integrand is differentiable in $t$ with $t$-derivative $-2\pi ix\,e^{-cx^2}e^{-2\pi ixt}$, whose modulus is $2\pi\abs x e^{-cx^2}$, integrable on $\RR$; so differentiation under the integral sign is legitimate and
$$
\varphi'(t)=-2\pi i\int_\RR x e^{-cx^2}e^{-2\pi ixt}\dd x .
$$
Since $\frac{\dd}{\dd x}e^{-cx^2}=-2cx\,e^{-cx^2}$, one has $xe^{-cx^2}=-\frac1{2c}\frac{\dd}{\dd x}e^{-cx^2}$, so
$$
\varphi'(t)=\frac{\pi i}{c}\int_\RR\Bigl(\frac{\dd}{\dd x}e^{-cx^2}\Bigr)e^{-2\pi ixt}\dd x .
$$
Integration by parts moves the derivative onto the exponential: the boundary term vanishes because $e^{-cx^2}\to0$ as $\abs x\to\infty$ while $\abs{e^{-2\pi ixt}}=1$, leaving
$$
\varphi'(t)=\frac{\pi i}{c}\int_\RR e^{-cx^2}\bigl(2\pi it\,e^{-2\pi ixt}\bigr)\dd x=\frac{\pi i}{c}\cdot2\pi it\,\varphi(t)=-\frac{2\pi^2}{c}t\,\varphi(t).
$$
Multiplying by $2c$ gives the required equation.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #solve}
(a) $\varphi(t)=Ke^{-\pi^2t^2/c}$ with $K=\varphi(0)=\sqrt{\pi/c}$, so $\widehat f_c(t)=\sqrt{\pi/c}\,e^{-\pi^2t^2/c}$.

::: pf-proof
The equation of step [](#diff){.pf-ref} is linear with $\varphi$ nowhere zero on $\RR$ (its value at $t=0$ is $\int e^{-cx^2}>0$), so dividing by $\varphi$ gives $(\log\varphi)'=-\frac{2\pi^2}{c}t$, and integrating, $\log\varphi=-\frac{\pi^2}{c}t^2+\text{const}$, that is $\varphi=Ke^{-\pi^2t^2/c}$. At $t=0$ the transform is $\int_\RR e^{-cx^2}\dd x=\sqrt{\pi/c}$, the classical Gaussian integral, by the substitution $x=u/\sqrt c$, which gives $\frac1{\sqrt c}\int_\RR e^{-u^2}\dd u=\sqrt{\pi/c}$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #uniq}
(b) $f_c=\widehat f_c$ holds for exactly one $c>0$, namely $c=\pi$.

::: pf-proof
If $f_c=\widehat f_c$ then at $t=0$ one gets $1=f_c(0)=\widehat f_c(0)=\sqrt{\pi/c}$, so $c=\pi$. Conversely for $c=\pi$ the amplitude is $\sqrt{\pi/\pi}=1$ and the exponent $-\pi^2t^2/c=-\pi t^2=-ct^2$, so $\widehat f_\pi=f_\pi$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conv}
(c) $\widehat{f_a*f_b}(t)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{ab}}e^{-\pi^2(a+b)t^2/(ab)}$.

::: pf-proof
The convolution theorem gives $\widehat{f_a*f_b}=\widehat f_a\cdot\widehat f_b$ for $f_a,f_b\in L^1$, which they are for $a,b>0$. Multiplying the values of step [](#solve){.pf-ref}, $\sqrt{\pi/a}\sqrt{\pi/b}=\pi/\sqrt{ab}$ and the exponents add: $-\pi^2t^2/a-\pi^2t^2/b=-\pi^2(a+b)t^2/(ab)$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #identify}
(c) With $d=\frac{ab}{a+b}$ and $\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a+b}}$, $\widehat{f_a*f_b}=\gamma\widehat f_d$, hence $f_a*f_b=\gamma f_d$.

::: pf-proof
For $d=ab/(a+b)>0$ one computes $\sqrt{\pi/d}=\sqrt{\pi(a+b)/(ab)}$ and $\pi^2/d=\pi^2(a+b)/(ab)$, so
$$
\gamma\widehat f_d(t)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a+b}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\pi(a+b)}{ab}}\,e^{-\pi^2(a+b)t^2/(ab)}=\frac\pi{\sqrt{ab}}e^{-\pi^2(a+b)t^2/(ab)},
$$
which is step [](#conv){.pf-ref}. Both $f_a*f_b$ and $\gamma f_d$ are continuous and integrable, and their transforms agree, so they are equal by the uniqueness half of Fourier inversion.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
Parts (a), (b) and (c) hold with $\widehat f_c=\sqrt{\pi/c}\,e^{-\pi^2t^2/c}$, the unique self-dual case $c=\pi$, and $f_a*f_b=\sqrt{\pi/(a+b)}\,f_{ab/(a+b)}$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#solve){.pf-ref}, [](#uniq){.pf-ref} and [](#identify){.pf-ref} give the three parts, with step [](#diff){.pf-ref} supplying the differential equation the exercise asks to be derived.
:::
