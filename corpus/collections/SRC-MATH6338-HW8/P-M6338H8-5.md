::: problem
For $c>0$, let $f_c(x)=e^{-cx^2}$ on $\mathbb R$.

1. Compute $\widehat f_c$ by deriving and solving a differential equation for it.
2. Determine the unique $c$ for which $f_c=\widehat f_c$.
3. Find $\gamma$ and $d$ such that $f_a*f_b=\gamma f_d$ for $a,b>0$.
:::

::: solution
Let $\varphi(t)=\widehat f_c(t)$. Differentiation under the integral and integration by parts give
\[
\varphi'(t)=-2\pi i\int x e^{-cx^2}e^{-2\pi ixt}\,dx
=-\frac{2\pi^2}{c}t\varphi(t).
\]
Thus
\[
c\varphi'(t)+2\pi^2t\varphi(t)=0,
\]
so
\[
\varphi(t)=K e^{-\pi^2t^2/c}.
\]
At $t=0$,
\[
K=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-cx^2}\,dx=\sqrt{\frac\pi c}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\widehat f_c(t)=\sqrt{\frac\pi c}\,e^{-\pi^2t^2/c}.}
\]

Equality $f_c=\widehat f_c$ at $t=0$ forces $\sqrt{\pi/c}=1$, hence $c=\pi$, and this value indeed makes the exponents agree. So $c=\pi$ is unique.

Finally,
\[
\widehat{f_a*f_b}(t)
=\widehat f_a(t)\widehat f_b(t)
=\frac\pi{\sqrt{ab}}
 e^{-\pi^2(a+b)t^2/(ab)}.
\]
Set
\[
d=\frac{ab}{a+b}.
\]
Since
\[
\widehat f_d(t)=\sqrt{\frac\pi d}\,e^{-\pi^2t^2/d},
\]
comparison gives
\[
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac\pi{a+b}}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{f_a*f_b=\sqrt{\frac\pi{a+b}}\,f_{ab/(a+b)}.}
\]
:::
