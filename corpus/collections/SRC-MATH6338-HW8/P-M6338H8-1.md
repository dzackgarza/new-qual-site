::: {.problem}
If $f\in L^1(\RR^n)$, prove that its Fourier transform
$$
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\RR^n}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,dx
$$
is uniformly continuous on $\RR^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $h,y\in\RR^n$,
$$
\widehat f(y+h)-\widehat f(y)
=\int_{\RR^n}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot y}
\bigl(e^{-2\pi i x\cdot h}-1\bigr)\,dx.
$$
Hence
$$
\sup_{y\in\RR^n}|\widehat f(y+h)-\widehat f(y)|
\le
\int_{\RR^n}|f(x)|\,|e^{-2\pi i x\cdot h}-1|\,dx.
$$
For each $x$, the factor $|e^{-2\pi i x\cdot h}-1|$ tends to $0$ as $h\to0$ and is bounded by $2$. Therefore the integrand is dominated by $2|f|\in L^1$. Dominated convergence gives
$$
\int |f(x)|\,|e^{-2\pi i x\cdot h}-1|\,dx\longrightarrow0.
$$
The bound is independent of $y$, so $\widehat f$ is uniformly continuous.
:::
