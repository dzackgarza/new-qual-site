::: {.problem}
Define the prime field of a field.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $F$ be a field. Its prime field is the smallest subfield of $F$, equivalently
the intersection of all subfields of $F$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The prime field is generated by $1_F$.

::: pf-proof

Every subfield of $F$ contains $1_F$, hence contains the subring generated by
$1_F$ and therefore the smallest field containing that subring. Conversely,
that field is itself a subfield of $F$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $\operatorname{char}F=0$, the prime field is isomorphic to $\mathbb Q$.

::: pf-proof

The map
\[
\mathbb Z\to F,
\qquad
n\mapsto n1_F
\]
is injective in characteristic $0$. Its fraction field therefore embeds in
$F$, giving a copy of $\mathbb Q$, and this is the field generated by $1_F$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $\operatorname{char}F=p>0$, the prime field is isomorphic to
$\mathbb F_p$.

::: pf-proof

The same map $\mathbb Z\to F$ has kernel $p\mathbb Z$, so its image is a copy
of $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$. Since this image is already a field, it is the field
generated by $1_F$.

:::
