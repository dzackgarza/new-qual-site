::: problem
Let $p(x)\in F[x]$ be irreducible.
Suppose $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are distinct roots of $p$ in an extension field.
Must $F(\alpha)$ and $F(\beta)$ be isomorphic as extensions of $F$?
Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The answer is **yes**: $F(\alpha)$ and $F(\beta)$ are always isomorphic as field extensions of $F$.
Proof: statement of the claim.

<1>2. Proof via the Evaluation Homomorphism and First Isomorphism Theorem:
<2>1. Define the evaluation homomorphism $\operatorname{ev}_\alpha: F[x] \to F(\alpha)$ by $\operatorname{ev}_\alpha(f(x)) = f(\alpha)$.
Proof: universal property of polynomial rings.
<2>2. The map $\operatorname{ev}_\alpha$ is a surjective ring homomorphism fixing the base field $F$ pointwise.
Proof: $F(\alpha) = \{g(\alpha)/h(\alpha) : g, h \in F[x], h(\alpha) \neq 0\} = \{f(\alpha) : f \in F[x]\}$ since $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$.
<2>3. Since $p(x) \in F[x]$ is irreducible and $p(\alpha) = 0$, $p(x)$ (up to a non-zero scaling factor in $F^\times$) is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$.
Thus $\ker(\operatorname{ev}_\alpha) = (p(x))$ is the principal ideal generated by $p(x)$.
Proof: $F[x]$ is a PID and $p$ is irreducible.
<2>4. By the First Isomorphism Theorem for rings, $\operatorname{ev}_\alpha$ induces an $F$-algebra isomorphism:
\[
\Phi_\alpha: F[x] / (p(x)) \xrightarrow{\sim} F(\alpha), \quad x + (p(x)) \mapsto \alpha, \quad c + (p(x)) \mapsto c \text{ for } c \in F.
\]
Proof: First Isomorphism Theorem for rings.
<2>5. Symmetrically, since $p(\beta) = 0$ and $p(x)$ is irreducible, $p(x)$ is also the minimal polynomial of $\beta$ over $F$, giving $\ker(\operatorname{ev}_\beta) = (p(x))$.
Thus the evaluation map $\operatorname{ev}_\beta$ induces an $F$-algebra isomorphism:
\[
\Phi_\beta: F[x] / (p(x)) \xrightarrow{\sim} F(\beta), \quad x + (p(x)) \mapsto \beta, \quad c + (p(x)) \mapsto c \text{ for } c \in F.
\]
Proof: First Isomorphism Theorem for rings applied to $\beta$.
<2>6. Define $\sigma = \Phi_\beta \circ \Phi_\alpha^{-1}: F(\alpha) \xrightarrow{\sim} F(\beta)$.
Proof: composition of isomorphisms is an isomorphism.
<2>7. For any element $\sum_{i=0}^{d-1} c_i \alpha^i \in F(\alpha)$ ($c_i \in F$):
\[
\sigma\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1} c_i \alpha^i\right) = \sum_{i=0}^{d-1} c_i \beta^i.
\]
In particular, $\sigma(c) = c$ for all $c \in F$, and $\sigma(\alpha) = \beta$.
Proof: definition of $\Phi_\alpha$ and $\Phi_\beta$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
$\sigma: F(\alpha) \to F(\beta)$ is an isomorphism of field extensions fixing $F$. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.
:::
