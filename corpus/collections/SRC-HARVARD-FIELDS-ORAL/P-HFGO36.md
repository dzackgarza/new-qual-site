::: {.problem}
Prove that any two fields with $27 = 3^3$ elements are **isomorphic**:
$$F_1 \cong F_2 \cong \mathbb{F}_{27}.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $F$ be a field with $27$ elements.

<1>1. $\operatorname{char}F=3$, and the prime subfield of $F$ is
$\mathbb F_3=\ZZ/3\ZZ$.

::: {.proof}
The additive order of $1$ is a prime dividing $\abs{F}=27$, hence $3$. The
subfield generated by $1$ is then the image of $\ZZ\to F$, which is
$\ZZ/3\ZZ$.
:::

<1>2. $x^{27}-x=\prod_{\alpha\in F}(x-\alpha)$ in $F[x]$.

::: {.proof}
The group $F^\times$ has order $26$, so by Lagrange's theorem
$\alpha^{26}=1$, hence $\alpha^{27}=\alpha$, for every $\alpha\ne0$; also
$0^{27}=0$. Thus the $27$ elements of $F$ are distinct roots of the monic
polynomial $x^{27}-x$ of degree $27$, which is therefore their product of
linear factors.
:::

<1>3. $F$ is a splitting field of $x^{27}-x$ over $\mathbb F_3$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2 the polynomial splits in $F$, and its roots are all of $F$, so
they generate $F$ over $\mathbb F_3$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, two fields $F_1,F_2$ with $27$ elements are splitting fields of
$x^{27}-x$ over $\mathbb F_3$. Splitting fields of a polynomial over a field
are unique up to isomorphism over that field, so $F_1\cong F_2$; calling this
field $\mathbb F_{27}$ gives $F_1\cong F_2\cong\mathbb F_{27}$.
:::
:::
