::: {.problem}
Is every extension of $\mathbb Q$ of degree $2$ normal?
Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Yes, every degree-$2$ extension of $\QQ$ is normal.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Let $K/\QQ$ have degree $2$, so $K = \QQ(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha \in K \setminus \QQ$.

::: pf-proof

any finite extension of a field of characteristic $0$ is simple (primitive element theorem).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The minimal polynomial $m_\alpha$ of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ has degree $2$.

::: pf-proof

$[\QQ(\alpha) : \QQ] = 2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$m_\alpha$ has both of its roots in $K$.

::: pf-proof

$m_\alpha$ is quadratic; one root is $\alpha \in K$, and the other root is $\frac{-b}{a} - \alpha$ (from $m_\alpha(X) = aX^2 + bX + c$), which lies in $K$ since $K$ is a field containing $\alpha$ and the coefficients.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $K$ is the splitting field of $m_\alpha$ over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

$K$ contains both roots of $m_\alpha$ and is generated by one of them.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $K/\QQ$ is normal.

::: pf-proof

a finite extension is normal iff it is the splitting field of some polynomial.

:::
