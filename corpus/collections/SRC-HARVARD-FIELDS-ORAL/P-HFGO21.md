::: problem
Explain the relation between finite normal extensions and splitting fields.
:::

::: solution
Let $E/F$ be a finite extension.

<1>1. If $E$ is the splitting field over $F$ of a polynomial in $F[x]$, then
$E/F$ is normal.
::: proof
Let $E$ be the splitting field of $f(x)\in F[x]$. Any $F$-embedding
\[
\sigma:E\hookrightarrow\overline F
\]
sends each root of $f$ to another root of $f$, because
\[
f(\sigma(\alpha))=\sigma(f(\alpha))=0.
\]
Since $E$ is generated by the roots of $f$, the image $\sigma(E)$ is again the
field generated by those roots, hence equals $E$. Thus every $F$-embedding of
$E$ into an algebraic closure has image $E$, which is the embedding criterion
for normality.
:::

<1>2. Conversely, every finite normal extension $E/F$ is the splitting field of
some polynomial in $F[x]$.
::: proof
Choose finitely many generators
\[
E=F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r).
\]
Let $m_i(x)$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha_i$ over $F$, and put
\[
f(x)=\prod_{i=1}^r m_i(x).
\]
Because $E/F$ is normal, every irreducible polynomial over $F$ having one root
in $E$ splits completely in $E$. Hence every $m_i$ splits in $E$, so $f$ splits
in $E$.

Any splitting field of $f$ contains every $\alpha_i$, hence contains
$E=F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)$. Therefore $E$ itself is the splitting field of
$f$.
:::

Thus finite normal extensions are exactly finite splitting-field extensions.
:::
