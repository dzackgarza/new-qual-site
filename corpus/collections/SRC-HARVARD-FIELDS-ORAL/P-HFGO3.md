::: problem
If $\alpha$ is algebraic over $\mathbb Q$, prove that $\mathbb Q[\alpha]$ is a field.
:::

::: solution
Let $m_\alpha(x)\in\mathbb Q[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$.

<1>1. Evaluation at $\alpha$ induces an isomorphism
\[
\mathbb Q[x]/(m_\alpha)\cong \mathbb Q[\alpha].
\]
::: proof
The evaluation homomorphism
\[
\operatorname{ev}_\alpha:\mathbb Q[x]\to\mathbb Q[\alpha],
\qquad f\mapsto f(\alpha),
\]
is surjective by definition of $\mathbb Q[\alpha]$. Its kernel consists exactly
of the polynomials vanishing at $\alpha$, which is the principal ideal
$(m_\alpha)$. The first isomorphism theorem gives the displayed isomorphism.
:::

<1>2. The ideal $(m_\alpha)$ is maximal in $\mathbb Q[x]$.
::: proof
The minimal polynomial $m_\alpha$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. Since
$\mathbb Q[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, an ideal generated by an
irreducible element is maximal.
:::

<1>3. Therefore $\mathbb Q[\alpha]$ is a field.
::: proof
By <1>2, the quotient $\mathbb Q[x]/(m_\alpha)$ is a field. Apply the
isomorphism in <1>1.
:::
:::
