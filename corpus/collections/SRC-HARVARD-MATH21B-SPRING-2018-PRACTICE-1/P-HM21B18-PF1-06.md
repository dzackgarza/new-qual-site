::: {.problem}
Let
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\binom{x(0)}{y(0)}=\binom{2000}{0}.
$$
(a) Find a closed form for the discrete system $v(t+1)=Av(t)$. (b) Find a closed form for the continuous system $v'(t)=Av(t)$. (c) Determine in which model $x(t)$ grows faster.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
u_+=\binom11,
\qquad
u_-=\binom{-1}{1}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The vectors $u_+$ and $u_-$ are eigenvectors of $A$ with
eigenvalues $3$ and $1$, respectively.


::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives
$$
Au_+
=
\binom33
=3u_+,
\qquad
Au_-
=
\binom{-1}{1}
=u_-.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The initial vector decomposes as
$$
v(0)=1000u_+-1000u_-.
$$


::: pf-proof

Indeed,
$$
1000\binom11-1000\binom{-1}{1}
=
\binom{2000}{0}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For the discrete system,
$$
v(t)
=1000\,3^t u_+-1000\,u_-.
$$


::: pf-proof

The recurrence gives $v(t)=A^tv(0)$ for integers $t\geq0$. Apply $A^t$
to the decomposition in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and use the eigenvalue equations from
step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}:
$$
A^tu_+=3^tu_+,
\qquad
A^tu_-=u_-.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Hence the discrete closed form is
$$
\boxed{
x(t)=1000(3^t+1),\qquad
y(t)=1000(3^t-1),\qquad t\in\ZZ_{\geq0}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand the vector formula in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} coordinatewise.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For the continuous system,
$$
v(t)
=1000e^{3t}u_+-1000e^t u_-.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $v'=Av$, an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\lambda$ evolves as
$e^{\lambda t}$ times that eigenvector. Applying this to the decomposition
in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and the two eigenpairs in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence the continuous closed form is
$$
\boxed{
x(t)=1000(e^{3t}+e^t),\qquad
y(t)=1000(e^{3t}-e^t),\qquad t\geq0.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand the vector formula in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} coordinatewise.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The continuous model has the faster-growing $x(t)$.


::: pf-proof

The dominant term in the discrete solution is
$$
1000\,3^t=1000e^{(\log3)t},
$$
whereas the dominant term in the continuous solution is $1000e^{3t}$.
Since $3>\log3$, one has
$$
\frac{e^{3t}}{3^t}
=e^{(3-\log3)t}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$
Thus the continuous $x(t)$ grows asymptotically faster.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} answer parts (a), (b), and (c),
respectively.

:::
