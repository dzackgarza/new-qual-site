::: {.problem}
On $[-\pi,\pi]$, let
$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&0<x\le1,\\
-1,&-1\le x\le0,\\
0,&|x|>1.
\end{cases}
$$
Use the course convention
$$
f(x)=\frac{a_0}{\sqrt2}
+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\bigl(a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\bigr).
$$
(a) Find the Fourier series of $f$. (b) Find the sum of the squares of all
Fourier coefficients.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The function $f$ is odd, so
$$
a_0=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a_n=0
\quad(n\geq1).
$$


::: pf-proof

For the constant coefficient, the integrand $f(x)$ is odd. For each
$n\geq1$, the product $f(x)\cos(nx)$ is also odd because $\cos(nx)$ is
even. Their integrals over $[-\pi,\pi]$ therefore vanish.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $n\geq1$,
$$
b_n=\frac{2(1-\cos n)}{\pi n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since both $f$ and $\sin(nx)$ are odd, their product is even. Moreover
$f(x)=1$ for $0<x\leq1$ and vanishes for $1<x\leq\pi$. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n
&=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac2\pi\int_0^1\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac2\pi\left[\frac{-\cos(nx)}n\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac{2(1-\cos n)}{\pi n}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Fourier series is
$$
\boxed{
f(x)\sim
\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\frac{2(1-\cos n)}{\pi n}\sin(nx).}
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute the coefficients from steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} into the stated
Fourier convention.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The sum of the squares of all Fourier coefficients is
$$
\boxed{\frac2\pi}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Parseval's identity for the stated normalization gives
$$
a_0^2+\sum_{n=1}^\infty(a_n^2+b_n^2)
=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx.
$$
The function has $|f|=1$ on $[-1,1]$ except at the single point $0$, and
$f=0$ elsewhere on $[-\pi,\pi]$. Therefore
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac1\pi\int_{-1}^{1}1\,dx
=\frac2\pi.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives part (a), and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives part (b).

:::
