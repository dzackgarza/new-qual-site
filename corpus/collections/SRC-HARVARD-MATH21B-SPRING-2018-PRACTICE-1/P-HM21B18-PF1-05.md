::: {.problem}
Using least squares, find
$$
ax^2+by^2=1
$$
that best fits
$$
(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(-1,-1).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

The four data points give the least-squares system
$$
M\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
\approx
\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1\\
1&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substituting the four points into $ax^2+by^2=1$ gives, respectively,
$$
a=1,\qquad b=1,\qquad a+b=1,\qquad a+b=1.
$$
These are exactly the four rows of the displayed system.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The normal equations are
$$
\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&3\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}3\\3\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives
$$
M^TM=
\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M^T
\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}3\\3\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The least-squares minimizer satisfies
$M^TM(a,b)^T=M^T(1,1,1,1)^T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The unique least-squares parameters are
$$
a=b=\frac35.
$$


::: pf-proof

Subtracting the two equations in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives $a=b$. Substitution then
gives $5a=3$, hence $a=b=3/5$. The normal-equation matrix has determinant
$9-4=5\neq0$, so the minimizer is unique.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore the best-fitting curve is
$$
\boxed{\frac35x^2+\frac35y^2=1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute the coefficients from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} into the model.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the required least-squares fit.

:::
