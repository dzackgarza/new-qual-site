::: {.problem}
Assume $2\pi$-periodic boundary conditions in the spatial variable $x$.

(a) Solve
$$
u_t=u_{xx}-u_{xxxx}+t
$$
with
$$
u(x,0)=4\sin x+5\sin(2x).
$$

(b) Solve
$$
u_{tt}=u_{xx}-u_{xxxx}+t
$$
with
$$
u(x,0)=7\sin(5x),
\qquad
u_t(x,0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}\sin(nx).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $n\geq1$,
$$
(\partial_x^2-\partial_x^4)\sin(nx)
=-(n^2+n^4)\sin(nx).
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
\partial_x^2\sin(nx)=-n^2\sin(nx)
$$
and
$$
\partial_x^4\sin(nx)=n^4\sin(nx).
$$
Subtracting gives the displayed eigenvalue equation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The homogeneous heat evolution of the initial position is
$$
u_h(x,t)
=4e^{-2t}\sin x+5e^{-20t}\sin(2x).
$$


::: pf-proof

For a mode $a_n(t)\sin(nx)$, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} reduces the homogeneous heat
equation to
$$
a_n'=-(n^2+n^4)a_n,
$$
so
$$
a_n(t)=a_n(0)e^{-(n^2+n^4)t}.
$$
For $n=1$ the rate is $1+1=2$, and for $n=2$ it is $4+16=20$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

A particular solution for the forcing term $t$ in part (a) is
$$
u_p(t)=\frac{t^2}{2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This function is independent of $x$, so its spatial derivatives vanish,
while
$$
\partial_tu_p=t.
$$
Also $u_p(0)=0$, so adding it does not change the prescribed initial
position.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The solution of part (a) is
$$
\boxed{
u(x,t)=4e^{-2t}\sin x+5e^{-20t}\sin(2x)+\frac{t^2}{2}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The differential operator is linear. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} solves the homogeneous
equation with the prescribed initial position, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} supplies a
zero-initial-position particular solution for the forcing $t$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For the homogeneous wave equation, the $n$th sine-mode amplitude
satisfies
$$
q_n''+\omega_n^2q_n=0,
\qquad
\omega_n=\sqrt{n^2+n^4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substituting $q_n(t)\sin(nx)$ into
$u_{tt}=u_{xx}-u_{xxxx}$ and using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
q_n''=-(n^2+n^4)q_n,
$$
which is the displayed oscillator equation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The part of the wave solution determined by the initial position is
$$
u_{\mathrm{pos}}(x,t)
=7\cos(\sqrt{650}\,t)\sin(5x).
$$


::: pf-proof

For $n=5$,
$$
\omega_5
=\sqrt{5^2+5^4}
=\sqrt{25+625}
=\sqrt{650}.
$$
The amplitude with initial value $7$ and initial derivative $0$ is
$7\cos(\sqrt{650}\,t)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The part determined by the initial velocity is
$$
u_{\mathrm{vel}}(x,t)
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin(\sqrt{n^2+n^4}\,t)}
{n^2\sqrt{n^2+n^4}}\sin(nx).
$$


::: pf-proof

The prescribed initial velocity coefficient of $\sin(nx)$ is $1/n^2$.
For the oscillator in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} with zero initial position and initial
velocity $1/n^2$, the amplitude is
$$
q_n(t)
=\frac{1}{n^2\omega_n}\sin(\omega_nt).
$$
Substituting $\omega_n=\sqrt{n^2+n^4}$ gives the displayed series.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

A particular solution for the forcing term $t$ in part (b) is
$$
u_{\mathrm{force}}(t)=\frac{t^3}{6}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This function is independent of $x$ and satisfies
$$
u_{\mathrm{force},tt}=t.
$$
Moreover
$$
u_{\mathrm{force}}(0)=0,
\qquad
\partial_tu_{\mathrm{force}}(0)=0,
$$
so it preserves both prescribed initial data.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The solution of part (b) is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u(x,t)
={}&7\cos(\sqrt{650}\,t)\sin(5x)\\
&+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin(\sqrt{n^2+n^4}\,t)}
{n^2\sqrt{n^2+n^4}}\sin(nx)
+\frac{t^3}{6}.
\end{aligned}}
$$


::: pf-proof

The operator
$$
L[u]=u_{tt}-u_{xx}+u_{xxxx}
$$
is linear. Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} solve $L[u]=0$ with, respectively, the
prescribed initial position and prescribed initial velocity. Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}
solves $L[u]=t$ with zero initial position and velocity. Their sum
therefore solves the forced equation and both initial conditions.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives part (a), and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives part (b).
