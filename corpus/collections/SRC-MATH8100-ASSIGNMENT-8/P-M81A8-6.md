::: {.problem}
For $1\leq p\leq\infty$, define $\Lambda_p(f)=\int_0^1x^2f(x)\,dx$ on $L^p([0,1])$.
Prove continuity and compute $\norm{\Lambda_p}$ in terms of $p$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
$\abs{\Lambda_p(f)}\le \int_0^1 x^2\abs{f} \le \norm{x^2}_q\norm{f}_p$ by Hölder, $1/p+1/q=1$.

::: pf-proof
Hölder.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #Lambda-continuous-bound}
Hence $\Lambda_p$ is continuous and $\norm{\Lambda_p}\le\norm{x^2}_q$.

::: pf-proof
For $1< p\le\infty$, with conjugate exponent $q<\infty$,
$$
\norm{x^2}_q=\left(\int_0^1x^{2q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}=(2q+1)^{-1/q}.
$$
For $p=1$ one has $q=\infty$ and $\norm{x^2}_\infty=1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
For $1<p<\infty$, equality is attained by the normalized Holder extremizer.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
f(x)=\frac{x^{2(q-1)}}{\norm{x^{2(q-1)}}_p}.
$$
Then $\norm{f}_p=1$, and equality holds in Holder because
$$
\abs{f}^p\propto (x^2)^q.
$$
Hence
$$
\norm{\Lambda_p}=\norm{x^2}_q=(2q+1)^{-1/q}.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The endpoint norms are $\norm{\Lambda_1}=1$ and $\norm{\Lambda_\infty}=1/3$.

::: pf-proof
For $p=\infty$, the upper bound gives
$$
\norm{\Lambda_\infty}\le\int_0^1x^2\,dx=\frac13,
$$
and equality holds at $f\equiv1$.

For $p=1$, Holder gives $\norm{\Lambda_1}\le1$. Define
$$
f_n=n\mathbf1_{[1-1/n,1]}.
$$
Then $\norm{f_n}_1=1$ and
$$
\Lambda_1(f_n)
=n\int_{1-1/n}^1x^2\,dx
\longrightarrow1.
$$
Thus $\norm{\Lambda_1}\ge1$, so $\norm{\Lambda_1}=1$.
:::
