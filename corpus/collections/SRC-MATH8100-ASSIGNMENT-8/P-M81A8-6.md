::: {.problem}
For $1\leq p\leq\infty$, define $\Lambda_p(f)=\int_0^1x^2f(x)\,dx$ on $L^p([0,1])$.
Prove continuity and compute $\|\Lambda_p\|$ in terms of $p$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $|\Lambda_p(f)|\le \int_0^1 x^2|f| \le \|x^2\|_q\|f\|_p$ by Hölder, $1/p+1/q=1$.
::: {.proof}
Hölder.
:::

<1>2. Hence $\Lambda_p$ is continuous and $\|\Lambda_p\|\le\|x^2\|_q$.
::: {.proof}
For $1< p\le\infty$, with conjugate exponent $q<\infty$,
\[
\|x^2\|_q=\left(\int_0^1x^{2q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}=(2q+1)^{-1/q}.
\]
For $p=1$ one has $q=\infty$ and $\|x^2\|_\infty=1$.
:::

<1>3. For $1<p<\infty$, equality is attained by the normalized Holder extremizer.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
f(x)=\frac{x^{2(q-1)}}{\|x^{2(q-1)}\|_p}.
\]
Then $\|f\|_p=1$, and equality holds in Holder because
\[
|f|^p\propto (x^2)^q.
\]
Hence
\[
\|\Lambda_p\|=\|x^2\|_q=(2q+1)^{-1/q}.
\]
:::

<1>4. The endpoint norms are $\|\Lambda_1\|=1$ and $\|\Lambda_\infty\|=1/3$.
::: {.proof}
For $p=\infty$, the upper bound gives
\[
\|\Lambda_\infty\|\le\int_0^1x^2\,dx=\frac13,
\]
and equality holds at $f\equiv1$.

For $p=1$, Holder gives $\|\Lambda_1\|\le1$. Define
\[
f_n=n\mathbf1_{[1-1/n,1]}.
\]
Then $\|f_n\|_1=1$ and
\[
\Lambda_1(f_n)
=n\int_{1-1/n}^1x^2\,dx
\longrightarrow1.
\]
Thus $\|\Lambda_1\|\ge1$, so $\|\Lambda_1\|=1$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.
