::: {.problem}
Let $H$ be a Hilbert space with orthonormal basis $(u_n)_{n\ge1}$.

(a) Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence of complex numbers. Prove that $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nu_n$ converges in $H$ if and only if $\sum_{n=1}^\infty\abs{a_n}^2<\infty$, and that in that case
$$
\norm{\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nu_n}=\Bigl(\sum_{n=1}^\infty\abs{a_n}^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
$$

(b) (i) Is there a continuous linear functional $L$ on $H$ with $L(u_n)=n^{-1}$ for all $n$? If so, find its norm.

(ii) Is there a continuous linear functional $L$ on $H$ with $L(u_n)=n^{-1/2}$ for all $n$? If so, find its norm.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
Convergence of $\sum a_n u_n$ and its norm:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Let $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^N a_n u_n$ be the partial sums in the Hilbert space $H$.
For any $M > N$, by the Pythagorean identity for orthonormal vectors:
$$
\norm{S_M - S_N}^2 = \norm{ \sum_{n=N+1}^M a_n u_n }^2 = \sum_{n=N+1}^M \abs{a_n}^2.
$$
:::

::: pf-step
The sequence $(S_N)_{N=1}^\infty$ is Cauchy in $H$ if and only if the sequence of real partial sums $\sum_{n=1}^N \abs{a_n}^2$ is Cauchy in $\RR$, which is equivalent to $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \abs{a_n}^2 < \infty$.
Since $H$ is a Hilbert space (complete), $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n u_n$ converges in $H$ if and only if $(a_n)_{n=1}^\infty \in \ell^2$.
:::

::: pf-step
By continuity of the inner product and norm, for $x = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n u_n$:
$$
\norm{x}^2 = \langle x, x \rangle = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \abs{a_n}^2 \implies \norm{x} = \left( \sum_{n=1}^\infty \abs{a_n}^2 \right)^{1/2} = \norm{(a_n)}_{\ell^2}.
$$
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Existence and norm of continuous linear functionals via the Riesz Representation Theorem:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
By the Riesz Representation Theorem, every continuous linear functional $L \in H^*$ has the form $L(x) = \langle x, y \rangle$ for a unique vector $y \in H$, and $\norm{L}_{H^*} = \norm{y}_H$.
For the orthonormal basis $(u_n)$, $\langle u_n, y \rangle = \overline{\langle y, u_n \rangle} = L(u_n) = c_n$, so $y = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \overline{c_n} u_n$.
Such a vector $y \in H$ exists if and only if $(c_n)_{n=1}^\infty \in \ell^2$.
:::

::: pf-step
**For $L(u_n) = n^{-1}$:**
Compute the sum:
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty \abs{c_n}^2 = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6} < \infty.
$$
Since $(n^{-1}) \in \ell^2$, a continuous linear functional exists, with norm:
$$
\norm{L} = \sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi^2}{6}} = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}}.
$$
:::

::: pf-step
**For $L(u_n) = n^{-1/2}$:**
Compute the sum:
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty \abs{c_n}^2 = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n} = \infty.
$$
Since $(n^{-1/2}) \notin \ell^2$, no such continuous linear functional exists.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Conclusion:
$\sum a_n u_n$ converges iff $(a_n) \in \ell^2$ with norm $\norm{(a_n)}_{\ell^2}$; $L(u_n) = n^{-1}$ exists with norm $\pi / \sqrt{6}$, while $L(u_n) = n^{-1/2}$ does not exist.
:::
