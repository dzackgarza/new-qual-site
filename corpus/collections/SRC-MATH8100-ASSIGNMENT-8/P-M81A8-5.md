::: problem
Let $(u_n)$ be an orthonormal basis of a Hilbert space.
Characterize convergence of $\sum a_nu_n$ and compute its norm.
Determine whether continuous linear functionals can satisfy $L(u_n)=n^{-1}$ or $L(u_n)=n^{-1/2}$, and compute the norm when one exists.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Convergence of $\sum a_n u_n$ and its norm:
<2>1. Let $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^N a_n u_n$ be the partial sums in the Hilbert space $H$.
For any $M > N$, by the Pythagorean identity for orthonormal vectors:
\[
\|S_M - S_N\|^2 = \left\| \sum_{n=N+1}^M a_n u_n \right\|^2 = \sum_{n=N+1}^M |a_n|^2.
\]
<2>2. The sequence $(S_N)_{N=1}^\infty$ is Cauchy in $H$ if and only if the sequence of real partial sums $\sum_{n=1}^N |a_n|^2$ is Cauchy in $\mathbb{R}$, which is equivalent to $\sum_{n=1}^\infty |a_n|^2 < \infty$.
Since $H$ is a Hilbert space (complete), $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n u_n$ converges in $H$ if and only if $(a_n)_{n=1}^\infty \in \ell^2$.
<2>3. By continuity of the inner product and norm, for $x = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n u_n$:
\[
\|x\|^2 = \langle x, x \rangle = \sum_{n=1}^\infty |a_n|^2 \implies \|x\| = \left( \sum_{n=1}^\infty |a_n|^2 \right)^{1/2} = \|(a_n)\|_{\ell^2}.
\]

<1>2. Existence and norm of continuous linear functionals via the Riesz Representation Theorem:
<2>1. By the Riesz Representation Theorem, every continuous linear functional $L \in H^*$ has the form $L(x) = \langle x, y \rangle$ for a unique vector $y \in H$, and $\|L\|_{H^*} = \|y\|_H$.
For the orthonormal basis $(u_n)$, $\langle u_n, y \rangle = \overline{\langle y, u_n \rangle} = L(u_n) = c_n$, so $y = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \overline{c_n} u_n$.
Such a vector $y \in H$ exists if and only if $(c_n)_{n=1}^\infty \in \ell^2$.
<2>2. **For $L(u_n) = n^{-1}$:**
Compute the sum:
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty |c_n|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6} < \infty.
\]
Since $(n^{-1}) \in \ell^2$, a continuous linear functional exists, with norm:
\[
\|L\| = \sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi^2}{6}} = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}}.
\]
<2>3. **For $L(u_n) = n^{-1/2}$:**
Compute the sum:
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty |c_n|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n} = \infty.
\]
Since $(n^{-1/2}) \notin \ell^2$, no such continuous linear functional exists.

<1>3. Conclusion:
$\sum a_n u_n$ converges iff $(a_n) \in \ell^2$ with norm $\|(a_n)\|_{\ell^2}$; $L(u_n) = n^{-1}$ exists with norm $\pi / \sqrt{6}$, while $L(u_n) = n^{-1/2}$ does not exist. Q.E.D.
:::
