::: problem
Prove that $C([0,1])$ is complete under the uniform norm and is not complete under the $L^1$ norm.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. $C([0,1])$ is complete in the uniform norm.
::: proof
Let $(f_n)$ be Cauchy in
\[
\|f\|_\infty=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f(x)|.
\]
For each fixed $x$, the scalar sequence $(f_n(x))$ is Cauchy, so define
\[
f(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(x).
\]

Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ such that
\[
\|f_n-f_m\|_\infty<\varepsilon
\qquad(m,n\ge N).
\]
Fix $n\ge N$ and let $m\to\infty$. For every $x$,
\[
|f_n(x)-f(x)|\le\varepsilon.
\]
Taking the supremum gives
\[
\|f_n-f\|_\infty\le\varepsilon.
\]
Thus $f_n\to f$ uniformly. A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, so
\[
f\in C([0,1]).
\]
Hence $C([0,1])$ is complete in the uniform norm.
:::

<1>2. $C([0,1])$ is not complete in the $L^1$ norm.
::: proof
Let
\[
f=\mathbf1_{[1/2,1]}.
\]
For each $n\ge2$, define $f_n\in C([0,1])$ by
\[
f_n(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\le x\le \frac12-\frac1n,\\
\frac n2\left(x-\frac12+\frac1n\right),
&\frac12-\frac1n<x<\frac12+\frac1n,\\
1,&\frac12+\frac1n\le x\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
Then $f_n$ differs from $f$ only on an interval of length $2/n$, and $|f_n-f|\le1$. Therefore
\[
\|f_n-f\|_1\le\frac2n\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $(f_n)$ is Cauchy in the $L^1$ norm.

Suppose it converged in $L^1$ to some $g\in C([0,1])$. Uniqueness of $L^1$ limits would imply
\[
g=f\quad\text{a.e.}
\]
But no continuous function equals $\mathbf1_{[1/2,1]}$ almost everywhere: continuity and a.e. equality force $g=0$ on the open interval $(0,1/2)$ and $g=1$ on $(1/2,1)$, contradicting continuity at $1/2$.

Therefore this $L^1$-Cauchy sequence has no limit in $C([0,1])$, so $C([0,1])$ is not complete under $\|\cdot\|_1$.
:::
:::
