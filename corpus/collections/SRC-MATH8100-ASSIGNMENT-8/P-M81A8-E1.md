::: problem
For nonnegative measurable $f,g$ on $[0,\infty)$, prove the stated bound
$$
\int_0^\infty\left(\int_0^xf(y)\,dy\right)\frac{g(x)}x\,dx\leq
\left(\int_0^\infty f(y)y^{-1/2}\,dy\right)\|g\|_2.
$$
:::

::: solution
Because $f,g\ge0$, Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
I
&:=\int_0^\infty
\left(\int_0^x f(y)\,dy\right)\frac{g(x)}x\,dx\\
&=\int_0^\infty
f(y)\left(\int_y^\infty\frac{g(x)}x\,dx\right)dy.
\end{aligned}
\]

For each $y>0$, Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_y^\infty\frac{g(x)}x\,dx
&\le
\left(\int_y^\infty |g(x)|^2\,dx\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_y^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le \|g\|_2\,y^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
I
&\le \|g\|_2\int_0^\infty f(y)y^{-1/2}\,dy\\
&=\left(\int_0^\infty f(y)y^{-1/2}\,dy\right)\|g\|_2,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is the desired estimate.
:::
