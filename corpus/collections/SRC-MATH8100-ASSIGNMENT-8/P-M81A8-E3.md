::: {.problem}
Let $1\leq p\leq\infty$ and suppose $\sum_k\norm{f_k}_p<\infty$.
Prove that $\sum_kf_k$ converges almost everywhere to an $L^p$ function and that $\norm{\sum_kf_k}_p\leq\sum_k\norm{f_k}_p$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
M=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\norm{f_k}_p<\infty.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step
Suppose $1\le p<\infty$.

::: pf-proof
For $N\ge1$, define
$$
G_N=\sum_{k=1}^N\abs{f_k}.
$$
By Minkowski's inequality,
$$
\norm{G_N}_p
\le\sum_{k=1}^N\norm{f_k}_p
\le M.
$$
Since $G_N\uparrow G:=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\abs{f_k}$ pointwise, monotone convergence applied to $G_N^p$ gives
$$
\norm{G}_p^p
=\int G^p
=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int G_N^p
\le M^p.
$$
Thus $G\in L^p$, so
$$
G(x)<\infty
$$
for almost every $x$. At each such $x$ the numerical series
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k(x)
$$
converges absolutely. Let its sum be $f(x)$ on this full-measure set, and define $f=0$ on the remaining null set. Then
$$
\abs{f}\le G
$$
almost everywhere, hence $f\in L^p$ and
$$
\norm{f}_p\le\norm{G}_p\le M.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\norm{\sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k}_p
\le\sum_{k=1}^\infty\norm{f_k}_p.}
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Suppose $p=\infty$.

::: pf-proof
Choose representatives so that for each $k$,
$$
\abs{f_k(x)}\le\norm{f_k}_\infty
$$
outside a null set $N_k$. The union
$$
N=\bigcup_{k=1}^\infty N_k
$$
is null. For $x\notin N$,
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty\abs{f_k(x)}
\le\sum_{k=1}^\infty\norm{f_k}_\infty
=M.
$$
Hence the series converges absolutely for every $x\notin N$ to a function $f$, and
$$
\abs{f(x)}\le M
$$
there. Thus $f\in L^\infty$ and
$$
\boxed{
\norm{f}_\infty
\le\sum_{k=1}^\infty\norm{f_k}_\infty.}
$$
:::
