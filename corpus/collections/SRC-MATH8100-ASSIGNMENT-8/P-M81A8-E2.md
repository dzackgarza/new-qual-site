::: problem
Let $\|f_k\|_2\leq1$ on $[0,1]$.
Analyze almost-everywhere and $L^1$ convergence: prove the limit lies in the closed $L^2$ unit ball, decide whether either hypothesis gives $L^2$ convergence, and prove that almost-everywhere convergence gives $L^1$ convergence.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Use the $L^2$ bound to control the limit and detect sharpness of convergence conclusions.

<1> Assume $f_k\to f$ almost everywhere.
    *Proof:* Fatou gives
    $$
    \int_0^1 |f|^2
    =\int_0^1 \liminf_{k\to\infty}|f_k|^2
    \le \liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_0^1 |f_k|^2
    \le 1.
    $$
    So $f\in L^2([0,1])$ and $\|f\|_2\le 1$.

<1> Neither hypothesis forces $L^2$ convergence.
    <2>1. Let $f_k=\sqrt{k}\,\mathbf 1_{(0,1/k]}$.
        Then $\|f_k\|_2=1$ for all $k$, $f_k\to0$ a.e., but $\|f_k-0\|_2=1\nrightarrow0$.
        So a.e. convergence does not imply $L^2$ convergence.
    <2>2. Let the same sequence be used to show $f_k\to0$ in $L^1$:
        $$
        \|f_k\|_1=\int_0^{1/k}\sqrt{k}\,dx=k^{-1/2}\to0.
        $$
        Yet $\|f_k\|_2\equiv1$, so $L^1$ convergence does not imply $L^2$ convergence either.

<1> Assume $f_k\to f$ almost everywhere.
    Let $g_k=f_k-f$.
    We have $\|g_k\|_2\le \|f_k\|_2+\|f\|_2\le2$ and $g_k\to0$ a.e.
    For $R>0$, split
    $$[0,1]=A_k^R\cup B_k^R,\qquad A_k^R=\{|g_k|\le R\},\ B_k^R=\{|g_k|>R\}.$$
    <2>1. By dominated convergence on $A_k^R$ (since $|g_k|\le R$ on it),
        \[
        \int_{A_k^R}|g_k|\to0 \quad \text{as }k\to\infty.
        \]
    <2>2. Chebyshev gives
        $$
        \mu(B_k^R)\le \frac{\|g_k\|_2^2}{R^2}\le\frac{4}{R^2}.
        $$
        So by Cauchy--Schwarz,
        $$
        \int_{B_k^R}|g_k|
        \le \|g_k\|_2\,\mu(B_k^R)^{1/2}
        \le \frac{2}{R}.
        $$
        This bound is uniform in $k$.
    <2>3. Given $\epsilon>0$, choose $R$ with $2/R<\epsilon/2$.
        Then choose $k$ large with $\int_{A_k^R}|g_k|<\epsilon/2$.
        Then $\|g_k\|_1<\epsilon$.
    <2>4. Hence $\|f_k-f\|_1\to0$. So a.e. convergence plus the $L^2$ bound implies $L^1$ convergence.

:::
