::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field and let $X$ be a finite set. Let
\[
R(X,F)=\{f:X\to F\}
\]
with pointwise addition and multiplication. Determine all maximal ideals of $R(X,F)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume first that $X\neq\varnothing$. For $x\in X$, define
$$
M_x
\coloneqq
\{f\in R(X,F):f(x)=0\}.
$$

<1>1. Each $M_x$ is a maximal ideal.

::: {.proof}
Evaluation at $x$ is a surjective ring homomorphism
$$
\operatorname{ev}_x:R(X,F)\longrightarrow F,
\qquad
f\longmapsto f(x).
$$
Its kernel is $M_x$. Hence
$$
R(X,F)/M_x\cong F,
$$
which is a field, so $M_x$ is maximal.
:::

<1>2. For each $x\in X$, let $e_x$ be the characteristic function of
$\{x\}$:
$$
e_x(y)
=
\begin{cases}
1,&y=x,\\
0,&y\neq x.
\end{cases}
$$
Then
$$
e_x^2=e_x,
\qquad
e_xe_y=0\quad(x\neq y),
\qquad
\sum_{x\in X}e_x=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
All three identities hold pointwise. The last sum is finite because $X$
is finite.
:::

<1>3. Let $M$ be any maximal ideal of $R(X,F)$. There is a unique
$x_0\in X$ such that
$$
e_{x_0}\notin M,
$$
and for every $x\neq x_0$,
$$
e_x\in M.
$$

::: {.proof}
The quotient
$$
K\coloneqq R(X,F)/M
$$
is a field. Let $\bar e_x$ denote the image of $e_x$ in $K$. By step
<1>2, each $\bar e_x$ is idempotent. A field has only the idempotents
$0$ and $1$, so
$$
\bar e_x\in\{0,1\}.
$$
Also
$$
\sum_{x\in X}\bar e_x=1,
$$
so at least one $\bar e_x$ equals $1$. Two distinct ones cannot both
equal $1$, because step <1>2 would then give
$$
1
=
\bar e_x\bar e_y
=
0.
$$
Thus exactly one, say $\bar e_{x_0}$, equals $1$, and all the others
are $0$. This is equivalent to the stated membership assertions.
:::

<1>4. Every maximal ideal $M$ equals $M_{x_0}$ for the point $x_0$
from step <1>3.

::: {.proof}
Every $f\in R(X,F)$ has the finite decomposition
$$
f
=
\sum_{x\in X}f(x)e_x.
$$
Modulo $M$, step <1>3 gives
$$
\bar f
=
f(x_0)\bar e_{x_0}
=
f(x_0).
$$
The map from $F$ to the quotient induced by constant functions is
injective, because its kernel is an ideal of the field $F$ and does not
contain $1$. Hence
$$
\bar f=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f(x_0)=0.
$$
Thus
$$
f\in M
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f\in M_{x_0},
$$
so $M=M_{x_0}$.
:::

<1>5. If $X\neq\varnothing$, the maximal ideals are exactly
$$
\boxed{
M_x=\{f:X\to F:f(x)=0\},
\qquad x\in X.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 shows that all displayed ideals are maximal, and step <1>4
shows that there are no others.
:::

<1>6. If $X=\varnothing$, then $R(X,F)$ is the zero ring and has no
maximal ideals.

::: {.proof}
There is exactly one function from the empty set to $F$, so the additive
and multiplicative identities coincide. The zero ring has no proper
ideals, hence no maximal ideals.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>5--<1>6 cover every finite set $X$.
:::
