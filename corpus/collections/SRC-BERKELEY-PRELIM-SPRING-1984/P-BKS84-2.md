::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a $p$-group of order $p^4$, and suppose
\[
|Z(G)|=p^2.
\]
Determine the number of conjugacy classes of $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #noncentral-class-size-p}
Every noncentral element of $G$ has conjugacy class of size $p$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x\in G\sm Z(G)$. Since both $Z(G)$ and $x$ lie in the centralizer
$C_G(x)$, the subgroup generated by them satisfies
$$
\langle Z(G),x\rangle\subseteq C_G(x).
$$
Because $x\notin Z(G)$ and $\abs{Z(G)}=p^2$, this subgroup has order
strictly larger than $p^2$, hence at least $p^3$.

The centralizer cannot be all of $G$, since that would make $x$ central.
As $\abs{G}=p^4$, it follows that
$$
\abs{C_G(x)}=p^3.
$$
Therefore the conjugacy class of $x$ has size
$$
\abs{x^G}
=
[G:C_G(x)]
=
p.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #class-count-boxed}
The number of conjugacy classes of $G$ is
$$
\boxed{p^3+p^2-p}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Each of the $p^2$ elements of $Z(G)$ forms a singleton conjugacy class.
The remaining
$$
p^4-p^2
$$
elements are noncentral, and by step [](#noncentral-class-size-p){.pf-ref} they occur in conjugacy classes
of size $p$. Hence the number of noncentral conjugacy classes is
$$
\frac{p^4-p^2}{p}
=
p^3-p.
$$
Adding the $p^2$ central classes gives
$$
p^2+(p^3-p)
=
p^3+p^2-p.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#class-count-boxed){.pf-ref} gives the required number of conjugacy classes.
:::
