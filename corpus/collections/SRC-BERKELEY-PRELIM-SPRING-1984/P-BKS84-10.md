::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain and let $I,J$ be nonzero ideals. Prove that
\[
IJ=I\cap J
\]
if and only if
\[
I+J=R.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For any two ideals $I,J$ in a commutative ring,
$$
IJ\subseteq I\cap J.
$$

::: {.proof}
Every generator $xy$ of the product ideal $IJ$, with $x\in I$ and
$y\in J$, lies in both $I$ and $J$. Hence every finite sum of such
products lies in $I\cap J$.
:::

<1>2. If
$$
I+J=R,
$$
then
$$
IJ=I\cap J.
$$

::: {.proof}
Choose $u\in I$ and $v\in J$ with
$$
u+v=1.
$$
If $x\in I\cap J$, then
$$
x
=
xu+xv.
$$
Since $x\in J$ and $u\in I$, one has $xu\in IJ$; since $x\in I$ and
$v\in J$, one has $xv\in IJ$. Thus $x\in IJ$, proving
$I\cap J\subseteq IJ$. Step <1>1 gives the reverse inclusion.
:::

<1>3. Suppose
$$
IJ=I\cap J.
$$
Write
$$
I=(a),
\qquad
J=(b),
\qquad
I+J=(d),
$$
with $a,b,d\neq0$. Then $d$ is a unit.

::: {.proof}
Since $(a)+(b)=(d)$, the element $d$ divides both $a$ and $b$. Write
$$
a=da',
\qquad
b=db'.
$$
The element
$$
c\coloneqq da'b'
$$
satisfies
$$
c=ab'
\in
(a)=I
$$
and
$$
c=ba'
\in
(b)=J.
$$
Hence
$$
c\in I\cap J=IJ=(ab).
$$
Thus for some $r\in R$,
$$
da'b'
=
r ab
=
r d^2a'b'.
$$
Because $R$ is a domain and $da'b'\neq0$, cancellation gives
$$
1=rd.
$$
Therefore $d$ is a unit.
:::

<1>4. If
$$
IJ=I\cap J,
$$
then
$$
I+J=R.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
I+J=(d)
$$
with $d$ a unit. Hence $(d)=R$.
:::

<1>5. Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
IJ=I\cap J
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
I+J=R
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The forward implication is step <1>4, and the reverse implication is
step <1>2.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the asserted equivalence.
:::
:::
