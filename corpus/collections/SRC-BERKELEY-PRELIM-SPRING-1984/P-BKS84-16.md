::: {.problem}
Prove or give a counterexample: if $A$ is an invertible complex $n\times n$ matrix and some positive power of $A$ is diagonal, then $A$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #eigenspaces-a-invariant}
Let $k\geq1$ be such that
$$
D\coloneqq A^k
$$
is diagonal. Then every eigenspace of $D$ is invariant under $A$.

::: pf-proof
Since $D=A^k$, one has
$$
AD=A^{k+1}=DA.
$$
If $v$ belongs to the eigenspace
$$
V_\mu\coloneqq\ker(D-\mu I),
$$
then
$$
D(Av)
=
A(Dv)
=
A(\mu v)
=
\mu Av.
$$
Thus $Av\in V_\mu$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #restriction-satisfies-power}
For every eigenvalue $\mu$ of $D$, one has $\mu\neq0$, and the
restriction
$$
A_\mu\coloneqq A|_{V_\mu}
$$
satisfies
$$
A_\mu^k=\mu I_{V_\mu}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Because $A$ is invertible, so is $D=A^k$; hence $0$ is not an eigenvalue
of $D$. Step [](#eigenspaces-a-invariant){.pf-ref} makes $A_\mu$ well-defined. On $V_\mu$,
$$
A_\mu^k
=
D|_{V_\mu}
=
\mu I_{V_\mu}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #restriction-diagonalizable}
Each restriction $A_\mu$ is diagonalizable over $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#restriction-satisfies-power){.pf-ref}, the minimal polynomial of $A_\mu$ divides
$$
p_\mu(x)=x^k-\mu.
$$
Since $\mu\neq0$,
$$
p_\mu'(x)=kx^{k-1}
$$
has no common root with $p_\mu$: a common root would have to be $0$,
but $p_\mu(0)=-\mu\neq0$. Thus $p_\mu$ has distinct roots over
$\CC$. Any divisor of it also has distinct roots, so the minimal
polynomial of $A_\mu$ is squarefree and splits over $\CC$. Hence
$A_\mu$ is diagonalizable.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-is-diagonalizable}
The matrix $A$ is diagonalizable.

::: pf-proof
Because $D$ is diagonal,
$$
\CC^n
=
\bigoplus_{\mu}V_\mu,
$$
where the sum ranges over the distinct diagonal values of $D$. By step
[](#eigenspaces-a-invariant){.pf-ref} these summands are $A$-invariant, and by step [](#restriction-diagonalizable){.pf-ref} each has a basis
of eigenvectors of the corresponding restriction of $A$. The union of
these bases is a basis of $\CC^n$ consisting of eigenvectors of $A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer-boxed}
Therefore the statement is
$$
\boxed{\text{true}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#a-is-diagonalizable){.pf-ref} proves the asserted diagonalizability.
:::

Step [](#answer-boxed){.pf-ref} answers the prove-or-counterexample question.
