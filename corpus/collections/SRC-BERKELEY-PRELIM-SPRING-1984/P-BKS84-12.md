::: {.problem}
Consider
\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=y-\sin y.
\]
Show that there is $\varepsilon>0$ such that, whenever $|y_0|<\varepsilon$, the solution $y=f(x)$ satisfying $f(0)=y_0$ obeys
\[
\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
F(y)\coloneqq y-\sin y.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #global-existence-uniqueness}
Every initial value $y(0)=y_0$ has a unique solution defined for
all $x\in\RR$.

::: pf-proof
Since
$$
F'(y)=1-\cos y,
$$
one has
$$
\abs{F'(y)}\leq2
$$
for every $y\in\RR$. Thus $F$ is globally Lipschitz. The global
existence and uniqueness theorem for autonomous ODEs therefore gives a
unique solution on all of $\RR$ for every initial value.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sign-matches}
For every $y\neq0$, the numbers $F(y)$ and $y$ have the same
sign.

::: pf-proof
For $y>0$, the standard strict inequality
$$
\sin y<y
$$
gives $F(y)>0$. Since $F$ is odd, $F(y)<0$ for $y<0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #positive-initial-limit-zero}
If $y_0>0$, then the corresponding solution satisfies
$$
\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
By uniqueness, the solution cannot meet the equilibrium solution
$y=0$ unless it is identically zero. Hence $f(x)>0$ for all $x$.
Step [](#sign-matches){.pf-ref} gives
$$
f'(x)=F(f(x))>0,
$$
so $f$ is strictly increasing. In particular, for $x\leq0$,
$$
0<f(x)\leq f(0)=y_0.
$$
Therefore the limit
$$
L\coloneqq\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)
$$
exists and satisfies $0\leq L\leq y_0$.

If $L>0$, then $F(L)>0$. By continuity of $F$, there are $c>0$ and
$X<0$ such that
$$
f'(x)=F(f(x))\geq c
$$
for all $x<X$. Hence, for $x<X$,
$$
f(X)-f(x)
=
\int_x^X f'(t)\,dt
\geq
c(X-x),
$$
whose right-hand side tends to $\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$. This
contradicts the boundedness of $f$ on $(-\infty,0]$. Thus $L=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #negative-initial-limit-zero}
If $y_0<0$, then the corresponding solution also satisfies
$$
\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
The function $F$ is odd, so if $f$ is the solution with initial value
$y_0<0$, then $-f$ is the solution with initial value $-y_0>0$.
Step [](#positive-initial-limit-zero){.pf-ref} applied to $-f$ gives
$$
\lim_{x\to-\infty}(-f(x))=0,
$$
and hence the same limit for $f$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion-for-all-y0}
The conclusion holds for every initial value $y_0\in\RR$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#positive-initial-limit-zero){.pf-ref} and [](#negative-initial-limit-zero){.pf-ref} cover $y_0\neq0$. If $y_0=0$, uniqueness gives the
constant solution $f\equiv0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #epsilon-boxed}
In particular, one may take
$$
\boxed{\varepsilon=1}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#conclusion-for-all-y0){.pf-ref} proves the desired limit for every $y_0$, hence certainly for
all $\abs{y_0}<1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#epsilon-boxed){.pf-ref} supplies the required positive $\varepsilon$.
:::
