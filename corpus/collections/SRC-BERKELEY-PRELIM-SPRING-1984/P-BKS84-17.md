::: {.problem}
Prove that every Taylor coefficient at the origin of
\[
f(z)=\frac{z}{e^z-1}
\]
is rational.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The singularity of $f$ at $0$ is removable, and its holomorphic
extension satisfies
$$
f(0)=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
e^z-1
=
z
\left(
1+\frac z{2!}+\frac{z^2}{3!}+\cdots
\right).
$$
The factor in parentheses is holomorphic and equals $1$ at $0$, hence
is nonzero near $0$. Therefore
$$
f(z)
=
\left(
1+\frac z{2!}+\frac{z^2}{3!}+\cdots
\right)^{-1}
$$
extends holomorphically across $0$ with value $1$.
:::

<1>2. Write the Taylor expansion
$$
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^n
$$
near $0$. Then
$$
a_0=1
$$
and, for every $n\geq1$,
$$
a_n
=
-\sum_{m=2}^{n+1}
\frac{a_{n+1-m}}{m!}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The identity
$$
(e^z-1)f(z)=z
$$
holds near $0$. Expanding,
$$
\left(
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^m}{m!}
\right)
\left(
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_jz^j
\right)
=
z.
$$
The coefficient of $z$ gives $a_0=1$. For $n\geq1$, the coefficient of
$z^{n+1}$ is zero, so
$$
\sum_{m=1}^{n+1}\frac{a_{n+1-m}}{m!}=0.
$$
The $m=1$ term is $a_n$, which gives the displayed recurrence.
:::

<1>3. Every coefficient $a_n$ is rational.

::: {.proof}
Proceed by induction on $n$. Step <1>2 gives
$$
a_0=1\in\QQ.
$$
Assume $a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\QQ$. The recurrence in step <1>2
expresses $a_n$ as a finite sum of the earlier coefficients multiplied
by the rational numbers $1/m!$. Hence $a_n\in\QQ$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves that every Taylor coefficient of $f$ at the origin is
rational.
:::
:::
