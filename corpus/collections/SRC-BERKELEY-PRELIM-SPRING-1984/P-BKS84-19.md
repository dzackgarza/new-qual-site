::: {.problem}
For $x>0$, let
\[
f(x)=x\log(1+x^{-1}).
\]

1. Show that $f$ is strictly increasing.
2. Compute
   \[
   \lim_{x\to0^+}f(x)
   \qquad\text{and}\qquad
   \lim_{x\to\infty}f(x).
   \]
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #log-inequality}
For every $u>0$,
$$
\log(1+u)>\frac{u}{1+u}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Define
$$
h(u)
\coloneqq
\log(1+u)-\frac{u}{1+u}.
$$
Then $h(0)=0$ and
$$
h'(u)
=
\frac1{1+u}-\frac1{(1+u)^2}
=
\frac{u}{(1+u)^2}
>
0
$$
for $u>0$. Hence $h(u)>0$ for every $u>0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-strictly-increasing}
The function $f$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\infty)$.

::: pf-proof
Differentiating gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x)
&=
\log(1+x^{-1})
+x\frac{-x^{-2}}{1+x^{-1}}\\
&=
\log(1+x^{-1})-\frac1{x+1}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Set $u=1/x>0$. Then
$$
\frac1{x+1}
=
\frac{u}{1+u},
$$
so step [](#log-inequality){.pf-ref} gives $f'(x)>0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #limit-at-zero-boxed}
One has
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{x\to0^+}f(x)=0
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
With $u=1/x$,
$$
f(x)
=
\frac{\log(1+u)}u,
$$
and $x\to0^+$ is equivalent to $u\to\infty$. By l'Hôpital's rule,
$$
\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\log(1+u)}u
=
\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac1{1+u}
=
0.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #limit-at-infinity-boxed}
One has
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=1
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Again set $u=1/x$. Then $u\to0^+$ and
$$
f(x)
=
\frac{\log(1+u)}u.
$$
Since
$$
\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\log(1+u)}u=1,
$$
the desired limit is $1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#f-strictly-increasing){.pf-ref} proves Part (1), and steps [](#limit-at-zero-boxed){.pf-ref} and [](#limit-at-infinity-boxed){.pf-ref} give the two limits in
Part (2).
:::
