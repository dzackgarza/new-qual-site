::: {.problem}
Prove or give a counterexample: if $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ has both a left limit and a right limit at every point, then $f$ has at most countably many discontinuities.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $x\in\RR$, write
$$
f(x-)
\coloneqq
\lim_{t\to x^-}f(t),
\qquad
f(x+)
\coloneqq
\lim_{t\to x^+}f(t),
$$
and define
$$
\Delta(x)
\coloneqq
\max\left\{
\abs{f(x)-f(x-)},
\abs{f(x)-f(x+)},
\abs{f(x-)-f(x+)}
\right\}.
$$

<1>1. The function $f$ is discontinuous at $x$ if and only if
$$
\Delta(x)>0.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $f$ is continuous at $x$, then both one-sided limits equal $f(x)$,
so $\Delta(x)=0$. Conversely, if $\Delta(x)=0$, then
$$
f(x-)=f(x)=f(x+),
$$
so the two-sided limit exists and equals $f(x)$. Hence $f$ is continuous
at $x$.
:::

<1>2. For positive integers $m,n$, set
$$
E_{m,n}
\coloneqq
\left\{
x\in[-m,m]:
\Delta(x)\geq\frac1n
\right\}.
$$
Then $E_{m,n}$ is finite.

::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that $E_{m,n}$ is infinite. By compactness of
$[-m,m]$, it has an accumulation point $c\in[-m,m]$. Choose distinct
points $x_j\in E_{m,n}$ with $x_j\to c$. After passing to a
subsequence, either all $x_j>c$ or all $x_j<c$. The two cases are
symmetric, so assume $x_j>c$.

Let
$$
L=f(c+).
$$
By the definition of the right-hand limit, with
$$
\eta\coloneqq\frac1{4n},
$$
there is $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{f(t)-L}<\eta
$$
whenever
$$
c<t<c+\delta.
$$
For all sufficiently large $j$,
$$
c<x_j<c+\frac\delta2.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{f(x_j)-L}<\eta.
$$
Moreover, all points sufficiently close to $x_j$ from either side still
lie in $(c,c+\delta)$. Passing to the one-sided limits at $x_j$ gives
$$
\abs{f(x_j-)-L}\leq\eta,
\qquad
\abs{f(x_j+)-L}\leq\eta.
$$
Therefore every pair among
$$
f(x_j),
\qquad
f(x_j-),
\qquad
f(x_j+)
$$
differs by at most
$$
2\eta=\frac1{2n}<\frac1n.
$$
Hence
$$
\Delta(x_j)<\frac1n,
$$
contradicting $x_j\in E_{m,n}$.
:::

<1>3. The set $D$ of discontinuities of $f$ satisfies
$$
D
=
\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}
E_{m,n}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, if $x\in D$, then $\Delta(x)>0$. Choose $m$ with
$\abs x\leq m$ and $n$ with $1/n\leq\Delta(x)$. Then
$x\in E_{m,n}$. The reverse inclusion follows immediately from step
<1>1.
:::

<1>4. The set of discontinuities of $f$ is at most countable.

::: {.proof}
Each $E_{m,n}$ is finite by step <1>2, and step <1>3 expresses $D$ as a
countable union of these finite sets.
:::

<1>5. Thus the proposed statement is
$$
\boxed{\text{true}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves it.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 answers the prove-or-counterexample question.
:::
