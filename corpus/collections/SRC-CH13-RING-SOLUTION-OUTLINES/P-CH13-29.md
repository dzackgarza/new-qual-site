::: {.problem}
(Subfield test) Let $F$ be a field and let $K$ be a subset of $F$ with at least two elements. Prove that $K$ is a subfield of $F$ if, for any $a, b$ ($b \neq 0$) in $K$, $a - b$ and $ab^{-1}$ belong to $K$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection retains its solution outline as `assets/attachments/Ch13Sltns.pdf`, whose entry "# 29" is the subfield test, solved by the two subgroup criteria. In the seventh edition the same exercise is printed as **Chapter 13 Exercise 25**; the outline's number is 29 and the card keeps it, so the two numbers name one exercise. The card states those criteria and the elements they require, and the wording below is its own. Let $F$ be a field and $K\subseteq F$ a subset with at least two elements such that $a-b\in K$ and $ab^{-1}\in K$ for all $a,b\in K$ with $b\ne0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #zero-one}
$0\in K$ and $1\in K$.

::: pf-proof
For $a\in K$ the condition on differences with $b=a$ gives $a-a=0\in K$. Since $K$ has at least two elements, some $a\in K$ is nonzero, and then the condition on quotients with $b=a$ gives $aa^{-1}=1\in K$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #additive-subgroup}
$K$ is a subgroup of $(F,+)$.

::: pf-proof
$K$ is nonempty by step [](#zero-one){.pf-ref}. For $b\in K$ the condition on differences with $a=0\in K$ gives $0-b=-b\in K$, so $K$ is closed under additive inverses; and for $a,b\in K$ the same condition applied to $a$ and $-b$, together with $-b\in K$, gives $a-(-b)=a+b\in K$, so $K$ is closed under addition. A nonempty subset of a group closed under the operation and inverses is a subgroup.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiplicative-subgroup}
$K\setminus\{0\}$ is a subgroup of $F^\times$.

::: pf-proof
$K\setminus\{0\}$ is nonempty because $1\in K\setminus\{0\}$ by step [](#zero-one){.pf-ref}, and it is contained in $F^\times$. For $a,b\in K$ with $b\ne0$ the condition gives $ab^{-1}\in K$, which is nonzero, so $K\setminus\{0\}$ is closed under the operation $(a,b)\mapsto ab^{-1}$ of $F^\times$; the subgroup criterion then makes $K\setminus\{0\}$ a subgroup of $F^\times$. In particular $K$ is closed under multiplication and contains the inverse of each of its nonzero elements.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$K$ is a subfield of $F$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#additive-subgroup){.pf-ref} and [](#multiplicative-subgroup){.pf-ref} show that $K$ is an additive subgroup of $F$, contains $1$, is closed under multiplication, and contains the multiplicative inverse of each of its nonzero elements; commutativity of multiplication is inherited from $F$. That is the definition of a subfield, so the two displayed conditions imply that $K$ is a subfield of $F$.
:::
