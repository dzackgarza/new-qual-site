::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field of order $2^{n}$. Prove that $\operatorname{char} F = 2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise, the collection's provenance being the textbook alone, so the argument is the card's own. Let $F$ be a field with $\abs{F}=2^n$ for an integer $n\ge1$, and let $p=\operatorname{char} F$ be the additive order of $1$ in $F$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #char-prime}
$p$ is a prime number.

::: pf-proof
The map $\ZZ\to F$, $k\mapsto k\cdot1$, is a ring homomorphism, and a field is in particular an integral domain, so its kernel is a prime ideal of $\ZZ$; kernels of ring homomorphisms are the ideals $(m)$ for $m\ge0$, and $(m)$ is prime only for $m=0$ or $m$ prime. The kernel is $(p)$ by definition of the characteristic, and $p\ne0$ because $F$ is finite: the elements $k\cdot1$, $k=0,1,2,\ldots$, are distinct while their kernel is trivial, which would give infinitely many elements of $F$. Hence $p$ is prime.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #prime-subfield}
The set $F_p=\{k\cdot1:k\in\ZZ\}$ is a subfield of $F$ isomorphic to $\ZZ/p\ZZ$, and $F$ is a finite-dimensional vector space over $F_p$.

::: pf-proof
The image of the ring homomorphism $k\mapsto k\cdot1$ is $\{k\cdot1\}$, a subring of $F$, and it is isomorphic to $\ZZ/\ker=\ZZ/p\ZZ$ by the first isomorphism theorem; since $p$ is prime, $\ZZ/p\ZZ$ is a field, so this image is a subfield of $F$. A field extension is a vector space over its base field with the inherited addition and scalar multiplication, and it is finite-dimensional here because $F$ is finite.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #counting}
If $F$ is a field with $\abs{F}=2^n$ then $\operatorname{char}F=2$.

::: pf-proof
Let $d=\dim_{F_p}F$, which is finite since $F$ is finite. Every element of $F$ is a unique $F_p$-linear combination of the $d$ elements of a basis, and $\abs{F_p}=p$, so $\abs{F}=p^d$. By hypothesis $p^d=2^n$; unique prime factorization forces $p=2$, and then $d=n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\boxed{\operatorname{char}F=2}$ for a field of order $2^n$ with $n\ge1$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#counting){.pf-ref} gives $p=2$, and $p$ is the characteristic, so the assertion follows; the same step shows the field has $2^n$ elements precisely when its dimension over its prime subfield is $n$.
:::
