::: {.problem}
Consider the equation $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$.

(a) How many solutions does this equation have in $\ZZ_7$?

(b) Find all solutions of this equation in $\ZZ_8$.

(c) Find all solutions of this equation in $\ZZ_{12}$.

(d) Find all solutions of this equation in $\ZZ_{14}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

$x^{2}-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)$, and the solutions are: (a) two, namely $2$ and $3$;
(b) $2$ and $3$; (c) $2$, $3$, $6$ and $11$; (d) $2$, $3$, $9$ and $10$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factor}

$x^{2}-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)$ holds in $\ZZ_{n}[x]$ for every $n$.

::: pf-proof

Expanding $(x-2)(x-3)=x^{2}-3x-2x+6=x^{2}-5x+6$ over the integers, and reduction
of coefficients modulo $n$ is a ring homomorphism $\ZZ[x]\to\ZZ_{n}[x]$, so the
identity survives. Hence $x$ is a solution in $\ZZ_{n}$ exactly when
$(x-2)(x-3)\equiv 0\pmod n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a}

In $\ZZ_{7}$ there are exactly two solutions, $2$ and $3$.

::: pf-proof

$\ZZ_{7}$ is a field, hence has no zero-divisors, so $(x-2)(x-3)=0$ forces
$x=2$ or $x=3$. Both are solutions since a product with a zero factor is zero.
So the equation has exactly two solutions in $\ZZ_{7}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}

In $\ZZ_{8}$ the solutions are $2$ and $3$.

::: pf-proof

Evaluate $x^{2}-5x+6$ modulo $8$ at all eight residues: for
$x=0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7$ the values are $6,2,0,0,2,6,4,4$. Indeed
$2^{2}-10+6=0$, $3^{2}-15+6=0$, $4^{2}-20+6=2$, $5^{2}-25+6=6$, $6^{2}-30+6=12
\equiv 4$, $7^{2}-35+6=20\equiv 4$, $1-5+6=2$ and $6$ at $0$. So exactly $2$ and
$3$ vanish. Note that $\ZZ_{8}$ has zero-divisors, so the two extra values are
not a contradiction to the factorization alone.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c}

In $\ZZ_{12}$ the solutions are $2$, $3$, $6$ and $11$.

::: pf-proof

By the Chinese remainder theorem $\ZZ_{12}\cong\ZZ_{4}\oplus\ZZ_{3}$ and
$x$ is a solution mod $12$ exactly when it is one in each factor. Modulo $4$ one
has $x^{2}-5x+6\equiv x^{2}+3x+2=(x+1)(x+2)$, which vanishes at $x\equiv 2$ and
$x\equiv 3$ and at no other residue ($0\mapsto 2$, $1\mapsto 2$). Modulo $3$,
$x^{2}-5x+6\equiv x^{2}+x=x(x+1)$, vanishing at $x\equiv 0$ and $x\equiv 2$.
Recombining the pairs $(2\bmod 4,0\bmod 3)=6$, $(2,2)=2$, $(3,0)=3$ and
$(3,2)=11$ gives the four solutions $2,3,6,11$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #d}

In $\ZZ_{14}$ the solutions are $2$, $3$, $9$ and $10$.

::: pf-proof

By the Chinese remainder theorem $\ZZ_{14}\cong\ZZ_{2}\oplus\ZZ_{7}$. Modulo $2$
the polynomial is $x^{2}+x=x(x+1)$, which vanishes at both residues $0$ and $1$;
modulo $7$ the solutions are $2$ and $3$ as in step [](#a){.pf-ref}. The four
pairs are $(0\bmod 2,2\bmod 7)=2$, $(0,3)=10$, $(1,2)=9$ and $(1,3)=3$, so the
solutions in $\ZZ_{14}$ are $2,3,9,10$. Here the extra solutions appear because
$\ZZ_{14}$ has the zero-divisors $2,7,4,10$.

:::
