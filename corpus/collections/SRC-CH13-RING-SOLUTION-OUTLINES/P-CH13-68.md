::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field of order $32$. Show that the only subfields of $F$ are $F$ itself and $\{0, 1\}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained solution outline for this chapter, **Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra*, seventh edition, Chapter 13 solution outlines, exercise 68, PDF p. 4** (`assets/attachments/Ch13Sltns.pdf`), restructured into steps, with the one point the outline leaves implicit made explicit: that the nonzero elements of a subfield form a subgroup of $F^{*}$, and that a subgroup's order divides $31$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #star}
$\cardinality{F^{*}}=31$ and $F^{*}$ is a group under multiplication.

::: pf-proof
$F$ has $32$ elements, of which exactly one is $0$, so $F^{*}=F\setminus\{0\}$ has $31$ elements; $F^{*}$ is closed under multiplication because a product of nonzero elements is nonzero in a field, and it is a group because every nonzero element has an inverse in $F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subgroup}
If $K\subseteq F$ is a subfield then $K^{*}=K\setminus\{0\}$ is a subgroup of $F^{*}$.

::: pf-proof
$K$ is a field under the operations inherited from $F$, so $K^{*}$ is a group under the same multiplication, and it is contained in $F^{*}$; a subset that is a group under the ambient operation is a subgroup.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order}
$\cardinality{K^{*}}$ divides $31$, so $\cardinality{K^{*}}\in\{1,31\}$.

::: pf-proof
By Lagrange's theorem applied to the subgroup $K^{*}\le F^{*}$, the order of $K^{*}$ divides $\cardinality{F^{*}}=31$ by step [](#star){.pf-ref}; the divisors of the prime $31$ are $1$ and $31$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cases}
$\cardinality{K^{*}}=1$ gives $K=\{0,1\}$, and $\cardinality{K^{*}}=31$ gives $K=F$.

::: pf-proof
If $\cardinality{K^{*}}=1$ then $K^{*}=\{1\}$, since $1\ne0$ is in every subfield; so $K=\{0,1\}$. If $\cardinality{K^{*}}=31$ then $K^{*}=F^{*}$, because a subset of $F^{*}$ with the same finite cardinality is all of it; so $K=F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The only subfields of $F$ are $F$ and $\{0,1\}$.

::: pf-proof
Every subfield gives one of the two cases of step [](#cases){.pf-ref}, and conversely both $\{0,1\}$ and $F$ are subfields:
$$
\boxed{\{K:K\subseteq F\text{ a subfield}\}=\bigl\{\{0,1\},\,F\bigr\}.}
$$
The argument uses only that $32-1=31$ is prime; for a field of order $p^{n}$ it shows that subfield orders are $p^{d}$ with $d$ dividing $n$.
:::
