::: {.problem}
Give an example of a commutative ring without zero-divisors that is not an integral domain.
:::

::: {.solution}

The example is $2\ZZ$, the ring of even integers under the usual addition and
multiplication.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #is-a-ring}

$2\ZZ$ is a commutative ring under the inherited operations.

::: pf-proof

Elements of $2\ZZ$ are $2a$ with $a\in\ZZ$. It is closed under subtraction and
multiplication, since $2a-2b=2(a-b)$ and $2a\cdot 2b=2(2ab)$, so $2\ZZ$ is a
subring of $\ZZ$ and a ring under the restricted operations. Associativity,
both distributive laws and commutativity of multiplication hold in $2\ZZ$
because they hold for all integers.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #no-zero-divisors}

$2\ZZ$ has no zero-divisors.

::: pf-proof

Let $2a,2b\in 2\ZZ$ with $2a\cdot 2b=0$. Then $4ab=0$ in $\ZZ$; since
$4\neq 0$ and $\ZZ$ is an integral domain, $ab=0$, and then $a=0$ or $b=0$
because $\ZZ$ has no zero-divisors. So $2a=0$ or $2b=0$: a product of nonzero
elements of $2\ZZ$ is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #no-unity}

$2\ZZ$ has no multiplicative identity, so it is not an integral domain.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $e=2k\in 2\ZZ$ were a unity, that is $ex=x$ for all $x\in 2\ZZ$. Taking
$x=2$ gives $4k=2$, which has no integer solution: $4k$ is divisible by $4$ and
$2$ is not. By definition an integral domain is a commutative ring with unity
$1\neq 0$ and no zero-divisors, so a commutative ring with no zero-divisors and
no unity is not one.

:::
