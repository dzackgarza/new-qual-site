::: {.problem}
Suppose that $R$ is a commutative ring without zero-divisors. Show that all the nonzero elements of $R$ have the same additive order.
:::

::: {.solution}

The retained solution outline for Chapter 13, Exercise 47, PDF p. 2 of `assets/attachments/Ch13Sltns.pdf`, argues by contradiction from two distinct finite additive orders, and that argument does not cover the case in which the elements have infinite additive order; the proof below is the repaired one, and says so. Here $R$ is a commutative ring without zero-divisors, not assumed unital, and the additive order of $x$ is the least positive $n$ with $nx=0$, or infinite when no such $n$ exists.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #swap}

For $x,y\in R$ and $n\in\ZZ$, $(nx)y=x(ny)$.

::: pf-proof

$(nx)y$ is the sum of $n$ copies of $xy$ and $x(ny)$ is the same sum, by distributivity and associativity; for $n<0$ use $(-n)x=-nx$. Commutativity of $R$ is not needed for this identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #propagate}

If $nx=0$ for some positive integer $n$ and some $x\neq0$, then $ny=0$ for every $y\in R$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#swap){.pf-ref}, $0=(nx)y=x(ny)$; since $R$ has no zero-divisors and $x\neq0$, $ny=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #finite}

If some nonzero element of $R$ has finite additive order, then every nonzero element has finite additive order, and all of them are equal.

::: pf-proof

Let $x,y\neq0$ and suppose $x$ has finite order $n$. By step [](#propagate){.pf-ref} $ny=0$, so $y$ has finite order $m$, which divides $n$; applying the same argument with the roles of $x$ and $y$ exchanged gives that $n$ divides $m$; hence $n=m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #infinite}

If no nonzero element has finite additive order, all nonzero elements have infinite additive order.

::: pf-proof

This is the negation of the hypothesis of step [](#finite){.pf-ref}, and additive order is either a positive integer or infinite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

All nonzero elements of $R$ have the same additive order.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#finite){.pf-ref} and [](#infinite){.pf-ref} cover the two cases: either all are infinite, or all are finite and equal.

:::
