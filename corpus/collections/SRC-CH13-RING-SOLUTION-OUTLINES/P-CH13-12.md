::: {.problem}
In $\ZZ_{7}$ give a reasonable interpretation for the expressions $\frac{1}{2}$, $-\frac{2}{3}$, $\sqrt{-3}$, and $-\frac{1}{6}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: each expression is interpreted by solving the corresponding equation in $\ZZ_{7}$, where every nonzero class is a unit.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #inverses}

$2\cdot4=1$, $3\cdot5=1$ and $6\cdot6=1$ in $\ZZ_{7}$.

::: pf-proof

$2\cdot4=8\equiv1$, $3\cdot5=15\equiv1$ and $6\cdot6=36\equiv1\pmod 7$. So $\frac12=4$, $\frac13=5$ and $\frac16=6$, these being the multiplicative inverses of $2$, $3$, $6$ in the field $\ZZ_{7}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #negfraction}

$-\frac23=4$ and $-\frac16=1$ in $\ZZ_{7}$.

::: pf-proof

$\frac23=2\cdot\frac13=2\cdot5=10\equiv3$, so $-\frac23=-3\equiv4$; alternatively $-\frac23=-1+\frac13=6+5=11\equiv4$. And $\frac16=6$, so $-\frac16=-6\equiv1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sqrt}

$\sqrt{-3}=2$ in $\ZZ_{7}$, i.e. the solutions of $x^{2}=-3$ are $x=\pm2$.

::: pf-proof

$-3\equiv4$ in $\ZZ_{7}$, and $2^{2}=4$; also $5^{2}=25\equiv4$. Since $x^{2}=4$ is a quadratic equation over the field $\ZZ_{7}$, it has at most two roots, so the complete solution set is $\{2,5\}=\{\pm2\}$. The reasonable interpretation of $\sqrt{-3}$ is therefore the class $2$ (or its negative $5$).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

In $\ZZ_{7}$: $\frac12=4$, $-\frac23=4$, $\sqrt{-3}=2$, $-\frac16=1$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#inverses){.pf-ref}, [](#negfraction){.pf-ref} and [](#sqrt){.pf-ref} give
$$
\boxed{\tfrac12=4,\qquad -\tfrac23=4,\qquad \sqrt{-3}=\pm2,\qquad -\tfrac16=1\quad\text{in }\ZZ_{7}.}
$$

:::
