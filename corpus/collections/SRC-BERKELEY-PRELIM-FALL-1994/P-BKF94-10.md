::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ satisfy:

(i) $f(K)$ is compact whenever $K$ is compact;

(ii) for every decreasing sequence of compact sets $K_n$,
\[
f\!\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}K_n\right)=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}f(K_n).
\]
Prove that $f$ is continuous.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix a sequence
$$
x_j\longrightarrow x
$$
in $\RR^n$. For each positive integer $m$, set
$$
K_m\coloneqq
\{x\}\cup\{x_j:j\geq m\}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Each $K_m$ is compact, the sequence $(K_m)$ is decreasing, and
$$
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}K_m=\{x\}.
$$

::: pf-proof

A convergent sequence together with its limit is compact, so every $K_m$ is
compact. The definition gives
$$
K_{m+1}\subseteq K_m.
$$
Certainly $x$ belongs to every $K_m$.

Now let $y\neq x$. Choose
$$
\varepsilon\coloneqq\frac{\norm{y-x}}2>0.
$$
Since $x_j\to x$, there is $m$ such that
$$
j\geq m
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\norm{x_j-x}<\varepsilon.
$$
Then no such $x_j$ can equal $y$, because
$$
\norm{y-x}=2\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $y\notin K_m$. Hence no point other than $x$ lies in every $K_m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Each $f(K_m)$ is compact and
$$
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}f(K_m)=\{f(x)\}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Hypothesis (i) and step [](#s1){.pf-ref} imply that every $f(K_m)$ is compact.
Hypothesis (ii), together with step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, gives
$$
\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}f(K_m)
=
f\left(\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}K_m\right)
=
f(\{x\})
=
\{f(x)\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

One has
$$
f(x_j)\longrightarrow f(x).
$$

::: pf-proof

Suppose not. Then there are $\varepsilon>0$ and a subsequence
$(x_{j_k})$ such that
$$
\norm{f(x_{j_k})-f(x)}
\geq
\varepsilon
$$
for every $k$.

All points $x_{j_k}$ lie in $K_1$, so all $f(x_{j_k})$ lie in the compact
set $f(K_1)$. Hence a further subsequence converges to some
$$
y\in f(K_1).
$$
Fix $m$. The indices of this further subsequence tend to infinity, so all
sufficiently late terms belong to $f(K_m)$. Since $f(K_m)$ is compact and
therefore closed, its limit $y$ lies in $f(K_m)$. This holds for every $m$,
so step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
y=f(x).
$$
But the inequality
$$
\norm{f(x_{j_k})-f(x)}
\geq
\varepsilon
$$
passes to the convergent further subsequence and gives
$$
\norm{y-f(x)}\geq\varepsilon,
$$
a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The function $f$ is continuous.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that $f$ carries every convergent sequence in $\RR^n$ to a
sequence converging to the image of its limit. Sequential continuity is
equivalent to continuity in the metric space $\RR^n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves the claim.
