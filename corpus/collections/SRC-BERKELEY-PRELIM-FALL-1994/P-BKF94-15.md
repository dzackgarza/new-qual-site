::: {.problem}
Let $A\in M_{7\times7}(\mathbb R)$ be diagonal with four diagonal entries $+1$ and three diagonal entries $-1$. Define $T(X)=AX-XA$. What is the dimension of the range of $T$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

After simultaneously reordering the standard basis coordinates, one
may assume
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
I_4&0\\
0&-I_3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

There are four diagonal entries equal to $+1$ and three equal to $-1$.
Conjugating by a permutation matrix merely reorders them. This conjugation
induces an invertible change of coordinates on $M_{7\times7}(\RR)$ and does
not change the dimension of the commutator map's range.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If
$$
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
B&C\\
D&E
\end{pmatrix}
$$
with block sizes $4$ and $3$, then
$$
T(X)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2C\\
-2D&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Direct block multiplication gives
$$
AX
=
\begin{pmatrix}
B&C\\
-D&-E
\end{pmatrix}
$$
and
$$
XA
=
\begin{pmatrix}
B&-C\\
D&-E
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Subtracting yields the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The range of $T$ is exactly the subspace
$$
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
0&C\\
D&0
\end{pmatrix}
:
C\in M_{4\times3}(\RR),
\quad
D\in M_{3\times4}(\RR)
\right\}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} shows that every value of $T$ has this form. Conversely, given
arbitrary blocks $C$ and $D$, choose an input with off-diagonal blocks
$$
\frac12C
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
-\frac12D.
$$
Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} then maps it to the prescribed off-diagonal-block matrix.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The dimension of the range is
$$
\boxed{24}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the upper-right block contributes
$$
\dim M_{4\times3}(\RR)=12
$$
independent coordinates, and the lower-left block contributes
$$
\dim M_{3\times4}(\RR)=12.
$$
Therefore
$$
\dim\operatorname{range}T
=
12+12
=24.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives the requested dimension.

:::
