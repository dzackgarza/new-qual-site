::: {.problem}
Write down as long a list as possible of pairwise nonsimilar complex $5\times5$ matrices whose characteristic polynomial is $x^5$ and whose minimal polynomial is $x^3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $J_m(0)$ denote the nilpotent Jordan block of size $m$.

<1>1. Every matrix satisfying the hypotheses is nilpotent and is similar to
a direct sum of nilpotent Jordan blocks whose sizes add to $5$.

::: {.proof}
The characteristic polynomial is $x^5$, so the only eigenvalue is $0$.
Over $\CC$, the Jordan normal form therefore consists entirely of blocks
$J_m(0)$. Their sizes add to the dimension $5$.
:::

<1>2. The condition that the minimal polynomial is $x^3$ is equivalent to
the largest Jordan block having size exactly $3$.

::: {.proof}
For a nilpotent Jordan block $J_m(0)$, the minimal polynomial is $x^m$.
For a direct sum of nilpotent Jordan blocks, the minimal polynomial is the
least common multiple of the block minimal polynomials, hence
$$
x^r,
$$
where $r$ is the largest block size. Thus the minimal polynomial is $x^3$
exactly when the largest block has size $3$.
:::

<1>3. The only partitions of $5$ whose largest part is exactly $3$ are
$$
5=3+2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
5=3+1+1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Once a part of size $3$ is present, the remaining sum is $2$. It is either
one part of size $2$ or two parts of size $1$. No other partition is
possible.
:::

<1>4. Representatives for all similarity classes are
$$
A_1=
J_3(0)\oplus J_2(0)
$$
and
$$
A_2=
J_3(0)\oplus J_1(0)\oplus J_1(0).
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1--<1>3, every matrix satisfying the hypotheses has exactly one
of these two Jordan forms. Conversely, both displayed matrices have total
size $5$, characteristic polynomial $x^5$, and largest Jordan block size
$3$, so step <1>2 gives minimal polynomial $x^3$.
:::

<1>5. The matrices $A_1$ and $A_2$ are not similar.

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
\operatorname{rank}A_1
=
\operatorname{rank}J_3(0)+\operatorname{rank}J_2(0)
=
2+1
=3,
$$
whereas
$$
\operatorname{rank}A_2
=
2.
$$
Similarity preserves rank, so $A_1$ and $A_2$ are not similar.
:::

<1>6. A longest possible list has exactly the two matrices
$$
\boxed{
J_3(0)\oplus J_2(0),
\qquad
J_3(0)\oplus0\oplus0
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 shows that every admissible matrix is similar to one of the two
displayed matrices, and step <1>5 shows that these two similarity classes
are distinct. Hence no longer pairwise nonsimilar list is possible.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 gives a maximal list.
:::
:::
