::: {.problem}
Find the maximum area among all triangles inscribed in an ellipse with semiaxes $a$ and $b$, and describe the triangles attaining the maximum.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
T:\RR^2\longrightarrow\RR^2,
\qquad
T(u,v)=(au,bv).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The map $T$ sends the unit circle bijectively onto the ellipse and
multiplies the area of every triangle by $ab$.

::: pf-proof

If
$$
u^2+v^2=1,
$$
then for $(x,y)=T(u,v)$,
$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}
=
u^2+v^2
=1.
$$
Since $a,b>0$, $T$ is invertible, so it gives a bijection from the unit
circle to the ellipse. Its determinant is
$$
\det T=ab,
$$
and an invertible linear map multiplies planar area by the absolute value of
its determinant. Thus triangle areas are multiplied by $ab$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If a triangle is inscribed in the unit circle and has angles
$A,B,C$, then its area is
$$
K=2\sin A\sin B\sin C.
$$

::: pf-proof

The circumradius is $1$. By the extended law of sines, its side lengths are
$$
2\sin A,\qquad
2\sin B,\qquad
2\sin C.
$$
For any triangle with side lengths $r,s,t$ and circumradius $R$,
$$
K=\frac{rst}{4R}.
$$
Here $R=1$, so
$$
K
=
\frac{(2\sin A)(2\sin B)(2\sin C)}4
=
2\sin A\sin B\sin C.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For
$$
A,B,C>0,
\qquad
A+B+C=\pi,
$$
one has
$$
\sin A\sin B\sin C
\leq
\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^3,
$$
with equality exactly when
$$
A=B=C=\frac{\pi}{3}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The function
$$
\log(\sin x)
$$
is strictly concave on $(0,\pi)$ because
$$
\frac{d^2}{dx^2}\log(\sin x)
=
-\csc^2x
<0.
$$
Jensen's inequality therefore gives
$$
\frac{
\log(\sin A)+\log(\sin B)+\log(\sin C)
}{3}
\leq
\log\sin\frac{A+B+C}{3}
=
\log\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
$$
Exponentiating gives the claimed product bound. Strict concavity gives
equality exactly when $A=B=C=\pi/3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The largest area of a triangle inscribed in the unit circle is
$$
\frac{3\sqrt3}{4},
$$
and the maximizing triangles are exactly the equilateral inscribed
triangles.

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}:
$$
K
\leq
2\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^3
=
\frac{3\sqrt3}{4}.
$$
Equality holds exactly when all three angles are $\pi/3$, which is exactly
the equilateral case.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The maximum area of a triangle inscribed in the ellipse is
$$
\boxed{\frac{3\sqrt3}{4}ab}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, every inscribed ellipse triangle is the image under $T$ of a
unique triangle inscribed in the unit circle, and its area is $ab$ times the
area of that circle triangle. Apply step [](#s4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The maximizing ellipse triangles are exactly those whose vertices,
for some real $t$, are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&(a\cos t,b\sin t),\\
&\left(a\cos\left(t+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right),
b\sin\left(t+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)\right),\\
&\left(a\cos\left(t+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right),
b\sin\left(t+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, the maximizing preimage triangle on the unit circle must be
equilateral. Its three vertices are separated by central angles
$2\pi/3$, so after a rotation they have parameters
$$
t,\qquad
t+\frac{2\pi}{3},\qquad
t+\frac{4\pi}{3}.
$$
Applying $T$ gives the displayed vertices. Conversely, every such triple
is the image of an equilateral unit-circle triangle and therefore attains
the maximum from step [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s5){.pf-ref} and [](#s6){.pf-ref} give the maximum area and all maximizing triangles.
