::: {.problem}
Let $a_0=1$, $a_1=-1$, and
\[
3a_n+4a_{n-1}-a_{n-2}=0\qquad(n\ge2).
\]
Find the radius of convergence of $\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n$ and the function to which it converges in its disk of convergence.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
A(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^n.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The recurrence gives the formal power-series identity
$$
(3+4z-z^2)A(z)=3+z.
$$

::: pf-proof

Multiply
$$
3a_n+4a_{n-1}-a_{n-2}=0
$$
by $z^n$ and sum over $n\geq2$. This gives
$$
3\sum_{n\geq2}a_nz^n
+4z\sum_{n\geq2}a_{n-1}z^{n-1}
-z^2\sum_{n\geq2}a_{n-2}z^{n-2}
=0.
$$
Using $a_0=1$ and $a_1=-1$,
$$
3(A-1+z)+4z(A-1)-z^2A=0.
$$
Collecting terms yields
$$
(3+4z-z^2)A=3+z.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The power series $A(z)$ is the Taylor series at $0$ of
$$
F(z)\coloneqq
\frac{3+z}{3+4z-z^2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The denominator has value $3$ at $z=0$, so $F$ is analytic in a
neighborhood of $0$ and has a Taylor series there. Multiplying that Taylor
series by $3+4z-z^2$ and comparing coefficients with
$$
(3+4z-z^2)F(z)=3+z
$$
shows that its coefficients have initial values
$$
a_0=1,\qquad a_1=-1
$$
and satisfy the given recurrence. The recurrence uniquely determines all
later coefficients, so this Taylor series is exactly $A(z)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The poles of $F$ are
$$
2+\sqrt7
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2-\sqrt7.
$$

::: pf-proof

The denominator vanishes when
$$
3+4z-z^2=0,
$$
equivalently
$$
z^2-4z-3=0.
$$
The quadratic formula gives the two displayed roots. Neither is cancelled
by the numerator $3+z$, because
$$
3+(2\pm\sqrt7)=5\pm\sqrt7\neq0.
$$
Thus both are genuine poles.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The radius of convergence is
$$
\boxed{\sqrt7-2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The radius of convergence of the Taylor series of a rational function is
the distance from the center to the nearest pole. By step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{2-\sqrt7}
=
\sqrt7-2
<
2+\sqrt7.
$$
Therefore the nearest pole has distance $\sqrt7-2$ from the origin.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

In its disk of convergence, the series sums to
$$
\boxed{
\frac{3+z}{3+4z-z^2}
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} identifies the series with the Taylor expansion of this rational
function, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} identifies its disk of convergence.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} give the requested radius and sum.

:::
