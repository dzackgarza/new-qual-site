::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a ring with identity and let $u\in R$ have a right inverse. Prove the following are equivalent:

(a) $u$ has more than one right inverse;

(b) $u$ is a zero divisor;

(c) $u$ is not a unit.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix a right inverse $v\in R$ of $u$, so
$$
uv=1.
$$

<1>1. The element $u$ cannot be a right zero divisor.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
xu=0.
$$
Multiplying on the right by $v$ gives
$$
x
=
x(uv)
=(xu)v
=0.
$$
Thus no nonzero element can annihilate $u$ on the left.
:::

<1>2. If $u$ has more than one right inverse, then $u$ is a zero divisor.

::: {.proof}
Let $w\neq v$ be another right inverse. Then
$$
uw=uv=1,
$$
so
$$
u(w-v)=0.
$$
Since $w-v\neq0$, the element $u$ annihilates a nonzero element on its
right. Hence $u$ is a zero divisor.
:::

<1>3. If $u$ is a zero divisor, then $u$ has more than one right inverse.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $u$ cannot be a right zero divisor. Therefore, under the
two-sided convention for zero divisors, there is a nonzero $x\in R$ such
that
$$
ux=0.
$$
Then
$$
u(v+x)
=
uv+ux
=1.
$$
Since $x\neq0$, one has $v+x\neq v$. Thus $u$ has at least two right
inverses.
:::

<1>4. If $u$ is a unit, then $u$ has exactly one right inverse.

::: {.proof}
If $u$ is a unit and $uw=1$, then multiplication on the left by $u^{-1}$
gives
$$
w=u^{-1}.
$$
Thus every right inverse equals the inverse of $u$.
:::

<1>5. If $u$ is not a unit, then $u$ is a zero divisor.

::: {.proof}
Since $uv=1$, the element $u$ would be a unit if also $vu=1$. Therefore
noninvertibility implies
$$
1-vu\neq0.
$$
But
$$
u(1-vu)
=
u-(uv)u
=
u-u
=0.
$$
Hence $u$ annihilates the nonzero element $1-vu$ and is a zero divisor.
:::

<1>6. Conditions (a), (b), and (c) are equivalent.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2 and <1>3 prove
$$
\text{(a)}\Longleftrightarrow\text{(b)}.
$$
Step <1>4 gives
$$
\text{(a)}\Longrightarrow\text{(c)},
$$
and step <1>5 gives
$$
\text{(c)}\Longrightarrow\text{(b)}.
$$
Together these implications make all three conditions equivalent.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required equivalence.
:::
