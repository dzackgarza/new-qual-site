::: {.problem}
Let $D$ be the open unit disk in $\mathbb R^2$ and let $u$ be a nonzero real $C^2$ function on $\overline D$, vanishing on $\partial D$, satisfying
\[
\Delta u=\lambda u.
\]
Prove
\[
\iint_D|\nabla u|^2\,dx\,dy+\lambda\iint_Du^2\,dx\,dy=0,
\]
and hence $\lambda<0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Green's first identity gives
$$
\iint_Du\,\Delta u\,dx\,dy
=
-\iint_D\norm{\nabla u}^2\,dx\,dy.
$$

::: {.proof}
Green's first identity states
$$
\iint_D
\left(
u\,\Delta u+\norm{\nabla u}^2
\right)
\,dx\,dy
=
\int_{\partial D}
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,ds.
$$
The boundary condition
$$
u=0
\qquad\text{on }\partial D
$$
makes the boundary integral zero. Rearranging gives the displayed identity.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
\iint_D\norm{\nabla u}^2\,dx\,dy
+
\lambda\iint_Du^2\,dx\,dy
=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The differential equation gives
$$
\iint_Du\,\Delta u\,dx\,dy
=
\lambda\iint_Du^2\,dx\,dy.
$$
Combine this with step <1>1 and move the gradient term to the left.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\iint_Du^2\,dx\,dy>0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The function $u$ is continuous and not identically zero, so there is a
point of $D$ at which $u^2>0$. By continuity, $u^2$ is positive on a
nonempty open neighborhood of that point. Therefore its integral over $D$
is strictly positive.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\lambda\leq0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives
$$
\lambda
=
-\frac{
\iint_D\norm{\nabla u}^2\,dx\,dy
}{
\iint_Du^2\,dx\,dy
}.
$$
The numerator is nonnegative and the denominator is positive by step
<1>3, so $\lambda\leq0$.
:::

<1>5. The equality $\lambda=0$ is impossible.

::: {.proof}
If $\lambda=0$, step <1>2 gives
$$
\iint_D\norm{\nabla u}^2\,dx\,dy=0.
$$
The continuous nonnegative function $\norm{\nabla u}^2$ must therefore
vanish identically on $D$. Hence $\nabla u=0$ throughout the connected disk
$D$, so $u$ is constant there. Continuity on $\overline D$ and the boundary
condition $u=0$ force that constant to be $0$, contradicting the assumption
that $u$ is nonzero.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\lambda<0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>4 and <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves the energy identity, and step <1>6 proves the asserted
sign of the eigenvalue.
:::
:::
