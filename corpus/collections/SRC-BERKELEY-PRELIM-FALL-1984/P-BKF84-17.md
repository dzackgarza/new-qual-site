::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be the $n\times n$ matrix over a field $F$ all of whose entries are $1$.
Find the Jordan canonical form of $M$, and discuss the extent to which the Jordan form depends on the characteristic of $F$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
u\coloneqq(1,\ldots,1)^T\in F^n.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^T\in F^n$,
$$
Mx=(x_1+\cdots+x_n)u.
$$
Consequently,
$$
\operatorname{im}M=Fu,
\qquad
\ker M=\left\{x\in F^n:x_1+\cdots+x_n=0\right\},
\qquad
M^2=(n\cdot1_F)M.
$$

::: pf-proof

Every row of $M$ is $(1,\ldots,1)$, so each coordinate of $Mx$ is $x_1+\cdots+x_n$. Since $Me_1=u\neq0$, the image is the one-dimensional space $Fu$, and the displayed description of the kernel follows from the same formula.

Applying $M$ to $u$ gives
$$
Mu=(n\cdot1_F)u.
$$
Thus for every $x$,
$$
M^2x=(x_1+\cdots+x_n)Mu=(n\cdot1_F)Mx,
$$
which proves the last identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $n\cdot1_F\neq0$, then the Jordan form of $M$ is
$$
\operatorname{diag}(n\cdot1_F,0,\ldots,0).
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
Mu=(n\cdot1_F)u,
$$
so $Fu$ is the eigenspace for the nonzero eigenvalue $n\cdot1_F$. Also $\ker M$ is the eigenspace for the eigenvalue $0$ and has dimension $n-1$.

Moreover,
$$
u\notin\ker M
$$
because the sum of the coordinates of $u$ is $n\cdot1_F\neq0$. Hence
$$
F^n=Fu\oplus\ker M.
$$
Choosing $u$ together with any basis of $\ker M$ therefore diagonalizes $M$, with the displayed diagonal form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $n\cdot1_F=0$, then the Jordan form of $M$ is
$$
J_2(0)\oplus 0_{n-2},
\qquad
J_2(0)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

In this case step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives $M^2=0$, while $M\neq0$ because $Me_1=u\neq0$. Also
$$
Mu=(n\cdot1_F)u=0,
$$
so $u\in\ker M$.

Since $\dim\ker M=n-1$, extend $u$ to a basis
$$
u,w_3,\ldots,w_n
$$
of $\ker M$. The vector $e_1$ is not in $\ker M$ because the sum of its coordinates is $1$. Hence
$$
u,e_1,w_3,\ldots,w_n
$$
is a basis of $F^n$. In this basis,
$$
Mu=0,
\qquad
Me_1=u,
\qquad
Mw_j=0,
$$
so the matrix of $M$ is exactly one block $J_2(0)$ together with $n-2$ zero $1\times1$ blocks.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The Jordan form depends on the characteristic of $F$ only through whether $n\cdot1_F$ vanishes.

::: pf-proof

If $F$ has characteristic $0$, then $n\cdot1_F\neq0$. If $F$ has positive characteristic $p$, then $n\cdot1_F=0$ exactly when $p\mid n$. Thus step [](#s2){.pf-ref} applies in characteristic $0$ and in characteristic $p\nmid n$, while step [](#s3){.pf-ref} applies precisely in characteristic $p\mid n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref} give all possible Jordan forms, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} describes precisely their dependence on the characteristic.

:::
