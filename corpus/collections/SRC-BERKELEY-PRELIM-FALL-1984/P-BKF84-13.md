::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ be a homeomorphism.
Prove that there is a sequence of polynomials $p_n$ such that $p_n\to f$ uniformly on $[0,1]$ and every $p_n$ is a homeomorphism of $[0,1]$ onto itself.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $n\geq1$, define the Bernstein polynomial
$$
p_n(x)\coloneqq
\sum_{k=0}^n
f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)
\binom{n}{k}x^k(1-x)^{n-k}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The homeomorphism $f$ is either strictly increasing with
$$
f(0)=0,
\qquad
f(1)=1,
$$
or strictly decreasing with
$$
f(0)=1,
\qquad
f(1)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

A continuous injective function on an interval is strictly monotone. Since $f$ is onto $[0,1]$, its endpoint values must be the two endpoints $0$ and $1$, in the order determined by its monotonicity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Every $p_n$ has the same strict monotonicity as $f$ and the same endpoint values.

::: pf-proof

The endpoint values follow immediately from the Bernstein formula:
$$
p_n(0)=f(0),
\qquad
p_n(1)=f(1).
$$
Differentiating and using the binomial identities gives
$$
p_n'(x)
=n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\left(
f\left(\frac{k+1}{n}\right)-f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)
\right)
\binom{n-1}{k}x^k(1-x)^{n-1-k}.
$$
The Bernstein weights in this sum are nonnegative and sum to $1$. If $f$ is strictly increasing, every displayed difference is positive, so $p_n'(x)>0$ for every $x\in[0,1]$. If $f$ is strictly decreasing, every difference is negative, so $p_n'(x)<0$ everywhere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every $p_n$ is a homeomorphism of $[0,1]$ onto itself.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $p_n$ is continuous and strictly monotone, with endpoint values equal to $0$ and $1$ in some order. Hence $p_n$ is a continuous bijection from the compact interval $[0,1]$ onto $[0,1]$. Its inverse is continuous, so $p_n$ is a homeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The sequence $p_n$ converges uniformly to $f$ on $[0,1]$.

::: pf-proof

For fixed $x\in[0,1]$, set
$$
w_{n,k}(x)\coloneqq
\binom{n}{k}x^k(1-x)^{n-k}.
$$
These weights satisfy
$$
\sum_{k=0}^n w_{n,k}(x)=1,
\qquad
\sum_{k=0}^n
\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^2w_{n,k}(x)
=\frac{x(1-x)}{n}.
$$

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $f$ is uniformly continuous, choose $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\abs{u-v}<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\abs{f(u)-f(v)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$
Because $0\leq f\leq1$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{p_n(x)-f(x)}
&\leq
\sum_{k=0}^n
w_{n,k}(x)
\abs{f(k/n)-f(x)}\\
&\leq
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
+\sum_{\abs{k/n-x}\geq\delta}w_{n,k}(x)\\
&\leq
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
+\frac1{\delta^2}
\sum_{k=0}^n
\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^2w_{n,k}(x)\\
&\leq
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac1{4n\delta^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For all sufficiently large $n$, the final term is less than $\varepsilon/2$, independently of $x$. Thus
$$
\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\abs{p_n(x)-f(x)}<\varepsilon,
$$
which proves uniform convergence.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves that every $p_n$ is a polynomial homeomorphism of $[0,1]$ onto itself, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves $p_n\to f$ uniformly.
