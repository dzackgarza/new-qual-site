::: {.problem}
Prove or give a counterexample:

1. Suppose $f,g:(0,1)\to\RR$ are $C^1$,
$$
\lim_{x\to0}f(x)=\lim_{x\to0}g(x)=0,
$$
$g$ and $g'$ never vanish, and
$$
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}=c.
$$
Must
$$
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=c?
$$

2. Answer the same question for complex-valued $f$ and $g$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For real-valued $f$ and $g$, the assertion is true:
$$
\boxed{\lim_{x\to0}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=c}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Set
$$
h(x)\coloneqq f(x)-c g(x).
$$
Then $h(x)\to0$ as $x\to0$ and
$$
\frac{h'(x)}{g'(x)}
=\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}-c
\longrightarrow0.
$$

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. For all sufficiently small $x>0$,
$$
\abs{h'(x)}\leq\varepsilon\abs{g'(x)}.
$$
Because $g'$ is continuous and never vanishes on the connected interval $(0,1)$, it has constant sign. Hence, for $0<y<x$ sufficiently small,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{h(x)-h(y)}
&\leq\int_y^x\abs{h'(t)}\,dt\\
&\leq\varepsilon\int_y^x\abs{g'(t)}\,dt\\
&=\varepsilon\abs{g(x)-g(y)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Letting $y\to0$ and using $h(y)\to0$ and $g(y)\to0$ gives
$$
\abs{h(x)}\leq\varepsilon\abs{g(x)}.
$$
Since $g(x)\neq0$,
$$
\abs{\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}-c}
=\frac{\abs{h(x)}}{\abs{g(x)}}
\leq\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $f(x)/g(x)\to c$.
:::

<1>2. For complex-valued functions the assertion is false.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
f(x)=x,
\qquad
g(x)=x e^{i/x},
\qquad
0<x<1.
$$
Both functions are $C^1$ and tend to $0$ as $x\to0$. Also $g(x)\neq0$, and
$$
g'(x)=e^{i/x}\left(1-\frac{i}{x}\right)\neq0.
$$
Moreover,
$$
\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}
=\frac{e^{-i/x}}{1-i/x},
$$
whose modulus is
$$
\frac1{\sqrt{1+x^{-2}}}
=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Thus the derivative quotient has the limit $c=0$.

On the other hand,
$$
\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=e^{-i/x}.
$$
For
$$
x_n=\frac1{2\pi n},
\qquad
y_n=\frac1{(2n+1)\pi},
$$
one has $x_n,y_n\to0$ but
$$
e^{-i/x_n}=1,
\qquad
e^{-i/y_n}=-1.
$$
Hence $f(x)/g(x)$ has no limit as $x\to0$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves the real-valued statement, while step <1>2 gives a counterexample to the complex-valued statement.
:::
:::
