::: {.problem}
Fix $0\le\theta,\varphi\le2\pi$.
Let $R:\RR^3\to\RR^3$ have matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
0&-\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}
$$
in the standard basis $\mathbf i,\mathbf j,\mathbf k$.

Let $S:\RR^3\to\RR^3$ have matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\varphi&\sin\varphi&0\\
-\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
with respect to the basis
$$
\left\{
\frac1{\sqrt2}(\mathbf i+\mathbf k),
\mathbf j,
\frac1{\sqrt2}(\mathbf i-\mathbf k)
\right\}.
$$
Prove that $T=R\circ S$ leaves a line invariant.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Both $R$ and $S$ lie in $SO(3)$; consequently $T=R\circ S$ lies in $SO(3)$.

::: pf-proof

The displayed matrix of $R$ has orthonormal columns and determinant $1$, so $R\in SO(3)$.

The basis
$$
\left\{
\frac1{\sqrt2}(\mathbf i+\mathbf k),
\mathbf j,
\frac1{\sqrt2}(\mathbf i-\mathbf k)
\right\}
$$
is orthonormal. Relative to this orthonormal basis, the displayed matrix of $S$ also has orthonormal columns and determinant $1$. Hence $S$ is an orientation-preserving orthogonal transformation, so $S\in SO(3)$.

Since $SO(3)$ is a group under composition,
$$
T=R\circ S\in SO(3).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Every $Q\in SO(3)$ has a nonzero fixed vector.

::: pf-proof

Because $Q$ is orthogonal,
$$
Q^{-1}=Q^{\mathsf T},
$$
and because $\det Q=1$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det(Q-I)
&=\det Q\,\det(I-Q^{-1})\\
&=\det(I-Q^{\mathsf T})\\
&=\det(I-Q).
\end{aligned}
$$
On the other hand, the matrices are $3\times3$, so
$$
\det(Q-I)=-\det(I-Q).
$$
Therefore $\det(I-Q)=0$. Hence there is a nonzero vector $v\in\RR^3$ such that
$$
(I-Q)v=0,
$$
or equivalently $Qv=v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$T$ leaves a line invariant.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $T\in SO(3)$. Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} therefore gives a nonzero vector $v\in\RR^3$ with $Tv=v$. The line
$$
L\coloneqq\RR v
$$
then satisfies $T(L)=L$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
