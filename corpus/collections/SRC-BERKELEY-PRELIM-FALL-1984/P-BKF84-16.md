::: {.problem}
Let $a$ be an element of a field $F$, and let $p$ be prime.
Assume that $a$ is not a $p$-th power in $F$.
Show that
$$
x^p-a
$$
is irreducible in $F[x]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. One has $a\neq0$.

::: {.proof}
If $a=0$, then $a=0^p$ would be a $p$-th power in $F$, contrary to the hypothesis.
:::

<1>2. If $x^p-a$ were reducible, then $a$ would be a $p$-th power in $F$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose $x^p-a$ is reducible. Choose a proper irreducible factor and let $\alpha$ be one of its roots in an algebraic closure of $F$. Set
$$
K\coloneqq F(\alpha),
\qquad
d\coloneqq[K:F].
$$
Since the chosen factor is proper and has positive degree,
$$
1\leq d<p.
$$
Also $\alpha^p=a$.

Let
$$
b\coloneqq N_{K/F}(\alpha)\in F.
$$
By step <1>1, $\alpha\neq0$, so $b\neq0$. Multiplicativity of the field norm gives
$$
b^p
=N_{K/F}(\alpha)^p
=N_{K/F}(\alpha^p)
=N_{K/F}(a)
=a^d.
$$

Because $p$ is prime and $1\leq d<p$, one has $\gcd(d,p)=1$. Choose integers $r,s$ such that
$$
rd+sp=1.
$$
Since $a,b\neq0$, integer powers with negative exponents are allowed in $F^\times$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
a
&=a^{rd+sp}\\
&=(a^d)^r(a^s)^p\\
&=(b^p)^r(a^s)^p\\
&=(b^r a^s)^p.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $a$ is a $p$-th power in $F$.
:::

<1>3. The polynomial $x^p-a$ is irreducible in $F[x]$.

::: {.proof}
If it were reducible, step <1>2 would contradict the hypothesis that $a$ is not a $p$-th power in $F$. Hence it is irreducible.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
