::: {.problem}
Let $P_n$ be the vector space of real polynomials of degree at most $n$, and let
\[
D:P_n\to P_n,
\qquad
D(p)=p'.
\]
Let $\pi$ be a real polynomial.
What is the minimal polynomial of the transformation $\pi(D)$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
a\coloneqq\pi(0).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The differentiation operator satisfies
$$
D^{n+1}=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D^j\neq0\quad(0\leq j\leq n).
$$

::: pf-proof

Every polynomial in $P_n$ is annihilated by $n+1$ differentiations, so $D^{n+1}=0$. Also $D^0=I\neq0$. For $1\leq j\leq n$,
$$
D^j(x^n)
=
n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1)x^{n-j},
$$
which is nonzero over $\RR$. Hence $D^j\neq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Suppose $\pi$ is nonconstant, and let $m\geq1$ be the least integer for which the coefficient of $t^m$ in $\pi(t)-a$ is nonzero. Then
$$
\pi(t)-a=t^m q(t)
$$
for a polynomial $q$ with $q(0)\neq0$, and $q(D)$ is invertible.

::: pf-proof

The factorization is the definition of $m$. Write
$$
q(t)=c+t s(t),
\qquad
c=q(0)\neq0.
$$
Then
$$
q(D)=c(I+N),
\qquad
N\coloneqq c^{-1}Ds(D).
$$
Since $N$ contains a factor $D$ and commutes with $D$, step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
N^{n+1}=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
(I+N)^{-1}
=
I-N+N^2-\cdots+(-1)^nN^n,
$$
so $q(D)$ is invertible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Under the hypotheses of step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the nilpotency index of
$$
\pi(D)-aI
$$
is
$$
s\coloneqq\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{m}\right\rceil.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\pi(D)-aI=D^m q(D).
$$
Because $D$ and $q(D)$ commute, for every $k\geq1$,
$$
(\pi(D)-aI)^k=D^{mk}q(D)^k.
$$
The factor $q(D)^k$ is invertible by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}. Hence
$$
(\pi(D)-aI)^k=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
D^{mk}=0.
$$
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, this occurs exactly when
$$
mk\geq n+1.
$$
The least such positive integer $k$ is $s=\lceil(n+1)/m\rceil$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

If $\pi$ is nonconstant, then the minimal polynomial of $\pi(D)$ is
$$
\boxed{(X-\pi(0))^{\left\lceil (n+1)/m\right\rceil}},
$$
where $m$ is the least positive degree occurring in $\pi(t)-\pi(0)$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
(\pi(D)-aI)^s=0
\qquad\text{but}\qquad
(\pi(D)-aI)^{s-1}\neq0.
$$
Thus $(X-a)^s$ annihilates $\pi(D)$. The minimal polynomial divides every annihilating polynomial, so it divides $(X-a)^s$ and hence is a power $(X-a)^j$ with $1\leq j\leq s$. The nonvanishing of the $(s-1)$-st power forces $j=s$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

If $\pi$ is constant, then the minimal polynomial of $\pi(D)$ is
$$
\boxed{X-\pi(0)}.
$$

::: pf-proof

If $\pi(t)=a$, then $\pi(D)=aI$. The nonzero polynomial $X-a$ annihilates this operator, and no nonzero constant polynomial can do so.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} give the minimal polynomial in the nonconstant and constant cases, respectively.
