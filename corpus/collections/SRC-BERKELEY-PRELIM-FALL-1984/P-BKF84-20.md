::: {.problem}
Let $f\in C^2(\mathbb R)$ be bounded with bounded second derivative.
Set
\[
A=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|f(x)|,
\qquad
B=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|f''(x)|.
\]
Prove that
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|f'(x)|\le 2\sqrt{AB}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $A=0$ or $B=0$, then
$$
f'\equiv0,
$$
so the required inequality holds.

::: {.proof}
If $A=0$, then $\abs{f(x)}=0$ for every $x$, hence $f\equiv0$.

If $B=0$, then $f''\equiv0$, so $f$ is affine:
$$
f(x)=cx+d.
$$
Since $f$ is bounded on $\RR$, one must have $c=0$. Thus in either case $f'\equiv0$, while $2\sqrt{AB}=0$.
:::

<1>2. Suppose $A>0$ and $B>0$. For every $x\in\RR$ and every $h>0$,
$$
\abs{f'(x)}
\leq
\frac{2A}{h}+\frac{Bh}{2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
$$
f(x+h)
=
f(x)+hf'(x)
+\int_0^h(h-t)f''(x+t)\,dt.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
h\abs{f'(x)}
&\leq
\abs{f(x+h)-f(x)}
+\int_0^h(h-t)\abs{f''(x+t)}\,dt\\
&\leq
2A+B\int_0^h(h-t)\,dt\\
&=
2A+\frac{Bh^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Dividing by $h$ yields the stated inequality.
:::

<1>3. If $A>0$ and $B>0$, then for every $x\in\RR$,
$$
\abs{f'(x)}\leq 2\sqrt{AB}.
$$

::: {.proof}
In step <1>2 choose
$$
h=2\sqrt{\frac AB}.
$$
Then
$$
\frac{2A}{h}=\sqrt{AB},
\qquad
\frac{Bh}{2}=\sqrt{AB},
$$
and hence
$$
\abs{f'(x)}\leq2\sqrt{AB}.
$$
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\sup_{x\in\RR}\abs{f'(x)}\leq2\sqrt{AB}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $A=0$ or $B=0$, this is step <1>1. Otherwise step <1>3 gives the bound pointwise for every $x\in\RR$, and taking the supremum preserves it.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required inequality.
:::
:::
