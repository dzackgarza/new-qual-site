::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
\frac{dx}{dt}=y,
\qquad
\frac{dy}{dt}=-ay-x^3-x^5,
\qquad a>0.
$$

1. Show that
$$
F(x,y)=\frac{y^2}{2}+\frac{x^4}{4}+\frac{x^6}{6}
$$
decreases along solutions.

2. Show that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a $\delta>0$ such that whenever
$$
\|(x(0),y(0))\|<\delta,
$$
there is a unique solution with that initial condition, defined for all $t\ge0$, satisfying
$$
\|(x(t),y(t))\|<\varepsilon
\qquad\text{for all }t\ge0.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Along every solution,
$$
\frac{d}{dt}F(x(t),y(t))=-ay(t)^2\leq0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
F_x=x^3+x^5,
\qquad
F_y=y,
$$
the differential equation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dF}{dt}
&=F_x\frac{dx}{dt}+F_y\frac{dy}{dt}\\
&=(x^3+x^5)y+y(-ay-x^3-x^5)\\
&=-ay^2\leq0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $F$ is nonincreasing along solutions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $\varepsilon>0$, the number
$$
m_\varepsilon
\coloneqq
\min_{x^2+y^2=\varepsilon^2}F(x,y)
$$
is strictly positive.

::: pf-proof

The function $F$ is continuous and
$$
F(x,y)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(x,y)=(0,0).
$$
The circle $x^2+y^2=\varepsilon^2$ is compact and does not contain the origin. Therefore $F$ attains a positive minimum on that circle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

There exists $\delta>0$ such that every solution with
$$
\norm{(x(0),y(0))}_2<\delta
$$
satisfies
$$
\norm{(x(t),y(t))}_2<\varepsilon
$$
throughout its interval of existence for $t\geq0$.

::: pf-proof

By continuity of $F$ at the origin and $F(0,0)=0$, choose $\delta>0$ so small that
$$
\norm{(x,y)}_2<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F(x,y)<m_\varepsilon.
$$
Let a solution start in this $\delta$-ball. Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
F(x(t),y(t))\leq F(x(0),y(0))<m_\varepsilon
$$
for every nonnegative $t$ in its interval of existence.

If the trajectory ever reached the circle $x^2+y^2=\varepsilon^2$, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} would give
$$
F(x(t),y(t))\geq m_\varepsilon,
$$
contradicting the preceding strict inequality. Hence the trajectory remains in the open $\varepsilon$-ball.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For the initial conditions in step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the solution is unique and defined for all $t\geq0$.

::: pf-proof

The vector field
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto(y,-ay-x^3-x^5)
$$
is polynomial, hence locally Lipschitz. Therefore every initial condition has a unique maximal local solution.

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the forward trajectory of the chosen solution remains in the bounded $\varepsilon$-ball. A solution of a locally Lipschitz vector field on $\RR^2$ that stays in a bounded set cannot have a finite maximal forward endpoint: on the compact closure of that ball the vector field is bounded and locally Lipschitz, so the standard continuation theorem extends the solution past any finite endpoint. Hence the maximal solution is defined for all $t\geq0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves part 1. Steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} give the required $\varepsilon$--$\delta$ stability, uniqueness, and global existence in part 2.

:::
