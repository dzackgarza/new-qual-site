::: {.problem}
Prove or give a counterexample: if $f$ is a continuous complex-valued function on a connected open subset of $\mathbb C$ and $f^2$ is analytic, then $f$ is analytic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $U$ be the connected open set on which $f$ is defined, and set
$$
h\coloneqq f^2.
$$

<1>1. If $h\equiv0$ on $U$, then $f$ is analytic on $U$.

::: {.proof}
If $h(z)=f(z)^2=0$ for every $z\in U$, then $f\equiv0$. Hence $f$ is analytic.
:::

<1>2. Suppose $h\not\equiv0$. At every point $z_0\in U$ with $f(z_0)\neq0$, the function $f$ is complex differentiable and
$$
f'(z_0)=\frac{h'(z_0)}{2f(z_0)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By continuity of $f$, for all $z$ sufficiently close to $z_0$ one has
$$
f(z)+f(z_0)\neq0.
$$
For such $z\neq z_0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{f(z)-f(z_0)}{z-z_0}
&=
\frac{f(z)^2-f(z_0)^2}
{(z-z_0)(f(z)+f(z_0))}\\
&=
\frac{h(z)-h(z_0)}{z-z_0}
\frac1{f(z)+f(z_0)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As $z\to z_0$, the first factor tends to $h'(z_0)$ because $h$ is analytic, while the second tends to $1/(2f(z_0))$ by continuity of $f$. Thus the difference quotient of $f$ has the displayed limit.
:::

<1>3. Under the hypothesis $h\not\equiv0$, the function $f$ is analytic at every zero of $h$.

::: {.proof}
Let $a\in U$ satisfy $h(a)=0$. Since $h$ is a nonzero analytic function on the connected open set $U$, its zeros are isolated. Choose $r>0$ such that
$$
\overline{D(a,r)}\subset U
$$
and $a$ is the only zero of $h$ in $D(a,r)$.

Because $h(z)=f(z)^2$, the functions $h$ and $f$ have the same zero set. Hence $f(z)\neq0$ on
$$
D(a,r)\setminus\{a\}.
$$
Step <1>2 therefore shows that $f$ is analytic on this punctured disk.

Continuity of $f$ at $a$ makes $f$ bounded on a sufficiently small disk about $a$. The removable singularity theorem then gives an analytic extension across $a$. Since the extension agrees with $f$ away from $a$, its value at $a$ is the limit
$$
\lim_{z\to a}f(z)=f(a)=0
$$
by continuity. Thus the extension is exactly $f$, so $f$ is analytic at $a$.
:::

<1>4. The assertion is true: $f$ is analytic on $U$.

::: {.proof}
If $h\equiv0$, this is step <1>1. Otherwise, step <1>2 proves analyticity at every point where $f$ is nonzero, and step <1>3 proves analyticity at every zero. These two sets exhaust $U$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
