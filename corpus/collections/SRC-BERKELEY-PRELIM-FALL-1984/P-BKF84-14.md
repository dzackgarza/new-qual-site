::: {.problem}
Evaluate:

1.
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(1+x+x^2)^2};
$$

2.
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+\sin\theta},
\qquad a>1.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The first integral is
$$
\boxed{
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(1+x+x^2)^2}
=\frac{4\pi}{3\sqrt3}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Complete the square and set
$$
u=\frac{2x+1}{\sqrt3}.
$$
Then
$$
x^2+x+1
=\left(x+\frac12\right)^2+\frac34
=\frac34(1+u^2),
\qquad
dx=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\,du.
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(1+x+x^2)^2}
=\frac{8\sqrt3}{9}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{du}{(1+u^2)^2}.
$$
With $u=\tan t$,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{du}{(1+u^2)^2}
=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^2t\,dt
=\frac\pi2.
$$
Hence
$$
\frac{8\sqrt3}{9}\cdot\frac\pi2
=\frac{4\pi\sqrt3}{9}
=\frac{4\pi}{3\sqrt3}.
$$
:::

<1>2. For $a>1$,
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+\sin\theta}
=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-1}}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Split the integral at $\theta=\pi$ and on each interval set
$$
t=\tan\frac\theta2.
$$
Then
$$
\sin\theta=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},
\qquad
d\theta=\frac{2\,dt}{1+t^2}.
$$
On $(0,\pi)$, $t$ runs from $0$ to $+\infty$, while on $(\pi,2\pi)$ it runs from $-\infty$ to $0$. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+\sin\theta}
&=2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{dt}{a(1+t^2)+2t}\\
&=\frac2a\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{dt}{(t+1/a)^2+(a^2-1)/a^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $a>1$, set
$$
b\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{a^2-1}}{a}>0.
$$
Using
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(t-c)^2+b^2}=\frac\pi b,
$$
one obtains
$$
\frac2a\cdot\frac\pi b
=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-1}}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 give the two requested values.
