::: {.problem}
Let $S _ { n }$ denote the group of permutations of n objects. Find four different subgroups of $S _ { 4 }$ isomorphic to $S _ { 3 }$ and nine isomorphic to $S _ { 2 }$
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Four subgroups isomorphic to $S_3$ are the point stabilizers.
::: {.proof}
For each $i\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, let
\[
H_i=\operatorname{Stab}_{S_4}(i)
=\{\sigma\in S_4:\sigma(i)=i\}.
\]
Each $H_i$ permutes the remaining three symbols arbitrarily, so restriction to
\(
\{1,2,3,4\}\setminus\{i\}
\)
gives an isomorphism
\[
H_i\cong S_3.
\]
The four subgroups are distinct because $H_i$ fixes $i$, while for $j\ne i$ the subgroup $H_j$ contains permutations moving $i$.

Explicitly,
\[
H_1\cong S_{\{2,3,4\}},\quad
H_2\cong S_{\{1,3,4\}},\quad
H_3\cong S_{\{1,2,4\}},\quad
H_4\cong S_{\{1,2,3\}}.
\]
:::

<1>2. Nine subgroups isomorphic to $S_2$ are generated by the nine involutions in $S_4$.
::: {.proof}
A subgroup isomorphic to $S_2$ has order $2$, hence is generated by its unique nonidentity element, which must have order $2$.

The elements of order $2$ in $S_4$ are exactly the six transpositions
\[
(12),(13),(14),(23),(24),(34)
\]
and the three double transpositions
\[
(12)(34),\qquad(13)(24),\qquad(14)(23).
\]
Therefore the following nine distinct subgroups are all isomorphic to $S_2$:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\langle(12)\rangle,
\langle(13)\rangle,
\langle(14)\rangle,
\langle(23)\rangle,
\langle(24)\rangle,
\langle(34)\rangle,\\
\langle(12)(34)\rangle,
\langle(13)(24)\rangle,
\langle(14)(23)\rangle.
\end{gathered}
\]
They are distinct because an order-two subgroup is determined by its unique nonidentity element.
:::
