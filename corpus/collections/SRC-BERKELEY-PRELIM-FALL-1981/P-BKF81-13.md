::: problem
Let $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ be $C^2$. Prove that every nondegenerate critical point of $f$ is isolated.
:::

::: solution
Let $x_0\in\mathbb R^n$ be a nondegenerate critical point of $f$. Define
$$
G=\nabla f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n.
$$
Since $f$ is $C^2$, the map $G$ is $C^1$. Moreover
$$
G(x_0)=0
$$
because $x_0$ is critical.

<1>1. The derivative of $G$ at $x_0$ is invertible.
::: proof
The Jacobian matrix of $G=\nabla f$ is precisely the Hessian matrix of $f$:
$$
DG(x_0)
=\left(
\frac{\partial^2 f}
{\partial x_i\partial x_j}(x_0)
\right)_{i,j}.
$$
By nondegeneracy of the critical point, this matrix is nonsingular.
:::

<1>2. The gradient has no other zero sufficiently near $x_0$.
::: proof
By the inverse function theorem, there is an open neighborhood $U$ of
$x_0$ such that
$$
G|_U:U\to G(U)
$$
is one-to-one. If $x\in U$ is another critical point, then
$$
G(x)=0=G(x_0).
$$
Injectivity of $G|_U$ forces
$$
x=x_0.
$$
Therefore $x_0$ is the only critical point in $U$, so it is isolated.
:::
:::
