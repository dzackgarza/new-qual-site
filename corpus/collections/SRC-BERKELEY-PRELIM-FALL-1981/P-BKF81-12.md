::: {.problem}
Let
\[
u(x,y)=\frac{a^2+b^2-x^2-y^2}{(a-x)^2+(b-y)^2}.
\]
Show that $u$ is harmonic on $\RR^2\setminus\{(a,b)\}$, and find a holomorphic function on $\CC\setminus\{a+ib\}$ whose real part is $u$. For which $(a,b)$ is $u$ the real part of an entire function?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
D(x,y)\coloneqq(a-x)^2+(b-y)^2,
\qquad
c\coloneqq a+ib,
\qquad
\Omega\coloneqq\CC\setminus\{c\}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #modified-u-formula}
On $\Omega$,
$$
u(x,y)
=
\operatorname{Re}
\left(
\frac{c+z}{c-z}
\right),
\qquad z=x+iy.
$$

::: pf-proof
Since
$$
\abs{c-z}^2=D(x,y),
$$
one has
$$
\frac{c+z}{c-z}
=
\frac{(c+z)(\overline c-\overline z)}
{\abs{c-z}^2}.
$$
The real part of the numerator is
$$
\operatorname{Re}
\bigl(
(c+z)(\overline c-\overline z)
\bigr)
=
\abs{c}^2-\abs{z}^2
=
a^2+b^2-x^2-y^2,
$$
because
$$
z\overline c-c\overline z
$$
is purely imaginary. Dividing by $D(x,y)$ gives the stated identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #harmonic}
The function $u$ is harmonic on $\RR^2\setminus\{(a,b)\}$, and it is the real part of the holomorphic function
$$
\boxed{
F(z)=\frac{a+ib+z}{a+ib-z}
}
$$
on $\Omega$.

::: pf-proof
The function $F$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$. By step [](#modified-u-formula){.pf-ref},
$u=\operatorname{Re}F$ there, and the real part of a holomorphic function is harmonic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #no-entire-nonzero-case}
If $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, no entire function has real part $u$ on $\Omega$.

::: pf-proof
When $c\ne0$,
$$
F(z)
=
-1+\frac{2c}{c-z}
$$
has a simple pole at $z=c$, so $u=\operatorname{Re}F$ is unbounded in every punctured
neighborhood of $c$. The real part of an entire function is continuous and
finite at $c$, so it cannot agree with $u$ on $\Omega$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #extends-zero-case}
If $(a,b)=(0,0)$, then $u$ is the real part of an entire function.

::: pf-proof
For $c=0$ and $z\ne0$,
$$
F(z)=\frac z{-z}=-1.
$$
Hence $u=-1$ on $\Omega$, and the constant entire function $-1$ has real part $u$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#harmonic){.pf-ref} proves harmonicity and gives the holomorphic function, and steps [](#no-entire-nonzero-case){.pf-ref} and [](#extends-zero-case){.pf-ref} show that $u$ is the real part of an entire function exactly when $\boxed{(a,b)=(0,0)}$.
:::
