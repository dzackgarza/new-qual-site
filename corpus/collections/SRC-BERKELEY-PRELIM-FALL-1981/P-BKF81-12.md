::: {.problem}
Let
\[
u(x,y)=\frac{a^2+b^2+x^2-y^2}{(a-x)^2+(b-y)^2}.
\]
Show that $u$ is harmonic. The original problem asks for an entire function whose real part is $u$, but the paper corrects this: explain why this is impossible, modify $u$ slightly, and solve the corrected problem.
:::

::: {.remark}
The problem is false as printed: the displayed $u$ is not harmonic.
On the complement of $(a,b)$,
\[
\Delta u=\frac{4\left(a^2+b^2+2ax-x^2-2by+y^2\right)}{\left((a-x)^2+(b-y)^2\right)^2},
\]
which is not identically zero; for $a=1$, $b=0$ it equals $4$ at the origin.
The printed correction also rules out an entire function with real part $u$, since $u$ is unbounded near $(a,b)$ whenever $a\neq0$, but it does not say which modification of $u$ is intended, and the source supplies no corrected formula.
The corrected problem is therefore not recoverable from the source.
:::
