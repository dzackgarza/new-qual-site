::: problem
Let $A,B$ be real $n\times n$ matrices. Suppose $A=UBU^{-1}$ for some complex invertible matrix $U$. Show that $A=VBV^{-1}$ for some real invertible matrix $V$.
:::

::: solution
The similarity relation is equivalent to
$$
AU=UB.
$$
Write
$$
U=C+iD,
$$
where $C,D$ are real $n\times n$ matrices.

<1>1. Both real and imaginary parts intertwine $A$ and $B$.
::: proof
Because $A$ and $B$ are real,
$$
A(C+iD)=(C+iD)B
$$
becomes
$$
AC+iAD=CB+iDB.
$$
Equality of real and imaginary parts gives
$$
AC=CB,
\qquad
AD=DB.
$$
Therefore for every real scalar $t$,
$$
A(C+tD)=(C+tD)B.
$$
:::

<1>2. Some real linear combination $C+tD$ is invertible.
::: proof
Consider the real polynomial
$$
p(t)=\det(C+tD).
$$
It is not the zero polynomial, because evaluating it at the complex number
$t=i$ gives
$$
p(i)=\det(C+iD)=\det U\ne0.
$$
A nonzero real polynomial has only finitely many real roots. Hence there is
some real $t_0$ such that
$$
p(t_0)\ne0.
$$
Thus
$$
V=C+t_0D
$$
is a real invertible matrix.
:::

<1>3. The real matrix $V$ gives the required similarity.
::: proof
By step <1>1,
$$
AV=VB.
$$
Since $V$ is invertible,
$$
\boxed{A=VBV^{-1}.}
$$
Thus real matrices which are similar over $\mathbb C$ are already similar
over $\mathbb R$.
:::
:::
