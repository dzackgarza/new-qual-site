::: problem
Let $G$ be a group of order $10$ with a normal subgroup of order $2$. Prove that $G$ is abelian.
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
H\trianglelefteq G,
\qquad |H|=2.
$$

<1>1. The subgroup of order $5$ is unique and normal.
::: proof
By Sylow's theorem, the number $n_5$ of Sylow $5$-subgroups satisfies
$$
n_5\equiv1\pmod5
$$
and
$$
n_5\mid2.
$$
The only possibility is
$$
n_5=1.
$$
Thus there is a unique subgroup
$$
K\le G,
\qquad |K|=5,
$$
and uniqueness makes $K$ normal in $G$.
:::

<1>2. The two normal subgroups commute elementwise.
::: proof
Since $|H|=2$ and $|K|=5$,
$$
H\cap K=\{e\}.
$$
For $h\in H$ and $k\in K$, the commutator
$$
[h,k]=hkh^{-1}k^{-1}
$$
belongs to $H$ because $H$ is normal, and belongs to $K$ because $K$ is
normal. Hence
$$
[h,k]\in H\cap K=\{e\}.
$$
Thus every element of $H$ commutes with every element of $K$.
:::

<1>3. The subgroups generate all of $G$ and are abelian.
::: proof
Because $H\cap K=\{e\}$,
$$
|HK|=\frac{|H||K|}{|H\cap K|}=10=|G|.
$$
Hence
$$
G=HK.
$$
The group $H$ has prime order $2$, so it is cyclic; likewise $K$ has prime
order $5$, so it is cyclic. Thus both are abelian, and step <1>2 shows they
commute with each other. Therefore
$$
G\cong H\times K\cong C_2\times C_5,
$$
which is abelian. Hence
$$
\boxed{G\text{ is abelian}.}
$$
:::
:::
