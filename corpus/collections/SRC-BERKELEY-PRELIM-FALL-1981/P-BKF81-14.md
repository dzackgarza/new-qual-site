::: problem
Let $V:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ be $C^1$ and let $x''_i=-\partial V/\partial x_i$ on a finite interval $(a,b)$.

(a) Show $H(t)=\frac12\langle x'(t),x'(t)\rangle+V(x(t))$ is constant.

(b) If $V\ge M> -\infty$, show $x,x',x''$ are bounded on $(a,b)$ and prove the limits of all three as $t\to b$ exist.
:::

::: solution
Write
$$
x(t)=(x_1(t),\ldots,x_n(t)).
$$
The system can be written vectorially as
$$
x''(t)=-\nabla V(x(t)).
$$

<1>1. The energy $H$ is constant.
::: proof
Differentiate
$$
H(t)=\frac12\|x'(t)\|^2+V(x(t)).
$$
Using the chain rule and the differential equation,
$$
\begin{aligned}
H'(t)
&=\langle x''(t),x'(t)\rangle
  +\langle\nabla V(x(t)),x'(t)\rangle\\
&=\langle-\nabla V(x(t)),x'(t)\rangle
  +\langle\nabla V(x(t)),x'(t)\rangle\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{H(t)=H_0}
$$
is constant on $(a,b)$.
:::

<1>2. A lower bound on $V$ gives a uniform bound on $x'$.
::: proof
Assume
$$
V(x)\ge M
$$
for all $x\in\mathbb R^n$. From energy conservation,
$$
\frac12\|x'(t)\|^2
=H_0-V(x(t))
\le H_0-M.
$$
Hence
$$
\|x'(t)\|\le C:=\sqrt{2(H_0-M)}
$$
for all $t\in(a,b)$.
:::

<1>3. The trajectory $x(t)$ is bounded and has a limit as $t\to b$.
::: proof
Fix $t_0\in(a,b)$. For $t\in[t_0,b)$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|x(t)-x(t_0)\|
&\le\int_{t_0}^t\|x'(s)\|\,ds\\
&\le C(b-a).
\end{aligned}
$$
The same estimate, with the endpoints of the integral reversed when
$t<t_0$, gives
$$
\|x(t)-x(t_0)\|\le C|t-t_0|\le C(b-a)
$$
for every $t\in(a,b)$. Thus $x$ is bounded on the whole interval.

Moreover, if $s,t\to b$, then
$$
\|x(s)-x(t)\|
\le C|s-t|\longrightarrow0.
$$
Thus $x(t)$ is Cauchy as $t\to b$. Completeness of $\mathbb R^n$ gives some
$$
c\in\mathbb R^n
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{x(t)\to c.}
$$
:::

<1>4. The acceleration is bounded.
::: proof
The set
$$
K=\overline{\{x(t):t\in(a,b)\}}
$$
is closed and bounded in $\mathbb R^n$, hence compact. Since $V$ is $C^1$,
$\nabla V$ is continuous and therefore bounded on $K$. Thus there is a
constant $D$ such that
$$
\|\nabla V(y)\|\le D
\qquad(y\in K).
$$
Using the equation of motion,
$$
\boxed{\|x''(t)\|\le D.}
$$
:::

<1>5. The velocity has a limit as $t\to b$.
::: proof
For $s,t\in(a,b)$,
$$
\|x'(s)-x'(t)\|
\le\int_{\min(s,t)}^{\max(s,t)}\|x''(r)\|\,dr
\le D|s-t|.
$$
Hence $x'(t)$ is Cauchy as $t\to b$. Therefore there is some
$$
v\in\mathbb R^n
$$
with
$$
\boxed{x'(t)\to v.}
$$
The uniform bound from step <1>2 already shows that $x'$ is bounded.
:::

<1>6. The acceleration also has a limit.
::: proof
By step <1>3 and continuity of $\nabla V$,
$$
\nabla V(x(t))\longrightarrow\nabla V(c).
$$
Since
$$
x''(t)=-\nabla V(x(t)),
$$
we obtain
$$
\boxed{x''(t)\longrightarrow-\nabla V(c).}
$$
Thus $x,x',x''$ are all bounded on $(a,b)$ and all three have limits as
$t\to b$.
:::
