::: problem
Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be a differentiable real matrix-valued function with $\det A>0$, and let $B=(b_{ij})=A^{-1}$. Prove
\[
\frac d{dt}\log\det A=\sum_{i,j=1}^n\frac{da_{ij}}{dt}b_{ji}.
\]
:::

::: solution
Because
$$
\det A(t)>0,
$$
the matrix $A(t)$ is invertible and the real logarithm of its determinant is
defined.

<1>1. Differentiate the determinant entry by entry.
::: proof
Let $C_{ij}$ denote the $(i,j)$ cofactor of $A$. The determinant is a
polynomial in the entries $a_{ij}$, and
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{ij}}\det A=C_{ij}.
$$
Thus the chain rule gives
$$
\frac d{dt}\det A
=\sum_{i,j=1}^n C_{ij}\frac{da_{ij}}{dt}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Express the cofactors using $A^{-1}$.
::: proof
The adjugate formula says
$$
A^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det A}\operatorname{adj}(A),
$$
and the $(j,i)$ entry of $\operatorname{adj}(A)$ is $C_{ij}$. Since
$B=A^{-1}$,
$$
b_{ji}=\frac{C_{ij}}{\det A}.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
C_{ij}=(\det A)b_{ji}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Differentiate the logarithm.
::: proof
By steps <1>1--<1>2,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac d{dt}\log\det A
&=\frac1{\det A}\frac d{dt}\det A\\
&=\frac1{\det A}
\sum_{i,j}C_{ij}\frac{da_{ij}}{dt}\\
&=\sum_{i,j}
\frac{da_{ij}}{dt}b_{ji}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
\frac d{dt}\log\det A
=\sum_{i,j=1}^n\frac{da_{ij}}{dt}b_{ji}.}
$$
:::
:::
