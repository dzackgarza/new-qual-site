::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be continuous on $[0,1]$. Evaluate
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1x^nf(x)\,dx
\]
and
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\int_0^1x^nf(x)\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Since $f$ is continuous on the compact interval $[0,1]$, it is bounded. Put
$$
M=\max_{0\le x\le1}|f(x)|.
$$

<1>1. The first limit is zero.
::: {.proof}
We have
$$
\left|\int_0^1x^nf(x)\,dx\right|
\le M\int_0^1x^n\,dx
=\frac{M}{n+1}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1x^nf(x)\,dx=0.}
$$
:::

<1>2. Reduce the second limit to an error term around $f(1)$.
::: {.proof}
Write
$$
n\int_0^1x^nf(x)\,dx
=f(1)\frac{n}{n+1}
+n\int_0^1x^n(f(x)-f(1))\,dx.
$$
The first term tends to $f(1)$. It remains to prove that the second term
tends to zero.
:::

<1>3. The error term tends to zero.
::: {.proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$. By continuity of $f$ at $1$, choose
$0<\delta<1$ such that
$$
|f(x)-f(1)|<\varepsilon
$$
whenever $1-\delta\le x\le1$.

Let
$$
C=\max_{0\le x\le1}|f(x)-f(1)|.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|n\int_0^1x^n(f(x)-f(1))\,dx\right|
&\le nC\int_0^{1-\delta}x^n\,dx
 +n\varepsilon\int_{1-\delta}^1x^n\,dx\\
&\le C\frac{n}{n+1}(1-\delta)^{n+1}
 +\varepsilon\frac{n}{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first term tends to zero, and the second is at most $\varepsilon$.
Since $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, the error tends to zero.
:::

<1>4. Conclude the second limit.
::: {.proof}
Combining steps <1>2--<1>3,
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\int_0^1x^nf(x)\,dx=f(1).}
$$
:::
:::
