::: problem
Let $f$ be a holomorphic self-map of the unit disk which is not the identity. Show that $f$ has at most one fixed point.
:::

::: solution
Suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ has two distinct fixed points
$$
a,b\in\mathbb D.
$$
Let
$$
\phi_a(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-\overline a z},
$$
the disk automorphism sending $a$ to $0$. Define
$$
g=\phi_a\circ f\circ\phi_a^{-1}.
$$
Then $g:\mathbb D\to\mathbb D$ is holomorphic and
$$
g(0)=0.
$$

<1>1. The second fixed point gives a nonzero fixed point of $g$.
::: proof
Set
$$
c=\phi_a(b).
$$
Since $a\ne b$, we have $c\ne0$. Also
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(c)
&=\phi_a\bigl(f(\phi_a^{-1}(c))\bigr)\\
&=\phi_a(f(b))\\
&=\phi_a(b)\\
&=c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $g$ fixes both $0$ and the nonzero point $c$.
:::

<1>2. Schwarz's lemma forces $g$ to be the identity.
::: proof
Since $g$ is a holomorphic self-map of the disk with $g(0)=0$, Schwarz's
lemma gives
$$
|g(z)|\le|z|
$$
for every $z\in\mathbb D$. At the nonzero point $c$ from step <1>1,
$$
|g(c)|=|c|.
$$
The equality case of Schwarz's lemma therefore implies
$$
g(z)=e^{i\theta}z
$$
for some real $\theta$. Since $g(c)=c$ and $c\ne0$,
$$
e^{i\theta}=1.
$$
Hence
$$
g(z)=z
$$
throughout $\mathbb D$.
:::

<1>3. Conclude the contradiction.
::: proof
From
$$
g=\phi_a\circ f\circ\phi_a^{-1}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb D},
$$
we obtain
$$
f=\phi_a^{-1}\circ g\circ\phi_a
=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb D},
$$
contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore a nonidentity holomorphic self-map of
the unit disk has at most one fixed point.
:::
:::
