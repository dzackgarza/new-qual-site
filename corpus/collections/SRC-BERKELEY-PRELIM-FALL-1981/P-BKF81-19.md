::: {.problem}
Prove or disprove each statement.

(a) If $g(t)\to b$ as $t\to a$ and $f(t)\to c$ as $t\to b$, then $f(g(t))\to c$ as $t\to a$.

(b) If $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ is continuous and $U$ is open, then $f(U)$ is open.

(c) If $f\in C^\infty(-1,1)$ and $|f^{(n)}(x)|\le1$ for all $n\ge1$ and $x\in(-1,1)$, then $f$ is real analytic.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Statement (a) is false.
::: {.proof}
Take
$$
a=b=c=0,
$$
and define
$$
g(t)=0
$$
for every $t$. Then
$$
g(t)\to0
$$
as $t\to0$.

Define
$$
f(s)=
\begin{cases}
1,&s=0,\\
0,&s\ne0.
\end{cases}
$$
Then
$$
f(s)\to0
$$
as $s\to0$, because a limit at $0$ does not depend on the value $f(0)$.
But
$$
f(g(t))=f(0)=1
$$
for all $t$, so
$$
f(g(t))\not\to0.
$$
Thus (a) is false.
:::

<1>2. Statement (b) is false.
::: {.proof}
Take the continuous constant function
$$
f(x)=0
$$
on $\mathbb R$ and the open set
$$
U=(0,1).
$$
Then
$$
f(U)=\{0\},
$$
which is not open in $\mathbb R$. Hence a continuous real-valued function
need not be an open map.
:::

<1>3. Statement (c) is true.
::: {.proof}
Fix
$$
x_0\in(-1,1).
$$
For any $x\in(-1,1)$ and integer $N\ge0$, Taylor's theorem with remainder
gives a point $\xi_N$ between $x_0$ and $x$ such that
$$
f(x)
=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{f^{(k)}(x_0)}{k!}(x-x_0)^k
+\frac{f^{(N+1)}(\xi_N)}{(N+1)!}(x-x_0)^{N+1}.
$$
The hypothesis gives
$$
|f^{(N+1)}(\xi_N)|\le1,
$$
so the remainder satisfies
$$
|R_N(x)|
\le\frac{|x-x_0|^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}.
$$
For fixed $x$, the right-hand side tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$.
Therefore
$$
f(x)
=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{f^{(k)}(x_0)}{k!}(x-x_0)^k
$$
for every $x\in(-1,1)$. In particular, $f$ agrees with its Taylor series in
a neighborhood of every $x_0$, so $f$ is real analytic.

Thus the answers are
$$
\boxed{\text{(a) false,\qquad (b) false,\qquad (c) true}.}
$$
:::
