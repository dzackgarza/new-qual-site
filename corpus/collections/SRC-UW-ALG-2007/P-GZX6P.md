::: problem
Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero and $f\in K[x]$ an irreducible polynomial of degree $n$.
Let $L$ be a splitting field for $f$.
Let $G$ be the group of automorphisms of $L$ which act trivially on $K$.

- Show that $G$ embeds in the symmetric group $S_n$.

- For each $n$, give an example of a field $K$ and polynomial $f$ such that $G=S_n$.

- What are the possible groups $G$ when $n=3$.
  Justify your answer.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. The natural action of $G$ on the roots of $f$ defines an injective homomorphism
\[
G\hookrightarrow S_n.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $\operatorname{char}K=0$, the irreducible polynomial $f$ is separable, so it has $n$ distinct roots in its splitting field $L$. Every $K$-automorphism $\sigma$ of $L$ sends a root $\alpha$ of $f$ to another root, since
\[
f(\sigma(\alpha))=\sigma(f(\alpha))=0.
\]
Thus $G$ acts on the set of $n$ roots and yields a homomorphism $G\to S_n$.

If an automorphism lies in the kernel, it fixes every root of $f$. Since $L$ is generated over $K$ by those roots, it fixes all of $L$. Hence the kernel is trivial, so the homomorphism is injective.
:::

<1>2. For every positive integer $n$, there is an example with Galois group $S_n$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
L=\mathbb Q(x_1,\dots,x_n)
\]
be the rational function field in $n$ algebraically independent variables. Let $S_n$ act on $L$ by permuting the variables, and define
\[
K=L^{S_n}.
\]
By Artin's fixed-field theorem for a finite group of field automorphisms,
\[
L/K
\]
is a finite Galois extension and
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)=S_n.
\]

Now set
\[
f(T)=\prod_{i=1}^n(T-x_i).
\]
Its coefficients are symmetric rational functions in the $x_i$, hence are fixed by $S_n$ and therefore lie in $K$. Thus $f\in K[T]$. It splits in $L$, and
\[
L=K(x_1,\dots,x_n),
\]
so $L$ is its splitting field.

It remains to show that $f$ is irreducible over $K$. Let $m(T)$ be the minimal polynomial of $x_1$ over $K$. For every $i$, choose $\sigma\in S_n$ with $\sigma(1)=i$. Since $\sigma$ fixes $K$,
\[
m(x_i)=m(\sigma(x_1))=\sigma(m(x_1))=0.
\]
Thus the $n$ distinct elements $x_1,\dots,x_n$ are all roots of $m$, so
\[
\deg m\ge n.
\]
But $m$ divides the degree-$n$ polynomial $f$, hence $\deg m=n$ and $m=f$. Therefore $f$ is irreducible and its Galois group is $S_n$.
:::

<1>3. If $n=3$, the only possibilities are
\[
G\cong A_3\cong C_3
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
G\cong S_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $G$ is a subgroup of $S_3$. Because $f$ is irreducible and separable, $G$ acts transitively on its three roots: any $K$-embedding from the field generated by one root to the field generated by another extends to a $K$-automorphism of the normal splitting field $L$.

For a transitive action on three points, orbit-stabilizer implies that $3$ divides $|G|$. Since $G\le S_3$, Lagrange's theorem gives $|G|\mid6$. Hence
\[
|G|=3\quad\text{or}\quad |G|=6.
\]
The subgroup of order $3$ is $A_3$, and a subgroup of order $6$ is all of $S_3$.
:::

<1>4. Both cubic possibilities occur over $\mathbb Q$.
::: {.proof}
For $S_3$, take
\[
f(x)=x^3-2.
\]
It is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by Eisenstein at $2$. If $\alpha=\sqrt[3]{2}$ and $\zeta_3$ is a primitive cube root of unity, its splitting field is
\[
\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta_3).
\]
The field $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ is real of degree $3$, while $\zeta_3\notin\mathbb R$, so adjoining $\zeta_3$ has degree $2$. Thus the splitting field has degree $6$, and its Galois group, being a subgroup of $S_3$ of order $6$, is $S_3$.

For $A_3$, take
\[
g(x)=x^3-3x-1.
\]
It is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by the rational-root test. Its discriminant is
\[
\Delta=-4(-3)^3-27(-1)^2=81=9^2.
\]
If $r_1,r_2,r_3$ are its roots, then
\[
\delta=(r_1-r_2)(r_1-r_3)(r_2-r_3)
\]
satisfies $\delta^2=\Delta$. Since $\Delta=81$, we have $\delta=\pm9\in\mathbb Q$. An automorphism permuting the roots multiplies $\delta$ by the sign of the induced permutation. Because every Galois automorphism fixes $\delta$, every induced permutation is even. Hence the Galois group lies in $A_3$. By <1>3 it is transitive, so its order is divisible by $3$; therefore it is exactly $A_3$.
:::
