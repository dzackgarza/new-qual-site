::: problem
Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero and $L$ a Galois extension of $K$.
Let $f$ be an irreducible polynomial in $K[x]$ of degree 7 and suppose $f$ has no zeroes in $L$.
Show that $f$ is irreducible in $L[x]$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Let $\alpha$ be a root of $f$ in an algebraic closure of $L$, and set
\[
E=K(\alpha).
\]
Then
\[
[E:K]=7.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $f$ is irreducible over $K$ and has degree $7$, it is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K$. Therefore
\[
[E:K]=\deg f=7.
\]
:::

<1>2. If $f$ were reducible over $L$, then it would be reducible over some finite Galois intermediate extension
\[
K\subseteq M\subseteq L.
\]
::: {.proof}
A nontrivial factorization of $f$ in $L[x]$ uses only finitely many coefficients from $L$. Let $N/K$ be the finite extension generated by those coefficients. Since $L/K$ is Galois, hence algebraic, normal, and separable, the normal closure over $K$ of the finite extension $N/K$ is a finite Galois extension $M/K$ contained in $L$. The same factorization is then defined over $M$, so $f$ is reducible in $M[x]$.
:::

<1>3. For every finite Galois intermediate extension $M/K$ inside $L$,
\[
[M(\alpha):M]=[E:E\cap M].
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the standard degree formula for a compositum when one of the two finite extensions is Galois over the base: since $M/K$ is Galois and $E/K$ is finite,
\[
[EM:M]=[E:E\cap M].
\]
Here $EM=M(\alpha)$ because $E=K(\alpha)$.
:::

<1>4. For every such $M$,
\[
E\cap M=K.
\]
::: {.proof}
By the tower law,
\[
[E:K]=[E:E\cap M][E\cap M:K].
\]
The left side is the prime number $7$ by <1>1. Hence
\[
[E\cap M:K]\in\{1,7\}.
\]
If this degree were $7$, then $E\cap M=E$, so $E\subseteq M\subseteq L$. In particular $\alpha\in L$, contradicting the hypothesis that $f$ has no zero in $L$. Therefore
\[
[E\cap M:K]=1,
\]
so $E\cap M=K$.
:::

<1>5. Hence for every finite Galois $M/K$ contained in $L$,
\[
[M(\alpha):M]=7.
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>3 and <1>4:
\[
[M(\alpha):M]
=[E:E\cap M]
=[E:K]
=7.
\]
Thus the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $M$ has degree $7$. Since it divides the degree-$7$ polynomial $f$, it must equal $f$. Therefore $f$ is irreducible over $M$.
:::

<1>6. The polynomial $f$ is irreducible in $L[x]$.
::: {.proof}
If $f$ were reducible over $L$, <1>2 would give a finite Galois intermediate field $M$ over which $f$ is reducible, contradicting <1>5.
:::
:::
