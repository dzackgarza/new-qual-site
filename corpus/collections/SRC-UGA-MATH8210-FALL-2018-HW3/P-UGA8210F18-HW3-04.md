::: {.problem}
Let $f \colon \RR^{m} \to \RR^{m}$ be a proper map.
In class we showed that $\deg(f) = \sum_{p \in f^{-1}(q)} \pm 1$ where the signs depend on $p$.

(a) Show that if $f \simeq g$ then $\deg(f) = \deg(g)$ using differential forms.

(b) The degree of $f$ mod two is $\deg(f) \bmod 2$. Give another proof that the degree mod two is homotopy invariant as follows.
Let $F$ be a homotopy between $f$ and $g$. Consider the restriction of $F$ to $\RR^{m} \times [0, 1]$. Study $F^{-1}(q)$. (To get started: what sort of space is $F^{-1}(q) \cap \RR^{m} \times \{t\}$ for $t \in [0, 1]$?)
:::

::: {.solution}

For both parts, the homotopy must be proper as a map
$$
F:\RR^m\times[0,1]\longrightarrow\RR^m,
\qquad
F(x,0)=f(x),\quad F(x,1)=g(x).
$$
Without this hypothesis the claim is false. For example, on $\RR$ the proper maps $x\mapsto x$ and $x\mapsto-x$ are ordinarily homotopic but have degrees $1$ and $-1$, while $x\mapsto x$ and $x\mapsto x^2$ are ordinarily homotopic proper maps whose degrees modulo two are $1$ and $0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Under a proper homotopy, $\boxed{\deg(f)=\deg(g)}$.


::: pf-proof

Choose a compactly supported $m$-form $\omega$ on $\RR^m$ with
$$
\int_{\RR^m}\omega=1.
$$
The degree of a proper smooth map $h:\RR^m\to\RR^m$ is characterized by
$$
\deg(h)=\int_{\RR^m}h^*\omega.
$$

Let
$$
K\omega
=
\int_0^1
\iota_{\partial_t}F^*\omega\,dt.
$$
The homotopy formula gives
$$
g^*\omega-f^*\omega
=d(K\omega)+K(d\omega).
$$
Since $\omega$ has top degree on $\RR^m$, $d\omega=0$. Moreover, properness of $F$ implies that
$$
\operatorname{supp}(F^*\omega)
\subseteq
F^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}\omega)
$$
is compact, so $K\omega$ is compactly supported on $\RR^m$. Therefore Stokes' theorem for compactly supported forms gives
$$
\deg(g)-\deg(f)
=
\int_{\RR^m}d(K\omega)
=0.
$$
Thus $\deg(f)=\deg(g)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

A common regular value turns the mod-two statement into a parity count of the boundary of a compact $1$-manifold.


::: pf-proof

Choose $q\in\RR^m$ that is simultaneously a regular value of $F$, $f$, and $g$. Such values form a dense set by Sard's theorem applied to the three maps. Since $F$ is proper,
$$
N\coloneqq F^{-1}(q)
$$
is compact. Regularity of $q$ for $F$ implies that $N$ is a smooth $1$-manifold, and regularity for the endpoint restrictions implies
$$
\partial N
=
\bigl(f^{-1}(q)\times\{0\}\bigr)
\sqcup
\bigl(g^{-1}(q)\times\{1\}\bigr).
$$
A compact $1$-manifold is a finite disjoint union of circles and closed intervals. Every interval contributes exactly two boundary points and every circle contributes none, hence
$$
\#\partial N\equiv0\pmod 2.
$$
Consequently
$$
\#f^{-1}(q)+\#g^{-1}(q)\equiv0\pmod2.
$$
For a regular value, the degree modulo two is exactly the parity of the preimage, so
$$
\deg(f)\equiv\deg(g)\pmod2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The degree modulo two is therefore homotopy invariant through proper maps.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves the asserted congruence by a second argument, independent of the differential-form computation in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
