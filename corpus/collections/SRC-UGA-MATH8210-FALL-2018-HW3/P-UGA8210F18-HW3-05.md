::: {.problem}
Degree examples:

(a) The unit circle in $\CC$ is the set $\norm{z} = 1$.
The map $z \mapsto z^k$ for $k \in \NN$ induces a smooth map $S^1 \to S^1$.
Compute the degree of this map.

(b) A nonconstant complex polynomial $p(z)$ induces a smooth map $\CC \to \CC$.
Show that the degree of this map is the degree of the polynomial.

(c) (The linking number) Let $M$ and $N$ be disjoint manifolds in $\RR^{r+1}$.
(We haven't talked much about embedded manifolds.
You can take $M$ to be a subspace of $\RR^{r+1}$ so that each $p \in M$ has a neighborhood $U$ so that $U \cap M$ is diffeomorphic to $\RR^m$.)
Define
$$
\lambda \colon M \times N \to \RR^{r+1}
$$
by
$$
\lambda(x, y) = \frac{x - y}{\norm{x - y}}.
$$
Verify that $\lambda(x, y)$ lands in the unit sphere in $\RR^{r+1}$ for any $x$ and $y$.
Show that $\lambda(x, y) = -\lambda(y, x)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

In part (a), the map $z\mapsto z^k$ has degree $\boxed{k}$.


::: pf-proof

If $k=0$, the map is constant and has degree $0$. Assume now that $k\ge1$.
Parametrize $S^1$ by $e^{i\theta}$. Then
$$
e^{i\theta}\longmapsto e^{ik\theta}.
$$
Take the regular value $1\in S^1$. Its preimages are
$$
e^{2\pi i j/k},
\qquad
j=0,1,\ldots,k-1.
$$
In the angular coordinate, the map is $\theta\mapsto k\theta$, whose derivative is the positive number $k$. Hence every preimage contributes local degree $+1$. Summing the $k$ local degrees gives
$$
\deg(z\mapsto z^k)=k.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $p:\CC\to\CC$ is a nonconstant polynomial of degree $n$, then its degree as a proper smooth map is $\boxed{n}$.


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
p(z)=a_nz^n+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0,
\qquad
a_n\ne0.
$$
Since $\abs{p(z)}\to\infty$ as $\abs{z}\to\infty$, the map $p:\CC\to\CC$ is proper.

Choose a regular value $w\in\CC$. By the fundamental theorem of algebra, the polynomial $p(z)-w$ has exactly $n$ complex roots counted with multiplicity. Regularity means $p'(z)\ne0$ at every root, so all these roots are simple and therefore there are exactly $n$ distinct points in $p^{-1}(w)$.

At a point $z_0\in p^{-1}(w)$, the real differential of $p$ is multiplication by the nonzero complex number $p'(z_0)$. Its real determinant is
$$
\abs{p'(z_0)}^2>0.
$$
Thus every preimage contributes local degree $+1$. Therefore
$$
\deg(p)
=
\sum_{z\in p^{-1}(w)}1
=n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

In part (c), $\lambda$ takes values in the unit sphere $S^r\subset\RR^{r+1}$.


::: pf-proof

Because $M$ and $N$ are disjoint, $x-y\ne0$ for every $(x,y)\in M\times N$, so $\lambda$ is well defined. Moreover,
$$
\norm{\lambda(x,y)}
=
\norm{\frac{x-y}{\norm{x-y}}}
=
\frac{\norm{x-y}}{\norm{x-y}}
=1.
$$
Hence $\lambda(x,y)\in S^r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The map satisfies $\boxed{\lambda(x,y)=-\lambda(y,x)}$.


::: pf-proof

For every $(x,y)\in M\times N$,
$$
\lambda(y,x)
=
\frac{y-x}{\norm{y-x}}
=
\frac{-(x-y)}{\norm{x-y}}
=
-\lambda(x,y),
$$
since $\norm{y-x}=\norm{x-y}$. Rearranging gives the asserted identity.

:::
