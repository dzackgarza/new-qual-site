::: {.problem}
(Page 37 of B&T) Let $S^n(r)$ be the sphere of radius $r$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.
Let
$$
\omega = \frac{1}{r} \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} (-1)^{i-1} x_i \, dx_1 \cdots \widehat{dx_i} \cdots dx_{n+1}
$$
be an $n$-form.
(The hat denotes omission, i.e. $dx_1 \widehat{dx_2} dx_3 = dx_1 dx_3$.)

(a) Compute $\int_{S^n(1)} \omega$.
(There might be a clever way to do this but at some point you have to really compute an integral.)
Use your computation to conclude that $\omega$ is not an exact form.

(b) Think of $r$ the radius function on $\mathbb{R}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\}$.
Show that $dr \wedge \omega = dx_1 \ldots dx_n$.

(c) B&T claim that your answer to the first part gives you a generator of $H^2(S^2)$.
Explain why and give a formula for the generator of $H^n(S^n)$.
:::

::: {.remark}
In (b) the source writes $dx_1 \ldots dx_n$, but $dr \wedge \omega$ is an $(n+1)$-form; for $n = 1$, $r\,dr = x_1 dx_1 + x_2 dx_2$ and $r\omega = x_1 dx_2 - x_2 dx_1$ give $dr \wedge \omega = dx_1 dx_2$.
The intended identity is $dr \wedge \omega = dx_1 \ldots dx_{n+1}$.
:::

Write
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb R^{n+1}}
\coloneqq
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}.
\]

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
\[
R
\coloneqq
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}
x_i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}
\]
be the radial vector field.
Then
\[
\iota_R
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb R^{n+1}}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}
(-1)^{i-1}
x_i,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge
\widehat{dx_i}
\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}.
\]


::: pf-proof

For the coordinate vector field
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i},
\]
contraction with the standard volume form removes the factor
\[
dx_i
\]
and contributes the sign
\[
(-1)^{i-1}.
\]
Linearity in
\[
R
=
\sum_i
x_i\partial_{x_i}
\]
gives the displayed expression.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence on
\[
\mathbb R^{n+1}\setminus\{0\},
\]
\[
\boxed{
\omega
=
\iota_{R/r}
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb R^{n+1}},
}
\]
where
\[
r=\sqrt{x_1^2+\cdots+x_{n+1}^2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Divide the formula in step <1>1 by $r$.
The vector field
\[
R/r
\]
is the outward unit radial vector field.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Restricted to
\[
S^n(1),
\]
the form
\[
\omega
\]
is the standard oriented Riemannian volume form of the unit sphere.


::: pf-proof

At a point
\[
x\in S^n(1),
\]
the outward unit normal is exactly
\[
R/r
=
x.
\]
The induced oriented volume form on an oriented hypersurface is contraction
of the ambient volume form with the outward unit normal.
Step <1>2 gives precisely that form.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
\[
\int_{S^n(1)}\omega
=
\operatorname{vol}(S^n).
\]


::: pf-proof

By step <1>3, integrating $\omega$ over the sphere computes its ordinary
Riemannian volume.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Gaussian integral in
\[
\mathbb R^{n+1}
\]
is
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^{n+1}}
e^{-|x|^2}
\,dx
=
\pi^{(n+1)/2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By Fubini,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^{n+1}}
e^{-(x_1^2+\cdots+x_{n+1}^2)}
\,dx_1\cdots dx_{n+1}
=
\left(
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
e^{-t^2}\,dt
\right)^{n+1}.
\]
The one-dimensional Gaussian integral equals
\[
\sqrt\pi.
\]
Hence the result.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Using polar coordinates,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^{n+1}}
e^{-|x|^2}
\,dx
=
\operatorname{vol}(S^n)
\int_0^\infty
e^{-r^2}r^n\,dr.
\]


::: pf-proof

The Euclidean volume element in polar coordinates is
\[
r^n\,dr\,d\operatorname{vol}_{S^n}.
\]
Since the integrand depends only on $r$, the angular integral contributes
\[
\operatorname{vol}(S^n).
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The radial integral is
\[
\int_0^\infty
e^{-r^2}r^n\,dr
=
\frac12
\Gamma
\left(
\frac{n+1}{2}
\right).
\]


::: pf-proof

Set
\[
u=r^2.
\]
Then
\[
du=2r\,dr,
\]
and
\[
r^n\,dr
=
\frac12
u^{(n-1)/2}\,du.
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_0^\infty
e^{-r^2}r^n\,dr
=
\frac12
\int_0^\infty
e^{-u}
u^{(n-1)/2}
\,du,
\]
which is the stated Gamma value.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Combining steps <1>5--<1>7,
\[
\boxed{
\int_{S^n(1)}\omega
=
\operatorname{vol}(S^n)
=
\frac{
2\pi^{(n+1)/2}
}{
\Gamma((n+1)/2)
}.
}
\]


::: pf-proof

Equate the Gaussian integral from step <1>5 with the polar-coordinate
expression from step <1>6 and solve for
\[
\operatorname{vol}(S^n).
\]
Then apply step <1>4.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The form
\[
\omega|_{S^n(1)}
\]
is not exact.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
\[
\omega=d\eta
\]
on
\[
S^n.
\]
Since $S^n$ is compact and has no boundary, Stokes' theorem gives
\[
\int_{S^n}\omega
=
\int_{S^n}d\eta
=
\int_{\partial S^n}\eta
=
0.
\]
But step <1>8 gives a strictly positive value.
Contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

On
\[
\mathbb R^{n+1}\setminus\{0\},
\]
one has
\[
dr
=
\frac1r
\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}
x_j,dx_j.
\]


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
\[
r
=
(x_1^2+\cdots+x_{n+1}^2)^{1/2}.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The corrected identity in part (b) is
\[
\boxed{
dr\wedge\omega
=
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}.
}
\]


::: pf-proof

Using the formulas for $dr$ and $\omega$,
\[
dr\wedge\omega
=
\frac1{r^2}
\sum_{j,i}
x_jx_i,
dx_j\wedge
(-1)^{i-1}
dx_1\wedge\cdots
\widehat{dx_i}
\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}.
\]

If
\[
j\ne i,
\]
then the wedge product contains
\[
dx_j
\]
twice and vanishes.
Only the terms
\[
j=i
\]
remain.
For such a term,
\[
dx_i\wedge
(-1)^{i-1}
dx_1\wedge\cdots
\widehat{dx_i}
\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}
=
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
dr\wedge\omega
&=
\frac1{r^2}
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}
x_i^2
\,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}\\
&=
\frac{r^2}{r^2}
\,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since
\[
H^n_{dR}(S^n)
\cong
\mathbb R,
\]
every nonzero cohomology class is a generator as a one-dimensional real
vector space.


::: pf-proof

By the de Rham theorem and the ordinary cohomology of the sphere,
\[
H^n_{dR}(S^n)
\cong
H^n(S^n;\mathbb R)
\cong
\mathbb R.
\]
Step <1>9 shows
\[
[\omega]\ne0.
\]
Therefore
\[
[\omega]
\]
is a basis vector of this one-dimensional vector space.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

A normalized generator
\[
\eta_n
\]
with integral $1$ is
\[
\boxed{
\eta_n
=
\frac{
\Gamma((n+1)/2)
}{
2\pi^{(n+1)/2}
}
\,
\omega|_{S^n(1)}.
}
\]


::: pf-proof

By step <1>8,
\[
\int_{S^n(1)}\omega
=
\frac{
2\pi^{(n+1)/2}
}{
\Gamma((n+1)/2)
}.
\]
Multiplying by its reciprocal normalizes the integral to $1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In particular, for
\[
n=2,
\]
\[
\int_{S^2}\omega
=
4\pi,
\]
so the normalized generator is
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{4\pi}
\left(
x_1,dx_2\wedge dx_3
-
x_2,dx_1\wedge dx_3
+
x_3,dx_1\wedge dx_2
\right)
\Big|_{S^2}.
}
\]


::: pf-proof

For
\[
n=2,
\]
the sphere area is
\[
4\pi.
\]
Substitute the formula for $\omega$ with
\[
r=1.
\]

:::
