::: {.problem}
Find a product of cyclic groups of prime power order isomorphic to $(\ZZ/1000000\ZZ)^*$ (the group of units of the ring of integers mod $1000000$).
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
1000000=10^6=2^6 5^6.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Chinese remainder theorem gives
$$
(\ZZ/1000000\ZZ)^\times
\cong
(\ZZ/2^6\ZZ)^\times
\times
(\ZZ/5^6\ZZ)^\times.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $2^6$ and $5^6$ are coprime, the Chinese remainder theorem gives a ring isomorphism
$$
\ZZ/(2^6 5^6)\ZZ
\cong
\ZZ/2^6\ZZ\times\ZZ/5^6\ZZ.
$$
An element of a product ring is a unit exactly when each component is a unit, so restricting this isomorphism to unit groups gives the displayed group isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
(\ZZ/2^6\ZZ)^\times
\cong
\ZZ/2\ZZ\times\ZZ/2^4\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

Modulo $64$, the element $5$ has order $16$: indeed
$$
5^2\equiv25,
\qquad
5^4\equiv49,
\qquad
5^8\equiv33,
\qquad
5^{16}\equiv1
\pmod{64}.
$$
Thus none of the proper divisors $1,2,4,8$ of $16$ is the order of $5$.

Every power of $5$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, whereas $-1$ is congruent to $3$ modulo $4$. Hence
$$
\langle-1\rangle\cap\langle5\rangle=\{1\}.
$$
The product $\langle-1\rangle\langle5\rangle$ therefore has $2\cdot16=32$ elements. Since
$$
\left|(\ZZ/64\ZZ)^\times\right|=\varphi(64)=32,
$$
it is the whole unit group, and the asserted direct-product decomposition follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The group $(\ZZ/5^6\ZZ)^\times$ is cyclic of order $4\cdot5^5$, generated by the residue class of $2$.


::: pf-proof

Reduction modulo $5$ shows that any exponent $d>0$ satisfying
$$
2^d\equiv1\pmod{5^6}
$$
must be divisible by $4$, because $2$ has order $4$ modulo $5$. Write $d=4m$. Since $2^4=16$, the lifting-the-exponent identity gives
$$
v_5(2^{4m}-1)
=
v_5(16^m-1)
=
v_5(16-1)+v_5(m)
=1+v_5(m).
$$
Thus $2^{4m}\equiv1\pmod{5^6}$ holds exactly when $5^5\mid m$. Consequently the order of $2$ modulo $5^6$ is
$$
4\cdot5^5.
$$
But
$$
\left|(\ZZ/5^6\ZZ)^\times\right|
=
\varphi(5^6)
=4\cdot5^5,
$$
so $2$ generates the entire unit group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
(\ZZ/5^6\ZZ)^\times
\cong
\ZZ/4\ZZ\times\ZZ/5^5\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the left-hand side is cyclic of order $4\cdot5^5$. Since $\gcd(4,5^5)=1$, the Chinese remainder theorem for cyclic groups gives
$$
\ZZ/(4\cdot5^5)\ZZ
\cong
\ZZ/4\ZZ\times\ZZ/5^5\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(\ZZ/1000000\ZZ)^\times
\cong
\ZZ/2\ZZ
\times
\ZZ/2^4\ZZ
\times
\ZZ/4\ZZ
\times
\ZZ/5^5\ZZ
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine the Chinese-remainder decomposition in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} with steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. Each displayed factor is cyclic and has prime-power order.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives the requested decomposition.
