::: {.problem}
Find a product of cyclic groups of prime power order isomorphic to $(\ZZ/1000000\ZZ)^*$ (the group of units of the ring of integers mod $1000000$).
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
1000000=10^6=2^6 5^6.
$$

<1>1. The Chinese remainder theorem gives
$$
(\mathbb Z/1000000\mathbb Z)^\times
\cong
(\mathbb Z/2^6\mathbb Z)^\times
\times
(\mathbb Z/5^6\mathbb Z)^\times.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $2^6$ and $5^6$ are coprime, the Chinese remainder theorem gives a ring isomorphism
$$
\mathbb Z/(2^6 5^6)\mathbb Z
\cong
\mathbb Z/2^6\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/5^6\mathbb Z.
$$
An element of a product ring is a unit exactly when each component is a unit, so restricting this isomorphism to unit groups gives the displayed group isomorphism.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
(\mathbb Z/2^6\mathbb Z)^\times
\cong
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2^4\mathbb Z.
$$

::: {.proof}
Modulo $64$, the element $5$ has order $16$: indeed
$$
5^2\equiv25,
\qquad
5^4\equiv49,
\qquad
5^8\equiv33,
\qquad
5^{16}\equiv1
\pmod{64}.
$$
Thus none of the proper divisors $1,2,4,8$ of $16$ is the order of $5$.

Every power of $5$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, whereas $-1$ is congruent to $3$ modulo $4$. Hence
$$
\langle-1\rangle\cap\langle5\rangle=\{1\}.
$$
The product $\langle-1\rangle\langle5\rangle$ therefore has $2\cdot16=32$ elements. Since
$$
\left|(\mathbb Z/64\mathbb Z)^\times\right|=\varphi(64)=32,
$$
it is the whole unit group, and the asserted direct-product decomposition follows.
:::

<1>3. The group $(\mathbb Z/5^6\mathbb Z)^\times$ is cyclic of order $4\cdot5^5$, generated by the residue class of $2$.

::: {.proof}
Reduction modulo $5$ shows that any exponent $d>0$ satisfying
$$
2^d\equiv1\pmod{5^6}
$$
must be divisible by $4$, because $2$ has order $4$ modulo $5$. Write $d=4m$. Since $2^4=16$, the lifting-the-exponent identity gives
$$
v_5(2^{4m}-1)
=
v_5(16^m-1)
=
v_5(16-1)+v_5(m)
=1+v_5(m).
$$
Thus $2^{4m}\equiv1\pmod{5^6}$ holds exactly when $5^5\mid m$. Consequently the order of $2$ modulo $5^6$ is
$$
4\cdot5^5.
$$
But
$$
\left|(\mathbb Z/5^6\mathbb Z)^\times\right|
=
\varphi(5^6)
=4\cdot5^5,
$$
so $2$ generates the entire unit group.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
(\mathbb Z/5^6\mathbb Z)^\times
\cong
\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/5^5\mathbb Z.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, the left-hand side is cyclic of order $4\cdot5^5$. Since $\gcd(4,5^5)=1$, the Chinese remainder theorem for cyclic groups gives
$$
\mathbb Z/(4\cdot5^5)\mathbb Z
\cong
\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/5^5\mathbb Z.
$$
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(\mathbb Z/1000000\mathbb Z)^\times
\cong
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
\times
\mathbb Z/2^4\mathbb Z
\times
\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z
\times
\mathbb Z/5^5\mathbb Z
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine the Chinese-remainder decomposition in step <1>1 with steps <1>2 and <1>4. Each displayed factor is cyclic and has prime-power order.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the requested decomposition.
