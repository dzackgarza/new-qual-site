::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the group given by the presentation
\[
\langle a, b \mid a^8 = 1,\; ba = a^{-1}b,\; b^2 = a^4 \rangle.
\]
Let $H$ be the subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ generated by
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix} \zeta & 0 \\ 0 & \zeta^{-1} \end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\]
where $\zeta$ is a primitive $8$th root of $1$.
Prove that $G \cong H$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The matrices $A$ and $B$ satisfy the defining relations of $G$.

::: pf-proof

Because $\zeta$ has order $8$,
\[
A^8=
\begin{pmatrix}
\zeta^8&0\\
0&\zeta^{-8}
\end{pmatrix}
=I.
\]
Also
\[
BA=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\zeta^{-1}\\
-\zeta&0
\end{pmatrix}
=A^{-1}B.
\]
Finally,
\[
B^2=-I.
\]
Since $\zeta$ is a primitive eighth root of unity, $\zeta^4=-1$, and hence
\[
A^4=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix}
=-I=B^2.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

There is a surjective homomorphism
\[
\phi:G\longrightarrow H,
\qquad
a\longmapsto A,
\quad
b\longmapsto B.
\]

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $A$ and $B$ satisfy all relations in the presentation of $G$, so the universal property of a presented group gives a homomorphism $\phi:G\to H$ with the stated values.
The subgroup $H$ is generated by $A$ and $B$, so $\phi$ is surjective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every element of $G$ is represented by one of the sixteen words
\[
a^i
\quad\text{or}\quad
a^i b,
\qquad 0\le i<8.
\]

::: pf-proof

From the relation
\[
ba=a^{-1}b
\]
one obtains by induction
\[
ba^r=a^{-r}b
\]
for every integer $r$.
Thus every occurrence of $b$ in a word can be moved to the far right.
Whenever two copies of $b$ meet, the relation $b^2=a^4$ replaces them by a power of $a$.
Consequently every word reduces to $a^r$ or $a^r b$.
Using $a^8=1$, the exponent may be reduced modulo $8$.
Hence
\[
|G|\le16.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The sixteen matrices
\[
A^i
\quad\text{and}\quad
A^iB,
\qquad 0\le i<8,
\]
are pairwise distinct elements of $H$.

::: pf-proof

The matrices $A^0,A^1,\ldots,A^7$ are distinct because $A$ has order $8$.

Each $A^i$ is diagonal, whereas
\[
A^iB=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\zeta^i\\
-\zeta^{-i}&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]
is off-diagonal, so no $A^i$ equals any $A^jB$.

Finally, if $A^iB=A^jB$, then right multiplication by $B^{-1}$ gives $A^i=A^j$, hence $i\equiv j\pmod8$.
Thus the eight matrices $A^iB$ are also distinct.
Therefore $H$ contains at least sixteen elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The homomorphism $\phi:G\to H$ is an isomorphism.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $|G|\le16$.
By step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, $|H|\ge16$.
But step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives a surjection $G\twoheadrightarrow H$, so
\[
|H|\le |G|.
\]
Hence
\[
16\le|H|\le|G|\le16,
\]
and therefore $|G|=|H|=16$.
A surjective homomorphism between finite groups of the same order is injective, so $\phi$ is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently $G\cong H$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref}.
