The prose argued from "the domain's fundamental group is generated by a circle, and a holomorphic function has a primitive exactly when its integral around this generator vanishes", which is true but was asserted rather than proved, and gave no primitive. ::: {.problem}
Consider the function
$$
f(z) = \frac{z^2 + 1}{z^2 - 1}
$$
in $G = \{z : \abs{z} > 2\}$.
Does $f$ have a primitive in $G$ (i.e., $F \in H(G)$ such that $F' = f$)? Prove your assertion.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

Yes, $f$ has a primitive on $G=\{\abs{z}>2\}$; in fact one exists on the larger region $\abs{z}>1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #partialfractions}
$f(z)=1+\dfrac1{z-1}-\dfrac1{z+1}$ for every $z$ with $\abs{z}\ne1$.

::: pf-proof
$1+\frac1{z-1}-\frac1{z+1}=\frac{(z^2-1)+(z+1)-(z-1)}{z^2-1}=\frac{z^2+1}{z^2-1}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #laurent}
On $\abs{z}>1$, $f(z)=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}z^{-2k}$, the series converging uniformly on every $\abs{z}\ge r>1$.

::: pf-proof
For $\abs{z}>1$, the geometric series give
$$
\frac1{z-1}=\frac1z\cdot\frac1{1-\frac1z}=\sum_{n\ge1}z^{-n},
\qquad
\frac1{z+1}=\frac1z\cdot\frac1{1+\frac1z}=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n-1}z^{-n}.
$$
Subtracting by step [](#partialfractions){.pf-ref}, the coefficient of $z^{-n}$ is $1-(-1)^{n-1}$, which is $0$ for $n$ odd and $2$ for $n$ even; so $f=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}z^{-2k}$. Both series converge uniformly on $\abs{z}\ge r$ because $\sum_n r^{-n}$ converges for $r>1$, and so does the difference.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #primitive}
$F(z)=z-2\sum_{k\ge1}\dfrac{z^{-(2k-1)}}{2k-1}$ is holomorphic on the region $\abs{z}>1$, and $F'=f$ there.

::: pf-proof
The series converges uniformly on every $\abs{z}\ge r>1$, since $\sum_k r^{-(2k-1)}/(2k-1)\le\sum_k r^{-(2k-1)}$ converges; so $F$ is holomorphic on the region $\abs{z}>1$ (Weierstrass), and termwise differentiation is legitimate there. Now $\frac{d}{dz}\left[-\frac{2z^{-(2k-1)}}{2k-1}\right]=2z^{-2k}$ for each $k$, so $F'(z)=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}z^{-2k}=f(z)$ by step [](#laurent){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #integral}
$\displaystyle\int_{\abs{z}=R}f(z)\,dz=0$ for every $R>2$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#partialfractions){.pf-ref} and linearity,
$\int_{\abs{z}=R}f=2\pi i\,(0)+(2\pi i)-(2\pi i)=0$, since $\int_{\abs{z}=R}dz=0$ and $\int_{\abs{z}=R}\frac{dz}{z-a}=2\pi i$ for $\abs{a}<R$, applied to $a=1$ and $a=-1$. This agrees with step [](#primitive){.pf-ref}, by which the integral of $f$ over any closed curve in $G$ vanishes.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$F$ restricts to a holomorphic primitive of $f$ on $G=\{\abs{z}>2\}$.

::: pf-proof
$G\subseteq\{\abs{z}>1\}$ and $F$ is holomorphic on $\{\abs{z}>1\}$ with $F'=f$ there, by step [](#primitive){.pf-ref}; so the restriction $F\restrict_G$ is holomorphic on $G$ with derivative $f\restrict_G$. This answers the question affirmatively.
:::
