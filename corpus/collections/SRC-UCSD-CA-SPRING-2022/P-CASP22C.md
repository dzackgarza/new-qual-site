::: {.problem}
Consider the function
$$
f(z) = \frac{z^2 + 1}{z^2 - 1}
$$
in $G = \{z : |z| > 2\}$.
Does $f$ have a primitive in $G$ (i.e., $F \in H(G)$ such that $F' = f$)? Prove your assertion.
:::

::: {.solution}
Yes. The domain $G=\{|z|>2\}$ has fundamental group generated by any circle
$|z|=R>2$. A holomorphic function on $G$ has a primitive exactly when its
integral around this generator vanishes.

Now
\[
f(z)=\frac{z^2+1}{z^2-1}
=1+\frac1{z-1}-\frac1{z+1}.
\]
Hence, for $R>2$,
\[
\int_{|z|=R}f(z)\,dz
=2\pi i\bigl(1-1\bigr)=0.
\]
Therefore every closed-curve integral of $f$ in $G$ vanishes, and $f$ has a
holomorphic primitive on $G$.
:::
