::: {.problem}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous map, and suppose that there are continuous maps $g,h:Y\to X$ with $f\circ g\simeq\operatorname{Id}_Y$ and $h\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$.

(a) Show that $g\simeq h$.

(b) Show, moreover, that $f$ is a homotopy equivalence.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. From $h\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$, postcomposition with $g$ gives
$$
h\circ f\circ g\simeq g.
$$
::: {.proof}
If $H:X\times I\to X$ is a homotopy from $h\circ f$ to $\operatorname{Id}_X$, then $(y,t)\mapsto H(g(y),t)$ is a homotopy from $hfg$ to $g$.
:::

<1>2. From $f\circ g\simeq\operatorname{Id}_Y$, precomposition by $h$ gives
$$
h\circ f\circ g\simeq h.
$$
::: {.proof}
If $K:Y\times I\to Y$ is a homotopy from $f\circ g$ to $\operatorname{Id}_Y$, then $(y,t)\mapsto h(K(y,t))$ is a homotopy from $hfg$ to $h$.
:::

<1>3. Hence $g\simeq h$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and <1>2, both $g$ and $h$ are homotopic to $hfg$. Homotopy is an equivalence relation, so $g\simeq h$.
:::

<1>4. We have $g\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, postcomposition with $f$ gives $g\circ f\simeq h\circ f$. By hypothesis $h\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$. Transitivity yields the claim.
:::

<1>5. The map $g$ is a homotopy inverse of $f$.
::: {.proof}
The hypothesis gives $f\circ g\simeq\operatorname{Id}_Y$, while <1>4 gives $g\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $f$ is a homotopy equivalence.
::: {.proof}
This is the definition of homotopy equivalence.
:::
:::
