::: {.problem}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous map, and suppose that there are continuous maps $g,h:Y\to X$ with $f\circ g\simeq\operatorname{Id}_Y$ and $h\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$.

(a) Show that $g\simeq h$.

(b) Show, moreover, that $f$ is a homotopy equivalence.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #both-homotopic-to-hfg}
$h\circ f\circ g\simeq g$ and $h\circ f\circ g\simeq h$.

::: pf-proof
If $H\colon X\times I\to X$ is a homotopy from $h\circ f$ to $\operatorname{Id}_X$, then $(y,t)\mapsto H(g(y),t)$ is a homotopy from $h\circ f\circ g$ to $g$.
If $K\colon Y\times I\to Y$ is a homotopy from $f\circ g$ to $\operatorname{Id}_Y$, then $(y,t)\mapsto h(K(y,t))$ is a homotopy from $h\circ f\circ g$ to $h$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) $g\simeq h$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#both-homotopic-to-hfg){.pf-ref} both are homotopic to $h\circ f\circ g$, and homotopy is an equivalence relation.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #gf-homotopic-id}
$g\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$.

::: pf-proof
Precomposing a homotopy from $g$ to $h$ (step [](#part-a){.pf-ref}) with $f$ gives $g\circ f\simeq h\circ f$, and $h\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$ by hypothesis.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#part-a){.pf-ref} proves (a).
For (b), $f\circ g\simeq\operatorname{Id}_Y$ by hypothesis and $g\circ f\simeq\operatorname{Id}_X$ by step [](#gf-homotopic-id){.pf-ref}, so $g$ is a homotopy inverse of $f$.
:::
