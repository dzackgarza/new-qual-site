::: {.problem}
Show, using covering space theory, that a finitely presented group $G$ has only finitely many distinct subgroups $H\le G$ of index $4$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Choose a finite presentation
$$
G=\langle g_1,\dots,g_m\mid r_1,\dots,r_s\rangle
$$
and let $X_G$ be its presentation $2$-complex.
::: {.proof}
The complex $X_G$ has one $0$-cell, one oriented $1$-cell for each generator, and one $2$-cell for each relator. By van Kampen, $\pi_1(X_G)\cong G$. Since the presentation is finite, $X_G$ has finite $1$-skeleton and finitely many $2$-cells.
:::

<1>2. Subgroups $H\le G$ of index $4$ correspond to pointed connected $4$-sheeted covering spaces of $X_G$.
::: {.proof}
Under the standard covering-space classification for connected CW complexes, a subgroup of $\pi_1(X_G)$ determines a connected covering together with a chosen point over the basepoint whose induced subgroup is exactly $H$. Using pointed coverings is essential here: unpointed connected coverings classify conjugacy classes, whereas the problem asks for actual subgroups.
:::

<1>3. A pointed $4$-sheeted covering determines, for each generator $g_i$, a permutation of the four points in the fiber over the unique $0$-cell.
::: {.proof}
Lift the oriented loop representing $g_i$ from each of the four fiber points. Its endpoint is again in that fiber. Path lifting therefore defines a permutation $\rho(g_i)\in S_4$. These permutations are the monodromy of the cover.
:::

<1>4. There are at most $24^m$ possible assignments of permutations to the generators.
::: {.proof}
The symmetric group $S_4$ has $24$ elements, and there are only $m$ generators.
:::

<1>5. The relators merely discard some of these assignments, and connectedness merely requires the generated action on the four points to be transitive.
::: {.proof}
A covering of the presentation complex must have trivial monodromy around each attaching loop $r_j$, so the chosen permutations must satisfy $r_j=1$ in $S_4$. Conversely, an assignment satisfying the relators gives a homomorphism $G\to S_4$, hence the corresponding covering of the $1$-skeleton extends over every $2$-cell. The total covering is connected exactly when the monodromy action on the fiber is transitive.
:::

<1>6. For each transitive monodromy action, choosing which of the four fiber points is the distinguished point gives at most four pointed coverings.
::: {.proof}
The underlying unpointed covering is determined by the monodromy action up to relabeling of the fiber. A pointed covering additionally specifies one point over the basepoint. Since the fiber has four points, there are at most four choices. Equivalently, the associated subgroup is the stabilizer of the distinguished point.
:::

<1>7. Hence $G$ has only finitely many subgroups of index $4$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4--<1>6, there are only finitely many pointed connected $4$-sheeted coverings of $X_G$. By <1>2, these are in bijection with the index-$4$ subgroups of $G$.
:::
:::
