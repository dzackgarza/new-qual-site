::: {.problem}
Suppose $X,X_1,X_2$ are path-connected spaces, $p:X_1\to X$ and $q:X_2\to X$ are (surjective) covering maps, and $f:X_1\to X_2$ is a continuous map with $q\circ f=p$.
Show that $f$ is also a covering map.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Fix $x\in X$. Choose a connected open neighborhood $U\ni x$ that is evenly covered by both $p$ and $q$.
::: {.proof}
Take evenly covered neighborhoods for $p$ and $q$ at $x$ and replace their intersection by the connected component containing $x$. Restricting an evenly covered neighborhood to an open connected subset preserves the evenly covered property.
:::

<1>2. If $V$ is a sheet of $p^{-1}(U)$, then $f(V)$ is contained in a unique sheet $W$ of $q^{-1}(U)$.
::: {.proof}
Since $q\circ f=p$, one has $f(V)\subseteq q^{-1}(U)$. The sheets of $q^{-1}(U)$ are pairwise disjoint open-and-closed subsets of $q^{-1}(U)$. Since $V$ is connected, its continuous image $f(V)$ lies in one such sheet $W$.
:::

<1>3. For the sheets $V,W$ in <1>2,
$$
 f|_V=(q|_W)^{-1}\circ p|_V:V\longrightarrow W,
$$
so $f|_V$ is a homeomorphism onto $W$.
::: {.proof}
Both $p|_V:V\to U$ and $q|_W:W\to U$ are homeomorphisms. The identity $qf=p$ forces the displayed formula.
:::

<1>4. For every sheet $W$ of $q^{-1}(U)$, either $f^{-1}(W)=\varnothing$ or $f^{-1}(W)$ is a disjoint union of sheets $V$ of $p^{-1}(U)$, each mapped homeomorphically onto $W$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2--<1>3, each sheet $V$ either maps homeomorphically onto one sheet $W$ or is disjoint from $f^{-1}(W)$. Taking the union over all sheets of $p^{-1}(U)$ gives the assertion.
:::

<1>5. The image $f(X_1)$ is both open and closed in $X_2$.
::: {.proof}
It is open because <1>3 shows that every point of the image has a neighborhood sheet contained in the image. Its complement is open as well: if $y\notin f(X_1)$, choose $U$ and the sheet $W\ni y$ as above. If any point of $W$ lay in the image, <1>4 would force all of $W$ to lie in the image, contradicting $y\notin f(X_1)$. Hence $W$ lies in the complement.
:::

<1>6. Since $X_1$ is nonempty and $X_2$ is connected, $f(X_1)=X_2$. Therefore $f$ is a covering map.
::: {.proof}
The image is nonempty and clopen by <1>5>, so connectedness of $X_2$ makes it all of $X_2$. Then <1>4 says that every point of $X_2$ has an open neighborhood evenly covered by $f$.
:::
:::
