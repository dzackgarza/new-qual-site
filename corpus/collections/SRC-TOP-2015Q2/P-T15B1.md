::: {.problem}
Suppose $X,X_1,X_2$ are path-connected spaces, $p:X_1\to X$ and $q:X_2\to X$ are (surjective) covering maps, and $f:X_1\to X_2$ is a continuous map with $q\circ f=p$.
Show that $f$ is also a covering map.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $x\in X$, choose a connected open neighborhood $U\ni x$ evenly covered by both $p$ and $q$: take the connected component containing $x$ of the intersection of evenly covered neighborhoods for $p$ and $q$.
This component is open when $X$ is locally connected, and an open subset of an evenly covered set is evenly covered.

<1>1. If $V$ is a sheet of $p^{-1}(U)$, then $f(V)$ lies in a unique sheet $W$ of $q^{-1}(U)$, and $f|_V=(q|_W)^{-1}\circ p|_V\colon V\to W$ is a homeomorphism.

::: {.proof}
Since $q\circ f=p$, $f(V)\subseteq q^{-1}(U)$.
The sheets of $q^{-1}(U)$ are pairwise disjoint open subsets covering $q^{-1}(U)$, and $f(V)$ is connected because $V\cong U$ is connected, so $f(V)$ lies in one sheet $W$.
Since $p|_V\colon V\to U$ and $q|_W\colon W\to U$ are homeomorphisms and $q\circ f=p$, we get $f|_V=(q|_W)^{-1}\circ p|_V$.
:::

<1>2. For every sheet $W$ of $q^{-1}(U)$, $f^{-1}(W)$ is empty or a disjoint union of sheets of $p^{-1}(U)$, each mapped homeomorphically onto $W$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, each sheet of $p^{-1}(U)$ either maps homeomorphically onto $W$ or is disjoint from $f^{-1}(W)$, and $f^{-1}(W)\subseteq p^{-1}(U)$.
:::

<1>3. $f(X_1)$ is open and closed in $X_2$.

::: {.proof}
If $y\in X_2$, choose $U$ as above for $x=q(y)$ and let $W$ be the sheet of $q^{-1}(U)$ containing $y$.
By step <1>2, either $W\subseteq f(X_1)$ or $W\cap f(X_1)=\varnothing$.
So both $f(X_1)$ and its complement are open.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
$f(X_1)$ is nonempty and, by step <1>3, clopen in the connected space $X_2$, so $f$ is surjective.
By step <1>2, every point of $X_2$ has an open neighborhood $W$ evenly covered by $f$.
:::
:::
