::: {.problem}
Let $f:(X,\mathcal T)\to(Y,\mathcal T')$ be a continuous map.
A *section* of $f$ is a continuous map $\sigma:(Y,\mathcal T')\to(X,\mathcal T)$ so that $f\circ\sigma=\operatorname{Id}_Y$.
Show that if $f$ has a section, then $f$ is a quotient map.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The map $f$ is surjective.
::: {.proof}
For every $y\in Y$, the section satisfies $f(\sigma(y))=y$.
:::

<1>2. Let $V\subseteq Y$ satisfy $f^{-1}(V)\in\mathcal T$.
::: {.proof}
To prove that the surjective continuous map $f$ is quotient, it remains to show that this condition forces $V\in\mathcal T'$.
:::

<1>3. We have
$$
V=\sigma^{-1}(f^{-1}(V)).
$$
::: {.proof}
For $y\in Y$,
$$
y\in\sigma^{-1}(f^{-1}(V))
\iff f(\sigma(y))\in V
\iff y\in V,
$$
because $f\circ\sigma=\operatorname{Id}_Y$.
:::

<1>4. The set $V$ is open in $Y$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X$, and $\sigma$ is continuous. Therefore its inverse image under $\sigma$ is open in $Y$. By <1>3 this inverse image is $V$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore $f$ is a quotient map.
::: {.proof}
The map is surjective by <1>1 and continuous by hypothesis. Moreover <1>2--<1>4 show that $V\subseteq Y$ is open whenever $f^{-1}(V)$ is open. This is exactly the quotient-map criterion.
:::
:::
