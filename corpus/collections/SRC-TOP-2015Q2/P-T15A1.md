::: {.problem}
Let $X=\prod_{n\in\ZZ}[0,1]=\{(x_n)_{n\in\ZZ}:x_n\in[0,1]\}$, with each $[0,1]$ having the usual Euclidean topology and $X$ having the product topology, and let $$A=\{(x_n)\in X:\text{for some }N\text{ we have }x_n=0\text{ for all }n\ge N\}.$$

(a) Show that $A$ is a dense subset of $X$, and is therefore not a closed subset of $X$.

(b) Show that, as a consequence, $A$ is not a compact subset of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $A$ meets every nonempty basic open set $B=\prod_{n\in\ZZ}U_n$ of $X$, where each $U_n\subseteq[0,1]$ is open and $U_n=[0,1]$ for all but finitely many $n$.

::: {.proof}
The set $F=\{n:U_n\neq[0,1]\}$ is finite; choose an integer $N$ larger than every element of $F$.
For $n<N$ choose $a_n\in U_n$, possible because $B$ is nonempty, and for $n\ge N$ set $a_n=0\in[0,1]=U_n$.
Then $a=(a_n)\in A\cap B$.
:::

<1>2. $A$ is dense and not closed in $X$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1 every nonempty open set meets $A$, so $\overline A=X$.
The constant sequence $(1)_{n\in\ZZ}$ is not in $A$, so $A\neq X=\overline A$ and $A$ is not closed.
:::

<1>3. $X$ is Hausdorff.

::: {.proof}
If two points differ in coordinate $n$, disjoint open neighborhoods of those coordinates in $[0,1]$ pull back under the projection $\pi_n$ to disjoint open neighborhoods in $X$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves (a).
For (b), a compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed, so by step <1>3 a compact $A$ would be closed, contrary to step <1>2.
:::
:::
