::: {.problem}
Let $X=\prod_{n\in\mathbb Z}[0,1]=\{(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}:x_n\in[0,1]\}$, with each $[0,1]$ having the usual Euclidean topology and $X$ having the product topology, and let $$A=\{(x_n)\in X:\text{for some }N\text{ we have }x_n=0\text{ for all }n\ge N\}.$$

(a) Show that $A$ is a dense subset of $X$, and is therefore not a closed subset of $X$.

(b) Show that, as a consequence, $A$ is not a compact subset of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $B=\prod_{n\in\mathbb Z}U_n$ be a nonempty basic open subset of $X$, where each $U_n\subseteq[0,1]$ is open and $U_n=[0,1]$ for all but finitely many $n$.
::: {.proof}
Such products form a basis for the product topology.
:::

<1>2. There exists $a=(a_n)\in A\cap B$.
::: {.proof}
Let $F=\{n:U_n\neq[0,1]\}$, which is finite. Choose an integer $N$ larger than every element of $F$. For each $n<N$, choose $a_n\in U_n$; this is possible because $B$ is nonempty. For every $n\ge N$, set $a_n=0$. Since no index $n\ge N$ lies in $F$, we have $U_n=[0,1]$, so $a_n=0\in U_n$. Thus $a\in B$, and by construction $a_n=0$ for all $n\ge N$, so $a\in A$.
:::

<1>3. Therefore $A$ is dense in $X$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1--<1>2, every nonempty basic open set meets $A$. Hence every nonempty open set meets $A$, so $\overline A=X$.
:::

<1>4. The subset $A$ is proper, hence not closed.
::: {.proof}
The constant sequence $(1)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$ does not belong to $A$, so $A\neq X$. Since $\overline A=X$ by <1>3, a closed $A$ would equal its closure and therefore equal $X$, a contradiction.
:::

<1>5. The product space $X$ is Hausdorff.
::: {.proof}
Each factor $[0,1]$ is Hausdorff, and an arbitrary product of Hausdorff spaces with the product topology is Hausdorff: if two points differ in coordinate $n$, disjoint open neighborhoods in that factor pull back to disjoint open neighborhoods under the projection.
:::

<1>6. The subset $A$ is not compact.
::: {.proof}
Every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed. If $A$ were compact in $X$, then <1>5 would imply that $A$ is closed in $X$, contradicting <1>4.
:::
:::
