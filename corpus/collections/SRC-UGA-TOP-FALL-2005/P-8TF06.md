::: problem
a. For a topological space $X$, define what a covering space of $X$ is and what a deck transformation (or covering transformation) of a covering space is.

b. If $X$ is the Klein bottle, describe the universal covering $p:\widetilde{X}\to X$ and its group of deck transformations.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A covering space of $X$ is a space $\widetilde X$ together with a continuous surjection
\[
p:\widetilde X\longrightarrow X
\]
such that every $x\in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ for which
\[
p^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{\alpha}U_\alpha
\]
with each $U_\alpha$ open in $\widetilde X$ and each restriction
\[
p|_{U_\alpha}:U_\alpha\longrightarrow U
\]
a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
This is the definition of a covering map, or equivalently of the covering space $\widetilde X$ over $X$.
:::

<1>2. A deck transformation of $p:\widetilde X\to X$ is a homeomorphism
\[
h:\widetilde X\longrightarrow\widetilde X
\]
satisfying
\[
p\circ h=p.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the definition of a deck transformation, also called a covering transformation.
:::

<1>3. Let $A,B:\RR^2\to\RR^2$ be the isometries
\[
A(x,y)=(x+1,y),
\qquad
B(x,y)=(-x,y+1).
\]
They satisfy
\[
BAB^{-1}=A^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
B^{-1}(x,y)=(-x,y-1),
\]
one computes
\[
BAB^{-1}(x,y)
=B(-x+1,y-1)
=(x-1,y)
=A^{-1}(x,y).
\]
:::

<1>4. The group
\[
\Gamma=\langle A,B\rangle
\cong
\langle A,B\mid BAB^{-1}=A^{-1}\rangle
\]
acts freely and properly discontinuously on $\RR^2$.
::: {.proof}
Using $BA=A^{-1}B$, every element of $\Gamma$ can be written as $A^mB^n$ with $m,n\in\ZZ$.
Moreover,
\[
A^mB^n(x,y)
=
\begin{cases}
(x+m,y+n),&n\text{ even},\\
(-x+m,y+n),&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
If such a normal form is the identity transformation, comparison of coordinates for arbitrary $(x,y)$ gives $n=0$ and then $m=0$.
Thus the normal forms are unique, and the displayed presentation has no additional relations.
If $A^mB^n$ fixes a point, comparison of the second coordinates gives $n=0$, and then comparison of the first coordinates gives $m=0$.
Thus the action is free.

For a compact set $K\subset\RR^2$, boundedness of the two coordinate projections shows that only finitely many integer pairs $(m,n)$ can satisfy
\[
A^mB^n(K)\cap K\ne\varnothing.
\]
Hence the action is properly discontinuous.
:::

<1>5. The quotient $\RR^2/\Gamma$ is the Klein bottle.
::: {.proof}
Use the unit square as a fundamental domain.
The transformation $A$ identifies the two vertical sides by
\[
(0,y)\sim(1,y).
\]
The transformation $B$ identifies the bottom and top sides by
\[
(x,0)\sim(-x,1)\sim(1-x,1),
\]
where the last equivalence uses the vertical-side identification.
Thus one pair of opposite sides is glued preserving direction and the other pair reversing direction, which is the standard square model of the Klein bottle.
:::

<1>6. Therefore the universal covering of the Klein bottle is
\[
p:\RR^2\longrightarrow\RR^2/\Gamma\cong K,
\]
and its deck-transformation group is
\[
\operatorname{Deck}(p)=\Gamma
\cong
\langle A,B\mid BAB^{-1}=A^{-1}\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, the quotient map is a covering map, and by <1>5 its base is the Klein bottle.
Since $\RR^2$ is simply connected, this covering is universal.
Every element of $\Gamma$ is a deck transformation because it preserves quotient fibers.
Conversely, if $h$ is a deck transformation and $z_0\in\RR^2$, then $h(z_0)$ lies in the same quotient fiber as $z_0$, so
\[
h(z_0)=\gamma(z_0)
\]
for some $\gamma\in\Gamma$.
The maps $h$ and $\gamma$ are then two lifts of $p$ that agree at $z_0$.
Uniqueness of lifts gives $h=\gamma$.
Hence $\operatorname{Deck}(p)=\Gamma$.
:::
