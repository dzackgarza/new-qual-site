::: problem
a. For a topological space $X$, define what a covering space of $X$ is and what a deck transformation (or covering transformation) of a covering space is.

b. If $X$ is the Klein bottle, describe the universal covering $p:\widetilde{X}\to X$ and its group of deck transformations.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Part (a).**

<1>1. A covering space of $X$ is a space $\tilde X$ together with a continuous surjection $p: \tilde X \to X$ such that every $x \in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ with $p^{-1}(U)$ a disjoint union of open sets, each mapped homeomorphically onto $U$ by $p$.
::: {.proof}
definition.
:::

<1>2. A deck transformation of $p: \tilde X \to X$ is a homeomorphism $f: \tilde X \to \tilde X$ with $p \circ f = p$.
::: {.proof}
definition.
:::

**Part (b).**

<1>1. $\pi_1(\text{Klein bottle}) = \langle a, b \mid aba^{-1}b \rangle$.
::: {.proof}
standard presentation of the fundamental group of the Klein bottle.
:::

<1>2. The universal cover of the Klein bottle is $\RR^2$.
::: {.proof}
the Klein bottle is a surface with $\RR^2$ as its universal cover (it is the quotient of $\RR^2$ by a group of isometries).
:::

<1>3. The group of deck transformations is $\pi_1(\text{Klein bottle}) = \langle a, b \mid aba^{-1}b \rangle$, acting on $\RR^2$ by the isometries $a(x, y) = (x+1, y)$ and $b(x, y) = (-x, y+1)$.
::: {.proof}
the deck group is isomorphic to $\pi_1(X)$, and the generators act as these isometries (translation by $1$ in the $x$-direction, and reflection in the $y$-axis followed by translation by $1$ in the $y$-direction).
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and <1>3.
:::
:::
