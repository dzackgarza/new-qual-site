::: problem
a. Compute the integral homology of $\RP^3$.

b. Is $\RP^3$ orientable?
How many distinct connected degree $2$ covering spaces does $\RP^3$ admit?
(Brief justification.)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The standard CW structure on $\RP^3$ has one cell in each dimension $0,1,2,3$, and its cellular chain complex is
\[
0\longrightarrow\ZZ
\xrightarrow{0}\ZZ
\xrightarrow{2}\ZZ
\xrightarrow{0}\ZZ
\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
For $\RP^n$, the standard CW structure has one $k$-cell for each $0\le k\le n$.
The cellular boundary in degree $k$ is multiplication by
\[
1+(-1)^k,
\]
because the two lifts of the attaching map differ by the antipodal map on $S^{k-1}$, whose degree is $(-1)^k$.
Thus the differential is multiplication by $2$ for even $k$ and is zero for odd $k$.
Specializing to $n=3$ gives the displayed complex.
:::

<1>2. Therefore the integral homology groups are
\[
H_i(\RP^3;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,3,\\
\ZZ/2\ZZ,&i=1,\\
0,&i=2\text{ or }i>3.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
From <1>1,
\[
H_3=\ker(0)\cong\ZZ,
\]
\[
H_2=\ker(2)/\operatorname{im}(0)=0,
\]
and
\[
H_1=\ker(0)/\operatorname{im}(2)
\cong
\ZZ/2\ZZ.
\]
Finally $H_0\cong\ZZ$ because $\RP^3$ is connected, and all higher cellular chain groups vanish.
:::

<1>3. The manifold $\RP^3$ is orientable.
::: {.proof}
The space $\RP^3$ is a connected closed $3$-manifold.
Such a manifold is orientable exactly when its top integral homology group is isomorphic to $\ZZ$.
By <1>2,
\[
H_3(\RP^3;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\]
so $\RP^3$ is orientable.
:::

<1>4. Up to equivalence of covering spaces, $\RP^3$ admits exactly one connected degree-$2$ cover.
::: {.proof}
The antipodal quotient
\[
p:S^3\longrightarrow\RP^3
\]
is a connected two-sheeted covering.
Since $S^3$ is simply connected, it is the universal cover and therefore
\[
\pi_1(\RP^3)\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ.
\]

Connected covering spaces of $\RP^3$ are classified, up to covering-space equivalence, by conjugacy classes of subgroups of $\pi_1(\RP^3)$, and the number of sheets equals the subgroup index.
The group $\ZZ/2\ZZ$ has exactly one subgroup of index $2$, namely the trivial subgroup.
Hence every connected double cover is equivalent to
\[
S^3\longrightarrow\RP^3.
\]
Thus there is exactly one such cover up to equivalence.
:::
:::
