::: {.problem}
Let $M_g$ be the closed orientable surface of genus $g$.
Let $f:M_g\to M_g$ be a continuous map homotopic to the identity.
For which $g$ does it follow that $f$ must have a fixed point?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #lefschetz-number}
For a map $f\simeq\operatorname{id}_{M_g}$, the Lefschetz number is
\[
L(f)=\chi(M_g)=2-2g.
\]

::: pf-proof
Homotopic maps induce the same maps on homology, so $f_*$ is the identity on every homology group of $M_g$.
Therefore the alternating sum of the traces defining the Lefschetz number is the Euler characteristic:
\[
L(f)=L(\operatorname{id}_{M_g})=\chi(M_g).
\]
A closed orientable genus-$g$ surface has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, $2g$ $1$-cells, and one $2$-cell.
Hence
\[
\chi(M_g)=1-2g+1=2-2g.
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fixed-point-forced}
If $g\ne1$, then every such $f$ has a fixed point.

::: pf-proof
For $g\ne1$, step [](#lefschetz-number){.pf-ref} gives
\[
L(f)=2-2g\ne0.
\]
The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem states that a continuous self-map of a finite CW complex with nonzero Lefschetz number has a fixed point.
Since $M_g$ is a finite CW complex, $f$ therefore has a fixed point.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #g1-counterexample}
For $g=1$, a fixed point is not forced.

::: pf-proof
Identify
\[
M_1=T^2=\RR^2/\ZZ^2.
\]
Let
\[
a=(1/2,0)
\]
and define the translation
\[
\tau_a([x])=[x+a].
\]
If $\tau_a([x])=[x]$, then $a\in\ZZ^2$, which is false.
Thus $\tau_a$ has no fixed point.

On the other hand,
\[
H:T^2\times[0,1]\longrightarrow T^2,
\qquad
H([x],t)=[x+ta],
\]
is well defined and continuous, with
\[
H(-,0)=\operatorname{id}_{T^2},
\qquad
H(-,1)=\tau_a.
\]
Hence $\tau_a$ is homotopic to the identity.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore the fixed-point conclusion holds exactly for
\[
g\ne1.
\]

::: pf-proof
Step [](#fixed-point-forced){.pf-ref} proves the conclusion for every $g\ne1$, while step [](#g1-counterexample){.pf-ref} supplies a counterexample when $g=1$.
:::
