::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be a field whose additive group is finitely generated.
Prove that $k$ is finite.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #char-not-zero}
The field $k$ cannot have characteristic zero.

::: pf-proof
Suppose instead that $\operatorname{char}k=0$. Then the prime
subfield of $k$ is isomorphic to $\QQ$, so the additive group
$\QQ$ is a subgroup of the additive group of $k$.

Every subgroup of a finitely generated abelian group is finitely
generated. Hence the hypothesis on the additive group of $k$ would
imply that the additive group of $\QQ$ is finitely generated.

This is impossible. Indeed, suppose
$$
\QQ
=
\ZZ\frac{a_1}{b_1}
+\cdots+
\ZZ\frac{a_r}{b_r},
$$
with nonzero integers $b_i$, and put
$$
B=\abs{b_1\cdots b_r}.
$$
Each generator $a_i/b_i$ belongs to $(1/B)\ZZ$, so the displayed
subgroup is contained in $(1/B)\ZZ$. Choose a prime $q$ not dividing
$B$. Then $1/q\notin(1/B)\ZZ$, a contradiction.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #char-is-prime-p}
The characteristic of $k$ is some prime $p$.

::: pf-proof
The characteristic of a field is either zero or prime. Step [](#char-not-zero){.pf-ref}
excludes characteristic zero, so
$$
\operatorname{char}k=p
$$
for some prime $p$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #xi-span-k}
If the additive group of $k$ is generated by
$x_1,\ldots,x_r$, then these elements span $k$ as a vector space
over $\FF_p$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x\in k$. By additive generation,
$$
x=n_1x_1+\cdots+n_rx_r
$$
for integers $n_i$. In characteristic $p$, multiplication by an
integer depends only on its residue modulo $p$. Thus, writing
$\bar n_i\in\FF_p$ for the residue class of $n_i$,
$$
x=\bar n_1x_1+\cdots+\bar n_rx_r.
$$
Hence the $x_i$ span $k$ over $\FF_p$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #k-finite}
The field $k$ is finite.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#xi-span-k){.pf-ref}, $k$ is a finite-dimensional $\FF_p$-vector space,
with dimension at most $r$. Therefore
$$
\abs k=p^{\dim_{\FF_p}k}<\infty.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#k-finite){.pf-ref} proves the required conclusion.
:::
