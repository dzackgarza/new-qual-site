::: {.problem}
Let $X=S^2$ (i.e., the subspace of $\RR^3$ consisting of points $(x,y,z)$ such that $x^2+y^2+z^2=1$). Define an equivalence relation on $X$ by $(x,y,z)\sim(x',y',z')$ if and only if $z=z'$.
Let $Y=X/\sim$ equipped with the quotient topology.
Prove $Y$ is homeomorphic to the closed interval $[-1,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $q\colon S^2\to Y$ be the quotient map, and let $h\colon S^2\to[-1,1]$ be the height map $h(x,y,z)=z$, which is continuous as the restriction of the third-coordinate projection $\RR^3\to\RR$.

<1>1. There is a unique continuous map $\bar h\colon Y\to[-1,1]$ with $h=\bar h\circ q$.

::: {.proof}
Equivalent points have the same $z$-coordinate, so $h$ is constant on equivalence classes.
By the universal property of the quotient topology, $h$ factors uniquely through $q$ by a continuous map.
:::

<1>2. $\bar h$ is bijective.

::: {.proof}
Two classes have the same image under $\bar h$ exactly when their representatives have the same $z$-coordinate, that is, when the classes are equal.
For each $t\in[-1,1]$, the point $(\sqrt{1-t^2},0,t)\in S^2$ has height $t$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
$Y=q(S^2)$ is compact as a continuous image of the compact space $S^2$, and $[-1,1]$ is Hausdorff.
By steps <1>1 and <1>2, $\bar h$ is a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, hence a homeomorphism.
:::
:::

::: {.remark}
The source writes $S^3$ for this space; its parenthetical defines the unit sphere in $\RR^3$, which is $S^2$, and the quotient by the height fibres is homeomorphic to $[-1,1]$ only for that sphere.
:::
