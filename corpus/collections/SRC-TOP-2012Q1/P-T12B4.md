::: {.problem}
Let $X=\mathbb R^3-S^1$, the complement of a single circle in $\mathbb R^3$.

(a) Describe a deformation retraction of $X$ onto $S^1\vee S^2$.

(b) Compute $\pi_1(X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $C=S^1\subset\mathbb R^3$ be the standard unknotted circle, and choose a closed tubular neighborhood
$$
N\cong S^1\times D^2
$$
of $C=S^1\times\{0\}$.
::: {.proof}
The standard circle has a product tubular neighborhood obtained by taking sufficiently small normal discs along the circle.
:::

<1>2. The complement $X=\mathbb R^3\setminus C$ deformation retracts onto
$$
X_0=\mathbb R^3\setminus\operatorname{int}(N).
$$
::: {.proof}
Inside $N\setminus C\cong S^1\times(D^2\setminus\{0\})$, push each nonzero normal vector radially outward until it lies on $S^1\times\partial D^2$; leave points outside $N$ fixed. This gives a deformation retraction onto the exterior of the open tubular neighborhood.
:::

<1>3. After adjoining the point at infinity, the exterior
$$
E=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}(N)
$$
is a solid torus.
::: {.proof}
The standard unknot gives the standard genus-one Heegaard decomposition of $S^3$: a tubular neighborhood of the unknot is one solid torus and the closure of its complement is the other. Thus $E\cong S^1\times D^2$.
:::

<1>4. The space $X_0=E\setminus\{\infty\}$ deformation retracts onto a solid torus with an open $3$-ball removed.
::: {.proof}
Choose a small closed ball $B\subset\operatorname{int}(E)$ centered at $\infty$. Radially push the punctured ball $B\setminus\{\infty\}$ onto $\partial B$ and leave $E\setminus B$ fixed. The resulting retract is
$$
E\setminus\operatorname{int}(B).
$$
:::

<1>5. A solid torus with an open $3$-ball removed deformation retracts onto an embedded copy of $S^2\vee S^1$.
::: {.proof}
View the solid torus as a $3$-ball with one $1$-handle attached. Choose the removed ball inside the $0$-handle. The punctured $0$-handle is a shell $S^2\times I$. Collapse this shell onto its inner boundary sphere together with two thin arcs from one chosen point of that sphere to the two attaching discs of the $1$-handle, and collapse the $1$-handle $D^1\times D^2$ onto its core $D^1\times\{0\}$. The two arcs together with the core form one circle meeting the retained sphere only at the chosen point. The resulting spine is therefore $S^2\vee S^1$, and the collapses are deformation retractions.
:::

<1>6. Combining <1>2, <1>4, and <1>5 gives a deformation retraction
$$
\mathbb R^3\setminus S^1\simeq_{\mathrm{def}} S^1\vee S^2.
$$
::: {.proof}
Compose the three deformation retractions described above.
:::

<1>7. Therefore
$$
\pi_1(\mathbb R^3\setminus S^1)\cong\pi_1(S^1\vee S^2)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
::: {.proof}
Deformation retracts have isomorphic fundamental groups. By van Kampen,
$$
\pi_1(S^1\vee S^2)\cong\pi_1(S^1)*\pi_1(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z*1\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
:::
:::
