::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the quotient space of $S^2$ under the identification $x\sim -x$ for $x$ in the equator.

(a) Write down a CW-decomposition of $X$.

(b) Write down the cellular chain complex for the CW structure from (a). Be sure to include the differentials of the complex.

(c) Compute the homology of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $E\subset S^2$ be the equator and $q\colon S^2\to X$ the quotient map.

<1>1. $q(E)$ is a circle, and $q|_E\colon E\to q(E)$ is a map $S^1\to S^1$ of degree $2$.

::: {.proof}
On $E\cong S^1\subset\CC$ the relation is $z\sim-z$, and $z\mapsto z^2$ induces a homeomorphism $S^1/(z\sim-z)\cong S^1$.
Under this identification $q|_E$ is $z\mapsto z^2$, which has degree $2$.
:::

<1>2. (a) $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$, one $1$-cell $e$ (so that $v\cup e=q(E)$), and two $2$-cells $u,w$, the images of the open northern and southern hemispheres, each attached by a map of degree $2$.

::: {.proof}
The relation identifies points only on the equator, so $q$ is injective on the interior of each hemisphere.
Each closed hemisphere is a disc whose boundary $E$ is attached to the $1$-skeleton $q(E)$ by $q|_E$, which has degree $2$ by step <1>1.
:::

<1>3. (b) Orient $u$ and $w$ so that both attaching degrees are $+2$. The cellular chain complex is
$$0\to\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{\partial_2}\ZZ\xrightarrow{\partial_1}\ZZ\to0,\qquad \partial_2(a,b)=2a+2b,\qquad\partial_1=0,$$
in the bases $(u,w)$, $e$, $v$.

::: {.proof}
The coefficient of a $2$-cell in the cellular boundary is the degree of its attaching map onto the unique $1$-cell, which is $2$ by step <1>2.
The $1$-cell is a loop at the unique $0$-cell, so its cellular boundary is zero.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2 and <1>3 answer (a) and (b).
For (c), by step <1>3: $H_0(X)=C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1\cong\ZZ$; $H_1(X)=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2=\ZZ/2\ZZ$; and, with no $3$-cells, $H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2=\ZZ(u-w)\cong\ZZ$.
Hence
$$H_k(X)\cong\begin{cases}\ZZ,&k=0,2,\\\ZZ/2\ZZ,&k=1,\\0,&k\ge3.\end{cases}$$
:::
