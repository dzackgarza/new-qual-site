::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the quotient space of $S^2$ under the identification $x\sim -x$ for $x$ in the equator.

(a) Write down a CW-decomposition of $X$.

(b) Write down the cellular chain complex for the CW structure from (a). Be sure to include the differentials of the complex.

(c) Compute the homology of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $E\subset S^2$ be the equator, and let $q:S^2\to X$ be the quotient map. The image $q(E)$ is a circle.
::: {.proof}
On $E\cong S^1$, the equivalence relation is $z\sim-z$. The quotient map
$$
S^1\longrightarrow S^1/(z\sim-z)
$$
is identified, after choosing coordinates, with the degree-two map $z\mapsto z^2$. Hence $q(E)\cong S^1$.
:::

<1>2. Give $q(E)$ the CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$ and one $1$-cell $e$. The interiors of the northern and southern hemispheres descend to two open $2$-cells $u$ and $w$.
::: {.proof}
The quotient relation identifies points only on the equator, so it is injective on the interior of each hemisphere. Each closed hemisphere is a disc whose boundary is attached to the quotient equator $q(E)$.
:::

<1>3. Each $2$-cell is attached to the $1$-skeleton by a map of degree $2$.
::: {.proof}
For either hemisphere, the attaching map is the composite
$$
S^1=\partial D^2\cong E\xrightarrow{q}E/(x\sim-x)\cong q(E)\cong S^1,
$$
which is the antipodal quotient map. Under the identification in <1>1 this is $z\mapsto z^2$, of degree $2$. Orient the two $2$-cells so that both attaching degrees are $+2$.
:::

<1>4. The cellular chain complex is
$$
0\longrightarrow C_2\cong\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}C_1\cong\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}C_0\cong\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0,
$$
with
$$
\partial_2(a,b)=2a+2b,
\qquad
\partial_1=0.
$$
::: {.proof}
The coefficient of a $2$-cell in the cellular boundary is the degree of its attaching map onto the unique $1$-cell, so <1>3 gives the row matrix $[\,2\ \ 2\,]$. The unique $1$-cell is a loop at the unique $0$-cell, hence its cellular boundary is zero.
:::

<1>5. The zeroth homology is $H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
Since $\partial_1=0$ and there is one $0$-cell,
$$
H_0(X)=C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
:::

<1>6. The first homology is $H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4,
$$
H_1(X)=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
$$
because $\operatorname{im}\partial_2=2\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>7. The second homology is $H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
There are no $3$-cells, so $H_2(X)=\ker\partial_2$. From <1>4,
$$
\ker\partial_2=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:a+b=0\}=\mathbb Z(1,-1)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
:::

<1>8. Therefore
$$
H_k(X)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,2,\\
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge3.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>5--<1>7 with the absence of cells above dimension $2$.
:::
:::
