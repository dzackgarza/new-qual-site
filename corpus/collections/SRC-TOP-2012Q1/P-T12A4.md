::: {.problem}
(a) State the definition of what it means for a topological space to be regular.
Since there is some ambiguity in the literature about the definition of the term “regular”, include the Hausdorff property in the definition (some use “regular Hausdorff” or “$T_3$” for the same thing).

(b) Prove that a subspace of a regular space is also regular.

(c) Prove that a product of two regular spaces (equipped with the product topology) is also regular.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #def-regular}
(a) A topological space $X$ is \dfn{regular} if $X$ is Hausdorff and, whenever $x\in X$ and $F\subseteq X$ is closed with $x\notin F$, there exist disjoint open sets $U,V\subseteq X$ with $x\in U$ and $F\subseteq V$.

:::

::: {.pf-step #shrink-neighborhood-lemma}
If $X$ is regular, $O\subseteq X$ is open, and $x\in O$, then there is an open set $U$ with $x\in U\subseteq\overline U\subseteq O$.

::: pf-proof
Apply step [](#def-regular){.pf-ref} to $x$ and the closed set $X\setminus O$ to obtain disjoint open sets $U,W$ with $x\in U$ and $X\setminus O\subseteq W$.
Since $U\subseteq X\setminus W$ and $X\setminus W$ is closed, $\overline U\subseteq X\setminus W\subseteq O$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b}
(b) Every subspace $A$ of a regular space $X$ is regular.

::: pf-proof
If $a\neq a'$ in $A$, disjoint open neighborhoods $U,U'$ of them in $X$ give disjoint open neighborhoods $U\cap A$, $U'\cap A$ in $A$; so $A$ is Hausdorff.
Let $a\in A$ and let $F\subseteq A$ be closed in $A$ with $a\notin F$.
Then $F=A\cap C$ for a closed set $C\subseteq X$, and $a\notin C$ because $a\in A\setminus F$.
By regularity of $X$, choose disjoint open sets $U,V\subseteq X$ with $a\in U$ and $C\subseteq V$.
Then $U\cap A$ and $V\cap A$ are disjoint open subsets of $A$ containing $a$ and $F$ respectively.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-c}
(c) If $X$ and $Y$ are regular, then $X\times Y$ is regular.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #product-hausdorff}
$X\times Y$ is Hausdorff.

::: pf-proof
If $(x_1,y_1)\neq(x_2,y_2)$, then $x_1\neq x_2$ or $y_1\neq y_2$.
Separate the differing coordinates by disjoint open sets in the corresponding Hausdorff factor and take their products with the other factor.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product-shrink-neighborhood}
For every $(x,y)\in X\times Y$ and every open $O\ni(x,y)$ there are open sets $U\subseteq X$, $V\subseteq Y$ with $(x,y)\in U\times V\subseteq\overline{U\times V}\subseteq O$.

::: pf-proof
Products of open sets form a basis of the product topology, so there are open $U_0\subseteq X$ and $V_0\subseteq Y$ with $(x,y)\in U_0\times V_0\subseteq O$.
By step [](#shrink-neighborhood-lemma){.pf-ref} in $X$ and in $Y$, there are open sets $U\ni x$ and $V\ni y$ with $\overline U\subseteq U_0$ and $\overline V\subseteq V_0$.
Since $\overline{U\times V}=\overline U\times\overline V$, we get $\overline{U\times V}\subseteq U_0\times V_0\subseteq O$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Let $F\subseteq X\times Y$ be closed with $(x,y)\notin F$.
Apply step [](#product-shrink-neighborhood){.pf-ref} to $O=(X\times Y)\setminus F$.
Then $U\times V$ and $(X\times Y)\setminus\overline{U\times V}$ are disjoint open sets containing $(x,y)$ and $F$ respectively.
Together with step [](#product-hausdorff){.pf-ref}, $X\times Y$ is regular.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#def-regular){.pf-ref} answers (a), step [](#part-b){.pf-ref} proves (b), and step [](#part-c){.pf-ref} proves (c).
:::
