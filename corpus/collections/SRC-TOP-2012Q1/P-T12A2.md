::: {.problem}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be two topological spaces and let $X\times Y$ be endowed with the product topology.

(a) Prove that if $X$ and $Y$ each have a countable dense subset, then so does $X\times Y$.

(b) Prove that if $X$ and $Y$ are both connected, then so is $X\times Y$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. (a) If $D\subseteq X$ and $E\subseteq Y$ are countable dense subsets, then $D\times E$ is a countable dense subset of $X\times Y$.

::: {.proof}
A finite Cartesian product of countable sets is countable.
For density it suffices to meet every nonempty basic open set $U\times V$ with $U\subseteq X$ and $V\subseteq Y$ open.
Since $D$ is dense, choose $d\in D\cap U$; since $E$ is dense, choose $e\in E\cap V$.
Then $(d,e)\in(D\times E)\cap(U\times V)$.
:::

<1>2. (b) If $X$ and $Y$ are connected, then $X\times Y$ is connected.

::: {.proof}
If $X$ or $Y$ is empty, then $X\times Y=\varnothing$ is connected.
Otherwise fix $y_0\in Y$, and for each $x\in X$ put $C_x=(X\times\{y_0\})\cup(\{x\}\times Y)$.
The subspaces $X\times\{y_0\}\cong X$ and $\{x\}\times Y\cong Y$ are connected and meet at $(x,y_0)$, so $C_x$ is connected.
Every $(x,y)\in X\times Y$ lies in $\{x\}\times Y\subseteq C_x$, so $X\times Y=\bigcup_{x\in X}C_x$.
Every $C_x$ contains the point $(x_1,y_0)$ for a fixed $x_1\in X$, and a union of connected subspaces with a common point is connected.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 prove (a) and (b).
:::
:::
