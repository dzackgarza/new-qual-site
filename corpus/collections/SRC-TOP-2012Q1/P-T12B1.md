::: {.problem}
Show that every map from $S^2$ to the torus is null-homotopic.
You may use without proof the fact that the fundamental group of $S^2$ is trivial.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $f:S^2\to T^2$ be continuous, and let
$$
p:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow T^2=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2
$$
be the universal covering map.
::: {.proof}
The plane $\mathbb R^2$ is simply connected, so this quotient map is the universal cover of the torus.
:::

<1>2. The map $f$ lifts to a continuous map $\widetilde f:S^2\to\mathbb R^2$ satisfying
$$
p\circ\widetilde f=f.
$$
::: {.proof}
Choose a basepoint $x_0\in S^2$ and a lift of $f(x_0)$ to $\mathbb R^2$. The covering-space lifting criterion requires
$$
f_*\bigl(\pi_1(S^2,x_0)\bigr)\subseteq p_*\bigl(\pi_1(\mathbb R^2,\widetilde f(x_0))\bigr).
$$
Both groups on the two sides of the inclusion are trivial because $\pi_1(S^2)=0$ by the hypothesis allowed in the problem and $\mathbb R^2$ is simply connected. Hence the lift exists.
:::

<1>3. The lift $\widetilde f$ is null-homotopic.
::: {.proof}
The plane $\mathbb R^2$ is contractible. Explicitly, fixing $a\in\mathbb R^2$, the homotopy
$$
H(x,t)=(1-t)\widetilde f(x)+ta
$$
contracts $\widetilde f$ to the constant map with value $a$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $f$ is null-homotopic.
::: {.proof}
Compose the homotopy in <1>3 with the covering map $p$. Since $p\circ\widetilde f=f$, this gives a homotopy from $f$ to the constant map with value $p(a)$.
:::
:::
