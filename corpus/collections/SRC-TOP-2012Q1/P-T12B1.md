::: {.problem}
Show that every map from $S^2$ to the torus is null-homotopic.
You may use without proof the fact that the fundamental group of $S^2$ is trivial.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $f\colon S^2\to T^2$ be continuous, and let $p\colon\RR^2\to T^2=\RR^2/\ZZ^2$ be the universal covering map.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #lift-exists}
$f$ lifts to a continuous map $\widetilde f\colon S^2\to\RR^2$ with $p\circ\widetilde f=f$.

::: pf-proof
Choose $x_0\in S^2$ and a point $t_0\in p^{-1}(f(x_0))$.
Since $\pi_1(S^2,x_0)=0$, the inclusion $f_*(\pi_1(S^2,x_0))\subseteq p_*(\pi_1(\RR^2,t_0))$ holds.
As $S^2$ is path-connected and locally path-connected, the lifting criterion for covering spaces gives a lift $\widetilde f$ with $\widetilde f(x_0)=t_0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lift-homotopy}
$\widetilde f$ is homotopic to a constant map.

::: pf-proof
Fix $a\in\RR^2$.
The homotopy $H(x,t)=(1-t)\widetilde f(x)+ta$ runs from $\widetilde f$ to the constant map with value $a$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
By step [](#lift-exists){.pf-ref}, $p\circ H$ is a homotopy from $p\circ\widetilde f=f$ to the constant map with value $p(a)$, with $H$ from step [](#lift-homotopy){.pf-ref}.
:::
