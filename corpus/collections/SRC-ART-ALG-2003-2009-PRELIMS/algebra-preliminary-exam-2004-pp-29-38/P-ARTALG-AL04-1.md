::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that all groups of size 15 are solvable.

(b) Prove there are no simple groups of size 36.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every group of order $15$ is solvable.

::: {.proof}
Let $|G|=15=3\cdot5$. The number $n_5$ of Sylow $5$-subgroups
divides $3$ and is $1$ modulo $5$, by Sylow's theorems [@DF04].
Of the divisors $1,3$ of $3$, only $1$ satisfies that congruence.
Thus there is a unique subgroup $P$ of order $5$; conjugation
preserves its order, so $P\lhd G$.

The group $P$ is cyclic of order $5$, and $G/P$ is cyclic of
order $3$, since a group of prime order is generated by any of
its nonidentity elements. Therefore
$$
G\triangleright P\triangleright\{1\}
$$
is a normal series with abelian factors, proving solvability.
Equivalently, the commutator subgroup $G'$ lies in $P$ because
$G/P$ is abelian, and then $G''\subseteq P'=\{1\}$ because
$P$ is abelian. This also proves solvability directly from the
derived-series definition.
:::

<1>2. Every group of order $36$ has a nontrivial proper normal subgroup.

::: {.proof}
Let $|G|=36=2^2\cdot3^2$. Sylow's theorems give
$n_3\mid4$ and $n_3\equiv1\pmod3$, so $n_3=1$ or $4$.
If $n_3=1$, the unique subgroup of order $9$ is normal and is
nontrivial and proper.

Suppose $n_3=4$. Conjugation on the four Sylow $3$-subgroups
gives a homomorphism $\rho:G\to S_4$. Its action is transitive
by Sylow conjugacy, so it is not the trivial action and
$\ker\rho\ne G$. It cannot be injective because
$|G|=36>24=|S_4|$, so $\ker\rho\ne\{1\}$.
The kernel is normal, and it is therefore a nontrivial proper
normal subgroup in this case as well. Neither possibility allows
$G$ to be simple.
:::
:::
