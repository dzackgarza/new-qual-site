::: {.problem}
(a) State Sylow's Theorem.

(b) Prove that no group of order 6545 is simple.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For part (a), Sylow's theorem states the following. If $H$ is a
finite group with
$$
\cardinality{ H}=p^a m,
\qquad
p\nmid m,
$$
then:

- $H$ has a subgroup of order $p^a$, called a Sylow $p$-subgroup;
- every $p$-subgroup of $H$ is contained in a Sylow $p$-subgroup, and all
  Sylow $p$-subgroups are conjugate;
- if $n_p$ is the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups, then
  $n_p\mid m$ and $n_p\equiv1\pmod p$. Equivalently, for any Sylow
  $p$-subgroup $P$,
  $n_p=[H:N_H(P)]$.

::: pf-proof

This is Sylow's theorem [@DF04].

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For a group $G$ of order $6545$, the number $n_5$ of Sylow
$5$-subgroups is $1$ or $11$.

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
6545=5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot17,
$$
Sylow's theorem gives
$$
n_5\mid1309=7\cdot11\cdot17,
\qquad
n_5\equiv1\pmod5.
$$
The positive divisors of $1309$ are
$$
1,\ 7,\ 11,\ 17,\ 77,\ 119,\ 187,\ 1309.
$$
Among these, only $1$ and $11$ are congruent to $1$ modulo $5$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

In either case, $G$ has a nontrivial proper normal subgroup, so
$G$ is not simple.

::: pf-proof

If $n_5=1$, the unique Sylow $5$-subgroup is normal, and its order $5$
makes it nontrivial and proper.

If $n_5=11$, conjugation on the eleven Sylow $5$-subgroups defines
$$
\rho:G\longrightarrow S_{11}.
$$
By conjugacy of Sylow subgroups, the action is transitive on a set with
more than one element, so $\rho$ is nontrivial and
$\ker\rho\ne G$. The homomorphism cannot be injective: otherwise
Lagrange's theorem would imply
$$
6545\mid11!,
$$
but $17\mid6545$ while $17\nmid11!$. Hence
$1\ne\ker\rho\ne G$. Since a kernel is normal, $G$ has a nontrivial
proper normal subgroup.

:::
