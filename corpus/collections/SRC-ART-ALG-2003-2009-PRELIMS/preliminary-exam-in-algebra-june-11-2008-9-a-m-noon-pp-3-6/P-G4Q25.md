::: {.problem}
Find the Galois groups, over $\QQ$, of the following polynomials.

(a) $x^4 - 25$.

(b) $x^3 - x + 1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The Galois group of $x^4-25$ over $\QQ$ is
$$
\boxed{C_2\times C_2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The factorization $(x^2-5)(x^2+5)$ gives the four roots
$\sqrt5,-\sqrt5,i\sqrt5,-i\sqrt5$. Hence the splitting field is
$$
L=\QQ(\sqrt5,i):
$$
it contains all the roots, and the field generated by the roots contains
$i=(i\sqrt5)/\sqrt5$. The polynomial $x^2-5$ is irreducible over
$\QQ$ by Eisenstein at $5$ [@DF04]. Since
$\QQ(\sqrt5)\subset\RR$ does not contain $i$, the tower law gives
$[L:\QQ]=2\cdot2=4$.

This is a splitting field in characteristic zero, so it is Galois and
has four automorphisms. Every automorphism sends $\sqrt5$ to one of
$\pm\sqrt5$ and $i$ to one of $\pm i$, and these images determine it.
The resulting injective homomorphism
$$
\Gal(L/\QQ)\longrightarrow \{\pm1\}\times\{\pm1\}
$$
is a bijection because both groups have order $4$. Thus independent
sign changes of $\sqrt5$ and $i$ give the claimed Klein four group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The polynomial
$$
h(x)=x^3-x+1
$$
is irreducible over $\QQ$, and the Galois group of its splitting field
acts transitively on its three roots.

::: pf-proof

A rational root of the monic polynomial $h$ must be $1$ or $-1$,
but $h(1)=h(-1)=1$. Thus $h$ is irreducible, since a reducible cubic
over a field has a linear factor. In characteristic zero its roots
are distinct. The Galois group of its splitting field acts faithfully
on those three roots, and irreducibility makes that action transitive
[@DF04]. Consequently it is a subgroup of $S_3$ whose order is
divisible by $3$, by orbit-stabilizer.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The polynomial $h$ has exactly one real root.

::: pf-proof

$h'(x)=3x^2-1$ has critical points $\pm1/\sqrt3$, and
$$
h(-1/\sqrt3)=1+\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}>0,\qquad
h(1/\sqrt3)=1-\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}>0.
$$
The function is strictly increasing from $-\infty$ to a positive value
on $(-\infty,-1/\sqrt3]$, so has precisely one root there. On the
remaining two monotonicity intervals its minimum is the positive
number $h(1/\sqrt3)$, so it has no further real root.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The Galois group of $x^3-x+1$ over $\QQ$ is
$$
\boxed{S_3}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the other two roots form a nonreal conjugate pair. Complex
conjugation preserves the splitting field, fixes the real root, and
interchanges the other two roots. It is therefore a transposition in
the Galois group, so that group's order is divisible by $2$ as well
as $3$ by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}. Being a subgroup of $S_3$, it has order at most
$6$. Its order is therefore $6$, and it is all of $S_3$.

:::
