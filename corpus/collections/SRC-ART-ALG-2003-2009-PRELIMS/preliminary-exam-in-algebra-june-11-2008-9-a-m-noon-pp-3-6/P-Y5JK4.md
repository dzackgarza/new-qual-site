::: {.problem}
a. Show that every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain.

In parts (b) and (c) assume that $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ is a Euclidean domain with respect to the norm function $N$ defined by $$N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2\text{ for all }a+bi\in\mathbf{Z}[i].$$

b. Let $I$ be a nonzero ideal of $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ generated by $\alpha$.
Show that every coset of $I$ in $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ contains an element of norm less than $N(\alpha)$.

c. Show that $\mathbf{Z}[i]/I$ is finite.
:::

::: {.solution}
**a.** We wish to show that every ideal in a Euclidean Domain is generated by a single element.
Let $R$ be a Euclidean Domain, and $I$ an ideal of $R$.
If $I$ is a zero ideal, then $I = \{0\} = (0)$ is generated by the single element $0$, so it is principal, and we are done.
Suppose $I$ is not the trivial ideal.
Then $I$ contains a non-zero element $d$ of minimum norm.
The ideal generated by this element is $(d)=\{rd:r\in R\}$.
However, since $I$ is an ideal, and $d\in R$, by definition of ideal, we may conclude $(d)\subseteq I$.
Now pick any arbitrary $a\in R$.
Since $R$ is a Euclidean domain, and $a,d\in R$ we know there exist elements $q,r\in R$ such that $a=qd+r$ where $N(r)=0$ or $N(r)<N(d)$.
However, we picked $d$ to be the element of minimum norm, hence $N(r)=0$, which implies $a=qd$, and hence $a\in(d)$.
Therefore, $I=(d)$, so any ideal in $R$ is principal and thus $R$ is a Principal Ideal Domain.

**b.** Suppose $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ is a Euclidean domain with respect to the norm $N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2\ \forall a+bi\in\mathbf{Z}[i]$.
Let $I$ be a nonzero ideal of $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ generated by $\alpha\in\mathbf{Z}[i]$.
We wish to show that every coset of $I$ in $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ contains an element of norm less than $N(\alpha)$.
We know $$(\alpha)=\{(a+bi)\alpha:a+bi\in\mathbf{Z}[i]\}$$ and any coset of $(\alpha)$ in $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ looks like $(x+iy)+(\alpha)$ for some fixed $x+iy\in\mathbf{Z}[i]$.
Since $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ is a Euclidean Domain we have the division algorithm in $R$, so for any elements $\alpha$ and $x+iy$ in $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ there exist elements $q$ and $r$ in $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ such that $x+iy=q\alpha+r$ where $N(r)=0$ or $N(r)<N(\alpha)$.
However, in this case $r=x+iy+(-q)\alpha$, which is just an element of the coset of $(\alpha)$ in $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ containing $x+iy$.
We know either $N(r)=0$ or $N(r)<N(\alpha)$, but since we picked $\alpha$ to be nonzero, in either case the norm of $r$ is less than the norm of $\alpha$.

**c.** Suppose $N(\alpha)=n\in\mathbf{Z}$.
By part (b), every coset of $I$ in $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ contains an element of norm less than $N(\alpha) = n$.
The elements of $\mathbf{Z}[i]$ of norm less than $n$ are exactly the Gaussian integers $a + bi$ with $a^2 + b^2 < n$, and there are only finitely many of them (each of $a$ and $b$ is bounded by $\sqrt{n}$ in absolute value).
Since every coset contains at least one of these finitely many elements, there are only finitely many cosets, so the quotient $\mathbf{Z}[i]/I$ is finite.
:::
