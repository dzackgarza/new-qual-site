:::{.problem}
a. List all abelian groups of size 90.
b. List all $\mathbf{R}[x]$ modules $M$ such that $\dim_{\mathbf{R}} M=4$ and $M$ is annihilated by $x^4-1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

<1>1. $90 = 2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$.
Proof: prime factorization.

<1>2. The abelian groups of order $90$ are: $\mathbb{Z}/90$, $\mathbb{Z}/30 \times \mathbb{Z}/3$, $\mathbb{Z}/6 \times \mathbb{Z}/15$ (up to isomorphism, these are $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/3^2 \times \mathbb{Z}/5$, $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}/5$, etc.; the invariant factor forms are $\mathbb{Z}/90$ and $\mathbb{Z}/30 \times \mathbb{Z}/3$).
Proof: fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups.

<1>3. More precisely, the abelian groups of order $90$ are $\mathbb{Z}/90$ and $\mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}/30$.
Proof: <1>2.

**(b).**

<1>1. $x^4 - 1 = (x-1)(x+1)(x^2+1)$ over $\mathbb{R}$.
Proof: factorization.

<1>2. The $\mathbb{R}[x]$-modules $M$ with $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} M = 4$ annihilated by $x^4 - 1$ are direct sums of modules $\mathbb{R}[x]/(p^k)$ where $p$ is an irreducible factor of $x^4 - 1$.
Proof: structure theorem for modules over a PID.

<1>3. The list is: $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^4-1)$, $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x-1) \oplus \mathbb{R}[x]/((x+1)(x^2+1))$, $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x+1) \oplus \mathbb{R}[x]/((x-1)(x^2+1))$, $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1) \oplus \mathbb{R}[x]/((x-1)(x+1))$, and $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x-1) \oplus \mathbb{R}[x]/(x+1) \oplus \mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1)$.
Proof: <1>2 (all partitions of the invariant factors dividing $x^4 - 1$ that give total $\mathbb{R}$-dimension $4$).

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>3 (a) and <1>3 (b).
:::
