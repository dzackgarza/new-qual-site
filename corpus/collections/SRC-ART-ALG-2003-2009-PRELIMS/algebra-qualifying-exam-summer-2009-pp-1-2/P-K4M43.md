::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^2 - 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

(a) What is the dimension $[K : \mathbb{Q}]$?
(b) What is the Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$? (Identify its isomorphism type and describe how its elements act as permutations on the roots of $f$).
(c) How many subfields does $K$ have of each dimension over $\mathbb{Q}$?
(d) How many subfields of $K$ of each dimension are Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $r_1=\sqrt{2+\sqrt7}>0$ and $r_2=i\sqrt{\sqrt7-2}$, so that $r_2^2=2-\sqrt7$.

<1>1. The roots of $f$ are $\pm r_1,\pm r_2$, and $K=\QQ(r_1,i\sqrt3)$.

::: {.proof}
With $u=x^2$, the equation $u^2-4u-3=0$ has roots $u=2\pm\sqrt7$, so the roots of $f$ are $r_1,r_2,-r_1,-r_2$.
Moreover $r_1r_2=i\sqrt{(\sqrt7+2)(\sqrt7-2)}=i\sqrt3$.
Thus $K=\QQ(r_1,r_2)$ contains $i\sqrt3$, and conversely $r_2=i\sqrt3/r_1\in\QQ(r_1,i\sqrt3)$.
:::

<1>2. The polynomial $f$ is irreducible over $\QQ$, and $[K:\QQ]=\boxed{8}$.

::: {.proof}
By the rational root theorem the only candidate rational roots are $\pm1,\pm3$, and $f(\pm1)=-6$, $f(\pm3)=42$.
A factorization into two monic quadratics over $\QQ$ would pair the four roots.
The pairing $\{r_1,-r_1\},\{r_2,-r_2\}$ gives $x^2-(2\pm\sqrt7)\notin\QQ[x]$.
The other two pairings give constant terms $\pm r_1r_2=\pm i\sqrt3\notin\QQ$.
Hence $f$ is irreducible and $[\QQ(r_1):\QQ]=4$.
The field $\QQ(r_1)$ is real and $i\sqrt3$ is not, so $[K:\QQ(r_1)]=2$ by step <1>1, and the tower law gives $[K:\QQ]=8$.
:::

<1>3. Number the roots $(r_1,r_2,-r_1,-r_2)$ as $1,2,3,4$. Then $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)$ is the dihedral group $D_4$ of order $8$ generated by $\sigma=(1\,2\,3\,4)$ and complex conjugation $\tau=(2\,4)$, with $\tau\sigma\tau=\sigma^{-1}$.

::: {.proof}
Each automorphism sends a root to a root and commutes with negation, so it preserves the partition $\{\{1,3\},\{2,4\}\}$.
The permutations of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ preserving this partition form a dihedral subgroup of $S_4$ of order $8$, and $\abs{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)}=[K:\QQ]=8$ by step <1>2, so the Galois group is this whole subgroup.
It contains $\sigma$ and $\tau$; complex conjugation fixes $\pm r_1$ and exchanges $r_2$ and $-r_2=\overline{r_2}$.
:::

<1>4. The quadratic subfields are $\QQ(\sqrt7)$, $\QQ(i\sqrt3)$, and $\QQ(i\sqrt{21})$, fixed respectively by $\langle\sigma^2,\tau\rangle$, $\langle\sigma^2,\sigma\tau\rangle$, and $\langle\sigma\rangle$.

::: {.proof}
We have $\sqrt7=r_1^2-2$ and $i\sqrt3=r_1r_2$.
The automorphism $\sigma$ sends $r_1\mapsto r_2$ and $r_2\mapsto-r_1$, so $\sigma(\sqrt7)=r_2^2-2=-\sqrt7$, $\sigma(i\sqrt3)=-r_2r_1=-i\sqrt3$, and $\sigma(i\sqrt{21})=\sigma(\sqrt7\cdot i\sqrt3)=i\sqrt{21}$.
The element $\sigma^2$ negates every root, so it fixes $\sqrt7$ and $i\sqrt3$; $\tau$ fixes $r_1$ and negates $r_2$, so it fixes $\sqrt7$ and negates $i\sqrt3$; hence $\sigma\tau$ negates $\sqrt7$ and fixes $i\sqrt3$.
The three subgroups of order $4$ in $D_4$ are $\langle\sigma\rangle$, $\langle\sigma^2,\tau\rangle$, and $\langle\sigma^2,\sigma\tau\rangle$, and each fixes the listed element of degree $2$; by the Galois correspondence it is the whole fixed field [@DF04].
:::

<1>5. The subfields of degree $4$ are the fixed fields of the five subgroups of order $2$: $\QQ(\sqrt7,i\sqrt3)$ for the center $\langle\sigma^2\rangle$, and four fields for $\langle\tau\rangle,\langle\sigma^2\tau\rangle,\langle\sigma\tau\rangle,\langle\sigma^3\tau\rangle$, among them $\QQ(r_1)$ for $\langle\tau\rangle$ and $\QQ(r_2)$ for $\langle\sigma^2\tau\rangle$.

::: {.proof}
$D_4$ has five elements of order $2$: $\sigma^2$ and the four reflections $\sigma^j\tau$.
The field $\QQ(\sqrt7,i\sqrt3)$ has degree $4$ and is fixed by $\sigma^2$.
The involution $\tau=(2\,4)$ fixes $r_1$, and $\sigma^2\tau=(1\,3)$ fixes $r_2$; each of $\QQ(r_1)$, $\QQ(r_2)$ has degree $4$ by step <1>2.
:::

<1>6. A subfield is Galois over $\QQ$ exactly when its subgroup is normal in $D_4$.
The normal subgroups are $D_4$, the three subgroups of order $4$, the center $\langle\sigma^2\rangle$, and $\{1\}$; the four reflection subgroups are not normal.

::: {.proof}
The first sentence is the Galois correspondence [@DF04].
Subgroups of index $2$ are normal, and the center is normal.
Conjugation by $\sigma$ sends $\sigma^j\tau$ to $\sigma^{j+2}\tau$, so no reflection subgroup is normal.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>2 through <1>6: (a) $[K:\QQ]=8$; (b) $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)\cong D_4$, acting on the roots as in step <1>3; (c) $K$ has one subfield of degree $1$, three of degree $2$, five of degree $4$, and one of degree $8$; (d) of these, one of degree $1$, three of degree $2$, one of degree $4$, and one of degree $8$ are Galois over $\QQ$.
:::
