::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^2 - 3$ over $\QQ$.

(a) What is the dimension $[K : \QQ]$?
(b) What is the Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)$? (Identify its isomorphism type and describe how its elements act as permutations on the roots of $f$).
(c) How many subfields does $K$ have of each dimension over $\QQ$?
(d) How many subfields of $K$ of each dimension are Galois over $\QQ$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
a=\sqrt{2+\sqrt7},
\qquad
b=\sqrt{2-\sqrt7},
\qquad
c=ab=\sqrt{-3}.
$$
Then the roots of $f(x)=x^4-4x^2-3$ are $a,b,-a,-b$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The splitting field is $K=\QQ(a,c)$ and
$$
[K:\QQ]=\boxed{8}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The substitution $u=x^2$ gives
$$
u^2-4u-3=0,
$$
whose roots are $2\pm\sqrt7$. Hence the four roots of $f$ are
$\pm a$ and $\pm b$, and
$$
ab=\sqrt{(2+\sqrt7)(2-\sqrt7)}=\sqrt{-3}=c.
$$
Thus $b=c/a$, so the splitting field is $K=\QQ(a,c)$.

It remains to compute the degree. The polynomial $f$ is irreducible over
$\QQ$. Indeed, if it factored into quadratics over $\QQ$, then because its
$x^3$- and $x$-coefficients vanish it would have one of the forms
$$
(x^2+r)(x^2+s)
$$
or
$$
(x^2+px+r)(x^2-px+r)
$$
with $p,r,s\in\QQ$. In the first case,
$r+s=-4$ and $rs=-3$, so $r,s$ would be the roots of
$t^2+4t-3$, whose discriminant $28$ is not a square in $\QQ$. In the
second case, comparing constant terms gives $r^2=-3$, impossible in
$\QQ$. Hence $f$ is irreducible, so $[\QQ(a):\QQ]=4$.

The field $\QQ(a)$ is contained in $\RR$, whereas
$c=\sqrt{-3}\notin\RR$. Therefore $c\notin\QQ(a)$ and
$[K:\QQ(a)]=2$. The tower law gives $[K:\QQ]=8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The Galois group is
$$
\Gal(K/\QQ)\cong\boxed{D_4}.
$$
With the roots ordered as
$$
(1,2,3,4)=(a,b,-a,-b),
$$
it is generated by
$$
r=(1\,2\,3\,4),
\qquad
s=(2\,4),
$$
with $r^4=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$. Thus its eight elements act as the
permutations $r^i$ and $sr^i$ for $0\le i<4$.

::: pf-proof

Since $K$ is the splitting field of the separable polynomial $f$ over
$\QQ$, the extension $K/\QQ$ is Galois. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref} its Galois group has
order $8$.

The $\QQ$-embedding $\QQ(a)\hookrightarrow K$ sending $a$ to the conjugate
root $b$ extends to an automorphism of the splitting field $K$. Since
$K=\QQ(a,c)$ and $c$ satisfies $T^2+3$, choose the extension for which
$c\mapsto-c$. Denote it by $r$; thus
$$
r(a)=b,
\qquad
r(c)=-c.
$$
Because $b=c/a$, this gives
$$
r(b)=\frac{-c}{b}=-a,
$$
and therefore
$$
a\mapsto b\mapsto -a\mapsto -b\mapsto a.
$$
Thus $r$ acts as $(1\,2\,3\,4)$. Complex conjugation fixes $a$ and sends
$b$ to $-b$, so it acts as $s=(2\,4)$. These permutations satisfy
$r^4=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$, and the subgroup they generate has order
$8$. Hence it is the full Galois group, isomorphic to the dihedral group
$D_4$ of order $8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The numbers of subfields of $K$ of degrees $1,2,4,8$ over $\QQ$ are,
respectively,
$$
\boxed{1,\ 3,\ 5,\ 1}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, degree-$d$ intermediate fields
correspond to subgroups of $D_4$ of index $d$. The subgroups of $D_4$ are:

- one subgroup of order $8$, namely $D_4$;
- three subgroups of order $4$, namely
  $\langle r\rangle$,
  $\langle r^2,s\rangle$, and
  $\langle r^2,rs\rangle$;
- five subgroups of order $2$, namely
  $\langle r^2\rangle$ and the four reflection subgroups
  $\langle s\rangle$,
  $\langle rs\rangle$,
  $\langle r^2s\rangle$,
  $\langle r^3s\rangle$;
- one trivial subgroup.

Their indices are $1,2,4,8$, respectively, which gives the claimed counts.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The numbers of subfields of degrees $1,2,4,8$ that are Galois over
$\QQ$ are, respectively,
$$
\boxed{1,\ 3,\ 1,\ 1}.
$$

::: pf-proof

An intermediate field is Galois over $\QQ$ exactly when the corresponding
subgroup of $D_4$ is normal. The whole group and the trivial subgroup are
normal. Every subgroup of index $2$ is normal, so all three order-$4$
subgroups are normal. Among the five order-$2$ subgroups, only
$\langle r^2\rangle$ is normal: it is the center of $D_4$, while the four
reflection subgroups form two conjugacy classes and are not normal.
Therefore the Galois intermediate fields occur in the stated numbers.

:::
