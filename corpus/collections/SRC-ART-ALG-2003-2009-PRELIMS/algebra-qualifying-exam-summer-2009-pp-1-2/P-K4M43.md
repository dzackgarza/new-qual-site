::: problem
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^2 - 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

(a) What is the dimension $[K : \mathbb{Q}]$?
(b) What is the Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$? (Identify its isomorphism type and describe how its elements act as permutations on the roots of $f$).
(c) How many subfields does $K$ have of each dimension over $\mathbb{Q}$?
(d) How many subfields of $K$ of each dimension are Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Compute the splitting field degree, Galois group $D_4$, and full subfield lattice (and normal subfields) for $x^4 - 4x^2 - 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

<1>1. Roots and Splitting Field Generation:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Setting $u = x^2$, the quadratic equation $u^2 - 4u - 3 = 0$ has roots:
        $$u = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 4(-3)}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{28}}{2} = 2 \pm \sqrt{7}.$$
    <2>2. The four roots of $f(x)$ are:
        $$r_1 = \sqrt{2 + \sqrt{7}}, \quad r_2 = \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{7}} = i \sqrt{\sqrt{7} - 2}, \quad r_3 = -r_1, \quad r_4 = -r_2.$$
    <2>3. Notice that $r_1 r_2 = \sqrt{(2 + \sqrt{7})(2 - \sqrt{7})} = \sqrt{4 - 7} = \sqrt{-3} = i\sqrt{3}$.
    <2>4. Thus the splitting field contains $\sqrt{7} = r_1^2 - 2$ and $i\sqrt{3} = r_1 r_2$, so $K = \mathbb{Q}(r_1, i\sqrt{3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2+\sqrt{7}}, i\sqrt{3})$.

<1>2. Part (a): Degree $[K : \mathbb{Q}]$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Irreducibility of $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^2 - 3$:
        - By Eisenstein's criterion at $p=3$: $3 \mid 4$, $3 \mid 3$, $3^2 \nmid 3$, but 3 doesn't divide leading coefficient 1, so $f(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$!
        - Thus $[\mathbb{Q}(r_1) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$.
    <2>2. Since $r_1 \in \mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{Q}(r_1) \subset \mathbb{R}$ is a real subfield.
    <2>3. However, $r_2 \notin \mathbb{R}$ is purely imaginary, so $K \not\subset \mathbb{R}$.
    <2>4. Since $K = \mathbb{Q}(r_1)(i\sqrt{3})$ and $i\sqrt{3} \notin \mathbb{R}$, $[K : \mathbb{Q}(r_1)] = 2$.
    <2>5. By the tower law:
        $$[K : \mathbb{Q}] = [K : \mathbb{Q}(r_1)] [\mathbb{Q}(r_1) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2 \cdot 4 = 8.$$

<1>3. Part (b): Galois Group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Numbering the roots as $(1, 2, 3, 4) = (r_1, r_2, r_3, r_4) = (r_1, r_2, -r_1, -r_2)$.
    <2>2. Any automorphism $\sigma$ preserving $f$ preserves opposite pairs $\{r_1, -r_1\}$ and $\{r_2, -r_2\}$, so $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ is a subgroup of the octic group (symmetries of the square) $D_4 \le S_4$.
    <2>3. Since $|\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})| = [K : \mathbb{Q}] = 8$, the Galois group is the full dihedral group:
        $$\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4 \text{ (order 8)}.$$
    <2>4. Explicit generators on roots $(1, 2, 3, 4)$:
        - 4-cycle rotation $\sigma = (1\,2\,3\,4)$: maps $r_1 \mapsto r_2 \mapsto -r_1 \mapsto -r_2 \mapsto r_1$.
        - Complex conjugation $\tau = (2\,4)$: fixes real roots $r_1, r_3 = -r_1$ and swaps complex roots $r_2 \leftrightarrow -r_2 = \overline{r_2}$.
        - Relation: $\tau \sigma \tau = \sigma^{-1} = \sigma^3$.

<1>4. Subgroup Lattice of $D_4$ and Subfield Lattice of $K$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, subfields of dimension $d$ correspond to subgroups of index $d$ (order $8/d$):
        - Subfields of degree 1 over $\mathbb{Q}$: correspond to subgroup of order 8 ($\mathbb{Q}$, 1 subfield).
        - Subfields of degree 2 over $\mathbb{Q}$: correspond to subgroups of index 2 (order 4).
          $D_4$ has **3 subgroups of order 4**:
          1. $\langle \sigma \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_4$ (normal).
          2. $\langle \sigma^2, \tau \rangle \cong V_4$ (normal).
          3. $\langle \sigma^2, \sigma\tau \rangle \cong V_4$ (normal).
          Corresponding fixed fields: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7}), \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{3}), \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{21})$.
        - Subfields of degree 4 over $\mathbb{Q}$: correspond to subgroups of index 4 (order 2).
          $D_4$ has **5 subgroups of order 2**:
          1. The center $Z(D_4) = \langle \sigma^2 \rangle = \langle (1\,3)(2\,4) \rangle$ (normal). Fixed field: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7}, i\sqrt{3})$ (Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$).
          2. $\langle \tau \rangle = \langle (2\,4) \rangle$ (non-normal, conjugate to $\langle \sigma^2\tau \rangle$). Fixed field: $\mathbb{Q}(r_1) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2+\sqrt{7}})$.
          3. $\langle \sigma^2\tau \rangle = \langle (1\,3) \rangle$ (non-normal). Fixed field: $\mathbb{Q}(r_2)$.
          4. $\langle \sigma\tau \rangle = \langle (1\,2)(3\,4) \rangle$ (non-normal, conjugate to $\langle \sigma^3\tau \rangle$).
          5. $\langle \sigma^3\tau \rangle = \langle (1\,4)(2\,3) \rangle$ (non-normal).
        - Subfields of degree 8 over $\mathbb{Q}$: correspond to subgroup of order 1 ($K$, 1 subfield).

<1>5. Part (c) & (d): Subfield Counts:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Degree 1:** 1 subfield ($\mathbb{Q}$), 1 is Galois.
    <2>2. **Degree 2:** 3 subfields ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7}), \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{3}), \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{21})$), all 3 are Galois (index 2 subgroups are always normal).
    <2>3. **Degree 4:** 5 subfields, exactly 1 is Galois (the biquadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7}, i\sqrt{3})$, corresponding to $Z(D_4)$).
    <2>4. **Degree 8:** 1 subfield ($K$), 1 is Galois.

<1>6. Conclusion:
    (a) $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 8$; (b) $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4$; (c) Total subfields: 1 of degree 1, 3 of degree 2, 5 of degree 4, 1 of degree 8; (d) Galois subfields: 1 of degree 1, 3 of degree 2, 1 of degree 4, 1 of degree 8. Q.E.D.
:::
