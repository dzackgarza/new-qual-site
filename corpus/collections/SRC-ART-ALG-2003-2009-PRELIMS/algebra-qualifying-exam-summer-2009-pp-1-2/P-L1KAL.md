::: {.problem}
Let $L/K$ be a Galois extension of fields, with Galois group $G = \{\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n\}$, and let $\alpha \in L$.
Prove that $L = K(\alpha)$ iff $\sigma_1(\alpha), \dots, \sigma_n(\alpha)$ are distinct.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $m_\alpha\in K[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$, and set
$$
d=\deg m_\alpha=[K(\alpha):K].
$$
Since $G=\{\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n\}$ is the Galois group of the finite
Galois extension $L/K$,
$$
[L:K]=\cardinality{ G}=n
$$
[@DF04].

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $L=K(\alpha)$, the $n$ displayed conjugates are distinct.

::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\sigma_i(\alpha)=\sigma_j(\alpha).
$$
Both automorphisms fix $K$. Hence they agree on the subfield generated by
$K$ and $\alpha$, namely $K(\alpha)=L$. Therefore
$\sigma_i=\sigma_j$, and so $i=j$. Thus the conjugates
$\sigma_1(\alpha),\ldots,\sigma_n(\alpha)$ are pairwise distinct.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If the $n$ displayed conjugates are distinct, then $L=K(\alpha)$.

::: pf-proof

For every $i$, because $\sigma_i$ fixes the coefficients of
$m_\alpha$,
$$
m_\alpha(\sigma_i(\alpha))
=\sigma_i(m_\alpha(\alpha))=0.
$$
Thus the $n$ distinct elements
$\sigma_1(\alpha),\ldots,\sigma_n(\alpha)$ are roots of
$m_\alpha$, so
$$
d\ge n.
$$
On the other hand, the tower law for
$$
K\subseteq K(\alpha)\subseteq L
$$
gives
$$
n=[L:K]=[L:K(\alpha)]d,
$$
so $d\le n$. Hence $d=n$ and $[L:K(\alpha)]=1$, which yields
$L=K(\alpha)$.

:::
