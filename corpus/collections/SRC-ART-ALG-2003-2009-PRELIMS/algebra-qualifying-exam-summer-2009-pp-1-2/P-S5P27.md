::: {.problem}
a. Let $F$ be a field and let $K$ be a field containing $F$.
Suppose that $\alpha, \beta \in K$ are both algebraic over $F$.
Prove that $\alpha + \beta$ is algebraic over $F$.

b. What is the minimal polynomial of $\sqrt{5} + 2\sqrt{7}$ over $\QQ$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For part (a), the extension $F(\alpha,\beta)/F$ is finite.

::: pf-proof

Since $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$,
$$
[F(\alpha):F]<\infty.
$$
The element $\beta$ is algebraic over $F$, hence also algebraic over the
larger field $F(\alpha)$. Therefore
$$
[F(\alpha,\beta):F(\alpha)]<\infty.
$$
The tower law gives
$$
[F(\alpha,\beta):F]
=[F(\alpha,\beta):F(\alpha)]\,[F(\alpha):F]
<\infty.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For part (a), $\alpha+\beta$ is algebraic over $F$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $F(\alpha,\beta)/F$ is finite, hence algebraic. Since
$\alpha+\beta\in F(\alpha,\beta)$, it is algebraic over $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For part (b), if
$$
\gamma=\sqrt5+2\sqrt7,
$$
then
$$
\gamma^4-66\gamma^2+529=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Squaring gives
$$
\gamma^2=33+4\sqrt{35}.
$$
Hence
$$
(\gamma^2-33)^2=16\cdot35=560.
$$
Expanding and simplifying yields
$$
\gamma^4-66\gamma^2+529=0.
$$
Thus $\gamma$ is a root of
$$
f(x)=x^4-66x^2+529\in\QQ[x].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The field $\QQ(\gamma)$ equals $\QQ(\sqrt5,\sqrt7)$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\sqrt{35}=\frac{\gamma^2-33}{4}\in\QQ(\gamma).
$$
Set
$$
\delta=\gamma\sqrt{35}.
$$
Then $\delta\in\QQ(\gamma)$ and
$$
\delta
=(\sqrt5+2\sqrt7)\sqrt{35}
=14\sqrt5+5\sqrt7.
$$
Together with
$$
\gamma=\sqrt5+2\sqrt7,
$$
this gives
$$
\sqrt5=\frac{2\delta-5\gamma}{23},
\qquad
\sqrt7=\frac{14\gamma-\delta}{23}.
$$
Therefore $\sqrt5,\sqrt7\in\QQ(\gamma)$, so
$\QQ(\sqrt5,\sqrt7)\subseteq\QQ(\gamma)$. The reverse inclusion follows
from the definition of $\gamma$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

One has
$$
[\QQ(\gamma):\QQ]=4.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s4){.pf-ref} it is enough to compute
$[\QQ(\sqrt5,\sqrt7):\QQ]$. Since
$[\QQ(\sqrt5):\QQ]=2$, it suffices to show
$\sqrt7\notin\QQ(\sqrt5)$.

Suppose instead that
$$
\sqrt7=a+b\sqrt5
\qquad(a,b\in\QQ).
$$
Squaring gives
$$
7=a^2+5b^2+2ab\sqrt5.
$$
Since $1,\sqrt5$ are linearly independent over $\QQ$, one has $ab=0$.
If $b=0$, then $a^2=7$, impossible for $a\in\QQ$. If $a=0$, then
$5b^2=7$, likewise impossible for $b\in\QQ$. Hence
$\sqrt7\notin\QQ(\sqrt5)$, and the tower law gives degree $4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The minimal polynomial of $\sqrt5+2\sqrt7$ over $\QQ$ is
$$
\boxed{x^4-66x^2+529}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the minimal polynomial divides
$x^4-66x^2+529$. By step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, the minimal polynomial has degree $4$.
Since the displayed polynomial is monic of degree $4$, it is the minimal
polynomial.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s6){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
