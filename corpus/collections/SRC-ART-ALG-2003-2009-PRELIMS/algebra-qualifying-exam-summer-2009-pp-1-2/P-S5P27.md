::: problem
a. Let $F$ be a field and let $K$ be a field containing $F$.
Suppose that $\alpha, \beta \in K$ are both algebraic over $F$.
Prove that $\alpha + \beta$ is algebraic over $F$.

b. What is the minimal polynomial of $\sqrt{5} + 2\sqrt{7}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

<1>1. Since $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$, $[F(\alpha) : F] < \infty$.
Proof: definition of algebraic.

<1>2. Since $\beta$ is algebraic over $F$, it is algebraic over $F(\alpha)$, so $[F(\alpha, \beta) : F(\alpha)] < \infty$.
Proof: <1>1 and algebraicity of $\beta$.

<1>3. Hence $[F(\alpha, \beta) : F] = [F(\alpha, \beta) : F(\alpha)] \cdot [F(\alpha) : F] < \infty$.
Proof: <1>1, <1>2, and the tower law.

<1>4. $\alpha + \beta \in F(\alpha, \beta)$, and every element of a finite extension is algebraic.
Proof: <1>3 (a finite extension is algebraic).

<1>5. Hence $\alpha + \beta$ is algebraic over $F$.
Proof: <1>4.

**(b).**

<1>1. Let $\gamma = \sqrt 5 + 2\sqrt 7$.
Proof: definition.

<1>2. $\gamma^2 = 5 + 4\sqrt{35} + 28 = 33 + 4\sqrt{35}$.
Proof: square.

<1>3. Hence $(\gamma^2 - 33)^2 = (4\sqrt{35})^2 = 16 \cdot 35 = 560$.
Proof: <1>2.

<1>4. Therefore $\gamma^4 - 66\gamma^2 + 1089 - 560 = 0$, i.e. $\gamma^4 - 66\gamma^2 + 529 = 0$.
Proof: <1>3, expanding.

<1>5. Hence $\gamma$ is a root of $f(x) = x^4 - 66x^2 + 529$.
Proof: <1>4.

<1>6. $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (it is Eisenstein at $23$: $529 = 23^2$, $66$ is divisible by $23$? No, $66$ is not divisible by $23$; instead, $f$ is irreducible because $[\mathbb{Q}(\gamma) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$, which follows since $\gamma$ generates $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 5, \sqrt 7)$ of degree $4$).
Proof: $\mathbb{Q}(\gamma) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 5, \sqrt 7)$ (since $\gamma = \sqrt 5 + 2\sqrt 7$ and $\sqrt 5, \sqrt 7$ are recoverable), and $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 5, \sqrt 7) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$.

<1>7. Hence the minimal polynomial of $\gamma$ is $x^4 - 66x^2 + 529$.
Proof: <1>5 and <1>6.

<1>8. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>5 (a) and <1>7 (b).
:::
