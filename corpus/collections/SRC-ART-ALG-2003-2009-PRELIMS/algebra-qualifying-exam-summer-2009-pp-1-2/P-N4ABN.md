::: {.problem}
If $A$ and $B$ are normal in $G$, and $G/A$ and $G/B$ are abelian, show that $G/(A \cap B)$ is also abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $G/A$ abelian means $[G, G] \subseteq A$.
::: {.proof}
Let $\pi_A:G\to G/A$ be the quotient map. Since $G/A$ is abelian, for every $x,y\in G$ one has
$$
\pi_A([x,y])=[\pi_A(x),\pi_A(y)]=1.
$$
Thus every commutator lies in $\ker\pi_A=A$. Since $[G,G]$ is generated by all commutators,
$$
[G,G]\subseteq A.
$$
:::

<1>2. Similarly $[G, G] \subseteq B$.
::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>1 to the quotient map $G\to G/B$.
:::

<1>3. Hence $[G, G] \subseteq A \cap B$.
::: {.proof}
By step <1>1 and step <1>2, every element of $[G,G]$ belongs to both $A$ and $B$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\boxed{G/(A\cap B)\text{ is abelian}}.
$$
::: {.proof}
For any normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$, the quotient $G/N$ is abelian exactly when every commutator maps to the identity, equivalently when $[G,G]\subseteq N$. Apply this criterion to
$$
N=A\cap B
$$
using step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
