::: problem
Describe the set $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}_\infty)$ of analytic isomorphisms on $\mathbb{C}_\infty$ explicitly, where $\mathbb{C}_\infty=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}$ is the Riemann sphere.
Here a function $f$ that has a pole at $z_0$ is considered to be analytic at $z_0$ as a mapping that sends $z_0$ to the point $\infty$ on the Riemann sphere $\mathbb{C}_\infty$.
:::

::: solution
The automorphisms are exactly the Möbius transformations
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\qquad ad-bc\ne0,}
\]
with the usual values at a pole and at $\infty$.

Indeed, let $f\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C_\infty)$. Choose a Möbius
transformation $M$ sending $f(\infty)$ to $\infty$. Then
$g=M\circ f$ is an automorphism of the sphere fixing $\infty$. Its restriction
to $\mathbb C$ is therefore an injective entire function.

The singularity of $g$ at $\infty$ cannot be essential, since injectivity
would contradict the Casorati--Weierstrass theorem (indeed Great Picard gives
an even stronger contradiction). It is not removable because $g(\infty)=\infty$.
Thus $\infty$ is a pole, so $g$ is a polynomial. An injective polynomial has
degree one: if its degree were at least two, its derivative would have a zero,
whereas an injective holomorphic function has nonzero derivative everywhere.
Hence $g(z)=\alpha z+\beta$ with $\alpha\ne0$. Therefore
$f=M^{-1}\circ g$ is Möbius.

Conversely every Möbius transformation with $ad-bc\ne0$ has the Möbius inverse
\[
w\longmapsto\frac{dw-b}{-cw+a},
\]
so it is an analytic automorphism of the Riemann sphere.
:::
