::: {.problem}
(a) Let $u(x,y)=y^3-3x^2 y$.
Find its harmonic conjugate $v(x,y)$.

(b) Let $\Omega$ be a connected domain in the complex plane and let $u$ be harmonic in $\Omega$.
Prove that $u$ satisfies the mean-value property in $\Omega$.
That is, for each $a$ in $\Omega$ there exists $r_0>0$ such that
$$
u(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}u\bigl(a+re^{it}\bigr)\,dt\qquad\text{for }0<r\le r_0.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's `provenance` is the Spring 2009 examination paper `assets/attachments/Complex_Analysis_Spring_2009.docx`, and no solutions sheet accompanies it, so the argument below is the card's own. Throughout $u$ is real-valued, and $B(a,r) = \theset{z \mid \abs{z-a} < r}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cr}
(a) A harmonic conjugate $v$ of $u(x,y) = y^{3} - 3x^{2}y$ must satisfy $v_{y} = u_{x} = -6xy$ and $v_{x} = -u_{y} = 3x^{2} - 3y^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
$u_{x} = -6xy$ and $u_{y} = 3y^{2} - 3x^{2}$. The Cauchy--Riemann equations for $F = u + iv$ are $u_{x} = v_{y}$ and $u_{y} = -v_{x}$, which give the two displays.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #integrate}
(a) The first equation forces $v(x,y) = -3xy^{2} + \varphi(x)$, and the second then forces $\varphi'(x) = 3x^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
Integrating $v_{y} = -6xy$ with respect to $y$ at fixed $x$ gives $v = -3xy^{2} + \varphi(x)$ for a differentiable function $\\varphi$ of $x$ alone. Differentiating in $x$ gives $v_{x} = -3y^{2} + \\varphi'(x)$, and comparing with $v_{x} = 3x^{2} - 3y^{2}$ leaves $\\varphi'(x) = 3x^{2}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #v}
(a) $v(x,y) = \boxed{x^{3} - 3xy^{2} + C}$ for a real constant $C$.

::: pf-proof
$\\varphi'(x) = 3x^{2}$ gives $\\varphi(x) = x^{3} + C$ on the connected interval where $x$ ranges, so $v = x^{3} - 3xy^{2} + C$. Conversely this $v$ has $v_{y} = -6xy = u_{x}$ and $v_{x} = 3x^{2} - 3y^{2} = -u_{y}$, so it is a harmonic conjugate; $u$ is harmonic, being the real part of the polynomial $-i\\zeta^{3}$ in $\\zeta = x + iy$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #disk}
(b) For $a \in \Omega$ there is $r_{0} > 0$ with $\overline{B(a,r_{0})} \subseteq \Omega$, and on $B(a,r_{0})$ the function $u$ has a harmonic conjugate $v$.

::: pf-proof
$\\Omega$ is open, so a closed disc about $a$ lies in it for small $r_{0}$. The open disc $B(a,r_{0})$ is convex, hence simply connected, and a harmonic function on a simply connected domain has a harmonic conjugate there.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #holomorphic}
(b) $F \coloneqq u + iv$ is holomorphic on $B(a,r_{0})$.

::: pf-proof
$u$ and $v$ satisfy the Cauchy--Riemann equations on $B(a,r_{0})$, by the definition of harmonic conjugate, and have continuous first partial derivatives there, since $u$ is harmonic and $v$ is its conjugate; the Cauchy--Riemann equations with continuous partials characterise holomorphy.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #mv}
(b) $u(a) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u\bigl(a + re^{it}\bigr)dt$ for every $0 < r \le r_{0}$.

::: pf-proof
Cauchy's integral formula applied to the holomorphic $F$ on the disc $B(a,r_{0})$, whose closure is in $\\Omega$ and which contains the circle of radius $r \\le r_{0}$ about $a$, gives $F(a) = \\frac{1}{2\\pi}\\int_{0}^{2\\pi}F(a+re^{it})dt$ after parametrising the circle. Taking real parts gives the displayed identity for $u$, and the left side is the real number $u(a)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
The two parts are answered: (a) $v(x,y) = x^{3} - 3xy^{2} + C$, and (b) each $a \in \Omega$ has a radius $r_{0}$ with the mean-value identity for $0 < r \\le r_{0}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#v){.pf-ref} is part (a); steps [](#disk){.pf-ref}, [](#holomorphic){.pf-ref} and [](#mv){.pf-ref} are part (b), which asks for the existence of $r_{0}$ at each point and the identity for the radii up to it.

:::
