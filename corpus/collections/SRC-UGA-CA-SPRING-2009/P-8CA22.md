::: {.problem}
Consider the transformation $w=z+\dfrac{1}{z}$ where $w=u+iv$ and $z=x+iy$.
Let $\Omega$ be the domain consisting of all the points in the upper half-plane $y>0$ which are exterior to the circle $\abs{z}=1$.

(a) Show that the image of $\Omega$ is the entire half-plane $v>0$.

(b) What is the image of the boundary of $\Omega$?
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this exercise: the card's own argument follows, with the inclusion, the converse and the boundary image as separate steps.

Let $w=z+1/z$ and $\Omega=\theset{z\mid \operatorname{Im}z>0,\ \abs{z}>1}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #inside}
For $z=re^{it}\in\Omega$ one has $\operatorname{Im}w=\big(r-\tfrac1r\big)\sin t>0$; so $w(\Omega)$ lies in the upper half-plane.

::: pf-proof
$\frac1z=\frac1r e^{-it}$, so $w=\big(r+\frac1r\big)\cos t+i\big(r-\frac1r\big)\sin t$ and $\operatorname{Im}w=(r-\frac1r)\sin t$; here $r>1$ gives $r-\frac1r>0$ and $0<t<\pi$ gives $\sin t>0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #onto}
Every $w$ with $\operatorname{Im}w>0$ is the image of exactly one point of $\Omega$.

::: pf-proof
The preimages of $w$ are the roots of $z^{2}-wz+1=0$, whose product is $1$; so they are $z$ and $1/z$, and they are distinct with $\abs{z}\ne1$ for both, because $\abs{z}=1$ would give $w=z+\bar z=2\operatorname{Re}z\in\RR$, contradicting $\operatorname{Im}w>0$. Hence exactly one root has $\abs{z}>1$. Writing that root as $z=re^{it}$ with $r>1$, step [](#inside){.pf-ref} gives $\operatorname{Im}w=(r-\frac1r)\sin t$, and since $r-\frac1r>0$ and $\operatorname{Im}w>0$ we get $\sin t>0$, that is $0<t<\pi$ and $\operatorname{Im}z>0$. So $z\in\Omega$ and $w(z)=w$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boundary}
The image of $\partial\Omega$ is the real axis $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
$\partial\Omega$ is the union of the upper unit semicircle $\theset{e^{it}\mid0\le t\le\pi}$ and the segment $[-1,1]$ on the real axis. On the semicircle $w=e^{it}+e^{-it}=2\cos t$ runs over $[-2,2]$. On the segment, $z$ real with $0<\abs{z}\le1$ gives $w=z+1/z$ real, equal to $2$ at $z=1$, tending to $+\infty$ as $z\to0^{+}$ and covering $[2,\infty)$; and for negative $z$ the same function covers $(-\infty,-2]$, with $w=-2$ at $z=-1$. So the image is $(-\infty,-2]\cup[-2,2]\cup[2,\infty)=\RR$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
(a) is steps [](#inside){.pf-ref} and [](#onto){.pf-ref}: $w$ maps $\Omega$ bijectively onto the half-plane $v>0$. (b) is step [](#boundary){.pf-ref}: the boundary of $\Omega$ maps onto the real axis, the boundary of the image half-plane, consistently with $w$ being a homeomorphism of $\Omega$ onto its image.
:::
