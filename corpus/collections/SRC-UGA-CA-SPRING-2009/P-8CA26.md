::: problem
(a) Show that $\dfrac{\pi^2}{\sin^2\pi z}$ and
\[
g(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(z-n)^2}
\]
have the same principal part at each integer point.

(b) Conclude that
\[
\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2\pi z}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(z-n)^2}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

<1>1. $\sin \pi z$ has a simple zero at each integer $n$, so $\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z}$ has a double pole at each integer.
Proof: $\sin(\pi z) = \pi(z - n) + O((z-n)^3)$ near $z = n$.

<1>2. Near $z = n$, $\sin \pi z = \pi(z-n) - \frac{\pi^3}{6}(z-n)^3 + \cdots$, so $\sin^2 \pi z = \pi^2(z-n)^2 - \frac{\pi^4}{3}(z-n)^4 + \cdots$.
Proof: Taylor expansion.

<1>3. Hence $\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z} = \frac{1}{(z-n)^2} + O(1)$ near $z = n$ (the principal part is exactly $\frac{1}{(z-n)^2}$).
Proof: <1>2, inverting the series.

<1>4. The term $\frac{1}{(z-n)^2}$ in $g(z)$ has principal part $\frac{1}{(z-n)^2}$ at $z = n$, and all other terms of $g$ are holomorphic at $z = n$.
Proof: each term $\frac{1}{(z-m)^2}$ for $m \neq n$ is holomorphic near $z = n$.

<1>5. Hence $\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z}$ and $g(z)$ have the same principal part $\frac{1}{(z-n)^2}$ at each integer $n$.
Proof: <1>3 and <1>4.

**(b).**

<1>1. Let $h(z) = \frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z} - g(z)$.
Proof: definition.

<1>2. $h$ is entire (the principal parts cancel at every integer by (a)).
Proof: <1>5 (a) (both functions have the same principal parts, so their difference has removable singularities at every integer).

<1>3. $h$ is bounded.
Proof: both $\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z}$ and $g(z)$ are bounded on the complement of a neighborhood of the integers (they are periodic and tend to $0$ as $|\operatorname{Im} z| \to \infty$), and $h$ is continuous (entire), so $h$ is bounded.

<1>4. By Liouville's theorem, $h$ is constant.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>3.

<1>5. $h \to 0$ as $\operatorname{Im} z \to \infty$ (both terms tend to $0$), so the constant is $0$.
Proof: <1>4 and the decay.

<1>6. Hence $\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z} = g(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(z-n)^2}$.
Proof: <1>5.

<1>7. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>5 (a) and <1>6 (b).
:::
