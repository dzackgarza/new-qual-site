::: problem
Compute the homology of the space $X$ obtained by attaching a Möbius band to $\RP^2$ via a homeomorphism of its boundary circle to the standard $\RP^1$ in $\RP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $M$ denote the Möbius band and let
\[
C=\partial M\cong S^1
\]
be the attaching circle, identified homeomorphically with the standard
\[
\RP^1\subset\RP^2.
\]

<1>1. The relevant homology groups are
\[
H_1(C)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_1(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ/2,
\qquad
H_1(M)\cong\ZZ,
\]
while
\[
H_2(C)=H_2(\RP^2)=H_2(M)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The circle has the usual homology.
The standard CW structure on $\RP^2$ has one cell in dimensions $0,1,2$ and cellular boundary
\[
\partial_2:\ZZ\longrightarrow\ZZ,
\qquad
\partial_2(n)=2n,
\]
so
\[
H_1(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ/2,
\qquad
H_2(\RP^2)=0.
\]
The Möbius band deformation retracts onto its core circle, hence
\[
H_1(M)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_2(M)=0.
\]
:::

<1>2. Under suitable choices of generators, the map induced by the two inclusions in Mayer--Vietoris is
\[
\psi:H_1(C)\longrightarrow H_1(\RP^2)\oplus H_1(M),
\qquad
\psi(n)=(\bar n,-2n).
\]
::: {.proof}
First consider
\[
C\cong\RP^1\hookrightarrow\RP^2.
\]
The standard $\RP^1$ is the $1$-skeleton of the usual CW structure on $\RP^2$.
Its fundamental loop maps to the generator of
\[
\pi_1(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ/2,
\]
so after abelianization the induced map
\[
H_1(C)\cong\ZZ\longrightarrow H_1(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ/2
\]
is reduction modulo $2$.

For the inclusion
\[
\partial M\hookrightarrow M,
\]
deform $M$ onto its core circle.
A single traversal of the boundary circle travels twice around the core, so on $H_1$ the inclusion is multiplication by $2$, up to an orientation sign.

The Mayer--Vietoris homomorphism is the difference of the two inclusion maps.
Choosing orientations so that the second inclusion is $+2$ gives
\[
\psi(n)=(\bar n,-2n).
\]
Changing either orientation does not alter its kernel or cokernel up to canonical isomorphism.
:::

<1>3. The Mayer--Vietoris sequence gives
\[
H_2(X)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using open neighborhoods of the two attached pieces that deformation retract onto $\RP^2$ and $M$, with intersection retracting onto $C$, the Mayer--Vietoris sequence contains
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_2(X)
\longrightarrow
H_1(C)
\xrightarrow{\psi}
H_1(\RP^2)\oplus H_1(M).
\]
By <1>2,
\[
\psi(n)=(\bar n,-2n).
\]
If $\psi(n)=0$, then its second component gives $2n=0$ in $\ZZ$, hence $n=0$.
Thus $\psi$ is injective, so exactness gives
\[
H_2(X)=0.
\]
:::

<1>4. One has
\[
H_1(X)\cong\ZZ/4.
\]
::: {.proof}
The next part of Mayer--Vietoris is
\[
H_1(C)
\xrightarrow{\psi}
H_1(\RP^2)\oplus H_1(M)
\longrightarrow
H_1(X)
\longrightarrow
H_0(C)
\longrightarrow
H_0(\RP^2)\oplus H_0(M).
\]
All three spaces $C,\RP^2,M$ are connected, so the last map is, up to sign,
\[
\ZZ\longrightarrow\ZZ\oplus\ZZ,
\qquad
n\longmapsto(n,-n),
\]
which is injective.
Exactness therefore gives
\[
H_1(X)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}\psi
\cong
\frac{\ZZ/2\oplus\ZZ}{\langle(\bar1,-2)\rangle}.
\]

Let $u$ denote the order-$2$ generator of the first summand and $v$ the generator of the second.
The quotient has relations
\[
2u=0,
\qquad
u-2v=0.
\]
Eliminating $u$ gives the single relation
\[
4v=0.
\]
Hence
\[
H_1(X)\cong\ZZ/4.
\]
:::

<1>5. Finally,
\[
H_0(X)\cong\ZZ
\]
and
\[
H_n(X)=0
\qquad(n\ge3).
\]
::: {.proof}
The two connected pieces are glued along the nonempty connected circle $C$, so $X$ is connected and therefore $H_0(X)\cong\ZZ$.

The space is obtained from two $2$-dimensional CW complexes by a cellular gluing along a circle, so it has the homotopy type of a $2$-dimensional CW complex.
Thus its homology vanishes in degrees at least $3$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_n(X;\ZZ)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&n=0,\\
\ZZ/4,&n=1,\\
0,&n\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
This combines <1>3--<1>5.
:::
