::: problem
Start with the unit disk $\DD^2$ and identify points on the boundary if their angles, thought of in polar coordinates, differ a multiple of $\pi/2$.

Let $X$ be the resulting space.
Use van Kampen's theorem to compute $\pi_1 (X, \ast)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The quotient of the boundary circle by the given relation is again a circle, and under the identification
\[
S^1/(z\sim iz)\cong S^1,
\qquad
[z]\longmapsto z^4,
\]
the quotient map of the boundary has degree $4$.
::: {.proof}
Two points $z,w\in S^1$ have angles differing by a multiple of $\pi/2$ exactly when
\[
w=i^jz
\]
for some $j\in\{0,1,2,3\}$.
This is equivalent to $w^4=z^4$.
Hence $z\mapsto z^4$ is constant exactly on the equivalence classes and induces a continuous bijection
\[
S^1/(z\sim iz)\longrightarrow S^1.
\]
The source is compact and the target is Hausdorff, so this bijection is a homeomorphism.
With this identification, traversing the original boundary circle once traverses the quotient circle four times.
:::

<1>2. Therefore $X$ is the adjunction space
\[
X\cong S^1\cup_{\varphi}D^2,
\qquad
\varphi:S^1\to S^1,
\qquad
\varphi(z)=z^4.
\]
::: {.proof}
The quotient does not identify any points in the interior of the disk.
Its only boundary identifications replace $\partial D^2$ by the orbit circle from <1>1.
Thus the image of the disk interior is one open $2$-cell, whose attaching map to the quotient boundary circle is precisely the boundary quotient map $\varphi$.
:::

<1>3. Choose open sets $U,V\subseteq X$ so that
\[
U\simeq S^1,
\qquad
V\simeq *,
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq S^1,
\]
and the homomorphism
\[
\pi_1(U\cap V)\longrightarrow\pi_1(U)
\]
induced by inclusion is multiplication by $4$.
::: {.proof}
Use the model in <1>2 and write points of the attached disk in polar coordinates $(r,z)$ with $0\le r\le1$ and $z\in S^1$, the boundary point $(1,z)$ being attached to $\varphi(z)$ on the $1$-skeleton.

Let $U$ consist of the $1$-skeleton together with the annular part $r>1/3$ of the $2$-cell, and let $V$ be the part $r<2/3$ of the open $2$-cell.
Their inverse images in the quotient disk are the saturated open sets $\{r>1/3\}$ and $\{r<2/3\}$, respectively, so $U$ and $V$ are open in $X$; together they cover $X$.

Radial motion from $r>1/3$ toward $r=1$ deformation retracts $U$ onto the $1$-skeleton $S^1$.
The set $V$ is an open disk and is contractible.
The intersection is an annulus and deformation retracts onto any circle $r=r_0$ with $1/3<r_0<2/3$.

Under the retraction $U\to S^1$, the loop
\[
z\longmapsto(r_0,z)
\]
is carried radially to the boundary and then attached by
\[
z\longmapsto z^4.
\]
Hence a generator of $\pi_1(U\cap V)\cong\ZZ$ maps to four times a generator of $\pi_1(U)\cong\ZZ$.
:::

<1>4. Van Kampen's theorem gives
\[
\pi_1(X,*)\cong\ZZ/4\ZZ.
\]
::: {.proof}
Choose the basepoint in $U\cap V$.
Let $a$ generate
\[
\pi_1(U)\cong\ZZ.
\]
Since $V$ is simply connected, van Kampen says that $\pi_1(X)$ is the quotient of $\pi_1(U)$ by the normal subgroup generated by the image of $\pi_1(U\cap V)$.
By <1>3 this image is generated by $a^4$.
Therefore
\[
\pi_1(X,*)
\cong
\langle a\mid a^4=1\rangle
\cong
\boxed{\ZZ/4\ZZ}.
\]
:::
