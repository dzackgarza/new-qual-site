::: problem
State, without proof, a criterion in terms of the fundamental group for a covering map $p:\widetilde{X}\to X$ to be regular.
Give an example of a covering map that is not regular, and show explicitly how it fails to satisfy this criterion.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let
\[
p:(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\longrightarrow(X,x_0)
\]
be a connected covering of a path-connected, locally path-connected space.
Set
\[
H=p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\le\pi_1(X,x_0).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{p\text{ is regular if and only if }H\trianglelefteq\pi_1(X,x_0).}
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the requested fundamental-group criterion; no proof of the criterion is required.
:::

<1>2. Let
\[
X=S^1_a\vee S^1_b,
\qquad
\pi_1(X,x_0)=F(a,b).
\]
Define a three-sheeted covering by the monodromy action on the fiber $\{1,2,3\}$
\[
\rho(a)=(23),
\qquad
\rho(b)=(12).
\]
This covering is connected.
::: {.proof}
Concretely, take three vertices $\widetilde x_1,\widetilde x_2,\widetilde x_3$ over $x_0$.
For each $i$, attach an oriented lift of the $a$-edge from $\widetilde x_i$ to $\widetilde x_{\rho(a)(i)}$, and an oriented lift of the $b$-edge from $\widetilde x_i$ to $\widetilde x_{\rho(b)(i)}$; each lifted edge maps homeomorphically to its labeled edge downstairs.
The resulting labeled graph is the standard covering graph determined by $\rho$.

The permutations $(23)$ and $(12)$ generate $S_3$, which acts transitively on $\{1,2,3\}$.
Equivalently, every vertex of the covering graph can be reached from every other by lifting a word in $a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}$.
Hence the covering graph is connected.
:::

<1>3. With basepoint $\widetilde x_1$, the subgroup
\[
H=p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_1)
\]
is exactly
\[
H=\{w\in F(a,b):\rho(w)(1)=1\}.
\]
::: {.proof}
A loop in $X$ based at $x_0$ represents an element of $H$ exactly when its lift starting at $\widetilde x_1$ is again a loop.
By the definition of monodromy, the endpoint of that lift is $\widetilde x_{\rho(w)(1)}$.
Thus the lift closes precisely when $\rho(w)(1)=1$.
:::

<1>4. The subgroup $H$ is not normal in $F(a,b)$.
::: {.proof}
Since
\[
\rho(a)=(23)
\]
fixes $1$, <1>3 gives
\[
a\in H.
\]
But
\[
\rho(bab^{-1})
=(12)(23)(12)
=(13),
\]
which does not fix $1$.
Therefore
\[
bab^{-1}\notin H.
\]
Thus $H$ contains $a$ but not its conjugate by $b$, so $H$ is not normal.
:::

<1>5. The covering in <1>2 is not regular.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4 its associated subgroup $H$ is not normal in $\pi_1(X,x_0)$.
The criterion in <1>1 therefore implies that the covering is not regular.
:::
