::: {.problem}
Show that if $X=\mathbb R$ is the set of real numbers with the infinite ray topology $\mathcal T_X=\{(a,\infty)\mid a\in\mathbb R\}\cup\{\varnothing,\mathbb R\}$, then there are compact subsets $C,D$ of $X$ such that $C\cap D$ is not compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
S=\left\{\frac1n:n\in\mathbb N,\ n\ge1\right\},
\qquad
C=S\cup\{0\},
\qquad
D=S\cup\{-1\}.
\]

<1>1. Every nonempty subset $K\subseteq X$ that has a minimum element is compact.
::: {.proof}
Let $m=\min K$, and let $\mathcal U$ be an open cover of $K$.
Choose $U\in\mathcal U$ with
\[
m\in U.
\]
The nonempty open set $U$ is either $X$ or a ray
\[
(a,\infty)
\]
for some $a\in\mathbb R$.

If $U=X$, then $U$ already covers $K$.
Otherwise $m\in(a,\infty)$, so $a<m$.
Every $x\in K$ satisfies $x\ge m>a$, hence
\[
x\in(a,\infty)=U.
\]
Thus in either case the single member $U$ covers $K$.
Every open cover therefore has a finite subcover, so $K$ is compact.
:::

<1>2. The sets $C$ and $D$ are compact.
::: {.proof}
Every element of $S$ is positive, so
\[
\min C=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\min D=-1.
\]
Apply <1>1 to each set.
:::

<1>3. We have
\[
C\cap D=S.
\]
::: {.proof}
Both sets contain $S$.
The only point of $C$ outside $S$ is $0$, which is not in $D$, and the only point of $D$ outside $S$ is $-1$, which is not in $C$.
Hence their intersection is exactly $S$.
:::

<1>4. The family
\[
\mathcal V
=
\left\{
\left(\frac1n,\infty\right):n\ge2
\right\}
\]
is an open cover of $S$.
::: {.proof}
Each member is open by the definition of the infinite-ray topology.
Take any $1/k\in S$.
Choose an integer
\[
n>\max\{k,1\}.
\]
Then
\[
\frac1n<\frac1k,
\]
so
\[
\frac1k\in\left(\frac1n,\infty\right).
\]
Thus every point of $S$ is covered.
:::

<1>5. The cover $\mathcal V$ has no finite subcover.
::: {.proof}
Take finitely many members, with indices
\[
n_1,\ldots,n_r,
\]
and put
\[
N=\max\{n_1,\ldots,n_r\}.
\]
Since the rays are nested,
\[
\bigcup_{j=1}^r
\left(\frac1{n_j},\infty\right)
=
\left(\frac1N,\infty\right).
\]
But
\[
\frac1{N+1}\in S
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac1{N+1}\le\frac1N,
\]
so this point is not in the finite union.
Hence no finite subfamily covers $S$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $C$ and $D$ are compact while $C\cap D$ is not compact.
::: {.proof}
Compactness of $C,D$ is <1>2.
By <1>3, their intersection is $S$, and <1>4--<1>5 exhibit an open cover of $S$ with no finite subcover.
Thus $C\cap D$ is not compact, as required.
:::
:::
