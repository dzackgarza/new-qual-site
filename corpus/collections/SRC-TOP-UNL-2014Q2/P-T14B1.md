::: {.problem}
Let $Y=(I\times I)/\sim$ be the quotient of the square using the equivalence relation generated by $(0,s)\sim(1,1-s)$ for all $s\in I$ and $(0,0)\sim(0,1)$.
That is, $Y$ is a Möbius band with two distinct points on its boundary glued together.

(a) Compute $\pi_1(Y)$.

(b) Describe a $\Delta$-complex structure on $Y$, and use your complex to compute the simplicial homology groups of $Y$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Label the vertices of the square counterclockwise by
\[
A=(0,0),\qquad B=(1,0),\qquad C=(1,1),\qquad D=(0,1),
\]
and triangulate the square by the diagonal $AC$.

Orient the following edges:
\[
a:A\to B,
\qquad
b:D\to C,
\qquad
x:B\to C,
\qquad
d:A\to C.
\]

<1>1. All four vertices have one common image $v$ in $Y$.
::: {.proof}
The Möbius identification
\[
(0,s)\sim(1,1-s)
\]
gives
\[
A\sim C,
\qquad
D\sim B.
\]
The additional identification gives
\[
A\sim D.
\]
By transitivity,
\[
A\sim B\sim C\sim D.
\]
:::

<1>2. The left edge $A\to D$ is identified with the right edge $C\to B$, hence with $x^{-1}$.
::: {.proof}
Parametrize the left edge by
\[
s\longmapsto(0,s),
\qquad 0\le s\le1.
\]
Under the defining side identification it maps to
\[
s\longmapsto(1,1-s),
\]
which runs down the right edge from $C$ to $B$.
Since $x$ is oriented from $B$ to $C$, this is the reverse orientation $x^{-1}$.
:::

<1>3. The quotient of the chosen triangulation is a $\Delta$-complex with one $0$-simplex $v$, four $1$-simplices $a,b,x,d$, and two $2$-simplices
\[
\sigma=[A,B,C],
\qquad
\tau=[A,C,D].
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 all original vertices become the single $0$-simplex $v$.
The bottom edge, top edge, and diagonal have distinct interiors and give the edge classes $a,b,d$.
The two vertical edges are identified with opposite orientations by <1>2 and give the single edge class $x$.

The quotient relation acts only on the boundary of the square, so the interiors of the two triangles remain disjoint and inject into $Y$.
Their face maps land in the four stated edge classes.
Thus the quotient characteristic maps satisfy the defining conditions of a $\Delta$-complex.
:::

<1>4. The two $2$-simplex attaching words are
\[
a x d^{-1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
d b^{-1}x.
\]
::: {.proof}
Traverse the oriented boundary of $\sigma=[A,B,C]$:
\[
A\to B\to C\to A.
\]
These three directed edges are respectively
\[
a,\quad x,\quad d^{-1},
\]
giving $a x d^{-1}$.

For $\tau=[A,C,D]$, traverse
\[
A\to C\to D\to A.
\]
The first edge is $d$.
Since $b$ is oriented $D\to C$, the edge $C\to D$ is $b^{-1}$.
Finally $A\to D$ is $x^{-1}$ by <1>2, so its reverse $D\to A$ is $x$.
Thus the word is
\[
d b^{-1}x.
\]
:::

<1>5. The fundamental group has presentation
\[
\pi_1(Y,v)
\cong
\left\langle
a,b,x,d
\ \middle|\
a x d^{-1}=1,\ d b^{-1}x=1
\right\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton of the $\Delta$-complex in <1>3 is a wedge of four circles because it has one vertex and four loop edges.
Its fundamental group is therefore free on $a,b,x,d$.
Attaching the two $2$-simplices quotients this free group by the normal closures of their attaching words from <1>4. This is the cellular form of the Seifert--van Kampen theorem and gives the displayed presentation.
:::

<1>6. We have
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(Y)\cong F_2.}
\]
::: {.proof}
From the first relator in <1>5,
\[
d=ax.
\]
From the second,
\[
d b^{-1}x=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b=xd.
\]
Thus both $d$ and $b$ can be eliminated by Tietze transformations, leaving no relation on $a$ and $x$:
\[
\pi_1(Y)
\cong
\langle a,x\mid\ \rangle
=F(a,x)\cong F_2.
\]
This proves part (a).
:::

<1>7. The simplicial chain complex of the $\Delta$-complex is
\[
0\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
\mathbb Z^4
\xrightarrow{0}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0,
\]
where, in the ordered edge basis $(a,b,x,d)$,
\[
\partial_2(\sigma)=a+x-d,
\qquad
\partial_2(\tau)=x+d-b.
\]
::: {.proof}
There are two $2$-simplices, four $1$-simplices, and one $0$-simplex by <1>3. Every edge begins and ends at $v$, so $\partial_1=0$.

For $\sigma=[A,B,C]$, the simplicial boundary formula gives
\[
\partial_2\sigma
=[B,C]-[A,C]+[A,B]
=x-d+a.
\]
For $\tau=[A,C,D]$,
\[
\partial_2\tau
=[C,D]-[A,D]+[A,C].
\]
Here
\[
[C,D]=-b,
\qquad
[A,D]=-x
\]
by the chosen orientation of $b$ and <1>2, so
\[
\partial_2\tau=-b+x+d=x+d-b.
\]
:::

<1>8. The homomorphism $\partial_2:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z^4$ is injective.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
m(a+x-d)+n(x+d-b)=0.
\]
The coefficient of $a$ is $m$, hence $m=0$.
The coefficient of $b$ is $-n$, hence $n=0$.
Therefore the kernel is zero.
:::

<1>9. We have
\[
H_2(Y)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H_i(Y)=0\quad(i\ge3).
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>8,
\[
H_2(Y)=\ker\partial_2=0.
\]
There are no simplices of dimension at least $3$, so all higher simplicial chain groups and homology groups vanish.
:::

<1>10. We have
\[
H_1(Y)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\partial_1=0$,
\[
H_1(Y)
\cong
\mathbb Z\langle a,b,x,d\rangle
\big/
\mathbb Z\langle a+x-d,\ x+d-b\rangle.
\]
The first relation gives
\[
d=a+x,
\]
and the second then gives
\[
b=x+d=a+2x.
\]
Hence every class is generated by the classes of $a$ and $x$.

To see that there is no relation between these two generators, define
\[
\psi:\mathbb Z^4\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2
\]
on the edge basis by
\[
\psi(a)=(1,0),
\quad
\psi(x)=(0,1),
\quad
\psi(d)=(1,1),
\quad
\psi(b)=(1,2).
\]
Then
\[
\psi(a+x-d)=0,
\qquad
\psi(x+d-b)=0,
\]
so $\psi$ descends to a homomorphism
\[
\overline\psi:H_1(Y)\to\mathbb Z^2
\]
sending the classes of $a,x$ to the standard basis.
The map
\[
\mathbb Z^2\to H_1(Y),
\qquad
(m,n)\mapsto m[a]+n[x]
\]
is therefore both surjective and inverse to $\overline\psi$.
Thus $H_1(Y)\cong\mathbb Z^2$.
:::

<1>11. We have
\[
H_0(Y)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
The $\Delta$-complex has one vertex and is nonempty, hence connected.
Equivalently, from <1>7,
\[
H_0(Y)=C_0(Y)/\operatorname{im}\partial_1
\cong
\mathbb Z/0
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>12. Therefore the simplicial homology groups are
\[
\boxed{
H_i(Y)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z, & i=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2, & i=1,\\
0, & i\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>9--<1>11. This proves part (b).
:::
