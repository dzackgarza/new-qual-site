::: {.problem}
A space $X$ is locally connected if for each $x\in X$ and each open set $U$ containing $x$, there is a connected open set $V$ in $X$ satisfying $x\in V\subseteq U$.
Let $X$ and $Y$ be locally connected spaces.
Determine whether or not the product space $X\times Y$ must also be locally connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
Yes: $X\times Y$ must be locally connected.

<1>1. If two connected subsets $C,D$ of a space have nonempty intersection, then $C\cup D$ is connected.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $C\cup D$ had a separation
\[
C\cup D=U\cup V
\]
into disjoint nonempty relatively open sets.
Connectedness of $C$ forces $C$ to lie entirely in one of $U,V$, and connectedness of $D$ forces the same for $D$.
Because $C\cap D\ne\varnothing$, the two sets cannot lie in different members of the separation.
Thus both lie in the same one, making the other empty, a contradiction.
:::

<1>2. The product of two nonempty connected spaces is connected.
::: {.proof}
Let $C,D$ be nonempty and connected, and fix
\[
c_0\in C,
\qquad
d_0\in D.
\]
The subset
\[
C\times\{d_0\}
\]
is homeomorphic to $C$, hence connected.
For each $c\in C$, the subset
\[
\{c\}\times D
\]
is homeomorphic to $D$, hence connected, and it meets $C\times\{d_0\}$ at $(c,d_0)$.
Therefore <1>1 implies that
\[
E_c=(C\times\{d_0\})\cup(\{c\}\times D)
\]
is connected.

Every $E_c$ contains the common connected subset $C\times\{d_0\}$.
If their union were separated, each connected $E_c$ would have to lie in one member of the separation, and the common subset would force every $E_c$ into the same member.
Hence the union is connected.
But
\[
\bigcup_{c\in C}E_c=C\times D.
\]
Thus $C\times D$ is connected.
:::

<1>3. Let $(x_0,y_0)\in X\times Y$ and let $W$ be an open neighborhood of $(x_0,y_0)$.
There are open sets $U\subseteq X$ and $V\subseteq Y$ such that
\[
(x_0,y_0)\in U\times V\subseteq W.
\]
::: {.proof}
The products of open sets form a basis for the product topology.
Since $W$ is open and contains $(x_0,y_0)$, it contains a basic product neighborhood of that point.
:::

<1>4. There are connected open sets $U_0\subseteq X$ and $V_0\subseteq Y$ such that
\[
x_0\in U_0\subseteq U,
\qquad
y_0\in V_0\subseteq V.
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply local connectedness of $X$ to the open neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$, and local connectedness of $Y$ to the open neighborhood $V$ of $y_0$.
:::

<1>5. The set $U_0\times V_0$ is a connected open neighborhood of $(x_0,y_0)$ contained in $W$.
::: {.proof}
It is open because $U_0$ and $V_0$ are open, and it contains $(x_0,y_0)$ by <1>4. The same step and <1>3 give
\[
U_0\times V_0\subseteq U\times V\subseteq W.
\]
Both $U_0$ and $V_0$ are nonempty and connected, so <1>2 shows that their product is connected.
:::

<1>6. Therefore $X\times Y$ is locally connected.
::: {.proof}
The point $(x_0,y_0)$ and its open neighborhood $W$ in <1>3 were arbitrary.
By <1>5, every such neighborhood contains a connected open neighborhood of the point.
This is exactly the definition of local connectedness.
:::
