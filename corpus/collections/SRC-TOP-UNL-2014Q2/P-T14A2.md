::: {.problem}
(a) Let $q:A\to B$ be continuous and $i:I\to I$ be the identity function.
Define the function $q\times i:A\times I\to B\times I$ by $(q\times i)(a,t):=(q(a),i(t))$.
Show that $q\times i$ is continuous.
You may assume the fact that if $q:A\to B$ is a quotient map, then so is $q\times i$.

(b) Let $X$ be a path-connected Hausdorff space.
The cone $CX$ of $X$ is the quotient of the space $X\times I$ by the smallest equivalence relation such that $(p,0)\sim(q,0)$ for all $p,q\in X$.
Prove that there is a deformation retraction from $CX$ to the singleton subspace $\{[(p,0)]\}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map
\[
q\times i:A\times I\longrightarrow B\times I,
\qquad
(a,t)\longmapsto(q(a),t),
\]
is continuous.

::: pf-proof

The sets $U\times V$, with $U\subseteq B$ open and $V\subseteq I$ open, form a basis for the product topology on $B\times I$.
For such a basic open set,
\[
(q\times i)^{-1}(U\times V)
=
q^{-1}(U)\times V.
\]
Since $q$ is continuous, $q^{-1}(U)$ is open in $A$, so the right-hand side is open in $A\times I$.
Thus the inverse image of every basis element is open, hence $q\times i$ is continuous.
This proves part (a).

Let
\[
Q:X\times I\longrightarrow CX
\]
be the quotient map, and denote the cone vertex by
\[
v=Q(x,0),
\]
which is independent of $x\in X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Define
\[
F:(X\times I)\times I\longrightarrow CX,
\qquad
F((x,t),s)=Q(x,(1-s)t).
\]
Then $F$ is continuous.

::: pf-proof

The map
\[
\Phi:(X\times I)\times I\longrightarrow X\times I,
\qquad
\Phi((x,t),s)=(x,(1-s)t)
\]
is continuous: its first coordinate is the projection to $X$, and its second coordinate is the product of the continuous real-valued functions $(t,s)\mapsto t$ and $(t,s)\mapsto1-s$.
Since $Q$ is continuous by definition of the quotient topology,
\[
F=Q\circ\Phi
\]
is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The map $F$ is constant on each fiber of
\[
Q\times i:(X\times I)\times I\longrightarrow CX\times I.
\]

::: pf-proof

Suppose
\[
(Q(x,t),s)=(Q(y,u),r).
\]
Then $s=r$ and $Q(x,t)=Q(y,u)$.
By the defining equivalence relation of the cone, either
\[
(x,t)=(y,u)
\]
or
\[
t=u=0.
\]
In the first case the two values of $F$ are visibly equal.
In the second case,
\[
F((x,0),s)=Q(x,0)=v=Q(y,0)=F((y,0),s).
\]
Thus $F$ is constant on every fiber.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

There is a unique continuous map
\[
H:CX\times I\longrightarrow CX
\]
satisfying
\[
H(Q(x,t),s)=Q(x,(1-s)t).
\]

::: pf-proof

The map $Q$ is a quotient map.
By the fact permitted in the problem, the product
\[
Q\times i:(X\times I)\times I\to CX\times I
\]
is also a quotient map.
By steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}, the continuous map $F$ is constant on its fibers.
Therefore the universal property of quotient maps gives a unique map $H$ with
\[
F=H\circ(Q\times i),
\]
and that induced map $H$ is continuous.
The displayed formula is exactly this factorization.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The homotopy $H$ satisfies
\[
H(z,0)=z,
\qquad
H(z,1)=v
\]
for every $z\in CX$.

::: pf-proof

Write $z=Q(x,t)$.
Using step [](#s4){.pf-ref},
\[
H(z,0)=Q(x,(1-0)t)=Q(x,t)=z,
\]
and
\[
H(z,1)=Q(x,(1-1)t)=Q(x,0)=v.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The vertex $v$ is fixed throughout the homotopy:
\[
H(v,s)=v
\qquad(0\le s\le1).
\]

::: pf-proof

For any $x\in X$,
\[
v=Q(x,0),
\]
so step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives
\[
H(v,s)=Q(x,(1-s)0)=Q(x,0)=v.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $H$ is a strong deformation retraction of $CX$ onto the singleton $\{v\}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, $H(-,0)$ is the identity on $CX$ and $H(-,1)$ has image in $\{v\}$ and restricts to the identity there.
By step [](#s6){.pf-ref}, every point of the retracting subspace $\{v\}$ is fixed at every intermediate time.
These are exactly the defining conditions for a strong deformation retraction.
This proves part (b).

:::
