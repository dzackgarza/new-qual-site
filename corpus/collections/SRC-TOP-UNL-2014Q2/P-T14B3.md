::: {.problem}
Use covering space theory to prove that if $H$ is a finite index subgroup of a finitely presented group, then $H$ is finitely presented.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
d=[G:H]<\infty,
\]
and choose a finite presentation
\[
G\cong\langle g_1,\ldots,g_n\mid r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle.
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

There is a connected finite $2$-dimensional CW complex $X$ with basepoint $x_0$ such that
\[
\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong G.
\]

::: pf-proof

Form the presentation complex: take one $0$-cell, attach $n$ oriented $1$-cells corresponding to the generators, and attach $m$ $2$-cells along loops spelling the relators.
The Seifert--van Kampen theorem gives the stated fundamental group.
There are finitely many cells because the presentation is finite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The subgroup $H\le\pi_1(X,x_0)$ corresponds to a connected $d$-sheeted covering
\[
p:(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\longrightarrow(X,x_0)
\]
with
\[
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)=H.
\]

::: pf-proof

The connected CW complex $X$ is locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected, so the classification theorem for connected based covering spaces applies.
It associates to $H$ a connected based cover whose induced subgroup is $H$.

For a connected covering, the number of sheets equals the index of its associated subgroup.
Thus the fiber over $x_0$ has
\[
d=[G:H]
\]
points, and the cover has $d$ sheets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The CW structure of $X$ lifts to a finite CW structure on $\widetilde X$ with exactly $d$ lifts of every cell of $X$.

::: pf-proof

For an open cell $e^k\subseteq X$, the restriction
\[
p:p^{-1}(e^k)\longrightarrow e^k
\]
is a $d$-sheeted covering.
Since the open cell $e^k$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^k$ and hence simply connected, this restricted covering is trivial: its inverse image is the disjoint union of $d$ components, each mapped homeomorphically onto $e^k$.

Equivalently, a characteristic map
\[
\chi:D^k\to X
\]
for the cell has one lift for each choice of a point over an interior basepoint, and the lifted boundary maps attach these $d$ disks to the lifted lower skeleton.
Doing this for every cell gives a CW structure upstairs in which each base cell has exactly $d$ lifts.

The base $X$ has finitely many cells by step [](#s1){.pf-ref} and $d<\infty$, so $\widetilde X$ has finitely many cells.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The fundamental group of a connected finite CW complex is finitely presented.

::: pf-proof

Let $Y$ be a connected finite CW complex.
Choose a maximal tree $T$ in its finite connected $1$-skeleton.
After collapsing $T$, the remaining finitely many $1$-cells form a wedge of finitely many circles and hence give finitely many generators.

Each of the finitely many $2$-cells has an attaching loop in the $1$-skeleton; after the tree collapse this loop gives one word in those generators.
The Seifert--van Kampen theorem says that attaching the $2$-cells imposes the normal closures of these finitely many words.
Attaching cells of dimension at least $3$ does not change the fundamental group, because their attaching spheres $S^{k-1}$ are simply connected for $k\ge3$.
Thus $\pi_1(Y)$ has finitely many generators and finitely many relators.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The subgroup $H$ is finitely presented.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $\widetilde X$ is a connected finite CW complex, so step [](#s4){.pf-ref} implies that
\[
\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)
\]
is finitely presented.
For a covering map, $p_*$ is injective, and by step [](#s2){.pf-ref} its image is $H$.
Hence
\[
H\cong\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0),
\]
so $H$ is finitely presented.

:::
