::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a path-connected Hausdorff space.
The suspension $SX$ is the quotient of the space $X\times I$ by the smallest equivalence relation such that $(p,0)\sim(q,0)$ and $(p,1)\sim(q,1)$ for all $p,q\in X$.
Prove that the homology groups satisfy $$H_i(SX)=H_{i-1}(X)$$ for all $i>1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
q:X\times I\longrightarrow SX
\]
be the quotient map, and write
\[
S=q(X\times\{0\}),
\qquad
N=q(X\times\{1\})
\]
for the two suspension vertices.
Define
\[
U=q\bigl(X\times[0,2/3)\bigr),
\qquad
V=q\bigl(X\times(1/3,1]\bigr).
\]

<1>1. The subsets $U$ and $V$ are open in $SX$ and satisfy
\[
SX=U\cup V.
\]
::: {.proof}
The only nontrivial equivalence classes for $q$ are $X\times\{0\}$ and $X\times\{1\}$.
Hence both
\[
X\times[0,2/3)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
X\times(1/3,1]
\]
are saturated for the quotient relation.
Therefore
\[
q^{-1}(U)=X\times[0,2/3),
\qquad
q^{-1}(V)=X\times(1/3,1].
\]
The intervals $[0,2/3)$ and $(1/3,1]$ are open in the subspace topology of $I=[0,1]$, so these inverse images are open in $X\times I$.
By the definition of the quotient topology, $U$ and $V$ are open.

Finally,
\[
[0,2/3)\cup(1/3,1]=I,
\]
so their images cover $SX$.
:::

<1>2. The open set $U$ is contractible.
::: {.proof}
Define on the preimage of $U$ the homotopy
\[
F_U:\bigl(X\times[0,2/3)\bigr)\times I\longrightarrow U,
\qquad
F_U((x,t),s)=q(x,(1-s)t).
\]
It is continuous because
\[
((x,t),s)\longmapsto(x,(1-s)t)
\]
is continuous and its image remains in $X\times[0,2/3)$.

The restricted map
\[
q_U:X\times[0,2/3)\longrightarrow U
\]
is a quotient map: the domain is a saturated open subset of $X\times I$, and restricting a quotient map to a saturated open subset again gives a quotient map.
By the quotient-product fact from part A2 of this same exam, $q_U\times\operatorname{id}_I$ is also a quotient map.

If two points have the same image under $q_U\times\operatorname{id}_I$, then their $s$-coordinates agree and either their $(x,t)$ coordinates agree or both have $t=0$.
In the latter case $F_U$ sends both points to the suspension vertex $S$.
Thus $F_U$ is constant on the fibers of $q_U\times\operatorname{id}_I$ and descends to a continuous homotopy
\[
H_U:U\times I\longrightarrow U,
\qquad
H_U(q(x,t),s)=q(x,(1-s)t).
\]
At $s=0$ this is the identity; at $s=1$ it is the constant map to $S$; and $S$ is fixed throughout.
Hence $U$ is contractible.
:::

<1>3. The open set $V$ is contractible.
::: {.proof}
Use the analogous homotopy toward the upper suspension vertex:
\[
H_V(q(x,t),s)
=
q\bigl(x,(1-s)t+s\bigr).
\]
For $t>1/3$ and $0\le s\le1$,
\[
(1-s)t+s=t+s(1-t)\ge t>1/3,
\]
so the homotopy remains in $V$.
As in <1>2, it descends continuously from
\[
((x,t),s)\longmapsto q\bigl(x,(1-s)t+s\bigr)
\]
through the quotient map on the saturated open subset $X\times(1/3,1]$.
At $s=0$ it is the identity, at $s=1$ it is constant at $N$, and it fixes $N$ throughout.
Thus $V$ is contractible.
:::

<1>4. The overlap $U\cap V$ is homeomorphic to
\[
X\times(1/3,2/3).
\]
::: {.proof}
Since no point with height strictly between $0$ and $1$ is identified with any other point under the suspension relation,
\[
q^{-1}(U\cap V)=X\times(1/3,2/3),
\]
and the restriction
\[
q_0:X\times(1/3,2/3)\longrightarrow U\cap V
\]
is bijective.

The domain is a saturated open subset of $X\times I$, so $q_0$ is a quotient map by the same restriction argument used in <1>2. A bijective quotient map is a homeomorphism: for any subset $W$ of its target,
\[
W\text{ is open}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q_0^{-1}(W)\text{ is open},
\]
which is precisely continuity of the inverse.
Hence $q_0$ is a homeomorphism.
:::

<1>5. The overlap $U\cap V$ deformation retracts onto a copy of $X$ and therefore
\[
H_k(U\cap V)\cong H_k(X)
\]
for every $k\ge0$.
::: {.proof}
Under the homeomorphism of <1>4, define
\[
R_s(x,t)=\bigl(x,(1-s)t+s/2\bigr).
\]
Because $(1/3,2/3)$ is convex and contains $1/2$, this remains in
\[
X\times(1/3,2/3)
\]
for every $s$.
It is a deformation retraction onto
\[
X\times\{1/2\},
\]
which is homeomorphic to $X$.
Homotopy invariance of singular homology gives the stated isomorphisms.
:::

<1>6. For every $i>1$, the Mayer--Vietoris connecting homomorphism gives an isomorphism
\[
H_i(SX)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }H_{i-1}(U\cap V).
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply the Mayer--Vietoris long exact sequence to the open cover
\[
SX=U\cup V
\]
from <1>1. The relevant part is
\[
H_i(U)\oplus H_i(V)
\longrightarrow
H_i(SX)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_{i-1}(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
H_{i-1}(U)\oplus H_{i-1}(V).
\]
By <1>2--<1>3, $U$ and $V$ are contractible, so
\[
H_j(U)=H_j(V)=0
\qquad(j>0).
\]
If $i>1$, then both $i$ and $i-1$ are positive.
Thus the two outer groups in the displayed exact segment vanish, and exactness forces $\delta$ to be both injective and surjective.
Hence it is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>7. For every $i>1$,
\[
\boxed{H_i(SX)\cong H_{i-1}(X).}
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>6,
\[
H_i(SX)\cong H_{i-1}(U\cap V).
\]
By <1>5,
\[
H_{i-1}(U\cap V)\cong H_{i-1}(X).
\]
Composing the two isomorphisms proves the required formula.
:::
:::
