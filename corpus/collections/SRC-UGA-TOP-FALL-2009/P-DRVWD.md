::: problem
Denote the points of $S^1 \times I$ by $(z, t)$ where $z$ is a unit complex number and $0 \leq t \leq 1$.
Let $X$ denote the quotient of $S^1 \times I$ given by identifying $(z, 1)$ and $(z^2 , 0)$ for all $z \in S^1$.

Give a cell structure, with attaching maps, for $X$, and use it to compute $\pi_1 (X, \ast)$ and $H_* (X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $X$ is the mapping torus of the map $f : S^1 \to S^1$, $f(z) = z^2$.
::: {.proof}
$X = (S^1 \times I)/((z,1) \sim (z^2,0))$.
:::

<1>2. Give $S^1$ a cell structure with one $0$-cell $e^0$ and one $1$-cell $e^1$.
::: {.proof}
standard CW structure on the circle.
:::

<1>3. $X$ has one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $a$ and $t$, and one $2$-cell.
::: {.proof}
the $0$-cell is $e^0 \times \{0\}$ (with $e^0 \times \{1\}$ identified to it since $1^2 = 1$); the $1$-cells are $a = e^1 \times \{0\}$ and $t = e^0 \times I$; the $2$-cell is $e^1 \times I$.
:::

<1>4. $\partial t = 0$.
::: {.proof}
$\partial(e^0 \times I) = e^0 \times \{1\} - e^0 \times \{0\} = 0$ after the identification.
:::

<1>5. The $2$-cell is attached by the word $a \cdot t \cdot a^{-2} \cdot t^{-1}$.
::: {.proof}
$\partial(e^1 \times I) = e^1 \times \{1\} - e^1 \times \{0\}$; the top edge $e^1 \times \{1\}$ is glued to $f_*(e^1) = 2a$ (degree $2$), the bottom edge $e^1 \times \{0\}$ to $a$, and the two vertical edges $\partial e^1 \times I$ contribute $t$ and $t^{-1}$ (the two endpoints of $e^1$ are the single $0$-cell, traversed in opposite directions).
:::

<1>6. $\pi_1(X) = \langle a, t \mid t a t^{-1} = a^2 \rangle$, the Baumslag–Solitar group $BS(1,2)$.
::: {.proof}
the $1$-skeleton is $S^1 \vee S^1$ with fundamental group $\langle a, t \rangle$, and the $2$-cell imposes the relation $a t a^{-2} t^{-1} = 1$, i.e. $t a t^{-1} = a^2$.
:::

<1>7. The cellular chain complex is $0 \to \ZZ \xrightarrow{d_2} \ZZ^2 \xrightarrow{d_1} \ZZ \to 0$.
::: {.proof}
one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells, one $2$-cell.
:::

<1>8. $d_1 = 0$ and $d_2 = (1, 0)$ (sends the generator to $a$).
::: {.proof}
$d_1 = 0$ (single $0$-cell); $d_2$ is the boundary of the $2$-cell, which by <1>5 is $a + t - 2a - t = -a$, i.e. $d_2 = (-1, 0)$ up to sign, so $\operatorname{im} d_2 = \langle a \rangle$.
:::

<1>9. Hence $H_0(X) = \ZZ$, $H_1(X) = \ZZ^2/\langle a \rangle = \ZZ$ (generated by $t$), and $H_2(X) = \ker d_2 = 0$.
::: {.proof}
<1>7 and <1>8; $d_2$ is injective so $H_2 = 0$, and $H_1 = \ker d_1 / \operatorname{im} d_2 = \ZZ^2 / \langle a \rangle \cong \ZZ$.
:::

<1>10. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>6 and <1>9.
:::
