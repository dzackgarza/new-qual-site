::: problem
Denote the points of $S^1 \times I$ by $(z, t)$ where $z$ is a unit complex number and $0 \leq t \leq 1$.
Let $X$ denote the quotient of $S^1 \times I$ given by identifying $(z, 1)$ and $(z^2 , 0)$ for all $z \in S^1$.

Give a cell structure, with attaching maps, for $X$, and use it to compute $\pi_1 (X, \ast)$ and $H_* (X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $X$ is the mapping torus of the map $f : S^1 \to S^1$, $f(z) = z^2$.
::: {.proof}
$X = (S^1 \times I)/((z,1) \sim (z^2,0))$.
:::

<1>2. Give $S^1$ a cell structure with one $0$-cell $e^0$ and one $1$-cell $e^1$.
::: {.proof}
standard CW structure on the circle.
:::

<1>3. $X$ has one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $a$ and $t$, and one $2$-cell.
::: {.proof}
the $0$-cell is $e^0 \times \{0\}$ (with $e^0 \times \{1\}$ identified to it since $1^2 = 1$); the $1$-cells are $a = e^1 \times \{0\}$ and $t = e^0 \times I$; the $2$-cell is $e^1 \times I$.
:::

<1>4. $\partial t = 0$.
::: {.proof}
$\partial(e^0 \times I) = e^0 \times \{1\} - e^0 \times \{0\} = 0$ after the identification.
:::

<1>5. The $2$-cell is attached by the word $a \cdot t \cdot a^{-2} \cdot t^{-1}$.
::: {.proof}
Traverse the boundary of the square $e^1\times I$ along the bottom edge from left to right, then the right edge upward, then the top edge from right to left, and finally the left edge downward.
The bottom edge contributes $a$ and the right edge contributes $t$.
The top edge is traversed in the reverse direction and is identified by the degree-$2$ map $f$, so it contributes $a^{-2}$.
The left edge contributes $t^{-1}$.
Thus the attaching word is
\[
a t a^{-2}t^{-1}.
\]
:::

<1>6. One has
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\left\langle a,t\mathrel{\Big|}t^{-1}at=a^2\right\rangle,
\]
which is isomorphic to the Baumslag--Solitar group $BS(1,2)$.
::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton is $S^1\vee S^1$ with fundamental group freely generated by $a$ and $t$.
By <1>5, the $2$-cell imposes
\[
a t a^{-2}t^{-1}=1.
\]
Multiplying on the left by $a^{-1}$ and on the right by $t$ gives
\[
t a^{-2}=a^{-1}t,
\]
and taking inverses gives
\[
a^2t^{-1}=t^{-1}a.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
t^{-1}at=a^2.
\]
If $s=t^{-1}$, this becomes $s a s^{-1}=a^2$, the standard $BS(1,2)$ presentation.
:::

<1>7. The cellular chain complex is $0 \to \ZZ \xrightarrow{d_2} \ZZ^2 \xrightarrow{d_1} \ZZ \to 0$.
::: {.proof}
one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells, one $2$-cell.
:::

<1>8. One has $d_1=0$, and after choosing the orientation of the $2$-cell one may take
\[
d_2=(1,0):\ZZ\longrightarrow\ZZ^2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because there is a single $0$-cell, $d_1=0$.
The cellular boundary of the $2$-cell is obtained by abelianizing its attaching word from <1>5:
\[
a+t-2a-t=-a.
\]
Thus with the boundary orientation used in <1>5 the matrix of $d_2$ is $(-1,0)$.
Reversing the orientation of the $2$-cell changes this to $(1,0)$, so in either convention
\[
\operatorname{im}d_2=\langle a\rangle.
\]
:::

<1>9. Hence
\[
H_k(X;\ZZ)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
The generator of $H_1(X;\ZZ)$ is represented by $t$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>7 and <1>8, $d_2$ is injective, so $H_2(X;\ZZ)=0$, while
\[
H_1(X;\ZZ)
=\ker d_1/\operatorname{im}d_2
=\ZZ^2/\langle a\rangle
\cong\ZZ,
\]
generated by the class of $t$.
Since the CW structure is connected, $H_0(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ$.
There are no cells in dimensions greater than $2$, so the cellular chain groups and hence the homology groups vanish in all degrees $k>2$.
:::

<1>10. Therefore the requested invariants are
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X)\cong\left\langle a,t\mathrel{\Big|}t^{-1}at=a^2\right\rangle}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X;\ZZ)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
<1>6 and <1>9.
:::
