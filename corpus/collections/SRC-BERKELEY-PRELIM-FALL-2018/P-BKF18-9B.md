::: {.problem}
Find the smallest $n$ for which the symmetric group $S_n$ contains a cyclic subgroup of order $111$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let the partition $n = n _ { 1 } + n _ { 2 } + \ldots + n _ { k }$ represent the cycle structure of an element $g \in S _ { n }$ , i.e. g is a products of commuting cycles of the lengths $n _ { 1 } \le n _ { 2 } \le \dots \le n _ { k }$ . The order of the cyclic subgroup generated by $g$ is obviously equal to the least common multiple of $n _ { 1 } , . . . , n _ { k }$ . We want this least common multiple to be $1 1 1 = 3 \cdot 3 7$ One of the possibilities is $( n _ { 1 } , n _ { 2 } , . . . , n _ { k } ) = ( 3 , 3 7 )$ in which case $n = 3 + 3 7 = 4 0$ . We claim that this value of n is the minimal possible.
Indeed, if 111 is the least common multiple of $n _ { 1 } , . . . , n _ { k }$ then each of the prime factors 3, 37 divides at least one of the numbers $n _ { i }$ and moreover, the sum of such factors dividing $n _ { i }$ does not exceed their product and thus does not exceed $n _ { i }$ . This implies $n = n _ { 1 } + . . . + n _ { k } \geq 3 + 3 7 = 4 0$ .
:::
