::: {.problem}
Let $G \subseteq \operatorname{GL}_3(\CC)$ be the subgroup generated by the matrices
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
The group $G$ acts on the polynomial ring $\CC[x_1, x_2, x_3]$ by linear substitutions.
Find an explicit generating set of the invariant ring $\CC[x_1, x_2, x_3]^G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
s=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
t=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $s$ swaps $x_1,x_2$, while $t$ fixes $x_1,x_2$ and sends $x_3\mapsto-x_3$. The two matrices commute, so $G\cong C_2\times C_2$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #t-invariant-subring}
The $t$-invariant subring is
$$
\CC[x_1,x_2,x_3]^{\langle t\rangle}
=\CC[x_1,x_2,x_3^2].
$$

::: pf-proof
Write a polynomial uniquely as
$$
f=\sum_{k\ge0} f_k(x_1,x_2)x_3^k.
$$
The condition $t\cdot f=f$ is
$$
\sum_k f_k(x_1,x_2)(-x_3)^k
=
\sum_k f_k(x_1,x_2)x_3^k.
$$
Hence $f_k=0$ for every odd $k$. Therefore exactly the even powers of $x_3$ occur, so the invariant ring is $\CC[x_1,x_2,x_3^2]$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s-invariant-of-t-invariant}
Taking $s$-invariants next gives
$$
\CC[x_1,x_2,x_3^2]^{\langle s\rangle}
=
\CC[x_1+x_2,x_1x_2,x_3^2].
$$

::: pf-proof
The element $x_3^2$ is fixed by $s$, and $s$ acts on $\CC[x_1,x_2]$ by interchanging the two variables. By the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials in two variables,
$$
\CC[x_1,x_2]^{S_2}
=\CC[x_1+x_2,x_1x_2].
$$
Since $x_3^2$ is an independent fixed variable, adjoining it gives the displayed invariant ring.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\CC[x_1,x_2,x_3]^G
=
\CC[x_1+x_2,\ x_1x_2,\ x_3^2]
}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Because $s$ and $t$ commute and generate $G$, a polynomial is $G$-invariant exactly when it is invariant under both generators. Apply step [](#t-invariant-subring){.pf-ref} and then step [](#s-invariant-of-t-invariant){.pf-ref}. The three displayed generators are themselves fixed by both $s$ and $t$, so the containment in the reverse direction is immediate.
:::
