::: {.problem}
• Prove that the exterior derivative is not dependent on the choice of charts by proving the following coordinate-free formula.
Let M be a smooth manifold.
Let $\omega \in \Omega ^ { p } ( M )$ , and let $X ^ { 0 } , \ldots , X ^ { p }$ be vector fields on M. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
(d\omega)(X^0,\dots,X^p)={}&\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^i X^i\,\omega(X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^i},\dots,X^p)\\
&+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\omega([X^i,X^j],X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^i},\dots,\widehat{X^j},\dots,X^p)
\end{aligned}
$$

where summing over something with a hat means skipping it.
(To clarify: $\omega(X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^i},\dots,X^p)$ is a function from $M\to\RR$ — why?)

Prove this formula.
(Hint: you need to compare this formula to the one in coordinates.
So you’d really like to be able to replace the $X^i$ with constant vector fields (defined only near m) like $\partial / \partial x _ { i }$ . Start by showing that if $X ^ { 0 } ( m ) = Y ^ { 0 } ( m )$ , then $(d\omega)(Y^0,X^1,\ldots,X^p)=(d\omega)(X^0,\ldots,X^p)$.)

• Your classmate Adam is confused by the previous problem: “why are we thinking about vector fields in the first place?
The hint tells us that we only need to think about the value of $X ^ { 0 }$ at m. So we may as well just define $( d \omega )$ on a $(p+1)$-tuple of vectors.
Our professor gives us obsfuscated exercises!”
Explain why Adam is wrong, at least about the mathematics.
:::

::: {.solution}

For vector fields $X^0,\dots,X^p$, define
$$
\begin{aligned}
D\omega(X^0,\dots,X^p)
={}&
\sum_{i=0}^{p}
(-1)^i
X^i
\left[
\omega(X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^i},\dots,X^p)
\right]\\
&+
\sum_{0\le i<j\le p}
(-1)^{i+j}
\omega(
[X^i,X^j],
X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^i},\dots,\widehat{X^j},\dots,X^p
).
\end{aligned}
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For smooth vector fields
$$
Y^1,\dots,Y^p,
$$
the expression
$$
m
\longmapsto
\omega_m(Y^1_m,\dots,Y^p_m)
$$
is a smooth real-valued function on $M$.


::: pf-proof

In local coordinates, write
$$
\omega
=
\sum_I f_I\,dx_I
$$
with smooth coefficient functions $f_I$, and write each vector field as
$$
Y^r
=
\sum_a Y^r_a
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_a}
$$
with smooth coefficient functions $Y^r_a$. Evaluating $\omega$ on the
vector fields gives a finite sum of products of the smooth functions $f_I$
and $Y^r_a$. Hence the result is smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The value
$$
D\omega(X^0,\dots,X^p)(m)
$$
depends $C^\infty$-linearly on the first vector-field slot at $m$:
for every smooth function $f$,
$$
\boxed{
D\omega(fX^0,X^1,\dots,X^p)(m)
=
f(m)
D\omega(X^0,\dots,X^p)(m)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand the defining formula with $fX^0$ in the first slot.

The term with $i=0$ in the first sum becomes
$$
fX^0
\left[
\omega(X^1,\dots,X^p)
\right].
$$
For $i=j\ge1$, the corresponding term in the first sum is
$$
(-1)^j
X^j
\left[
f\,
\omega(
X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^j},\dots,X^p
)
\right].
$$
By the product rule, this equals
$$
\begin{aligned}
(-1)^j X^j(f)\,
\omega(
X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^j},\dots,X^p
)
\\
+
f(-1)^j
X^j
\left[
\omega(
X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^j},\dots,X^p
)
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Now consider the bracket term for the pair $(0,j)$. The Leibniz rule for the
Lie bracket gives
$$
[fX^0,X^j]
=
f[X^0,X^j]
-
X^j(f)X^0.
$$
Hence that bracket term contains
$$
-(-1)^j X^j(f)\,
\omega(
X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^j},\dots,X^p
),
$$
which cancels exactly with the derivative-of-$f$ term above.

Every remaining term contains $f$ only as an overall multiplicative factor.
Thus the whole expression equals
$$
f\,D\omega(X^0,\dots,X^p).
$$
Evaluating at $m$ gives the claimed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The same $C^\infty$-linearity holds in every vector-field slot.


::: pf-proof

The calculation in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} uses only the product rule
$$
X(fa)=X(f)a+fX(a)
$$
and the bracket identities
$$
[fX,Y]
=
f[X,Y]-Y(f)X,
\qquad
[X,fY]
=
f[X,Y]+X(f)Y.
$$
Replacing any slot $X^r$ by $fX^r$ produces exactly one derivative-of-$f$
term from the first sum for each other slot, and the corresponding bracket
term cancels it with the opposite sign. All other terms acquire the common
factor $f$. Thus $D\omega$ is $C^\infty$-linear in each slot.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Consequently,
$$
D\omega(X^0,\dots,X^p)(m)
$$
depends only on the tangent vectors
$$
X^0(m),\dots,X^p(m).
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
X^0(m)=Y^0(m).
$$
Then
$$
Z=X^0-Y^0
$$
vanishes at $m$. In local coordinates near $m$,
$$
Z
=
\sum_a f_a
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_a}
$$
with
$$
f_a(m)=0.
$$
By linearity and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
D\omega(
Z,X^1,\dots,X^p
)(m)
=
\sum_a
f_a(m)
D\omega\left(
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_a},
X^1,\dots,X^p
\right)(m)
=0.
$$
Thus replacing $X^0$ by $Y^0$ does not change the value. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
the same conclusion in every slot.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Fix $m\in M$ and choose local coordinates
$$
x_1,\dots,x_n
$$
near $m$. To prove
$$
D\omega_m=(d\omega)_m,
$$
it suffices to evaluate both sides on tuples of coordinate vectors
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_0}},
\dots,
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_p}}
\evalfrom_m.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, both sides depend only on the tangent vectors at $m$, not on
their chosen local extensions. The coordinate vectors form a basis of
$T_mM$. Since step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} makes $D\omega_m$ multilinear in the tangent-vector
arguments, and $(d\omega)_m$ is multilinear by definition of a differential
form, equality on all ordered basis tuples implies equality on all tuples.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For coordinate vector fields,
$$
\left[
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_a},
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_b}
\right]
=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
D\omega
\left(
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_0}},
\dots,
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_p}}
\right)
=
\sum_{r=0}^{p}
(-1)^r
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_r}}
\left[
\omega
\left(
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_0}},
\dots,
\widehat{
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_r}}
},
\dots,
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_p}}
\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$


::: pf-proof

Coordinate vector fields commute, so every term in the bracket sum vanishes.
The displayed formula is what remains from the first sum.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The coordinate definition
$$
\omega
=
\sum_I f_I\,dx_I
$$
gives
$$
d\omega
=
\sum_I df_I\wedge dx_I,
$$
and evaluating this expression on the same coordinate-vector tuple yields
exactly the right-hand side of step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

For a $1$-form $\alpha$ and a $p$-form $\beta$,
$$
(\alpha\wedge\beta)(V_0,\dots,V_p)
=
\sum_{r=0}^{p}
(-1)^r
\alpha(V_r)
\beta(V_0,\dots,\widehat{V_r},\dots,V_p).
$$
Apply this with
$$
\alpha=df_I,
\qquad
\beta=dx_I,
\qquad
V_r=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_r}}.
$$
Since
$$
df_I(V_r)
=
\frac{\partial f_I}{\partial x_{i_r}},
$$
summing over $I$ gives
$$
\sum_{r=0}^{p}
(-1)^r
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_r}}
\left[
\omega(
V_0,\dots,\widehat{V_r},\dots,V_p
)
\right],
$$
which is the expression in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence the coordinate-free identity is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
(d\omega)(X^0,\dots,X^p)
={}&
\sum_{i=0}^{p}
(-1)^i
X^i
\left[
\omega(X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^i},\dots,X^p)
\right]\\
&+
\sum_{i<j}
(-1)^{i+j}
\omega(
[X^i,X^j],
X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^i},\dots,\widehat{X^j},\dots,X^p
).
\end{aligned}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} show equality at every point on a basis of tangent
vectors. Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that the displayed right-hand side is tensorial, so
the equality holds for arbitrary vector fields.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The formula is independent of the choice of coordinates.


::: pf-proof

The right-hand side in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is expressed entirely in terms of the
intrinsic operations
$$
\omega,\qquad
X(f),\qquad
[X,Y],
$$
and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves that it agrees with the coordinate definition of
$d\omega$. Thus different coordinate charts give the same differential form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Adam's proposed replacement of vector fields by tangent vectors at a
single point does not make the formula meaningful.


::: pf-proof

The final value
$$
(d\omega)_m(v_0,\dots,v_p)
$$
does depend only on the tangent vectors $v_i\in T_mM$, as step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves.
However, the formula used to compute that value contains
$$
X^i
\left[
\omega(X^0,\dots,\widehat{X^i},\dots,X^p)
\right]
$$
and
$$
[X^i,X^j].
$$
The first requires the other arguments to be extended to vector fields so
that their evaluation under $\omega$ is a function near $m$ which $X^i$ can
differentiate. The second is a Lie bracket, which is defined for vector
fields and is not canonically defined for two tangent vectors at one point.

Thus local vector-field extensions are needed to write the coordinate-free
formula. The nontrivial tensoriality proved in steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is
exactly what guarantees that the final answer does not depend on which
extensions were chosen.

:::
