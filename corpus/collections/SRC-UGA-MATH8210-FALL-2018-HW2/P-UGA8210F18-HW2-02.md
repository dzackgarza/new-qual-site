::: {.problem}
Let $M$, $\phi$, $\psi$, $m$, and $U$ be as above.
Let $\omega$ be a $k$-form on $U$ so that $\omega = \sum_{I} f_{I}\, dx_{I}$ in $\phi$-coordinates and $\omega = \sum_{J} g_{J}\, dy_{J}$ in $\psi$-coordinates.
Determine $g_{J}$ in terms of $f_{I}$, $\phi$, and $\psi$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
\phi=(x_1,\dots,x_n),
\qquad
\psi=(y_1,\dots,y_n),
$$
and let
$$
T=\phi\circ\psi^{-1}:\psi(U)\to\phi(U).
$$
For an increasing multi-index
$$
I=(i_1<\cdots<i_k),
$$
write
$$
dx_I
=
dx_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{i_k},
$$
and similarly for $dy_J$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

In the $y$-coordinates,
$$
\boxed{
dx_i
=
\sum_{j=1}^n
\frac{\partial T_i}{\partial y_j}\,dy_j},
$$
where
$$
T_i=x_i\circ\psi^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

As functions on $U$,
$$
x_i
=
T_i\circ\psi.
$$
Apply the ordinary differential:
$$
dx_i
=
d(T_i\circ\psi)
=
\sum_{j=1}^n
\frac{\partial T_i}{\partial y_j}\,dy_j.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every increasing multi-index
$$
I=(i_1<\cdots<i_k),
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{
dx_I
=
\sum_{J}
\det\!\left(
\frac{\partial T_{i_a}}{\partial y_{j_b}}
\right)_{1\le a,b\le k}
dy_J},
$$
where the sum runs over increasing
$$
J=(j_1<\cdots<j_k).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
dx_{i_a}
=
\sum_{j=1}^n
\frac{\partial T_{i_a}}{\partial y_j}dy_j.
$$
Taking the wedge product over $a=1,\dots,k$ and expanding multilinearly,
every term with a repeated $dy_j$ vanishes. For a fixed increasing
$J=(j_1,\dots,j_k)$, the coefficient of
$$
dy_{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_{j_k}
$$
is the alternating sum over permutations
$$
\sum_{\sigma\in S_k}
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
\prod_{a=1}^k
\frac{\partial T_{i_a}}
{\partial y_{j_{\sigma(a)}}},
$$
which is exactly the displayed determinant.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In $y$-coordinates, the coefficient functions $f_I$ become
$$
f_I\circ T.
$$


::: pf-proof

The expression
$$
\omega
=
\sum_I f_I(x)\,dx_I
$$
uses the coordinate variable
$$
x=\phi(p).
$$
If
$$
y=\psi(p),
$$
then
$$
x
=
\phi(\psi^{-1}(y))
=
T(y).
$$
Hence the coefficient at the same point is
$$
f_I(T(y)).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore, for
$$
J=(j_1<\cdots<j_k),
$$
the coefficient of $dy_J$ is
$$
\boxed{
g_J(y)
=
\sum_I
f_I(T(y))
\det\!\left(
\frac{\partial T_{i_a}}{\partial y_{j_b}}(y)
\right)_{1\le a,b\le k}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute the expansion from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} into
$$
\omega
=
\sum_I
(f_I\circ T)\,dx_I.
$$
Collect the coefficient of the fixed basis form $dy_J$. The contribution
from each $I$ is
$$
(f_I\circ T)
\det\!\left(
\frac{\partial T_{i_a}}{\partial y_{j_b}}
\right),
$$
and summing over $I$ gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Equivalently, if
$$
A(y)=D(\phi\circ\psi^{-1})_y,
$$
then the coefficient vector of a $k$-form transforms by the induced map
$$
\Lambda^k A(y)^*,
$$
whose matrix entries are the $k\times k$ minors appearing in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

The transformation rule for $1$-forms is the dual Jacobian transformation
in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Exterior multiplication sends the dual map
$$
A(y)^*
$$
to
$$
\Lambda^kA(y)^*
$$
on $k$-covectors. In the coordinate wedge bases, the matrix of this exterior
power consists exactly of the determinants computed in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the requested formula for every $g_J$ in terms of the
$f_I$ and the transition map $\phi\circ\psi^{-1}$.

:::
