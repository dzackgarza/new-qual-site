::: {.problem}
Let $\phi , \psi : U \to \RR ^ { n }$ be two coordinate charts on a smooth manifold M. Let $m \in U$ Let $v \in T _ { m } U$ be given in φ-coordinates by $\sum v _ { i } \frac { \partial } { \partial x _ { i } }$ and in ψ-coordinates by $\sum w _ { i } \frac { \partial } { \partial y _ { i } }$ Determine $w _ { i }$ in terms of $v _ { i }$ and the maps $\phi \circ \psi ^ { \dot { - } 1 } , \psi \circ \phi ^ { - 1 }$ . (Start by making sure you understand what’s meant by φ-coordinates.)
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
\phi=(x_1,\dots,x_n),
\qquad
\psi=(y_1,\dots,y_n).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Saying that
$$
v
=
\sum_{i=1}^n v_i
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\evalfrom_m
$$
means that
$$
d\phi_m(v)
=
\sum_{i=1}^n v_i e_i
\in
T_{\phi(m)}\RR^n\cong\RR^n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The coordinate vector
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\evalfrom_m
$$
is defined by
$$
d\phi_m
\left(
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\evalfrom_m
\right)
=
e_i.
$$
Thus the coefficients $v_i$ are exactly the components of the pushed-forward
tangent vector in $\phi$-coordinates.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Similarly,
$$
d\psi_m(v)
=
\sum_{j=1}^n w_j e_j.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the same definition using the chart $\psi$ and its coordinate vector
fields
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial y_j}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The coordinate representatives are related by
$$
d\psi_m
=
d(\psi\circ\phi^{-1})_{\phi(m)}
\circ
d\phi_m.
$$


::: pf-proof

On $U$,
$$
\psi
=
(\psi\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi.
$$
Apply the chain rule at $m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
w_1\\
\vdots\\
w_n
\end{pmatrix}
=
D(\psi\circ\phi^{-1})_{\phi(m)}
\begin{pmatrix}
v_1\\
\vdots\\
v_n
\end{pmatrix}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} to $v$ and use steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. Under the canonical
identification of tangent spaces of $\RR^n$ with $\RR^n$, the
differential of the transition map is its Jacobian matrix.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Componentwise,
$$
\boxed{
w_j
=
\sum_{i=1}^n
v_i
\frac{\partial
\bigl(y_j\circ\phi^{-1}\bigr)}
{\partial x_i}
\evalfrom_{\phi(m)}
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The $j$th component of
$$
\psi\circ\phi^{-1}
$$
is
$$
y_j\circ\phi^{-1}.
$$
Reading the $j$th row of the matrix equation in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Conversely,
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
v_1\\
\vdots\\
v_n
\end{pmatrix}
=
D(\phi\circ\psi^{-1})_{\psi(m)}
\begin{pmatrix}
w_1\\
\vdots\\
w_n
\end{pmatrix}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Interchange the roles of $\phi$ and $\psi$. Equivalently, the transition maps
$$
\psi\circ\phi^{-1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\phi\circ\psi^{-1}
$$
are inverse diffeomorphisms, so their differentials at corresponding points
are inverse linear maps.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} give the requested expression for the $w_i$, while step
[](#s1-6){.pf-ref} records the inverse change of coordinates in terms of
$\phi\circ\psi^{-1}$.

:::
