::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be an $n\times n$ complex matrix and define
\[
G_M=\{\lambda\in\CC:\lambda M\text{ is similar to }M\}.
\]

1. Determine $G_M$ when
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&4\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
2. Assume $M$ is not nilpotent. Prove that $G_M$ is finite.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For the matrix in part (1), every nonzero scalar belongs to $G_M$.

::: pf-proof

Let $\lambda\in\CC^\times$ and set
$$
P_\lambda
\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&\lambda
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $P_\lambda$ is invertible, and direct multiplication gives
$$
P_\lambda^{-1}MP_\lambda
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&4\lambda\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}
=
\lambda M.
$$
Thus $\lambda M$ is similar to $M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For the matrix in part (1), $0\notin G_M$.

::: pf-proof

The matrix $M$ has rank $1$, whereas $0M$ has rank $0$. Similar matrices
have the same rank, so $0M$ is not similar to $M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Therefore, in part (1),
$$
\boxed{G_M=\CC^\times}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves $\CC^\times\subseteq G_M$, and step [](#s2){.pf-ref} excludes the
only remaining scalar.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

If a complex matrix $M$ is not nilpotent, then $M$ has a nonzero
eigenvalue.

::: pf-proof

If every eigenvalue of $M$ were $0$, then its characteristic polynomial
would be
$$
\chi_M(t)=t^n.
$$
By the Cayley--Hamilton theorem,
$$
M^n=0,
$$
which would make $M$ nilpotent. The contrapositive gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Fix a nonzero eigenvalue $\alpha$ of the nonnilpotent matrix $M$.
If $\lambda\in G_M$, then
$$
\lambda\alpha\in\Spec(M).
$$

::: pf-proof

Choose a nonzero vector $v$ with
$$
Mv=\alpha v.
$$
Then
$$
(\lambda M)v=\lambda\alpha v,
$$
so $\lambda\alpha$ is an eigenvalue of $\lambda M$. Since
$\lambda\in G_M$, the matrices $\lambda M$ and $M$ are similar and hence
have the same eigenvalues. Therefore $\lambda\alpha$ is an eigenvalue of
$M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

If $M$ is not nilpotent, then $G_M$ is finite.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, choose a nonzero eigenvalue $\alpha$ of $M$. Step [](#s5){.pf-ref}
implies
$$
G_M
\subseteq
\left\{
\frac{\beta}{\alpha}:
\beta\in\Spec(M)
\right\}.
$$
The spectrum of an $n\times n$ complex matrix is a finite set, so the set
on the right is finite. Hence $G_M$ is finite.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} answers part (1), and step [](#s6){.pf-ref} proves part (2).
