::: {.problem}
Let $f(x)\in\FF_p[x]$ and let
\[
g(x)=x^p-x.
\]
Show that $\gcd(f,g)$ is the product of the distinct linear factors of $f$ in $\FF_p[x]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
A\coloneqq\{a\in\FF_p:f(a)=0\}
$$
and define
$$
h(x)\coloneqq\prod_{a\in A}(x-a),
$$
with the empty product interpreted as $1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

In $\FF_p[x]$,
$$
x^p-x=\prod_{a\in\FF_p}(x-a),
$$
and all these linear factors are distinct.

::: pf-proof

Every $a\in\FF_p$ satisfies
$$
a^p=a,
$$
so every element of $\FF_p$ is a root of $x^p-x$. These are $p$
distinct roots of a monic polynomial of degree $p$, so the displayed
factorization follows. Equivalently, its derivative is
$$
(x^p-x)'=px^{p-1}-1=-1
$$
in characteristic $p$, which is nonzero and confirms that none of the
roots is repeated.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The polynomial $h$ divides both $f$ and $g$.

::: pf-proof

For each $a\in A$, the factor theorem gives
$$
x-a\mid f.
$$
The distinct linear polynomials $x-a$ are pairwise coprime, so their
product $h$ divides $f$. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, the same product is a subproduct of
the factorization of $g$, hence $h\mid g$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every common divisor of $f$ and $g$ divides $h$.

::: pf-proof

Let $d$ be a monic common divisor of $f$ and $g$. Since $d\mid g$, step
[](#s1){.pf-ref} and unique factorization in $\FF_p[x]$ show that $d$ is a product of
some of the distinct factors
$$
x-a,
\qquad a\in\FF_p.
$$
If $x-a$ occurs in $d$, then $x-a\mid f$ because $d\mid f$, so the factor
theorem gives $f(a)=0$. Thus $a\in A$. Every factor of $d$ therefore
occurs in $h$, and hence $d\mid h$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The monic greatest common divisor is
$$
\boxed{
\gcd(f,x^p-x)=\prod_{\substack{a\in\FF_p\\f(a)=0}}(x-a),
}
$$
the product of the distinct linear factors of $f$ over $\FF_p$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} shows that $h$ is a common divisor, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that
every common divisor divides $h$. Hence $h$ is the monic greatest common
divisor. Its factors are exactly the monic linear factors $x-a$ of $f$,
each occurring once.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is the required identity.

:::
