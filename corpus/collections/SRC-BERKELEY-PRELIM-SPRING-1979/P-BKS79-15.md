::: {.problem}
Which of the following matrices are similar over $\RR$?
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&1&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\0&1&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The four matrices $A,C,D,E$ have characteristic polynomial
$$
(t-1)^3,
$$
whereas $B$ and $F$ have characteristic polynomial
$$
(t-1)^2(t+1).
$$

::: {.proof}
The matrices $A,C,D,E$ are triangular with every diagonal entry equal to
$1$, so their characteristic polynomial is $(t-1)^3$.

For $B$,
$$
\det(tI-B)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
t&0&-1\\
0&t-1&0\\
-1&0&t
\end{pmatrix}
=
(t-1)(t^2-1)
=
(t-1)^2(t+1).
$$
Similarly,
$$
\det(tI-F)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
t&-1&-1\\
0&t-1&0\\
-1&0&t
\end{pmatrix}
=
(t-1)(t^2-1).
$$
:::

<1>2. The matrices $A,C,D,E$ have the following Jordan data at the sole
eigenvalue $1$:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{matrix}&\rank(M-I)&\text{nilpotency index of }M-I\\
\hline
A&0&1\\
C&1&2\\
D&2&3\\
E&1&2
\end{array}
$$
where the zero matrix is assigned nilpotency index $1$ for this table.

::: {.proof}
Clearly $A-I=0$.

For $C$ and $E$, respectively,
$$
C-I=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E-I=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Each has rank $1$, is nonzero, and has square zero.

Finally,
$$
D-I=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
which has rank $2$, has nonzero square
$$
(D-I)^2=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and has cube zero.
:::

<1>3. The matrices $C$ and $E$ are similar over $\RR$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, both $C-I$ and $E-I$ are nonzero nilpotent $3\times3$
matrices with square zero and rank $1$. Hence each has exactly one Jordan
block of size $2$ and one block of size $1$ for eigenvalue $0$. Therefore
both $C$ and $E$ have Jordan form
$$
J_2(1)\oplus[1],
$$
whose entries are real. Thus $C$ and $E$ are similar over $\RR$.
:::

<1>4. No two distinct matrices among $A,C,D,E$, except $C$ and $E$, are
similar.

::: {.proof}
Similarity preserves the ranks of polynomial expressions in a matrix and
the nilpotency index of $M-I$. Step <1>2 distinguishes $A$, the common
class of $C,E$, and $D$ by these invariants.
:::

<1>5. The matrices $B$ and $F$ are not similar.

::: {.proof}
For $B$, solving $(B-I)v=0$ gives
$$
v=
\begin{pmatrix}
a\\b\\a
\end{pmatrix},
$$
so
$$
\dim\ker(B-I)=2.
$$
For $F$,
$$
F-I=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&1&1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The equations $(F-I)v=0$ give $x=z$ and $y=0$, hence
$$
\ker(F-I)
=
\RR
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\0\\1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
and
$$
\dim\ker(F-I)=1.
$$
The dimension of an eigenspace is invariant under similarity, so $B$ and
$F$ are not similar.
:::

<1>6. No matrix in $\{A,C,D,E\}$ is similar to $B$ or $F$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the two sets have different characteristic polynomials, and
the characteristic polynomial is invariant under similarity.
:::

<1>7. Therefore the only distinct matrices in the list that are similar
are
$$
\boxed{C\sim E.}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives this similarity. Steps <1>4--<1>6 exclude every other
pair.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 is the complete classification requested.
:::
