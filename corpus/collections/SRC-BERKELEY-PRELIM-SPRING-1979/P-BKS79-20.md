::: {.problem}
Give an example of a function $f:\RR\to\RR$ such that

- $f(x)=0$ for $x<0$ and for $x>2$;
- $f'(1)=1$;
- $f$ has derivatives of all orders.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
\eta(t)
\coloneqq
\begin{cases}
e^{-1/t},&t>0,\\
0,&t\le0,
\end{cases}
$$
and then define
$$
\boxed{
f(x)\coloneqq e(x-1)\eta\bigl(x(2-x)\bigr).
}
$$

<1>1. The function $\eta$ is $C^\infty$ on $\RR$, and
$$
\eta^{(k)}(0)=0
$$
for every $k\ge0$.

::: {.proof}
For $t>0$, repeated differentiation shows that for every $k\ge0$ there
is a polynomial $P_k$ such that
$$
\eta^{(k)}(t)
=
P_k(1/t)e^{-1/t}.
$$
For every integer $m\ge0$,
$$
\lim_{t\downarrow0}t^{-m}e^{-1/t}=0,
$$
because after putting $s=1/t$ this becomes
$$
\lim_{s\to\infty}s^me^{-s}=0.
$$
Hence every derivative from the right tends to $0$ at $t=0$. On
$(-\infty,0]$, the function is identically zero. Inductively, the
one-sided derivatives of every order match at $0$, all with value $0$.
Thus $\eta\in C^\infty(\RR)$ and is flat at $0$.
:::

<1>2. The function
$$
\phi(x)\coloneqq\eta\bigl(x(2-x)\bigr)
$$
is $C^\infty$ on $\RR$ and vanishes for $x\le0$ and for $x\ge2$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $\eta$ is smooth, and $x\mapsto x(2-x)$ is a polynomial,
so their composition $\phi$ is smooth. If $x\le0$ or $x\ge2$, then
$$
x(2-x)\le0,
$$
and therefore $\phi(x)=0$ by the definition of $\eta$.
:::

<1>3. The displayed function $f$ is $C^\infty$ and satisfies
$$
f(x)=0
$$
for $x<0$ and for $x>2$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $\phi$ is smooth, so the product
$$
f(x)=e(x-1)\phi(x)
$$
is smooth. The same step shows that $\phi$, hence $f$, vanishes outside
$[0,2]$.
:::

<1>4. The derivative of $f$ at $1$ is
$$
\boxed{f'(1)=1.}
$$

::: {.proof}
The product rule gives
$$
f'(x)=e\phi(x)+e(x-1)\phi'(x).
$$
Since
$$
1(2-1)=1,
$$
we have
$$
\phi(1)=\eta(1)=e^{-1}.
$$
Therefore
$$
f'(1)=e\phi(1)=e\cdot e^{-1}=1.
$$
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves the support and smoothness requirements, and step <1>4
proves the prescribed derivative condition.
:::
:::
