::: {.problem}
Consider the system
\[
x'=y+tz,
\qquad
y'=z+t^2x,
\qquad
z'=x+e^t y.
\]
Prove that there exists a solution on $[0,1]$ satisfying
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\
4&5&6\\
7&8&9
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
x(0)\\y(0)\\z(0)
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\0\\0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and also
\[
\int_0^1\bigl(x(t)^2+y(t)^2+z(t)^2\bigr)\,dt=1.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the system as
$$
v'(t)=A(t)v(t),
\qquad
A(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&t\\
t^2&0&1\\
1&e^t&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
where
$$
v(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
x(t)\\y(t)\\z(t)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Also put
$$
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\
4&5&6\\
7&8&9
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The vector
$$
v_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\-2\\1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
is nonzero and satisfies
$$
Bv_0=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives
$$
Bv_0
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-4+3\\
4-10+6\\
7-16+9
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\0\\0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its first entry is $1$, so $v_0\ne0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

There is a unique solution
$$
u:[0,1]\longrightarrow\RR^3
$$
of
$$
u'(t)=A(t)u(t),
\qquad
u(0)=v_0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Every entry of $A(t)$ is continuous on $[0,1]$. The standard
existence-and-uniqueness theorem for linear systems therefore gives a
unique solution on the whole interval for the prescribed initial value.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The number
$$
I\coloneqq\int_0^1\|u(t)\|^2\,dt
$$
is finite and strictly positive.

::: pf-proof

The solution $u$ is continuous, so $\|u(t)\|^2$ is continuous on the
compact interval $[0,1]$ and therefore integrable with finite integral.
Moreover,
$$
\|u(0)\|^2=\|v_0\|^2=6>0.
$$
By continuity, $\|u(t)\|^2$ remains positive on some interval
$[0,\delta]$, so its integral over $[0,1]$ is strictly positive.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Define
$$
v(t)\coloneqq I^{-1/2}u(t).
$$
Then $v$ is a solution of the same differential system and satisfies the
displayed initial condition.

::: pf-proof

The system is homogeneous and linear, so multiplication of a solution by
the constant $I^{-1/2}$ gives another solution. By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
Bv(0)
=
I^{-1/2}Bu(0)
=
I^{-1/2}Bv_0
=
0.
$$
Thus the required matrix equation at $t=0$ holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The solution $v$ from step [](#s4){.pf-ref} satisfies
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^1\bigl(x(t)^2+y(t)^2+z(t)^2\bigr)\,dt=1.
}
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $\|v(t)\|^2=I^{-1}\|u(t)\|^2$, the definition of $I$ gives
$$
\int_0^1\|v(t)\|^2\,dt
=
I^{-1}\int_0^1\|u(t)\|^2\,dt
=
1.
$$
In coordinates, $\|v(t)\|^2=x(t)^2+y(t)^2+z(t)^2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives a solution satisfying the displayed initial condition, and
step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives the required normalization.

:::
