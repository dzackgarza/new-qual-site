::: {.problem}
Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional complex vector space with Hermitian inner product $\langle x,y\rangle$.

1. Prove that $E$ has an orthonormal basis.
2. Let $f:E\times E\to\mathbb C$ be linear in the first variable and conjugate-linear in the second. Show that there is a linear map $A:E\to E$ such that
\[
f(x,y)=\langle Ax,y\rangle
\]
for all $x,y\in E$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
m\coloneqq\dim_\CC E.
$$
If $m=0$, the empty set is an orthonormal basis and part (2) holds for
the unique map $A:0\to0$. Assume henceforth that $m\ge1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Every linearly independent family
$$
v_1,\ldots,v_r
$$
in $E$ can be converted into an orthonormal family
$$
e_1,\ldots,e_r
$$
with
$$
\operatorname{span}(e_1,\ldots,e_j)
=
\operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_j)
$$
for every $j$.

::: pf-proof

Apply the Gram--Schmidt construction. Set
$$
u_1=v_1,
\qquad
e_1=\frac{u_1}{\|u_1\|}.
$$
Inductively, after constructing
$$
e_1,\ldots,e_{j-1},
$$
define
$$
u_j
\coloneqq
v_j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}
\langle v_j,e_i\rangle e_i.
$$
For
$$
1\le k<j,
$$
orthonormality gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle u_j,e_k\rangle
&=
\langle v_j,e_k\rangle
-
\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}
\langle v_j,e_i\rangle
\langle e_i,e_k\rangle\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Moreover $u_j\ne0$: otherwise $v_j$ would belong to the span of
$v_1,\ldots,v_{j-1}$, contradicting linear independence. Hence
$$
e_j\coloneqq\frac{u_j}{\|u_j\|}
$$
is defined, has norm $1$, and is orthogonal to the preceding vectors.
The defining formula also gives the asserted equality of spans at
each stage.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The space $E$ has an orthonormal basis.

::: pf-proof

Choose any basis
$$
v_1,\ldots,v_m
$$
of $E$. Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} produces an orthonormal family
$$
e_1,\ldots,e_m
$$
with the same span as the original basis. Hence it is an orthonormal
basis of $E$. This proves part (1).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Fix an orthonormal basis
$$
e_1,\ldots,e_m
$$
as in step [](#s2){.pf-ref} and define
$$
Ax
\coloneqq
\sum_{i=1}^m f(x,e_i)e_i.
$$
Then
$$
A:E\to E
$$
is linear.

::: pf-proof

For
$$
x,x'\in E
$$
and
$$
\alpha,\beta\in\CC,
$$
linearity of $f$ in its first variable gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
A(\alpha x+\beta x')
&=
\sum_{i=1}^m
f(\alpha x+\beta x',e_i)e_i\\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^m
\bigl(
\alpha f(x,e_i)+\beta f(x',e_i)
\bigr)e_i\\
&=
\alpha Ax+\beta Ax'.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For every
$$
y=\sum_{i=1}^m y_i e_i,
$$
one has
$$
f(x,y)
=
\sum_{i=1}^m
\overline{y_i}\,f(x,e_i).
$$

::: pf-proof

The form $f$ is conjugate-linear in its second variable. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x,y)
&=
f\left(
x,
\sum_{i=1}^m y_i e_i
\right)\\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^m
\overline{y_i}\,f(x,e_i).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

For all
$$
x,y\in E,
$$
the map $A$ from step [](#s3){.pf-ref} satisfies
$$
\boxed{
f(x,y)=\langle Ax,y\rangle.
}
$$

::: pf-proof

Write
$$
y=\sum_{i=1}^m y_i e_i.
$$
Using the convention in the problem that the Hermitian inner product
is linear in the first variable and conjugate-linear in the second,
orthonormality gives
$$
\langle e_i,y\rangle=\overline{y_i}.
$$
Hence step [](#s3){.pf-ref} yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle Ax,y\rangle
&=
\left\langle
\sum_{i=1}^m f(x,e_i)e_i,
y
\right\rangle\\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^m
f(x,e_i)\langle e_i,y\rangle\\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^m
\overline{y_i}\,f(x,e_i)\\
&=
f(x,y)
\end{aligned}
$$
by step [](#s4){.pf-ref}. This proves part (2).

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves existence of an orthonormal basis, and steps
[](#s3){.pf-ref}, [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} construct the required linear operator and verify its
representation formula.
