::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group with normal subgroups $N_1,N_2,N_3$. Suppose that for every $i\ne j$,
\[
N_i\cap N_j=\{e\},
\qquad
N_iN_j=G.
\]
Show that $G$ is abelian and that $N_i\cong N_j$ for all $i,j$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $i\ne j$, then every element of $N_i$ commutes with every
element of $N_j$.

::: {.proof}
Let $x\in N_i$ and $y\in N_j$. Since both subgroups are normal,
$$
[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}
$$
lies in $N_i$ and in $N_j$. Thus
$$
[x,y]\in N_i\cap N_j=\{e\},
$$
so $[x,y]=e$.
:::

<1>2. Each subgroup $N_i$ is contained in the center $Z(G)$.

::: {.proof}
Fix $i$, and let $j,k$ be the other two indices. By step <1>1, every
element of $N_i$ commutes with every element of both $N_j$ and $N_k$.
The hypothesis gives
$$
G=N_jN_k.
$$
Hence every element of $G$ is a product $yz$ with
$$
y\in N_j,
\qquad
z\in N_k.
$$
An element of $N_i$ commutes with both factors and therefore with their
product. Thus $N_i\subseteq Z(G)$.
:::

<1>3. The group $G$ is abelian.

::: {.proof}
For example,
$$
G=N_1N_2
$$
by hypothesis. Step <1>2 gives
$$
N_1,N_2\subseteq Z(G),
$$
so every element of $G$ lies in $Z(G)$. Hence $G=Z(G)$ and $G$ is
abelian.
:::

<1>4. For every pair $i\ne j$, the quotient map
$$
q_i:G\longrightarrow G/N_i
$$
restricts to an isomorphism
$$
q_i|_{N_j}:N_j\xrightarrow{\sim}G/N_i.
$$

::: {.proof}
Its kernel is
$$
N_j\cap N_i=\{e\},
$$
so the restriction is injective. Its image is
$$
q_i(N_j)=N_iN_j/N_i=G/N_i
$$
because $N_iN_j=G$. Thus the restriction is also surjective.
:::

<1>5. The three subgroups are pairwise isomorphic:
$$
\boxed{N_i\cong N_j\quad\text{for all }i,j.}
$$

::: {.proof}
For any distinct indices $i,j,k$, step <1>4 gives
$$
N_i\cong G/N_k\cong N_j.
$$
The case $i=j$ is immediate.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves that $G$ is abelian, and step <1>5 proves the required
pairwise isomorphisms.
:::
:::
