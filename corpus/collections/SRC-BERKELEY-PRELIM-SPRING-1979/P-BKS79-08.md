::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a real nonsingular $3\times3$ matrix.
Prove that there are real matrices $S$ and $U$ such that
\[
M=SU=US,
\]
all eigenvalues of $U$ are equal to $1$, and $S$ is diagonalizable over $\CC$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $M$ has a nonreal eigenvalue, then $M$ is diagonalizable over
$\CC$.

::: {.proof}
The characteristic polynomial of the real $3\times3$ matrix $M$ is a real
cubic. Hence a nonreal eigenvalue $\lambda$ occurs together with its
conjugate $\overline\lambda$. The remaining root $\mu$ is real. Thus
$$
\lambda,\quad \overline\lambda,\quad \mu
$$
are three distinct eigenvalues: the first two are distinct and nonreal,
whereas $\mu$ is real. A $3\times3$ complex matrix with three distinct
eigenvalues is diagonalizable over $\CC$.
:::

<1>2. In the case of step <1>1, the choice
$$
S=M,
\qquad
U=I
$$
has all the required properties.

::: {.proof}
Both matrices are real and
$$
SU=US=M.
$$
Every eigenvalue of $U=I$ equals $1$, while step <1>1 says that
$S=M$ is diagonalizable over $\CC$.
:::

<1>3. Suppose now that every eigenvalue of $M$ is real. Then there is a
real invertible matrix $P$ such that
$$
J\coloneqq P^{-1}MP
$$
is block diagonal with real Jordan blocks
$$
J_r(\lambda)=\lambda I_r+N_r,
$$
where $N_r$ is the nilpotent Jordan block of size $r$ and every
$\lambda\ne0$.

::: {.proof}
Since the characteristic polynomial splits over $\RR$ in this case, the
Jordan normal form theorem can be applied over $\RR$, so the Jordan basis
may be chosen real. Nonsingularity of $M$ means that $0$ is not an
eigenvalue, hence every block eigenvalue $\lambda$ is nonzero.
:::

<1>4. For each Jordan block from step <1>3, define
$$
S_r\coloneqq\lambda I_r,
\qquad
U_r\coloneqq I_r+\lambda^{-1}N_r.
$$
Then
$$
J_r(\lambda)=S_rU_r=U_rS_r,
$$
$S_r$ is diagonal, and every eigenvalue of $U_r$ equals $1$.

::: {.proof}
Because $S_r$ is scalar,
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_rU_r
&=
\lambda I_r
\left(I_r+\lambda^{-1}N_r\right)\\
&=
\lambda I_r+N_r
=
J_r(\lambda),
\end{aligned}
$$
and the same computation gives $U_rS_r=J_r(\lambda)$. The matrix $S_r$
is already diagonal. The matrix $U_r$ is upper triangular with every
diagonal entry equal to $1$, so all its eigenvalues equal $1$.
:::

<1>5. Let $S_0$ and $U_0$ be the block-diagonal matrices obtained by
placing the matrices $S_r$ and $U_r$ from step <1>4 in the same block
positions as the Jordan blocks of $J$. Then
$$
J=S_0U_0=U_0S_0,
$$
$S_0$ is diagonal, and every eigenvalue of $U_0$ equals $1$.

::: {.proof}
All three assertions hold block by block by step <1>4. A block-diagonal
matrix whose blocks are diagonal scalar matrices is diagonal, and the
eigenvalues of a block-diagonal matrix are the eigenvalues of its blocks.
:::

<1>6. Define
$$
S\coloneqq PS_0P^{-1},
\qquad
U\coloneqq PU_0P^{-1}.
$$
Then $S$ and $U$ are real and satisfy all the required properties.

::: {.proof}
The matrices $P$, $S_0$, and $U_0$ are real, so $S$ and $U$ are real.
Using step <1>5,
$$
\begin{aligned}
SU
&=
P(S_0U_0)P^{-1}
=
PJP^{-1}
=
M,\\
US
&=
P(U_0S_0)P^{-1}
=
PJP^{-1}
=
M.
\end{aligned}
$$
Similarity preserves eigenvalues, so every eigenvalue of $U$ equals $1$.
Also $S$ is similar over $\RR$ to the diagonal matrix $S_0$, hence is
diagonalizable over $\RR$ and therefore over $\CC$.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
If $M$ has a nonreal eigenvalue, step <1>2 gives the required matrices.
If all eigenvalues are real, step <1>6 gives them. These cases exhaust all
real $3\times3$ matrices.
:::
:::
