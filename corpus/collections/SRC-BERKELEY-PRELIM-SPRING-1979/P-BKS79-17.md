::: {.problem}
Let $P,Q\in\CC[z]$ with
\[
\deg Q\ge\deg P+2.
\]
Prove that there exists $r>0$ such that if $C$ is a closed curve lying outside the circle $|z|=r$, then
\[
\int_C\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\,dz=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
If $P=0$, the conclusion is immediate. Assume henceforth that $P\ne0$, and
write
$$
m\coloneqq\deg P,
\qquad
n\coloneqq\deg Q,
\qquad
d\coloneqq n-m\ge2.
$$

<1>1. There is a neighborhood of $w=0$ on which
$$
\frac{P(1/w)}{Q(1/w)}=w^dH(w)
$$
for a holomorphic function $H$.

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
\widetilde P(w)\coloneqq w^mP(1/w),
\qquad
\widetilde Q(w)\coloneqq w^nQ(1/w).
$$
These are polynomials in $w$. Moreover,
$$
\widetilde Q(0)
$$
is the leading coefficient of $Q$, hence is nonzero. Therefore
$$
H(w)\coloneqq\frac{\widetilde P(w)}{\widetilde Q(w)}
$$
is holomorphic near $0$, and for $w\ne0$ sufficiently small,
$$
\frac{P(1/w)}{Q(1/w)}
=
w^{n-m}
\frac{\widetilde P(w)}{\widetilde Q(w)}
=
w^dH(w).
$$
:::

<1>2. There exists $r>0$ such that, for $|z|>r$,
$$
\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}
=
\sum_{j=0}^\infty c_j z^{-(j+d)}
$$
with locally uniform convergence.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $H$ has a Taylor series
$$
H(w)=\sum_{j=0}^\infty c_jw^j
$$
for $|w|<\rho$ for some $\rho>0$. Choose $r>1/\rho$. Substituting
$w=1/z$ into the identity from step <1>1 gives, whenever $|z|>r$,
$$
\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}
=
z^{-d}H(1/z)
=
\sum_{j=0}^\infty c_jz^{-(j+d)}.
$$
Taylor-series convergence is uniform on compact subsets of $|w|<\rho$,
so the resulting Laurent series is uniform on compact subsets of
$|z|>r$.
:::

<1>3. The rational function $P/Q$ has a holomorphic primitive on the
exterior domain $|z|>r$.

::: {.proof}
Because $d\ge2$, every exponent in step <1>2 is at most $-2$. Define
$$
F(z)
\coloneqq
-\sum_{j=0}^\infty
\frac{c_j}{j+d-1}
z^{-(j+d-1)}.
$$
The differentiated Laurent series has the same radius of convergence as
the series in step <1>2, so $F$ is holomorphic for $|z|>r$ and termwise
differentiation gives
$$
F'(z)
=
\sum_{j=0}^\infty c_jz^{-(j+d)}
=
\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}.
$$
The condition $d\ge2$ is precisely what prevents a $z^{-1}$ term, whose
antiderivative would require a logarithm.
:::

<1>4. If $C$ is any closed piecewise $C^1$ curve lying in $|z|>r$, then
$$
\boxed{
\int_C\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\,dz=0.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, the integrand is $F'$ on the whole exterior domain. The
integral of a holomorphic derivative over a closed curve is zero.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves the assertion for the radius chosen in step <1>2.
:::
