::: {.problem}
Let $1<\lambda<\infty$ and
\[
f_\lambda(z)=z+\lambda-e^z.
\]
Prove that $f_\lambda$ has exactly one zero in the half-plane $\operatorname{Re}z<0$, and that this zero is real.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every zero of $f_\lambda$ in the half-plane
$$
\operatorname{Re}z<0
$$
is real.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
z=x+iy,
\qquad
x<0,
$$
and $f_\lambda(z)=0$. Then
$$
z+\lambda=e^z=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y).
$$
Taking imaginary parts gives
$$
y=e^x\sin y.
$$
If $y\ne0$, then
$$
|y|
=
e^x|\sin y|
\le
e^x|y|
<
|y|,
$$
because $x<0$ implies $e^x<1$. This is impossible. Hence $y=0$, so
$z$ is real.
:::

<1>2. The real function
$$
g(x)\coloneqq x+\lambda-e^x
$$
has exactly one zero on $(-\infty,0)$.

::: {.proof}
For $x<0$,
$$
g'(x)=1-e^x>0,
$$
so $g$ is strictly increasing on $(-\infty,0)$. Moreover,
$$
\lim_{x\to-\infty}g(x)=-\infty
$$
because $e^x\to0$, while
$$
g(0)=\lambda-1>0.
$$
The intermediate value theorem therefore gives a zero
$$
x_\lambda\in(-\infty,0),
$$
and strict monotonicity makes it unique.
:::

<1>3. The number $x_\lambda$ from step <1>2 is the unique zero of
$f_\lambda$ in the half-plane $\operatorname{Re}z<0$.

::: {.proof}
Since $x_\lambda$ is real,
$$
f_\lambda(x_\lambda)=g(x_\lambda)=0,
$$
so a zero in the required half-plane exists. By step <1>1, every zero in
that half-plane is real, and step <1>2 shows that the only possible real
zero there is $x_\lambda$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves both uniqueness and reality of the zero in
$\operatorname{Re}z<0$.
:::
:::
