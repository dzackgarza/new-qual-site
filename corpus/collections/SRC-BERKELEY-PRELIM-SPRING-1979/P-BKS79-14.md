::: {.problem}
Let $M_{n\times n}$ be the real vector space of $n\times n$ real matrices, with $n\ge2$, and let
\[
\det:M_{n\times n}\to\RR
\]
be the determinant map.

1. Show that $\det$ is $C^\infty$.
2. Show that the derivative of $\det$ at $A$ is the zero linear map if and only if $\rank A\le n-2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $A=(a_{ij})$, write
$$
C_{ij}(A)
\coloneqq
(-1)^{i+j}\det A_{\widehat i,\widehat j}
$$
for the $(i,j)$-cofactor of $A$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The map
$$
\det:M_{n\times n}\longrightarrow\RR
$$
is $C^\infty$.

::: pf-proof

In the matrix-entry coordinates,
$$
\det(A)
=
\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
\sgn(\sigma)
\prod_{i=1}^n a_{i,\sigma(i)}.
$$
Thus $\det$ is a polynomial function on the real vector space
$M_{n\times n}\cong\RR^{n^2}$. Every polynomial function on a finite-dimensional
real vector space is $C^\infty$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $A,H\in M_{n\times n}$,
$$
D(\det)_A(H)
=
\sum_{i,j=1}^n C_{ij}(A)H_{ij}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $\det$ is a polynomial in the entries, its differential is obtained
from its coordinate partial derivatives. Laplace expansion along row $i$
shows that
$$
\frac{\partial\det}{\partial a_{ij}}(A)=C_{ij}(A).
$$
Therefore, for the direction $H=(H_{ij})$,
$$
D(\det)_A(H)
=
\sum_{i,j=1}^n
\frac{\partial\det}{\partial a_{ij}}(A)H_{ij}
=
\sum_{i,j=1}^n C_{ij}(A)H_{ij}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The differential $D(\det)_A$ is the zero linear map if and only if
every $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ minor of $A$ is zero.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, if every cofactor $C_{ij}(A)$ is zero, then
$D(\det)_A(H)=0$ for every $H$. Conversely, if the differential is zero,
apply it to the matrix unit $E_{ij}$ to obtain
$$
0=D(\det)_A(E_{ij})=C_{ij}(A).
$$
Hence every cofactor vanishes. Cofactors differ only by signs from the
$(n-1)\times(n-1)$ minors, so the two conditions are equivalent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Every $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ minor of $A$ vanishes if and only if
$$
\rank A\le n-2.
$$

::: pf-proof

The rank of a matrix over a field is the largest size of a nonzero minor.
Thus a matrix has rank at least $n-1$ exactly when it has a nonzero
$(n-1)\times(n-1)$ minor. Negating this condition gives the displayed
equivalence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
D(\det)_A=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rank A\le n-2.
}
$$

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves part (1), and step [](#s5){.pf-ref} proves part (2).
