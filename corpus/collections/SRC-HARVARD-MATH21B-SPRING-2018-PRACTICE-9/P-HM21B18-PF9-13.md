::: {.problem}
(a) Find the Fourier series on $[-\pi,\pi]$ of
$$
f(x)=\sin(x/2).
$$

(b) Evaluate
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{n^2}{(1-4n^2)^2}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Fourier series of $f(x)=\sin(x/2)$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$ is
$$
\boxed{
\sin\frac x2
\sim
\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\frac{8n(-1)^n}{\pi(1-4n^2)}\sin(nx).}
$$


::: pf-proof

The function is odd, so its constant and cosine Fourier coefficients vanish. Its sine coefficient is
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n
&=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\sin\frac x2\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi
\left(
\cos\bigl((n-\tfrac12)x\bigr)
-\cos\bigl((n+\tfrac12)x\bigr)
\right)dx\\
&=\frac1\pi
\left(
\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n-1/2}
-\frac{(-1)^n}{n+1/2}
\right)\\
&=\frac{8n(-1)^n}{\pi(1-4n^2)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the displayed sine series is the Fourier series. Its periodic extension has a jump between the endpoint values $-1$ and $1$, so the series converges to $0$ at $x=\pm\pi$ and to $\sin(x/2)$ for $-\pi<x<\pi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The normalized squared $L^2$ norm of $f$ is $1$:
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)^2\,dx=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $f^2$ is even,
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^2\frac x2\,dx
=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\sin^2\frac x2\,dx.
$$
Using $\sin^2(x/2)=(1-\cos x)/2$, the last integral is $\pi/2$, so the normalized value is $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The requested series equals
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{n^2}{(1-4n^2)^2}
=\frac{\pi^2}{64}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Parseval's identity for the Fourier coefficients from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)^2\,dx
=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n^2,
$$
because all constant and cosine coefficients vanish. By steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
1
=\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\frac{64n^2}{\pi^2(1-4n^2)^2}.
$$
Multiplying by $\pi^2/64$ gives the displayed value.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} answers part (a), and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} answers part (b).
