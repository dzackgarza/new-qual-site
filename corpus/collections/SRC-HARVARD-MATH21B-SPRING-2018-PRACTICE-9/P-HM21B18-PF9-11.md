::: {.problem}
Consider
\[
\dot x=y-xy,
\qquad
\dot y=x+xy.
\]

(a) Find the nullclines.

(b) Find all equilibrium points.

(c) Determine the stability of each equilibrium point.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The nullclines are
\[
\boxed{y=0\text{ or }x=1}
\]
for $\dot x=0$, and
\[
\boxed{x=0\text{ or }y=-1}
\]
for $\dot y=0$.

::: {.proof}
The right-hand sides factor as
\[
\dot x=y(1-x),
\qquad
\dot y=x(1+y).
\]
Thus each vanishes exactly on the displayed pair of lines.
:::

<1>2. The equilibrium points are
\[
\boxed{(0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(1,-1).}
\]

::: {.proof}
An equilibrium lies simultaneously on an $x$-nullcline and a $y$-nullcline. Intersecting the two unions of lines from step <1>1 gives exactly $(0,0)$ and $(1,-1)$.
:::

<1>3. The equilibrium $(0,0)$ is $\boxed{\text{unstable}}$.

::: {.proof}
The Jacobian is
\[
J(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-y&1-x\\
1+y&x
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
At $(0,0)$,
\[
J(0,0)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$. Since the linearization has an eigenvalue with positive real part, the equilibrium is unstable.
:::

<1>4. The equilibrium $(1,-1)$ is $\boxed{\text{unstable}}$.

::: {.proof}
At $(1,-1)$,
\[
J(1,-1)=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both eigenvalues are $1$, so the linearization again has positive real part. Hence the equilibrium is unstable.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>4 answer parts (a)--(c).
:::
:::
