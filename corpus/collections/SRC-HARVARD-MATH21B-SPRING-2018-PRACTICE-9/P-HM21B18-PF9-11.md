::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
\dot x=y-xy,
\qquad
\dot y=x+xy.
$$

(a) Find the nullclines.

(b) Find all equilibrium points.

(c) Determine the stability of each equilibrium point.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The nullclines are
$$
\boxed{y=0\text{ or }x=1}
$$
for $\dot x=0$, and
$$
\boxed{x=0\text{ or }y=-1}
$$
for $\dot y=0$.


::: pf-proof

The right-hand sides factor as
$$
\dot x=y(1-x),
\qquad
\dot y=x(1+y).
$$
Thus each vanishes exactly on the displayed pair of lines.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The equilibrium points are
$$
\boxed{(0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(1,-1).}
$$


::: pf-proof

An equilibrium lies simultaneously on an $x$-nullcline and a $y$-nullcline. Intersecting the two unions of lines from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives exactly $(0,0)$ and $(1,-1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The equilibrium $(0,0)$ is $\boxed{\text{unstable}}$.


::: pf-proof

The Jacobian is
$$
J(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-y&1-x\\
1+y&x
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
At $(0,0)$,
$$
J(0,0)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$. Since the linearization has an eigenvalue with positive real part, the equilibrium is unstable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The equilibrium $(1,-1)$ is $\boxed{\text{unstable}}$.


::: pf-proof

At $(1,-1)$,
$$
J(1,-1)=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Both eigenvalues are $1$, so the linearization again has positive real part. Hence the equilibrium is unstable.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} answer parts (a)--(c).

:::
