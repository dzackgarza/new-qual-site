::: {.problem}
Match each statement below to one of the named results A--J, using no letter twice.

1. $\dim\ker A+\dim\operatorname{im}A$ equals the number of columns of $A$.
2. If $p$ has degree $n$, then $p(D)=a(D-\lambda_1)\cdots(D-\lambda_n)$.
3. If $f$ is odd, then
$$
\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi f(x)^2\,dx
=\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\left(\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx\right)^2.
$$
4. If $\operatorname{tr}A<0$ and $\det A>0$, then the corresponding two-dimensional continuous linear dynamical system is stable.
5. $\dim\{f\in C^\infty(\RR):p(D)f=0\}=\deg p$.
6. If $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)g(x)\,dx=0$, then
$$
\int f^2+\int g^2=\int(f+g)^2.
$$
7. $\cos\theta+i\sin\theta=e^{i\theta}$.

Choices:

A. Pythagorean theorem for inner products; B. Fundamental theorem of algebra; C. stability criterion for continuous planar linear systems; D. Parseval identity; E. rank-nullity theorem; F. Euler's formula; G. sine-Fourier expansion; H. stability criterion for discrete planar linear systems; I. parameterization of the homogeneous solution space; J. Laplace expansion.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Statement 1 matches $\boxed{E}$, the rank-nullity theorem.


::: pf-proof

For the linear map represented by an $m\times n$ matrix $A$, rank-nullity states
$$
\dim\ker A+\dim\operatorname{im}A=n,
$$
and $n$ is the number of columns of $A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Statement 2 matches $\boxed{B}$, the fundamental theorem of algebra.


::: pf-proof

Over $\CC$, the fundamental theorem of algebra factors a degree-$n$ polynomial as
$$
p(z)=a(z-\lambda_1)\cdots(z-\lambda_n),
$$
counting roots with multiplicity. Substituting the commuting operator $D$ for $z$ gives the displayed factorization of $p(D)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Statement 3 matches $\boxed{D}$, Parseval's identity.


::: pf-proof

For an odd function, only sine coefficients occur in its Fourier expansion. With the normalization in the statement, Parseval's identity is exactly
$$
\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi f(x)^2\,dx
=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n^2,
\qquad
b_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Statement 4 matches $\boxed{C}$, the stability criterion for continuous planar linear systems.


::: pf-proof

For a real $2\times2$ matrix, both eigenvalues have negative real part exactly when
$$
\operatorname{tr}A<0,
\qquad
\det A>0.
$$
That is the standard trace-determinant stability criterion for the continuous system $\dot x=Ax$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Statement 5 matches $\boxed{I}$, the parameterization of the homogeneous solution space.


::: pf-proof

For a degree-$n$ constant-coefficient operator $p(D)$, the homogeneous equation $p(D)f=0$ has an $n$-dimensional solution space, with the usual exponential-polynomial basis obtained from the roots of $p$. Thus the displayed dimension statement is the dimension assertion underlying that parameterization.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Statement 6 matches $\boxed{A}$, the Pythagorean theorem for inner products.


::: pf-proof

The condition
$$
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)g(x)\,dx=0
$$
says that $f$ and $g$ are orthogonal for the $L^2$ inner product. Hence
$$
\|f+g\|^2=\|f\|^2+\|g\|^2,
$$
which is the displayed integral identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Statement 7 matches $\boxed{F}$, Euler's formula.


::: pf-proof

Euler's formula is precisely
$$
e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} give the matching
$$
\boxed{E,\ B,\ D,\ C,\ I,\ A,\ F}
$$
in statement order, with no letter repeated.

:::
