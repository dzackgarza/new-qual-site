::: {.problem}
Solve the wave equation
$$
f_{tt}=16f_{xx}
$$
on $[0,\pi]$ with initial position
$$
f(x,0)=\sin x
$$
and initial velocity
$$
f_t(x,0)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\pi/3\le x\le2\pi/3,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

For $n\ge1$, set
$$
b_n=\frac{2}{n\pi}
\left(
\cos\frac{n\pi}{3}-\cos\frac{2n\pi}{3}
\right).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Fourier sine series of the initial velocity is
$$
g(x)\sim\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin(nx),
$$
where $g$ denotes the piecewise constant function in the problem.


::: pf-proof

The sine coefficient of $g$ on $[0,\pi]$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n
&=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi g(x)\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac2\pi\int_{\pi/3}^{2\pi/3}\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac{2}{n\pi}
\left(
\cos\frac{n\pi}{3}-\cos\frac{2n\pi}{3}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the displayed series is the Fourier sine expansion of $g$. It converges to $g$ at every continuity point and to the midpoint value $1/2$ at the two jump points, and represents $g$ in $L^2(0,\pi)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

With homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions at $0$ and $\pi$, the solution is
$$
\boxed{
f(x,t)
=\cos(4t)\sin x
+\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\frac{b_n}{4n}\sin(4nt)\sin(nx).}
$$


::: pf-proof

For each $n\ge1$, a separated sine mode for
$$
f_{tt}=16f_{xx}
$$
has the form
$$
\bigl(A_n\cos(4nt)+B_n\sin(4nt)\bigr)\sin(nx),
$$
because differentiating twice in $t$ multiplies by $-(4n)^2$ and differentiating twice in $x$ multiplies by $-n^2$. The initial position $\sin x$ therefore gives
$$
A_1=1,
\qquad
A_n=0\quad(n\ne1).
$$
At $t=0$, the time derivative of the $n$th sine mode is $4nB_n\sin(nx)$. Matching the sine coefficients $b_n$ from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
B_n=\frac{b_n}{4n}.
$$
Substitution yields exactly the displayed series. Its sine factors also give $f(0,t)=f(\pi,t)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The series has the prescribed initial data in the Fourier sense.


::: pf-proof

At $t=0$,
$$
f(x,0)=\sin x.
$$
Differentiating the temporal factors and setting $t=0$ gives
$$
f_t(x,0)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin(nx),
$$
which is the Fourier sine series of $g$ by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Hence it equals $g$ in $L^2(0,\pi)$ and pointwise at every continuity point of $g$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} give the standard fixed-end wave solution. Without boundary conditions, the problem statement alone does not specify a unique solution on the bounded interval.

:::
