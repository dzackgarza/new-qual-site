::: problem
Let $L$ be a positive constant.
Let $\{a_n\}_{n=0}^\infty$ be a strictly decreasing sequence of positive numbers, where $a_n\ge L$ for all $n$.
Let $\mathbb{E}\subset\mathbb{C}$ denote the open unit disk.

(a) Prove that $f(z):=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n z^n$ defines an analytic function in $\mathbb{E}$.

(b) What is the radius of convergence of the power series $f(z)$?

(c) Show that $f$ has no zeros in $\mathbb{E}$.
:::

::: solution
Since $(a_n)$ is decreasing and bounded below by $L>0$, it converges to some
$A\ge L$. In particular $(a_n)$ is bounded, so for $|z|<1$ the series
\[
\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nz^n
\]
converges absolutely and locally uniformly. Hence it defines a holomorphic
function on $\mathbb E$.

Moreover $a_n\to A>0$, so
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_n^{1/n}=1.
\]
Thus the radius of convergence is exactly
\[
\boxed{1}.
\]

Finally,
\[
(1-z)f(z)=a_0-\sum_{n=1}^\infty (a_{n-1}-a_n)z^n.
\]
Set $b_n=a_{n-1}-a_n>0$. Since
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n=a_0-A<a_0,
\]
for $|z|<1$ we have
\[
\left|\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_nz^n\right|
<\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n
<a_0.
\]
Therefore $(1-z)f(z)\ne0$. Since $1-z\ne0$ in $\mathbb E$, it follows that
\[
\boxed{f(z)\ne0\quad(|z|<1)}.
\]
:::
