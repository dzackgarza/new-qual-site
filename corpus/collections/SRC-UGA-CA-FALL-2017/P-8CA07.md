::: {.problem}
Let $L$ be a positive constant.
Let $\{a_n\}_{n=0}^\infty$ be a strictly decreasing sequence of positive numbers, where $a_n\ge L$ for all $n$.
Let $\bE\subset\CC$ denote the open unit disk.

(a) Prove that $f(z):=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n z^n$ defines an analytic function in $\bE$.

(b) What is the radius of convergence of the power series $f(z)$?

(c) Show that $f$ has no zeros in $\bE$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this problem: the collection's `provenance` names `assets/attachments/Complex_Analysis_Fall_2017.docx` and its PDF, the examination and statement list, and no solution sheet. The argument below is the card's own and follows the card's previous prose, whose three steps are those of the printed problem.

Throughout $(a_n)_{n\ge0}$ is strictly decreasing with $a_n\ge L>0$ for a positive constant $L$, $\DD=\theset{\abs z<1}$, and $f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n$ wherever the series converges.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #convergence}
$(a_n)$ converges to some $A$ with $L\le A<a_0$, and $f$ is analytic on $\DD$.

::: pf-proof
A decreasing sequence bounded below converges, to $A\ge L$, by monotone convergence; strict decrease gives $A<a_0$ when $n\ge1$ terms exist, and in any case $A\le a_1<a_0$. In particular $a_n\le a_0$ for all $n$. For $\abs z\le\rho<1$ we have $\abs{a_nz^n}\le a_0\rho^n$ and $\sum_n a_0\rho^n<\infty$, so the series converges absolutely and uniformly on every closed disk $\abs z\le\rho\subset\DD$; a locally uniform limit of polynomials is analytic, so $f$ is analytic on $\DD$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #radius}
The radius of convergence of $\sum_na_nz^n$ is exactly $1$.

::: pf-proof
$a_n\to A>0$, so $\log a_n\to\log A$, hence $\frac1n\log a_n\to0$ and $a_n^{1/n}\to1$. By the Cauchy–Hadamard formula the radius is $1/\limsup_n a_n^{1/n}=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #factorization}
For $\abs z<1$, $\ (1-z)f(z)=a_0-\sum_{n\ge1}b_nz^n$ with $b_n\coloneqq a_{n-1}-a_n>0$.

::: pf-proof
$f(z)=\sum_na_nz^n$ and $zf(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^{n+1}=\sum_{n\ge1}a_{n-1}z^n$, both series converging absolutely for $\abs z<1$ and hence addable term by term; subtracting gives $(1-z)f(z)=a_0+\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n-a_{n-1})z^n=a_0-\sum_{n\ge1}b_nz^n$. The $b_n$ are positive because the sequence is strictly decreasing.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #telescoping}
$\sum_{n\ge1}b_n=a_0-A<a_0$.

::: pf-proof
The partial sums are $\sum_{n=1}^Nb_n=a_0-a_N$, which tends to $a_0-A$; since $A\ge L>0$, this is strictly less than $a_0$. So the series of positive terms $b_n$ converges with that sum.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #no-zeros}
$f(z)\ne0$ for every $z\in\DD$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\abs z<1$. By step [](#telescoping){.pf-ref} and $b_n>0$,
$$
\abs{\sum_{n\ge1}b_nz^n}\le\sum_{n\ge1}b_n\abs z^n<\sum_{n\ge1}b_n=a_0-A<a_0,
$$
the strict inequality because $b_1>0$ and $\abs z<1$. So $(1-z)f(z)=a_0-h(z)$ with $\abs{h(z)}<a_0$, which cannot vanish: if it did, $h(z)=a_0$, a real number of modulus $a_0$. Since $1-z\ne0$ for $\abs z<1$, it follows that $f(z)\ne0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
$f$ is analytic on $\DD$, its radius of convergence is $1$, and it has no zeros in $\DD$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#convergence){.pf-ref}, [](#radius){.pf-ref} and [](#no-zeros){.pf-ref} are (a), (b) and (c). The answer to (b) is the radius of the power series about $0$, which is $1$. That the series cannot be continued past $z=1$ is also visible from the factorization: as $z\to1$ through real $z<1$, $(1-z)f(z)\to a_0\ne0$, so $\abs{f(z)}\to\infty$ and $1$ is a singularity.

:::
