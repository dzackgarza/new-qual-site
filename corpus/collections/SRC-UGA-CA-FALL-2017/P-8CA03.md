::: {.problem}
Let $\{f_k(z)\}$, $k=1,2,3,\ldots$, be a sequence of analytic functions in a star-shaped domain $D\subset\CC$.
Suppose that the sequence of partial sums
$$
F_n(z):=\sum_{k=1}^n f_k(z),\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots
$$
is uniformly convergent in $D$ to the limit function $F(z)$.
Prove that $F(z)$ is analytic in $D$.
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is the exam paper (`assets/attachments/Complex_Analysis_Fall_2017.docx`, with its PDF rendering) and no solutions sheet accompanies it, so the argument below is the card's own. It uses three standard facts, each named where it is applied: that a finite sum of holomorphic functions is holomorphic, that the integral of a holomorphic function over the boundary of a triangle contained in its domain vanishes, and Morera's theorem.

Throughout, $D\subset\CC$ is a star-shaped domain, in particular open, the functions $f_{k}$ are analytic on $D$, and $F_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}$ converges to $F$ uniformly on $D$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #partial-sums}
Each $F_{n}$ is holomorphic on $D$.

::: pf-proof
$F_{n}$ is a finite sum of the holomorphic functions $f_{1},\dots,f_{n}$, and finite sums of holomorphic functions are holomorphic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #continuous}
$F$ is continuous on $D$.

::: pf-proof
Each $F_{n}$ is continuous, being holomorphic, and $F_{n}\to F$ uniformly on $D$; a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #triangle}
For every closed solid triangle $T$ contained in $D$, with $\partial T$ its boundary traversed once,
$$
\int_{\partial T}F(z)\,dz=0.
$$

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #each-vanishes}
$\int_{\partial T}F_{n}(z)\,dz=0$ for every $n$.

::: pf-proof
$F_{n}$ is holomorphic on the open set $D$, and $T$ together with its interior and boundary is a compact subset of $D$ when $T$ is contained in $D$; the triangle theorem for holomorphic functions gives a zero integral over $\partial T$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #interchange}
$\int_{\partial T}F_{n}\to\int_{\partial T}F$.

::: pf-proof
$\big|\int_{\partial T}F-\int_{\partial T}F_{n}\big|\leq\int_{\partial T}\abs{F-F_{n}}\leq\operatorname{length}(\partial T)\cdot\sup_{z\in\partial T}\abs{F(z)-F_{n}(z)}$, and the supremum over $\partial T\subset D$ is at most the supremum over $D$, which tends to $0$ by uniform convergence; the length of $\partial T$ is a fixed finite number.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The integral vanishes.

::: pf-proof
A sequence of complex numbers all equal to $0$ has limit $0$, so step [](#each-vanishes){.pf-ref} and step [](#interchange){.pf-ref} together give $\int_{\partial T}F=0$.
:::

:::

::::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore $F$ is analytic in $D$.

::: pf-proof
$F$ is continuous on the open set $D$ by step [](#continuous){.pf-ref}, and its integral over the boundary of every closed triangle inside $D$ vanishes by step [](#triangle){.pf-ref}; these are exactly the hypotheses of Morera's theorem, which concludes that $F$ is holomorphic, that is analytic, on $D$. The star-shaped hypothesis is not used beyond the openness of $D$ and the connectedness that makes ``domain'' the setting: uniform convergence on $D$ alone, with $D$ open, suffices for the argument.
:::
