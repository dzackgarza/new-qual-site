::: problem
Let $\{f_k(z)\}$, $k=1,2,3,\ldots$, be a sequence of analytic functions in a star-shaped domain $D\subset\mathbb{C}$.
Suppose that the sequence of partial sums
\[
F_n(z):=\sum_{k=1}^n f_k(z),\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots
\]
is uniformly convergent in $D$ to the limit function $F(z)$.
Prove that $F(z)$ is analytic in $D$.
:::

::: solution
Each partial sum $F_n$ is holomorphic. Uniform convergence on $D$ implies
uniform convergence on every triangle contained in $D$. Let $T\Subset D$ be
such a triangle. Then
\[
\int_{\partial T}F(z)\,dz
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\partial T}F_n(z)\,dz
=0,
\]
because each $F_n$ is holomorphic. The uniform limit $F$ is continuous, so
Morera's theorem implies that $F$ is holomorphic on $D$.

The star-shaped hypothesis is stronger than needed for this argument; openness
of the domain and local uniform convergence suffice.
:::
