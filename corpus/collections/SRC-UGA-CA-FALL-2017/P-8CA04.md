::: {.problem}
Find Laurent series about the indicated singularity for each of the following functions.
Classify the type of singularity in each case and describe the (maximal) domain of convergence of each series:

(a) $\dfrac{z-\sin z}{z^3}$, $z=0$;

(b) $\dfrac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}$, $z=-2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a}
$\dfrac{z-\sin z}{z^3}=\dfrac16-\dfrac{z^2}{120}+\dfrac{z^4}{5040}-\cdots$ for $z\ne0$, so $0$ is removable and the series converges on $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
$\sin z=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k\frac{z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}$ converges for all $z$, so
$$
z-\sin z=\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!},
$$
whose first terms are $\frac{z^3}{6}-\frac{z^5}{120}+\frac{z^7}{5040}$. Dividing by $z^3$ gives $\frac16-\frac{z^2}{120}+\frac{z^4}{5040}-\cdots$; the power series has no negative powers, so the singularity at $0$ is removable, and the extended function is entire, so the series converges on all of $\CC$ and $\CC$ is the maximal domain of convergence.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #binside}
With $w=z+2$, $\dfrac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}=\dfrac2w+\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge0}w^n$ for $0<\abs w<1$.

::: pf-proof
Partial fractions: $\frac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}=\frac{-1}{z+1}+\frac2{z+2}$ (the residues at the two simple poles are $-1$ and $2$), and $z+1=w-1$, so
$$
\frac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}=-\frac1{w-1}+\frac2w=\frac{1}{1-w}+\frac2w=\sum_{n\ge0}w^n+\frac2w
$$
for $\abs w<1$, using the geometric series. The term $\frac2w$ is the principal part, so $z=-2$ is a simple pole.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #bmaximal}
That annulus is maximal, the only singularity other than $-2$ being $-1$ at distance $1$.

::: pf-proof
The rational function has poles exactly at $-1$ and $-2$; a Laurent series about $-2$ converges on an annulus about $-2$ not meeting any other singularity, and $\abs{z+1}=\abs{w-1}$ vanishes only at $w=1$, so the annulus $0<\abs w<1$ is the largest bounded one.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #boutside}
$\dfrac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}=\dfrac1w-\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge2}w^{-n}$ for $\abs w>1$.

::: pf-proof
For $\abs w>1$, $\frac1{1-w}=\frac1w\cdot\frac1{\frac1w-1}=-\frac1w\sum_{n\ge0}w^{-n}=-\sum_{n\ge1}w^{-n}$. Adding $\frac2w$ gives $\frac2w-\frac1w-\sum_{n\ge2}w^{-n}=\frac1w-\sum_{n\ge2}w^{-n}$, valid for $\abs w>1$, where it is the expansion on the maximal annulus $\abs w>1$ around $-2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The two classifications are: (a) removable at $0$, series valid on $\CC$; (b) simple pole at $-2$, with $0<\abs{z+2}<1$ and $\abs{z+2}>1$ as the two maximal annuli.

::: pf-proof
