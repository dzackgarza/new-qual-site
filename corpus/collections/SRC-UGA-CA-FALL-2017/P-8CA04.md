::: {.problem}
Find Laurent series about the indicated singularity for each of the following functions.
Classify the type of singularity in each case and describe the (maximal) domain of convergence of each series:

(a) $\dfrac{z-\sin z}{z^3}$, $z=0$;

(b) $\dfrac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}$, $z=-2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For (a), using the Taylor series of sine,
\[
\frac{z-\sin z}{z^3}
=\frac16-\frac{z^2}{120}+\frac{z^4}{5040}-\cdots.
\]
Thus $z=0$ is removable. After filling in the removable value $1/6$, the
series is entire, so its maximal domain of convergence is all of $\mathbb C$.

For (b), put $w=z+2$. Partial fractions give
\[
\frac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}=-\frac1{z+1}+\frac2{z+2}
=-\frac1{w-1}+\frac2w.
\]
For $0<|w|<1$,
\[
-\frac1{w-1}=\frac1{1-w}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty w^n,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\frac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}=\frac2w+\sum_{n=0}^\infty w^n,\qquad 0<|w|<1.}
\]
Hence $z=-2$ is a simple pole. The nearest other singularity is $z=-1$, at
distance $1$, so this annulus is maximal.

There is also the Laurent expansion on the other maximal annulus $|w|>1$:
\[
-\frac1{w-1}=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty w^{-n},
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{\frac{z}{(z+1)(z+2)}=w^{-1}-\sum_{n=2}^\infty w^{-n},\qquad |w|>1.}
\]
:::
