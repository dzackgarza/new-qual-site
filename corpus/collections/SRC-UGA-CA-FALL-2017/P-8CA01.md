::: {.problem}
The straight lines $y=\sqrt{3}x$ and $x+y=1$ in the $z$-plane are mapped onto the $w$-plane (endowed with $(u,v)$ coordinates) by the transformation
$$
w=z^2.
$$

(a) What are the images of these lines in the $w$-plane?
Express your answer analytically, i.e., in terms of the $(u,v)$ coordinates, and sketch the images.

(b) What is the angle of intersection between the images?
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.
Write $z=x+iy$ and $w=u+iv=z^2$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #coordinates}
$u=x^2-y^2$ and $v=2xy$.

::: pf-proof
$(x+iy)^2=x^2-y^2+2ixy$, and $u,v$ are its real and imaginary parts.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #line-one}
The image of the line $y=\sqrt3x$ is the ray $\{v=-\sqrt3u,\ u\le0\}$.

::: pf-proof
On that line $u=x^2-3x^2=-2x^2$ and $v=2\sqrt3x^2$, so $u\le0$ and $v=-\sqrt3u$; as $x$ ranges over $\RR$, $x^2$ ranges over $[0,\infty)$, so $u=-2x^2$ ranges over $(-\infty,0]$ and every point of the ray occurs.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #line-two}
The image of the line $x+y=1$ is the parabola $\{v=\frac{1-u^2}{2},\ u\in\RR\}$, traced once as $x$ runs over $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
On that line $y=1-x$, so $u=x^2-(1-x)^2=2x-1$ and $v=2x(1-x)$; substituting $x=\frac{u+1}{2}$ gives $v=\frac{u+1}{2}\left(1-\frac{u+1}{2}\right)=\frac{(u+1)(1-u)}{2}=\frac{1-u^2}{2}$. The map $x\mapsto u=2x-1$ is a bijection of $\RR$, so this is the whole image, and it opens downward.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conformal}
The two lines meet at a point $z_0\ne0$, and $z\mapsto z^2$ is conformal there.

::: pf-proof
The equations $y=\sqrt3x$ and $x+y=1$ give $x(1+\sqrt3)=1$, so $z_0=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt3}(1+i\sqrt3)\ne0$. The derivative of $z^2$ is $2z\ne0$ at $z_0$, so the map is conformal there and preserves the angle between curves through $z_0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(b) The images meet at $\frac{7\pi}{12}$, whose smaller angle is $\boxed{\frac{5\pi}{12}=75^\circ}$.

::: pf-proof
The line $y=\sqrt3x$ has direction $e^{i\pi/3}$ and the line $x+y=1$ has direction $1-i$ of argument $-\frac{\pi}{4}$, so the angle between the directions is $\frac{\pi}{3}+\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac{7\pi}{12}$; as this exceeds $\frac{\pi}{2}$, the smaller angle between the lines is $\frac{5\pi}{12}$. By step [](#conformal){.pf-ref} the images of steps [](#line-one){.pf-ref} and [](#line-two){.pf-ref} meet at that same angle, that is $75^\circ$ measured as the smaller angle.
:::
