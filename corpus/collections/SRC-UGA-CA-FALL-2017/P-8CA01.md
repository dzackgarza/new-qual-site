::: problem
The straight lines $y=\sqrt{3}x$ and $x+y=1$ in the $z$-plane are mapped onto the $w$-plane (endowed with $(u,v)$ coordinates) by the transformation
\[
w=z^2.
\]

(a) What are the images of these lines in the $w$-plane?
Express your answer analytically, i.e., in terms of the $(u,v)$ coordinates, and sketch the images.

(b) What is the angle of intersection between the images?
:::

::: solution
Write $w=u+iv=z^2$, so
\[
u=x^2-y^2,\qquad v=2xy.
\]

For $y=\sqrt3\,x$,
\[
u=-2x^2,\qquad v=2\sqrt3\,x^2,
\]
so the image is the ray
\[
\boxed{v=-\sqrt3\,u,\qquad u\le0.}
\]

For $x+y=1$, write $y=1-x$. Then
\[
u=2x-1,
\qquad
v=2x(1-x).
\]
Since $x=(u+1)/2$,
\[
\boxed{v=\frac{1-u^2}{2},\qquad u\in\mathbb R.}
\]
Thus the second image is a downward-opening parabola.

The original lines meet at a nonzero point, and $z\mapsto z^2$ has nonzero
derivative there, so it preserves the angle of intersection. The lines have
arguments $\pi/3$ and $-\pi/4$, whose smaller angle is
\[
\boxed{\frac{5\pi}{12}=75^\circ}.
\]
:::
