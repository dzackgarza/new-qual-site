::: {.problem}
(a) Let $f(z)$ be analytic inside and on a circle $C$ of radius $r$ centered at $a$.
Prove that
$$
f(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f\bigl(a+re^{i\theta}\bigr)\,d\theta.
$$

(b) Let $L$ be a simple closed curve.
Suppose that $f(z)$ is analytic inside and on $L$.
Prove that the maximum value of $\abs{f(z)}$ occurs on $L$, unless $f(z)$ is a constant.
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this exercise: the card's own two arguments follow, with the substitution in (a) and the averaging argument in (b) as separate steps.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #mean-value}
(a) If $f$ is analytic inside and on the circle $C$ of radius $r$ about $a$, then $f(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(a+re^{i\theta})d\theta$.

::: pf-proof
$f(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{f(z)}{z-a}dz$ by Cauchy's integral formula. Parametrising $z=a+re^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le2\pi$, gives $dz=ire^{i\theta}d\theta$ and $z-a=re^{i\theta}$, so $\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{f(z)}{z-a}dz=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{f(a+re^{i\theta})}{re^{i\theta}}ire^{i\theta}d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(a+re^{i\theta})d\theta$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #bound}
(b) If $\abs{f}$ attains its maximum $M$ at an interior point $a$ of the region bounded by $L$, then $\abs{f}\equiv M$ on a disc around $a$.

::: pf-proof
Choose $r>0$ with $\overline{D(a,r)}$ inside $L$. By step [](#mean-value){.pf-ref} and the triangle inequality, $M=\abs{f(a)}\le\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\abs{f(a+re^{i\theta})}d\theta\le M$, so equality holds throughout: the continuous function $M-\abs{f(a+re^{i\theta})}$ is nonnegative with zero integral, hence vanishes for all $\theta$. As $r$ was arbitrary, $\abs{f}=M$ on a neighbourhood of $a$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #constant}
If $\abs{f}$ attains its maximum at an interior point, then $f$ is constant.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#bound){.pf-ref} the set $E=\theset{z:\abs{f(z)}=M}$ is open in the region; it is also closed there, being the preimage of $\{M\}$ under the continuous function $\abs{f}$. A nonempty subset of a region that is both open and closed is the whole region, so $\abs{f}=M$ everywhere; then $f$ maps the region into the circle $\abs{w}=M$, which has empty interior, so by the open mapping theorem $f$ is constant.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
By step [](#constant){.pf-ref} a non-constant $f$ analytic on and inside $L$ cannot attain $\abs{f}$'s maximum inside $L$; since $L\cup(\text{interior})$ is compact and $\abs{f}$ continuous, the maximum is attained, and therefore at a point of $L$. This is (b).
:::
