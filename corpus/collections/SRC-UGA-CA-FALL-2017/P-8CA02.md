::: {.problem}
(a) Let $f(z)$ be analytic inside and on a circle $C$ of radius $r$ centered at $a$.
Prove that
\[
f(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f\bigl(a+re^{i\theta}\bigr)\,d\theta.
\]

(b) Let $L$ be a simple closed curve.
Suppose that $f(z)$ is analytic inside and on $L$.
Prove that the maximum value of $|f(z)|$ occurs on $L$, unless $f(z)$ is a constant.
:::

::: {.solution}
For (a), Cauchy's integral formula gives
\[
f(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_C\frac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz.
\]
With $z=a+re^{i\theta}$ and $dz=ire^{i\theta}d\theta$, this becomes
\[
\boxed{f(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(a+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.}
\]

For (b), let $M$ be the maximum of $|f|$ on the compact region consisting of
$L$ and its interior. If the maximum occurs at an interior point $a$, choose a
small circle centered at $a$ lying in the interior. By (a),
\[
f(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(a+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.
\]
Taking absolute values and using $|f|\le M=|f(a)|$ forces equality throughout
the triangle inequality. Hence $f$ has the constant value $f(a)$ on that
circle, and the identity theorem then makes $f$ constant on the whole region.
Therefore, unless $f$ is constant, its maximum modulus is attained on $L$.
:::
