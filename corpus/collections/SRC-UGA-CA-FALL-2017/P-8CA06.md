::: problem
Let $\mathbb{E}\subset\mathbb{C}$ denote the open unit disk.
Let $f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{E}$ be an analytic function which is not identically zero in some neighborhood of the origin.
Prove that:

(a) There exists a positive integer $m$ such that
\[
f(0)=f'(0)=\cdots=f^{(m-1)}(0)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad f^{(m)}(0)\neq 0.
\]

(b) For this number $m$, the following hold for all $z\in\mathbb{E}$:
\[
|f(z)|\le|z|^m\qquad\text{and}\qquad |f^{(m)}(0)|\le m!.
\]
:::

::: solution
The statement is false as printed because it does not assume $f(0)=0$. For
example, $f\equiv1/2$ satisfies the stated hypotheses but no positive integer
$m$ can satisfy part (a).

With the missing hypothesis $f(0)=0$, the result is the generalized Schwarz
lemma. Since $f$ is not identically zero near the origin, its zero at $0$ has
some finite order $m\ge1$:
\[
f(z)=z^m g(z),
\qquad g(0)\ne0.
\]
Schwarz's lemma applied to $f$ gives $|f(z)|\le|z|$. Thus $f(z)/z$ extends
holomorphically across $0$ and has modulus at most $1$ on $\DD$ (apply the
maximum principle on disks of radius tending to $1$). Repeating this argument
$m$ times gives
\[
|g(z)|\le1,
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{|f(z)|\le|z|^m}.
\]
Since $f^{(m)}(0)=m!g(0)$, we also obtain
\[
\boxed{|f^{(m)}(0)|\le m!}.
\]
:::
