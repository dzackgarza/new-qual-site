::: {.problem}
Prove that every group of order $30$ has a cyclic subgroup of order $15$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be the group. For primes $p\mid30$, let $n_p$ be the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$. Then $n_p\equiv1\pmod p$ and $n_p\mid30/p$. In particular $n_3$ is $1$ or $10$, and $n_5$ is $1$ or $6$. There are $(p-1)n_p$ elements of exact order 3 in $G$. If $n_3=10$ and $n_5=6$, then $(3-1)n_3+(5-1)n_5=44>\#G$, so either $n_3=1$ or $n_5=1$.

Suppose $n_3=1$. Then there is a unique Sylow $3$-subgroup $P$, and it is normal. Let $g$ be an element of order $5$ in $G$. Conjugation by $g$ restricts to an automorphism of $P$ of order dividing $5$, but $\#\Aut P=\#(\ZZ/3\ZZ)^*=2$, so this automorphism is trivial. Thus $g$ commutes with every element of $P$. Hence the group generated by $g$ and $P$ is isomorphic to $\ZZ/3\ZZ\times\ZZ/5\ZZ\simeq\ZZ/15\ZZ$.

If instead $n_5=1$, the same argument with $3$ and $5$ reversed works, since $3$ does not divide $\#(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^*=4$.
:::
