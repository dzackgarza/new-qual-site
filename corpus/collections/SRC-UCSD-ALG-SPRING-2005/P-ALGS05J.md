::: problem
Classify all rings with identity elements that have nine elements.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $R$ be a ring with identity and $|R|=9$.
Its characteristic is either $3$ or $9$.
::: {.proof}
The characteristic is the additive order of $1_R$, so it divides the order of the additive group $R^+$, namely $9$.
Since $1_R\neq0$, the characteristic is not $1$, hence is $3$ or $9$.
:::

<1>2. If $\operatorname{char}R=9$, then $R\cong\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}$.
::: {.proof}
The element $1_R$ has additive order $9$, so it generates the entire additive group.
Thus every element of $R$ has the form $n1_R$, and the canonical unital homomorphism
\[
\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow R,
\qquad \bar n\longmapsto n1_R,
\]
is bijective.
Hence it is a ring isomorphism.
:::

<1>3. Suppose $\operatorname{char}R=3$.
Then $R$ is a $2$-dimensional algebra over $\mathbb{F}_3$.
::: {.proof}
The prime subring generated by $1_R$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{F}_3$ and lies in the center of $R$.
Therefore $R$ is naturally an $\mathbb{F}_3$-vector space.
Since $|R|=9=3^2$, its dimension is $2$.
:::

<1>4. Choose $x\in R\setminus\mathbb{F}_3\cdot1$.
Then $R=\mathbb{F}_3[ x ]$, and there are $a,b\in\mathbb{F}_3$ such that
\[
x^2=a+bx.
\]
Hence
\[
R\cong\mathbb{F}_3[t]/(t^2-bt-a).
\]
::: {.proof}
The elements $1,x$ form an $\mathbb{F}_3$-basis of $R$, so $x^2=a+bx$ for unique $a,b\in\mathbb{F}_3$.
The evaluation homomorphism $\mathbb{F}_3[t]\to R$, $t\mapsto x$, is surjective.
Its kernel contains the monic quadratic $q(t)=t^2-bt-a$.
The quotient by $(q)$ has $3^2=9$ elements, the same number as $R$, so the induced surjection is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>5. Up to ring isomorphism, there are exactly three possibilities in characteristic $3$, according to the factorization type of the quadratic $q$.
::: {.proof}
A monic quadratic over $\mathbb{F}_3$ is either irreducible, a product of two distinct linear factors, or the square of a linear factor.

If $q$ is irreducible, then $\mathbb{F}_3[t]/(q)$ is the field $\mathbb{F}_9$.

If $q=(t-r)(t-s)$ with $r\neq s$, the Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
\mathbb{F}_3[t]/(q)\cong\mathbb{F}_3\times\mathbb{F}_3.
\]

If $q=(t-r)^2$, replacing $t-r$ by $\varepsilon$ gives
\[
\mathbb{F}_3[t]/(q)\cong\mathbb{F}_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
These three rings are pairwise nonisomorphic: the first is a field, the second has nontrivial idempotents and no nonzero nilpotents, and the third has a nonzero nilpotent.
:::

<1>6. Therefore the rings with identity and nine elements, up to isomorphism, are
\[
\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z},\qquad
\mathbb{F}_9,\qquad
\mathbb{F}_3\times\mathbb{F}_3,\qquad
\mathbb{F}_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine the characteristic-$9$ case with the three characteristic-$3$ cases from <1>5. The characteristic-$9$ ring cannot be isomorphic to any characteristic-$3$ ring.
:::
