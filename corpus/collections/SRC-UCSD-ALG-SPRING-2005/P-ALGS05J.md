::: problem
Classify all rings with identity elements that have nine elements.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $R$ be a ring with identity and $|R|=9$. Its additive group is isomorphic either to $C_9$ or to $C_3\times C_3$.
::: {.proof}
These are the two abelian groups of order $9$.
:::

<1>2. If $\operatorname{char}R=9$, then
\[
R\cong\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
The subring generated by $1_R$ is the image of the canonical homomorphism
\[
\mathbb Z\longrightarrow R,
\qquad
n\longmapsto n1_R.
\]
If $\operatorname{char}R=9$, this image has $9$ elements. Since $R$ itself has $9$ elements, the image is all of $R$, giving the asserted isomorphism.
:::

<1>3. If $\operatorname{char}R=3$, then $R$ is a two-dimensional algebra over $\mathbb F_3$.
::: {.proof}
The prime subring generated by $1_R$ is $\mathbb F_3$. Since the additive group has order $3^2$, it is a two-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb F_3$.
:::

<1>4. In the characteristic-$3$ case, choose $x\in R\setminus\mathbb F_3 1$. Then $R=\mathbb F_3[x]$ and
\[
R\cong\mathbb F_3[t]/(q(t))
\]
for a monic quadratic polynomial $q$.
::: {.proof}
The vectors $1,x$ are linearly independent, hence form an $\mathbb F_3$-basis of the two-dimensional vector space $R$. Thus every element of $R$ is of the form $a+bx$, so $R=\mathbb F_3[x]$.

Because $1,x$ is a basis, there exist $a,b\in\mathbb F_3$ with
\[
x^2=ax+b.
\]
Hence the evaluation map $\mathbb F_3[t]\to R$ is surjective and its kernel contains the monic quadratic
\[
q(t)=t^2-at-b.
\]
The quotient by $(q)$ has $3^2=9$ elements, the same number as $R$, so the induced surjection
\[
\mathbb F_3[t]/(q)\longrightarrow R
\]
is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>5. If $q$ is irreducible, then
\[
R\cong\mathbb F_9.
\]
::: {.proof}
The quotient of $\mathbb F_3[t]$ by an irreducible quadratic is a field with $3^2=9$ elements. All fields of order $9$ are isomorphic.
:::

<1>6. If $q$ splits as two distinct linear factors, then
\[
R\cong\mathbb F_3\times\mathbb F_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
q(t)=(t-a)(t-b),
\qquad a\ne b.
\]
The two linear ideals are comaximal, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
\mathbb F_3[t]/(q)
\cong
\mathbb F_3[t]/(t-a)\times\mathbb F_3[t]/(t-b)
\cong
\mathbb F_3\times\mathbb F_3.
\]
:::

<1>7. If $q$ has a repeated linear factor, then
\[
R\cong\mathbb F_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
::: {.proof}
Write $q(t)=(t-a)^2$. Replacing $t$ by $\varepsilon+a$ gives
\[
\mathbb F_3[t]/((t-a)^2)
\cong
\mathbb F_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
:::

<1>8. The four rings
\[
\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\mathbb F_9,
\qquad
\mathbb F_3\times\mathbb F_3,
\qquad
\mathbb F_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)
\]
are pairwise nonisomorphic.
::: {.proof}
The first has characteristic $9$, whereas the other three have characteristic $3$. Among the characteristic-$3$ rings, $\mathbb F_9$ is a field; $\mathbb F_3\times\mathbb F_3$ is reduced but has nontrivial idempotents; and $\mathbb F_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ has the nonzero nilpotent element $\varepsilon$. These properties are preserved by ring isomorphism.
:::

<1>9. These four rings exhaust all rings with identity having nine elements.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, the characteristic is either $9$ or $3$. The characteristic-$9$ case is <1>2. In characteristic $3$, <1>4 reduces the classification to the three possible factorization types of a quadratic polynomial, handled in <1>5--<1>7.
:::
:::
