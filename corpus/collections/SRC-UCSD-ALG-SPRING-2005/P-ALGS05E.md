::: problem
Prove that a group of order 120 is not simple.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Assume for contradiction that \(G\) is simple and \(|G|=120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5\). Then the number \(n_5\) of Sylow \(5\)-subgroups is \(6\).
::: {.proof}
Sylow's theorem gives \(n_5\equiv1\pmod5\) and \(n_5\mid24\), so \(n_5\in\{1,6\}\). Simplicity excludes \(n_5=1\), since a unique Sylow subgroup is normal.
:::

<1>2. Conjugation on the six Sylow \(5\)-subgroups gives an injective homomorphism
\[
\rho:G\hookrightarrow S_6.
\]
::: {.proof}
The conjugation action is nontrivial because there is more than one Sylow \(5\)-subgroup. Its kernel is normal in \(G\). Since \(G\) is simple, the kernel is either \(1\) or \(G\); nontriviality rules out \(G\), so the kernel is \(1\).
:::

<1>3. In fact \(\rho(G)\subseteq A_6\).
::: {.proof}
Compose \(\rho\) with the sign homomorphism \(S_6\to\{\pm1\}\cong C_2\). Its kernel is normal in \(G\). A nontrivial homomorphism from simple \(G\) to \(C_2\) would be injective, impossible because \(|G|=120>2\). Hence the sign is trivial on \(\rho(G)\), so \(\rho(G)\le A_6\).
:::

<1>4. The group \(A_6\) is perfect.
::: {.proof}
The group \(A_6\) is generated by \(3\)-cycles. It therefore suffices to show that every \(3\)-cycle lies in the commutator subgroup. One has
\[
(1\ 2\ 3)=\big[(2\ 3)(5\ 6),(1\ 2)(5\ 6)\big],
\]
where \([x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}\). Both factors lie in \(A_6\). Since all \(3\)-cycles are conjugate in \(A_6\), every \(3\)-cycle is a commutator. Thus \([A_6,A_6]=A_6\).
:::

<1>5. The subgroup \(\rho(G)\) has index \(3\) in \(A_6\), so the action of \(A_6\) on its three left cosets gives a nontrivial homomorphism
\[
\psi:A_6\longrightarrow S_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since \(|A_6|=360\) and \(|\rho(G)|=120\), the index is \(3\). The corresponding coset action is transitive on three points, hence nontrivial.
:::

<1>6. This is impossible, so \(G\) is not simple.
::: {.proof}
The image of a perfect group under any homomorphism is perfect. By <1>4, \(\psi(A_6)\) is therefore a perfect subgroup of \(S_3\). But every nontrivial subgroup of \(S_3\) has order \(2\), \(3\), or \(6\); the groups of order \(2\) and \(3\) are abelian, while \([S_3,S_3]=A_3\ne S_3\). Hence \(S_3\) has no nontrivial perfect subgroup. This contradicts <1>5.
:::
:::
