::: problem
Let $p(x) = x^3 + 3x^2 + 2$.
Find the Galois group of the splitting field of $p(x)$ over the rationals and over the field of five elements.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Over $\mathbb{Q}$, the polynomial $p(x)$ is irreducible.
::: {.proof}
A reducible cubic over a field has a root in that field.
The only possible rational roots are $\pm1,\pm2$, and
\[
p(1)=6,\qquad p(-1)=4,\qquad p(2)=22,\qquad p(-2)=6.
\]
Hence $p$ has no rational root and is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.
:::

<1>2. The discriminant of $p(x)=x^3+3x^2+2$ is $-324$.
::: {.proof}
For $x^3+bx^2+cx+d$, the discriminant is
\[
\Delta=b^2c^2-4c^3-4b^3d-27d^2+18bcd.
\]
Here $b=3$, $c=0$, and $d=2$, so
\[
\Delta=-4\cdot 3^3\cdot2-27\cdot2^2=-216-108=-324.
\]
:::

<1>3. The Galois group of the splitting field of $p$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $S_3$.
::: {.proof}
For an irreducible cubic over a field of characteristic not $2$, the Galois group is a transitive subgroup of $S_3$, hence is either $A_3$ or $S_3$; it is contained in $A_3$ exactly when the discriminant is a square in the base field.
Since $-324$ is not a square in $\mathbb{Q}$, the group is $S_3$.
:::

<1>4. Over $\mathbb{F}_5$, the polynomial $p$ is irreducible.
::: {.proof}
A cubic over a field is reducible exactly when it has a root.
Directly,
\[
p(0)=2,\quad p(1)=1,\quad p(2)=2,\quad p(3)=1,\quad p(4)=4
\]
in $\mathbb{F}_5$, so $p$ has no root in $\mathbb{F}_5$.
:::

<1>5. Its splitting field over $\mathbb{F}_5$ is $\mathbb{F}_{5^3}$.
::: {.proof}
If $\alpha$ is a root, then $[\mathbb{F}_5(\alpha):\mathbb{F}_5]=3$, so $\mathbb{F}_5(\alpha)\cong\mathbb{F}_{5^3}$.
Every irreducible polynomial of degree $3$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$ splits over $\mathbb{F}_{5^3}$, so this field is already the splitting field.
:::

<1>6. Therefore the Galois group over $\mathbb{F}_5$ is cyclic of order $3$.
::: {.proof}
The Galois group of $\mathbb{F}_{5^3}/\mathbb{F}_5$ is generated by the Frobenius automorphism $x\mapsto x^5$ and has order $3$.
Hence it is isomorphic to $C_3$.
:::
:::
