::: problem
Let $p(x) = x^3 + 3x^2 + 2$.
Find the Galois group of the splitting field of $p(x)$ over the rationals and over the field of five elements.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The polynomial $p(x)=x^3+3x^2+2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
::: {.proof}
A reducible cubic over $\mathbb Q$ has a rational root. By the rational-root theorem, the only possible rational roots are $\pm1,\pm2$. Directly,
\[
p(1)=6,\quad p(-1)=4,\quad p(2)=22,\quad p(-2)=6,
\]
so none is a root.
:::

<1>2. The discriminant of $p$ is $-324$, which is not a square in $\mathbb Q$.
::: {.proof}
For $x^3+ax^2+bx+c$, the discriminant is
\[
a^2b^2-4b^3-4a^3c-27c^2+18abc.
\]
Here $a=3$, $b=0$, and $c=2$, so
\[
\Delta=-4(3^3)(2)-27(2^2)=-216-108=-324.
\]
Since $-324<0$, it is not a square in $\mathbb Q$.
:::

<1>3. Over $\mathbb Q$, the Galois group of the splitting field of $p$ is $S_3$.
::: {.proof}
For an irreducible separable cubic over a field of characteristic different from $2$, the Galois group is a transitive subgroup of $S_3$, hence is either $A_3$ or $S_3$; it lies in $A_3$ exactly when the discriminant is a square. By <1>1 and <1>2, the discriminant is not a square, so the group is $S_3$.
:::

<1>4. The reduction of $p$ modulo $5$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_5$.
::: {.proof}
A cubic over a field is reducible exactly when it has a root. Evaluating at the five field elements gives
\[
p(0)=2,\quad p(1)=1,\quad p(2)=2,\quad p(3)=1,\quad p(4)=4
\]
in $\mathbb F_5$. Thus $p$ has no root and is irreducible.
:::

<1>5. Over $\mathbb F_5$, the splitting field is $\mathbb F_{5^3}$ and its Galois group is cyclic of order $3$.
::: {.proof}
If $\alpha$ is a root, irreducibility gives
\[
[\mathbb F_5(\alpha):\mathbb F_5]=3,
\]
so $\mathbb F_5(\alpha)\cong\mathbb F_{5^3}$. Every irreducible polynomial over a finite field is separable, and every root of an irreducible cubic over $\mathbb F_5$ lies in $\mathbb F_{5^3}$. Hence this field is the splitting field. Its Galois group is generated by Frobenius
\[
x\longmapsto x^5
\]
and has order $3$, so it is isomorphic to $C_3$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(p/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Gal}(p/\mathbb F_5)\cong C_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>3 and <1>5.
:::
:::
