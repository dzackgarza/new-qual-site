::: {.problem}
(a) State Tutte's theorem concerning graphs having a $1$-factor.

(b) Prove that every connected, bridgeless, $3$-regular graph has a $1$-factor.

(c) Find a connected $3$-regular graph with no $1$-factor.
:::

::: {.solution}

## Part (a): Tutte's theorem

::: pf

::: pf-step

A $1$-factor of a finite graph is a spanning $1$-regular subgraph.


::: pf-proof

Equivalently, a $1$-factor is a perfect matching: a set of pairwise
disjoint edges meeting every vertex exactly once.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Tutte's $1$-factor theorem states:
$$
\boxed{
G\text{ has a }1\text{-factor}
\iff
o(G-S)\le \cardinality{S}
\text{ for every }S\subseteq V(G)},
$$
where $o(G-S)$ denotes the number of odd-order connected components of
$G-S$.


::: pf-proof

This is the statement of Tutte's theorem requested in part (a).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
S\subseteq V(G)
$$
be arbitrary.


::: pf-proof

To apply Tutte's theorem, it suffices to verify the odd-component inequality
for every $S$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
C
$$
be an odd-order connected component of $G-S$.


::: pf-proof

We analyze the edge cut from $C$ to $S$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
\delta(C)
$$
denote the set of edges with one endpoint in $C$ and the other in
$V(G)-C$.


::: pf-proof

Since $C$ is a component of $G-S$, every edge leaving $C$ has its
other endpoint in $S$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The degree-sum formula on the vertices of $C$ gives
$$
\boxed{
3\cardinality{V(C)}
=
2\cardinality{E(C)}+\cardinality{\delta(C)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every internal edge of $C$ contributes $2$ to the sum of degrees of
vertices in $C$, while every edge of $\delta(C)$ contributes $1$.
Since $G$ is $3$-regular, the total degree sum over $C$ is
$3\cardinality{V(C)}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Since $\cardinality{V(C)}$ is odd,
$$
\boxed{
\cardinality{\delta(C)}
\text{ is odd}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The left side
$$
3\cardinality{V(C)}
$$
of step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is odd. The term
$$
2\cardinality{E(C)}
$$
is even. Therefore the remaining term $\cardinality{\delta(C)}$ must be odd.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

One cannot have
$$
\cardinality{\delta(C)}=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

If exactly one edge joined $C$ to the rest of $G$, deleting that edge
would disconnect $C$ from the remainder of the connected graph $G$.
That edge would be a bridge, contradicting the hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore every odd component $C$ of $G-S$ satisfies
$$
\boxed{
\cardinality{\delta(C)}\ge3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, the cut size is a positive odd integer. Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} rules out
the value $1$, so the smallest possibility is $3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Let
$$
q=o(G-S).
$$
Then the odd components contribute at least
$$
\boxed{3q}
$$
edges from $G-S$ to $S$.


::: pf-proof

Each of the $q$ odd components has at least three boundary edges by step
[](#s1-9){.pf-ref}. Distinct components have disjoint sets of such edges.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

At most
$$
\boxed{3\cardinality{S}}
$$
edges can run from $G-S$ to $S$.


::: pf-proof

Every vertex of $S$ has degree $3$, so the total number of incidences of
edges at vertices of $S$ is $3\cardinality{S}$. Edges from $G-S$ to $S$ use one
such incidence each.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Hence
$$
3q\le3\cardinality{S}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compare the lower bound from step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} with the upper bound from step
[](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
o(G-S)\le\cardinality{S}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Divide step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} by $3$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $S$ was arbitrary, Tutte's condition holds for $G$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} applies to every subset $S\subseteq V(G)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
G\text{ has a }1\text{-factor}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply Tutte's theorem from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Start with three disjoint copies
$$
K_4^{(1)},K_4^{(2)},K_4^{(3)}
$$
of $K_4$.


::: pf-proof

Each $K_4$ is $3$-regular on four vertices.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In each copy, choose one edge and subdivide it by inserting a new
vertex $x_i$.


::: pf-proof

Replacing an edge $uv$ by
$$
u-x_i-v
$$
leaves $u$ and $v$ of degree $3$, while the new vertex $x_i$ has
degree $2$. Each resulting component has $5$ vertices.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Add one new vertex $c$, and add the three edges
$$
cx_1,\qquad cx_2,\qquad cx_3.
$$


::: pf-proof

This joins the three subdivided $K_4$'s through the central vertex $c$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

The resulting graph $H$ is connected.


::: pf-proof

Each subdivided $K_4$ is connected, and each is joined to $c$ by the edge
$cx_i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

The graph $H$ is $3$-regular.


::: pf-proof

Every original $K_4$ vertex still has degree $3$. Each new subdivision
vertex $x_i$ has two internal incident edges plus the edge $cx_i$, so
degree $3$. The central vertex $c$ has exactly the three incident edges
$cx_1,cx_2,cx_3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

Deleting $c$ leaves exactly three connected components.


::: pf-proof

The three subdivided copies of $K_4$ become disconnected from one another
when their only common attachment point $c$ is removed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

Each component of
$$
H-\{c\}
$$
has $5$ vertices, hence odd order.


::: pf-proof

Each component is a copy of $K_4$ with one subdivided edge, which has
$$
4+1=5
$$
vertices.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-23}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
o(H-\{c\})=3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are three components by step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref}, and all three are odd by step
[](#s1-22){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For
$$
S=\{c\},
$$
one has
$$
o(H-S)=3>\cardinality{S}=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Use step [](#s1-23){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-25}

Hence $H$ has no $1$-factor.


::: pf-proof

The Tutte condition from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} fails for the subset $S=\{c\}$.
Therefore $H$ has no perfect matching.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-26}

Thus $H$ is a connected $3$-regular graph with no $1$-factor.


::: pf-proof

Connectedness is step [](#s1-19){.pf-ref}, cubic regularity is step [](#s1-20){.pf-ref}, and failure of
a $1$-factor is step [](#s1-25){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::
