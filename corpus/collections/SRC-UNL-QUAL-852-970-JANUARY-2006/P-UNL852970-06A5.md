::: {.problem}
(a) State Tutte's theorem concerning graphs having a $1$-factor.

(b) Prove that every connected, bridgeless, $3$-regular graph has a $1$-factor.

(c) Find a connected $3$-regular graph with no $1$-factor.
:::

::: {.solution}
## Part (a): Tutte's theorem

<1>1. A \(1\)-factor of a finite graph is a spanning \(1\)-regular subgraph.

::: {.proof}
Equivalently, a \(1\)-factor is a perfect matching: a set of pairwise
disjoint edges meeting every vertex exactly once.
:::

<1>2. Tutte's \(1\)-factor theorem states:
\[
\boxed{
G\text{ has a \(1\)-factor}
\iff
o(G-S)\le |S|
\text{ for every }S\subseteq V(G)},
\]
where \(o(G-S)\) denotes the number of odd-order connected components of
\(G-S\).

::: {.proof}
This is the statement of Tutte's theorem requested in part (a).
:::

## Part (b): cubic bridgeless graphs

Let \(G\) be connected, bridgeless, and \(3\)-regular.

<1>3. Let
\[
S\subseteq V(G)
\]
be arbitrary.

::: {.proof}
To apply Tutte's theorem, it suffices to verify the odd-component inequality
for every \(S\).
:::

<1>4. Let
\[
C
\]
be an odd-order connected component of \(G-S\).

::: {.proof}
We analyze the edge cut from \(C\) to \(S\).
:::

<1>5. Let
\[
\delta(C)
\]
denote the set of edges with one endpoint in \(C\) and the other in
\(V(G)-C\).

::: {.proof}
Since \(C\) is a component of \(G-S\), every edge leaving \(C\) has its
other endpoint in \(S\).
:::

<1>6. The degree-sum formula on the vertices of \(C\) gives
\[
\boxed{
3|V(C)|
=
2|E(C)|+|\delta(C)|}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Every internal edge of \(C\) contributes \(2\) to the sum of degrees of
vertices in \(C\), while every edge of \(\delta(C)\) contributes \(1\).
Since \(G\) is \(3\)-regular, the total degree sum over \(C\) is
\(3|V(C)|\).
:::

<1>7. Since \(|V(C)|\) is odd,
\[
\boxed{
|\delta(C)|
\text{ is odd}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The left side
\[
3|V(C)|
\]
of step <1>6 is odd. The term
\[
2|E(C)|
\]
is even. Therefore the remaining term \(|\delta(C)|\) must be odd.
:::

<1>8. One cannot have
\[
|\delta(C)|=1.
\]

::: {.proof}
If exactly one edge joined \(C\) to the rest of \(G\), deleting that edge
would disconnect \(C\) from the remainder of the connected graph \(G\).
That edge would be a bridge, contradicting the hypothesis.
:::

<1>9. Therefore every odd component \(C\) of \(G-S\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
|\delta(C)|\ge3}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>7, the cut size is a positive odd integer. Step <1>8 rules out
the value \(1\), so the smallest possibility is \(3\).
:::

<1>10. Let
\[
q=o(G-S).
\]
Then the odd components contribute at least
\[
\boxed{3q}
\]
edges from \(G-S\) to \(S\).

::: {.proof}
Each of the \(q\) odd components has at least three boundary edges by step
<1>9. Distinct components have disjoint sets of such edges.
:::

<1>11. At most
\[
\boxed{3|S|}
\]
edges can run from \(G-S\) to \(S\).

::: {.proof}
Every vertex of \(S\) has degree \(3\), so the total number of incidences of
edges at vertices of \(S\) is \(3|S|\). Edges from \(G-S\) to \(S\) use one
such incidence each.
:::

<1>12. Hence
\[
3q\le3|S|.
\]

::: {.proof}
Compare the lower bound from step <1>10 with the upper bound from step
<1>11.
:::

<1>13. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
o(G-S)\le|S|}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Divide step <1>12 by \(3\).
:::

<1>14. Since \(S\) was arbitrary, Tutte's condition holds for \(G\).

::: {.proof}
Step <1>13 applies to every subset \(S\subseteq V(G)\).
:::

<1>15. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
G\text{ has a \(1\)-factor}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply Tutte's theorem from step <1>2.
:::

## Part (c): a cubic connected graph with no \(1\)-factor

<1>16. Start with three disjoint copies
\[
K_4^{(1)},K_4^{(2)},K_4^{(3)}
\]
of \(K_4\).

::: {.proof}
Each \(K_4\) is \(3\)-regular on four vertices.
:::

<1>17. In each copy, choose one edge and subdivide it by inserting a new
vertex \(x_i\).

::: {.proof}
Replacing an edge \(uv\) by
\[
u-x_i-v
\]
leaves \(u\) and \(v\) of degree \(3\), while the new vertex \(x_i\) has
degree \(2\). Each resulting component has \(5\) vertices.
:::

<1>18. Add one new vertex \(c\), and add the three edges
\[
cx_1,\qquad cx_2,\qquad cx_3.
\]

::: {.proof}
This joins the three subdivided \(K_4\)'s through the central vertex \(c\).
:::

<1>19. The resulting graph \(H\) is connected.

::: {.proof}
Each subdivided \(K_4\) is connected, and each is joined to \(c\) by the edge
\(cx_i\).
:::

<1>20. The graph \(H\) is \(3\)-regular.

::: {.proof}
Every original \(K_4\) vertex still has degree \(3\). Each new subdivision
vertex \(x_i\) has two internal incident edges plus the edge \(cx_i\), so
degree \(3\). The central vertex \(c\) has exactly the three incident edges
\(cx_1,cx_2,cx_3\).
:::

<1>21. Deleting \(c\) leaves exactly three connected components.

::: {.proof}
The three subdivided copies of \(K_4\) become disconnected from one another
when their only common attachment point \(c\) is removed.
:::

<1>22. Each component of
\[
H-\{c\}
\]
has \(5\) vertices, hence odd order.

::: {.proof}
Each component is a copy of \(K_4\) with one subdivided edge, which has
\[
4+1=5
\]
vertices.
:::

<1>23. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
o(H-\{c\})=3}.
\]

::: {.proof}
There are three components by step <1>21, and all three are odd by step
<1>22.
:::

<1>24. For
\[
S=\{c\},
\]
one has
\[
o(H-S)=3>|S|=1.
\]

::: {.proof}
Use step <1>23.
:::

<1>25. Hence \(H\) has no \(1\)-factor.

::: {.proof}
The Tutte condition from step <1>2 fails for the subset \(S=\{c\}\).
Therefore \(H\) has no perfect matching.
:::

<1>26. Thus \(H\) is a connected \(3\)-regular graph with no \(1\)-factor.

::: {.proof}
Connectedness is step <1>19, cubic regularity is step <1>20, and failure of
a \(1\)-factor is step <1>25.
:::

<1>27. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 answers part (a), step <1>15 proves part (b), and step <1>26 gives
the example requested in part (c).
:::
:::
