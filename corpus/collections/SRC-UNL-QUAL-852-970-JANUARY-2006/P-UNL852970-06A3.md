::: {.problem}
Prove that every planar graph $G$ satisfies $\chi(G)\le 5$.
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove the theorem by induction on the number of vertices.

<1>1. Every simple planar graph with \(n\ge3\) vertices and \(m\) edges satisfies
\[
\boxed{m\le 3n-6}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Fix a planar embedding. If the graph is connected, Euler's formula gives
\[
n-m+f=2.
\]
Every face has boundary length at least \(3\), and each edge is counted
twice among the face boundaries, so
\[
3f\le2m.
\]
Thus
\[
2=n-m+f
\le
n-m+\frac{2m}{3}
=
n-\frac m3,
\]
hence
\[
m\le3n-6.
\]

If the graph is disconnected, add planar edges between components until it
becomes connected without adding vertices; applying the connected case to
the larger planar graph gives the same inequality for the original graph.
:::

<1>2. Therefore every planar graph has a vertex of degree at most \(5\).

::: {.proof}
If every vertex had degree at least \(6\), then the handshaking lemma would
give
\[
2m
=
\sum_v\deg(v)
\ge
6n,
\]
so
\[
m\ge3n,
\]
contradicting step <1>1 for \(n\ge3\). Graphs with fewer than three vertices
are trivial.
:::

<1>3. The theorem holds for graphs with at most five vertices.

::: {.proof}
Any graph on at most five vertices is colorable with at most five colors by
assigning a different color to each vertex.
:::

<1>4. Assume inductively that every planar graph with fewer than \(n\)
vertices is \(5\)-colorable, and let \(G\) be planar with \(n\) vertices.

::: {.proof}
This is the induction hypothesis and induction step.
:::

<1>5. Choose a vertex
\[
v\in V(G)
\]
with
\[
\boxed{\deg(v)\le5}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>2.
:::

<1>6. The graph
\[
G-v
\]
is planar and has \(n-1\) vertices, so it has a proper coloring with colors
\[
\{1,2,3,4,5\}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Deleting a vertex preserves planarity. Apply the induction hypothesis.
:::

<1>7. If the neighbors of \(v\) use at most four colors, the coloring extends
to \(G\).

::: {.proof}
Choose one of the five colors not used by any neighbor of \(v\), and assign
that color to \(v\). No edge incident to \(v\) becomes monochromatic.
:::

<1>8. Hence the only remaining case is
\[
\deg(v)=5
\]
and the five neighbors of \(v\) use all five colors.

::: {.proof}
If \(\deg(v)<5\), at most four colors appear among its neighbors. If
\(\deg(v)=5\) but two neighbors share a color, again at most four colors are
used. Both cases are handled by step <1>7.
:::

<1>9. List the five neighbors in cyclic order around \(v\) in a planar
embedding:
\[
v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4,v_5.
\]
After relabeling colors, assume
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{col}(v_i)=i}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The neighbors occur in a cyclic order around \(v\). Since they use all five
colors distinctly, rename the color labels accordingly.
:::

<1>10. Consider the subgraph induced by vertices colored \(1\) or \(3\).

::: {.proof}
Every connected component of this subgraph is a Kempe component for the pair
of colors \(\{1,3\}\).
:::

<1>11. If \(v_1\) and \(v_3\) lie in different \(1\)-\(3\) Kempe
components, swap colors \(1\) and \(3\) on the component containing \(v_1\).

::: {.proof}
Swapping two colors on an entire connected component preserves properness:
every edge inside the component still joins distinct colors, and any edge
leaving the component has its other endpoint colored neither \(1\) nor
\(3\).
:::

<1>12. After the swap in step <1>11, color \(1\) is absent from the
neighbors of \(v\), so \(v\) can be colored \(1\).

::: {.proof}
The neighbor \(v_1\) changes from color \(1\) to color \(3\). The vertex
\(v_3\) remains color \(3\) because it lies in a different Kempe component.
No other neighbor had color \(1\). Hence color \(1\) is free at \(v\).
:::

<1>13. Thus we may assume that \(v_1\) and \(v_3\) lie in the same
\(1\)-\(3\) Kempe component.

::: {.proof}
Otherwise steps <1>11--<1>12 finish the coloring.
:::

<1>14. Choose a path
\[
P_{13}
\]
from \(v_1\) to \(v_3\) whose vertices use only colors \(1\) and \(3\).

::: {.proof}
Such a path exists because \(v_1\) and \(v_3\) are in the same connected
component of the \(1\)-\(3\) subgraph.
:::

<1>15. The union of \(P_{13}\) with the two edges
\[
vv_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
vv_3
\]
contains a simple closed curve \(C\) in the plane.

::: {.proof}
Choose \(P_{13}\) simple. Since \(v\) is not on \(P_{13}\), the path together
with the two radial edges from \(v\) to its endpoints forms a simple cycle
in the planar embedding.
:::

<1>16. The vertices \(v_2\) and \(v_4\) lie on opposite sides of \(C\).

::: {.proof}
The neighbors occur around \(v\) in the cyclic order
\[
v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4,v_5.
\]
The two edges \(vv_1\) and \(vv_3\) cut a sufficiently small disk around
\(v\) into two sectors. The edge \(vv_2\) starts in one sector, while
\(vv_4\) starts in the other. By the Jordan curve theorem, these sectors
lie in different complementary components of \(C\).
:::

<1>17. Therefore there is no path from \(v_2\) to \(v_4\) using only colors
\(2\) and \(4\).

::: {.proof}
Any such path would be disjoint from \(P_{13}\), because its vertices have
colors \(2\) or \(4\), while the vertices of \(P_{13}\) have colors \(1\)
or \(3\). It would also avoid \(v\), which is currently uncolored.

Thus such a path would connect opposite sides of the Jordan curve \(C\)
without meeting \(C\), impossible.
:::

<1>18. Hence \(v_2\) and \(v_4\) lie in different \(2\)-\(4\) Kempe
components.

::: {.proof}
If they were in the same component, there would be a \(2\)-\(4\) path
between them, contradicting step <1>17.
:::

<1>19. Swap colors \(2\) and \(4\) on the \(2\)-\(4\) component containing
\(v_2\).

::: {.proof}
As in step <1>11, swapping the two colors on a Kempe component preserves
properness.
:::

<1>20. After this swap, color \(2\) is absent from the neighbors of \(v\).

::: {.proof}
The neighbor \(v_2\) changes from color \(2\) to \(4\). The vertex \(v_4\)
remains color \(4\) because it lies in a different Kempe component. No other
neighbor originally had color \(2\).
:::

<1>21. Color \(v\) with color \(2\).

::: {.proof}
By step <1>20, no neighbor of \(v\) has color \(2\), so properness is
preserved.
:::

<1>22. Thus every planar graph with \(n\) vertices is \(5\)-colorable.

::: {.proof}
The coloring extends in the easy case by step <1>7. In the degree-\(5\)
case, either steps <1>11--<1>12 or steps <1>13--<1>21 extend the coloring.
:::

<1>23. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\chi(G)\le5}
\]
for every planar graph \(G\).

::: {.proof}
Induction begins at step <1>3 and closes at step <1>22.
:::

<1>24. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>23 is the five-color theorem.
:::
:::
