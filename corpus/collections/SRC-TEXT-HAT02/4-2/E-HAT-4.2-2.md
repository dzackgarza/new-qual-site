::: {.problem}
Show the action of $\pi_1(\RP^n)$ on $\pi_n(\RP^n) \approx \ZZ$ is trivial for $n$ odd and nontrivial for $n$ even.
:::

::: {.solution}
This solution is transcribed from DeMason, *M382C Full Credit HW Problems*, §4.2 Exercise 2, and each step is checked against Hatcher: the action is the deck action on $\pi_n(S^n)$ with deck group $\ZZ_2$ (Theorem 1.38, Proposition 4.1), the nontrivial deck transformation is the antipodal map, and $\pi_n(S^n)\cong\ZZ$ is the degree isomorphism of Corollary 4.25. The route and the split into the $n=1$ and $n\ge2$ cases are that solution's; the steps below add the numbered references and nothing else. A second retained set covers it: **Nicholas Proudfoot, *Math 634: Algebraic Topology I*, homework 7, §4.2 Exercise 2** (`tmp/sources/uoregon-proudfoot-634-hw7.md`). Beyond those two, Guanyu Li's set ends with §3.3 and the Stanford 215C sets cover §3.B and §3.3 exercises.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #n-equals-one}
For $n=1$ the action is trivial.

::: pf-proof
$\RP^1\cong S^1$, and $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ$ is abelian, so the action of $\pi_1$ on itself by conjugation is trivial.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #covering}
For $n\ge2$ the map $p:S^n\to\RP^n$ is the universal covering, with deck group $\ZZ_2$ generated by the antipodal map $a(x)=-x$, and $p_*:\pi_n(S^n)\to\pi_n(\RP^n)$ is an isomorphism.

::: pf-proof
$S^n$ is simply connected for $n\ge2$, so $p$ is the universal covering, and Theorem 1.38 identifies the connected coverings of $\RP^n$ with the subgroups of $\pi_1(\RP^n)=\ZZ_2$; the nontrivial deck transformation is the antipodal map, since it is the unique nontrivial covering transformation of the universal cover. Proposition 4.1 states that a covering projection induces isomorphisms on $\pi_k$ for $k\ge2$, so $\pi_n(\RP^n)\cong\pi_n(S^n)\cong\ZZ$ by Corollary 4.25.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #action-is-deck}
Under this identification the $\pi_1(\RP^n)$-action on $\pi_n(\RP^n)$ is the action of the deck group on $\pi_n(S^n)$.

::: pf-proof
An element $\gamma\in\pi_1(\RP^n)$ acts on $\pi_n(\RP^n)\cong\pi_n(S^n)$ as follows: a representative $S^n\to\RP^n$ lifts to $S^n\to S^n$ by Proposition 1.30 applied to the universal cover, and applying the deck transformation belonging to $\gamma$ gives another lift, hence another representative; the resulting class depends only on $\gamma$ and the original class, which is the action. Under the identification of step [](#covering){.pf-ref} this is the action of the deck group on $\pi_n(S^n)$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #degree}
The antipodal map $a:S^n\to S^n$ has degree $(-1)^{n+1}$ and acts on $\pi_n(S^n)\cong\ZZ$ as multiplication by that degree.

::: pf-proof
The antipodal map is the composition of the reflections in the coordinate hyperplanes, one for each of the $n+1$ coordinates, and a reflection has degree $-1$; hence $\deg a=(-1)^{n+1}$. For a self-map of $S^n$ of degree $d$, the induced endomorphism of $\pi_n(S^n)\cong\ZZ$ is multiplication by $d$, because the degree is the induced map on $H_n(S^n)\cong\ZZ$ and the Hurewicz isomorphism identifies $\pi_n(S^n)$ with $H_n(S^n)$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
The action is trivial for $n$ odd and multiplication by $-1$, hence nontrivial, for $n$ even.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#action-is-deck){.pf-ref} and [](#degree){.pf-ref} the action of the nontrivial element is multiplication by $(-1)^{n+1}$, which is $+1$ for $n$ odd and $-1$ for $n$ even. Together with step [](#n-equals-one){.pf-ref} this gives the stated description of the action.
:::
