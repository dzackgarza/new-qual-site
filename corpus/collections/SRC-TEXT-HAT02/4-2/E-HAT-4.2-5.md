Let $f: S_\alpha^2 \vee S_\beta^2 \to S_\alpha^2 \vee S_\beta^2$ be the map which is the identity on the $S_\alpha^2$ summand and which on the $S_\beta^2$ summand is the sum of the identity map and a homeomorphism $S_\beta^2 \to S_\alpha^2$.
Let $X$ be the mapping torus of $f$.
The mapping torus of the restriction of $f$ to $S_\alpha^2$ forms a subspace $A = S^1 \times S_\alpha^2 \subset X$.
Show that the maps $\pi_2(A) \to \pi_2(X) \to \pi_2(X, A)$ form a short exact sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0$, and compute the action of $\pi_1(A)$ on these three groups.
In particular, show the action of $\pi_1(A)$ is trivial on $\pi_2(A)$ and $\pi_2(X, A)$ but is nontrivial on $\pi_2(X)$.

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** For the mapping torus $X = M_f$ of the self-map $f$ of $S^2_\alpha \vee S^2_\beta$, show $\pi_2(A) \to \pi_2(X) \to \pi_2(X, A)$ is the short exact sequence $0 \to \ZZ \to \ZZ \oplus \ZZ \to \ZZ \to 0$, and compute the $\pi_1(A)$-action on the three groups.

<1>1. Identify the map $f_*$ on $\pi_2$.
<2>1. $\pi_2(S^2_\alpha \vee S^2_\beta) = \ZZ \oplus \ZZ$, generated by the classes $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of the two spheres.
::: {.proof}
the wedge of two $2$-spheres has $\pi_2$ the free abelian group on the two sphere classes.
:::
<2>2. $f_*(\alpha) = \alpha$ and $f_*(\beta) = \beta + \alpha$.
::: {.proof}
$f$ is the identity on $S^2_\alpha$, and on $S^2_\beta$ it is $\id_\beta + h$ where $h: S^2_\beta \to S^2_\alpha$ is a homeomorphism, so $f_*(\beta) = \beta + h_*(\beta) = \beta \pm \alpha$; taking $h$ of degree $+1$, $f_*(\beta) = \beta + \alpha$.
:::
<2>3. So $f_* = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ in the basis $(\alpha, \beta)$.
::: {.proof}
read off the columns from <1>2.2.
:::

<1>2. Compute $\pi_2(X)$.
<2>1. $X = M_f$ fibers over $S^1$ with fiber $F = S^2_\alpha \vee S^2_\beta$.
::: {.proof}
the mapping torus is the total space of a fibration $F \to M_f \to S^1$.
:::
<2>2. $\pi_2(X) \cong \pi_2(F) = \ZZ \oplus \ZZ$.
::: {.proof}
in the long exact sequence of the fibration, $\pi_2(S^1) = 0$ and $\pi_3(S^1) = 0$, so $\pi_2(F) \to \pi_2(X)$ is an isomorphism.
:::
<2>3. $\pi_1(X) = \ZZ$.
::: {.proof}
$\pi_1(F) = 0$ and $\pi_0(F) = 0$, so $\pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(S^1) = \ZZ$ is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>3. The subspace $A$ and its homotopy groups.
<2>1. $A = S^1 \times S^2_\alpha$ is the mapping torus of $f|_{S^2_\alpha} = \id$.
::: {.proof}
the restriction of $f$ to $S^2_\alpha$ is the identity, so its mapping torus is $S^1 \times S^2_\alpha$.
:::
<2>2. $\pi_2(A) = \pi_2(S^1 \times S^2) = \ZZ$ and $\pi_1(A) = \ZZ$.
::: {.proof}
$\pi_2(S^1 \times S^2) = \pi_2(S^2) = \ZZ$ and $\pi_1(S^1 \times S^2) = \pi_1(S^1) = \ZZ$.
:::

<1>4. The short exact sequence.
<2>1. The inclusion $A \hookrightarrow X$ induces on $\pi_2$ the map $\ZZ \to \ZZ \oplus \ZZ$ sending the generator to $\alpha$.
::: {.proof}
$A = S^1 \times S^2_\alpha$ contributes the $\alpha$ summand of $\pi_2(X)$.
:::
<2>2. This map is injective, and its cokernel is $\ZZ$.
::: {.proof}
the image is $\ZZ\alpha \subset \ZZ\alpha \oplus \ZZ\beta$, and the quotient is $\ZZ\beta \cong \ZZ$.
:::
<2>3. The boundary map $\pi_2(X, A) \to \pi_1(A)$ is zero.
::: {.proof}
$\pi_1(A) \to \pi_1(X)$ is an isomorphism (both $\ZZ$), so the boundary map in the LES of the pair is zero.
:::
<2>4. Hence $0 \to \pi_2(A) \to \pi_2(X) \to \pi_2(X, A) \to 0$ is exact, i.e. $0 \to \ZZ \to \ZZ \oplus \ZZ \to \ZZ \to 0$.
::: {.proof}
combine <1>4.1, <1>4.2, <1>4.3.
:::

<1>5. The $\pi_1(A)$-action.
<2>1. The action on $\pi_2(A) = \ZZ$ is trivial.
::: {.proof}
$A = S^1 \times S^2$ is a product, and $\pi_1(S^1)$ acts trivially on $\pi_2(S^2)$ (the loop in the $S^1$ factor does not move the $S^2$ factor).
:::
<2>2. The action on $\pi_2(X) = \ZZ \oplus \ZZ$ is nontrivial, given by $f_*$.
::: {.proof}
the generator of $\pi_1(X) = \ZZ$ acts on $\pi_2(X)$ by the monodromy $f_* = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, which sends $\beta \mapsto \beta + \alpha \neq \beta$.
:::
<2>3. The action on $\pi_2(X, A) = \ZZ$ is trivial.
::: {.proof}
$\pi_2(X, A) \cong \ZZ\beta$ (the quotient by $\ZZ\alpha$), and $f_*$ acts trivially on the quotient $\ZZ\beta$ (since $f_*(\beta) = \beta + \alpha \equiv \beta \pmod{\ZZ\alpha}$).
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>4.4 gives the short exact sequence; <1>5 gives the action (trivial on $\pi_2(A)$ and $\pi_2(X,A)$, nontrivial on $\pi_2(X)$).
:::
:::
