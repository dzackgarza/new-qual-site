::: {.problem}
Let the CW complex $X$ be obtained from $S^1 \vee S^n$, $n \geq 2$, by attaching a cell $e^{n+1}$ by a map representing the polynomial $p(t) \in \ZZ[t, t^{-1}] \approx \pi_n(S^1 \vee S^n)$, so $\pi_n(X) \approx \ZZ[t, t^{-1}] / \bigl(p(t)\bigr)$.
Show $\pi_n'(X)$ is cyclic and compute its order in terms of $p(t)$.
Give examples showing that the group $\pi_n(X)$ can be finitely generated or not, independently of whether $\pi_n'(X)$ is finite or infinite.
:::

::: {.solution}
A retained solution covers this exercise: **Steven V. Sam, *Solutions to Algebraic Topology* (2008), §4.2 Exercise 10** (`tmp/sources/hatcher-solutions-sam-2008.md`). Sam's example $p(t)=t^2+t+1$, which he calls not finitely generated, is not used below and is not correct — $\ZZ[t,t^{-1}]/(t^2+t+1)$ is free of rank two as an abelian group — and the four examples on this card are its own, as is the argument, checked against Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology* (2002), §4.2 Exercise 10.

Let $R=\ZZ[t,t^{-1}]$ and $M=\pi_n(X)\cong R/(p(t))$, which is a module over $R$ because $\pi_n(S^1\vee S^n)$ is free on one generator as a $\ZZ[t,t^{-1}]$-module.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #coinvariants}
$\pi_n'(X)\cong M/(t-1)M$.

::: pf-proof
$\pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(S^1\vee S^n)\cong\ZZ$ acts on $\pi_n(X)$ through the module structure, with the generator acting as multiplication by $t$. The group $\pi_n'(X)$ is $\pi_n(X)$ with the relations $\gamma\alpha=\alpha$ for every $\gamma\in\pi_1(X)$ imposed; it is enough to impose them for the generator, so $\pi_n'(X)$ is the quotient of $M$ by the subgroup generated by $(t-1)\alpha$ over all $\alpha\in M$, that is $M/(t-1)M$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #computation}
$M/(t-1)M\cong R/(p(t),t-1)\cong\ZZ/(p(1))$.

::: pf-proof
Quotienting the cyclic module $R/(p)$ by the submodule generated by $t-1$ gives $R/(p,t-1)$. The evaluation map $R\to\ZZ$, $t\mapsto1$, is a surjective ring homomorphism with kernel $(t-1)$, so $R/(t-1)\cong\ZZ$; under it the class of $p(t)$ goes to $p(1)$, hence $R/(p,t-1)\cong\ZZ/(p(1))$ by the third isomorphism theorem.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order}
$\pi_n'(X)$ is cyclic of order $\abs{p(1)}$ when $p(1)\ne0$, and infinite cyclic when $p(1)=0$.

::: pf-proof
A quotient $\ZZ/(m)$ is cyclic of order $\abs{m}$ for $m\ne0$, and $\ZZ/(0)=\ZZ$ is infinite cyclic, generated by the image of $1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #examples}
The four combinations of finite generation of $\pi_n(X)$ with finiteness of $\pi_n'(X)$ all occur.

::: pf-proof
With $p=t+1$ one has $t=-1$ in $M$, so $M\cong\ZZ$ is finitely generated and $\pi_n'(X)\cong\ZZ/2$ is finite. With $p=t-1$ one has $M\cong\ZZ$ finitely generated and $\pi_n'(X)\cong\ZZ$ infinite; here $p(1)=0$, and the check is that $R/(t-1)\cong\ZZ$. With $p=2t-1$ one has $t=1/2$ in $M$, so $M\cong\ZZ[1/2]$, not finitely generated as an abelian group, while $p(1)=1$ and $\pi_n'(X)=0$. With $p=0$ one has $M=R$, not finitely generated, while $p(1)=0$ and $\pi_n'(X)\cong\ZZ$ is infinite. So finite generation of $\pi_n(X)$ and finiteness of $\pi_n'(X)$ are independent, as the exercise asks.
:::
