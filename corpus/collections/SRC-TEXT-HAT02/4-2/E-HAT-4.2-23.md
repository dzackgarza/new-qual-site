::: {.problem}
Extend the Hurewicz theorem by showing that if $X$ is an $(n-1)$-connected CW complex, then the Hurewicz homomorphism $h: \pi_{n+1}(X) \to H_{n+1}(X)$ is surjective when $n > 1$, and when $n = 1$ show there is an isomorphism $H_2(X) / h\bigl(\pi_2(X)\bigr) \approx H_2\bigl(K(\pi_1(X), 1)\bigr)$. [Build a $K(\pi_n(X), n)$ from $X$ by attaching cells of dimension $n + 2$ and greater, and then consider the homology sequence of the pair $(Y, X)$ where $Y$ is $X$ with the $(n + 2)$-cells of $K(\pi_n(X), n)$ attached. Note that the image of the boundary map $H_{n+2}(Y, X) \to H_{n+1}(X)$ coincides with the image of $h$, and $H_{n+1}(Y) \approx H_{n+1}(K(\pi_n(X), n))$. The previous exercise is needed for the case $n > 1$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X$ be $(n-1)$-connected. Attach cells of dimensions at least $n+2$ to $X$ to kill $\pi_{n+1},\pi_{n+2},\dots$, obtaining a $K(\pi_nX,n)$, say $K$. The inclusion
$$
X\hookrightarrow K
$$
preserves $\pi_n$.

For $n>1$, Exercise 22 gives
$$
H_{n+1}(K)=0.
$$
In the relative cellular chain complex $C_*(K,X)$, the first possible generators occur in degree $n+2$. Hence the exact sequence contains
$$
H_{n+2}(K,X)\xrightarrow{\partial}H_{n+1}(X)\longrightarrow H_{n+1}(K)=0.
$$
Thus $\partial$ is surjective. The generators of $H_{n+2}(K,X)$ come from the attached $(n+2)$-cells, and their boundaries are precisely the Hurewicz classes of their attaching maps in $\pi_{n+1}(X)$. Consequently
$$
h:\pi_{n+1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow H_{n+1}(X)
$$
is surjective.

When $n=1$, perform the same construction to obtain $K(\pi_1X,1)$ by attaching cells of dimension at least $3$. The same relative exact sequence now gives
$$
H_2(X)/\operatorname{im}\partial\cong H_2(K(\pi_1X,1)).
$$
Again $\operatorname{im}\partial$ is exactly the subgroup generated by Hurewicz images of attaching maps in $\pi_2(X)$, hence
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial=h(\pi_2(X)).
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{H_2(X)/h(\pi_2(X))\cong H_2(K(\pi_1(X),1)).}
$$
This proves both assertions.
:::
