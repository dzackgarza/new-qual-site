::: {.problem}
By Exercise 35 in §4.2 there is a bundle $S^2 \to \CP^3 \to S^4$.
Let $S^2 \to E_k \to S^4$ be the pullback of this bundle via a degree $k$ map $S^4 \to S^4$, $k > 1$.
Use the Leray-Hirsch theorem to show that $H^*(E_k; \ZZ)$ is additively isomorphic to $H^*(\CP^3; \ZZ)$ but has a different cup product structure in which the square of a generator of $H^2(E_k; \ZZ)$ is $k$ times a generator of $H^4(E_k; \ZZ)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise, so the argument is the card's own; it is the Leray--Hirsch computation applied to the pullback of the bundle of §4.2 Exercise 35.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #pullback}
Let $p\colon\CP^3\to S^4$ be the bundle of §4.2 Exercise 35 and $f\colon S^4\to S^4$ a map of degree $k$. The pullback gives a bundle $p_k\colon E_k\to S^4$ with a map $F\colon E_k\to\CP^3$ over $f$.

::: pf-proof
The bundle $S^2\to\CP^3\to S^4$ exists by §4.2 Exercise 35 with $n=1$, since $\quatprojective^1=S^4$. The pullback $E_k=\{(e,s)\in\CP^3\times S^4:p(e)=f(s)\}$ with the two projections is a bundle with the same fibre, and the square with $F(e,s)=e$ and $p_k(e,s)=s$ commutes by construction.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #leray-hirsch}
The classes $1$ and $x$ restrict to a basis of $H^*(S^2;\ZZ)$ on each fibre, so Leray--Hirsch applies to $p_k$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x\in H^2(\CP^3;\ZZ)$ be a class restricting to a generator of $H^2$ of each fibre $S^2$; such a class exists because the bundle has a section over the $3$-skeleton, or equivalently because $H^2(\CP^3)$ is generated by the first Chern class of the tautological bundle, which is fibrewise a generator. Then $F^*x$, again written $x$, restricts to a generator on each fibre of $E_k$, since $F$ carries fibres to fibres by homeomorphisms. The cohomology $H^*(S^2;\ZZ)$ is free on $1$ and the fibre generator, both restrictions of global classes, so Theorem 4D.1 applies and $H^*(E_k;\ZZ)$ is a free $H^*(S^4;\ZZ)$-module on $1$ and $x$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #additive}
$H^*(E_k;\ZZ)\cong H^*(\CP^3;\ZZ)$ additively.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#leray-hirsch){.pf-ref}, $H^n(E_k;\ZZ)\cong\bigoplus_{i+j=n}H^i(S^4;\ZZ)\cdot\{1,x\}$, which is $\ZZ$ for $n=0,2,4,6$ and $0$ otherwise. $H^*(\CP^3;\ZZ)$ is $\ZZ$ in those same degrees, generated by $1,x,x^2,x^3$, so the two abelian groups are isomorphic in each degree.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cup-product}
In $H^*(E_k;\ZZ)$ the square of the generator $x$ of $H^2$ is $k$ times a generator of $H^4$.

::: pf-proof
Let $u$ be a generator of $H^4(S^4;\ZZ)$ and normalise the sign of $x$ in $\CP^3$ so that $x^2=p^*u$, possible because $H^4(\CP^3;\ZZ)=\ZZ\,x^2=\ZZ\,p^*u$. Put $v=p_k^*u$, a generator of $H^4(E_k;\ZZ)$ by step [](#additive){.pf-ref}. Naturality of the pullback square gives
$$
x^2=F^*(x^2)=F^*p^*u=p_k^*f^*u=k\,p_k^*u=k\,v,
$$
using $F^*x=x$ and $f^*u=ku$ for a degree-$k$ map.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\boxed{H^*(E_k;\ZZ)\cong H^*(\CP^3;\ZZ)\text{ additively, but }x^2=kv\text{ with }k>1}$.

::: pf-proof
The additive statement is step [](#additive){.pf-ref} and the product statement is step [](#cup-product){.pf-ref}. In $H^*(\CP^3;\ZZ)$ the square of the generator of $H^2$ is a generator of $H^4$, whereas here it is $k$ times a generator with $k>1$; a ring isomorphism preserves the property of an element to have a square that is a generator, so the two rings are not isomorphic although their additive groups are.
:::
