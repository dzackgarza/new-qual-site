Verify that the Hopf algebras $\Lambda_R[\alpha]$ and $\mathbb{Z}_p[\alpha]/(\alpha^p)$ are self-dual.

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Duality for the exterior Hopf algebra $\Lambda_R[\alpha]$:
<2>1. As an $R$-module, $A = \Lambda_R[\alpha]$ has basis $\{1, \alpha\}$ with $\alpha^2 = 0$.
The comultiplication is determined by the primitive generator:
\[
\Delta(1) = 1 \otimes 1, \qquad \Delta(\alpha) = \alpha \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \alpha.
\]
Proof: definition of exterior Hopf algebra on a single generator.
<2>2. Let $\{1^*, \alpha^*\}$ be the dual basis in $A^* = \operatorname{Hom}_R(A, R)$.
The product in $A^*$ is given by the dual of the coproduct $m^* = \Delta^*$:
\[
\langle \alpha^* \cdot \alpha^*, \alpha \rangle = \langle \alpha^* \otimes \alpha^*, \Delta(\alpha) \rangle = \langle \alpha^* \otimes \alpha^*, \alpha \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \alpha \rangle = 0.
\]
Since $A^*$ is trivial in degrees $> \deg(\alpha)$, $(\alpha^*)^2 = 0$, so $A^* \cong \Lambda_R[\alpha^*]$ as algebras.
Proof: duality between product and coproduct.
<2>3. The coproduct on $A^*$ is given by the dual of multiplication $\Delta^* = m^*$:
\[
\langle \Delta^*(\alpha^*), x \otimes y \rangle = \langle \alpha^*, x y \rangle \implies \Delta^*(\alpha^*) = \alpha^* \otimes 1^* + 1^* \otimes \alpha^*.
\]
Thus $\alpha \mapsto \alpha^*$ defines an isomorphism of graded Hopf algebras $\Lambda_R[\alpha] \cong (\Lambda_R[\alpha])^*$.
Proof: identification of Hopf algebra operations.

<1>2. Duality for the truncated polynomial Hopf algebra $A = \mathbb{Z}_p[\alpha]/(\alpha^p)$:
<2>1. As an $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space, $A$ has basis $\{1, \alpha, \alpha^2, \dots, \alpha^{p-1}\}$ with $\alpha^p = 0$.
Since $\alpha$ is primitive ($\Delta(\alpha) = \alpha \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \alpha$), the coproduct on basis elements is:
\[
\Delta(\alpha^k) = (\alpha \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \alpha)^k = \sum_{j=0}^k \binom{k}{j} \alpha^j \otimes \alpha^{k-j}.
\]
Proof: binomial theorem for commuting tensor factors.
<2>2. Let $\{\beta_0, \beta_1, \dots, \beta_{p-1}\}$ be the dual basis of $A^*$ with $\langle \beta_i, \alpha^j \rangle = \delta_{ij}$.
The product in $A^*$ satisfies:
\[
\langle \beta_i \beta_j, \alpha^k \rangle = \langle \beta_i \otimes \beta_j, \Delta(\alpha^k) \rangle = \binom{k}{i} \delta_{i+j, k} \implies \beta_i \beta_j = \binom{i+j}{i} \beta_{i+j} \quad (i+j < p),
\]
and $\beta_i \beta_j = 0$ for $i+j \ge p$.
Proof: dual multiplication formula.
<2>3. In particular, $\beta_1^k = k! \beta_k$ for all $0 \le k < p$.
Since $k < p$, $k! \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, so $\beta_k = \frac{\beta_1^k}{k!}$.
Furthermore, $\beta_1^p = p! \beta_p = 0$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$.
Thus $A^* \cong \mathbb{F}_p[\beta_1]/(\beta_1^p)$ as an algebra generated by $\beta_1$.
Proof: factorials are non-zero modulo $p$ for $k < p$.
<2>4. The dual coproduct on $\beta_1$ satisfies:
\[
\langle \Delta^*(\beta_1), \alpha^i \otimes \alpha^j \rangle = \langle \beta_1, \alpha^i \alpha^j \rangle = \begin{cases} 1 & (i, j) \in \{(1, 0), (0, 1)\}, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases}
\]
which gives $\Delta^*(\beta_1) = \beta_1 \otimes 1^* + 1^* \otimes \beta_1$.
Thus $\beta_1$ is primitive.
The map $\alpha \mapsto \beta_1$ defines an isomorphism of graded Hopf algebras $A \cong A^*$.
Proof: matching of generators, relations, and primitive coproducts.

<1>3. Conclusion:
Both $\Lambda_R[\alpha]$ and $\mathbb{Z}_p[\alpha]/(\alpha^p)$ are self-dual Hopf algebras. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.
