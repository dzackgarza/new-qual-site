::: {.problem}
Show that the splitting in the topological Künneth formula cannot be natural by considering the map $f \times \id: M(\ZZ_m, n) \times M(\ZZ_m, n) \to S^{n+1} \times M(\ZZ_m, n)$ where $f$ collapses the $n$-skeleton of $M(\ZZ_m, n) = S^n \cup e^{n+1}$ to a point.
:::

::: {.solution}
This is Hatcher Exercise 3.B.3. The computation and the non-naturality conclusion are in the retained Stanford Math 215C Solution Set 2 (`tmp/sources/stanford-math215c-spring2009-solution2.pdf`, printed p. 5); the argument below follows it and the card's prose.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #moore}
For $M=M(\ZZ_{m},n)=S^{n}\cup_{m}e^{n+1}$ the reduced cellular chain complex is $\ZZ\langle a\rangle\xrightarrow{m}\ZZ\langle b\rangle$ with $\abs{a}=n+1$, $\abs{b}=n$, and the quotient map $f\colon M\to M/S^{n}=S^{n+1}$ sends $a\mapsto u$ and $b\mapsto0$; hence $f_{*}=0$ on all reduced homology groups.

::: pf-proof
The cell structure has one cell in each of the dimensions $n$ and $n+1$, and the attaching map of the top cell is the degree $m$ map of $S^{n}$, so $\partial a=mb$ and $\partial b=0$. Then $H_{n}(M)=\ZZ\langle b\rangle/m\ZZ\langle b\rangle\cong\ZZ_{m}$ and $H_{n+1}(M)=0$, since $\partial a\neq0$. Collapsing the $n$-skeleton sends $b$ to the collapsed basepoint, that is to $0$, and sends the top cell homeomorphically onto the top cell of $S^{n+1}$, so $f(a)=u$. Thus $f_{*}(b)=0$ and $H_{n+1}(M)=0$, so $f_{*}$ vanishes on reduced homology in every degree.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #product}
The cellular chains of $M\times M$ in degrees $2n+2,2n+1,2n$ are $\ZZ\langle a\otimes a\rangle\to\ZZ\langle a\otimes b,b\otimes a\rangle\to\ZZ\langle b\otimes b\rangle$ with $\partial(a\otimes a)=m(b\otimes a+(-1)^{n+1}a\otimes b)$, $\partial(a\otimes b)=m(b\otimes b)$ and $\partial(b\otimes a)=(-1)^{n}m(b\otimes b)$.

::: pf-proof
The product cell structure gives these cells, and the boundary is Leibniz's rule $\partial(x\otimes y)=\partial x\otimes y+(-1)^{\abs{x}}x\otimes\partial y$ with $\partial a=mb$ and $\partial b=0$: for $a\otimes a$ one gets $mb\otimes a+(-1)^{n+1}a\otimes mb$, and for $a\otimes b$, $b\otimes a$ the two displayed values.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #tor}
$H_{2n+1}(M\times M)\cong\ZZ_{m}$, generated by the class of $g=a\otimes b+(-1)^{n+1}b\otimes a$.

::: pf-proof
$\ker\partial_{2n+1}$ consists of $x(a\otimes b)+y(b\otimes a)$ with $mx+(-1)^{n}my=0$ in $\ZZ$, that is $x=-(-1)^{n}y$; this is the infinite cyclic group generated by $g$. The image of $\partial_{2n+2}$ is generated by $m(b\otimes a+(-1)^{n+1}a\otimes b)$, which is $\pm m g$. Hence the homology is $\ZZ\langle g\rangle/m\ZZ\langle g\rangle\cong\ZZ_{m}$. This is the group $\Tor(H_{n}(M),H_{n}(M))=\Tor(\ZZ_{m},\ZZ_{m})$ of the Künneth formula.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #sphere}
$H_{2n+1}(S^{n+1}\times M)\cong\ZZ_{m}$, on the cycle $u\otimes b$.

::: pf-proof
In $S^{n+1}\times M$ the relevant chains are $\ZZ\langle u\otimes a\rangle\to\ZZ\langle u\otimes b\rangle$ with $\partial(u\otimes a)=\partial u\otimes a+(-1)^{n+1}u\otimes\partial a=(-1)^{n+1}m(u\otimes b)$, since $\partial u=0$. So the kernel is $\ZZ\langle u\otimes b\rangle$ and the image is $m$ times it, giving $\ZZ_{m}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #iso}
$(f\times\id)_{*}\colon H_{2n+1}(M\times M)\to H_{2n+1}(S^{n+1}\times M)$ is an isomorphism.

::: pf-proof
$(f\times\id)$ sends $g=a\otimes b+(-1)^{n+1}b\otimes a$ to $u\otimes b+(-1)^{n+1}f(b)\otimes a=u\otimes b$, which generates the target by step [](#sphere){.pf-ref}. A generator of the source mapping to a generator of the target of the same order is an isomorphism.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #naturality}
The splitting of the topological Künneth short exact sequence is not natural.

::: pf-proof
Were the splitting natural, the Tor summand of $H_{2n+1}(M\times M)$ would map into the Tor summand of the target by the map induced on $\Tor$ of the homology of the factors, which involves $f_{*}\colon H_{n}(M)\to H_{n}(S^{n+1})$ and hence is zero by step [](#moore){.pf-ref}. But step [](#iso){.pf-ref} shows the actual induced map is an isomorphism on these summands. So no natural splitting exists.
:::
