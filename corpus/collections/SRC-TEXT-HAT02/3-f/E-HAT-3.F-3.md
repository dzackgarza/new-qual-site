::: {.problem}
Show that $\operatorname{Ext}(A, \QQ) = 0$ for all $A$.
[Consider the homology with $\QQ$ coefficients of a Moore space $M(A, n)$.]
:::

::: {.solution}
The exercise is Exercise 3 of §3.F, printed p. 320, and the proof below follows its printed hint. All groups are $\ZZ$-modules and $\operatorname{Ext}=\operatorname{Ext}^1_\ZZ$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every abelian group $A$ and every $n\ge1$ there is a CW complex $X=M(A,n)$ with $\widetilde H_n(X)=A$ and $\widetilde H_i(X)=0$ for $i\ne n$, whose cellular chain complex is concentrated in degrees $n$ and $n+1$.

::: pf-proof

Choose a free resolution $0\to F_1\xrightarrow{d}F_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}A\to0$, which exists by the construction on printed p. 194. Take the wedge of one $n$-sphere for each element of a basis of $F_0$, and attach one $(n+1)$-cell for each element of a basis of $F_1$, the attaching map of the cell belonging to $\beta\in F_1$ being a map $S^n\to\bigvee S^n$ whose induced homomorphism $\ZZ\to H_n(\bigvee S^n)=\bigoplus_{\mathrm{basis}}F_0$ sends the generator to $d(\beta)$; such a map exists because $H_n$ of the wedge is generated by the inclusions and $n\ge1$ allows maps to be added by pinching. By Theorem 2.35 (printed p. 139) the cellular chain complex of $X$ is then $F_1\xrightarrow{d}F_0$ in degrees $n+1$ and $n$, so $\widetilde H_n(X)=\operatorname{coker}d=A$ and $\widetilde H_{n+1}(X)=\ker d=0$, with all other reduced groups zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For such an $X$, both $H_{n+1}(X;\QQ)$ and $H^{n+1}(X;\QQ)$ vanish.

::: pf-proof

Tensoring the two-term complex with $\QQ$ keeps it exact in the same two degrees, because $\QQ$ is torsion-free and hence flat, so $H_{n+1}(X;\QQ)=\ker(F_1\otimes\QQ\to F_0\otimes\QQ)=0$ and the only nonzero homology of $C_*(X;\QQ)$ is $H_n(X;\QQ)=A\otimes\QQ$. For a complex of vector spaces over a field, the cohomology of the dual complex is the dual of the homology: dualizing the short exact sequences $0\to Z_k\to C_k\to B_{k-1}\to0$ and $0\to B_k\to Z_k\to H_k\to0$, which split over a field, gives $H^k(\operatorname{Hom}_\QQ(C_*,\QQ))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_\QQ(H_k(C_*),\QQ)$. Hence $H^{n+1}(X;\QQ)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_\QQ(H_{n+1}(X;\QQ),\QQ)=0$, and this is the cohomology of $X$ with $\QQ$ coefficients because $C^*(X;\QQ)=\operatorname{Hom}(C_*(X),\QQ)=\operatorname{Hom}_\QQ(C_*(X;\QQ),\QQ)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$\operatorname{Ext}(A,\QQ)=0$.

::: pf-proof

The universal coefficient sequence of Theorem 3.2 (printed p. 195) in degree $n+1$, for the complex $X$ of the first step and with $H_n(X)=A$ and $H_{n+1}(X)=0$, is
$$
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}(A,\QQ)\longrightarrow H^{n+1}(X;\QQ)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(H_{n+1}(X),\QQ)\longrightarrow0,
$$
and the middle group is zero, so $\operatorname{Ext}(A,\QQ)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The same vanishing follows algebraically, from the divisibility of $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

$\QQ$ is divisible, $n(q/n)=q$ for $q\in\QQ$ and $n\ne0$, so it is an injective $\ZZ$-module by Baer's criterion; hence $\operatorname{Hom}(-,\QQ)$ carries the free resolution of $A$ to a short exact sequence
$$
0\to\operatorname{Hom}(A,\QQ)\to\operatorname{Hom}(F_0,\QQ)\xrightarrow{d^*}\operatorname{Hom}(F_1,\QQ)\to0,
$$
and $\operatorname{Ext}(A,\QQ)=\operatorname{coker}d^*=0$. Baer's criterion is the one step of this route that Hatcher does not print; the Moore-space route above uses only Hatcher's Theorem 3.2 and the construction of p. 194.

:::
