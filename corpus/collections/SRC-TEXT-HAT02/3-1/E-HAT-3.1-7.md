# E-HAT-3.1-7

Show that the functors $h^n(X) = \operatorname{Hom}(H_n(X), \mathbb{Z})$ do not define a cohomology theory on the category of CW complexes.

::: {.solution}
The functors
\[
h^n(X)=\operatorname{Hom}(H_n(X),\mathbb Z)
\]
fail the exactness axiom.

<1>1. Let $X=M(\mathbb Z_m,1)$ be obtained from $S^1=A$ by attaching a $2$-cell by a map of degree $m>1$.
Then
\[
H_1(A)=\mathbb Z,\qquad H_1(X)=\mathbb Z_m,\qquad H_2(X,A)=\mathbb Z.
\]
The boundary map
\[
\partial:H_2(X,A)\to H_1(A)
\]
is multiplication by $m$.
::: {.proof}
The relative group is generated by the attached $2$-cell, and its relative boundary is the attaching map, which has degree $m$ on the circle.
:::

<1>2. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-,\mathbb Z)$ gives
\[
h^1(X)=0,\qquad h^1(A)=\mathbb Z,\qquad h^2(X,A)=\mathbb Z,\qquad h^2(X)=0,
\]
and the map induced by $\partial$ is multiplication by $m$:
\[
\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\,m\,}\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
One has $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_m,\mathbb Z)=0$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$. Contravariance sends multiplication by $m$ to multiplication by $m$.
:::

<1>3. If the $h^n$ formed a cohomology theory, the long exact sequence of the pair would contain
\[
0=h^1(X)\longrightarrow h^1(A)=\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\,m\,}h^2(X,A)=\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow h^2(X)=0,
\]
which would have to be exact.
::: {.proof}
Exactness at $h^2(X,A)$ would require multiplication by $m$ to be surjective, which it is not for $m>1$.
:::

Hence these functors do not define a cohomology theory.
:::
