::: {.problem}
For $n>0$, show that if $f: S^n \to S^n$ has degree $d$ then $f^*: H^n(S^n; G) \to H^n(S^n; G)$ is multiplication by $d$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $n>0$ one has $H_n(S^n)\cong\ZZ$, generated by the fundamental class $[S^n]$ (§2.2), and Theorem 3.2 gives
$$
H^n(S^n;G)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_n(S^n),G)\cong G,
$$
because $\operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1}(S^n),G)$ vanishes: $H_{n-1}(S^n)$ is $0$ for $n\ge2$ and $\ZZ$ for $n=1$, both free. The exercise is Exercise 9 of §3.1, and it needs $n>0$, since $H^0(S^0;G)\cong G^2$ and the conclusion fails there.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Evaluation on the fundamental class, $\alpha\mapsto\langle\alpha,[S^n]\rangle$, is an isomorphism $H^n(S^n;G)\to G$.

::: pf-proof

By Theorem 3.2 and the vanishing of the Ext term, an element of $H^n(S^n;G)$ is a homomorphism $H_n(S^n)\to G$, and a homomorphism out of $\ZZ$ is determined by its value on the generator $[S^n]$, which is exactly $\langle\alpha,[S^n]\rangle$. So the evaluation map is the identity map of $\operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ,G)=G$ under this identification, in particular bijective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For a map $f\colon S^n\to S^n$, a cocycle $\varphi$ and a cycle $\sigma$, the cochain formula $(f^*\varphi)(\sigma)=\varphi(f_\#\sigma)$ gives $\langle f^*\alpha,\sigma\rangle=\langle\alpha,f_\#\sigma\rangle$, and this passes to homology classes $\alpha$, $\sigma$.

::: pf-proof

$(f^*\varphi)(\sigma)=\varphi(f_\#\sigma)$ is the definition of the cochain map dual to $f_\#$. If $\varphi$ is replaced by a cohomologous cocycle the difference is $\delta\psi$, and $(\delta\psi)(\sigma)=\psi(\partial\sigma)=0$ for a cycle $\sigma$; if $\sigma$ is replaced by a homologous cycle the difference is a boundary $\partial\tau$ and $\varphi(\partial\tau)=(\delta\varphi)(\tau)=0$ for a cocycle $\varphi$. Hence the pairing of classes is well defined and the formula holds for them.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$f_*[S^n]=d[S^n]$, where $d$ is the degree of $f$.

::: pf-proof

This is the definition of degree in §2.2: $H_n(S^n)$ is infinite cyclic, so $f_*$ is multiplication by an integer on it, and that integer is $d$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

$f^*\alpha=d\alpha$ for every $\alpha\in H^n(S^n;G)$.

::: pf-proof

By the naturality of the pairing and the definition of degree,
$$
\langle f^*\alpha,[S^n]\rangle=\langle\alpha,f_*[S^n]\rangle=\langle\alpha,d[S^n]\rangle=d\langle\alpha,[S^n]\rangle=\langle d\alpha,[S^n]\rangle .
$$
Since evaluation on $[S^n]$ is injective, $f^*\alpha=d\alpha$.

:::
