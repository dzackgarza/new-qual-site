::: {.problem}
From the fact that $\operatorname{Tor}(A, B) = 0$ if $A$ is free, deduce that $\operatorname{Tor}(A, B) = 0$ if $A$ is torsionfree by applying the previous problem to the directed system of finitely generated subgroups $A_\alpha$ of $A$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the torsionfree $A$ as the directed union of its finitely generated subgroups; each of those is finitely generated and torsionfree, hence free, so Tor with $B$ vanishes on it by the exercise's hypothesis, and the preceding problem of the section (Tor commutes with direct limits in the first variable, Proposition 3A.5(3) of printed p.~265) carries the vanishing to $A$. Hatcher prints a complete proof of the torsionfree case on printed pp.~265--266, so the conclusion of this exercise is published; the route along the printed hint is this card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #union}
$A=\varinjlim_\alpha A_\alpha$, the direct limit running over the finitely generated subgroups $A_\alpha$ of $A$ ordered by inclusion, and that index set is directed.

::: pf-proof
Every element of $A$ lies in the finitely generated subgroup it generates, so $A$ is the union of its finitely generated subgroups. The index set is directed: for subgroups generated by finite sets $S_\alpha$ and $S_\beta$, the subgroup generated by $S_\alpha\cup S_\beta$ is finitely generated and contains both. A directed union of this kind is the direct limit of the system of inclusions.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #free}
Each $A_\alpha$ is finitely generated and torsionfree, hence free abelian.

::: pf-proof
$A_\alpha$ is a subgroup of the torsionfree $A$, hence torsionfree. By the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups, $A_\alpha\cong\ZZ^r\oplus\ZZ_{n_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\ZZ_{n_k}$ with $n_i\ge2$; each finite summand is a nontrivial torsion subgroup, which torsionfreeness forbids, so $k=0$ and $A_\alpha\cong\ZZ^r$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #vanish}
$\operatorname{Tor}(A_\alpha,B)=0$ for every $\alpha$.

::: pf-proof
This is the hypothesis of the exercise — $\operatorname{Tor}$ of a free group with anything vanishes — which holds because a free resolution $0\to F_1\to F_0\to A_\alpha\to0$ has $F_1=0$ when $A_\alpha$ is free, so the tensor complex with $B$ is exact in positive degrees.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #limit}
$\operatorname{Tor}(A,B)\cong\varinjlim_\alpha\operatorname{Tor}(A_\alpha,B)=0$.

::: pf-proof
The preceding problem of this section is that $\operatorname{Tor}$ commutes with direct limits in its first variable, because $-\otimes B$ does and homology of a filtered colimit of complexes is the colimit of the homologies. Applied to step [](#union){.pf-ref} it gives the isomorphism, and the colimit of trivial groups is trivial by step [](#vanish){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #printed}
The book proves the same statement outright in §3.A, and that proof is recorded here.

::: pf-proof
Let $0\to F_1\xrightarrow{\varphi}F_0\to A\to0$ be a free resolution and $B$ torsionfree; we show $\varphi\otimes1_B$ is injective. An element $x\in F_1\otimes B$ is a finite sum $\sum_if_i\otimes b_i$, so it lies in the image of $F_1\otimes B_0$ for the subgroup $B_0$ generated by the finitely many $b_i$, which is finitely generated and torsionfree, hence free. Since $B_0$ is a subgroup of the torsionfree $B$, the quotient $B/B_0$ is torsionfree, so $\operatorname{Tor}(F_1,B/B_0)=0$ because $F_1$ is free, and the sequences $0\to F_i\otimes B_0\to F_i\otimes B\to F_i\otimes(B/B_0)\to0$ are exact for $i=0,1$. From the diagram of complexes it follows that $x=0$ in $F_1\otimes B$ whenever $x$ maps to $0$ in $F_1\otimes B_0$ and $x$ is a cycle: that is, $\ker(\varphi\otimes1_B)$ injects into $\ker(\varphi\otimes1_{B_0})$, which is $\operatorname{Tor}(A,B_0)=0$ because $B_0$ is free. So the kernel is zero.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
$\operatorname{Tor}(A,B)=0$ for every torsionfree $A$ and every $B$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#limit){.pf-ref} is the derivation the exercise asks for, from the free case via the direct-limit property of the preceding problem; step [](#printed){.pf-ref} records that the book proves the same statement directly on printed pp.~265--266. Only torsionfreeness of $A$ and the free case are used.
:::
