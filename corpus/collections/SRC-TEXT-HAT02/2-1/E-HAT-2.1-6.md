::: {.problem}
Compute the simplicial homology groups of the $\Delta$-complex obtained from $n+1$ 2 simplices $\Delta_0^2, \cdots, \Delta_n^2$ by identifying all three edges of $\Delta_0^2$ to a single edge, and for $i > 0$ identifying the edges $[v_0, v_1]$ and $[v_1, v_2]$ of $\Delta_i^2$ to a single edge and the edge $[v_0, v_2]$ to the edge $[v_0, v_1]$ of $\Delta_{i-1}^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\sigma_i$ denote the image of $\Delta_i^2$ and let $e_i$ denote the common image of the two edges $[v_0,v_1]$ and $[v_1,v_2]$ of $\Delta_i^2$ for $i>0$. For $i=0$, let $e_0$ be the common image of all three edges.

All vertices are identified, so
$$
C_0\cong\ZZ,\qquad C_1\cong\ZZ^{n+1},\qquad C_2\cong\ZZ^{n+1}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map $\partial_1$ is zero.

::: pf-proof

Every edge begins and ends at the unique vertex of the quotient.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

With the ordered bases $\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_n$ and $e_0,\dots,e_n$,
$$
\partial_2(\sigma_0)=e_0,
\qquad
\partial_2(\sigma_i)=2e_i-e_{i-1}\quad(i>0).
$$

::: pf-proof

For $\sigma_0$ the three oriented faces are all identified with $e_0$, preserving the ordering of vertices, hence
$$
\partial_2(\sigma_0)=e_0-e_0+e_0=e_0.
$$
For $i>0$, the first and third faces are both $e_i$, while the middle face is $e_{i-1}$, so
$$
\partial_2(\sigma_i)=e_i-e_{i-1}+e_i=2e_i-e_{i-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The map $\partial_2$ is injective.

::: pf-proof

Its matrix is triangular after ordering the bases as above, with diagonal entries
$$
1,2,2,\dots,2.
$$
Its determinant is therefore $2^n\neq0$, so it is injective over $\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The cokernel of $\partial_2$ is cyclic of order $2^n$.

::: pf-proof

The relations in the cokernel are
$$
e_0=0,
\qquad
2e_i=e_{i-1}\quad(1\le i\le n).
$$
Hence
$$
e_{n-k}=2^k e_n,
$$
and in particular
$$
2^n e_n=e_0=0.
$$
Conversely, all $e_i$ are generated by $e_n$, and the determinant computation in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that the cokernel has order $2^n$. Thus
$$
\operatorname{coker}\partial_2\cong\ZZ/2^n\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_0(X)\cong\ZZ,\qquad
H_1(X)\cong\ZZ/2^n\ZZ,\qquad
H_k(X)=0\ (k\ge2).
}
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $\partial_1=0$, one has
$$
H_1=C_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2\cong\operatorname{coker}\partial_2.
$$
Injectivity of $\partial_2$ gives $H_2=0$, and there are no simplices in higher dimensions. The quotient is connected, so $H_0\cong\ZZ$.

:::
