::: {.problem}
Compute the simplicial homology groups of the $\Delta$-complex obtained from $n+1$ 2 simplices $\Delta_0^2, \cdots, \Delta_n^2$ by identifying all three edges of $\Delta_0^2$ to a single edge, and for $i > 0$ identifying the edges $[\nu_0, \nu_1]$ and $[\nu_1, \nu_2]$ of $\Delta_i^2$ to a single edge and the edge $[\nu_0, \nu_2]$ to the edge $[\nu_0, \nu_1]$ of $\Delta_{i-1}^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\sigma_i$ denote the image of $\Delta_i^2$ and let $e_i$ denote the common image of the two edges $[\nu_0,\nu_1]$ and $[\nu_1,\nu_2]$ of $\Delta_i^2$ for $i>0$. For $i=0$, let $e_0$ be the common image of all three edges.

All vertices are identified, so
\[
C_0\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad C_1\cong\mathbb Z^{n+1},\qquad C_2\cong\mathbb Z^{n+1}.
\]

<1>1. The map $\partial_1$ is zero.
::: {.proof}
Every edge begins and ends at the unique vertex of the quotient.
:::

<1>2. With the ordered bases $\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_n$ and $e_0,\dots,e_n$,
\[
\partial_2(\sigma_0)=e_0,
\qquad
\partial_2(\sigma_i)=2e_i-e_{i-1}\quad(i>0).
\]
::: {.proof}
For $\sigma_0$ the three oriented faces are all identified with $e_0$, preserving the ordering of vertices, hence
\[
\partial_2(\sigma_0)=e_0-e_0+e_0=e_0.
\]
For $i>0$, the first and third faces are both $e_i$, while the middle face is $e_{i-1}$, so
\[
\partial_2(\sigma_i)=e_i-e_{i-1}+e_i=2e_i-e_{i-1}.
\]
:::

<1>3. The map $\partial_2$ is injective.
::: {.proof}
Its matrix is triangular after ordering the bases as above, with diagonal entries
\[
1,2,2,\dots,2.
\]
Its determinant is therefore $2^n\neq0$, so it is injective over $\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>4. The cokernel of $\partial_2$ is cyclic of order $2^n$.
::: {.proof}
The relations in the cokernel are
\[
e_0=0,
\qquad
2e_i=e_{i-1}\quad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Hence
\[
e_{n-k}=2^k e_n,
\]
and in particular
\[
2^n e_n=e_0=0.
\]
Conversely, all $e_i$ are generated by $e_n$, and the determinant computation in <1>3 shows that the cokernel has order $2^n$. Thus
\[
\operatorname{coker}\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z/2^n\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad
H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z/2^n\mathbb Z,\qquad
H_k(X)=0\ (k\ge2).
}
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\partial_1=0$, one has
\[
H_1=C_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2\cong\operatorname{coker}\partial_2.
\]
Injectivity of $\partial_2$ gives $H_2=0$, and there are no simplices in higher dimensions. The quotient is connected, so $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::
