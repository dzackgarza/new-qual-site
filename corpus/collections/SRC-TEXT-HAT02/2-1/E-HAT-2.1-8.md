Construct a 3 dimensional $\Delta$-complex $X$ from $n$ tetrahedra $T_1, \cdots, T_n$ by the following two steps.
First arrange the tetrahedra in a cyclic pattern as in the figure, so that each $T_i$ shares a common vertical face with its two neighbors $T_{i-1}$ and $T_{i+1}$, subscripts being taken mod $n$.
Then identify the bottom face of $T_i$ with the top face of $T_{i+1}$ for each $i$.
Show the simplicial homology groups of $X$ in dimensions 0, 1, 2, 3 are $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}_n$, $0$, $\mathbb{Z}$, respectively.
[The space $X$ is an example of a lens space; see Example 2.43 for the general case.]

::: {.solution}
Use the notation in Hatcher's figure. After all identifications there are two vertices $v,w$, $n+2$ edges, $2n$ faces, and $n$ tetrahedra. Choose generators
\[
p,b,f_1,\dots,f_n\in C_1,
\qquad
A_1,\dots,A_n,B_1,\dots,B_n\in C_2,
\qquad
T_1,\dots,T_n\in C_3,
\]
with subscripts modulo $n$.

<1>1. The boundary map $\partial_1:C_1\to C_0$ has rank $1$.
::: {.proof}
The edges $p$ and $b$ are loops, while each $f_i$ joins $v$ to $w$. Thus
\[
\partial_1(p)=\partial_1(b)=0,
\qquad
\partial_1(f_i)=w-v.
\]
Hence $\operatorname{im}\partial_1=\mathbb Z(w-v)$ has rank one. Therefore
\[
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>2. The boundaries of the tetrahedra can be oriented so that
\[
\partial_3(T_i)=A_i-A_{i-1}+B_{i-1}-B_i.
\]
Consequently $\ker\partial_3$ is generated by
\[
T_1+\cdots+T_n.
\]
::: {.proof}
The displayed formula follows directly by taking the alternating sum of the four oriented faces of $T_i$ and applying the prescribed face identifications. If
\[
\sum_i c_i\partial_3(T_i)=0,
\]
then the coefficient of $A_i$ is $c_i-c_{i+1}$, hence all $c_i$ are equal. Conversely, the sum of all $\partial_3(T_i)$ telescopes to zero. Thus
\[
\ker\partial_3\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Since there are no $4$-simplices,
\[
H_3(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>3. After elementary integral row and column operations, the matrix of $\partial_2:C_2\to C_1$ has Smith normal form with nonzero diagonal entries
\[
1,\dots,1,n,
\]
where there are $n$ entries equal to $1$ and one entry equal to $n$.
::: {.proof}
For the chosen orientations, the $A_i$- and $B_i$-boundaries are
\[
\partial_2(A_i)=p+f_i-f_{i+1},
\qquad
\partial_2(B_i)=b+f_i-f_{i+1},
\]
with indices modulo $n$.
Subtract the $A_i$-columns successively from $A_{i+1}$ and likewise for the $B_i$-columns. The difference columns isolate the $f_i-f_{i+1}$ relations. Next subtract one $A$-column from the corresponding $B$-column to isolate $b-p$. These operations split off $n$ unit invariant factors. The sole remaining relation is the sum of the cyclic differences, which contributes the invariant factor $n$. Equivalently, the quotient of $\ker\partial_1$ by $\operatorname{im}\partial_2$ is generated by one class with relation $n$ times that class equal to zero.
:::

<1>4. Hence
\[
H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\ker\partial_1$ has rank
\[
(n+2)-1=n+1,
\]
and the Smith form in <1>3 has rank $n+1$, the quotient has no free part. Its only nontrivial invariant factor is $n$, giving
\[
H_1(X)=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>5. One has
\[
H_2(X)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Write a general $2$-chain as
\[
z=\sum_i a_iA_i+\sum_i b_iB_i.
\]
Using the formulas from <1>3, the coefficients of $p$ and $b$ in $\partial_2z$ are respectively
\[
\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
\sum_i b_i,
\]
and the coefficient of $f_i$ is
\[
(a_i+b_i)-(a_{i-1}+b_{i-1}).
\]
Hence $\partial_2z=0$ exactly when all $a_i+b_i$ are equal and both displayed sums vanish. If the common value is $c$, then
\[
0=\sum_i(a_i+b_i)=nc,
\]
so $c=0$ in $\mathbb Z$. Thus $b_i=-a_i$ for all $i$ and $\sum_i a_i=0$.

Therefore $\ker\partial_2$ consists precisely of the chains
\[
\sum_i a_i(A_i-B_i)
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\sum_i a_i=0.
\]
This group is generated by the differences
\[
(A_i-B_i)-(A_{i-1}-B_{i-1})
=\partial_3(T_i),
\]
using <1>2. Hence
\[
\ker\partial_2=\operatorname{im}\partial_3,
\]
so $H_2(X)=0$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z,
\quad
H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,
\quad
H_2(X)=0,
\quad
H_3(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
}
\]
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>1--<1>5.
:::
