::: {.problem}
Determine whether there exists a short exact sequence $0 \to \ZZ_4 \to \ZZ_8 \oplus \ZZ_2 \to \ZZ_4 \to 0$.
More generally, determine which abelian groups $A$ fit into a short exact sequence $0 \to \ZZ_{p^m} \to A \to \ZZ_{p^n} \to 0$ with $p$ prime.
What about the case of short exact sequences $0 \to \ZZ \to A \to \ZZ_n \to 0$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

There is a short exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow\ZZ_4
\longrightarrow\ZZ_8\oplus\ZZ_2
\longrightarrow\ZZ_4
\longrightarrow0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Define
$$
i(1)=(2,1)
$$
and
$$
q(a,b)=a-2b\pmod4.
$$
The element $(2,1)$ has order $4$, so $i$ is injective. The map $q$ is surjective since $q(1,0)=1$. Its kernel has order
$$
\frac{\cardinality{\ZZ_8\oplus\ZZ_2}}{4}=4
$$
and contains the order-$4$ subgroup generated by $(2,1)$, hence
$$
\ker q=\operatorname{im}i.
$$

Now fix a prime $p$ and positive integers $m,n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Every extension
$$
0\to\ZZ_{p^m}\to A\to\ZZ_{p^n}\to0
$$
has a presentation
$$
A\cong
\left\langle x,y\ \presentationbar\ p^m x=0,\ p^n y=cx\right\rangle
$$
for some integer $c$, well-defined modulo $p^m$ up to the changes arising from replacing the chosen lift $y$.

::: pf-proof

Let $x$ be the image of a generator of the kernel. Choose $y\in A$ mapping to a generator of the quotient. Since the quotient class of $y$ has order $p^n$, one has
$$
p^n y=cx
$$
for some $c$. The kernel is generated by $x$ with relation $p^m x=0$, and $x,y$ generate $A$, giving the presentation.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Put
$$
r=\min\{v_p(c),m,n\},
$$
with $v_p(0)=\infty$. Then
$$
A\cong
\ZZ_{p^r}\oplus\ZZ_{p^{m+n-r}},
$$
where $\ZZ_{p^0}$ denotes the trivial group.

::: pf-proof

The relation matrix for the presentation in step [](#s2){.pf-ref} is
$$
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
p^m&0\\-c&p^n\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The product of the Smith invariant factors is
$$
\abs{\det M}=p^{m+n}.
$$
The first invariant factor is the gcd of all entries of $M$, namely
$$
p^{\min(m,n,v_p(c))}=p^r.
$$
Hence the second invariant factor is $p^{m+n-r}$, giving the stated decomposition.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Conversely, every value
$$
0\le r\le\min(m,n)
$$
occurs. Thus the possible middle groups are exactly
$$
\boxed{
\ZZ_{p^r}\oplus\ZZ_{p^{m+n-r}}
\qquad(0\le r\le\min(m,n)).
}
$$

::: pf-proof

For $r<\min(m,n)$ use the presentation
$$
\langle x,y\mid p^m x=0,\ p^n y=p^r x\rangle.
$$
For $r=\min(m,n)$ take $c=0$. In every case the subgroup generated by $x$ has order exactly $p^m$: if $kx$ lies in the relation subgroup in $\ZZ x\oplus\ZZ y$, comparison of $y$-coordinates forces the coefficient of the second relation to be zero, so $p^m\mid k$. The quotient by $\langle x\rangle$ is generated by the class of $y$ with exact order $p^n$. Hence these presentations really give the required short exact sequences.

For the final question consider
$$
0\to\ZZ\to A\to\ZZ_n\to0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every such $A$ has a presentation
$$
A\cong\langle x,y\mid ny=cx\rangle
$$
for some $c\in\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

Choose $x$ generating the infinite cyclic kernel and choose a lift $y$ of a generator of the quotient. Then $ny$ lies in the kernel, so $ny=cx$. As before, $x$ and $y$ generate $A$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If
$$
d=\gcd(c,n),
$$
then
$$
A\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ_d.
$$
Consequently the possible middle groups are exactly
$$
\boxed{\ZZ\oplus\ZZ_d\qquad(d\mid n),}
$$
with $\ZZ_1=0$.

::: pf-proof

The single relation vector is $(-c,n)$. Smith normal form replaces it by $(d,0)$, so
$$
\ZZ^2/\langle(-c,n)\rangle
\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ_d.
$$
Every divisor $d$ of $n$ occurs by taking $c=d$ (and $d=n$ can also be represented by $c=0$). The element $x$ remains of infinite order, since a relation killing a nonzero multiple of $x$ would have to have zero $y$-coordinate and hence zero coefficient of $(-c,n)$. Thus each presentation gives the required extension.

:::
