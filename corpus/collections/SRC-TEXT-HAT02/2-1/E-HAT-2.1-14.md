::: {.problem}
Determine whether there exists a short exact sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Z}_4 \to \mathbb{Z}_8 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_2 \to \mathbb{Z}_4 \to 0$.
More generally, determine which abelian groups $A$ fit into a short exact sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Z}_{p^m} \to A \to \mathbb{Z}_{p^n} \to 0$ with $p$ prime.
What about the case of short exact sequences $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to A \to \mathbb{Z}_n \to 0$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. There is a short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_4
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_8\oplus\mathbb Z_2
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_4
\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Define
\[
i(1)=(2,1)
\]
and
\[
q(a,b)=a-2b\pmod4.
\]
The element $(2,1)$ has order $4$, so $i$ is injective. The map $q$ is surjective since $q(1,0)=1$. Its kernel has order
\[
\frac{|\mathbb Z_8\oplus\mathbb Z_2|}{4}=4
\]
and contains the order-$4$ subgroup generated by $(2,1)$, hence
\[
\ker q=\operatorname{im}i.
\]
:::

Now fix a prime $p$ and positive integers $m,n$.

<1>2. Every extension
\[
0\to\mathbb Z_{p^m}\to A\to\mathbb Z_{p^n}\to0
\]
has a presentation
\[
A\cong
\left\langle x,y\ \middle|\ p^m x=0,\ p^n y=cx\right\rangle
\]
for some integer $c$, well-defined modulo $p^m$ up to the changes arising from replacing the chosen lift $y$.
::: {.proof}
Let $x$ be the image of a generator of the kernel. Choose $y\in A$ mapping to a generator of the quotient. Since the quotient class of $y$ has order $p^n$, one has
\[
p^n y=cx
\]
for some $c$. The kernel is generated by $x$ with relation $p^m x=0$, and $x,y$ generate $A$, giving the presentation.
:::

<1>3. Put
\[
r=\min\{v_p(c),m,n\},
\]
with $v_p(0)=\infty$. Then
\[
A\cong
\mathbb Z_{p^r}\oplus\mathbb Z_{p^{m+n-r}},
\]
where $\mathbb Z_{p^0}$ denotes the trivial group.
::: {.proof}
The relation matrix for the presentation in <1>2 is
\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
p^m&0\\-c&p^n\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The product of the Smith invariant factors is
\[
|\det M|=p^{m+n}.
\]
The first invariant factor is the gcd of all entries of $M$, namely
\[
p^{\min(m,n,v_p(c))}=p^r.
\]
Hence the second invariant factor is $p^{m+n-r}$, giving the stated decomposition.
:::

<1>4. Conversely, every value
\[
0\le r\le\min(m,n)
\]
occurs. Thus the possible middle groups are exactly
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb Z_{p^r}\oplus\mathbb Z_{p^{m+n-r}}
\qquad(0\le r\le\min(m,n)).
}
\]
::: {.proof}
For $r<\min(m,n)$ use the presentation
\[
\langle x,y\mid p^m x=0,\ p^n y=p^r x\rangle.
\]
For $r=\min(m,n)$ take $c=0$. In every case the subgroup generated by $x$ has order exactly $p^m$: if $kx$ lies in the relation subgroup in $\mathbb Zx\oplus\mathbb Zy$, comparison of $y$-coordinates forces the coefficient of the second relation to be zero, so $p^m\mid k$. The quotient by $\langle x\rangle$ is generated by the class of $y$ with exact order $p^n$. Hence these presentations really give the required short exact sequences.
:::

For the final question consider
\[
0\to\mathbb Z\to A\to\mathbb Z_n\to0.
\]

<1>5. Every such $A$ has a presentation
\[
A\cong\langle x,y\mid ny=cx\rangle
\]
for some $c\in\mathbb Z$.
::: {.proof}
Choose $x$ generating the infinite cyclic kernel and choose a lift $y$ of a generator of the quotient. Then $ny$ lies in the kernel, so $ny=cx$. As before, $x$ and $y$ generate $A$.
:::

<1>6. If
\[
d=\gcd(c,n),
\]
then
\[
A\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z_d.
\]
Consequently the possible middle groups are exactly
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z_d\qquad(d\mid n),}
\]
with $\mathbb Z_1=0$.
::: {.proof}
The single relation vector is $(-c,n)$. Smith normal form replaces it by $(d,0)$, so
\[
\mathbb Z^2/\langle(-c,n)\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z_d.
\]
Every divisor $d$ of $n$ occurs by taking $c=d$ (and $d=n$ can also be represented by $c=0$). The element $x$ remains of infinite order, since a relation killing a nonzero multiple of $x$ would have to have zero $y$-coordinate and hence zero coefficient of $(-c,n)$. Thus each presentation gives the required extension.
:::
