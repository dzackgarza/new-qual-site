::: {.problem}
Prove by induction on dimension the following facts about the homology of a finite-dimensional CW complex $X$, using the observation that $X^n/X^{n-1}$ is a wedge sum of $n$ spheres:

(a) If $X$ has dimension $n$ then $H_i(X) = 0$ for $i > n$ and $H_n(X)$ is free.

(b) $H_n(X)$ is free with basis in bijective correspondence with the $n$-cells if there are no cells of dimension $n-1$ or $n+1$.

(c) If $X$ has $k$ $n$-cells, then $H_n(X)$ is generated by at most $k$ elements.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X^r$ denote the $r$-skeleton. Since
\[
X^r/X^{r-1}\cong\bigvee_{\alpha\in I_r}S^r,
\]
where $I_r$ indexes the $r$-cells, one has
\[
H_i(X^r,X^{r-1})\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{(I_r)},&i=r,\\
0,&i\ne r.
\end{cases}
\]

<1>1. If $X$ has dimension $n$, then $H_i(X)=0$ for $i>n$.
::: {.proof}
Induct on $n$. The assertion is immediate for a $0$-complex. Assume it for $(n-1)$-complexes and apply the long exact sequence of $(X,X^{n-1})$. For $i>n$ both
\[
H_i(X,X^{n-1})=0
\]
and $H_i(X^{n-1})=0$ by induction. Exactness gives $H_i(X)=0$.
:::

<1>2. If $X$ has dimension $n$, then $H_n(X)$ is free abelian.
::: {.proof}
The relevant part of the long exact sequence is
\[
0=H_n(X^{n-1})\longrightarrow H_n(X)
\longrightarrow H_n(X,X^{n-1})\cong\mathbb Z^{(I_n)}.
\]
Hence $H_n(X)$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of a free abelian group. Every subgroup of a free abelian group is free abelian, so $H_n(X)$ is free.
:::

<1>3. If there are no $(n-1)$-cells, then
\[
H_n(X^n)\cong\mathbb Z^{(I_n)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Now $X^{n-1}=X^{n-2}$ has dimension at most $n-2$, so by <1>1
\[
H_n(X^{n-1})=H_{n-1}(X^{n-1})=0.
\]
The long exact sequence of $(X^n,X^{n-1})$ therefore gives
\[
H_n(X^n)\cong H_n(X^n,X^{n-1})\cong\mathbb Z^{(I_n)}.
\]
The standard relative generators correspond bijectively to the $n$-cells.
:::

<1>4. If in addition there are no $(n+1)$-cells, then the inclusion $X^n\hookrightarrow X$ induces an isomorphism on $H_n$.
::: {.proof}
Passing from $X^n$ to $X$ attaches cells only in dimensions at least $n+2$. For each $r\ge n+2$, the relative group
\[
H_i(X^r,X^{r-1})
\]
vanishes for $i=n,n+1$. The long exact sequence therefore shows inductively that attaching these cells does not change $H_n$. Hence
\[
H_n(X)\cong H_n(X^n)\cong\mathbb Z^{(I_n)}.
\]
This proves part (b).
:::

<1>5. If $X$ has $k$ $n$-cells, then $H_n(X)$ is generated by at most $k$ elements.
::: {.proof}
By the argument of <1>2,
\[
H_n(X^n)\hookrightarrow H_n(X^n,X^{n-1})\cong\mathbb Z^k.
\]
Thus $H_n(X^n)$ is free of rank at most $k$. Attaching $(n+1)$-cells can only quotient $H_n$, while attaching cells of dimension at least $n+2$ does not alter it. Therefore $H_n(X)$ is a quotient of $H_n(X^n)$ and hence is generated by at most $k$ elements.
:::

This proves (a), (b), and (c).
:::
