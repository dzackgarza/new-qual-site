Let $a$ and $b$ be the generators of $\pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1)$ corresponding to the two $S^1$ summands.
Draw a picture of the covering space of $S^1 \vee S^1$ corresponding to the normal subgroup generated by $a^2$, $b^2$, and $(ab)^4$, and prove that this covering space is indeed the correct one.

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
F=\pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)=F(a,b)
\]
and let
\[
N=\langle\!\langle a^2,b^2,(ab)^4\rangle\!\rangle
\]
be the indicated normal closure.

<1>1. The quotient group is
\[
F/N\cong
\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=(ab)^4=1\rangle
\cong D_8,
\]
the dihedral group of order $8$.
::: {.proof}
Put
\[
r=ab.
\]
Then $r^4=1$, and since $a^2=b^2=1$ one has
\[
a r a
=a(ab)a
=ba
=r^{-1}.
\]
Thus the presentation becomes
\[
\langle r,a\mid r^4=a^2=1,\ ara=r^{-1}\rangle,
\]
which is the standard presentation of the symmetry group of a square.
Every element has one of the eight forms
\[
1,r,r^2,r^3,a,ar,ar^2,ar^3,
\]
and these are realized by distinct square symmetries, so the group has exactly eight elements.
:::

<1>2. Construct a covering graph $\widetilde X$ with one vertex $v_g$ for each
\[
g\in D_8.
\]
For every $g$ attach one oriented $a$-edge
\[
v_g\longrightarrow v_{ga}
\]
and one oriented $b$-edge
\[
v_g\longrightarrow v_{gb}.
\]
Map every $a$-edge homeomorphically around the $a$-circle of $S^1\vee S^1$ and every $b$-edge around the $b$-circle.
::: {.proof}
At each vertex there is exactly one outgoing and one incoming edge of each label.
Hence a small star neighborhood of the wedge point lifts to a disjoint union of star neighborhoods of the vertices $v_g$, each mapped homeomorphically to the base star.
Away from the wedge point each edge maps locally homeomorphically to the corresponding circle.
Thus
\[
p:\widetilde X\to S^1\vee S^1
\]
is an eight-sheeted covering.
:::

<1>3. A picture of $\widetilde X$ is obtained by arranging the eight vertices in the cyclic order
\[
1,\ a,\ ab,\ aba,\ (ab)^2,\ a(ab)^2,\ (ab)^3,\ a(ab)^3
\]
and joining consecutive vertices alternately by $a$- and $b$-labelled edge pairs.
::: {.proof}
Right multiplication by $a$ and $b$ moves between the two cosets in exactly this alternating pattern.
Because $a^2=b^2=1$ in the quotient, the two directed lifts of a given labelled base loop between a pair of adjacent vertices are distinct parallel edges with opposite endpoint order.
After eight alternating steps one returns to the initial vertex because
\[
(ab)^4=1.
\]
This is the labelled eight-vertex Cayley covering graph of $D_8$ with generators $a,b$.
:::

<1>4. The subgroup $K=p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,v_1)$ contains $N$.
::: {.proof}
The monodromy homomorphism of the labelled cover is the quotient map
\[
\rho:F(a,b)\to D_8
\]
given by right multiplication on the vertex labels.
A word represents an element of $K$ exactly when its lift from $v_1$ is closed, equivalently when $\rho$ sends it to the identity.
Now
\[
\rho(a^2)=\rho(b^2)=\rho((ab)^4)=1.
\]
Therefore the kernel of $\rho$, hence $K$, contains the normal closure of these three elements.
Thus
\[
N\subseteq K.
\]
:::

<1>5. Both $N$ and $K$ have index $8$ in $F$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1,
\[
[F:N]=|F/N|=8.
\]
By <1>2, the covering has eight sheets, so the covering-subgroup index theorem gives
\[
[F:K]=8.
\]
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
K=N.
\]
::: {.proof}
The inclusion $N\subseteq K$ from <1>4 together with equality of finite indices from <1>5 forces equality.
:::

<1>7. Hence the eight-sheeted labelled Cayley graph in <1>2--<1>3 is exactly the covering corresponding to the normal subgroup generated by
\[
a^2,\qquad b^2,\qquad (ab)^4.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is <1>6 and the classification of connected based coverings by subgroups of the fundamental group.
:::
