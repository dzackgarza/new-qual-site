Determine the covering space of $S^1 \vee S^1$ corresponding to the subgroup of $\pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1)$ generated by the cubes of all elements.
The covering space is 27-sheeted and can be drawn on a torus so that the complementary regions are nine triangles with edges labeled $aaa$, nine triangles with edges labeled $bbb$, and nine hexagons with edges labeled $ababab$.

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
F=F(a,b)=\pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)
\]
and let $N$ be the subgroup generated by all cubes $w^3$, $w\in F$.
Since conjugates of cubes are cubes,
\[
g w^3 g^{-1}=(gwg^{-1})^3,
\]
the subgroup $N$ is normal.

<1>1. The quotient $G=F/N$ is the free two-generator group of exponent $3$.
::: {.proof}
By construction every element of $G$ satisfies
\[
x^3=1.
\]
Conversely, $N$ is the smallest normal subgroup with this property, since any quotient of $F$ in which every element has cube $1$ must kill every $w^3$.
:::

<1>2. In $G$, the commutator
\[
c=[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
\]
is central.
::: {.proof}
We first prove a general calculation valid in every group of exponent $3$.
Write
\[
X=x^{-1},\qquad Y=y^{-1}.
\]
Then, using $x^2=X$, $y^2=Y$, and the fact that the cube of every word is $1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
[[x,y],y]
&=xyXY\,y\,(xyXY)^{-1}Y\\
&=xyXY\,yyxYXY\\
&=xyXyxx yx(XY)^3\\
&=xyXyXyx\\
&=xx(Xy)^3x\\
&=x^3\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the third and fifth lines only insert cube relations and use $x^2=X$, while the other steps are free cancellation.
Thus $[x,y]$ commutes with $y$.
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ shows that $[y,x]=[x,y]^{-1}$ commutes with $x$, hence $[x,y]$ commutes with $x$ as well.
Applying this with $x=a$, $y=b$ proves that $c$ commutes with both generators and is therefore central in $G$.
:::

<1>3. Every element of $G$ has the form
\[
a^i b^j c^k,
\qquad i,j,k\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $c$ is central and
\[
ab=cba,
\]
one may move all $a$'s to the left of all $b$'s at the cost of powers of $c$.
The exponent-three relations reduce the exponents modulo $3$.
Thus $|G|\le27$.
:::

<1>4. The group $G$ has at least $27$ elements.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
U=UT_3(\mathbf F_3)
=\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
1&x&z\\
0&1&y\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}:x,y,z\in\mathbf F_3
\right\}.
\]
This group has $27$ elements.
It is generated by
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every element of $U$ has cube $1$, so the assignment
\[
a\mapsto A,
\qquad b\mapsto B
\]
induces a surjection
\[
G\twoheadrightarrow U.
\]
Hence $|G|\ge27$.
Together with <1>3,
\[
|G|=27
\]
and $G\cong U$.
:::

<1>5. The required covering is the labelled Cayley cover of $G$ with generators $a,b$.
::: {.proof}
Take one vertex $v_g$ for each $g\in G$.
For every $g$, attach an oriented $a$-edge
\[
v_g\to v_{ga}
\]
and an oriented $b$-edge
\[
v_g\to v_{gb}.
\]
Map all $a$-edges around the $a$-circle and all $b$-edges around the $b$-circle.
As in the standard Cayley-cover construction, each vertex has exactly one incoming and outgoing lift of each oriented base edge, so this is a connected normal $27$-sheeted cover.
Its based subgroup is the kernel of
\[
F\to G=F/N,
\]
hence exactly $N$.
:::

<1>6. The $a$-edges decompose into nine directed $3$-cycles, and the $b$-edges do likewise.
::: {.proof}
Right multiplication by $a$ and by $b$ both have order $3$ and act freely on the $27$ vertices.
Hence each action has
\[
27/3=9
\]
orbits, each of length $3$.
These give nine triangular $aaa$ cycles and nine triangular $bbb$ cycles.
:::

<1>7. The alternating word $ab$ also has order $3$, so the alternating $a,b$ edges form nine hexagonal boundary cycles labelled
\[
ababab.
\]
::: {.proof}
The element $ab$ has cube $1$ in the exponent-three quotient.
Starting at a vertex and alternately traversing an $a$-edge and a $b$-edge returns after three such pairs, producing a six-edge cycle.
Again there are $27/3=9$ orbits under right multiplication by $ab$.
:::

<1>8. These cycles give Hatcher's torus drawing of the covering graph.
::: {.proof}
Attach a $2$-cell to each of the nine $aaa$ cycles, each of the nine $bbb$ cycles, and each of the nine $ababab$ cycles.
The resulting cell complex has
\[
V=27,
\qquad
E=54,
\qquad
F=27,
\]
so
\[
\chi=27-54+27=0.
\]
The labelled incidence pattern is connected and each edge is incident with two of these faces, giving a closed orientable surface of Euler characteristic $0$, hence a torus.
Removing the interiors of these faces leaves exactly the covering graph from <1>5, with the stated nine $aaa$ triangles, nine $bbb$ triangles, and nine $ababab$ hexagons as complementary regions.
:::

<1>9. Therefore the subgroup generated by all cubes corresponds to the $27$-sheeted normal Cayley covering described above.
::: {.proof}
This follows from <1>4--<1>8.
:::
