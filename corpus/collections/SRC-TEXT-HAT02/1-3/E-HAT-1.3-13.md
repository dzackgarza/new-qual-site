::: {.problem}
Determine the covering space of $S^1 \vee S^1$ corresponding to the subgroup of $\pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1)$ generated by the cubes of all elements.
The covering space is 27-sheeted and can be drawn on a torus so that the complementary regions are nine triangles with edges labeled $aaa$, nine triangles with edges labeled $bbb$, and nine hexagons with edges labeled $ababab$.
[For the analogous problem with sixth powers instead of cubes, the resulting covering space would have $2^{28}3^{25}$ sheets! And for $k^{\mathrm{th}}$ powers with $k$ sufficiently large, the covering space would have infinitely many sheets. The underlying group theory question here, whether the quotient of $\ZZ * \ZZ$ obtained by factoring out all $k^{\mathrm{th}}$ powers is finite, is known as Burnside's problem. It can also be asked for a free group on $n$ generators.]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
F=F(a,b)=\pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)
$$
and let $N$ be the subgroup generated by all cubes $w^3$, $w\in F$.
Since conjugates of cubes are cubes,
$$
g w^3 g^{-1}=(gwg^{-1})^3,
$$
the subgroup $N$ is normal.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The quotient $G=F/N$ is the free two-generator group of exponent $3$.

::: pf-proof

By construction every element of $G$ satisfies
$$
x^3=1.
$$
Conversely, $N$ is the smallest normal subgroup with this property, since any quotient of $F$ in which every element has cube $1$ must kill every $w^3$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In $G$, the commutator
$$
c=[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
$$
is central.

::: pf-proof

We first prove a general calculation valid in every group of exponent $3$.
Write
$$
X=x^{-1},\qquad Y=y^{-1}.
$$
Then, using $x^2=X$, $y^2=Y$, and the fact that the cube of every word is $1$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
[[x,y],y]
&=xyXY\,y\,(xyXY)^{-1}Y &&\text{definition, }(xyXY)^{-1}=yxYX\\
&=xyXY\,yyxYXY &&\text{free cancellation}\\
&=xyXyxx\,yx(XY)^3 &&\text{free, with }x^2=X,\ y^2=Y\\
&=xyXyXyx &&(XY)^3=1,\ \text{then }xx=X\\
&=xx(Xy)^3x &&\text{free, with }x^2=X,\ y^2=Y\\
&=x^3 &&(Xy)^3=1\\
&=1 &&x^3=1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Lines 2, 3 and 5 are rewrites that use only $x^2=X$, $y^2=Y$ and free cancellation.
The cube relations enter at line 4, where $(XY)^3=1$ deletes the cube inserted in line 3 and $xx=X$ then cancels the remaining pair; at line 6, where $(Xy)^3=1$ leaves $x^3$; and at line 7, where $x^3=1$.
Thus $[x,y]$ commutes with $y$.
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ shows that $[y,x]=[x,y]^{-1}$ commutes with $x$, hence $[x,y]$ commutes with $x$ as well.
Applying this with $x=a$, $y=b$ proves that $c$ commutes with both generators and is therefore central in $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every element of $G$ has the form
$$
a^i b^j c^k,
\qquad i,j,k\in\{0,1,2\}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $c$ is central and
$$
ab=cba,
$$
one may move all $a$'s to the left of all $b$'s at the cost of powers of $c$.
The exponent-three relations reduce the exponents modulo $3$.
Thus $\cardinality{G}\le27$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The group $G$ has at least $27$ elements.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
U=UT_3(\FF_3)
=\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
1&x&z\\
0&1&y\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}:x,y,z\in\FF_3
\right\}.
$$
This group has $27$ elements.
It is generated by
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Every element of $U$ has cube $1$: if $M=I+N$ with $N$ strictly upper triangular then $N^3=0$, and $3=0$ in $\FF_3$, so
$$
M^3=(I+N)^3=I+3N+3N^2+N^3=I.
$$
The matrices $A$ and $B$ generate $U$. Write $(x,y,z)$ for the matrix with $x$ in position $(1,2)$, $y$ in position $(2,3)$ and $z$ in position $(1,3)$; multiplying,
$$
(x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',\,y+y',\,z+z'+xy').
$$
Hence $A=(1,0,0)$, $B=(0,1,0)$ and
$$
ABA^{-1}B^{-1}=(1,1,1)(-1,0,0)(0,-1,0)=(0,0,-1)=I-E_{13},
$$
so $I+E_{13}=(0,0,1)$ and
$$
A^jB^k(I+E_{13})^l=(j,k,l)
\qquad(j,k,l\in\FF_3).
$$
These are the $27$ elements of $U$.

Since every element of $U$ has cube $1$, the assignment
$$
a\mapsto A,
\qquad b\mapsto B
$$
sends each cube $w^3$ to $1$, hence kills the normal subgroup $N$ and induces a homomorphism $G=F/N\to U$; it is surjective because $A$ and $B$ generate $U$.
Hence $\cardinality{G}\ge27$.
Together with step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
\cardinality{G}=27
$$
and $G\cong U$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The required covering is the labelled Cayley cover of $G$ with generators $a,b$.

::: pf-proof

Take one vertex $v_g$ for each $g\in G$.
For every $g$, attach an oriented $a$-edge
$$
v_g\to v_{ga}
$$
and an oriented $b$-edge
$$
v_g\to v_{gb}.
$$
Map all $a$-edges around the $a$-circle and all $b$-edges around the $b$-circle.
As in the standard Cayley-cover construction, each vertex has exactly one incoming and outgoing lift of each oriented base edge, so this is a connected normal $27$-sheeted cover.
Its based subgroup is the kernel of
$$
F\to G=F/N,
$$
hence exactly $N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The $a$-edges decompose into nine directed $3$-cycles, and the $b$-edges do likewise.

::: pf-proof

Right multiplication by $a$ and by $b$ both have order $3$ and act freely on the $27$ vertices.
Hence each action has
$$
27/3=9
$$
orbits, each of length $3$.
These give nine triangular $aaa$ cycles and nine triangular $bbb$ cycles.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

The alternating word $ab$ also has order $3$, so the alternating $a,b$ edges form nine hexagonal boundary cycles labelled
$$
ababab.
$$

::: pf-proof

The element $ab$ has cube $1$ in the exponent-three quotient.
Starting at a vertex and alternately traversing an $a$-edge and a $b$-edge returns after three such pairs, producing a six-edge cycle.
Again there are $27/3=9$ orbits under right multiplication by $ab$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

These cycles give Hatcher's torus drawing of the covering graph.

::: pf-proof

Attach a $2$-cell to each of the nine $aaa$ cycles, each of the nine $bbb$ cycles, and each of the nine $ababab$ cycles.
The resulting cell complex has
$$
V=27,
\qquad
E=54,
\qquad
F=27,
$$
so
$$
\chi=27-54+27=0.
$$
The labelled incidence pattern is connected, and every edge lies in exactly two of the faces: an $a$-edge in one $aaa$ triangle and one $ababab$ hexagon, a $b$-edge in one $bbb$ triangle and one $ababab$ hexagon, because the orbits of steps [](#s6){.pf-ref} and [](#s7){.pf-ref} partition the $27$ $a$-edges and the $27$ $b$-edges and each hexagon uses three $a$-edges and three $b$-edges.

The vertex links are single cycles. At a vertex $v$ there are exactly two $a$-edge ends and two $b$-edge ends, one outgoing and one incoming of each label, since $a^3=b^3=1$. The faces at $v$ are the $aaa$ triangle, the $bbb$ triangle and two hexagons: the hexagon that leaves $v$ along $v\to va$ returns to $v$ along a $b$-edge, so it uses the outgoing $a$-end and the incoming $b$-end, and the hexagon that leaves along $v\to vb$ uses the outgoing $b$-end and the incoming $a$-end. The four faces therefore pair the four edge ends into the single cycle
$$
\{a_{\mathrm{out}},a_{\mathrm{in}}\},\quad
\{a_{\mathrm{in}},b_{\mathrm{out}}\},\quad
\{b_{\mathrm{out}},b_{\mathrm{in}}\},\quad
\{b_{\mathrm{in}},a_{\mathrm{out}}\},
$$
so every vertex has a neighbourhood that is a cone on a $4$-cycle, and the complex is a closed surface.

It is orientable: orient each $aaa$ triangle against the direction of the $a$-edges and each $bbb$ triangle against the direction of the $b$-edges. Then each $a$-edge is traversed once against its direction, by its triangle, and once along its direction, by its hexagon, and each $b$-edge likewise, so the two faces at every edge traverse it in opposite directions and the orientation of the faces orients the surface. A closed orientable surface with $\chi=0$ is a torus.
Removing the interiors of these faces leaves exactly the covering graph from step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, with the stated nine $aaa$ triangles, nine $bbb$ triangles, and nine $ababab$ hexagons as complementary regions.

:::

::: pf-proof
