::: {.problem}
Let $\varphi: \RR^2 \to \RR^2$ be the linear transformation $\varphi(x,y) = (2x, y/2)$.
This generates an action of $\ZZ$ on $X = \RR^2 - \{0\}$.
Show this action is a covering space action and compute $\pi_1(X/\ZZ)$.
Show the orbit space $X/\ZZ$ is non-Hausdorff, and describe how it is a union of four subspaces homeomorphic to $S^1 \times \RR$, coming from the complementary components of the $x$-axis and the $y$-axis.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
\varphi^n(x,y)=(2^n x,2^{-n}y).
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The $\ZZ$-action generated by $\varphi$ is free.

::: pf-proof

If $\varphi^n(x,y)=(x,y)$ for $(x,y)\ne(0,0)$, then
$$
(2^n-1)x=0,\qquad (2^{-n}-1)y=0.
$$
At least one of $x,y$ is nonzero, so $2^n=1$, hence $n=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The action is a covering space action.

::: pf-proof

Fix $z=(x,y)\in X$.
If $x\ne0$, choose an interval $I$ about $x$, lying on the same side of zero, such that the ratio of its largest to its smallest absolute value is less than $2$.
Then $2^nI\cap I=\varnothing$ for every nonzero integer $n$.
Any open rectangle $U\subseteq X$ about $z$ whose first-coordinate interval is $I$ therefore satisfies
$$
\varphi^n(U)\cap U=\varnothing
\qquad(n\ne0).
$$
If $x=0$, then $y\ne0$, and the same construction uses the second-coordinate interval and the dilation $2^{-n}$.
Thus every point has a neighborhood disjoint from all its nontrivial translates.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The quotient map
$$
p:X\to X/\ZZ
$$
is a normal covering with deck group $\ZZ$.
Moreover
$$
\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ.
$$

::: pf-proof

The first assertion follows from step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.
Since
$$
X=\RR^2\setminus\{0\}\simeq S^1,
$$
its fundamental group is infinite cyclic.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\boxed{\pi_1(X/\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^2.}
$$

::: pf-proof

For a normal covering there is an exact sequence
$$
1\to\pi_1(X)\xrightarrow{p_*}\pi_1(X/\ZZ)\to\ZZ\to1.
$$
Let $a$ be a positively oriented meridian around the origin.
The linear map $\varphi$ has determinant $1$, so it is orientation preserving and induces the identity on
$$
\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ.
$$
Hence conjugation by a lift $t$ of the generator of the deck group fixes $a$:
$$
tat^{-1}=a.
$$
Since the quotient group is infinite cyclic, choose $t$ mapping to its generator.
The extension then has presentation
$$
\langle a,t\mid [a,t]=1\rangle\cong\ZZ^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The orbit space is not Hausdorff.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
p=(1,0),\qquad q=(0,1).
$$
Their orbits are distinct because every iterate of $p$ lies on the $x$-axis and every iterate of $q$ lies on the $y$-axis.
Suppose their orbit classes had disjoint neighborhoods in the quotient.
Their inverse images would be disjoint saturated open neighborhoods $U$ of the orbit of $p$ and $V$ of the orbit of $q$.
Choose $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$
(1,s)\in U\quad\text{for }\abs{s}<\varepsilon,
$$
and choose $\delta>0$ such that
$$
(r,1)\in V\quad\text{for }\abs{r}<\delta.
$$
For $n$ large enough, set
$$
z_n=(1,2^{-n}).
$$
Then $z_n\in U$, while
$$
\varphi^{-n}(z_n)=(2^{-n},1)\in V.
$$
Since $U$ is saturated, $\varphi^{-n}(z_n)\in U$ as well, contradicting $U\cap V=\varnothing$.
Thus the two orbit classes cannot be separated.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The two components of the complement of the $x$-axis and the two components of the complement of the $y$-axis are invariant under the action. Each has quotient homeomorphic to $S^1\times\RR$, and their four images cover $X/\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

On either half-plane $y>0$ or $y<0$, the coordinates
$$
u=\log\abs{y},\qquad v=xy
$$
give a homeomorphism with $\RR^2$: from $(u,v)$ one recovers $y=\pm e^u$ with the chosen sign and $x=v/y$.
In these coordinates $\varphi$ acts by
$$
(u,v)\longmapsto(u-\log2,v).
$$
Thus the quotient of either half-plane is
$$
 (\RR/\log2\,\ZZ)\times\RR\cong S^1\times\RR.
$$
On either half-plane $x>0$ or $x<0$, use instead
$$
u=\log\abs{x},\qquad v=xy.
$$
These coordinates again identify the half-plane with $\RR^2$, and $\varphi$ sends $(u,v)$ to $(u+\log2,v)$, giving the same quotient.
Every point of $X$ has $x\ne0$ or $y\ne0$, so these four invariant half-planes cover $X$. Their orbit images therefore cover $X/\ZZ$.

:::
