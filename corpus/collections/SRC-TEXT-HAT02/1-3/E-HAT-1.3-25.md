Let $\varphi: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be the linear transformation $\varphi(x,y) = (2x, y/2)$.
This generates an action of $\mathbb{Z}$ on $X = \mathbb{R}^2 - \{0\}$.
Show this action is a covering space action and compute $\pi_1(X/\mathbb{Z})$.
Show the orbit space $X/\mathbb{Z}$ is non-Hausdorff, and describe how it is a union of four subspaces homeomorphic to $S^1 \times \mathbb{R}$, coming from the complementary components of the $x$-axis and the $y$-axis.


::: {.solution}
Write
\[
\varphi^n(x,y)=(2^n x,2^{-n}y).
\]

<1>1. The $\mathbb Z$-action generated by $\varphi$ is free.
::: {.proof}
If $\varphi^n(x,y)=(x,y)$ for $(x,y)\ne(0,0)$, then
\[
(2^n-1)x=0,\qquad (2^{-n}-1)y=0.
\]
At least one of $x,y$ is nonzero, so $2^n=1$, hence $n=0$.
:::

<1>2. The action is a covering space action.
::: {.proof}
Fix $z=(x,y)\in X$.
If $x\ne0$, then the first coordinates of $\varphi^n(z)$ are $2^nx$, so only finitely many orbit points lie in any compact horizontal annulus
\[
c\le |u|\le C.
\]
Choose a small rectangle $U$ about $z$ contained in such an annulus and disjoint from the finitely many distinct points $\varphi^n(z)$ that remain in a slightly larger rectangle.
Shrinking $U$ if necessary gives
\[
\varphi^n(U)\cap U=\varnothing
\qquad(n\ne0).
\]
The case $y\ne0$ is identical using the second coordinate $2^{-n}y$.
Thus every point has a neighborhood disjoint from all its nontrivial translates.
:::

<1>3. The quotient map
\[
p:X\to X/\mathbb Z
\]
is a normal covering with deck group $\mathbb Z$.
Moreover
\[
\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
::: {.proof}
The first assertion follows from <1>2.
Since
\[
X=\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}\simeq S^1,
\]
its fundamental group is infinite cyclic.
:::

<1>4. One has
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X/\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2.}
\]
::: {.proof}
For a normal covering there is an exact sequence
\[
1\to\pi_1(X)\xrightarrow{p_*}\pi_1(X/\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z\to1.
\]
Let $a$ be a positively oriented meridian around the origin.
The linear map $\varphi$ has determinant $1$, so it is orientation preserving and induces the identity on
\[
\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Hence conjugation by a lift $t$ of the generator of the deck group fixes $a$:
\[
tat^{-1}=a.
\]
Since the quotient group is infinite cyclic, choose $t$ mapping to its generator.
The extension then has presentation
\[
\langle a,t\mid [a,t]=1\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
:::

<1>5. The orbit space is not Hausdorff.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
p=(1,0),\qquad q=(0,1).
\]
Their orbits are distinct because every iterate of $p$ lies on the $x$-axis and every iterate of $q$ lies on the $y$-axis.
Suppose their orbit classes had disjoint neighborhoods in the quotient.
Their inverse images would be disjoint saturated open neighborhoods $U$ of the orbit of $p$ and $V$ of the orbit of $q$.
Choose $\epsilon>0$ such that
\[
(1,s)\in U\quad\text{for }|s|<\epsilon,
\]
and choose $\delta>0$ such that
\[
(r,1)\in V\quad\text{for }|r|<\delta.
\]
For $n$ large enough, set
\[
z_n=(1,2^{-n}).
\]
Then $z_n\in U$, while
\[
\varphi^{-n}(z_n)=(2^{-n},1)\in V.
\]
Since $U$ is saturated, $\varphi^{-n}(z_n)\in U$ as well, contradicting $U\cap V=\varnothing$.
Thus the two orbit classes cannot be separated.
:::

<1>6. Each open quadrant is invariant under the action, and its quotient is homeomorphic to $S^1\times\mathbb R$.
::: {.proof}
On any quadrant introduce logarithmic coordinates
\[
u=\log|x|,\qquad v=\log|y|.
\]
Then $\varphi$ becomes translation by
\[
(u,v)\longmapsto(u+\log2,v-\log2).
\]
Use coordinates
\[
s=u+v,\qquad r=u-v.
\]
The action fixes $s$ and translates $r$ by $2\log2$.
Therefore the quotient is
\[
\mathbb R_s\times(\mathbb R_r/2\log2\,\mathbb Z)
\cong\mathbb R\times S^1.
\]
The four quadrants are precisely the complementary components of the union of the two coordinate axes, so their images give the four stated $S^1\times\mathbb R$ subspaces of the orbit space.
:::
:::
