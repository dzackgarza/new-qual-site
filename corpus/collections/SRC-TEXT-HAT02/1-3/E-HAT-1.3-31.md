Show that the normal covering spaces of $S^1 \vee S^1$ are precisely the graphs that are Cayley graphs of groups with two generators.
More generally, the normal covering spaces of the wedge sum of $n$ circles are the Cayley graphs of groups with $n$ generators.


::: {.solution}
Let
\[
R_n=\bigvee_{i=1}^n S^1,
\qquad
F_n=\pi_1(R_n)=\langle x_1,\dots,x_n\rangle.
\]

<1>1. Let
\[
p:\widetilde R\to R_n
\]
be a connected normal covering corresponding to a normal subgroup
\[
H\triangleleft F_n.
\]
Then the vertex set of $\widetilde R$ is naturally the quotient group
\[
Q=F_n/H.
\]
::: {.proof}
The fiber over the unique vertex of $R_n$ is the set of right cosets $H\backslash F_n$.
Since $H$ is normal, these cosets form the group $F_n/H$.
Connectedness makes every fiber point a vertex of the covering graph.
:::

<1>2. For each $q\in Q$ and each generator $x_i$, the lift of the $i$th circle gives an oriented edge
\[
q\longrightarrow q\bar x_i,
\]
where $\bar x_i$ is the image of $x_i$ in $Q$.
::: {.proof}
This is the standard coset action of $F_n$ on the fiber by lifting loops.
The endpoint of the lift of $x_i$ beginning at the vertex $q$ is the vertex represented by $q\bar x_i$.
:::

<1>3. Therefore $\widetilde R$ is exactly the Cayley graph
\[
\operatorname{Cay}(Q;\bar x_1,\dots,\bar x_n).
\]
::: {.proof}
The vertices and labelled oriented edges in <1>1--<1>2 are precisely the defining data of this Cayley graph.
:::

<1>4. Conversely, let $Q$ be any group generated by elements
\[
q_1,\dots,q_n.
\]
Then its Cayley graph with these generators is a connected normal covering of $R_n$.
::: {.proof}
The epimorphism
\[
F_n\twoheadrightarrow Q,
\qquad
x_i\mapsto q_i,
\]
has normal kernel $H$.
The covering corresponding to $H$ is normal, and by <1>3 its graph is exactly
\[
\operatorname{Cay}(Q;q_1,\dots,q_n).
\]
:::

<1>5. Hence the normal connected covers of a wedge of $n$ circles are precisely Cayley graphs of groups with $n$ specified generators.
::: {.proof}
The two directions are <1>1--<1>3 and <1>4.
For $n=2$ this is the first assertion of the exercise.
:::
:::
