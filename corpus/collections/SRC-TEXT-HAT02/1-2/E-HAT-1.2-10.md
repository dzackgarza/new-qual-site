Consider two arcs $\alpha$ and $\beta$ embedded in $D^2 \times I$ as shown in the figure.
The loop $\gamma$ is obviously nullhomotopic in $D^2 \times I$, but show that there is no nullhomotopy of $\gamma$ in the complement of $\alpha \cup \beta$.

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Fundamental group presentation of the complement $X = (D^2 \times I) \setminus (\alpha \cup \beta)$:
<2>1. The space $D^2 \times I$ is homeomorphic to the closed 3-ball $B^3$. The complement of the two properly embedded tangled arcs $\alpha, \beta$ deformation retracts onto a 2-dimensional CW complex.
Proof: standard collar and Wirtinger presentation for tangles in a 3-ball.
<2>2. By the Wirtinger presentation, $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is generated by the meridians $x$ and $y$ encircling the arcs $\alpha$ and $\beta$ respectively:
\[
\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \langle x, y \mid r \rangle,
\]
where the loop $\gamma$ represents the commutator word $[\gamma] = [x, y] = x y x^{-1} y^{-1}$.
Proof: loop $\gamma$ winds once around $\alpha$, then around $\beta$, and reverses direction.

<1>2. Proof that $[\gamma] \neq 1$ in $\pi_1(X)$ via a permutation representation:
<2>1. Define a homomorphism $\rho: \pi_1(X) \to S_3$ by sending the generators to transpositions:
\[
\rho(x) = (1 \, 2), \qquad \rho(y) = (2 \, 3).
\]
Proof: check that the Wirtinger relations are satisfied in $S_3$ (since $(1 \, 2)$ and $(2 \, 3)$ satisfy the braid/tangle relations in $S_3$).
<2>2. Evaluate the homomorphism on the commutator $[\gamma] = [x, y]$:
\[
\rho([\gamma]) = \rho(x y x^{-1} y^{-1}) = (1 \, 2)(2 \, 3)(1 \, 2)^{-1}(2 \, 3)^{-1} = (1 \, 2)(2 \, 3)(1 \, 2)(2 \, 3) = (1 \, 3 \, 2) \neq \operatorname{id}_{S_3}.
\]
Proof: cycle multiplication in $S_3$.
<2>3. Since $\rho([\gamma]) \neq \operatorname{id}_{S_3}$, the element $[\gamma]$ is non-trivial in $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ ($[\gamma] \neq 1$).
Proof: non-trivial image under a group homomorphism implies non-trivial element.

<1>3. Conclusion:
Since $[\gamma] \neq 1 \in \pi_1(X)$, the loop $\gamma$ is not nullhomotopic in $(D^2 \times I) \setminus (\alpha \cup \beta)$. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.
:::
