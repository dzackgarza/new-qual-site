::: {.problem}
Compute the fundamental group of the space obtained from two tori $S^1 \times S^1$ by identifying a circle $S^1 \times \{x_0\}$ in one torus with the corresponding circle $S^1 \times \{x_0\}$ in the other torus.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $a$ for the common circle, and $b_1,b_2$ for the other coordinate circles in the two tori.

::: pf

::: pf-step
The quotient has a CW structure with one vertex, the three $1$-cells $a,b_1,b_2$, and two $2$-cells attached along $[a,b_1]$ and $[a,b_2]$.

::: pf-proof
Give each torus its standard cell structure with one vertex, two coordinate $1$-cells, and a $2$-cell attached by their commutator. Identifying the first coordinate circle of the two tori identifies their vertices and their first $1$-cells. The other cells remain distinct, with the stated attaching maps.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The fundamental group is
$$
\pi_1(X)\cong
\langle a,b_1,b_2\mid [a,b_1],[a,b_2]\rangle
\cong \ZZ\times F(b_1,b_2).
$$

::: pf-proof
The cell presentation of the fundamental group gives the first group from the attaching words in the first step. Its relations say that $a$ commutes with both free generators $b_1,b_2$. Sending $a$ to $(1,1)$ and $b_i$ to $(0,b_i)$ defines a homomorphism to $\ZZ\times F(b_1,b_2)$. Conversely, the central element $a$ and the subgroup generated by $b_1,b_2$ define a homomorphism from that direct product to the presented group. These maps fix every generator in both directions, so they are inverse isomorphisms.
:::
