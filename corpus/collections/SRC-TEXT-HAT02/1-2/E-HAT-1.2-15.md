Given a space $X$ with basepoint $x_0 \in X$, we may construct a CW complex $L(X)$ having a single 0 cell, a 1 cell $e^1_\gamma$ for each loop $\gamma$ in $X$ based at $x_0$, and a 2 cell $e^2_\tau$ for each map $\tau$ of a standard triangle $PQR$ into $X$ taking the three vertices $P$, $Q$, and $R$ of the triangle to $x_0$.
The 2 cell $e^2_\tau$ is attached to the three 1 cells that are the loops obtained by restricting $\tau$ to the three oriented edges $PQ$, $PR$, and $QR$.
Show that the natural map $L(X) \longrightarrow X$ induces an isomorphism $\pi_1\big(L(X)\big) \approx \pi_1(X, x_0)$.

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
F:L(X)\longrightarrow X
\]
be the natural map: the $1$-cell $e^1_\gamma$ is mapped around the loop $\gamma$, and each $2$-cell $e^2_\tau$ is mapped by the corresponding triangle map $\tau$.

<1>1. The induced homomorphism
\[
F_*:\pi_1(L(X))\to\pi_1(X,x_0)
\]
is surjective.
::: {.proof}
Every element of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ is represented by some based loop $\gamma$.
The corresponding $1$-cell $e^1_\gamma$ is a loop in $L(X)$, and by construction
\[
F_*([e^1_\gamma])=[\gamma].
\]
Thus every fundamental-group class of $X$ lies in the image.
:::

<1>2. Let $c$ denote the constant loop at $x_0$.
Then
\[
[e^1_c]=1\in\pi_1(L(X)).
\]
::: {.proof}
Take the constant map
\[
\tau:\Delta^2\to\{x_0\}\subseteq X.
\]
All three oriented edge restrictions are the constant loop $c$.
The corresponding $2$-cell is therefore attached along the word
\[
e_c e_c e_c^{-1}=e_c.
\]
Attaching this $2$-cell makes $[e_c]$ trivial in the fundamental group.
:::

<1>3. If two based loops $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ are homotopic relative to the basepoint, then
\[
[e^1_{\gamma_0}]=[e^1_{\gamma_1}]
\quad\text{in }\pi_1(L(X)).
\]
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
H:I\times I\to X
\]
be a based homotopy from $\gamma_0$ to $\gamma_1$.
Its two vertical edges are constantly $x_0$.
Collapse these two vertical edges of the square to vertices and identify the resulting disk with a triangle.
The induced triangle map has oriented edge loops $\gamma_0$, $\gamma_1$, and $c$, up to reversing the orientation of one edge.
Hence the associated $2$-cell imposes a relation of the form
\[
[e_{\gamma_0}][e_c]=[e_{\gamma_1}].
\]
By <1>2, $[e_c]=1$, so
\[
[e_{\gamma_0}]=[e_{\gamma_1}].
\]
:::

<1>4. Define
\[
G:\pi_1(X,x_0)\longrightarrow\pi_1(L(X))
\]
by
\[
G([\gamma])=[e^1_\gamma].
\]
Then $G$ is well defined.
::: {.proof}
If $[\gamma_0]=[\gamma_1]$ in $\pi_1(X,x_0)$, then the loops are based-homotopic.
By <1>3 their corresponding $1$-cells represent the same element of $\pi_1(L(X))$.
:::

<1>5. The map $G$ is a homomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be based loops.
The boundary loop
\[
\alpha\cdot\beta\cdot\overline{\alpha\cdot\beta}
\]
is nullhomotopic.
Equivalently, there is a triangle map whose oriented edge restrictions are $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\alpha\cdot\beta$.
The corresponding $2$-cell in $L(X)$ gives the relation
\[
[e_\alpha][e_\beta]=[e_{\alpha\cdot\beta}].
\]
Therefore
\[
G([\alpha][\beta])
=G([\alpha\cdot\beta])
=[e_{\alpha\cdot\beta}]
=[e_\alpha][e_\beta]
=G([\alpha])G([\beta]).
\]
:::

<1>6. The composite
\[
F_*\circ G
\]
is the identity on $\pi_1(X,x_0)$.
::: {.proof}
For every based loop $\gamma$,
\[
(F_*G)([\gamma])
=F_*([e_\gamma])
=[\gamma]
\]
by the definition of $F$.
:::

<1>7. The composite
\[
G\circ F_*
\]
is the identity on $\pi_1(L(X))$.
::: {.proof}
The fundamental group of the CW complex $L(X)$ is generated by the loops given by its $1$-cells $e_\gamma$.
For each such generator,
\[
(GF_*)([e_\gamma])
=G([\gamma])
=[e_\gamma].
\]
Since a homomorphism that fixes a generating set is the identity, the composite is the identity on all of $\pi_1(L(X))$.
:::

<1>8. Hence
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(L(X))\cong\pi_1(X,x_0)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>6--<1>7, $F_*$ and $G$ are mutually inverse homomorphisms.
:::
:::
