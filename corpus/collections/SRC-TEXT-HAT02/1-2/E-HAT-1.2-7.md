::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the quotient space of $S^2$ obtained by identifying the north and south poles to a single point.
Put a cell complex structure on $X$ and use this to compute $\pi_1(X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $N$ and $S$ be the poles, let $v \in X$ be their common image, and let $m \subset S^2$ be a meridian arc from $N$ to $S$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cells}

$X$ has a cell structure with one $0$-cell $v$, one $1$-cell $a$ (the image of $m$), and one $2$-cell attached along the word $a a^{-1}$.

::: pf-proof

Give $S^2$ the cell structure with $0$-cells $N, S$, the $1$-cell $m$, and one $2$-cell $D$: cutting $S^2$ along $m$ leaves an open disc whose boundary runs along $m$ from $N$ to $S$ and back along $m$ from $S$ to $N$, so $\partial D$ reads $m\, m^{-1}$.
The identification $N \sim S$ only merges the two $0$-cells, so $X$ inherits the cells $v$, $a$, $D$; the endpoints of $a$ are both $v$, so $a$ is a loop, and $D$ is attached along $a a^{-1}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\boxed{\pi_1(X) \cong \ZZ}$.

::: pf-proof

For a cell complex with one $0$-cell, $\pi_1$ is generated by the $1$-cells with one relation for each $2$-cell, read off its attaching word (Hatcher, Proposition 1.26).
By step [](#cells){.pf-ref}, $\pi_1(X) \cong \langle a \mid a a^{-1} \rangle$.
The relation $a a^{-1} = 1$ is trivial, so $\pi_1(X)$ is free on $a$, that is, $\pi_1(X) \cong \ZZ$.

:::
