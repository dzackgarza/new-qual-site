::: {.problem}
Give an example of a weak homotopy equivalence $X \to Y$ for which there does not exist a weak homotopy equivalence $Y \to X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The exercise is §4.1 Exercise 18. No retained solution set holds a solution: Sam's §4.1 has no entry for the exercise, and Proudfoot's Homework #6 covers Exercises 4, 11, 12, 14 and 15. The example below is the card's.

Let $P = \{a, b, c, d\}$ be the pseudocircle, with open sets generated by $\{a\}$, $\{b\}$, $\{a, b, c\}$ and $\{a, b, d\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

There is a weak homotopy equivalence $f: S^1 \to P$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

The order complex of $P$ is a square, so its realization is $S^1$: its simplices are the chains of $P$, four vertices and the four edges $ac$, $ad$, $bc$, $bd$, which form one cycle. Which of the two points of a comparable pair is written below the other depends on the convention for the specialization order, and the chains, hence the order complex, are the same either way.

::: pf-proof

Its simplices are the chains of $P$: four vertices and the four edges $ac$, $ad$, $bc$, $bd$, which form one cycle.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

McCord's map from the realization of the order complex to $P$ is a weak homotopy equivalence.

::: pf-proof

This is McCord's theorem, in M. C. McCord, "Singular homology groups and homotopy groups of finite topological spaces", *Duke Mathematical Journal* 33 (1966): for a finite $T_0$ space, the map from the realization of its order complex to the space, sending a point to a minimal element of its support, is a weak homotopy equivalence. That paper is not retained on this machine, so the result is cited by author and year and not by a numbered statement.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

There is no weak homotopy equivalence $P \to S^1$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Every continuous map $g: P \to S^1$ is null-homotopic.

::: pf-proof

The image $g(P)$ is a finite subspace of the Hausdorff space $S^1$, so it is discrete. $P$ is path-connected: the McCord map $f$ is onto, every point of $P$ being a vertex of its order complex, and $S^1$ is path-connected; equivalently, a weak homotopy equivalence induces a bijection on $\pi_0$. So $g(P)$, a continuous image of $P$, is connected, hence one point. So $g$ is constant.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $g$ cannot induce an isomorphism on $\pi_1$.

::: pf-proof

$g_*: \pi_1(P) \cong \ZZ \to \pi_1(S^1) \cong \ZZ$ is the zero map (a null-homotopic map induces the trivial map on homotopy groups), not an isomorphism.

:::
