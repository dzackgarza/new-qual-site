::: {.problem}
Consider the equivalence relation $\simeq_w$ generated by weak homotopy equivalence: $X \simeq_w Y$ if there are spaces $X = X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n = Y$ with weak homotopy equivalences $X_i \to X_{i+1}$ or $X_i \gets X_{i+1}$ for each $i$.
Show that $X \simeq_w Y$ if and only if $X$ and $Y$ have a common CW approximation.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is that of retained solutions: **Steven V. Sam, *Solutions to Algebraic Topology* (2008), §4.1 Exercise 19** (`tmp/sources/hatcher-solutions-sam-2008.pdf`). Sam takes the forward implication from the zigzag $X\leftarrow K\to Y$ that a common CW approximation gives, and proves the converse by reducing the zigzag to a single weak homotopy equivalence $X\to Y$, comparing the chosen CW approximations through Proposition 4.18, and applying Whitehead's theorem, Theorem 4.5, to the comparison map. The bookkeeping for a zigzag longer than two steps is the card's, as is the form of Whitehead's theorem recorded in step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, which handles every arrow of a zigzag between CW complexes at once. Existence of a CW approximation is Proposition 4.13.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $X$ and $Y$ have a common CW approximation then $X\simeq_wY$.

::: pf-proof

Let $K$ be a common CW approximation, so there are weak homotopy equivalences $K\xrightarrow{u}X$ and $K\xrightarrow{v}Y$, giving a zigzag
$$
X\xleftarrow{\ u\ }K\xrightarrow{\ v\ }Y
$$
of weak homotopy equivalences, which is one of the zigzags generating $\simeq_w$. Hence $X\simeq_wY$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Weakly equivalent CW complexes are homotopy equivalent.

::: pf-proof

Let $K$ and $L$ be CW complexes with a zigzag of weak homotopy equivalences $K=K_1\leftrightarrow K_2\leftrightarrow\cdots\leftrightarrow K_n=L$. Each arrow of that zigzag is a map of CW complexes inducing an isomorphism on $\pi_i$ for every $i$, hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem (Theorem 4.5, whose hypothesis is an isomorphism on $\pi_n$ for all $n$, which is exactly what a weak homotopy equivalence between CW complexes provides); a zigzag of homotopy equivalences composes to a homotopy equivalence. Theorem 4.5 is printed for connected CW complexes, and a weak homotopy equivalence induces a bijection on $\pi_0$, so it carries components to components and is a weak homotopy equivalence on each, where the theorem applies.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $X\simeq_wY$ then $X$ and $Y$ have a common CW approximation.

::: pf-proof

Suppose first that $X=W_1,W_2,\dots,W_m=Y$ is a zigzag with a weak homotopy equivalence $W_i\to W_{i+1}$ or $W_i\leftarrow W_{i+1}$ for each $i$. For each $i$ choose a CW approximation $K_i\to W_i$.

*Claim: $K_i$ and $K_{i+1}$ are homotopy equivalent.* If $W_i\to W_{i+1}$ is a weak homotopy equivalence, then $K_i\to W_i\to W_{i+1}$ is a weak homotopy equivalence, so $K_i$ is also a CW approximation of $W_{i+1}$; and any two CW approximations of one space are homotopy equivalent: the paragraph following Proposition 4.13 deforms the composite $K_i\to W_{i+1}$ into $K_{i+1}$ by the compression lemma, and the comparison map so obtained is a weak homotopy equivalence between CW complexes, hence a homotopy equivalence by Theorem 4.5 as in step [](#s2){.pf-ref}. The case of $W_i\leftarrow W_{i+1}$ is the same with the roles exchanged.

By transitivity of homotopy equivalence, $K_1\simeq K_m$, say through a homotopy equivalence $h:K_1\to K_m$. Now $K_1\to X$ is a weak homotopy equivalence, and $K_1\xrightarrow{h}K_m\to Y$ is a weak homotopy equivalence because $h$ is a homotopy equivalence and $K_m\to Y$ is a weak homotopy equivalence. So $K_1$ is a common CW approximation of $X$ and $Y$.

:::
