::: {.problem}
Show that the spaces $(S^1 \times \CP^\infty) / (S^1 \times \{x_0\})$ and $S^3 \times \CP^\infty$ have isomorphic cohomology rings with $\ZZ$ or any other coefficients.
[An exercise for §4.L is to show these two spaces are not homotopy equivalent.]
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: identify the quotient's cohomology with a relative cohomology ring, compute that ring by the cross product, and compare it with the product $S^{3}\times\CP^{\infty}$. Coefficients are a commutative ring $R$.

Let $Y=\CP^{\infty}$ with basepoint $y_{0}$ and $Q=(S^{1}\times Y)/(S^{1}\times\{y_{0}\})$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #good-pair}
$\widetilde H^{*}(Q;R)\cong H^{*}(S^{1}\times Y,\,S^{1}\times\{y_{0}\};R)$ as rings.

::: pf-proof
$S^{1}\times Y$ is a CW complex and $S^{1}\times\{y_{0}\}$ is a subcomplex, so the pair is a good pair: the quotient map induces $H^{*}(Q;R)\cong H^{*}(S^{1}\times Y,S^{1}\times\{y_{0}\};R)$, and the reduced cohomology of $Q$ is the cohomology of the pair because the pair has a collar neighbourhood that deformation retracts onto the subspace. The identification is one of rings, cup product on the quotient corresponding to the relative cup product.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #additive}
$H^{*}(S^{1}\times Y,S^{1}\times\{y_{0}\};R)\cong H^{*}(S^{1};R)\otimes_{R}\widetilde H^{*}(Y;R)$, so additively the positive-degree classes of $Q$ are $u^{k}$ and $s u^{k}$ for $k\ge1$.

::: pf-proof
The relative cross product $H^{i}(S^{1};R)\otimes H^{j}(Y,\{y_{0}\};R)\to H^{i+j}(S^{1}\times Y,S^{1}\times\{y_{0}\};R)$ is an isomorphism because the cohomology of $Y$ is free in each degree, $H^{*}(\CP^{\infty};R)\cong R[u]$ with $\abs u=2$ (for $R=\ZZ$ this is the standard computation, and freeness makes the change of rings harmless); this is the relative Künneth theorem for the good pair, and it identifies the two sides as graded $R$-modules. Here $\widetilde H^{*}(Y;R)$ is spanned by $u^{k}$, $k\ge1$, and $H^{*}(S^{1};R)$ by $1$ and $s$, with $\abs s=1$; the tensor product is therefore spanned by $u^{k}$ and $s\otimes u^{k}$, written $su^{k}$, for $k\ge1$, together with the unit in degree $0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiplicative}
The products are $u^{i}u^{j}=u^{i+j}$, $(su^{i})u^{j}=su^{i+j}$, $s^{2}=0$, so the ring is $R[u,v]/(v^{2})$ with $v=su$ of degree $3$.

::: pf-proof
The cross product is compatible with cup products, so the product of $a\otimes b$ and $a'\otimes b'$ is $aa'\otimes bb'$; hence $u^{i}u^{j}=u^{i+j}$ and $su^{i}\cdot u^{j}=su^{i+j}$. For two odd classes $su^{i}\cdot su^{j}=s^{2}u^{i+j}$, and $s^{2}\in H^{2}(S^{1};R)=0$ because $H^{2}(S^{1};R)=0$; so the product vanishes (also $s$ has odd degree, so $s^{2}=-s^{2}$ by graded commutativity). Writing $v=su\in H^{3}(Q;R)$, every odd-degree class is $vu^{k-1}$ for some $k\ge1$ by step [](#additive){.pf-ref}, every even-degree one is $u^{k}$, and $v^{2}=0$; so the ring is generated by $u$ and $v$ with the single relation $v^{2}=0$, that is
$$
H^{*}(Q;R)\cong R[u,v]/(v^{2}),\qquad \gradeddegree{u}=2,\ \gradeddegree{v}=3 .
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product}
$H^{*}(S^{3}\times\CP^{\infty};R)\cong R[u,v]/(v^{2})$ with $\abs u=2$, $\abs v=3$, so the two rings are isomorphic.

::: pf-proof
The Künneth theorem applies since $H^{*}(\CP^{\infty};R)=R[u]$ is free in each degree, and gives
$$
H^{*}(S^{3}\times\CP^{\infty};R)\cong H^{*}(S^{3};R)\otimes_{R}R[u]
$$
with the product being the tensor product of the products. Now $H^{*}(S^{3};R)$ is $R$ in degrees $0$ and $3$ and zero between and above, so the tensor product is $R[u]\oplus vR[u]$ with $v$ the degree-$3$ generator, and $v^{2}\in H^{6}(S^{3};R)=0$; thus the ring is $R[u,v]/(v^{2})$, which is the ring of step [](#multiplicative){.pf-ref}. Hence $Q$ and $S^{3}\times\CP^{\infty}$ have isomorphic cohomology rings with coefficients $R$, in particular with $\ZZ$ coefficients.
:::
