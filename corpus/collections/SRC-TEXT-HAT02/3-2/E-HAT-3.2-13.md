::: {.problem}
Describe $H^*(\CP^\infty / \CP^1; \ZZ)$ as a ring with finitely many multiplicative generators.
How does this ring compare with $H^*(S^6 \times \quatprojective^\infty; \ZZ)$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $u\in H^2(\CP^\infty;\ZZ)$ be the standard generator. For
$$
Q=\CP^\infty/\CP^1
$$
one has
$$
\widetilde H^*(Q;\ZZ)
\cong H^*(\CP^\infty,\CP^1;\ZZ).
$$
Since $\CP^1$ contains exactly the cells in dimensions $0$ and $2$, the cellular cochain complex of the pair, which computes this group by Theorem 3.5 (p.203) applied to the quotient $Q$, has $\ZZ$ in each even degree $\ge4$ and zero otherwise, so the relative group is infinite cyclic in each even degree $\ge4$ and zero otherwise. Under the map to $H^*(\CP^\infty)$, its degree-$2k$ generator maps to $u^k$ for $k\ge2$, and multiplication is inherited from $\ZZ[u]$.

Hence the positive-degree part is the subring generated by $u^2$ and $u^3$. Put
$$
a=u^2,\qquad b=u^3.
$$
Then $\gradeddegree{a}=4$, $\gradeddegree{b}=6$, and the only relation is
$$
b^2=a^3.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{H^*(Q;\ZZ)\cong
\ZZ[a,b]/(b^2-a^3),\qquad \gradeddegree{a}=4,\ \gradeddegree{b}=6.}
$$

For comparison, $H^*(\quatprojective^\infty;\ZZ)=\ZZ[y]$ with $|y|=4$ by Example 3.12 (p.213), and $H^*(S^6;\ZZ)=\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ in degrees $0$ and $6$, so Theorem 3.15 (p.216) gives
$$
H^*(S^6\times\quatprojective^\infty;\ZZ)
\cong \ZZ[x,y]/(x^2),
\qquad \gradeddegree{x}=6,\ \gradeddegree{y}=4.
$$
The two graded groups are isomorphic: both have one copy of $\ZZ$ in degree $0$ and in every even degree $\ge4$. But the rings are not isomorphic. In $H^*(Q)$ the degree-$6$ generator $b$ satisfies
$$
b^2=a^3\ne0,
$$
whereas in $H^*(S^6\times\quatprojective^\infty)$ the degree-$6$ generator $x$ has square zero.
:::
