::: {.problem}
Describe $H^*(\CP^\infty / \CP^1; \ZZ)$ as a ring with finitely many multiplicative generators.
How does this ring compare with $H^*(S^6 \times \quatprojective^\infty; \ZZ)$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The statement is Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology* (2002), §3.2 Exercise 13, printed p. 230. No retained solution set covers it — Guanyu Li's set stops at §3.2 Exercise 11 and Stephen V. Sam's at Exercise 11 as well, so neither reaches Exercise 13 — and the argument below is the card's own.

Write $Q=\CP^\infty/\CP^1$ and let $u\in H^2(\CP^\infty;\ZZ)$ be the standard generator, so $H^*(\CP^\infty;\ZZ)=\ZZ[u]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #relative}
$\widetilde H^*(Q;\ZZ)\cong H^*(\CP^\infty,\CP^1;\ZZ)$ as rings.

::: pf-proof
$(\CP^\infty,\CP^1)$ is a CW pair, so the quotient map $\CP^\infty\to Q$ collapsing $\CP^1$ to a point induces isomorphisms $\widetilde H^n(Q)\cong H^n(\CP^\infty,\CP^1)$ for all $n$ (Proposition 2.22, printed p. 123); the cup product is carried over because the collapsing map is a map of pairs and the relative cup product restricts to the absolute one.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #groups}
$H^n(\CP^\infty,\CP^1;\ZZ)=\ZZ$ for even $n\ge4$ and $0$ otherwise.

::: pf-proof
$\CP^\infty$ has exactly one cell in each even dimension and none in odd dimensions, and $\CP^1$ consists of the cells in dimensions $0$ and $2$ (Example 1.14 and the cell structure of projective space, printed p. 7). Hence the relative cellular chain complex has $\ZZ$ in each even degree $\ge4$ and zero elsewhere, and all its boundary maps vanish because they land in odd degrees. Cohomology with $\ZZ$ coefficients of this complex is computed by Theorem 3.5, printed p. 203, giving the stated groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #generators}
The degree-$2k$ generator of $H^*(\CP^\infty,\CP^1;\ZZ)$ maps to $u^k$ under the map induced by the inclusion of pairs $(\CP^\infty,\varnothing)\to(\CP^\infty,\CP^1)$, for every $k\ge2$.

::: pf-proof
Both groups are free with one generator in degree $2k$, and the map in question is the quotient map of cochain complexes identifying the absolute cellular cochain in degree $2k$ with the relative one. The absolute generator in degree $2k$ is $u^k$ because $H^*(\CP^\infty)=\ZZ[u]$ with $u$ the degree-$2$ generator and cup product corresponding to the product of cochains on the cell structure with one cell per even dimension.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ring}
$H^*(Q;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ[a,b]/(b^2-a^3)$ with $\abs a=4$ and $\abs b=6$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#relative){.pf-ref} and [](#generators){.pf-ref} the positive-degree part of $H^*(Q)$ is the image of $\ZZ[u]$ in degrees $\ge4$, that is $\ZZ\oplus u^2\ZZ[u]$, with multiplication inherited from $\ZZ[u]$. Set $a=u^2$ and $b=u^3$, of degrees $4$ and $6$. Every $u^k$ with $k\ge2$ is a product of $a$'s and $b$'s, since every $k\ge2$ is a nonnegative combination of $2$ and $3$, so $a$ and $b$ generate. The kernel of $\ZZ[a,b]\to\ZZ[u]$, $a\mapsto u^2$, $b\mapsto u^3$, is generated by $b^2-a^3$: in degree $4k$ the monomials $a^{i}b^{j}$ with $2i+3j=k$ are distinct basis elements of the free abelian group $\ZZ[u^{2k}]$, and the only relation among the exponents of the semigroup $\langle2,3\rangle\subset\ZZ_{\ge0}$ is $3\cdot2=2\cdot3$, corresponding to $a^3=b^2$. Hence the subring is $\ZZ[a,b]/(b^2-a^3)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product}
$H^*(S^6\times\HP^\infty;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ[x,y]/(x^2)$ with $\abs x=6$ and $\abs y=4$.

::: pf-proof
$H^*(\HP^\infty;\ZZ)=\ZZ[y]$ with $\abs y=4$ (Example 3.12, printed p. 213), and $H^*(S^6;\ZZ)=\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ in degrees $0$ and $6$. The Künneth formula for cohomology over a field applies directly; over $\ZZ$ the relevant statement is that the cohomology ring of the product of a free finitely generated complex with another is the tensor product of the rings, Theorem 3.15 with Theorem 3.16's ring statement, printed p. 216. The tensor product $\ZZ[y]\otimes\ZZ[x]/(x^2)$ with $\abs x=6$ is $\ZZ[x,y]/(x^2)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #comparison}
The graded groups of $H^*(Q)$ and of $H^*(S^6\times\HP^\infty)$ are isomorphic, but the rings are not.

::: pf-proof
Both rings are free abelian of rank one in degree $0$ and in every even degree $\ge4$: for $H^*(Q)$ this is step [](#groups){.pf-ref}, and for $\ZZ[x,y]/(x^2)$ the monomials $y^i$ and $xy^i$ of degrees $4i$ and $4i+6$ exhibit one basis element in each even degree $\ge4$, none in odd degrees, and $x^2=0$ removes no basis element of degree $\ge4$ other than the redundant $x^2$. So the graded groups coincide. In $H^*(Q)$ the degree-$6$ part is $\ZZ\cdot b$ by step [](#ring){.pf-ref}, and $b^2=a^3\ne0$; in $\ZZ[x,y]/(x^2)$ the degree-$6$ part is $\ZZ\cdot x$ (a degree-$2$ element does not exist, and $y$ has degree $4$, so no combination produces another degree-$6$ class), and $x^2=0$. A ring isomorphism would carry a generator of the degree-$6$ group to one, hence would carry $b$ to $\pm x$, and $b^2\ne0$ would force $x^2\ne0$. So no ring isomorphism exists.

:::
