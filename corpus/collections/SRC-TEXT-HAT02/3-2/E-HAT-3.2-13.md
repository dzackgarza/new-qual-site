Describe $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^\infty / \mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z})$ as a ring with finitely many multiplicative generators.
How does this ring compare with $H^*(S^6 \times \mathbb{HP}^\infty; \mathbb{Z})$?

::: {.solution}
Let $u\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^\infty;\mathbb Z)$ be the standard generator. For
\[
Q=\mathbb{CP}^\infty/\mathbb{CP}^1
\]
one has
\[
\widetilde H^*(Q;\mathbb Z)
\cong H^*(\mathbb{CP}^\infty,\mathbb{CP}^1;\mathbb Z).
\]
Since $\mathbb{CP}^1$ contains exactly the cells in dimensions $0$ and $2$, this relative group is infinite cyclic in each even degree $\ge4$ and zero otherwise. Under the map to $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^\infty)$, its degree-$2k$ generator maps to $u^k$ for $k\ge2$, and multiplication is inherited from $\mathbb Z[u]$.

Hence the positive-degree part is the subring generated by $u^2$ and $u^3$. Put
\[
a=u^2,\qquad b=u^3.
\]
Then $|a|=4$, $|b|=6$, and the only relation is
\[
b^2=a^3.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{H^*(Q;\mathbb Z)\cong
\mathbb Z[a,b]/(b^2-a^3),\qquad |a|=4,\ |b|=6.}
\]

For comparison,
\[
H^*(S^6\times\mathbb{HP}^\infty;\mathbb Z)
\cong \mathbb Z[x,y]/(x^2),
\qquad |x|=6,\ |y|=4.
\]
The two graded groups are isomorphic: both have one copy of $\mathbb Z$ in degree $0$ and in every even degree $\ge4$. But the rings are not isomorphic. In $H^*(Q)$ the degree-$6$ generator $b$ satisfies
\[
b^2=a^3\ne0,
\]
whereas in $H^*(S^6\times\mathbb{HP}^\infty)$ the degree-$6$ generator $x$ has square zero.
:::
