::: {.problem}
Use cup products to compute the map $H^*(\CP^n; \ZZ) \to H^*(\CP^n; \ZZ)$ induced by the map $\CP^n \to \CP^n$ that is a quotient of the map $\CC^{n+1} \to \CC^{n+1}$ raising each coordinate to the $d$th power, $(z_0, \ldots, z_n) \mapsto (z_0^d, \ldots, z_n^d)$, for a fixed integer $d > 0$.
[First do the case $n = 1$.]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For $n = 1$, the map $\CP^1 \to \CP^1$ is $[z_0 : z_1] \mapsto [z_0^d : z_1^d]$, which has degree $d$.

::: pf-proof

Under $\CP^1=\CC\cup\{\infty\}=S^2$ the map is $z\mapsto z^d$. A point $y\ne0,\infty$ has the $d$ distinct preimages $z$ with $z^d=y$, and at each of them the map is locally injective, hence of local degree $1$; Proposition 2.30 (p.136) then gives total degree $d$. (Hatcher's Example 2.31, p.135, is the same computation for $z\mapsto z^k$ on $S^1$.)

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Hence on $H^2(\CP^1) = \ZZ$ (generated by $x$), the induced map sends $x \mapsto d x$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref}: the degree is defined by $f_*[S^2]=d[S^2]$ (p.134), and $H^2(S^2;\ZZ)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_2(S^2),\ZZ)$ by Theorem 3.2 (p.195), an identification under which $f^*$ becomes precomposition with $f_*$, so $f^*x=dx$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For general $n$, $H^*(\CP^n) = \ZZ[x]/(x^{n+1})$ with $\gradeddegree{x} = 2$.

::: pf-proof

Example 3.12 (p.213): $H^*(\CP^n;\ZZ)=\ZZ[x]/(x^{n+1})$ with $|x|=2$, so $H^{2k}=\ZZ$ for $k\le n$ and $0$ otherwise.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The map $f : \CP^n \to \CP^n$ satisfies $f^*(x) = d x$.

::: pf-proof

Naturality (Proposition 3.10, p.210) applied to the inclusion $i:\CP^1\hookrightarrow\CP^n$ of a coordinate line gives $i^*f^*(x)=(fi)^*(x)$; the restriction $fi$ is the map of step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, so $(fi)^*(i^*x)=d\,i^*x$ by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, and $i^*:H^2(\CP^n)\to H^2(\CP^1)$ is an isomorphism of copies of $\ZZ$ (Example 3.12, p.213). Hence $f^*(x)=dx$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Hence $f^*(x^k) = f^*(x)^k = (dx)^k = d^k x^k$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} and the ring homomorphism property (cup product).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Therefore the induced map $f^* : H^*(\CP^n) \to H^*(\CP^n)$ is multiplication by $d^k$ on $H^{2k}$, i.e. $f^*(x^k) = d^k x^k$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

:::
