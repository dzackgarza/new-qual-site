Determine all cohomology operations $H^1(X; \mathbb{Z}) \to H^n(X; \mathbb{Z})$, $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z}) \to H^n(X; \mathbb{Z})$, and $H^1(X; \mathbb{Z}_p) \to H^n(X; \mathbb{Z}_p)$ for $p$ prime.

::: {.solution}
Cohomology operations
\[
H^m(-;G)\longrightarrow H^n(-;H)
\]
are represented by classes in
\[
H^n(K(G,m);H).
\]
We apply this to the three cases.

For \(H^1(-;\mathbb Z)\), the representing space is
\[
K(\mathbb Z,1)=S^1.
\]
Hence
\[
H^n(S^1;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&n=1,\\
0,&n>1.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the only positive-degree operations are
\[
\boxed{u\longmapsto a\,u\qquad(a\in\mathbb Z)}
\]
in degree \(1\); all operations to \(H^n\) for \(n>1\) are zero.

For \(H^2(-;\mathbb Z)\), use
\[
K(\mathbb Z,2)=\mathbb{CP}^\infty,
\qquad
H^*(\mathbb{CP}^\infty;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[x],\quad |x|=2.
\]
Therefore
\[
H^n(K(\mathbb Z,2);\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z\,x^j,&n=2j,\\
0,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Hence every operation is
\[
\boxed{u\longmapsto a\,u^j\quad(n=2j),\qquad a\in\mathbb Z,}
\]
and there are no nonzero operations in odd target degree.

Finally consider \(H^1(-;\mathbb Z_p)\). The representing space is \(K(\mathbb Z_p,1)\). For \(p=2\),
\[
H^*(K(\mathbb Z_2,1);\mathbb Z_2)=\mathbb Z_2[x],
\qquad |x|=1.
\]
Thus in every degree \(n>0\) the group of operations is one-dimensional, generated by
\[
\boxed{u\longmapsto u^n.}
\]

For odd \(p\),
\[
H^*(K(\mathbb Z_p,1);\mathbb Z_p)
=\Lambda(x)\otimes\mathbb Z_p[y],
\qquad |x|=1,\quad |y|=2,\quad y=\beta x,
\]
where \(\beta\) is the mod-\(p\) Bockstein. Thus there is again one basis operation in every positive degree:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
H^1(-;\mathbb Z_p)&\to H^{2j}(-;\mathbb Z_p),
&u&\mapsto (\beta u)^j,\\
H^1(-;\mathbb Z_p)&\to H^{2j+1}(-;\mathbb Z_p),
&u&\mapsto u(\beta u)^j.
\end{aligned}}
\]
All operations are scalar multiples of these by elements of \(\mathbb Z_p\).
:::
