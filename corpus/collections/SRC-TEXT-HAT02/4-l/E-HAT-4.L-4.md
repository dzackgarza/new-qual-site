::: {.problem}
Show there is no fiber bundle $S^7 \to S^{23} \to \OP^2$. [Compute the cohomology ring of the mapping cone of the projection $S^{23} \to \OP^2$ via Poincaré duality or the Thom isomorphism.]
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the Hatcher sets in the checkout hold nothing from §4.L. The argument is the card's own, checked against Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology* (2002), §4.L Exercise 4; the two theorems cited are Corollary 4D.9, and Theorem 4L.12.

Suppose there is a fiber bundle $S^7\to S^{23}\xrightarrow{p}\OP^2$, and let $C_p=\OP^2\cup_pCS^{23}$ be the mapping cone of the projection.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #manifold}
$C_p$ is a closed $24$-manifold, and its cohomology is $H^*(C_p;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ\{1,x,x^2,x^3\}$ with $\abs{x}=8$.

::: pf-proof
The disk bundle $D^8\to E\to\OP^2$ associated to the sphere bundle has dimension $8+16=24$, and its boundary is the sphere bundle, which is $S^{23}$ by hypothesis. Coning each fibre turns $E$ into the mapping cone $C_p=E/\partial E$, the one-point compactification of $E$; that is a closed $24$-manifold. Its cohomology is computed by duality for the compact manifold $E$ with boundary: $H^k(C_p;\ZZ)=H^k(E,\partial E;\ZZ)\cong H_{24-k}(E;\ZZ)$ by Poincaré--Lefschetz duality, and $E$ deformation retracts to $\OP^2$, whose cohomology is $\ZZ$ in degrees $0,8,16$. So $H^k(C_p;\ZZ)$ is $\ZZ$ for $k=0,8,16,24$ and $0$ otherwise, generated by $1,x,x^2,x^3$ with $\abs{x}=8$; this is the Thom isomorphism of Corollary 4D.9, for the disk bundle.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #top-class}
$x^3\ne0$: it generates $H^{24}(C_p;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#manifold){.pf-ref} the group $H^{24}(C_p;\ZZ)$ is infinite cyclic. The cup product pairing $H^8\otimes H^{16}\to H^{24}$ is unimodular, by Poincaré duality for the closed oriented manifold $C_p$; since $H^8$ and $H^{16}$ are generated by $x$ and $x^2$, the product $x\cdot x^2=x^3$ generates $H^{24}$. So $x^3\ne0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #instability}
Modulo $3$, $P^4x=x^3$.

::: pf-proof
Theorem 4L.12, gives the instability axiom $P^ix=x^p$ when $2i=\abs{x}$ and $x$ is the reduction of an integral class. Here $\abs{x}=8$ and $i=4$ satisfy $2\cdot4=8$, and $x$ is the mod $3$ reduction of the integral generator of $H^8(C_p;\ZZ)$ from step [](#manifold){.pf-ref}; the axiom is stated for $p=3$, so $P^4x=x^3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #adem}
In the Steenrod algebra $\mathcal A_3$, $P^1P^3=P^4$.

::: pf-proof
The Adem relation for odd $p$ (printed p. 506) is
$$
P^aP^b=\sum_j(-1)^{a+j}\binom{(p-1)(b-j)-1}{a-pj}P^{a+b-j}P^j,
$$
the sum over $0\le j\le\lfloor a/p\rfloor$. With $p=3$, $a=1$, $b=3$ we have $\lfloor1/3\rfloor=0$, so only $j=0$ occurs, with coefficient
$$
(-1)^{1}\binom{(3-1)(3-0)-1}{1-0}=-\binom{5}{1}=-5\equiv1\pmod 3 .
$$
Hence $P^1P^3=P^4$ in $\mathcal A_3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #contradiction}
$P^3x=0$ and hence $x^3=0$, contradicting step [](#top-class){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof
$P^3$ raises degree by $2\cdot3\cdot(3-1)=12$, so $P^3x\in H^{20}(C_p;\ZZ_3)$, which is $0$ by step [](#manifold){.pf-ref} since the integral cohomology of $C_p$ vanishes in degree $20$ and $H^{20}(-;\ZZ_3)$ is computed from it by the universal coefficient theorem with all relevant groups free. So $P^3x=0$. Then steps [](#instability){.pf-ref} and [](#adem){.pf-ref} give
$$
x^3=P^4x=P^1P^3x=P^1(0)=0,
$$
which contradicts $x^3\ne0$. Therefore no such fiber bundle exists.
:::
