::: {.problem}
Show that the subalgebra of $\mathcal{A}_2$ generated by $Sq^i$ for $i \leq 2$ has dimension 8 as a vector space over $\ZZ_2$, with multiplicative structure encoded in the following diagram, where diagonal lines indicate left-multiplication by $Sq^1$ and horizontal lines indicate left-multiplication by $Sq^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $a=Sq^1$ and $b=Sq^2$. The Adem relations give $a^2=0$, $ab=Sq^3$ and $aba=b^2$; from these come $b^2a=ab^2=b^4=0$ and $abab=b^3=baba$, so the eight words $1,a,b,ab,ba,b^2,bab,b^3$ span the subalgebra. Their independence is read off from the admissible basis, and the left multiplication table follows from the same relations. The argument is this card's own: no retained solution set covers this exercise.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #adem}
$a^2=0$, $ab=Sq^3$, and $aba=b^2$.

::: pf-proof
The mod-2 Adem relations (printed p.~506) read $Sq^aSq^b=\sum_{c=0}^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}\binom{b-c-1}{a-2c}Sq^{a+b-c}Sq^c$ when $a<2b$. For $a=b=1$ the only term is $c=0$, with coefficient $\binom{1-0-1}{1-0}=\binom01=0$, so $a^2=0$. For $Sq^1Sq^2$ one has $a=1<4=2b$ and the only coefficient is $\binom{2-0-1}{1-0}=\binom11=1$, so $ab=Sq^3$. For $Sq^2Sq^2$ one has $a=b=2$, with coefficients $\binom{2-c-1}{2-2c}$: for $c=0$ this is $\binom12=0$ and for $c=1$ it is $\binom01=1$, so $b^2=Sq^3Sq^1$. Hence $aba=Sq^3Sq^1=b^2$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #bab}
$bab=Sq^5+Sq^4Sq^1$ and $b^3=Sq^5Sq^1$.

::: pf-proof
$bab=b(ab)=Sq^2Sq^3$, and here $a=2<6=2b$ with coefficients $\binom{3-1}{2}=\binom22=1$ for $c=0$ and $\binom{3-1-1}{2-2}=\binom10=1$ for $c=1$, so $bab=Sq^5+Sq^4Sq^1$. Then $b^3=b\cdot b^2=Sq^2Sq^3Sq^1=(Sq^5+Sq^4Sq^1)Sq^1=Sq^5Sq^1+Sq^4Sq^1Sq^1$, and the second term vanishes by $a^2=Sq^1Sq^1=0$ from step [](#adem){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #consequences}
$b^2a=0$, $ab^2=0$, $b^4=0$, and $abab=b^3=baba$.

::: pf-proof
$b^2a=Sq^3Sq^1Sq^1=Sq^3a^2=0$ by step [](#adem){.pf-ref}. Also $Sq^1Sq^3=0$: in the Adem relation with $a=1<6=2b$ the only coefficient is $\binom{3-1}{1}=\binom21=2\equiv0\pmod2$; hence $ab^2=Sq^1Sq^3Sq^1=0$ and $b^4=b^2b^2=Sq^3Sq^1Sq^3Sq^1=Sq^3(Sq^1Sq^3)Sq^1=0$. For the last pair, $abab=(ab)(ab)=Sq^3Sq^3$, and the Adem relation for $Sq^3Sq^3$ has coefficients $\binom{3-0-1}{3-0}=\binom23=0$ at $c=0$ and $\binom{3-1-1}{3-2}=\binom11=1$ at $c=1$, giving $Sq^5Sq^1=b^3$ by step [](#bab){.pf-ref}; while $baba=(bab)a=(Sq^5+Sq^4Sq^1)Sq^1=Sq^5Sq^1+Sq^4(Sq^1Sq^1)=Sq^5Sq^1=b^3$, again by $a^2=0$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #span}
Every word in $a$ and $b$ is a linear combination of $1,a,b,ab,ba,b^2,bab,b^3$.

::: pf-proof
Use the identities above as rewrite rules. A word containing $aa$ is zero by $a^2=0$, so a nonzero word is alternating; in an alternating word every factor $aba$ may be replaced by $b^2$, and repeating shortens the word until no $aba$ remains. In an alternating word with no factor $aba$, consecutive $a$'s are separated by at least three letters, so at most one $a$ survives and the word is $b^i$, $b^ia$, $ab^j$ or $b^iab^j$ with $i,j\ge0$. The powers are bounded by $b^4=0$; the four words with a factor $ab^2$, $ab^3$, $b^2a$ or $b^3a$ are zero by steps [](#consequences){.pf-ref} and [](#bab){.pf-ref}, since $b^3a=Sq^5Sq^1Sq^1=0$ and $ab^3=Sq^1Sq^5Sq^1=0$ because $Sq^1Sq^5=Sq^6$ with $\binom{5-1}{1}=4\equiv0$ and then $Sq^1Sq^1=0$. What remains is $1,a,b,b^2,b^3,ab,ba,bab$, the eight listed elements. As every rewrite is an identity in the algebra, the original word is a linear combination of the eight.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #indep}
The eight elements $1,a,b,ab,ba,b^2,bab,b^3$ are linearly independent over $\ZZ_2$.

::: pf-proof
Identify them with admissible monomials in degrees: $1$ in degree $0$, $a=Sq^1$ in degree $1$, $b=Sq^2$ in degree $2$, $ab=Sq^3$ and $ba=Sq^2Sq^1$ in degree $3$ (distinct admissible monomials, since $3\ge2\cdot1$ and $2\ge2\cdot1$), $b^2=Sq^3Sq^1$ in degree $4$ (admissible, $3\ge2\cdot1$), $bab=Sq^5+Sq^4Sq^1$ in degree $5$, and $b^3=Sq^5Sq^1$ in degree $6$ (admissible). By step [](#bab){.pf-ref} $bab$ is a nonzero combination of the two distinct admissible monomials in degree $5$, hence nonzero. The admissible monomials form a $\ZZ_2$-basis of $\mathcal A_2$ (printed p.~500). A linear relation among the eight decomposes by degree, and the only degree carrying two of them is degree $3$, where $Sq^3$ and $Sq^2Sq^1$ are independent basis elements; so all coefficients vanish.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #table}
Left multiplication by $a$ and by $b$ on the basis is
$$
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
z&1&a&b&ab&ba&b^2&bab&b^3\\ \hline
az&a&0&ab&0&b^2&0&b^3&0\\
bz&b&ba&b^2&bab&0&b^3&0&0.
\end{array}
$$

::: pf-proof
Row $a$: $a\cdot1=a$, $a\cdot a=a^2=0$, $a\cdot b=ab$, $a\cdot ab=a^2b=0$, $a\cdot ba=aba=b^2$, $a\cdot b^2=ab^2=0$, $a\cdot bab=(aba)b=b^2b=b^3$, $a\cdot b^3=ab^3=0$. Row $b$: $b\cdot1=b$, $b\cdot a=ba$, $b\cdot b=b^2$, $b\cdot ab=bab$, $b\cdot ba=b^2a=0$, $b\cdot b^2=b^3$, $b\cdot bab=b^2ab=(b^2a)b=0$, $b\cdot b^3=b^4=0$. Every vanishing used is from steps [](#adem){.pf-ref}, [](#bab){.pf-ref} and [](#consequences){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
The subalgebra generated by $Sq^i$ for $i\le2$ has dimension $8$ over $\ZZ_2$, and its left multiplications are the displayed table.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#span){.pf-ref} and [](#indep){.pf-ref} make the eight elements a basis, giving dimension $8$; step [](#table){.pf-ref} gives left multiplication by $Sq^1$ and by $Sq^2$, which are the diagonal and horizontal arrows of the diagram.
:::
