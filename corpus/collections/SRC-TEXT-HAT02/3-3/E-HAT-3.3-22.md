::: {.problem}
Show that $H_c^n(X \times \RR; G) \approx H_c^{n-1}(X; G)$ for all $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K\subset X$ be compact and $I=[-a,a]\subset\RR$ with $a>0$. The sets $K\times I$ are cofinal among compact subsets of $X\times\RR$: the projections of any compact subset are compact and lie in such a product. The reduction is the relative cross product with a generator $1_I\in H^1(\RR,\RR-I;\ZZ)$,
$$
H^{n-1}(X,X-K;\ZZ)\longrightarrow H^n(X\times\RR,(X\times\RR)-(K\times I);\ZZ),
\qquad \alpha\longmapsto\alpha\times1_I .
$$
Notice that $(X\times\RR)-(K\times I)$ is exactly $(X-K)\times\RR\,\cup\,X\times(\RR-I)$, so this is the product pair of $(X,X-K)$ and $(\RR,\RR-I)$.

The isomorphism cannot be inferred from the cohomology cross product for CW pairs: $X$ is arbitrary, and relative singular cochain groups need not be free. Instead work with relative singular **chain** complexes and apply $\operatorname{Hom}(-,G)$ only after the degree shift. Write
$$
C=S_*(X,X-K).
$$
Its degree-$j$ group is free on those singular $j$-simplices of $X$ that do not lie in $X-K$.

For the interval factor, let $J$ be the free chain complex with a single basis element $e$ in degree $1$ and with zero differential. The inclusion $e:(I,\partial I)\to(\RR,\RR-I)$ defines a relative cycle and hence a chain map $J\to S_*(\RR,\RR-I)$. To determine its homology, use the pair sequence
$$
0=H_1(\RR)\longrightarrow H_1(\RR,\RR-I)
\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }\widetilde H_0(\RR-I)\longrightarrow\widetilde H_0(\RR)=0 .
$$
The middle target is $\ZZ$, generated by $[a]-[-a]$ because $\RR-I$ has two contractible components. In every other degree the pair group vanishes since $H_*(\RR)$ and $H_*(\RR-I)$ vanish above degree zero. Thus $e$ is a generator: $J\to S_*(\RR,\RR-I)$ is a quasi-isomorphism of bounded-below free chain complexes. Over $\ZZ$, such a quasi-isomorphism has a chain-homotopy inverse, so its degree-one generator is an honest free model of the interval pair; this does not call that pair an excision of $(I,\partial I)$.

Now take the Eilenberg--Zilber tensor product for the product relative pair. Since $J$ is $\ZZ$ concentrated in degree $1$, $C\otimes J$ is simply the suspension of $C$. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-,G)$ to the explicit isomorphism $C_j\otimes\ZZ e\cong C_j$ therefore identifies the cohomology of the product relative pair with
$$
H^n\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}(C\otimes J,G)\bigr)
\cong H^{n-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}(C,G)\bigr)
=H^{n-1}(X,X-K;G).
$$
Up to the sign convention for tensor products, the generator on the interval coordinate is $1_I$, and this isomorphism is precisely the relative cross product $\alpha\mapsto\alpha\times1_I$. No claim about freeness of cochain groups and no comparison of Hom and Ext terms is needed. These maps commute with inclusions when $K$ and $I$ increase. Taking the filtered direct limit gives
$$
H_c^n(X\times\RR;G)\cong H_c^{n-1}(X;G).
$$
:::
