::: {.problem}
Show that $H_c^n(X \times \RR; G) \approx H_c^{n-1}(X; G)$ for all $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K\subset X$ be compact and $I=[-a,a]\subset\RR$ with $a>0$. The sets $K\times I$ are cofinal among compact subsets of $X\times\RR$: the projections of any compact subset are compact and lie in such a product. The reduction is the relative cross product with a generator $1_I\in H^1(\RR,\RR-I;\ZZ)$,
$$
H^{n-1}(X,X-K;\ZZ)\longrightarrow H^n(X\times\RR,(X\times\RR)-(K\times I);\ZZ),
\qquad \alpha\longmapsto\alpha\times1_I .
$$
Notice that $(X\times\RR)-(K\times I)$ is exactly $(X-K)\times\RR\,\cup\,X\times(\RR-I)$, so this is the product pair of $(X,X-K)$ and $(\RR,\RR-I)$.

The isomorphism cannot be cited directly to Theorem 3.18, whose hypothesis is that both pairs are CW pairs; $X$ in this exercise is arbitrary. Instead use the same algebraic Künneth argument for free relative singular cochain complexes. The pair $(\RR,\RR-I)$ has a free cellular cochain model concentrated entirely in degree $1$, where it is $\ZZ$ with zero differential: excision identifies it with $(I,\partial I)$, and its relative cellular computation is the same computation as for $H^1(S^1)$. Hence its only cohomology is the free rank-one group $H^1$, generated by $1_I$. Consequently the algebraic Künneth formula for the product of this model with the free relative singular cochain complex of $(X,X-K)$ has one term in total degree $n$, namely $H^{n-1}(X,X-K;\ZZ)\otimes1_I$, and no Tor term. The product map is therefore the displayed isomorphism, with no CW condition on $X$.

For arbitrary coefficients $G$, naturality of the universal coefficient sequence (Theorem 3.2, p. 195) for $(X,X-K)$ and for the product pair completes the argument: the induced Hom and Ext groups in the two sequences agree because the integral cross-product map is an isomorphism. Thus
$$
H^{n-1}(X,X-K;G)\xrightarrow{\ \alpha\mapsto\alpha\times1_I\ }
H^n(X\times\RR,(X\times\RR)-(K\times I);G)
$$
is an isomorphism. These maps commute with inclusions when $K$ and $I$ increase. Taking the filtered direct limit gives
$$
H_c^n(X\times\RR;G)\cong H_c^{n-1}(X;G).
$$
:::
