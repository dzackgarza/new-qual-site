::: {.problem}
Make the transfer sequence explicit in the case of a trivial covering $\tilde{X} \to X$, where $\tilde{X} = X \times S^0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Hatcher's complete published transfer construction in **§2.B**, the passage that sets up Proposition 2B.6, specializes directly to $X\times S^0\to X$. He defines the short exact sequence of chain complexes by sending a singular simplex to the sum of its two lifts and projecting from the total space. The computation below carries out that construction in the two-sheet coordinates; no separate exactness argument is needed. This is a checked instantiation of the general theorem, not a found complete published solution of Exercise 2.B.9, whose source obligation remains open.

Hatcher does not call his sequence of chain complexes split, and it is not split in general. A chain map $s$ with $p_\sharp s=\mathrm{id}$ would exhibit $C_*(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\cong C_*(X;\ZZ_2)\oplus C_*(X;\ZZ_2)$ as chain complexes and hence $H_*(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\cong H_*(X;\ZZ_2)^{\oplus2}$, which for the double cover $S^1\to S^1$, $z\mapsto z^2$, fails in degree $1$: the left side is $H_1(S^1;\ZZ_2)=\ZZ_2$, the right side would be $H_1(S^1;\ZZ_2)^{\oplus2}=\ZZ_2^{\oplus2}$. What is split is the transfer sequence of the *trivial* covering, whose two-sheet coordinates are the point of the exercise.

Use $\ZZ_2$ coefficients, as in the transfer sequence of §2.B.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #trivial-chains}
In two-sheet coordinates the transfer and projection are $\tau(c)=(c,c)$ and $p_\#(a,b)=a+b$, giving the exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow C_*(X;\ZZ_2)
\xrightarrow{\Delta}
C_*(X;\ZZ_2)^2
\xrightarrow{+}
C_*(X;\ZZ_2)\longrightarrow0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Since $X\times S^0=X\amalg X$, its singular chain complex is $C_*(X;\ZZ_2)\oplus C_*(X;\ZZ_2)$. The two lifts of a base simplex are its images in the two copies, hence $\tau(c)=(c,c)$; projection adds them. Here $\tau$ is the sum of the two lifts and $p_\#$ the projection as Hatcher's §2.B constructs them and as the general $n$-sheeted transfer is defined in §3.G; the book gives the §2.B transfer as a construction in the text, before the proof of Proposition 2B.6, and §3.G extends it to any coefficient group. The two-sheet coordinate form for the trivial covering is the card's own reading of that construction. The diagonal is injective, addition is surjective, and $(a,b)$ lies in its kernel exactly when $b=-a=a$ in characteristic two, which is exactly the diagonal image.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #chain-section}
The short exact sequence in step [](#trivial-chains){.pf-ref} splits as a sequence of chain complexes.

::: pf-proof
The chain map $s(c)=(c,0)$ satisfies $p_\#\circ s=\operatorname{id}$, so it is a section of projection. The argument of this step is the card's own: this is what the trivial covering adds to Hatcher's general construction in §2.B and §3.G, whose sequence of chain complexes need not split, and the book neither states nor proves the splitting.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
All connecting homomorphisms vanish, and the transfer sequence is the split short exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow H_n(X;\ZZ_2)
\xrightarrow{x\mapsto(x,x)}
H_n(X;\ZZ_2)^2
\xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto a+b}
H_n(X;\ZZ_2)\longrightarrow0
$$
in each degree $n$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#chain-section){.pf-ref}, the long exact sequence associated to a split short exact sequence of chain complexes has zero connecting homomorphisms. Exactness at each term therefore gives the displayed homology sequence.
:::

*The cohomological form.* The transfer sequence has a cohomological form, and it is proved in full by Hausmann, *Mod Two Homology and Cohomology* (Springer, 2014), Proposition 4.3.9, "Transfer exact sequence", for a 2-fold covering $p:\tilde X\to X$ over a CW complex. The transfer there is the dual $\tau^*:C^*(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\to C^*(X;\ZZ_2)$ of the chain-level map $\tau$, and the sequence is
$$
\cdots\to H^m(X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{H^*p}H^m(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{\tau^*}H^m(X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{w(p)\smile-}H^{m+1}(X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{H^*p}\cdots,
$$
exact and functorial for induced coverings, where $w(p)\in H^1(X;\ZZ_2)$ is the characteristic class of $p$. The proof dualises the chain-level sequence of an arbitrary 2-fold covering — the sequence of Hatcher's §2.B, $0\to C_*(X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{\tau}C_*(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{p_\sharp}C_*(X;\ZZ_2)\to0$ with $\tau$ the sum of the two lifts, which Hausmann's own §4.3.3 builds by the same rule for a general $p$ — and that sequence is exact over $\ZZ_2$ and functorial for induced coverings. Kronecker duality gives from it the short exact sequence of cochain complexes $0\to C^*(X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{C^*p}C^*(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{\tau^*}C^*(X;\ZZ_2)\to0$, whose long exact sequence is Hausmann's Proposition 2.6.2; the connecting homomorphism is then identified with the cup product by $w(p)$, which is where the characteristic class enters.

For the trivial covering $\tilde X=X\times S^0$ the characteristic class vanishes: Hausmann's Lemma 4.3.6(3) states that over a CW complex a 2-fold covering is trivial if and only if its characteristic class is zero. The connecting homomorphism is therefore zero, and exactness makes the cohomological transfer sequence the short exact sequence
$$
0\to H^m(X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{a\mapsto(a,a)}H^m(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\alpha+\beta}H^m(X;\ZZ_2)\to0
$$
in each degree, which is the same two-sheet computation at the cochain level: over $\ZZ_2$ the cochain complex of $\tilde X$ is $C^*(X;\ZZ_2)\oplus C^*(X;\ZZ_2)$, and $C^*p$ is the diagonal while $\tau^*$ is addition, because $C_*p(a,b)=a+b$ is addition and $\tau(c)=(c,c)$ is the diagonal. The sequence splits by $\alpha\mapsto(\alpha,0)$, so $H^m(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\cong H^m(X;\ZZ_2)^{\oplus2}$ for every $m$. Hausmann asks for exactly this computation as Exercise 4.4 of Chapter 4, "Write the transfer exact sequence for a trivial 2-fold covering", and prints no solution to it; the two-sheet coordinates supplied here are the cohomological counterpart of those of the exercise as Hatcher states it.

::: {.remark title="Sources"}
Hausmann, *Mod Two Homology and Cohomology* (Springer, 2014), read in `tmp/sources/hausmann-mod-two-homology-cohomology.md`: Proposition 4.3.9 proves the transfer exact sequence in cohomology, over a CW complex, with the characteristic class $w(p)$ as the connecting map, and his Lemma 4.3.6(3) identifies $w(p)=0$ with triviality of the covering; the corresponding computation for the trivial 2-fold covering is asked for as Exercise 4.4 of Chapter 4. Neither prints the two-sheet cochain coordinates for it. Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology* (2002), read in `tmp/sources/hatcher-2002-algebraic-topology.md`: the transfer for a two-sheeted covering in §2.B, $\tau$ the sum of the two lifts, the short exact sequence of chain complexes $0\to C_n(X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{\tau}C_n(\tilde X;\ZZ_2)\xrightarrow{p_\sharp}C_n(X;\ZZ_2)\to0$, and the long exact sequence it yields; the general transfer for an $n$-sheeted covering is §3.G, "Transfer Homomorphisms", where $\tau$ is defined as the sum of the $n$ lifts and $\tau^*\pi^*=n$ is proved, and §2.B points there. §2.B's transfer sequence is over $\ZZ_2$ — the book says so in its proof, "Because we are using $\ZZ_2$ coefficients, the kernel of $p_\sharp$ is generated by the sums" — and that is the sequence the exercise names, so the $\ZZ_2$ computation is the exercise's own case and not a weakening of it. §3.G defines $\tau$ as the sum of the $n$ lifts for any coefficient group $G$, and proves $\tau^*\pi^*=n$. Over $\ZZ$ the transfer of $X\times S^0\to X$ is therefore the same map as over $\ZZ_2$, $\tau(c)=(c,c)$, and $p_\sharp\tau=2\cdot\operatorname{id}\ne0$: the untwisted $\ZZ$ sequence is not a complex, so it has no exactness to ask for, and $(0,c)$ lying in the kernel of addition and not in the diagonal for $c\ne0$ is only the visible symptom. That is why the book does not state the sequence over $\ZZ$, and says in §2.B that a generalization to other coefficients "uses a more elaborate form of homology called homology with local coefficients, as we show in §3.H". Where $(c,-c)$ belongs is the anti-invariant part of Hatcher's Example 3H.3: for a two-sheeted covering with deck involution $T$ (written $\tau$ in the book) the chains of $\tilde X$ carry the eigenspaces $C_n^+$ spanned by the sums $\sigma+T\sigma$ and $C_n^-$ spanned by the differences $\sigma-T\sigma$, the sequences
$$
0\to C_n^-\to C_n(\tilde X)\xrightarrow{\Sigma}C_n^+\to0,\qquad
0\to C_n^+\to C_n(\tilde X)\xrightarrow{\Delta}C_n^-\to0
$$
with $\Sigma(\alpha)=\alpha+T_\sharp\alpha$ and $\Delta(\alpha)=\alpha-T_\sharp\alpha$ are exact over $\ZZ$ and split, and Hatcher's identification in Example 3H.3, in the case his example sets up, makes $C_n^-$ the local-coefficient chains $C_n(\tilde X)\otimes_\pi\ZZ$. In the trivial cover's coordinates $X\times S^0=X\amalg X$ the involution swaps the two copies, so $C_n^+=\{(c,c)\}$ and $C_n^-=\{(c,-c)\}$; with the sum of the lifts identifying $C_n^+$ with $C_n(X;\ZZ)$ the two sequences read
$$
0\to C_*(X;\ZZ)\xrightarrow{c\mapsto(c,-c)}C_*(X;\ZZ)^2\xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto a+b}C_*(X;\ZZ)\to0,
$$
$$
0\to C_*(X;\ZZ)\xrightarrow{c\mapsto(c,c)}C_*(X;\ZZ)^2\xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto a-b}C_*(X;\ZZ)\to0,
$$
each split by $c\mapsto(c,0)$. The transfer is the first map of the second, whose projection is therefore subtraction; the first map of the first is the inclusion of the anti-invariant chains, not the transfer. Neither passage works out the trivial covering. Hatcher neither calls the §2.B sequence split nor proves it split, and it is not split as chain complexes in general; for the trivial covering it is, by the two-sheet coordinates, which is what the exercise asks for. The exercise is Exercise 2.B.9, and the trivial covering is that construction with $\tilde X=X\times S^0$.
