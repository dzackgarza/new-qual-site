::: {.problem}
For $SX$ the suspension of $X$, show by a Mayer–Vietoris sequence that there are isomorphisms $\tilde{H}_n(SX) \approx \tilde{H}_{n-1}(X)$ for all $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own, from the reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence; the cover and the two vanishing cone terms are the whole content.

Let $SX$ be the suspension of $X$, the union of the upper and lower cones $C_{+}X$ and $C_{-}X$ meeting along the equatorial copy $X\times\{\tfrac12\}$; write $\widetilde H_{n}$ for reduced homology.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cover}
$SX=U\cup V$ with $U,V$ open, $U$ and $V$ contractible, and $U\cap V$ homotopy equivalent to $X$.

::: pf-proof
Let $a_{+}$ and $a_{-}$ be the two cone points and put $U=SX\setminus\{a_{-}\}$ and $V=SX\setminus\{a_{+}\}$. Both are open, and their union is $SX$. Each is a cone with its apex removed, hence deformation retracts onto the opposite closed cone: in coordinates $SX=X\times[0,1]/(X\times\{0\})\sim(X\times\{1\})$, the set $U$ is the image of $X\times(0,1]$ and retracts by $(x,t)\mapsto(x,\max(t,\tfrac12))$, which is continuous and fixes the upper half. So $U$ and $V$ are contractible, being homotopy equivalent to the contractible cones $C_{+}X$, $C_{-}X$. Finally
$$
U\cap V=X\times(0,1),
$$
which deformation retracts onto $X\times\{\tfrac12\}\cong X$ by $(x,t)\mapsto(x,\tfrac12)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cones}
$\widetilde H_{n}(U)=\widetilde H_{n}(V)=0$ for every $n\ge0$.

::: pf-proof
$U$ and $V$ are contractible by step [](#cover){.pf-ref}, and a contractible space has the reduced homology of a point, which vanishes in every degree: $\widetilde H_{n}(\ast)=0$ for all $n\ge0$ because $H_{0}(\ast)\cong\ZZ$ is generated by the class of the point itself, and $H_{n}(\ast)=0$ for $n>0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #isomorphism}
$\widetilde H_{n}(SX)\cong\widetilde H_{n-1}(X)$ for every $n\ge0$.

::: pf-proof
The reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence of the open cover $\{U,V\}$ is exact, and a segment of it reads
$$
\widetilde H_{n}(U)\oplus\widetilde H_{n}(V)\longrightarrow\widetilde H_{n}(SX)\xrightarrow{\ \delta\ }\widetilde H_{n-1}(U\cap V)\longrightarrow\widetilde H_{n-1}(U)\oplus\widetilde H_{n-1}(V).
$$
By step [](#cones){.pf-ref} the two outer groups vanish, so exactness forces $\delta$ to be injective (its kernel is the image of a zero map) and surjective (its image is the kernel of a zero map out of a vanishing group). Hence $\delta$ is an isomorphism. Since $U\cap V\simeq X$ by step [](#cover){.pf-ref}, homotopy invariance gives $\widetilde H_{n-1}(U\cap V)\cong\widetilde H_{n-1}(X)$, and composing with $\delta$ yields
$$
\boxed{\widetilde H_{n}(SX)\cong\widetilde H_{n-1}(X)}.
$$
For $n=0$ this says $\widetilde H_{0}(SX)\cong\widetilde H_{-1}(X)=0$, which is right since $SX$ is connected and $\widetilde H_{-1}=0$ by the convention that reduced homology vanishes in negative degrees.
:::
