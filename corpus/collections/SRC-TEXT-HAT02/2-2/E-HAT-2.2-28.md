::: {.problem}
(a) Use the Mayer–Vietoris sequence to compute the homology groups of the space obtained from a torus $S^1 \times S^1$ by attaching a Möbius band via a homeomorphism from the boundary circle of the Möbius band to the circle $S^1 \times \{x_0\}$ in the torus.

(b) Do the same for the space obtained by attaching a Möbius band to $\RP^2$ via a homeomorphism of its boundary circle to the standard $\RP^1 \subset \RP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For each attachment, use Mayer--Vietoris with the original space and the Möbius band, whose intersection is the common attaching circle. The Mayer--Vietoris sequence is the one in the subsection of that name beginning on p. 149, derived there from Theorem 2.16 (p. 117) and Proposition 2.21 (p. 119); it applies because in each case the space is the union of the interiors of the surface and of a collar of the Möbius band. The Möbius band deformation retracts onto its core circle, and its boundary circle represents twice the core generator in $H_1$: the boundary is a connected $2$-sheeted covering of the core, so the inclusion $\partial M\to M$ has degree $2$ on $H_1$ by Proposition 2.30 (p. 136), the degree being the sum of local degrees over the two preimages. The Möbius band and its core circle are described in §1.3, where the band is the quotient of $S^1\times[0,1]$ by $(z,0)\sim(-z,0)$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

In part (a), let
$$
Y=T^2\cup_{S^1}M,
$$
where the boundary of the Möbius band $M$ is attached to the circle $S^1\times\{x_0\}$ in the torus. Then
$$
H_i(Y)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,2,\\
\ZZ^2,&i=1,\\
0,&i>2.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf-proof

Choose generators $a,b$ for
$$
H_1(T^2)\cong\ZZ^2
$$
with $a$ represented by the attaching circle, and let $c$ generate
$$
H_1(M)\cong\ZZ.
$$
The torus's homology is that of Example 2.36 (p. 141) with $g=1$: $H_1(T^2)=\ZZ^2$ and $H_2(T^2)=\ZZ$.
The Mayer--Vietoris map from the intersection is
$$
H_1(S^1)=\ZZ
\longrightarrow
H_1(T^2)\oplus H_1(M)=\ZZ^3,
\qquad
1\longmapsto(a,-2c).
$$
This map is injective, and its image is primitive because the coordinate of $a$ is $1$. Since $H_2(S^1)=H_2(M)=0$, the exact sequence gives
$$
0\to H_2(T^2)=\ZZ\to H_2(Y)\to\ker(1\mapsto(a,-2c))=0,
$$
so $H_2(Y)\cong\ZZ$. In degree one,
$$
H_1(Y)\cong\ZZ^3/\langle(a,-2c)\rangle\cong\ZZ^2.
$$
Connectedness gives $H_0(Y)=\ZZ$.

:::

::: pf-step

In part (b), let
$$
Z=\RP^2\cup_{\RP^1}M.
$$
Then
$$
H_i(Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,\\
\ZZ_4,&i=1,\\
0,&i\ge2.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf-proof

Let $u$ generate
$$
H_1(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ_2
$$
and let $c$ generate $H_1(M)\cong\ZZ$. The group $H_1(\RP^2)=\ZZ_2$ and its generator the class of the 1-skeleton $\RP^1$ are those of Example 2.42 (p. 144); the standard circle $\RP^1$ represents $u$, while the boundary of $M$ represents $2c$. Hence the Mayer--Vietoris map is
$$
\ZZ\longrightarrow\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ,
\qquad
1\longmapsto(u,-2c).
$$
It is injective because of the infinite cyclic second coordinate, so $H_2(Z)=0$. Its cokernel has presentation
$$
\langle u,c\mid 2u=0,\ u=2c\rangle
\cong
\langle c\mid4c=0\rangle
\cong\ZZ_4.
$$
Thus $H_1(Z)\cong\ZZ_4$, and connectedness gives $H_0(Z)=\ZZ$.
