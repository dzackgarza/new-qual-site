::: {.problem}
Compute the homology groups of the following 2 complexes:

(a) The quotient of $S^2$ obtained by identifying north and south poles to a point.

(b) $S^1 \times (S^1 \lor S^1)$.

(c) The space obtained from $D^2$ by first deleting the interiors of two disjoint subdisks in the interior of $D^2$ and then identifying all three resulting boundary circles together via homeomorphisms preserving clockwise orientations of these circles.

(d) The quotient space of $S^1 \times S^1$ obtained by identifying points in the circle $S^1 \times \{x_0\}$ that differ by $2\pi/m$ rotation and identifying points in the circle $\{x_0\} \times S^1$ that differ by $2\pi/n$ rotation.
:::

::: {.solution}
The four spaces are computed separately, each with the cellular chain complex of a CW structure and Theorem 2.35 (Hatcher p. 139), which identifies cellular and singular homology.

::: pf

::: pf-step

In part (a), if $Y$ is obtained from $S^2$ by identifying the north and south poles, then
$$
Y\simeq S^2\vee S^1.
$$
Hence
$$
H_i(Y)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,2,\\
\ZZ,&i=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf-proof

Example 0.8 (Hatcher p. 12) shows $S^2/S^0\simeq S^1\vee S^2$, and Corollary 2.25 (p. 126) gives $\widetilde H_n(S^1\vee S^2)\cong\widetilde H_n(S^1)\oplus\widetilde H_n(S^2)$, which is $\ZZ$ for $n=1,2$ and $0$ otherwise by Corollary 2.14 (p. 114). Equivalently $Y$ has a CW structure with one $0$ cell, one $1$ cell and one $2$ cell, the $2$ cell attached by a constant map, so both cellular boundary maps vanish.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In part (b), for
$$
Y=S^1\times(S^1\vee S^1),
$$
one has
$$
H_i(Y)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,\\
\ZZ^3,&i=1,\\
\ZZ^2,&i=2,\\
0,&i>2.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf-proof

Take the product CW structure of $S^1$ with one $0$ cell and one $1$ cell and of $S^1\vee S^1$ with one $0$ cell and two $1$ cells. Then $Y$ has one $0$ cell, three $1$ cells and two $2$ cells. The cellular boundary of a product cell is $\partial(a\times b)=\partial a\times b+(-1)^{\dim a}a\times\partial b$ (the cellular boundary formula, Hatcher p. 141), and $\partial$ of the one-cell of $S^1$ and of the two one-cells of $S^1\vee S^1$ is zero; hence all cellular boundary maps vanish and the chain groups themselves are the homology, by Theorem 2.35 (p. 139).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In part (c), let $P$ be the pair of pants and let $Y$ be the quotient obtained by identifying its three boundary circles, preserving their clockwise orientations. Then
$$
H_i(Y)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,\\
\ZZ^2,&i=1,\\
0,&i\ge2.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf-proof

Let $C\subset Y$ be the common image of the three boundary circles, a circle which is a deformation retract of its image of a closed collar of $\partial P$. Then $Y/C\cong P/\partial P$, so the quotient maps of the good pairs $(Y,C)$ and $(P,\partial P)$ induce isomorphisms $H_*(Y,C)\cong\widetilde H_*(Y/C)\cong\widetilde H_*(P/\partial P)\cong H_*(P,\partial P)$ by Proposition 2.22 (Hatcher p. 124).
For an oriented pair of pants, the exact sequence of the pair (Theorem 2.13, p. 114),
$$
0=H_2(P)\to H_2(P,\partial P)\to H_1(\partial P)\xrightarrow{\alpha}H_1(P)\to H_1(P,\partial P)\to H_0(\partial P)\to H_0(P),
$$
together with $H_1(\partial P)=\ZZ^3$, $H_1(P)=\ZZ^2$ generated by the two inner boundary circles, and the outer boundary circle bounding in $P$, gives $H_2(P,\partial P)\cong\ker\alpha\cong\ZZ$, $H_1(P,\partial P)\cong\ZZ^2$ and $H_0(P,\partial P)=0$.
The relevant part of the exact sequence of the pair $(Y,C)$ is
$$
0\to H_2(Y)\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{\delta}\ZZ\to H_1(Y)\to\ZZ^2\to H_0(C)\to H_0(Y)\to0,
$$
where the last map is an isomorphism. Take the generator of $H_1(C)$ to be the clockwise orientation. For the standard orientation of the planar pair of pants the induced boundary orientation is counterclockwise on the outer boundary and clockwise on each inner boundary, so the boundary of the relative fundamental class of $P$ maps to
$$
(-1)+1+1=1
$$
times the generator of $H_1(C)$, $\delta$ is an isomorphism, $H_2(Y)=0$ and the map $H_1(C)\to H_1(Y)$ is zero, leaving $H_1(Y)\cong\ZZ^2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In part (d), let $Y_{m,n}$ be the stated quotient of the torus. Then
$$
H_i(Y_{m,n})\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,2,\\
\ZZ^2,&i=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf-proof

Use the square CW structure on the torus, one $0$ cell, two $1$ cells and one $2$ cell. After the quotient the horizontal circle is still a circle $a$, traversed $m$ times by the image of the horizontal edge, and the vertical circle is a circle $b$ traversed $n$ times, while the open $2$ cell is unchanged. Hence the attaching word of the $2$ cell becomes
$$
a^mb^na^{-m}b^{-n}.
$$
The cellular boundary $d_2:C_2\to C_1$ records exponent sums in the two $1$ cells, and both exponent sums are zero, so the cellular chain complex is
$$
0\longrightarrow\ZZ\xrightarrow{0}\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{0}\ZZ\longrightarrow0,
$$
and Theorem 2.35 (Hatcher p. 139) yields the displayed groups.
