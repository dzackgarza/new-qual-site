::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the quotient space of $S^2$ under the identifications $x \sim -x$ for $x$ in the equator $S^1$.
Compute the homology groups $H_i(X)$.
Do the same for $S^3$ with antipodal points of the equatorial $S^2 \subset S^3$ identified.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $X_n$ for the quotient of $S^n$ by the identification $x\sim -x$ on the equator.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For $n=2$, the quotient has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, one $1$-cell $e$ (the quotient $\RP^1\cong S^1$ of the equator), and two $2$-cells $f_1,f_2$ (the two hemispheres). Since traveling once around $\RP^1$ means traveling twice around the equator before identification, each $2$-cell attaches by a degree-$2$ map. The cellular complex is therefore
$$
0\longrightarrow\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{\ \partial_2\ }\ZZ\xrightarrow{\ \partial_1=0\ }\ZZ\longrightarrow0,
\qquad \partial_2(a,b)=2(a+b).
$$
Thus $\ker\partial_2=\langle(1,-1)\rangle\cong\ZZ$ and $\operatorname{im}\partial_2=2\ZZ$, so
$$
H_0(X_2)=\ZZ,\qquad H_1(X_2)=\ZZ_2,\qquad H_2(X_2)=\ZZ,
$$
and the other integral homology groups vanish.

::: pf-proof

This is DeMason's computation for Hatcher 2.2.10, printed p. 76 of `tmp/sources/demason-m382c-hatcher-20210404.pdf`. Hatcher's CW boundary map (Theorem 2.35, p. 139) is the one used above; no assertion is made that $\ZZ\to\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2$ is injective.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For $n=3$, the quotient can be written as $\RP^2$ with two $3$-cells attached. With the usual cell decomposition and attaching maps, each $3$-cell traverses directions in opposite orientations, so $\partial_3=0$. The chain complex simplifies to
$$
\ZZ^2\xrightarrow{\partial_3=0}\ZZ\xrightarrow{\ \partial_2=2\ }\ZZ\xrightarrow{\ \partial_1=0\ }\ZZ
$$
in degrees $3,2,1,0$. Consequently,
$$
H_0(X_3)=\ZZ,\qquad H_1(X_3)=\ZZ_2,\qquad H_2(X_3)=0,\qquad H_3(X_3)=\ZZ^2.
$$

::: pf-proof

The attaching maps and opposite orientations are shown by DeMason on printed p. 77 of `tmp/sources/demason-m382c-hatcher-20210404.pdf`. The relation $\partial_2[e]=2[e]$ and $\partial_3=0$ then give the displayed homology directly. These answers also agree with the repaired Mayer--Vietoris computation using two $\RP^3$ hemispheres with intersection $\RP^2$: $H_2(\RP^3)=0$, while $H_3(\RP^3)=\ZZ$ accounts for the two free summands in degree $3$.
:::
