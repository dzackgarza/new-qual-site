::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a subgroup generated by a 5-cycle in $S_5$. Find the order of $N_{S_5}(G)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
G=\langle g\rangle,
$$
where $g$ is a $5$-cycle.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The subgroup $G$ has order $5$ and is a Sylow $5$-subgroup of $S_5$.


::: pf-proof

A $5$-cycle has order $5$, so
$$
\abs G=5.
$$
Since
$$
\abs{S_5}=120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5,
$$
the largest power of $5$ dividing $\abs{S_5}$ is $5$ itself.
Thus every subgroup of order $5$ is Sylow.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There are $24$ five-cycles in $S_5$.


::: pf-proof

The number of cycles of length $5$ on five letters is
$$
(5-1)!=24.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Each Sylow $5$-subgroup contains exactly four five-cycles.


::: pf-proof

A subgroup of order $5$ is cyclic.
Its four nonidentity elements all have order $5$, hence are $5$-cycles.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Distinct Sylow $5$-subgroups share no nonidentity element.


::: pf-proof

If two subgroups of order $5$ shared a nonidentity element $h$, then
$$
\langle h\rangle
$$
would have order $5$ and would equal both subgroups.
Thus distinct Sylow $5$-subgroups intersect only in the identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The number of Sylow $5$-subgroups of $S_5$ is
$$
n_5=\frac{24}{4}=6.
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the $24$ five-cycles are partitioned into sets of four, one set for each Sylow $5$-subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Under the conjugation action of $S_5$ on its Sylow $5$-subgroups, the stabilizer of $G$ is exactly $N_{S_5}(G)$.


::: pf-proof

A permutation $\sigma\in S_5$ stabilizes the subgroup $G$ under conjugation exactly when
$$
\sigma G\sigma^{-1}=G,
$$
which is precisely the definition of membership in the normalizer.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The conjugacy orbit of $G$ has size $6$.


::: pf-proof

All Sylow $5$-subgroups are conjugate by Sylow's theorem.
By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} there are six of them, so the orbit of $G$ under conjugation has six elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
[S_5:N_{S_5}(G)]=6.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the orbit-stabilizer theorem to the conjugation action, using steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\abs{N_{S_5}(G)}=\frac{120}{6}=20}.
$$


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{S_5}
=
6\abs{N_{S_5}(G)}.
$$
Since $\abs{S_5}=120$, the normalizer has order $20$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is the required order.

:::
