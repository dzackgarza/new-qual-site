::: {.problem}
(a) What is the order of the group $GL_3(F_3)$ of $3 \times 3$ invertible matrices with entries in $F_3$?

(b) What is the order of the group $GL_3(F_3)$ of $3 \times 3$ invertible upper triangular matrices with entries in $F_3$?

(c) Does there exist a nonabelian group of order 27? Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.remark}
Part (b) of the source reuses the name $GL_3(F_3)$ for the subgroup of invertible upper triangular matrices; the group asked about in (b) is that subgroup of $GL_3(F_3)$, not $GL_3(F_3)$ itself.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

To choose an invertible $3\times3$ matrix over $\mathbb F_3$, its first column may be any nonzero vector.


::: pf-proof

The vector space
$$
\mathbb F_3^3
$$
has
$$
3^3=27
$$
vectors.
The first column of an invertible matrix must be nonzero, so there are
$$
27-1=26
$$
choices.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

After choosing the first column, the second column has
$$
27-3=24
$$
choices.


::: pf-proof

The second column must lie outside the span of the first.
The span of a nonzero vector over $\mathbb F_3$ has
$$
3
$$
elements.
Hence
$$
27-3=24
$$
vectors are available.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

After choosing two linearly independent columns, the third column has
$$
27-9=18
$$
choices.


::: pf-proof

Two linearly independent vectors span a $2$-dimensional subspace of
$$
\mathbb F_3^3,
$$
which has
$$
3^2=9
$$
elements.
The third column must lie outside this span.
Thus there are
$$
27-9=18
$$
choices.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\abs{GL_3(\mathbb F_3)}
=(27-1)(27-3)(27-9)
=
11232.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Multiply the independent choices from steps <1>1--<1>3:
$$
26\cdot24\cdot18
=
11232.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

An upper triangular $3\times3$ matrix over $\mathbb F_3$ is invertible if and only if all three diagonal entries are nonzero.


::: pf-proof

The determinant of an upper triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries.
Thus the determinant is nonzero exactly when each diagonal entry is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There are
$$
2^3
$$
choices for the diagonal entries of an invertible upper triangular matrix.


::: pf-proof

The nonzero elements of
$$
\mathbb F_3
$$
are
$$
1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2.
$$
Each of the three diagonal entries therefore has two choices.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There are
$$
3^3
$$
choices for the entries strictly above the diagonal.


::: pf-proof

A $3\times3$ upper triangular matrix has three positions strictly above the diagonal:
$$
(1,2),
\qquad
(1,3),
\qquad
(2,3).
$$
Each may be any element of
$$
\mathbb F_3,
$$
giving
$$
3^3
$$
choices.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence the invertible upper triangular subgroup has order
$$
\boxed{
2^3\cdot3^3
=
216.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>6--<1>7.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
U
\coloneqq
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
1&a&c\\
0&1&b\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
:
a,b,c\in\mathbb F_3
\right\}.
$$
Then
$$
\abs U=27.
$$


::: pf-proof

The three parameters
$$
a,b,c
$$
may be chosen independently from
$$
\mathbb F_3.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs U
=
3^3
=
27.
$$
The set is closed under multiplication and inverses because it is the upper unitriangular subgroup of
$$
GL_3(\mathbb F_3).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The group $U$ is nonabelian.


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
X
\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
and
$$
Y
\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&1&1\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then
$$
XY
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1\\
0&1&1\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whereas
$$
YX
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0\\
0&1&1\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus
$$
XY\ne YX.
$$
Hence $U$ is nonabelian.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore there exists a nonabelian group of order $27$.


::: pf-proof

The group $U$ from step <1>9 has order $27$, and step <1>10 shows it is nonabelian.
Thus
$$
\boxed{\text{yes, such a group exists}.}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>4 proves part (a), step <1>8 proves part (b), and step <1>11 proves part (c).
