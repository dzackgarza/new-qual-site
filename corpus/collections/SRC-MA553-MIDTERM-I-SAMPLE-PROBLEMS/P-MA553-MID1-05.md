::: {.problem}
Show that for any element $\sigma$ of order 2 in the alternating group $A _ { n }$ there exists $\tau \in S _ { n }$ such that $\tau ^ { 2 } = \sigma$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\sigma\in A_n
$$
have order $2$.

<1>1. The disjoint-cycle decomposition of $\sigma$ consists only of transpositions and fixed points.

::: {.proof}
Because
$$
\sigma^2=1,
$$
every cycle length in the disjoint-cycle decomposition divides $2$.
Thus every nontrivial cycle has length $2$.
Hence
$$
\sigma
=
(a_1\,b_1)(a_2\,b_2)\cdots(a_m\,b_m)
$$
for pairwise disjoint transpositions.
:::

<1>2. The number $m$ of transpositions is even.

::: {.proof}
The sign of a product of $m$ disjoint transpositions is
$$
(-1)^m.
$$
Since
$$
\sigma\in A_n,
$$
its sign is $+1$.
Therefore
$$
(-1)^m=1,
$$
so
$$
m=2k
$$
for some
$$
k\ge1.
$$
:::

<1>3. For four distinct symbols $a,b,c,d$,
$$
\boxed{
(a\,c\,b\,d)^2
=
(a\,b)(c\,d).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The cycle
$$
\gamma
\coloneqq
(a\,c\,b\,d)
$$
sends
$$
a\mapsto c\mapsto b
$$
and
$$
c\mapsto b\mapsto d,
$$
so
$$
\gamma^2(a)=b,
\qquad
\gamma^2(b)=a,
$$
and similarly
$$
\gamma^2(c)=d,
\qquad
\gamma^2(d)=c.
$$
Thus
$$
\gamma^2=(a\,b)(c\,d).
$$
:::

<1>4. Pair the $2k$ transpositions in $\sigma$ as
$$
(a_1\,b_1)(a_2\,b_2),
\quad
(a_3\,b_3)(a_4\,b_4),
\quad
\ldots,
\quad
(a_{2k-1}\,b_{2k-1})(a_{2k}\,b_{2k}).
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 shows that the number of transpositions is even, so such a pairing is possible.
:::

<1>5. For
$$
j=1,\ldots,k,
$$
define
$$
\tau_j
\coloneqq
(a_{2j-1}\,a_{2j}\,b_{2j-1}\,b_{2j}).
$$
Then
$$
\tau_j^2
=
(a_{2j-1}\,b_{2j-1})
(a_{2j}\,b_{2j}).
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>3 with
$$
a=a_{2j-1},
\quad
b=b_{2j-1},
\quad
c=a_{2j},
\quad
d=b_{2j}.
$$
:::

<1>6. The cycles
$$
\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_k
$$
are pairwise disjoint and therefore commute.

::: {.proof}
The original transpositions in the disjoint-cycle decomposition of $\sigma$ have pairwise disjoint supports.
Each $\tau_j$ uses exactly the four letters from one pair of those transpositions.
Different $\tau_j$ therefore have disjoint supports.
Disjoint permutations commute.
:::

<1>7. Define
$$
\tau
\coloneqq
\tau_1\tau_2\cdots\tau_k.
$$
Then
$$
\tau^2
=
\tau_1^2\tau_2^2\cdots\tau_k^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6, the factors commute.
Therefore
$$
(\tau_1\cdots\tau_k)^2
=
\tau_1^2\cdots\tau_k^2.
$$
:::

<1>8. One has
$$
\tau^2=\sigma.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>5 and <1>7,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau^2
&=
\prod_{j=1}^{k}
\tau_j^2\\
&=
\prod_{j=1}^{k}
(a_{2j-1}\,b_{2j-1})
(a_{2j}\,b_{2j})\\
&=
(a_1\,b_1)
\cdots
(a_{2k}\,b_{2k})\\
&=
\sigma.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>9. Therefore there exists
$$
\boxed{\tau\in S_n}
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{\tau^2=\sigma}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The element $\tau$ constructed in step <1>7 belongs to $S_n$, and step <1>8 verifies the required square.
:::

<1>10. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>9 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
