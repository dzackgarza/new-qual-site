::: {.problem}
Let G be a finite group, let p be a prime divisor of the order |G|, and let P be a Sylow p-subgroup of G. Let $N _ { G } ( P )$ be the normalizer of $P ,$ and $C _ { G } ( P ) \subset N _ { G } ( P )$ the centralizer of P .

(a) Show that the index $[ N _ { G } ( P ) : C _ { G } ( P ) ]$ is the order of a subgroup of the automorphism group of P.

(b) Show that p divides $[ N _ { G } ( P ) : C _ { G } ( P ) ]$ if, and only if, P is nonabelian.
(Hint: P is abelian iff $C _ { G } ( P ) \supset P . )$

(c) Show that if P is cyclic and the $\operatorname* { g c d } ( | G | , p - 1 ) = 1$ then $C _ { G } ( P ) =$ $N _ { G } ( P )$ . (Hint: Consider conjugation morphism $N _ { G } ( P ) \to \operatorname { A u t } ( P ) )$
:::

Write
$$
N\coloneqq N_G(P),
\qquad
C\coloneqq C_G(P).
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Conjugation defines a homomorphism
$$
\varphi:N\to\operatorname{Aut}(P),
\qquad
\varphi(g)(x)=gxg^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $g\in N_G(P)$, then $gPg^{-1}=P$, so conjugation by $g$ restricts to an automorphism of $P$.
Also
$$
\varphi(gh)=\varphi(g)\varphi(h),
$$
so $\varphi$ is a group homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\ker\varphi=C.
$$


::: pf-proof

An element $g\in N$ lies in the kernel exactly when
$$
gxg^{-1}=x
$$
for every $x\in P$.
This is equivalent to $gx=xg$ for every $x\in P$, namely
$$
g\in C_G(P)=C.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
N/C\cong\operatorname{im}\varphi\le\operatorname{Aut}(P).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the first isomorphism theorem to steps <1>1--<1>2.
Hence
$$
[N:C]
=
\abs{\operatorname{im}\varphi},
$$
the order of a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(P)$.
This proves part (a).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The subgroup $P$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $N$.


::: pf-proof

Certainly $P\le N$.
If $N$ contained a $p$-subgroup larger than $P$, then so would $G$, contradicting that $P$ is Sylow in $G$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
P\cap C=Z(P).
$$


::: pf-proof

An element of $P\cap C_G(P)$ is precisely an element of $P$ commuting with every element of $P$, which is the definition of $Z(P)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The subgroup $Z(P)$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $C$.


::: pf-proof

Let $Q\le C$ be a $p$-subgroup.
Since $Q$ centralizes $P$, the product $PQ$ is a subgroup of $N$ and is a $p$-group.
By step <1>4, $P$ is Sylow in $N$, so
$$
PQ=P.
$$
Hence $Q\le P$.
Because also $Q\le C$, step <1>5 gives
$$
Q\le P\cap C=Z(P).
$$
Thus $Z(P)$ contains every $p$-subgroup of $C$ and is therefore Sylow in $C$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The $p$-part of the index $[N:C]$ is
$$
\abs{P:Z(P)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The highest power of $p$ dividing $\abs N$ is $\abs P$ by step <1>4.
The highest power of $p$ dividing $\abs C$ is $\abs{Z(P)}$ by step <1>6.
Therefore the $p$-part of
$$
[N:C]
=
\frac{\abs N}{\abs C}
$$
is
$$
\frac{\abs P}{\abs{Z(P)}}
=
\abs{P:Z(P)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
\boxed{
p\mid[N:C]
\iff
P\text{ is nonabelian}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>7, $p$ divides the index exactly when
$$
\abs{P:Z(P)}>1,
$$
equivalently $P\ne Z(P)$.
A group equals its center exactly when it is abelian.
This proves part (b).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Assume now that $P$ is cyclic of order $p^m$.
Then
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(P)}
=
\varphi(p^m)
=
p^{m-1}(p-1).
$$


::: pf-proof

An automorphism of a cyclic group is determined by the image of a generator, and the image must again be a generator.
There are
$$
\varphi(p^m)=p^{m-1}(p-1)
$$
generators.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $P$ is cyclic, then
$$
p\nmid[N:C].
$$


::: pf-proof

A cyclic group is abelian.
Apply part (b), proved in step <1>8.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $P$ is cyclic, then
$$
[N:C]\mid p-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

By part (a), step <1>3,
$$
[N:C]
$$
divides
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(P)}
=
p^{m-1}(p-1).
$$
By step <1>10, the index is not divisible by $p$.
Therefore it divides $p-1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One also has
$$
[N:C]\mid\abs G.
$$


::: pf-proof

By Lagrange's theorem,
$$
[N:C]\mid\abs N,
$$
and
$$
\abs N\mid\abs G.
$$
Thus
$$
[N:C]\mid\abs G.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If
$$
\gcd(\abs G,p-1)=1,
$$
then
$$
[N:C]=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps <1>11--<1>12 show that the positive integer $[N:C]$ divides both $p-1$ and $\abs G$.
Their greatest common divisor is $1$.
Hence
$$
[N:C]=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
C_G(P)=N_G(P).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>13 gives
$$
[N:C]=1,
$$
so $N=C$.
This proves part (c).

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>3 proves part (a), step <1>8 proves part (b), and step <1>14 proves part (c).
