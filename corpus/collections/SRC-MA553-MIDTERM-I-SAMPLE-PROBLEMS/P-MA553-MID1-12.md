::: {.problem}
Prove that there is no simple group of order $5^{3} \cdot k$ which has a subgroup of index $8$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
G
$$
is simple,
$$
\abs G=5^3k,
$$
and
$$
H\le G
$$
has index
$$
[G:H]=8.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The left action of $G$ on the coset set
$$
G/H
$$
defines a homomorphism
$$
\varphi:G\to S_8.
$$


::: pf-proof

The set
$$
G/H
$$
has eight elements.
For
$$
g\in G,
$$
define
$$
\varphi(g)(xH)
\coloneqq
gxH.
$$
This is a permutation of the eight cosets, and
$$
\varphi(g_1g_2)
=
\varphi(g_1)\varphi(g_2).
$$
Thus
$$
\varphi:G\to S_8
$$
is a group homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The homomorphism $\varphi$ is nontrivial.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\varphi
$$
were trivial, every element of $G$ would fix every coset.
In particular, every
$$
g\in G
$$
would fix the coset
$$
H,
$$
so
$$
gH=H.
$$
This implies
$$
g\in H
$$
for every $g\in G$, hence
$$
G=H,
$$
contradicting
$$
[G:H]=8.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The kernel
$$
\ker\varphi
$$
is a normal subgroup of $G$.


::: pf-proof

The kernel of every group homomorphism is normal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Since $G$ is simple and $\varphi$ is nontrivial,
$$
\ker\varphi=\{1\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By simplicity, the only normal subgroups of $G$ are
$$
\{1\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
G.
$$
Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} rules out
$$
\ker\varphi=G.
$$
Hence
$$
\ker\varphi=\{1\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\varphi
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

A group homomorphism is injective exactly when its kernel is trivial.
Apply step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Consequently,
$$
\abs G
\mid
\abs{S_8}
=
8!.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
G
\cong
\varphi(G)
\le
S_8.
$$
Lagrange's theorem gives
$$
\abs G
\mid
\abs{S_8}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The integer $8!$ is divisible by $5$ but not by $25$, hence not by $125$.


::: pf-proof

Among the integers
$$
1,2,\ldots,8,
$$
the only multiple of $5$ is
$$
5.
$$
Thus the exponent of $5$ in the prime factorization of
$$
8!
$$
is exactly $1$:
$$
v_5(8!)=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
25\nmid8!,
$$
and in particular
$$
125\nmid8!.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

But
$$
125
\mid
\abs G.
$$


::: pf-proof

By hypothesis,
$$
\abs G
=
5^3k
=
125k.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} give a contradiction.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\abs G\mid8!
$$
and
$$
125\mid\abs G,
$$
then transitivity of divisibility would give
$$
125\mid8!.
$$
This contradicts step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore no such simple group exists.


::: pf-proof

The assumption that a simple group of order
$$
5^3k
$$
has a subgroup of index $8$ leads to the contradiction in step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.
Hence
$$
\boxed{
\text{no simple group of order }5^3k
\text{ has a subgroup of index }8.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
