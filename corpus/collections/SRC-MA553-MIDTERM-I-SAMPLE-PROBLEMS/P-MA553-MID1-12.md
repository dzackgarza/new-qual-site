::: {.problem}
Prove that there is no simple group of order $5 ^ { 3 }$ ·k which has a subgroup of index 8.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
G
$$
is simple,
$$
\abs G=5^3k,
$$
and
$$
H\le G
$$
has index
$$
[G:H]=8.
$$

<1>1. The left action of $G$ on the coset set
$$
G/H
$$
defines a homomorphism
$$
\varphi:G\to S_8.
$$

::: {.proof}
The set
$$
G/H
$$
has eight elements.
For
$$
g\in G,
$$
define
$$
\varphi(g)(xH)
\coloneqq
gxH.
$$
This is a permutation of the eight cosets, and
$$
\varphi(g_1g_2)
=
\varphi(g_1)\varphi(g_2).
$$
Thus
$$
\varphi:G\to S_8
$$
is a group homomorphism.
:::

<1>2. The homomorphism $\varphi$ is nontrivial.

::: {.proof}
If
$$
\varphi
$$
were trivial, every element of $G$ would fix every coset.
In particular, every
$$
g\in G
$$
would fix the coset
$$
H,
$$
so
$$
gH=H.
$$
This implies
$$
g\in H
$$
for every $g\in G$, hence
$$
G=H,
$$
contradicting
$$
[G:H]=8.
$$
:::

<1>3. The kernel
$$
\ker\varphi
$$
is a normal subgroup of $G$.

::: {.proof}
The kernel of every group homomorphism is normal.
:::

<1>4. Since $G$ is simple and $\varphi$ is nontrivial,
$$
\ker\varphi=\{1\}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By simplicity, the only normal subgroups of $G$ are
$$
\{1\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
G.
$$
Step <1>2 rules out
$$
\ker\varphi=G.
$$
Hence
$$
\ker\varphi=\{1\}.
$$
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\varphi
$$
is injective.

::: {.proof}
A group homomorphism is injective exactly when its kernel is trivial.
Apply step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Consequently,
$$
\abs G
\mid
\abs{S_8}
=
8!.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>5,
$$
G
\cong
\varphi(G)
\le
S_8.
$$
Lagrange's theorem gives
$$
\abs G
\mid
\abs{S_8}.
$$
:::

<1>7. The integer $8!$ is divisible by $5$ but not by $25$, hence not by $125$.

::: {.proof}
Among the integers
$$
1,2,\ldots,8,
$$
the only multiple of $5$ is
$$
5.
$$
Thus the exponent of $5$ in the prime factorization of
$$
8!
$$
is exactly $1$:
$$
v_5(8!)=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
25\nmid8!,
$$
and in particular
$$
125\nmid8!.
$$
:::

<1>8. But
$$
125
\mid
\abs G.
$$

::: {.proof}
By hypothesis,
$$
\abs G
=
5^3k
=
125k.
$$
:::

<1>9. Steps <1>6--<1>8 give a contradiction.

::: {.proof}
If
$$
\abs G\mid8!
$$
and
$$
125\mid\abs G,
$$
then transitivity of divisibility would give
$$
125\mid8!.
$$
This contradicts step <1>7.
:::

<1>10. Therefore no such simple group exists.

::: {.proof}
The assumption that a simple group of order
$$
5^3k
$$
has a subgroup of index $8$ leads to the contradiction in step <1>9.
Hence
$$
\boxed{
\text{no simple group of order }5^3k
\text{ has a subgroup of index }8.
}
$$
:::

<1>11. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>10 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
