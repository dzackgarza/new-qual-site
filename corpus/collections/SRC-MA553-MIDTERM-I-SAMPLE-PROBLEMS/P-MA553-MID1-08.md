::: {.problem}
Prove that the order of the automorphism group of $( Z / 3 ) ^ { 4 }$ is $8 0 \times 7 8 \times$ $7 2 \times 5 4$ . Describe an example of a group of order 405 which is not the direct product of $Z _ { 5 }$ with a group of order 81. Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
V
\coloneqq
(\ZZ/3)^4,
$$
viewed as the $4$-dimensional vector space
$$
\mathbb F_3^4.
$$

<1>1. One has
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(V)
\cong
GL_4(\mathbb F_3).
$$

::: {.proof}
An automorphism of the additive group
$$
V=(\ZZ/3)^4
$$
is exactly an invertible $\mathbb F_3$-linear transformation of the vector space
$$
\mathbb F_3^4.
$$
Thus its automorphism group is
$$
GL_4(\mathbb F_3).
$$
:::

<1>2. The first column of an invertible $4\times4$ matrix over $\mathbb F_3$ has
$$
3^4-1
=
80
$$
choices.

::: {.proof}
The first column may be any nonzero vector of
$$
\mathbb F_3^4.
$$
There are
$$
3^4=81
$$
vectors total and one zero vector.
:::

<1>3. After choosing the first column, the second column has
$$
3^4-3
=
78
$$
choices.

::: {.proof}
The second column must lie outside the $1$-dimensional span of the first.
That span has
$$
3
$$
elements.
Thus there are
$$
81-3=78
$$
choices.
:::

<1>4. After choosing two linearly independent columns, the third column has
$$
3^4-3^2
=
72
$$
choices.

::: {.proof}
The first two columns span a $2$-dimensional subspace with
$$
3^2=9
$$
elements.
The third column must lie outside it.
:::

<1>5. After choosing three linearly independent columns, the fourth column has
$$
3^4-3^3
=
54
$$
choices.

::: {.proof}
The first three columns span a $3$-dimensional subspace with
$$
3^3=27
$$
elements.
The fourth column must lie outside this span.
Thus
$$
81-27=54
$$
choices remain.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(V)}
=
80\cdot78\cdot72\cdot54.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Multiply the independent choices from steps <1>2--<1>5.
By step <1>1 this is the order of
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(V).
$$
:::

<1>7. The group $\operatorname{Aut}(V)$ contains an element
$$
\varphi
$$
of order $5$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6,
$$
5
\mid
\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(V)}
$$
because
$$
5\mid80.
$$
By Cauchy's theorem, there exists
$$
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(V)
$$
with
$$
\operatorname{ord}(\varphi)=5.
$$
:::

<1>8. Let
$$
C
\coloneqq
\langle t\rangle
\cong
C_5,
$$
and let $C$ act on $V$ by
$$
t\cdot v
\coloneqq
\varphi(v).
$$
Define
$$
G
\coloneqq
V\rtimes_\varphi C.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
\varphi^5=1,
$$
the assignment
$$
t\mapsto\varphi
$$
defines a homomorphism
$$
C_5\to\operatorname{Aut}(V).
$$
Hence the corresponding semidirect product is defined.
:::

<1>9. The group $G$ has order
$$
\boxed{405}.
$$

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
\abs V
=
3^4
=
81
$$
and
$$
\abs C
=
5.
$$
The underlying set of the semidirect product is
$$
V\times C,
$$
so
$$
\abs G
=
81\cdot5
=
405.
$$
:::

<1>10. The action of $C$ on $V$ is nontrivial.

::: {.proof}
The automorphism
$$
\varphi
$$
has order $5$, so
$$
\varphi\ne1.
$$
Thus the generator
$$
t
$$
acts nontrivially on $V$.
:::

<1>11. The subgroup
$$
C
\cong
C_5
$$
is not normal in $G$.

::: {.proof}
The subgroup
$$
V
$$
is normal in the semidirect product.
Suppose
$$
C
$$
were also normal.
Since
$$
V\cap C=1,
$$
the commutator subgroup
$$
[V,C]
$$
would lie in both normal subgroups:
$$
[V,C]
\subseteq
V\cap C
=
1.
$$
Hence every element of $C$ would commute with every element of $V$.
That would make the conjugation action of $C$ on $V$ trivial, contradicting step <1>10.
Therefore
$$
C
$$
is not normal.
:::

<1>12. The group $G$ has no normal subgroup of order $5$.

::: {.proof}
Any subgroup of order $5$ is a Sylow $5$-subgroup because
$$
\abs G
=
3^4\cdot5.
$$
All Sylow $5$-subgroups are conjugate.
If one Sylow $5$-subgroup were normal, it would be the unique Sylow $5$-subgroup.
But the displayed subgroup
$$
C
$$
is a Sylow $5$-subgroup and is not normal by step <1>11.
Hence no Sylow $5$-subgroup is normal.
:::

<1>13. Let
$$
H
$$
be any group of order $81$.
Then
$$
C_5\times H
$$
has a normal subgroup of order $5$.

::: {.proof}
The subgroup
$$
C_5\times\{1\}
$$
has order $5$ and is a direct factor.
Therefore it is normal.
:::

<1>14. The group
$$
G
=
V\rtimes_\varphi C_5
$$
is not isomorphic to
$$
C_5\times H
$$
for any group $H$ of order $81$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>12, $G$ has no normal subgroup of order $5$.
By step <1>13, every group of the form
$$
C_5\times H
$$
does have a normal subgroup of order $5$.
Existence of a normal subgroup of a given order is preserved by group isomorphism.
Therefore no such direct product is isomorphic to $G$.
:::

<1>15. Hence an example with the required property is
$$
\boxed{
(\ZZ/3)^4
\rtimes_\varphi
\ZZ/5,
}
$$
where $\varphi$ has order $5$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>9 gives order $405$, and step <1>14 proves that this group is not a direct product
$$
\ZZ/5\times H
$$
with
$$
\abs H=81.
$$
:::

<1>16. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 proves the automorphism-group order, and step <1>15 gives and verifies the required group of order $405$.
:::
