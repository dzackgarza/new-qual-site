::: {.problem}
Show that $P$ is abelian whenever $\operatorname{Aut}(P)$ is cyclic. (Hint: A subgroup of cyclic group is cyclic)
:::

::: {.remark}
The source never introduces $P$; Problem 19 of the sample problems says only what is quoted above. The statement is read with $P$ an arbitrary group, for which it holds as stated.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

The group of inner automorphisms
$$
\operatorname{Inn}(P)
$$
is a subgroup of
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(P).
$$


::: pf-proof

For each
$$
g\in P,
$$
conjugation
$$
c_g:P\to P,
\qquad
c_g(x)=gxg^{-1},
$$
is an automorphism.
The set of all such conjugation automorphisms is
$$
\operatorname{Inn}(P),
$$
which is therefore a subgroup of
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(P).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The group
$$
\operatorname{Inn}(P)
$$
is cyclic.


::: pf-proof

By hypothesis,
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
$$
is cyclic.
Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic.
Apply this to step <1>1.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There is a natural isomorphism
$$
P/Z(P)
\cong
\operatorname{Inn}(P).
$$


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
\varphi:P\to\operatorname{Inn}(P),
\qquad
\varphi(g)=c_g.
$$
This map is surjective by definition.
Its kernel consists of those
$$
g\in P
$$
for which
$$
gxg^{-1}=x
$$
for every
$$
x\in P.
$$
That kernel is exactly
$$
Z(P).
$$
The first isomorphism theorem gives
$$
P/Z(P)
\cong
\operatorname{Inn}(P).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The quotient
$$
P/Z(P)
$$
is cyclic.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>2,
$$
\operatorname{Inn}(P)
$$
is cyclic.
Apply the isomorphism from step <1>3.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If a group modulo its center is cyclic, then the group is abelian.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
P/Z(P)
=
\langle gZ(P)\rangle.
$$
Let
$$
x,y\in P.
$$
Then for some integers
$$
m,n
$$
and some
$$
z_1,z_2\in Z(P),
$$
one has
$$
x=g^mz_1,
\qquad
y=g^nz_2.
$$
Since
$$
z_1,z_2
$$
are central,
$$
\begin{aligned}
xy
&=
g^mz_1g^nz_2\\
&=
g^{m+n}z_1z_2\\
&=
g^{n+m}z_2z_1\\
&=
g^nz_2g^mz_1\\
&=
yx.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every pair of elements commutes.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
P
\text{ is abelian}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>4 shows
$$
P/Z(P)
$$
is cyclic.
Step <1>5 then implies that $P$ is abelian.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>6 is the required conclusion.

:::
