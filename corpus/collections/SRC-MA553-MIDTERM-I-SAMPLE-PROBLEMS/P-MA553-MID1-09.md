::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group of order $p^{3}$, where $p$ is prime, and suppose $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ with $\cardinality{H} = p$. Prove or disprove that there must exist a subgroup $K$ of $G$ such that $H \subseteq K$ and $\cardinality{K} = p^{2}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The statement is true.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let $H$ act on the set of left cosets
$$
G/H
$$
by left multiplication:
$$
h\cdot(gH)
\coloneqq
hgH.
$$


::: pf-proof

For
$$
h_1,h_2\in H,
$$
one has
$$
h_1\cdot(h_2\cdot gH)
=
(h_1h_2)gH
=
(h_1h_2)\cdot gH,
$$
so this is a group action.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

A coset
$$
gH
$$
is fixed by every element of $H$ if and only if
$$
g\in N_G(H).
$$


::: pf-proof

The coset $gH$ is fixed by $H$ exactly when
$$
hgH=gH
$$
for every
$$
h\in H.
$$
This is equivalent to
$$
g^{-1}hg\in H
$$
for every $h\in H$, hence to
$$
g^{-1}Hg\subseteq H.
$$
Both subgroups have order $p$, so the inclusion is equality:
$$
g^{-1}Hg=H.
$$
This is precisely
$$
g\in N_G(H).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The set of fixed points of the action is naturally identified with
$$
N_G(H)/H.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the fixed cosets are exactly those of the form
$$
gH
$$
with
$$
g\in N_G(H).
$$
These are precisely the left cosets of $H$ inside the normalizer.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The number of fixed points is divisible by $p$.


::: pf-proof

The acting group $H$ has order $p$.
Every orbit therefore has size $1$ or $p$.
The total number of points is
$$
\abs{G/H}
=
\frac{p^3}{p}
=
p^2.
$$
Hence, modulo $p$, the total number of points is congruent to the number of orbits of size $1$, i.e. the number of fixed points.
Thus
$$
\abs{(G/H)^H}
\equiv
p^2
\equiv
0
\pmod p.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The action has at least one fixed point.


::: pf-proof

The coset
$$
H
$$
itself is fixed by left multiplication by every
$$
h\in H,
$$
because
$$
hH=H.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
\abs{N_G(H)/H}
\ge
p.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the number of fixed points is
$$
\abs{N_G(H)/H}.
$$
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} this number is divisible by $p$, and by step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} it is positive.
Hence it is at least $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Consequently
$$
\abs{N_G(H)}
\ge
p^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\abs H=p,
$$
step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{N_G(H)}
=
\abs H\,
\abs{N_G(H)/H}
\ge
p\cdot p
=
p^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The subgroup $H$ is normal in $N_G(H)$.


::: pf-proof

This is the defining property of the normalizer:
$$
N_G(H)
=
\{g\in G:gHg^{-1}=H\}.
$$
Thus every element of $N_G(H)$ normalizes $H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The quotient
$$
N_G(H)/H
$$
has order divisible by $p$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{N_G(H)}
$$
is either
$$
p^2
$$
or
$$
p^3,
$$
because it divides
$$
\abs G=p^3.
$$
Therefore
$$
\abs{N_G(H)/H}
$$
is either
$$
p
$$
or
$$
p^2.
$$
In either case it is divisible by $p$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists a subgroup
$$
\overline K
\le
N_G(H)/H
$$
of order $p$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
p
\mid
\abs{N_G(H)/H}.
$$
Cauchy's theorem gives an element of order $p$, whose cyclic subgroup has order $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Let
$$
\pi:N_G(H)\to N_G(H)/H
$$
be the quotient map and define
$$
K
\coloneqq
\pi^{-1}(\overline K).
$$
Then
$$
H\subseteq K.
$$


::: pf-proof

The kernel of $\pi$ is
$$
H.
$$
Since the kernel is contained in the preimage of every subgroup of the quotient,
$$
H\subseteq K.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The subgroup $K$ has order
$$
p^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The quotient
$$
K/H
$$
is isomorphic to
$$
\overline K.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{K/H}
=
p.
$$
Since
$$
\abs H=p,
$$
one gets
$$
\abs K
=
\abs H\,
\abs{K/H}
=
p^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore there exists a subgroup
$$
\boxed{
H\subseteq K\le G,
\qquad
\abs K=p^2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The subgroup $K$ constructed in step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} satisfies the inclusion there and has the required order by step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} proves the statement.
