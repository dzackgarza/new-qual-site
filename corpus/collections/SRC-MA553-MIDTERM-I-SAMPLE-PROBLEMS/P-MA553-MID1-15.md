::: {.problem}
Show that a simple group which has a subgroup of index $n > 2$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of the alternating group $A_{n}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
G
$$
be simple and let
$$
H\le G
$$
satisfy
$$
[G:H]=n>2.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The left action of $G$ on the coset set
$$
G/H
$$
defines a homomorphism
$$
\varphi:G\to S_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The set
$$
G/H
$$
has exactly $n$ elements.
For
$$
g\in G,
$$
define
$$
\varphi(g)(xH)
\coloneqq
gxH.
$$
Then
$$
\varphi(g_1g_2)
=
\varphi(g_1)\varphi(g_2),
$$
so $\varphi$ is a group homomorphism into the symmetric group on the $n$ cosets.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The homomorphism $\varphi$ is nontrivial.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\varphi
$$
were trivial, every element of $G$ would fix the coset
$$
H.
$$
Thus
$$
gH=H
$$
for every
$$
g\in G,
$$
which implies
$$
G=H.
$$
This contradicts
$$
[G:H]=n>2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The kernel of $\varphi$ is trivial.


::: pf-proof

The kernel
$$
\ker\varphi
$$
is normal in $G$.
Since $G$ is simple, the kernel is either
$$
\{1\}
$$
or
$$
G.
$$
Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} rules out
$$
\ker\varphi=G.
$$
Hence
$$
\ker\varphi=\{1\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\varphi
$$
embeds $G$ into $S_n$.


::: pf-proof

A homomorphism with trivial kernel is injective.
Thus
$$
G
\cong
\varphi(G)
\le
S_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consider the composite homomorphism
$$
\chi
\coloneqq
\operatorname{sgn}\circ\varphi:
G\to\{\pm1\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The sign map
$$
\operatorname{sgn}:S_n\to\{\pm1\}
$$
is a homomorphism.
Composing it with $\varphi$ gives the displayed homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The homomorphism $\chi$ is trivial.


::: pf-proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
\chi
$$
is nontrivial.
Then its image is all of
$$
\{\pm1\},
$$
so
$$
\ker\chi
$$
is a normal subgroup of index $2$ in $G$.

Since $G$ is simple, a proper normal subgroup must be trivial.
Thus
$$
\ker\chi=\{1\},
$$
and the first isomorphism theorem gives
$$
G
\cong
\{\pm1\}.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs G=2.
$$

But
$$
\abs G
=
[G:H]\abs H
\ge
n
>
2,
$$
a contradiction.
Therefore
$$
\chi
$$
is trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Hence
$$
\varphi(G)
\subseteq
A_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Triviality of
$$
\chi
=
\operatorname{sgn}\circ\varphi
$$
means
$$
\operatorname{sgn}(\varphi(g))
=
1
$$
for every
$$
g\in G.
$$
Thus every permutation in
$$
\varphi(G)
$$
is even.
Hence
$$
\varphi(G)\le A_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
G
\text{ is isomorphic to a subgroup of }A_n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives an injective homomorphism
$$
\varphi:G\hookrightarrow S_n,
$$
and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} shows its image lies in
$$
A_n.
$$
Therefore
$$
G
\cong
\varphi(G)
\le
A_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
