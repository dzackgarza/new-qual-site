::: {.problem}
Let G be a finite group, $p > 0$ a prime number, and H a normal subgroup of G. Prove the following assertions.

(a) Any Sylow p-subgroup of H is the intersection $P \cap H$ of a Sylow p-subgroup P of G and subgroup H.

(b) Any Sylow p-subgroup of G/H is the quotient $PH/H$ for some Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let
$$
Q
\le
H
$$
be a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.
Then $Q$ is a $p$-subgroup of $G$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
Q\le H\le G,
$$
it is a subgroup of $G$.
Its order is a power of $p$ by definition of Sylow subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There exists a Sylow $p$-subgroup
$$
P
\le
G
$$
such that
$$
Q\subseteq P.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every $p$-subgroup of a finite group is contained in a Sylow $p$-subgroup.
Apply this to the $p$-subgroup $Q$ from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The intersection
$$
P\cap H
$$
is a $p$-subgroup of $H$ containing $Q$.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
P
$$
is a $p$-group, every subgroup of $P$ is a $p$-group.
Thus
$$
P\cap H
$$
is a $p$-subgroup.
It lies in $H$ by definition.
Since
$$
Q\subseteq P
$$
and
$$
Q\subseteq H,
$$
one has
$$
Q\subseteq P\cap H.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
Q=P\cap H.
$$


::: pf-proof

The subgroup $Q$ is Sylow in $H$, so no $p$-subgroup of $H$ properly contains it.
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives a $p$-subgroup
$$
P\cap H
$$
of $H$ containing $Q$.
Therefore equality holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

This proves part (a):
$$
\boxed{
\text{every Sylow }p\text{-subgroup of }H
\text{ equals }P\cap H
\text{ for some Sylow }P\le G.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For part (b), let
$$
\overline Q
\le
G/H
$$
be a Sylow $p$-subgroup.
Let
$$
\pi:G\to G/H
$$
be the quotient map and set
$$
K
\coloneqq
\pi^{-1}(\overline Q).
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
\overline Q
$$
is a subgroup of the quotient, its full preimage
$$
K
$$
is a subgroup of $G$ containing $H$.
Moreover,
$$
K/H
\cong
\overline Q.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The quotient
$$
K/H
$$
is a $p$-group.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
K/H
\cong
\overline Q.
$$
The subgroup
$$
\overline Q
$$
is Sylow in
$$
G/H,
$$
so its order is a power of $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Let
$$
Q
\le
K
$$
be a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $K$.
Then
$$
QH=K.
$$


::: pf-proof

The subgroup
$$
QH/H
$$
of
$$
K/H
$$
has index
$$
[K:QH].
$$
Because
$$
K/H
$$
is a $p$-group by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, this index is a power of $p$.

On the other hand,
$$
Q\subseteq QH\subseteq K,
$$
so
$$
[K:QH]
$$
divides
$$
[K:Q].
$$
Since $Q$ is Sylow in $K$,
$$
[K:Q]
$$
is relatively prime to $p$.
Therefore
$$
[K:QH]
$$
is both a power of $p$ and relatively prime to $p$.
Hence
$$
[K:QH]=1,
$$
so
$$
QH=K.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Choose a Sylow $p$-subgroup
$$
P
\le
G
$$
containing $Q$.


::: pf-proof

The subgroup $Q$ is a $p$-subgroup of $G$ because
$$
Q\le K\le G.
$$
Every $p$-subgroup is contained in a Sylow $p$-subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The quotient
$$
PH/H
$$
is a $p$-subgroup of
$$
G/H.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $H\trianglelefteq G$,
$$
PH/H
\cong
P/(P\cap H).
$$
The group $P$ is a $p$-group, so every quotient of $P$ is a $p$-group.
Thus
$$
PH/H
$$
is a $p$-subgroup of
$$
G/H.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

One has
$$
\overline Q
\subseteq
PH/H.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
K=QH.
$$
By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
Q\subseteq P.
$$
Hence
$$
K
=
QH
\subseteq
PH.
$$
Passing to the quotient by $H$ gives
$$
K/H
\subseteq
PH/H.
$$
But
$$
K/H
=
\overline Q
$$
by construction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore
$$
PH/H
=
\overline Q.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref},
$$
PH/H
$$
is a $p$-subgroup of
$$
G/H.
$$
By step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}, it contains the Sylow $p$-subgroup
$$
\overline Q.
$$
A Sylow $p$-subgroup is maximal among $p$-subgroups, so equality follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

This proves part (b):
$$
\boxed{
\text{every Sylow }p\text{-subgroup of }G/H
\text{ is }PH/H
\text{ for some Sylow }P\le G.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} construct the required Sylow subgroup $P$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
