::: {.problem}
Let G be a group having order 2k, where k is an odd integer.
Prove that G has a subgroup of order k.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\lambda:G\to S_G
$$
be the left-regular permutation representation, where
$$
\lambda(g)(x)
\coloneqq
gx
$$
for
$$
g,x\in G.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The map $\lambda$ is a group homomorphism.


::: pf-proof

For
$$
g,h,x\in G,
$$
one has
$$
\lambda(gh)(x)
=(gh)x
=
g(hx)
=
\lambda(g)(\lambda(h)(x)).
$$
Thus
$$
\lambda(gh)
=
\lambda(g)\lambda(h).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $\abs G=2k$ is even, $G$ contains an element $t$ of order $2$.


::: pf-proof

By Cauchy's theorem, because
$$
2\mid\abs G,
$$
there exists
$$
t\in G
$$
with
$$
\operatorname{ord}(t)=2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The permutation $\lambda(t)$ has no fixed points.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\lambda(t)(x)=x.
$$
Then
$$
tx=x.
$$
Multiplying on the right by
$$
x^{-1}
$$
gives
$$
t=1,
$$
contradicting
$$
\operatorname{ord}(t)=2.
$$
Thus $\lambda(t)$ fixes no element of $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The permutation $\lambda(t)$ is a product of exactly $k$ disjoint transpositions.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
t^2=1,
$$
the permutation
$$
\lambda(t)
$$
has order $2$.
Therefore every cycle has length $1$ or $2$.
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} there are no fixed points, so every cycle has length $2$.
The set being permuted has
$$
2k
$$
elements, hence there are exactly
$$
k
$$
disjoint transpositions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The permutation $\lambda(t)$ is odd.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
\lambda(t)
$$
is a product of $k$ transpositions.
Its sign is therefore
$$
(-1)^k.
$$
Since $k$ is odd,
$$
(-1)^k=-1.
$$
Thus $\lambda(t)$ is odd.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
\chi:G\to\{\pm1\}
$$
by
$$
\chi(g)
\coloneqq
\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda(g)).
$$
Then $\chi$ is a group homomorphism.


::: pf-proof

The regular representation
$$
\lambda
$$
is a homomorphism by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, and the sign map
$$
\operatorname{sgn}:S_G\to\{\pm1\}
$$
is a homomorphism.
Therefore their composition
$$
\chi
=
\operatorname{sgn}\circ\lambda
$$
is a homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The homomorphism $\chi$ is nontrivial.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
\chi(t)
=
\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda(t))
=
-1.
$$
Hence
$$
\chi
$$
is not the trivial homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The image of $\chi$ is all of $\{\pm1\}$.


::: pf-proof

The image is a subgroup of
$$
\{\pm1\}.
$$
By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} it contains
$$
-1,
$$
and it always contains
$$
1.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{im}\chi
=
\{\pm1\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The kernel
$$
H
\coloneqq
\ker\chi
$$
has index $2$ in $G$.


::: pf-proof

By the first isomorphism theorem,
$$
G/\ker\chi
\cong
\operatorname{im}\chi.
$$
Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\abs{\operatorname{im}\chi}=2.
$$
Hence
$$
[G:H]=2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\abs H=k.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs G
=
2\abs H.
$$
Since
$$
\abs G=2k,
$$
one obtains
$$
\abs H=k.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Hence $G$ has a subgroup of order $k$.


::: pf-proof

The subgroup
$$
H=\ker\chi
$$
constructed above satisfies
$$
\boxed{\abs H=k}
$$
by step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
