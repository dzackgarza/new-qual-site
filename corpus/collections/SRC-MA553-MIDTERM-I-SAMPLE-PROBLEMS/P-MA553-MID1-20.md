::: {.problem}
(a) Find all abelian groups of order $40$, up to isomorphism.

(b) Find the number of elements of order $2$ in each of them.
:::

::: {.solution}

Since
$$
40=2^3\cdot5,
$$
the classification of finite abelian groups reduces the problem to the abelian groups of orders $8$ and $5$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Up to isomorphism, the abelian groups of order $8$ are
$$
C_8,
\qquad
C_4\times C_2,
\qquad
C_2\times C_2\times C_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The partitions of the exponent
$$
3
$$
are
$$
3,
\qquad
2+1,
\qquad
1+1+1.
$$
The fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups therefore gives exactly the three displayed $2$-groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Up to isomorphism, the only abelian group of order $5$ is
$$
C_5.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every group of prime order is cyclic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore the abelian groups of order $40$ are exactly
$$
\boxed{
C_8\times C_5,
\qquad
C_4\times C_2\times C_5,
\qquad
C_2\times C_2\times C_2\times C_5.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For a finite abelian group $G$ of order
$$
2^3\cdot5,
$$
the Sylow $2$-subgroup and Sylow $5$-subgroup are characteristic and
$$
G
\cong
G_{(2)}
\times
G_{(5)}.
$$
Combine steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

In a direct product
$$
H\times C_5,
$$
an element has order $2$ if and only if its $C_5$-coordinate is the identity and its $H$-coordinate has order $2$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
(h,z)\in H\times C_5.
$$
If it has order $2$, then
$$
(h,z)^2
=
(h^2,z^2)
=
(1,1).
$$
Thus
$$
z^2=1
$$
in the group $C_5$.
Since $C_5$ has odd order, its only element whose order divides $2$ is the identity.
Hence
$$
z=1.
$$
The element then has order $2$ exactly when $h$ has order $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The group
$$
C_8\times C_5
$$
has exactly one element of order $2$.


::: pf-proof

The cyclic group
$$
C_8
$$
has a unique element of order $2$, namely the fourth power of a generator.
Apply step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.
Thus the number is
$$
\boxed1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The group
$$
C_4\times C_2\times C_5
$$
has exactly three elements of order $2$.


::: pf-proof

In
$$
C_4\times C_2,
$$
the elements whose order divides $2$ are those whose first coordinate lies in the unique subgroup
$$
C_2\le C_4
$$
and whose second coordinate is arbitrary.
Thus there are
$$
2\cdot2
=
4
$$
elements whose order divides $2$.
One is the identity, so exactly
$$
4-1
=
\boxed3
$$
have order exactly $2$.
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows the $C_5$ factor contributes no further choices.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The group
$$
C_2\times C_2\times C_2\times C_5
$$
has exactly seven elements of order $2$.


::: pf-proof

Every nonidentity element of
$$
C_2^3
$$
has order $2$.
There are
$$
2^3-1
=
\boxed7
$$
such elements.
Again, step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} forces the $C_5$-coordinate to be the identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence the complete answer is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{group} & \#\{\text{elements of order }2\}\\
\hline
C_8\times C_5 & 1\\
C_4\times C_2\times C_5 & 3\\
C_2^3\times C_5 & 7.
\end{array}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives all isomorphism classes, and steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} give the corresponding counts.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} answers both parts.

:::
