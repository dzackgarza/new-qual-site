::: {.problem}
Assume G contains a normal Sylow 2-subgroup P which is a cyclic group, and such that $G / P$ is cyclic.

(a) Show that the action of $G$ by conjugation on $P$ is trivial. (Hint: consider the induced action of $G/P$ on $P$.)

(b) Show that G is abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\rho:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(P)
$$
be the conjugation action:
$$
\rho(g)(x)
\coloneqq
gxg^{-1}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Every element of $P$ acts trivially on $P$ by conjugation.


::: pf-proof

The subgroup $P$ is cyclic, hence abelian.
Thus for
$$
p,x\in P,
$$
one has
$$
pxp^{-1}=x.
$$
Therefore
$$
P\subseteq\ker\rho.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The action $\rho$ factors through a homomorphism
$$
\overline\rho:
G/P
\to
\operatorname{Aut}(P).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
P\subseteq\ker\rho.
$$
Since $P$ is normal, the universal property of the quotient gives a unique homomorphism
$$
\overline\rho:G/P\to\operatorname{Aut}(P)
$$
such that
$$
\rho
=
\overline\rho\circ\pi,
$$
where
$$
\pi:G\to G/P
$$
is the quotient map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The order of $G/P$ is odd.


::: pf-proof

Since $P$ is a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $G$, the full power of $2$ dividing
$$
\abs G
$$
is
$$
\abs P.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{G/P}
=
\frac{\abs G}{\abs P}
$$
is odd.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The group $\operatorname{Aut}(P)$ has order a power of $2$.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
P=1,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(P)=1.
$$

Otherwise write
$$
\abs P=2^n
$$
with
$$
n\ge1.
$$
Since $P$ is cyclic,
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(P)}
=
\varphi(2^n)
=
2^{n-1}.
$$
Thus in every case
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
$$
is a $2$-group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The image of $\overline\rho$ is trivial.


::: pf-proof

By Lagrange's theorem,
$$
\abs{\operatorname{im}\overline\rho}
$$
divides
$$
\abs{G/P},
$$
which is odd by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

It also divides
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(P)},
$$
which is a power of $2$ by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

The only positive integer dividing both an odd number and a power of $2$ is $1$.
Hence
$$
\abs{\operatorname{im}\overline\rho}
=
1.
$$
Thus
$$
\overline\rho
$$
is trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore the conjugation action of $G$ on $P$ is trivial.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\rho
=
\overline\rho\circ\pi.
$$
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} shows
$$
\overline\rho
$$
is trivial.
Hence
$$
\rho
$$
is trivial:
$$
gxg^{-1}=x
$$
for every
$$
g\in G,
\qquad
x\in P.
$$
This proves part (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Thus
$$
P\subseteq Z(G).
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} says every element of $G$ commutes with every element of $P$.
That is exactly
$$
P\subseteq Z(G).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Choose
$$
g\in G
$$
such that
$$
gP
$$
generates the cyclic group
$$
G/P.
$$


::: pf-proof

The quotient
$$
G/P
$$
is cyclic by hypothesis.
Thus it has a generator
$$
gP
$$
for some
$$
g\in G.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Every element of $G$ has the form
$$
g^i p
$$
for some integer $i$ and some
$$
p\in P.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
x\in G.
$$
Since
$$
gP
$$
generates
$$
G/P,
$$
there exists $i$ such that
$$
xP
=
g^iP.
$$
Hence
$$
g^{-i}x
\in
P.
$$
Write
$$
p
\coloneqq
g^{-i}x.
$$
Then
$$
x=g^ip.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Any two elements of $G$ commute.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
x=g^ip,
\qquad
y=g^jq
$$
with
$$
p,q\in P
$$
as in step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.
By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, the elements $p$ and $q$ are central in $G$.
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
xy
&=
g^ipg^jq\\
&=
g^{i+j}pq\\
&=
g^{j+i}qp\\
&=
g^jqg^ip\\
&=
yx.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every pair of elements commutes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
G
\text{ is abelian}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} shows that all elements of $G$ commute.
This proves part (b).

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
