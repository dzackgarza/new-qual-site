::: {.problem}
Describe the Sylow $p$-subgroups in $A_{4}$, $S_{4}$, $A_{5}$, $S_{5}$ and find their number.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For $A_4$, a Sylow $2$-subgroup is
$$
V
\coloneqq
\{
1,
(12)(34),
(13)(24),
(14)(23)
\}
\cong
C_2\times C_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group $A_4$ has order
$$
12=2^2\cdot3.
$$
Thus a Sylow $2$-subgroup has order $4$.
The displayed set is closed under multiplication and has four elements, so it is a Sylow $2$-subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The Sylow $2$-subgroup of $A_4$ is unique.


::: pf-proof

The three nonidentity elements of $V$ are exactly the double transpositions in $A_4$.
Any subgroup of order $4$ in $A_4$ cannot be cyclic, because $A_4$ has no element of order $4$.
Thus every Sylow $2$-subgroup is a Klein four group and contains three elements of order $2$.
There are exactly three such elements in $A_4$, so every Sylow $2$-subgroup equals $V$.
Hence
$$
\boxed{n_2(A_4)=1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

A Sylow $3$-subgroup of $A_4$ is
$$
\langle(123)\rangle
\cong
C_3,
$$
and there are four of them.


::: pf-proof

There are
$$
\binom43\cdot2
=
8
$$
$3$-cycles in $A_4$.
Each subgroup of order $3$ has exactly two nonidentity elements, both $3$-cycles.
Distinct subgroups of order $3$ intersect trivially.
Therefore
$$
n_3(A_4)
=
\frac82
=
\boxed4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For $S_4$, a Sylow $2$-subgroup is
$$
P
\coloneqq
\langle
(1234),(13)
\rangle
\cong
D_8.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group $S_4$ has order
$$
24=2^3\cdot3.
$$
Thus a Sylow $2$-subgroup has order $8$.

Let
$$
r=(1234),
\qquad
s=(13).
$$
Then
$$
r^4=s^2=1
$$
and
$$
srs=r^{-1}.
$$
Hence
$$
\langle r,s\rangle
$$
is a dihedral group of order $8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

There are exactly three Sylow $2$-subgroups of $S_4$.


::: pf-proof

Consider the three partitions of
$$
\{1,2,3,4\}
$$
into two unordered pairs:
$$
\{\{1,2\},\{3,4\}\},
$$
$$
\{\{1,3\},\{2,4\}\},
$$
and
$$
\{\{1,4\},\{2,3\}\}.
$$
The setwise stabilizer in $S_4$ of each such partition has order
$$
2\cdot2\cdot2=8:
$$
one may independently swap the entries in each pair and swap the two pairs.
Thus each stabilizer is a Sylow $2$-subgroup.
The three stabilizers are distinct, so
$$
n_2(S_4)\ge3.
$$
Sylow's theorem gives
$$
n_2(S_4)\mid3
$$
and
$$
n_2(S_4)\equiv1\pmod2.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{n_2(S_4)=3}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

A Sylow $3$-subgroup of $S_4$ is
$$
\langle(123)\rangle,
$$
and there are four of them.


::: pf-proof

As in $A_4$, there are exactly eight $3$-cycles in $S_4$.
Each subgroup of order $3$ contains two nonidentity $3$-cycles.
Hence
$$
\boxed{n_3(S_4)=4}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For $A_5$, a Sylow $2$-subgroup is
$$
V_5
\coloneqq
\{
1,
(12)(34),
(13)(24),
(14)(23)
\}
\cong
C_2\times C_2,
$$
which fixes the point $5$.


::: pf-proof

The group $A_5$ has order
$$
60=2^2\cdot3\cdot5.
$$
Thus a Sylow $2$-subgroup has order $4$.
The displayed subgroup has order $4$ and lies in $A_5$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

There are exactly five Sylow $2$-subgroups of $A_5$.


::: pf-proof

An element of order $2$ in $A_5$ must be a double transposition.
To choose one, first choose its fixed point in $5$ ways, then partition the remaining four points into two pairs in $3$ ways.
Thus there are
$$
5\cdot3=15
$$
double transpositions.

Every Sylow $2$-subgroup has order $4$ and cannot be cyclic, because $A_5$ has no element of order $4$.
Hence it is a Klein four group and contains exactly three nonidentity double transpositions.

The three double transpositions fixing a given point form one such subgroup, and a double transposition has a unique fixed point.
Therefore the $15$ double transpositions split into sets of three according to their fixed point:
$$
n_2(A_5)
=
\frac{15}{3}
=
\boxed5.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

A Sylow $3$-subgroup of $A_5$ is
$$
\langle(123)\rangle,
$$
and there are ten of them.


::: pf-proof

There are
$$
\binom53\cdot2
=
20
$$
$3$-cycles in $A_5$.
Each subgroup of order $3$ has two nonidentity elements.
Thus
$$
n_3(A_5)
=
\frac{20}{2}
=
\boxed{10}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

A Sylow $5$-subgroup of $A_5$ is
$$
\langle(12345)\rangle,
$$
and there are six of them.


::: pf-proof

Every $5$-cycle is even, and there are
$$
(5-1)!
=
24
$$
$5$-cycles in $A_5$.
Each subgroup of order $5$ has four nonidentity elements, all $5$-cycles.
Therefore
$$
n_5(A_5)
=
\frac{24}{4}
=
\boxed6.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

For $S_5$, a Sylow $3$-subgroup is cyclic of order $3$, and there are ten.


::: pf-proof

The group $S_5$ has order
$$
120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5.
$$
A Sylow $3$-subgroup has order $3$ and is generated by a $3$-cycle.
There are
$$
\binom53\cdot2
=
20
$$
$3$-cycles, two in each subgroup.
Hence
$$
\boxed{n_3(S_5)=10}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

For $S_5$, a Sylow $5$-subgroup is cyclic of order $5$, and there are six.


::: pf-proof

There are
$$
24
$$
$5$-cycles in $S_5$, and each subgroup of order $5$ has four nonidentity $5$-cycles.
Thus
$$
\boxed{n_5(S_5)=6}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Every Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_5$ fixes a point.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
P
\le
S_5
$$
be a Sylow $2$-subgroup.
Then
$$
\abs P=8.
$$
The $P$-orbits on the five-point set have sizes dividing $\abs P$, hence powers of $2$.
Their sizes sum to $5$, an odd number.
Therefore at least one orbit has size $1$.
Thus $P$ fixes a point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

A Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_5$ fixes exactly one point.


::: pf-proof

If a subgroup
$$
P
$$
of order $8$ fixed two distinct points, then it would act faithfully on the remaining three points and embed in
$$
S_3.
$$
But
$$
8\nmid6,
$$
so $S_3$ has no subgroup of order $8$.
Hence a Sylow $2$-subgroup cannot fix two points.
Combined with step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}, it fixes exactly one.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

There are exactly fifteen Sylow $2$-subgroups of $S_5$.


::: pf-proof

Fix a point.
Its stabilizer in $S_5$ is isomorphic to
$$
S_4.
$$
By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, this stabilizer contains exactly three Sylow $2$-subgroups.
Each such subgroup has order $8$, so it is also a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_5$.

There are five choices of fixed point.
By step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}, each Sylow $2$-subgroup has exactly one fixed point, so no subgroup is counted twice.
Therefore
$$
n_2(S_5)
=
5\cdot3
=
\boxed{15}.
$$
Each is isomorphic to the dihedral group $D_8$ from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

The complete list is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
G & p & \text{Sylow }p\text{-subgroup and number}\\
\hline
A_4 & 2 & V_4,\ n_2=1\\
    & 3 & C_3,\ n_3=4\\
\hline
S_4 & 2 & D_8,\ n_2=3\\
    & 3 & C_3,\ n_3=4\\
\hline
A_5 & 2 & V_4,\ n_2=5\\
    & 3 & C_3,\ n_3=10\\
    & 5 & C_5,\ n_5=6\\
\hline
S_5 & 2 & D_8,\ n_2=15\\
    & 3 & C_3,\ n_3=10\\
    & 5 & C_5,\ n_5=6.
\end{array}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

This table collects steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}.

:::
