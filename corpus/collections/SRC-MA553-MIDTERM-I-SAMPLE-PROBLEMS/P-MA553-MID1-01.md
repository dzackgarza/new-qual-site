::: {.problem}
Does the symmetric group $S_{5}$ have a subgroup of order $10$? Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
r
\coloneqq
(1\,2\,3\,4\,5)
$$
and
$$
s
\coloneqq
(2\,5)(3\,4)
$$
in $S_5$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The element $r$ has order $5$.


::: pf-proof

The permutation $r$ is a $5$-cycle, so
$$
r^5=1
$$
and no smaller positive power is the identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The element $s$ has order $2$.


::: pf-proof

The permutation $s$ is the product of the two disjoint transpositions
$$
(2\,5)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(3\,4).
$$
Hence
$$
s^2=1.
$$
Since $s\ne1$, its order is $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
srs=r^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $s=s^{-1}$, conjugation sends the cycle
$$
(1\,2\,3\,4\,5)
$$
to
$$
(s(1)\,s(2)\,s(3)\,s(4)\,s(5)).
$$
Using
$$
s(1)=1,
\quad
s(2)=5,
\quad
s(3)=4,
\quad
s(4)=3,
\quad
s(5)=2,
$$
one obtains
$$
srs
=
(1\,5\,4\,3\,2)
=
r^{-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Let
$$
H
\coloneqq
\langle r,s\rangle.
$$
Every element of $H$ can be written in the form
$$
r^i
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
r^is
$$
with
$$
0\le i\le4.
$$


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
sr
=
r^{-1}s.
$$
Thus in any word in $r$ and $s$, every occurrence of $s$ can be moved to the far right by replacing
$$
sr^j
$$
with
$$
r^{-j}s.
$$
Because
$$
s^2=1
$$
and
$$
r^5=1,
$$
every word reduces to either
$$
r^i
$$
or
$$
r^is
$$
with
$$
0\le i\le4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The five elements
$$
1,r,r^2,r^3,r^4
$$
are distinct.


::: pf-proof

This follows from
$$
\operatorname{ord}(r)=5
$$
in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The five elements
$$
s,rs,r^2s,r^3s,r^4s
$$
are distinct.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
r^is=r^js,
$$
then multiplying on the right by $s$ gives
$$
r^i=r^j.
$$
By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
i=j
$$
modulo $5$, and with
$$
0\le i,j\le4
$$
this gives
$$
i=j.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

No element
$$
r^i
$$
equals an element
$$
r^js.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
r^i=r^js.
$$
Then
$$
s=r^{i-j}.
$$
The right-hand side belongs to the cyclic group
$$
\langle r\rangle
$$
of order $5$, so its order divides $5$.
But $s$ has order $2$ by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
This is impossible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore $H$ has exactly $10$ elements.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that $H$ has at most the ten elements
$$
r^i,\ r^is
\qquad
(0\le i\le4).
$$
Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} show that all ten are distinct.
Hence
$$
\abs{H}=10.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Thus $S_5$ has a subgroup of order $10$.


::: pf-proof

The subgroup
$$
H=\langle r,s\rangle
$$
lies in $S_5$ and has order $10$ by step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.
Hence
$$
\boxed{\text{$S_5$ does have a subgroup of order $10$.}}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives the required subgroup.

:::
