::: {.problem}
(a) List representatives for each conjugacy class in the symmetric group $S_{4}$ and state the number of elements in each conjugacy class.

(b) List representatives for each conjugacy class in the alternating group $A_{4}$ and state the number of elements in each conjugacy class.

(c) Determine the number of elements of order 2 in the symmetric group $S_{5}$.

(d) Determine the number of elements of order 2 in the alternating group $A_{5}$.

(e) Find the number of 2-subgroups in $A_{4}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Conjugacy classes in $S_4$ are determined by cycle type.


::: pf-proof

Two permutations in a symmetric group are conjugate if and only if they have the same cycle type.
The partitions of $4$ are
$$
1+1+1+1,
\quad
2+1+1,
\quad
2+2,
\quad
3+1,
\quad
4.
$$
Thus there are five conjugacy classes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The identity class in $S_4$ has representative $1$ and size $1$.


::: pf-proof

The identity is fixed by conjugation, so its conjugacy class contains only itself.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The transposition class in $S_4$ has representative $(12)$ and size $6$.


::: pf-proof

A transposition is determined by choosing its two moved points.
Hence there are
$$
\binom42
=
6
$$
transpositions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The double-transposition class in $S_4$ has representative $(12)(34)$ and size $3$.


::: pf-proof

The three pairings of four points into two unordered pairs are
$$
(12)(34),
\qquad
(13)(24),
\qquad
(14)(23).
$$
These are exactly the permutations of cycle type $(2,2)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The $3$-cycle class in $S_4$ has representative $(123)$ and size $8$.


::: pf-proof

Choose the three moved points in
$$
\binom43=4
$$
ways.
On a fixed set of three points there are
$$
(3-1)!=2
$$
$3$-cycles.
Thus there are
$$
4\cdot2=8.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The $4$-cycle class in $S_4$ has representative $(1234)$ and size $6$.


::: pf-proof

The number of $4$-cycles on four letters is
$$
(4-1)!
=
6.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore the conjugacy classes of $S_4$ are
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{representative} & \text{class size}\\
\hline
1 & 1\\
(12) & 6\\
(12)(34) & 3\\
(123) & 8\\
(1234) & 6.
\end{array}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} give the five cycle types and their sizes, whose total is
$$
1+6+3+8+6
=
24
=
\abs{S_4}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

In $A_4$, the identity forms a conjugacy class of size $1$.


::: pf-proof

As in every group, the identity is central.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The three double transpositions form one conjugacy class in $A_4$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
V
=
\{
1,
(12)(34),
(13)(24),
(14)(23)
\}.
$$
Conjugation by the $3$-cycle $(123)$ sends
$$
(12)(34)
$$
to
$$
(23)(14),
$$
which is
$$
(14)(23),
$$
and further conjugation cycles through the three nonidentity elements of $V$.
Hence those three elements are conjugate in $A_4$.
Thus the class has size $3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The centralizer in $A_4$ of $(123)$ has order $3$.


::: pf-proof

In $S_4$, a permutation commuting with $(123)$ must preserve its support
$$
\{1,2,3\}
$$
and fix $4$.
The centralizer is therefore
$$
\langle(123)\rangle,
$$
which has order $3$ and lies in $A_4$.
Thus
$$
C_{A_4}((123))
=
\langle(123)\rangle.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The conjugacy class of $(123)$ in $A_4$ has size $4$.


::: pf-proof

By the orbit-stabilizer formula for conjugation,
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Cl}_{A_4}((123))}
=
\frac{\abs{A_4}}{\abs{C_{A_4}((123))}}
=
\frac{12}{3}
=
4.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The elements $(123)$ and $(132)$ are not conjugate in $A_4$.


::: pf-proof

Any permutation in $S_4$ conjugating $(123)$ to its inverse $(132)$ normalizes the subgroup
$$
\langle(123)\rangle
$$
and induces the nontrivial automorphism of that cyclic group.
The transposition $(23)$ does this.
Every other such conjugator differs from $(23)$ by an element of the centralizer
$$
\langle(123)\rangle.
$$
All elements of
$$
\langle(123)\rangle
$$
are even, whereas $(23)$ is odd.
Hence every conjugator sending $(123)$ to $(132)$ is odd.
Therefore no such conjugator lies in $A_4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The eight $3$-cycles in $A_4$ split into two conjugacy classes of size $4$, represented by $(123)$ and $(132)$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} shows every $3$-cycle class in $A_4$ has size $4$.
There are eight $3$-cycles total.
Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} shows $(123)$ and $(132)$ lie in different classes.
Thus these two classes exhaust all eight $3$-cycles.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Therefore the conjugacy classes of $A_4$ are
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{representative} & \text{class size}\\
\hline
1 & 1\\
(12)(34) & 3\\
(123) & 4\\
(132) & 4.
\end{array}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}.
The sizes sum to
$$
1+3+4+4
=
12
=
\abs{A_4}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

An element of order $2$ in $S_5$ has cycle type either $(2,1,1,1)$ or $(2,2,1)$.


::: pf-proof

The order of a permutation is the least common multiple of its cycle lengths.
For the order to be exactly $2$, every nontrivial cycle must have length $2$.
On five letters there can be either one transposition or two disjoint transpositions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

There are $10$ transpositions in $S_5$.


::: pf-proof

A transposition is determined by choosing two of the five points:
$$
\binom52
=
10.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

There are $15$ products of two disjoint transpositions in $S_5$.


::: pf-proof

Choose the fixed point in $5$ ways.
The remaining four points can be partitioned into two unordered pairs in
$$
3
$$
ways.
Hence the count is
$$
5\cdot3
=
15.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Therefore the number of elements of order $2$ in $S_5$ is
$$
\boxed{10+15=25}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The two cycle types in step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} are disjoint and exhaust all involutions.
Apply steps [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-17){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

The number of elements of order $2$ in $A_5$ is
$$
\boxed{15}.
$$


::: pf-proof

A single transposition is odd and therefore does not belong to $A_5$.
A product of two disjoint transpositions is even and does belong to $A_5$.
By step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref}, there are $15$ such elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

Every $2$-subgroup of $A_4$ lies in the unique Sylow $2$-subgroup
$$
V
=
\{
1,
(12)(34),
(13)(24),
(14)(23)
\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group $V$ has order $4$ and is the unique Sylow $2$-subgroup of $A_4$.
Every finite $2$-subgroup is contained in some Sylow $2$-subgroup, so every $2$-subgroup lies in $V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

The group $V\cong C_2\times C_2$ has exactly five subgroups.


::: pf-proof

Its subgroups are
$$
\{1\},
$$
the three order-$2$ subgroups
$$
\langle(12)(34)\rangle,
\qquad
\langle(13)(24)\rangle,
\qquad
\langle(14)(23)\rangle,
$$
and $V$ itself.
There are no others by Lagrange's theorem.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

Thus $A_4$ has
$$
\boxed{5}
$$
$2$-subgroups under the standard convention that the trivial subgroup is a $2$-subgroup.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-20){.pf-ref}, all $2$-subgroups occur among the subgroups of $V$.
Step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref} counts five of them.
If only nontrivial $2$-subgroups are intended, the count is $4$.

:::
