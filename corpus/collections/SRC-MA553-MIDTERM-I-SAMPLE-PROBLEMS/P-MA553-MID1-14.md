::: {.problem}
Let G be a group of order 66.

(a) Show that G has a unique subgroup of order 11.

(b) Show that G has a cyclic subgroup-call it H-of order 33.

(c) Show that G has a unique subgroup of order 3.

(d) How many elements of order $\leq 2$ does the automorphism group of H have?
(Justify your answer.)

(e) Any group of order 66 is isomorphic to one and only one of $Z _ { 6 6 } , S _ { 3 } \times$ $Z _ { 1 1 } ~ , Z _ { 3 } \times D _ { 2 2 }$ , or $D _ { 6 6 }$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\abs G
=
66
=
2\cdot3\cdot11.
$$

<1>1. The number $n_{11}$ of Sylow $11$-subgroups satisfies
$$
n_{11}\mid6
$$
and
$$
n_{11}\equiv1\pmod{11}.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is Sylow's theorem.
:::

<1>2. Therefore
$$
n_{11}=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
The positive divisors of $6$ are
$$
1,2,3,6.
$$
Among these, only $1$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $11$.
Hence
$$
\boxed{n_{11}=1}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
N
$$
be the unique Sylow $11$-subgroup.
Then
$$
N\trianglelefteq G
$$
and
$$
N\cong C_{11}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Uniqueness of a Sylow subgroup implies normality.
Every group of prime order is cyclic.
:::

<1>4. Let
$$
P
$$
be any Sylow $3$-subgroup of $G$.
Then
$$
NP
$$
is a subgroup of $G$ of order $33$.

::: {.proof}
Because
$$
N\trianglelefteq G,
$$
the product
$$
NP
$$
is a subgroup.
Also
$$
N\cap P=1
$$
because their orders $11$ and $3$ are coprime.
Hence
$$
\abs{NP}
=
\frac{\abs N\,\abs P}{\abs{N\cap P}}
=
11\cdot3
=
33.
$$
:::

<1>5. Every group of order $33$ is cyclic.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
K
$$
have order
$$
33=3\cdot11.
$$
The number of Sylow $11$-subgroups divides $3$ and is congruent to $1$ modulo $11$, so it is $1$.
The number of Sylow $3$-subgroups divides $11$ and is congruent to $1$ modulo $3$.
The divisors of $11$ are $1$ and $11$, and
$$
11\equiv2\pmod3.
$$
Thus the Sylow $3$-subgroup is also unique.

Let
$$
A\cong C_3
$$
and
$$
B\cong C_{11}
$$
be these normal Sylow subgroups.
Their commutator subgroup
$$
[A,B]
$$
lies in
$$
A\cap B=1,
$$
so $A$ and $B$ commute.
Hence
$$
K
=
A\times B
\cong
C_3\times C_{11}
\cong
C_{33}.
$$
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H
\coloneqq
NP
\cong
C_{33}
}
$$
is a cyclic subgroup of order $33$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 gives
$$
\abs H=33,
$$
and step <1>5 shows every group of that order is cyclic.
This proves part (b).
:::

<1>7. The subgroup $H$ is normal in $G$.

::: {.proof}
Its index is
$$
[G:H]
=
\frac{66}{33}
=
2.
$$
Every subgroup of index $2$ is normal.
:::

<1>8. Every Sylow $3$-subgroup of $G$ is contained in $H$.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
Q
$$
be a Sylow $3$-subgroup.
Consider the quotient map
$$
\pi:G\to G/H.
$$
The image
$$
\pi(Q)
$$
has order dividing
$$
\abs Q=3
$$
and also dividing
$$
\abs{G/H}=2.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{\pi(Q)}=1.
$$
Hence
$$
Q\subseteq H.
$$
:::

<1>9. The group $G$ has a unique subgroup of order $3$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6,
$$
H\cong C_{33}
$$
is cyclic.
A cyclic group has a unique subgroup of each order dividing its order.
Thus $H$ contains exactly one subgroup
$$
A
$$
of order $3$.
By step <1>8, every Sylow $3$-subgroup of $G$ lies in $H$, so every such subgroup equals $A$.
Hence
$$
\boxed{n_3(G)=1}.
$$
This proves part (c).
:::

<1>10. The subgroup
$$
H
$$
is the unique subgroup of $G$ of order $33$.

::: {.proof}
Any subgroup of order $33$ contains a Sylow $11$-subgroup of $G$ and a Sylow $3$-subgroup of $G$.
Both are unique by steps <1>2 and <1>9.
Therefore any subgroup of order $33$ contains
$$
N
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A,
$$
and hence contains
$$
NA=H.
$$
Since both have order $33$, they are equal.
:::

<1>11. Since
$$
H\cong C_{33},
$$
one has
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(H)
\cong
(\ZZ/33)^\times.
$$

::: {.proof}
An automorphism of a cyclic group
$$
\langle h\rangle
$$
of order $33$ is determined by
$$
h\mapsto h^u,
$$
where
$$
u
$$
is invertible modulo $33$.
Thus the automorphism group is the unit group
$$
(\ZZ/33)^\times.
$$
:::

<1>12. The elements of $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$ of order dividing $2$ correspond to solutions of
$$
u^2\equiv1\pmod{33}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The automorphism
$$
h\mapsto h^u
$$
has square
$$
h\mapsto h^{u^2}.
$$
Its order divides $2$ exactly when
$$
u^2\equiv1\pmod{33}.
$$
:::

<1>13. There are exactly four solutions to
$$
u^2\equiv1\pmod{33}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By the Chinese remainder theorem,
$$
\ZZ/33
\cong
\ZZ/3\times\ZZ/11.
$$
The congruence
$$
u^2\equiv1\pmod{33}
$$
is equivalent to the pair
$$
u^2\equiv1\pmod3
$$
and
$$
u^2\equiv1\pmod{11}.
$$
Over either prime field, the only solutions are
$$
u\equiv\pm1.
$$
Thus there are
$$
2\cdot2
=
4
$$
solutions modulo $33$.
:::

<1>14. Hence $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$ has exactly four elements of order at most $2$.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>11--<1>13, the elements whose orders divide $2$ are exactly the four automorphisms corresponding to the four pairs of signs
$$
(\pm1,\pm1)
$$
under the Chinese remainder decomposition.
Thus
$$
\boxed{4}
$$
automorphisms have order $1$ or $2$.
This proves part (d).
:::

<1>15. By Cauchy's theorem, $G$ contains an element
$$
t
$$
of order $2$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
2\mid\abs G,
$$
Cauchy's theorem gives an involution.
:::

<1>16. The involution $t$ does not belong to $H$.

::: {.proof}
The group $H$ has odd order
$$
33.
$$
It therefore contains no element of order $2$.
:::

<1>17. One has
$$
G
=
H\rtimes\langle t\rangle.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>7,
$$
H\trianglelefteq G.
$$
By step <1>16,
$$
H\cap\langle t\rangle=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
\abs{H\langle t\rangle}
=
\abs H\cdot\abs{\langle t\rangle}
=
33\cdot2
=
66
=
\abs G.
$$
Hence
$$
G=H\langle t\rangle,
$$
with $H$ normal, so $G$ is the displayed semidirect product.
:::

<1>18. Conjugation by $t$ defines an automorphism
$$
\alpha
\in
\operatorname{Aut}(H)
$$
whose order divides $2$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
H\trianglelefteq G,
$$
conjugation by $t$ preserves $H$.
Thus
$$
\alpha(h)
\coloneqq
tht^{-1}
$$
defines an automorphism of $H$.
Because
$$
t^2=1,
$$
one has
$$
\alpha^2=\operatorname{id}_H.
$$
:::

<1>19. Let
$$
A
\le H
$$
and
$$
B
\le H
$$
be the unique subgroups of orders $3$ and $11$, respectively.
Then
$$
H=A\times B.
$$

::: {.proof}
The cyclic group
$$
H\cong C_{33}
$$
has unique subgroups of orders $3$ and $11$.
Their intersection is trivial, they commute, and their product has order
$$
3\cdot11
=
33.
$$
Thus their product is all of $H$.
:::

<1>20. The automorphism $\alpha$ preserves both $A$ and $B$.

::: {.proof}
The subgroups $A$ and $B$ are the unique subgroups of $H$ of their respective orders.
Every automorphism preserves subgroup order, so uniqueness forces
$$
\alpha(A)=A
$$
and
$$
\alpha(B)=B.
$$
:::

<1>21. On $A\cong C_3$, the restriction of $\alpha$ is either the identity or inversion.

::: {.proof}
The automorphism group
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(C_3)
$$
has order
$$
\varphi(3)=2.
$$
Its two elements are the identity and inversion.
:::

<1>22. On $B\cong C_{11}$, the restriction of $\alpha$ is either the identity or inversion.

::: {.proof}
The automorphism group
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(C_{11})
\cong
(\ZZ/11)^\times
$$
is cyclic of order $10$.
It has exactly one element of order $2$, namely inversion.
Since
$$
\alpha^2=1,
$$
the restriction has order $1$ or $2$, hence is either identity or inversion.
:::

<1>23. If $\alpha$ is trivial on both $A$ and $B$, then
$$
G\cong C_{66}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Then the semidirect product from step <1>17 is direct:
$$
G
\cong
C_{33}\times C_2.
$$
Because
$$
\gcd(33,2)=1,
$$
this direct product is cyclic of order $66$:
$$
G\cong C_{66}.
$$
:::

<1>24. If $\alpha$ is inversion on $A$ and trivial on $B$, then
$$
G
\cong
S_3\times C_{11}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The factor $B$ is fixed pointwise by $t$, so it is central and splits off.
On
$$
A\cong C_3,
$$
the element $t$ acts by inversion.
Thus
$$
A\rtimes\langle t\rangle
\cong
D_6
\cong
S_3.
$$
Therefore
$$
G
\cong
(C_3\rtimes C_2)\times C_{11}
\cong
S_3\times C_{11}.
$$
:::

<1>25. If $\alpha$ is trivial on $A$ and inversion on $B$, then
$$
G
\cong
C_3\times D_{22}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Now $A$ is fixed pointwise and splits off.
On
$$
B\cong C_{11},
$$
the involution acts by inversion.
Thus
$$
B\rtimes C_2
\cong
D_{22},
$$
the dihedral group of order $22$.
Hence
$$
G
\cong
C_3\times D_{22}.
$$
:::

<1>26. If $\alpha$ is inversion on both $A$ and $B$, then
$$
G
\cong
D_{66}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
H=A\times B
\cong
C_{33},
$$
inversion on both factors is inversion on all of $H$.
Therefore
$$
G
\cong
C_{33}\rtimes_{\mathrm{inv}}C_2,
$$
which is the dihedral group of order $66$:
$$
G\cong D_{66}.
$$
:::

<1>27. The four groups in steps <1>23--<1>26 are pairwise nonisomorphic.

::: {.proof}
Their centers have different orders.

For
$$
C_{66},
$$
the whole group is abelian, so
$$
\abs Z(C_{66})=66.
$$

For
$$
S_3\times C_{11},
$$
one has
$$
Z(S_3)=1,
$$
so
$$
Z(S_3\times C_{11})
=
C_{11}
$$
and the center has order $11$.

For
$$
C_3\times D_{22},
$$
the dihedral group $D_{22}$ has trivial center because its rotation subgroup has odd order $11$.
Thus the center has order $3$.

For
$$
D_{66},
$$
the rotation subgroup has odd order $33$, so the dihedral group has trivial center.
Thus the center has order $1$.

The center orders
$$
66,\ 11,\ 3,\ 1
$$
are pairwise distinct.
Therefore the four groups are pairwise nonisomorphic.
:::

<1>28. Hence every group of order $66$ is isomorphic to exactly one of
$$
\boxed{
C_{66},
\quad
S_3\times C_{11},
\quad
C_3\times D_{22},
\quad
D_{66}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>17--<1>22 show that every group of order $66$ is determined by one of the four possible involutive actions on
$$
H=C_3\times C_{11}.
$$
Steps <1>23--<1>26 identify the resulting groups.
Step <1>27 shows the four possibilities are mutually nonisomorphic.
This proves part (e).
:::

<1>29. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves part (a), step <1>6 proves part (b), step <1>9 proves part (c), step <1>14 proves part (d), and step <1>28 proves part (e).
