::: {.problem}
Let H be a normal subgroup of a finite group G, and let $N \subset H$ be a normal Sylow subgroup of H. Prove that N is a normal subgroup of G.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose
$$
N
$$
is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$ for some prime $p$.

<1>1. The subgroup $N$ is the unique Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.

::: {.proof}
All Sylow $p$-subgroups of $H$ are conjugate in $H$.
Since
$$
N\trianglelefteq H,
$$
every conjugate
$$
hNh^{-1}
$$
with
$$
h\in H
$$
equals $N$.
Therefore every Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$ equals $N$.
:::

<1>2. Let
$$
g\in G.
$$
Then
$$
gNg^{-1}
\subseteq
H.
$$

::: {.proof}
Because
$$
N\subseteq H
$$
and
$$
H\trianglelefteq G,
$$
one has
$$
gHg^{-1}=H.
$$
Hence
$$
gNg^{-1}
\subseteq
gHg^{-1}
=
H.
$$
:::

<1>3. The subgroup
$$
gNg^{-1}
$$
is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.

::: {.proof}
Conjugation preserves group order, so
$$
\abs{gNg^{-1}}
=
\abs N.
$$
Since $N$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$, its order is the largest power of $p$ dividing
$$
\abs H.
$$
Step <1>2 shows
$$
gNg^{-1}
\le
H.
$$
Thus
$$
gNg^{-1}
$$
is also a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
gNg^{-1}
=
N.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
gNg^{-1}
$$
is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.
By uniqueness from step <1>1, it must equal $N$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
N\trianglelefteq G.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 holds for every
$$
g\in G.
$$
Thus $N$ is invariant under conjugation by all elements of $G$, which is exactly normality in $G$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
