::: {.problem}
Let H be a normal subgroup of a finite group G, and let $N \subset H$ be a normal Sylow subgroup of H. Prove that N is a normal subgroup of G.
:::

::: {.solution}

Suppose
$$
N
$$
is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$ for some prime $p$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The subgroup $N$ is the unique Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.


::: pf-proof

All Sylow $p$-subgroups of $H$ are conjugate in $H$.
Since
$$
N\trianglelefteq H,
$$
every conjugate
$$
hNh^{-1}
$$
with
$$
h\in H
$$
equals $N$.
Therefore every Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$ equals $N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Let
$$
g\in G.
$$
Then
$$
gNg^{-1}
\subseteq
H.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
N\subseteq H
$$
and
$$
H\trianglelefteq G,
$$
one has
$$
gHg^{-1}=H.
$$
Hence
$$
gNg^{-1}
\subseteq
gHg^{-1}
=
H.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The subgroup
$$
gNg^{-1}
$$
is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.


::: pf-proof

Conjugation preserves group order, so
$$
\abs{gNg^{-1}}
=
\abs N.
$$
Since $N$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$, its order is the largest power of $p$ dividing
$$
\abs H.
$$
Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows
$$
gNg^{-1}
\le
H.
$$
Thus
$$
gNg^{-1}
$$
is also a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
gNg^{-1}
=
N.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
gNg^{-1}
$$
is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.
By uniqueness from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, it must equal $N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
N\trianglelefteq G.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} holds for every
$$
g\in G.
$$
Thus $N$ is invariant under conjugation by all elements of $G$, which is exactly normality in $G$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
