::: {.problem}
Write down a formula that gives a conformal equivalence between the vertical half-strip
$$
\mathcal{S}=\{z\in\CC:\ 0<\operatorname{Re}(z)<1,\ \operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
$$
and the upper half-plane
$$
\halfplane=\{z\in\CC:\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.
Write $F(z)\coloneqq-\cos(\pi z)$ and $W(\zeta)\coloneqq-\frac12\left(\zeta+\frac1\zeta\right)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #imaginary-part}
For $z=x+iy$ with $0<x<1$ and $y>0$, $\operatorname{Im}F(z)=\sin(\pi x)\sinh(\pi y)>0$.

::: pf-proof
$\cos(\pi z)=\cos(\pi x)\cosh(\pi y)-i\sin(\pi x)\sinh(\pi y)$, from $\cos(u+iv)=\cos u\cosh v-i\sin u\sinh v$; multiplying by $-1$ gives the displayed imaginary part. Both factors are positive, since $0<\pi x<\pi$ and $y>0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #factorisation}
$F(z)=W(\zeta)$ for $\zeta=e^{i\pi z}$, and $z\mapsto e^{i\pi z}$ maps $\mathcal S$ biholomorphically onto the upper half disc $\{\zeta:\abs{\zeta}<1,\ \operatorname{Im}\zeta>0\}$.

::: pf-proof
$\cos(\pi z)=\frac{\zeta+\zeta^{-1}}{2}$ by the definition of $\zeta$, so $F=W\circ\exp_\pi$. For $z=x+iy\in\mathcal S$ one has $\zeta=e^{-\pi y}e^{i\pi x}$ with $\abs{\zeta}=e^{-\pi y}<1$ and $\arg\zeta=\pi x\in(0,\pi)$, so $\zeta$ is in the upper half disc; conversely $re^{i\theta}$ with $0<r<1$, $0<\theta<\pi$ is the image of $\frac{\theta}{\pi}-i\frac{\log r}{\pi}\in\mathcal S$. The exponential has nonzero derivative $i\pi e^{i\pi z}$, so the map is biholomorphic onto its image.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #landing}
For $\zeta=re^{i\theta}$ with $0<r<1$ and $0<\theta<\pi$,
$$
W(\zeta)=-\frac12\left(r+r^{-1}\right)\cos\theta+\frac i2\left(r^{-1}-r\right)\sin\theta,
$$
so $\operatorname{Im}W(\zeta)>0$.

::: pf-proof
$\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)+r^{-1}(\cos\theta-i\sin\theta)=(r+r^{-1})\cos\theta+i(r-r^{-1})\sin\theta$; multiplying by $-\frac12$ gives the displayed form. Since $0<r<1$ one has $r^{-1}-r>0$, and $\sin\theta>0$ for $0<\theta<\pi$, so the imaginary part is positive.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #injective}
$W$ is injective on the upper half disc.

::: pf-proof
If $W(\zeta)=W(\zeta')$ then $\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=\zeta'+\zeta'^{-1}$, that is $\zeta-\zeta'=\zeta'^{-1}-\zeta^{-1}=\frac{\zeta-\zeta'}{\zeta\zeta'}$, hence $(\zeta-\zeta')\left(1-\frac{1}{\zeta\zeta'}\right)=0$. So either $\zeta=\zeta'$, or $\zeta\zeta'=1$, which is impossible inside the unit disc because $\abs{\zeta\zeta'}<1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #surjective}
For every $w\in\halfplane$ there is $\zeta$ in the upper half disc with $W(\zeta)=w$.

::: pf-proof
$W(\zeta)=w$ is equivalent to $\zeta^2+2w\zeta+1=0$. That quadratic has two roots $\zeta_1,\zeta_2$ with product $1$; they cannot both lie on the unit circle, because then $\zeta_2=\overline{\zeta_1}$ and $-2w=\zeta_1+\zeta_1^{-1}=2\operatorname{Re}\zeta_1$ would be real, contradicting $\operatorname{Im}w>0$. Since the product is $1$, exactly one root has modulus below $1$; call it $\zeta=re^{i\theta}$. Then $w=W(\zeta)$ and step [](#landing){.pf-ref} gives $\operatorname{Im}w=\frac12(r^{-1}-r)\sin\theta>0$ with $r^{-1}-r>0$, so $\sin\theta>0$ and $\theta\in(0,\pi)$, that is $\zeta$ lies in the upper half disc.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conformal}
$F$ is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of $\mathcal S$ with $F'(z)=\pi\sin(\pi z)\ne0$ there.

::: pf-proof
$F'=-\frac{d}{dz}\cos(\pi z)=\pi\sin(\pi z)$, which vanishes only where $\pi z$ is a multiple of $\pi$, that is at the integers; no integer has real part strictly between $0$ and $1$. The sine is entire, so $F$ is holomorphic on $\CC$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$F(z)=-\cos(\pi z)$ is a conformal equivalence of $\mathcal S$ onto $\halfplane$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#imaginary-part){.pf-ref} and [](#conformal){.pf-ref} show $F$ is holomorphic with nonvanishing derivative and takes $\mathcal S$ into $\halfplane$; step [](#factorisation){.pf-ref} writes $F=W\circ\exp_\pi$ with $\exp_\pi$ a biholomorphism onto the upper half disc and steps [](#landing){.pf-ref}, [](#injective){.pf-ref}, [](#surjective){.pf-ref} show $W$ is a bijection of that half disc onto $\halfplane$. Hence $F$ is a bijection onto $\halfplane$ and, having nonvanishing derivative, is conformal, with conformal inverse $W^{-1}\circ\exp_\pi^{-1}$. So $\boxed{F(z)=-\cos(\pi z)}$.
:::
