::: {.problem}
Find the Laurent series expansion of
$$
f(z)=\frac{1}{(z-1)(z-2)}
$$
valid in the annulus $1<\abs{z}<2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #partial-fractions}
$f(z) = -\frac{1}{z-1}+\frac{1}{z-2}$.

::: pf-proof
Combine the right side over the common denominator $(z-1)(z-2)$; the numerator is $-(z-2)+(z-1)=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #first}
For $\abs{z}>1$, $-\frac{1}{z-1} = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n}$.

::: pf-proof
$-\frac{1}{z-1} = -\frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1-z^{-1}}$, and $\abs{z^{-1}}<1$, so the geometric series applies.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #second}
For $\abs{z}<2$, $\frac{1}{z-2} = -\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+1}}$.

::: pf-proof
$\frac{1}{z-2} = -\frac12\cdot\frac{1}{1-z/2}$, and $\abs{z/2}<1$, so the geometric series applies.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
On $1<\abs{z}<2$,
$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{(z-1)(z-2)} = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n} - \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+1}}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Add the expansions of steps [](#first){.pf-ref} and [](#second){.pf-ref}, which both hold on the annulus, and use step [](#partial-fractions){.pf-ref}.
:::
