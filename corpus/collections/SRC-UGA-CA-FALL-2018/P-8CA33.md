::: {.problem}
Let $p(z)$ be a nonconstant polynomial of even degree with all roots inside the disc $\abs{z}<R$.
Show that there is a holomorphic function $f(z)$ on the annulus $\abs{z}>R$ with $f(z)^2=p(z)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this problem: the collection's `provenance` names `assets/attachments/Complex_Analysis_Fall_2018.docx` and `UGA_Complex_Analysis_Fall_2018.pdf`, which are the examination and its statement list, not a solution sheet. The argument below is the card's own, and its second paragraph — the "equivalently" remark about winding numbers — is replaced by the factorization argument it was gesturing at, since a winding number of $p$ about $0$ is not defined for a polynomial without first producing the very square root being sought.

Throughout $p$ is a nonconstant polynomial of degree $2m$ with leading coefficient $a\ne0$, all of whose roots $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_N$ (with multiplicities $\nu_1,\dots,\nu_N$) satisfy $\abs{\alpha_j}<R$, and the annulus is $A=\theset{z\in\CC\mid \abs z>R}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factor}
$p(z)=az^{2m}\prod_{j=1}^N\Bigl(1-\dfrac{\alpha_j}{z}\Bigr)^{\nu_j}$ on $\CC-\theset0$, with $\sum_j\nu_j=2m$.

::: pf-proof
The roots together with their multiplicities account for the degree, $\sum_j\nu_j=\deg p=2m$, so $p(z)=a\prod_j(z-\alpha_j)^{\nu_j}$. Factoring $z$ out of each linear factor gives $p(z)=az^{\sum\nu_j}\prod_j\bigl(1-\alpha_j/z\bigr)^{\nu_j}$, and $\sum_j\nu_j=2m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #log}
For $\abs z>R$ each factor $1-\alpha_j/z$ is holomorphic and nonzero, and has a holomorphic logarithm on $A$.

::: pf-proof
$\abs z>R>\abs{\alpha_j}$ makes $z\ne0$ and $\alpha_j/z$ well defined with $\abs{\alpha_j/z}<1$, so $1-\alpha_j/z$ lies in the open disk of centre $1$ and radius $1$, which does not contain $0$; in particular the factor is nonzero on $A$ and holomorphic there. On the simply connected domain $A$ — which is conformally a disk via $z\mapsto1/z$ — a nonvanishing holomorphic function has a holomorphic logarithm; concretely $\Log(1-w)=-\sum_{k\ge1}w^k/k$ converges for $\abs w<1$, so $\Log(1-\alpha_j/z)$ is the composite of that power series with the holomorphic $\alpha_j/z$ on $A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sqrt}
Choose $\sqrt a$ and set
$$
f(z)=\sqrt a\,z^{m}\exp\Bigl(\frac12\sum_{j=1}^N\nu_j\Log\Bigl(1-\frac{\alpha_j}{z}\Bigr)\Bigr).
$$
Then $f$ is holomorphic on $A$ and $f(z)^2=p(z)$ there.

::: pf-proof
$z^m$ is holomorphic on $A$, the logarithms are holomorphic by step [](#log){.pf-ref}, and $\exp$ is entire, so $f$ is holomorphic. Squaring,
$$
f(z)^2=a\,z^{2m}\exp\Bigl(\sum_j\nu_j\Log\bigl(1-\alpha_j/z\bigr)\Bigr)=a\,z^{2m}\prod_j\Bigl(1-\frac{\alpha_j}{z}\Bigr)^{\nu_j}=p(z),
$$
using $\exp(\nu\Log w)=w^{\nu}$ for the integer $\nu$ and step [](#factor){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
There is a holomorphic $f$ on $\abs z>R$ with $f^2=p$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#sqrt){.pf-ref} provides it. The hypothesis that the degree be even is what makes the exponent $m$ of $z$ an integer, and it is not removable: for $p(z)=z$ and any $R>0$ no holomorphic square root exists on $\abs z>R$, since the identity map on a punctured neighbourhood of $0$ has no holomorphic square root.

:::
