::: {.problem}
For each real number $a$, compute
$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos(ax)}{x^4+5x^2+4}\,dx.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factor}
$x^4+5x^2+4=(x^2+1)(x^2+4)$ and
$$
\frac1{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}=\frac13\left(\frac1{x^2+1}-\frac1{x^2+4}\right).
$$

::: pf-proof
$(x^2+1)(x^2+4)=x^4+5x^2+4$ by expansion, and
$\frac13\left(\frac1{x^2+1}-\frac1{x^2+4}\right)=\frac13\cdot\frac{(x^2+4)-(x^2+1)}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}=\frac13\cdot\frac3{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #standard}
For $b>0$ and real $a$,
$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos(ax)}{x^2+b^2}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2b}e^{-b\abs{a}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
First let $a\ge0$. Put $h(z)=e^{iaz}/(z^2+b^2)$, holomorphic off the simple poles $\pm ib$, and let $\Gamma_R$ be the closed contour consisting of $[-R,R]$ and the upper semicircle of radius $R>4b$. The only singularity inside is $z=ib$, whose residue is the value at $z=ib$ of $\dfrac{e^{iaz}}{z+ib}$, namely $\dfrac{e^{-ab}}{2ib}$, so by the residue theorem
$$
\int_{\Gamma_R}h=2\pi i\cdot\frac{e^{-ab}}{2ib}=\frac{\pi}{b}e^{-ab}.
$$
On the arc, $\abs{e^{iaz}}=e^{-a\operatorname{Im}z}\le1$ because $a\ge0$, $\operatorname{Im}z\ge0$, and $\abs{z^2+b^2}\ge\abs{z}^2-b^2=R^2-b^2$; the arc integral is therefore at most $\pi R/(R^2-b^2)\to0$ as $R\to\infty$. Hence
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{iax}}{x^2+b^2}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{b}e^{-ab},
$$
and taking real parts, $\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\cos(ax)}{x^2+b^2}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{b}e^{-ab}$. The integrand $\cos(ax)/(x^2+b^2)$ is even in $x$, so $\int_0^\infty=\frac{\pi}{2b}e^{-ab}$.
For $a<0$ the identity $\cos(ax)=\cos(\abs{a}x)$ is the evenness of $\cos$, so the same value holds with $\abs{a}$ in place of $a$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #combine}
For every real $a$,
$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos(ax)}{x^4+5x^2+4}\,dx=\frac13\left(\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-\abs{a}}-\frac{\pi}{4}e^{-2\abs{a}}\right)=\frac{\pi}{12}\left(2e^{-\abs{a}}-e^{-2\abs{a}}\right).
$$

::: pf-proof
The integrand is bounded by $1/(x^4+5x^2+4)$, which is integrable on $(0,\infty)$, so both sides are integrable and the integral of the difference decomposition of step [](#factor){.pf-ref} is the difference of the integrals. Applying step [](#standard){.pf-ref} with $b=1$ and with $b=2$, and multiplying by $\frac13$:
$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos(ax)}{x^4+5x^2+4}\,dx=\frac13\left(\frac{\pi}{2\cdot1}e^{-\abs{a}}-\frac{\pi}{2\cdot2}e^{-2\abs{a}}\right),
$$
which is the displayed answer. As a check, at $a=0$ it gives $\frac{\pi}{12}(2-1)=\frac{\pi}{12}$, while $\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}=\frac13\bigl[\arctan x-\frac12\arctan\frac x2\bigr]_0^\infty=\frac13\bigl(\frac\pi2-\frac\pi4\bigr)=\frac{\pi}{12}$.
:::
