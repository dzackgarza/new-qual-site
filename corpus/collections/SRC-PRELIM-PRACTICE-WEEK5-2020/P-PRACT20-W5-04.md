::: {.problem}
Find all homomorphisms $\phi : U_4 \to U_4$ where $U_4 = \{1, i, -1, -i\}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The group $U_4$ is cyclic of order $4$, generated by $i$.

::: {.proof}
One has
\[
i^0=1,\qquad i^1=i,\qquad i^2=-1,\qquad i^3=-i,
\]
so every element of $U_4$ is a power of $i$.
:::

<1>2. Every homomorphism $\phi:U_4\to U_4$ is uniquely determined by the
single value $\phi(i)$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, every $z\in U_4$ has the form $z=i^r$. Hence
\[
\phi(z)
=\phi(i^r)
=\phi(i)^r.
\]
Thus two homomorphisms agreeing on $i$ agree everywhere.
:::

<1>3. Each of the four choices
\[
\phi(i)\in\{1,i,-1,-i\}
\]
defines a homomorphism $U_4\to U_4$.

::: {.proof}
The only relation on the generator is
\[
i^4=1.
\]
Every element $y\in U_4$ satisfies $y^4=1$. Therefore assigning $i\mapsto y$
respects the defining relation and extends uniquely to a homomorphism.
:::

<1>4. The four homomorphisms are exactly
\[
\boxed{
\phi_m(z)=z^m,
\qquad
m=0,1,2,3 }.
\]

::: {.proof}
For $m=0,1,2,3$,
\[
\phi_m(i)=i^m
\]
runs through
\[
1,\ i,\ -1,\ -i.
\]
By steps <1>2 and <1>3 these four choices produce all homomorphisms, with no
duplicates.
:::

<1>5. In particular, the homomorphism with $\phi(i)=-i$ is
\[
\phi(z)=z^3,
\]
not $z\mapsto-z$.

::: {.proof}
Since
\[
i^3=-i,
\]
step <1>4 identifies this homomorphism as $z\mapsto z^3$. The map
$z\mapsto-z$ cannot be a group homomorphism because it sends the identity
$1$ to $-1$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 gives the complete list, and step <1>5 corrects the erroneous map
in the supplied source solution.
:::
