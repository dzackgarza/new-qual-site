::: {.problem}
Find all homomorphisms $\phi : U_4 \to U_4$ where $U_4 = \{1, i, -1, -i\}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The group $U_4$ is cyclic of order $4$, generated by $i$.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
i^0=1,\qquad i^1=i,\qquad i^2=-1,\qquad i^3=-i,
$$
so every element of $U_4$ is a power of $i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every homomorphism $\phi:U_4\to U_4$ is uniquely determined by the
single value $\phi(i)$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, every $z\in U_4$ has the form $z=i^r$. Hence
$$
\phi(z)
=\phi(i^r)
=\phi(i)^r.
$$
Thus two homomorphisms agreeing on $i$ agree everywhere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Each of the four choices
$$
\phi(i)\in\{1,i,-1,-i\}
$$
defines a homomorphism $U_4\to U_4$.


::: pf-proof

The only relation on the generator is
$$
i^4=1.
$$
Every element $y\in U_4$ satisfies $y^4=1$. Therefore assigning $i\mapsto y$
respects the defining relation and extends uniquely to a homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The four homomorphisms are exactly
$$
\boxed{
\phi_m(z)=z^m,
\qquad
m=0,1,2,3 }.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $m=0,1,2,3$,
$$
\phi_m(i)=i^m
$$
runs through
$$
1,\ i,\ -1,\ -i.
$$
By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} these four choices produce all homomorphisms, with no
duplicates.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

In particular, the homomorphism with $\phi(i)=-i$ is
$$
\phi(z)=z^3,
$$
not $z\mapsto-z$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
i^3=-i,
$$
step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} identifies this homomorphism as $z\mapsto z^3$. The map
$z\mapsto-z$ cannot be a group homomorphism because it sends the identity
$1$ to $-1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the complete list, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} corrects the erroneous map
in the supplied source solution.

:::
