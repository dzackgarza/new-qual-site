::: {.problem}
Prove that

(a) $\displaystyle \lim_{z \to (2k+1)\pi/2} \tan z = \infty$,

(b) $\displaystyle \lim_{z \to (2k+1)\pi/2} [z - (2k+1)\pi/2] \tan z = -1$.

[Hint: The proof of (b) is very short if done the "right" way.]
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix $k\in\ZZ$ and set
$$
a\coloneqq\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2}.
$$
Then
$$
\cos a=0,
\qquad
\sin a=(-1)^k\ne0.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The zero of $\cos z$ at $z=a$ is simple and
$$
\lim_{z\to a}\frac{\cos z}{z-a}
=
-\sin a.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $\cos a=0$, the definition of the derivative gives
$$
\lim_{z\to a}
\frac{\cos z-\cos a}{z-a}
=
\cos'(a)
=
-\sin a.
$$
Because $\sin a\ne0$, the derivative does not vanish, so the zero is simple.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{z\to a}(z-a)\tan z=-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $z\ne a$ near $a$,
$$
(z-a)\tan z
=
\frac{\sin z}{\cos z/(z-a)}.
$$
By continuity of $\sin$ and step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\lim_{z\to a}(z-a)\tan z
=
\frac{\sin a}{-\sin a}
=-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

In the extended complex plane,
$$
\boxed{\lim_{z\to a}\tan z=\infty}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
(z-a)\tan z\longrightarrow-1.
$$
Hence there is a punctured neighborhood of $a$ in which
$$
\abs{(z-a)\tan z}\ge\frac12.
$$
Therefore
$$
\abs{\tan z}
\ge
\frac1{2\abs{z-a}}
\longrightarrow\infty
$$
as $z\to a$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
