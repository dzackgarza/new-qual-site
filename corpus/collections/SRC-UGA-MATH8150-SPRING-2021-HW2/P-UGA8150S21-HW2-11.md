::: {.problem}
Show that if $\abs{\alpha} < r < \abs{\beta}$, then
$$
\int_{\gamma} \frac{dz}{(z-\alpha)(z-\beta)} = \frac{2\pi i}{\alpha-\beta}
$$
where $\gamma$ denotes the circle centered at the origin, of radius $r$, with positive orientation.
:::

::: {.remark title="Variations"}
The same identity is also asked for without Cauchy's integral formula.
Step 2 is the only step that uses it, and a series computation replaces the whole proof.
On $\abs{z} = r$, the geometric series give
$$
\frac{1}{z-\alpha} = \sum_{m=0}^\infty \alpha^m z^{-m-1},
\qquad
\frac{1}{z-\beta} = -\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{z^n}{\beta^{n+1}},
$$
with ratios $\abs{\alpha}/r < 1$ and $r/\abs{\beta} < 1$, so both converge absolutely and uniformly on $\gamma$.
The product may be integrated term by term.
Direct parametrization gives $\int_\gamma z^k\,dz = 0$ for $k \neq -1$ and $\int_\gamma z^{-1}\,dz = 2\pi i$.
The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the product is $-\sum_{m\geq 0} \alpha^m/\beta^{m+1} = \frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}$, which gives the identity.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The integrand has the partial-fraction decomposition
$$
\frac1{(z-\alpha)(z-\beta)}
=
\frac1{\alpha-\beta}
\left(
\frac1{z-\alpha}
-\frac1{z-\beta}
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

Combining the two fractions on the right gives
$$
\frac1{\alpha-\beta}
\frac{(z-\beta)-(z-\alpha)}
{(z-\alpha)(z-\beta)}
=
\frac1{(z-\alpha)(z-\beta)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
\int_\gamma\frac{dz}{z-\alpha}=2\pi i,
\qquad
\int_\gamma\frac{dz}{z-\beta}=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $\abs\alpha<r$, the positively oriented circle $\gamma$ winds once around $\alpha$, so the Cauchy integral formula gives the first equality.

Because $r<\abs\beta$, the function
$$
z\longmapsto\frac1{z-\beta}
$$
is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disc bounded by $\gamma$. Cauchy's theorem therefore gives the second equality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\int_\gamma
\frac{dz}{(z-\alpha)(z-\beta)}
=
\frac{2\pi i}{\alpha-\beta}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Integrating the identity of step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and substituting the two integrals from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_\gamma
\frac{dz}{(z-\alpha)(z-\beta)}
&=
\frac1{\alpha-\beta}
\left(2\pi i-0\right)\\
&=
\frac{2\pi i}{\alpha-\beta}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the required identity.

:::
