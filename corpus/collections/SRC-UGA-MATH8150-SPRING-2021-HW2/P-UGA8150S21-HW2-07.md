::: {.problem}
Show that the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n}}{n} z^{n(n+1)}
$$
has radius of convergence $1$. Examine convergence at $z = 1, -1$ and $i$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Set
$$
S(z)\coloneqq
\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\frac{(-1)^n}{n}z^{n(n+1)}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The series converges absolutely for every $\abs z<1$.


::: pf-proof

If $r=\abs z<1$, then $n(n+1)\ge n$ and therefore
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\abs{\frac{(-1)^n}{n}z^{n(n+1)}}
=
\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\frac{r^{n(n+1)}}{n}
\le
\sum_{n=1}^\infty r^n
<\infty.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The series diverges for every $\abs z>1$.


::: pf-proof

If $\abs z=r>1$, then
$$
\abs{\frac{(-1)^n}{n}z^{n(n+1)}}
=
\frac{r^{n(n+1)}}{n},
$$
which does not tend to zero. Hence the series cannot converge.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The radius of convergence is $\boxed{1}$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives convergence throughout the open unit disc, while step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives divergence at every point outside the closed unit disc. Thus the radius is exactly $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

At $z=1$, the series converges conditionally.


::: pf-proof

At $z=1$,
$$
S(1)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{n},
$$
which converges by the alternating-series test. It is not absolutely convergent because
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1n
$$
diverges.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

At $z=-1$, the series also converges conditionally.


::: pf-proof

The integer $n(n+1)$ is always even, so
$$
(-1)^{n(n+1)}=1.
$$
Hence
$$
S(-1)
=
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{n},
$$
and the conclusion follows exactly as in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

At $z=i$, the series converges conditionally.


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
b_n\coloneqq(-1)^n i^{n(n+1)}.
$$
According to $n$ modulo $4$, the sequence $(b_n)$ is periodic with pattern
$$
1,-1,-1,1.
$$
Indeed, $i^{n(n+1)}$ has pattern
$$
-1,-1,1,1,
$$
while $(-1)^n$ has pattern
$$
-1,1,-1,1.
$$
The sum of one period of $(b_n)$ is zero, so its partial sums are bounded. Since $1/n$ decreases to zero, Dirichlet's test gives convergence of
$$
S(i)
=
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{b_n}{n}.
$$
Again
$$
\abs{b_n/n}=1/n,
$$
so the convergence is not absolute.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the radius of convergence, and steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} give the behavior at the three requested boundary points.

:::
