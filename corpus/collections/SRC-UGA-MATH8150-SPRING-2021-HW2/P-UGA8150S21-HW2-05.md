::: {.problem}
Prove the following:

(a) The power series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n z^n$ does not converge at any point of the unit circle.

(b) The power series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n^2}$ converges at every point of the unit circle.

(c) The power series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n}$ converges at every point of the unit circle except at $z = 1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume throughout that $\abs z=1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty nz^n
$$
diverges for every $z$ on the unit circle.


::: pf-proof

A necessary condition for convergence of a series is that its terms tend to zero. Here
$$
\abs{nz^n}
=
n\abs z^n
=n,
$$
so $nz^n$ does not tend to zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{z^n}{n^2}
$$
converges absolutely at every $z$ on the unit circle.


::: pf-proof

Since $\abs z=1$,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\abs{\frac{z^n}{n^2}}
=
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n^2},
$$
and the latter converges.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $\abs z=1$ and $z\ne1$, then the partial sums of $\sum z^n$ are uniformly bounded:
$$
\abs{\sum_{n=1}^N z^n}
\le
\frac{2}{\abs{1-z}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The finite geometric-series identity gives
$$
\sum_{n=1}^N z^n
=
\frac{z(1-z^N)}{1-z}.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{\sum_{n=1}^N z^n}
\le
\frac{\abs z(1+\abs{z^N})}{\abs{1-z}}
=
\frac2{\abs{1-z}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $\abs z=1$ and $z\ne1$, then
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{z^n}{n}
$$
converges.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the partial sums of $z^n$ are bounded. The real sequence $1/n$ is decreasing and tends to zero. Dirichlet's test therefore gives convergence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

At $z=1$, the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{z^n}{n}
$$
diverges.


::: pf-proof

At $z=1$ it becomes the harmonic series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1n,
$$
which diverges.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves part (b), and steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} prove part (c).

:::
