::: {.problem}
(a) Express $\sin z$ in the form $u + iv$. Do the same for $\cos z$.

(b) Show that there exists a sequence $z_{n}$ such that $\sin z_{n} \to \infty$. Do the same for $\cos z$.

(This shows that unlike their real counterparts, $\sin z$ and $\cos z$ are unbounded.)
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
z=x+iy,
\qquad
x,y\in\RR.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The real and imaginary parts of $\sin z$ are
$$
\boxed{
\sin(x+iy)
=
\sin x\cosh y
+i\cos x\sinh y}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using
$$
\sin z=\frac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{2i},
$$
one has
$$
e^{i(x+iy)}=e^{-y}(\cos x+i\sin x)
$$
and
$$
e^{-i(x+iy)}=e^y(\cos x-i\sin x).
$$
Substitution and the identities
$$
\cosh y=\frac{e^y+e^{-y}}2,
\qquad
\sinh y=\frac{e^y-e^{-y}}2
$$
give the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The real and imaginary parts of $\cos z$ are
$$
\boxed{
\cos(x+iy)
=
\cos x\cosh y
-i\sin x\sinh y}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using
$$
\cos z=\frac{e^{iz}+e^{-iz}}2
$$
and the exponential expressions from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives the formula directly.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The complex sine is unbounded.


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
z_n=\frac\pi2+in.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\sin z_n
=
\cosh n.
$$
Since $\cosh n\to+\infty$, the sequence $\sin z_n$ tends to $+\infty$ along the real axis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The complex cosine is unbounded.


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
w_n=in.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\cos w_n
=
\cosh n
\longrightarrow+\infty.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} prove part (a), and steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove part (b).

:::
