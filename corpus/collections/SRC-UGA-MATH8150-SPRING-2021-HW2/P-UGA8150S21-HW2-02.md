::: {.problem}
Show that $U ( x ^ { 2 } - y ^ { 2 } , 2 x y )$ is harmonic if and only if $U ( x , y )$ is.
:::

Define
$$
V(x,y)\coloneqq U(x^2-y^2,2xy).
$$
This is the composition of $U$ with the holomorphic map
$$
f(z)=z^2.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The Laplacians satisfy
$$
\boxed{
\Delta V(x,y)
=
4(x^2+y^2)
(\Delta U)(x^2-y^2,2xy)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $f(z)=z^2$,
$$
f'(z)=2z,
\qquad
\abs{f'(z)}^2=4\abs z^2=4(x^2+y^2).
$$
Applying the holomorphic change-of-variables formula from the preceding problem gives the stated identity.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $U$ is harmonic, then $V$ is harmonic.


::: pf-proof

If $\Delta U=0$, step <1>1 immediately gives $\Delta V=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $V$ is harmonic, then $\Delta U(w)=0$ for every $w\ne0$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $w\in\CC\setminus\{0\}$. Choose $z\in\CC\setminus\{0\}$ with
$$
z^2=w.
$$
Since $V$ is harmonic,
$$
0
=
\Delta V(z)
=
4\abs z^2(\Delta U)(w)
$$
by step <1>1. Because $z\ne0$, the coefficient $4\abs z^2$ is nonzero, hence
$$
(\Delta U)(w)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $V$ is harmonic, then $U$ is harmonic on all of $\CC$.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>3 gives $\Delta U=0$ on $\CC\setminus\{0\}$. Since $U$ has continuous second partial derivatives, $\Delta U$ is continuous. Therefore
$$
(\Delta U)(0)
=
\lim_{w\to0,\,w\ne0}(\Delta U)(w)
=0.
$$
Thus $\Delta U=0$ everywhere.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
U(x^2-y^2,2xy)\text{ is harmonic}
\iff
U(x,y)\text{ is harmonic}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>2 proves the forward implication from harmonicity of $U$ to harmonicity of the composition, and step <1>4 proves the converse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>5 is the required equivalence.

:::

:::
