::: problem
Let $X=\{(x,\sin(1/x))\mid x>0\}$ and let $Y=\{(0,y)\mid -1\leq y\leq 1\}$.
Show that $X\cup Y$ is not a path-connected subset of $\RR^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose for contradiction there is a path $\gamma: [0,1] \to X \cup Y$ with $\gamma(0) \in Y$ and $\gamma(1) \in X$.
::: {.proof}
to show $X \cup Y$ is not path-connected, it suffices to show no point of $Y$ can be joined to a point of $X$ by a path.
:::

<1>2. Write $\gamma(t) = (x(t), y(t))$; then $x(t)$ is continuous, $x(0) = 0$, and $x(1) > 0$.
::: {.proof}
the first coordinate is continuous.
:::

<1>3. Let $t_0 = \sup\{t : x(t) = 0\}$.
::: {.proof}
the set $\{t : x(t) = 0\}$ is nonempty (contains $0$) and closed, so its supremum $t_0$ satisfies $x(t_0) = 0$.
:::

<1>4. For $t > t_0$ sufficiently close to $t_0$, $x(t) > 0$, so $\gamma(t) \in X$ and $y(t) = \sin(1/x(t))$.
::: {.proof}
by definition of $t_0$ and continuity of $x$.
:::

<1>5. As $t \to t_0^+$, $x(t) \to 0$, so $1/x(t) \to \infty$ and $\sin(1/x(t))$ oscillates without a limit.
::: {.proof}
the sine function has no limit as its argument tends to infinity.
:::

<1>6. Hence $y(t)$ has no limit as $t \to t_0^+$, contradicting the continuity of $y$ at $t_0$.
::: {.proof}
a continuous function must have a limit at every point of its domain.
:::

<1>7. Therefore no such path exists, so $X \cup Y$ is not path-connected.
::: {.proof}
<1>1–<1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>7.
:::
:::
