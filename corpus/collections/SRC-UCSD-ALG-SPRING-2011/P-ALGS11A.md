::: problem
Let $G$ be a group with $|G| = 117 = 9 \cdot 13$ elements which contains an element of order exactly $9$.
Classify all such groups $G$ up to isomorphism.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Classify groups of order $117 = 3^2 \cdot 13$ containing an element of order 9 by analyzing Sylow subgroups and semidirect products.

<1>1. Sylow Subgroup Analysis:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The prime factorization of the group order is:
        $$|G| = 117 = 3^2 \cdot 13 = 9 \cdot 13.$$
    <2>2. Let $n_p$ denote the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$:
        - For $p = 13$: $n_{13} \equiv 1 \pmod{13}$ and $n_{13} \mid 9$.
          The divisors of 9 are $\{1, 3, 9\}$, and only $n_{13} = 1$ satisfies $1 \equiv 1 \pmod{13}$.
          Thus $n_{13} = 1$, which means the Sylow 13-subgroup $P_{13} \cong \mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}$ is **unique and normal**:
          $$P_{13} = \langle y \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_{13}, \qquad P_{13} \trianglelefteq G.$$
        - For $p = 3$: $n_3 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $n_3 \mid 13$.
          The divisors of 13 are $\{1, 13\}$, and $13 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, so $n_3 \in \{1, 13\}$.
    <2>3. Let $P_3$ be a Sylow 3-subgroup of $G$ of order $|P_3| = 9$.
    <2>4. We are given that $G$ contains an element of order 9, say $x \in G$.
    <2>5. The subgroup generated by $x$ has order $|\langle x \rangle| = 9 = |P_3|$, so the Sylow 3-subgroup must be cyclic:
        $$P_3 = \langle x \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}.$$

<1>2. Semidirect Product Structure $G \cong P_{13} \rtimes P_3$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $P_{13} \trianglelefteq G$ and $\gcd(|P_{13}|, |P_3|) = \gcd(13, 9) = 1$, we have $P_{13} \cap P_3 = \{e\}$ and $|P_{13} P_3| = 117 = |G|$.
    <2>2. Thus $G$ is a **semidirect product**:
        $$G \cong P_{13} \rtimes_\theta P_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_{13} \rtimes_\theta \mathbb{Z}_9$$
        determined by a group homomorphism:
        $$\theta: \mathbb{Z}_9 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{13}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong \mathbb{Z}_{12}.$$

<1>3. Homomorphisms $\theta: \mathbb{Z}_9 \to \mathbb{Z}_{12}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The generator $x \in \mathbb{Z}_9$ must map to an automorphism $\theta(x) \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{13})$ whose order divides both $|\mathbb{Z}_9| = 9$ and $|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{13})| = 12$.
    <2>2. We compute:
        $$\gcd(9, 12) = 3.$$
    <2>3. Thus the order of $\theta(x)$ must be either **$1$ or $3$**.
    <2>4. **Case 1: Order 1 (Trivial Homomorphism $\theta_0$):**
        - $\theta(x) = \operatorname{id}$.
        - In this case, the semidirect product is the direct product:
          $$G_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}_{13} \times \mathbb{Z}_9 \cong \mathbb{Z}_{117} \text{ (cyclic)}.$$
    <2>5. **Case 2: Order 3 (Non-Trivial Homomorphisms):**
        - The group $(\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has order 12, so it has a unique cyclic subgroup of order 3:
          $$\{u \in (\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^\times \mid u^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{13}\} = \{1, 3, 9\}.$$
        - The two elements of order 3 modulo 13 are $3$ (since $3^3 = 27 \equiv 1$) and $9 \equiv 3^2$ (since $9^3 = 729 = 13 \cdot 56 + 1 \equiv 1$).
        - Thus $\theta(x)$ can send $y \mapsto y^3$ or $y \mapsto y^9$.
        - These two choices correspond to changing the generator $x \leftrightarrow x^2$ of the Sylow 3-subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_9$, so they define **isomorphic non-abelian groups**:
          $$G_2 \cong \langle x, y \mid x^9 = 1, y^{13} = 1, x y x^{-1} = y^3 \rangle.$$

<1>4. Verification that Both Groups Contain an Element of Order 9:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For $G_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}_{117}$, the generator $(1, 1)$ has order 117, and $(0, 1)$ has order 9.
    <2>2. For $G_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}_{13} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_9$, the element $x \in G_2$ has order 9 in $G_2$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    Up to isomorphism, there are exactly **two** such groups:
    1. $\mathbb{Z}_{117} \cong \mathbb{Z}_{13} \times \mathbb{Z}_9$ (abelian/cyclic),
    2. $\mathbb{Z}_{13} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_9 = \langle x, y \mid x^9 = 1, y^{13} = 1, x y x^{-1} = y^3 \rangle$ (non-abelian).
    Q.E.D.
