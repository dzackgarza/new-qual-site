::: problem
Let $G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ be the order $4$ matrix group generated by
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.
\]

(a) What is the Hilbert series of the invariant ring $\mathbb{R}[x,y]^G$?

(b) Do there exist algebraically independent homogeneous polynomials $f_1,f_2\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]^G$ such that $\mathbb{R}[x,y]^G=\mathbb{R}[f_1,f_2]$?
Why or why not?
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

<1>1. $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is a rotation by $90^\circ$, so $G = \langle A \rangle \cong \ZZ/4$ acts on $\mathbb{R}[x,y]$ by $A \cdot x = y$, $A \cdot y = -x$.
::: {.proof}
the action of $A$ on the variables.
:::

<1>2. The invariant ring $\mathbb{R}[x,y]^G$ is generated by $x^2 + y^2$ and $x^4 + y^4$ (or equivalently $x^2 + y^2$ and $x^2 y^2$).
::: {.proof}
the invariants of the cyclic group of order $4$ acting by rotation.
:::

<1>3. The Hilbert series of $\mathbb{R}[x,y]^G$ is $\frac{1 + t^4}{(1 - t^2)(1 - t^4)}$.
::: {.proof}
Molien's theorem (or direct computation: the invariant ring is generated by $x^2 + y^2$ (degree $2$) and $x^2 y^2$ (degree $4$), with a relation).
:::

<1>4. Hence the Hilbert series is $\frac{1 + t^4}{(1 - t^2)(1 - t^4)}$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3.
:::

**(b).**

<1>1. No, such $f_1, f_2$ do not exist.
::: {.proof}
answer.
:::

<1>2. If $\mathbb{R}[x,y]^G = \mathbb{R}[f_1, f_2]$ with $f_1, f_2$ algebraically independent, then $\mathbb{R}[x,y]^G$ would be a polynomial ring in two variables, whose Hilbert series is $\frac{1}{(1 - t^{d_1})(1 - t^{d_2})}$.
::: {.proof}
the Hilbert series of a polynomial ring.
:::

<1>3. But the Hilbert series $\frac{1 + t^4}{(1 - t^2)(1 - t^4)}$ is not of the form $\frac{1}{(1 - t^{d_1})(1 - t^{d_2})}$ (the numerator $1 + t^4 \neq 1$).
::: {.proof}
<1>3 (a) and <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Hence $\mathbb{R}[x,y]^G$ is not a polynomial ring, so no such $f_1, f_2$ exist.
::: {.proof}
<1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>4 (a) and <1>4 (b).
:::
:::
