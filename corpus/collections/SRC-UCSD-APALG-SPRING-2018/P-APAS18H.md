::: {.problem}
Let $G\subseteq\GL_2(\RR)$ be the order $4$ matrix group generated by
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

(a) What is the Hilbert series of the invariant ring $\RR[x,y]^G$?

(b) Do there exist algebraically independent homogeneous polynomials $f_1,f_2\in\RR[x,y]^G$ such that $\RR[x,y]^G=\RR[f_1,f_2]$?
Why or why not?
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $p\coloneqq x^2+y^2$, $q\coloneqq x^2y^2$ and $r\coloneqq xy(x^2-y^2)$.
The matrix $A$ acts on the variables by $x\mapsto y$, $y\mapsto -x$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #molien}
(a) The Hilbert series is $\boxed{\dfrac{1+t^4}{(1-t^2)(1-t^4)}}$.

::: pf-proof
The group is $\{I,A,A^2,A^3\}$ with $A^2=-I$. The matrices $A$ and $A^3$ have eigenvalues $\pm i$, so $\det(I-tA)=\det(I-tA^3)=1+t^2$; also $\det(I-tI)=(1-t)^2$ and $\det(I+tI)=(1+t)^2$. By Molien's theorem,
$$
H(t)=\frac14\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^2}+\frac{2}{1+t^2}+\frac{1}{(1+t)^2}\right)=\frac{1+t^4}{(1-t)^2(1+t)^2(1+t^2)}=\frac{1+t^4}{(1-t^2)(1-t^4)}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #gens}
$\RR[x,y]^G=\RR[p,q,r]$, and $r^2=q\,(p^2-4q)$.

::: pf-proof
Each of $p,q,r$ is invariant: $A$ sends $x^2+y^2$ to $y^2+x^2$, $x^2y^2$ to $y^2x^2$, and $xy(x^2-y^2)$ to $y(-x)(y^2-x^2)=xy(x^2-y^2)$. The identity holds because $(x^2-y^2)^2=p^2-4q$. The polynomials $p,q$ are algebraically independent, so $\RR[p,q]\oplus r\,\RR[p,q]$ has Hilbert series $(1+t^4)/((1-t^2)(1-t^4))$, the series of step [](#molien){.pf-ref}; this sum is direct because $r\notin\RR(p,q)$ ($r$ changes sign under $x\leftrightarrow y$, which fixes $p$ and $q$). It lies in the invariant ring, and the two series agree degree by degree, so it is the whole invariant ring.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
(b) $\boxed{\text{No}}$: $\RR[x,y]^G$ is not a polynomial ring in two homogeneous generators.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $\RR[x,y]^G=\RR[f_1,f_2]$ with $f_1,f_2$ algebraically independent and homogeneous of degrees $d_1\le d_2$. Then $H(t)=1/((1-t^{d_1})(1-t^{d_2}))$. Step [](#molien){.pf-ref} gives $H(t)=1+t^2+3t^4+\cdots$. The coefficient of $t^2$ forces $d_1=2$, and a polynomial ring with $d_1=2$ has at most two independent elements of degree $4$ ($f_1^2$ and possibly $f_2$), against the coefficient $3$. Equivalently, the degree-$4$ invariants $p^2,q,r$ of step [](#gens){.pf-ref} are linearly independent, so a single degree-$4$ generator cannot be added to $f_1=p$.
:::
