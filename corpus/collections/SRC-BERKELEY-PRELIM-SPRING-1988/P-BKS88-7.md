::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity element and $a\in R$. Let $n$ and $m$ be positive integers, and write $d=\gcd\{n,m\}$. Prove that the ideal of $R$ generated by $a^n-1$ and $a^m-1$ is the same as the ideal generated by $a^d-1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For positive integers $r,s$, write
$$
I(r,s)\coloneqq(a^r-1,a^s-1)\subseteq R.
$$

<1>1. If $r>s>0$, then
$$
I(r,s)=I(s,r-s).
$$

::: {.proof}
The identity
$$
a^r-1-a^{r-s}(a^s-1)=a^{r-s}-1
$$
shows that $a^{r-s}-1\in I(r,s)$, so
$$
I(s,r-s)\subseteq I(r,s).
$$
Conversely,
$$
a^r-1
=a^{r-s}(a^s-1)+(a^{r-s}-1)
\in I(s,r-s),
$$
and $a^s-1$ is already a generator of $I(s,r-s)$. Hence
$$
I(r,s)\subseteq I(s,r-s).
$$
:::

<1>2. Repeated application of step <1>1 reduces $I(n,m)$ to $I(d,d)$, where $d=\gcd(n,m)$.

::: {.proof}
The subtraction form of the Euclidean algorithm repeatedly replaces an ordered pair of unequal positive integers by the smaller integer and their positive difference. Step <1>1 shows that this replacement leaves the associated ideal unchanged. The process terminates at $(d,d)$, because the common divisor of the pair is unchanged at every replacement and the terminal equal value is therefore $\gcd(n,m)=d$. Consequently
$$
I(n,m)=I(d,d).
$$
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
I(d,d)=(a^d-1).
$$

::: {.proof}
Both generators of $I(d,d)$ are the same element $a^d-1$, so the two-generated ideal is exactly the principal ideal generated by that element.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(a^n-1,a^m-1)=(a^d-1)
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required equality of ideals.
:::
:::
