::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be analytic in the open unit disc of the complex plane and real-valued on the radii $[0,1)$ and $[0,e^{i\pi\sqrt2})$.
Prove that $f$ is constant.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the Taylor expansion at the origin as
$$
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^n,
\qquad |z|<1.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #real-coefficients}
Every coefficient $a_n$ is real.

::: pf-proof
For $0\leq r<1$,
$$
f(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nr^n
$$
is real by hypothesis. Hence
$$
0=\operatorname{Im}f(r)
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Im}(a_n)r^n
$$
for every $r\in[0,1)$. By uniqueness of power-series coefficients, $\operatorname{Im}(a_n)=0$ for every $n$, so $a_n\in\RR$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #constant-coefficients}
For every $n\geq1$, one has $a_n=0$.

::: pf-proof
Set $\theta:=\pi\sqrt2$. For $0\leq r<1$, the second hypothesis gives
$$
f(re^{i\theta})
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_ne^{in\theta}r^n
\in\RR.
$$
Therefore, again by uniqueness of power-series coefficients,
$$
\operatorname{Im}(a_ne^{in\theta})=0
$$
for every $n$. By [](#real-coefficients){.pf-ref}, $a_n$ is real, so
$$
a_n\sin(n\theta)=0.
$$
If $n\geq1$ and $\sin(n\theta)=0$, then $n\theta=k\pi$ for some $k\in\ZZ$, which would give $n\sqrt2=k$, impossible because $\sqrt2$ is irrational. Hence $\sin(n\theta)\neq0$ and therefore $a_n=0$ for every $n\geq1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
By [](#constant-coefficients){.pf-ref},
$$
f(z)=a_0
$$
throughout the unit disc, so $f$ is constant.
:::
