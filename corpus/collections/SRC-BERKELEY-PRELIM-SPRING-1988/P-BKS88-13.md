::: {.problem}
Find all groups G such that every automorphism of G is trivial.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #abelian}
Any group $G$ whose only automorphism is the identity is abelian.

::: pf-proof
For every $g\in G$, conjugation
\[
c_g(x):=gxg^{-1}
\]
is an automorphism of $G$. By hypothesis $c_g$ is the identity for every $g$, so every $g$ commutes with every $x\in G$. Hence $G$ is abelian.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #exponent-two}
Every element of such a group $G$ has order dividing $2$.

::: pf-proof
By [](#abelian){.pf-ref}, $G$ is abelian, so inversion
\[
\iota(x):=x^{-1}
\]
is an automorphism of $G$. It must therefore be the identity. Thus $x=x^{-1}$ for every $x\in G$, equivalently $x^2=e$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #at-most-two-elements}
Such a group has at most two elements.

::: pf-proof
By [](#abelian){.pf-ref} and [](#exponent-two){.pf-ref}, $G$ is an elementary abelian $2$-group, hence a vector space over $\FF_2$. If its dimension were at least $2$, choose linearly independent vectors $u,v$ and extend them to a basis. Swapping $u$ and $v$ while fixing the other basis vectors defines a nonidentity linear automorphism of $G$, contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore $\dim_{\FF_2}G\leq1$, so $|G|\leq2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #examples}
Both the trivial group and $C_2$ have only the identity automorphism.

::: pf-proof
The trivial group has only one self-map. In $C_2$, the identity element must be fixed by every automorphism, and the unique nonidentity element must therefore also be fixed. Thus every automorphism is the identity.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
By [](#at-most-two-elements){.pf-ref} and [](#examples){.pf-ref}, the groups are exactly
\[
\boxed{\{e\}\ \text{and}\ C_2}.
\]
:::
