::: {.problem}
Find all groups G such that every automorphism of G is trivial.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Any group $G$ whose only automorphism is the identity is abelian.

::: {.proof}
For every $g\in G$, conjugation
\[
c_g(x):=gxg^{-1}
\]
is an automorphism of $G$. By hypothesis $c_g$ is the identity for every $g$, so every $g$ commutes with every $x\in G$. Hence $G$ is abelian.
:::

<1>2. Every element of such a group $G$ has order dividing $2$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $G$ is abelian, so inversion
\[
\iota(x):=x^{-1}
\]
is an automorphism of $G$. It must therefore be the identity. Thus $x=x^{-1}$ for every $x\in G$, equivalently $x^2=e$.
:::

<1>3. Such a group has at most two elements.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1 and <1>2, $G$ is an elementary abelian $2$-group, hence a vector space over $\mathbb F_2$. If its dimension were at least $2$, choose linearly independent vectors $u,v$ and extend them to a basis. Swapping $u$ and $v$ while fixing the other basis vectors defines a nonidentity linear automorphism of $G$, contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore $\dim_{\mathbb F_2}G\leq1$, so $|G|\leq2$.
:::

<1>4. Both the trivial group and $C_2$ have only the identity automorphism.

::: {.proof}
The trivial group has only one self-map. In $C_2$, the identity element must be fixed by every automorphism, and the unique nonidentity element must therefore also be fixed. Thus every automorphism is the identity.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>3 and <1>4, the groups are exactly
\[
\boxed{\{e\}\ \text{and}\ C_2}.
\]
:::
:::
