::: {.problem}
Show that one can represent the set of nonnegative integers, $\ZZ_{+}$, as the union of two disjoint subsets $N_{1}$ and $N_{2}$ ($N_{1} \cap N_{2} = \emptyset$, $N_{1} \cup N_{2} = \ZZ_{+}$) such that neither $N_{1}$ nor $N_{2}$ contains an infinite arithmetic progression.
:::

::: {.solution}
Partition the positive integers into consecutive dyadic blocks and alternate the blocks between the two sets.

::: pf

::: pf-step
Define
$$
N_1\coloneqq\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{j\geq0}[2^{2j},2^{2j+1})\cap\ZZ,
\qquad
N_2\coloneqq\bigcup_{j\geq0}[2^{2j+1},2^{2j+2})\cap\ZZ.
$$
Then $N_1$ and $N_2$ are disjoint and their union is the set of nonnegative integers.

::: pf-proof
The half-open intervals
$$
[1,2),[2,4),[4,8),[8,16),\ldots
$$
are pairwise disjoint and partition the positive integers. The definition assigns alternating intervals to $N_1$ and $N_2$, and assigns $0$ to $N_1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dyadic-blocks}
Every infinite arithmetic progression of nonnegative integers meets every sufficiently large dyadic block.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
P=\{a+kd:k\geq0\},
$$
where $a\geq0$ and $d\geq1$. Consider a dyadic block $[2^m,2^{m+1})$ with $2^m>d$ and $2^m>a$. Let $k$ be the least nonnegative integer for which
$$
a+kd\geq2^m.
$$
Minimality gives
$$
a+kd<2^m+d<2^{m+1}.
$$
Thus $a+kd\in[2^m,2^{m+1})$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Neither $N_1$ nor $N_2$ contains an infinite arithmetic progression.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $P$ is an infinite arithmetic progression. By [](#dyadic-blocks){.pf-ref}, $P$ meets every sufficiently large block $[2^m,2^{m+1})$. Infinitely many such blocks have even $m$ and belong to $N_1$, while infinitely many have odd $m$ and belong to $N_2$. Hence $P$ meets both $N_1$ and $N_2$, so it is contained in neither.
:::
