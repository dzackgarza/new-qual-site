::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a set and $V$ a real vector space of real valued functions on $X$ of dimension $n$, $0<n<\infty$. Prove that there are $n$ points $x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n$ in $X$ such that the map $f\mapsto(f(x_1),\ldots,f(x_n))$ of $V$ to $\RR^n$ is an isomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
For each $x\in X$, let
$$
\operatorname{ev}_x:V\to\RR,
\qquad
\operatorname{ev}_x(f)=f(x),
$$
be the evaluation functional.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #spanning-evaluations}
The evaluation functionals span $V^*$.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
W\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{ev}_x:x\in X\}
\subseteq V^*.
$$
Suppose $W\neq V^*$. Since $V$ is finite-dimensional, the annihilator
$$
W^\circ
=
\{f\in V:\lambda(f)=0\text{ for every }\lambda\in W\}
$$
has dimension
$$
\dim W^\circ
=\dim V-\dim W
>0.
$$
Choose $0\neq f\in W^\circ$. For every $x\in X$, the functional $\operatorname{ev}_x$ belongs to $W$, so
$$
f(x)=\operatorname{ev}_x(f)=0.
$$
Thus $f$ is the zero function on $X$, contradicting $f\neq0$ as an element of the function space $V$. Hence $W=V^*$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #evaluation-basis}
There are points $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in X$ such that
$$
\operatorname{ev}_{x_1},\ldots,\operatorname{ev}_{x_n}
$$
form a basis of $V^*$.

::: pf-proof
By [](#spanning-evaluations){.pf-ref}, the set of evaluation functionals spans the $n$-dimensional vector space $V^*$. Every spanning set of a finite-dimensional vector space contains a basis, so some $n$ evaluation functionals form a basis. Choose corresponding points $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in X$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #injective-evaluation-map}
The map
$$
E:V\to\RR^n,
\qquad
E(f)=\bigl(f(x_1),\ldots,f(x_n)\bigr),
$$
is injective.

::: pf-proof
If $E(f)=0$, then
$$
\operatorname{ev}_{x_i}(f)=0
\qquad(1\leq i\leq n).
$$
By [](#evaluation-basis){.pf-ref} these evaluation functionals form a basis of $V^*$, so every $\lambda\in V^*$ satisfies $\lambda(f)=0$. If $f\neq0$, extend $f$ to a basis of $V$ and take the coordinate functional that sends $f$ to $1$; this contradicts the preceding assertion. Hence $f=0$, and $E$ is injective.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The map $E$ is an isomorphism.

::: pf-proof
Both $V$ and $\RR^n$ have dimension $n$. By [](#injective-evaluation-map){.pf-ref}, $E$ is an injective linear map between finite-dimensional vector spaces of equal dimension. Therefore $E$ is bijective, hence an isomorphism.
:::
