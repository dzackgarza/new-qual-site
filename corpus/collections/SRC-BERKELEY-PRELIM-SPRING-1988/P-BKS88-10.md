::: {.problem}
1. Let $G$ be an open connected subset of the complex plane, $f$ an analytic function in $G$, not identically $0$, and $n$ a positive integer.
   Assume that $f$ has an analytic $n$th root in $G$; that is, there is an analytic function $g$ in $G$ such that $g^n=f$. Prove that $f$ has exactly $n$ analytic $n$th roots in $G$.

2. Give an example of a continuous real valued function on [0, 1] that has more than two continuous square roots on [0, 1].
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix one analytic function $g$ on $G$ such that $g^n=f$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #root-quotient}
If $h$ is any analytic $n$th root of $f$, then $h/g$ extends to a nowhere-zero analytic function $q$ on all of $G$ satisfying $q^n=1$.

::: pf-proof
Away from the zeros of $g$, define $q=h/g$. Then
$$
q^n=\frac{h^n}{g^n}=\frac{f}{f}=1.
$$
Let $z_0$ be a zero of $g$ of order $m$. Since $g^n=f=h^n$, the function $f$ has a zero of order $nm$, so $h$ also has a zero of order $m$. Thus near $z_0$ one may write
$$
g(z)=(z-z_0)^m u(z),
\qquad
h(z)=(z-z_0)^m v(z),
$$
where $u$ and $v$ are analytic and nonzero at $z_0$. Hence $h/g=v/u$ extends analytically and nonvanishingly across $z_0$. Doing this at every zero of $g$ gives an analytic nowhere-zero function $q$ on $G$, and the identity $q^n=1$ extends across those zeros by continuity.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Every analytic $n$th root of $f$ is of the form $\zeta g$ for a unique $n$th root of unity $\zeta$.

::: pf-proof
By [](#root-quotient){.pf-ref}, the analytic function $q=h/g$ takes values only in the finite set
$$
\mu_n:=\{\zeta\in\CC:\zeta^n=1\}.
$$
Since $G$ is connected and $q$ is continuous, $q(G)$ is connected. The only connected subsets of the finite set $\mu_n$ are singletons, so $q$ is constant: $q\equiv\zeta$ for some $\zeta\in\mu_n$. Hence $h=\zeta g$. Conversely, every $\zeta\in\mu_n$ gives an analytic root $\zeta g$. These roots are distinct because $g$ is not identically zero. Since $\mu_n$ has exactly $n$ elements, $f$ has exactly $n$ analytic $n$th roots.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The continuous function
$$
F(x):=\left(x-\frac12\right)^2,
\qquad 0\leq x\leq 1,
$$
has more than two continuous real-valued square roots.

::: pf-proof
The four continuous functions
$$
x-\frac12,
\qquad
\frac12-x,
\qquad
\abs{x-\frac12},
\qquad
-\abs{x-\frac12}
$$
are pairwise distinct, and the square of each is $F(x)$. Thus $F$ has at least four continuous square roots on $[0,1]$.
:::
