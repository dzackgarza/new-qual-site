::: {.problem}
1. Let $f$ be an analytic function that maps the open unit disc, $D$, into itself and vanishes at the origin.
   Prove that $\abs{f(z)+f(-z)}\leq2\abs{z}^2$ in $D$.

2. Prove that the inequality in Part 1 is strict, except at the origin, unless $f$ has the form $f(z)=\lambda z^2$ with $\lambda$ a constant of absolute value one.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define the even part of $f$ by
$$
g(z)\coloneqq\frac{f(z)+f(-z)}2.
$$

<1>1. The function $g$ maps $D$ into $D$ and satisfies
$$
g(0)=g'(0)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The function $g$ is analytic. Since $f(z),f(-z)\in D$,
$$
|g(z)|
\leq\frac{|f(z)|+|f(-z)|}{2}
<1,
$$
so $g(D)\subset D$. Also $g(0)=f(0)=0$. Finally $g$ is even, hence $g'(0)=0$.
:::

<1>2. For every $z\in D$,
$$
|g(z)|\leq |z|^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Schwarz's lemma applied to $g$ gives $g(z)=zh(z)$ for an analytic function $h$ with $|h(z)|\leq1$. Since $h(0)=g'(0)=0$, the maximum-modulus principle shows that $h(D)\subset D$ (the only alternative would be a constant of modulus $1$, impossible because $h(0)=0$). Schwarz's lemma applied once more gives
$$
|h(z)|\leq|z|.
$$
Therefore $|g(z)|\leq|z|^2$.
:::

<1>3. For every $z\in D$,
$$
|f(z)+f(-z)|\leq2|z|^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
By the definition of $g$,
$$
|f(z)+f(-z)|=2|g(z)|,
$$
and step <1>2 gives the result.
:::

<1>4. If equality in step <1>3 holds at some $z_0\neq0$, then
$$
g(z)=\lambda z^2
$$
for some constant $\lambda$ with $|\lambda|=1$.

::: {.proof}
With $g=zh$ as in step <1>2, equality at $z_0\neq0$ gives
$$
|h(z_0)|=|z_0|.
$$
The equality case of Schwarz's lemma therefore gives
$$
h(z)=\lambda z
$$
for some $|\lambda|=1$. Hence $g(z)=\lambda z^2$.
:::

<1>5. If $g(z)=\lambda z^2$ with $|\lambda|=1$, then in fact
$$
f(z)=\lambda z^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_kz^k.
$$
The identity $g(z)=\lambda z^2$ says that $a_2=\lambda$. For $0<r<1$, Parseval's identity for the power series on the circle $|z|=r$ gives
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty |a_k|^2r^{2k}
=
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^2\,dt
\leq1.
$$
Letting $r\uparrow1$ yields
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty|a_k|^2\leq1.
$$
But $|a_2|=|\lambda|=1$, so every $a_k$ with $k\neq2$ must vanish. Thus $f(z)=\lambda z^2$.
:::

<1>6. Unless $f(z)=\lambda z^2$ with $|\lambda|=1$, the inequality in Part 1 is strict for every $z\neq0$.

::: {.proof}
If equality held at any nonzero point, steps <1>4 and <1>5 would force precisely that exceptional form. Conversely, for $f(z)=\lambda z^2$ with $|\lambda|=1$,
$$
|f(z)+f(-z)|=2|z|^2
$$
for every $z\in D$.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves Part 1, and step <1>6 proves Part 2.
:::
