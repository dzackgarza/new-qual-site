::: {.problem}
1. Let $f$ be an analytic function that maps the open unit disc, $D$, into itself and vanishes at the origin.
   Prove that $\abs{f(z)+f(-z)}\leq2\abs{z}^2$ in $D$.

2. Prove that the inequality in Part 1 is strict, except at the origin, unless $f$ has the form $f(z)=\lambda z^2$ with $\lambda$ a constant of absolute value one.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define the even part of $f$ by
$$
g(z)\coloneqq\frac{f(z)+f(-z)}2.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step
The function $g$ maps $D$ into $D$ and satisfies
$$
g(0)=g'(0)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
The function $g$ is analytic. Since $f(z),f(-z)\in D$,
$$
\abs{g(z)}
\leq\frac{\abs{f(z)}+\abs{f(-z)}}{2}
<1,
$$
so $g(D)\subset D$. Also $g(0)=f(0)=0$. Finally $g$ is even, hence $g'(0)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #schwarz-bound}
For every $z\in D$,
$$
\abs{g(z)}\leq \abs{z}^2.
$$

::: pf-proof
Schwarz's lemma applied to $g$ gives $g(z)=zh(z)$ for an analytic function $h$ with $\abs{h(z)}\leq1$. Since $h(0)=g'(0)=0$, the maximum-modulus principle shows that $h(D)\subset D$ (the only alternative would be a constant of modulus $1$, impossible because $h(0)=0$). Schwarz's lemma applied once more gives
$$
\abs{h(z)}\leq\abs{z}.
$$
Therefore $\abs{g(z)}\leq\abs{z}^2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #required-bound}
For every $z\in D$,
$$
\abs{f(z)+f(-z)}\leq2\abs{z}^2.
$$

::: pf-proof
By the definition of $g$,
$$
\abs{f(z)+f(-z)}=2\abs{g(z)},
$$
and [](#schwarz-bound){.pf-ref} gives the result.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equality-case}
If equality in [](#required-bound){.pf-ref} holds at some $z_0\neq0$, then
$$
g(z)=\lambda z^2
$$
for some constant $\lambda$ with $\abs{\lambda}=1$.

::: pf-proof
With $g=zh$ as in [](#schwarz-bound){.pf-ref}, equality at $z_0\neq0$ gives
$$
\abs{h(z_0)}=\abs{z_0}.
$$
The equality case of Schwarz's lemma therefore gives
$$
h(z)=\lambda z
$$
for some $\abs{\lambda}=1$. Hence $g(z)=\lambda z^2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coefficient-rigidity}
If $g(z)=\lambda z^2$ with $\abs{\lambda}=1$, then in fact
$$
f(z)=\lambda z^2.
$$

::: pf-proof
Write
$$
f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_kz^k.
$$
The identity $g(z)=\lambda z^2$ says that $a_2=\lambda$. For $0<r<1$, Parseval's identity for the power series on the circle $\abs{z}=r$ gives
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \abs{a_k}^2r^{2k}
=
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\abs{f(re^{it})}^2\,dt
\leq1.
$$
Letting $r\uparrow1$ yields
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty\abs{a_k}^2\leq1.
$$
But $\abs{a_2}=\abs{\lambda}=1$, so every $a_k$ with $k\neq2$ must vanish. Thus $f(z)=\lambda z^2$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Unless $f(z)=\lambda z^2$ with $\abs{\lambda}=1$, the inequality in Part 1 is strict for every $z\neq0$.

::: pf-proof
If equality held at any nonzero point, [](#equality-case){.pf-ref} and [](#coefficient-rigidity){.pf-ref} would force precisely that exceptional form. Conversely, for $f(z)=\lambda z^2$ with $\abs{\lambda}=1$,
$$
\abs{f(z)+f(-z)}=2\abs{z}^2
$$
for every $z\in D$.
:::
