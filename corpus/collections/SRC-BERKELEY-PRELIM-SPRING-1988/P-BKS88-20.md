::: {.problem}
Does there exist a continuous real valued function $f(x)$, $0\leq x\leq1$, such that

$$
\int_0^1 xf(x)\,dx=1\quad\text{and}\quad\int_0^1 x^nf(x)\,dx=0
$$

for $n=0,2,3,4,\ldots$? Give an example or a proof that no such $f$ exists.
:::

::: {.solution}
No such continuous function exists.

<1>1. For each integer $m\geq1$, define
$$
p_m(x)\coloneqq x\bigl(1-(1-x)^m\bigr).
$$
Then $p_m$ is a linear combination of $x^2,x^3,\ldots,x^{m+1}$.

::: {.proof}
The polynomial $1-(1-x)^m$ has zero constant term. Multiplication by $x$ therefore makes every monomial in $p_m$ have degree at least $2$.
:::

<1>2. The sequence $(p_m)$ converges uniformly to $x$ on $[0,1]$.

::: {.proof}
For $0\leq x\leq1$,
$$
|x-p_m(x)|=x(1-x)^m.
$$
The maximum of the right-hand side occurs at $x=1/(m+1)$ and equals
$$
\frac1{m+1}\left(\frac m{m+1}\right)^m\leq\frac1{m+1}.
$$
Hence $\lVert x-p_m\rVert_\infty\to0$.
:::

<1>3. If a continuous $f$ satisfied the stated moment conditions, then
$$
\int_0^1 p_m(x)f(x)\,dx=0
$$
for every $m\geq1$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $p_m$ is a finite linear combination of the monomials $x^n$ with $n\geq2$. Every corresponding moment of $f$ is assumed to vanish, so linearity of the integral gives the claim.
:::

<1>4. Such an $f$ would also satisfy
$$
\int_0^1 xf(x)\,dx=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Continuity of $f$ on the compact interval gives $\lVert f\rVert_\infty<\infty$. By steps <1>2 and <1>3,
$$
\left|\int_0^1 xf(x)\,dx\right|
=
\left|\int_0^1 (x-p_m(x))f(x)\,dx\right|
\leq
\lVert x-p_m\rVert_\infty\lVert f\rVert_\infty.
$$
Letting $m\to\infty$ makes the right-hand side tend to $0$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 contradicts the required condition
$$
\int_0^1 xf(x)\,dx=1.
$$
Therefore no continuous real-valued function with the stated moments exists.
:::
:::
