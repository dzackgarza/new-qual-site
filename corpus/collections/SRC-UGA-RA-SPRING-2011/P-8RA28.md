::: problem
(a) Let $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$.
Give a precise definition of what it means for a function $f:A\to\mathbb{R}$ to be uniformly continuous on $A$.

(b) Let $K$ be a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
Prove that if $f:K\to\mathbb{R}$ is continuous on $K$, then $f$ is uniformly continuous on $K$.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. State the definition.
::: proof
A function $f:A\to\mathbb R$ is uniformly continuous on $A$ if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that for all $x,y\in A$,
\[
|x-y|<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|f(x)-f(y)|<\varepsilon.
\]
The essential point is that $\delta$ depends only on $\varepsilon$, not on the point of $A$.
:::

<1>2. Prove uniform continuity on a compact set.
::: proof
Let $K\subset\mathbb R^n$ be compact and let $f:K\to\mathbb R$ be continuous. Fix $\varepsilon>0$. For each $x\in K$, continuity gives $r_x>0$ such that
\[
|f(y)-f(x)|<\frac\varepsilon2
\qquad\text{whenever }y\in K,\ |y-x|<r_x.
\]
The balls
\[
B(x,r_x/2),\qquad x\in K,
\]
cover $K$. By compactness, choose a finite subcover
\[
B(x_j,r_j/2),\qquad j=1,\dots,N.
\]
Set
\[
\delta:=\frac12\min_{1\le j\le N}r_j>0.
\]
If $x,y\in K$ and $|x-y|<\delta$, choose $j$ with $x\in B(x_j,r_j/2)$. Then
\[
|y-x_j|\le |y-x|+|x-x_j|<\delta+r_j/2\le r_j.
\]
Thus both $x$ and $y$ lie within $r_j$ of $x_j$, and
\[
|f(x)-f(y)|
\le |f(x)-f(x_j)|+|f(x_j)-f(y)|
<\varepsilon.
\]
Therefore $f$ is uniformly continuous on $K$.
:::
