::: {.problem}
Let $A,B\subseteq\RR$ be bounded measurable sets with positive Lebesgue measure.
For each $t\in\RR$ define
$$
g(t)=m\bigl(A\cap(t-B)\bigr),
$$
where $t-B=\{t-b:b\in B\}$.

(a) Prove that $g$ is a continuous function and
$$
\int_{\RR}g(t)\,dt=m(A)m(B).
$$

(b) Conclude that the sumset
$$
A+B=\{a+b:a\in A\text{ and }b\in B\}
$$
contains a non-empty open subset of $\RR$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Express $g$ as a convolution and prove continuity.

::: pf-proof

For $t\in\RR$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(t)
&=\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_A(x)\mathbf1_{t-B}(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_A(x)\mathbf1_B(t-x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
g=\mathbf1_A*\mathbf1_B.
$$
Because $A$ and $B$ have finite measure,
$$
\mathbf1_A,\mathbf1_B\in L^2(\RR).
$$
For $h\in\RR$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{g(t+h)-g(t)}
&\le \norm{\mathbf1_A}_2
\,\norm{\mathbf1_B(t+h-\cdot)-\mathbf1_B(t-\cdot)}_2\\
&=\norm{\mathbf1_A}_2
\,\norm{\tau_h\mathbf1_B-\mathbf1_B}_2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Translations are continuous in $L^2$, so the right-hand side tends to $0$ as $h\to0$, uniformly in $t$. Hence $g$ is continuous.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Compute the integral of $g$.

::: pf-proof

By Tonelli's theorem,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\RR}g(t)\,dt
&=\int_{\RR}\int_{\RR}
\mathbf1_A(x)\mathbf1_B(t-x)\,dx\,dt\\
&=\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_A(x)
\left(\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_B(t-x)\,dt\right)dx\\
&=m(B)\int_{\RR}\mathbf1_A(x)\,dx\\
&=m(A)m(B).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\int_{\RR}g(t)\,dt=m(A)m(B).}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Deduce that $A+B$ has nonempty interior.

::: pf-proof

Since $g\ge0$ and
$$
\int_{\RR}g(t)\,dt=m(A)m(B)>0,
$$
there exists $t_0\in\RR$ such that $g(t_0)>0$. By continuity, there is an open interval $I$ containing $t_0$ such that
$$
g(t)>0
\qquad(t\in I).
$$
If $g(t)>0$, then
$$
m(A\cap(t-B))>0,
$$
so $A\cap(t-B)$ is nonempty. Choose $a\in A\cap(t-B)$. Then $a=t-b$ for some $b\in B$, hence
$$
t=a+b\in A+B.
$$
Therefore
$$
I\subseteq A+B.
$$
Thus $A+B$ contains a nonempty open interval.

:::
