::: {.problem}
Show that if $X$ and $Y$ are topological spaces, and $X\times Y$, with the product topology, is contractible, then both $X$ and $Y$ are contractible.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. Since $X\times Y$ is contractible, it is nonempty; choose $x_0\in X$ and $y_0\in Y$.
::: {.proof}
A contractible space is homotopy equivalent to a point and therefore nonempty under the standard definition. Thus both factors are nonempty.
:::

<1>2. Let $H:(X\times Y)\times I\to X\times Y$ be a contraction from the identity to the constant map at some $(x_*,y_*)$.
::: {.proof}
This is the definition of contractibility of $X\times Y$.
:::

<1>3. The formula
$$
H_X(x,t)=\pi_X(H((x,y_0),t))
$$
defines a homotopy from $\operatorname{id}_X$ to the constant map at $x_*$.
::: {.proof}
The map is continuous as a composite of continuous maps. At $t=0$,
$$
H_X(x,0)=\pi_X(x,y_0)=x,
$$
while at $t=1$,
$$
H_X(x,1)=\pi_X(x_*,y_*)=x_*.
$$
:::

<1>4. Hence $X$ is contractible.
::: {.proof}
The homotopy in <1>3 is a contraction of $X$.
:::

<1>5. Similarly,
$$
H_Y(y,t)=\pi_Y(H((x_0,y),t))
$$
is a contraction of $Y$ to $y_*$.
::: {.proof}
The same endpoint calculation as in <1>3 applies after interchanging the two factors.
:::

<1>6. Therefore both $X$ and $Y$ are contractible.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>4 and <1>5.
:::
:::
