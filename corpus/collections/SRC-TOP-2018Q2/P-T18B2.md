::: {.problem}
A topological space $X$ is called *locally Euclidean* if every point $x\in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ such that $U$ is homeomorphic to an open set in $\mathbb R^n$ for some $n$.
Prove that if $X$ is locally Euclidean and $p:\widetilde X\to X$ is a covering space, then $\widetilde X$ is locally Euclidean.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $\widetilde x\in\widetilde X$ and put $x=p(\widetilde x)$.
::: {.proof}
We construct a Euclidean neighborhood of the arbitrary point $\widetilde x$.
:::

<1>2. Choose an evenly covered open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ for $p$, and let $W$ be the sheet of $p^{-1}(U)$ containing $\widetilde x$.
::: {.proof}
Since $p$ is a covering map,
$$
p^{-1}(U)=\bigsqcup_\beta W_\beta
$$
with each restriction $p|_{W_\beta}:W_\beta\to U$ a homeomorphism. Take the unique sheet containing $\widetilde x$.
:::

<1>3. Shrink $U$ if necessary so that it is homeomorphic to an open subset of some $\mathbb R^n$.
::: {.proof}
Because $X$ is locally Euclidean, choose an open Euclidean neighborhood $V$ of $x$. Replacing $U$ by $U\cap V$ preserves the evenly covered property: each old sheet intersects $p^{-1}(U\cap V)$ in a sheet mapping homeomorphically to $U\cap V$. The intersection is open in the Euclidean chart and hence is itself homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$.
:::

<1>4. The sheet $W$ is an open neighborhood of $\widetilde x$ homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$.
::: {.proof}
The restriction $p|_W:W\to U$ is a homeomorphism by the covering property, and $U$ is Euclidean by <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Therefore $\widetilde X$ is locally Euclidean.
::: {.proof}
The point $\widetilde x$ was arbitrary.
:::
:::
