::: {.problem}
Recall that, for $A\subseteq X$, the set $X/A$ is the set of equivalence classes under the relation $x\sim y$ if $x=y$ or $x,y\in A$.
Show that if $X$ is the real line $\mathbb R$ (with the usual topology) and $A=(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)\subseteq\mathbb R$, that $X/A$, with the quotient topology, is compact but not Hausdorff.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Show $X/A$ is compact but not Hausdorff for $X = \RR$ and $A = (-\infty, 0) \cup (1, \infty)$.

<1>1. $X/A$ is compact.
<2>1. $X/A$ is the image of $[0, 1]$ under the quotient map.
::: {.proof}
every point of $\RR$ is either in $A$ (collapsed to the point $[A]$) or in $[0,1]$; so $X/A = q([0,1])$.
:::
<2>2. $[0,1]$ is compact, and the quotient map is continuous.
::: {.proof}
$[0,1]$ is compact, and $q$ is continuous by definition of the quotient topology.
:::
<2>3. Hence $X/A = q([0,1])$ is compact.
::: {.proof}
the continuous image of a compact set is compact.
:::

<1>2. $X/A$ is not Hausdorff.
<2>1. Let $q:\mathbb R\to X/A$ be the quotient map, and write $a=q(A)$ for the collapsed point and $x=q(0)$.
::: {.proof}
Since $0\notin A$, these are distinct points of the quotient.
:::
<2>2. Every open neighborhood of $x$ contains $a$.
::: {.proof}
If $V\subset X/A$ is open and $x\in V$, then $q^{-1}(V)$ is an open saturated subset of $\mathbb R$ containing $0$. Hence it contains some interval $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ and therefore meets $A=(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)$. Because $q^{-1}(V)$ is saturated, meeting the equivalence class $A$ forces it to contain all of $A$. Thus $a=q(A)\in V$.
:::
<2>3. Therefore $a$ and $x$ cannot have disjoint open neighborhoods, so $X/A$ is not Hausdorff.
::: {.proof}
Any neighborhood of $x$ contains $a$ by <2>2, and hence meets every neighborhood of $a$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 shows compactness; <1>2 shows non-Hausdorffness.
:::
:::
