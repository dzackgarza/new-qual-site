::: {.problem}
Recall that, for $A\subseteq X$, the set $X/A$ is the set of equivalence classes under the relation $x\sim y$ if $x=y$ or $x,y\in A$.
Show that if $X$ is the real line $\mathbb R$ (with the usual topology) and $A=(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)\subseteq\mathbb R$, that $X/A$, with the quotient topology, is compact but not Hausdorff.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Show $X/A$ is compact but not Hausdorff for $X = \RR$ and $A = (-\infty, 0) \cup (1, \infty)$.

<1>1. $X/A$ is compact.
<2>1. $X/A$ is the image of $[0, 1]$ under the quotient map.
Proof: every point of $\RR$ is either in $A$ (collapsed to the point $[A]$) or in $[0,1]$; so $X/A = q([0,1])$.
<2>2. $[0,1]$ is compact, and the quotient map is continuous.
Proof: $[0,1]$ is compact, and $q$ is continuous by definition of the quotient topology.
<2>3. Hence $X/A = q([0,1])$ is compact.
Proof: the continuous image of a compact set is compact.

<1>2. $X/A$ is not Hausdorff.
<2>1. The point $[A]$ (the collapsed set) and the point $[0]$ (or $[1]$) cannot be separated.
Proof: consider the point $[A]$ and the point $[0]$.
<2>2. Any open neighborhood of $[A]$ in $X/A$ corresponds to an open set $U \subseteq \RR$ containing $A$ that is saturated.
Proof: by definition of the quotient topology, open neighborhoods of $[A]$ are images of saturated open sets containing $A$.
<2>3. Any open set $U$ containing $A = (-\infty, 0) \cup (1, \infty)$ must contain points arbitrarily close to $0$ (from the left) and to $1$ (from the right).
Proof: $A$ contains $(-\infty, 0)$, so any open set containing $A$ contains an interval $(-\epsilon, 0)$ for some $\epsilon > 0$; hence it contains points of $[0,1]$ arbitrarily close to $0$.
<2>4. Hence any open neighborhood of $[A]$ meets any open neighborhood of $[0]$.
Proof: an open neighborhood of $[0]$ contains points of $[0,1]$ near $0$, and by <1>2.3 the neighborhood of $[A]$ also contains such points.
<2>5. Hence $[A]$ and $[0]$ cannot be separated by disjoint open sets, so $X/A$ is not Hausdorff.
Proof: <1>2.4.

<1>3. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 shows compactness; <1>2 shows non-Hausdorffness.
:::
