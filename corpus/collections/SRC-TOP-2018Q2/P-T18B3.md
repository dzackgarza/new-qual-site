::: {.problem}
Let $F_n$ denote the free group on $n$ letters.
Use covering space theory to show that for all $n$, there exists a subgroup $H_n$ of $F_2$ such that $H_n\cong F_n$.
For your choices of $H_n$ constructed, determine if each $H_n$ is normal in $F_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $X=S^1\vee S^1$ with vertex $v$ and oriented circles labeled $a$ and $b$, so that $\pi_1(X,v)\cong F_2=\langle a,b\rangle$ by the Seifert--van Kampen theorem.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #hn-for-nge2}
For $n\ge2$, let $d=n-1$, let $\phi\colon F_2\to\ZZ/d$ be the surjective homomorphism with $\phi(a)=1$ and $\phi(b)=0$, and let $H_n=\ker\phi$. Then $H_n\cong F_n$ and $H_n$ is normal in $F_2$.

::: pf-proof
$\phi$ exists by the universal property of the free group, and $[F_2:H_n]=d$.
The covering $\widetilde X_d\to X$ corresponding to $H_n$ is the graph with vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_{d-1}$, $a$-edges $v_i\to v_{i+1\bmod d}$, and a $b$-loop at each $v_i$: each vertex has one incoming and one outgoing edge of each label, and a word lifts to a loop at $v_0$ exactly when $\phi$ vanishes on it.
$\widetilde X_d$ is a connected finite graph with $d$ vertices and $2d$ edges, so $\chi(\widetilde X_d)=-d$, and its fundamental group is free of rank $1-\chi=1+d=n$.
Hence $H_n\cong\pi_1(\widetilde X_d,v_0)\cong F_n$.
$H_n$ is normal as the kernel of a homomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #h1-case}
For $n=1$, $H_1=\langle a\rangle\cong\ZZ=F_1$, and $H_1$ is not normal in $F_2$.

::: pf-proof
The $a$-circle $A\subseteq X$ is a retract of $X$ (collapse the $b$-circle to $v$), so the inclusion induces an injection $\pi_1(A,v)\cong\ZZ\to\pi_1(X,v)$ with image $\langle a\rangle$.
The corresponding based covering has core graph a single $a$-loop at the base vertex, with trees attached.
The reduced word $bab^{-1}$ is not a power of $a$, so $bab^{-1}\notin\langle a\rangle$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Steps [](#hn-for-nge2){.pf-ref} and [](#h1-case){.pf-ref} give $H_n\cong F_n$ for every $n\ge1$; $H_n$ is normal for $n\ge2$ and $H_1$ is not.
For $n=0$, the trivial subgroup, corresponding to the universal covering, is $F_0$ and is normal.
:::
