::: {.problem}
Let $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta'_2$ be distinct $2$-simplices, and let $X$ be the quotient space obtained by identifying the six vertices of $\Delta_2\sqcup\Delta'_2$ to a single point.
Identify a $\Delta$-complex structure for $X$ and compute the simplicial homology groups $H^\Delta_n(X)$ for all $n$.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. After identifying all six vertices, $X$ has a $\Delta$-complex structure with one $0$-simplex $v$, six distinct $1$-simplices $e_0,e_1,e_2,e'_0,e'_1,e'_2$, and two $2$-simplices $\sigma,\sigma'$.
::: {.proof}
Only the vertices of the two disjoint triangles are identified. Their edges and interiors remain distinct, so each original triangle descends to one $2$-simplex whose three faces are three distinct loop edges at the single vertex.
:::

<1>2. The simplicial chain groups are
$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2\xrightarrow{\partial_2}\mathbb Z^6\xrightarrow{\partial_1}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$
::: {.proof}
This follows from the cell count in <1>1.
:::

<1>3. We have $\partial_1=0$.
::: {.proof}
Every $1$-simplex has both endpoints equal to the unique vertex $v$, so its boundary is $v-v=0$.
:::

<1>4. After orienting the triangles,
$$
\partial_2(\sigma)=e_0-e_1+e_2,
\qquad
\partial_2(\sigma')=e'_0-e'_1+e'_2.
$$
In particular, $\partial_2$ is injective.
::: {.proof}
These are the usual alternating simplicial boundaries. The two displayed vectors have disjoint supports in the basis of $C_1\cong\mathbb Z^6$, so they are linearly independent over $\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>5. Hence $H_2^\Delta(X)=0$.
::: {.proof}
There are no $3$-simplices, and $\ker\partial_2=0$ by <1>4.
:::

<1>6. We have
$$
H_1^\Delta(X)\cong\mathbb Z^4.
$$
::: {.proof}
Since $\partial_1=0$,
$$
H_1^\Delta(X)=\mathbb Z^6/\langle e_0-e_1+e_2,\ e'_0-e'_1+e'_2\rangle.
$$
Each relation is primitive and involves a disjoint triple of basis elements, so each quotient $\mathbb Z^3/\langle(1,-1,1)\rangle$ is free of rank $2$. Thus the direct sum quotient is $\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z^2\cong\mathbb Z^4$.
:::

<1>7. Finally $H_0^\Delta(X)\cong\mathbb Z$, and all other homology groups vanish.
::: {.proof}
The complex is connected and has no simplices above dimension $2$. Combining this with <1>5--<1>6 gives the result.
:::
:::
