::: {.problem}
Show that a closed subset $A\subseteq X$ of a normal (i.e., $T_1$ and $T_4$) space $(X,\mathcal T)$, with the subspace topology, is normal.
:::
::: {.solution}
<1>1. The closed subspace $A$ is $T_1$.
::: {.proof}
A subspace of a $T_1$ space is $T_1$: if $a\in A$, then $\{a\}$ is closed in $X$, hence $\{a\}=A\cap\{a\}$ is closed in $A$.
:::

<1>2. Let $C,D\subseteq A$ be disjoint closed subsets of the subspace $A$.
::: {.proof}
To prove normality of $A$, it remains to separate arbitrary such $C$ and $D$ by disjoint open subsets of $A$.
:::

<1>3. The sets $C$ and $D$ are closed in $X$.
::: {.proof}
Because $A$ is closed in $X$, every subset closed in $A$ is closed in $X$: write $C=A\cap F$ and $D=A\cap G$ for closed $F,G\subseteq X$.
:::

<1>4. There exist disjoint open sets $U,V\subseteq X$ with $C\subseteq U$ and $D\subseteq V$.
::: {.proof}
The ambient space $X$ is normal and $C,D$ are disjoint closed subsets of $X$ by <1>2--<1>3.
:::

<1>5. The sets $U\cap A$ and $V\cap A$ are disjoint open neighborhoods of $C$ and $D$ in $A$.
::: {.proof}
They are open in the subspace topology, contain $C$ and $D$, and their intersection is $A\cap U\cap V=\varnothing$.
:::

<1>6. Hence $A$ is normal.
::: {.proof}
Combine the $T_1$ property in <1>1 with the separation property in <1>2--<1>5.
:::
:::
