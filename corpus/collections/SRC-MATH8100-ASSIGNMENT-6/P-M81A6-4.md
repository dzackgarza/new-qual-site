::: problem
1. If $xf(x)\in L^1(\mathbb R)$, prove that $(\widehat f)'=-2\pi i\widehat{xf}$.

2. If $f\in C_0^1(\mathbb R)$ and $f'\in L^1$, prove that $\widehat{f'}(\xi)=2\pi i\xi\widehat f(\xi)$.

3. Prove that the Fourier transform of $G(x)=e^{-\pi x^2}$ equals $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Prove the three Fourier transform identities.

<1>1. (1) $(\hat f)' = -2\pi i \widehat{xf}$ when $xf(x) \in L^1$.
<2>1. $\hat f(\xi) = \int f(x) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx$.
Proof: definition.
<2>2. Differentiate under the integral: $(\hat f)'(\xi) = \int f(x) \frac{\partial}{\partial \xi} e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx$.
Proof: justified since $xf(x) \in L^1$ (the derivative of the integrand is $-2\pi i x f(x) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}$, dominated by $2\pi |x f(x)| \in L^1$).
<2>3. $= \int f(x)(-2\pi i x) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx = -2\pi i \int x f(x) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx = -2\pi i \widehat{xf}(\xi)$.
Proof: the last integral is the Fourier transform of $xf$.

<1>2. (2) $\widehat{f'}(\xi) = 2\pi i \xi \hat f(\xi)$ for $f \in C_0^1$ with $f' \in L^1$.
<2>1. $\widehat{f'}(\xi) = \int f'(x) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx$.
Proof: definition.
<2>2. Integrate by parts: $= \qty[f(x) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}]_{-\infty}^{\infty} - \int f(x)(-2\pi i \xi) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx$.
Proof: integration by parts with $u = e^{-2\pi i x\xi}$, $dv = f'(x)\,dx$.
<2>3. The boundary term vanishes (since $f \in C_0^1$ vanishes at $\pm\infty$).
Proof: $f$ has compact support.
<2>4. Hence $\widehat{f'}(\xi) = 2\pi i \xi \int f(x) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx = 2\pi i \xi \hat f(\xi)$.
Proof: the remaining integral is $\hat f(\xi)$.

<1>3. (3) $\hat G = G$ for $G(x) = e^{-\pi x^2}$.
<2>1. $G$ satisfies the ODE $G'(x) = -2\pi x G(x)$.
Proof: differentiate $e^{-\pi x^2}$.
<2>2. Taking Fourier transforms and using (1) and (2): $2\pi i \xi \hat G(\xi) = -2\pi i \widehat{xG}(\xi) = -2\pi i \cdot \frac{1}{-2\pi i}(\hat G)'(\xi) = (\hat G)'(\xi)$.
Proof: by (2), $\widehat{G'} = 2\pi i \xi \hat G$; by (1), $\widehat{xG} = \frac{1}{-2\pi i}(\hat G)'$; and $G' = -2\pi x G$ gives $\widehat{G'} = -2\pi \widehat{xG}$.
<2>3. Hence $\hat G$ satisfies the same ODE $(\hat G)'(\xi) = -2\pi \xi \hat G(\xi)$.
Proof: rearrange <1>3.2.
<2>4. $\hat G(0) = \int e^{-\pi x^2}\,dx = 1$.
Proof: the Gaussian integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\pi x^2}\,dx = 1$.
<2>5. Hence $\hat G(\xi) = e^{-\pi \xi^2} = G(\xi)$.
Proof: the unique solution of the ODE $y' = -2\pi \xi y$ with $y(0) = 1$ is $y(\xi) = e^{-\pi \xi^2}$.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1, <1>2, <1>3 prove (1), (2), (3).
