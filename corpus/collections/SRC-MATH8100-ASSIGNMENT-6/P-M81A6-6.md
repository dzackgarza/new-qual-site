::: problem
For $\varepsilon>0$, prove that $(1+|\xi|^2)^{-\varepsilon}$ is the Fourier transform of an $L^1(\mathbb R^n)$ function by integrating scaled Gaussians.
:::

::: solution
For $t>0$, set
\[
G_t(x)=t^{-n}e^{-\pi|x|^2/t^2}.
\]
By scaling the standard Gaussian,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^n}G_t(x)\,dx=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\widehat{G_t}(\xi)=e^{-\pi t^2|\xi|^2}.
\]

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and define
\[
C_\varepsilon
=\int_0^\infty e^{-\pi t^2}t^{2\varepsilon-1}\,dt.
\]
This constant is finite and strictly positive: near $0$ the integrand is comparable to $t^{2\varepsilon-1}$, which is integrable because $\varepsilon>0$, and at infinity the Gaussian factor gives convergence.

Now define
\[
g(x)
=\frac1{C_\varepsilon}
\int_0^\infty G_t(x)e^{-\pi t^2}t^{2\varepsilon-1}\,dt.
\]
The integrand is nonnegative, so Tonelli gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|g\|_1
&=\frac1{C_\varepsilon}
\int_0^\infty e^{-\pi t^2}t^{2\varepsilon-1}
\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}G_t(x)\,dx\right)dt\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $g\in L^1(\mathbb R^n)$.

The same absolute-integrability estimate justifies Fubini in the Fourier transform:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat g(\xi)
&=\frac1{C_\varepsilon}
\int_0^\infty
\widehat{G_t}(\xi)e^{-\pi t^2}t^{2\varepsilon-1}\,dt\\
&=\frac1{C_\varepsilon}
\int_0^\infty
e^{-\pi t^2(1+|\xi|^2)}t^{2\varepsilon-1}\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
With
\[
s=t\sqrt{1+|\xi|^2},
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat g(\xi)
&=\frac{(1+|\xi|^2)^{-\varepsilon}}{C_\varepsilon}
\int_0^\infty e^{-\pi s^2}s^{2\varepsilon-1}\,ds\\
&=(1+|\xi|^2)^{-\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence $(1+|\xi|^2)^{-\varepsilon}$ is the Fourier transform of the $L^1$ function $g$.
:::
