::: {.problem}
For $\varepsilon>0$, prove that $(1+\norm{\xi}^2)^{-\varepsilon}$ is the Fourier transform of an $L^1(\RR^n)$ function by integrating scaled Gaussians.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: average the scaled Gaussians $G_{t}$ against the weight $t^{2\varepsilon-1}$, which turns $\pi t^{2}$ into $\pi t^{2}(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})$; the substitution $s=t\sqrt{1+\norm{\xi}^{2}}$ then cancels everything.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #gauss}

$\displaystyle\int_{\RR^{n}}G_{t}=1$ and $\widehat{G_{t}}(\xi)=e^{-\pi t^{2}\norm{\xi}^{2}}$ for $G_{t}(x)=t^{-n}e^{-\pi\norm{x}^{2}/t^{2}}$, $t>0$.

::: pf-proof

Substituting $x=tu$ gives $\int G_{t}=\int t^{-n}e^{-\pi\norm{u}^{2}}t^{n}du=\int e^{-\pi\norm{u}^{2}}du=1$. For the transform, $\widehat{G_{t}}(\xi)=t^{-n}\int e^{-\pi\norm{x}^{2}/t^{2}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}dx$; substituting $x=tu$ makes this $\int e^{-\pi\norm{u}^{2}}e^{-2\pi itu\cdot\xi}du=\widehat{G_{1}}(t\xi)=e^{-\pi\norm{t\xi}^{2}}$ by the known transform $e^{-\pi\norm{\cdot}^{2}}$ of itself.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #constant}

$C_{\varepsilon}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi t^{2}}t^{2\varepsilon-1}dt$ is finite and positive.

::: pf-proof

Near $t=0$ the Gaussian factor is bounded below by a positive constant and $t^{2\varepsilon-1}$ is integrable on $(0,1)$ exactly when $2\varepsilon-1>-1$, i.e. $\varepsilon>0$; at infinity $e^{-\pi t^{2}}t^{2\varepsilon-1}$ is bounded by $e^{-\pi t^{2}/2}$ times a constant, which is integrable. The integrand is positive, so the integral is $>0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #define}

$g(x)=\frac{1}{C_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{t}(x)e^{-\pi t^{2}}t^{2\varepsilon-1}dt$ is in $L^{1}(\RR^{n})$.

::: pf-proof

$\int_{\RR^{n}}\abs{g(x)}dx\le\frac{1}{C_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int_{\RR^{n}}G_{t}(x)dx\Bigr)e^{-\pi t^{2}}t^{2\varepsilon-1}dt=\frac{1}{C_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi t^{2}}t^{2\varepsilon-1}dt=1$ by Tonelli, using $G_{t}\ge0$ and step [](#gauss){.pf-ref}. So $g\in L^{1}$ with $\norm{g}_{1}\le1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #transform}

$\widehat g(\xi)=(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})^{-\varepsilon}$.

::: pf-proof

By Fubini, justified by the same absolute convergence, $\widehat g(\xi)=\frac{1}{C_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{G_{t}}(\xi)e^{-\pi t^{2}}t^{2\varepsilon-1}dt=\frac{1}{C_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi t^{2}(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})}t^{2\varepsilon-1}dt$. Substituting $s=t\sqrt{1+\norm{\xi}^{2}}$, so that $t=s(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})^{-1/2}$ and $dt=ds\,(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})^{-1/2}$,
$$
\widehat g(\xi)=\frac{(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})^{-\varepsilon}}{C_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi s^{2}}s^{2\varepsilon-1}ds=(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})^{-\varepsilon},
$$
the last integral being $C_{\varepsilon}$ again.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})^{-\varepsilon}$ is the Fourier transform of the $L^{1}$ function $g$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#define){.pf-ref} and [](#transform){.pf-ref} exhibit $g\in L^{1}(\RR^{n})$ with $\widehat g(\xi)=(1+\norm{\xi}^{2})^{-\varepsilon}$:
$$
\boxed{\widehat g(\xi)=(1+\abs{\xi}^{2})^{-\varepsilon},\qquad g\in L^{1}(\RR^{n}).}
$$

:::
