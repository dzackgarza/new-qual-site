::: {.problem}
Prove that $\widehat f(\xi)\to0$ as $\norm{\xi}\to\infty$ for every $f\in L^1(\RR^n)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $\xi\ne0$ and set
$$
h=\frac{\xi}{2\norm{\xi}^2}.
$$
Then
$$
e^{2\pi i h\cdot\xi}=e^{\pi i}=-1.
$$
By the change of variables $x=y+h$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat f(\xi)
&=\int_{\RR^n}f(y+h)e^{-2\pi i(y+h)\cdot\xi}\,dy\\
&=-\int_{\RR^n}f(y+h)e^{-2\pi i y\cdot\xi}\,dy.
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding this identity to the defining formula for $\widehat f(\xi)$ gives
$$
2\widehat f(\xi)
=\int_{\RR^n}\bigl(f(y)-f(y+h)\bigr)e^{-2\pi i y\cdot\xi}\,dy.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{\widehat f(\xi)}
\le \frac12\norm{f-\tau_{-h}f}_1,
$$
where $(\tau_{-h}f)(y)=f(y+h)$.

Translations are continuous in $L^1(\RR^n)$: if $g\in C_c(\RR^n)$ approximates $f$ in $L^1$, then
$$
\norm{\tau_{-h}f-f}_1
\le 2\norm{f-g}_1+\norm{\tau_{-h}g-g}_1,
$$
and the last term tends to $0$ as $h\to0$ by uniform continuity of $g$ and compact support.

As $\norm{\xi}\to\infty$,
$$
\norm{h}=\frac1{2\norm{\xi}}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\abs{\widehat f(\xi)}
\le \frac12\norm{f-\tau_{-h}f}_1\longrightarrow0.
$$
This is the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma.
:::
