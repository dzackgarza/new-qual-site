::: problem
1. Prove that $\widehat{f*g}=\widehat f\,\widehat g$ for $f,g\in L^1(\mathbb R^n)$.

2. Deduce that convolution is commutative and associative almost everywhere.

3. Prove that $L^1(\mathbb R^n)$ has no convolution identity.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Part 1: Fourier transform of a convolution:
<2>1. For $f, g \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, the convolution is $(f * g)(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x - y) g(y) \, dy$.
Compute its Fourier transform:
\[
\widehat{f * g}(\xi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \left( \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x - y) g(y) \, dy \right) e^{-2\pi i x \cdot \xi} \, dx.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of Fourier transform.
:::
<2>2. We justify exchanging the order of integration using Tonelli’s Theorem:
\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |f(x - y) g(y) e^{-2\pi i x \cdot \xi}| \, dx \, dy = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |g(y)| \left( \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |f(x - y)| \, dx \right) dy = \|f\|_{L^1} \|g\|_{L^1} < \infty.
\]
::: {.proof}
Tonelli's Theorem and translation invariance of Lebesgue measure.
:::
<2>3. By Fubini’s Theorem, substitute $u = x - y$ (so $x = u + y$ and $dx = du$):
\[
\widehat{f * g}(\xi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} g(y) \left( \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(u) e^{-2\pi i (u + y) \cdot \xi} \, du \right) dy = \left( \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(u) e^{-2\pi i u \cdot \xi} \, du \right) \left( \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} g(y) e^{-2\pi i y \cdot \xi} \, dy \right) = \widehat{f}(\xi) \widehat{g}(\xi).
\]
::: {.proof}
Fubini's Theorem and $e^{-2\pi i(u+y)\cdot\xi} = e^{-2\pi i u\cdot\xi} e^{-2\pi i y\cdot\xi}$.
:::

<1>2. Part 2: Commutativity and associativity of convolution:
<2>1. **Commutativity:**
For all $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^n$:
\[
\widehat{f * g}(\xi) = \widehat{f}(\xi) \widehat{g}(\xi) = \widehat{g}(\xi) \widehat{f}(\xi) = \widehat{g * f}(\xi).
\]
Since the Fourier transform $\mathcal{F}: L^1(\mathbb{R}^n) \to C_0(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is injective, $\widehat{f * g} = \widehat{g * f}$ implies $f * g = g * f$ almost everywhere.
::: {.proof}
commutativity of multiplication in $\mathbb{C}$ and injectivity of the Fourier transform.
:::
<2>2. **Associativity:**
For all $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^n$:
\[
\widehat{(f * g) * h}(\xi) = \widehat{f * g}(\xi) \widehat{h}(\xi) = (\widehat{f}(\xi) \widehat{g}(\xi)) \widehat{h}(\xi) = \widehat{f}(\xi) (\widehat{g}(\xi) \widehat{h}(\xi)) = \widehat{f * (g * h)}(\xi).
\]
By injectivity of the Fourier transform, $(f * g) * h = f * (g * h)$ almost everywhere.
::: {.proof}
associativity of multiplication in $\mathbb{C}$ and injectivity of the Fourier transform.
:::

<1>3. Part 3: Non-existence of a convolution identity in $L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$:
<2>1. Suppose there exists an identity element $e \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ such that $f * e = f$ for all $f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$.
::: {.proof}
proof by contradiction setup.
:::
<2>2. Taking the Fourier transform yields:
\[
\widehat{f}(\xi) \widehat{e}(\xi) = \widehat{f}(\xi) \quad \text{for all } \xi \in \mathbb{R}^n \text{ and all } f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n).
\]
::: {.proof}
Part 1.
:::
<2>3. For the Gaussian function $f_0(x) = e^{-\pi |x|^2} \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, its Fourier transform is $\widehat{f_0}(\xi) = e^{-\pi |\xi|^2} > 0$ for all $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^n$.
Dividing $\widehat{f_0}(\xi) \widehat{e}(\xi) = \widehat{f_0}(\xi)$ by $\widehat{f_0}(\xi)$ gives:
\[
\widehat{e}(\xi) = 1 \quad \text{for all } \xi \in \mathbb{R}^n.
\]
::: {.proof}
non-vanishing Fourier transform of Gaussian.
:::
<2>4. By the Riemann–Lebesgue Lemma, since $e \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, we must have:
\[
\lim_{|\xi| \to \infty} \widehat{e}(\xi) = 0.
\]
However, $\lim_{|\xi| \to \infty} 1 = 1 \neq 0$, which is a contradiction.
Thus no such convolution identity $e \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ exists.
::: {.proof}
Riemann–Lebesgue Lemma.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
$\widehat{f*g} = \widehat{f}\widehat{g}$, convolution is commutative and associative a.e., and $L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ has no convolution identity. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>3.
