::: {.problem}
(a) Let $D=\chi_{[-1/2,1/2]}$. Show that $\widehat D(\xi)=\dfrac{\sin\pi\xi}{\pi\xi}$.

(b) Let $F(x)=1-\abs{x}$ for $\abs{x}\le1$ and $F(x)=0$ otherwise.

(i) Show that $\widehat F(\xi)=\left(\dfrac{\sin\pi\xi}{\pi\xi}\right)^2$.

(ii) Find, with full justification, the Fourier transform of $f(x)=\left(\dfrac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}\right)^2$.

(c) Let $P(x)=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}$. Show that
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi\abs{\xi}}e^{2\pi i x\xi}\,d\xi=P(x),
$$
and hence, with full justification, that $\widehat P(\xi)=e^{-2\pi\abs{\xi}}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
D(x)=\mathbf 1_{[-1/2,1/2]}(x),
\qquad
F(x)=(1-\abs{x})_+,
\qquad
P(x)=\frac1{\pi(1+x^2)}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step
The Dirichlet kernel profile.

::: pf-proof
For $\xi\ne0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat D(\xi)
&=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx\\
&=\frac{e^{-\pi i\xi}-e^{\pi i\xi}}{-2\pi i\xi}
=\frac{\sin(\pi\xi)}{\pi\xi}.
\end{aligned}
$$
At $\xi=0$ the value is $1$, which is also the continuous extension of the displayed formula.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #tent-function}
The tent function.

::: pf-proof
For every $x$,
$$
(D*D)(x)
=m\bigl([-1/2,1/2]\cap[x-1/2,x+1/2]\bigr)
=(1-\abs{x})_+
=F(x).
$$
Therefore the convolution theorem gives
$$
\widehat F(\xi)
=\widehat D(\xi)^2
=\left(\frac{\sin(\pi\xi)}{\pi\xi}\right)^2.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The Fourier transform of the squared sinc function.

::: pf-proof
Set
$$
f(x)=\left(\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}\right)^2.
$$
By step [](#tent-function){.pf-ref}, $f=\widehat F$. Moreover $F\in L^1(\RR)$ and $f\in L^1(\RR)$: near the origin $f$ is bounded, while for $\abs{x}\ge1$,
$$
0\le f(x)\le \frac1{\pi^2x^2}.
$$
Thus the Fourier inversion theorem applies to $F$ and yields
$$
\widehat f(\xi)
=\widehat{\widehat F}(\xi)
=F(-\xi).
$$
Since $F$ is even,
$$
\boxed{\widehat f(\xi)=(1-\abs{\xi})_+.}
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The Poisson kernel.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
q(\xi)=e^{-2\pi\abs{\xi}}.
$$
Then $q\in L^1(\RR)$ and, for every $x\in\RR$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\RR}q(\xi)e^{2\pi i x\xi}\,d\xi
&=2\int_0^\infty e^{-2\pi\xi}\cos(2\pi x\xi)\,d\xi\\
&=2\,\frac{2\pi}{(2\pi)^2+(2\pi x)^2}\\
&=\frac1{\pi(1+x^2)}
=P(x).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since also $P\in L^1(\RR)$, Fourier inversion gives
$$
\boxed{\widehat P(\xi)=e^{-2\pi\abs{\xi}}.}
$$
:::
