::: {.problem}
Let $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ be continuous real-valued functions on $[a,b]$.
Show that $\{f_1,\ldots,f_n\}$ is linearly dependent on $[a,b]$ if and only if
\[
\det\left(\int_a^b f_i(x)f_j(x)\,dx\right)_{i,j=1}^n=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
G
\coloneqq
\left(
\int_a^b f_i(x)f_j(x)\,dx
\right)_{i,j=1}^n.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ are linearly dependent, then
$$
\det G=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Choose real numbers
$$
c_1,\ldots,c_n,
$$
not all zero, such that
$$
\sum_{j=1}^n c_jf_j(x)=0
$$
for every $x\in[a,b]$. Let
$$
c=(c_1,\ldots,c_n)^t.
$$
For each $i$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(Gc)_i
&=
\sum_{j=1}^n
c_j
\int_a^b f_i(x)f_j(x)\,dx\\
&=
\int_a^b
f_i(x)
\left(
\sum_{j=1}^n c_jf_j(x)
\right)
\,dx\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
Gc=0
$$
with $c\neq0$, so $G$ is singular and $\det G=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Suppose
$$
\det G=0.
$$
Then there is a nonzero vector
$$
c=(c_1,\ldots,c_n)^t\in\RR^n
$$
such that
$$
Gc=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

A square matrix has determinant zero exactly when its kernel is nontrivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For the vector $c$ from step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\int_a^b
\left(
\sum_{j=1}^n c_jf_j(x)
\right)^2
\,dx
=0.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $Gc=0$,
$$
c^tGc=0.
$$
Expanding the left side,
$$
\begin{aligned}
c^tGc
&=
\sum_{i,j=1}^n
c_ic_j
\int_a^b f_i(x)f_j(x)\,dx\\
&=
\int_a^b
\left(
\sum_{i=1}^n c_if_i(x)
\right)
\left(
\sum_{j=1}^n c_jf_j(x)
\right)
\,dx,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is the displayed integral.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One has
$$
\sum_{j=1}^n c_jf_j(x)=0
$$
for every $x\in[a,b]$.

::: pf-proof

The function
$$
F(x)
\coloneqq
\left(
\sum_{j=1}^n c_jf_j(x)
\right)^2
$$
is continuous and nonnegative. Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\int_a^bF(x)\,dx=0.
$$
If $F(x_0)>0$ at some point, continuity would make $F$ positive on a
nonempty interval around $x_0$, forcing its integral to be positive. Hence
$F\equiv0$, which gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

If $\det G=0$, then $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ are linearly dependent.

::: pf-proof

The coefficients $c_j$ from step [](#s2){.pf-ref} are not all zero, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref}
shows that their linear combination of the $f_j$ vanishes identically.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves one implication and step [](#s5){.pf-ref} proves the converse.

:::
