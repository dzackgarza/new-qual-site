::: {.problem}
Let $f(x)\in\mathbb Q[x]$.
Show that there exists a nonzero $g(x)\in\mathbb Q[x]$ such that every nonzero term of
\[
f(x)g(x)
\]
has prime exponent; equivalently,
\[
f(x)g(x)=a_2x^2+a_3x^3+a_5x^5+\cdots+a_px^p
\]
for suitable coefficients and primes.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $f=0$, the assertion holds.

::: pf-proof

Take
$$
g(x)=1.
$$
Then
$$
f(x)g(x)=0,
$$
which has no nonzero terms, so the condition on nonzero exponents is
vacuously satisfied.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $f$ is a nonzero constant, the assertion holds.

::: pf-proof

Write
$$
f(x)=c
$$
with $c\in\QQ^\times$, and take
$$
g(x)=c^{-1}x^2.
$$
Then
$$
f(x)g(x)=x^2,
$$
whose only exponent is the prime $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Suppose
$$
d\coloneqq\deg f\geq1.
$$
Then the quotient
$$
V\coloneqq\QQ[x]/(f)
$$
is a $d$-dimensional vector space over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

Every polynomial has a unique remainder of degree less than $d$ upon
division by $f$. Therefore the residue classes of
$$
1,x,\ldots,x^{d-1}
$$
form a basis of $V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Choose distinct primes
$$
p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_d.
$$
Then there are rational numbers
$$
a_0,\ldots,a_d,
$$
not all zero, such that
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_jx^{p_j}
\equiv0
\pmod f.
$$

::: pf-proof

The $d+1$ residue classes
$$
[x^{p_0}],\ldots,[x^{p_d}]
$$
lie in the $d$-dimensional vector space $V$ from step [](#s3){.pf-ref}. They are
therefore linearly dependent, giving coefficients $a_j\in\QQ$, not all
zero, whose displayed linear combination represents the zero class.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The polynomial
$$
h(x)\coloneqq
\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_jx^{p_j}
$$
is nonzero and divisible by $f$.

::: pf-proof

The exponents $p_0,\ldots,p_d$ are distinct and the coefficients $a_j$ are
not all zero, so the monomials cannot cancel identically; hence $h\neq0$.
The congruence in step [](#s4){.pf-ref} says exactly that
$$
f\mid h
$$
in $\QQ[x]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

There is a nonzero polynomial
$$
g(x)\in\QQ[x]
$$
such that every nonzero term of $f(x)g(x)$ has prime exponent.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, write
$$
h(x)=f(x)g(x)
$$
for some $g\in\QQ[x]$. Since both $f$ and $h$ are nonzero, $g\neq0$.
Moreover,
$$
f(x)g(x)
=
h(x)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_jx^{p_j},
$$
and every exponent $p_j$ occurring in this expression is prime.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref} handle the cases $\deg f\leq0$, while step [](#s6){.pf-ref}
handles every nonconstant polynomial.

:::
