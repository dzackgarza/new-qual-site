::: {.problem}
Show that for $|z|<1$,
\[
(1+z+\cdots+z^9)
(1+z^{10}+\cdots+z^{90})
(1+z^{100}+\cdots+z^{900})\cdots
=\frac1{1-z}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For $j\geq0$, define
$$
F_j(z)
\coloneqq
\sum_{d=0}^{9}z^{d10^j}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $j\geq0$ and every $z$ with $z^{10^j}\neq1$,
$$
F_j(z)
=
\frac{1-z^{10^{j+1}}}{1-z^{10^j}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply the finite geometric-series formula
$$
1+w+\cdots+w^9
=
\frac{1-w^{10}}{1-w}
$$
with
$$
w=z^{10^j}.
$$
Then
$$
w^{10}=z^{10^{j+1}},
$$
which gives the stated identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $\abs{z}<1$, then for every $N\geq0$,
$$
\prod_{j=0}^{N}F_j(z)
=
\frac{1-z^{10^{N+1}}}{1-z}.
$$

::: pf-proof

When $\abs z<1$, none of the numbers $z^{10^j}$ equals $1$, so step [](#s1){.pf-ref}
applies to every factor. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\prod_{j=0}^{N}F_j(z)
&=
\prod_{j=0}^{N}
\frac{1-z^{10^{j+1}}}{1-z^{10^j}}\\
&=
\frac{1-z^{10^{N+1}}}{1-z},
\end{aligned}
$$
because all intermediate factors cancel.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $\abs{z}<1$, then
$$
z^{10^{N+1}}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(N\to\infty).
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $0\leq\abs z<1$,
$$
\abs{z^{10^{N+1}}}
=
\abs z^{10^{N+1}}
\longrightarrow0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore, for $\abs z<1$,
$$
\boxed{
\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}
\left(
\sum_{d=0}^{9}z^{d10^j}
\right)
=
\frac1{1-z}
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

The infinite product is the limit of its finite partial products. By step
[](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\prod_{j=0}^{N}F_j(z)
=
\frac{1-z^{10^{N+1}}}{1-z},
$$
and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} makes the numerator tend to $1$. Hence the limit is
$1/(1-z)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is exactly the claimed product identity.

:::
