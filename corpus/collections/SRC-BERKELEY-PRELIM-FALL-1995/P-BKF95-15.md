::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be $C^\infty$ and suppose $f$ has a local minimum at $0$.
Prove that there is a disk centered on the $y$-axis which lies above the graph of $f$ and is tangent to the graph at
\[
(0,f(0)).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
c\coloneqq f(0).
$$

<1>1. One has
$$
f'(0)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
A differentiable function at an interior local minimum has derivative zero.
:::

<1>2. There are constants $\delta>0$ and $C\geq0$ such that
$$
f(x)\leq c+Cx^2
$$
whenever
$$
\abs{x}\leq\delta.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $f''$ is continuous, it is bounded on some interval
$[-\delta,\delta]$. Let
$$
M\coloneqq
\max_{\abs{t}\leq\delta}\abs{f''(t)}.
$$
Taylor's theorem, together with step <1>1, gives for each
$\abs{x}\leq\delta$ a point $\xi$ between $0$ and $x$ such that
$$
f(x)
=
c+\frac12f''(\xi)x^2.
$$
Hence
$$
f(x)
\leq
c+\frac M2x^2.
$$
Take $C=M/2$.
:::

<1>3. Choose $R>0$ such that
$$
R\leq\delta
$$
and
$$
2CR\leq1.
$$
Let
$$
\mathcal D
\coloneqq
\left\{
(x,y)\in\RR^2:
x^2+(y-c-R)^2\leq R^2
\right\}.
$$
Then $\mathcal D$ is a disk centered on the $y$-axis and
$(0,c)\in\partial\mathcal D$.

::: {.proof}
If $C=0$, choose any $0<R\leq\delta$. If $C>0$, choose
$$
0<R\leq\min\left\{\delta,\frac1{2C}\right\}.
$$
The displayed set is the closed disk of radius $R$ centered at
$$
(0,c+R),
$$
which lies on the $y$-axis. The distance from this center to $(0,c)$ is
$R$, so $(0,c)$ lies on its boundary.
:::

<1>4. For every $\abs{x}\leq R$, the lower boundary of $\mathcal D$ is
$$
\ell(x)
\coloneqq
c+R-\sqrt{R^2-x^2},
$$
and
$$
\ell(x)
\geq
c+\frac{x^2}{2R}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Rationalizing gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R-\sqrt{R^2-x^2}
&=
\frac{x^2}{R+\sqrt{R^2-x^2}}\\
&\geq
\frac{x^2}{2R},
\end{aligned}
$$
because
$$
\sqrt{R^2-x^2}\leq R.
$$
Adding $c$ gives the claim.
:::

<1>5. The disk $\mathcal D$ lies above the graph of $f$.

::: {.proof}
If $\abs{x}\leq R$, then step <1>2 applies because $R\leq\delta$, and
step <1>3 gives
$$
C\leq\frac1{2R}.
$$
Therefore
$$
f(x)
\leq
c+Cx^2
\leq
c+\frac{x^2}{2R}
\leq
\ell(x)
$$
by step <1>4.

Every point $(x,y)\in\mathcal D$ has $\abs{x}\leq R$ and
$$
y\geq\ell(x).
$$
Hence
$$
y\geq f(x)
$$
for every point of the disk, which is precisely that the disk lies above
the graph.
:::

<1>6. The disk $\mathcal D$ is tangent to the graph at $(0,c)$.

::: {.proof}
The lower boundary function satisfies
$$
\ell(0)=c
$$
and
$$
\ell'(0)=0.
$$
By step <1>1,
$$
f(0)=c
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f'(0)=0.
$$
Thus the graph and the circle boundary meet at $(0,c)$ with the same
horizontal tangent line. Step <1>5 shows that the disk stays on the upper
side of the graph there.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3, <1>5, and <1>6 give a disk centered on the $y$-axis, lying
above the graph, and tangent to it at $(0,f(0))$.
:::
:::
