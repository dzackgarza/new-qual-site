::: {.problem}
Let $A,B$ be nonsimilar complex $n\times n$ matrices with the same minimal polynomial and the same characteristic polynomial.
Show that
\[
n\ge4
\]
and that the minimal polynomial is not equal to the characteristic polynomial.
:::

::: {.solution}
For an eigenvalue $\lambda$, let
$$
m_\lambda
$$
denote its algebraic multiplicity and let
$$
s_\lambda
$$
denote the size of the largest Jordan block for $\lambda$.

<1>1. The characteristic polynomial determines every
$m_\lambda$, and the minimal polynomial determines every
$s_\lambda$.

::: {.proof}
In Jordan normal form, the exponent of
$$
t-\lambda
$$
in the characteristic polynomial is the sum of the sizes of all Jordan
blocks for $\lambda$, which is $m_\lambda$. The exponent of
$t-\lambda$ in the minimal polynomial is the largest such block size,
which is $s_\lambda$.
:::

<1>2. If
$$
m_\lambda\leq3,
$$
then the pair $(m_\lambda,s_\lambda)$ uniquely determines the multiset of
Jordan-block sizes for $\lambda$.

::: {.proof}
The possible partitions are
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
m_\lambda&\text{partitions by block sizes}\\
\hline
1&(1)\\
2&(2),\ (1,1)\\
3&(3),\ (2,1),\ (1,1,1).
\end{array}
$$
For each fixed value of $m_\lambda$ in this table, the possible partitions
have distinct largest parts. Thus specifying $s_\lambda$ selects exactly
one partition.
:::

<1>3. If $n\leq3$, then two complex $n\times n$ matrices with the same
characteristic polynomial and the same minimal polynomial are similar.

::: {.proof}
For every eigenvalue,
$$
m_\lambda\leq n\leq3.
$$
By step <1>1, the common characteristic and minimal polynomials give the
same pair $(m_\lambda,s_\lambda)$ for the two matrices. Step <1>2 then
gives the same Jordan-block partition for every eigenvalue. Hence the two
Jordan normal forms are identical up to reordering blocks, so the matrices
are similar.
:::

<1>4. Under the hypotheses of the problem,
$$
\boxed{n\geq4}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The matrices $A$ and $B$ are assumed nonsimilar. Step <1>3 rules out
$n\leq3$.
:::

<1>5. If a complex matrix has equal minimal and characteristic
polynomials, then for each eigenvalue it has exactly one Jordan block.

::: {.proof}
Let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue. Equality of the two polynomials makes the
exponent of $t-\lambda$ the same in both. By step <1>1, this says
$$
s_\lambda=m_\lambda.
$$
The largest Jordan block for $\lambda$ therefore already has size equal to
the total size of all Jordan blocks for $\lambda$. No second nonempty block
can occur.
:::

<1>6. Two complex matrices with the same characteristic polynomial, each
of which also has minimal polynomial equal to that characteristic
polynomial, are similar.

::: {.proof}
The common characteristic polynomial fixes every eigenvalue and its
algebraic multiplicity $m_\lambda$. By step <1>5, each matrix has exactly
one Jordan block
$$
J_{m_\lambda}(\lambda)
$$
for each eigenvalue. Thus their Jordan normal forms are the same up to
block order, so the matrices are similar.
:::

<1>7. Under the hypotheses of the problem, the common minimal polynomial
is not equal to the common characteristic polynomial.

::: {.proof}
If they were equal, step <1>6 would imply that $A$ and $B$ are similar,
contrary to hypothesis.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves $n\geq4$, and step <1>7 proves that the minimal and
characteristic polynomials are unequal.
:::
