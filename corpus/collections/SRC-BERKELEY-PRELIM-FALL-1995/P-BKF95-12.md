::: {.problem}
Let $f$ and $f'$ be continuous on $[0,\infty)$, and suppose
\[
f(x)=0\qquad(x\ge10^{10}).
\]
Show that
\[
\int_0^\infty f(x)^2\,dx
\le
2\sqrt{\int_0^\infty x^2f(x)^2\,dx}
\sqrt{\int_0^\infty f'(x)^2\,dx}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

One has
$$
\left(xf(x)^2\right)'
=
f(x)^2+2x f(x)f'(x).
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply the product rule and the chain rule:
$$
\frac d{dx}\left(xf^2\right)
=
f^2+x(2ff').
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

One has
$$
\int_0^\infty f(x)^2\,dx
=
-2\int_0^\infty x f(x)f'(x)\,dx.
$$

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
R=10^{10}.
$$
The hypothesis gives
$$
f(x)=0
\qquad
x\geq R.
$$
Integrating step [](#s1){.pf-ref} over $[0,R]$ gives
$$
\left[xf(x)^2\right]_0^R
=
\int_0^R f(x)^2\,dx
+2\int_0^R x f(x)f'(x)\,dx.
$$
At $x=R$, $f(R)=0$, while at $x=0$ the factor $x$ makes the boundary term
zero. Therefore
$$
0
=
\int_0^R f(x)^2\,dx
+2\int_0^R x f(x)f'(x)\,dx.
$$
Since $f$ is identically zero for $x\geq R$, one also has $f'(x)=0$ for
$x>R$. Extending both integrals to $[0,\infty)$ therefore does not change
them. Rearranging gives the displayed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

One has
$$
\abs{
\int_0^\infty
x f(x)f'(x)\,dx
}
\leq
\sqrt{
\int_0^\infty x^2f(x)^2\,dx
}
\sqrt{
\int_0^\infty f'(x)^2\,dx
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality in $L^2([0,\infty))$ to the functions
$$
x f(x)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f'(x).
$$
Both vanish outside a bounded interval, so the displayed integrals are
finite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore
$$
\int_0^\infty f(x)^2\,dx
\leq
2
\sqrt{
\int_0^\infty x^2f(x)^2\,dx
}
\sqrt{
\int_0^\infty f'(x)^2\,dx
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\int_0^\infty f^2
=
2\abs{
\int_0^\infty xff'
},
$$
because the left side is nonnegative. Apply step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is the required inequality.

:::
