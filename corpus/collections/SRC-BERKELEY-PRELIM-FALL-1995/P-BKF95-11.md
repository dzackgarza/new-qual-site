::: {.problem}
Let $f=u+iv$ be holomorphic on $|z|<1$, with $u,v$ real-valued.
Assume
\[
u(0)^2=v(0)^2.
\]
Show that for every $0<r<1$,
\[
\int_0^{2\pi}u(re^{i\theta})^2\,d\theta
=
\int_0^{2\pi}v(re^{i\theta})^2\,d\theta.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The function
$$
U(z)\coloneqq u(z)^2-v(z)^2
$$
is harmonic on the unit disk.

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
f=u+iv
$$
is holomorphic, so is
$$
f^2
=(u^2-v^2)+2iuv.
$$
The real part of a holomorphic function is harmonic. Hence
$$
U=\Re(f^2)
$$
is harmonic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $0<r<1$,
$$
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
\left(
u(re^{i\theta})^2-v(re^{i\theta})^2
\right)
\,d\theta
=
u(0)^2-v(0)^2.
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply the mean-value property to the harmonic function $U$ from step
[](#s1){.pf-ref} on the circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every $0<r<1$,
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}
u(re^{i\theta})^2\,d\theta
=
\int_0^{2\pi}
v(re^{i\theta})^2\,d\theta.
$$

::: pf-proof

The hypothesis gives
$$
u(0)^2-v(0)^2=0.
$$
Therefore step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}
\left(
u(re^{i\theta})^2-v(re^{i\theta})^2
\right)
\,d\theta
=0.
$$
Move the second integral to the other side.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} is the required identity.

:::
