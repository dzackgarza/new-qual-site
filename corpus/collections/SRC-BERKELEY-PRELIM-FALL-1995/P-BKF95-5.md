::: {.problem}
Prove that
\[
\mathbb Q[x,y]/(x^2+y^2-1)
\]
is an integral domain and that its field of fractions is isomorphic to
\[
\mathbb Q(t).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
q(x,y)\coloneqq x^2+y^2-1.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The polynomial $q$ is irreducible in $\QQ[x,y]$.

::: pf-proof

Regard $q$ as a monic quadratic polynomial in $y$ over the UFD $\QQ[x]$:
$$
q=y^2+(x^2-1).
$$
If it were reducible, its monicity would give a factorization
$$
q=(y+a(x))(y+b(x))
$$
with $a,b\in\QQ[x]$. Comparing the coefficient of $y$ gives
$$
b=-a,
$$
and comparing constant terms gives
$$
a(x)^2=1-x^2.
$$
This is impossible. Indeed, $a$ cannot be constant because the right side
has degree $2$. Thus $\deg a=1$, but then the leading coefficient of
$a(x)^2$ is a square in $\QQ$, whereas the leading coefficient of
$1-x^2$ is $-1$, which is not a square in $\QQ$.

Therefore no such factorization exists, so $q$ is irreducible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The quotient
$$
R\coloneqq
\QQ[x,y]/(q)
$$
is an integral domain.

::: pf-proof

The ring $\QQ[x,y]$ is a UFD. In a UFD, every irreducible element is prime.
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $q$ is irreducible and therefore prime, so the principal ideal
$(q)$ is prime. Hence the quotient by $(q)$ is an integral domain.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Let $X,Y\in R$ be the residue classes of $x,y$. Then $X$ is
transcendental over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

The natural map
$$
\QQ[x]\longrightarrow R
$$
is injective. Indeed, if a polynomial $p(x)\in\QQ[x]$ belonged to $(q)$,
then
$$
p(x)=q(x,y)h(x,y)
$$
for some $h\in\QQ[x,y]$. If $h\neq0$, the right side has degree in $y$ at
least $2$, whereas the left side has degree in $y$ equal to $0$, a
contradiction. Thus $p=0$.

Therefore no nonzero polynomial over $\QQ$ vanishes at $X$, so $X$ is
transcendental.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

In the fraction field
$$
K\coloneqq\operatorname{Frac}(R),
$$
define
$$
t\coloneqq\frac{Y}{1+X}.
$$
Then
$$
t^2=\frac{1-X}{1+X}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives $1+X\neq0$, so $t$ is defined in $K$. The relation
$$
X^2+Y^2=1
$$
holds in $R$, hence in $K$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
t^2
&=
\frac{Y^2}{(1+X)^2}\\
&=
\frac{1-X^2}{(1+X)^2}\\
&=
\frac{1-X}{1+X}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

One has
$$
X=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Y=\frac{2t}{1+t^2}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
t^2(1+X)=1-X.
$$
Thus
$$
X(1+t^2)=1-t^2.
$$
Also,
$$
1+t^2
=
1+\frac{1-X}{1+X}
=
\frac{2}{1+X},
$$
so $1+t^2\neq0$. Hence
$$
X=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}.
$$
Finally,
$$
Y=t(1+X)
=
t\left(
1+\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}
\right)
=
\frac{2t}{1+t^2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The fraction field $K$ is equal to $\QQ(t)$.

::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
t=\frac{Y}{1+X}\in K,
$$
so
$$
\QQ(t)\subseteq K.
$$
Conversely, step [](#s5){.pf-ref} shows
$$
X,Y\in\QQ(t).
$$
Since $K$ is generated as a field by the residue classes $X$ and $Y$,
$$
K=\QQ(X,Y)\subseteq\QQ(t).
$$
Thus $K=\QQ(t)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

The element $t$ is transcendental over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

If $t$ were algebraic over $\QQ$, then every element of $\QQ(t)$ would be
algebraic over $\QQ$. Step [](#s6){.pf-ref} would then make $X$ algebraic over $\QQ$,
contradicting step [](#s3){.pf-ref}. Hence $t$ is transcendental.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Frac}\left(
\QQ[x,y]/(x^2+y^2-1)
\right)
\cong
\QQ(t)
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s6){.pf-ref} identifies the fraction field with the field generated by $t$,
and step [](#s7){.pf-ref} shows that $t$ is transcendental. Therefore that field is a
rational function field in one variable over $\QQ$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves that the quotient is an integral domain, and step [](#s8){.pf-ref}
identifies its field of fractions.

:::
