::: {.problem}
Evaluate
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x(x-\pi)}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The displayed integrand has removable singularities at $0$ and
$\pi$, and its improper integral over $\mathbb R$ converges.

::: {.proof}
As $x\to0$,
$$
\frac{\sin x}{x(x-\pi)}
\longrightarrow
-\frac1\pi.
$$
Writing $x=\pi+h$ gives, as $h\to0$,
$$
\frac{\sin(\pi+h)}{(\pi+h)h}
\longrightarrow
-\frac1\pi.
$$
Thus both apparent singularities are removable. For large $\abs{x}$,
$$
\left|
\frac{\sin x}{x(x-\pi)}
\right|
=O(\abs{x}^{-2}),
$$
so the tails are absolutely integrable.
:::

<1>2. The Dirichlet integral satisfies
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin u}{u}\,du=\pi.
$$

::: {.proof}
The evaluation of [[P-BKS83-10|$\int_0^\infty(\sin x/x)^2\,dx$]] establishes
$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin(2x)}x\,dx=\frac\pi2.
$$
With $u=2x$, this becomes
$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin u}{u}\,du=\frac\pi2.
$$
Since $\sin u/u$ is even, the integral over the whole real line is
$\pi$.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x-\pi}\,dx=-\pi.
$$

::: {.proof}
Substitute
$$
u=x-\pi.
$$
Since
$$
\sin(u+\pi)=-\sin u,
$$
step <1>2 gives
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x-\pi}\,dx
=
-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin u}{u}\,du
=
-\pi.
$$
:::

<1>4. The requested integral is
$$
\boxed{-2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The partial-fraction identity
$$
\frac1{x(x-\pi)}
=
\frac1\pi
\left(
\frac1{x-\pi}-\frac1x
\right)
$$
gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin x}{x(x-\pi)}\,dx
&=
\frac1\pi
\left(
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x-\pi}\,dx
-
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
\right)\\
&=
\frac1\pi(-\pi-\pi)
=
-2,
\end{aligned}
$$
by steps <1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 gives the requested value.
:::
:::
