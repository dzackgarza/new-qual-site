::: {.problem}
Determine all analytic functions $f$ on the unit disk satisfying
\[
f''\left(\frac1n\right)+f\left(\frac1n\right)=0
\]
for every integer $n\ge2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every function satisfying the stated conditions obeys
$$
f''(z)+f(z)=0
$$
throughout the unit disk.

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
h(z)\coloneqq f''(z)+f(z).
$$
The function $h$ is analytic on the unit disk, and the hypothesis gives
$$
h(1/n)=0
\qquad
(n\ge2).
$$
The distinct zeros $1/n$ converge to $0$, which lies in the unit disk.
The identity theorem therefore gives $h\equiv0$.
:::

<1>2. If
$$
f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^\infty a_mz^m,
$$
then step <1>1 is equivalent to the recurrence
$$
a_{m+2}
=
-\frac{a_m}{(m+2)(m+1)}
\qquad
(m\ge0).
$$

::: {.proof}
Termwise differentiation gives
$$
f''(z)
=
\sum_{m=0}^\infty(m+2)(m+1)a_{m+2}z^m.
$$
Thus $f''+f=0$ exactly when every coefficient satisfies
$$
(m+2)(m+1)a_{m+2}+a_m=0,
$$
which is the displayed recurrence.
:::

<1>3. Every such function has the form
$$
\boxed{f(z)=a\cos z+b\sin z}
$$
for uniquely determined $a,b\in\mathbb C$.

::: {.proof}
The recurrence in step <1>2 determines all even coefficients from
$a_0$ and all odd coefficients from $a_1$:
$$
a_{2k}=\frac{(-1)^ka_0}{(2k)!},
\qquad
a_{2k+1}=\frac{(-1)^ka_1}{(2k+1)!}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(z)
&=
a_0\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^kz^{2k}}{(2k)!}
+
a_1\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^kz^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\\
&=
a_0\cos z+a_1\sin z.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking $a=a_0=f(0)$ and $b=a_1=f'(0)$ proves uniqueness of the two
constants.
:::

<1>4. Conversely, every function $f(z)=a\cos z+b\sin z$ satisfies the
original conditions.

::: {.proof}
For such a function,
$$
f''(z)+f(z)=0
$$
for every $z\in\mathbb C$, hence in particular at every $z=1/n$ with
$n\ge2$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 prove necessity, and step <1>4 proves sufficiency.
:::
:::
