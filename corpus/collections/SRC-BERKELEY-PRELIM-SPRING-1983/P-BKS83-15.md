::: {.problem}
Let $f,g$ be complex polynomials and suppose
\[
\deg g\ge\deg f+2.
\]
Show that there is $r>0$ such that
\[
\int_C\frac{f(z)}{g(z)}\,dz=0
\]
for every simple closed curve $C$ disjoint from the closed disk $|z|\le r$.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $f=0$, the assertion is immediate. Assume henceforth that $f\ne0$, and
set
$$
R(z)\coloneqq\frac{f(z)}{g(z)}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #decay-estimate}
There are constants $A>0$ and $r_0>0$ such that
$$
\abs{R(z)}\le\frac{A}{\abs{z}^2}
\qquad
(\abs{z}\ge r_0).
$$

::: pf-proof
Write
$$
\deg f=m,
\qquad
\deg g=d.
$$
The hypothesis gives $d-m\ge2$. Dividing numerator and denominator by
$z^d$ shows that for sufficiently large $\abs{z}$,
$$
R(z)
=
z^{m-d}
\frac{a_m+a_{m-1}z^{-1}+\cdots}
{b_d+b_{d-1}z^{-1}+\cdots},
$$
where $b_d\ne0$. The quotient on the right is bounded for large
$\abs{z}$, while $m-d\le-2$. This yields the stated estimate.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #residue-sum-zero}
The sum of the residues of $R$ at all its finite poles is zero.

::: pf-proof
Let $\mathcal P$ be the finite set of poles of $R$. Choose $R_0$ so large
that every zero of $g$ lies in $\abs{z}<R_0$ and step [](#decay-estimate){.pf-ref} applies for
$\abs{z}\ge R_0$. For $\rho>R_0$, the residue theorem gives
$$
\int_{\abs{z}=\rho}R(z)\,dz
=
2\pi i
\sum_{a\in\mathcal P}\operatorname{Res}_{z=a}R.
$$
On the other hand, step [](#decay-estimate){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\left|
\int_{\abs{z}=\rho}R(z)\,dz
\right|
\le
2\pi\rho\frac{A}{\rho^2}
=
\frac{2\pi A}{\rho}
\longrightarrow0.
$$
The residue sum is independent of $\rho$, so it must equal zero.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #disk-separates-poles}
Choose $r>0$ so that every zero of $g$ lies in the open disk
$$
D_r\coloneqq\{z:\abs{z}<r\}.
$$
For every simple closed curve $C$ disjoint from the closed disk
$\overline D_r$, the interior of $C$ contains either every pole of $R$ or
no pole of $R$.

::: pf-proof
By the Jordan curve theorem,
$$
\mathbb C-C
$$
has exactly two components, the bounded interior and the unbounded
exterior. The closed disk $\overline D_r$ is connected and is disjoint
from $C$, so it lies entirely in one of those two components. Since every
pole of $R$ lies in $D_r$, either all poles lie in the interior of $C$ or
all poles lie in its exterior.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #integral-vanishes-boxed}
For the $r$ chosen in step [](#disk-separates-poles){.pf-ref},
$$
\boxed{\int_C\frac{f(z)}{g(z)}\,dz=0}
$$
for every simple closed curve $C$ disjoint from $\abs{z}\le r$.

::: pf-proof
If the interior of $C$ contains no pole, Cauchy's theorem gives zero.
If it contains every pole, the residue theorem and step [](#residue-sum-zero){.pf-ref} give
$$
\int_C R(z)\,dz
=
2\pi i
\sum_{a\in\mathcal P}\operatorname{Res}_{z=a}R
=0.
$$
Reversing the orientation of $C$ only changes the sign, so the conclusion
is independent of the chosen orientation.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#integral-vanishes-boxed){.pf-ref} establishes the required choice of $r$ and the asserted
vanishing for every admissible curve.
:::
