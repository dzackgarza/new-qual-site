::: {.problem}
Prove that $[0,1]$ cannot be written as a countably infinite disjoint union of closed subintervals of $[0,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose for contradiction that
$$
[0,1]
=
\bigsqcup_{n=1}^\infty F_n,
$$
where every $F_n$ is a nonempty closed subinterval.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #avoidance-lemma}
Let $J$ be a nonempty closed interval covered by pairwise disjoint
closed sets
$$
J=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n
$$
of which at least two are nonempty. For any prescribed index $m$, there is
a nonempty closed subinterval $J'\subseteq J$ such that
$$
J'\cap E_m=\varnothing
$$
and $J'$ still meets at least two of the sets $E_n$.

::: pf-proof
If $E_m=\varnothing$, take $J'=J$.

Suppose $E_m\ne\varnothing$. Choose $k\ne m$ with $E_k\ne\varnothing$
and choose $a\in E_k$. Since $E_m$ is a closed subset of the compact
interval $J$, there is $b\in E_m$ minimizing the distance from $a$ to
$E_m$. The sets are disjoint, so $a\ne b$.

Assume first that $a<b$. By the minimality of $b$,
$$
(a,b)\cap E_m=\varnothing.
$$
Also $b\notin E_k$, and $E_k$ is closed, so some point
$$
c\in(a,b)
$$
sufficiently close to $b$ does not belong to $E_k$. Set
$$
J'\coloneqq[a,c].
$$
Then $J'$ is disjoint from $E_m$. It meets $E_k$ at $a$, while $c$ lies
in some $E_\ell$ because the $E_n$ cover $J$. Since $c\notin E_k$, one
has $\ell\ne k$. Hence $J'$ meets at least two pieces.

If $b<a$, the same argument with left and right reversed produces
$c\in(b,a)$ with $c\notin E_k$ and takes
$$
J'=[c,a].
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nested-sequence-constructed}
There is a nested sequence of nonempty closed intervals
$$
[0,1]=J_0\supseteq J_1\supseteq J_2\supseteq\cdots
$$
such that for every $m\ge1$,
$$
J_m\cap F_m=\varnothing,
$$
and each $J_m$ meets at least two of the original sets $F_n$.

::: pf-proof
Assume $J_{m-1}$ has already been constructed and meets at least two
$F_n$. The sets
$$
E_n\coloneqq F_n\cap J_{m-1}
$$
are pairwise disjoint closed subsets of $J_{m-1}$, they cover
$J_{m-1}$, and at least two are nonempty. Apply step [](#avoidance-lemma){.pf-ref} with the
prescribed index $m$. It produces a nonempty closed subinterval
$$
J_m\subseteq J_{m-1}
$$
which is disjoint from
$$
E_m=F_m\cap J_{m-1}
$$
and still meets at least two $E_n$, hence at least two $F_n$. Starting
from $J_0=[0,1]$ gives the sequence inductively.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cover-cannot-exist}
The assumed disjoint countable cover cannot exist.

::: pf-proof
By the nested interval theorem,
$$
\bigcap_{m=0}^\infty J_m\ne\varnothing.
$$
Choose
$$
x\in\bigcap_{m=0}^\infty J_m.
$$
For every $m\ge1$, one has $x\in J_m$ and
$J_m\cap F_m=\varnothing$, so
$$
x\notin F_m.
$$
Thus $x$ belongs to none of the sets $F_m$, contradicting
$$
[0,1]=\bigcup_{m=1}^\infty F_m.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#cover-cannot-exist){.pf-ref} contradicts the existence of the proposed decomposition.
:::
