::: {.problem}
Let $V$ be a real vector space of dimension $n$, and let
\[
S:V\times V\to\mathbb R
\]
be a nondegenerate bilinear form. Suppose $W\subset V$ is a subspace such that
\[
S|_{W\times W}=0.
\]
Prove that
\[
\dim W\le\frac n2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The linear map
$$
\Phi:V\longrightarrow W^*,
\qquad
\Phi(v)(w)\coloneqq S(w,v),
$$
is surjective.

::: {.proof}
Nondegeneracy of $S$ means that
$$
B:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
B(v)(u)\coloneqq S(u,v),
$$
is injective. Since
$$
\dim V=\dim V^*=n,
$$
the map $B$ is an isomorphism.

The restriction map
$$
\rho:V^*\longrightarrow W^*,
\qquad
\rho(\ell)=\ell|_W,
$$
is surjective: a basis of $W$ can be extended to a basis of $V$, and any
linear functional on $W$ can then be extended arbitrarily on the added
basis vectors. Since
$$
\Phi=\rho\circ B,
$$
the map $\Phi$ is surjective.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
W\subseteq\ker\Phi.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $v\in W$, then for every $w\in W$ the hypothesis
$S|_{W\times W}=0$ gives
$$
\Phi(v)(w)=S(w,v)=0.
$$
Thus $\Phi(v)=0$.
:::

<1>3. The dimension of $W$ satisfies
$$
\boxed{\dim W\le\frac n2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1 and rank--nullity,
$$
\dim\ker\Phi
=
\dim V-\dim W^*
=
n-\dim W.
$$
Step <1>2 gives
$$
\dim W
\le
\dim\ker\Phi
=
n-\dim W.
$$
Hence
$$
2\dim W\le n,
$$
which is the claimed inequality.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the desired bound.
:::
:::
