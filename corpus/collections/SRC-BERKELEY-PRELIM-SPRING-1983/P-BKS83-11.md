::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be an invertible real $n\times n$ matrix. Show that there is a decomposition
\[
M=UT,
\]
where $U$ is orthogonal and $T$ is upper triangular with positive diagonal entries. Is this decomposition unique?
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the columns of $M$ as
$$
v_1,\ldots,v_n.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #gram-schmidt-basis}
Applying Gram--Schmidt to $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ produces an orthonormal
basis $q_1,\ldots,q_n$ such that
$$
v_j\in\operatorname{span}(q_1,\ldots,q_j)
$$
and the coefficient of $q_j$ in $v_j$ is positive.

::: pf-proof
Since $M$ is invertible, its columns are linearly independent. Define
recursively
$$
w_j
\coloneqq
v_j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\inner{v_j}{q_i}q_i,
\qquad
q_j
\coloneqq
\frac{w_j}{\norm{w_j}}.
$$
Linear independence of $v_1,\ldots,v_j$ implies $w_j\ne0$, so this is
well-defined. The Gram--Schmidt construction makes the $q_j$ orthonormal,
and rearranging the definition of $w_j$ gives
$$
v_j
=
\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\inner{v_j}{q_i}q_i
+
\norm{w_j}q_j.
$$
Thus only $q_1,\ldots,q_j$ occur, and the coefficient of $q_j$ is
$\norm{w_j}>0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #decomposition-exists}
There exist an orthogonal matrix $U$ and an upper-triangular matrix
$T$ with positive diagonal such that
$$
M=UT.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $U$ be the matrix whose $j$th column is $q_j$. Since these columns form
an orthonormal basis,
$$
U^{\mathsf T}U=I,
$$
so $U$ is orthogonal.

For each $j$, let the $j$th column of $T$ be the coordinates of $v_j$ in
the basis $q_1,\ldots,q_n$. Step [](#gram-schmidt-basis){.pf-ref} shows that its entries below row $j$
are zero and that
$$
t_{jj}=\norm{w_j}>0.
$$
Hence $T$ is upper triangular with positive diagonal. Column by column,
$UT$ has columns $v_1,\ldots,v_n$, so $UT=M$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #upper-triangular-orthogonal-is-diagonal}
Every upper-triangular orthogonal real matrix is diagonal, and each
diagonal entry is $\pm1$.

::: pf-proof
Let $Q=(q_{ij})$ be upper triangular and orthogonal. Its first column has
only one possibly nonzero entry, $q_{11}$. Since that column has norm $1$,
$$
q_{11}=\pm1.
$$
Orthogonality of the first column with every later column then gives
$$
q_{1j}=0
\qquad
(j>1).
$$
Deleting the first row and first column leaves an upper-triangular
orthogonal $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ matrix. Repeating the same argument proves
inductively that all off-diagonal entries of $Q$ vanish and every diagonal
entry is $\pm1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #decomposition-unique}
The decomposition in step [](#decomposition-exists){.pf-ref} is unique.

::: pf-proof
Suppose
$$
M=UT=U'T',
$$
where $U,U'$ are orthogonal and $T,T'$ are upper triangular with positive
diagonal entries. Then
$$
Q\coloneqq U'^{\mathsf T}U=T'T^{-1}.
$$
The left-hand expression shows that $Q$ is orthogonal. The right-hand
expression shows that $Q$ is upper triangular, because the inverse of an
invertible upper-triangular matrix is upper triangular. Moreover, its
diagonal entries are
$$
q_{ii}=\frac{t'_{ii}}{t_{ii}}>0.
$$
By step [](#upper-triangular-orthogonal-is-diagonal){.pf-ref}, every diagonal entry of $Q$ is $\pm1$ and all off-diagonal
entries vanish. Positivity therefore forces $Q=I$. Hence $U=U'$ and then
$T=T'$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#decomposition-exists){.pf-ref} proves existence, and step [](#decomposition-unique){.pf-ref} proves uniqueness.
:::
