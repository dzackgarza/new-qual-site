::: {.problem}
Evaluate
\[
\int_0^\infty\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The improper integral
$$
I\coloneqq
\int_0^\infty\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2\,dx
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
As $x\to0$,
$$
\frac{\sin x}{x}\longrightarrow1,
$$
so the integrand is bounded near $0$. For $x\ge1$,
$$
0\le
\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2
\le
\frac1{x^2},
$$
and $\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\,dx$ converges.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
I=
\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin(2x)}x\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $0<\varepsilon<R$, integration by parts gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{\sin^2x}{x^2}\,dx
&=\left[-\frac{\sin^2x}{x}\right]_\varepsilon^R
+\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{\sin(2x)}x\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary term tends to $0$ as $R\to\infty$ and
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$, because
$$
\frac{\sin^2R}{R}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{\sin^2\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Step <1>1 therefore gives the claimed identity.
:::

<1>3. For $t>0$ and $b\ge0$, define
$$
J_t(b)\coloneqq
\int_0^\infty e^{-tx}\frac{\sin(bx)}x\,dx.
$$
Then
$$
J_t(b)=\arctan\!\left(\frac bt\right).
$$

::: {.proof}
For fixed $t>0$, differentiation under the integral sign is justified on
every bounded $b$-interval by the integrable majorant $e^{-tx}$. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
J_t'(b)
&=
\int_0^\infty e^{-tx}\cos(bx)\,dx\\
&=
\operatorname{Re}
\int_0^\infty e^{-(t-ib)x}\,dx\\
&=
\operatorname{Re}\frac1{t-ib}
=
\frac{t}{t^2+b^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $J_t(0)=0$,
$$
J_t(b)
=
\int_0^b\frac{t}{t^2+s^2}\,ds
=
\arctan\!\left(\frac bt\right).
$$
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\lim_{t\downarrow0}J_t(2)
=
\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin(2x)}x\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $t\ge0$, set
$$
h_t(x)\coloneqq\frac{e^{-tx}}x.
$$
This function is positive and decreasing. For $S>R>0$, integration by
parts gives
$$
\int_R^S h_t(x)\sin(2x)\,dx
=\left[-\frac12h_t(x)\cos(2x)\right]_R^S
+\frac12\int_R^S h_t'(x)\cos(2x)\,dx.
$$
Since $h_t'\le0$,
$$
\left|
\int_R^S h_t(x)\sin(2x)\,dx
\right|
\le
\frac{h_t(R)+h_t(S)}2+\frac{h_t(R)-h_t(S)}2
=
h_t(R)
\le
\frac1R.
$$
Letting $S\to\infty$ yields the same $1/R$ bound for the tail, uniformly
for $t\ge0$.

On the finite interval $[0,R]$,
$$
e^{-tx}\frac{\sin(2x)}x
\longrightarrow
\frac{\sin(2x)}x
$$
pointwise as $t\downarrow0$, and its absolute value is at most $2$.
Dominated convergence therefore applies on $[0,R]$. Combining this with
the uniform tail bound and then letting $R\to\infty$ proves the claim.
:::

<1>5. The value of the integral is
$$
\boxed{\frac\pi2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
J_t(2)
=
\arctan\!\left(\frac2t\right)
\longrightarrow
\frac\pi2
\qquad
(t\downarrow0).
$$
Step <1>4 identifies this limit with the Dirichlet integral in step <1>2,
and step <1>2 identifies that integral with $I$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the requested evaluation.
:::
