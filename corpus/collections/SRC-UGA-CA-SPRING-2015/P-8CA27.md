::: {.problem}
Suppose $f$ is entire and maps a circle $|z-a|=r$ into $\mathbb{R}$.
Prove that $f$ is constant.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
\[
g(z)=\overline{f\!\left(a+\frac{r^2}{\overline{z-a}}\right)}
\]
for $z\ne a$. Because $f$ is entire, $g$ is holomorphic on
$\mathbb C\setminus\{a\}$. If $|z-a|=r$, then
\[
a+\frac{r^2}{\overline{z-a}}=z,
\]
and since $f(z)\in\mathbb R$ on the circle,
\[
g(z)=\overline{f(z)}=f(z).
\]
Thus the holomorphic functions $f$ and $g$ agree on the circle, hence by the
identity theorem they agree on the connected punctured plane.

Therefore, for every $z\ne a$,
\[
f(z)=\overline{f\!\left(a+\frac{r^2}{\overline{z-a}}\right)}.
\]
As $z\to\infty$, the point inside $f$ on the right tends to $a$, so the
right-hand side tends to $\overline{f(a)}$. Hence $f(z)$ has a finite limit at
infinity. An entire function with a finite limit at infinity is bounded, so by
Liouville's theorem $f$ is constant.
:::
