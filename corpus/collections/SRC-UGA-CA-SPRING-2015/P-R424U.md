::: {.problem}
Let $\displaystyle{\psi_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{\alpha-z}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}}$ with $\abs{\alpha}<1$ and ${\DD}=\{z:\ \abs{z}<1\}$.
Prove that

- $\displaystyle{\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{{\DD}} \abs{\psi'_{\alpha}}^2 dx dy =1}$.

- $\displaystyle{\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{{\DD}} \abs{\psi'_{\alpha}} dx dy =\frac{1-\abs{\alpha}^2}{\abs{\alpha}^2} \log \frac{1}{1-\abs{\alpha}^2}}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $\alpha$ for the parameter and $dA=dx\,dy$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #deriv}
$\displaystyle\psi_\alpha'(z)=-\frac{1-\abs{\alpha}^2}{(1-\bar\alpha z)^2}$, and $\psi_\alpha$ is a bijection of $\DD$ onto itself.

::: pf-proof
The quotient rule gives the derivative. For $\abs{z}=1$, $\abs{1-\bar\alpha z}=\abs{\bar z-\bar\alpha}=\abs{z-\alpha}$, so $\abs{\psi_\alpha}=1$ on the unit circle; $\psi_\alpha$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline\DD$ since its pole $1/\bar\alpha$ lies outside it, so by the maximum principle $\psi_\alpha(\DD)\subseteq\DD$. A direct computation gives $\psi_\alpha\circ\psi_\alpha=\id$, so $\psi_\alpha$ is a bijection of $\DD$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\displaystyle\frac1\pi\iint_\DD\abs{\psi_\alpha'}^2\,dA=1$.

::: pf-proof
By the Cauchy--Riemann equations, the real Jacobian determinant of a holomorphic map $\psi$ is $\abs{\psi'}^2$. By step [](#deriv){.pf-ref}, $\psi_\alpha$ is a diffeomorphism of $\DD$ onto itself, so the change of variables formula gives $\iint_\DD\abs{\psi_\alpha'}^2\,dA=\operatorname{Area}(\DD)=\pi$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #circle}
For $0\le c<1$, $\displaystyle\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{\abs{1-ce^{i\theta}}^2}=\frac1{1-c^2}$.

::: pf-proof
$(1-ce^{i\theta})^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}c^ke^{ik\theta}$ converges uniformly in $\theta$. Expanding $\abs{\cdot}^2$ as a double series and integrating term by term, the orthogonality of $e^{ik\theta}$ leaves $\sum_{k\ge0}c^{2k}=(1-c^2)^{-1}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
For $\alpha\neq0$, $\displaystyle\frac1\pi\iint_\DD\abs{\psi_\alpha'}\,dA=\frac{1-\abs{\alpha}^2}{\abs{\alpha}^2}\log\frac1{1-\abs{\alpha}^2}$.

::: pf-proof
Put $s=\abs{\alpha}$. By step [](#deriv){.pf-ref}, $\abs{\psi_\alpha'(z)}=(1-s^2)/\abs{1-\bar\alpha z}^2$. In polar coordinates $z=\rho e^{i\theta}$, $\abs{1-\bar\alpha z}=\abs{1-s\rho e^{i(\theta-\arg\alpha)}}$, and by step [](#circle){.pf-ref} with $c=s\rho$ and periodicity in $\theta$,
$$
\frac1\pi\iint_\DD\abs{\psi_\alpha'}\,dA
=\frac{1-s^2}{\pi}\int_0^1\frac{2\pi\rho}{1-s^2\rho^2}\,d\rho
=(1-s^2)\Bigl[-\frac{1}{s^2}\log(1-s^2\rho^2)\Bigr]_0^1
=\frac{1-s^2}{s^2}\log\frac1{1-s^2}.
$$
:::

::: remark
The exam asks only for the second identity, with the hint that polar coordinates may come in handy. At $\alpha=0$ the right-hand side is read as its limit $1$.
:::
