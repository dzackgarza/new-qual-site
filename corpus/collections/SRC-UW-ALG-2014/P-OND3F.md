::: {.problem}
Let $\overline{\FF_p}$ denote the algebraic closure of $\FF_p$.
Show that the Galois group $\Gal(\overline{\FF_p}/\FF_p)$ has no nontrivial finite subgroups.
:::

::: {.solution}
Throughout $p$ is prime, $\overline{\FF}_{p}$ is an algebraic closure of $\FF_{p}$, $G \coloneqq \Gal(\overline{\FF}_{p}/\FF_{p})$, $\operatorname{Frob}_{p}(x) = x^{p}$ is the Frobenius automorphism, $\widehat{\ZZ} \coloneqq \varprojlim_{n}\ZZ/n\ZZ$ is the profinite completion, and $\ZZ_{q} \coloneqq \varprojlim_{k}\ZZ/q^{k}\ZZ$ is the ring of $q$-adic integers. The problem is Problem 6 of the 2014 Algebra Preliminary Examination (University of Washington, September 8, 2014), printed p. 2 of the two-page paper retained at `assets/attachments/UW-Algebra-Qual-2014.pdf`; no solutions sheet accompanies it, so the argument below is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #finite-fields}
For every $n \ge 1$ there is a subfield $\FF_{p^{n}} \subseteq \overline{\FF}_{p}$ with $p^{n}$ elements, unique as a subfield of that order, and $\overline{\FF}_{p}$ is the union of these; moreover $\Gal(\FF_{p^{n}}/\FF_{p}) = \generators{\operatorname{Frob}_{p}}$ is cyclic of order $n$.

::: pf-proof
In $\overline{\FF}_{p}$ the polynomial $x^{p^{n}} - x$ is separable because $(x^{p^{n}}-x)' = -1 \ne 0$ in characteristic $p$, so it has $p^{n}$ distinct roots, and they form a subfield since the roots are closed under addition and multiplication in characteristic $p$; that subfield is $\FF_{p^{n}}$ and it is the unique subfield with $p^{n}$ elements. Every element of $\overline{\FF}_{p}$ is algebraic over $\FF_{p}$, hence lies in some finite extension, which is one of the $\FF_{p^{n}}$; so the union is everything. For the Galois group: $\operatorname{Frob}_{p}$ fixes $\FF_{p}$ and its $n$-fold iterate is $x \mapsto x^{p^{n}}$, the identity on $\FF_{p^{n}}$, while no smaller positive iterate is; these powers give $n$ distinct automorphisms, and the group has at most $n = [\FF_{p^{n}}:\FF_{p}]$ elements, so it is cyclic of order $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #profinite}
$G \cong \varprojlim_{n}\ZZ/n\ZZ = \widehat{\ZZ} \cong \prod_{q\text{ prime}}\ZZ_{q}$.

::: pf-proof
The extensions $\FF_{p^{n}}/\FF_{p}$ form a directed system under inclusion, with $\FF_{p^{m}} \subseteq \FF_{p^{n}}$ exactly when $m \mid n$, and the fundamental theorem of infinite Galois theory identifies $G$ with the inverse limit of the finite Galois groups over this system. By step [](#finite-fields){.pf-ref} that limit is $\varprojlim_{n}\ZZ/n\ZZ$ over the divisibility poset, which is $\widehat{\ZZ}$. The Chinese remainder theorem, applied to the compatible system of isomorphisms $\ZZ/n \cong \prod_{q^{k}\mid n}\ZZ/q^{k}$, gives $\widehat{\ZZ} \cong \prod_{q}\varprojlim_{k}\ZZ/q^{k}\ZZ = \prod_{q}\ZZ_{q}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #torsion-free}
$\widehat{\ZZ}$ is torsion-free as an abelian group.

::: pf-proof
Under the isomorphism of step [](#profinite){.pf-ref} an element is a tuple $(\sigma_{q})_{q}$, and $m\sigma = 0$ exactly when $m\sigma_{q} = 0$ in $\ZZ_{q}$ for every $q$. The additive group of $\ZZ_{q}$ is torsion-free: it is the inverse limit of the groups $\ZZ/q^{k}\ZZ$, it has characteristic $0$ (the map $\ZZ \to \ZZ_{q}$ is injective because $q^{k} \nmid m$ for $k$ large when $m \ne 0$), and a ring of characteristic $0$ that is an integral domain has torsion-free additive group, since $mx = 0$ with $m \ne 0$ gives $m \cdot x = 0$ in a domain, forcing $x = 0$. Hence $m\sigma_{q} = 0$ for all $q$ gives $\sigma_{q} = 0$ for all $q$, that is $\sigma = 0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
$G$ has no nontrivial finite subgroup.

::: pf-proof
Let $H \le G$ be finite of order $k$. Every $h \in H$ satisfies $h^{k} = 1$ by Lagrange's theorem, that is $k \cdot h = 0$ in the abelian group $G$; step [](#torsion-free){.pf-ref}, transported through the isomorphism of step [](#profinite){.pf-ref}, gives $h = 0 = \mathrm{id}$. So $H$ is trivial. Equivalently, an algebraically closed field is a finite extension of a proper subfield only in degree $2$ over a real closed field of characteristic $0$ (Artin--Schreier), which is excluded here because $\operatorname{char}\overline{\FF}_{p} = p > 0$; that is a second route to the same conclusion and is recorded rather than used.

:::
