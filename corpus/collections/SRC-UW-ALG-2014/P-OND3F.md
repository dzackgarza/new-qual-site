::: problem
Let $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ denote an algebraic closure of the finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$.
Show that the absolute Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ has **no non-trivial finite subgroups** (i.e. is torsion-free).
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that the profinite group $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is torsion-free, so it has no non-trivial finite subgroups.

<1>1. Structure of the Galois Group $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p)$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The finite subfields of $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ are the unique fields $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ of size $p^n$ for each integer $n \ge 1$.
    <2>2. The extension $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p$ is the direct limit (union) of the finite Galois extensions $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$:
        $$\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{F}_{p^n}.$$
    <2>3. For each $n \ge 1$, the finite Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the Frobenius automorphism $\operatorname{Frob}_p(x) = x^p$.
    <2>4. By the **Fundamental Theorem of Infinite Galois Theory**, the absolute Galois group is the **inverse limit (profinite completion)**:
        $$G = \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \varprojlim_{n} \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \varprojlim_{n} \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} = \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}.$$
    <2>5. By the **Chinese Remainder Theorem** for profinite rings:
        $$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \prod_{q \text{ prime}} \mathbb{Z}_q$$
        where $\mathbb{Z}_q$ is the ring of **$q$-adic integers** (under addition, $\mathbb{Z}_q \cong \varprojlim_k \mathbb{Z}/q^k\mathbb{Z}$).

<1>2. Torsion-Freeness of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\sigma = (\sigma_q)_{q} \in \prod_q \mathbb{Z}_q \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ be an element of finite order $m \ge 1$.
    <2>2. Then $m \cdot \sigma = 0 \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, which means for every prime $q$:
        $$m \cdot \sigma_q = 0 \in \mathbb{Z}_q.$$
    <2>3. The additive group of the $q$-adic integers $(\mathbb{Z}_q, +)$ is an **integral domain** (char 0), so it is a **torsion-free abelian group**:
        $$\text{If } m \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0} \text{ and } m \cdot x = 0 \text{ in } \mathbb{Z}_q, \text{ then } x = 0.$$
    <2>4. Therefore, $\sigma_q = 0$ for every prime $q$.
    <2>5. This implies $\sigma = 0 = \operatorname{id}$.
    <2>6. Thus the only element of finite order in $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is the identity $\operatorname{id}$.

<1>3. Absence of Non-Trivial Finite Subgroups:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $H \le G$ be a finite subgroup of order $|H| = k \ge 1$.
    <2>2. By Lagrange's Theorem, every element $h \in H$ satisfies $h^k = 1$.
    <2>3. By Step 2, since $G$ is torsion-free, the only element satisfying $h^k = 1$ is $h = 1$.
    <2>4. Thus $H = \{1\}$.

<1>4. Alternative Direct Field-Theoretic Proof:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose $H \le \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is a finite subgroup with $|H| = k > 1$.
    <2>2. Let $K = (\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)^H$ be the fixed field of $H$.
    <2>3. By **Artin's Theorem on Galois extensions**, $[\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p : K] = |H| = k < \infty$.
    <2>4. Thus $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ would be a finite-degree extension of the field $K$.
    <2>5. Since $K$ is of characteristic $p$, $K$ cannot have an algebraically closed finite extension of degree $> 1$ other than the Artin-Schreier theorem case where the closure is algebraically closed (which requires char 0 and degree 2, $\mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$).
    <2>6. Specifically, by the **Artin-Schreier Theorem on formally real fields**, if an algebraically closed field is a finite non-trivial extension of a subfield $K$, then the characteristic must be 0 and the extension degree must be 2.
    <2>7. Since $\operatorname{char}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p) = p > 0$, this is impossible.
    <2>8. Thus no such finite subgroup $H$ can exist.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \prod_q \mathbb{Z}_q$ is torsion-free, hence has no non-trivial finite subgroups. Q.E.D.
:::
