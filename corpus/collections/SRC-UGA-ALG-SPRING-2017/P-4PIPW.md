::: {.problem}
a. How many isomorphism classes of abelian groups of order 56 are there?
Give a representative for one of each class.

b. Prove that if $G$ is a group of order 56, then either the Sylow-2 subgroup or the Sylow-7 subgroup is normal.

c. Give two non-isomorphic groups of order 56 where the Sylow-7 subgroup is normal and the Sylow-2 subgroup is *not* normal.
Justify that these two groups are not isomorphic.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

Write $C_m$ for the cyclic group of order $m$, $\operatorname{Aut}(C_7)$ for the automorphism group of $C_7$, and $\iota$ for the inversion automorphism $x\mapsto x^{-1}$ of $C_7$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #abelian}
An abelian group of order $56=2^3\cdot7$ is the direct product of its Sylow $7$-subgroup $C_7$ and its Sylow $2$-subgroup, and there are three abelian groups of order $8$.

::: pf-proof
In an abelian group the Sylow subgroups are normal, and Cauchy's theorem gives a subgroup of order $7$ and one of order $8$; both are unique (a normal Sylow subgroup is the only one of its order) and their orders are coprime, so the group is their direct product. The abelian groups of order $8=2^3$ correspond to the partitions of $3$, namely $3=3$, $3=2+1$ and $3=1+1+1$, giving $C_8$, $C_4\times C_2$ and $C_2^3$; the fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups gives no others.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #parta}
There are three isomorphism classes of abelian groups of order $56$, represented by
$$
C_{56},\qquad C_{28}\times C_2,\qquad C_{14}\times C_2\times C_2 .
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#abelian){.pf-ref} each class is $C_7\times P$ with $P$ one of the three groups of order $8$. Since $\gcd(7,8)=1$, $C_7\times C_8\cong C_{56}$, $C_7\times C_4\times C_2\cong C_{28}\times C_2$ and $C_7\times C_2^3\cong C_{14}\times C_2\times C_2$, using that $C_7\times C_4\cong C_{28}$ and $C_7\times C_2\cong C_{14}$ for coprime orders. Distinct classes stay distinct because $C_7\times-$ is an equivalence of categories of groups of coprime order, or directly because the three representatives have different numbers of elements of order $2$ ($1$, $3$ and $7$).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #n7}
For a group $G$ of order $56$, the number $n_7$ of Sylow $7$-subgroups is $1$ or $8$.

::: pf-proof
Sylow's theorem gives $n_7\equiv1\pmod 7$ and $n_7\mid8$. The divisors of $8$ are $1,2,4,8$, and only $1$ and $8$ are $\equiv1\pmod 7$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eight}
If $n_7=8$ then $G$ has exactly one Sylow $2$-subgroup, namely the set of its $8$ elements whose order is not $7$.

::: pf-proof
Two distinct Sylow $7$-subgroups have intersection of order dividing $7$ and not $7$, hence trivial; each has $6$ nonidentity elements, and distinct subgroups contribute disjoint sets of nonidentity elements, so the $8$ Sylow $7$-subgroups contribute $8\cdot6=48$ distinct elements of order $7$. The remaining $56-48=8$ elements include the identity and have order different from $7$. A Sylow $2$-subgroup $P$ has $8$ elements, all of $2$-power order, hence none of them of order $7$; so $P$ is contained in that set of $8$ elements, and $8=\cardinality P=\cardinality{\text{that set}}$ forces equality. Every Sylow $2$-subgroup of $G$ is such a $P$, so there is exactly one.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #partb}
Every group of order $56$ has a normal Sylow $2$-subgroup or a normal Sylow $7$-subgroup.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#n7){.pf-ref} either $n_7=1$, in which case the Sylow $7$-subgroup is the only one of its order and hence normal ($gPg^{-1}$ is again a Sylow subgroup), or $n_7=8$, in which case step [](#eight){.pf-ref} gives a unique Sylow $2$-subgroup, which is then normal. This is part (b).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #automorphisms}
$\operatorname{Aut}(C_7)\cong C_6$ has a unique subgroup of order $2$, generated by $\iota$; hence $\iota$ generates the image of any homomorphism from a group of $2$-power order into $\operatorname{Aut}(C_7)$.

::: pf-proof
$C_7$ has $\varphi(7)=6$ generators, and an automorphism is determined by the image of a generator, so $\operatorname{Aut}(C_7)$ has order $6$; it is cyclic, being the multiplicative group of the field of seven elements, so it is $\cong C_6$. A cyclic group has exactly one subgroup of each order dividing its order, so exactly one of order $2$; that subgroup consists of the elements of order $\le2$, and $\iota\ne\operatorname{id}$ has order $2$. The image of a homomorphism from a group of order $2^a$ has order dividing $2^a$ and $\cardinality{\operatorname{Aut}(C_7)}=6$, hence order dividing $2$, so it is trivial or $\langle\iota\rangle$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #define}
$G_1=C_7\rtimes_{\alpha}C_8$ with $\alpha$ sending a generator to $\iota$, and $G_2=C_7\rtimes_{\beta}(C_4\times C_2)$ with $\beta$ sending the generator of $C_4$ to $\iota$ and the generator of $C_2$ to $\operatorname{id}$, are groups of order $56$ in which $C_7$ is normal.

::: pf-proof
$\alpha$ is a homomorphism $C_8\to\operatorname{Aut}(C_7)$ because $\iota^2=\operatorname{id}$ makes $\alpha(k)=\iota^k$ well defined; $\beta$ is a homomorphism $C_4\times C_2\to\operatorname{Aut}(C_7)$ by the same reason and step [](#automorphisms){.pf-ref}. A semidirect product $N\rtimes H$ has order $\cardinality N\cardinality H=7\cdot8=56$ and contains $N$ as a normal subgroup by construction.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #notnormal}
In both $G_1$ and $G_2$ the Sylow $2$-subgroup is not normal.

::: pf-proof
Let $G=N\rtimes H$ be either of the two groups of step [](#define){.pf-ref}, with $N=C_7$, $H$ the complement of order $8$; then $H$ is a Sylow $2$-subgroup, since $\cardinality H=8$ and $8\parallel56$, and $N\cap H=1$ with $NH=G$. If $H$ were normal, then for $n\in N$, $h\in H$ the commutator $[n,h]=n(h n^{-1}h^{-1})$ would lie in $N$ (as $N$ is normal) and in $H$ (as $H$ is normal), hence in $N\cap H=1$; so $hnh^{-1}=n$ for all $h,n$, the action would be trivial, and so $\alpha$ (respectively $\beta$) would be trivial, contradicting the choice of $\iota\ne\operatorname{id}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #orders}
$G_1$ has an element of order $8$, and $G_2$ has none.

::: pf-proof
The complement $C_8$ of $G_1$ has a generator of order $8$. In $G_2$, an element of order $8$ would generate a Sylow $2$-subgroup, which is a conjugate of the complement $C_4\times C_2$ and hence isomorphic to it; but $C_4\times C_2$ has exponent $4$ (every element satisfies $x^4=1$), so no element of order $8$ lies in it. Equivalently: if $g\in G_2$ has $2$-power order then $\langle g\rangle$ is a $2$-subgroup, hence lies in some Sylow $2$-subgroup, i.e. in a conjugate of $C_4\times C_2$, and all of those have exponent $4$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #partc}
$G_1$ and $G_2$ are non-isomorphic groups of order $56$ with normal Sylow $7$-subgroup and non-normal Sylow $2$-subgroup.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#define){.pf-ref}, [](#notnormal){.pf-ref} and [](#orders){.pf-ref} give the stated properties; an isomorphism preserves the set of orders of elements, so the element of order $8$ in $G_1$ and the absence of one in $G_2$ (step [](#orders){.pf-ref}) rule out an isomorphism. This is part (c).
:::
