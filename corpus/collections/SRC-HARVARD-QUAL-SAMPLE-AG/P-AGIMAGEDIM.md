::: {.problem}
What can you say about the dimension of the image of a map from $\PP^n$ to $\PP^m$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
f:\mathbb P^n_k\longrightarrow\mathbb P^m_k
\]
be a morphism.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #pullback-o1}
There is an integer $d\ge0$ such that
\[
f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^m}(1)
\cong
\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(d).
\]

::: pf-proof
The Picard group of projective space is
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^n)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
generated by $\mathcal O(1)$.  Thus the pullback of $\mathcal O(1)$ is $\mathcal O(d)$ for some integer $d$.

Because $f^*\mathcal O(1)$ is globally generated, $d$ cannot be negative.  Hence $d\ge0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #zero-degree-constant}
If $d=0$, then $f$ is constant.

::: pf-proof
A morphism to projective space is given by $m+1$ global sections of the pullback line bundle with no common zero.  If
\[
f^*\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n},
\]
then these sections are global functions on $\mathbb P^n$, hence constants.  Therefore all projective coordinates of $f$ are constant and $f$ is constant.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #fibers-zero-dimensional}
If $f$ is nonconstant, then $d>0$, and every fibre of $f$ is zero-dimensional.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#zero-degree-constant){.pf-ref}, nonconstancy implies $d>0$.  The line bundle
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(d)
\]
is ample.

Suppose a fibre
\[
F=f^{-1}(y)
\]
had a positive-dimensional component.  On that fibre,
\[
f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^m}(1)|_F
\]
is the pullback of a one-dimensional vector space from the point $y$, hence is the trivial line bundle.  But by step [](#pullback-o1){.pf-ref} this same restriction is
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(d)|_F,
\]
the restriction of an ample line bundle, and is therefore ample on $F$.  The trivial line bundle on a positive-dimensional proper scheme is not ample.  This is a contradiction.

Thus every fibre is zero-dimensional.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #finite-onto-image}
A nonconstant $f$ is finite onto its image.

::: pf-proof
The source $\mathbb P^n$ is projective, so $f$ is proper.  Step [](#fibers-zero-dimensional){.pf-ref} shows that $f$ is quasi-finite.  A proper quasi-finite morphism is finite.

Because a proper morphism has closed image, we may regard this as a finite surjective morphism
\[
\mathbb P^n\longrightarrow f(\mathbb P^n).
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #image-dimension}
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\dim f(\mathbb P^n)
=
\begin{cases}
0,&f\text{ constant},\\
n,&f\text{ nonconstant}.
\end{cases}
}
\]

::: pf-proof
The constant case is immediate.  In the nonconstant case, step [](#finite-onto-image){.pf-ref} gives a finite surjective morphism from $\mathbb P^n$ to its image.  Finite surjective morphisms preserve Krull dimension, so
\[
\dim f(\mathbb P^n)=\dim\mathbb P^n=n.
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pn-to-pm-constant}
In particular, if $n>m$, every morphism
\[
\mathbb P^n\longrightarrow\mathbb P^m
\]
is constant.

::: pf-proof
A nonconstant map would have an $n$-dimensional image by step [](#image-dimension){.pf-ref}, but every closed subvariety of $\mathbb P^m$ has dimension at most $m<n$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#image-dimension){.pf-ref} is the requested image-dimension statement, with step [](#pn-to-pm-constant){.pf-ref} as its immediate consequence.
:::
