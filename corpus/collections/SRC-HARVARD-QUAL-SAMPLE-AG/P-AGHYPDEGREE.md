::: {.problem}
Show that a hypersurface defined by an equation of degree $d$ has degree $d$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
X=V(F)\subseteq\mathbb P^n_k
\]
be a hypersurface cut out by a nonzero homogeneous polynomial $F$ of degree $d$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #hypersurface-exact-sequence}
There is an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(-d)
\xrightarrow{\cdot F}
\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X
\longrightarrow0.
\]

::: pf-proof
The ideal sheaf of the hypersurface $X$ is the principal invertible ideal generated by $F$, hence isomorphic to
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(-d).
\]
Quotienting the structure sheaf by this ideal gives $\mathcal O_X$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hilbert-polynomial-hypersurface}
The Hilbert polynomial of $X$ is
\[
P_X(m)
=\binom{m+n}{n}
-\binom{m-d+n}{n}
\]
for $m\gg0$.

::: pf-proof
Twist the exact sequence in step [](#hypersurface-exact-sequence){.pf-ref} by $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(m)$ and take Euler characteristics:
\[
\chi(\mathcal O_X(m))
=\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(m))
-\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(m-d)).
\]
For $m\gg0$,
\[
\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(m))
=h^0(\mathbb P^n,\mathcal O(m))
=\binom{m+n}{n},
\]
and similarly for $m-d$.  This gives the displayed polynomial.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #leading-term}
The leading term of $P_X(m)$ is
\[
\frac{d}{(n-1)!}m^{n-1}.
\]

::: pf-proof
The polynomial
\[
\binom{m+n}{n}
\]
has leading part
\[
\frac1{n!}m^n+\frac{n+1}{2(n-1)!}m^{n-1}+\cdots.
\]
Replacing $m$ by $m-d$ changes the degree-$n$ term by
\[
\frac1{n!}\left(m^n-(m-d)^n\right)
=\frac{d}{(n-1)!}m^{n-1}+O(m^{n-2}).
\]
The remaining degree-$(n-1)$ coefficients cancel between the two binomial polynomials.  Hence the difference in step [](#hilbert-polynomial-hypersurface){.pf-ref} has the stated leading term.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #degree-equals-d}
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\deg X=d.}
\]

::: pf-proof
A projective scheme of dimension $r$ has Hilbert polynomial
\[
P_X(m)
=\frac{\deg X}{r!}m^r+O(m^{r-1}).
\]
Here $X$ is a hypersurface in $\mathbb P^n$, so
\[
r=n-1.
\]
Comparing this definition with the leading coefficient from step [](#leading-term){.pf-ref} gives
\[
\frac{\deg X}{(n-1)!}
=\frac d{(n-1)!},
\]
hence $\deg X=d$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#degree-equals-d){.pf-ref} is the desired equality.
:::
