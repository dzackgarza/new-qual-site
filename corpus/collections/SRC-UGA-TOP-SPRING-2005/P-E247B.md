::: problem
Do one of the following:

a. Give (with justification) a contractible subset $X \subset \RR^2$ which is not a retract of $\RR^2$ .

b. Give (with justification) two topological spaces that have the same homology groups but that are not homotopy equivalent.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For alternative (a), let
\[
A=(0,1)\times\{0\}\subset\RR^2.
\]
Then $A$ is contractible.
::: {.proof}
The homotopy
\[
H:A\times I\longrightarrow A,
\qquad
H((x,0),t)=\bigl((1-t)x+t/2,0\bigr)
\]
is well-defined because $(0,1)$ is convex.
At $t=0$ it is the identity, and at $t=1$ it is the constant map with value $(1/2,0)$.
Thus $A$ is contractible.
:::

<1>2. The subset $A$ is not a retract of $\RR^2$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose there were a retraction
\[
r:\RR^2\longrightarrow A.
\]
Let $i:A\hookrightarrow\RR^2$ denote inclusion.
Since $r|_A=\id_A$,
\[
A
=
\{z\in\RR^2:(i\circ r)(z)=z\}.
\]

The plane is Hausdorff, so its diagonal
\[
\Delta=\{(z,z):z\in\RR^2\}
\]
is closed in $\RR^2\times\RR^2$.
The map
\[
z\longmapsto\bigl(z,(i\circ r)(z)\bigr)
\]
is continuous, hence its inverse image of $\Delta$, which is exactly $A$, would be closed in $\RR^2$.
But $A$ is not closed: for example $(0,0)\notin A$ lies in its closure.
This contradiction proves that no retraction exists.
:::

<1>3. Thus alternative (a) is answered by
\[
\boxed{A=(0,1)\times\{0\}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
This combines <1>1 and <1>2.
:::

<1>4. For alternative (b), take
\[
X=T^2,
\qquad
Y=S^1\vee S^1\vee S^2.
\]
Then $X$ and $Y$ have isomorphic integral homology groups in every degree.
::: {.proof}
The integral homology of the torus is
\[
H_k(T^2;\ZZ)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
\ZZ^2,&k=1,\\
\ZZ,&k=2,\\
0,&k\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]

For a finite wedge of connected based CW complexes, reduced homology is the direct sum of the reduced homology groups of the wedge summands.
Therefore
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^1\vee S^1\vee S^2;\ZZ)
\cong
\widetilde H_k(S^1;\ZZ)
\oplus
\widetilde H_k(S^1;\ZZ)
\oplus
\widetilde H_k(S^2;\ZZ).
\]
Hence
\[
H_k(Y;\ZZ)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
\ZZ^2,&k=1,\\
\ZZ,&k=2,\\
0,&k\ge3,
\end{cases}
\]
the same groups as for $T^2$.
:::

<1>5. The spaces $X$ and $Y$ are not homotopy equivalent.
::: {.proof}
Their fundamental groups are
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\pi_1(T^2)
\cong
\ZZ^2
\]
and, by van Kampen,
\[
\pi_1(Y)
\cong
\pi_1(S^1)*\pi_1(S^1)*\pi_1(S^2)
\cong
\ZZ*\ZZ
\cong
F_2.
\]
The group $\ZZ^2$ is abelian, while the free group $F_2$ is nonabelian, so they are not isomorphic.
A homotopy equivalence induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups.
Consequently $X$ and $Y$ cannot be homotopy equivalent.
:::

<1>6. Thus alternative (b) is answered by
\[
\boxed{T^2\quad\text{and}\quad S^1\vee S^1\vee S^2.}
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>4 they have the same homology groups, and by <1>5 they are not homotopy equivalent.
:::
