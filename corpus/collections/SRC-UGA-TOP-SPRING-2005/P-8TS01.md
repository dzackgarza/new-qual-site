::: problem
Let $X$ be the topological space formed by identifying the edges of a pentagon, as follows: all five edges are identified according to a common orientation (the boundary word $a^5$).

Calculate $\pi_1(X)$ and give the universal covering space of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The quotient $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, one oriented $1$-cell $a$, and one $2$-cell attached by the degree-$5$ map
\[
S^1\longrightarrow S^1,
\qquad
z\longmapsto z^5.
\]
::: {.proof}
All five vertices of the pentagon are identified, and all five boundary edges are identified with the same orientation.
Thus the quotient of the boundary is one circle $a$, while traversing the boundary of the pentagon once reads the word
\[
a^5.
\]
The interior of the pentagon is the single open $2$-cell.
:::

<1>2. The fundamental group is
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ/5\ZZ}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The CW presentation from <1>1 gives
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a\mid a^5=1\rangle
\cong
\ZZ/5\ZZ.
\]
:::

<1>3. Define $\widetilde X$ to be the space obtained from five closed disks
\[
D_0,D_1,D_2,D_3,D_4
\]
by identifying all five boundary circles to one common circle $C$.
Give $C$ five vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_4$ in cyclic order and five oriented edges
\[
e_j:v_j\longrightarrow v_{j+1}
\qquad(j\bmod 5).
\]
Then there is a connected $5$-sheeted covering
\[
p:\widetilde X\longrightarrow X
\]
that sends every $v_j$ to the unique $0$-cell of $X$, every $e_j$ homeomorphically and orientation-preservingly to $a$, and every open disk $\mathring D_j$ homeomorphically to the open $2$-cell of $X$.
::: {.proof}
On the common boundary circle, the five edges map consecutively to $a$, so going once around $C$ maps to the attaching loop $a^5$ of the $2$-cell of $X$.
Hence the characteristic map of each $D_j$ is compatible with the quotient description in <1>1.

This is the standard lifted CW construction for the covering corresponding to the trivial subgroup of
\[
\langle a\mid a^5\rangle.
\]
The five vertices are indexed by the five elements of $\ZZ/5\ZZ$, and the lift of the $a$-edge sends $v_j$ to $v_{j+1}$.
Each $2$-cell of $X$ has one lift beginning at each vertex, giving precisely the five disks $D_j$.
Therefore the resulting cellular map is a connected $5$-sheeted covering.
:::

<1>4. The covering space $\widetilde X$ is simply connected.
::: {.proof}
Its $1$-skeleton is the circle $C$.
After attaching the first disk $D_0$ along $C$, this generator of the fundamental group becomes nullhomotopic.
Attaching the remaining $2$-cells cannot create new elements of the fundamental group: each such attachment only quotients the existing fundamental group by the normal subgroup generated by its attaching loop.
Thus
\[
\pi_1(\widetilde X)=0.
\]
:::

<1>5. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\widetilde X=D_0\cup_C D_1\cup_C D_2\cup_C D_3\cup_C D_4}
\]
is the universal covering space of $X$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3 it is a connected covering of $X$, and by <1>4 it is simply connected.
Hence it is universal.
:::
