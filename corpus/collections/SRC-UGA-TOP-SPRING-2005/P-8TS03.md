::: problem
Define a linear map $\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ by the matrix
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let $T^2=\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ be endowed with the quotient topology.

(a) Prove that $A$ induces a well-defined, continuous map $f:T^2\to T^2$.

(b) Suppose $g:T^2\to T^2$ is a continuous map that is homotopic to $f$.
Prove or disprove: $g$ has a fixed point.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The formula
\[
f([x])=[Ax]
\qquad(x\in\RR^2)
\]
defines a well-defined map $f:T^2\to T^2$.
::: {.proof}
If $[x]=[y]$ in $T^2=\RR^2/\ZZ^2$, then
\[
x-y\in\ZZ^2.
\]
All entries of $A$ are integers, so
\[
A(x-y)\in\ZZ^2.
\]
Hence $[Ax]=[Ay]$, proving that the displayed formula is independent of the representative.
:::

<1>2. The induced map $f$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
q:\RR^2\longrightarrow T^2
\]
be the quotient map.
By construction,
\[
f\circ q=q\circ A.
\]
The right-hand side is continuous because both the linear map $A$ and $q$ are continuous.
Since $q$ is a quotient map, a map out of $T^2$ is continuous exactly when its composite with $q$ is continuous.
Therefore $f$ is continuous.
:::

<1>3. On integral homology, the induced maps of $f$ have traces
\[
\operatorname{tr}(f_*|H_0)=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(f_*|H_1)=3,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(f_*|H_2)=3.
\]
::: {.proof}
The torus is connected, so $f_*$ is the identity on
\[
H_0(T^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\]
and has trace $1$.

Under the standard basis of
\[
H_1(T^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^2
\]
given by the two coordinate circles, the map induced by $f$ is represented by the same integer matrix
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1\\
1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus its trace is
\[
\operatorname{tr}(A)=1+2=3.
\]

Finally,
\[
H_2(T^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\]
and the induced map on the fundamental class is multiplication by the degree of the torus endomorphism.
For an endomorphism induced by an integer matrix, this degree is
\[
\det A=1\cdot2-(-1)\cdot1=3.
\]
Hence the trace on $H_2$ is $3$.
:::

<1>4. The Lefschetz number of $f$ is
\[
L(f)=1.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using <1>3 and the fact that the torus has no homology above degree $2$,
\[
L(f)
=
\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k\operatorname{tr}(f_*|H_k)
=1-3+3
=1.
\]
Equivalently, for this torus map,
\[
L(f)=\det(I-A)=1.
\]
:::

<1>5. Every continuous map $g:T^2\to T^2$ homotopic to $f$ has a fixed point.
::: {.proof}
Homotopic maps induce the same homomorphisms on homology, so
\[
L(g)=L(f)=1\ne0.
\]
The torus is a finite CW complex.
The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem therefore implies that $g$ has a fixed point.
Thus the assertion in part (b) is true.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
\boxed{f([x])=[Ax]\text{ is well-defined and continuous, and every }g\simeq f\text{ has a fixed point}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
Parts (a) and (b) are <1>1--<1>2 and <1>3--<1>5 respectively.
:::
