::: problem
(a) Suppose $F/\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is a finite field extension where $p$ is prime and $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
Prove that $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{F}_{p^n}) = \langle \sigma^n \rangle$, where $\sigma(a) := a^p$ is the Frobenius automorphism of $F$.
(You may use without proof the fact that $F/\mathbb{F}_p$ is a Galois extension and $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{F}_p) = \langle \sigma \rangle$.)

(b) Suppose $g(x)$ is an irreducible factor of $x^{p^n} - x + 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}[x]$ where $p$ is prime and $n$ is a positive integer.
Prove that $\deg g = p$.

Hint: Suppose $\alpha$ is a zero of $g(x)$ and notice that $\sigma^n(\alpha) = \alpha - 1$.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let \(F/\mathbb F_{p^n}\) be finite. Since \(F/\mathbb F_p\) is Galois with cyclic Galois group generated by Frobenius \(\sigma(a)=a^p\), the subgroup fixing \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\) is \(\langle \sigma^n\rangle\).
::: {.proof}
By the allowed fact, \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb F_p)=\langle\sigma\rangle\). The restriction of \(\sigma\) to \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\) has order exactly \(n\), because \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)=\langle\sigma\rangle\) has order \(n\). Hence a power \(\sigma^k\) fixes \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\) pointwise exactly when \(n\mid k\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb F_{p^n})=\langle\sigma^n\rangle.
\]
This proves part (a).
:::

<1>2. Let \(g(x)\) be an irreducible factor of \(x^{p^n}-x+1\), let \(\alpha\) be a root, and put \(K=\mathbb F_{p^n}(\alpha)\). Then \(K/\mathbb F_{p^n}\) is a finite extension and
\[
\sigma^n(\alpha)=\alpha-1.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because \(g\) is algebraic over the finite field \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\), the extension \(K\) is finite. Since \(\alpha\) is a root of \(x^{p^n}-x+1\),
\[
\alpha^{p^n}-\alpha+1=0,
\]
so \(\alpha^{p^n}=\alpha-1\), which is exactly the displayed identity.
:::

<1>3. For every integer \(j\ge0\),
\[
(\sigma^n)^j(\alpha)=\alpha-j,
\]
where \(j\) is viewed in the prime field \(\mathbb F_p\subseteq\mathbb F_{p^n}\).
::: {.proof}
Induct on \(j\). The case \(j=0\) is trivial. If the formula holds for \(j\), then \(\sigma^n\) fixes \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\), hence fixes the scalar \(j\), and
\[
(\sigma^n)^{j+1}(\alpha)=\sigma^n(\alpha-j)=\sigma^n(\alpha)-j=(\alpha-1)-j=\alpha-(j+1).
\]
:::

<1>4. The orbit of \(\alpha\) under \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb F_{p^n})=\langle\sigma^n\rangle\) has exactly \(p\) elements.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, \((\sigma^n)^p(\alpha)=\alpha-p=\alpha\). For \(1\le j<p\), the scalar \(j\) is nonzero in characteristic \(p\), so \(\alpha-j\ne\alpha\). Thus the least positive power of \(\sigma^n\) fixing \(\alpha\) is \(p\), and the orbit has size \(p\).
:::

<1>5. Therefore \(\deg g=p\).
::: {.proof}
The extension \(K/\mathbb F_{p^n}\) is Galois because every finite extension of a finite field is Galois. Since \(K=\mathbb F_{p^n}(\alpha)\), an automorphism of \(K\) over \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\) that fixes \(\alpha\) is the identity. Hence the Galois orbit of \(\alpha\) has size
\[
|\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb F_{p^n})|=[K:\mathbb F_{p^n}]=\deg g.
\]
By <1>4>, this size is \(p\). Hence \(\deg g=p\).
:::
