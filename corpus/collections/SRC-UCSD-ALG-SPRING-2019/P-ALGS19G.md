::: {.problem}
(a) Suppose $F/\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is a finite field extension where $p$ is prime and $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
Prove that $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{F}_{p^n}) = \langle \sigma^n \rangle$, where $\sigma(a) := a^p$ is the Frobenius automorphism of $F$.
(You may use without proof the fact that $F/\mathbb{F}_p$ is a Galois extension and $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{F}_p) = \langle \sigma \rangle$.)

(b) Suppose $g(x)$ is an irreducible factor of $x^{p^n} - x + 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}[x]$ where $p$ is prime and $n$ is a positive integer.
Prove that $\deg g = p$.

Hint: Suppose $\alpha$ is a zero of $g(x)$ and notice that $\sigma^n(\alpha) = \alpha - 1$.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Since \(F/\mathbb F_{p^n}\) is finite, \(F\) is a finite field. Write
\[
[F:\mathbb F_{p^n}]=d.
\]
Then \(|F|=p^{nd}\).
::: {.proof}
A finite extension of a finite field is finite, and extension degree multiplies the exponent in the field cardinality.
:::

<1>2. The group \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb F_p)\) is cyclic of order \(nd\), generated by \(\sigma(a)=a^p\), and \(\sigma^n\) fixes \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\) pointwise.
::: {.proof}
The first assertion is allowed by the problem statement. For \(a\in\mathbb F_{p^n}\), one has \(a^{p^n}=a\), so \(\sigma^n(a)=a\).
:::

<1>3. One has
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb F_{p^n})=\langle\sigma^n\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
The element \(\sigma^n\) has order \(d\) inside the cyclic group \(\langle\sigma\rangle\) of order \(nd\). Thus \(\langle\sigma^n\rangle\) has \(d\) elements and, by <1>2, is contained in \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb F_{p^n})\). But
\[
|\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb F_{p^n})|=[F:\mathbb F_{p^n}]=d,
\]
since finite-field extensions are Galois. Hence the two groups are equal.
:::

<1>4. Let \(g(x)\in\mathbb F_{p^n}[x]\) be irreducible and divide \(x^{p^n}-x+1\), and let \(\alpha\) be a root. Put \(F=\mathbb F_{p^n}(\alpha)\). Then
\[
\sigma^n(\alpha)=\alpha-1.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because \(g\mid x^{p^n}-x+1\), the element \(\alpha\) satisfies
\[
\alpha^{p^n}-\alpha+1=0.
\]
Since \(\sigma^n(\alpha)=\alpha^{p^n}\), this rearranges to the stated identity.
:::

<1>5. For every integer \(k\ge0\),
\[
\sigma^{kn}(\alpha)=\alpha-k,
\]
where \(k\) is viewed in the prime field \(\mathbb F_p\subseteq F\).
::: {.proof}
Induct on \(k\). The case \(k=0\) is immediate. If the formula holds for \(k\), then \(\sigma^n\) fixes \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\), hence fixes the element \(k\in\mathbb F_p\), and therefore
\[
\sigma^{(k+1)n}(\alpha)
=\sigma^n(\alpha-k)
=\sigma^n(\alpha)-k
=(\alpha-1)-k
=\alpha-(k+1).
\]
:::

<1>6. The orbit of \(\alpha\) under \(\langle\sigma^n\rangle=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb F_{p^n})\) has exactly \(p\) elements.
::: {.proof}
By <1>5, the first \(p\) orbit elements are
\[
\alpha,\alpha-1,\ldots,\alpha-(p-1),
\]
and they are distinct because the elements \(0,1,\ldots,p-1\) are distinct in \(\mathbb F_p\). Also
\[
\sigma^{pn}(\alpha)=\alpha-p=\alpha
\]
in characteristic \(p\). Thus the orbit size is exactly \(p\).
:::

<1>7. Therefore \(\deg g=p\).
::: {.proof}
The polynomial \(g\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\). Since \(F/\mathbb F_{p^n}\) is finite Galois, the distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\) are exactly its Galois orbit, whose size equals the degree of the minimal polynomial. By <1>3 and <1>6 that orbit has size \(p\). Hence \(\deg g=p\).
:::
