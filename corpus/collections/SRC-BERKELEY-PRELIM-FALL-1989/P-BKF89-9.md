::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field and let $R$ be a subring of $F[x]$ with $F\subset R$. Prove that there exist finitely many polynomials $f_1,\ldots,f_n\in F[x]$ such that
\[
R=F[f_1,\ldots,f_n].
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $R=F$, the conclusion holds.

::: pf-proof

For example,
$$
R=F[0].
$$
Thus one may assume below that $R$ contains a nonconstant polynomial.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
D=\{\deg h:0\neq h\in R\}\subseteq\NN.
$$
Then $D$ is an additive submonoid of $\NN$ containing a positive integer.

::: pf-proof

Since $F\subset R$, nonzero constants show that $0\in D$. If $h_1,h_2\in R$ are nonzero, then $F[x]$ is a domain and
$$
\deg(h_1h_2)
=
\deg h_1+\deg h_2.
$$
Thus $D$ is closed under addition. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, the case under consideration contains a nonconstant polynomial, so $D$ contains a positive integer.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The additive monoid $D$ is finitely generated.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
d=\min(D\setminus\{0\}).
$$
For each residue class $r\pmod d$ that occurs among elements of $D$, choose the least such element $s_r\in D$. There are at most $d$ choices.

If $s\in D$ and $s\equiv r\pmod d$, then by minimality
$$
s\geq s_r,
$$
and
$$
s-s_r=qd
$$
for some integer $q\geq0$. Hence
$$
s=s_r+qd.
$$
Therefore $D$ is generated by the finite set consisting of $d$ together with the nonzero $s_r$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Choose positive generators
$$
d_1,\ldots,d_s
$$
for $D$, and choose $f_i\in R$ with
$$
\deg f_i=d_i.
$$
Put
$$
R_0=F[f_1,\ldots,f_s].
$$
Then
$$
R_0\subseteq R.
$$

::: pf-proof

The generators $d_i$ exist by step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, and by definition of $D$ each is the degree of some nonzero polynomial $f_i\in R$. Since $R$ is a subring containing both $F$ and all the $f_i$, it contains the $F$-subalgebra they generate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Every element of $R$ belongs to $R_0$.

::: pf-proof

Proceed by induction on degree. Every element of degree $0$ is a constant in $F$, hence lies in $R_0$.

Now let $0\neq h\in R$ have degree $m>0$, and assume every element of $R$ of degree less than $m$ lies in $R_0$. Since the $d_i$ generate $D$ and $m\in D$, write
$$
m=\sum_{i=1}^s e_i d_i
$$
with $e_i\in\NN$. Then
$$
P=\prod_{i=1}^s f_i^{e_i}\in R_0
$$
has degree $m$.

Let $c_h,c_P\in F^\times$ be the leading coefficients of $h$ and $P$, and put
$$
\lambda=\frac{c_h}{c_P}\in F.
$$
The leading terms of $h$ and $\lambda P$ agree, so either
$$
h-\lambda P=0
$$
or
$$
\deg(h-\lambda P)<m.
$$
Moreover $h-\lambda P\in R$. In the first case $h=\lambda P\in R_0$; in the second, the induction hypothesis gives $h-\lambda P\in R_0$, and hence again
$$
h=(h-\lambda P)+\lambda P\in R_0.
$$
Thus the induction closes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{R=F[f_1,\ldots,f_s]}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives $R_0\subseteq R$, while step [](#s5){.pf-ref} gives $R\subseteq R_0$. Hence equality holds.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s6){.pf-ref} cover all cases.

:::
