::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a real $n\times n$ matrix. Prove that $A^TA$ and $A^T$ have the same range. Equivalently, prove that for every $y$,
\[
y=A^TAx
\]
has a solution $x$ if and only if
\[
y=A^Tz
\]
has a solution $z$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

One has
$$
\operatorname{ran}(A^TA)
\subseteq
\operatorname{ran}(A^T).
$$

::: pf-proof

Every vector in the range of $A^TA$ has the form
$$
A^TAx
=
A^T(Ax)
$$
for some $x$. Hence it lies in the range of $A^T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

One has
$$
\ker(A^TA)=\ker A.
$$

::: pf-proof

If $Ax=0$, then certainly $A^TAx=0$, so
$$
\ker A\subseteq\ker(A^TA).
$$
Conversely, if $A^TAx=0$, then
$$
0
=
x^TA^TAx
=
\norm{Ax}^2.
$$
Thus $Ax=0$, proving the reverse inclusion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The ranges of $A^TA$ and $A^T$ have the same dimension.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref} and rank--nullity,
$$
\operatorname{rank}(A^TA)
=
n-\dim\ker(A^TA)
=
n-\dim\ker A
=
\operatorname{rank}A.
$$
The rank of a matrix equals the rank of its transpose, so
$$
\operatorname{rank}(A^T)=\operatorname{rank}A.
$$
Hence
$$
\dim\operatorname{ran}(A^TA)
=
\dim\operatorname{ran}(A^T).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{ran}(A^TA)
=
\operatorname{ran}(A^T)
}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives an inclusion between two finite-dimensional
subspaces, and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that they have the same dimension.
Therefore the inclusion is equality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Equivalently, for every $y$, the equation
$$
y=A^TAx
$$
has a solution if and only if
$$
y=A^Tz
$$
has a solution.

::: pf-proof

The first equation is solvable exactly when
$y\in\operatorname{ran}(A^TA)$, and the second is solvable exactly
when $y\in\operatorname{ran}(A^T)$. Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} identifies these two
ranges.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref} give the two equivalent formulations requested.

:::
