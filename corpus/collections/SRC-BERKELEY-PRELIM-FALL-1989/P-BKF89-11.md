::: {.problem}
Let $a>1$ and $k>0$ be integers. Prove that
\[
k\mid\varphi(a^k-1),
\]
where $\varphi$ is Euler's totient function.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $k=1$, the assertion is immediate.

::: {.proof}
Every integer is divisible by $1$, so
$$
1\mid\varphi(a-1).
$$
:::

<1>2. Assume $k>1$ and put
$$
N=a^k-1.
$$
Then the residue class of $a$ is an element of
$$
(\ZZ/N\ZZ)^\times.
$$

::: {.proof}
Any common divisor of $a$ and $N=a^k-1$ divides both $a^k$ and $a^k-1$, hence divides $1$. Thus
$$
\gcd(a,N)=1,
$$
so $a$ is invertible modulo $N$.
:::

<1>3. The order of the residue class of $a$ in $(\ZZ/N\ZZ)^\times$ is exactly $k$.

::: {.proof}
By definition of $N$,
$$
a^k\equiv1\pmod N,
$$
so the order divides $k$.

Suppose for some positive $j<k$ that
$$
a^j\equiv1\pmod N.
$$
Then
$$
N\mid a^j-1.
$$
But $a>1$ and $j<k$ give
$$
0<a^j-1<a^k-1=N,
$$
which is impossible for a positive multiple of $N$. Hence no positive exponent smaller than $k$ gives $1$, and the order is exactly $k$.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\boxed{k\mid\varphi(a^k-1)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The finite group
$$
(\ZZ/N\ZZ)^\times
$$
has order $\varphi(N)$. By step <1>3 it contains an element of order $k$. Lagrange's theorem therefore gives
$$
k\mid |(\ZZ/N\ZZ)^\times|
=
\varphi(N)
=
\varphi(a^k-1).
$$
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 handles $k=1$, and step <1>4 handles $k>1$.
:::
:::
