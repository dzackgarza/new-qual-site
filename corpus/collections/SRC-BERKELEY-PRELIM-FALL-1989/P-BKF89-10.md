::: {.problem}
Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$. Prove that every real sequence satisfying
\[
x_{n+1}=\alpha x_n+(1-\alpha)x_{n-1}
\]
has a limit, and express the limit in terms of $\alpha,x_0,x_1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Define
$$
d_n=x_n-x_{n-1}
\qquad
(n\geq1).
$$
Then
$$
d_{n+1}=(\alpha-1)d_n.
$$

::: {.proof}
Using the recurrence,
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{n+1}
&=
x_{n+1}-x_n\\
&=
\alpha x_n+(1-\alpha)x_{n-1}-x_n\\
&=
-(1-\alpha)(x_n-x_{n-1})\\
&=
(\alpha-1)d_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. For every $n\geq1$,
$$
d_n=(\alpha-1)^{n-1}(x_1-x_0).
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 is a first-order geometric recurrence with initial value
$$
d_1=x_1-x_0.
$$
Iteration gives the formula.
:::

<1>3. For every $n\geq1$,
$$
x_n
=
x_0
+
(x_1-x_0)
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\alpha-1)^j.
$$

::: {.proof}
Telescope the first differences:
$$
x_n-x_0
=
\sum_{k=1}^n(x_k-x_{k-1})
=
\sum_{k=1}^n d_k.
$$
Substitute step <1>2 and reindex.
:::

<1>4. The sequence $(x_n)$ converges and
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n
=
\frac{x_1+(1-\alpha)x_0}{2-\alpha}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Because $0<\alpha<1$,
$$
|\alpha-1|=1-\alpha<1.
$$
Thus the geometric series in step <1>3 converges to
$$
\frac{1}{1-(\alpha-1)}
=
\frac{1}{2-\alpha}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n
&=
x_0+\frac{x_1-x_0}{2-\alpha}\\
&=
\frac{x_1+(1-\alpha)x_0}{2-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 proves convergence and gives the requested limit.
:::
:::
