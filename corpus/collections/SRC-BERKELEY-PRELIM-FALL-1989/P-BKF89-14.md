::: {.problem}
Let $X\subset\mathbb R^n$ be compact and let $f:X\to\mathbb R$ be continuous. Given $\varepsilon>0$, show that there exists $M$ such that for all $x,y\in X$,
\[
|f(x)-f(y)|\le M|x-y|+\varepsilon.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. There is a number $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\norm{x-y}<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}<\varepsilon
$$
for all $x,y\in X$.

::: {.proof}
Because $X$ is compact and $f$ is continuous, $f$ is uniformly continuous on $X$.
:::

<1>2. There is a constant $B\ge0$ such that
$$
\abs{f(x)}\le B
$$
for every $x\in X$.

::: {.proof}
The continuous function $x\mapsto\abs{f(x)}$ attains a maximum on the compact set $X$. Let
$$
B\coloneqq\max_{x\in X}\abs{f(x)}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Set
$$
M\coloneqq\frac{2B}{\delta}.
$$
If $\norm{x-y}\ge\delta$, then
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le M\norm{x-y}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}
\le
\abs{f(x)}+\abs{f(y)}
\le 2B.
$$
Since $\norm{x-y}\ge\delta$,
$$
M\norm{x-y}
=
\frac{2B}{\delta}\norm{x-y}
\ge2B.
$$
:::

<1>4. For all $x,y\in X$,
$$
\boxed{\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le M\norm{x-y}+\varepsilon}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $\norm{x-y}<\delta$, step <1>1 gives
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}<\varepsilon\le M\norm{x-y}+\varepsilon.
$$
If $\norm{x-y}\ge\delta$, step <1>3 gives
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le M\norm{x-y}\le M\norm{x-y}+\varepsilon.
$$
These two cases exhaust all pairs $x,y\in X$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required estimate for the constant $M$ defined in step <1>3.
:::
:::
