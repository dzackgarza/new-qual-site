::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a finite abelian group, and let $m$ be the maximum of the orders of the elements of $A$. Put
\[
S=\{a\in A:|a|=m\}.
\]
Prove that $A$ is generated by $S$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $A$ is trivial, the assertion holds.

::: {.proof}
Then $m=1$, the identity belongs to $S$, and $A=\langle S\rangle$.
:::

<1>2. Suppose $A$ is nontrivial. By the invariant-factor form of the structure theorem,
$$
A\cong
C_{d_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{d_r},
\qquad
d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_r.
$$
Then
$$
m=d_r.
$$

::: {.proof}
Every element
$$
(a_1,\ldots,a_r)
$$
has order equal to the least common multiple of the orders of its coordinates. The order of the $i$th coordinate divides $d_i$, and $d_i\mid d_r$, so every element of $A$ has order dividing $d_r$.

On the other hand, a generator of the last cyclic factor has order $d_r$. Hence the maximum element order is exactly $d_r$.
:::

<1>3. Let $e_i$ denote a standard generator of the $i$th cyclic factor. Then
$$
e_r\in S
$$
and, for every $i<r$,
$$
e_i+e_r\in S.
$$

::: {.proof}
The element $e_r$ has order $d_r=m$ by step <1>2.

For $i<r$, the last coordinate of $e_i+e_r$ is $e_r$. Hence if
$$
k(e_i+e_r)=0,
$$
then in particular $ke_r=0$, so $d_r\mid k$. Conversely, $d_r$ kills both $e_i$ and $e_r$ because $d_i\mid d_r$. Thus
$$
|e_i+e_r|=d_r=m,
$$
so $e_i+e_r\in S$.
:::

<1>4. Every standard generator $e_i$ belongs to $\langle S\rangle$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives $e_r\in S$. For $i<r$, both $e_i+e_r$ and $e_r$ lie in $S$, so
$$
e_i
=
(e_i+e_r)-e_r
\in
\langle S\rangle.
$$
Thus all standard generators lie in $\langle S\rangle$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{A=\langle S\rangle}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The standard generators $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ generate $A$, and step <1>4 places all of them in $\langle S\rangle$. Hence
$$
A\subseteq\langle S\rangle.
$$
The reverse inclusion is automatic because $S\subseteq A$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>5 cover the trivial and nontrivial cases.
:::
