::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group, let $G'$ be its commutator subgroup, and let $N\triangleleft G$ be cyclic. Prove that
\[
gn=ng
\]
for every $g\in G'$ and $n\in N$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Conjugation defines a homomorphism
$$
\varphi:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(N).
$$

::: {.proof}
Because $N\triangleleft G$, conjugation by any $g\in G$ maps $N$ to itself. Define
$$
\varphi(g)(n)=gng^{-1}.
$$
Each $\varphi(g)$ is an automorphism of $N$, and
$$
\varphi(gh)=\varphi(g)\varphi(h),
$$
so $\varphi$ is a group homomorphism.
:::

<1>2. The group $\operatorname{Aut}(N)$ is abelian.

::: {.proof}
Let $N=\langle a\rangle$. Every automorphism of $N$ is determined by the image of $a$, and has the form
$$
a\longmapsto a^u
$$
where, if $N$ has finite order $m$, the exponent $u$ is a unit modulo $m$, while if $N$ is infinite, $u\in\{1,-1\}$. If two automorphisms correspond to exponents $u$ and $v$, then their composites send
$$
a\longmapsto a^{uv}
$$
in either order. Hence all automorphisms commute.
:::

<1>3. The commutator subgroup $G'$ lies in $\ker\varphi$.

::: {.proof}
Since the target $\operatorname{Aut}(N)$ is abelian by step <1>2, every commutator in $G$ maps to the identity. Therefore the subgroup generated by all commutators satisfies
$$
G'\subseteq\ker\varphi.
$$
:::

<1>4. Every element of $G'$ centralizes every element of $N$:
$$
\boxed{gn=ng\qquad(g\in G',\ n\in N)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $g\in G'$, then step <1>3 gives $\varphi(g)=\operatorname{id}_N$. Thus for every $n\in N$,
$$
gng^{-1}=n.
$$
Multiplying on the right by $g$ gives $gn=ng$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
