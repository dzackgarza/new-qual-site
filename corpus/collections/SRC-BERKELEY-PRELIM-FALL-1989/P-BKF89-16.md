::: {.problem}
Prove that
\[
p(z)=z^{47}-z^{23}+2z^{11}-z^5+4z^2+1
\]
has at least one root in the disk $|z|<1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
p(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{47}a_kz^k,
$$
and let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{47}$ be the roots of $p$, counted
with multiplicity.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $p$ has no root in the disk $\abs{z}<1$, then
$$
\abs{\alpha_j}\geq 1
$$
for every $j$.

::: pf-proof

This is exactly the negation of the existence of a root with modulus
strictly less than $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Under the assumption in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, every root of $p$ lies on the
unit circle.

::: pf-proof

The polynomial is monic of odd degree $47$ and has constant term $1$.
Vieta's formula therefore gives
$$
1
=
(-1)^{47}\prod_{j=1}^{47}\alpha_j
=
-\prod_{j=1}^{47}\alpha_j.
$$
Hence
$$
\prod_{j=1}^{47}\abs{\alpha_j}=1.
$$
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, every factor in this product is at least $1$, so every
factor must equal $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Define the reciprocal-conjugate polynomial
$$
p^*(z)
\coloneqq
\sum_{k=0}^{47}\overline{a_k}z^{47-k}.
$$
For $z\neq0$,
$$
p^*(z)=z^{47}\overline{p\left(1/\overline z\right)}.
$$
Under the assumption in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, one has
$$
p^*(z)=p(z).
$$

::: pf-proof

The leading coefficient of $p^*$ is $\overline{a_0}=1$, so $p^*$ is
monic of degree $47$. Its roots, counted with multiplicity, are
$$
\frac{1}{\overline{\alpha_1}},\ldots,
\frac{1}{\overline{\alpha_{47}}}.
$$
By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $\abs{\alpha_j}=1$, hence
$$
\frac{1}{\overline{\alpha_j}}=\alpha_j
$$
for every $j$. Thus $p$ and $p^*$ are monic polynomials of the same
degree with the same roots counted with multiplicity, so they are
equal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The equality in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} is impossible.

::: pf-proof

Comparing the coefficient of $z^{23}$ in
$$
p^*(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{47}\overline{a_k}z^{47-k}
$$
with the coefficient of $z^{23}$ in $p$ gives
$$
a_{23}=\overline{a_{24}}.
$$
For the given polynomial,
$$
a_{23}=-1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a_{24}=0,
$$
which is a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The polynomial $p$ has at least one root with
$$
\abs{z}<1.
$$

::: pf-proof

If no such root existed, step [](#s1){.pf-ref} would hold, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} would
give a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
