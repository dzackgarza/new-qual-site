::: {.problem}
Let $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ be $C^1$, with
\[
f(0)=f(1)=0,
\]
and suppose $f'$ is nonincreasing, so that $f$ is concave. Prove that the arclength of the graph of $f$ does not exceed $3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

There exists $c\in(0,1)$ such that $f'(c)=0$.

::: pf-proof

Since $f$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, differentiable on $(0,1)$, and
$$
f(1)-f(0)=0,
$$
the mean value theorem gives $c\in(0,1)$ with
$$
f'(c)=\frac{f(1)-f(0)}{1-0}=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

One has
$$
f'(x)\ge0\quad(0\le x\le c),
\qquad
f'(x)\le0\quad(c\le x\le1).
$$

::: pf-proof

The derivative $f'$ is nonincreasing. Thus $x\le c$ implies
$$
f'(x)\ge f'(c)=0,
$$
while $x\ge c$ implies
$$
f'(x)\le f'(c)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The total variation of $f$ satisfies
$$
\int_0^1\abs{f'(x)}\,dx=2f(c)\le2.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref} and the fundamental theorem of calculus,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\abs{f'(x)}\,dx
&=\int_0^c f'(x)\,dx-\int_c^1 f'(x)\,dx\\
&=f(c)-f(0)+f(c)-f(1)\\
&=2f(c).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $f$ takes values in $[0,1]$, one has $f(c)\le1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The arclength $L$ of the graph of $f$ satisfies
$$
\boxed{L\le3}.
$$

::: pf-proof

For every real number $t$,
$$
\sqrt{1+t^2}\le1+\abs t,
$$
because both sides are nonnegative and
$$
1+t^2\le(1+\abs t)^2.
$$
Therefore, using step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
L
&=\int_0^1\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx\\
&\le\int_0^1\left(1+\abs{f'(x)}\right)\,dx\\
&=1+\int_0^1\abs{f'(x)}\,dx\\
&\le3.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives the required arclength bound.

:::
