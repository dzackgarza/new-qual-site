::: {.problem}
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space over a field of characteristic $0$. Prove that $V$ is not the union of finitely many subspaces of dimension $n-1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $F$ be the base field of $V$. The case $n=0$ is immediate, so assume $n\ge1$.

<1>1. The field $F$ is infinite.

::: {.proof}
Since $F$ has characteristic $0$, the elements
$$
0,1,1+1,1+1+1,\ldots
$$
are pairwise distinct.
:::

<1>2. If $H\subset V$ has dimension $n-1$, then there is a nonzero linear functional
$$
\lambda_H:V\to F
$$
such that
$$
H=\ker\lambda_H.
$$

::: {.proof}
Choose a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_{n-1}$ of $H$ and extend it to a basis
$$
e_1,\ldots,e_{n-1},e_n
$$
of $V$. Define $\lambda_H(e_i)=0$ for $i<n$ and $\lambda_H(e_n)=1$. Then $\lambda_H$ is nonzero and its kernel is exactly $H$.
:::

<1>3. For any finite collection of hyperplanes $H_1,\ldots,H_m\subset V$, the function
$$
P\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^m\lambda_{H_j}
$$
is a nonzero polynomial function on $V$ and vanishes on $H_1\cup\cdots\cup H_m$.

::: {.proof}
Choose coordinates $V\cong F^n$. Each $\lambda_{H_j}$ is then a nonzero linear polynomial in $F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$. Since this polynomial ring is an integral domain, their product $P$ is nonzero. If $v\in H_j$, then $\lambda_{H_j}(v)=0$, so $P(v)=0$.
:::

<1>4. A nonzero polynomial in $F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ does not vanish at every point of $F^n$.

::: {.proof}
This follows by induction on $n$, using that $F$ is infinite by step <1>1. For $n=1$, a nonzero polynomial has only finitely many roots. For $n>1$, write a nonzero polynomial $Q$ as
$$
Q(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
=
\sum_{k=0}^d Q_k(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})x_n^k
$$
with some $Q_k\ne0$. By induction there is
$$
a\in F^{n-1}
$$
for which that coefficient $Q_k(a)$ is nonzero. Hence $Q(a,x_n)$ is a nonzero one-variable polynomial, so it is nonzero for some $x_n\in F$.
:::

<1>5. The space $V$ is not a finite union of $(n-1)$-dimensional subspaces.

::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that
$$
V=H_1\cup\cdots\cup H_m.
$$
Then step <1>3 gives a nonzero polynomial $P$ that vanishes at every point of $V\cong F^n$, contradicting step <1>4. Therefore
$$
\boxed{V\ne H_1\cup\cdots\cup H_m}.
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 proves the assertion for every finite family of hyperplanes.
:::
