::: {.problem}
Let $n$ be a positive integer, $C_n$ the cyclic group of order $n$, and $S_n$ the symmetric group on $n$ points.
For each of the groups
$$
A=\RR^*,\qquad B=\CC^*,\qquad C=C_2\times C_3,\qquad D=S_4,\qquad E=\operatorname{SL}_2(\RR)
$$
prove or disprove that $C_6$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of it.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained solution sheet's: **Berkeley preliminary examination, Fall 2009, solutions** (`assets/attachments/f09solutions.pdf`), Problem 8B. Throughout $C_{n}$ is the cyclic group of order $n$, so that $C_{6}$ is cyclic of order $6$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #criterion}
$C_{6}$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of a group $G$ exactly when $G$ has an element of order $6$.

::: pf-proof
A subgroup isomorphic to the cyclic group of order $6$ is generated by an element of order $6$, so such a subgroup gives such an element; conversely an element $g$ of order $6$ generates a cyclic subgroup of order $6$, which is isomorphic to $C_{6}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #reals}
$C_{6}$ is not a subgroup of $A = \RR^{*}$.

::: pf-proof
$\RR^{*} \cong \theset{\pm1} \times \RR_{>0}$, and $\RR_{>0} \cong \RR$ under the logarithm is torsion-free, so every element of $\RR^{*}$ has order $1$ or $2$: for $r \ne \pm1$ one has $r^{6} \ne 1$. Step [](#criterion){.pf-ref} then applies.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #complex}
$C_{6}$ is a subgroup of $B = \CC^{*}$.

::: pf-proof
The complex number $\zeta = \cos(\pi/3) + i\sin(\pi/3) = e^{i\pi/3}$ satisfies $\zeta^{6} = e^{2\pi i} = 1$ and $\zeta^{k} \ne 1$ for $1 \le k \le 5$, so it has order $6$; by step [](#criterion){.pf-ref} the group $\langle\zeta\rangle \cong C_{6}$ is a subgroup of $\CC^{*}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product}
$C_{6}$ is a subgroup of $C = C_{2} \times C_{3}$, which is itself isomorphic to $C_{6}$.

::: pf-proof
By the Chinese remainder theorem, applicable since $2$ and $3$ are coprime, $\ZZ/6 \cong \ZZ/2 \times \ZZ/3$, so $C$ is a cyclic group of order $6$ and equals its own subgroup $C_{6}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #symmetric}
$C_{6}$ is not a subgroup of $D = S_{4}$.

::: pf-proof
An element of $S_{4}$ has a cycle decomposition of one of the types $1$, $(ab)$, $(ab)(cd)$, $(abc)$, $(abcd)$, with orders $1$, $2$, $2$, $3$, $4$ respectively; no element has order $6$. By step [](#criterion){.pf-ref} there is no subgroup isomorphic to $C_{6}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #linear}
$C_{6}$ is a subgroup of $E = \operatorname{SL}_{2}(\RR)$.

::: pf-proof
The rotation matrix $R_{\theta} = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $\cos^{2}\theta + \sin^{2}\theta = 1$, so it lies in $\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\RR)$, and $R_{\theta}R_{\phi} = R_{\theta+\phi}$; hence $R_{\pi/3}$ has order $6$, being the smallest positive multiple of $\pi/3$ that is a multiple of $2\pi$. By step [](#criterion){.pf-ref} the group $\langle R_{\pi/3}\rangle$ is a subgroup isomorphic to $C_{6}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
$C_{6}$ embeds in $B = \CC^{*}$, in $C = C_{2}\times C_{3}$ and in $E = \operatorname{SL}_{2}(\RR)$, and in neither $A = \RR^{*}$ nor $D = S_{4}$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#complex){.pf-ref}, [](#product){.pf-ref} and [](#linear){.pf-ref} give the three embeddings, and steps [](#reals){.pf-ref} and [](#symmetric){.pf-ref} the two exclusions.

:::
