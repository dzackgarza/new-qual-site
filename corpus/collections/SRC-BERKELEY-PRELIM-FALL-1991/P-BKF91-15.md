::: {.problem}
Let $I$ be the ideal of $\mathbb Z[x]$ generated by $x-7$ and $15$. Prove that
\[
\mathbb Z[x]/I\cong\mathbb Z/15\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Define
$$
\varphi:\ZZ[x]\longrightarrow\ZZ/15\ZZ,
\qquad
\varphi(p)=p(7)\pmod{15}.
$$
Then $\varphi$ is a surjective ring homomorphism.

::: {.proof}
Evaluation at $7$ is a ring homomorphism $\ZZ[x]\to\ZZ$, and reduction modulo $15$ is a ring homomorphism $\ZZ\to\ZZ/15\ZZ$, so their composite is a ring homomorphism. It is surjective because every residue class is the image of a constant polynomial.
:::

<1>2. The ideal
$$
I=(x-7,15)
$$
is contained in $\ker\varphi$.

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
\varphi(x-7)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\varphi(15)=0.
$$
Therefore the ideal generated by these two elements lies in the kernel.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\ker\varphi\subseteq I.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $p\in\ker\varphi$. Then
$$
p(7)=15m
$$
for some $m\in\ZZ$. By the polynomial remainder theorem,
$$
p(x)-p(7)=(x-7)q(x)
$$
for some $q(x)\in\ZZ[x]$. Hence
$$
p(x)=(x-7)q(x)+15m\in(x-7,15)=I.
$$
:::

<1>4. Thus
$$
\ker\varphi=I.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\ZZ[x]/I\cong\ZZ/15\ZZ}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $\varphi$ is surjective, and by step <1>4 its kernel is $I$. The first isomorphism theorem for rings gives the result.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required isomorphism.
:::
:::
