::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be entire and suppose
\[
|f(z)|\le |\operatorname{Re}z|^{-1/2}
\]
whenever $\operatorname{Re}z\ne0$. Prove that $f$ is constant.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The constant
$$
C\coloneqq\int_0^{2\pi}\abs{\cos\theta}^{-1/2}\,d\theta
$$
is finite.

::: {.proof}
The only singularities occur at
$$
\theta=\frac\pi2,
\qquad
\theta=\frac{3\pi}{2}.
$$
Near either point, $\abs{\cos\theta}$ is comparable to the distance from that point, so the integrand is comparable to $\abs t^{-1/2}$, which is locally integrable.
:::

<1>2. For every $y\in\RR$ and every $R>0$,
$$
\abs{f(iy)}\le\frac{C}{2\pi\sqrt R}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Cauchy's formula on the circle
$$
z=iy+Re^{i\theta}
$$
gives
$$
f(iy)
=
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}f(iy+Re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.
$$
For all $\theta$ except the two points where $\cos\theta=0$,
$$
\operatorname{Re}(iy+Re^{i\theta})=R\cos\theta\ne0,
$$
so the hypothesis gives
$$
\abs{f(iy+Re^{i\theta})}
\le
R^{-1/2}\abs{\cos\theta}^{-1/2}.
$$
The two exceptional angles have measure zero. Therefore, by step <1>1,
$$
\abs{f(iy)}
\le
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}\abs{f(iy+Re^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
\le
\frac{C}{2\pi\sqrt R}.
$$
:::

<1>3. The function $f$ vanishes on the imaginary axis.

::: {.proof}
Fix $y\in\RR$. The estimate in step <1>2 holds for every $R>0$. Letting $R\to\infty$ gives
$$
\abs{f(iy)}=0.
$$
Thus $f(iy)=0$ for every real $y$.
:::

<1>4. Hence
$$
\boxed{f\equiv0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, the zero set of the entire function $f$ contains the imaginary axis, which has accumulation points in $\CC$. The identity theorem therefore gives $f\equiv0$.
:::

<1>5. In particular, $f$ is constant.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 shows that the only possible function is the constant zero function.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
