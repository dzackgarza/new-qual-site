::: {.problem}
Let $M_{n\times n}$ be the space of real $n\times n$ matrices with metric
\[
d(A,B)=\sum_{i,j=1}^n|a_{ij}-b_{ij}|.
\]
Prove that the set of nilpotent matrices in $M_{n\times n}$ is closed.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. An $n\times n$ matrix $A$ is nilpotent if and only if
$$
A^n=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $A^n=0$, then $A$ is nilpotent by definition.

Conversely, suppose $A$ is nilpotent, so $A^m=0$ for some positive integer $m$. Over $\CC$, every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $A$ satisfies
$$
0=A^m v=\lambda^m v
$$
for a corresponding nonzero eigenvector $v$, hence $\lambda=0$. Therefore the characteristic polynomial of $A$ is $t^n$. By the Cayley--Hamilton theorem,
$$
A^n=0.
$$
:::

<1>2. The map
$$
\Phi:M_{n\times n}\longrightarrow M_{n\times n},
\qquad
\Phi(A)=A^n,
$$
is continuous for the stated metric.

::: {.proof}
Each entry of $A^n$ is a polynomial in the $n^2$ entries of $A$. Polynomial functions on the finite-dimensional space $\RR^{n^2}$ are continuous, and the stated metric induces its usual topology.
:::

<1>3. The set of nilpotent matrices is
$$
\Phi^{-1}(\{0\}).
$$

::: {.proof}
This is exactly the equivalence proved in step <1>1.
:::

<1>4. The set of nilpotent matrices is closed.

::: {.proof}
The singleton $\{0\}$ is closed in the metric space $M_{n\times n}$. By step <1>2, $\Phi$ is continuous, so its inverse image
$$
\Phi^{-1}(\{0\})
$$
is closed. Step <1>3 identifies this inverse image with the nilpotent matrices.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is the required conclusion.
:::
