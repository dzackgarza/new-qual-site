::: {.problem}
Let
\[
B=\{r\in\mathbb R^3:\|r\|\le1\}
\]
and let $J=(J_1,J_2,J_3)$ be a smooth vector field on $\mathbb R^3$ that vanishes outside $B$ and satisfies
\[
\nabla\cdot J=0.
\]

1. For every smooth scalar-valued function $f$ defined on a neighborhood of $B$, prove that
\[
\int_B (\nabla f)\cdot J\,dx\,dy\,dz=0.
\]

2. Prove that
\[
\int_B J_1\,dx\,dy\,dz=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The vector field $J$ vanishes on the boundary $\partial B$.

::: {.proof}
Let $r\in\partial B$. There are points $r_m$ outside $B$ with $r_m\to r$. By hypothesis $J(r_m)=0$ for every $m$, and smoothness implies continuity. Hence
$$
J(r)=\lim_{m\to\infty}J(r_m)=0.
$$
:::

<1>2. For every admissible scalar function $f$,
$$
\nabla\cdot(fJ)=(\nabla f)\cdot J.
$$

::: {.proof}
The product rule for divergence gives
$$
\nabla\cdot(fJ)
=(\nabla f)\cdot J+f(\nabla\cdot J).
$$
The second term is zero by the hypothesis $\nabla\cdot J=0$.
:::

<1>3. For every such $f$,
$$
\boxed{\int_B(\nabla f)\cdot J\,dx\,dy\,dz=0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2 and the divergence theorem,
$$
\int_B(\nabla f)\cdot J\,dV
=
\int_B\nabla\cdot(fJ)\,dV
=
\int_{\partial B}fJ\cdot n\,dS.
$$
The boundary integral is zero by step <1>1.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\boxed{\int_B J_1\,dx\,dy\,dz=0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>3 to the smooth function
$$
f(x,y,z)=x.
$$
Then
$$
\nabla f=(1,0,0),
$$
so
$$
(\nabla f)\cdot J=J_1.
$$
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves part 1, and step <1>4 proves part 2.
:::
:::
