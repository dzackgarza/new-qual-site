::: {.problem}
Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be a real $n\times n$ matrix with nonnegative entries such that
\[
\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}=1
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Prove that no eigenvalue of $A$ has absolute value greater than $1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let $\lambda\in\CC$ be an eigenvalue of $A$, and choose a nonzero vector
$$
v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)^T\in\CC^n
$$
such that
$$
Av=\lambda v.
$$

::: pf-proof

Although $A$ is real, its eigenvalues may be complex, so it is enough to work over $\CC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Choose an index $i$ such that
$$
\abs{v_i}=\max_{1\le j\le n}\abs{v_j}.
$$
Then $v_i\ne0$.

::: pf-proof

Such an index exists because there are finitely many coordinates. Since $v\ne0$, the maximum modulus is positive.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

One has
$$
\abs\lambda\le1.
$$

::: pf-proof

Taking the $i$th coordinate of $Av=\lambda v$ and using nonnegativity of the entries gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs\lambda\,\abs{v_i}
&=\abs{\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}v_j}\\
&\le\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}\abs{v_j}\\
&\le\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}\abs{v_i}\\
&=\abs{v_i}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $v_i\ne0$ by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, division by $\abs{v_i}$ yields $\abs\lambda\le1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore no eigenvalue of $A$ has absolute value greater than $1$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} applies to an arbitrary eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $A$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
