::: {.problem}
Let $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ be differentiable and suppose $f'$ is Riemann integrable. Prove that
\[
\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx=f(b)-f(a).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For each positive integer $N$, let
$$
x_i
\coloneqq
a+\frac{i}{N}(b-a),
\qquad
i=0,\ldots,N.
$$

<1>1. For every $i=1,\ldots,N$, there is a point
$$
c_i\in(x_{i-1},x_i)
$$
such that
$$
f(x_i)-f(x_{i-1})
=
f'(c_i)(x_i-x_{i-1}).
$$

::: {.proof}
The differentiable function $f$ is continuous. Apply the mean value theorem
to $f$ on each interval $[x_{i-1},x_i]$.
:::

<1>2. For every $N$,
$$
f(b)-f(a)
=
\sum_{i=1}^N
f'(c_i)(x_i-x_{i-1}).
$$

::: {.proof}
Sum the identities from step <1>1. The left-hand side telescopes:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^N
\bigl(f(x_i)-f(x_{i-1})\bigr)
=
f(x_N)-f(x_0)
=
f(b)-f(a).
$$
:::

<1>3. The right-hand side in step <1>2 is a tagged Riemann sum for $f'$
whose mesh tends to zero as $N\to\infty$.

::: {.proof}
The points $c_i$ are tags satisfying
$$
c_i\in[x_{i-1},x_i].
$$
The partition is uniform, with mesh
$$
\max_i(x_i-x_{i-1})
=
\frac{b-a}{N}
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Thus the displayed sum is precisely a tagged Riemann sum.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\sum_{i=1}^N
f'(c_i)(x_i-x_{i-1})
=
\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
The function $f'$ is Riemann integrable by hypothesis. For a Riemann
integrable function, every sequence of tagged Riemann sums whose mesh tends
to zero converges to the Riemann integral.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx
=
f(b)-f(a)
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The sums in step <1>2 are equal to the fixed number $f(b)-f(a)$ for every
$N$, while step <1>4 says that the same sums converge to the integral.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required identity.
:::
:::
