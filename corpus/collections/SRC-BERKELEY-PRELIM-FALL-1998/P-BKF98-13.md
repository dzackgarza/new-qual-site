::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be entire and let
\[
\Omega=\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0].
\]
Suppose that for every $z\in\Omega$,
\[
|f(z)|\le |\log z|,
\]
where $\log z$ is the principal branch. What can be concluded about $f$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #f-of-one-zero}
One has
$$
f(1)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
The point $1$ lies in $\Omega$, and
$$
\log1=0.
$$
The hypothesis therefore gives
$$
\abs{f(1)}
\leq0,
$$
so $f(1)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #bound-on-circle}
Fix $R>1$. For every point
$$
z=Re^{i\theta}
$$
on the circle with
$$
-\pi<\theta<\pi,
$$
one has
$$
\abs{f(z)}
\leq
\sqrt{(\log R)^2+\pi^2}.
$$

::: pf-proof
On the principal branch,
$$
\log z
=
\log R+i\theta.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{\log z}^2
=
(\log R)^2+\theta^2
\leq
(\log R)^2+\pi^2.
$$
Apply the assumed inequality for $f$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #bound-at-negative-R}
The same bound holds at the remaining point
$$
z=-R
$$
of the circle.

::: pf-proof
Approach $-R$ along the circle from within $\Omega$, for example with
$$
z_\theta=Re^{i\theta},
\qquad
\theta\uparrow\pi.
$$
Since $f$ is entire,
$$
f(z_\theta)\longrightarrow f(-R).
$$
Step [](#bound-on-circle){.pf-ref} bounds every $\abs{f(z_\theta)}$ by the same constant. Passing
to the limit gives
$$
\abs{f(-R)}
\leq
\sqrt{(\log R)^2+\pi^2}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coefficient-bound}
If
$$
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^n,
$$
then for every $n\geq1$ and every $R>1$,
$$
\abs{a_n}
\leq
\frac{\sqrt{(\log R)^2+\pi^2}}{R^n}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#bound-on-circle){.pf-ref} and [](#bound-at-negative-R){.pf-ref} give
$$
\max_{\abs z=R}\abs{f(z)}
\leq
\sqrt{(\log R)^2+\pi^2}.
$$
Cauchy's coefficient estimate at the origin gives the displayed bound.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coefficients-vanish}
For every $n\geq1$,
$$
a_n=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $R\to\infty$ in step [](#coefficient-bound){.pf-ref}. Since
$$
\frac{\sqrt{(\log R)^2+\pi^2}}{R^n}
\longrightarrow0,
$$
one obtains $\abs{a_n}=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-constant}
The entire function $f$ is constant.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#coefficients-vanish){.pf-ref} shows that every positive-degree Taylor coefficient of $f$ at the
origin vanishes. Hence
$$
f(z)=a_0
$$
for all $z$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-identically-zero}
One has
$$
\boxed{f\equiv0}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#f-constant){.pf-ref} makes $f$ constant, and step [](#f-of-one-zero){.pf-ref} shows that this constant is
zero.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#f-identically-zero){.pf-ref} is the conclusion forced by the hypotheses.
:::
