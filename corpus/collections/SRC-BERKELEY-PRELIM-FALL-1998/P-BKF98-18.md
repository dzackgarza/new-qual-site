::: {.problem}
Show that the field
\[
\mathbb Q(t_1,\ldots,t_n)
\]
of rational functions in $n$ variables over $\mathbb Q$ is isomorphic to a subfield of $\mathbb R$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #countable-algebraic-set}
If $K\subseteq\RR$ is countable, then the set of real numbers
algebraic over $K$ is countable.

::: pf-proof
The polynomial ring $K[x]$ is countable, since each polynomial is a finite
sequence of elements of the countable set $K$. Every nonzero polynomial has
only finitely many real roots. Hence the set of real numbers algebraic over
$K$ is a countable union of finite sets and is therefore countable.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #algebraically-independent-reals-exist}
There exist $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in\RR$ that are algebraically
independent over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
Choose the numbers inductively. Since $\QQ$ is countable, step [](#countable-algebraic-set){.pf-ref} shows
that only countably many real numbers are algebraic over $\QQ$. As $\RR$ is
uncountable, choose $\alpha_1$ transcendental over $\QQ$.

Suppose $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k$ have been chosen algebraically
independent over $\QQ$. The field
$$
K_k=\QQ(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)
$$
is countable: it consists of quotients of values of polynomials with rational
coefficients, and there are only countably many such pairs of polynomials.
By step [](#countable-algebraic-set){.pf-ref}, only countably many reals are algebraic over $K_k$. Choose
$\alpha_{k+1}\in\RR$ outside that set. Then $\alpha_{k+1}$ is
transcendental over $K_k$, so
$\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{k+1}$ are algebraically independent over $\QQ$.
Continuing finitely many times gives the required $n$ numbers.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #evaluation-homomorphism-injective}
Evaluation at $(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)$ defines an injective ring
homomorphism
$$
\varphi:\QQ[t_1,\ldots,t_n]\longrightarrow\RR.
$$

::: pf-proof
Define
$$
\varphi(f)=f(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n).
$$
This is a ring homomorphism. By the algebraic independence obtained in step
[](#algebraically-independent-reals-exist){.pf-ref}, no nonzero polynomial in $\QQ[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ vanishes at
$(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)$. Thus $\ker\varphi=0$, so $\varphi$ is
injective.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #extension-to-fraction-field}
The map in step [](#evaluation-homomorphism-injective){.pf-ref} extends to an injective field homomorphism
$$
\Phi:\QQ(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\longrightarrow\RR.
$$

::: pf-proof
For $f,g\in\QQ[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ with $g\neq0$, set
$$
\Phi\left(\frac fg\right)
=
\frac{\varphi(f)}{\varphi(g)}.
$$
Step [](#evaluation-homomorphism-injective){.pf-ref} gives $\varphi(g)\neq0$, so this quotient is defined. The universal
property of the fraction field shows that $\Phi$ is a field homomorphism
extending $\varphi$. Since a field homomorphism sending $1$ to $1$ has zero
kernel, $\Phi$ is injective.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #embeds-in-R}
The field $\QQ(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$ is isomorphic to a subfield of
$\RR$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#extension-to-fraction-field){.pf-ref}, $\Phi$ is an injective field homomorphism. Therefore it
identifies $\QQ(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$ with the subfield $\Phi(\QQ(t_1,\ldots,t_n))$
of $\RR$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#embeds-in-R){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
:::
