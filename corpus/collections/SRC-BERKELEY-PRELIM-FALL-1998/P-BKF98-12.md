::: {.problem}
Let $\varphi(x,y)$ have continuous second partial derivatives and suppose on $\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}$ that
\[
\varphi_{xx}+\varphi_{yy}+\varphi_x=0.
\]
Writing $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, suppose also that as $r\to0$,
\[
r\varphi_x\longrightarrow \frac{x}{2\pi r},
\qquad
r\varphi_y\longrightarrow \frac{y}{2\pi r}.
\]
Let $C_R$ be the circle $x^2+y^2=R^2$. Show that
\[
\int_{C_R}e^x(-\varphi_y\,dx+\varphi_x\,dy)
\]
is independent of $R$, and evaluate it.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define the one-form
$$
\omega
\coloneqq
e^x\left(
-\varphi_y\,dx+\varphi_x\,dy
\right).
$$

<1>1. The form $\omega$ is closed on
$$
\RR^2\setminus\{0\}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
\omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy
$$
with
$$
P=-e^x\varphi_y,
\qquad
Q=e^x\varphi_x.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_x-P_y
&=
\partial_x(e^x\varphi_x)
-\partial_y(-e^x\varphi_y)\\
&=
e^x\varphi_x
+e^x\varphi_{xx}
+e^x\varphi_{yy}\\
&=
e^x
\left(
\varphi_{xx}+\varphi_{yy}+\varphi_x
\right)\\
&=0
\end{aligned}
$$
by the differential equation.
:::

<1>2. If
$$
0<R_1<R_2,
$$
then
$$
\int_{C_{R_2}}\omega
=
\int_{C_{R_1}}\omega,
$$
where both circles have counterclockwise orientation.

::: {.proof}
Apply Green's theorem to the annulus
$$
\{(x,y):R_1\leq r\leq R_2\}.
$$
Its positively oriented boundary consists of the outer circle
$C_{R_2}$ counterclockwise and the inner circle $C_{R_1}$ clockwise.
Therefore
$$
\int_{C_{R_2}}\omega
-\int_{C_{R_1}}\omega
=
\iint
(Q_x-P_y)\,dx\,dy.
$$
Step <1>1 makes the right-hand side zero.
:::

<1>3. The integral is independent of $R>0$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 shows that any two positive radii give the same value.
:::

<1>4. Parametrize $C_R$ counterclockwise by
$$
x=R\cos\theta,
\qquad
y=R\sin\theta,
\qquad
0\leq\theta\leq2\pi.
$$
Then
$$
\int_{C_R}\omega
=
\int_0^{2\pi}
e^{R\cos\theta}
\left(
R\varphi_x\cos\theta
+R\varphi_y\sin\theta
\right)
\,d\theta.
$$

::: {.proof}
Along the parametrization,
$$
dx=-R\sin\theta\,d\theta,
\qquad
dy=R\cos\theta\,d\theta.
$$
Substitute these expressions into
$$
e^x(-\varphi_y\,dx+\varphi_x\,dy).
$$
:::

<1>5. As $R\to0$,
$$
R\varphi_x(R\cos\theta,R\sin\theta)
\longrightarrow
\frac{\cos\theta}{2\pi}
$$
and
$$
R\varphi_y(R\cos\theta,R\sin\theta)
\longrightarrow
\frac{\sin\theta}{2\pi},
$$
uniformly in $\theta$.

::: {.proof}
The hypotheses state
$$
r\varphi_x-\frac{x}{2\pi r}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
r\varphi_y-\frac{y}{2\pi r}\longrightarrow0
$$
as $(x,y)\to(0,0)$ through the punctured plane. By the definition of a
limit in $\RR^2$, the error estimates hold simultaneously for all points
with sufficiently small $r$, hence uniformly on each circle $r=R$.
Substituting
$$
\frac{x}{r}=\cos\theta,
\qquad
\frac{y}{r}=\sin\theta
$$
gives the two limits.
:::

<1>6. One has
$$
\lim_{R\to0}
\int_{C_R}\omega
=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>4 and <1>5,
$$
e^{R\cos\theta}
\left(
R\varphi_x\cos\theta
+R\varphi_y\sin\theta
\right)
$$
converges uniformly to
$$
\frac{
\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta
}{2\pi}
=
\frac1{2\pi}.
$$
Therefore the integrals converge to
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
=1.
$$
:::

<1>7. For every $R>0$,
$$
\boxed{
\int_{C_R}
e^x(-\varphi_y\,dx+\varphi_x\,dy)
=1
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 says the integral is constant as a function of $R$, and step
<1>6 computes its limit as $R\to0$. The constant must therefore equal $1$.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3 and <1>7 prove radius-independence and evaluate the common
value.
:::
:::
