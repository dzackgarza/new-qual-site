::: {.problem}
Let $B$ be a $3\times3$ matrix whose null space is two-dimensional, and let $\chi(\lambda)$ be its characteristic polynomial. For each assertion, give a proof or a counterexample.

1. $\lambda^2$ divides $\chi(\lambda)$.
2. The trace of $B$ is an eigenvalue of $B$.
3. $B$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Assertion (1) is true: $\lambda^2$ divides $\chi(\lambda)$.

::: {.proof}
The null space of $B$ is the eigenspace for the eigenvalue $0$, so the
geometric multiplicity of $0$ is $2$. The geometric multiplicity of an
eigenvalue is at most its algebraic multiplicity. Hence $0$ has algebraic
multiplicity at least $2$, which says exactly that $\lambda^2$ divides
$\chi(\lambda)$.
:::

<1>2. The characteristic polynomial has the form
$$
\chi(\lambda)=\lambda^2(\lambda-\operatorname{tr} B).
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1 and the fact that $\chi$ is monic of degree $3$, there is a
scalar $c$ such that
$$
\chi(\lambda)=\lambda^2(\lambda-c)
=\lambda^3-c\lambda^2.
$$
For a $3\times3$ matrix, the coefficient of $\lambda^2$ in
$\det(\lambda I-B)$ is $-\operatorname{tr} B$. Comparing coefficients gives
$c=\operatorname{tr} B$.
:::

<1>3. Assertion (2) is true: $\operatorname{tr} B$ is an eigenvalue of $B$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
\chi(\operatorname{tr} B)=0.
$$
Thus $\operatorname{tr} B$ is a root of the characteristic polynomial and
hence an eigenvalue of $B$.
:::

<1>4. Assertion (3) is false.

::: {.proof}
Consider
$$
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its kernel is
$$
\ker B
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}
\right\},
$$
which is two-dimensional. Also $B^2=0$ but $B\neq0$. If $B$ were
diagonalizable, then $B=SDS^{-1}$ for a diagonal matrix $D$, and $B^2=0$
would imply $D^2=0$. Every diagonal entry of $D$ would therefore be zero, so
$D=0$ and hence $B=0$, a contradiction. Thus this $B$ is not diagonalizable.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>3 prove assertions (1) and (2), respectively, while step
<1>4 supplies a counterexample to assertion (3).
:::
:::
