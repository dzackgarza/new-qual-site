::: problem
Show that a principal ideal domain has Krull dimension zero or one.
:::

::: solution
Let $R$ be a PID.

<1>1. Every nonzero prime ideal of $R$ is maximal.
::: proof
Let $P=(p)$ be a nonzero prime ideal. Then $p$ is a prime element, hence
irreducible. In a PID an ideal generated by an irreducible element is maximal:
if
\[
(p)\subseteq(a)\subseteq R,
\]
then $p=ab$ for some $b\in R$. Irreducibility forces either $a$ or $b$ to be
a unit, so $(a)=R$ or $(a)=(p)$.
:::

<1>2. Hence every chain of prime ideals has length at most one.
::: proof
The zero ideal is prime because a PID is a domain. By <1>1, any nonzero prime
ideal is already maximal, so a strict chain can only have the form
\[
(0)\subsetneq P.
\]
Thus $\dim R\le1$.
:::

<1>3. If $R$ is a field, then $\dim R=0$; otherwise $\dim R=1$.
::: proof
For a field, $(0)$ is its only prime ideal. If $R$ is not a field, choose a
nonzero nonunit $a$. Factoring $a$ into irreducibles gives an irreducible
$p$, and then $(p)$ is a nonzero prime ideal. Thus
\[
(0)\subsetneq(p)
\]
is a prime chain of length one.
:::
:::
