::: {.problem}
Let $I$ be an ideal in a polynomial ring.
For $f \in I$, let $\operatorname{lt}(f)$ denote the sum of the terms of highest degree in $f$.
Explain how to compute
\[
\operatorname{lt}(I)=\langle \operatorname{lt}(f) \mid f \in I\rangle.
\]
:::


::: {.solution}
Write the polynomial ring as $R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and introduce a new
variable $t$. For $f\in R$ of degree $d$, let
$f^h=t^df(x_1/t,\ldots,x_n/t)\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n,t]$ be its total-degree
homogenization, and let $I^h$ be the ideal generated by $\{f^h:f\in I\}$.

<1>1. Let $I=(f_1,\ldots,f_r)$ and
$$
J=(f_1^h,\ldots,f_r^h):t^\infty.
$$
Then $J=I^h$.

::: {.proof}
If $f\in I$, write $f=\sum a_i f_i$. After homogenizing, multiplying by a
sufficient power of $t$ clears the degree discrepancies among the summands,
so
$$
t^N f^h\in(f_1^h,\ldots,f_r^h)
$$
for some $N$. Hence $f^h\in J$, and $I^h\subseteq J$.

Conversely, $J$ is homogeneous, being the saturation of a homogeneous ideal
with respect to the homogeneous element $t$. Let $F\in J$ be homogeneous, so
$t^NF\in(f_i^h)$ for some $N$. Setting $t=1$ gives
$g=F(x,1)\in I$. Dehomogenizing and rehomogenizing removes exactly the
power of $t$ dividing $F$, so $F=t^ag^h$ for some $a\ge0$, and $F\in I^h$.
:::

<1>2. If $f\in R$ has degree $d$, then
$$
f^h(x_1,\ldots,x_n,0)=\operatorname{lt}(f).
$$

::: {.proof}
Write $f=f_d+f_{d-1}+\cdots+f_0$ with each $f_j$ homogeneous of degree
$j$. Then
$$
f^h=f_d+t f_{d-1}+\cdots+t^d f_0.
$$
Setting $t=0$ leaves $f_d=\operatorname{lt}(f)$.
:::

<1>3. Under $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n,t]/(t)\cong k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$,
$$
\operatorname{lt}(I)=\frac{J+(t)}{(t)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $J$ is generated by the $f^h$ with $f\in I$. By step <1>2,
reduction modulo $t$ sends each $f^h$ to $\operatorname{lt}(f)$. The image of
$J$ is therefore the ideal generated by the $\operatorname{lt}(f)$ with
$f\in I$.
:::

<1>4. The following computes $\operatorname{lt}(I)$: compute
$(f_1^h,\ldots,f_r^h):t^\infty$ by elimination, as
$\bigl((f_1^h,\ldots,f_r^h)+(1-ut)\bigr)\cap k[x_1,\ldots,x_n,t]$ with a new
variable $u$, and set $t=0$ in its generators.

::: {.proof}
The elimination ideal is $J$, and step <1>3 identifies the image of $J$
modulo $t$ with $\operatorname{lt}(I)$.
:::
:::

::: {.example}
The ideal $(\operatorname{lt}(f_1),\ldots,\operatorname{lt}(f_r))$ can be
strictly smaller than $\operatorname{lt}(I)$. In $k[x]$, let $f_1=x+1$ and
$f_2=x$. Then $I=R$ and $\operatorname{lt}(I)=R$, since $1=f_1-f_2$ has
$\operatorname{lt}(1)=1$, while
$(\operatorname{lt}(f_1),\operatorname{lt}(f_2))=(x)$.
:::
