::: problem
Can a surjective homomorphism of rings fail to be finite?
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Decide whether a surjective ring homomorphism is always finite.

<1>1. A surjective ring homomorphism $\phi: R \to S$ is always finite.
<2>1. $S$ is generated as an $R$-module by the single element $1_S$.
Proof: for any $s \in S$, surjectivity gives $r \in R$ with $\phi(r) = s$, so $s = \phi(r) = \phi(r \cdot 1_R) = r \cdot \phi(1_R) = r \cdot 1_S$.
<2>2. Hence $S$ is a finitely generated $R$-module.
Proof: it is generated by one element.
<2>3. Hence $\phi$ is finite.
Proof: a ring homomorphism is finite iff the target is a finitely generated module over the source.

<1>2. Q.E.D. Proof: <1>1 shows a surjective homomorphism is always finite; the answer is "no, it cannot fail."
:::
