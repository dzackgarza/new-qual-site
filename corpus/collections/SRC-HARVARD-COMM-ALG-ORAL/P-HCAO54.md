::: problem
Give an example of a surface which has exactly one singular point but is not normal.
:::


::: solution
Work over \(k=\mathbb C\). Let
\[
A=k[s^4,s^3t,st^3,t^4]\subset k[s,t]
\]
and let
\[
X=\operatorname{Spec}A.
\]
This is the affine cone over the projective curve
\[
C:\mathbf P^1\longrightarrow\mathbf P^3,
\qquad
[s:t]\longmapsto[s^4:s^3t:st^3:t^4].
\]
We show that \(X\) is a surface with exactly one singular point and that \(A\)
is not normal.

<1>1. The map defining \(C\) is a closed immersion of \(\mathbf P^1\) onto a
smooth rational quartic.
::: proof
On the chart \(s\ne0\), write \(u=t/s\). The map becomes
\[
u\longmapsto[1:u:u^3:u^4],
\]
and \(u\) is recovered from the ratio of the second coordinate to the first.
On the chart \(t\ne0\), write \(v=s/t\). The map becomes
\[
v\longmapsto[v^4:v^3:v:1],
\]
and \(v\) is recovered from the ratio of the third coordinate to the fourth.
These two regular inverses show that the map is an isomorphism onto its image.
Hence \(C\cong\mathbf P^1\), so \(C\) is smooth.
:::

<1>2. \(X\) is a two-dimensional affine variety whose only possible singular
point is the cone vertex
\[
\mathfrak m=(s^4,s^3t,st^3,t^4).
\]
::: proof
The ring \(A\) is a finitely generated positively graded domain with
\(\operatorname{Proj}A=C\), so
\[
\dim A=\dim C+1=2.
\]
The punctured cone \(X\setminus\{\mathfrak m\}\) is the total space of the
\(\mathbf G_m\)-bundle associated with the tautological line bundle
\(\mathcal O_C(-1)\), with its zero section removed. Since \(C\) and
\(\mathbf G_m\) are smooth, this punctured cone is smooth. Therefore every
singular point of \(X\) must be the vertex.
:::

<1>3. The vertex \(\mathfrak m\) is singular.
::: proof
The maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) is generated by the four degree-one elements
of the graded ring \(A\). There are no linear relations among these four
projective coordinates, so their classes are linearly independent in
\[
\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2.
\]
Hence the embedding dimension at the vertex is
\[
\dim_k\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2=4.
\]
But \(\dim A_{\mathfrak m}=2\). A regular local ring has embedding dimension
equal to its Krull dimension, so \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) is not regular. Thus the
vertex is singular. Combined with <1>2, it is the unique singular point.
:::

<1>4. \(A\) is not integrally closed.
::: proof
Set
\[
z=s^2t^2.
\]
First \(z\in\operatorname{Frac}(A)\), since
\[
z=\frac{(s^3t)^2}{s^4}.
\]
Also
\[
z^2=s^4t^4\in A,
\]
so \(z\) is integral over \(A\), being a root of the monic polynomial
\[
T^2-s^4t^4\in A[T].
\]
However \(z\notin A\). Indeed, \(A\) is the semigroup ring generated by the
exponent vectors
\[
(4,0),(3,1),(1,3),(0,4).
\]
The monomial \(s^2t^2\) has exponent \((2,2)\). Since every generator has total
degree \(4\), any expression of \((2,2)\) as a nonnegative integral combination
of the generators would have to use exactly one generator, and \((2,2)\) is not
among them. Thus \(s^2t^2\notin A\).

Therefore \(A\) is not normal.
:::

<1>5. Hence \(X\) is a surface with exactly one singular point which is not
normal.
::: proof
By <1>2 and <1>3, \(X\) is a surface with unique singular point the vertex. By
<1>4, its coordinate ring is not integrally closed, so \(X\) is not normal.
:::
:::
