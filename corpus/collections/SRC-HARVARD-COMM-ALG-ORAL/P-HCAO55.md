::: problem
Let
\[
R=k[x,y]/(y^2-x^3).
\]

a. Prove that $R$ is not integrally closed.

b. State Serre's criterion for normality.

c. Determine the prime ideals of $R$ and interpret them on the curve.

d. Determine which parts of Serre's criterion $R$ satisfies.

e. Prove that $R_{(x,y)}$ is not a discrete valuation ring.

For the last part, use Nakayama's lemma to show that its maximal ideal cannot be generated by one element.
:::


::: solution
Use the parametrization
\[
R=k[t^2,t^3]\subset k[t],
\qquad x=t^2,\quad y=t^3.
\]
Its fraction field is \(k(t)\), and \(k[t]\) is the normalization of \(R\).

<1>1. \(R\) is not integrally closed.
::: proof
In \(\operatorname{Frac}(R)\),
\[
t=\frac{y}{x}.
\]
The element \(t\) is integral over \(R\), since it satisfies
\[
T^2-x=0.
\]
But \(t\notin k[t^2,t^3]\): every nonconstant monomial in this subring has
degree belonging to the numerical semigroup generated by \(2\) and \(3\), and
\(1\) is not in that semigroup. Thus \(R\) is not integrally closed.
:::

<1>2. Serre's criterion says that a Noetherian domain \(A\) is normal if and
only if it satisfies \(R_1\) and \(S_2\).
::: proof
Here \(R_1\) means that \(A_{\mathfrak p}\) is regular for every prime
\(\mathfrak p\) of height at most \(1\). The condition \(S_2\) means
\[
\operatorname{depth}A_{\mathfrak p}
\ge \min\{2,\dim A_{\mathfrak p}\}
\]
for every prime \(\mathfrak p\).
:::

<1>3. The prime ideals of \(R\) are the generic prime \((0)\) and the closed
points obtained by contracting primes of the normalization \(k[t]\).
::: proof
The extension \(R\subset k[t]\) is integral because \(t\) is integral over
\(R\). Since \(R\) is a one-dimensional domain, every nonzero prime is maximal.
Lying over gives every such prime as
\[
(q(t))\cap R
\]
for an irreducible polynomial \(q(t)\in k[t]\). Away from the cusp, the
normalization map is an isomorphism: if \(x=t^2\ne0\), then
\[
t=y/x\in R_x,
\]
so \(R_x=k[t]_{t^2}\). Thus there is exactly one point of the normalization over
each noncusp point, and the only point over the cusp is \((t)\), contracting to
\[
\mathfrak m=(x,y).
\]

If \(k\) is algebraically closed, this becomes the concrete list
\[
(0),\qquad
\mathfrak m_a=(x-a^2,\,y-a^3)\quad(a\in k).
\]
Geometrically, \((0)\) is the generic point of the cusp curve and the
\(\mathfrak m_a\) are its closed points; \(a=0\) is the cusp.
:::

<1>4. \(R\) satisfies \(S_2\) but fails \(R_1\).
::: proof
The ring \(R\) is a one-dimensional Noetherian domain. At every nonzero prime
\(\mathfrak p\), the local ring \(R_{\mathfrak p}\) has dimension \(1\), and
any nonzero element of \(\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}\) is a nonzerodivisor.
Hence
\[
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak p}\ge1
=\min\{2,\dim R_{\mathfrak p}\}.
\]
At the generic point the required depth is \(0\). Thus \(S_2\) holds.

At every noncusp closed point, <1>3 identifies the local ring with the
corresponding localization of \(k[t]\), hence with a DVR; these points are
regular. At the cusp \(\mathfrak m=(x,y)\), however,
\[
\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2=2,
\]
while
\[
\dim R_{\mathfrak m}=1.
\]
So \(R_{\mathfrak m}\) is not regular. Therefore \(R_1\) fails precisely at the
cusp. This agrees with <1>1 and Serre's criterion.
:::

<1>5. The local ring \(R_{\mathfrak m}\) is not a discrete valuation ring.
::: proof
Let \(A=R_{\mathfrak m}\) and let
\[
\mathfrak n=\mathfrak mA=(x,y)A.
\]
Its residue field is \(k\). Since \(y^2=x^3\),
\[
\mathfrak n^2=(x^2,xy,y^2)A=(x^2,xy,x^3)A.
\]
The classes of \(x\) and \(y\) in
\[
\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2
\]
are \(k\)-linearly independent, so
\[
\dim_k\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2=2.
\]
By Nakayama's lemma, the minimal number of generators of the finitely generated
\(A\)-module \(\mathfrak n\) is this dimension. Hence \(\mathfrak n\) requires
two generators and is not principal.

A DVR has principal maximal ideal. Therefore \(R_{\mathfrak m}\) is not a DVR.
:::
