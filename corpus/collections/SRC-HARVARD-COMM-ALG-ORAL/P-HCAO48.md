::: problem
Prove that every ideal in a Dedekind domain is generated by at most two elements.
:::


::: solution
Let \(A\) be a Dedekind domain and \(I\subseteq A\) a nonzero ideal. (The zero
ideal needs no generators.) We prove that \(I=(a,b)\) for suitable \(a,b\in I\).

<1>1. Choose \(0
e a\in I\). For every nonzero prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\),
write \(v_{\mathfrak p}(J)\) for the exponent of \(\mathfrak p\) in the unique
factorization of a nonzero ideal \(J\).
::: proof
Unique factorization of nonzero ideals into prime ideals is one of the defining
properties of a Dedekind domain. Since \(a\in I\), one has
\[
(a)\subseteq I,
\]
so
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}((a))\ge v_{\mathfrak p}(I)
\]
for every \(\mathfrak p\).
:::

<1>2. There exists \(b\in I\) such that
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}((b))=v_{\mathfrak p}(I)
\]
for every prime \(\mathfrak p\) dividing \((a)\).
::: proof
Only finitely many primes divide \((a)\). By the approximation theorem for
Dedekind domains, one may choose an element \(x\) of the fraction field with
prescribed valuations
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}(x)=v_{\mathfrak p}(I)
\]
at those primes and with
\[
v_{\mathfrak q}(x)\ge v_{\mathfrak q}(I)
\]
at all remaining primes. The latter inequalities say exactly that \(x\in I\).
Set \(b=x\).
:::

<1>3. The ideal generated by \(a\) and \(b\) equals \(I\).
::: proof
For nonzero ideals in a Dedekind domain,
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}(J+K)
=\min\{v_{\mathfrak p}(J),v_{\mathfrak p}(K)\}.
\]
Hence
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}((a,b))
=\min\{v_{\mathfrak p}((a)),v_{\mathfrak p}((b))\}.
\]
If \(\mathfrak p\mid(a)\), then by <1>2 this minimum is
\(v_{\mathfrak p}(I)\). If \(\mathfrak p
mid(a)\), then
\(v_{\mathfrak p}((a))=0\); because \(a\in I\), <1>1 then forces
\(v_{\mathfrak p}(I)=0\), so the minimum is again \(v_{\mathfrak p}(I)\).
Thus \((a,b)\) and \(I\) have the same valuation at every nonzero prime ideal.
Unique ideal factorization gives
\[
I=(a,b).
\]
:::

<1>4. Therefore every ideal of \(A\) is generated by at most two elements.
::: proof
The zero ideal is generated by \(0\), and every nonzero ideal is handled by
<1>1--<1>3.
:::
:::
