::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be an integral domain and let $F$ be its field of fractions.
For any nonzero element $f$ of $A$, let $A[f^{-1}]$ be the subring of $F$ generated by $A$ and $f^{-1}$.
Suppose that the ideal generated by $f_1, \ldots, f_k \in A \setminus \{0\}$ is the entire ring $A$, i.e. $(f_1, \ldots, f_k) = A$.

(a) Prove that for any positive integers $n_1, \ldots, n_k$ we have that
\[
(f_1^{n_1}, \ldots, f_k^{n_k}) = A.
\]

(b) Prove that $A = A[f_1^{-1}] \cap A[f_2^{-1}] \cap \cdots \cap A[f_k^{-1}]$.
(Hint: for $x$ in the right hand side consider $\{a \in A \mid ax \in A\}$).
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For arbitrary positive integers $n_1,\dots,n_k$, one has $(f_1^{n_1},\dots,f_k^{n_k})=A$.

::: pf-proof

Choose $a_1,\dots,a_k\in A$ with
\[
1=a_1f_1+\cdots+a_kf_k.
\]
Set
\[
N=(n_1-1)+\cdots+(n_k-1)+1.
\]
Then
\[
1=(a_1f_1+\cdots+a_kf_k)^N.
\]
Every monomial in the expansion has total degree $N$ in the factors $f_i$.
If every exponent of $f_i$ were at most $n_i-1$, the total degree would be at most
\[
(n_1-1)+\cdots+(n_k-1)=N-1,
\]
a contradiction.
Hence each monomial is divisible by $f_i^{n_i}$ for some $i$.
Therefore every monomial, and thus their sum $1$, lies in $(f_1^{n_1},\dots,f_k^{n_k})$.
So this ideal is $A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The inclusion
\[
A\subseteq A[f_1^{-1}]\cap\cdots\cap A[f_k^{-1}]
\]
is immediate.

::: pf-proof

Each localization subring $A[f_i^{-1}]$ contains $A$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let $x\in A[f_1^{-1}]\cap\cdots\cap A[f_k^{-1}]$ and define
\[
I_x=\{a\in A:ax\in A\}.
\]
Then $I_x$ is an ideal of $A$.

::: pf-proof

If $a,b\in I_x$, then $(a-b)x=ax-bx\in A$.
If $r\in A$ and $a\in I_x$, then $(ra)x=r(ax)\in A$.
Thus $I_x$ is an ideal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For each $i$, there exists $n_i\ge1$ such that $f_i^{n_i}\in I_x$.

::: pf-proof

Since $x\in A[f_i^{-1}]$, there are $a_i\in A$ and $n_i\ge0$ with
\[
x=\frac{a_i}{f_i^{n_i}}.
\]
Thus $f_i^{n_i}x=a_i\in A$, so $f_i^{n_i}\in I_x$.
If $n_i=0$, then $x\in A$ already and there is nothing to prove; otherwise take $n_i\ge1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore $I_x=A$ and hence $x\in A$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s4){.pf-ref}, the ideal $I_x$ contains $(f_1^{n_1},\dots,f_k^{n_k})$, which equals $A$ by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Hence $1\in I_x$.
By definition of $I_x$, this means $x=1\cdot x\in A$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently,
\[
A=A[f_1^{-1}]\cap\cdots\cap A[f_k^{-1}].
\]

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

:::
