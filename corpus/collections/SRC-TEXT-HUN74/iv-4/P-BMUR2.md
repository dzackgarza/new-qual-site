:::{.problem}
Show the following:

1.  For any abelian group $A$ and any positive integer $m$,
  \[
  \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_m, A) \cong A[m] \coloneqq \{ a\in A \mid ma = 0\}
  .\]

2.  $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_m, \mathbb{Z}_n) \cong \mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{gcd}(m,n)}$.

3.  As a $\mathbb{Z}-$module, $\mathbb{Z}_m^* = 0$.

4.  For each $k\geq 1$, $\mathbb{Z}_m$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_{mk}-$module,
    and as a $\mathbb{Z}_{mk}$ module,
    $\mathbb{Z}_m^* \cong \mathbb{Z}_m$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Part 1.**

<1>1. Define $\Phi: \operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ_m, A) \to A[m]$ by $\Phi(f) = f(\bar 1)$.
Proof: definition.

<1>2. $\Phi$ is well-defined: $f(\bar 1) \in A[m]$.
Proof: $m f(\bar 1) = f(m \bar 1) = f(\bar 0) = 0$, so $f(\bar 1) \in A[m]$.

<1>3. $\Phi$ is a homomorphism.
Proof: $\Phi(f + g) = (f+g)(\bar 1) = f(\bar 1) + g(\bar 1) = \Phi(f) + \Phi(g)$.

<1>4. $\Phi$ is injective.
Proof: if $\Phi(f) = f(\bar 1) = 0$, then $f(\bar k) = k f(\bar 1) = 0$ for all $k$, so $f = 0$.

<1>5. $\Phi$ is surjective.
Proof: given $a \in A[m]$, define $f(\bar k) = ka$; this is well-defined (if $\bar k = \bar k'$, then $k - k' = qm$, so $ka - k'a = qma = 0$), and $\Phi(f) = f(\bar 1) = a$.

<1>6. Hence $\operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ_m, A) \cong A[m]$.
Proof: <1>3–<1>5.

**Part 2.**

<1>1. By part 1, $\operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ_n) \cong \ZZ_n[m] = \{a \in \ZZ_n : ma = 0\}$.
Proof: part 1 with $A = \ZZ_n$.

<1>2. $\ZZ_n[m] = \{a \in \ZZ_n : ma \equiv 0 \pmod n\}$, which is the subgroup of $\ZZ_n$ of order $\gcd(m, n)$.
Proof: the solutions of $ma \equiv 0 \pmod n$ form the subgroup generated by $n/\gcd(m,n)$, of order $\gcd(m,n)$.

<1>3. Hence $\operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ_n) \cong \ZZ_{\gcd(m,n)}$.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.

**Part 3.**

<1>1. $\ZZ_m^* = \operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ) = 0$.
Proof: by part 1, $\operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ) \cong \ZZ[m] = \{a \in \ZZ : ma = 0\} = 0$ (since $\ZZ$ is torsion-free).

**Part 4.**

<1>1. $\ZZ_m$ is a $\ZZ_{mk}$-module via $\bar r \cdot \bar a = \overline{ra}$ (where $\bar r \in \ZZ_{mk}$ and $\bar a \in \ZZ_m$).
Proof: this is well-defined since $mk$ annihilates $\ZZ_m$ (i.e. $mk \cdot a = 0$ for all $a \in \ZZ_m$).

<1>2. $\ZZ_m^* = \operatorname{Hom}_{\ZZ_{mk}}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ_{mk})$.
Proof: definition of the dual.

<1>3. $\operatorname{Hom}_{\ZZ_{mk}}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ_{mk}) \cong \ZZ_m$.
<2>1. A $\ZZ_{mk}$-module homomorphism $f: \ZZ_m \to \ZZ_{mk}$ is determined by $f(\bar 1) \in \ZZ_{mk}$, subject to $m f(\bar 1) = 0$.
Proof: $f$ is determined by the image of the generator $\bar 1$, and $m \bar 1 = 0$ in $\ZZ_m$ forces $m f(\bar 1) = 0$.
<2>2. The elements $b \in \ZZ_{mk}$ with $mb = 0$ are exactly the multiples of $k$, i.e. $\{0, k, 2k, \ldots, (m-1)k\}$, which form a subgroup isomorphic to $\ZZ_m$.
Proof: $mb \equiv 0 \pmod{mk}$ iff $b$ is a multiple of $k$; there are $m$ such elements, forming a cyclic subgroup of order $m$.
<2>3. Hence $\operatorname{Hom}_{\ZZ_{mk}}(\ZZ_m, \ZZ_{mk}) \cong \ZZ_m$.
Proof: <2>1 and <2>2.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>6 (1), <1>3 (2), <1>1 (3), and <1>3 (4).
