::: {.problem}
Show that if $[F: K] < \infty$, then the following conditions are equivalent:

1. $F$ is Galois over $K$

2. $F$ is separable over $K$ and $F$ is a splitting field of some polynomial $f\in K[x]$.

3. $F$ is a splitting field over $K$ of some polynomial $f\in K[x]$ whose irreducible factors are separable.
:::

::: solution
Assume throughout that $[F:K]<\infty$.

<1>1. Condition 1 implies condition 2.
::: proof
Suppose $F/K$ is Galois. Then $F/K$ is separable and normal.

Choose finitely many elements $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r\in F$ generating $F$
over $K$; such a finite generating set exists because $F/K$ is finite. Let
$m_i\in K[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha_i$ over $K$ and set
\[
f=\prod_{i=1}^r m_i.
\]
Normality implies that every $m_i$ splits over $F$. Hence $f$ splits over $F$.
Conversely, any splitting field of $f$ over $K$ contains every $\alpha_i$, and
therefore contains
\[
K(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)=F.
\]
Thus $F$ is the splitting field of $f$ over $K$. Together with separability,
this is condition 2.
:::

<1>2. Condition 2 implies condition 3.
::: proof
Suppose $F/K$ is separable and $F$ is the splitting field of
$f\in K[x]$. Let $p\in K[x]$ be an irreducible factor of $f$. Since $f$ splits
over $F$, the polynomial $p$ has a root $\alpha\in F$. Because $F/K$ is
separable, $\alpha$ is separable over $K$. Its minimal polynomial over $K$ is
$p$ up to a nonzero scalar, so $p$ is separable. Thus every irreducible factor
of $f$ is separable, giving condition 3.
:::

<1>3. Condition 3 implies that $F/K$ is normal.
::: proof
Suppose $F$ is the splitting field over $K$ of a polynomial $f\in K[x]$ whose
irreducible factors are separable. Every splitting-field extension is normal:
if an irreducible polynomial over $K$ has one root in $F$ among the roots used
to generate the splitting field, all of its conjugate roots also occur in the
splitting field. Equivalently, a splitting field of a family of polynomials over
$K$ is normal over $K$. Hence $F/K$ is normal.
:::

<1>4. Condition 3 implies that $F/K$ is separable.
::: proof
Write the distinct irreducible factors of $f$ as
\[
p_1,\ldots,p_s.
\]
By hypothesis each $p_i$ is separable, so every root of every $p_i$ is separable
over $K$. The field $F$ is generated over $K$ by finitely many such roots.
A field generated by finitely many elements separable over $K$ is a separable
extension of $K$. Therefore $F/K$ is separable.
:::

<1>5. Condition 3 implies condition 1.
::: proof
By <1>3 and <1>4, $F/K$ is both normal and separable. Since it is finite by
hypothesis, it is Galois.
:::

<1>6. The three conditions are equivalent.
::: proof
The implications <1>1, <1>2, and <1>5 give
\[
1\Longrightarrow2\Longrightarrow3\Longrightarrow1.
\]
:::
:::
