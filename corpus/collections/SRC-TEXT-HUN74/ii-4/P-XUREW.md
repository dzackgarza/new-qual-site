::: {.problem}
Let $Z(G)$ be the center of $G$.
Show that if $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, then $G$ is abelian.

> Note that Hungerford uses the notation $C(G)$ for the center.
:::

::: solution
Assume that $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, generated by $xZ(G)$ for some $x\in G$.

<1>1. Every element of $G$ has the form $x^m z$ with $m\in\ZZ$ and
$z\in Z(G)$.
::: proof
Let $g\in G$. Since $xZ(G)$ generates $G/Z(G)$, there is $m\in\ZZ$ such that
\[
gZ(G)=x^mZ(G).
\]
Hence $x^{-m}g\in Z(G)$. Setting $z=x^{-m}g$ gives $g=x^m z$.
:::

<1>2. Any two elements of $G$ commute.
::: proof
Let
\[
g=x^m z,\qquad h=x^n w
\]
with $z,w\in Z(G)$, as in <1>1. Since $z$ and $w$ commute with every element
of $G$,
\[
gh=x^mzx^nw=x^{m+n}zw=x^{n+m}wz=x^nwx^mz=hg.
\]
:::

<1>3. Therefore $G$ is abelian.
::: proof
By <1>2 every pair of elements of $G$ commutes.
:::
:::
