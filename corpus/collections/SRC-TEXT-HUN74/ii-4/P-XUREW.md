::: {.problem}
Let $Z(G)$ be the center of $G$.
Show that if $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, then $G$ is abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume that $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, generated by $xZ(G)$ for some $x\in G$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Every element of $G$ has the form $x^m z$ with $m\in\ZZ$ and
$z\in Z(G)$.

::: pf-proof

Let $g\in G$. Since $xZ(G)$ generates $G/Z(G)$, there is $m\in\ZZ$ such that
$$
gZ(G)=x^mZ(G).
$$
Hence $x^{-m}g\in Z(G)$. Setting $z=x^{-m}g$ gives $g=x^m z$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Any two elements of $G$ commute.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
g=x^m z,\qquad h=x^n w
$$
with $z,w\in Z(G)$, as in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Since $z$ and $w$ commute with every element
of $G$,
$$
gh=x^mzx^nw=x^{m+n}zw=x^{n+m}wz=x^nwx^mz=hg.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $G$ is abelian.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref} every pair of elements of $G$ commutes.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark}
Hungerford writes $C(G)$ for the center.
:::
