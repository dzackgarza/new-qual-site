::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be a subfield of $\mathbb{R}$ and let $f \in K[x]$ be an irreducible quartic.
If $f$ has exactly 2 real roots, show that the Galois group of $f$ is either $S_{4}$ or $D_{4}$.
:::

::: solution
Let $F\subseteq\CC$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $K$, and let
\[
G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K).
\]
Label the four roots of $f$ by $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\alpha_4$.

<1>1. The natural action of $G$ on the four roots is faithful and transitive.
::: proof
An automorphism of the splitting field is determined by its action on the roots,
since those roots generate $F$ over $K$. Hence the action is faithful.

Because $f$ is irreducible over $K$, the Galois group of its splitting field
acts transitively on its roots: for any two roots, the corresponding
$K$-embeddings of their simple extensions extend to automorphisms of the
splitting field. Thus $G$ is a transitive subgroup of $S_4$.
:::

<1>2. The group $G$ contains a transposition.
::: proof
Since $K\subseteq\RR$, complex conjugation fixes $K$. It preserves the splitting
field $F$, because it permutes the roots of the polynomial $f\in K[x]$.
Therefore complex conjugation restricts to an element of $G$.

Exactly two roots of $f$ are real, so conjugation fixes those two roots. The two
nonreal roots form one conjugate pair and are interchanged. Thus, as a
permutation of the four roots, complex conjugation is a single transposition.
:::

<1>3. A transitive subgroup of $S_4$ is isomorphic to one of
\[
C_4,\qquad V_4,\qquad D_4,\qquad A_4,\qquad S_4.
\]
::: proof
Let $H\le S_4$ be transitive. Orbit-stabilizer shows that $4\mid|H|$, so by
Lagrange
\[
|H|\in\{4,8,12,24\}.
\]

If $|H|=24$, then $H=S_4$. If $|H|=12$, it has index $2$ in $S_4$, hence is
$A_4$. If $|H|=8$, it is a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_4$, hence isomorphic to the
dihedral group $D_4$ of order $8$.

If $|H|=4$, then $H$ is either cyclic or Klein four. Transitivity rules out the
Klein subgroup containing ordinary transpositions and leaves, up to conjugacy,
either the regular cyclic subgroup generated by a $4$-cycle or the regular
Klein four subgroup
\[
\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}.
\]
Thus the abstract possibilities are $C_4$ and $V_4$.
:::

<1>4. Among the five groups in <1>3, only $D_4$ and $S_4$ contain a
transposition in their transitive degree-$4$ actions.
::: proof
The cyclic group generated by a $4$-cycle has elements of cycle types
\[
1,\quad (4),\quad (2,2),\quad (4),
\]
so it contains no transposition. The regular Klein four group consists of the
identity and three double transpositions, so it contains none either. The group
$A_4$ contains only even permutations and hence no transposition.

The dihedral subgroup $D_4$ contains reflections across diagonals of the square;
in the action on its four vertices these are transpositions. Of course $S_4$
also contains transpositions.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
G\cong D_4\qquad\text{or}\qquad G\cong S_4.
\]
::: proof
By <1>1, $G$ is transitive; by <1>2, it contains a transposition. Apply <1>3 and
<1>4.
:::
:::
