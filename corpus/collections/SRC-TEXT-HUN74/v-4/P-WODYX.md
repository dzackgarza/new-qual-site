::: {.problem}
Determine the Galois groups of the following polynomials over the corresponding fields:

1. $x^4- 5$ over $\mathbb Q, \mathbb Q(\sqrt 5), \mathbb Q(i\sqrt 5)$.

2. $x^3 - 2$ over $\mathbb Q$.

3. $(x^3-2)(x^2-5)$ over $\mathbb Q$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Let $\alpha=\sqrt[4]{5}$. The splitting field of $x^4-5$ over
$\QQ$ is
\[
L=\QQ(\alpha,i).
\]
::: proof
The roots are
\[
\alpha,-\alpha,i\alpha,-i\alpha,
\]
so $\QQ(\alpha,i)$ contains all roots and is generated by them.
:::

<1>2. Over $\QQ$, the Galois group of $x^4-5$ is the dihedral group
$D_4$ of order $8$.
::: proof
The polynomial $x^4-5$ is Eisenstein at $5$, hence
\[
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4.
\]
Since $\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq\RR$, it does not contain $i$, so
\[
[L:\QQ]=8.
\]
Define
\[
r(\alpha)=i\alpha,\quad r(i)=i,
\qquad
s(\alpha)=\alpha,\quad s(i)=-i.
\]
Then $r$ has order $4$, $s$ has order $2$, and
\[
srs=r^{-1}.
\]
These automorphisms generate $8$ elements, hence all of
$\operatorname{Gal}(L/\QQ)$. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(x^4-5/\QQ)\cong D_4.
\]
:::

<1>3. Over $\QQ(\sqrt5)$, the Galois group of $x^4-5$ is
$\ZZ_2\times\ZZ_2$.
::: proof
Since $\sqrt5=\alpha^2$, the fixed subgroup is
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\QQ(\sqrt5))
=\langle r^2,s\rangle.
\]
Both $r^2$ and $s$ have order $2$ and they commute. The subgroup has order
$4$, as required because $[\QQ(\sqrt5):\QQ]=2$ and $[L:\QQ]=8$. Hence it is
the Klein four group.
:::

<1>4. Over $\QQ(i\sqrt5)$, the Galois group of $x^4-5$ is also
$\ZZ_2\times\ZZ_2$.
::: proof
Put $\beta=i\alpha^2=i\sqrt5$. Under $r$ one has $r(\beta)=-\beta$, while
under $s$ one also has $s(\beta)=-\beta$. Thus the elements fixing $\beta$ are
\[
1,\quad r^2,\quad rs,\quad r^3s.
\]
They form the subgroup
\[
\langle r^2,rs\rangle.
\]
Both generators are involutions and commute, so this subgroup is
$\ZZ_2\times\ZZ_2$. Its order is $4$, agreeing with
$[L:\QQ(i\sqrt5)]=4$.
:::

<1>5. The Galois group of $x^3-2$ over $\QQ$ is $S_3$.
::: proof
Let $\gamma=\sqrt[3]{2}$ and let $\omega$ be a primitive cube root of unity.
The splitting field is
\[
M=\QQ(\gamma,\omega).
\]
The polynomial $x^3-2$ is Eisenstein at $2$, so
$[\QQ(\gamma):\QQ]=3$. Since $\QQ(\gamma)\subseteq\RR$ and
$\omega\notin\RR$,
\[
[M:\QQ]=6.
\]
The Galois group acts faithfully on the three roots
$\gamma,\omega\gamma,\omega^2\gamma$, hence embeds in $S_3$. Since its order is
$6$, it is all of $S_3$.
:::

<1>6. The splitting field of $(x^3-2)(x^2-5)$ over $\QQ$ is
\[
N=M(\sqrt5),
\]
where $M=\QQ(\sqrt[3]{2},\omega)$.
::: proof
The first factor splits in $M$ and the second in $\QQ(\sqrt5)$, so their
product has splitting field equal to the compositum of these two splitting
fields.
:::

<1>7. One has
\[
M\cap\QQ(\sqrt5)=\QQ.
\]
::: proof
The intersection is a subfield of the quadratic extension
$\QQ(\sqrt5)/\QQ$, so it is either $\QQ$ or $\QQ(\sqrt5)$.

If $\QQ(\sqrt5)\subseteq M$, it would be a quadratic subfield of the
$S_3$-Galois extension $M/\QQ$. Such subfields correspond to index-$2$
subgroups of $S_3$. The unique index-$2$ subgroup is $A_3$, whose fixed field is
the quadratic field generated by the square root of the discriminant of
$x^3-2$. Since
\[
\operatorname{disc}(x^3-2)=-108=-3\cdot6^2,
\]
that field is $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})=\QQ(\omega)$, not $\QQ(\sqrt5)$.
Therefore the intersection is $\QQ$.
:::

<1>8. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Gal}((x^3-2)(x^2-5)/\QQ)
\cong S_3\times\ZZ_2.
\]
::: proof
Both $M/\QQ$ and $\QQ(\sqrt5)/\QQ$ are finite Galois extensions and their
intersection is $\QQ$ by <1>7. Therefore restriction gives
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(N/\QQ)
\cong
\operatorname{Gal}(M/\QQ)\times
\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\sqrt5)/\QQ)
\cong S_3\times\ZZ_2.
\]
:::
