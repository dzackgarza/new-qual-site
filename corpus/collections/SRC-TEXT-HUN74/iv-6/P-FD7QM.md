::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a PID, and let $A,B$ be cyclic $R$-modules of nonzero orders $r,s$
respectively, where $r$ is not relatively prime to $s$.
Show that the invariant factors of $A \oplus B$ are $\mathrm{gcd}(r, s)$ and $\mathrm{lcm}(r, s)$.
:::

::: solution
Since $A$ and $B$ are cyclic of orders $r$ and $s$,
\[
A\cong R/(r),
\qquad
B\cong R/(s).
\]
Thus $A\oplus B$ is presented by the diagonal matrix
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}r&0\\0&s\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Let
\[
d=\gcd(r,s),
\qquad
m=\operatorname{lcm}(r,s),
\]
with representatives chosen up to multiplication by units.

<1>1. The first determinantal divisor of $D$ is associated to $d$.
::: proof
The first determinantal divisor is a generator of the ideal generated by all
$1\times1$ minors of $D$, namely
\[
(r,s).
\]
In a PID this ideal is generated by $\gcd(r,s)=d$.
:::

<1>2. The second determinantal divisor of $D$ is associated to $rs$.
::: proof
The only $2\times2$ minor is
\[
\det D=rs.
\]
Hence the second determinantal divisor is associated to $rs$.
:::

<1>3. If $d_1,d_2$ are the invariant factors of $A\oplus B$, then
\[
d_1\sim d,
\qquad
d_1d_2\sim rs.
\]
::: proof
For a presentation matrix over a PID, the Smith normal form theorem identifies
the products of the first $j$ invariant factors with the $j$th determinantal
divisors. Apply this for $j=1,2$ and use <1>1 and <1>2.
:::

<1>4. The second invariant factor is associated to $m$.
::: proof
In a PID,
\[
\gcd(r,s)\operatorname{lcm}(r,s)\sim rs.
\]
Thus $dm\sim rs$. Combining this with <1>3 and $d_1\sim d$ gives
$d_2\sim m$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore the invariant factors of $A\oplus B$ are
\[
\gcd(r,s),
\qquad
\operatorname{lcm}(r,s).
\]
::: proof
By <1>3 and <1>4 the invariant factors are associated to $d$ and $m$.
Moreover $d\mid m$, as required for invariant-factor order. Invariant factors are
defined only up to multiplication by units, so these representatives give the
claimed pair.
:::
:::
