::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be a field, $f\in K[x]$ an irreducible polynomial of degree $n\geq 5$ and $F$ a splitting field of $f$ over $K$.
Assume that $Aut_{k}(F)\simeq S_{n}$.
Let $u$ be a root of $f$ in $F$.
Then,

1. $K(u)$ is not Galois over $K$; $[K(u):K]=n$ and $Aut_{K}(K(u))=1$ (and hence solvable).

2. Every normal closure over $K$ that contains $u$ also contains an isomorphic copy of $F$.

3. There is no radical extension field $E$ of $K$ such that $K\subset K(u)\subset E$.
:::


::: solution
Let
\[
G=\operatorname{Aut}_K(F)\cong S_n.
\]
We first identify \(F/K\) as a Galois extension with this full Galois group.

<1>1. One has
\[
[F:K]=n!,
\]
and \(F/K\) is Galois.
::: proof
Because \(F\) is a splitting field of a polynomial of degree \(n\), adjoining
its roots one at a time gives
\[
[F:K]\le n!.
\]
For every finite extension, the number of \(K\)-automorphisms is at most its
degree, so
\[
n!=|\operatorname{Aut}_K(F)|\le [F:K]\le n!.
\]
Hence equality holds throughout. A finite extension has as many
\(K\)-automorphisms as its degree exactly when it is Galois. Thus \(F/K\) is
Galois and
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)=G\cong S_n.
\]
:::

<1>2. The action of \(G\) on the \(n\) roots of \(f\) is the natural faithful
transitive action of \(S_n\).
::: proof
Since \(f\) is irreducible, \(G\) acts transitively on its roots. Since \(F\)
is generated by those roots, an automorphism fixing every root is the identity,
so the action is faithful. A faithful action of a group of order \(n!\) on an
\(n\)-element set identifies \(G\) with the full symmetric group on that set.
:::

<1>3. Let
\[
H=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K(u)).
\]
Then \(H\) is the stabilizer of the root \(u\), hence \(H\cong S_{n-1}\), and
\[
[K(u):K]=[G:H]=n.
\]
::: proof
By the Galois correspondence, \(H\) consists exactly of the automorphisms of
\(F\) fixing \(u\). Under the action in <1>2, this is the stabilizer of one
point, so it is isomorphic to \(S_{n-1}\). Therefore
\[
[K(u):K]=[G:H]=\frac{n!}{(n-1)!}=n.
\]
:::

<1>4. \(K(u)/K\) is not Galois and
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_K(K(u))=1.
\]
::: proof
The extension \(K(u)/K\) is Galois if and only if its corresponding subgroup
\(H\) is normal in \(G\). A point stabilizer \(S_{n-1}\) is not normal in
\(S_n\) for \(n\ge3\), so \(K(u)/K\) is not Galois.

For an intermediate field \(F^H\) of a finite Galois extension, restriction
induces
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_K(F^H)\cong N_G(H)/H.
\]
Here \(H\) is a point stabilizer. If \(g\in N_G(H)\), then
\[
gHg^{-1}=H.
\]
But \(gHg^{-1}\) is the stabilizer of \(g(u)\). Distinct points have distinct
stabilizers in the natural \(S_n\)-action, so \(g(u)=u\), hence \(g\in H\).
Thus \(N_G(H)=H\), and therefore
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_K(K(u))\cong H/H=1.
\]
:::

<1>5. Every normal extension of \(K\) containing \(u\) contains a \(K\)-copy
of \(F\); inside a fixed algebraic closure it contains \(F\) itself.
::: proof
Let \(N/K\) be normal and suppose \(u\in N\). Since \(f\) is the minimal
polynomial of \(u\) over \(K\), normality implies that every \(K\)-conjugate of
\(u\), equivalently every root of \(f\), lies in \(N\). The field generated by
all roots of \(f\) is its splitting field. Hence \(N\) contains the splitting
field of \(f\), which is \(K\)-isomorphic to \(F\), and equals \(F\) after
choosing both inside the same algebraic closure.
:::

<1>6. There is no radical extension \(E/K\) satisfying
\[
K\subset K(u)\subset E.
\]
::: proof
By <1>1, \(K(u)/K\) is finite separable, and by <1>5 its normal closure is
\(F\). The solvability-by-radicals theorem says that a finite separable
extension is contained in a radical extension only if the Galois group of its
normal closure is solvable. Here that group is
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)\cong S_n.
\]
For \(n\ge5\), \(S_n\) is not solvable because it contains the nonabelian simple
normal subgroup \(A_n\). Therefore \(K(u)\) cannot be contained in any radical
extension of \(K\).
:::
