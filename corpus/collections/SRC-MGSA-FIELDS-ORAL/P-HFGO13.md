::: {.problem}
Define the splitting field of a polynomial over a field.
:::

::: {.solution}
The question asks for a definition, so the answer is the definition itself; the numbered steps state it and justify the accompanying claims — the equivalent description inside an algebraic closure, the minimality clause, and existence and uniqueness. The exam compilation in this collection's `provenance` (`assets/attachments/MGSA_Fields_and_Galois_Theory_Oral_Questions.pdf`) is a question list carrying no solutions, so the definition given is the standard one (Lang, *Algebra*, V §3) and the uniqueness statement is cited, not reproved.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #definition}
Let $f\in F[x]$ be nonconstant. A **splitting field** of $f$ over $F$ is an extension $K\supseteq F$ such that (1) $f$ factors into linear factors in $K[x]$, and (2) $K=F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)$ where $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r$ are the roots of $f$ in $K$.

::: pf-proof
A definition, needing no proof. Both clauses are essential: $\CC$ satisfies (1) for every $f\in\QQ[x]$ but not (2) for $f=X^2+1$, whose splitting field is $\QQ(i)\subsetneq\CC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #minimality}
Clause (2) is equivalent to: no proper intermediate field $F\subseteq L\subsetneq K$ contains all roots of $f$; equivalently $K$ is generated over $F$ by the roots.

::: pf-proof
The field generated by a set $S$ over $F$ is the intersection of all subfields of $K$ containing $F$ and $S$, so $K=F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)$ iff no proper subfield of $K$ contains $F$ and all $\alpha_j$. If $L$ is a proper intermediate field containing all the roots, then $F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)\subseteq L\subsetneq K$, contradicting $K=F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)$; conversely, if $K=F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)$ then any intermediate field containing $F$ and the roots contains the field they generate, namely $K$, so it is not proper.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inside-closure}
Inside a fixed algebraic closure $\overline F$, the roots of $f$ are the $\alpha_j$, and the splitting field is the subfield $F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)\subseteq\overline F$.

::: pf-proof
$f$ has a root in $\overline F$, because $\overline F$ is algebraically closed; dividing by the corresponding linear factor and repeating, $f$ splits in $\overline F[x]$, with roots $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r$ (distinct, and of multiplicities $m_j$) all lying in $\overline F$. Since $f$ splits in $F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)$, that field satisfies the two clauses of step [](#definition){.pf-ref}, and it is contained in $\overline F$ because $\overline F$ is a field containing $F$ and the $\alpha_j$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #existence-uniqueness}
A splitting field of $f$ over $F$ exists, is finite over $F$ of degree at most $\deg f$, and any two splitting fields of $f$ over $F$ are isomorphic over $F$.

::: pf-proof
Existence and the degree bound: step [](#inside-closure){.pf-ref} produces one as $F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)$, and adjoining a root of a polynomial of degree $d$ gives an extension of degree at most $d$, so $[K:F]\le\deg f$ by induction on the number of roots adjoined. Uniqueness: if $K,K'$ are splitting fields inside algebraic closures, the isomorphism of step [](#inside-closure){.pf-ref} extends $F$-embeddings, and an $F$-embedding of $K$ into $\overline F'$ carries roots of $f$ to roots of $f$, hence carries the generating set $\theset{\alpha_j}$ onto the corresponding generating set of $K'$, so its image is $K'$ and it is an $F$-isomorphism $K\to K'$. So the splitting field is well defined up to $F$-isomorphism, and it makes sense to speak of *the* splitting field.

:::
