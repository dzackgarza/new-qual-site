::: problem
Let $R$ be a PID.

a. Classify irreducible $R\dash$modules up to isomorphism.

b. Classify indecomposable $R\dash$modules up to isomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The irreducible \(R\)-modules are exactly the modules \(R/(p)\), where \(p\in R\) is irreducible.
::: {.proof}
Let \(M\neq0\) be irreducible and choose \(0\neq m\in M\). Then \(Rm\) is a nonzero submodule, hence \(Rm=M\), so \(M\) is cyclic. Thus \(M\cong R/\operatorname{Ann}(m)\). Simplicity says that \(\operatorname{Ann}(m)\) is maximal. In a PID every nonzero maximal ideal is generated by an irreducible element \(p\), giving \(M\cong R/(p)\). Conversely, \(R/(p)\) is a field as an \(R\)-algebra, so it is simple as an \(R\)-module.
:::

<1>2. Part (b), as literally stated for arbitrary \(R\)-modules, is not classified by the finitely generated structure theorem and the usual answer \(R\) or \(R/(p^n)\) is false without a finite-generation hypothesis.
::: {.proof}
Take \(R=\mathbb Z\) and \(M=\mathbb Q\). If \(\mathbb Q=A\oplus B\) as \(\mathbb Z\)-modules, the corresponding projection \(\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q\) is an idempotent endomorphism. Every group endomorphism of \(\mathbb Q\) is multiplication by a rational number, so an idempotent endomorphism is multiplication by \(0\) or \(1\). Hence one of \(A,B\) is zero, and \(\mathbb Q\) is indecomposable. But \(\mathbb Q\) is neither \(\mathbb Z\) nor \(\mathbb Z/(p^n)\).
:::

<1>3. Under the standard intended hypothesis that \(M\) is finitely generated, the indecomposable \(R\)-modules are exactly \(R\) and \(R/(p^n)\), where \(p\) is irreducible and \(n\ge1\).
::: {.proof}
By the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID,
\[
M\cong R^r\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^t R/(p_i^{n_i}).
\]
If \(M\) is indecomposable, this direct sum can have exactly one nonzero summand. Thus either \(r=1,t=0\), giving \(M\cong R\), or \(r=0,t=1\), giving \(M\cong R/(p^n)\).

Conversely, \(R\) is indecomposable because a decomposition \(R=A\oplus B\) would give a nontrivial idempotent in the domain \(R\), impossible. Also \(R/(p^n)\) is indecomposable because its submodules form the chain
\[
0\subset (p^{n-1})/(p^n)\subset\cdots\subset (p)/(p^n)\subset R/(p^n),
\]
so two nonzero submodules cannot have zero intersection and hence cannot form a nontrivial internal direct sum.
:::
:::
