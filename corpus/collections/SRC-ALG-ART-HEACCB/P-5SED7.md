::: problem
Let $R$ be a PID.

a. Classify irreducible $R\dash$modules up to isomorphism.

b. Classify indecomposable $R\dash$modules up to isomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The irreducible $R$-modules are exactly the quotients $R/\mathfrak m$, where $\mathfrak m$ is a maximal ideal of $R$.
::: {.proof}
Let $M\neq 0$ be irreducible and choose $0\neq x\in M$.
Then $Rx$ is a nonzero submodule, so $Rx=M$.
Hence $M\cong R/\operatorname{Ann}(x)$.
Submodules of $R/\operatorname{Ann}(x)$ correspond to ideals containing $\operatorname{Ann}(x)$; irreducibility therefore says $\operatorname{Ann}(x)$ is maximal.
Conversely, if $\mathfrak m$ is maximal, then $R/\mathfrak m$ has no nonzero proper $R$-submodules.

If $R$ is not a field, every maximal ideal of the PID $R$ is generated by an irreducible element $p$, so these are precisely the modules $R/(p)$.
If $R$ is a field, the unique irreducible module up to isomorphism is $R=R/(0)$.
:::

<1>2. Part (b), as written without a finite-generation hypothesis, does not have the finite list asserted by the standard PID structure theorem.
::: {.proof}
The structure theorem used in the source solution applies to finitely generated modules, not arbitrary modules.
For example, with $R=\mathbb Z$, the $\mathbb Z$-module $\mathbb Q$ is indecomposable but is neither $\mathbb Z$ nor $\mathbb Z/(p^n)$.

Indeed, every $\mathbb Z$-linear endomorphism $f:\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q$ is multiplication by $f(1)\in\mathbb Q$: for $a/b\in\mathbb Q$,
\[
bf(a/b)=f(a)=a f(1),
\]
so $f(a/b)=(a/b)f(1)$.
Thus
\[
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q.
\]
If $\mathbb Q=M_1\oplus M_2$ were a nontrivial direct sum, projection onto $M_1$ would be a nontrivial idempotent endomorphism.
But a field has only the idempotents $0$ and $1$.
Hence $\mathbb Q$ is indecomposable.
:::

<1>3. Under the standard intended hypothesis that $M$ is finitely generated, the indecomposable $R$-modules are exactly
\[
R
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
R/(p^n),
\]
where $p$ is irreducible and $n\ge 1$ (with the torsion family absent when $R$ is a field).
::: {.proof}
By the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID,
\[
M\cong R^r\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^t R/(p_i^{n_i}).
\]
If $M$ is indecomposable, this direct sum can have only one nonzero summand.
Hence either $M\cong R$ or $M\cong R/(p^n)$.

Conversely, $R$ is indecomposable because $\operatorname{End}_R(R)\cong R$, and a domain has no idempotents other than $0$ and $1$.
Also $\operatorname{End}_R(R/(p^n))\cong R/(p^n)$.
The quotient $R/(p^n)$ is local with unique maximal ideal generated by the class of $p$.
A local ring has only the idempotents $0$ and $1$: if $e^2=e$, then $e(1-e)=0$, and one of $e,1-e$ is a unit, forcing $e=1$ or $e=0$.
Therefore $R/(p^n)$ is indecomposable.
:::
