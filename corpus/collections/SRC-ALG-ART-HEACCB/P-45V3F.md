::: problem
Exhibit the rational canonical form for

- $A\in M_6(\QQ)$ with minimal polynomial $(x-1)(x^2 + 1)^2$.

- $A\in M_{10}(\QQ)$ with minimal polynomial $(x^2+1)^2(x^3 + 1)$.
:::


::: {.solution}
For a monic polynomial \(f\), write \(C(f)\) for its companion matrix. In rational canonical form, the matrix is block diagonal with companion blocks \(C(d_1),\dots,C(d_r)\), where the invariant factors satisfy
\[
d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_r,
\]
their degrees sum to the matrix size, and \(d_r\) is the minimal polynomial.

<1>1. For the \(6\times6\) matrix, put
\[
m_1=(x-1)(x^2+1)^2.
\]
Then \(\deg m_1=5\), so the invariant factors must be
\[
x-1,\qquad m_1.
\]
Hence the rational canonical form is
\[
C(x-1)\oplus C\big((x-1)(x^2+1)^2\big).
\]
::: {.proof}
The largest invariant factor must equal \(m_1\) and already contributes degree \(5\). The remaining invariant factors must have total degree \(1\) and divide \(m_1\). Over \(\mathbb Q\), the only monic linear divisor of \(m_1\) is \(x-1\). Thus the displayed pair is forced, and its degrees sum to \(6\).
:::

<1>2. For the \(10\times10\) matrix, put
\[
m_2=(x^2+1)^2(x^3+1)
=(x^2+1)^2(x+1)(x^2-x+1).
\]
Then \(\deg m_2=7\), so the invariant factors before \(m_2\) must have total degree \(3\).
::: {.proof}
Again the largest invariant factor is the minimal polynomial. Since the matrix size is \(10\), the remaining block degrees sum to \(10-7=3\).
:::

<1>3. There are exactly three possible invariant-factor lists:
\[
(x+1)(x^2+1),\quad m_2,
\]
\[
(x+1)(x^2-x+1),\quad m_2,
\]
and
\[
x+1,\quad x+1,\quad x+1,\quad m_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
Factor \(m_2=a^2bc\), where
\[
a=x^2+1,\qquad b=x+1,\qquad c=x^2-x+1,
\]
with \(\deg a=\deg c=2\) and \(\deg b=1\). If there is one earlier invariant factor, it must be a degree-\(3\) divisor of \(m_2\), hence either \(ab\) or \(bc\). If there are two earlier invariant factors, their positive degrees must be \(1\) and \(2\); the degree-\(1\) factor must be \(b\), but no degree-\(2\) divisor of \(m_2\) is divisible by \(b\), so this case is impossible. If there are three earlier invariant factors, all must have degree \(1\), hence all are \(b\). More earlier factors are impossible because their positive degrees would sum to more than \(3\).
:::

<1>4. Therefore the possible rational canonical forms in the \(10\times10\) case are
\[
C\big((x+1)(x^2+1)\big)\oplus C(m_2),
\]
\[
C\big((x+1)(x^2-x+1)\big)\oplus C(m_2),
\]
and
\[
C(x+1)\oplus C(x+1)\oplus C(x+1)\oplus C(m_2).
\]
::: {.proof}
Each displayed list satisfies the divisibility condition, has total degree \(10\), and has largest invariant factor \(m_2\). Conversely, <1>3 exhausts all possible invariant-factor lists.
:::
:::
