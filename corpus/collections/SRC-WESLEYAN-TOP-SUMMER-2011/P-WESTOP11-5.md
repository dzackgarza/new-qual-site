::: {.problem}
1. Give a detailed definition of the singular homology group $H_p(X)$ for a fixed positive integer $p$.
2. Find a CW complex $X$ such that
   \[
   H_5(X)\cong\ZZ/2011\ZZ,
   \qquad
   H_6(X)\cong\ZZ,
   \]
   and all other positive-dimensional homology groups vanish.
3. State the Mayer--Vietoris theorem.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let
\[
\Delta^p
=
\left\{
(t_0,\dots,t_p)\in\RR^{p+1}:
t_i\ge0,
\quad
\sum_{i=0}^p t_i=1
\right\}
\]
be the standard $p$-simplex.
A singular $p$-simplex in $X$ is a continuous map
\[
\sigma:\Delta^p\to X.
\]


::: pf-proof

This is the basic generator used in the singular chain complex.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The singular $p$-chain group
\[
C_p(X)
\]
is the free abelian group generated by all singular $p$-simplices
\[
\sigma:\Delta^p\to X.
\]


::: pf-proof

Thus an element of $C_p(X)$ is a finite formal sum
\[
c
=
\sum_{j=1}^m n_j\sigma_j,
\qquad
n_j\in\ZZ.
\]
Addition is coefficientwise.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For
\[
0\le i\le p,
\]
let
\[
\delta_i:\Delta^{p-1}\to\Delta^p
\]
be the affine inclusion of the $i$th face, obtained by inserting a zero in
the $i$th coordinate. Define
\[
\partial_p:C_p(X)\to C_{p-1}(X)
\]
on generators by
\[
\partial_p\sigma
=
\sum_{i=0}^p
(-1)^i
\sigma\circ\delta_i
\]
and extend linearly.


::: pf-proof

This is the singular boundary homomorphism. The alternating signs encode the
orientations of the faces of the simplex.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The boundary maps satisfy
\[
\partial_{p-1}\circ\partial_p=0.
\]


::: pf-proof

Every codimension-$2$ face of $\Delta^p$ occurs twice in
\[
\partial_{p-1}(\partial_p\sigma),
\]
once for each order in which its two omitted vertices are removed.
The two occurrences have opposite signs, so they cancel.

Thus
\[
\partial^2=0.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Define the group of $p$-cycles and the group of $p$-boundaries by
\[
Z_p(X)
\coloneqq
\ker\partial_p
\]
and
\[
B_p(X)
\coloneqq
\operatorname{im}\partial_{p+1}.
\]
Then
\[
B_p(X)\subseteq Z_p(X).
\]


::: pf-proof

The inclusion follows from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}:
if
\[
b=\partial_{p+1}c,
\]
then
\[
\partial_p b
=
\partial_p\partial_{p+1}c
=
0.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The $p$th singular homology group is
\[
\boxed{
H_p(X)
\coloneqq
Z_p(X)/B_p(X).
}
\]


::: pf-proof

Thus two cycles represent the same homology class precisely when their
difference is the boundary of a $(p+1)$-chain.
This completes the requested detailed definition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Let
\[
M
\coloneqq
S^5\cup_{d_{2011}}e^6,
\]
where the attaching map
\[
d_{2011}:S^5\to S^5
\]
has degree $2011$.
Then
\[
\widetilde H_5(M)
\cong
\ZZ/2011
\]
and all other reduced homology groups of $M$ vanish.


::: pf-proof

Use the CW structure with one $0$-cell, one $5$-cell, and one $6$-cell.
The cellular chain complex in positive degrees is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
\ZZ
\xrightarrow{\times2011}
\ZZ
\longrightarrow
0,
\]
where the first copy of $\ZZ$ is $C_6$ and the second is $C_5$.

Therefore
\[
H_6(M)
=
\ker(\times2011)
=
0
\]
and
\[
H_5(M)
=
\operatorname{coker}(\times2011)
\cong
\ZZ/2011.
\]
There are no other positive-dimensional cells.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Define
\[
X
\coloneqq
M\vee S^6.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
H_5(X)
\cong
\ZZ/2011,
\qquad
H_6(X)
\cong
\ZZ,
}
\]
and
\[
H_i(X)=0
\]
for every other positive integer $i$.


::: pf-proof

Give $X$ the CW structure with one $0$-cell, one $5$-cell, and two
$6$-cells.
One $6$-cell is the degree-$2011$ cell from $M$; the other is the top cell
of the wedged sphere $S^6$, attached by the constant map.

Thus
\[
C_6(X)
\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ,
\qquad
C_5(X)
\cong
\ZZ,
\]
and the cellular boundary is
\[
\partial_6(m,n)
=
2011m.
\]
Hence
\[
\ker\partial_6
=
\{(0,n):n\in\ZZ\}
\cong
\ZZ,
\]
so
\[
H_6(X)\cong\ZZ.
\]
Also
\[
H_5(X)
=
\ZZ/(2011\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/2011.
\]
All other positive cellular chain groups vanish.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

**Mayer--Vietoris Theorem.**

Let
\[
X=A\cup B
\]
where $A$ and $B$ form an excisive pair, for example when $A$ and $B$ are
open subsets of $X$.
Then there is a natural long exact sequence
\[
\cdots
\longrightarrow
H_n(A\cap B)
\xrightarrow{(i_*,-j_*)}
H_n(A)\oplus H_n(B)
\xrightarrow{k_*+\ell_*}
H_n(X)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
H_{n-1}(A\cap B)
\longrightarrow
\cdots,
\]
where
\[
i:A\cap B\hookrightarrow A,
\qquad
j:A\cap B\hookrightarrow B,
\]
and
\[
k:A\hookrightarrow X,
\qquad
\ell:B\hookrightarrow X
\]
are the inclusions.


::: pf-proof

This is the standard homological Mayer--Vietoris exact sequence.
The same theorem may be written in reduced homology as
\[
\cdots
\to
\widetilde H_n(A\cap B)
\to
\widetilde H_n(A)\oplus\widetilde H_n(B)
\to
\widetilde H_n(X)
\to
\widetilde H_{n-1}(A\cap B)
\to
\cdots.
\]

:::
