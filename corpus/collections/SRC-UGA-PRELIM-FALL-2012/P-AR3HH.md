::: {.problem}
Suppose $R$ is a commutative ring (with 1), $I$ is a proper ideal in $R$, and $a \in R$.
Suppose $(a) + I = R$.
Prove that $a+I$ is a unit (i.e., invertible) in the quotient ring $R/I$.

(For half credit: Prove that if $a$ and $n$ are relatively prime integers, then $a+n\ZZ$ is a unit in $\ZZ/n\ZZ$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set covers this problem.

Throughout, $R$ is a commutative ring with $1$, $I$ a proper ideal, $(a)$ the principal ideal generated by $a$, and $\pi: R\to R/I$ the quotient map.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #element}
The hypothesis $(a)+I=R$ gives elements $r\in R$ and $i\in I$ with $ra+i=1$.

::: pf-proof
The sum $(a)+I$ consists of the elements $ra+i$ with $r\in R$ and $i\in I$, so $1\in(a)+I$ is such an element, because $1\in R$ by hypothesis.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$a+I$ is a unit of $R/I$, with inverse $r+I$.

::: pf-proof
Applying the quotient map to the identity of step [](#element){.pf-ref} and using that $\pi$ is a ring homomorphism with $\pi(a)=a+I$, $\pi(r)=r+I$ and $\pi(i)=0$ because $i\in I$, we get
$$
(r+I)(a+I)=\pi(r)\pi(a)=\pi(ra)=1\pi(1)=1+I,
$$
so $r+I$ is an inverse of $a+I$ in $R/I$, which is therefore a unit. For $R=\ZZ$ and $I=n\ZZ$ the hypothesis $(a)+n\ZZ=\ZZ$ is the condition $\gcd(a,n)=1$, since $(a)+n\ZZ$ is generated by the greatest common divisor of $a$ and $n$; that is the half-credit statement, with $r$ a Bézout coefficient.
:::
