::: {.problem}
Let $\tau$ be the topology on $\RR$ generated by the sets $[a, b)$ with $a, b \in \RR$, $a < b$. Which of the following are true in the topological space $(\RR, \tau)$?

(A) $[0, 1]$ is compact

(B) $[0, 1]$ is Hausdorff

(C) $[0, 1]$ is connected
:::

::: {.solution}
Only (B) is true.

(A) is false: in this lower limit topology every compact set is countable, and $[0,1]$ is not. Suppose $C\subseteq\RR$ is compact and nonempty, and take $c\in C$. The sets

$$
U_0=[c,\infty),\qquad U_n=\left(-\infty,c-\frac1n\right),\quad n\in\NN,
$$

are open and cover $C$. Since $C$ is compact there is a finite subcover. Because $U_n\subseteq U_{n+1}$ for $n\geq1$ and $c\notin U_n$ for $n\geq1$, the subcover may be taken to be $\{U_0,U_{N_c}\}$ for some $N_c\in\NN$. Then $C\subseteq(-\infty,c-\frac{1}{N_c})\cup[c,\infty)$, so $I_c=\left(c-\frac{1}{N_c},c\right]$ contains no point of $C$ other than $c$. If $c,d\in C$ with $c<d$, then $c\notin I_d$, so $I_c\cap I_d=\emptyset$. Choosing a rational $q_c\in I_c$ for each $c\in C$ gives an injective map $C\to\QQ$, so $C$ is countable.

(B) is true. Take $x,y\in[0,1]$ with $x<y$. The open sets $U_x=\left[x,\frac{x+y}{2}\right)$ and $U_y=\left[\frac{x+y}{2},y+1\right)$ are disjoint and contain $x$ and $y$ respectively.

(C) is false. The sets $A=\left[0,\frac12\right)$ and $B=\left[\frac12,2\right)$ are disjoint, open, both meet $[0,1]$, and $[0,1]\subseteq A\cup B$, so $[0,1]$ is disconnected.
:::
