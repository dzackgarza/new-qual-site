::: {.problem}
Let $\tau$ be the topology on $\RR$ generated by the sets $[a, b)$ with $a, b \in \RR$, $a < b$. Which of the following are true in the topological space $(\RR, \tau)$?

(A) $[0, 1]$ is compact

(B) $[0, 1]$ is Hausdorff

(C) $[0, 1]$ is connected
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\tau$ be the topology on $\RR$ generated by the half-open intervals
$[a,b)$, and give $[0,1]$ the subspace topology $\tau_{[0,1]}$, so that its
open sets are the $[a,b)\cap[0,1]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #notcompact}

(A) fails: $[0,1]$ is not compact in $\tau_{[0,1]}$.

::: pf-proof

The family $\{[0,1-\frac1n):n\ge2\}\cup\{\{1\}\}$ consists of
$\tau_{[0,1]}$-open sets: $[0,1-\frac1n)=[0,1-\frac1n)\cap[0,1]$ and
$\{1\}=[1,2)\cap[0,1]$. It covers $[0,1]$, because a point $x<1$ satisfies
$x<1-\frac1n$ for large $n$ and the point $1$ lies in $\{1\}$. No finite
subfamily covers: a finite subfamily involves only $n\le N$, so its union is
$[0,1-\frac1N)\cup\{1\}$, which misses every point of $(1-\frac1N,1)$. So the
cover has no finite subcover and $[0,1]$ is not compact.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hausdorff}

(B) holds: $[0,1]$ is Hausdorff in $\tau_{[0,1]}$.

::: pf-proof

The topology $\tau$ is finer than the usual topology, since
$(a,b)=\bigcup_{n}[a+\frac1n,b)$ is $\tau$-open; hence distinct points $x\ne
y$ of $[0,1]$, which the usual topology separates by disjoint open intervals,
are separated by the corresponding $\tau$-open sets, intersected with $[0,1]$.
So the subspace is Hausdorff.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #disconnected}

(C) fails: $[0,1]$ is disconnected in $\tau_{[0,1]}$.

::: pf-proof

$[0,\frac12)=[0,\frac12)\cap[0,1]$ is open, and its complement
$[\frac12,1]=[\frac12,2)\cap[0,1]$ is also open in the subspace; both are
nonempty, and they are disjoint with union $[0,1]$. So $[0,1]$ is the union of
two nonempty disjoint open sets, that is disconnected. So the only true
statement is (B).

:::
