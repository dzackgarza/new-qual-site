::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be an abelian group.
Prove that the group ring $\ZZ[G]$ is noetherian if and only if $G$ is finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $G$ be abelian and $\ZZ[G]$ its group ring; let $\varepsilon:\ZZ[G]\to\ZZ$ be the augmentation, $\varepsilon(\sum a_g g)=\sum a_g$, with kernel the augmentation ideal $I$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $G$ is finitely generated then $\ZZ[G]$ is Noetherian.

::: pf-proof

By the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups, $G\cong\ZZ^r\oplus F$ with $F$ finite, say of order $m$. The group ring of a direct sum is the tensor product of the group rings, so
$$
\ZZ[G]\cong\ZZ[\ZZ^r]\otimes_{\ZZ}\ZZ[F].
$$
Now $\ZZ[\ZZ^r]\cong\ZZ[x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_r^{\pm1}]$, a Laurent polynomial ring, which is a localisation of the polynomial ring $\ZZ[x_1,\dots,x_r]$ and hence Noetherian. Also $\ZZ[F]$ is a free $\ZZ$-module of rank $m$. Therefore $\ZZ[G]$ is a free module of rank $m$ over the Noetherian ring $\ZZ[\ZZ^r]$, so it is a Noetherian module over it, so its ideals are finitely generated; in particular $\ZZ[G]$ is a Noetherian ring.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $\ZZ[G]$ is Noetherian then $G$ is finitely generated.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $\ZZ[G]$ is Noetherian. Then the augmentation ideal $I$ is finitely generated, say $I=(x_1,\dots,x_m)$ with $x_j=\sum_g a_{jg}g$ finite sums.

Let $H$ be the subgroup of $G$ generated by all $g\in G$ that occur with $a_{jg}\ne0$ in some $x_j$. Since each $x_j$ is a finite sum, only finitely many $g$ occur, so $H$ is generated by a finite set; it is a finitely generated subgroup of $G$.

Since $G$ is abelian, $H$ is normal and $G/H$ is a group. The quotient map $\pi:G\to G/H$ extends to a ring homomorphism $\varphi:\ZZ[G]\to\ZZ[G/H]$, $\varphi(\sum a_gg)=\sum a_g\pi(g)$. Every $g$ occurring in $x_j$ lies in $H$, so $\pi(g)=1$ and
$$
\varphi(x_j)=\sum_g a_{jg}\cdot1=\varepsilon(x_j)\cdot1=0,
$$
because $x_j\in I=\ker\varepsilon$. Hence $\varphi$ vanishes on the generators of $I$, so $\varphi(I)=0$.

Now let $g\in G$. Then $g-1\in I$, so $\varphi(g-1)=0$, that is $\pi(g)-1=0$ in $\ZZ[G/H]$, which forces $\pi(g)=1$, that is $g\in H$. Hence $G=H$, and $G$ is finitely generated.

:::
