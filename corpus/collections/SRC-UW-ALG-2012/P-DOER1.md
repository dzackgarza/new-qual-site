::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be the $\CC$-algebra $\CC[x,y]$, and define algebra automorphisms $\sigma$ and $\tau$ of $A$ by
$$
\sigma(x)=y,\quad \sigma(y)=x
$$
and
$$
\tau(x)=x,\quad \tau(y)=\xi y,
$$
where $\xi\in\CC$ is a primitive third root of unity (namely, $\xi\neq1$ and $\xi^3=1$).
Let $G$ be the group of algebra automorphisms of $A$ generated by $\sigma$ and $\tau$.
Define
$$
A^G=\{f\in A\mid \phi(f)=f \text{ for all } \phi\in G\}.
$$
Then $A^G$ is a subalgebra of $A$ -- you do not need to prove this.
Describe the algebra $A^G$ by finding a set of generators and a set of relations.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $A=\CC[x,y]$, let $\sigma(x,y)=(y,x)$, $\tau(x,y)=(x,\xi y)$ with $\xi^3=1$, $\xi\ne1$, and let $G=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$G$ contains the diagonal subgroup $H=\{g_{a,b}:(x,y)\mapsto(\xi^ax,\xi^by)\ |\ a,b\in\ZZ/3\}$, of order $9$, and $A^H=\CC[x^3,y^3]$.

::: pf-proof

$\tau=g_{0,1}$. Conjugating by $\sigma$: for $g=g_{a,b}$,
$$
\sigma g\sigma(x)=\sigma g(y)=\sigma(\xi^by)=\xi^bx=g_{b,a}(x),
\qquad
\sigma g\sigma(y)=\sigma g(x)=\sigma(\xi^ax)=\xi^ay=g_{b,a}(y),
$$
so $\sigma g_{a,b}\sigma=g_{b,a}$; in particular $\sigma\tau\sigma=g_{1,0}$ is in $G$, and together with $\tau=g_{0,1}$ it generates $H$, which has order $9$.

$H$ acts diagonally, so it preserves each monomial: $g_{a,b}\cdot x^my^n=\xi^{am+bn}x^my^n$. Hence $x^my^n$ is $H$-invariant if and only if $am+bn\equiv0 \pmod 3$ for all $a,b$. Taking $(a,b)=(1,0)$ gives $3\mid m$, and $(a,b)=(0,1)$ gives $3\mid n$. So the $H$-invariants are spanned by the monomials $x^{3i}y^{3j}$, that is $A^H=\CC[x^3,y^3]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$A^G=\CC[x^3+y^3,\,x^3y^3]$, a polynomial ring in the two algebraically independent elements $x^3+y^3$ and $x^3y^3$.

::: pf-proof

Since $H\subseteq G$, we have $A^G\subseteq A^H=\CC[x^3,y^3]$ by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Write $u=x^3$, $v=y^3$, so that $A^H=\CC[u,v]$, and note that $\sigma$ acts on $\CC[u,v]$ by $u\leftrightarrow v$, because $\sigma(u)=y^3=v$ and $\sigma(v)=u$; the elements $x^3+y^3=u+v$ and $x^3y^3=uv$ are invariant under $\sigma$ and under $\tau$ (indeed $\tau(u)=u$, $\tau(v)=\xi^3v=v$), so they lie in $A^G$.

Conversely, an element of $A^H$ fixed by $\sigma$ is a symmetric polynomial in $u$ and $v$, and the symmetric polynomials in two variables are $\CC[u+v,uv]$. Hence $A^G=\CC[x^3+y^3,x^3y^3]$.

The two elements are algebraically independent: if $P(U,V)$ is a nonzero polynomial with $P(x^3+y^3,x^3y^3)=0$ in $\CC[x,y]$, then $P$ vanishes on the image of the map $\CC^2\to\CC^2$, $(x,y)\mapsto(x^3+y^3,x^3y^3)$. For fixed $s$, the fibre of $U=s$ consists of pairs $(u,v)$ with $u+v=s$, and $V=uv$ then takes the values $\{u(s-u)\}$; a nonzero polynomial $P$ in $V$ with coefficients depending on $s$ cannot vanish identically on that set for every $s$ unless $P=0$. Equivalently, $A^G$ has the two algebraically independent generators exhibited, so there is no relation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Consistency checks on the answer.

::: pf-proof

*(Degrees.)* If $A^G$ is a polynomial ring with generators of degrees $d_1,d_2$, then $d_1d_2=\abs G$ (the ring of coinvariants has dimension $\abs G$ over $\CC$). Here the generators have degrees $3$ and $6$, and indeed $18=3\cdot 6$; and $\abs G=18$, since $G=H\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle$ with $\abs H=9$ and $\sigma\notin H$ but $\sigma^2=1$.

*(Pseudo-reflections.)* Each of the three generators of $G$ — namely $\tau$ fixing the line $y=0$, $\sigma\tau\sigma$ fixing $x=0$, and $\sigma$ fixing the line $x=y$ — is a pseudo-reflection. By the Chevalley--Shephard--Todd theorem, the invariant ring of a finite group generated by pseudo-reflections (in characteristic $0$) is a polynomial ring, which is consistent with $A^G=\CC[x^3+y^3,x^3y^3]$.

:::
