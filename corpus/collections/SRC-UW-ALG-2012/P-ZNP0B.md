::: {.problem}
Prove that if $A$ is a finite abelian group, then $\Hom_\ZZ(A,\QQ/\ZZ)\cong\Ext^1_\ZZ(A,\ZZ)\cong A$.
(Here, $\Ext^1_\ZZ(-,-)$ is also sometimes written as $\Ext(-,-)$.)
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For $n\ge1$, $\Hom_\ZZ(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\QQ/\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/n\ZZ$.

::: {.pf-proof}

A homomorphism $\ZZ/n\ZZ\to\QQ/\ZZ$ is the same as an element $x\in\QQ/\ZZ$ with $nx=0$, i.e. an element of the $n$-torsion subgroup. In $\QQ/\ZZ$ the elements of order dividing $n$ are exactly the classes $a/n + \ZZ$ for $a=0,\dots,n-1$, which form a cyclic group of order $n$ generated by $1/n+\ZZ$. Hence $\Hom(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\QQ/\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/n\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For $n\ge1$, $\Ext^1_\ZZ(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/n\ZZ$.

::: {.pf-proof}

Apply $\Hom_\ZZ(-,\ZZ)$ to the free resolution $0\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{\ \cdot n\ }\ZZ\to\ZZ/n\ZZ\to0$. This gives
$$
0\to\Hom(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\ZZ)\to\Hom(\ZZ,\ZZ)\xrightarrow{\ \cdot n\ }\Hom(\ZZ,\ZZ)\to\Ext^1(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\ZZ)\to\Ext^1(\ZZ,\ZZ)\to\cdots
$$
Since $\ZZ$ is free, hence projective, $\Ext^1_\ZZ(\ZZ,\ZZ)=0$ and $\Hom(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\ZZ)=0$. So
$$
\Ext^1_\ZZ(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\ZZ)\cong\operatorname{coker}\bigl(\cdot n:\ZZ\cong\Hom(\ZZ,\ZZ)\to\Hom(\ZZ,\ZZ)\cong\ZZ\bigr)=\ZZ/n\ZZ .
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Both functors are additive over direct sums, so for $A=\ZZ/n_1\ZZ\oplus\cdots\oplus\ZZ/n_r\ZZ$,
$$
\Hom_\ZZ(A,\QQ/\ZZ)\cong\bigoplus_{j}\ZZ/n_j\ZZ\cong A,\qquad
\Ext^1_\ZZ(A,\ZZ)\cong\bigoplus_{j}\ZZ/n_j\ZZ\cong A .
$$

::: {.pf-proof}

$\Hom_\ZZ(\bigoplus_j\ZZ/n_j\ZZ,\QQ/\ZZ)\cong\prod_j\Hom_\ZZ(\ZZ/n_j\ZZ,\QQ/\ZZ)\cong\prod_j\ZZ/n_j\ZZ$, and a finite product of finite groups is their direct sum, so this is $\bigoplus_j\ZZ/n_j\ZZ\cong A$.

$\Ext^1_\ZZ$ is additive in its second variable over arbitrary direct sums and in its first variable over finite direct sums (it is a derived functor of an additive functor, and a finite direct sum of injectives/projectives is again such), so
$$
\Ext^1_\ZZ\Bigl(\bigoplus_j\ZZ/n_j\ZZ,\ZZ\Bigr)\cong\bigoplus_j\Ext^1_\ZZ(\ZZ/n_j\ZZ,\ZZ)\cong\bigoplus_j\ZZ/n_j\ZZ\cong A
$$
by step [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

By the structure theorem for finite abelian groups, every finite abelian group is of this form, so the statement holds for all finite abelian $A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Both isomorphisms are natural, and no hypothesis beyond finiteness of $A$ is used.

::: {.pf-proof}

Each of the identifications $\Hom(\ZZ/n,\QQ/\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/n$ and $\Ext^1(\ZZ/n,\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/n$ is canonical: the first sends a homomorphism to the image of $1/n+\ZZ$, the second is the connecting map of the displayed resolution, which is the "multiplication by $1/n$" class. Both are compatible with the projections of $A$ onto its cyclic summands, so the isomorphisms are natural in $A$.

Finiteness matters only for the decomposition into cyclic summands and for the finite direct sum in the first variable of $\Ext^1$; for infinite torsion groups the statement would require a direct limit argument instead.

:::
