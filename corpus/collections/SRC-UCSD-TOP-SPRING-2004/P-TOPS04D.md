::: problem
Consider the polynomial ring $R := \mathbb{Q}[x, y]$ and make $\mathbb{Q}$ an $R$-module via the zero actions of $x$ and $y$.
Compute for all $i$
$$
\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{Q})
$$
and show that every $R$-module has a free resolution of length $2$ (one longer than a PID).
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $R=\mathbb Q[x,y]$ and $k=R/(x,y)\cong\mathbb Q$. The Koszul complex on the regular sequence $x,y$ is a free resolution
$$
0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{\binom{-y}{x}}R^2\xrightarrow{(x\;y)}R\longrightarrow k\longrightarrow0.
$$
::: {.proof}
The map $R^2\to R$ has image $(x,y)$ and kernel generated by $(-y,x)$. Since $R$ is a domain, the left map is injective. Thus the displayed complex is exact.
:::

<1>2. Tensoring this resolution with $k$ makes both differentials zero. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(k,k)\cong
\begin{cases}
k,&i=0,2,\\
k^2,&i=1,\\
0,&i>2.
\end{cases}}
$$
::: {.proof}
The elements $x,y$ act by zero on $k$, so after tensoring the Koszul differentials vanish. The homology is therefore just the resulting graded vector space $k\leftarrow k^2\leftarrow k$.
:::

<1>3. Every $R$-module has projective dimension at most $2$.
::: {.proof}
Hilbert's syzygy theorem, equivalently the global-dimension theorem for polynomial rings over a field, gives
$$\operatorname{gl.dim}\mathbb Q[x,y]=2.$$
Thus for every $R$-module $M$ there is a projective resolution
$$0\to P_2\to P_1\to P_0\to M\to0.$$
:::

<1>4. Every projective $R$-module is free.
::: {.proof}
For finitely generated projective modules this is the Quillen--Suslin theorem. For projective modules that are not finitely generated, Bass's theorem on big projective modules says that a non-finitely-generated projective module over a connected commutative Noetherian ring is free. The ring $R$ is a Noetherian integral domain, hence connected, so the two results cover all projective $R$-modules.
:::

<1>5. Consequently every $R$-module admits a free resolution of length at most $2$:
$$
\boxed{0\to F_2\to F_1\to F_0\to M\to0.}
$$
::: {.proof}
Apply <1>4 to each projective module in the resolution from <1>3. The bound is sharp because <1>2 gives $\operatorname{Tor}_2^R(k,k)\ne0$, so $k$ cannot have projective (hence free) dimension less than $2$.
:::
:::
