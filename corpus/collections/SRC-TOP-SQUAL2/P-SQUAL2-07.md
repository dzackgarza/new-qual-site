::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be obtained by identifying a point $a$ of the $2$-sphere with a point $b$ of the $2$-torus.

Find a $\Delta$-complex structure on $X$ and compute the simplicial homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The space is
\[
X=S^2\vee T^2.
\]

## A \(\Delta\)-complex structure

### Sphere summand

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Give \(S^2\) the \(\Delta\)-complex structure obtained from two \(2\)-simplices
\[
\sigma_+,\sigma_-
\]
by identifying their corresponding boundary edges.


::: pf-proof

Take two copies of a triangle and glue their entire boundaries by the identity. The resulting quotient is the double of a disk, hence is homeomorphic to \(S^2\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Denote the three sphere vertices by
\[
v_0,v_1,v_2
\]
and the three sphere edges by
\[
e_{01},e_{12},e_{02}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The boundary identification keeps corresponding vertices and corresponding edges identified between the two triangles.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Choose opposite orientations on \(\sigma_+\) and \(\sigma_-\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\partial\sigma_+
=
e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\partial\sigma_-
=
-(e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01})}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The two triangles induce opposite boundary orientations when they are glued to form the oriented sphere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Give \(T^2\) the standard square \(\Delta\)-complex structure obtained by identifying opposite sides of a square and dividing the square by a diagonal.


::: pf-proof

The quotient of the square by opposite-side identifications is the torus. Adding one diagonal gives two \(2\)-simplices.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

This torus \(\Delta\)-complex has one vertex, which identify with the sphere vertex \(v_0\), three \(1\)-simplices
\[
a,b,c,
\]
and two \(2\)-simplices
\[
\tau_1,\tau_2.
\]


::: pf-proof

All four square vertices are identified to one vertex. The horizontal and vertical edge classes give \(a,b\), and the diagonal gives \(c\). The two triangular halves give \(\tau_1,\tau_2\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Orient the torus triangles so that
\[
\boxed{
\partial\tau_1
=
a+b-c}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\partial\tau_2
=
a+b-c}.
\]


::: pf-proof

With the standard orientations of the two triangles in the square, their three edge classes read the same relation after the opposite-side identifications.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Identifying the torus vertex with \(v_0\) gives a \(\Delta\)-complex structure on
\[
\boxed{X=S^2\vee T^2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The two \(\Delta\)-complexes meet in the single common \(0\)-simplex \(v_0\). Their simplex interiors remain disjoint, so their union is again a \(\Delta\)-complex.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The sphere chain groups are
\[
C_2(S^2)\cong\ZZ^2,
\qquad
C_1(S^2)\cong\ZZ^3,
\qquad
C_0(S^2)\cong\ZZ^3.
\]


::: pf-proof

There are two \(2\)-simplices, three edges, and three vertices.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The kernel of
\[
\partial_2:C_2(S^2)\to C_1(S^2)
\]
is generated by
\[
\boxed{\sigma_++\sigma_-}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
\[
\partial(x\sigma_++y\sigma_-)
=
(x-y)
(e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}).
\]
This vanishes exactly when \(x=y\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_2(S^2)\cong\ZZ}.
\]


::: pf-proof

There are no \(3\)-simplices, so
\[
H_2=\ker\partial_2.
\]
Use step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

One has
\[
\boxed{
H_1(S^2)=0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The boundary map \(\partial_1\) is the usual boundary map of the triangle graph:
\[
\partial e_{ij}=v_j-v_i.
\]
Its kernel is generated by the cycle
\[
e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}.
\]
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, this cycle is exactly the image of \(\partial_2\). Thus
\[
\ker\partial_1=\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Since the torus has one vertex,
\[
\boxed{\partial_1=0}
\]
on the generators \(a,b,c\).


::: pf-proof

Each edge begins and ends at the same vertex.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The image of \(\partial_2\) is generated by
\[
\boxed{a+b-c}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Both \(2\)-simplices have boundary \(a+b-c\) by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_1(T^2)
\cong
\ZZ^3/\langle a+b-c\rangle
\cong
\ZZ^2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref},
\[
\ker\partial_1=C_1\cong\ZZ^3.
\]
Quotient by the rank-one subgroup from step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}. Since the relation has primitive coefficients, the quotient is free of rank \(2\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

The kernel of the torus \(\partial_2\) is generated by
\[
\boxed{\tau_1-\tau_2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
\[
\partial(x\tau_1+y\tau_2)
=
(x+y)(a+b-c).
\]
The boundary vanishes exactly when \(y=-x\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_2(T^2)\cong\ZZ}.
\]


::: pf-proof

There are no \(3\)-simplices, so
\[
H_2=\ker\partial_2.
\]
Apply step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The reduced simplicial chain complex of the wedge splits as
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde C_*(S^2\vee T^2)
\cong
\widetilde C_*(S^2)
\oplus
\widetilde C_*(T^2)}.
\]


::: pf-proof

In positive degrees, the simplices of the wedge are exactly the disjoint union of the positive-dimensional simplices of the two summands, so the chain groups split directly and the boundary maps preserve the two summands.

In degree \(0\), choose the common wedge vertex \(v_0\) as basepoint. The reduced \(0\)-chains are generated by differences of the other vertices with \(v_0\), which come only from the sphere summand; the torus has no additional vertex. Thus the reduced degree-\(0\) chain group also has the required direct-sum form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde H_k(X)
\cong
\widetilde H_k(S^2)
\oplus
\widetilde H_k(T^2)}
\]
for every \(k\).


::: pf-proof

Homology of a direct sum of chain complexes is the direct sum of their homology groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_2(X)\cong\ZZ^2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-18){.pf-ref},
\[
H_2(X)
\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

Also,
\[
\boxed{
H_1(X)\cong\ZZ^2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-18){.pf-ref},
\[
H_1(X)
\cong
0\oplus\ZZ^2.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

Since \(X\) is connected,
\[
\boxed{
H_0(X)\cong\ZZ}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The wedge of two connected spaces is connected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-22}

For every \(k\ge3\),
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X)=0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The \(\Delta\)-complex has no simplices in dimensions \(>2\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-23}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
\ZZ^2,&k=1,2,\\
0,&k\ge3.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-19){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-22){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} give the requested \(\Delta\)-complex structure, and step [](#s1-23){.pf-ref} gives all simplicial homology groups.

:::
