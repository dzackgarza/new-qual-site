::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be obtained by identifying a point $a$ of the $2$-sphere with a point $b$ of the $2$-torus.

Find a $\Delta$-complex structure on $X$ and compute the simplicial homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The space is
\[
X=S^2\vee T^2.
\]

## A \(\Delta\)-complex structure

### Sphere summand

<1>1. Give \(S^2\) the \(\Delta\)-complex structure obtained from two \(2\)-simplices
\[
\sigma_+,\sigma_-
\]
by identifying their corresponding boundary edges.

::: {.proof}
Take two copies of a triangle and glue their entire boundaries by the identity. The resulting quotient is the double of a disk, hence is homeomorphic to \(S^2\).
:::

<1>2. Denote the three sphere vertices by
\[
v_0,v_1,v_2
\]
and the three sphere edges by
\[
e_{01},e_{12},e_{02}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The boundary identification keeps corresponding vertices and corresponding edges identified between the two triangles.
:::

<1>3. Choose opposite orientations on \(\sigma_+\) and \(\sigma_-\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\partial\sigma_+
=
e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\partial\sigma_-
=
-(e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01})}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The two triangles induce opposite boundary orientations when they are glued to form the oriented sphere.
:::

### Torus summand

<1>4. Give \(T^2\) the standard square \(\Delta\)-complex structure obtained by identifying opposite sides of a square and dividing the square by a diagonal.

::: {.proof}
The quotient of the square by opposite-side identifications is the torus. Adding one diagonal gives two \(2\)-simplices.
:::

<1>5. This torus \(\Delta\)-complex has one vertex, which identify with the sphere vertex \(v_0\), three \(1\)-simplices
\[
a,b,c,
\]
and two \(2\)-simplices
\[
\tau_1,\tau_2.
\]

::: {.proof}
All four square vertices are identified to one vertex. The horizontal and vertical edge classes give \(a,b\), and the diagonal gives \(c\). The two triangular halves give \(\tau_1,\tau_2\).
:::

<1>6. Orient the torus triangles so that
\[
\boxed{
\partial\tau_1
=
a+b-c}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\partial\tau_2
=
a+b-c}.
\]

::: {.proof}
With the standard orientations of the two triangles in the square, their three edge classes read the same relation after the opposite-side identifications.
:::

<1>7. Identifying the torus vertex with \(v_0\) gives a \(\Delta\)-complex structure on
\[
\boxed{X=S^2\vee T^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The two \(\Delta\)-complexes meet in the single common \(0\)-simplex \(v_0\). Their simplex interiors remain disjoint, so their union is again a \(\Delta\)-complex.
:::

## Homology of the sphere summand

<1>8. The sphere chain groups are
\[
C_2(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
C_1(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z^3,
\qquad
C_0(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z^3.
\]

::: {.proof}
There are two \(2\)-simplices, three edges, and three vertices.
:::

<1>9. The kernel of
\[
\partial_2:C_2(S^2)\to C_1(S^2)
\]
is generated by
\[
\boxed{\sigma_++\sigma_-}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
\[
\partial(x\sigma_++y\sigma_-)
=
(x-y)
(e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}).
\]
This vanishes exactly when \(x=y\).
:::

<1>10. Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_2(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
There are no \(3\)-simplices, so
\[
H_2=\ker\partial_2.
\]
Use step <1>9.
:::

<1>11. One has
\[
\boxed{
H_1(S^2)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The boundary map \(\partial_1\) is the usual boundary map of the triangle graph:
\[
\partial e_{ij}=v_j-v_i.
\]
Its kernel is generated by the cycle
\[
e_{12}-e_{02}+e_{01}.
\]
By step <1>3, this cycle is exactly the image of \(\partial_2\). Thus
\[
\ker\partial_1=\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
\]
:::

## Homology of the torus summand

<1>12. Since the torus has one vertex,
\[
\boxed{\partial_1=0}
\]
on the generators \(a,b,c\).

::: {.proof}
Each edge begins and ends at the same vertex.
:::

<1>13. The image of \(\partial_2\) is generated by
\[
\boxed{a+b-c}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Both \(2\)-simplices have boundary \(a+b-c\) by step <1>6.
:::

<1>14. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_1(T^2)
\cong
\mathbb Z^3/\langle a+b-c\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>12,
\[
\ker\partial_1=C_1\cong\mathbb Z^3.
\]
Quotient by the rank-one subgroup from step <1>13. Since the relation has primitive coefficients, the quotient is free of rank \(2\).
:::

<1>15. The kernel of the torus \(\partial_2\) is generated by
\[
\boxed{\tau_1-\tau_2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6,
\[
\partial(x\tau_1+y\tau_2)
=
(x+y)(a+b-c).
\]
The boundary vanishes exactly when \(y=-x\).
:::

<1>16. Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_2(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
There are no \(3\)-simplices, so
\[
H_2=\ker\partial_2.
\]
Apply step <1>15.
:::

## Homology of the wedge

<1>17. The reduced simplicial chain complex of the wedge splits as
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde C_*(S^2\vee T^2)
\cong
\widetilde C_*(S^2)
\oplus
\widetilde C_*(T^2)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
In positive degrees, the simplices of the wedge are exactly the disjoint union of the positive-dimensional simplices of the two summands, so the chain groups split directly and the boundary maps preserve the two summands.

In degree \(0\), choose the common wedge vertex \(v_0\) as basepoint. The reduced \(0\)-chains are generated by differences of the other vertices with \(v_0\), which come only from the sphere summand; the torus has no additional vertex. Thus the reduced degree-\(0\) chain group also has the required direct-sum form.
:::

<1>18. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde H_k(X)
\cong
\widetilde H_k(S^2)
\oplus
\widetilde H_k(T^2)}
\]
for every \(k\).

::: {.proof}
Homology of a direct sum of chain complexes is the direct sum of their homology groups.
:::

<1>19. Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>10, <1>16, and <1>18,
\[
H_2(X)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>20. Also,
\[
\boxed{
H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>11, <1>14, and <1>18,
\[
H_1(X)
\cong
0\oplus\mathbb Z^2.
\]
:::

<1>21. Since \(X\) is connected,
\[
\boxed{
H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The wedge of two connected spaces is connected.
:::

<1>22. For every \(k\ge3\),
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The \(\Delta\)-complex has no simplices in dimensions \(>2\).
:::

<1>23. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&k=1,2,\\
0,&k\ge3.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>19--<1>22.
:::

<1>24. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>7 give the requested \(\Delta\)-complex structure, and step <1>23 gives all simplicial homology groups.
:::
:::
