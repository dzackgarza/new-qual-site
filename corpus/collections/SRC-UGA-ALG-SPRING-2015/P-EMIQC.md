::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a PID and $M$ a finitely generated $R\dash$module.

a. Prove that there are $R\dash$submodules
$$
0 = M_0 \subset M_1 \subset \cdots \subset M_n = M
$$
such that for all $0\leq i \leq n-1$, the module $M_{i+1}/M_i$ is cyclic.

b. Is the integer $n$ in part (a) uniquely determined by $M$?
Prove your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a}
$M$ has a finite filtration $0=M_0\subset M_1\subset\cdots\subset M_n=M$ with every $M_{i+1}/M_i$ cyclic.

::: pf-proof
$M$ is finitely generated, so choose $m_1,\dots,m_n\in M$ with $M=Rm_1+\cdots+Rm_n$ (if $M=0$ take $n=0$ and $M_0=M$). Put $M_i=Rm_1+\cdots+Rm_i$ for $0\le i\le n$, so $M_0=0$ and $M_n=M$. Each $M_i$ is an $R$-submodule and $M_i\subseteq M_{i+1}$, so the chain is a filtration. For $0\le i\le n-1$ the quotient $M_{i+1}/M_i$ is generated as an $R$-module by the single coset $m_{i+1}+M_i$, because
$$
M_{i+1}/M_i=(Rm_1+\cdots+Rm_{i+1})/(Rm_1+\cdots+Rm_i)=R\,(m_{i+1}+M_i),
$$
the last equality by the third isomorphism theorem for modules. A module generated by one element is cyclic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
$n$ is not determined by $M$.

::: pf-proof
Take $R=\ZZ$ and $M=\ZZ$. The chain $0=M_0\subset M_1=\ZZ$ has length $n=1$ and its single quotient is $\ZZ/0\cong\ZZ$, cyclic. The chain $0\subset2\ZZ\subset\ZZ$ has length $n=2$ and quotients $2\ZZ/0\cong\ZZ$ (via $2k\mapsto k$) and $\ZZ/2\ZZ$, both cyclic. So the same module admits such filtrations of two different lengths, and the integer $n$ of part (a) is not uniquely determined by $M$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Answer to (b): no.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#b){.pf-ref} exhibits one module with two filtrations of different lengths, which refutes uniqueness; step [](#a){.pf-ref} is part (a).
:::
