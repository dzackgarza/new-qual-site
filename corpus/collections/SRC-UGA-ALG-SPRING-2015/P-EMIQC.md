::: problem
Let $R$ be a PID and $M$ a finitely generated $R\dash$module.

a. Prove that there are $R\dash$submodules
\[
0 = M_0 \subset M_1 \subset \cdots \subset M_n = M
\]
such that for all $0\leq i \leq n-1$, the module $M_{i+1}/M_i$ is cyclic.

b. Is the integer $n$ in part (a) uniquely determined by $M$?
Prove your answer.
:::

::: solution
For (a), choose generators
\[
m_1,\ldots,m_n
\]
of the finitely generated $R$-module $M$, and define
\[
M_i=Rm_1+\cdots+Rm_i
\]
for $0\le i\le n$, with $M_0=0$. Then $M_n=M$, and
\[
M_{i+1}/M_i
\]
is generated by the single coset $m_{i+1}+M_i$. Hence every successive quotient is cyclic.

For (b), the integer $n$ is not uniquely determined by $M$. Take
\[
R=\mathbb Z,
\qquad
M=\mathbb Z.
\]
There is a filtration of length $1$,
\[
0\subset \mathbb Z,
\]
whose only quotient is cyclic. There is also a filtration of length $2$,
\[
0\subset 2\mathbb Z\subset \mathbb Z.
\]
Its successive quotients are
\[
2\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\]
both cyclic. Thus the same module admits such filtrations with different lengths, so $n$ is not an invariant of $M$.
:::
