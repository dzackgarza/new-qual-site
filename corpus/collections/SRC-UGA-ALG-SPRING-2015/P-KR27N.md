::: {.problem}
Let $f(x) = x^4 - 5 \in \QQ[x]$.

a. Compute the Galois group of $f$ over $\QQ$.

b. Compute the Galois group of $f$ over $\QQ(\sqrt{5})$.
:::

::: {.solution}
This is a problem of the University of Georgia algebra qualifying examination of Spring 2015 (`assets/attachments/Algebra_Spring_2015.docx`). No published solution set is transcribed: the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #field}
The roots of $x^{4}-5$ are $\alpha=5^{1/4}$, $-\alpha$, $i\alpha$, $-i\alpha$, so the splitting field is $E=\QQ(\alpha,i)$.

::: pf-proof
The four complex fourth roots of $5$ are the numbers $5^{1/4}\zeta$ with $\zeta^{4}=1$, that is $\zeta\in\{1,-1,i,-i\}$, and $\alpha=5^{1/4}>0$ is one of them. Hence the field generated by all the roots is $\QQ(\alpha,i\alpha)=\QQ(\alpha,i)$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #degree}
$[E:\QQ]=8$.

::: pf-proof
$x^{4}-5$ is Eisenstein at the prime $5$, so it is irreducible over $\QQ$ and $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4$. Since $\QQ(\alpha)\subset\RR$ and $i\notin\RR$, one has $i\notin\QQ(\alpha)$, and $i$ satisfies $x^{2}+1$ there, so $[E:\QQ(\alpha)]=2$ and $[E:\QQ]=8$ by multiplicativity.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #generators}
The assignments $r(\alpha)=i\alpha$, $r(i)=i$ and $s(\alpha)=\alpha$, $s(i)=-i$ define automorphisms of $E$ with $r^{4}=1$, $s^{2}=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$.

::: pf-proof
An automorphism of $E$ over $\QQ$ is determined by the images of $\alpha$ and $i$, and those images must be roots of $x^{4}-5$ and of $x^{2}+1$ respectively; the assignments above send generators to roots of their minimal polynomials and hence extend to embeddings of $E$ in $\CC$, which are automorphisms because $E$ is the splitting field. Directly on the generators, $r$ sends $\alpha\mapsto i\alpha\mapsto-\alpha\mapsto-i\alpha\mapsto\alpha$, so $r$ has order $4$; $s$ fixes $\alpha$ and conjugates $i$, so $s^{2}=1$; and $srs(\alpha)=sr(\alpha)=s(i\alpha)=s(i)s(\alpha)=-i\alpha=r^{-1}(\alpha)$ while $srs(i)=i=r^{-1}(i)$, so $srs=r^{-1}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #parta}
(a) $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\QQ)=\langle r,s\rangle\cong D_{4}$, the dihedral group of order $8$.

::: pf-proof
The eight elements $1,r,r^{2},r^{3},s,rs,r^{2}s,r^{3}s$ are distinct: they are distinct on $\alpha$ up to the action of $r$, and $s\notin\langle r\rangle$ because $s$ moves $i$ and $r$ does not. Since $\abs{\operatorname{Gal}(E/\QQ)}=[E:\QQ]=8$, they exhaust the group. The presentation $\langle r,s\mid r^{4}=s^{2}=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle$ presents a group of order $8$ with a normal cyclic subgroup of order $4$ and an element inverting it, which is the dihedral group of order $8$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #partb}
(b) $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\QQ(\sqrt5))\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}$.

::: pf-proof
$\QQ(\sqrt5)=\QQ(\alpha^{2})$, since $\alpha^{2}=\sqrt5$. An element of $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\QQ)$ fixes $\alpha^{2}$ exactly when it fixes $\alpha$ up to sign: $r(\alpha^{2})=(i\alpha)^{2}=-\alpha^{2}$, so $r$ does not fix $K=\QQ(\sqrt5)$, while $r^{2}(\alpha^{2})=\alpha^{2}$, $s(\alpha^{2})=\alpha^{2}$ and $r^{2}s(\alpha^{2})=\alpha^{2}$. So $\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)=\{1,r^{2},s,r^{2}s\}$, of order $[E:K]=4$. Every nonidentity element has order $2$: $r^{4}=1$ gives $(r^{2})^{2}=1$, $s^{2}=1$, and $(r^{2}s)^{2}=r^{2}sr^{2}s=r^{2}(srs)^{2}=r^{2}r^{-2}=1$. A group of order $4$ with all nonidentity elements of order $2$ is $C_{2}\times C_{2}$.
:::
