::: {.problem}
For $0<a<b$, evaluate
\[
I=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{|ae^{i\theta}-b|^4}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
$$
r=\frac ab,
$$
so $0<r<1$.

<1>1.
$$
I
=
\frac1{b^4}\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{d\theta}{\abs{1-re^{i\theta}}^4}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
ae^{i\theta}-b=-b(1-re^{i\theta}),
$$
taking fourth powers of absolute values gives the identity.
:::

<1>2. For every $\theta$,
$$
\frac1{(1-re^{i\theta})^2}
=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)r^m e^{im\theta}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $\abs z<1$,
$$
\frac1{1-z}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^m.
$$
Differentiating gives
$$
\frac1{(1-z)^2}
=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)z^m.
$$
Substitute $z=re^{i\theta}$. Since $r<1$, the series converges absolutely
and uniformly in $\theta$.
:::

<1>3.
$$
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{d\theta}{\abs{1-re^{i\theta}}^4}
=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^2r^{2m}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The integrand is the squared modulus of the function in step <1>2. Expanding
the absolutely convergent series and using
$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i(m-n)\theta}\,d\theta
=
\begin{cases}
1,&m=n,\\
0,&m\neq n,
\end{cases}
$$
leaves exactly the displayed sum.
:::

<1>4. For $\abs x<1$,
$$
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^2x^m
=
\frac{1+x}{(1-x)^3}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Starting from
$$
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)x^m=\frac1{(1-x)^2},
$$
differentiate after multiplying by $x$:
$$
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^2x^m
=
\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{x}{(1-x)^2}\right)
=
\frac{1+x}{(1-x)^3}.
$$
:::

<1>5.
$$
I
=
\boxed{\frac{a^2+b^2}{(b^2-a^2)^3}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1, <1>3, and <1>4 with $x=r^2$,
$$
I
=
\frac1{b^4}\frac{1+r^2}{(1-r^2)^3}.
$$
Substituting $r=a/b$ and simplifying gives
$$
I
=
\frac1{b^4}
\frac{1+a^2/b^2}{(1-a^2/b^2)^3}
=
\frac{a^2+b^2}{(b^2-a^2)^3}.
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the requested value.
:::
