::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ complex matrix such that
\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^k)=0
\qquad(k=1,\ldots,n).
\]
Prove that $A$ is nilpotent.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$ be the eigenvalues of $A$, counted with
algebraic multiplicity, and set
$$
p_k=\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_j^k.
$$

<1>1. For $1\leq k\leq n$,
$$
p_k=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
Over $\CC$, the matrix $A$ is triangularizable. In an upper-triangular form
its diagonal entries are $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$, and the diagonal
entries of its $k$th power are
$\lambda_1^k,\ldots,\lambda_n^k$. Hence
$$
p_k
=
\operatorname{tr}(A^k)
=0
$$
by hypothesis.
:::

<1>2. Every elementary symmetric polynomial
$$
e_r=e_r(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n),
\qquad 1\leq r\leq n,
$$
vanishes.

::: {.proof}
Newton's identities state that, with $e_0=1$,
$$
r e_r
=
\sum_{k=1}^r(-1)^{k-1}e_{r-k}p_k.
$$
For $1\leq r\leq n$, every $p_k$ occurring on the right side is zero by
step <1>1. Thus $r e_r=0$. Since the ground field has characteristic zero,
$e_r=0$.
:::

<1>3. The characteristic polynomial of $A$ is
$$
\chi_A(t)=t^n.
$$

::: {.proof}
The characteristic polynomial is
$$
\chi_A(t)
=
\prod_{j=1}^n(t-\lambda_j)
=
t^n-e_1t^{n-1}+e_2t^{n-2}-\cdots+(-1)^ne_n.
$$
All $e_r$ vanish by step <1>2.
:::

<1>4.
$$
A^n=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By the Cayley--Hamilton theorem,
$$
\chi_A(A)=0.
$$
Using step <1>3 gives $A^n=0$.
:::

<1>5. The matrix $A$ is nilpotent.

::: {.proof}
This is immediate from step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
