::: {.problem}
Let $f,g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be continuous and suppose
\[
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}f(x)=0,
\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|g(x)|\,dx<\infty.
\]
Define
\[
h(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x-y)g(y)\,dy.
\]
Prove that
\[
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}h(x)=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #f-bounded}
The function $f$ is bounded on $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
Since $f(x)\to0$ as $\abs{x}\to\infty$, there is $R>0$ such that
$\abs{f(x)}\leq1$ for $\abs{x}\geq R$. On the compact interval
$[-R,R]$, continuity makes $f$ bounded. Hence there is $M<\infty$ with
$$
\abs{f(x)}\leq M
$$
for all $x\in\RR$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #tail-integral-small}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$. There is $R>0$ such that
$$
M\int_{\abs y>R}\abs{g(y)}\,dy<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Since $g\in L^1(\RR)$, its tail integrals tend to zero. If $M=0$, the
claim is immediate; otherwise choose $R$ so that
$$
\int_{\abs y>R}\abs{g(y)}\,dy<\frac{\varepsilon}{2M}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #near-integral-small}
There is $S>0$ such that, whenever $\abs{x}>S$,
$$
\int_{\abs y\leq R}\abs{f(x-y)g(y)}\,dy
<
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Set
$$
G_R=\int_{\abs y\leq R}\abs{g(y)}\,dy.
$$
If $G_R=0$, the integral is zero. Otherwise, because
$f(t)\to0$ as $\abs t\to\infty$, choose $T>0$ such that
$$
\abs{f(t)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2G_R}
$$
whenever $\abs t>T$. Put $S=T+R$. If $\abs{x}>S$ and $\abs y\leq R$,
then
$$
\abs{x-y}\geq\abs{x}-\abs y>T.
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_{\abs y\leq R}\abs{f(x-y)g(y)}\,dy
<
\frac{\varepsilon}{2G_R}
\int_{\abs y\leq R}\abs{g(y)}\,dy
=
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #h-bound-epsilon}
If $\abs{x}>S$, then
$$
\abs{h(x)}<\varepsilon.
$$

::: pf-proof
Split the defining integral at $\abs y=R$. By the triangle inequality,
steps [](#tail-integral-small){.pf-ref} and [](#near-integral-small){.pf-ref}, and the bound $\abs f\leq M$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{h(x)}
&\leq
\int_{\abs y\leq R}\abs{f(x-y)g(y)}\,dy
+
\int_{\abs y>R}\abs{f(x-y)g(y)}\,dy\\
&<
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
+
M\int_{\abs y>R}\abs{g(y)}\,dy\\
&<
\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #h-limit-zero}
$$
\lim_{\abs{x}\to\infty}h(x)=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#h-bound-epsilon){.pf-ref} is exactly the $\varepsilon$ definition of this limit.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#h-limit-zero){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.
:::
