::: {.problem}
Let
\[
K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[10]{2}).
\]
Prove that $[K:\mathbb Q]=10$ and that the automorphism group of $K$ has order $2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\alpha=\sqrt[10]{2},
\qquad
K=\QQ(\alpha).
$$

<1>1. The polynomial $x^{10}-2$ is irreducible over $\QQ$.

::: {.proof}
Eisenstein's criterion at the prime $2$ applies: every nonleading coefficient
is divisible by $2$, while the constant term $-2$ is not divisible by $4$.
Thus $x^{10}-2$ is irreducible in $\QQ[x]$.
:::

<1>2.
$$
[K:\QQ]=10.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ is
$x^{10}-2$, which has degree $10$. Therefore
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=10.
$$
:::

<1>3. Every $\QQ$-automorphism $\sigma$ of $K$ sends $\alpha$ to either
$\alpha$ or $-\alpha$.

::: {.proof}
Since $\sigma$ fixes $\QQ$,
$$
\sigma(\alpha)^{10}
=
\sigma(\alpha^{10})
=
2.
$$
Thus $\sigma(\alpha)$ is a root of $x^{10}-2$. Also
$K\subseteq\RR$, because $K$ is generated over $\QQ$ by the real number
$\alpha$. The real roots of $x^{10}-2$ are exactly $\alpha$ and
$-\alpha$. Hence
$$
\sigma(\alpha)\in\{\alpha,-\alpha\}.
$$
:::

<1>4. Both assignments
$$
\alpha\longmapsto\alpha
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\alpha\longmapsto-\alpha
$$
define $\QQ$-automorphisms of $K$.

::: {.proof}
The first assignment is the identity. For the second, define
$$
\tau\bigl(p(\alpha)\bigr)=p(-\alpha)
$$
for $p\in\QQ[x]$. Since $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$ have the same minimal
polynomial $x^{10}-2$, evaluation at $-\alpha$ induces a well-defined field
isomorphism
$$
\QQ[x]/(x^{10}-2)\longrightarrow\QQ(-\alpha)=K.
$$
Under the standard identification of the source with $K$, this is a
$\QQ$-automorphism satisfying $\tau(\alpha)=-\alpha$.
:::

<1>5.
$$
\abs{\operatorname{Aut}_{\QQ}(K)}=\boxed{2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
An automorphism of $K=\QQ(\alpha)$ is determined by the image of $\alpha$.
Step <1>3 gives at most two possibilities, and step <1>4 realizes both.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2 and <1>5 prove the two requested assertions.
:::
:::
