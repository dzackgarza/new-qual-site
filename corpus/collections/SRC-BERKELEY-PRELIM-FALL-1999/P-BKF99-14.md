::: {.problem}
Show that every infinite closed subset of $\mathbb R^2$ is the closure of a countable set.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $F\subseteq\RR^2$ be an infinite closed set.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #countable-basis}
The family
$$
\mathcal B
=
\{B(q,r):q\in\QQ^2,\ r\in\QQ_{>0}\}
$$
is a countable basis for the Euclidean topology on $\RR^2$.

::: pf-proof
The sets $\QQ^2$ and $\QQ_{>0}$ are countable, so $\mathcal B$ is
countable. Given an open set $U\subseteq\RR^2$ and $x\in U$, choose
$\varepsilon>0$ with $B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq U$. Density of $\QQ^2$
and $\QQ$ permits a rational center $q$ sufficiently close to $x$ and a
positive rational radius $r$ such that
$$
x\in B(q,r)\subseteq B(x,\varepsilon).
$$
Thus $\mathcal B$ is a basis.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #countable-set-C}
There is a countable set $C\subseteq F$ meeting every
$B\in\mathcal B$ for which $B\cap F\neq\varnothing$.

::: pf-proof
For each basis element $B\in\mathcal B$ satisfying
$B\cap F\neq\varnothing$, choose one point
$$
x_B\in B\cap F,
$$
and let $C$ be the set of all chosen points. Since $\mathcal B$ is countable
by step [](#countable-basis){.pf-ref}, so is $C$. By construction, $C\subseteq F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #C-dense-in-F}
The set $C$ is dense in $F$.

::: pf-proof
Fix $x\in F$ and an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ in $\RR^2$. By step
[](#countable-basis){.pf-ref}, there is $B\in\mathcal B$ with
$$
x\in B\subseteq U.
$$
Since $x\in F$, the intersection $B\cap F$ is nonempty. Step [](#countable-set-C){.pf-ref} therefore
provides $x_B\in C\cap B\subseteq C\cap U$. Thus every neighborhood of
$x$ meets $C$, so $x\in\overline C$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #closure-C-equals-F}
$$
\overline C=F.
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#C-dense-in-F){.pf-ref} gives $F\subseteq\overline C$. Conversely, step [](#countable-set-C){.pf-ref} gives
$C\subseteq F$, and $F$ is closed, so $\overline C\subseteq F$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
The set $C$ is countable by step [](#countable-set-C){.pf-ref} and has closure $F$ by step [](#closure-C-equals-F){.pf-ref}.
:::
