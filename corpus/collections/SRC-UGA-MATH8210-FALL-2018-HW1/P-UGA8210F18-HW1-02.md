::: {.problem}
Let $\omega$ be a $k$-form on $\RR^n$.
We say that $\omega$ is *compactly supported* if there is some compact set $K$ so that $\omega = 0$ on $\RR^n \setminus K$.
Write $\Omega_c^*(\RR^n)$ for the set of compactly supported $k$-forms.

- Show that $\Omega_c^*(\RR^n)$ is a subalgebra of $\Omega^*(\RR^n)$.
- Show that the exterior derivative operator $d$ restricts to an operation
  $$
  d : \Omega_c^*(\RR^n) \to \Omega_c^*(\RR^n).
  $$
- Define the *compactly supported de Rham cohomology of $\RR^n$* to be
  $$
  H_{c,dR}^k(\RR^n) = \frac{\ker(d|_{\Omega_c^k})}{(d|_{\Omega_c^{k-1}})}.
  $$
  Compute $H_{c,dR}^k(\RR^0)$ and $H_{c,dR}^k(\RR^1)$.
  Compare your results and methods with the ones we used in class.
:::

::: {.remark}
The source prints the denominator as $(d|_{\Omega_c^{k-1}})$; the quotient is by the image $\operatorname{im}(d|_{\Omega_c^{k-1}})$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The compactly supported forms form a graded subalgebra
$$
\Omega_c^*(\RR^n)\subseteq\Omega^*(\RR^n).
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\omega$ and $\eta$ have compact supports contained in compact sets
$K_\omega$ and $K_\eta$. Then
$$
\operatorname{supp}(\omega+\eta)
\subseteq
K_\omega\cup K_\eta,
$$
which is compact. Scalar multiples clearly remain compactly supported.

For the wedge product,
$$
\operatorname{supp}(\omega\wedge\eta)
\subseteq
\operatorname{supp}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\eta)
\subseteq
K_\omega\cap K_\eta,
$$
which is compact. Thus compactly supported forms are closed under the graded
algebra operations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The exterior derivative preserves compact support:
$$
\boxed{
d:\Omega_c^k(\RR^n)\longrightarrow
\Omega_c^{k+1}(\RR^n)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose $\omega=0$ outside a compact set $K$. The complement
$$
\RR^n\setminus K
$$
is open, and $\omega$ vanishes identically on that open set. Therefore all
of its coefficient functions and their derivatives vanish there, so
$$
d\omega=0
$$
on $\RR^n\setminus K$. Hence
$$
\operatorname{supp}(d\omega)\subseteq K.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For the one-point manifold $\RR^0$,
$$
\boxed{
H_{c,dR}^k(\RR^0)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&k=0,\\
0,&k\ne0.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

The space $\RR^0$ is a single compact point. Thus every smooth
$0$-form is compactly supported and
$$
\Omega_c^0(\RR^0)\cong\RR.
$$
There are no nonzero forms in positive degree, and the exterior derivative
is zero. The displayed cohomology follows immediately.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

On $\RR$,
$$
\boxed{H_{c,dR}^0(\RR)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

A compactly supported $0$-form is a smooth compactly supported function
$f$. It is closed exactly when
$$
df=f'(x)\,dx=0,
$$
so $f$ is constant. A constant function on the noncompact space $\RR$
has compact support only when it is zero. Hence the degree-zero kernel is
zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Every compactly supported $1$-form on $\RR$ has the form
$$
\omega=g(x)\,dx
$$
with $g\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$, and every such $1$-form is closed.


::: pf-proof

The cotangent bundle of $\RR$ is generated by $dx$, so every smooth
$1$-form has the displayed form. There are no nonzero $2$-forms on a
one-dimensional manifold, so
$$
d\omega=0
$$
for every $1$-form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

A compactly supported $1$-form
$$
\omega=g(x)\,dx
$$
is exact through a compactly supported primitive if and only if
$$
\int_{\RR}g(x)\,dx=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
g(x)\,dx=df
$$
for some compactly supported smooth $f$, then $g=f'$ and
$$
\int_{\RR}g(x)\,dx
=
\int_{\RR}f'(x)\,dx
=0,
$$
because $f$ vanishes at both ends of the real line.

Conversely, suppose
$$
\int_{\RR}g(x)\,dx=0.
$$
Define
$$
F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x g(t)\,dt.
$$
Then $F$ is smooth and
$$
F'(x)=g(x).
$$
If $\operatorname{supp}(g)\subseteq[a,b]$, then $F=0$ for $x<a$. For
$x>b$,
$$
F(x)
=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(t)\,dt
=0.
$$
Thus $F$ is compactly supported and
$$
dF=g(x)\,dx.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Integration induces an isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
H_{c,dR}^1(\RR)
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\RR,
\qquad
[g(x)\,dx]\longmapsto\int_{\RR}g(x)\,dx}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, every compactly supported $1$-form is closed. By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
the kernel of the integration functional is exactly
$$
\operatorname{im}
\left(
d:\Omega_c^0(\RR)\to\Omega_c^1(\RR)
\right).
$$
The functional is surjective: choose any compactly supported smooth bump
function $\rho$ with
$$
\int_{\RR}\rho(x)\,dx=1.
$$
The first isomorphism theorem therefore gives the displayed isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

In all other degrees,
$$
H_{c,dR}^k(\RR)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no differential forms of degree $k\ge2$ on the one-dimensional
manifold $\RR$, and negative-degree forms are zero by convention.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Compared with ordinary de Rham cohomology,
$$
H_{dR}^0(\RR)\cong\RR,
\qquad
H_{dR}^1(\RR)=0,
$$
whereas compact support gives
$$
H_{c,dR}^0(\RR)=0,
\qquad
H_{c,dR}^1(\RR)\cong\RR.
$$


::: pf-proof

Ordinary degree-zero cohomology consists of locally constant functions; on
connected $\RR$ these are constants. Every ordinary $1$-form
$g(x)\,dx$ has a smooth primitive
$$
F(x)=\int_0^xg(t)\,dt,
$$
with no compact-support requirement, so ordinary degree-one cohomology
vanishes. Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} give the compactly supported comparison.

:::
