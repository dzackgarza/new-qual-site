::: {.problem}
Prove (not just by picture!)
that the union of the $x \mathrm { - }$ and y-axes in $\RR ^ { 2 }$ is not a manifold.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
X
=
\bigl(\RR\times\{0\}\bigr)
\cup
\bigl(\{0\}\times\RR\bigr)
\subseteq\RR^2
$$
with the subspace topology, and let
$$
o=(0,0).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The punctured space
$$
X\setminus\{o\}
$$
has exactly four connected components:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x^+&=\{(x,0):x>0\},&
R_x^-&=\{(x,0):x<0\},\\
R_y^+&=\{(0,y):y>0\},&
R_y^-&=\{(0,y):y<0\}.
\end{aligned}
$$


::: pf-proof

Each displayed ray is connected. They are pairwise disjoint and are open and
closed in the subspace
$$
X\setminus\{o\}.
$$
Indeed, on the punctured cross the sign of the unique nonzero coordinate
cannot change along a connected set without passing through the missing
origin. Thus these four rays are exactly the connected components.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $U\subseteq X$ is any open neighborhood of $o$, then
$$
U\setminus\{o\}
$$
meets each of the four components in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

Since $U$ is open in the subspace topology and contains $o$, there is
$\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$
X\cap B_\varepsilon(o)\subseteq U.
$$
The cross
$$
X\cap B_\varepsilon(o)
$$
contains nonzero points on each of the four rays. Hence so does
$U\setminus\{o\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every open neighborhood $U$ of $o$ in $X$, the punctured
neighborhood
$$
U\setminus\{o\}
$$
has at least four connected components.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the set $U\setminus\{o\}$ meets each of the four connected
components of $X\setminus\{o\}$. A connected subset of
$U\setminus\{o\}$ is also connected as a subset of
$X\setminus\{o\}$, so it cannot meet two distinct components from step
[](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Therefore the four nonempty intersections with the four rays lie in
four distinct connected components of $U\setminus\{o\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For an open Euclidean ball
$$
B\subseteq\RR^n
$$
and a point $p\in B$, the punctured ball
$$
B\setminus\{p\}
$$
has fewer than four connected components.


::: pf-proof

If $n=0$, then $B=\{p\}$ and the punctured ball is empty.

If $n=1$, an open ball is an interval, and deleting its center produces
exactly two connected intervals.

If $n\ge2$, the punctured ball is path connected: given two points in the
punctured ball, one may join them by a polygonal path inside the convex ball
and perturb any segment that passes through $p$ by a small detour in a
two-dimensional affine plane through $p$. Thus there is exactly one connected
component.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The space $X$ is not a topological manifold of any dimension.


::: pf-proof

Suppose that $X$ were an $n$-manifold. Then the origin would have a chart
$$
\phi:U\longrightarrow V\subseteq\RR^n
$$
with $U$ open in $X$, $o\in U$, and $V$ open.

Choose an open Euclidean ball
$$
B\subseteq V
$$
centered at
$$
\phi(o).
$$
Set
$$
W=\phi^{-1}(B).
$$
Then $W$ is an open neighborhood of $o$ in $X$, and
$$
\phi:
W\setminus\{o\}
\longrightarrow
B\setminus\{\phi(o)\}
$$
is a homeomorphism.

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the domain has at least four connected components. By step
[](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the codomain has fewer than four connected components. Homeomorphisms
preserve connected components, a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves that the union of the two coordinate axes is not a
manifold.

:::
