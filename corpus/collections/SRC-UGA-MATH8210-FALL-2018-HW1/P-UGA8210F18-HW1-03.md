::: {.problem}
- Let $M$ be a smooth manifold, let $U \subset M$, and let $\phi : U \to \RR^k$ be a chart.
  Let $f : U \to \RR^m$ be a continuous function.
  Suppose that $f \circ \phi^{-1}$ is smooth.
  Let $\psi : U \to \RR^k$ be another chart with the same domain.
  Show that $f \circ \phi^{-1}$ is smooth.
- A function $f : M \to \RR^k$ is smooth if for every $p \in M$ there is a smooth chart $(U, \phi)$ with $p \in U$ so that $f \circ \phi^{-1}$ is smooth on $\phi(U)$.
  Show that if $f$ is smooth, then $f \circ \psi^{-1}$ is smooth for any chart $(V, \psi)$ around $p$.
- Show that the composition of smooth functions between manifolds is smooth.
  Show that the composition of diffeomorphisms is again a diffeomorphism.
- Write down a smooth function $\RR \to \RR$ which is invertible but not a diffeomorphism.
:::

::: {.remark}
In the first item the source's conclusion repeats the hypothesis; the intended conclusion is that $f \circ \psi^{-1}$ is smooth.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Under the first set of hypotheses, the intended conclusion holds:
$$
\boxed{f\circ\psi^{-1}\text{ is smooth}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

On $\psi(U)$,
$$
f\circ\psi^{-1}
=
(f\circ\phi^{-1})
\circ
(\phi\circ\psi^{-1}).
$$
The first factor is smooth by hypothesis. Because $\phi$ and $\psi$ are
smooth charts in the same smooth atlas, the transition map
$$
\phi\circ\psi^{-1}:\psi(U)\to\phi(U)
$$
is smooth. The composition is therefore smooth.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $f:M\to\RR^k$ is smooth, then for every smooth chart
$(V,\psi)$ on $M$,
$$
\boxed{f\circ\psi^{-1}:\psi(V)\to\RR^k\text{ is smooth}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Fix $q\in V$. By the definition of smoothness, there is a chart
$(U,\phi)$ containing $q$ such that
$$
f\circ\phi^{-1}
$$
is smooth on $\phi(U)$. On the open set $\psi(U\cap V)$,
$$
f\circ\psi^{-1}
=
(f\circ\phi^{-1})
\circ
(\phi\circ\psi^{-1}).
$$
The transition map is smooth, so this expression is smooth near
$\psi(q)$. Since $q\in V$ was arbitrary, $f\circ\psi^{-1}$ is smooth at
every point of $\psi(V)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The composition of smooth maps between smooth manifolds is smooth.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
F:M\to N,
\qquad
G:N\to P
$$
be smooth, and fix $p\in M$. Choose charts
$$
(U,\phi)\text{ about }p,\qquad
(V,\psi)\text{ about }F(p),\qquad
(W,\chi)\text{ about }G(F(p))
$$
and shrink the neighborhoods so that
$$
F(U)\subseteq V,
\qquad
G(V)\subseteq W.
$$
The coordinate representatives
$$
\psi\circ F\circ\phi^{-1}
$$
and
$$
\chi\circ G\circ\psi^{-1}
$$
are smooth. Therefore
$$
\chi\circ(G\circ F)\circ\phi^{-1}
=
(\chi\circ G\circ\psi^{-1})
\circ
(\psi\circ F\circ\phi^{-1})
$$
is smooth. Since $p$ was arbitrary, $G\circ F$ is smooth.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The composition of diffeomorphisms is a diffeomorphism.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
F:M\to N,
\qquad
G:N\to P
$$
be diffeomorphisms. By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $G\circ F$ is smooth. It is bijective, and
its inverse is
$$
(G\circ F)^{-1}
=
F^{-1}\circ G^{-1}.
$$
The maps $F^{-1}$ and $G^{-1}$ are smooth, so step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that the
inverse is smooth. Hence $G\circ F$ is a diffeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The map
$$
\boxed{h:\RR\to\RR,\qquad h(x)=x^3}
$$
is smooth and invertible but is not a diffeomorphism.


::: pf-proof

The map $h$ is a polynomial, hence smooth. It is strictly increasing and
onto, so it is bijective. Its inverse is
$$
h^{-1}(y)=y^{1/3}.
$$
For $y\ne0$,
$$
(h^{-1})'(y)
=
\frac1{3y^{2/3}},
$$
which is unbounded as $y\to0$; equivalently, the difference quotient at
$0$ is
$$
\frac{h^{-1}(y)-h^{-1}(0)}y
=
y^{-2/3},
$$
which has no finite limit. Thus $h^{-1}$ is not differentiable at $0$, so
$h$ is not a diffeomorphism.

:::
