::: {.problem}
Let $V$ be a finite dimensional vector space over $\CC$.
Let $n=\dim_\CC(V)$.
Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear map.
Suppose that the following statement is true.

For every $c\in\CC$, the subspace $\{v\in V\mid T(v)=cv\}$ of $V$ has dimension $0$ or $1$.

Prove that there exists a vector $w\in V$ such that $\{w,\ T(w),\ \ldots,\ T^{n-1}(w)\}$ is a linearly independent set.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $V$ be a finite dimensional $\CC$-vector space of dimension $n$ and $T:V\to V$ linear, and suppose $\dim\ker(T-c)\le1$ for every $c\in\CC$. Regard $V$ as a module over $\CC[x]$ with $x$ acting as $T$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$V=\bigoplus_{\lambda}V_\lambda$, where $\lambda$ runs over the distinct eigenvalues of $T$ and $V_\lambda=\ker\bigl((T-\lambda)^{n}\bigr)$; moreover $\ker(T-\lambda)\subseteq V_\lambda$.

::: pf-proof

The characteristic polynomial $\chi_T$ splits over $\CC$, say $\chi_T=\prod_\lambda(x-\lambda)^{m_\lambda}$. By the primary decomposition over the PID $\CC[x]$, $V$ is the direct sum of the kernels of the primary components, that is $V=\bigoplus_\lambda\ker\bigl((T-\lambda)^{m_\lambda}\bigr)$, and these are contained in $\ker\bigl((T-\lambda)^n\bigr)$ since $m_\lambda\le n$; conversely $(T-\lambda)^n$ is a power of $(T-\lambda)^{m_\lambda}$ modulo coprime factors. Hence $V=\bigoplus_\lambda V_\lambda$.

If $(T-\lambda)v=0$ then $(T-\lambda)^nv=0$, so $\ker(T-\lambda)\subseteq V_\lambda$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For each $\lambda$, the generalised eigenspace $V_\lambda$ is a cyclic $\CC[x]$-module, annihilated by $(x-\lambda)^{e_\lambda}$ for some $e_\lambda\ge1$.

::: pf-proof

$V_\lambda$ is a finite dimensional module over the PID $\CC[x]$, and it is annihilated by $(x-\lambda)^n$; since $x-\lambda$ is irreducible, the structure theorem gives
$$
V_\lambda\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\CC[x]\big/\bigl((x-\lambda)^{e_j}\bigr)
$$
with $e_j\ge1$. On a single summand $\CC[x]/((x-\lambda)^{e})$, multiplication by $x-\lambda$ has kernel of dimension $1$ (generated by $(x-\lambda)^{e-1}$), so $\dim\ker(T-\lambda)|_{V_\lambda}=s$.

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, $\ker(T-\lambda)\subseteq V_\lambda$, so $s=\dim\ker(T-\lambda)\le1$; as $V_\lambda\ne0$ we get $s=1$. Hence $V_\lambda\cong\CC[x]/((x-\lambda)^{e_\lambda})$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$V$ is a cyclic $\CC[x]$-module, so there is $w$ with $\{w,Tw,\dots,T^{n-1}w\}$ linearly independent.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
V\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda}\CC[x]\big/\bigl((x-\lambda)^{e_\lambda}\bigr).
$$
The polynomials $(x-\lambda)^{e_\lambda}$ for distinct $\lambda$ are pairwise coprime, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives
$$
\bigoplus_{\lambda}\CC[x]\big/\bigl((x-\lambda)^{e_\lambda}\bigr)\cong\CC[x]\Big/\Bigl(\prod_\lambda(x-\lambda)^{e_\lambda}\Bigr).
$$
The product has degree $\sum_\lambda e_\lambda=\dim V=n$, so $V$ is a cyclic module generated by some $w$: every element of $V$ is $p(T)w$ for a polynomial $p$ of degree $<n$.

Hence $\{w,Tw,\dots,T^{n-1}w\}$ spans $V$. It consists of $n$ vectors in an $n$-dimensional space, so it is linearly independent, which is the required conclusion.

:::
