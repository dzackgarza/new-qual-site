::: {.problem}
(a) State and prove Burnside's Lemma.

(b) How many distinguishable ways are there to color the faces of an octahedral die with the colors red, white, and blue?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Burnside's Lemma states that if a finite group $G$ acts on a finite set
$X$, then the number of orbits is
$$
\boxed{
\cardinality{X/G}
=
\frac1{\cardinality{G}}
\sum_{g\in G}\cardinality{\operatorname{Fix}(g)} },
$$
where
$$
\operatorname{Fix}(g)=\{x\in X:g\cdot x=x\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Count the set
$$
S=\{(g,x)\in G\times X:g\cdot x=x\}.
$$
Counting first by $g$ gives
$$
\cardinality{S}=\sum_{g\in G}\cardinality{\operatorname{Fix}(g)}.
$$
Counting first by $x$ gives
$$
\cardinality{S}=\sum_{x\in X}\cardinality{G_x},
$$
where $G_x$ is the stabilizer of $x$.

Now sum orbit by orbit. If $\mathcal O$ is the orbit of $x$, then every
point in $\mathcal O$ has a stabilizer of the same size and the
orbit-stabilizer theorem gives
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal O}\,\cardinality{G_x}=\cardinality{G}.
$$
Hence each orbit contributes exactly $\cardinality{G}$ to
$\sum_{x\in X}\cardinality{G_x}$. Therefore
$$
\cardinality{S}=\cardinality{G}\,\cardinality{X/G},
$$
which yields the formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The rotational symmetry group of the octahedron has $24$ elements, and
its action on the eight faces is equivalent to the action of the rotational
symmetry group of the cube on the cube's eight vertices.


::: pf-proof

The cube and octahedron are dual polyhedra: each face of the octahedron
corresponds to a vertex of the cube. Rotations preserve this duality, so the
induced permutation action on the eight objects is the same. The rotational
symmetry group of either polyhedron has order $24$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The cycle types of the $24$ rotations on the eight cube vertices are:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{number of rotations}
&
\text{cycle structure}
&
\text{fixed colorings}\\
\hline
1&1^8&3^8\\
6&4^2&3^2\\
3&2^4&3^4\\
8&1^2\,3^2&3^4\\
6&2^4&3^4
\end{array}
$$


::: pf-proof

The identity fixes all eight vertices.

There are three axes through centers of opposite faces. On each axis, the
$90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$ rotations give two $4$-cycles, producing
$6$ rotations of type $4^2$. The $180^\circ$ rotation on each of those three
axes gives four $2$-cycles.

There are four body diagonals. The two nontrivial $120^\circ$ rotations about
each diagonal fix the two vertices on the axis and cycle the remaining six
vertices in two $3$-cycles, giving $8$ rotations of type $1^2 3^2$.

Finally, there are six axes through midpoints of opposite edges. The
$180^\circ$ rotation about each such axis gives four $2$-cycles.

A coloring is fixed by a permutation exactly when it is constant on each
cycle, so a permutation with $r$ cycles fixes $3^r$ colorings.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The number of distinguishable face colorings is
$$
\boxed{333}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply Burnside's Lemma using step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N
&=
\frac1{24}
\left(
3^8
+6\cdot3^2
+3\cdot3^4
+8\cdot3^4
+6\cdot3^4
\right)\\
&=
\frac{6561+54+17\cdot81}{24}\\
&=
\frac{7992}{24}\\
&=333.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::
