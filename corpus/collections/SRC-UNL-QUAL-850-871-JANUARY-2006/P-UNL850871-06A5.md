::: {.problem}
Let $C$ be a binary code of length $n$ with minimum distance $d>n/2$ and $M$ codewords, enumerated as $C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_M\}$.

(a) Prove that
$$
M(M-1)d\le \sum_{i=1}^M\sum_{j=1}^M d(c_i,c_j)\le \frac{nM^2}{2}.
$$

(b) Deduce that $C$ has at most
$$
\frac{2d}{2d-n}
$$
codewords.
This is the Plotkin bound.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The total ordered-pair distance satisfies
$$
\sum_{i=1}^M\sum_{j=1}^M d(c_i,c_j)
\ge
M(M-1)d.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $i=j$,
$$
d(c_i,c_i)=0.
$$
For every ordered pair with $i\ne j$, the definition of minimum distance
gives
$$
d(c_i,c_j)\ge d.
$$
There are exactly $M(M-1)$ ordered pairs with $i\ne j$, so summing yields the
stated lower bound.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Fix a coordinate position $\ell$, and let $r_\ell$ be the number of
codewords having a $1$ in that coordinate. Then the number of ordered pairs
$(c_i,c_j)$ that differ in coordinate $\ell$ is
$$
2r_\ell(M-r_\ell).
$$


::: pf-proof

There are $r_\ell$ choices for a word with a $1$ and $M-r_\ell$ choices for a
word with a $0$. Choosing the $1$-word first gives
$$
r_\ell(M-r_\ell)
$$
ordered pairs, and choosing the $0$-word first gives the same number again.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every coordinate $\ell$,
$$
2r_\ell(M-r_\ell)\le\frac{M^2}{2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every real number $r$,
$$
r(M-r)
=\frac{M^2}{4}-\left(r-\frac M2\right)^2
\le
\frac{M^2}{4}.
$$
Multiplying by $2$ gives the claimed bound.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The total ordered-pair distance satisfies
$$
\sum_{i=1}^M\sum_{j=1}^M d(c_i,c_j)
\le
\frac{nM^2}{2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Hamming distance between two codewords is the number of coordinates in
which they differ. Therefore summing all pairwise distances and then
interchanging the order of summation gives
$$
\sum_{i,j}d(c_i,c_j)
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^n
2r_\ell(M-r_\ell).
$$
Apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} to each of the $n$ coordinates:
$$
\sum_{i,j}d(c_i,c_j)
\le
n\frac{M^2}{2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Combining the two estimates gives
$$
\boxed{
M(M-1)d
\le
\sum_{i,j}d(c_i,c_j)
\le
\frac{nM^2}{2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} together.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Since $d>n/2$,
$$
\boxed{
M\le\frac{2d}{2d-n}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
M(M-1)d\le\frac{nM^2}{2}.
$$
Since $M>0$, divide by $M$:
$$
(M-1)d\le\frac{nM}{2}.
$$
Rearranging,
$$
M\left(d-\frac n2\right)\le d.
$$
The factor $d-n/2$ is positive by hypothesis, so division gives
$$
M
\le
\frac{d}{d-n/2}
=
\frac{2d}{2d-n}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves the Plotkin bound in part
(b).

:::
