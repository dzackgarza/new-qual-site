::: {.problem}
(a) State and prove the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion.

(b) How many surjective maps are there from $\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ to $\{1,2,\ldots,k\}$?
Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For finite sets $A_1,\dots,A_m$,
\[
\boxed{
\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^m A_i\right|
=
\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}}
(-1)^{|J|+1}
\left|\bigcap_{j\in J}A_j\right| }.
\]

::: {.proof}
Fix an element $x$ belonging to exactly $s\ge1$ of the sets $A_i$. On the
right-hand side, $x$ is counted once for each nonempty subset of those $s$
sets, with sign determined by the subset size. Its total coefficient is
\[
\sum_{r=1}^s(-1)^{r+1}\binom sr.
\]
Since
\[
0=(1-1)^s
=\sum_{r=0}^s(-1)^r\binom sr,
\]
the displayed coefficient equals $1$. Elements outside the union contribute
$0$ to both sides. Hence every element has the same coefficient on both
sides, proving the formula.
:::

<1>2. For each $j\in\{1,\dots,k\}$, let $B_j$ be the set of functions
\[
f:\{1,\dots,n\}\to\{1,\dots,k\}
\]
whose image omits $j$. Then a function is surjective exactly when it lies
outside
\[
\bigcup_{j=1}^k B_j.
\]

::: {.proof}
A map fails to be surjective precisely when at least one codomain element has
no preimage, which is exactly membership in one of the sets $B_j$.
:::

<1>3. If $J\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}$ has $r$ elements, then
\[
\left|\bigcap_{j\in J}B_j\right|=(k-r)^n.
\]

::: {.proof}
A function in this intersection must avoid every value in $J$, so each of
the $n$ domain elements may be sent independently to any of the remaining
$k-r$ codomain values.
:::

<1>4. The number of surjective maps is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^k
(-1)^r\binom kr(k-r)^n }.
\]

::: {.proof}
There are $k^n$ functions in total. By step <1>2, the nonsurjective maps form
$\bigcup_{j=1}^kB_j$. Apply inclusion-exclusion from step <1>1 and group
intersections according to their cardinality $r$. There are $\binom kr$
subsets $J$ of cardinality $r$, and step <1>3 gives $(k-r)^n$ maps for each.
Subtracting the union from the total gives the displayed alternating sum.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 states and proves inclusion-exclusion, and step <1>4 gives the
requested count of surjections.
:::
:::
