::: {.problem}
(a) Compute
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom n{2k}.
$$

(b) Let $c_n$ be the number of monotonically increasing functions from $\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ to itself satisfying $f(i)\le i$ for every $i$.
Find a recurrence relation satisfied by $(c_n)_{n\ge1}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For $n\ge1$,
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom n{2k}
=2^{n-1}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
E=\sum_{k}\binom n{2k},
\qquad
O=\sum_k\binom n{2k+1}.
$$
The binomial theorem gives
$$
E+O=(1+1)^n=2^n
$$
and, for $n\ge1$,
$$
E-O=(1-1)^n=0.
$$
Thus $E=O=2^{n-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

An admissible nondecreasing map
$$
f:\{1,\dots,n\}\to\{1,\dots,n\}
$$
is uniquely determined by the integers
$$
a_j=\#\{i:f(i)=j\},
\qquad
j=1,\dots,n,
$$
which satisfy
$$
a_1+\cdots+a_j\ge j
\qquad(1\le j\le n),
$$
and
$$
a_1+\cdots+a_n=n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $f$ is nondecreasing, its values consist of $a_1$ copies of $1$,
then $a_2$ copies of $2$, and so on, so the counts determine $f$.

For a fixed $j$, the inequality
$$
f(j)\le j
$$
holds exactly when at least the first $j$ values of the nondecreasing
sequence are at most $j$, which is equivalent to
$$
a_1+\cdots+a_j\ge j.
$$
The total count is $n$ because the domain has $n$ elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The maps counted by $c_n$ are in bijection with Dyck paths of
semilength $n$.


::: pf-proof

Given the counts from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, build a lattice path from $(0,0)$ to $(n,n)$
as follows: for each $j=1,\dots,n$, take $a_j$ north steps followed by one
east step. After the $j$th east step the path is at
$$
\left(j,a_1+\cdots+a_j\right).
$$
The inequalities in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} say exactly that this point lies on or above
the diagonal $y=x$. Between east steps the path moves only upward, so the
whole path stays on or above the diagonal. It has $n$ north and $n$ east
steps, hence is a Dyck path.

Conversely, any Dyck path may be read uniquely in blocks consisting of some
number $a_j\ge0$ of north steps followed by its $j$th east step. Staying on
or above the diagonal gives the inequalities in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, so these counts
recover an admissible nondecreasing map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

With the convention
$$
c_0=1,
$$
the sequence satisfies
$$
\boxed{
c_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}c_jc_{n-1-j}
\qquad(n\ge1)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, count Dyck paths of semilength $n$. Every nonempty Dyck path
has a unique first-return decomposition
$$
N\,P\,E\,Q,
$$
where $N$ is the first north step, the displayed $E$ is the east step on
which the path first returns to the diagonal, $P$ is a Dyck path of some
semilength $j$, and $Q$ is a Dyck path of semilength $n-1-j$.

For each $j=0,\dots,n-1$, there are
$$
c_jc_{n-1-j}
$$
choices for the ordered pair $(P,Q)$. Summing over the unique possible value
of $j$ gives the recurrence.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} answers part (a), and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the recurrence requested in
part (b).
