::: {.problem}
State and prove the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem concerning the maximum size of an intersecting family of $k$-sets.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Erdős--Ko--Rado theorem states that if
$$
n\ge2k
$$
and
$$
\mathcal F\subseteq\binom{[n]}k
$$
is intersecting, meaning that
$$
A\cap B\ne\varnothing
\qquad(A,B\in\mathcal F),
$$
then
$$
\boxed{\cardinality{\mathcal F}\le\binom{n-1}{k-1}}.
$$
The bound is attained by every full star
$$
\mathcal F_x=\{A\in\binom{[n]}k:x\in A\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

A star is intersecting and has
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal F_x}
=\binom{n-1}{k-1},
$$
since the remaining $k-1$ elements may be chosen arbitrarily from
$[n]\setminus\{x\}$. The upper bound is proved in the following steps.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

In any cyclic ordering of $[n]$, an intersecting family of cyclic
intervals of length $k$ has at most $k$ members.


::: pf-proof

If the family is empty, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose one
member and rotate the labels so that it is
$$
I=\{1,2,\dots,k\}.
$$
There are exactly $k$ cyclic $k$-intervals containing the point $1$.

Any family member not containing $1$ must intersect $I$, so it has the form
$$
J_s=\{s,s+1,\dots,s+k-1\},
\qquad
2\le s\le k.
$$
Associate to $J_s$ the preceding cyclic interval
$$
C_s=\{s-k,s-k+1,\dots,s-1\},
$$
with indices read modulo $n$. Since $2\le s\le k$, the interval $C_s$
contains $1$. Since $n\ge2k$, the two consecutive blocks $C_s$ and $J_s$,
each of length $k$, are disjoint. Therefore, if $J_s$ belongs to the
intersecting family, $C_s$ cannot belong to it. Distinct values of $s$ give
distinct intervals $C_s$.

Thus the family members not containing $1$ inject into the cyclic
$k$-intervals containing $1$ that are absent from the family. Hence
$$
\#\{\text{members containing }1\}
+\#\{\text{members not containing }1\}
\le k.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

A fixed $k$-set $A\subseteq[n]$ occurs as a cyclic interval in exactly
$$
k!(n-k)!
$$
cyclic orderings of $[n]$.


::: pf-proof

Treat the elements of $A$ as one block. Together with the $n-k$ remaining
elements, this gives $n-k+1$ objects arranged cyclically, which can be done
in
$$
(n-k)!
$$
cyclic orders. The $k$ elements inside the block may be ordered in $k!$
ways. Multiplying gives the stated number.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For $n\ge2k$,
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal F}
\le
\binom{n-1}{k-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Count pairs
$$
(A,\sigma),
$$
where $A\in\mathcal F$ and $\sigma$ is a cyclic ordering of $[n]$ in which
$A$ appears as a cyclic interval.

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, counting first by $A$ gives
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal F}\,k!(n-k)!
$$
pairs. There are $(n-1)!$ cyclic orderings of $[n]$, and by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} each
contains at most $k$ members of $\mathcal F$ as cyclic intervals. Therefore
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal F}\,k!(n-k)!
\le
k(n-1)!.
$$
Dividing gives
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal F}
\le
\frac{(n-1)!}{(k-1)!(n-k)!}
=
\binom{n-1}{k-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $n<2k$, every two $k$-subsets of $[n]$ intersect, so the maximum
size is
$$
\boxed{\binom nk}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Two disjoint $k$-sets would contain $2k>n$ distinct elements, which is
impossible. Hence the entire family $\binom{[n]}k$ is intersecting.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove the Erdős--Ko--Rado theorem in its usual range
$n\ge2k$, including sharpness. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} records the complementary trivial
range.

:::
