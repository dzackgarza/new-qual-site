::: problem
In $L^2([0,1])$, prove that $1$ and $\sqrt3(2x-1)$ are orthonormal.
Show that the closest polynomial of degree at most one to $x^2$ is $x-1/6$, and compute the distance.
:::

::: solution
Let
\[
e_0(x)=1,
\qquad
e_1(x)=\sqrt3(2x-1).
\]

<1>1. The pair $e_0,e_1$ is orthonormal.
::: proof
We have
\[
\|e_0\|_2^2=\int_0^1 1\,dx=1,
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|e_1\|_2^2
&=3\int_0^1(2x-1)^2\,dx\\
&=3\int_0^1(4x^2-4x+1)\,dx\\
&=3\left(\frac43-2+1\right)=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Also
\[
\langle e_0,e_1\rangle
=\sqrt3\int_0^1(2x-1)\,dx=0.
\]
Thus $e_0,e_1$ is an orthonormal basis of the two-dimensional subspace of polynomials of degree at most one.
:::

<1>2. Compute the orthogonal projection of $f(x)=x^2$.
::: proof
The projection of $f$ onto $\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_1\}$ is
\[
Pf=\langle f,e_0\rangle e_0+\langle f,e_1\rangle e_1.
\]
Now
\[
\langle f,e_0\rangle
=\int_0^1x^2\,dx
=\frac13,
\]
while
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle f,e_1\rangle
&=\sqrt3\int_0^1x^2(2x-1)\,dx\\
&=\sqrt3\left(\frac12-\frac13\right)
=\frac{\sqrt3}{6}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
Pf(x)
&=\frac13+\frac{\sqrt3}{6}\sqrt3(2x-1)\\
&=\frac13+\frac12(2x-1)\\
&=x-\frac16.
\end{aligned}
\]
By the projection theorem, this is the unique polynomial of degree at most one closest to $x^2$ in $L^2([0,1])$.
:::

<1>3. Compute the distance.
::: proof
Because $Pf$ is the orthogonal projection,
\[
\|f-Pf\|_2^2
=\|f\|_2^2-|\langle f,e_0\rangle|^2-|\langle f,e_1\rangle|^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|f-Pf\|_2^2
&=\int_0^1x^4\,dx-\frac19-\frac1{12}\\
&=\frac15-\frac19-\frac1{12}
=\frac1{180}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{\|x^2-(x-1/6)\|_2=\frac1{\sqrt{180}}=\frac1{6\sqrt5}.}
\]
:::
:::
