::: problem
Let $U$ be unitary on a Hilbert space, let $M=\{x:Ux=x\}$, and let $S_N=N^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U^n$.
Prove that $S_Nx$ converges in norm to the orthogonal projection of $x$ onto $M$.
:::

::: solution
Let
\[
M=\ker(I-U)=\{x:Ux=x\}.
\]
Since $I-U$ is bounded, $M$ is closed, so the orthogonal projection
\[
P:H\to M
\]
is defined.

<1>1. Identify the orthogonal complement of the fixed-point space.
::: proof
For any bounded operator $T$ on a Hilbert space,
\[
\overline{\operatorname{ran}T}=(\ker T^*)^\perp.
\]
Apply this to
\[
T=I-U.
\]
Because $U$ is unitary,
\[
T^*=I-U^*.
\]
Moreover
\[
U^*x=x
\iff Ux=x,
\]
so
\[
\ker(I-U^*)=M.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{M^\perp=\overline{\operatorname{ran}(I-U)}.}
\]
:::

<1>2. The ergodic averages vanish on $\operatorname{ran}(I-U)$.
::: proof
For any $y\in H$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_N(I-U)y
&=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U^n(y-Uy)\\
&=\frac1N(y-U^Ny).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $U$ is unitary,
\[
\|U^Ny\|=\|y\|,
\]
and hence
\[
\|S_N(I-U)y\|
\le\frac{2\|y\|}{N}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
:::

<1>3. The convergence extends to all of $M^\perp$.
::: proof
Each $S_N$ is a contraction:
\[
\|S_Nx\|
\le\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\|U^nx\|
=\|x\|.
\]
Let $z\in M^\perp$. By <1>1, choose $z_k\in\operatorname{ran}(I-U)$ with
\[
z_k\to z.
\]
For fixed $k$,
\[
S_Nz_k\to0
\]
by <1>2. Also
\[
\|S_N(z-z_k)\|\le\|z-z_k\|.
\]
Given $\varepsilon>0$, first choose $k$ so that $\|z-z_k\|<\varepsilon/2$, then choose $N$ so large that $\|S_Nz_k\|<\varepsilon/2$. Thus
\[
\|S_Nz\|<\varepsilon.
\]
Hence
\[
S_Nz\to0
\qquad(z\in M^\perp).
\]
:::

<1>4. Conclude the theorem.
::: proof
For arbitrary $x\in H$, decompose
\[
x=Px+z,
\qquad z\in M^\perp.
\]
Since $U(Px)=Px$,
\[
S_N(Px)=Px
\]
for every $N$. By <1>3,
\[
S_Nz\to0.
\]
Therefore
\[
S_Nx
=Px+S_Nz
\longrightarrow Px
\]
in norm.
:::
:::
