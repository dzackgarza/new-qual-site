::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that $\ell^2(\NN)$ is complete.

(b) Let $H$ be a Hilbert space. Prove the polarization identity: for all $x,y\in H$,
$$
\langle x,y\rangle=\frac14\left(\norm{x+y}^2-\norm{x-y}^2+i\norm{x+iy}^2-i\norm{x-iy}^2\right).
$$

(c) Deduce that an invertible linear map between Hilbert spaces is unitary if and only if it is isometric.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
The space $\ell^2(\NN)$ is complete.

::: pf-proof
Let $(x^{(m)})$ be Cauchy in $\ell^2$, where
$$
x^{(m)}=(x^{(m)}_j)_{j\ge1}.
$$
For each fixed $j$,
$$
\abs{x^{(m)}_j-x^{(k)}_j}
\le \norm{x^{(m)}-x^{(k)}}_2,
$$
so $(x^{(m)}_j)_m$ is Cauchy in $\CC$. Let
$$
x_j=\lim_{m\to\infty}x^{(m)}_j.
$$

Choose $m_0$ so that
$$
\norm{x^{(m)}-x^{(k)}}_2<1
\qquad(m,k\ge m_0).
$$
For fixed $m\ge m_0$, Fatou's lemma for counting measure gives
$$
\sum_{j=1}^\infty \abs{x_j-x^{(m)}_j}^2
\le \liminf_{k\to\infty}
\sum_{j=1}^\infty \abs{x^{(k)}_j-x^{(m)}_j}^2
\le1.
$$
Hence $x-x^{(m)}\in\ell^2$, and therefore $x\in\ell^2$.

Now let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose $M$ such that
$$
\norm{x^{(m)}-x^{(k)}}_2<\varepsilon
\qquad(m,k\ge M).
$$
For $m\ge M$, the same Fatou argument yields
$$
\norm{x-x^{(m)}}_2^2
\le \liminf_{k\to\infty}\norm{x^{(k)}-x^{(m)}}_2^2
\le\varepsilon^2.
$$
Thus $x^{(m)}\to x$ in $\ell^2$, proving completeness.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The complex polarization identity holds.

::: pf-proof
Using the convention that the inner product is linear in its first variable,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\norm{x+y}^2-\norm{x-y}^2
&=4\operatorname{Re}\langle x,y\rangle,\\
\norm{x+iy}^2-\norm{x-iy}^2
&=4\operatorname{Im}\langle x,y\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\langle x,y\rangle
=\frac14\left(
\norm{x+y}^2-\norm{x-y}^2
+i\norm{x+iy}^2-i\norm{x-iy}^2
\right).}
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
For an invertible linear map, unitarity is equivalent to isometry.

::: pf-proof
If $U:H_1\to H_2$ is unitary, then
$$
\norm{Ux}_{H_2}^2
=\langle Ux,Ux\rangle_{H_2}
=\langle x,x\rangle_{H_1}
=\norm{x}_{H_1}^2,
$$
so $U$ is an isometry.

Conversely, suppose $U$ is linear, invertible, and isometric. Applying the polarization identity in $H_2$ and using linearity and norm preservation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle Ux,Uy\rangle_{H_2}
&=\frac14\bigl(
\norm{U(x+y)}^2-\norm{U(x-y)}^2\\
&\qquad+i\norm{U(x+iy)}^2-i\norm{U(x-iy)}^2
\bigr)\\
&=\langle x,y\rangle_{H_1}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $U$ preserves inner products; with invertibility already assumed, $U$ is unitary.
:::
