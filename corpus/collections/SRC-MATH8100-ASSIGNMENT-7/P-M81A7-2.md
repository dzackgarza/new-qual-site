::: problem
For $E$ in a Hilbert space $H$, prove that $E^\perp$ is a closed subspace and that $(E^\perp)^\perp$ is the smallest closed subspace containing $E$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. The orthogonal complement $E^\perp$ is a closed subspace.
::: proof
For each $y\in E$, the map
\[
\varphi_y:H\to\mathbb C,
\qquad
\varphi_y(x)=\langle x,y\rangle,
\]
is continuous and linear. Hence
\[
E^\perp
=\{x:\langle x,y\rangle=0\text{ for every }y\in E\}
=\bigcap_{y\in E}\ker\varphi_y.
\]
Each kernel is a closed subspace, so their intersection is a closed subspace.
:::

<1>2. The double orthogonal complement is the closed linear span of $E$.
::: proof
Let
\[
M=\overline{\operatorname{span}}(E).
\]
If $x\in M$ and $z\in E^\perp$, then $z$ is orthogonal to every finite linear combination of elements of $E$, hence by continuity to every element of $M$. Thus
\[
M\subseteq(E^\perp)^\perp.
\]

Conversely, let $x\in(E^\perp)^\perp$. Since $M$ is a closed subspace of the Hilbert space $H$, the projection theorem gives a decomposition
\[
x=m+z,
\qquad
m\in M,\quad z\in M^\perp.
\]
Because $E\subseteq M$, we have
\[
M^\perp\subseteq E^\perp,
\]
so $z\in E^\perp$. Also $m\in M\subseteq(E^\perp)^\perp$, and therefore
\[
z=x-m\in(E^\perp)^\perp.
\]
Thus $z$ is orthogonal to every element of $E^\perp$, in particular to itself. Hence
\[
\|z\|^2=\langle z,z\rangle=0,
\]
so $z=0$ and $x=m\in M$.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{(E^\perp)^\perp=\overline{\operatorname{span}}(E).}
\]
Since the right-hand side is precisely the smallest closed subspace containing $E$, the result follows.
:::
