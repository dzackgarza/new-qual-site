::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be the set of all real valued functions on $[0,1]$ for which $f(0)=0$ and $|f(t)-f(s)|^{1/2}\le t-s$ for all $0\le s<t\le 1$.

(a) Prove that $A$ is a compact subset of $C[0,1]$.

(b) Prove that $A$ is a compact subset of $L^1[0,1]$.

Don't forget to check that $A$ is closed in the given Banach spaces.
:::

::: {.solution}
The defining inequality is equivalent to
\[
|f(t)-f(s)|\le (t-s)^2
\qquad(0\le s<t\le1).
\]

<1>1. Every element of $A$ is continuous, and $A$ is uniformly bounded in $C[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
For $s,t\in[0,1]$,
\[
|f(t)-f(s)|\le |t-s|^2.
\]
Hence every $f\in A$ is continuous.

Also, since $f(0)=0$,
\[
|f(t)|=|f(t)-f(0)|\le t^2\le1,
\]
so
\[
\|f\|_\infty\le1
\qquad(f\in A).
\]
Thus $A$ is uniformly bounded.
:::

<1>2. $A$ is equicontinuous.
::: {.proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
\[
\delta=\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
\]
If $|t-s|<\delta$, then for every $f\in A$,
\[
|f(t)-f(s)|\le |t-s|^2<\delta^2=\varepsilon.
\]
Thus the same $\delta$ works for every member of $A$, so $A$ is equicontinuous.
:::

<1>3. $A$ is closed in $C[0,1]$ with the supremum norm.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $f_n\in A$ and
\[
\|f_n-f\|_\infty\longrightarrow0
\]
for some $f\in C[0,1]$.
Then
\[
f(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(0)=0.
\]
For $0\le s<t\le1$,
\[
|f_n(t)-f_n(s)|\le (t-s)^2.
\]
Passing to the limit gives
\[
|f(t)-f(s)|\le (t-s)^2.
\]
Hence $f\in A$, so $A$ is closed in $C[0,1]$.
:::

<1>4. $A$ is compact in $C[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and <1>2, $A$ is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous.
Arzelà--Ascoli therefore implies that the closure of $A$ in $C[0,1]$ is compact.
By <1>3, $A$ is already closed, so $A$ itself is compact.
:::

<1>5. The inclusion $C[0,1]\hookrightarrow L^1[0,1]$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
For every $f\in C[0,1]$,
\[
\|f\|_1
=\int_0^1 |f(x)|\,dx
\le \|f\|_\infty.
\]
Thus the identity map
\[
i:C[0,1]\longrightarrow L^1[0,1]
\]
is bounded, hence continuous.
:::

<1>6. $A$ is compact, and hence closed, in $L^1[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, $A$ is compact in $C[0,1]$.
Its image under the continuous map $i$ from <1>5 is therefore compact in $L^1[0,1]$.
Since $i$ is the identity on the underlying functions, this image is exactly $A$.
Hence $A$ is compact in $L^1[0,1]$.

Because $L^1[0,1]$ is a metric space, every compact subset is closed.
Thus $A$ is also closed in $L^1[0,1]$.
:::
