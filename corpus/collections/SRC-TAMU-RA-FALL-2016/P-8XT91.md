::: {.problem}
Let $(f_n)$ be a uniformly bounded sequence of continuous functions on $[0,1]$ that converges pointwise to zero.
Prove that $0$ is in the norm closure in $C[0,1]$ of the convex hull of $(f_n)$ (the norm is of course the sup norm on $C[0,1]$).
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
M=\sup_n\|f_n\|_\infty<\infty.
\]

<1>1. $f_n\rightharpoonup0$ weakly in $C[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
Let $\Lambda\in C[0,1]^*$.
By the Riesz--Markov representation theorem, there is a finite signed regular Borel measure $\mu$ on $[0,1]$ such that
\[
\Lambda(f)=\int_{[0,1]} f\,d\mu
\qquad(f\in C[0,1]).
\]
Hence
\[
|f_n(x)|\le M
\qquad(x\in[0,1],\ n\ge1),
\]
and by hypothesis
\[
f_n(x)\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }x\in[0,1].
\]
Since the total variation measure $|\mu|$ is finite, the constant function $M$ is integrable with respect to $|\mu|$.
Dominated convergence therefore gives
\[
\int_{[0,1]} |f_n|\,d|\mu|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently
\[
|\Lambda(f_n)|
=
\left|\int f_n\,d\mu\right|
\le
\int |f_n|\,d|\mu|
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since this holds for every $\Lambda\in C[0,1]^*$,
\[
f_n\rightharpoonup0.
\]
:::

<1>2. Let
\[
K=\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{f_n:n\ge1\}}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}.
\]
Then $0\in K$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that $0\notin K$.
The set $K$ is nonempty, closed, and convex in the normed space $C[0,1]$.
By the geometric Hahn--Banach separation theorem, there exist a continuous linear functional
\[
\Lambda\in C[0,1]^*
\]
and a number $\alpha>0$ such that, after changing the sign of $\Lambda$ if necessary,
\[
\Lambda(g)\ge\alpha
\qquad\text{for every }g\in K.
\]
Each $f_n$ belongs to the convex hull and therefore to $K$, so
\[
\Lambda(f_n)\ge\alpha
\qquad\text{for every }n.
\]
But <1>1 gives
\[
\Lambda(f_n)\longrightarrow0,
\]
a contradiction.
Hence $0\in K$.
:::

Thus $0$ lies in the norm closure of the convex hull of the sequence.
:::
