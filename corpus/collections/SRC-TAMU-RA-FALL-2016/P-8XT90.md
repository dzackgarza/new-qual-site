::: {.problem}
Prove or disprove that there exists a sequence $(P_n)$ of polynomials such that $(P_n(t))$ converges to one for every $t\in[0,1]$ but $\int_0^1 P_n(t)\,dt$ converges to two as $n\to\infty$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Such a sequence does exist.

<1>1. Construct continuous functions with pointwise limit $1$ and integral exactly $2$.
::: {.proof}
For $n\ge2$, define $f_n:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ by
\[
f_n(x)=
\begin{cases}
1+n^2x, & 0\le x\le 1/n,\\
1+n^2(2/n-x), & 1/n\le x\le2/n,\\
1, & 2/n\le x\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
The pieces agree at the endpoints, so $f_n$ is continuous.

For each fixed $x>0$, eventually $2/n<x$, hence eventually
\[
f_n(x)=1.
\]
Also $f_n(0)=1$ for every $n$.
Therefore
\[
f_n(x)\longrightarrow1
\qquad\text{for every }x\in[0,1].
\]

Moreover, $f_n-1$ is the triangular spike with base $2/n$ and height $n$.
Hence
\[
\int_0^1(f_n(x)-1)\,dx
=\frac12\frac{2}{n}n
=1.
\]
Thus
\[
\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx=2
\qquad\text{for every }n.
\]
:::

<1>2. Approximate each $f_n$ uniformly by a polynomial.
::: {.proof}
By the Weierstrass approximation theorem, for every $n$ there exists a polynomial $P_n$ such that
\[
\|P_n-f_n\|_\infty<2^{-n}.
\]
:::

<1>3. The polynomials $P_n$ converge pointwise to $1$.
::: {.proof}
Fix $x\in[0,1]$.
Then
\[
|P_n(x)-1|
\le
|P_n(x)-f_n(x)|+|f_n(x)-1|
\le
2^{-n}+|f_n(x)-1|.
\]
By <1>1, the second term tends to zero, and the first term also tends to zero.
Therefore
\[
P_n(x)\longrightarrow1.
\]
:::

<1>4. The integrals of $P_n$ converge to $2$.
::: {.proof}
Using <1>1 and the uniform approximation from <1>2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int_0^1P_n(x)\,dx-2\right|
&=
\left|\int_0^1(P_n(x)-f_n(x))\,dx\right|\\
&\le
\int_0^1|P_n(x)-f_n(x)|\,dx\\
&\le
\|P_n-f_n\|_\infty\\
&<2^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
\int_0^1P_n(x)\,dx\longrightarrow2.
\]
This proves the asserted existence.
:::
:::
