::: problem
a. What does it mean for a finite group $G$ to be solvable?

b. Prove that $S_4$ is solvable.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Part (a).**

<1>1. A finite group $G$ is solvable if it has a subnormal series
$$1 = G_0 \trianglelefteq G_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq G_k = G$$
such that each quotient $G_{i+1}/G_i$ is abelian.
Proof: definition of solvability.

<1>2. Equivalently, $G$ is solvable iff its derived series terminates at the trivial subgroup.
Proof: standard equivalent characterization.

**Part (b).**

<1>1. $S_4$ has a normal subgroup $V_4 = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$ (the Klein four-group).
Proof: $V_4$ is the union of the identity and the three double transpositions, and is normal in $S_4$ (it is the kernel of the action of $S_4$ on the three partitions of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ into two pairs).

<1>2. $V_4 \cong \ZZ/2 \times \ZZ/2$ is abelian.
Proof: every element has order $2$.

<1>3. $S_4 / V_4 \cong S_3$.
Proof: $S_4$ acts transitively on the three partitions of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ into two pairs, with kernel $V_4$; hence $S_4/V_4 \cong S_3$.

<1>4. $S_3$ is solvable.
Proof: $1 \trianglelefteq A_3 \trianglelefteq S_3$ with $A_3 \cong \ZZ/3$ abelian and $S_3/A_3 \cong \ZZ/2$ abelian.

<1>5. Hence $S_4$ is solvable.
Proof: the series $1 \trianglelefteq V_4 \trianglelefteq A_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$ has abelian quotients: $V_4/1 \cong \ZZ/2 \times \ZZ/2$, $A_4/V_4 \cong \ZZ/3$, and $S_4/A_4 \cong \ZZ/2$.

<1>6. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>5.
:::
