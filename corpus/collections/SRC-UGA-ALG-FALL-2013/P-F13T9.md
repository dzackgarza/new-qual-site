::: {.problem}
a. Suppose that $T$ is an $n\times n$ matrix over a field $F$ such that $T^2 = I$.
Show that if the characteristic of $F$ is not equal to $2$, then $T$ may be diagonalized, and enumerate the possibilities for the diagonal form of $T$.

b. If $F$ has characteristic $2$, give an example of a matrix $T$ such that $T^2 = I$ but $T$ is not diagonalizable.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $\operatorname{char}F\neq2$, the matrix $T$ is diagonalizable and all possible diagonal forms, up to permutation, are
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{diag}(I_r,-I_{n-r}),\qquad 0\leq r\leq n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $T^2=I$, its minimal polynomial divides $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$. In characteristic different from two, these linear factors are distinct. Thus the minimal polynomial is a product of distinct linear factors, which implies diagonalizability. Its eigenvalues belong to $\{1,-1\}$, yielding the stated forms. Conversely, each displayed diagonal matrix squares to $I$, so every possibility occurs.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

In characteristic two, the matrix
$$
\boxed{T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}}
$$
satisfies $T^2=I$ but is not diagonalizable.


::: pf-proof

Direct multiplication gives $T^2=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{psmallmatrix}=I$ in characteristic two. The only eigenvalue is $1$, but $T\neq I$. A diagonalizable matrix whose only eigenvalue is $1$ equals $I$, so this $T$ cannot be diagonalizable.

:::
