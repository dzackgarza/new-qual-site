::: {.problem}
Let $f,g\in L^1([0,1])$.
Assume for all functions $\varphi\in C^\infty[0,1]$ with $\varphi(0)=\varphi(1)$, we have $$\int_0^1 f(t)\varphi'(t)\,dt=-\int_0^1 g(t)\varphi(t)\,dt.$$ Show that $f$ is absolutely continuous and $f'=g$ a.e.
More precisely, show that the $L^1$ class of $f$ has an absolutely continuous representative $\widetilde f$ satisfying $\widetilde f'=g$ almost everywhere.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
\[
G(x)=\int_0^x g(t)\,dt.
\]
Since $g\in L^1([0,1])$, the function $G$ is absolutely continuous and
\[
G'=g\qquad\text{a.e. on }[0,1].
\]

<1>1. The function $h:=f-G$ has zero distributional derivative on $(0,1)$.
::: {.proof}
Every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(0,1)$ satisfies $\varphi(0)=\varphi(1)=0$, so the hypothesis gives
\[
\int_0^1 f\varphi'=-\int_0^1 g\varphi.
\]
Because $G$ is absolutely continuous with derivative $g$ a.e., integration by parts gives
\[
\int_0^1 G\varphi'=-\int_0^1 g\varphi.
\]
Subtracting,
\[
\int_0^1 h(t)\varphi'(t)\,dt=0
\qquad(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(0,1)).
\]
:::

<1>2. Any $h\in L^1(0,1)$ satisfying the identity in <1>1 is constant almost everywhere.
::: {.proof}
First let $\psi\in C_c^\infty(0,1)$ satisfy
\[
\int_0^1\psi(t)\,dt=0.
\]
Then
\[
\varphi(x):=\int_0^x\psi(t)\,dt
\]
belongs to $C_c^\infty(0,1)$ and $\varphi'=\psi$. Hence <1>1 gives
\[
\int_0^1 h\psi=0.
\]

Choose $\eta\in C_c^\infty(0,1)$ with $\int_0^1\eta=1$, and put
\[
c:=\int_0^1 h\eta.
\]
For arbitrary $\psi\in C_c^\infty(0,1)$, the function
\[
\psi_0:=\psi-\left(\int_0^1\psi\right)\eta
\]
has integral zero. Therefore
\[
0=\int_0^1 h\psi_0
=\int_0^1 h\psi-c\int_0^1\psi,
\]
so
\[
\int_0^1 (h-c)\psi=0
\qquad(\psi\in C_c^\infty(0,1)).
\]
The fundamental lemma for distributions then implies
\[
h=c\qquad\text{a.e. on }(0,1).
\]
:::

<1>3. Construct the absolutely continuous representative of $f$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2,
\[
f(x)=c+G(x)
\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in[0,1].
\]
Define
\[
\widetilde f(x)=c+\int_0^x g(t)\,dt.
\]
Then $\widetilde f$ is absolutely continuous, $\widetilde f=f$ almost everywhere, and
\[
\widetilde f'(x)=g(x)
\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in[0,1].
\]
Thus the $L^1$ class represented by $f$ has the asserted absolutely continuous representative.
