::: {.problem}
Let $E$ be a measurable subset of $[0,1]$.
Suppose there exists $\alpha\in(0,1)$ such that $$m(E\cap J)\ge\alpha\cdot m(J)$$ for all subintervals $J$ of $[0,1]$.
Prove that $m(E)=1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The same lower bound holds for every relatively open set $U\subseteq[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
Write $U$ as a countable disjoint union of intervals $J_k\subseteq[0,1]$.
Then
\[
m(E\cap U)
=\sum_k m(E\cap J_k)
\ge \alpha\sum_k m(J_k)
=\alpha m(U).
\]
:::

<1>2. The complement of $E$ has measure zero.
::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that
\[
a:=m([0,1]\setminus E)>0.
\]
By outer regularity of Lebesgue measure, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a relatively open set $U\subseteq[0,1]$ such that
\[
[0,1]\setminus E\subseteq U
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
m(U)<a+\varepsilon.
\]
Since $U$ contains $E^c$,
\[
m(E\cap U)=m(U)-m(E^c)<\varepsilon.
\]
On the other hand, <1>1 gives
\[
m(E\cap U)\ge \alpha m(U)\ge \alpha m(E^c)=\alpha a>0.
\]
Choosing $0<\varepsilon<\alpha a$ is a contradiction.
Hence $m(E^c)=0$, and therefore
\[
m(E)=1.
\]
:::
:::
