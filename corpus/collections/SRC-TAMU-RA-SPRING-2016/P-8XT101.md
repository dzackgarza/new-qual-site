::: {.problem}
Let $f,g\in L^1([0,1])$.
Suppose $$\int_0^1 x^n f(x)\,dx=\int_0^1 x^n g(x)\,dx$$ for all integers $n\ge 0$.
Prove that $f(x)=g(x)$ a.e.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Vanishing of the integral against polynomials:
<2>1. Define $h = f - g \in L^1([0, 1])$.
By hypothesis, for every $n \ge 0$:
\[
\int_0^1 x^n h(x) \, dx = \int_0^1 x^n f(x) \, dx - \int_0^1 x^n g(x) \, dx = 0.
\]
<2>2. By linearity, for every polynomial $p(x) = \sum_{k=0}^N a_k x^k \in \mathbb{R}[x]$:
\[
\int_0^1 p(x) h(x) \, dx = \sum_{k=0}^N a_k \int_0^1 x^k h(x) \, dx = 0.
\]

<1>2. Vanishing of the integral against continuous functions:
<2>1. Let $\phi \in C([0, 1])$.
By the Weierstrass Approximation Theorem, there exists a sequence of polynomials $(p_k)_{k=1}^\infty$ such that $p_k \to \phi$ uniformly on $[0, 1]$, i.e., $\lim_{k\to\infty} \|\phi - p_k\|_\infty = 0$.
<2>2. For every $k \ge 1$, $\int_0^1 p_k(x) h(x) \, dx = 0$.
Estimating the difference:
\[
\left| \int_0^1 \phi(x) h(x) \, dx \right| = \left| \int_0^1 (\phi(x) - p_k(x)) h(x) \, dx \right| \le \|\phi - p_k\|_\infty \|h\|_{L^1([0, 1])}.
\]
Taking $k \to \infty$ yields:
\[
\int_0^1 \phi(x) h(x) \, dx = 0 \quad \text{for all } \phi \in C([0, 1]).
\]

<1>3. Vanishing of the indefinite integral and conclusion $h = 0$ a.e.:
<2>1. For any subinterval $[a, b] \subseteq [0, 1]$, construct a sequence of continuous functions $\phi_k \in C([0, 1])$ with $0 \le \phi_k \le 1$ such that $\phi_k(x) \to \mathbf{1}_{[a, b]}(x)$ for every $x \in [0, 1] \setminus \{a, b\}$.
<2>2. Because $|\phi_k(x) h(x)| \le |h(x)| \in L^1([0, 1])$, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem:
\[
\int_a^b h(x) \, dx = \int_0^1 \mathbf{1}_{[a, b]}(x) h(x) \, dx = \lim_{k \to \infty} \int_0^1 \phi_k(x) h(x) \, dx = 0.
\]
<2>3. Since $\int_a^b h(x) \, dx = 0$ for all $0 \le a < b \le 1$, by the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem:
\[
h(x) = \frac{d}{dx} \int_0^x h(t) \, dt = 0 \quad \text{for almost every } x \in [0, 1].
\]
Thus $f(x) = g(x)$ for almost every $x \in [0, 1]$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
$f(x) = g(x)$ almost everywhere on $[0, 1]$. Q.E.D.
:::
