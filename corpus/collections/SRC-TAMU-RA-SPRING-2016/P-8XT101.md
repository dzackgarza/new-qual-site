::: {.problem}
Let $f,g\in L^1([0,1])$.
Suppose $$\int_0^1 x^n f(x)\,dx=\int_0^1 x^n g(x)\,dx$$ for all integers $n\ge 0$.
Prove that $f(x)=g(x)$ a.e.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $h=f-g\in L^1$, then $\int_0^1 x^n h=0$ for all $n\ge0$.
Proof: hypothesis.

<1>2. By Weierstrass, polynomials are dense in $C[0,1]$; for any $\epsilon>0$ and $p$ polynomial, $\int_0^1 p h=0$.
Proof: <1>1 and linearity.

<1>3. For any $u\in L^\infty$, choose $p_n\to u$ in $L^1$? Actually approximate $\operatorname{sgn}h$ by continuous functions.
Proof: $C[0,1]$ dense in $L^1$.

<1>4. Taking $p_n\to\operatorname{sgn}h$, $\int_0^1|h|=\lim\int p_n h=0$.
Proof: <1>2 and dominated.

<1>5. Hence $h=0$ a.e., i.e. $f=g$ a.e.
Proof: <1>4.

<1>6. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>5.
:::
