::: {.problem}
Suppose $E$ is a measurable subset of $[0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure $m(E)=\frac{99}{100}$.
Show that there exists a number $x\in[0,1]$ such that for all $r\in(0,1)$, $$m\bigl(E\cap(x-r,x+r)\bigr)\ge\frac{r}{4}.$$ Hint: Use the Hardy--Littlewood maximal inequality: $$m\bigl(\{x\in\mathbb{R}:Mf(x)\ge\alpha\}\bigr)\le\frac{3}{\alpha}\|f\|_1$$ for all $f\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$.
Here $Mf$ denotes the Hardy--Littlewood Maximal function of $f$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Control points having a bad small radius.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
A=[0,1]\setminus E,
\qquad m(A)=\frac1{100},
\]
and extend $\mathbf 1_A$ by zero outside $[0,1]$.
Suppose $x\in[1/4,3/4]$ and for some $0<r\le1/4$,
\[
m(E\cap(x-r,x+r))<\frac r4.
\]
Since $(x-r,x+r)\subset[0,1]$,
\[
m(A\cap(x-r,x+r))
=2r-m(E\cap(x-r,x+r))
>\frac{7r}{4}.
\]
Hence
\[
M\mathbf1_A(x)
\ge \frac1{2r}m(A\cap(x-r,x+r))
>\frac78.
\]
Therefore every point in $[1/4,3/4]$ having a bad radius $r\le1/4$ belongs to
\[
B:=\{x\in\mathbb R:M\mathbf1_A(x)\ge7/8\}.
\]
By the stated Hardy--Littlewood maximal inequality,
\[
m(B)\le \frac{3}{7/8}\|\mathbf1_A\|_1
=\frac{24}{7}\cdot\frac1{100}
=\frac{6}{175}<\frac12.
\]
Since $m([1/4,3/4])=1/2$, there exists
\[
x\in[1/4,3/4]\setminus B.
\]
For this $x$,
\[
m(E\cap(x-r,x+r))\ge\frac r4
\]
for every $0<r\le1/4$.
:::

<1>2. Larger radii satisfy the estimate automatically.
::: {.proof}
Fix the $x$ from <1>1 and let $1/4<r<1$.
Because $x\in[1/4,3/4]$, the interval $(x-r,x+r)$ meets $[0,1]$ in a set of measure at least
\[
\min\left\{1,r+\frac14\right\}.
\]
If $1/4<r\le3/4$, then
\[
m(E\cap(x-r,x+r))
\ge r+\frac14-\frac1{100}>\frac r4.
\]
If $3/4<r<1$, then $(x-r,x+r)$ contains all of $[0,1]$, so
\[
m(E\cap(x-r,x+r))=m(E)=\frac{99}{100}>\frac r4.
\]
Thus the required inequality holds for every $r>1/4$.
Combining the two ranges proves the claim.
:::
