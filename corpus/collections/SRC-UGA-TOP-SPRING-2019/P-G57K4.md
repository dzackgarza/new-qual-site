::: problem
Let $\Sigma$ be a compact connected surface and let  $p_1, \cdots , p_k \in \Sigma$.

Prove that $H_2 \qty{\Sigma \setminus \union_{i=1}^k {p_i} } = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The notation $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ presupposes $k\ge 1$.
Set
\[
M=\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^k\{p_i\}.
\]

<1>1. Every singular $2$-cycle in $M$ is contained in a compact connected subsurface $N\subset M$ with nonempty boundary.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
z=\sum_{j=1}^r n_j\sigma_j
\]
be a singular $2$-cycle in $M$.
Its support
\[
K=\bigcup_{j=1}^r\sigma_j(\Delta^2)
\]
is compact, since it is a finite union of compact sets.
Moreover $K\cap\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}=\varnothing$.

Choose pairwise disjoint sufficiently small coordinate neighborhoods $U_i$ of the points $p_i$ whose closures are disjoint from $K$.
For an interior point take $U_i$ to be an open disk; for a boundary point take a relative open half-disk.
After shrinking them if necessary, the complement
\[
N=\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^k U_i
\]
is a compact surface with nonempty boundary and contains $K$.
Corners arising from half-disks can be straightened without changing the homeomorphism type.

The complement is connected: a path in $\Sigma$ joining two points of $N$ can be modified, one neighborhood at a time, by replacing each segment that enters an $U_i$ by an arc along the corresponding boundary circle or boundary arc.
Thus $N$ is a compact connected subsurface of $M$ containing the image of every simplex occurring in $z$.
:::

<1>2. If $N$ is a compact connected surface with nonempty boundary, then
\[
H_2(N;\ZZ)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Triangulate $N$ by a finite triangulation in which $\partial N$ is a subcomplex.
It is enough to compute simplicial homology, since simplicial and singular homology agree for a triangulated space.

Let
\[
c=\sum_{\tau} a_\tau\tau\in C_2(N;\ZZ)
\]
be a simplicial $2$-cycle.
Because $\partial N\ne\varnothing$, some boundary edge $e$ belongs to exactly one $2$-simplex $\tau_0$.
The coefficient of $e$ in $\partial c$ is therefore $\pm a_{\tau_0}$.
Since $\partial c=0$, one has
\[
a_{\tau_0}=0.
\]

If two $2$-simplices $\tau$ and $\tau'$ share an interior edge and $a_\tau=0$, then the coefficient of that edge in $\partial c$ is $\pm a_{\tau'}$, hence $a_{\tau'}=0$ as well.
The adjacency graph of the $2$-simplices of a connected triangulated surface is connected: a path between points in the interiors of two triangles can be perturbed away from vertices and then crosses a finite sequence of common edges.
Starting from $\tau_0$ and propagating across this graph gives
\[
a_\tau=0
\]
for every $2$-simplex $\tau$.
Thus the only simplicial $2$-cycle is zero, so
\[
H_2(N;\ZZ)=0.
\]
:::

<1>3. Every singular $2$-cycle in $M$ is a boundary in $M$.
::: {.proof}
Let $z$ be any singular $2$-cycle in $M$.
By <1>1, regard $z$ as a singular cycle in a compact connected subsurface $N\subset M$ with nonempty boundary.
By <1>2,
\[
H_2(N;\ZZ)=0.
\]
Hence there is a singular $3$-chain $b$ in $N$ such that
\[
\partial b=z.
\]
Since $N\subset M$, the same equation holds in the singular chain complex of $M$.
Thus $z$ is a boundary in $M$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
\[
\boxed{H_2\!\left(\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^k\{p_i\};\ZZ\right)=0}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>3 every singular $2$-cycle represents the zero homology class, which is exactly the asserted vanishing.
:::
