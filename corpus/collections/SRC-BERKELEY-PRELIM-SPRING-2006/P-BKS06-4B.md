::: {.problem}
Let $n$ be a positive integer, and let $\GL_{n}(\RR)$ be the group of invertible $n \times n$ matrices.
Let $S$ be the set of $A \in \GL_{n}(\RR)$ such that $A - I$ has rank $\leq 2$. Prove that $S$ generates $\GL_{n}(\RR)$ as a group.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$\GL_n(\RR)$ is generated by the elementary matrices: the matrices obtained from $I$ by interchanging two rows, by multiplying one row by a nonzero scalar, or by adding a multiple of one row to another row.

::: pf-proof

Gaussian elimination reduces every $A\in\GL_n(\RR)$ to $I$ by elementary row operations, so $E_k\cdots E_1A=I$ for elementary matrices $E_i$. The inverse of an elementary matrix is elementary, so $A=E_1^{-1}\cdots E_k^{-1}$ is a product of elementary matrices.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Every elementary matrix lies in $S$.

::: pf-proof

An elementary matrix $E$ differs from $I$ in at most two rows, so $E-I$ has at most two nonzero rows and rank at most $2$. It is invertible.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the subgroup generated by $S$ contains a generating set of $\GL_n(\RR)$, so it is $\GL_n(\RR)$.

:::
