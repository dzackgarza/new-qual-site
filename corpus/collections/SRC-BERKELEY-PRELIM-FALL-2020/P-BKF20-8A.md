::: {.problem}
Let $F=\mathbb Z/(179)$.

(a) Prove that $10$ is not a square in $F$.

(b) Prove that $10$ generates the multiplicative group $F^\times$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The element $-1$ is not a square in $F$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose for contradiction that
$$
y^2=-1
$$
for some $y\in F$. Then $y\ne0$, so Fermat's little theorem gives
$$
y^{178}=1.
$$
On the other hand,
$$
y^{178}
=
(y^2)^{89}
=
(-1)^{89}
=
-1,
$$
a contradiction. Thus $-1$ is not a square.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

In $F$,
$$
10=-13^2.
$$

::: pf-proof

One has
$$
13^2=169\equiv-10\pmod{179}.
$$
Multiplying by $-1$ gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The element $10$ is not a square in $F$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
10=x^2
$$
for some $x\in F$. Since $13\ne0$ in $F$, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
-1
=
\frac{x^2}{13^2}
=
\left(\frac{x}{13}\right)^2.
$$
This contradicts step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Hence $10$ is not a square. This proves
part (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One has
$$
10^{89}=-1
$$
in $F$.

::: pf-proof

Using step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
10^{89}
&=
(-13^2)^{89}\\
&=
-13^{178}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $13\ne0$ in $F$, Fermat's little theorem gives
$$
13^{178}=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
10^{89}=-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The multiplicative order of $10$ in $F^\times$ is
$$
178.
$$

::: pf-proof

The group $F^\times$ has
$$
179-1=178=2\cdot89
$$
elements. By Lagrange's theorem, the order of $10$ divides $178$, whose
positive divisors are
$$
1,\ 2,\ 89,\ 178.
$$

The order is not $1$, since $10\ne1$. It is not $2$, since
$$
10^2=100\ne1
$$
in $F$. It is not $89$ because step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
10^{89}=-1\ne1.
$$
Thus the only remaining possibility is order $178$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Hence $10$ generates $F^\times$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, the cyclic subgroup generated by $10$ has $178$ elements.
Since $F^\times$ itself has $178$ elements,
$$
\langle10\rangle=F^\times.
$$
This proves part (b).

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s6){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
