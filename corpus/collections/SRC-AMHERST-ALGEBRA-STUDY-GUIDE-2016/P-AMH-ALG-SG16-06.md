::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group, and let $s\in G$.
The centralizer of $s$ in $G$ is defined to be
$$
C(s)=\{g\in G:gs=sg\}.
$$
(a) Prove that $C(s)$ is a subgroup of $G$.
(b) Prove that $\langle s\rangle$ is contained in $C(s)$.
(Recall that $\langle s\rangle$ is the subgroup of $G$ generated by $s$.)
(c) Prove that $\langle s\rangle$ is a normal subgroup of $C(s)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
The centralizer $C(s)$ is a subgroup of $G$.

::: pf-proof
The identity satisfies
$$
es=s=se,
$$
so $e\in C(s)$.

If $g,h\in C(s)$, then
$$
(gh)s
=g(hs)
=g(sh)
=(gs)h
=(sg)h
=s(gh),
$$
so $gh\in C(s)$.

If $g\in C(s)$, then
$$
gs=sg.
$$
Multiplying on the left and right by $g^{-1}$ gives
$$
g^{-1}s=sg^{-1},
$$
so $g^{-1}\in C(s)$. Therefore $C(s)\le G$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The cyclic subgroup generated by $s$ is contained in $C(s)$:
$$
\boxed{\langle s\rangle\le C(s)}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Every element of $\langle s\rangle$ has the form $s^n$ for some $n\in\ZZ$. Since powers of the same element commute,
$$
s^ns=s^{n+1}=ss^n.
$$
Thus $s^n\in C(s)$ for every $n$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The subgroup $\langle s\rangle$ is normal in $C(s)$.

::: pf-proof
Let $g\in C(s)$ and let $h=s^n\in\langle s\rangle$. Since $g$ commutes with $s$, it commutes with every integer power $s^n$. Hence
$$
ghg^{-1}
=gs^ng^{-1}
=s^ngg^{-1}
=s^n
=h.
$$
Therefore conjugation by every $g\in C(s)$ preserves $\langle s\rangle$, so
$$
\boxed{\langle s\rangle\trianglelefteq C(s)}.
$$
:::
