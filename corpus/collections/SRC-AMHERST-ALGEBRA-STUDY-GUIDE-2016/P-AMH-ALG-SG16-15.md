::: {.problem}
Fix an integer $n\ge 2$, and write $S_n$ for the permutation group on $n$ letters.
Let $\varphi:S_n\to G$ be a homomorphism, where $G$ is a group of odd order.
(I.e., $G$ is a finite group with an odd number of elements.)
(a) Prove that every transposition (i.e., $2$-cycle) $\tau\in S_n$ is in $\ker\varphi$.
That is, prove that $\varphi(\tau)=e$.
(b) Prove that $\varphi$ is the trivial homomorphism; i.e., prove that for all $\sigma\in S_n$, we have $\varphi(\sigma)=e$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}
Every transposition $\tau\in S_n$ lies in $\ker\varphi$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\tau$ be a transposition. Then
$$
\tau^2=e_{S_n}.
$$
Applying $\varphi$ gives
$$
\varphi(\tau)^2
=\varphi(\tau^2)
=e_G.
$$
Hence the order of $\varphi(\tau)$ divides $2$.

On the other hand, $\varphi(\tau)\in G$, so by Lagrange's theorem its order divides $\cardinality{G}$. Since $\cardinality{G}$ is odd,
$$
\gcd(2,\cardinality{G})=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{ord}(\varphi(\tau))=1,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{\varphi(\tau)=e_G}.
$$
Thus every transposition lies in $\ker\varphi$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The homomorphism $\varphi$ is trivial.

::: pf-proof
The symmetric group $S_n$ is generated by transpositions. Hence every
$$
\sigma\in S_n
$$
can be written as
$$
\sigma=\tau_1\cdots\tau_r
$$
for transpositions $\tau_i$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\varphi(\tau_i)=e_G
$$
for every $i$. Therefore
$$
\varphi(\sigma)
=\varphi(\tau_1)\cdots\varphi(\tau_r)
=e_G.
$$
Since $\sigma$ was arbitrary,
$$
\boxed{\varphi\text{ is trivial}}.
$$
:::
