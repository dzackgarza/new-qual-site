::: {.problem}
Let $f(x)=x^2+1\in\FF_3[x]$, where $\FF_3=\{0,1,2\}$ is the field of three elements.
Let $I=\langle f\rangle$ be the principal ideal in $\FF_3[x]$ generated by $f$.
(a) Prove that $f$ is irreducible in $\FF_3[x]$.
(b) Prove that the quotient ring $\FF_3[x]/I$ is a field.
(c) Find the multiplicative inverse of $I+x$ in the quotient ring $\FF_3[x]/I$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}
The polynomial $f(x)=x^2+1$ has no root in $\FF_3$.

::: pf-proof
Direct evaluation gives
$$
f(0)=1,
\qquad
f(1)=1+1=2,
$$
and
$$
f(2)=2^2+1=4+1=5\equiv2\pmod3.
$$
Thus $f(a)\neq0$ for every $a\in\FF_3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}
The polynomial $f$ is irreducible in $\FF_3[x]$.

::: pf-proof
A polynomial of degree $2$ over a field is reducible if and only if it has a
root in that field.  Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that $f$ has no root in $\FF_3$.
Therefore $f$ is irreducible.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Part (a) follows.

::: pf-proof
This is step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}
The ideal
$$
I=\langle f\rangle
$$
is maximal in $\FF_3[x]$.

::: pf-proof
The ring $\FF_3[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, and in a polynomial ring over
a field the principal ideal generated by an irreducible polynomial is maximal.
Apply this to the irreducible polynomial from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}
The quotient ring
$$
\boxed{\FF_3[x]/I\text{ is a field}.}
$$

::: pf-proof
A quotient $R/M$ of a commutative ring with identity is a field exactly when
$M$ is a maximal ideal.  Apply this criterion to step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Part (b) follows.

::: pf-proof
This is step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}
In $\FF_3[x]/I$ one has
$$
x^2=-1=2.
$$

::: pf-proof
Since $x^2+1=f\in I$,
$$
I+x^2+1=I,
$$
so
$$
I+x^2=I-1=I+2.
$$
Thus the relation $x^2=2$ holds in the quotient.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}
The multiplicative inverse of $I+x$ is
$$
\boxed{I+2x}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Using step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
(I+x)(I+2x)
&=I+2x^2\\
&=I+2\cdot2\\
&=I+4\\
&=I+1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $I+2x$ is a multiplicative inverse of $I+x$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Part (c) follows.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the requested inverse.
:::
