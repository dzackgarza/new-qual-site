::: {.problem}
Let $\beta\in\CC$.

(a) Show that for every $n\ge0$,
$$
\left(\frac{\beta^n}{n!}\right)^2
=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D(0,1)}
\frac{\beta^n e^{\beta z}}{n!z^{n+1}}\,dz.
$$

(b) Show that
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\beta^n}{n!}\right)^2
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{2\beta\cos\theta}\,d\theta.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is that of the retained solution sheet of the collection: the solutions to *Math 185 Problem Set 7* (Fall 2009/10), Problem 1, PDF p. 1 (`assets/attachments/math185f09-hw7sol.pdf`), whose (a) applies the generalised Cauchy integral formula and whose (b) expands $e^{\beta/z}$ and integrates termwise; the two integration steps are justified here.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) For every $n\ge0$, $\Bigl(\dfrac{\beta^n}{n!}\Bigr)^2=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\displaystyle\int_{\partial D(0,1)}\frac{\beta^ne^{\beta z}}{n!\,z^{n+1}}\,dz$.

::: pf-proof
The function $f(z)=e^{\beta z}$ is entire, so the generalised Cauchy integral formula applies to the circle $\partial D(0,1)$ and its interior:
$$
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D(0,1)}\frac{e^{\beta z}}{z^{n+1}}\,dz=\frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}=\frac{\beta^n}{n!},
$$
the $n$-th derivative of $e^{\beta z}$ being $\beta^ne^{\beta z}$ and its value at $0$ being $\beta^n$. Multiplying both sides by the constant $\beta^n/n!$ gives the identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #uniform}
(b) On $\abs z=1$ the series $\sum_{n\ge0}\beta^n/(n!z^n)$ converges uniformly to $e^{\beta/z}$.

::: pf-proof
For $\abs z=1$ one has $\abs{\beta^n/(n!z^n)}=\abs{\beta}^n/n!$, and $\sum_n\abs{\beta}^n/n!=e^{\abs\beta}<\infty$, so the series converges uniformly on the circle by the Weierstrass $M$-test. Its sum is $e^{\beta/z}$, the exponential series evaluated at $\beta/z$, which is legitimate for every $z\ne0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #series}
(b) $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{\beta^n}{n!}\Bigr)^2=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D(0,1)}\frac{1}{z}e^{\beta(z+1/z)}\,dz$.

::: pf-proof
Multiplying the uniform expansion of step [](#uniform){.pf-ref} by the bounded function $e^{\beta z}/z$ preserves uniform convergence, so
$$
\frac1z e^{\beta(z+1/z)}=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{\beta^ne^{\beta z}}{n!\,z^{n+1}}
$$
uniformly on the circle. Integrating term by term, which uniform convergence on a compact curve permits, and applying step [](#part-a){.pf-ref} to each term gives
$$
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D(0,1)}\frac{1}{z}e^{\beta(z+1/z)}\,dz=\sum_{n\ge0}\Bigl(\frac{\beta^n}{n!}\Bigr)^2.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(b) $\boxed{\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{\beta^n}{n!}\Bigr)^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{2\beta\cos\theta}\,d\theta}$.

::: pf-proof
Parametrise the circle by $z=e^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le2\pi$; then $dz/z=i\,d\theta$ and $z+z^{-1}=2\cos\theta$, so the integral of step [](#series){.pf-ref} becomes
$$
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{2\beta\cos\theta}\,i\,d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{2\beta\cos\theta}\,d\theta.
$$
That is the stated identity.
:::
