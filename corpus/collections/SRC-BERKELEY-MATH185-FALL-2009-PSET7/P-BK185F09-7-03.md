::: {.problem}
Let $f:\CC\to\CC$ be entire.

(a) Suppose there are $\alpha,\beta\in\CC^\times$ with $\alpha/\beta\notin\RR$ and
$$
f(z+\alpha)=f(z),\qquad f(z+\beta)=f(z)
$$
for all $z$. Show that $f$ is constant.

(b) Suppose
$$
\lim_{\abs{z}\to\infty}\frac{f(z)}z=0.
$$
Show that $f$ is constant.

(c) Find all entire functions satisfying
$$
f''(1/n)+f(1/n)=0
$$
for every $n\in\NN$.

(d) Fix $n\ge2$. Find all entire functions satisfying
$$
f(z^n)=f(z)^n
$$
for all $z\in\CC$.
:::

::: {.solution}
This is Problem 3 of the retained MATH 185 (Fall 2009) homework 7 solutions, `assets/attachments/math185f09-hw7sol.pdf`, PDF pp. 2--4, and the four arguments below are that solution's, with its part (d) coefficient comparison written out.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a}
(a) If $\alpha/\beta\notin\RR$ and $f$ is periodic with periods $\alpha$ and $\beta$, then $f$ is constant.

::: pf-proof
$\alpha/\beta\notin\RR$ means that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are linearly independent over $\RR$, so they span $\CC$ and every $z$ is $x\alpha+y\beta$ with $x,y\in\RR$. Writing $x=[x]+\langle x\rangle$ with $[x]\in\ZZ$ and $\langle x\rangle\in[0,1)$, periodicity gives
$$f(z)=f(x\alpha+y\beta)=f(\alpha\langle x\rangle+\beta\langle y\rangle),$$
so $f(\CC)=f(P)$ where $P$ is the compact parallelogram $\theset{s\alpha+t\beta\mid0\leq s,t\leq1}$. Since $f$ is continuous, $\abs{f}$ is bounded on $P$; hence $f$ is bounded on $\CC$ and Liouville's theorem makes it constant.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
(b) If $\lim_{\abs{z}\to\infty}f(z)/z=0$ then $f$ is constant.

::: pf-proof
Define $g(z)=\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z}$ for $z\neq0$ and $g(0)=f'(0)$. Then $g$ is entire: away from $0$ it is a quotient with nonzero denominator, and at $0$ it is the difference quotient whose limit is $f'(0)$. The hypothesis gives $\frac{f(z)}{z}\to0$, so $g(z)=\frac{f(z)}{z}-\frac{f(0)}{z}\to0$ as $\abs{z}\to\infty$; in particular $g$ is bounded. The maximum modulus principle gives $\abs{g}\leq\limsup_{\abs{z}\to\infty}\abs{g}=0$, so $g\equiv0$, and $f=f(0)$ is constant.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #c}
(c) $f''+f$ vanishes at every $1/n$, so $f''+f\equiv0$ and $f(z)=A\cos z+B\sin z$.

::: pf-proof
$h=f''+f$ is entire, and $h(1/n)=0$ for every $n$. Since $1/n\to0$ and $h$ is continuous, $h(0)=0$ as well; the zeros of $h$ accumulate at $0$, an interior point of the domain, so the identity theorem gives $h\equiv0$. The solutions of $y''+y=0$ are $A\cos z+B\sin z$: the functions $\cos$ and $\sin$ are entire, satisfy the equation, and are linearly independent, so by the standard existence-and-uniqueness argument for second order linear equations every entire solution is of that form.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #d}
(d) For $n\geq2$, the entire solutions of $f(z^{n})=f(z)^{n}$ are $f\equiv0$ and $f(z)=\zeta z^{k}$ with $k\geq0$ and $\zeta^{n-1}=1$.

::: pf-proof
Write $f(z)=\sum_{m\geq0}a_{m}z^{m}$, so that $a_{0}=f(0)=f(0)^{n}=a_{0}^{n}$. If $a_{0}\neq0$ then $a_{0}^{n-1}=1$; we show $f$ is then constant. Otherwise let $k\geq1$ be the least index with $a_{k}\neq0$. Comparing the two sides near $0$,
$$f(z^{n})=a_{0}+a_{k}z^{kn}+\cdots,\qquad f(z)^{n}=a_{0}^{n}+na_{0}^{n-1}a_{k}z^{k}+\cdots,$$
so the coefficient of $z^{k}$ on the left is $0$ while on the right it is $na_{0}^{n-1}a_{k}\neq0$, since $n\geq2$, $a_{0}\neq0$ and $a_{k}\neq0$. This contradiction shows $f\equiv a_{0}$ with $a_{0}^{n-1}=1$. If $a_{0}=0$, factor $f(z)=z^{k}g(z)$ with $k\geq1$ and $g$ entire, $g(0)\neq0$; then $z^{nk}g(z^{n})=f(z^{n})=f(z)^{n}=z^{nk}g(z)^{n}$ gives $g(z^{n})=g(z)^{n}$, and the previous paragraph makes $g$ constant, say $g\equiv\zeta$ with $\zeta^{n-1}=1$, so $f(z)=\zeta z^{k}$. The zero function satisfies the equation as well, and every function of the displayed form does: $(\zeta z^{k})^{n}=\zeta^{n}z^{nk}=\zeta z^{nk}=f(z^{n})$ because $\zeta^{n-1}=1$.
:::
