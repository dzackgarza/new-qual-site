::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ be entire.

(a) Suppose there are $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C^\times$ with $\alpha/\beta\notin\mathbb R$ and
\[
f(z+\alpha)=f(z),\qquad f(z+\beta)=f(z)
\]
for all $z$. Show that $f$ is constant.

(b) Suppose
\[
\lim_{|z|\to\infty}\frac{f(z)}z=0.
\]
Show that $f$ is constant.

(c) Find all entire functions satisfying
\[
f''(1/n)+f(1/n)=0
\]
for every $n\in\mathbb N$.

(d) Fix $n\ge2$. Find all entire functions satisfying
\[
f(z^n)=f(z)^n
\]
for all $z\in\mathbb C$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For (a), $\alpha$ and $\beta$ span $\mathbb C$ as a real vector space. Periodicity reduces every value of $f$ to its value on a fixed compact fundamental parallelogram, so $f$ is bounded and therefore constant by Liouville's theorem.

For (b), define
\[
g(z)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{f(z)-f(0)}z,&z\ne0,\\
f'(0),&z=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Then $g$ is entire and tends to $0$ at infinity. The maximum-modulus principle makes $g$ identically zero, so $f$ is constant.

For (c), the entire function $f''+f$ vanishes at every $1/n$ and hence, by continuity, at $0$. Since these zeros accumulate at $0$, the identity theorem gives $f''+f\equiv0$. Thus
\[
f(z)=A\cos z+B\sin z,
\qquad A,B\in\mathbb C.
\]

For (d), writing $f(z)=\sum_{m\ge0}a_mz^m$ gives $a_0=a_0^n$. If $a_0\ne0$, comparison of the least positive-degree term shows that $f$ must be constant, with $a_0^{n-1}=1$. If $a_0=0$, factor $f(z)=z^kg(z)$ with $g(0)\ne0$; then $g(z^n)=g(z)^n$, so the previous case makes $g$ constant. Hence the solutions are
\[
f(z)=\zeta z^k,
\]
where $k\ge0$ and $\zeta^{\,n-1}=1$ (including the zero function separately).
:::
