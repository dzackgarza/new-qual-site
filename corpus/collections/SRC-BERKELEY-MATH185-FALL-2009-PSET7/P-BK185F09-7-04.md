::: {.problem}
Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb C$ be a region, let $f$ be analytic on $\Omega$, and let $z_0\in\Omega$ with $f'(z_0)\ne0$. Show that there is $r>0$ such that, for $\Gamma=\partial D(z_0,r)$,
\[
\int_\Gamma \frac{f'(z_0)}{f(z)-f(z_0)}\,dz=2\pi i.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Because $f'(z_0)\ne0$, for sufficiently small $r>0$ the quotient
\[
g(z)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{f(z)-f(z_0)}{z-z_0},&z\ne z_0,\\
f'(z_0),&z=z_0
\end{cases}
\]
is analytic and nonzero on $D(z_0,r)$. Thus $h=1/g$ is analytic there. Cauchy's integral formula gives
\[
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma\frac{h(z)}{z-z_0}\,dz=h(z_0)=\frac1{f'(z_0)}.
\]
Since $h(z)/(z-z_0)=1/(f(z)-f(z_0))$ on $\Gamma$, multiplying by $f'(z_0)$ gives the required integral.
:::
