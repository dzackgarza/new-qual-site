::: {.problem}
Let $\Omega\subseteq\CC$ be a region, let $f$ be analytic on $\Omega$, and let $z_0\in\Omega$ with $f'(z_0)\ne0$. Show that there is $r>0$ such that, for $\Gamma=\partial D(z_0,r)$,
$$
\int_\Gamma \frac{f'(z_0)}{f(z)-f(z_0)}\,dz=2\pi i.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this problem: the collection's provenance is the problem set alone, so the argument is the card's own. Let $\Omega\subseteq\CC$ be a region, $f$ analytic on $\Omega$, $z_0\in\Omega$ with $f'(z_0)\ne0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #quotient}
The function $g(z)=\dfrac{f(z)-f(z_0)}{z-z_0}$ for $z\ne z_0$, $g(z_0)=f'(z_0)$, is analytic near $z_0$, and there is $r>0$ with $g\ne0$ on $D(z_0,r)$.

::: pf-proof
For $z\ne z_0$ the quotient is a quotient of analytic functions with nonvanishing denominator, hence analytic. At $z_0$ the limit of the quotient is by definition $f'(z_0)$, so the singularity is removable and $g$ extends analytically with that value. Since $g(z_0)=f'(z_0)\ne0$ and $g$ is continuous at $z_0$, there is $r>0$ with $\abs{g(z)-g(z_0)}<\abs{f'(z_0)}/2$ on $D(z_0,r)$, hence $\abs{g(z)}>\abs{f'(z_0)}/2>0$ there.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #factor}
On $D(z_0,r)$ one has $f(z)-f(z_0)=(z-z_0)g(z)$ and
$$
\frac{f'(z_0)}{f(z)-f(z_0)}=\frac{f'(z_0)}{g(z)}\cdot\frac{1}{z-z_0}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The factorization is the definition of $g$ rearranged, valid for $z\ne z_0$; at $z_0$ both sides vanish to first order. Dividing by $(z-z_0)g(z)$, which is legitimate away from $z_0$ because $g\ne0$ by step [](#quotient){.pf-ref}, gives the display. Since $f'(z_0)/g$ is analytic and nonzero at $z_0$, the product has a simple pole at $z_0$ and no other singularity in the disc.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #residue}
$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\dfrac{f'(z_0)}{f(z)-f(z_0)}=1$.

::: pf-proof
For a simple pole at $z_0$ the residue is $\lim_{z\to z_0}(z-z_0)\cdot\frac{f'(z_0)}{(z-z_0)g(z)}=\frac{f'(z_0)}{g(z_0)}=\frac{f'(z_0)}{f'(z_0)}=1$, using the value $g(z_0)=f'(z_0)$ from step [](#quotient){.pf-ref} and $f'(z_0)\ne0$ for the division.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #integral}
$\displaystyle\int_\Gamma\frac{f'(z_0)}{f(z)-f(z_0)}\,dz=2\pi i$.

::: pf-proof
Take $\Gamma=\partial D(z_0,r)$ positively oriented. By step [](#factor){.pf-ref} the integrand has no singularity in the closed disc except the simple pole at $z_0$, since $f(z)=f(z_0)$ there would force $g(z)=0$, excluded by the choice of $r$. The residue theorem gives $2\pi i$ times the residue, which is $2\pi i\cdot1$ by step [](#residue){.pf-ref}. This is the asserted identity.
:::
