::: {.problem}
Given any group $G$, define a binary operation $\ast$ on the set $H=G\times G$ by
\[
(g_1,h_1)\ast(g_2,h_2)=(g_1g_2,g_2^{-1}h_1g_2h_2).
\]

(a) Show that $(H,\ast)$ is a group.

(b) In the case that $G$ is the alternating group $A_n$ on $n$ letters with $n\ge5$, prove that $H$ has no subgroup of index $2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
\Phi:H\longrightarrow G\times G,
\qquad
\Phi(g,h)\coloneqq(g,gh),
$$
where the codomain carries the usual direct-product multiplication.

<1>1. The map $\Phi$ is a bijection.

::: {.proof}
Its inverse is
$$
\Phi^{-1}(g,k)=(g,g^{-1}k).
$$
Indeed,
$$
\Phi(g,g^{-1}k)=(g,k)
$$
and
$$
\Phi^{-1}(g,gh)=(g,h).
$$
:::

<1>2. The map $\Phi$ carries $\ast$ to the ordinary product on $G\times G$.

::: {.proof}
For $(g_1,h_1),(g_2,h_2)\in H$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi\bigl((g_1,h_1)\ast(g_2,h_2)\bigr)
&=
\Phi\bigl(g_1g_2,g_2^{-1}h_1g_2h_2\bigr)\\
&=
\bigl(g_1g_2,g_1h_1g_2h_2\bigr)\\
&=
(g_1,g_1h_1)(g_2,g_2h_2)\\
&=
\Phi(g_1,h_1)\Phi(g_2,h_2).
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>3. The operation $\ast$ makes $H$ a group.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1 and <1>2, $\Phi$ is an isomorphism from $(H,\ast)$ onto the
direct-product group $G\times G$. Transporting the group structure across
this bijection proves part (a).
:::

<1>4. For $n\ge5$, the group $A_n$ is perfect.

::: {.proof}
The group $A_n$ is generated by $3$-cycles. To see this, write an even
permutation as a product of an even number of transpositions and group the
transpositions in pairs. A pair sharing one letter is a $3$-cycle, while a
pair of disjoint transpositions can be written as
$$
(a\ b)(c\ d)=(a\ c\ b)(a\ c\ d).
$$

It therefore suffices to show that every $3$-cycle lies in the commutator
subgroup. Let
$$
x=(a\ b\ c)
$$
be a $3$-cycle. Since $n\ge5$, choose distinct letters $d,e$ outside
$\{a,b,c\}$ and set
$$
y=(a\ b)(d\ e)\in A_n.
$$
Conjugation by $y$ interchanges $a$ and $b$, so
$$
yxy^{-1}=x^{-1}.
$$
Hence, with $[y,x]=yxy^{-1}x^{-1}$,
$$
[y,x]
=
x^{-1}x^{-1}
=
x,
$$
because $x^3=1$. Thus every generating $3$-cycle belongs to
$[A_n,A_n]$, and therefore
$$
[A_n,A_n]=A_n.
$$
:::

<1>5. For $G=A_n$ with $n\ge5$, the group $H$ is perfect.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
H\cong A_n\times A_n.
$$
Commutators in a direct product are taken coordinatewise, so step <1>4
gives
$$
[A_n\times A_n,A_n\times A_n]
=
[A_n,A_n]\times[A_n,A_n]
=
A_n\times A_n.
$$
Thus $H=[H,H]$.
:::

<1>6. The group $H$ has no subgroup of index $2$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose $K\leq H$ had index $2$. Every subgroup of index $2$ is normal,
so the quotient $H/K$ would be a group of order $2$, hence a nontrivial
abelian quotient of $H$. But every homomorphism from a perfect group to an
abelian group is trivial, since it kills the commutator subgroup. This
contradicts step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves part (a), and step <1>6 proves part (b).
