::: {.problem}
Let $x _ { 1 } , x _ { 2 } , . . .$ . be an infinite sequence of real numbers such that every subsequence contains a subsequence converging to 0. Must the original sequence converge?
:::

::: {.solution}
Yes; in fact it must converge to 0. If not, there would exist $\epsilon > 0$ such that for infinitely many n, we have $| x _ { n } | > \epsilon$ Choose a subsequence $S$ consisting of such $x _ { n }$ . If $T$ is a subsequence of S, then T also consists of numbers of absolute value greater than , so $T$ cannot converge to 0. Thus S has no subsequence converging to 0. This contradicts the given hypothesis.
:::
