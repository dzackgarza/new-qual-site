::: {.problem}
Let $n \geq 3$, and let $A_{n}$ be the alternating subgroup of the symmetric group on $n$ letters.
Prove that $A_{n}$ is generated by $(123)$ and $(12 \cdots n)$ if $n$ is odd, or by $(123)$ and $(2 \cdots n)$ if $n$ is even.
:::

::: {.solution}

Products of permutations are composed from right to left.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Let $G_n$ be the subgroup generated by the two given permutations. Then $G_n\subseteq A_n$, and $G_n$ acts transitively on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$.

::: pf-proof

A $3$-cycle is even, an $n$-cycle with $n$ odd is even, and an $(n-1)$-cycle with $n$ even is even. If $n$ is odd, the $n$-cycle is transitive. If $n$ is even, $(2\cdots n)$ is transitive on $\{2,\ldots,n\}$ and $(123)$ sends $1$ to $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For $n\ge4$, $G_n$ contains $(234)$, and it contains $(3\cdots n)$ if $n$ is odd and $(2\cdots n)$ if $n$ is even.

::: pf-proof

Recall that $\sigma(a_1a_2a_3)\sigma^{-1}=(\sigma(a_1)\,\sigma(a_2)\,\sigma(a_3))$. If $n$ is odd, conjugating $(123)$ by $(12\cdots n)$ gives $(234)$, and $(123)^{-1}(12\cdots n)=(3\cdots n)$, since this product fixes $1$ and $2$, sends $n$ to $3$, and sends $j$ to $j+1$ for $3\le j<n$. If $n$ is even, conjugating $(123)$ by $(2\cdots n)$ gives $(134)$, conjugating $(123)$ by $(134)$ gives $(324)$, and $(324)^{-1}=(234)$. The cycle $(2\cdots n)$ is a generator.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every $n\ge3$, $G_n=A_n$.

::: pf-proof

Use induction on $n$. For $n=3$, $A_3=\langle(123)\rangle$. Let $n\ge4$. The alternating group on the $n-1$ letters $2,\ldots,n$ corresponds to $A_{n-1}$ under $j\mapsto j-1$. Under this correspondence, $(234)$ is $(123)$, and the permutation of step [](#s2){.pf-ref} is $(2\cdots(n-1))$ when $n-1$ is even and $(12\cdots(n-1))$ when $n-1$ is odd. By the induction hypothesis and step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the stabilizer of $1$ in $G_n$ contains the full alternating group on $\{2,\ldots,n\}$, of order $\abs{A_{n-1}}$. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref} and the orbit-stabilizer theorem, $\abs{G_n}\ge n\abs{A_{n-1}}=\abs{A_n}$, so $G_n=A_n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

This is step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.
