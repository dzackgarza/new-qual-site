::: {.problem}
Let $f,g:X\to\RR$ be measurable functions on a measurable space $(X,\mathcal B)$.
Prove that $f+g$ is measurable.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $(X,\mathcal B)$ be a measurable space and $f,g\colon X\to\RR$ measurable,
where $\RR$ carries the Borel $\sigma$-algebra.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #pair}

$F=(f,g)\colon X\to\RR^2$ is measurable for the product $\sigma$-algebra on
$\RR^2$.

::: pf-proof

The product $\sigma$-algebra is generated by the rectangles $A\times B$ with
$A,B$ Borel in $\RR$, so it suffices that $F^{-1}(A\times B)\in\mathcal B$ for
such rectangles. And $F^{-1}(A\times B)=\{x:f(x)\in A,\ g(x)\in
B\}=f^{-1}(A)\cap g^{-1}(B)$, an intersection of two sets in $\mathcal B$
because $f$ and $g$ are measurable. The preimages of a generating family lying
in $\mathcal B$ means $F$ is measurable, since the family of sets whose
preimage lies in $\mathcal B$ is a $\sigma$-algebra.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #addition}

The addition map $S\colon\RR^2\to\RR$, $S(u,v)=u+v$, is Borel measurable.

::: pf-proof

$S$ is continuous: if $(u_n,v_n)\to(u,v)$ then $u_n+v_n\to u+v$ by the algebra
of limits in $\RR$. A continuous map between metric spaces is Borel
measurable, because the preimage of an open set is open, hence Borel.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sum}

$f+g=S\circ F$ is measurable.

::: pf-proof

A composition of measurable maps is measurable: for Borel $C\subseteq\RR$,
$(S\circ F)^{-1}(C)=F^{-1}(S^{-1}(C))$ and $S^{-1}(C)$ is Borel by step
[](#addition){.pf-ref}, so the preimage under $F$ is in $\mathcal B$ by step
[](#pair){.pf-ref}. So $f+g$ is measurable. Finiteness of $f$ and $g$ is what
allows $f+g$ to be defined at every point; for extended-valued functions the
sum fails to be defined where $f=\infty$ and $g=-\infty$.

:::
