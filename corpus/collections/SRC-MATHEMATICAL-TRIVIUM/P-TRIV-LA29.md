::: {.problem}
Consider the infinite dimensional vector space with the orthonormal basis $\vec { e _ { i } }$ $i = 1 , 2 , \dots$ Construct the continuous family of linear transformations $U ( \lambda )$ $0 \leqslant \lambda \leqslant 1$ , acting in this space such that $U ( 1 ) = E$ and $U ( 0 ) \vec { e } _ { i } = \vec { e } _ { i + 1 } , i = 1 , 2 ,$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $S$ denote the unilateral shift
$$
Se_i=e_{i+1},
\qquad
i=1,2,\ldots.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Define
$$
\boxed{
U(\lambda)
=
\lambda I
+
(1-\lambda)S,
\qquad
0\leq\lambda\leq1.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For each fixed $\lambda$, this is a linear combination of two linear
transformations, hence is linear.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

On the basis vectors,
$$
U(\lambda)e_i
=
\lambda e_i
+
(1-\lambda)e_{i+1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the definition in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and the relation $Se_i=e_{i+1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

At $\lambda=1$,
$$
U(1)=I.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
U(1)=1\cdot I+0\cdot S=I.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

At $\lambda=0$,
$$
U(0)e_i=e_{i+1}
$$
for every $i$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
U(0)e_i=Se_i=e_{i+1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The path $\lambda\mapsto U(\lambda)$ is continuous in operator norm.


::: pf-proof

For $\lambda,\mu\in[0,1]$,
$$
U(\lambda)-U(\mu)
=
(\lambda-\mu)(I-S).
$$
Hence
$$
\norm{U(\lambda)-U(\mu)}
\leq
\abs{\lambda-\mu}
\bigl(\norm{I}+\norm{S}\bigr).
$$
Since $S$ is an isometry on the Hilbert space generated by the orthonormal
basis,
$$
\norm{S}=1,
$$
so
$$
\norm{U(\lambda)-U(\mu)}
\leq
2\abs{\lambda-\mu}.
$$
Thus the path is Lipschitz, hence continuous.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} verify the two endpoint conditions and continuity of the
family defined in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::
