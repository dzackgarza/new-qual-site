::: {.problem}
"Brachistochrone curve" On a vertical plane $xOy$ consider the two points $A$ and $B$. Find a curve $y = y(x)$ connecting these points and such that an ideal pointlike body, that starts at rest at the point $A$ and moves along this curve without friction under constant gravity, reaches the point $B$ within the shortest time.
:::

::: {.solution}

Translate coordinates so that
$$
A=(0,0),
$$
and choose the positive $y$-axis vertically downward. Let
$$
B=(x_B,y_B).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

At height coordinate $y$, conservation of energy gives the speed
$$
v(y)=\sqrt{2gy}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The particle starts from rest at $A$. After descending a vertical distance
$y$, the loss of gravitational potential energy is $mgy$, so
$$
\frac12mv^2=mgy.
$$
Solve for $v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Along a graph $y=y(x)$,
$$
ds=\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Euclidean line element satisfies
$$
ds^2=dx^2+dy^2
=
\left(1+(y')^2\right)dx^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore the travel time is
$$
T[y]
=
\frac1{\sqrt{2g}}
\int
\frac{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}{\sqrt y}
\,dx.
$$


::: pf-proof

Time satisfies
$$
dt=\frac{ds}{v}.
$$
Substitute steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The integrand
$$
F(y,y')
=
\frac{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}{\sqrt y}
$$
does not depend explicitly on $x$.


::: pf-proof

The displayed formula contains only $y$ and $y'$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence every extremal satisfies Beltrami's identity
$$
F-y'\frac{\partial F}{\partial y'}
=
C
$$
for some constant $C>0$.


::: pf-proof

For an autonomous variational problem, differentiating
$$
F-y'F_{y'}
$$
along an Euler--Lagrange solution gives zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For this $F$,
$$
F-y'F_{y'}
=
\frac1{\sqrt y\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
F_{y'}
=
\frac{y'}{\sqrt y\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}.
$$
Therefore
$$
F-y'F_{y'}
=
\frac{1+(y')^2-(y')^2}
{\sqrt y\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Write
$$
C=\frac1{\sqrt{2a}},
\qquad
a>0.
$$
Then the extremal equation is
$$
\boxed{y\left(1+(y')^2\right)=2a}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and square both sides.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Introduce a parameter $\theta$ by
$$
y=a(1-\cos\theta).
$$


::: pf-proof

Then
$$
2a-y
=
a(1+\cos\theta),
$$
so the quotient appearing in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} becomes a trigonometric square.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Equation [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\frac{dy}{dx}
=
\cot\frac\theta2
$$
on the descending branch.


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
(y')^2
=
\frac{2a-y}{y}.
$$
Using step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
\frac{2a-y}{y}
=
\frac{1+\cos\theta}{1-\cos\theta}
=
\cot^2\frac\theta2.
$$
Choose the sign compatible with the orientation of the cycloidal arc.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Since
$$
\frac{dy}{d\theta}=a\sin\theta,
$$
one obtains
$$
\frac{dx}{d\theta}
=
a(1-\cos\theta).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
\frac{dx}{dy}
=
\tan\frac\theta2.
$$
Hence
$$
\frac{dx}{d\theta}
=
a\sin\theta
\tan\frac\theta2.
$$
Using
$$
\sin\theta\tan\frac\theta2
=
1-\cos\theta
$$
gives the result.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

With $x=0$ at $\theta=0$, integration gives
$$
\boxed{
x=a(\theta-\sin\theta),
\qquad
y=a(1-\cos\theta).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Integrate step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}:
$$
x
=
a\int_0^\theta(1-\cos s)\,ds
=
a(\theta-\sin\theta).
$$
The formula for $y$ is step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

The constants $a>0$ and terminal parameter $\theta_B$ are determined
by the endpoint equations
$$
\boxed{
x_B
=
a(\theta_B-\sin\theta_B),
\qquad
y_B
=
a(1-\cos\theta_B).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The cycloidal arc from step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} must pass through the prescribed endpoint
$B=(x_B,y_B)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Thus the brachistochrone is an arc of an inverted cycloid with a cusp
at the starting point $A$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} is precisely the standard cycloid parametrization generated by a
circle of radius $a$, with its cusp at $\theta=0$. The variational derivation
shows that every time-minimizing extremal has this form, and step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} selects
the member passing through $B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

The extremal satisfies the strengthened Legendre condition
$$
\frac{\partial^2 F}{\partial(y')^2}
=
\frac1{\sqrt y\left(1+(y')^2\right)^{3/2}}
>0
$$
away from the initial cusp.


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
F(y,y')
=
\frac{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}{\sqrt y}
$$
twice with respect to $y'$. Positivity holds for every $y>0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} derive the cycloidal extremal, and step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} records its
strict local minimizing character in the regular part of the arc.

:::
