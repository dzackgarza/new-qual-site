::: {.problem}
QConsider the points A and B lying on the plane xOy.
Find a curve $y = y ( x )$ connecting these points and such that rotation of $y ( x )$ around the axis Ox gives a surface in $\mathbb { R } ^ { 3 }$ with a minimal area.
:::

Write
$$
A=(x_A,y_A),
\qquad
B=(x_B,y_B),
$$
and assume the generating curve has
$$
y(x)>0
$$
on its regular interior.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The surface area generated by rotating the graph $y=y(x)$ about the
$x$-axis is
$$
\mathcal A[y]
=
2\pi
\int_{x_A}^{x_B}
y\sqrt{1+(y')^2}
\,dx.
$$


::: pf-proof

An infinitesimal arc of length
$$
ds=\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx
$$
sweeps out a ribbon of circumference $2\pi y$ and width $ds$. Thus
$$
dA=2\pi y\,ds.
$$
Integrating gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Up to the constant factor $2\pi$, the variational integrand is
$$
F(y,y')
=
y\sqrt{1+(y')^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the integrand from step <1>1 after omitting the irrelevant constant
factor $2\pi$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $F$ has no explicit $x$-dependence, every regular extremal obeys
Beltrami's identity
$$
F-y'F_{y'}=a
$$
for some constant $a$.


::: pf-proof

For an autonomous first-order variational problem, the quantity
$$
F-y'F_{y'}
$$
is constant along every Euler--Lagrange solution.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Here
$$
F-y'F_{y'}
=
\frac{y}{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate:
$$
F_{y'}
=
y
\frac{y'}{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
F-y'F_{y'}
&=
y\sqrt{1+(y')^2}
-
y\frac{(y')^2}{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}\\
&=
\frac{y}{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore every positive regular extremal satisfies
$$
\boxed{
\frac{y}{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}
=
a
}
$$
with $a>0$.


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>3--<1>4. Since $y>0$ and the denominator is positive, the
constant must satisfy $a>0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Equation <1>5 is equivalent to
$$
(y')^2
=
\frac{y^2}{a^2}-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Square step <1>5:
$$
\frac{y^2}{1+(y')^2}=a^2.
$$
Solve for $(y')^2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Separating variables gives
$$
\frac{dy}{\sqrt{y^2-a^2}}
=
\pm\frac{dx}{a}.
$$


::: pf-proof

From step <1>6,
$$
\frac{dy}{dx}
=
\pm
\frac{\sqrt{y^2-a^2}}{a}.
$$
Rearrange.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Integrating yields
$$
\operatorname{arcosh}\frac ya
=
\pm\frac{x-b}{a}
$$
for some constant $b$.


::: pf-proof

Use
$$
\frac{d}{dy}
\operatorname{arcosh}\frac ya
=
\frac1{\sqrt{y^2-a^2}}.
$$
Absorb the integration constant into $b$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence the extremal profile is
$$
\boxed{
y(x)
=
a\cosh\left(\frac{x-b}{a}\right),
\qquad
a>0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the hyperbolic cosine to step <1>8. Since $\cosh$ is even, the choice of
sign is absorbed into the parameter $b$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The constants $a$ and $b$ are determined by the endpoint equations
$$
\boxed{
y_A
=
a\cosh\left(\frac{x_A-b}{a}\right),
\qquad
y_B
=
a\cosh\left(\frac{x_B-b}{a}\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The generating curve in step <1>9 must pass through both prescribed points.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Rotating the catenary in step <1>9 about the $x$-axis produces a
catenoid.


::: pf-proof

The surface of revolution has parametrization
$$
X(x,\phi)
=
\left(
x,
a\cosh\left(\frac{x-b}{a}\right)\cos\phi,
a\cosh\left(\frac{x-b}{a}\right)\sin\phi
\right),
$$
which is the standard catenoid.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The integrand satisfies the strengthened Legendre condition
$$
F_{y'y'}
=
\frac{y}{\left(1+(y')^2\right)^{3/2}}
>0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
F_{y'}
=
y\frac{y'}{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}
$$
once more with respect to $y'$. Positivity follows from $y>0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus the regular minimal-surface profile is a catenary, and the
surface of revolution is a catenoid.


::: pf-proof

Steps <1>3--<1>10 determine every regular extremal, step <1>11 identifies the
surface, and step <1>12 gives the local minimizing condition for the area
functional.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>13 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
