::: {.problem}
Mark each statement true or false.
No justifications are required.

1. If $A$ is a real $n\times n$ matrix, then $A+A^T$ is diagonalizable.

2. The equation $z^3=-1$ has three distinct complex solutions, and $z=-1$ is one of them.

3. There is a $2\times2$ projection matrix $A$ projecting onto a line and a $2\times2$ reflection matrix $B$ such that $AB$ is a rotation matrix.

4. The map $T(f)(x)=f(f(x))$ is linear on $C^\infty$.

5. If a continuous function on $[-\pi,\pi]$ is both even and odd, then it is constant.

6. If $A$ is a $2\times2$ matrix, then the characteristic polynomial of $AA^T$ is $\lambda^2+c\lambda$ for some real $c$.

7. The function $f(t)=e^t$ is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue $1$ of $T=D^2$, where $Df=f'$.

8. The initial-value problem $f''(x)+f(x)=\sin x$, $f''(0)=1$, $f'(0)=1$ has exactly one solution.

9. The map $L(A)=A+A^T$ is linear on $M_n$, and its only eigenvalues are $0$ and $2$.

10. The smooth functions $f(x,y,z)$ satisfying $f_{xx}+f_{yy}+f_{zz}=f$ form a vector space.

11. If a $5\times5$ matrix has rank $2$, then the eigenvalue $0$ has algebraic multiplicity $3$.

12. Every equilibrium point of a nonlinear system $\dot x=f(x,y)$, $\dot y=g(x,y)$ lies on at least one nullcline.

13. The map $T(A)=\operatorname{rank}(A)$ is linear from $M_2$ to $\mathbb R$.

14. If $A$ is an $n\times n$ reflection matrix, then $A=QR$ with $Q=A$ and $R=I_n$ is its QR decomposition.

15. If all eigenvalues of a $2\times2$ matrix $A$ are zero, then $A$ is the zero matrix.

16. The discrete dynamical system $x(t+1)=7x(t)+3x(t-1)$ has the property that $|x(t)|$ stays bounded for every initial condition $(x(0),x(1))$.

17. $\|6\sin x+8\cos(9x)\|=10$ for the norm induced by
\[
\langle f,g\rangle=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)g(x)\,dx.
\]

18. Every reflection has real eigenvalues and an orthonormal eigenbasis.

19. Let $A$ be an $n\times m$ matrix with trivial kernel.
    If
\[
A(A^TA)^{-1}A^Tb=b
\]
for every $b$, then $n=m$.
20. If $A,B$ are $2\times2$ matrices, $Ax=0$ has infinitely many solutions, and $Bx=0$ has exactly one solution, then $ABx=0$ has infinitely many solutions.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Determine the truth value of each of the twenty statements and justify every answer.

The truth values are
$$
\boxed{T,T,F,F,T,F,T,T,T,T,F,T,F,T,F,F,T,T,T,T}.
$$

<1>1. Statements 1--5.
::: {.proof}
    <2>1. **True.** The matrix $A+A^T$ is real symmetric, hence orthogonally diagonalizable.

    <2>2. **True.** The polynomial $z^3+1$ has the three distinct roots
    $$
    e^{i\pi/3},\quad -1,\quad e^{5i\pi/3}.
    $$

    <2>3. **False.** A projection onto a line in $\mathbb R^2$ has rank one and is singular. Hence its product with any reflection is singular, whereas a rotation matrix is invertible.

    <2>4. **False.** For example, with $f(x)=x^2$,
    $$
    T(2f)(x)=2f(2f(x))=8x^4,
    $$
    while
    $$
    2T(f)(x)=2x^4.
    $$

    <2>5. **True.** If $f$ is both even and odd, then
    $$
    f(x)=f(-x)=-f(x),
    $$
    so $f\equiv0$, which is constant.
:::

<1>2. Statements 6--10.
::: {.proof}
    <2>1. **False.** The constant term of the characteristic polynomial of $AA^T$ is
    $$
    \det(AA^T)=(\det A)^2,
    $$
    which need not vanish; take $A=I_2$.

    <2>2. **True.** Since
    $$
    D^2e^t=e^t,
    $$
    $e^t$ is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue $1$.

    <2>3. **True.** Evaluating the differential equation at $x=0$ gives
    $$
    1+f(0)=0,
    $$
    so $f(0)=-1$. Thus the data are the standard initial conditions
    $f(0)=-1$ and $f'(0)=1$, which determine a unique solution of the second-order linear ODE.

    <2>4. **True.** The map is linear. On symmetric matrices it acts as multiplication by $2$, and on skew-symmetric matrices it acts as $0$. Since
    $$
    M_n=\operatorname{Sym}_n\oplus\operatorname{Skew}_n,
    $$
    its only possible eigenvalues are $0$ and $2$.

    <2>5. **True.** The equation
    $$
    f_{xx}+f_{yy}+f_{zz}-f=0
    $$
    is homogeneous and linear in $f$, so its solution set is a vector space.
:::

<1>3. Statements 11--15.
::: {.proof}
    <2>1. **False.** Rank $2$ only forces nullity $3$, hence geometric multiplicity $3$ for the eigenvalue $0$. Its algebraic multiplicity may be larger. For example,
    $$
    J_2(0)\oplus J_2(0)\oplus(0)
    $$
    has rank $2$ and characteristic polynomial $\lambda^5$.

    <2>2. **True.** At an equilibrium point one has
    $$
    f(x,y)=g(x,y)=0,
    $$
    so the point lies on both nullclines.

    <2>3. **False.** For instance,
    $$
    \operatorname{rank}(2I_2)=2\ne4=2\operatorname{rank}(I_2).
    $$

    <2>4. **True.** A reflection matrix is orthogonal. Thus
    $$
    A=QI_n
    $$
    with $Q=A$ gives a QR factorization, and $I_n$ is upper triangular with positive diagonal.

    <2>5. **False.** The nonzero nilpotent matrix
    $$
    \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
    $$
    has both eigenvalues equal to zero.
:::

<1>4. Statements 16--20.
::: {.proof}
    <2>1. **False.** The characteristic equation is
    $$
    r^2-7r-3=0,
    $$
    whose roots are
    $$
    \frac{7\pm\sqrt{61}}2.
    $$
    One root has modulus greater than $1$, so suitable initial conditions give an unbounded solution.

    <2>2. **True.** The functions $\sin x$ and $\cos(9x)$ are orthogonal for the given inner product, and each has norm $1$. Hence
    $$
    \|6\sin x+8\cos(9x)\|^2=36+64=100.
    $$

    <2>3. **True.** A Euclidean reflection is represented by a real symmetric orthogonal matrix. Its eigenvalues are $1$ on the reflecting subspace and $-1$ on its orthogonal complement, and the spectral theorem gives an orthonormal eigenbasis.

    <2>4. **True.** Trivial kernel gives $\operatorname{rank}A=m$. The matrix
    $$
    P=A(A^TA)^{-1}A^T
    $$
    is the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{col}(A)$. If $Pb=b$ for every $b$, then $P=I_n$, so $\operatorname{col}(A)=\mathbb R^n$ and $\operatorname{rank}A=n$. Hence $m=n$.

    <2>5. **True.** The first hypothesis says $\ker A\ne0$, while the second says $B$ is invertible. Therefore
    $$
    \ker(AB)=B^{-1}(\ker A)
    $$
    is nontrivial. A nontrivial kernel of a linear map over $\mathbb R$ contains infinitely many vectors.
:::
