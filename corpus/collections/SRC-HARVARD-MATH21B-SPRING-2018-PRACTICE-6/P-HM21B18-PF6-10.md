::: {.problem}
Solve the following differential equations subject to the stated initial conditions when given.

(a)
\[
f''+16f=\cos(4x),\qquad f(0)=f'(0)=1.
\]

(b)
\[
f''=\sin(4x),\qquad f(0)=f'(0)=1.
\]

(c)
\[
f''-f'+f=1,\qquad f(0)=2,\qquad f'(0)=3.
\]

(d)
\[
f''-2f'+f=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Solve part (a), including the resonant forcing.
::: {.proof}
The homogeneous equation
$$
f''+16f=0
$$
has solution
$$
f_h=C_1\cos(4x)+C_2\sin(4x).
$$
Because the forcing $\cos(4x)$ is resonant, try
$$
f_p=A x\sin(4x).
$$
A direct differentiation gives
$$
f_p''+16f_p=8A\cos(4x),
$$
so $A=1/8$. Thus
$$
f=C_1\cos(4x)+C_2\sin(4x)+\frac{x}{8}\sin(4x).
$$
The condition $f(0)=1$ gives $C_1=1$. Also
$$
f'(0)=4C_2,
$$
because the derivative of the resonant term vanishes at zero. Hence
$C_2=1/4$, and
$$
\boxed{f(x)=\cos(4x)+\frac14\sin(4x)+\frac{x}{8}\sin(4x).}
$$
:::

<1>2. Solve part (b) by integrating twice.
::: {.proof}
From
$$
f''=\sin(4x)
$$
we obtain
$$
f'=-\frac14\cos(4x)+C_1.
$$
The condition $f'(0)=1$ gives $C_1=5/4$. Integrating again,
$$
f=-\frac1{16}\sin(4x)+\frac54x+C_2.
$$
Since $f(0)=1$, $C_2=1$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{f(x)=1+\frac54x-\frac1{16}\sin(4x).}
$$
:::

<1>3. Solve part (c).
::: {.proof}
A constant particular solution is $f_p=1$. The characteristic polynomial of
the homogeneous equation is
$$
r^2-r+1,
$$
whose roots are
$$
r=\frac12\pm i\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
$$
Hence, with $\omega=\sqrt3/2$,
$$
f(x)=1+e^{x/2}\bigl(C_1\cos(\omega x)+C_2\sin(\omega x)\bigr).
$$
The condition $f(0)=2$ gives $C_1=1$. Differentiating and evaluating at zero,
$$
f'(0)=\frac12C_1+\omega C_2=3.
$$
Thus
$$
C_2=\frac{5}{\sqrt3},
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
f(x)=1+e^{x/2}\left(
\cos\frac{\sqrt3x}{2}
+\frac5{\sqrt3}\sin\frac{\sqrt3x}{2}
\right).}
$$
:::

<1>4. Solve part (d).
::: {.proof}
The characteristic polynomial is
$$
r^2-2r+1=(r-1)^2.
$$
The repeated root $r=1$ gives the general solution
$$
\boxed{f(x)=(C_1+C_2x)e^x,\qquad C_1,C_2\in\mathbb R.}
$$
:::
