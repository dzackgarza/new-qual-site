::: {.problem}
Compute the eigenvalues and an orthonormal eigenbasis for
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
3&1&1\\
1&3&1\\
1&1&3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: remark
The source-provided solution gives eigenvalues $1$ and $3$, which is
incorrect for the displayed matrix. The three orthonormal vectors it later
lists are useful, but their correct eigenvalues are $2,2,5$.
:::

::: solution
Write
$$
A=2I_3+J,
$$
where $J$ is the $3\times3$ all-ones matrix.

<1>1. Determine the eigenspaces.
::: proof
The vector
$$
u=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}
$$
satisfies $Ju=3u$, so
$$
Au=(2I+J)u=5u.
$$
Thus $5$ is an eigenvalue with eigenspace
$$
E_5=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1,1)^T\}.
$$

If $x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)^T$ satisfies
$$
x_1+x_2+x_3=0,
$$
then $Jx=0$, hence
$$
Ax=2x.
$$
Therefore
$$
E_2=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:x_1+x_2+x_3=0\},
$$
which has dimension $2$. Consequently the eigenvalues are
$$
\boxed{5,2,2}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Choose an orthonormal eigenbasis.
::: proof
Two orthogonal vectors in $E_2$ are
$$
v_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v_2=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
They have norms $\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt6$, respectively. The eigenvector $u$ has
norm $\sqrt3$ and is orthogonal to both because each of $v_1,v_2$ has coordinate sum zero.

Thus an orthonormal eigenbasis is
$$
\boxed{
\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\frac1{\sqrt6}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix},\quad
\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
The first two vectors have eigenvalue $2$, and the third has eigenvalue $5$.
:::
:::
