::: {.problem}
The source graph defines a piecewise-constant function on $[-\pi,\pi]$ taking value $1$ on $[\pi/4,3\pi/4]$, value $-1$ on $[-3\pi/4,-\pi/4]$, and value $0$ elsewhere.

![Source page containing the graph of the piecewise-constant function.](../../../assets/attachments/harvard-math21b-spring-2018-practice-final-6/problem-12.png)

(a) Find its Fourier series.

(b) Use Parseval's theorem to evaluate
\[
\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\ n\text{ odd}}}\frac1{n^2}
=1+\frac1{3^2}+\frac1{5^2}+\cdots.
\]
The Fourier coefficients from part (a) vanish for even $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The function is odd, so its Fourier series contains only sine terms.

<1>1. Compute the sine coefficients.
::: {.proof}
With the convention
$$
f(x)\sim\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n\ge1}
\bigl(a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\bigr),
$$
oddness gives
$$
a_0=a_n=0.
$$
Moreover,
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n
&=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac2\pi\int_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}\sin(nx)\,dx\\
&=\frac{2}{\pi n}
\left(
\cos\frac{n\pi}{4}-\cos\frac{3n\pi}{4}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{
f(x)\sim
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{2}{\pi n}
\left(
\cos\frac{n\pi}{4}-\cos\frac{3n\pi}{4}
\right)\sin(nx).}
$$

If $n$ is even, the two cosine values are equal, so $b_n=0$. If $n$ is odd,
their difference is $\pm\sqrt2$, hence
$$
|b_n|=\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi n}
\qquad(n\text{ odd}).
$$
:::

<1>2. Apply Parseval's identity.
::: {.proof}
The function has absolute value $1$ on two intervals of length $\pi/2$ and is
zero elsewhere. Therefore
$$
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac1\pi\cdot\pi
=1.
$$
Parseval gives
$$
1
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n^2
=\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\n\text{ odd}}}
\frac{8}{\pi^2n^2}.
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\n\text{ odd}}}\frac1{n^2}
=\frac{\pi^2}{8}.}
$$
:::
:::
