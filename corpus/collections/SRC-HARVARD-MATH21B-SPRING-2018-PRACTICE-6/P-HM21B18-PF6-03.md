::: {.problem}
No justifications are required.

(a) For each eigenvalue triple below, identify the corresponding transformation from the list.
Exactly five of the six transformations occur, each once.

| $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$ | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| $(1,1,1)$ | projection onto a plane |
| $(-1,-1,1)$ | reflection at a plane |
| $(0,1,0)$ | reflection at a line |
| $(0,1,1)$ | projection onto a line |
| $(1,-1,1)$ | identity; rotation about an axis |

(b) For each of the five matrices shown on the source page, match one of phase portraits 1--6, decide whether the continuous system $x'=Ax$ is asymptotically stable, decide whether the discrete system $x(t+1)=Ax(t)$ is asymptotically stable, and decide whether $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb C$.
One phase portrait is unused.

![Source page containing the five matrices and six phase portraits.](../../../assets/attachments/harvard-math21b-spring-2018-practice-final-6/problem-3.png)
:::

::: {.remark}
The source's intended one-use matching assigns $(-1,-1,1)$ to “reflection
at a line” and leaves “rotation about an axis” unused. Literally, however,
reflection in a line in three-dimensional Euclidean space is rotation by
$\pi$ about that line, so those two descriptions overlap for this eigenvalue
triple. The solution below records the source's intended matching and this
non-uniqueness separately.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Match the eigenvalue triples to the geometric transformations.
::: {.proof}
In the displayed order the answers are
$$
\boxed{\text{identity},\ \text{reflection at a line},\
\text{projection onto a line},\ \text{projection onto a plane},\
\text{reflection at a plane}.}
$$
The corresponding eigenvalue patterns are respectively
$(1,1,1)$, $(1,-1,-1)$, $(1,0,0)$, $(1,1,0)$, and $(1,1,-1)$.
Thus “rotation about an axis” is the unused choice.
:::

<1>2. Analyze the five matrices and match the phase portraits.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\0&3\end{pmatrix},\quad
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}-2&0\\4&3\end{pmatrix},\quad
A_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-3\end{pmatrix},
$$
$$
A_4=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_5=-2I.
$$
Their spectra are
$$
\{2,3\},\quad \{-2,3\},\quad \{0,-3\},\quad
\{1+i,1-i\},\quad \{-2\},
$$
respectively.

For $x'=Ax$, asymptotic stability requires all eigenvalues to have negative
real part, so only $A_5$ is asymptotically stable. For
$x(t+1)=Ax(t)$, asymptotic stability requires all eigenvalues to have modulus
strictly less than $1$, so none of the five is asymptotically stable.

The first two and fourth matrices have distinct eigenvalues, the third is
diagonal, and the fifth is scalar; hence all five are diagonalizable over
$\mathbb C$.

The source figures then match as follows: $A_1$ is the unstable node (1),
$A_2$ the saddle (2), $A_3$ the neutral-line/contraction portrait (3),
$A_4$ the unstable spiral (5), and $A_5$ the stable radial node (6).
Thus phase portrait 4 is unused.

| Matrix | Phase | continuous stable? | discrete stable? | diagonalizable? |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| $A_1$ | 1 | no | no | yes |
| $A_2$ | 2 | no | no | yes |
| $A_3$ | 3 | no | no | yes |
| $A_4$ | 5 | no | no | yes |
| $A_5$ | 6 | yes | no | yes |
