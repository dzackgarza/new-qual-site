::: {.problem}
(a) Using the least-squares method, find the best fit of the form
\[
y=ax^2+b2^x
\]
to the data points $(1,2)$, $(0,1)$, and $(2,4)$.

(b) For the data points $(0,1)$, $(0,2)$, and $(0,0)$ there is more than one minimizing fit; for example, both $y=x^2+2^x$ and $y=2x^2+2^x$ minimize the error.
Why does the least-squares formula fail here?
Choose exactly one:

1. the matrix $A$ in the least-squares formula is undefined;

2. the matrix $A$ is not invertible;

3. the matrix $A^TA$ is not invertible.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Form the least-squares system for the first data set.
::: proof
For the model
$$
y=ax^2+b2^x,
$$
the three data points give the design matrix and data vector
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
0&1\\
4&4
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
c=
\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\4\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The least-squares coefficients satisfy
$$
A^TA\binom ab=A^Tc.
$$
Direct calculation gives
$$
A^TA=
\begin{pmatrix}17&18\\18&21\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A^Tc=inom{18}{21}.
$$
Since
$$
\det(A^TA)=17\cdot21-18^2=33,
$$
the normal matrix is invertible, with
$$
(A^TA)^{-1}
=\frac1{33}
\begin{pmatrix}21&-18\\-18&17\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus
$$
\binom ab
=(A^TA)^{-1}A^Tc
=\binom01.
$$
Therefore the best fit is
$$
\boxed{y=2^x}.
$$
Indeed this function passes through all three data points, so the least-squares residual is actually zero.
:::

<1>2. Identify why the second least-squares formula fails.
::: proof
For the three points with $x=0$, the design matrix is
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
0&1\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Hence
$$
A^TA=
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is singular. Thus the inverse in
$$
(A^TA)^{-1}A^Tc
$$
does not exist. The correct choice is therefore
$$
\boxed{3.\ \text{$A^TA$ is not invertible}.}
$$
The matrix $A$ itself is still perfectly well-defined; it is simply rectangular and rank-deficient.
:::
:::
