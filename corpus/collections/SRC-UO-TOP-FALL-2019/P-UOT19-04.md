::: {.problem}
Let
\[
\phi:S^2\times S^2\to S^2\times S^2,\qquad \phi(x,y)=(y,x),
\]
and let
\[
T_\phi=(S^2\times S^2\times[0,1])/((x,y,1)\sim(y,x,0))
\]
be the mapping torus of $\phi$. Compute the homology groups of $T_\phi$.
:::

Let
\[
X=S^2\times S^2.
\]

::: pf

::: pf-step

The homology of \(X\) is
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,4,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&k=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Apply the Künneth theorem to
\[
S^2\times S^2.
\]
There is one generator in degree \(0\), two generators in degree \(2\), and
one generator in degree \(4\).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

On
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\langle a\rangle
\oplus
\mathbb Z\langle b\rangle,
\]
where
\[
a=[S^2\times\{\ast\}],
\qquad
b=[\{\ast\}\times S^2],
\]
the map \(\phi_*\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{\phi_*(a)=b,\qquad \phi_*(b)=a}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The map \(\phi\) exchanges the two factors, so it exchanges the two
fundamental \(2\)-cycles.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map \(\phi_*\) is the identity on
\[
H_0(X;\mathbb Z)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H_4(X;\mathbb Z).
\]


::: pf-proof

Every map of a connected space induces the identity on \(H_0\).

For the top class,
\[
[X]
=
[S^2]\times[S^2].
\]
Swapping two homology factors of degrees \(2\) and \(2\) introduces the sign
\[
(-1)^{2\cdot2}=1.
\]
Hence \(\phi\) preserves the orientation class and acts as the identity on
\(H_4\).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For a mapping torus, the Wang exact sequence is
\[
\cdots
\longrightarrow
H_k(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_k(X)
\longrightarrow
H_k(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow
H_{k-1}(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_{k-1}(X)
\longrightarrow
\cdots.
\]


::: pf-proof

This is the standard long exact sequence associated to the fibration
\[
X\longrightarrow T_\phi\longrightarrow S^1.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In degree \(2\),
\[
\operatorname{id}-\phi_*:
\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z^2
\]
is
\[
\boxed{
(m,n)\longmapsto(m-n,n-m)}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step <1>2,
\[
(\operatorname{id}-\phi_*)(m a+n b)
=(m-n)a+(n-m)b.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The kernel and cokernel of the map in step <1>5 are both isomorphic to
\[
\mathbb Z.
\]


::: pf-proof

The kernel consists of the diagonal pairs
\[
\{(m,m):m\in\mathbb Z\}
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The image is generated by
\[
(1,-1),
\]
so
\[
\mathbb Z^2/\langle(1,-1)\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
\[
\boxed{H_0(T_\phi;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The mapping torus of a map of a connected space is connected.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
\[
\boxed{H_1(T_\phi;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The Wang sequence in low degree gives
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_1(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow
H_0(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_0(X).
\]
By step <1>3, the final map is zero. Thus
\[
H_1(T_\phi)
\cong
H_0(X)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
\[
\boxed{H_2(T_\phi;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Since
\[
H_1(X)=0,
\]
the Wang sequence gives
\[
H_2(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_2(X)
\longrightarrow
H_2(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence
\[
H_2(T_\phi)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}(\operatorname{id}-\phi_*|_{H_2}),
\]
which is \(\mathbb Z\) by step <1>6.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
\[
\boxed{H_3(T_\phi;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Since
\[
H_3(X)=0,
\]
the relevant Wang segment is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_3(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow
H_2(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_2(X).
\]
Thus
\[
H_3(T_\phi)
\cong
\ker(\operatorname{id}-\phi_*|_{H_2}),
\]
which is \(\mathbb Z\) by step <1>6.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
\[
\boxed{H_4(T_\phi;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Since
\[
H_3(X)=0
\]
and \(\operatorname{id}-\phi_*=0\) on \(H_4(X)\) by step <1>3, the Wang
sequence gives
\[
H_4(X)
\xrightarrow{0}
H_4(X)
\longrightarrow
H_4(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence
\[
H_4(T_\phi)\cong H_4(X)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
\[
\boxed{H_5(T_\phi;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Since \(X\) has dimension \(4\),
\[
H_5(X)=0.
\]
The Wang sequence gives
\[
0
\longrightarrow
H_5(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow
H_4(X)
\xrightarrow{0}
H_4(X).
\]
Therefore
\[
H_5(T_\phi)
\cong
H_4(X)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For \(k>5\),
\[
\boxed{H_k(T_\phi;\mathbb Z)=0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The mapping torus is a \(5\)-dimensional CW complex, so it has no homology
above degree \(5\).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus
\[
\boxed{
H_k(T_\phi;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&0\le k\le5,\\
0,&k>5.
\end{cases}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>7 through <1>13.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>14 is the complete homology computation.

:::

:::
