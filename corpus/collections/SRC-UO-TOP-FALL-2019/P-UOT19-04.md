::: {.problem}
Let
$$
\phi:S^2\times S^2\to S^2\times S^2,\qquad \phi(x,y)=(y,x),
$$
and let
$$
T_\phi=(S^2\times S^2\times[0,1])/((x,y,1)\sim(y,x,0))
$$
be the mapping torus of $\phi$. Compute the homology groups of $T_\phi$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
X=S^2\times S^2.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The homology of $X$ is
$$
\boxed{
H_k(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,4,\\
\ZZ^2,&k=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the Künneth theorem to
$$
S^2\times S^2.
$$
There is one generator in degree $0$, two generators in degree $2$, and
one generator in degree $4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

On
$$
H_2(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ\langle a\rangle
\oplus
\ZZ\langle b\rangle,
$$
where
$$
a=[S^2\times\{\ast\}],
\qquad
b=[\{\ast\}\times S^2],
$$
the map $\phi_*$ satisfies
$$
\boxed{\phi_*(a)=b,\qquad \phi_*(b)=a}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The map $\phi$ exchanges the two factors, so it exchanges the two
fundamental $2$-cycles.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The map $\phi_*$ is the identity on
$$
H_0(X;\ZZ)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H_4(X;\ZZ).
$$


::: pf-proof

Every map of a connected space induces the identity on $H_0$.

For the top class,
$$
[X]
=
[S^2]\times[S^2].
$$
Swapping two homology factors of degrees $2$ and $2$ introduces the sign
$$
(-1)^{2\cdot2}=1.
$$
Hence $\phi$ preserves the orientation class and acts as the identity on
$H_4$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For a mapping torus, the Wang exact sequence is
$$
\cdots
\longrightarrow
H_k(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_k(X)
\longrightarrow
H_k(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow
H_{k-1}(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_{k-1}(X)
\longrightarrow
\cdots.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard long exact sequence associated to the fibration
$$
X\longrightarrow T_\phi\longrightarrow S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

In degree $2$,
$$
\operatorname{id}-\phi_*:
\ZZ^2\to\ZZ^2
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
(m,n)\longmapsto(m-n,n-m)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
(\operatorname{id}-\phi_*)(m a+n b)
=(m-n)a+(n-m)b.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The kernel and cokernel of the map in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} are both isomorphic to
$$
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

The kernel consists of the diagonal pairs
$$
\{(m,m):m\in\ZZ\}
\cong\ZZ.
$$
The image is generated by
$$
(1,-1),
$$
so
$$
\ZZ^2/\langle(1,-1)\rangle
\cong\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

One has
$$
\boxed{H_0(T_\phi;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The mapping torus of a map of a connected space is connected.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\boxed{H_1(T_\phi;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Wang sequence in low degree gives
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H_1(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow
H_0(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_0(X).
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the final map is zero. Thus
$$
H_1(T_\phi)
\cong
H_0(X)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\boxed{H_2(T_\phi;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
H_1(X)=0,
$$
the Wang sequence gives
$$
H_2(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_2(X)
\longrightarrow
H_2(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Hence
$$
H_2(T_\phi)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}(\operatorname{id}-\phi_*\restrict_{H_2}),
$$
which is $\ZZ$ by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\boxed{H_3(T_\phi;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
H_3(X)=0,
$$
the relevant Wang segment is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H_3(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow
H_2(X)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\phi_*}
H_2(X).
$$
Thus
$$
H_3(T_\phi)
\cong
\ker(\operatorname{id}-\phi_*\restrict_{H_2}),
$$
which is $\ZZ$ by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\boxed{H_4(T_\phi;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
H_3(X)=0
$$
and $\operatorname{id}-\phi_*=0$ on $H_4(X)$ by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the Wang
sequence gives
$$
H_4(X)
\xrightarrow{0}
H_4(X)
\longrightarrow
H_4(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Hence
$$
H_4(T_\phi)\cong H_4(X)\cong\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\boxed{H_5(T_\phi;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $X$ has dimension $4$,
$$
H_5(X)=0.
$$
The Wang sequence gives
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H_5(T_\phi)
\longrightarrow
H_4(X)
\xrightarrow{0}
H_4(X).
$$
Therefore
$$
H_5(T_\phi)
\cong
H_4(X)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

For $k>5$,
$$
\boxed{H_k(T_\phi;\ZZ)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The mapping torus is a $5$-dimensional CW complex, so it has no homology
above degree $5$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_k(T_\phi;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&0\le k\le5,\\
0,&k>5.
\end{cases}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} is the complete homology computation.
