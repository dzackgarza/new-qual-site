::: {.problem}
Let $Y$ be a $2$-connected space and let
\[
p:Y\to \operatorname{Gr}_3^+(\mathbb R^\infty)
\]
be a fibration such that $p_*:\pi_i(Y)\to\pi_i(\operatorname{Gr}_3^+(\mathbb R^\infty))$ is an isomorphism for $i>2$; thus $Y$ is a $2$-connected cover of $\operatorname{Gr}_3^+(\mathbb R^\infty)$.

Define the primary obstruction in cohomology to lifting a map
\[
f:X\to \operatorname{Gr}_3^+(\mathbb R^\infty)
\]
to a map $\widetilde f:X\to Y$, and give an example in which the obstruction does not vanish.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
\[
B=\operatorname{Gr}_3^+(\mathbb R^\infty).
\]
From the preceding computation,
\[
\pi_1(B)=0,
\qquad
\pi_2(B)\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]

<1>1. Let \(F\) be the fiber of
\[
p:Y\to B.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{F\simeq K(\mathbb Z/2,1)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The long exact sequence in homotopy for the fibration gives
\[
\cdots
\to
\pi_i(F)
\to
\pi_i(Y)
\xrightarrow{p_*}
\pi_i(B)
\to
\pi_{i-1}(F)
\to
\cdots.
\]

For \(i>2\), the map \(p_*\) is an isomorphism by hypothesis, so
\[
\pi_i(F)=0
\qquad(i>1).
\]

For \(i=2\), since \(Y\) is \(2\)-connected,
\[
\pi_2(Y)=\pi_1(Y)=0.
\]
The exact segment
\[
0
\to
\pi_2(B)
\to
\pi_1(F)
\to
0
\]
therefore gives
\[
\pi_1(F)
\cong
\pi_2(B)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Thus \(F\) has exactly the homotopy groups of \(K(\mathbb Z/2,1)\).
:::

<1>2. The fibration \(p:Y\to B\) is the homotopy fiber of a classifying map
\[
\boxed{
g:B\to K(\mathbb Z/2,2)}.
\]

::: {.proof}
A principal \(K(A,1)\)-fibration over a simply connected base is classified
by a map to
\[
BK(A,1)\simeq K(A,2).
\]
Here the fiber is \(K(\mathbb Z/2,1)\) by step <1>1 and the base \(B\) is
simply connected. Thus there is a classifying map
\[
g:B\to K(\mathbb Z/2,2)
\]
whose homotopy fiber is \(Y\).
:::

<1>3. A map
\[
f:X\to B
\]
lifts through \(p\),
\begin{tikzcd}
& Y \arrow[d,"p"]\\
X \arrow[r,"f"'] \arrow[ur,dashed,"\widetilde f"] & B,
\end{tikzcd}
if and only if
\[
\boxed{g\circ f:X\to K(\mathbb Z/2,2)}
\]
is nullhomotopic.

::: {.proof}
Since \(Y\) is the homotopy fiber of \(g\), a lift of \(f\) to \(Y\) is
equivalent to a nullhomotopy of the composite \(g\circ f\). This is the
defining universal property of the homotopy fiber.
:::

<1>4. Let
\[
\iota
\in
H^2(K(\mathbb Z/2,2);\mathbb Z/2)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2
\]
be the fundamental cohomology class. Then
\[
\boxed{
o(f)
\coloneqq
(g\circ f)^*(\iota)
=
f^*g^*(\iota)
\in
H^2(X;\mathbb Z/2)}
\]
is the primary obstruction to lifting \(f\).

::: {.proof}
For every CW complex \(X\),
\[
[X,K(\mathbb Z/2,2)]
\cong
H^2(X;\mathbb Z/2),
\]
with the homotopy class of a map \(h\) corresponding to
\[
h^*(\iota).
\]
Thus step <1>3 says that \(f\) lifts if and only if the class represented by
\(g\circ f\) is zero, namely if and only if
\[
(g\circ f)^*(\iota)=0.
\]
:::

<1>5. The class
\[
\boxed{
g^*(\iota)
\in
H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)}
\]
is nonzero.

::: {.proof}
Because the homotopy fiber of \(g\) is \(2\)-connected, the long exact
sequence of the fibration
\[
Y\to B\xrightarrow{g}K(\mathbb Z/2,2)
\]
shows that
\[
g_*:
\pi_2(B)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\pi_2(K(\mathbb Z/2,2))
\cong
\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Hence \(g\) is not nullhomotopic on the \(2\)-skeleton, so the corresponding
cohomology class
\[
g^*(\iota)
\]
is nonzero.

Equivalently, since \(B\) is simply connected and
\[
\pi_2(B)\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\]
the Hurewicz and universal coefficient theorems give
\[
H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2,
\]
and \(g^*(\iota)\) is its generator.
:::

<1>6. An explicit example with nonvanishing obstruction is
\[
\boxed{
X=B,
\qquad
f=\operatorname{id}_B}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For the identity map,
\[
o(\operatorname{id}_B)
=
(\,g\circ\operatorname{id}_B\,)^*(\iota)
=
g^*(\iota),
\]
which is nonzero by step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Hence the identity map
\[
\operatorname{id}_B:B\to B
\]
does not lift to \(Y\).

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4, a lift exists exactly when the obstruction vanishes. Step
<1>6 gives a nonzero obstruction.
:::

<1>8. Under the usual identification
\[
B\simeq BSO(3),
\]
the universal obstruction class
\[
g^*(\iota)
\]
is the universal second Stiefel--Whitney class
\[
w_2\in H^2(BSO(3);\mathbb Z/2).
\]

::: {.proof}
The \(2\)-connected cover of \(BSO(3)\) is obtained by killing
\[
\pi_2(BSO(3))\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
The corresponding Postnikov \(k\)-invariant in degree \(2\) is precisely the
universal obstruction to lifting an oriented \(3\)-plane bundle to the
2-connected cover, conventionally denoted \(w_2\).
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 defines the primary obstruction, and step <1>6 supplies an example
where it does not vanish.
:::
:::
