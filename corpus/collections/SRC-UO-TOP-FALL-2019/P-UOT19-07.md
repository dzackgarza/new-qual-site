::: {.problem}
Consider the knot $5_2$.

![Source knot diagram for Problem 7.](../../../assets/attachments/uo-top-fall-2019/problem-7.png)

(a) Could $S^3\setminus 5_2$ have a normal covering space with deck transformation group $\mathbb Z/537\mathbb Z$? Justify your answer.

(b) Could it have a normal covering space with deck transformation group $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$? Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
X=S^3\setminus 5_2.
\]

<1>1. A connected normal covering of \(X\) with deck transformation group
\(G\) exists if and only if there is a normal subgroup
\[
N\trianglelefteq\pi_1(X)
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X)/N\cong G}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By the classification of connected covering spaces, connected covers
correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups of \(\pi_1(X)\). A cover is
normal exactly when the corresponding subgroup is normal, and for a normal
cover the deck group is the quotient of \(\pi_1(X)\) by that normal
subgroup.
:::

<1>2. If \(G\) is abelian and
\[
\pi_1(X)\twoheadrightarrow G,
\]
then this surjection factors through
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\pi_1(X)_{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Every homomorphism from a group to an abelian group kills the commutator
subgroup. Thus if
\[
\varphi:\pi_1(X)\to G
\]
is a homomorphism with \(G\) abelian, then
\[
[\pi_1(X),\pi_1(X)]
\subseteq
\ker\varphi,
\]
so \(\varphi\) factors uniquely through the abelianization.
:::

<1>3. Conversely, if
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\twoheadrightarrow G
\]
for an abelian group \(G\), then \(X\) has a connected normal covering with
deck group \(G\).

::: {.proof}
Compose the abelianization map
\[
\pi_1(X)\twoheadrightarrow H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
\]
with the given surjection onto \(G\). The composite
\[
\pi_1(X)\twoheadrightarrow G
\]
has normal kernel \(N\), and
\[
\pi_1(X)/N\cong G.
\]
Apply step <1>1.
:::

<1>4. One has
\[
\boxed{
H_1(S^3\setminus 5_2;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Alexander duality for the knot
\[
5_2\cong S^1\subset S^3
\]
gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(S^3\setminus 5_2;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\widetilde H^{2-i}(S^1;\mathbb Z).
\]
For \(i=1\),
\[
\widetilde H_1(S^3\setminus 5_2)
\cong
\widetilde H^1(S^1)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
\]
Since \(H_1\) is already reduced in positive degree, the claim follows.
:::

<1>5. There is a surjection
\[
\mathbb Z\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z/537\mathbb Z.
\]

::: {.proof}
Take reduction modulo \(537\):
\[
n\longmapsto[n]_{537}.
\]
This is surjective.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\text{yes, such a normal cover with deck group }\mathbb Z/537\mathbb Z\text{ exists}.}
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>3, <1>4, and <1>5.
:::

<1>7. There is no surjection
\[
\mathbb Z\twoheadrightarrow
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

::: {.proof}
Every quotient of the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z\) is cyclic. The group
\[
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
\]
is not cyclic: every nonzero element has order \(2\), so no single element
generates all four elements.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{no normal cover with deck group }
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
\text{ exists}.}
\]

::: {.proof}
If such a normal cover existed, steps <1>1 and <1>2 would produce a
surjection
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z
\twoheadrightarrow
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\]
contradicting step <1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 answers part (a), and step <1>8 answers part (b).
:::
:::
