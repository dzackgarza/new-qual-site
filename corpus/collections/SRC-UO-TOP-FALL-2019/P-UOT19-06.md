::: {.problem}
Let $(\mathbb{RP}^2)^{2019}$ be the product of 2019 copies of $\mathbb{RP}^2$. Suppose
\[
f:(\mathbb{RP}^2)^{2019}\to(\mathbb{RP}^2)^{2019}
\]
is continuous. Show that $f$ has a fixed point.
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
X=(\mathbb{RP}^2)^{2019}.
\]

<1>1. The integral homology of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is
\[
\boxed{
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z/2,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
This is the standard cellular-homology computation for the CW structure on
\(\mathbb{RP}^2\) with one cell in each dimension \(0,1,2\). The cellular
boundary map
\[
C_2\to C_1
\]
is multiplication by \(2\), while
\[
C_1\to C_0
\]
is zero.
:::

<1>2. With rational coefficients,
\[
\boxed{
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&k=0,\\
0,&k>0.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Tensor the groups in step <1>1 with \(\mathbb Q\). The torsion group
\[
\mathbb Z/2
\]
vanishes after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\), and there are no higher groups.
Equivalently, apply the universal coefficient theorem over the field
\(\mathbb Q\).
:::

<1>3. The rational homology of \(X\) is
\[
\boxed{
H_k(X;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&k=0,\\
0,&k>0.
\end{cases}}
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply the Künneth theorem over the field \(\mathbb Q\). Each factor has
nonzero rational homology only in degree \(0\) by step <1>2. Therefore the
tensor product contributing to the homology of the product is nonzero only
when every factor contributes degree \(0\), which gives total degree \(0\).
:::

<1>4. The induced map
\[
f_*:
H_0(X;\mathbb Q)\to H_0(X;\mathbb Q)
\]
is the identity.

::: {.proof}
The space \(X\) is connected because each factor \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is
connected. Thus
\[
H_0(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q
\]
is generated by the class of any point. Every continuous map of a connected
space to itself sends a point to a point in the same connected component, so
the induced map on \(H_0\) is the identity.
:::

<1>5. The Lefschetz number of \(f\) is
\[
\boxed{L(f)=1}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By definition,
\[
L(f)
=
\sum_{k\ge0}
(-1)^k
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
f_*:H_k(X;\mathbb Q)\to H_k(X;\mathbb Q)
\right).
\]
By step <1>3, all terms with \(k>0\) vanish. By step <1>4, the degree-zero
trace is \(1\). Hence
\[
L(f)=1.
\]
:::

<1>6. The space \(X\) is a compact finite CW complex.

::: {.proof}
The real projective plane is a finite CW complex. A finite product of finite
CW complexes is again a finite CW complex, and \(X\) is a finite product
with \(2019\) factors. It is compact because each factor is compact.
:::

<1>7. The map \(f\) has a fixed point.

::: {.proof}
The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem applies to self-maps of finite CW
complexes: if the Lefschetz number is nonzero, then the map has a fixed
point. Steps <1>5 and <1>6 give
\[
L(f)=1\ne0.
\]
Therefore there exists
\[
x\in X
\]
such that
\[
f(x)=x.
\]
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 is the required conclusion.
:::
