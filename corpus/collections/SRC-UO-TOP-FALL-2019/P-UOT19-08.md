::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finitely generated abelian group. Show that no closed $3$-manifold is a $K(G,2)$. (Hint: reduce to the orientable case and consider the homology of a $K(G,2)$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that a closed \(3\)-manifold \(M\) is a
\(K(G,2)\).

<1>1. One has
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(M)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By definition, a \(K(G,2)\) has
\[
\pi_2(M)\cong G
\]
and
\[
\pi_i(M)=0
\qquad(i\ne2).
\]
In particular, \(\pi_1(M)=0\).
:::

<1>2. The manifold \(M\) is orientable.

::: {.proof}
If a connected manifold is nonorientable, its orientation character gives
a nontrivial homomorphism
\[
\pi_1(M)\to\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Equivalently, it has a nontrivial orientation double cover. Step <1>1 says
\(\pi_1(M)=0\), so this is impossible. Hence \(M\) is orientable.
:::

<1>3. One has
\[
\boxed{H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The degree-one Hurewicz theorem identifies
\[
H_1(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\pi_1(M)_{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]
Step <1>1 gives \(\pi_1(M)=0\).
:::

<1>4. One has
\[
\boxed{H^1(M;\mathbb Z)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The universal coefficient theorem for cohomology gives
\[
H^1(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(M;\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z),
\]
because
\[
\operatorname{Ext}(H_0(M;\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
Apply step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Poincaré duality gives
\[
\boxed{H_2(M;\mathbb Z)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The manifold \(M\) is closed, connected, orientable, and \(3\)-dimensional.
Thus Poincaré duality gives
\[
H_2(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H^{1}(M;\mathbb Z).
\]
Step <1>4 makes the right-hand side zero.
:::

<1>6. The Hurewicz theorem gives
\[
\boxed{\pi_2(M)\cong H_2(M;\mathbb Z)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The space \(M\) is simply connected by step <1>1. For a simply connected
space, the Hurewicz homomorphism
\[
\pi_2(M)\to H_2(M;\mathbb Z)
\]
is an isomorphism. Apply step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Therefore
\[
\boxed{G=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Because \(M\) is a \(K(G,2)\),
\[
\pi_2(M)\cong G.
\]
Step <1>6 gives \(\pi_2(M)=0\).
:::

<1>8. The defining property of \(K(0,2)\) gives
\[
\boxed{\pi_3(M)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For a \(K(G,2)\), all homotopy groups other than \(\pi_2\) vanish. Step
<1>7 identifies \(G=0\), so in particular
\[
\pi_3(M)=0.
\]
:::

<1>9. On the other hand,
\[
\boxed{H_3(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Every closed connected orientable \(3\)-manifold has a fundamental class.
By Poincaré duality,
\[
H_3(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H^0(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>10. Since
\[
\pi_1(M)=\pi_2(M)=0,
\]
the Hurewicz theorem in degree \(3\) gives
\[
\boxed{
\pi_3(M)\cong H_3(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For a \(2\)-connected space, the first nontrivial Hurewicz homomorphism
\[
\pi_3(M)\to H_3(M;\mathbb Z)
\]
is an isomorphism. Steps <1>1 and <1>6 show that \(M\) is \(2\)-connected,
and step <1>9 computes the homology group.
:::

<1>11. Steps <1>8 and <1>10 contradict one another.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 gives
\[
\pi_3(M)=0,
\]
while step <1>10 gives
\[
\pi_3(M)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>12. Therefore no closed \(3\)-manifold is a \(K(G,2)\).

::: {.proof}
The assumption that such an \(M\) exists leads to the contradiction in step
<1>11.
:::

<1>13. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>12 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
