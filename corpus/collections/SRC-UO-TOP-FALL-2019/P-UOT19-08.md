::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finitely generated abelian group. Show that no closed $3$-manifold is a $K(G,2)$. (Hint: reduce to the orientable case and consider the homology of a $K(G,2)$.)
:::

::: {.solution}

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that a closed $3$-manifold $M$ is a
$K(G,2)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\boxed{\pi_1(M)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition, a $K(G,2)$ has
$$
\pi_2(M)\cong G
$$
and
$$
\pi_i(M)=0
\qquad(i\ne2).
$$
In particular, $\pi_1(M)=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The manifold $M$ is orientable.


::: pf-proof

If a connected manifold is nonorientable, its orientation character gives
a nontrivial homomorphism
$$
\pi_1(M)\to\ZZ/2.
$$
Equivalently, it has a nontrivial orientation double cover. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} says
$\pi_1(M)=0$, so this is impossible. Hence $M$ is orientable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
\boxed{H_1(M;\ZZ)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The degree-one Hurewicz theorem identifies
$$
H_1(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\pi_1(M)_{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$
Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives $\pi_1(M)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
\boxed{H^1(M;\ZZ)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The universal coefficient theorem for cohomology gives
$$
H^1(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(M;\ZZ),\ZZ),
$$
because
$$
\operatorname{Ext}(H_0(M;\ZZ),\ZZ)=0.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Poincaré duality gives
$$
\boxed{H_2(M;\ZZ)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The manifold $M$ is closed, connected, orientable, and $3$-dimensional.
Thus Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_2(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^{1}(M;\ZZ).
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} makes the right-hand side zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The Hurewicz theorem gives
$$
\boxed{\pi_2(M)\cong H_2(M;\ZZ)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The space $M$ is simply connected by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. For a simply connected
space, the Hurewicz homomorphism
$$
\pi_2(M)\to H_2(M;\ZZ)
$$
is an isomorphism. Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{G=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $M$ is a $K(G,2)$,
$$
\pi_2(M)\cong G.
$$
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives $\pi_2(M)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The defining property of $K(0,2)$ gives
$$
\boxed{\pi_3(M)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a $K(G,2)$, all homotopy groups other than $\pi_2$ vanish. Step
[](#s1-7){.pf-ref} identifies $G=0$, so in particular
$$
\pi_3(M)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

On the other hand,
$$
\boxed{H_3(M;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every closed connected orientable $3$-manifold has a fundamental class.
By Poincaré duality,
$$
H_3(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^0(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Since
$$
\pi_1(M)=\pi_2(M)=0,
$$
the Hurewicz theorem in degree $3$ gives
$$
\boxed{
\pi_3(M)\cong H_3(M;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a $2$-connected space, the first nontrivial Hurewicz homomorphism
$$
\pi_3(M)\to H_3(M;\ZZ)
$$
is an isomorphism. Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} show that $M$ is $2$-connected,
and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} computes the homology group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} contradict one another.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\pi_3(M)=0,
$$
while step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\pi_3(M)\cong\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore no closed $3$-manifold is a $K(G,2)$.


::: pf-proof

The assumption that such an $M$ exists leads to the contradiction in step
[](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
