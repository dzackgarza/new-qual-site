::: {.problem}
Let $M(3,1)$ be the result of attaching a $2$-cell to $S^1$ by the map $z\mapsto z^3$. Describe explicitly, with proof, all connected covering spaces of
\[
M(3,1)\times \RP^2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
\[
M=M(3,1).
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(M)\cong\ZZ/3}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Start with
\[
\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ
\]
generated by the loop \(a\). Attaching a \(2\)-cell by the degree-\(3\) map
adds the relation
\[
a^3=1.
\]
By van Kampen's theorem,
\[
\pi_1(M)
\cong
\langle a\mid a^3=1\rangle
\cong
\ZZ/3.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Also,
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(\RP^2)
\cong
\ZZ/2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The standard double cover
\[
S^2\to\RP^2
\]
is universal, with deck group \(\ZZ/2\). Hence the fundamental group is
\(\ZZ/2\).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(M\times\RP^2)
\cong
\ZZ/3\times\ZZ/2
\cong
\ZZ/6}.
\]


::: pf-proof

For path-connected spaces,
\[
\pi_1(X\times Y)
\cong
\pi_1(X)\times\pi_1(Y).
\]
Apply steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. Since \(2\) and \(3\) are coprime, the product
group is cyclic of order \(6\).

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Up to equivalence, connected covering spaces of
\[
M\times\RP^2
\]
are classified by subgroups of \(\ZZ/6\).


::: pf-proof

The space \(M\times\RP^2\) is path connected, locally path connected,
and semilocally simply connected, so the classification theorem for
connected covering spaces applies.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The group
\[
\ZZ/6
\]
has exactly four subgroups, of orders
\[
1,\quad2,\quad3,\quad6.
\]


::: pf-proof

A cyclic group has exactly one subgroup for each divisor of its order. The
positive divisors of \(6\) are
\[
1,2,3,6.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The two connected covers of \(\RP^2\) are
\[
\operatorname{id}:\RP^2\to\RP^2
\]
and
\[
p_2:S^2\to\RP^2.
\]


::: pf-proof

The group \(\ZZ/2\) has exactly two subgroups: the full group and the
trivial subgroup. Under covering-space classification these correspond to
the identity cover and the universal double cover.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The two connected covers of \(M\) are
\[
\operatorname{id}:M\to M
\]
and a \(3\)-sheeted universal cover
\[
p_3:X\to M.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the group \(\pi_1(M)\cong\ZZ/3\) has only the full
subgroup and the trivial subgroup. Hence \(M\) has exactly the identity
cover and its universal \(3\)-sheeted cover.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

An explicit model for \(X\) is
\[
\boxed{
X
=
\bigl(D^2\times\{0,1,2\}\bigr)/\sim},
\]
where
\[
(x,i)\sim(x,j)
\qquad
\text{for every }x\in\partial D^2
\text{ and all }i,j.
\]
Thus \(X\) is three disks glued along their common boundary circle.


::: pf-proof

Let
\[
q:D^2\to M
\]
be the quotient map in the standard model of \(M(3,1)\), where the boundary
circle is attached by the degree-\(3\) map.
Let
\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\]
Define
\[
p_3([x,j])
=
q(\omega^j x).
\]
If \(x\in\partial D^2\), then
\[
(\omega^j x)^3=x^3
\]
for every \(j\), so the three boundary values have the same image under
\(q\). Hence the formula is well-defined on the quotient.

Away from the common boundary, each of the three disk interiors maps
homeomorphically to the open \(2\)-cell of \(M\). Near the common boundary,
the three sectors map to the three local sheets produced by the degree-\(3\)
attaching map. Thus \(p_3\) is a \(3\)-sheeted connected covering. Since its
degree equals
\[
|\pi_1(M)|=3,
\]
its fundamental group is trivial, so it is the universal cover.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The four connected covering spaces of
\[
M\times\RP^2
\]
are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&M\times\RP^2,\\
&M\times S^2,\\
&X\times\RP^2,\\
&X\times S^2,
\end{aligned}}
\]
with covering maps respectively
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{id}_M\times\operatorname{id}_{\RP^2},\\
&\operatorname{id}_M\times p_2,\\
&p_3\times\operatorname{id}_{\RP^2},\\
&p_3\times p_2.
\end{aligned}}
\]


::: pf-proof

Under
\[
\pi_1(M\times\RP^2)
\cong
\ZZ/3\times\ZZ/2,
\]
the four subgroups are
\[
\ZZ/3\times\ZZ/2,
\qquad
\ZZ/3\times\{0\},
\qquad
\{0\}\times\ZZ/2,
\qquad
\{0\}\times\{0\}.
\]
The displayed product covers induce exactly these four subgroups. By step
[](#s1-5){.pf-ref} there are no others.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Their covering degrees are respectively
\[
\boxed{1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 6}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The degree is the index of the corresponding subgroup, equivalently the
product of the degrees of the factor covers.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives the complete explicit list, and steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}
prove that the list is exhaustive.

:::
