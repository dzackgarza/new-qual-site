::: problem
Use the Mayer–Vietoris sequence to calculate the homology groups of $S^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For $n\ge1$, choose open sets $U,V\subset S^n$ obtained by slightly enlarging the northern and southern hemispheres.
Then
\[
U\simeq *,
\qquad
V\simeq *,
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq S^{n-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Each enlarged hemisphere deformation retracts to a point.
Their overlap is an open equatorial band, which deformation retracts onto the equator $S^{n-1}$.
:::

<1>2. The reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence gives natural isomorphisms
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^n;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\widetilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1};\mathbb Z)
\]
for every $k$.
::: {.proof}
The reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence for
\[
S^n=U\cup V
\]
contains
\[
\widetilde H_k(U)\oplus\widetilde H_k(V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_k(S^n)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
\widetilde H_{k-1}(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_{k-1}(U)\oplus\widetilde H_{k-1}(V).
\]
By <1>1, $U$ and $V$ are contractible, so both outside terms vanish.
Exactness therefore makes the connecting map an isomorphism
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^n)
\xrightarrow{\cong}
\widetilde H_{k-1}(U\cap V)
\cong
\widetilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1}).
\]
:::

<1>3. The reduced homology of $S^0$ is
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^0;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
0,&k\ne0.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
The space $S^0$ consists of two points, so
\[
H_0(S^0;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Passing to reduced homology removes the diagonal copy of $\mathbb Z$, leaving
\[
\widetilde H_0(S^0;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
There are no positive-dimensional cells, so all higher homology groups vanish.
:::

<1>4. Iterating <1>2 and using <1>3 gives
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^n;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=n,\\
0,&k\ne n
\end{cases}
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
::: {.proof}
Repeatedly applying <1>2 yields
\[
\widetilde H_k(S^n)
\cong
\widetilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1})
\cong\cdots\cong
\widetilde H_{k-n}(S^0).
\]
By <1>3, the last group is $\mathbb Z$ exactly when $k-n=0$, and is zero otherwise.
:::

<1>5. Therefore, for $n\ge1$,
\[
\boxed{
H_k(S^n;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]
For $n=0$, one has $H_0(S^0;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2$ and all other groups vanish.
::: {.proof}
For $n\ge1$, the sphere $S^n$ is path-connected, so
\[
H_0(S^n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The positive-degree groups agree with reduced homology, so <1>4 gives the displayed answer.
The $n=0$ case is <1>3 before reduction.
:::
:::
