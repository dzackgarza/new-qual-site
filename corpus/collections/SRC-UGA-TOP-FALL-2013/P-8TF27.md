::: problem
Prove that the product of two compact spaces is compact.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that if $X$ and $Y$ are compact topological spaces, then their Cartesian product $X \times Y$ (endowed with the product topology) is compact.

<1>1. Cover of a compact slice $\{x\} \times Y$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an arbitrary open cover of $X \times Y$.
    <2>2. Fix a point $x \in X$. The slice $\{x\} \times Y$ is homeomorphic to $Y$, and is therefore compact.
    <2>3. For each $y \in Y$, the point $(x, y) \in X \times Y$ belongs to some open set $U_{x,y} \in \mathcal{A}$.
    <2>4. By the definition of the product topology, there exist open sets $V_{x,y} \subseteq X$ and $W_{x,y} \subseteq Y$ such that
    $$(x, y) \in V_{x,y} \times W_{x,y} \subseteq U_{x,y}.$$
    <2>5. The family $\{W_{x,y} : y \in Y\}$ is an open cover of $Y$.
    <2>6. Since $Y$ is compact, there exist finitely many points $y_1, y_2, \dots, y_{k(x)} \in Y$ such that
    $$Y = \bigcup_{i=1}^{k(x)} W_{x, y_i}.$$

<1>2. Construction of a tube neighborhood $N_x \times Y$ (The Tube Lemma):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define
    $$N_x = \bigcap_{i=1}^{k(x)} V_{x, y_i}.$$
    <2>2. Since $N_x$ is a finite intersection of open sets containing $x$, $N_x$ is an open neighborhood of $x$ in $X$.
    <2>3. For each $i \in \{1, \dots, k(x)\}$, $N_x \subseteq V_{x, y_i}$, so $N_x \times W_{x, y_i} \subseteq V_{x, y_i} \times W_{x, y_i} \subseteq U_{x, y_i}$.
    <2>4. Thus the tube $N_x \times Y$ is covered by finitely many elements of $\mathcal{A}$:
    $$N_x \times Y = N_x \times \left( \bigcup_{i=1}^{k(x)} W_{x, y_i} \right) = \bigcup_{i=1}^{k(x)} (N_x \times W_{x, y_i}) \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{k(x)} U_{x, y_i}.$$

<1>3. Extraction of a finite subcover for $X \times Y$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The collection $\{N_x : x \in X\}$ forms an open cover of $X$.
    <2>2. Because $X$ is compact, there exist finitely many points $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_m \in X$ such that
    $$X = \bigcup_{j=1}^m N_{x_j}.$$
    <2>3. Taking products with $Y$:
    $$X \times Y = \left( \bigcup_{j=1}^m N_{x_j} \right) \times Y = \bigcup_{j=1}^m (N_{x_j} \times Y) \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^m \bigcup_{i=1}^{k(x_j)} U_{x_j, y_i}.$$
    <2>4. The collection
    $$\mathcal{A}' = \left\{ U_{x_j, y_i} : 1 \le j \le m, \; 1 \le i \le k(x_j) \right\} \subseteq \mathcal{A}$$
    is a finite subcollection of $\mathcal{A}$ that covers $X \times Y$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    *Proof:*
    Every open cover $\mathcal{A}$ of $X \times Y$ has a finite subcover $\mathcal{A}'$. Therefore $X \times Y$ is compact.
:::
