::: problem
Let $X$ be a space obtained by attaching two $2$-cells to the torus $S^1\times S^1$, one along a simple closed curve $1\times S^1$ and the other along $S^1\times 1$.
Calculate the fundamental group and homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Give the torus its standard CW structure with one $0$-cell, two oriented $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell $c$ attached by the commutator
\[
aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]
Take $a$ to represent $S^1\times 1$ and $b$ to represent $1\times S^1$.
::: {.proof}
The usual square model of the torus has one vertex after identifying all four corners, two $1$-cells from the two pairs of opposite sides, and one $2$-cell whose boundary word is the commutator.
Thus
\[
\pi_1(T^2)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z^2.
\]
:::

<1>2. Attaching the two additional $2$-cells kills both generators of the fundamental group, so
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(X)=1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $e_a^2$ be the new $2$-cell attached along $S^1\times1$, and let $e_b^2$ be the one attached along $1\times S^1$.
By Seifert--van Kampen, attaching a $2$-cell along a loop adds the relation represented by that loop.
Hence
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b\mid [a,b],a,b\rangle
=1.
\]
:::

<1>3. The cellular chain complex of $X$ in positive degrees is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
C_2(X)\cong\mathbb Z^3
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
C_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
C_0(X)\cong\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0.
\]
::: {.proof}
There is one $0$-cell, the two original $1$-cells $a,b$, and three $2$-cells: the original torus cell $c$ and the two newly attached cells $e_a^2,e_b^2$.
There are no cells in dimensions at least $3$.
Since both ends of each $1$-cell are the unique $0$-cell,
\[
\partial_1=0.
\]
:::

<1>4. With ordered bases
\[
C_2(X)=\mathbb Z\langle c,e_a^2,e_b^2\rangle,
\qquad
C_1(X)=\mathbb Z\langle a,b\rangle,
\]
the cellular boundary map is
\[
\partial_2(c)=0,
\qquad
\partial_2(e_a^2)=a,
\qquad
\partial_2(e_b^2)=b.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
[\partial_2]
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
For a $2$-cell in a CW complex with one vertex, its cellular boundary records the total degree with which its attaching map traverses each $1$-cell.

The original torus cell has attaching word
\[
aba^{-1}b^{-1},
\]
whose exponent sum in both $a$ and $b$ is zero, so its cellular boundary is zero.
The cell $e_a^2$ is attached once around $a$, giving boundary $a$, and $e_b^2$ is attached once around $b$, giving boundary $b$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_i(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,2,\\
0,&i\ne0,2.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>4, $\partial_2$ is surjective, so
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
=
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\mathbb Z^2/\mathbb Z^2
=0.
\]
Also
\[
\ker\partial_2
=
\mathbb Z\langle c\rangle,
\]
and there is no $C_3$, so
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)
=
\ker\partial_2
\cong
\mathbb Z.
\]
The space $X$ is connected, hence $H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$, and there are no cells above dimension $2$, so $H_i(X;\mathbb Z)=0$ for $i\ge3$.
:::
