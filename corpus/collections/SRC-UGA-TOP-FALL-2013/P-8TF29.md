::: problem
Give the definition of a covering space $\widehat{X}$ (and covering map $p:\widehat{X}\to X$) for a topological space $X$.
Prove that if $X$ is simply connected, and $(\widehat{X},p)$ is a covering space for $X$, then $p$ is a homeomorphism.
Make your proof as elementary as possible.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A covering map is a continuous surjection
\[
p:\widehat X\to X
\]
such that every $x\in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ for which
\[
p^{-1}(U)=\coprod_{\alpha\in A}V_\alpha
\]
is a disjoint union of open subsets of $\widehat X$, and every restriction
\[
p|_{V_\alpha}:V_\alpha\to U
\]
is a homeomorphism.
The pair $(\widehat X,p)$ is then called a covering space of $X$, and $U$ is said to be evenly covered.
::: {.proof}
This is the standard definition.
In particular, a covering map is a local homeomorphism.
:::

<1>2. With this definition, the conclusion printed in the problem is false unless an additional hypothesis is imposed on $\widehat X$.
::: {.proof}
Take
\[
X=\mathbb R
\]
and
\[
\widehat X=\mathbb R\amalg\mathbb R.
\]
Let $p$ restrict to the identity map on each copy of $\mathbb R$.
Then $p$ is a two-sheeted covering map of the simply connected space $\mathbb R$, but it is not injective and hence is not a homeomorphism.

Thus the statement in the source is not correct for arbitrary covering spaces.
The intended conclusion is correct, in particular, when $\widehat X$ is path-connected.
:::

<1>3. Assume henceforth that $X$ is simply connected and that $\widehat X$ is path-connected.
Then every fiber of $p$ contains at most one point.
::: {.proof}
Fix $x\in X$ and suppose
\[
\widehat x_0,\widehat x_1\in p^{-1}(x).
\]
Since $\widehat X$ is path-connected, choose a path
\[
\alpha:[0,1]\to\widehat X
\]
from $\widehat x_0$ to $\widehat x_1$.
Its projection
\[
\beta=p\circ\alpha
\]
is a loop in $X$ based at $x$.

Because $X$ is simply connected, $\beta$ is homotopic relative to its endpoints to the constant loop $c_x$.
We use the homotopy-lifting property for covering maps: if two paths in the base are homotopic relative to their endpoints, then their lifts with the same initial point have the same terminal point.

The path $\alpha$ is the lift of $\beta$ beginning at $\widehat x_0$.
The constant path $c_{\widehat x_0}$ is the lift of $c_x$ beginning at the same point.
Therefore their terminal points agree:
\[
\widehat x_1
=
\alpha(1)
=
c_{\widehat x_0}(1)
=
\widehat x_0.
\]
Hence $p^{-1}(x)$ has at most one point.
:::

<1>4. Under the path-connectedness hypothesis, $p$ is a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
By the definition in <1>1, $p$ is surjective.
By <1>3, every fiber has at most one point, so $p$ is injective.
Thus $p$ is bijective.

A covering map is a local homeomorphism, hence an open map: if $O\subseteq\widehat X$ is open and $\widehat x\in O$, choose an evenly covered neighborhood $U$ of $p(\widehat x)$ and the sheet $V$ over $U$ containing $\widehat x$.
Then
\[
p(O\cap V)
\]
is open in $U$ and therefore open in $X$; these sets cover $p(O)$ as $\widehat x$ ranges over $O$.
So $p(O)$ is open.

Consequently a bijective covering map is a continuous open bijection, and its inverse is continuous.
Therefore
\[
\boxed{p:\widehat X\xrightarrow{\cong}X}
\]
is a homeomorphism.
:::

<1>5. Hence the source conclusion is valid with the intended additional assumption that the covering space $\widehat X$ is path-connected; without such an assumption, <1>2 is a counterexample.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly <1>2 together with <1>4.
:::
:::
