::: {.problem}
Let $L/K$ be an algebraic extension.
Let
\[
K^{\mathrm{ab}} = \{\alpha \in L \mid K[\alpha]/K \text{ is Galois and } \mathrm{Gal}(K[\alpha]/K) \text{ is abelian}\}.
\]

(a) Prove that if $K \subseteq E \subseteq L$, $E/K$ is Galois and $\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)$ is abelian, then $E \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ab}}$.

(b) Prove that $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is a subfield of $L$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If \(K\subseteq E\subseteq L\), \(E/K\) is Galois, and \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) is abelian, then every \(\alpha\in E\) belongs to \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\).
::: {.proof}
Fix \(\alpha\in E\) and set
\[
M:=K[\alpha].
\]
Because \(L/K\) is algebraic, \(\alpha\) is algebraic over \(K\), so \(M=K(\alpha)\) is a finite intermediate extension of \(E/K\).
Let
\[
G:=\operatorname{Gal}(E/K),
\qquad
H:=\operatorname{Gal}(E/M).
\]
Since \(G\) is abelian, every subgroup of \(G\), in particular \(H\), is normal.
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, \(M/K\) is therefore Galois and
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)\cong G/H.
\]
A quotient of an abelian group is abelian, so \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)\) is abelian.
Thus \(\alpha\in K^{\mathrm{ab}}\).
Since \(\alpha\) was arbitrary,
\[
E\subseteq K^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]
:::

<1>2. The compositum of two finite abelian Galois subextensions of \(L/K\) is again a finite abelian Galois subextension.
::: {.proof}
Let \(E/K\) and \(F/K\) be finite Galois extensions contained in \(L\), with both Galois groups abelian.
Because finite Galois extensions are splitting fields of separable polynomials, choose separable polynomials \(f,g\in K[x]\) such that \(E\) and \(F\) are their splitting fields.
Then the compositum
\[
EF
\]
is the splitting field of \(fg\), which is separable.
Hence \(EF/K\) is finite Galois.

Restriction defines a homomorphism
\[
\rho:\operatorname{Gal}(EF/K)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\times\operatorname{Gal}(F/K),
\qquad
\sigma\longmapsto(\sigma|_E,\sigma|_F).
\]
This map is injective: if \(\sigma\) is the identity on both \(E\) and \(F\), then it is the identity on the field generated by them, namely \(EF\).
Therefore \(\operatorname{Gal}(EF/K)\) is isomorphic to a subgroup of the abelian group
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\times\operatorname{Gal}(F/K),
\]
and is consequently abelian.
Since \(E,F\subseteq L\) and \(L\) is a field, also \(EF\subseteq L\).
:::

<1>3. The set \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\) contains \(K\) and is closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication.
::: {.proof}
If \(a\in K\), then
\[
K[a]=K,
\]
whose Galois group over \(K\) is trivial, hence abelian.
Thus
\[
K\subseteq K^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]

Now let \(\alpha,\beta\in K^{\mathrm{ab}}\).
Set
\[
E:=K[\alpha],
\qquad
F:=K[\beta].
\]
By definition, \(E/K\) and \(F/K\) are finite Galois with abelian Galois groups.
By <1>2, \(EF/K\) is finite Galois and abelian.
Part (a), proved in <1>1, then gives
\[
EF\subseteq K^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]
Since
\[
\alpha+\beta,
\quad
\alpha-\beta,
\quad
\alpha\beta
\in EF,
\]
all three elements lie in \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\).
:::

<1>4. The set \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\) is closed under inverses of nonzero elements.
::: {.proof}
Let \(0\neq\alpha\in K^{\mathrm{ab}}\).
Then
\[
E:=K[\alpha]
\]
is a field contained in \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\) by <1>1, applied to the abelian Galois extension \(E/K\).
Since \(\alpha^{-1}\in E\), it follows that
\[
\alpha^{-1}\in K^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]
:::

<1>5. Therefore \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\) is a subfield of \(L\).
::: {.proof}
By definition \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\subseteq L\).
By <1>3 it contains \(K\) and is closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication, and by <1>4 it is closed under inverses of nonzero elements.
Hence it is a subfield of \(L\).
:::
:::
