::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f:S^n\times S^n\longrightarrow S^n\times S^n
$$
be a homeomorphism.
Let $z$ generate $H^n(S^n)$ and put
$$
\alpha=z\times1,\qquad \beta=1\times z
$$
in $H^n(S^n\times S^n)$.
Write
$$
f^*(\alpha)=k\alpha+l\beta,\qquad f^*(\beta)=p\alpha+q\beta
$$
for integers $k,l,p,q$.

Prove that if $n$ is even, then
$$
\begin{pmatrix}k&l\\ p&q\end{pmatrix}
$$
is one of the eight signed permutation matrices.
Also find homeomorphisms $f$ realizing all eight possibilities.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
X=S^n\times S^n,
$$
and let
$$
\alpha=z\times1,
\qquad
\beta=1\times z
$$
generate
$$
H^n(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^2.
$$

Because $n$ is even, graded commutativity gives
$$
\boxed{\alpha\smile\beta=\beta\smile\alpha}.
$$

Also,
$$
\boxed{\alpha^2=\beta^2=0}
$$
because $H^{2n}(S^n)=0$.

Finally,
$$
\boxed{
H^{2n}(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ}
$$
with generator
$$
\alpha\beta.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Since $f$ is a homeomorphism,
$$
f^*:H^n(X;\ZZ)\to H^n(X;\ZZ)
$$
is an automorphism.


::: pf-proof

A homeomorphism induces an isomorphism on cohomology.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the matrix
$$
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
k&l\\
p&q
\end{pmatrix}
$$
lies in
$$
\boxed{GL_2(\ZZ)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The displayed matrix represents $f^*$ in the basis $(\alpha,\beta)$.
An automorphism of $\ZZ^2$ has determinant $\pm1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $\alpha^2=0$,
$$
0
=
f^*(\alpha^2)
=
(f^*\alpha)^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Cohomology pullback preserves cup products.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Using
$$
f^*\alpha=k\alpha+l\beta,
$$
one gets
$$
0
=
(k\alpha+l\beta)^2
=
2kl\,\alpha\beta.
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand:
$$
k^2\alpha^2
+
kl\,\alpha\beta
+
lk\,\beta\alpha
+
l^2\beta^2.
$$
The square terms vanish, and since $n$ is even,
$$
\beta\alpha=\alpha\beta.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence
$$
\boxed{kl=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group
$$
H^{2n}(X;\ZZ)
$$
is infinite cyclic generated by $\alpha\beta$, so
$$
2kl\,\alpha\beta=0
$$
implies $2kl=0$, hence $kl=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Similarly,
$$
\boxed{pq=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the same argument to
$$
0=f^*(\beta^2)=(p\alpha+q\beta)^2=2pq\,\alpha\beta.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Since the first row $(k,l)$ is nonzero and satisfies $kl=0$, exactly one of $k,l$ is nonzero.


::: pf-proof

The row cannot be zero because $M$ is invertible. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} says at least one entry is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Since the second row $(p,q)$ is nonzero and satisfies $pq=0$, exactly one of $p,q$ is nonzero.


::: pf-proof

Use invertibility and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The nonzero entries of the two rows must occur in different columns.


::: pf-proof

If they occurred in the same column, the other column would be zero, making
$$
\det M=0,
$$
contrary to $M\in GL_2(\ZZ)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $M$ has one of the two forms
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
k&0\\
0&q
\end{pmatrix}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}
0&l\\
p&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} say each row and each column has exactly one nonzero entry.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In the diagonal case,
$$
\det M=kq=\pm1,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{k,q\in\{\pm1\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The only integers whose product is $\pm1$ are $\pm1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In the off-diagonal case,
$$
\det M=-lp=\pm1,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{l,p\in\{\pm1\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Again, an integer product equal to $\pm1$ forces both factors to be units in $\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Hence $M$ is one of the eight signed permutation matrices:
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}\pm1&0\\0&\pm1\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\begin{pmatrix}0&\pm1\\\pm1&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

There are four sign choices in each of the two permutation patterns.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define homeomorphisms
$$
A:S^n\to S^n,\quad A(x)=-x;
\qquad
h_1=\mathrm{id}_{S^n},\quad h_{-1}=A;
\qquad
\tau:X\to X,\quad \tau(x,y)=(y,x).
$$


::: pf-proof

Each map is continuous and is its own inverse.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $n$ is even,
$$
\boxed{\deg A=-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The antipodal map on $S^n$ has degree
$$
(-1)^{n+1}.
$$
For even $n$, this equals $-1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The homeomorphism
$$
F_{\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2}(x,y)
=
\bigl(
h_{\varepsilon_1}(x),
h_{\varepsilon_2}(y)
\bigr)
$$
induces
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon_1&0\\
0&\varepsilon_2
\end{pmatrix}}
$$
on $H^n(X)$.


::: pf-proof

On the first factor,
$$
h_{\varepsilon_1}^*z=\varepsilon_1 z,
$$
and similarly on the second factor. Hence
$$
F^*\alpha=\varepsilon_1\alpha,
\qquad
F^*\beta=\varepsilon_2\beta.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Therefore all four diagonal signed permutation matrices occur.


::: pf-proof

Choose independently
$$
\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\in\{\pm1\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\boxed{
\tau^*\alpha=\beta,
\qquad
\tau^*\beta=\alpha}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Under $\tau$, the first projection becomes the second and the second becomes the first.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $\tau$ induces the matrix
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Read the coefficients of $\tau^*\alpha$ and $\tau^*\beta$ in the basis $(\alpha,\beta)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For
$$
G_{\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2}
=
F_{\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2}\circ\tau,
$$
the induced matrix is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\varepsilon_1\\
\varepsilon_2&0
\end{pmatrix}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
G^*=\tau^*\circ F^*,
$$
one gets
$$
G^*\alpha
=
\tau^*(\varepsilon_1\alpha)
=
\varepsilon_1\beta,
$$
and
$$
G^*\beta
=
\tau^*(\varepsilon_2\beta)
=
\varepsilon_2\alpha.
$$
This is the displayed matrix in the convention of the problem.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

Therefore all four off-diagonal signed permutation matrices occur.


::: pf-proof

Choose independently
$$
\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\in\{\pm1\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

Thus every one of the eight signed permutation matrices is realized by a homeomorphism of
$$
S^n\times S^n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The diagonal four are realized in step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} and the off-diagonal four in step [](#s1-20){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} proves the restriction on the matrix, and step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref} realizes all eight possibilities.

:::
