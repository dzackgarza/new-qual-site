::: {.problem}
Let $n\ge2$.
Prove that there do not exist an integer $k$ and a space $X$ such that
\[
\CP^n\simeq S^k\vee X.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that there is a homotopy equivalence
\[
\CP^n\simeq S^k\vee X.
\]

Let
\[
x\in H^2(\CP^n;\ZZ)
\]
be the standard generator.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The integral cohomology ring is
\[
\boxed{
H^*(\CP^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ[x]/(x^{n+1})},
\qquad
|x|=2.
\]


::: pf-proof

This is the standard cellular cohomology and cup-product computation for complex projective space.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Hence
\[
H^m(\CP^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&m=0,2,4,\dots,2n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]


::: pf-proof

The classes
\[
1,x,x^2,\dots,x^n
\]
give one generator in each even degree up to \(2n\), and there are no other classes.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since \(S^k\) is a wedge summand, its reduced cohomology contributes a direct summand
\[
\ZZ
\]
to \(H^k(\CP^n;\ZZ)\).


::: pf-proof

For pointed CW complexes,
\[
\widetilde H^*(S^k\vee X)
\cong
\widetilde H^*(S^k)\oplus\widetilde H^*(X).
\]
Homotopy equivalence preserves cohomology.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{k=2j}
\]
for some
\[
1\le j\le n.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the only positive degrees with nonzero cohomology are \(2,4,\dots,2n\). Since the sphere contributes a nonzero class in degree \(k\), \(k\) must be one of these.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Under the homotopy equivalence, \(u\) corresponds to
\[
\boxed{\pm x^j}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
\[
H^{2j}(\CP^n;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ
\]
is generated by \(x^j\). Since the sphere generator is primitive in the direct summand \(\ZZ\), its image under an isomorphism of free rank-one groups is \(\pm x^j\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The sphere class \(u\) has zero cup product with every positive-degree class coming from \(X\).


::: pf-proof

For a wedge \(A\vee B\), reduced cohomology classes supported on different wedge summands have zero cup product. This follows from the inclusions and the fact that the reduced diagonal has no mixed contribution between the two summands.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Also,
\[
\boxed{u^2=0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The class \(u\) comes from \(H^{2j}(S^{2j})\), but
\[
H^{4j}(S^{2j})=0
\]
for \(j>0\). Thus its square vanishes.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The class
\[
x^{n-j}
\]
has positive degree.


::: pf-proof

Since \(j<n\),
\[
n-j\ge1,
\]
so its degree
\[
2(n-j)>0.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Under the wedge decomposition, the class corresponding to
\[
x^{n-j}
\]
lies entirely in the \(X\)-summand unless \(n-j=j\), in which case it may have a sphere component as well.


::: pf-proof

The sphere summand has reduced cohomology in exactly one positive degree, namely \(2j\). Therefore if
\[
2(n-j)\ne2j,
\]
the entire class lies in \(H^{2(n-j)}(X)\). If the degrees coincide, the class may decompose into sphere and \(X\) components.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

In either case,
\[
\boxed{
u\smile x^{n-j}=0}
\]
under the wedge decomposition.


::: pf-proof

If \(x^{n-j}\) lies in the \(X\)-summand, use step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

If \(n-j=j\), write
\[
x^{n-j}=au+v
\]
with \(v\) from \(X\). Then
\[
u\smile x^{n-j}
=
a\,u^2+u\smile v
=
0
\]
by steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Transporting step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} back to \(\CP^n\) gives
\[
x^j\smile x^{n-j}=0.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, \(u\) corresponds to \(\pm x^j\). Cup product is preserved by cohomology-ring isomorphisms induced by homotopy equivalences.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

But
\[
\boxed{
x^j\smile x^{n-j}=x^n\ne0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The ring relation in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} only kills \(x^{n+1}\) and higher powers. The class \(x^n\) generates
\[
H^{2n}(\CP^n;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Thus the case \(j<n\) is impossible.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} contradict one another.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

The sphere summand contributes all of
\[
H^{2n}(S^{2n}\vee X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
\]


::: pf-proof

The total group is isomorphic to
\[
H^{2n}(\CP^n;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
\]
The sphere already contributes a direct summand \(\ZZ\), so the \(X\)-summand in degree \(2n\) must be zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H^{2n}(X;\ZZ)=0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

This is the direct-sum conclusion from step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since \(n\ge2\), the classes
\[
x\in H^2(\CP^n)
\]
and
\[
x^{n-1}\in H^{2n-2}(\CP^n)
\]
both correspond to classes in the \(X\)-summand.


::: pf-proof

The sphere \(S^{2n}\) has no positive cohomology in degrees \(2\) or \(2n-2\), because
\[
0<2,\,2n-2<2n
\]
when \(n\ge2\). Hence these cohomology groups of the wedge come entirely from \(X\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Their cup product therefore lies in
\[
H^{2n}(X;\ZZ)=0.
\]


::: pf-proof

Cup product of two classes both supported on the \(X\)-summand is the cup product computed in \(X\), which lands in \(H^{2n}(X)\). Apply step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

Transporting back to \(\CP^n\), step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref} says
\[
x\smile x^{n-1}=0.
\]


::: pf-proof

The homotopy equivalence induces a ring isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

But
\[
\boxed{
x\smile x^{n-1}=x^n\ne0}.
\]


::: pf-proof

As in step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}, \(x^n\) generates the top cohomology.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-20}

Thus the case \(j=n\) is also impossible.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-18){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-19){.pf-ref} contradict one another.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-21}

Therefore no integer \(k\) and space \(X\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\CP^n\simeq S^k\vee X}
\]
for \(n\ge2\).


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} reduces to the two cases \(j<n\) and \(j=n\), which are excluded by steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-20){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-21){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
