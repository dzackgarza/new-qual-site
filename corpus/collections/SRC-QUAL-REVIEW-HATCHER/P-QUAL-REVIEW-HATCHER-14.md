::: {.problem}
Let $n\ge2$.
Prove that there do not exist an integer $k$ and a space $X$ such that
\[
\mathbb{CP}^n\simeq S^k\vee X.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that there is a homotopy equivalence
\[
\mathbb{CP}^n\simeq S^k\vee X.
\]

Let
\[
x\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)
\]
be the standard generator.

<1>1. The integral cohomology ring is
\[
\boxed{
H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z[x]/(x^{n+1})},
\qquad
|x|=2.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is the standard cellular cohomology and cup-product computation for complex projective space.
:::

<1>2. Hence
\[
H^m(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&m=0,2,4,\dots,2n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

::: {.proof}
The classes
\[
1,x,x^2,\dots,x^n
\]
give one generator in each even degree up to \(2n\), and there are no other classes.
:::

<1>3. Since \(S^k\) is a wedge summand, its reduced cohomology contributes a direct summand
\[
\mathbb Z
\]
to \(H^k(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)\).

::: {.proof}
For pointed CW complexes,
\[
\widetilde H^*(S^k\vee X)
\cong
\widetilde H^*(S^k)\oplus\widetilde H^*(X).
\]
Homotopy equivalence preserves cohomology.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
\[
\boxed{k=2j}
\]
for some
\[
1\le j\le n.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, the only positive degrees with nonzero cohomology are \(2,4,\dots,2n\). Since the sphere contributes a nonzero class in degree \(k\), \(k\) must be one of these.
:::

Let
\[
u\in H^{2j}(S^{2j}\vee X;\mathbb Z)
\]
be the class corresponding to the generator of
\[
\widetilde H^{2j}(S^{2j};\mathbb Z).
\]

<1>5. Under the homotopy equivalence, \(u\) corresponds to
\[
\boxed{\pm x^j}.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
\[
H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
is generated by \(x^j\). Since the sphere generator is primitive in the direct summand \(\mathbb Z\), its image under an isomorphism of free rank-one groups is \(\pm x^j\).
:::

<1>6. The sphere class \(u\) has zero cup product with every positive-degree class coming from \(X\).

::: {.proof}
For a wedge \(A\vee B\), reduced cohomology classes supported on different wedge summands have zero cup product. This follows from the inclusions and the fact that the reduced diagonal has no mixed contribution between the two summands.
:::

<1>7. Also,
\[
\boxed{u^2=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The class \(u\) comes from \(H^{2j}(S^{2j})\), but
\[
H^{4j}(S^{2j})=0
\]
for \(j>0\). Thus its square vanishes.
:::

## Case 1: \(j<n\)

<1>8. The class
\[
x^{n-j}
\]
has positive degree.

::: {.proof}
Since \(j<n\),
\[
n-j\ge1,
\]
so its degree
\[
2(n-j)>0.
\]
:::

<1>9. Under the wedge decomposition, the class corresponding to
\[
x^{n-j}
\]
lies entirely in the \(X\)-summand unless \(n-j=j\), in which case it may have a sphere component as well.

::: {.proof}
The sphere summand has reduced cohomology in exactly one positive degree, namely \(2j\). Therefore if
\[
2(n-j)\ne2j,
\]
the entire class lies in \(H^{2(n-j)}(X)\). If the degrees coincide, the class may decompose into sphere and \(X\) components.
:::

<1>10. In either case,
\[
\boxed{
u\smile x^{n-j}=0}
\]
under the wedge decomposition.

::: {.proof}
If \(x^{n-j}\) lies in the \(X\)-summand, use step <1>6.

If \(n-j=j\), write
\[
x^{n-j}=au+v
\]
with \(v\) from \(X\). Then
\[
u\smile x^{n-j}
=
a\,u^2+u\smile v
=
0
\]
by steps <1>6--<1>7.
:::

<1>11. Transporting step <1>10 back to \(\mathbb{CP}^n\) gives
\[
x^j\smile x^{n-j}=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>5, \(u\) corresponds to \(\pm x^j\). Cup product is preserved by cohomology-ring isomorphisms induced by homotopy equivalences.
:::

<1>12. But
\[
\boxed{
x^j\smile x^{n-j}=x^n\ne0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The ring relation in step <1>1 only kills \(x^{n+1}\) and higher powers. The class \(x^n\) generates
\[
H^{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>13. Thus the case \(j<n\) is impossible.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>11--<1>12 contradict one another.
:::

## Case 2: \(j=n\)

Now
\[
k=2n.
\]

<1>14. The sphere summand contributes all of
\[
H^{2n}(S^{2n}\vee X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

::: {.proof}
The total group is isomorphic to
\[
H^{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The sphere already contributes a direct summand \(\mathbb Z\), so the \(X\)-summand in degree \(2n\) must be zero.
:::

<1>15. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H^{2n}(X;\mathbb Z)=0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
This is the direct-sum conclusion from step <1>14.
:::

<1>16. Since \(n\ge2\), the classes
\[
x\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^n)
\]
and
\[
x^{n-1}\in H^{2n-2}(\mathbb{CP}^n)
\]
both correspond to classes in the \(X\)-summand.

::: {.proof}
The sphere \(S^{2n}\) has no positive cohomology in degrees \(2\) or \(2n-2\), because
\[
0<2,\,2n-2<2n
\]
when \(n\ge2\). Hence these cohomology groups of the wedge come entirely from \(X\).
:::

<1>17. Their cup product therefore lies in
\[
H^{2n}(X;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
Cup product of two classes both supported on the \(X\)-summand is the cup product computed in \(X\), which lands in \(H^{2n}(X)\). Apply step <1>15.
:::

<1>18. Transporting back to \(\mathbb{CP}^n\), step <1>17 says
\[
x\smile x^{n-1}=0.
\]

::: {.proof}
The homotopy equivalence induces a ring isomorphism.
:::

<1>19. But
\[
\boxed{
x\smile x^{n-1}=x^n\ne0}.
\]

::: {.proof}
As in step <1>12, \(x^n\) generates the top cohomology.
:::

<1>20. Thus the case \(j=n\) is also impossible.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>18--<1>19 contradict one another.
:::

<1>21. Therefore no integer \(k\) and space \(X\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb{CP}^n\simeq S^k\vee X}
\]
for \(n\ge2\).

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 reduces to the two cases \(j<n\) and \(j=n\), which are excluded by steps <1>13 and <1>20.
:::

<1>22. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>21 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
