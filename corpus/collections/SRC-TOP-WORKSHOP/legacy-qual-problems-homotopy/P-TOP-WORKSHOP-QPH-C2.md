::: {.problem}
Let
\[
H=\langle x\mid x^6\rangle.
\]
Find the presentation complex $X_H$ and then find its universal cover $\widetilde X_H$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The presentation complex $X_H$ has one $0$-cell, one $1$-cell, and one $2$-cell.

::: {.proof}
There is one generator $x$, so the $1$-skeleton consists of one loop attached to one vertex and is therefore a circle.

There is one relator $x^6$, so one $2$-cell is attached to that circle by traversing the generator loop six times.
:::

<1>2. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
X_H
\cong
S^1\cup_{z\mapsto z^6}D^2.
}
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the attaching map of the unique $2$-cell wraps its boundary six times around the unique $1$-cell. After identifying both boundary and $1$-skeleton with $S^1$, this is exactly the degree-$6$ map
\[
z\longmapsto z^6.
\]
:::

<1>3. The fundamental group of $X_H$ is
\[
\pi_1(X_H)
\cong
\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z.
\]

::: {.proof}
The $1$-skeleton has fundamental group
\[
\pi_1(S^1)
\cong
\langle x\rangle.
\]
Attaching the $2$-cell along the loop $x^6$ imposes the relation
\[
x^6=1.
\]
Hence van Kampen gives
\[
\pi_1(X_H)
\cong
\langle x\mid x^6\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z.
\]
:::

<1>4. Define a connected CW complex $Y$ with vertices
\[
v_0,v_1,\dots,v_5,
\]
indices taken modulo $6$, and oriented edges
\[
e_j:v_j\longrightarrow v_{j+1}.
\]
Its $1$-skeleton is a hexagon.

::: {.proof}
This is the Cayley graph of
\[
\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z
\]
with respect to the generator $x$: multiplication by $x$ sends the vertex labeled $j$ to the vertex labeled $j+1$.
:::

<1>5. Attach six $2$-cells
\[
D_0,dots,D_5
\]
to the hexagon, where the boundary of $D_j$ follows
\[
e_j e_{j+1}e_{j+2}e_{j+3}e_{j+4}e_{j+5}.
\]
Then every $D_j$ is attached homeomorphically around the entire hexagonal $1$-skeleton once.

::: {.proof}
Starting at $v_j$, the path successively traverses all six edges and returns to $v_j$. No edge is repeated before the path closes, so its boundary map is a degree-$1$ parametrization of the hexagonal circle.
:::

<1>6. There is a cellular map
\[
p:Y\to X_H
\]
that sends every vertex $v_j$ to the unique $0$-cell of $X_H$, every edge $e_j$ homeomorphically onto the generator $1$-cell, and every $D_j$ homeomorphically onto the unique open $2$-cell.

::: {.proof}
On the boundary of $D_j$, the six consecutive edges all map to the generator loop $x$. Hence the boundary maps under $p$ to
\[
x^6,
\]
which is exactly the attaching map of the $2$-cell of $X_H$. Therefore the map on the $1$-skeleton extends over every $D_j$.
:::

<1>7. The map $p$ in step <1>6 is a $6$-sheeted covering map.

::: {.proof}
Away from the cells' attaching loci this is immediate: each open cell of $X_H$ has six disjoint open-cell lifts, one for each index $j$.

Near the unique $0$-cell, take a sufficiently small cellular neighborhood meeting the $1$-cell in two short arcs and the $2$-cell in six narrow sectors corresponding to the six passages of the attaching map $x^6$. Its inverse image splits into six neighborhoods, one around each $v_j$, each mapped homeomorphically onto the chosen neighborhood.

Thus every point of $X_H$ has an evenly covered neighborhood, and every fiber contains six points.
:::

<1>8. The space $Y$ is simply connected.

::: {.proof}
Its $1$-skeleton is a circle, so
\[
\pi_1(Y^{(1)})
\cong
\mathbb Z,
\]
generated by one trip around the hexagon.

Already the first $2$-cell $D_0$ is attached exactly along that generator. By van Kampen, attaching $D_0$ kills the generator and makes the resulting subcomplex simply connected.

Attaching the remaining $2$-cells cannot create new fundamental-group elements; each only imposes an additional relation. Hence
\[
\pi_1(Y)=0.
\]
:::

<1>9. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde X_H\cong Y,
}
\]
where $Y$ is six disks glued along their common boundary circle.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>7 and <1>8,
\[
p:Y\to X_H
\]
is a connected covering whose total space is simply connected. Therefore it is the universal covering.

By step <1>5, the common $1$-skeleton is the hexagonal circle and each of the six $2$-cells is attached to it by a homeomorphism of its boundary. Thus, as a space, $Y$ is precisely six closed disks glued along the same boundary circle.
:::

<1>10. In particular,
\[
\widetilde X_H
\simeq
\bigvee_{j=1}^{5}S^2.
\]

::: {.proof}
The universal cover $Y$ is a simply connected $2$-dimensional CW complex with
\[
\chi(Y)
=
6-6+6
=6.
\]
Its cellular chain complex has
\[
C_2\cong\mathbb Z^6,
\qquad
C_1\cong\mathbb Z^6,
\qquad
C_0\cong\mathbb Z^6.
\]
The six $2$-cell boundaries all represent the same generator of
\[
\ker(\partial_1)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=1
\]
and therefore
\[
H_2(Y)\cong\mathbb Z^5.
\]
Since $Y$ is simply connected and has no homology above degree $2$, choosing five maps
\[
S^2\to Y
\]
representing a basis of $H_2(Y)$ gives a homology isomorphism
\[
\bigvee^5S^2\to Y.
\]
Both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, so Whitehead's theorem makes this map a homotopy equivalence.
:::

<1>11. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 identifies the presentation complex exactly, and step <1>9 identifies its universal cover exactly; step <1>10 records its familiar homotopy type.
:::
