::: {.problem}
(Arizona Aug ’06) Compute the singular homology groups $H_*(X,\mathbb Z)$ of the space $X=\mathbb R^3\setminus A$, where $A$ is a subset of $\mathbb R^3$ homeomorphic to the disjoint union of two unlinked circles.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Application of Alexander Duality in $\mathbb{R}^3$:
<2>1. Let $A = S_1^1 \sqcup S_2^1 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be the disjoint union of two unlinked circles. $A$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^3$.
Proof: compact submanifolds of $\mathbb{R}^3$.
<2>2. By Alexander Duality for compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (with $n = 3$), the reduced singular homology of the complement $X = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A$ satisfies:
\[
\widetilde{H}_k(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \widetilde{H}^{3 - k - 1}(A; \mathbb{Z}) = \widetilde{H}^{2 - k}(A; \mathbb{Z}).
\]
Proof: Alexander Duality Theorem (Hatcher Theorem 3.44).

<1>2. Cohomology of the subspace $A = S^1 \sqcup S^1$:
<2>1. In degree 0, $A$ has 2 path components, so $H^0(A; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, and the reduced cohomology is:
\[
\widetilde{H}^0(A; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{2 - 1} = \mathbb{Z}.
\]
Proof: reduced cohomology in degree 0.
<2>2. In degree 1, $H^1(A; \mathbb{Z}) \cong H^1(S^1; \mathbb{Z}) \oplus H^1(S^1; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}^2$, so:
\[
\widetilde{H}^1(A; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2.
\]
Proof: cohomology of disjoint union.
<2>3. In degrees $k \ge 2$, since $\dim(A) = 1$:
\[
\widetilde{H}^k(A; \mathbb{Z}) = 0 \quad \text{for all } k \ge 2.
\]
Proof: dimension of 1-manifold.

<1>3. Computation of homology groups $H_k(X; \mathbb{Z})$:
<2>1. For $k = 0$:
\[
\widetilde{H}_0(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \widetilde{H}^2(A; \mathbb{Z}) = 0 \implies H_0(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}.
\]
Thus $X$ is path-connected.
Proof: <1>1 and <2>3.
<2>2. For $k = 1$:
\[
H_1(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \widetilde{H}_1(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \widetilde{H}^{2 - 1}(A; \mathbb{Z}) = \widetilde{H}^1(A; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2.
\]
Proof: <1>1 and <2>2.
<2>3. For $k = 2$:
\[
H_2(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \widetilde{H}_2(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \widetilde{H}^{2 - 2}(A; \mathbb{Z}) = \widetilde{H}^0(A; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}.
\]
(Geometrically, this is generated by a 2-sphere separating the two unlinked circles).
Proof: <1>1 and <2>1.
<2>4. For $k \ge 3$:
\[
H_k(X; \mathbb{Z}) = 0.
\]
Proof: $X \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is an open 3-manifold.

<1>4. Conclusion:
The singular homology groups of $X = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus (S^1 \sqcup S^1)$ are:
\[
H_k(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \begin{cases}
\mathbb{Z} & k = 0, \\
\mathbb{Z}^2 & k = 1, \\
\mathbb{Z} & k = 2, \\
0 & k \ge 3.
\end{cases}
\]
Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>3.
