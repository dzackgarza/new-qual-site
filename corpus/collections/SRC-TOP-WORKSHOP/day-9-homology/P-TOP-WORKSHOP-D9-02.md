::: {.problem}
(Michigan Jan ’10) Let $S^1=\{z\in\CC\mid \abs{z}=1\}$.
Let $X$ be obtained from the space $S^1\times[0,1]$ by identifying every point $(z,0)$ with the point $(z^4,1)$, with the quotient topology.
Compute the fundamental group and homology of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and the two computations below are cross-checked against each other.

Let $X$ be the quotient of $S^{1}\times[0,1]$ that identifies $(z,0)$ with $(z^{4},1)$; thus $X$ is the mapping torus of the degree-$4$ map $f:S^{1}\to S^{1}$, $f(z)=z^{4}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #pi1}
$\pi_{1}(X)\cong\langle a,t\mid tat^{-1}=a^{4}\rangle$.

::: pf-proof
Cover $S^{1}\times[0,1]$ by $S^{1}\times[0,1/2]$ and $S^{1}\times[1/2,1]$, whose images in $X$ are open, path connected and simply connected in intersection, and apply van Kampen. The first contains the circle $S^{1}\times\{0\}$ and the second the circle $S^{1}\times\{1\}$, each carrying a generator $a$; the intersection contains a loop moving in the $[0,1]$ direction, whose image is a generator $t$ with $\pi_{1}(S^{1}\times[0,1/2]\cap\cdots)\cong\ZZ$. The attaching identification sends the loop $a$ of the level $0$ to the loop $f(a)=a^{4}$ of the level $1$, so the relation $t\,a\,t^{-1}=a^{4}$ is imposed; that is the displayed presentation.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #wang}
The Wang sequence of $X$ as the mapping torus of $f$ gives $H_{2}(X)=\ker\bigl(1-f_{*}\bigr)$ on $H_{1}(S^{1})$ and an exact sequence
$$
0\to\operatorname{coker}\bigl(1-f_{*}\bigr)\ \text{on } H_{1}(S^{1})\to H_{1}(X)\to\ker\bigl(1-f_{*}\bigr)\ \text{on } H_{0}(S^{1})\to0,
$$
where $1-f_{*}$ is multiplication by $1-4=-3$ on $H_{1}(S^{1})=\ZZ$ and $0$ on $H_{0}(S^{1})=\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
For the mapping torus of $f$, the Wang sequence reads $\cdots\to H_{n}(S^{1})\xrightarrow{1-f_{*}}H_{n}(S^{1})\to H_{n}(X)\to H_{n-1}(S^{1})\xrightarrow{1-f_{*}}\cdots$. The induced map $f_{*}$ on $H_{1}(S^{1})$ is multiplication by the degree $4$, so $1-f_{*}$ is multiplication by $-3$; on $H_{0}(S^{1})$ every self-map induces the identity, so $1-f_{*}$ is $0$ there. Reading the sequence at $n=2$ gives $H_{2}(X)\cong\ker(-3\colon\ZZ\to\ZZ)$ and at $n=1$ gives the short exact sequence used below.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #homology}
$H_{2}(X)=0$, $H_{1}(X)\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/3$ and $H_{0}(X)\cong\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
Multiplication by $-3$ on $\ZZ$ is injective, so its kernel is $0$ and $H_{2}(X)=0$. From the Wang sequence at $n=1$, $0\to\ZZ/3\to H_{1}(X)\to\ZZ\to0$, where the quotient is the cokernel of $1-f_{*}$ on $H_{0}(S^{1})$, which is $\ZZ$ because that map is $0$; since $\ZZ$ is free the sequence splits, giving $H_{1}(X)\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/3$. Finally $X$ is path connected, so $H_{0}(X)=\ZZ$, and $H_{n}(X)=0$ for $n\ge2$ by the vanishing of the groups it is built from.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The abelianization of the displayed $\pi_{1}$ is $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/3$, confirming the homology.

::: pf-proof
In $\langle a,t\mid tat^{-1}=a^{4}\rangle$ the relation abelianizes to $a=a^{4}$, that is $3a=0$, while $t$ is subject to no further relation; so the abelianization is generated by $a$ of order $3$ and $t$ of infinite order, that is $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/3$, which is $H_{1}(X)$ by step [](#homology){.pf-ref}. So $\pi_{1}(X)\cong\langle a,t\mid tat^{-1}=a^{4}\rangle$ and the homology is $H_{0}=\ZZ$, $H_{1}=\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/3$, $H_{2}=0$, as computed.
:::
