::: {.problem}
(June ’05) Show, using covering spaces, that the fundamental group of the Klein bottle is not abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the standard universal covering \(\mathbb R^2\to K\) of the Klein bottle. Its deck group is generated by
\[
T(x,y)=(x+1,y),
\qquad
S(x,y)=(-x,y+1).
\]
These transformations generate the usual Klein-bottle identifications, and \(\mathbb R^2\) is simply connected, so the deck group is naturally isomorphic to \(\pi_1(K)\).

A direct computation gives
\[
STS^{-1}=T^{-1}.
\]
Indeed \(S^{-1}(x,y)=(-x,y-1)\), hence
\[
STS^{-1}(x,y)
=S(-x+1,y-1)
=(x-1,y)=T^{-1}(x,y).
\]
Since \(T\ne T^{-1}\), the transformations \(S\) and \(T\) do not commute. Therefore the deck group, and hence \(\pi_1(K)\), is nonabelian.
:::
