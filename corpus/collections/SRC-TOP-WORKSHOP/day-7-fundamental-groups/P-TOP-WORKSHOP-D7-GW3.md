::: {.problem}
Find $\pi_1(T^2)$, where $T^2=S^1\times S^1$ is the standard two torus, in TWO different ways.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own; no source text or solution set is retained for it.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #product}
$\pi_{1}(T^{2})\cong\pi_{1}(S^{1})\times\pi_{1}(S^{1})$.

::: pf-proof
For a product $X\times Y$ of path-connected spaces the map $\pi_{1}(X\times Y,(x_{0},y_{0}))\to\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})\times\pi_{1}(Y,y_{0})$ induced by the two projections is an isomorphism, the inverse sending $([\alpha],[\beta])$ to the class of $t\mapsto(\alpha(t),\beta(t))$; both directions are immediate from the definition of the product topology and of the fundamental group. $S^{1}$ is path-connected, so $\pi_{1}(T^{2})\cong\pi_{1}(S^{1})\times\pi_{1}(S^{1})$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #circle}
$\pi_{1}(S^{1},1)\cong\ZZ$, generated by the class of the loop $t\mapsto e^{2\pi it}$.

::: pf-proof
The map $\ZZ\to\pi_{1}(S^{1},1)$, $n\mapsto$ the class of $t\mapsto e^{2\pi int}$, is an isomorphism, by the standard computation via the covering $\RR\to S^{1}$ and path lifting.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #first}
The product theorem gives $\pi_{1}(T^{2})\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#product){.pf-ref} and [](#circle){.pf-ref} combine: $\pi_{1}(T^{2})\cong\ZZ\times\ZZ$, and a product of two copies of $\ZZ$ is $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$ written additively.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cw}
$T^{2}$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $a,b$ and one $2$-cell attached along the word $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.

::: pf-proof
Take the square $[0,1]^{2}$ and identify $(0,t)\sim(1,t)$ and $(s,0)\sim(s,1)$. The four corners become a single $0$-cell; the bottom and right edges become the $1$-cells $b$ and $a$; and the interior is the $2$-cell. Traversing the boundary of the square gives the loop $a\,b\,a^{-1}b^{-1}$, which is the attaching map of the $2$-cell.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cover}
The open cover used for van Kampen is $U=T^{2}\setminus\{p\}$ and $V$ a small open disc about the $0$-cell, intersecting in an open annulus.

::: pf-proof
Take $p$ in the interior of the $2$-cell and $V$ a disc contained in the interior of the $2$-cell together with a small neighbourhood of the $0$-cell, chosen so that $V$ misses $p$; then $U\cup V=T^{2}$. $U$ deformation retracts onto the wedge $S^{1}\vee S^{1}$ formed by the two $1$-cells, and $V$ is contractible. Their intersection $U\cap V$ is a disc with a point removed, hence homotopy equivalent to a circle.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #second}
Van Kampen gives $\pi_{1}(T^{2})\cong\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#cover){.pf-ref} the hypotheses of van Kampen hold: $U,V,U\cap V$ are path connected and open with $U\cup V=T^{2}$. So $\pi_{1}(T^{2})$ is the free product $\pi_{1}(U)*\pi_{1}(V)$ amalgamated over $\pi_{1}(U\cap V)$. Now $\pi_{1}(V)=1$, $\pi_{1}(U)$ is free on the two generators $a,b$ by step [](#cw){.pf-ref}, and the generator of $\pi_{1}(U\cap V)\cong\ZZ$ maps to the class of the boundary word in $U$, namely $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ — the attaching word of the $2$-cell, traversed once round the removed point. Amalgamating over that class is imposing the single relation $aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #commutative}
$\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
The relation $aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1$ is equivalent to $ab=ba$: multiply on the left by $a^{-1}$ and on the right by $b$, using that $a$, $b$ are invertible in the group presented. A group presented by two generators with the single relation $ab=ba$ is the free abelian group on two generators, that is $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\pi_{1}(T^{2})\cong\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$, obtained in two ways.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#first){.pf-ref} and [](#second){.pf-ref} with [](#commutative){.pf-ref} compute the group by the product theorem and by van Kampen respectively, and both give $\ZZ\oplus\ZZ$.
:::
