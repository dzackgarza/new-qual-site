::: {.problem}
(Michigan May ’09) Let $X$ be the space obtained from $S^1\times\RR$ by removing the interior of $k$ disjoint $2$-disks.

(a) Compute the fundamental group $\pi_1(X)$.

(b) What would be your answer to part (a) if $S^1\times\RR$ is replaced by $S^2\times\RR$ and $2$-disks are replaced by $3$-balls?

(c) Let $Y$ be the union of two copies of the real projective plane $\RP^2$ having exactly one point $y$ in common.
Compute $\pi_1(Y,y)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this item, the collection's provenance being the worksheet, so the argument is the card's own; it is van Kampen applied to the compact pieces of each space.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) $\pi_1(X)\cong F_{k+1}$, the free group on $k+1$ generators.

::: pf-proof
Choose $N$ large enough that all $k$ deleted discs lie in $S^1\times[-N,N]$, a compact annulus. The complement of it in $S^1\times\RR$ is the union of the two ends $S^1\times(N,\infty)$ and $S^1\times(-\infty,-N)$, each homeomorphic to $S^1\times(0,\infty)$, which deformation retracts onto its boundary circle $S^1\times\{N\}$, respectively $S^1\times\{-N\}$; so $X$ deformation retracts onto the compact annulus with the interiors of $k$ disjoint discs removed. That compact surface has genus $0$ and $k+2$ boundary components, so it deformation retracts onto a wedge of $k+1$ circles; a genus-$g$ surface with $b$ boundary components has the homotopy type of a wedge of $2g+b-1$ circles, and here $g=0$, $b=k+2$. Hence $\pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(\bigvee_{k+1}S^1)=F_{k+1}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b}
(b) For $S^2\times\RR$ with the interiors of $k$ disjoint $3$-balls removed, the fundamental group is trivial.

::: pf-proof
$S^2\times\RR$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^2$, hence simply connected. Removing the interior of one embedded closed ball $B^3$ does not change $\pi_1$: writing $M=(M\setminus\operatorname{int}B^3)\cup B^3$ and applying van Kampen to that pair, whose intersection is a thickened $2$-sphere and hence simply connected, gives $\pi_1(M)=\pi_1(M\setminus\operatorname{int}B^3)$. Applying this successively to the $k$ disjoint balls, each of which is an embedded closed $3$-ball, gives $\pi_1=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(c) $\pi_1(Y,y)\cong(\ZZ/2)*(\ZZ/2)=\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=1\rangle$.

::: pf-proof
$Y$ is the union of two copies of $\RP^2$ meeting in the single point $y$, that is the wedge $\RP^2\vee\RP^2$. Van Kampen's theorem applied to the two copies, which are closed with intersection the contractible set $\{y\}$, gives $\pi_1(Y,y)\cong\pi_1(\RP^2)*\pi_1(\RP^2)$; and $\pi_1(\RP^2)=\ZZ/2$, generated by the nontrivial loop, so the free product is $(\ZZ/2)*(\ZZ/2)$, presented by $\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=1\rangle$. Parts (a) and (b) are steps [](#part-a){.pf-ref} and [](#part-b){.pf-ref}.
:::
