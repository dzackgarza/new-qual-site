::: problem
Let $f_1 = x^2 y^2 - xy$ and $f_2 = x^3 y - 1$.

(a) Calculate a Gröbner basis for $\langle f_1, f_2 \rangle \cap k[x]$ and for $\langle f_1, f_2 \rangle$, where $\langle f_1, f_2 \rangle$ is the ideal in $k[x, y]$ generated by $f_1$ and $f_2$.

(b) What is the variety $V(f_1, f_2) = \{(a, b) : f(a, b) = 0,\; f \in \langle f_1, f_2 \rangle\}$ for $k = \mathbb{C}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Use lexicographic order with
\[
y>x,
\]
so that the elimination theorem applies to the subring $k[x]$.
Let
\[
f_1=x^2y^2-xy,
\qquad
f_2=x^3y-1.
\]

<1>1. The ideal generated by $f_1,f_2$ is
\[
I=(y-x,\ x^2-1).
\]
::: {.proof}
First,
\[
yf_2-xf_1
=(x^3y^2-y)-(x^3y^2-x^2y)
=y(x^2-1),
\]
so
\[
y(x^2-1)\in I.
\]
Then
\[
x\,y(x^2-1)-f_2
=x^3y-xy-(x^3y-1)
=1-xy,
\]
so
\[
xy-1\in I.
\]
Consequently
\[
f_2-x^2(xy-1)=x^2-1\in I.
\]
Finally,
\[
y(x^2-1)-x(xy-1)=x-y,
\]
so $y-x\in I$.
Thus
\[
(y-x,x^2-1)\subseteq I.
\]
Conversely,
\[
f_1=xy(xy-1)
\]
and
\[
xy-1=x(y-x)+(x^2-1),
\]
so $f_1\in(y-x,x^2-1)$.
Also
\[
f_2=x^2(xy-1)+(x^2-1),
\]
so $f_2$ belongs to the same ideal.
Hence equality holds.
:::

<1>2. The set
\[
\mathcal G=\{y-x,\ x^2-1\}
\]
is a Gröbner basis for $I$ with respect to lex order $y>x$.
::: {.proof}
The leading monomials are
\[
\operatorname{LM}(y-x)=y,
\qquad
\operatorname{LM}(x^2-1)=x^2.
\]
Their least common multiple is $x^2y$, and the only $S$-polynomial is
\[
S=x^2(y-x)-y(x^2-1)=y-x^3.
\]
Reducing first by $y-x$ gives
\[
x-x^3=-x(x^2-1),
\]
which reduces to zero by $x^2-1$.
Buchberger's criterion therefore shows that $\mathcal G$ is a Gröbner basis.
:::

<1>3. A Gröbner basis for the elimination ideal
\[
I\cap k[x]
\]
is
\[
\{x^2-1\}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, $\mathcal G$ is a Gröbner basis for lex order $y>x$.
The elimination theorem says that the elements of $\mathcal G$ involving only $x$ form a Gröbner basis for $I\cap k[x]$.
The only such element is $x^2-1$.
:::

<1>4. For $k=\mathbb C$,
\[
V(f_1,f_2)=\{(1,1),(-1,-1)\}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, a point $(a,b)$ lies in the variety exactly when
\[
b-a=0,
\qquad
a^2-1=0.
\]
Thus $a=\pm1$ and $b=a$, giving precisely the two displayed points.
:::
