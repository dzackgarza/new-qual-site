::: problem
Let $G$ be the subgroup of $S_4$ generated by the permutations $(12)$ and $(34)$, and let $V$ be the simple representation of $S_4$ labeled by the Young diagram $[2,1,1]$.

(a) Calculate the Molien series of $k[x_1, x_2, x_3]^G$.

(b) Let $\tilde{G} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ with generators $g_1$ and $g_2$, and let $W$ be the three-dimensional $\tilde{G}$-module with basis $w_1, w_2, w_3$ such that the action of $\tilde{G}$ is given by $$g_1 w_i = \begin{cases} -w_i & \text{if } i = 1, 2 \\ w_3 & \text{if } i = 3 \end{cases} \qquad g_2 w_i = \begin{cases} w_1 & \text{if } i = 1 \\ -w_i & \text{if } i = 2, 3 \end{cases}$$ Write down a Hironaka decomposition for $k[y_1, y_2, y_3]^{\tilde{G}}$.

(c) Find presentations of the rings $k[y_1, y_2, y_3]^{\tilde{G}}$ and $k[x_1, x_2, x_3]^G$ via generators and relations.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Part (a).**

<1>1. $G = \langle (12), (34) \rangle \cong \ZZ/2 \times \ZZ/2$, and its elements act on $V$ with eigenvalues: $e \mapsto (1,1,1)$, $(12) \mapsto (1,1,-1)$, $(34) \mapsto (1,1,-1)$, $(12)(34) \mapsto (1,-1,-1)$.
::: {.proof}
the restriction of the sign-twisted standard representation $[2,1,1]$ to $G$; each transposition acts as a reflection.
:::

<1>2. The Molien series is
$$M_G(t) = \frac{1}{4}\left[ \frac{1}{(1-t)^3} + \frac{2}{(1-t)^2(1+t)} + \frac{1}{(1-t)(1+t)^2} \right] = \frac{1}{(1-t)(1-t^2)^2}.$$
::: {.proof}
Molien's formula $M_G(t) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \frac{1}{\det(1 - tg)}$, using the eigenvalues of <1>1.
:::

**Part (b).**

<1>1. The invariants of $\tilde{G}$ are generated by $y_1^2, y_2^2, y_3^2$ (primary invariants) and $y_1 y_2 y_3$ (secondary invariant).
::: {.proof}
each $g_i$ acts by sign changes, so the monomials fixed by $\tilde{G}$ are exactly those where each variable appears to an even power, together with $y_1 y_2 y_3$ (which is fixed since each $g_i$ flips an even number of signs).
:::

<1>2. A Hironaka decomposition is
$$k[y_1, y_2, y_3]^{\tilde{G}} = k[y_1^2, y_2^2, y_3^2] \oplus y_1 y_2 y_3 \cdot k[y_1^2, y_2^2, y_3^2].$$
::: {.proof}
the primary invariants $y_1^2, y_2^2, y_3^2$ form a polynomial subring, and the invariant ring is a free module over it with basis $\{1, y_1 y_2 y_3\}$.
:::

**Part (c).**

<1>1. $k[y_1, y_2, y_3]^{\tilde{G}} \cong k[a, b, c, d]/(d^2 - abc)$, where $a = y_1^2$, $b = y_2^2$, $c = y_3^2$, $d = y_1 y_2 y_3$.
::: {.proof}
the generators $a, b, c, d$ satisfy the single relation $d^2 = y_1^2 y_2^2 y_3^2 = abc$, and this is the only relation (the Hironaka decomposition of <1>2 shows the invariant ring is a hypersurface).
:::

<1>2. $k[x_1, x_2, x_3]^G$ is a polynomial ring in three generators of degrees $1, 2, 2$.
::: {.proof}
the Molien series $\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-t^2)^2}$ of part (a) is the Hilbert series of a polynomial ring with generators of degrees $1, 2, 2$.
:::

<1>3. Hence $k[x_1, x_2, x_3]^G \cong k[u, v, w]$ with $\deg u = 1$, $\deg v = \deg w = 2$, and no relations.
::: {.proof}
<1>2; a polynomial ring has a trivial presentation.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 (a), <1>2 (b), and <1>1, <1>3 (c).
:::
