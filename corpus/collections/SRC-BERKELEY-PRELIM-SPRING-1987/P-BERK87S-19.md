::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a field. Prove that every finite subgroup of the multiplicative group $F^\times$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G\leq F^\times$ be finite. Choose
$$
g\in G
$$
whose order
$$
m\coloneqq\operatorname{ord}(g)
$$
is maximal among the orders of elements of $G$.

<1>1. If two commuting elements $x,y$ have finite coprime orders $r,s$,
then
$$
\operatorname{ord}(xy)=rs.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $x$ and $y$ commute,
$$
(xy)^{rs}=x^{rs}y^{rs}=1.
$$
If $(xy)^k=1$, then
$$
x^k=y^{-k}.
$$
Raising to the $s$th power gives
$$
x^{ks}=1,
$$
so $r\mid ks$. Since $\gcd(r,s)=1$, one has $r\mid k$.
Similarly, raising $x^k=y^{-k}$ to the $r$th power gives $s\mid k$.
Thus $rs\mid k$. Therefore the least positive such $k$ is $rs$.
:::

<1>2. Every $h\in G$ has order dividing $m$.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
n\coloneqq\operatorname{ord}(h).
$$
Suppose, for contradiction, that $n\nmid m$. Then there is a prime $p$
such that
$$
p^a\mid m,\quad p^{a+1}\nmid m,
$$
and
$$
p^b\mid n,\quad p^{b+1}\nmid n
$$
with $b>a$.

Write
$$
m=p^a r,
\qquad
n=p^b s,
$$
where $p\nmid r,s$. The element
$$
x\coloneqq g^{p^a}
$$
has order $r$, while
$$
y\coloneqq h^s
$$
has order $p^b$. Their orders are coprime. Since $G\subset F^\times$ is
abelian, step <1>1 gives
$$
\operatorname{ord}(xy)
=
rp^b.
$$
Because $b>a$,
$$
rp^b>rp^a=m,
$$
contradicting the maximality of $m$. Therefore $n\mid m$.
:::

<1>3. Every element of $G$ is a root of the polynomial
$$
X^m-1\in F[X].
$$

::: {.proof}
For $h\in G$, step <1>2 gives
$$
\operatorname{ord}(h)\mid m.
$$
Hence
$$
h^m=1,
$$
so $h$ is a root of $X^m-1$.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\abs{G}\leq m.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, all distinct elements of $G$ are roots of the nonzero
degree-$m$ polynomial $X^m-1$. A nonzero polynomial of degree $m$ over a
field has at most $m$ roots.
:::

<1>5. One also has
$$
\abs{G}\geq m.
$$

::: {.proof}
The cyclic subgroup
$$
\langle g\rangle
$$
has exactly $m$ elements and is contained in $G$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{G=\langle g\rangle}
$$
and $G$ is cyclic.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4 and <1>5 give
$$
\abs{G}=m.
$$
But $\langle g\rangle\subseteq G$ already has $m$ elements, so the two
finite sets are equal.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 proves the assertion for an arbitrary finite subgroup
$G\leq F^\times$.
:::
