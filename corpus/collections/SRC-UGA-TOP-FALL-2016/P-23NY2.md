::: problem
Let $\Sigma$ be a closed orientable surface of genus $g$.
Compute $H_i(S^1 \times \Sigma; \ZZ)$ for $i = 0, 1, 2, 3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The integral homology of the closed orientable genus-$g$ surface is
\[
H_i(\Sigma;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ, & i=0,\\
\ZZ^{2g}, & i=1,\\
\ZZ, & i=2,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the standard CW structure on $\Sigma$ with one $0$-cell, $2g$ $1$-cells
\[
a_1,b_1,\dots,a_g,b_g,
\]
and one $2$-cell attached by the word
\[
[a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_g,b_g].
\]
The cellular differential
\[
d_1:C_1\to C_0
\]
is zero because every $1$-cell is a loop at the unique vertex.
The differential
\[
d_2:C_2\to C_1
\]
is also zero because the total exponent of every $a_i$ and $b_i$ in the attaching word is zero.
Thus the cellular chain complex is
\[
0\longrightarrow\ZZ
\xrightarrow{0}
\ZZ^{2g}
\xrightarrow{0}
\ZZ
\longrightarrow0,
\]
which gives the displayed homology groups.
:::

<1>2. The homology of $S^1$ is
\[
H_i(S^1;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ, & i=0,1,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the CW structure on $S^1$ with one $0$-cell and one $1$-cell.
Its cellular differential is zero.
:::

<1>3. The integral Künneth theorem gives
\[
H_n(S^1\times\Sigma;\ZZ)
\cong
\bigoplus_{p+q=n}
H_p(S^1;\ZZ)\otimes H_q(\Sigma;\ZZ).
\]
::: {.proof}
In the integral Künneth short exact sequence, the remaining terms are
\[
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\ZZ}
\bigl(H_p(S^1;\ZZ),H_q(\Sigma;\ZZ)\bigr).
\]
By <1>1 and <1>2, every nonzero homology group of either factor is free abelian.
Hence all Tor terms vanish, leaving the displayed direct sum of tensor products.
:::

<1>4. In degree $0$,
\[
\boxed{H_0(S^1\times\Sigma;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.}
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>3,
\[
H_0(S^1\times\Sigma)
\cong
H_0(S^1)\otimes H_0(\Sigma)
\cong
\ZZ\otimes\ZZ
\cong\ZZ.
\]
:::

<1>5. In degree $1$,
\[
\boxed{H_1(S^1\times\Sigma;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^{2g+1}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
The Künneth decomposition is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_1(S^1\times\Sigma)
&\cong
\bigl(H_1(S^1)\otimes H_0(\Sigma)\bigr)
\oplus
\bigl(H_0(S^1)\otimes H_1(\Sigma)\bigr)\\
&\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ^{2g}
\cong\ZZ^{2g+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>6. In degree $2$,
\[
\boxed{H_2(S^1\times\Sigma;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^{2g+1}.}
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $H_2(S^1)=0$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_2(S^1\times\Sigma)
&\cong
\bigl(H_1(S^1)\otimes H_1(\Sigma)\bigr)
\oplus
\bigl(H_0(S^1)\otimes H_2(\Sigma)\bigr)\\
&\cong
\ZZ^{2g}\oplus\ZZ
\cong\ZZ^{2g+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>7. In degree $3$,
\[
\boxed{H_3(S^1\times\Sigma;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.}
\]
::: {.proof}
The only nonzero tensor product with total degree $3$ is
\[
H_1(S^1)\otimes H_2(\Sigma)
\cong
\ZZ\otimes\ZZ
\cong\ZZ.
\]
:::

<1>8. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_i(S^1\times\Sigma;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ, & i=0,3,\\
\ZZ^{2g+1}, & i=1,2,\\
0, & i>3.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4--<1>7 give the requested degrees.
There are no tensor or Tor contributions above degree $3$ because the factors have homology only in degrees at most $1$ and $2$, respectively.
:::
