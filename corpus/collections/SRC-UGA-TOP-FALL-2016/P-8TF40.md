::: problem
Show that if $p:X\to\CP^n$ is a covering space map, then $X$ is homeomorphic to $\CP^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. As printed, the statement is false if covering spaces are allowed to be disconnected.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
X=\CP^n\sqcup\CP^n
\]
and define
\[
p:X\longrightarrow\CP^n
\]
to be the identity map on each component.
Every sufficiently small open set $U\subseteq\CP^n$ has inverse image
\[
p^{-1}(U)=U\sqcup U,
\]
with each copy mapped homeomorphically onto $U$.
Thus $p$ is a two-sheeted covering map.

But $X$ is disconnected whereas $\CP^n$ is connected, so
\[
X\not\cong\CP^n.
\]
Hence a connectedness hypothesis on $X$ is necessary for the intended conclusion.
:::

<1>2. The space $\CP^n$ is simply connected for every $n\ge0$.
::: {.proof}
For $n=0$, $\CP^0$ is a point.

For $n\ge1$, complex projective space has the standard CW decomposition
\[
\CP^n=e^0\cup e^2\cup e^4\cup\cdots\cup e^{2n}.
\]
In particular its $1$-skeleton is a point.
By cellular approximation, every loop
\[
S^1\longrightarrow\CP^n
\]
is homotopic to a cellular map and therefore to a map into the $1$-skeleton.
Such a map is constant.
Hence
\[
\pi_1(\CP^n)=0.
\]
:::

<1>3. Assume now that $X$ is connected and that
\[
p:X\longrightarrow\CP^n
\]
is a covering map.
Then every fiber of $p$ contains exactly one point.
::: {.proof}
The base $\CP^n$ is locally path-connected, so the covering space $X$ is locally path-connected as well.
Since $X$ is connected, it is therefore path-connected.

Fix $y\in\CP^n$ and suppose
\[
x_0,x_1\in p^{-1}(y).
\]
Choose a path
\[
\widetilde\gamma:[0,1]\longrightarrow X
\]
from $x_0$ to $x_1$.
Then
\[
\gamma=p\circ\widetilde\gamma
\]
is a loop at $y$.
By <1>2, $\gamma$ is homotopic relative endpoints to the constant loop at $y$.

Lift such a homotopy through the covering, starting at $x_0$.
Homotopy lifting implies that the endpoints of the lifts of the two endpoint-homotopic paths agree.
The lift of the constant loop beginning at $x_0$ is constant and ends at $x_0$, while $\widetilde\gamma$ ends at $x_1$.
Therefore
\[
x_1=x_0.
\]
Thus every fiber has at most one point; by surjectivity of a covering map, every fiber has exactly one point.
:::

<1>4. Under the intended hypothesis that $X$ is connected, the covering map $p$ is a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
By <1>3, $p$ is bijective.
A covering map is a local homeomorphism.
A bijective local homeomorphism is an open continuous bijection, hence a homeomorphism.
Therefore
\[
\boxed{X\cong\CP^n.}
\]
:::

<1>5. Consequently, the printed problem becomes correct after adding the hypothesis that $X$ is connected.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives a counterexample to the source wording, while <1>2--<1>4 prove the intended connected version.
:::
:::
