::: {.problem}
Let $G = U(32)$ and $H = \{1, 31\}$. The group $G / H$ is isomorphic to one of $\ZZ_{8}$, $\ZZ_{4} \oplus \ZZ_{2}$, or $\ZZ_{2} \oplus \ZZ_{2} \oplus \ZZ_{2}$. Determine which one by elimination.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
G=U(32)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H=\{1,31\}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The quotient $G/H$ has order $8$.


::: pf-proof

Euler's totient formula gives
$$
\cardinality{U(32)}
=
\varphi(32)
=
32-16
=
16.
$$
The subgroup $H$ has order $2$, since
$$
31^2\equiv1\pmod{32}
$$
and $31\not\equiv1\pmod{32}$. Because $G$ is abelian, $H$ is normal,
and therefore
$$
\cardinality{G/H}=\frac{16}{2}=8.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The coset $3H$ has order $8$ in $G/H$.


::: pf-proof

Modulo $32$,
$$
3^4=81\equiv17
$$
and
$$
3^8\equiv17^2=289\equiv1.
$$
Thus
$$
(3H)^8=H.
$$
However,
$$
(3H)^4=17H\neq H,
$$
because $17\notin\{1,31\}=H$.

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and Lagrange's theorem, the order of $3H$ divides $8$.
Since its fourth power is nontrivial, its order does not divide $4$.
Hence its order is $8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The quotient is cyclic:
$$
\boxed{G/H\cong\ZZ_8}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives an element of order $8$ in the group $G/H$, which has
order $8$ by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Therefore $3H$ generates $G/H$, so the quotient
is cyclic of order $8$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} selects $\ZZ_8$ from the three listed possibilities.

:::
