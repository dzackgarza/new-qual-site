::: {.problem}
Let G = U(16), H = {1, 15} and $K = \{ 1 , 9 \}$ . Are H and K isomorphic?
Are G/H and $G / K$ isomorphic?
:::

Let
$$
G=U(16),
\qquad
H=\{1,15\},
\qquad
K=\{1,9\}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The subgroups $H$ and $K$ are isomorphic:
$$
\boxed{H\cong K\cong\mathbb Z_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both sets contain the identity and one nonidentity element whose square
is the identity:
$$
15^2\equiv1\pmod{16},
\qquad
9^2\equiv1\pmod{16}.
$$
Thus both $H$ and $K$ are cyclic groups of order $2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The quotient $G/H$ is cyclic of order $4$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
|U(16)|=\varphi(16)=8
$$
and $|H|=2$, the quotient has order $4$. Consider $3H$. One has
$$
3^2\equiv9\notin H\pmod{16},
$$
so $(3H)^2\neq H$, while
$$
3^4=81\equiv1\pmod{16},
$$
so $(3H)^4=H$. Hence $3H$ has order $4$ and
$$
G/H\cong\mathbb Z_4.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every nonidentity element of $G/K$ has order $2$.


::: pf-proof

Let $u\in U(16)$. Since $u$ is odd, write $u=2m+1$. Then
$$
u^2
=
4m(m+1)+1.
$$
The integer $m(m+1)$ is even, so $4m(m+1)$ is congruent to either
$0$ or $8$ modulo $16$. Therefore
$$
u^2\equiv1\ \text{or}\ 9\pmod{16},
$$
which means $u^2\in K$. Hence
$$
(uK)^2=K
$$
for every $uK\in G/K$.

Also $|G/K|=8/2=4$. Thus $G/K$ is the noncyclic group of order $4$,
namely
$$
G/K\cong\mathbb Z_2\oplus\mathbb Z_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The quotient groups are not isomorphic:
$$
\boxed{G/H\not\cong G/K}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>2, $G/H$ contains an element of order $4$. By step <1>3,
every nonidentity element of $G/K$ has order $2$. An isomorphism
preserves element orders, so the quotients cannot be isomorphic.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>1 answers the first question, and step <1>4 answers the second.

:::

:::
