::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite group and let $H$ be a normal subgroup of $G$. Prove that the order of the element $gH$ in $G/H$ must divide the order of $g$ in $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
m=\operatorname{ord}_G(g)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
d=\operatorname{ord}_{G/H}(gH).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The coset $gH$ satisfies
$$
(gH)^m=H.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition of $m$,
$$
g^m=e.
$$
Therefore, in the quotient group,
$$
(gH)^m=g^mH=eH=H.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If an element $x$ has finite order $d$ and $x^m=e$, then
$d\mid m$.


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
m=qd+r,
\qquad
0\leq r<d.
$$
Then
$$
e=x^m=(x^d)^q x^r=x^r.
$$
By minimality of the positive integer $d=\operatorname{ord}(x)$, the
only $r$ with $0\leq r<d$ and $x^r=e$ is $r=0$. Hence $d\mid m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The order of $gH$ divides the order of $g$:
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{ord}_{G/H}(gH)
\mid
\operatorname{ord}_G(g)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} to the element $x=gH$. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$x^m=H$, the identity of $G/H$. Therefore $d\mid m$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the required divisibility statement.

:::
