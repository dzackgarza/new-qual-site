::: {.problem}
Let G be a finite group and let H be a normal subgroup of G. Prove that the order of the element gH in $G / H$ must divide the order of g in G.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
m=\operatorname{ord}_G(g)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
d=\operatorname{ord}_{G/H}(gH).
$$

<1>1. The coset $gH$ satisfies
$$
(gH)^m=H.
$$

::: {.proof}
By definition of $m$,
$$
g^m=e.
$$
Therefore, in the quotient group,
$$
(gH)^m=g^mH=eH=H.
$$
:::

<1>2. If an element $x$ has finite order $d$ and $x^m=e$, then
$d\mid m$.

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
m=qd+r,
\qquad
0\leq r<d.
$$
Then
$$
e=x^m=(x^d)^q x^r=x^r.
$$
By minimality of the positive integer $d=\operatorname{ord}(x)$, the
only $r$ with $0\leq r<d$ and $x^r=e$ is $r=0$. Hence $d\mid m$.
:::

<1>3. The order of $gH$ divides the order of $g$:
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{ord}_{G/H}(gH)
\mid
\operatorname{ord}_G(g)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>2 to the element $x=gH$. Step <1>1 gives
$x^m=H$, the identity of $G/H$. Therefore $d\mid m$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the required divisibility statement.
:::
:::
