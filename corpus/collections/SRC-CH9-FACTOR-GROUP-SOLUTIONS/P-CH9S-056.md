::: {.problem}
Show that the intersection of two normal subgroups of G is a normal subgroup of G. Generalize.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $H\trianglelefteq G$ and $K\trianglelefteq G$, then $H\cap K$ is a subgroup of $G$.

::: {.proof}
The identity belongs to both $H$ and $K$, hence to $H\cap K$. If
$x,y\in H\cap K$, then $x,y\in H$ and $x,y\in K$. Since both $H$ and
$K$ are subgroups,
$$
xy^{-1}\in H
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
xy^{-1}\in K.
$$
Thus $xy^{-1}\in H\cap K$, so the subgroup criterion gives
$H\cap K\leq G$.
:::

<1>2. The subgroup $H\cap K$ is normal in $G$.

::: {.proof}
Let $g\in G$ and $x\in H\cap K$. Since $H\trianglelefteq G$ and
$K\trianglelefteq G$,
$$
gxg^{-1}\in H
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
gxg^{-1}\in K.
$$
Hence $gxg^{-1}\in H\cap K$. Therefore
$g(H\cap K)g^{-1}\subseteq H\cap K$ for every $g\in G$. Applying the
same inclusion to $g^{-1}$ gives the reverse inclusion, so
$$
g(H\cap K)g^{-1}=H\cap K
$$
for every $g\in G$. Thus $H\cap K\trianglelefteq G$.
:::

<1>3. More generally, the intersection of any family of normal subgroups of $G$ is normal in $G$.

::: {.proof}
Let $\{N_i\}_{i\in I}$ be a family with $N_i\trianglelefteq G$ for
every $i$, and put
$$
N=\bigcap_{i\in I}N_i.
$$
If $I$ is empty, then by convention $N=G$, which is normal in itself.
Assume $I$ is nonempty. The identity lies in every $N_i$, and if
$x,y\in N$, then $x,y\in N_i$ for every $i$, so
$xy^{-1}\in N_i$ for every $i$. Hence $xy^{-1}\in N$, and $N\leq G$.

For $g\in G$ and $x\in N$, one has $x\in N_i$ for every $i$. Since
each $N_i$ is normal,
$$
gxg^{-1}\in N_i
$$
for every $i$, hence $gxg^{-1}\in N$. Therefore $gNg^{-1}\subseteq N$
for every $g$, and applying this to $g^{-1}$ gives equality. Thus
$N\trianglelefteq G$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves the two-subgroup case, and step <1>3 gives the stated
generalization.
:::
