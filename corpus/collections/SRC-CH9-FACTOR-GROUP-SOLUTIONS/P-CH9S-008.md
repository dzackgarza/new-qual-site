::: {.problem}
Viewing $< 3 >$ and $< 1 2 >$ as subgroups of $\ZZ ,$ prove that $< 3 > / < 1 2 >$ is isomorphic to $\ZZ _ { 4 }$ . Similarly, prove that $< 8 > / < 4 8 >$ is isomorphic to $Z _ { 6 }$ Generalize to arbitrary integers k and n.
:::

::: {.solution}

For an integer $m$, write
$$
\langle m\rangle=m\ZZ
$$
as a subgroup of the additive group $\ZZ$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

There is an isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
\langle3\rangle/\langle12\rangle\cong\ZZ_4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
\phi_4:\langle3\rangle/\langle12\rangle\longrightarrow\ZZ_4,
\qquad
3r+\langle12\rangle\longmapsto [r]_4.
$$
This is well-defined because
$$
3r+\langle12\rangle=3s+\langle12\rangle
$$
if and only if
$$
3(r-s)\in12\ZZ,
$$
which is equivalent to $r-s\in4\ZZ$, hence to
$[r]_4=[s]_4$. The map is a homomorphism, is surjective because every
$[r]_4$ has preimage $3r+\langle12\rangle$, and is injective by the
same equivalence. Thus it is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There is an isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
\langle8\rangle/\langle48\rangle\cong\ZZ_6}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
\phi_6:\langle8\rangle/\langle48\rangle\longrightarrow\ZZ_6,
\qquad
8r+\langle48\rangle\longmapsto[r]_6.
$$
Exactly as in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
8r+\langle48\rangle=8s+\langle48\rangle
$$
if and only if
$$
8(r-s)\in48\ZZ,
$$
equivalently $r-s\in6\ZZ$. Hence $\phi_6$ is well-defined and
bijective, and it is plainly a homomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

More generally, if $k,n\in\ZZ\setminus\{0\}$ and
$k\mid n$, then
$$
\boxed{
\langle k\rangle/\langle n\rangle
\cong
\ZZ_{\abs{n/k}}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
n=kq,
\qquad q\in\ZZ\setminus\{0\}.
$$
Since $n\ZZ\subseteq k\ZZ$, the quotient is defined.
Set
$$
\Phi:k\ZZ/n\ZZ\longrightarrow\ZZ_{\abs{q}},
\qquad
kr+n\ZZ\longmapsto[r]_{\abs{q}}.
$$
For integers $r,s$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
kr+n\ZZ=ks+n\ZZ
&\iff k(r-s)\in kq\ZZ \\
&\iff r-s\in q\ZZ \\
&\iff [r]_{\abs{q}}=[s]_{\abs{q}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $\Phi$ is well-defined and injective. It is a homomorphism, and
every class $[r]_{\abs{q}}$ is the image of $kr+n\ZZ$, so it is
surjective. Therefore $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Equivalently, for $k\neq0$ and every positive integer $m$,
$$
\langle k\rangle/\langle km\rangle\cong\ZZ_m.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} with $n=km$. Then
$\abs{n/k}=m$.

:::
