::: {.problem}
Viewing $< 3 >$ and $< 1 2 >$ as subgroups of $\mathbb { Z } ,$ prove that $< 3 > / < 1 2 >$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb { Z } _ { 4 }$ . Similarly, prove that $< 8 > / < 4 8 >$ is isomorphic to $Z _ { 6 }$ Generalize to arbitrary integers k and n.
:::

::: {.solution}
For an integer $m$, write
$$
\langle m\rangle=m\mathbb Z
$$
as a subgroup of the additive group $\mathbb Z$.

<1>1. There is an isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
\langle3\rangle/\langle12\rangle\cong\mathbb Z_4}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
\phi_4:\langle3\rangle/\langle12\rangle\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_4,
\qquad
3r+\langle12\rangle\longmapsto [r]_4.
$$
This is well-defined because
$$
3r+\langle12\rangle=3s+\langle12\rangle
$$
if and only if
$$
3(r-s)\in12\mathbb Z,
$$
which is equivalent to $r-s\in4\mathbb Z$, hence to
$[r]_4=[s]_4$. The map is a homomorphism, is surjective because every
$[r]_4$ has preimage $3r+\langle12\rangle$, and is injective by the
same equivalence. Thus it is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>2. There is an isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
\langle8\rangle/\langle48\rangle\cong\mathbb Z_6}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
\phi_6:\langle8\rangle/\langle48\rangle\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_6,
\qquad
8r+\langle48\rangle\longmapsto[r]_6.
$$
Exactly as in step <1>1,
$$
8r+\langle48\rangle=8s+\langle48\rangle
$$
if and only if
$$
8(r-s)\in48\mathbb Z,
$$
equivalently $r-s\in6\mathbb Z$. Hence $\phi_6$ is well-defined and
bijective, and it is plainly a homomorphism.
:::

<1>3. More generally, if $k,n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$ and
$k\mid n$, then
$$
\boxed{
\langle k\rangle/\langle n\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z_{|n/k|}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Write
$$
n=kq,
\qquad q\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.
$$
Since $n\mathbb Z\subseteq k\mathbb Z$, the quotient is defined.
Set
$$
\Phi:k\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_{|q|},
\qquad
kr+n\mathbb Z\longmapsto[r]_{|q|}.
$$
For integers $r,s$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
kr+n\mathbb Z=ks+n\mathbb Z
&\iff k(r-s)\in kq\mathbb Z \\
&\iff r-s\in q\mathbb Z \\
&\iff [r]_{|q|}=[s]_{|q|}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $\Phi$ is well-defined and injective. It is a homomorphism, and
every class $[r]_{|q|}$ is the image of $kr+n\mathbb Z$, so it is
surjective. Therefore $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>4. Equivalently, for $k\neq0$ and every positive integer $m$,
$$
\langle k\rangle/\langle km\rangle\cong\mathbb Z_m.
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>3 with $n=km$. Then
$|n/k|=m$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 prove the two requested examples, while steps
<1>3--<1>4 give the general form.
:::
