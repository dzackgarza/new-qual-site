::: {.problem}
Let $\phi$ be an isomorphism from a group $G$ onto a group $\bar{G}$. Prove that if $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$, then $\phi(H)$ is a normal subgroup of $\bar{G}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The set $\phi(H)$ is a subgroup of $\bar G$.


::: pf-proof

Since $H$ is a subgroup of $G$, it contains the identity, and hence
$\phi(H)$ contains the identity of $\bar G$. If $\phi(h_1),\phi(h_2)\in
\phi(H)$, where $h_1,h_2\in H$, then
$$
\phi(h_1)\phi(h_2)^{-1}=\phi(h_1h_2^{-1})\in\phi(H),
$$
because $h_1h_2^{-1}\in H$. Thus the subgroup criterion gives
$\phi(H)\leq \bar G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $y\in\bar G$,
$$
y\phi(H)y^{-1}=\phi(H).
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $\phi$ is onto, there is an $x\in G$ such that $y=\phi(x)$.
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
y\phi(H)y^{-1}
&=\phi(x)\phi(H)\phi(x)^{-1}\\
&=\phi(xHx^{-1})\\
&=\phi(H),
\end{aligned}
$$
where the last equality follows from $H\trianglelefteq G$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that $\phi(H)$ is a subgroup of $\bar G$, and step
[](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that it is invariant under conjugation by every element of
$\bar G$. Hence $\phi(H)\trianglelefteq\bar G$.

:::
