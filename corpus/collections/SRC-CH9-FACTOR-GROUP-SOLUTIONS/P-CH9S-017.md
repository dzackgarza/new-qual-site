::: {.problem}
Let $G = \ZZ/\langle 20 \rangle$ and $H = \langle 4 \rangle/\langle 20 \rangle$. List the elements of $H$ and $G/H$.
:::

::: {.solution}

For $a\in\ZZ$, write
$$
[a]=a+\langle20\rangle\in G.
$$
Then $H=\langle[4]\rangle$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The elements of $H$ are
$$
\boxed{
H=\{[0],[4],[8],[12],[16]\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Successive multiples of $[4]$ are
$$
[0],[4],[8],[12],[16],[20]=[0].
$$
The first five classes are distinct modulo $20$, so these are exactly
the elements generated by $[4]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The four cosets of $H$ in $G$ are
$$
\boxed{
G/H=\{H,\,[1]+H,\,[2]+H,\,[3]+H\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every integer $a$ has a unique residue
$$
r\in\{0,1,2,3\}
$$
modulo $4$. Thus $a-r$ is divisible by $4$, so
$$
[a]-[r]\in H
$$
and hence
$$
[a]+H=[r]+H.
$$
Therefore the four displayed cosets exhaust $G/H$.

They are distinct: if $0\leq r<s\leq3$ and
$[r]+H=[s]+H$, then $[s-r]\in H$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, every representative
of an element of $H$ is divisible by $4$ modulo $20$, whereas
$s-r\in\{1,2,3\}$, a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Explicitly, the nontrivial cosets are
$$
\begin{aligned}
[1]+H&=\{[1],[5],[9],[13],[17]\},\\
[2]+H&=\{[2],[6],[10],[14],[18]\},\\
[3]+H&=\{[3],[7],[11],[15],[19]\}.
\end{aligned}
$$


::: pf-proof

Add $[1]$, $[2]$, and $[3]$, respectively, to each element of the list
for $H$ in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} lists $H$, while steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} list all elements of the
factor group $G/H$.

:::
