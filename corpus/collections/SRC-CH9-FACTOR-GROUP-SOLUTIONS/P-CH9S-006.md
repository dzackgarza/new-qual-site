::: {.problem}
Let $H = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & d \end{bmatrix} \,\middle|\, a, b, d \in \RR,\ ad \neq 0 \right\}$. Is $H$ a normal subgroup of $GL(2, \RR)$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The set $H$ is a subgroup of $GL(2,\RR)$.


::: pf-proof

The identity matrix lies in $H$. If
$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
a&b\\
0&d
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
B=
\begin{bmatrix}
e&f\\
0&h
\end{bmatrix}
\in H,
$$
then
$$
AB=
\begin{bmatrix}
ae&af+bh\\
0&dh
\end{bmatrix}
\in H,
$$
because $aedh\neq0$. Moreover,
$$
A^{-1}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
a^{-1}&-a^{-1}bd^{-1}\\
0&d^{-1}
\end{bmatrix}
\in H.
$$
Thus $H\leq GL(2,\RR)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The subgroup $H$ is not normal in $GL(2,\RR)$:
$$
\boxed{\text{$H$ is not a normal subgroup of $GL(2,\RR)$.}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&1\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}
\in H
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
P=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{bmatrix}
\in GL(2,\RR).
$$
Since $P^{-1}=P$,
$$
PAP^{-1}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
1&1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
This conjugate does not lie in $H$, because its lower-left entry is
$1$ rather than $0$. Hence $PHP^{-1}\nsubseteq H$, so $H$ is not
normal.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} verifies that $H$ is a subgroup, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives an
explicit conjugate of an element of $H$ that leaves $H$.

:::
