::: {.problem}
Recall that a subgroup $N$ of a group $G$ is called characteristic if $\phi(N) = N$ for all automorphisms $\phi$ of $G$. If $N$ is a characteristic subgroup of $G$, show that $N$ is a normal subgroup of $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $g\in G$, conjugation by $g$ defines an automorphism
$$
c_g:G\longrightarrow G,
\qquad
c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $x,y\in G$,
$$
c_g(xy)=gxyg^{-1}=(gxg^{-1})(gyg^{-1})=c_g(x)c_g(y),
$$
so $c_g$ is a homomorphism. Its inverse is $c_{g^{-1}}$, because
$$
c_{g^{-1}}(c_g(x))=x=c_g(c_{g^{-1}}(x))
$$
for every $x\in G$. Hence $c_g$ is an automorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $g\in G$,
$$
gNg^{-1}=N.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $N$ is characteristic, every automorphism of $G$ carries $N$
to itself. Applying this to the automorphism $c_g$ from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
c_g(N)=N.
$$
By definition of $c_g$, this equality is exactly
$gNg^{-1}=N$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} is the conjugation criterion for $N\trianglelefteq G$.

:::
