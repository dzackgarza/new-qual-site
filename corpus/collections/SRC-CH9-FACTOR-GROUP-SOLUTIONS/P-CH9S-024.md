::: {.problem}
The group $(\ZZ_{4} \oplus \ZZ_{12}) / \langle (2, 2) \rangle$ is isomorphic to one of $\ZZ_{8}$, $\ZZ_{4} \oplus \ZZ_{2}$, or $\ZZ_{2} \oplus \ZZ_{2} \oplus \ZZ_{2}$. Determine which one by elimination.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
A=\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_{12},
\qquad
H=\langle(2,2)\rangle,
\qquad
Q=A/H.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The quotient $Q$ has order $8$.


::: pf-proof

The element $2\in\ZZ_4$ has order $2$, while
$2\in\ZZ_{12}$ has order $6$. Hence
$$
\operatorname{ord}(2,2)=\operatorname{lcm}(2,6)=6,
$$
so $\cardinality{H}=6$. Since
$$
\cardinality{A}=4\cdot12=48,
$$
Lagrange's theorem gives
$$
\cardinality{Q}=\frac{48}{6}=8.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The group $Q$ has no element of order $8$.


::: pf-proof

Every $x\in A$ satisfies
$$
12x=0,
$$
because the exponent of
$\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_{12}$ divides $12$. Hence every coset
$x+H\in Q$ satisfies
$$
12(x+H)=H,
$$
so its order divides $12$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and Lagrange's theorem, its
order also divides $8$. Therefore every element order in $Q$ divides
$$
\gcd(12,8)=4.
$$
In particular, $Q$ has no element of order $8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The group $Q$ has an element of order $4$.


::: pf-proof

Consider
$$
x=(0,1)+H.
$$
Since
$$
4(0,1)=(0,4)=2(2,2)\in H,
$$
one has $4x=H$. On the other hand,
$$
2(0,1)=(0,2)\notin H.
$$
Indeed, if $(0,2)=k(2,2)$, then
$$
2k\equiv0\pmod4
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2k\equiv2\pmod{12}.
$$
The first congruence forces $k$ even, whereas the second forces
$k\equiv1\pmod6$, hence $k$ odd, a contradiction. Thus $2x\neq H$,
so $x$ has order $4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Among the three listed possibilities,
$$
\boxed{
Q\cong\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group $\ZZ_8$ contains an element of order $8$, while step
[](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that $Q$ does not. Thus $Q\not\cong\ZZ_8$.

Every nonidentity element of
$\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2\oplus\ZZ_2$ has order $2$, whereas
step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives an element of order $4$ in $Q$. Thus $Q$ is not
isomorphic to that group either. The only remaining listed possibility
is $\ZZ_4\oplus\ZZ_2$.

:::
