::: {.problem}
Determine the order of $( \ZZ \oplus \ZZ ) / < ( 4 , 2 ) >$ . Is the group cyclic?
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
H=\langle(4,2)\rangle
\subseteq\ZZ\oplus\ZZ
$$
and write
$$
Q=(\ZZ\oplus\ZZ)/H.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The coset $(1,1)+H$ has infinite order.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
n\bigl((1,1)+H\bigr)=H
$$
for some integer $n$. Then
$$
(n,n)\in H,
$$
so there is an integer $k$ such that
$$
(n,n)=k(4,2)=(4k,2k).
$$
Hence
$$
n=4k
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n=2k.
$$
Subtracting gives $2k=0$, so $k=0$ and therefore $n=0$.
Thus no positive multiple of $(1,1)+H$ is the identity coset.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The quotient group has infinite order:
$$
\boxed{\cardinality{Q}=\infty}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $Q$ contains an element of infinite order. In particular,
$Q$ has infinitely many distinct elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The quotient $Q$ contains a nonidentity element of order $2$.


::: pf-proof

Consider
$$
y=(2,1)+H.
$$
It is nontrivial: if $(2,1)\in H$, then
$$
(2,1)=k(4,2)
$$
for some integer $k$, forcing simultaneously $k=1/2$, impossible.
But
$$
2y=(4,2)+H=H.
$$
Therefore $y$ has order $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The quotient group is not cyclic:
$$
\boxed{\text{$Q$ is not cyclic}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, a cyclic $Q$ would have to be infinite cyclic. Every
nonidentity element of an infinite cyclic group has infinite order,
whereas step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} exhibits a nonidentity element of order $2$.
Therefore $Q$ cannot be cyclic.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} determines the order of the quotient, and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} answers
the cyclicity question.

:::
