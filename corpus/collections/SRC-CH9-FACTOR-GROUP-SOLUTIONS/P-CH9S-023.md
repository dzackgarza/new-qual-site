::: {.problem}
Determine the order of $( \mathbb { Z } \oplus \mathbb { Z } ) / < ( 4 , 2 ) >$ . Is the group cyclic?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
H=\langle(4,2)\rangle
\subseteq\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z
$$
and write
$$
Q=(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z)/H.
$$

<1>1. The coset $(1,1)+H$ has infinite order.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
n\bigl((1,1)+H\bigr)=H
$$
for some integer $n$. Then
$$
(n,n)\in H,
$$
so there is an integer $k$ such that
$$
(n,n)=k(4,2)=(4k,2k).
$$
Hence
$$
n=4k
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n=2k.
$$
Subtracting gives $2k=0$, so $k=0$ and therefore $n=0$.
Thus no positive multiple of $(1,1)+H$ is the identity coset.
:::

<1>2. The quotient group has infinite order:
$$
\boxed{|Q|=\infty}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, $Q$ contains an element of infinite order. In particular,
$Q$ has infinitely many distinct elements.
:::

<1>3. The quotient $Q$ contains a nonidentity element of order $2$.

::: {.proof}
Consider
$$
y=(2,1)+H.
$$
It is nontrivial: if $(2,1)\in H$, then
$$
(2,1)=k(4,2)
$$
for some integer $k$, forcing simultaneously $k=1/2$, impossible.
But
$$
2y=(4,2)+H=H.
$$
Therefore $y$ has order $2$.
:::

<1>4. The quotient group is not cyclic:
$$
\boxed{\text{$Q$ is not cyclic}.}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, a cyclic $Q$ would have to be infinite cyclic. Every
nonidentity element of an infinite cyclic group has infinite order,
whereas step <1>3 exhibits a nonidentity element of order $2$.
Therefore $Q$ cannot be cyclic.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 determines the order of the quotient, and step <1>4 answers
the cyclicity question.
:::
:::
