::: {.problem}
Let H be a normal subgroup of a finite group G and let $x \in G$ . If $g c d ( \elementorder{ x } , \cardinality{ G / H } ) = 1$ , show that $x \in H$ .
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
m=\operatorname{ord}_G(x),
\qquad
n=\operatorname{ord}_{G/H}(xH).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The integer $n$ divides $m$.


::: pf-proof

Since $x$ has order $m$,
$$
x^m=e.
$$
Therefore
$$
(xH)^m=x^mH=H.
$$
The order of an element divides every positive exponent that gives the
identity, so $n\mid m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The integer $n$ divides $\cardinality{G/H}$.


::: pf-proof

The quotient $G/H$ is finite because $G$ is finite. By Lagrange's
theorem, the order $n$ of the element $xH\in G/H$ divides the order of
$G/H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has $n=1$.


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the positive integer $n$ divides both $m$ and
$\cardinality{G/H}$. The hypothesis
$$
\gcd(m,\cardinality{G/H})=1
$$
therefore forces $n=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The element $x$ lies in $H$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the coset $xH$ has order $1$ in $G/H$, so it is the
identity coset:
$$
xH=H.
$$
This is equivalent to $x\in H$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
