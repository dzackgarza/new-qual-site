::: {.problem}
Let $N$ be a normal subgroup of $G$ and let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$. If $N$ is a subgroup of $H$, prove that $H/N$ is a normal subgroup of $G/N$ if and only if $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $H\trianglelefteq G$, then $H/N\trianglelefteq G/N$.


::: pf-proof

Because $N\leq H$, the set $H/N$ is a subgroup of $G/N$. Let
$gN\in G/N$ and $hN\in H/N$. Then
$$
(gN)(hN)(gN)^{-1}=(ghg^{-1})N.
$$
Since $H\trianglelefteq G$, one has $ghg^{-1}\in H$, so
$(ghg^{-1})N\in H/N$. Thus $H/N$ is invariant under conjugation by
every element of $G/N$, and hence $H/N\trianglelefteq G/N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $H/N\trianglelefteq G/N$, then $H\trianglelefteq G$.


::: pf-proof

Assume $H/N\trianglelefteq G/N$. Let $g\in G$ and $h\in H$. Then
$$
(gN)(hN)(gN)^{-1}=(ghg^{-1})N\in H/N.
$$
Hence there is an $h_0\in H$ such that
$$
(ghg^{-1})N=h_0N.
$$
Therefore
$$
h_0^{-1}ghg^{-1}\in N\leq H,
$$
and since $h_0\in H$, it follows that $ghg^{-1}\in H$. Thus
$$
gHg^{-1}\subseteq H
$$
for every $g\in G$. Applying the same inclusion to $g^{-1}$ gives
$g^{-1}Hg\subseteq H$, and conjugating by $g$ yields
$H\subseteq gHg^{-1}$. Hence $gHg^{-1}=H$ for every $g\in G$, so
$H\trianglelefteq G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
H/N\trianglelefteq G/N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H\trianglelefteq G.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves the forward implication from normality of $H$ to
normality of $H/N$, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves the converse implication.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is exactly the required equivalence.

:::
