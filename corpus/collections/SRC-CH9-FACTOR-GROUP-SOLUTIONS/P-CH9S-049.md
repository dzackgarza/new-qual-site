::: {.problem}
Suppose that G is a non-Abelian group of order $p ^ { 3 }$ where p is prime and $Z ( G ) \neq \{ e \}$ . Prove that $\cardinality{ Z ( G ) } = p$ .
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The only possible orders of $Z(G)$ are $p$ and $p^2$.


::: pf-proof

Since $Z(G)$ is a subgroup of the finite group $G$, Lagrange's theorem
gives
$$
\cardinality{Z(G)}\mid \cardinality{G}=p^3.
$$
The hypothesis $Z(G)\neq\{e\}$ excludes order $1$. If
$\cardinality{Z(G)}=p^3$, then $Z(G)=G$, which would make $G$ Abelian, contrary to
the hypothesis. Thus $\cardinality{Z(G)}$ is either $p$ or $p^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $\cardinality{Z(G)}=p^2$, then $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic.


::: pf-proof

Under this assumption,
$$
\cardinality{G/Z(G)}=\frac{\cardinality{G}}{\cardinality{Z(G)}}=p.
$$
Every group of prime order is cyclic, so $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, then $G$ is Abelian.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
G/Z(G)=\langle gZ(G)\rangle.
$$
For arbitrary $x,y\in G$, there are integers $m,n$ and elements
$z_1,z_2\in Z(G)$ such that
$$
x=g^m z_1,
\qquad
y=g^n z_2.
$$
Since $z_1$ and $z_2$ commute with every element of $G$,
$$
xy=g^{m+n}z_1z_2=g^{n+m}z_2z_1=yx.
$$
Thus every pair of elements of $G$ commutes, so $G$ is Abelian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\cardinality{Z(G)}=\boxed{p}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $\cardinality{Z(G)}=p^2$, then step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} would make $G/Z(G)$ cyclic, and step
[](#s1-3){.pf-ref} would make $G$ Abelian, a contradiction. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} leaves only
$\cardinality{Z(G)}=p$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves the required order of the center.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark title="Variations"}
The hypothesis $Z(G)\neq\{e\}$ holds for every group of order $p^3$, by the class equation. The statement is often posed without it: a group of order $p^3$ is either abelian or has $\cardinality{Z(G)}=p$.
:::
