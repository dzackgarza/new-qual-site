::: {.problem}
Let $M,N$ be manifolds. Show that
$$
M\times N
$$
is orientable if and only if both $M$ and $N$ are orientable.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\dim M=m,
\qquad
\dim N=n.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $M$ and $N$ are orientable, then $M\times N$ is orientable.


::: pf-proof

Choose smooth orientations on $M$ and $N$.
At
$$
(p,q)\in M\times N,
$$
there is a canonical vector-space isomorphism
$$
T_{(p,q)}(M\times N)
\cong
T_pM
\oplus
T_qN.
$$

Declare an ordered basis
$$
(v_1,\ldots,v_m,w_1,\ldots,w_n)
$$
of
$$
T_pM\oplus T_qN
$$
to be positive when
$$
(v_1,\ldots,v_m)
$$
is positive in $T_pM$ and
$$
(w_1,\ldots,w_n)
$$
is positive in $T_qN$.

In oriented coordinate charts
$$
(x_1,\ldots,x_m)
$$
on $M$ and
$$
(y_1,\ldots,y_n)
$$
on $N$, the product chart
$$
(x_1,\ldots,x_m,y_1,\ldots,y_n)
$$
induces exactly this orientation.
Transition maps between product charts have Jacobian matrices of block-diagonal form
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
D\phi&0\\
0&D\psi
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose determinant is
$$
\det(D\phi)\det(D\psi)>0
$$
because both factors are orientation-preserving.
Thus these local orientations are compatible and define an orientation on
$$
M\times N.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Suppose $M\times N$ is orientable.
Fix a point
$$
q_0\in N.
$$
Then the restricted tangent bundle along the slice
$$
M\times\{q_0\}
$$
satisfies
$$
T(M\times N)|_{M\times\{q_0\}}
\cong
TM
\oplus
\underline{T_{q_0}N},
$$
where
$$
\underline{T_{q_0}N}
$$
is the trivial bundle over $M$ with fiber $T_{q_0}N$.


::: pf-proof

At a point
$$
(p,q_0),
$$
the canonical splitting is
$$
T_{(p,q_0)}(M\times N)
\cong
T_pM
\oplus
T_{q_0}N.
$$
As $p$ varies, the second summand is the same fixed vector space
$$
T_{q_0}N,
$$
so it forms a trivial bundle over $M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Choose once and for all an orientation of the vector space
$$
T_{q_0}N.
$$
Then the orientation of
$$
T(M\times N)|_{M\times\{q_0\}}
$$
induces an orientation of
$$
TM.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
(w_1,\ldots,w_n)
$$
be a fixed positively oriented basis of
$$
T_{q_0}N.
$$
At each
$$
p\in M,
$$
declare an ordered basis
$$
(v_1,\ldots,v_m)
$$
of
$$
T_pM
$$
to be positive exactly when
$$
(v_1,\ldots,v_m,w_1,\ldots,w_n)
$$
is positive in
$$
T_{(p,q_0)}(M\times N).
$$

Because the product orientation varies continuously and the chosen basis of
$$
T_{q_0}N
$$
is fixed, this defines a continuous orientation of $TM$.
Thus
$$
M
$$
is orientable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Similarly, if $M\times N$ is orientable, then $N$ is orientable.


::: pf-proof

Fix
$$
p_0\in M.
$$
Along the slice
$$
\{p_0\}\times N,
$$
one has
$$
T(M\times N)|_{\{p_0\}\times N}
\cong
\underline{T_{p_0}M}
\oplus
TN.
$$
Choose an orientation of the fixed vector space
$$
T_{p_0}M.
$$
Using the given orientation of the product bundle and the same direct-sum convention as in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives a continuous orientation of $TN$.
Hence $N$ is orientable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
M\times N
\text{ is orientable}
\iff
M\text{ and }N\text{ are both orientable}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves the forward construction from orientations of the two factors.
Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove that an orientation of the product induces orientations on each factor.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} is the required equivalence.

:::
