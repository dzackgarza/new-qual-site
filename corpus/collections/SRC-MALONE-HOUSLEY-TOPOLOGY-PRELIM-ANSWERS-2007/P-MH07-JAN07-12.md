::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a closed connected $5$-manifold such that
\[
\pi_1(M)\cong\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z.
\]
Assume
\[
H_2(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Compute all the other homology and cohomology groups of $M$ with integral coefficients.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

The manifold $M$ is orientable.


::: pf-proof

For a connected manifold, the orientation character is a homomorphism
$$
w_1:
\pi_1(M)
\to
\mathbb Z/2.
$$
But
$$
\pi_1(M)
\cong
\mathbb Z/7
$$
has no nontrivial homomorphism to
$$
\mathbb Z/2,
$$
because the image of an element of order $7$ must have order dividing both $7$ and $2$.
Hence the orientation character is trivial, so $M$ is orientable.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $M$ is connected,
$$
H_0(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard zeroth-homology computation for a connected space.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
H_1(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z/7.
$$


::: pf-proof

For any path-connected space,
$$
H_1(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\pi_1(M)^{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$
The group
$$
\mathbb Z/7
$$
is already abelian.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

By hypothesis,
$$
H_2(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the second assumption.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The universal coefficient theorem gives
$$
H^0(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
H_0(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z,
$$
one has
$$
H^0(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The universal coefficient theorem gives
$$
H^1(M;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The degree-$1$ exact sequence is
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}
\bigl(
H_0(M),\mathbb Z
\bigr)
\to
H^1(M;\mathbb Z)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}
\bigl(
H_1(M),\mathbb Z
\bigr)
\to
0.
$$
Now
$$
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)=0
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/7,\mathbb Z)=0.
$$
Hence
$$
H^1(M;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The universal coefficient theorem gives
$$
H^2(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/7.
$$


::: pf-proof

The degree-$2$ sequence is
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}
\bigl(
H_1(M),\mathbb Z
\bigr)
\to
H^2(M;\mathbb Z)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}
\bigl(
H_2(M),\mathbb Z
\bigr)
\to
0.
$$
By steps <1>3--<1>4,
$$
\operatorname{Ext}
\left(
\mathbb Z/7,\mathbb Z
\right)
\cong
\mathbb Z/7
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z
\right)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$
Thus
$$
0
\to
\mathbb Z/7
\to
H^2(M;\mathbb Z)
\to
\mathbb Z
\to
0.
$$
Because $\mathbb Z$ is free, this short exact sequence splits.
Hence
$$
H^2(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/7.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_5(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>1, $M$ is a closed orientable $5$-manifold.
Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_i(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H^{5-i}(M;\mathbb Z).
$$
For
$$
i=5,
$$
use step <1>5.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_4(M;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
H_4(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H^1(M;\mathbb Z),
$$
and step <1>6 gives the latter group as zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_3(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/7.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
H_3(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H^2(M;\mathbb Z).
$$
Apply step <1>7.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the integral homology groups are
$$
\boxed{
H_i(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,2,5,\\
\mathbb Z/7,&i=1,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/7,&i=3,\\
0,&i=4,\\
0,&i>5.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>2--<1>4 and <1>8--<1>10.
Homology vanishes above the dimension of $M$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H^3(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H_2(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

Set
$$
i=2
$$
in
$$
H_i(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H^{5-i}(M;\mathbb Z).
$$
Then
$$
H^3(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H_2(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H^4(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H_1(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z/7.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
i=1
$$
in Poincaré duality and apply step <1>3.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H^5(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H_0(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
i=0
$$
and apply step <1>2.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the integral cohomology groups are
$$
\boxed{
H^i(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,3,5,\\
0,&i=1,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/7,&i=2,\\
\mathbb Z/7,&i=4,\\
0,&i>5.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Use steps <1>5--<1>7 and <1>12--<1>14.
There is no cohomology above degree $5$ for a closed $5$-manifold.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>11 and <1>15 give all homology and cohomology groups requested.

:::
