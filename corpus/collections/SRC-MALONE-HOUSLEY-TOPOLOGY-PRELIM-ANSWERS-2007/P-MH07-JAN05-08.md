::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f:\RR^n\to\RR
\]
be smooth.
For
\[
a\in\RR^n,
\]
define
\[
f_a(x)=f(x)+x\cdot a.
\]
Show that for almost every
\[
a\in\RR^n,
\]
the function $f_a$ is Morse.
:::

::: {.solution}

Define the gradient map
$$
F:\RR^n\to\RR^n,
\qquad
F(x)=\nabla f(x).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

A point
$$
x\in\RR^n
$$
is critical for
$$
f_a
$$
if and only if
$$
\boxed{F(x)=-a}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
f_a(x)
=
f(x)+x\cdot a,
$$
its gradient is
$$
\nabla f_a(x)
=
\nabla f(x)+a
=
F(x)+a.
$$
Thus
$$
\nabla f_a(x)=0
$$
exactly when
$$
F(x)=-a.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every
$$
x\in\RR^n,
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{
D F_x
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f)
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f_a)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The derivative of the gradient map
$$
F=\nabla f
$$
is the Hessian matrix of
$$
f.
$$

The added function
$$
x\longmapsto x\cdot a
$$
is linear, so all of its second derivatives vanish.
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f_a)
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f)
=
D F_x.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If
$$
-a
$$
is a regular value of
$$
F,
$$
then every critical point of
$$
f_a
$$
is nondegenerate.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
x
$$
be a critical point of
$$
f_a.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
F(x)=-a.
$$

If
$$
-a
$$
is a regular value of
$$
F,
$$
then
$$
D F_x:\RR^n\to\RR^n
$$
is surjective.
Because domain and codomain have the same finite dimension,
$$
D F_x
$$
is an isomorphism.

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} this map is the Hessian of
$$
f_a
$$
at
$$
x.
$$
Thus the Hessian is nonsingular, so
$$
x
$$
is a nondegenerate critical point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If
$$
-a
$$
is a regular value of
$$
F,
$$
then
$$
\boxed{f_a\text{ is Morse}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

A smooth real-valued function is Morse precisely when all of its critical points are nondegenerate.
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves this for
$$
f_a.
$$
If
$$
f_a
$$
has no critical points, the condition is vacuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

By Sard's theorem, the set
$$
\operatorname{CritVal}(F)
$$
of critical values of
$$
F
$$
has Lebesgue measure zero in
$$
\RR^n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
F=\nabla f
$$
is smooth because
$$
f
$$
is smooth.
Sard's theorem states that the set of critical values of a smooth map between finite-dimensional manifolds has measure zero in the target.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The set
$$
E
\coloneqq
\{
a\in\RR^n:
-a\in\operatorname{CritVal}(F)
\}
$$
has measure zero.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
E
=
-\operatorname{CritVal}(F).
$$
The reflection
$$
y\longmapsto-y
$$
preserves Lebesgue measure.
Hence step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} implies
$$
m(E)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For every
$$
a\in\RR^n\setminus E,
$$
the function
$$
f_a
$$
is Morse.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
a\notin E,
$$
then
$$
-a
$$
is not a critical value of
$$
F;
$$
equivalently, it is a regular value.
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} therefore shows that
$$
f_a
$$
is Morse.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
f_a(x)=f(x)+x\cdot a
\text{ is Morse for almost every }
a\in\RR^n.}
$$


::: pf-proof

The exceptional set
$$
E
$$
has measure zero by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves the Morse property outside that set.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
