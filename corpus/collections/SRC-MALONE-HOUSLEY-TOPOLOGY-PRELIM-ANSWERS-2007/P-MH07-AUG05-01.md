::: {.problem}
Identify $\CC$ with $\RR^2$ in the usual way. In
$$
\CC^2\setminus\left\{(0,0),\left(0,\tfrac12\right)\right\}
$$
consider the submanifolds
$$
M_1=\{(z,w)\in\CC^2:z^2+w^2=1\},
$$
$$
M_2=\{(z,w)\in\CC^2:z^2-w^2+w=1\},
$$
and
$$
M_3=\{(x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)\in\RR^4:1-x_1+x_2^2+y_1^2+y_2^2=0\}.
$$
Which pairs $M_i,M_j$ are transverse at the point $p=(1,0)\in\CC^2$?
:::

::: {.solution}

Identify
$$
\CC^2
\cong
\RR^4
$$
by
$$
(z,w)
=
(x_1+iy_1,x_2+iy_2).
$$
The point
$$
p=(1,0)
\in
\CC^2
$$
corresponds to
$$
(1,0,0,0).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The tangent space of $M_1$ at $p$ is
$$
\boxed{
T_pM_1
=
\{
(0,0,c,d)
:
c,d\in\RR
\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
F_1:\CC^2\to\CC
$$
by
$$
F_1(z,w)
\coloneqq
z^2+w^2.
$$
Then
$$
M_1
=
F_1^{-1}(1).
$$
The complex differential at
$$
p=(1,0)
$$
is
$$
dF_{1,p}(\delta z,\delta w)
=
2\delta z.
$$
Hence
$$
T_pM_1
=
\ker dF_{1,p}
=
\{
(\delta z,\delta w)
:
\delta z=0
\}.
$$
Writing
$$
\delta w=c+id
$$
gives the displayed real-coordinate description.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The tangent space of $M_2$ at $p$ is
$$
\boxed{
T_pM_2
=
\{
(a,b,-2a,-2b)
:
a,b\in\RR
\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
F_2:\CC^2\to\CC
$$
by
$$
F_2(z,w)
\coloneqq
z^2-w^2+w.
$$
Then
$$
M_2
=
F_2^{-1}(1).
$$
At
$$
p=(1,0),
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
dF_{2,p}(\delta z,\delta w)
&=
2\delta z
+
(-2w+1)\delta w\evalfrom_{w=0}\\
&=
2\delta z+\delta w.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
T_pM_2
=
\{
(\delta z,\delta w)
:
2\delta z+\delta w=0
\}.
$$
Writing
$$
\delta z=a+ib
$$
gives
$$
\delta w
=
-2a-2ib,
$$
which is the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Using the retained equation
$$
1-x_1+x_2^2+y_1^2+y_2^2=0,
$$
the tangent space of $M_3$ at $p$ is
$$
\boxed{
T_pM_3
=
\{
(0,b,c,d)
:
b,c,d\in\RR
\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
G:\RR^4\to\RR
$$
by
$$
G(x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)
\coloneqq
1-x_1+x_2^2+y_1^2+y_2^2.
$$
Then
$$
M_3
=
G^{-1}(0).
$$
At
$$
p=(1,0,0,0),
$$
$$
dG_p
=
-dx_1.
$$
Therefore
$$
T_pM_3
=
\ker dG_p
=
\{
(\delta x_1,\delta y_1,\delta x_2,\delta y_2)
:
\delta x_1=0
\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The intersection
$$
T_pM_1
\cap
T_pM_2
$$
is zero.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
v
\in
T_pM_1
\cap
T_pM_2.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
v
=
(0,0,c,d).
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, it must also have the form
$$
(a,b,-2a,-2b).
$$
Comparing the first two coordinates gives
$$
a=b=0.
$$
Then the last two coordinates give
$$
c=d=0.
$$
Thus
$$
v=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The pair
$$
M_1,M_2
$$
is transverse at $p$.


::: pf-proof

Both tangent spaces have dimension $2$.
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, their intersection has dimension $0$.
Therefore
$$
\dim
\left(
T_pM_1+T_pM_2
\right)
=
2+2-0
=
4.
$$
Hence
$$
T_pM_1+T_pM_2
=
T_p\RR^4.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{
M_1\pitchfork M_2
\text{ at }p.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

One has
$$
T_pM_1
\subset
T_pM_3.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every vector in
$$
T_pM_1
$$
has the form
$$
(0,0,c,d)
$$
by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
Every such vector satisfies the defining condition
$$
\delta x_1=0
$$
for
$$
T_pM_3
$$
from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The pair
$$
M_1,M_3
$$
is not transverse at $p$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
T_pM_1+T_pM_3
=
T_pM_3.
$$
But
$$
\dim T_pM_3=3<4.
$$
Therefore
$$
T_pM_1+T_pM_3
\ne
T_p\RR^4.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
M_1
\not\pitchfork
M_3
\text{ at }p.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The intersection
$$
T_pM_2
\cap
T_pM_3
$$
is one-dimensional:
$$
\boxed{
T_pM_2
\cap
T_pM_3
=
\{
(0,b,0,-2b)
:
b\in\RR
\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
v
\in
T_pM_2.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
v
=
(a,b,-2a,-2b).
$$
For $v$ to lie in
$$
T_pM_3,
$$
step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} requires
$$
a=0.
$$
Thus
$$
v
=
(0,b,0,-2b),
$$
with $b$ arbitrary.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The pair
$$
M_2,M_3
$$
is transverse at $p$.


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim T_pM_2=2,
\qquad
\dim T_pM_3=3.
$$
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim
\left(
T_pM_2
\cap
T_pM_3
\right)
=
1.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dim
\left(
T_pM_2+T_pM_3
\right)
&=
2+3-1\\
&=
4.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
T_pM_2+T_pM_3
=
T_p\RR^4,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{
M_2\pitchfork M_3
\text{ at }p.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore the transverse pairs at $p$ are exactly
$$
\boxed{
(M_1,M_2)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(M_2,M_3).
}
$$
The pair
$$
(M_1,M_3)
$$
is not transverse.


::: pf-proof

This is the combined conclusion of steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::
