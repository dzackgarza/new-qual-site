::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a nonempty compact manifold without boundary. Prove that there is no compact manifold
\[
W\subset M\times M
\]
whose boundary is the diagonal
\[
\Delta=\{(x,x):x\in M\}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
m
\coloneqq
\dim M.
$$
Work throughout with coefficients in
$$
\mathbb F_2
\coloneqq
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
$$

<1>1. The closed manifold $M$ has a nonzero mod-$2$ fundamental class
$$
[M]
\in
H_m(M;\mathbb F_2).
$$

::: {.proof}
Every compact manifold without boundary has a fundamental class with
$$
\mathbb F_2
$$
coefficients, regardless of orientability.
Because $M$ is nonempty, at least one connected component contributes a nonzero top-dimensional mod-$2$ class.
Hence
$$
[M]\ne0.
$$
:::

<1>2. The diagonal map
$$
d:M\to M\times M,
\qquad
d(x)=(x,x),
$$
is a diffeomorphism from $M$ onto $\Delta$.

::: {.proof}
The map $d$ is smooth and injective.
Its inverse on the image is either coordinate projection:
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1|_\Delta
:
\Delta\to M.
$$
Thus
$$
\Delta
\cong
M
$$
smoothly.
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
[\Delta]
\in
H_m(\Delta;\mathbb F_2)
$$
be the mod-$2$ fundamental class corresponding to $[M]$ under the diffeomorphism in step <1>2.

::: {.proof}
Define
$$
[\Delta]
\coloneqq
d_*[M].
$$
Since $d$ is a diffeomorphism onto $\Delta$, this is precisely the fundamental class of the diagonal.
:::

<1>4. Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a compact manifold
$$
W\subset M\times M
$$
with
$$
\partial W=\Delta.
$$
Then
$$
\dim W=m+1.
$$

::: {.proof}
The boundary of a smooth manifold has codimension $1$.
Since
$$
\partial W=\Delta
$$
and
$$
\dim\Delta=\dim M=m,
$$
one must have
$$
\dim W=m+1.
$$
:::

<1>5. The compact manifold with boundary $W$ has a relative mod-$2$ fundamental class
$$
[W,\partial W]
\in
H_{m+1}(W,\partial W;\mathbb F_2).
$$

::: {.proof}
Every compact manifold with boundary has a relative fundamental class with
$$
\mathbb F_2
$$
coefficients, with no orientability assumption required.
:::

<1>6. The connecting homomorphism in the long exact sequence of the pair
$$
(W,\partial W)
$$
sends the relative fundamental class to the boundary fundamental class:
$$
\partial[W,\partial W]
=
[\partial W]
=
[\Delta].
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the defining boundary property of the relative fundamental class of a manifold with boundary.
Since
$$
\partial W=\Delta,
$$
its boundary class is exactly
$$
[\Delta].
$$
:::

<1>7. The inclusion
$$
j:\Delta=\partial W\hookrightarrow W
$$
sends
$$
[\Delta]
$$
to zero in
$$
H_m(W;\mathbb F_2).
$$

::: {.proof}
The relevant part of the long exact sequence of the pair is
$$
H_{m+1}(W,\partial W;\mathbb F_2)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
H_m(\partial W;\mathbb F_2)
\xrightarrow{j_*}
H_m(W;\mathbb F_2).
$$
By exactness,
$$
j_*
\left(
\operatorname{im}\partial
\right)
=
0.
$$
Step <1>6 shows
$$
[\Delta]
\in
\operatorname{im}\partial.
$$
Hence
$$
j_*[\Delta]=0.
$$
:::

<1>8. Let
$$
i:W\hookrightarrow M\times M
$$
be the inclusion.
Then the inclusion
$$
\iota:\Delta\hookrightarrow M\times M
$$
sends
$$
[\Delta]
$$
to zero:
$$
\iota_*[\Delta]=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The inclusion factors as
$$
\Delta
\xrightarrow{j}
W
\xrightarrow{i}
M\times M.
$$
Thus
$$
\iota_*
=
i_*\circ j_*.
$$
By step <1>7,
$$
j_*[\Delta]=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\iota_*[\Delta]=0.
$$
:::

<1>9. On the other hand, the first projection
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1:M\times M\to M
$$
satisfies
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\iota\circ d
=
\operatorname{id}_M.
$$

::: {.proof}
For
$$
x\in M,
$$
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1
\left(
\iota(d(x))
\right)
=
\operatorname{pr}_1(x,x)
=
x.
$$
:::

<1>10. Therefore
$$
(\operatorname{pr}_1)_*
\left(
\iota_*[\Delta]
\right)
=
[M]
\ne
0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
[\Delta]
=
d_*[M].
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname{pr}_1)_*
\left(
\iota_*[\Delta]
\right)
&=
(\operatorname{pr}_1)_*
\iota_*
d_*[M]\\
&=
(\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\iota\circ d)_*[M]\\
&=
(\operatorname{id}_M)_*[M]\\
&=
[M].
\end{aligned}
$$
Step <1>1 gives
$$
[M]\ne0.
$$
:::

<1>11. Steps <1>8 and <1>10 contradict each other.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 gives
$$
\iota_*[\Delta]=0.
$$
Applying
$$
(\operatorname{pr}_1)_*
$$
would therefore give zero.
But step <1>10 gives the nonzero class
$$
[M].
$$
Contradiction.
:::

<1>12. Therefore no such compact manifold $W$ exists.

::: {.proof}
The assumption in step <1>4 leads to the contradiction in step <1>11.
Hence
$$
\boxed{
\text{there is no compact }W\subset M\times M
\text{ with }\partial W=\Delta.
}
$$
:::

<1>13. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>12 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
