::: {.problem}
View $SL_2(\RR)$, the set of real $2\times2$ matrices of determinant $1$, as a subset of $\RR^4$ by choosing an ordering of the matrix entries.

Show that $SL_2(\RR)$ is a submanifold of $\RR^4$, and compute its tangent space at the identity matrix.
:::

::: {.solution}

The problem is Problem 6 of the January 2007 examination in the retained Malone-Housley packet, printed on p. 10 of its Chapter 2 (`assets/attachments/Topology_Prelim_Answers_-_Unknown.pdf`, whose p. 8 is the chapter's p. 7). The packet prints the problem and no solution follows it, so nothing here is transcribed: the argument is the card's own, and it exhibits $SL_2(\RR)$ as $\det^{-1}(1)$ with $1$ a regular value, then computes the tangent space at the identity.

Identify
$$
M_2(\RR)
\cong
\RR^4
$$
by the matrix entries, and define
$$
F:M_2(\RR)\to\RR,
\qquad
F(A)\coloneqq\det A.
$$

The argument is checked against Bredon's *Topology and Geometry*, Corollary II.7.4, that the fibre over a regular value is an embedded submanifold of dimension $m-n$, together with Example II.7.5, where a sphere is obtained in exactly this way as the fibre of $\sum x_i^2$ over $1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
SL_2(\RR)=F^{-1}(1).
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
SL_2(\RR)
=
\{A\in M_2(\RR):\det A=1\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix}
$$
and
$$
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta
\end{pmatrix},
$$
then
$$
\boxed{
dF_A(H)
=
d\alpha
-
c\beta
-
b\gamma
+
a\delta.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
F(a,b,c,d)=ad-bc,
$$
the differential is
$$
dF
=
d\,da
-
c\,db
-
b\,dc
+
a\,dd.
$$
Evaluating on
$$
H=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)
$$
gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If
$$
A\in SL_2(\RR),
$$
then
$$
dF_A
$$
is nonzero.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
dF_A=0.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, all coefficients of the linear functional must vanish:
$$
d=0,
\qquad
c=0,
\qquad
b=0,
\qquad
a=0.
$$
Thus
$$
A=0,
$$
contradicting
$$
\det A=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
dF_A\ne0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Hence
$$
dF_A:M_2(\RR)\to\RR
$$
is surjective for every
$$
A\in SL_2(\RR).
$$


::: pf-proof

A nonzero linear map to the one-dimensional vector space
$$
\RR
$$
is surjective.
Apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
1
$$
is a regular value of $F$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the preimage of $1$ is
$$
SL_2(\RR).
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives surjectivity of the differential at every point of this preimage.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
SL_2(\RR)
\text{ is a smooth }3\text{-dimensional submanifold of }\RR^4.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By the regular value theorem,
$$
F^{-1}(1)
$$
is a smooth submanifold of codimension $1$.
Since
$$
\dim M_2(\RR)=4,
$$
its dimension is
$$
4-1=3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The tangent space at the identity is
$$
T_I SL_2(\RR)
=
\ker dF_I.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a regular level set
$$
F^{-1}(1),
$$
the regular value theorem identifies the tangent space at a point $A$ with
$$
\ker dF_A.
$$
Apply this at
$$
A=I.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

For
$$
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta
\end{pmatrix},
$$
one has
$$
dF_I(H)
=
\alpha+\delta
=
\operatorname{tr}H.
$$


::: pf-proof

At
$$
I=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
dF_I(H)
=
1\cdot\alpha
-
0\cdot\beta
-
0\cdot\gamma
+
1\cdot\delta
=
\alpha+\delta.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
T_I SL_2(\RR)
=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&-a
\end{pmatrix}
:
a,b,c\in\RR
\right\}
=
\{H\in M_2(\RR):\operatorname{tr}H=0\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
H\in T_I SL_2(\RR)
\iff
\operatorname{tr}H=0.
$$
Writing the trace condition
$$
\alpha+\delta=0
$$
as
$$
\delta=-\alpha
$$
gives the displayed parametrization.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves the submanifold statement, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} computes the tangent space.

:::
