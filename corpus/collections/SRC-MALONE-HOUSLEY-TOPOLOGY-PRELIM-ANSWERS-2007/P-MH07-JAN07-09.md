::: {.problem}
Define a $\Delta$-complex structure on the Klein bottle $K$ and compute its homology with coefficients in $\mathbb Z$ and in $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$.
:::

Represent the Klein bottle as the quotient of a square in which one pair of opposite sides is identified in the same direction and the other pair in opposite directions.
Triangulate the square by a diagonal.
After the edge identifications, all four vertices become a single vertex $V$.
Let the three edge classes be
$$
A,\qquad B,\qquad C,
$$
where $C$ is the diagonal, and let the two triangles be
$$
U,\qquad L.
$$
Choose orientations so that
$$
\boxed{
\partial U=A+B-C,
\qquad
\partial L=-A+B+C.
}
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

This gives a $\Delta$-complex structure on $K$ with one vertex, three edges, and two $2$-simplices.


::: pf-proof

The square quotient is the standard polygon model of the Klein bottle.
Adding a diagonal subdivides the square into two triangles.
The edge identifications descend to identifications of simplex faces, so the quotient is a $\Delta$-complex.
All corner vertices are identified, leaving one $0$-simplex.
The two boundary edge classes together with the diagonal give three $1$-simplices, and the two halves of the square give the two $2$-simplices $U$ and $L$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

With coefficients in an abelian group $G$, the chain complex is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
G^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
G^3
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
G
\longrightarrow
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>1 gives two $2$-simplices, three $1$-simplices, and one vertex.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The boundary map
$$
\partial_1
$$
is zero.


::: pf-proof

Every edge begins and ends at the single vertex $V$.
Thus
$$
\partial_1(A)
=
\partial_1(B)
=
\partial_1(C)
=
V-V
=
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Relative to the bases
$$
(U,L)
$$
and
$$
(A,B,C),
$$
the map $\partial_2$ is
$$
\partial_2(U)
=
A+B-C,
$$
$$
\partial_2(L)
=
-A+B+C.
$$


::: pf-proof

These are exactly the oriented face boundaries in the chosen triangulated square model.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Over $\mathbb Z$, the map
$$
\partial_2:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z^3
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
r\,\partial U
+
s\,\partial L
=
0.
$$
Using step <1>4, the coefficient of $A$ gives
$$
r-s=0,
$$
so
$$
r=s.
$$
The coefficient of $B$ gives
$$
r+s=0.
$$
Thus
$$
2r=0
$$
in $\mathbb Z$, so
$$
r=s=0.
$$
Hence the kernel is zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-simplices, so
$$
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step <1>5.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The subgroup
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\subseteq
\mathbb Z^3
$$
has quotient
$$
\mathbb Z^3/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The relations imposed on the free abelian group generated by $A,B,C$ are
$$
A+B-C=0
$$
and
$$
-A+B+C=0.
$$
The first gives
$$
C=A+B.
$$
Substituting this into the second gives
$$
-A+B+(A+B)
=
2B
=
0.
$$
Thus the quotient is generated by
$$
A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B
$$
with the single relation
$$
2B=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\mathbb Z^3/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>3,
$$
\ker\partial_1
=
\mathbb Z^3.
$$
Thus
$$
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)
=
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\mathbb Z^3/\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
$$
Apply step <1>7.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $K$ is connected,
$$
H_0(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

The polygon quotient is connected.
The zeroth homology of a connected space is $\mathbb Z$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_k(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>6, <1>8, and <1>9.
There are no cells above dimension $2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Now take coefficients in
$$
\mathbb F_2
\coloneqq
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
$$
Then
$$
\partial_2(U)
=
\partial_2(L)
=
A+B+C.
$$


::: pf-proof

Modulo $2$,
$$
-1=1.
$$
Thus both boundary formulas from step <1>4 reduce to the same vector
$$
A+B+C.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Over $\mathbb F_2$, the map $\partial_2$ has rank $1$ and kernel of dimension $1$.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>11, the two basis vectors $U,L$ have the same nonzero image.
Therefore the image is the one-dimensional span of
$$
A+B+C.
$$
Since the domain has dimension $2$, rank-nullity gives
$$
\dim\ker\partial_2
=
2-1
=
1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
H_2(K;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-simplices, so
$$
H_2
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step <1>12.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
H_1(K;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
\partial_1
$$
is still zero, so
$$
\ker\partial_1
=
\mathbb F_2^3.
$$
By step <1>12,
$$
\dim\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
1.
$$
Hence
$$
\dim H_1
=
3-1
=
2.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_1(K;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2^2.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $K$ is connected,
$$
H_0(K;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the field-coefficient version of step <1>9.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_k(K;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb F_2,&k=0,2,\\
\mathbb F_2^2,&k=1,\\
0,&k>2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>13--<1>15.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>1 gives the explicit $\Delta$-complex structure, step <1>10 gives the integral homology, and step <1>16 gives the mod-$2$ homology.
