::: {.problem}
Define a $\Delta$-complex structure on the Klein bottle $K$ and compute its homology with coefficients in $\ZZ$ and in $\ZZ/2\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Represent the Klein bottle as the quotient of a square in which one pair of opposite sides is identified in the same direction and the other pair in opposite directions.
Triangulate the square by a diagonal.
After the edge identifications, all four vertices become a single vertex $V$.
Let the three edge classes be
$$
A,\qquad B,\qquad C,
$$
where $C$ is the diagonal, and let the two triangles be
$$
U,\qquad L.
$$
Choose orientations so that
$$
\boxed{
\partial U=A+B-C,
\qquad
\partial L=-A+B+C.
}
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

This gives a $\Delta$-complex structure on $K$ with one vertex, three edges, and two $2$-simplices.


::: pf-proof

The square quotient is the standard polygon model of the Klein bottle.
Adding a diagonal subdivides the square into two triangles.
The edge identifications descend to identifications of simplex faces, so the quotient is a $\Delta$-complex.
All corner vertices are identified, leaving one $0$-simplex.
The two boundary edge classes together with the diagonal give three $1$-simplices, and the two halves of the square give the two $2$-simplices $U$ and $L$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

With coefficients in an abelian group $G$, the chain complex is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
G^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
G^3
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
G
\longrightarrow
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives two $2$-simplices, three $1$-simplices, and one vertex.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The boundary map
$$
\partial_1
$$
is zero.


::: pf-proof

Every edge begins and ends at the single vertex $V$.
Thus
$$
\partial_1(A)
=
\partial_1(B)
=
\partial_1(C)
=
V-V
=
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Relative to the bases
$$
(U,L)
$$
and
$$
(A,B,C),
$$
the map $\partial_2$ is
$$
\partial_2(U)
=
A+B-C,
$$
$$
\partial_2(L)
=
-A+B+C.
$$


::: pf-proof

These are exactly the oriented face boundaries in the chosen triangulated square model.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Over $\ZZ$, the map
$$
\partial_2:\ZZ^2\to\ZZ^3
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
r\,\partial U
+
s\,\partial L
=
0.
$$
Using step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the coefficient of $A$ gives
$$
r-s=0,
$$
so
$$
r=s.
$$
The coefficient of $B$ gives
$$
r+s=0.
$$
Thus
$$
2r=0
$$
in $\ZZ$, so
$$
r=s=0.
$$
Hence the kernel is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
H_2(K;\ZZ)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-simplices, so
$$
H_2(K;\ZZ)
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The subgroup
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\subseteq
\ZZ^3
$$
has quotient
$$
\ZZ^3/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The relations imposed on the free abelian group generated by $A,B,C$ are
$$
A+B-C=0
$$
and
$$
-A+B+C=0.
$$
The first gives
$$
C=A+B.
$$
Substituting this into the second gives
$$
-A+B+(A+B)
=
2B
=
0.
$$
Thus the quotient is generated by
$$
A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B
$$
with the single relation
$$
2B=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\ZZ^3/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
H_1(K;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\ker\partial_1
=
\ZZ^3.
$$
Thus
$$
H_1(K;\ZZ)
=
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\ZZ^3/\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Since $K$ is connected,
$$
H_0(K;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

The polygon quotient is connected.
The zeroth homology of a connected space is $\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_k(K;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.
There are no cells above dimension $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Now take coefficients in
$$
\FF_2
\coloneqq
\ZZ/2\ZZ.
$$
Then
$$
\partial_2(U)
=
\partial_2(L)
=
A+B+C.
$$


::: pf-proof

Modulo $2$,
$$
-1=1.
$$
Thus both boundary formulas from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} reduce to the same vector
$$
A+B+C.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Over $\FF_2$, the map $\partial_2$ has rank $1$ and kernel of dimension $1$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}, the two basis vectors $U,L$ have the same nonzero image.
Therefore the image is the one-dimensional span of
$$
A+B+C.
$$
Since the domain has dimension $2$, rank-nullity gives
$$
\dim\ker\partial_2
=
2-1
=
1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore
$$
H_2(K;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-simplices, so
$$
H_2
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

One has
$$
H_1(K;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
\partial_1
$$
is still zero, so
$$
\ker\partial_1
=
\FF_2^3.
$$
By step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
1.
$$
Hence
$$
\dim H_1
=
3-1
=
2.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_1(K;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2^2.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Since $K$ is connected,
$$
H_0(K;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the field-coefficient version of step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_k(K;\FF_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\FF_2,&k=0,2,\\
\FF_2^2,&k=1,\\
0,&k>2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives the explicit $\Delta$-complex structure, step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives the integral homology, and step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} gives the mod-$2$ homology.
