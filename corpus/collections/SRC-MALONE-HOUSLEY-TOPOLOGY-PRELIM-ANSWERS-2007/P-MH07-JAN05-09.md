::: {.problem}
On
\[
\RR^3\setminus\{0\},
\]
consider the $2$-form
\[
\omega
=
(x^2+y^2+z^2)^\alpha
\left(
x\,dy\wedge dz
+y\,dz\wedge dx
+z\,dx\wedge dy
\right).
\]
Find all
\[
\alpha\in\RR
\]
for which $\omega$ is closed. For that value of $\alpha$, show that $\omega$ is not exact.
:::

::: {.solution}

Set
$$
r^2=x^2+y^2+z^2
$$
and
$$
\beta
\coloneqq
x\,dy\wedge dz
+y\,dz\wedge dx
+z\,dx\wedge dy.
$$
Then
$$
\omega=r^{2\alpha}\beta.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\boxed{
d\beta
=
3\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since the coordinate $1$-forms are closed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
d(x\,dy\wedge dz)
&=
dx\wedge dy\wedge dz,\\
d(y\,dz\wedge dx)
&=
dy\wedge dz\wedge dx,\\
d(z\,dx\wedge dy)
&=
dz\wedge dx\wedge dy.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each cyclic permutation has positive sign, so their sum is
$$
3\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
d(r^{2\alpha})
=
2\alpha r^{2\alpha-2}
(x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz).
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
r^{2\alpha}
=(x^2+y^2+z^2)^\alpha
$$
by the chain rule.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The wedge product satisfies
$$
\boxed{
(x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz)\wedge\beta
=
r^2\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand the product.
All terms containing a repeated coordinate differential vanish.
The surviving terms are
$$
x^2\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz,
$$
$$
y^2\,dy\wedge dz\wedge dx,
$$
and
$$
z^2\,dz\wedge dx\wedge dy.
$$
Again the cyclic permutations have positive sign, so the sum is
$$
(x^2+y^2+z^2)
dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
d\omega
=
(2\alpha+3)r^{2\alpha}
dx\wedge dy\wedge dz}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using the product rule and steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\omega
&=
d(r^{2\alpha})\wedge\beta
+r^{2\alpha}d\beta\\
&=
2\alpha r^{2\alpha-2}
r^2\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz
+3r^{2\alpha}
dx\wedge dy\wedge dz\\
&=
(2\alpha+3)r^{2\alpha}
dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
d\omega=0
\iff
\alpha=-\frac32}.
$$


::: pf-proof

On
$$
\RR^3\setminus\{0\},
$$
the factor
$$
r^{2\alpha}
$$
never vanishes.
Thus step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} vanishes identically exactly when
$$
2\alpha+3=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Assume
$$
\alpha=-\frac32.
$$
Let
$$
i:S^2\hookrightarrow
\RR^3\setminus\{0\}
$$
be the inclusion of the unit sphere.
Then
$$
i^*\omega
$$
is the standard oriented area form on
$$
S^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

On
$$
S^2
$$
one has
$$
r=1,
$$
so
$$
i^*\omega=i^*\beta.
$$
For tangent vectors
$$
u,v\in T_pS^2,
$$
the form
$$
\beta_p(u,v)
$$
equals
$$
p\cdot(u\times v),
$$
which is precisely the standard area form induced by the outward orientation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\int_{S^2}i^*\omega
=
4\pi
\ne0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, the integral is the surface area of the unit sphere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The closed form
$$
\omega
$$
is not exact.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\omega=d\eta
$$
for some smooth
$$
1
$$
-form
$$
\eta
$$
on
$$
\RR^3\setminus\{0\}.
$$
Pullback commutes with exterior differentiation, so
$$
i^*\omega
=
d(i^*\eta).
$$
Since
$$
S^2
$$
has empty boundary, Stokes' theorem gives
$$
\int_{S^2}i^*\omega
=
\int_{S^2}d(i^*\eta)
=
\int_{\partial S^2}i^*\eta
=
0,
$$
contradicting step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\alpha=-\frac32
}
$$
is the unique value for which $\omega$ is closed, and for this value $\omega$ is not exact.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives the unique closed case, and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves nonexactness.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
