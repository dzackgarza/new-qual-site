::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f:S^2\to S^2
$$
be a map whose degree is not $-1$. Show that $f$ has a fixed point.
:::

::: {.solution}

The problem is Problem 4 of the 6510 Final Exam in the retained Malone-Housley packet, printed on p. 18 of its Chapter 3 (`assets/attachments/Topology_Prelim_Answers_-_Unknown.pdf`, whose p. 18 carries the chapter's p. 17). The packet prints the problem and no solution follows it — the next entry is Problem 5 — so nothing here is transcribed: the argument is the card's own, and it runs through the Lefschetz number: a fixed-point-free map is excluded by $L(f)=1+\deg f\ne 0$ when $\deg f\ne-1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The rational homology of $S^2$ is
$$
H_i(S^2;\QQ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\QQ,&i=0,2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard homology computation for the $2$-sphere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The induced map
$$
f_*:H_0(S^2;\QQ)\to H_0(S^2;\QQ)
$$
is the identity.


::: pf-proof

The sphere is path-connected.
Any continuous self-map of a connected space induces the identity on
$$
H_0(-;\QQ)\cong\QQ.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{tr}(f_*\restrict H_0)=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

On
$$
H_2(S^2;\QQ),
$$
the map $f_*$ is multiplication by
$$
\deg f.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
[S^2]\in H_2(S^2;\ZZ)
$$
be the fundamental class.
By definition of the degree,
$$
f_*[S^2]
=
(\deg f)[S^2].
$$
After tensoring with $\QQ$, the same formula holds on
$$
H_2(S^2;\QQ).
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{tr}(f_*\restrict H_2)=\deg f.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The Lefschetz number of $f$ is
$$
\boxed{
L(f)=1+\deg f.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
L(f)
=
\sum_{i\ge0}
(-1)^i
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
f_*:
H_i(S^2;\QQ)\to H_i(S^2;\QQ)
\right).
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, only degrees $0$ and $2$ contribute.
Since both signs are positive,
$$
L(f)
=
1+\deg f
$$
by steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Since
$$
\deg f\ne-1,
$$
one has
$$
L(f)\ne0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
L(f)=1+\deg f.
$$
This vanishes exactly when
$$
\deg f=-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore $f$ has a fixed point.


::: pf-proof

The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem states that if the Lefschetz number of a self-map of a compact triangulable space is nonzero, then the map has a fixed point.
The sphere
$$
S^2
$$
is compact and triangulable, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
L(f)\ne0.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
f\text{ has a fixed point}.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
