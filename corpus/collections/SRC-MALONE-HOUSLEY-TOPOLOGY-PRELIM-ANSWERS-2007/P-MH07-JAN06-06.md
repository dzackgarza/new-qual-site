::: {.problem}
Let $\omega$ be a compactly supported smooth $n$-form on $\RR^n$. Prove that the following are equivalent:

1. There exists a compactly supported smooth $(n-1)$-form $\eta$ such that $\omega=d\eta$.
2. One has
   $$
   \int_{\RR^n}\omega=0.
   $$
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
\omega
=
f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n,
$$
where
$$
f\in C_c^\infty(\RR^n).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If
$$
\omega=d\eta
$$
for some compactly supported
$$
(n-1)\text{-form }\eta,
$$
then
$$
\int_{\RR^n}\omega=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose
$$
R>0
$$
so large that
$$
\operatorname{supp}\eta
\subset
B_R(0).
$$
Then
$$
\eta
$$
vanishes on a neighborhood of
$$
\partial B_R(0).
$$
By Stokes' theorem,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\RR^n}\omega
&=
\int_{B_R(0)}d\eta\\
&=
\int_{\partial B_R(0)}\eta\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For the converse, assume
$$
\int_{\RR^n}\omega=0.
$$
We prove by induction on $n$ that $\omega$ has a compactly supported primitive.


::: pf-proof

The induction statement is:
every compactly supported smooth top-degree form on
$$
\RR^m
$$
with integral zero is the exterior derivative of a compactly supported smooth
$$
(m-1)\text{-form}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Base case $n=1$.


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
\omega=f(x)\,dx
$$
with
$$
\int_{\RR}f(x)\,dx=0.
$$
Define
$$
F(x)
\coloneqq
\int_{-\infty}^{x}f(t)\,dt.
$$
Then
$$
dF
=
f(x)\,dx
=
\omega.
$$

Because $f$ has compact support, $F$ is identically zero sufficiently far to the left.
For $x$ sufficiently far to the right,
$$
F(x)
=
\int_{\RR}f(t)\,dt
=
0.
$$
Hence
$$
F\in C_c^\infty(\RR).
$$
Thus the result holds in dimension $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Assume now
$$
n\ge2
$$
and that the result holds in dimension
$$
n-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the induction hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Write
$$
x'
\coloneqq
(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})
\in
\RR^{n-1}.
$$
Define
$$
g(x')
\coloneqq
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
f(x',t)\,dt.
$$
Then
$$
g\in C_c^\infty(\RR^{n-1}).
$$


::: pf-proof

Smoothness follows by differentiation under the integral sign, because
$$
f
$$
is smooth and compactly supported.

If
$$
x'
$$
lies outside the projection of
$$
\operatorname{supp}f
$$
to
$$
\RR^{n-1},
$$
then
$$
f(x',t)=0
$$
for all
$$
t,
$$
so
$$
g(x')=0.
$$
The projection of a compact set is compact, hence
$$
g
$$
has compact support.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

One has
$$
\int_{\RR^{n-1}}g(x')\,dx'
=
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By Fubini's theorem,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\RR^{n-1}}g(x')\,dx'
&=
\int_{\RR^{n-1}}
\int_{\RR}
f(x',t)\,dt\,dx'\\
&=
\int_{\RR^n}
f(x)\,dx\\
&=
\int_{\RR^n}\omega\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

By the induction hypothesis, there exists
$$
\beta
\in
\Omega_c^{n-2}(\RR^{n-1})
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{
d\beta
=
g(x')\,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n-1}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The top-degree form
$$
g(x')\,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n-1}
$$
is compactly supported by step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and has integral zero by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.
Apply the induction hypothesis from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Choose
$$
\chi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)
$$
such that
$$
\int_{\RR}\chi(t)\,dt=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take any nonnegative nonzero bump function and divide it by its positive integral.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Define
$$
h(x',x_n)
\coloneqq
f(x',x_n)
-
g(x')\chi(x_n).
$$
Then
$$
h\in C_c^\infty(\RR^n)
$$
and for every
$$
x'\in\RR^{n-1},
$$
$$
\boxed{
\int_{\RR}
h(x',t)\,dt
=
0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Compact support and smoothness follow from those of
$$
f,
g,
\chi.
$$

For fixed
$$
x',
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\RR}h(x',t)\,dt
&=
\int_{\RR}f(x',t)\,dt
-
g(x')
\int_{\RR}\chi(t)\,dt\\
&=
g(x')-g(x')\\
&=
0,
\end{aligned}
$$
using steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Define
$$
H(x',x_n)
\coloneqq
\int_{-\infty}^{x_n}
h(x',t)\,dt.
$$
Then
$$
H\in C_c^\infty(\RR^n)
$$
and
$$
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_n}
=
h.
$$


::: pf-proof

Smoothness and the derivative identity follow from differentiation under the integral sign and the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Because $h$ has compact support, there is
$$
R>0
$$
such that
$$
h(x',t)=0
$$
whenever
$$
|x'|>R
$$
or
$$
|t|>R.
$$
Hence
$$
H(x',x_n)=0
$$
whenever
$$
|x'|>R
$$
or
$$
x_n<-R.
$$

If
$$
x_n>R,
$$
then step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives
$$
H(x',x_n)
=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(x',t)\,dt
=
0.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{supp}H
$$
is compact.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Define the compactly supported
$$
(n-1)\text{-form}
$$
$$
\eta_h
\coloneqq
(-1)^{n-1}
H\,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n-1}.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
d\eta_h
=
h\,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Only the
$$
dx_n
$$
component of
$$
dH
$$
survives when wedged with
$$
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n-1}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\eta_h
&=
(-1)^{n-1}
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_n}
dx_n
\wedge
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n-1}\\
&=
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_n}
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n\\
&=
h\,
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n,
\end{aligned}
$$
using step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Regard
$$
\beta
$$
as pulled back from
$$
\RR^{n-1}
$$
to
$$
\RR^n.
$$
Define
$$
\eta_g
\coloneqq
\chi(x_n)\,
\beta\wedge dx_n.
$$
Then
$$
\eta_g
$$
is compactly supported and
$$
\boxed{
d\eta_g
=
g(x')\chi(x_n)
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Compact support follows from compact support of
$$
\beta
$$
and
$$
\chi.
$$

Now
$$
d(\chi\beta\wedge dx_n)
=
d(\chi\beta)\wedge dx_n.
$$
Expanding,
$$
d(\chi\beta)
=
d\chi\wedge\beta
+
\chi\,d\beta.
$$
The term
$$
d\chi\wedge\beta\wedge dx_n
$$
vanishes because
$$
d\chi
=
\chi'(x_n)\,dx_n
$$
contains another factor
$$
dx_n.
$$
Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\eta_g
&=
\chi\,d\beta\wedge dx_n\\
&=
g(x')\chi(x_n)
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n-1}\wedge dx_n
\end{aligned}
$$
by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Define
$$
\eta
\coloneqq
\eta_h+\eta_g.
$$
Then
$$
\eta\in\Omega_c^{n-1}(\RR^n).
$$


::: pf-proof

Both
$$
\eta_h
$$
and
$$
\eta_g
$$
are smooth and compactly supported by steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.
Their sum has the same properties.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

One has
$$
\boxed{
d\eta=\omega.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-12){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\eta
&=
h\,dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n\\
&\qquad
+
g(x')\chi(x_n)
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n\\
&=
\left(
h+g\chi
\right)
dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
By the definition of $h$ in step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
h+g\chi=f.
$$
Therefore
$$
d\eta
=
f\,dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n
=
\omega.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\omega=d\eta
\text{ for some }
\eta\in\Omega_c^{n-1}(\RR^n)
\iff
\int_{\RR^n}\omega=0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The forward implication is step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
The reverse implication is proved by the induction construction in steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} is the required equivalence.
