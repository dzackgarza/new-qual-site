::: {.problem}
State the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem and the needed fact about the ring structure of cohomology. Use them to show that every map
\[
f:\mathbb{CP}^n\to\mathbb{CP}^n
\]
has a fixed point when $n$ is even. Construct a fixed-point-free self-map when $n$ is odd.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
u
\in
H^2(\mathbb{CP}^n;\ZZ)
$$
be the standard generator.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Lefschetz fixed-point theorem.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
X
$$
be a compact triangulable space and
$$
f:X\to X
$$
continuous.
Its Lefschetz number is
$$
L(f)
\coloneqq
\sum_{i\ge0}
(-1)^i
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
f_*:
H_i(X;\QQ)
\to
H_i(X;\QQ)
\right).
$$
If
$$
L(f)\ne0,
$$
then $f$ has a fixed point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The integral cohomology ring of $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is
$$
\boxed{
H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ[u]/(u^{n+1}),
\qquad
\deg u=2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Complex projective space has one cell in each even dimension
$$
0,2,\ldots,2n,
$$
and no odd-dimensional cells.
The standard degree-$2$ class $u$ generates the cohomology ring, with
$$
u^j
$$
generating
$$
H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ
$$
for
$$
0\le j\le n,
$$
and
$$
u^{n+1}=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every continuous self-map
$$
f:\mathbb{CP}^n\to\mathbb{CP}^n,
$$
there is an integer
$$
k
$$
such that
$$
f^*(u)=ku.
$$


::: pf-proof

The group
$$
H^2(\mathbb{CP}^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ
$$
is generated by $u$.
Every endomorphism of this infinite cyclic group is multiplication by an integer.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every
$$
0\le j\le n,
$$
one has
$$
f^*(u^j)
=
k^ju^j.
$$


::: pf-proof

Pullback preserves cup products:
$$
\begin{aligned}
f^*(u^j)
&=
f^*(u)^j\\
&=
(ku)^j\\
&=
k^ju^j.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

On rational cohomology,
$$
f^*:
H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\QQ)
\to
H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\QQ)
$$
has trace
$$
k^j,
$$
and all odd-degree cohomology groups vanish.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\QQ)
$$
is one-dimensional with basis
$$
u^j.
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that $f^*$ acts by multiplication by $k^j$.
There are no odd-dimensional cohomology groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The Lefschetz number of $f$ is
$$
\boxed{
L(f)
=
1+k+k^2+\cdots+k^n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Over a field, the trace of the induced map on homology equals the trace of the dual map on cohomology in the same degree.
By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, only even degrees contribute, so every sign in the Lefschetz sum is positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
L(f)
&=
\sum_{j=0}^{n}
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
f^*:
H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\QQ)
\to
H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^n;\QQ)
\right)\\
&=
\sum_{j=0}^{n}k^j.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Assume
$$
n
$$
is even.
If
$$
k=1,
$$
then
$$
L(f)=n+1\ne0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute
$$
k=1
$$
into the formula from step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Assume
$$
n
$$
is even and
$$
k\ne1.
$$
Then
$$
L(f)
=
\frac{k^{n+1}-1}{k-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the finite geometric-series identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

If
$$
n
$$
is even, then
$$
L(f)\ne0
$$
for every integer
$$
k.
$$


::: pf-proof

The case
$$
k=1
$$
is step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

Now let
$$
k\ne1.
$$
If
$$
L(f)=0,
$$
step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} would give
$$
k^{n+1}=1.
$$
Since
$$
n
$$
is even,
$$
n+1
$$
is odd.
The only integer satisfying
$$
k^{n+1}=1
$$
is
$$
k=1,
$$
contrary to the present assumption.
Therefore
$$
L(f)\ne0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore every self-map
$$
f:\mathbb{CP}^n\to\mathbb{CP}^n
$$
has a fixed point when
$$
n
$$
is even.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref},
$$
L(f)\ne0.
$$
Apply the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
Thus
$$
\boxed{
n\text{ even}
\implies
\text{every self-map of }\mathbb{CP}^n
\text{ has a fixed point}.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Now assume
$$
n
$$
is odd.
Then
$$
n+1
=
2m
$$
for some positive integer
$$
m.
$$


::: pf-proof

An odd integer plus $1$ is even.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define an anti-linear map
$$
J:\CC^{2m}\to\CC^{2m}
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
J(z_0,z_1,\ldots,z_{2m-2},z_{2m-1})
=
(-\overline{z_1},\overline{z_0},
\ldots,
-\overline{z_{2m-1}},\overline{z_{2m-2}}).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The coordinates are grouped into
$$
m
$$
pairs.
On each pair
$$
(z_{2r},z_{2r+1}),
$$
the map is
$$
(z_{2r},z_{2r+1})
\longmapsto
(-\overline{z_{2r+1}},\overline{z_{2r}}).
$$
This is real-linear and anti-complex-linear.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

The map $J$ satisfies
$$
\boxed{
J^2=-I.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

On one coordinate pair,
$$
\begin{aligned}
J^2(z_{2r},z_{2r+1})
&=
J(-\overline{z_{2r+1}},\overline{z_{2r}})\\
&=
(-z_{2r},-z_{2r+1}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $J^2=-I$ on every pair and hence on all of
$$
\CC^{2m}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

The map $J$ induces a well-defined smooth self-map
$$
\overline J:
\mathbb{CP}^{2m-1}
\to
\mathbb{CP}^{2m-1}
$$
by
$$
\overline J([z])
\coloneqq
[Jz].
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
z'
=
\lambda z
$$
with
$$
\lambda\in\CC^\times,
$$
then anti-linearity gives
$$
Jz'
=
J(\lambda z)
=
\overline\lambda\,Jz.
$$
Thus
$$
[Jz']
=
[Jz].
$$
Hence the formula is independent of the chosen representative.
Smoothness follows because complex conjugation and the coordinate operations defining $J$ are smooth as real maps.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

The projective map
$$
\overline J
$$
has no fixed point.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
[z]
$$
were fixed.
Then
$$
[Jz]
=
[z],
$$
so there would exist
$$
\lambda\in\CC^\times
$$
with
$$
Jz
=
\lambda z.
$$

Apply $J$ again.
Because $J$ is anti-linear,
$$
\begin{aligned}
J^2z
&=
J(\lambda z)\\
&=
\overline\lambda\,Jz\\
&=
\overline\lambda\lambda z\\
&=
|\lambda|^2z.
\end{aligned}
$$
But step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} gives
$$
J^2z
=
-z.
$$
Therefore
$$
|\lambda|^2z
=
-z.
$$
Since
$$
z\ne0,
$$
this implies
$$
|\lambda|^2=-1,
$$
which is impossible.
Hence there is no fixed point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Thus when
$$
n
$$
is odd, there exists a fixed-point-free self-map of
$$
\mathbb{CP}^n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
n=2m-1
$$
as in step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.
Then the map
$$
\overline J
$$
constructed in step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} is a self-map of
$$
\mathbb{CP}^n
$$
and is fixed-point-free by step [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}.
Thus
$$
\boxed{
n\text{ odd}
\implies
\mathbb{CP}^n
\text{ admits a fixed-point-free self-map}.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} proves the even-dimensional fixed-point statement, and step [](#s1-16){.pf-ref} gives the required fixed-point-free map for odd $n$.
