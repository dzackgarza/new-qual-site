::: {.problem}
Prove or give a counterexample, with proof, to the following assertion:

If $M$ is a closed submanifold of $\RR^n$, then its tangent bundle is trivial if and only if its normal bundle is trivial.
:::

::: {.solution}

The assertion is false.
Take
$$
M=S^2
\subset
\RR^3
$$
with its standard embedding.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The normal bundle of $S^2\subset\RR^3$ is trivial.


::: pf-proof

At a point
$$
p\in S^2,
$$
the tangent plane is
$$
T_pS^2
=
\{v\in\RR^3:\langle v,p\rangle=0\}.
$$
Thus the normal line is
$$
\nu_p(S^2)
=
\RR p.
$$

Define a normal vector field
$$
\eta:S^2\to\RR^3
$$
by
$$
\eta(p)=p.
$$
This is smooth and nowhere zero, and for every $p$ it spans the normal line
$$
\nu_p(S^2).
$$
Hence
$$
\nu(S^2)
\cong
S^2\times\RR.
$$
Therefore the normal bundle is trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The tangent bundle
$$
TS^2
$$
is not trivial.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
TS^2
$$
were trivial, then it would admit a global frame of two smooth tangent vector fields.
In particular, it would admit a nowhere-vanishing continuous tangent vector field.

But the hairy-ball theorem states that every continuous tangent vector field on the even-dimensional sphere
$$
S^2
$$
vanishes somewhere.
This is a contradiction.
Therefore
$$
TS^2
$$
is not trivial.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus triviality of the normal bundle does not imply triviality of the tangent bundle.


::: pf-proof

For the standard embedding
$$
S^2\subset\RR^3,
$$
step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives a trivial normal bundle, while step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives a nontrivial tangent bundle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore the proposed equivalence is false:
$$
\boxed{
\text{a closed submanifold may have trivial normal bundle and nontrivial tangent bundle}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The standard sphere
$$
S^2\subset\RR^3
$$
is the required counterexample.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} disproves the assertion.

:::

:::

:::
