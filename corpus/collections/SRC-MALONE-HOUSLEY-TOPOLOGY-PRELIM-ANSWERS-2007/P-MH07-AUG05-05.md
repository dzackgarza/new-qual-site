::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^3,
\qquad
f(x,y)=(x^2-y,\,x+y^2,\,\sin x),
\]
and let $\omega$ be the $2$-form on $\mathbb R^3$ with coordinates $(u,v,w)$ given by
\[
\omega=u\,dv\wedge dw.
\]
Compute the pullback $f^*\omega$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the coordinate functions of $f$ as
$$
u\circ f
=
x^2-y,
$$
$$
v\circ f
=
x+y^2,
$$
and
$$
w\circ f
=
\sin x.
$$

<1>1. One has
$$
f^*u
=
x^2-y.
$$

::: {.proof}
The first coordinate function of
$$
f(x,y)
$$
is
$$
x^2-y.
$$
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
f^*(dv)
=
d(v\circ f)
=
dx+2y\,dy.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since pullback commutes with exterior differentiation,
$$
f^*(dv)
=
d(f^*v)
=
d(x+y^2)
=
dx+2y\,dy.
$$
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
f^*(dw)
=
d(w\circ f)
=
\cos x\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
Again,
$$
f^*(dw)
=
d(f^*w)
=
d(\sin x)
=
\cos x\,dx.
$$
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
f^*(dv\wedge dw)
&=
f^*(dv)\wedge f^*(dw)\\
&=
(dx+2y\,dy)
\wedge
(\cos x\,dx).
\end{aligned}
$$

::: {.proof}
Pullback preserves wedge products.
Apply steps <1>2--<1>3.
:::

<1>5. The wedge product in step <1>4 simplifies to
$$
-2y\cos x\,dx\wedge dy.
$$

::: {.proof}
Expand:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(dx+2y\,dy)\wedge(\cos x\,dx)
&=
\cos x\,dx\wedge dx
+
2y\cos x\,dy\wedge dx\\
&=
0
+
2y\cos x\,dy\wedge dx\\
&=
-2y\cos x\,dx\wedge dy.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here
$$
dx\wedge dx=0
$$
and
$$
dy\wedge dx
=
-dx\wedge dy.
$$
:::

<1>6. Hence
$$
\boxed{
f^*\omega
=
-2y(x^2-y)\cos x\,
dx\wedge dy.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
\omega
=
u\,dv\wedge dw,
$$
one has
$$
f^*\omega
=
(f^*u)
f^*(dv\wedge dw).
$$
Apply step <1>1 and step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required pullback.
:::
:::
