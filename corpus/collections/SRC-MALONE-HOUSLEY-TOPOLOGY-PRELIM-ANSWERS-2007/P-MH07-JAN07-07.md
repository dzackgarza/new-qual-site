::: {.problem}
Consider the vector fields on $\mathbb R^3$
\[
X=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y},
\qquad
Y=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.
\]
Show that there is no nonempty surface $S\subset\mathbb R^3$ tangent to both vector fields at each of its points.
:::

Write
$$
X
=
x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
+
\frac{\partial}{\partial y},
\qquad
Y
=
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
-
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The vector fields $X$ and $Y$ are linearly independent at every point of $\mathbb R^3$.


::: pf-proof

At a point $(x,y,z)$,
$$
X=(x,1,0),
\qquad
Y=(1,0,-1).
$$
Suppose
$$
aX+bY=0.
$$
The $y$-coordinate gives
$$
a=0,
$$
and then the $z$-coordinate gives
$$
b=0.
$$
Thus $X$ and $Y$ are independent everywhere.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Their Lie bracket is
$$
\boxed{
[X,Y]
=
-
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For vector fields
$$
X=\sum_i X^i\partial_i,
\qquad
Y=\sum_i Y^i\partial_i,
$$
one has
$$
[X,Y]
=
\sum_i
\bigl(
X(Y^i)-Y(X^i)
\bigr)\partial_i.
$$

Here the coefficients of $Y$ are constant, so
$$
X(Y^i)=0
$$
for every $i$.
The only nonconstant coefficient of $X$ is
$$
X^x=x,
$$
and
$$
Y(x)
=
\frac{\partial x}{\partial x}
-
\frac{\partial x}{\partial z}
=
1.
$$
Therefore
$$
[X,Y]
=
-\partial_x.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

At no point of $\mathbb R^3$ does
$$
-\partial_x
$$
belong to
$$
\operatorname{span}\{X,Y\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose at some point there were scalars $a,b$ such that
$$
a(x,1,0)+b(1,0,-1)
=
(-1,0,0).
$$
The $y$-coordinate gives
$$
a=0.
$$
The $z$-coordinate then gives
$$
b=0.
$$
The $x$-coordinate would consequently read
$$
0=-1,
$$
a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a nonempty smooth surface
$$
S\subset\mathbb R^3
$$
tangent to both $X$ and $Y$ at each of its points.
Then for every
$$
p\in S,
$$
$$
T_pS
=
\operatorname{span}\{X_p,Y_p\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By hypothesis,
$$
X_p,Y_p\in T_pS.
$$
By step <1>1 they are linearly independent.
Since $S$ is a surface,
$$
\dim T_pS=2.
$$
Thus the two independent tangent vectors form a basis of $T_pS$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Lie bracket
$$
[X,Y]_p
$$
must belong to
$$
T_pS
$$
for every $p\in S$.


::: pf-proof

The restrictions
$$
X|_S
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Y|_S
$$
are vector fields tangent to the submanifold $S$.
The Lie bracket of vector fields tangent to a submanifold is again tangent to that submanifold.
Equivalently, the rank-$2$ distribution
$$
\mathcal D
=
\operatorname{span}\{X,Y\}
$$
would have the integral surface $S$, so Frobenius involutivity requires
$$
[X,Y]_p\in\mathcal D_p=T_pS.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Steps <1>3--<1>5 give a contradiction.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>2,
$$
[X,Y]_p
=
-\partial_x.
$$
Step <1>5 says this vector must lie in
$$
T_pS
=
\operatorname{span}\{X_p,Y_p\}.
$$
But step <1>3 says that this never occurs at any point of $\mathbb R^3$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\text{there is no nonempty surface tangent to both }X\text{ and }Y.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The existence of such a surface leads to the contradiction in step <1>6.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>7 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
