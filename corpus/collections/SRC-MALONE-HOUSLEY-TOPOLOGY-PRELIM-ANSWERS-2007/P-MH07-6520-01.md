::: {.problem}
Let
\[
X=\mathbb{RP}^2\vee\mathbb{RP}^2.
\]
Give an example of an irregular covering space
\[
\widetilde X\longrightarrow X.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
X
=
\mathbb{RP}^2
\vee
\mathbb{RP}^2.
$$
Choose the wedge point as basepoint.

<1>1. One has
$$
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2
*
\mathbb Z/2.
$$

::: {.proof}
The wedge point has a neighborhood satisfying the hypotheses of van Kampen's theorem.
Since
$$
\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2,
$$
van Kampen gives
$$
\pi_1
\left(
\mathbb{RP}^2\vee\mathbb{RP}^2
\right)
\cong
\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)
*
\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2*\mathbb Z/2.
$$
:::

<1>2. Write
$$
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle
a,b
\mid
a^2=b^2=1
\rangle.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the standard presentation of the free product
$$
\mathbb Z/2*\mathbb Z/2.
$$
The generator $a$ comes from the first copy of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ and $b$ from the second.
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
H
\coloneqq
\langle b\rangle
=
\{1,b\}.
$$
Then $H$ is not normal in $\pi_1(X)$.

::: {.proof}
Conjugating $b$ by $a$ gives
$$
aba^{-1}
=
aba
$$
because
$$
a^{-1}=a.
$$
The word
$$
aba
$$
is reduced in the free product
$$
\langle a\rangle*\langle b\rangle.
$$
Therefore it is neither
$$
1
$$
nor
$$
b.
$$
Hence
$$
aba
\notin
H.
$$
Thus
$$
aHa^{-1}
\ne
H,
$$
so $H$ is not normal.
:::

<1>4. There exists a connected covering
$$
p_H:\widetilde X_H\to X
$$
corresponding to the subgroup $H$.

::: {.proof}
The space $X$ is connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected.
Therefore the classification theorem for connected covering spaces applies.
It assigns to the subgroup
$$
H
\le
\pi_1(X)
$$
a connected covering
$$
p_H:\widetilde X_H\to X
$$
whose induced subgroup is $H$ up to conjugacy.

Equivalently, if
$$
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{univ}}
\to
X
$$
is the universal cover, one may take
$$
\widetilde X_H
\coloneqq
H\backslash
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{univ}}.
$$
:::

<1>5. A connected covering of $X$ is regular if and only if its corresponding subgroup of $\pi_1(X)$ is normal.

::: {.proof}
This is the regular-covering criterion in the classification of covering spaces:
for a connected covering corresponding to
$$
H
\le
\pi_1(X),
$$
the covering is regular exactly when
$$
H
\trianglelefteq
\pi_1(X).
$$
:::

<1>6. The covering
$$
p_H:\widetilde X_H\to X
$$
is irregular.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
H
\not\trianglelefteq
\pi_1(X).
$$
Step <1>5 therefore implies that the connected covering corresponding to $H$ is not regular.
Hence it is irregular.
:::

<1>7. Therefore an example is the covering corresponding to
$$
\boxed{
H=\langle b\rangle
\le
\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=1\rangle
\cong
\pi_1
\left(
\mathbb{RP}^2\vee\mathbb{RP}^2
\right).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 constructs this covering and step <1>6 proves that it is irregular.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 gives the required irregular covering space.
:::
:::
