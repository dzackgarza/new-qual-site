::: {.problem}
Let
$$
X=\RP^2\vee\RP^2.
$$
Give an example of an irregular covering space
$$
\widetilde X\longrightarrow X.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
X
=
\RP^2
\vee
\RP^2.
$$
Choose the wedge point as basepoint.

::: pf

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\ZZ/2
*
\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The wedge point has a neighborhood satisfying the hypotheses of van Kampen's theorem.
Since
$$
\pi_1(\RP^2)
\cong
\ZZ/2,
$$
van Kampen gives
$$
\pi_1
\left(
\RP^2\vee\RP^2
\right)
\cong
\pi_1(\RP^2)
*
\pi_1(\RP^2)
\cong
\ZZ/2*\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Write
$$
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle
a,b
\mid
a^2=b^2=1
\rangle.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard presentation of the free product
$$
\ZZ/2*\ZZ/2.
$$
The generator $a$ comes from the first copy of $\RP^2$ and $b$ from the second.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let
$$
H
\coloneqq
\langle b\rangle
=
\{1,b\}.
$$
Then $H$ is not normal in $\pi_1(X)$.


::: pf-proof

Conjugating $b$ by $a$ gives
$$
aba^{-1}
=
aba
$$
because
$$
a^{-1}=a.
$$
The word
$$
aba
$$
is reduced in the free product
$$
\langle a\rangle*\langle b\rangle.
$$
Therefore it is neither
$$
1
$$
nor
$$
b.
$$
Hence
$$
aba
\notin
H.
$$
Thus
$$
aHa^{-1}
\ne
H,
$$
so $H$ is not normal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

There exists a connected covering
$$
p_H:\widetilde X_H\to X
$$
corresponding to the subgroup $H$.


::: pf-proof

The space $X$ is connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected.
Therefore the classification theorem for connected covering spaces applies.
It assigns to the subgroup
$$
H
\le
\pi_1(X)
$$
a connected covering
$$
p_H:\widetilde X_H\to X
$$
whose induced subgroup is $H$ up to conjugacy.

Equivalently, if
$$
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{univ}}
\to
X
$$
is the universal cover, one may take
$$
\widetilde X_H
\coloneqq
H\backslash
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{univ}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

A connected covering of $X$ is regular if and only if its corresponding subgroup of $\pi_1(X)$ is normal.


::: pf-proof

This is the regular-covering criterion in the classification of covering spaces:
for a connected covering corresponding to
$$
H
\le
\pi_1(X),
$$
the covering is regular exactly when
$$
H
\trianglelefteq
\pi_1(X).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The covering
$$
p_H:\widetilde X_H\to X
$$
is irregular.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
H
\not\trianglelefteq
\pi_1(X).
$$
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} therefore implies that the connected covering corresponding to $H$ is not regular.
Hence it is irregular.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore an example is the covering corresponding to
$$
\boxed{
H=\langle b\rangle
\le
\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=1\rangle
\cong
\pi_1
\left(
\RP^2\vee\RP^2
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} constructs this covering and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves that it is irregular.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives the required irregular covering space.

:::
