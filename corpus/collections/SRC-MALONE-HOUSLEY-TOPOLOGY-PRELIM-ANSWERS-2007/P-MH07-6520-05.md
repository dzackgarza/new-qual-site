::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a closed connected $5$-manifold and assume that
$$
\pi_1(M)\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ,
\qquad
H_2(M;\ZZ)=0.
$$
Compute $H_i(M;\ZZ)$ for all $i$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The manifold $M$ is orientable.


::: pf-proof

For a connected manifold, orientability is controlled by the orientation character
$$
w_1:
\pi_1(M)
\to
\{\pm1\}
\cong
\ZZ/2.
$$
The group
$$
\pi_1(M)
\cong
\ZZ/3
$$
has no nontrivial homomorphism to
$$
\ZZ/2,
$$
because the image of an element of order $3$ must have order dividing both $3$ and $2$.
Hence the orientation character is trivial.
Therefore $M$ is orientable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Since $M$ is connected,
$$
H_0(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

The zeroth homology of a connected space is $\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
H_1(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/3.
$$


::: pf-proof

For any path-connected space,
$$
H_1(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\pi_1(M)^{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$
The group
$$
\pi_1(M)
\cong
\ZZ/3
$$
is already abelian.
Thus
$$
H_1(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

By hypothesis,
$$
H_2(M;\ZZ)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is one of the assumptions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The universal coefficient theorem gives
$$
H^0(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

The degree-$0$ universal coefficient sequence is
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}
\bigl(
H_{-1}(M),\ZZ
\bigr)
\to
H^0(M;\ZZ)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}
\bigl(
H_0(M),\ZZ
\bigr)
\to
0.
$$
The first term is zero and, by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ,\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The universal coefficient theorem gives
$$
H^1(M;\ZZ)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The degree-$1$ sequence is
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}
\bigl(
H_0(M),\ZZ
\bigr)
\to
H^1(M;\ZZ)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}
\bigl(
H_1(M),\ZZ
\bigr)
\to
0.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{Ext}(\ZZ,\ZZ)=0.
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
\ZZ/3,\ZZ
\right)
=
0.
$$
Therefore
$$
H^1(M;\ZZ)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The universal coefficient theorem gives
$$
H^2(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/3.
$$


::: pf-proof

The degree-$2$ sequence is
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}
\bigl(
H_1(M),\ZZ
\bigr)
\to
H^2(M;\ZZ)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}
\bigl(
H_2(M),\ZZ
\bigr)
\to
0.
$$
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{Hom}(H_2(M),\ZZ)=0.
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{Ext}
\left(
\ZZ/3,\ZZ
\right)
\cong
\ZZ/3.
$$
Thus
$$
H^2(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_5(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^0(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $M$ is a closed orientable $5$-manifold.
Poincaré duality therefore gives
$$
H_i(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^{5-i}(M;\ZZ)
$$
for all $i$.
Take
$$
i=5
$$
and apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_4(M;\ZZ)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
i=4
$$
in Poincaré duality:
$$
H_4(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^1(M;\ZZ).
$$
Apply step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_3(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/3.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
i=3:
$$
$$
H_3(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^2(M;\ZZ).
$$
Apply step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_i(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,5,\\
\ZZ/3,&i=1,3,\\
0,&i=2,4,\\
0,&i>5.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} give degrees $0,1,2$.
Steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} give degrees $5,4,3$.
Homology vanishes above the dimension of the manifold.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives all integral homology groups of $M$.

:::
