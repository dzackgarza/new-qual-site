::: {.problem}
Give a careful definition of the tangent bundle of a manifold $X$.
Show that the total space of the tangent bundle of $S^2$ is not diffeomorphic to $S^2\times\RR$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The total space of $TS^2$ is a $4$-manifold and $S^2\times\RR$ is a $3$-manifold, so the second sentence follows from invariance of dimension.
The August 2005 exam asks the substantive version, with $S^2\times\RR^2$ in place of $S^2\times\RR$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $X$ be a smooth manifold of dimension $n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The tangent space at a point, and the tangent bundle as a set.

::: pf-proof

For $p\in X$ let $T_pX$ be the set of derivations of the algebra $C^\infty_p$ of germs of smooth functions at $p$, that is the $\RR$-linear maps $v:C^\infty_p\to\RR$ with $v(fg)=v(f)g(p)+f(p)v(g)$. Equivalently, $T_pX$ is the set of equivalence classes of smooth curves $\gamma:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to X$ with $\gamma(0)=p$, two curves being equivalent when they have the same velocity in one, hence every, chart. Both descriptions give an $n$-dimensional real vector space.

As a set, the tangent bundle is the disjoint union
$$
TX=\bigsqcup_{p\in X}T_pX
$$
with projection $\pi:TX\to X$ sending $T_pX$ to $p$; the fibre $\pi^{-1}(p)$ is $T_pX$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The smooth structure and vector-bundle structure on $TX$.

::: pf-proof

Let $(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)$ be a smooth atlas of $X$, with $\varphi_\alpha:U_\alpha\to\RR^n$ and $n=\dim X$. Put $TU_\alpha=\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ and define
$$
\Phi_\alpha:TU_\alpha\longrightarrow\RR^n\times\RR^n,
\qquad
\Phi_\alpha(v)=\bigl(\varphi_\alpha(\pi(v)),\ d(\varphi_\alpha)_{\pi(v)}(v)\bigr),
$$
where $d(\varphi_\alpha)_p:T_pX\to\RR^n$ is the derivative, which is an isomorphism of vector spaces.

The maps $\Phi_\alpha$ are bijections onto open subsets of $\RR^{2n}$. On overlaps,
$$
\Phi_\beta\circ\Phi_\alpha^{-1}(u,w)=\bigl(\psi(u),\ d\psi_u(w)\bigr),
\qquad \psi=\varphi_\beta\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1},
$$
and this is smooth, because $\psi$ is smooth and $d\psi_u$ is its Jacobian matrix, whose entries are smooth functions of $u$. Hence the $\Phi_\alpha$ form a smooth atlas, and $\pi$ is smooth for the resulting structure. Moreover each $\Phi_\alpha$ restricts on each fibre to a linear isomorphism onto $\{u\}\times\RR^n$, and the transitions are fibrewise linear; so $TX$ is a smooth real vector bundle of rank $n$ over $X$, in particular a manifold of dimension $2n$, and $\pi$ restricted to $TX\setminus\{0\}$ (the complement of the zero section) is a bundle of $2n$-dimensional manifolds with $(n-1)$-sphere fibres.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$\dim TS^2=4$ and $\dim(S^2\times\RR)=3$, so $TS^2$ is not diffeomorphic to $S^2\times\RR$.

::: pf-proof

$\dim S^2=2$, so by step [](#s2){.pf-ref} $\dim TS^2=2\cdot2=4$, while $\dim(S^2\times\RR)=2+1=3$. A diffeomorphism is in particular a homeomorphism, and homeomorphic manifolds have the same dimension (invariance of dimension), so no diffeomorphism exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The substantive version, the one the August 2005 exam asks, concerns the two vector bundles over $S^2$: $TS^2$ and $S^2\times\RR^2$ are not isomorphic as vector bundles over $S^2$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $\Phi:TS^2\to S^2\times\RR^2$ were an isomorphism of vector bundles over $S^2$, that is a diffeomorphism carrying each fibre $T_pS^2$ linearly onto $\{p\}\times\RR^2$. The constant section $s(p)=(p,(1,0))$ of $S^2\times\RR^2$ vanishes nowhere, so its preimage
$$
V(p)=\Phi^{-1}\bigl(p,(1,0)\bigr)\in T_pS^2
$$
defines a smooth section $V$ of $TS^2$ that vanishes nowhere, that is, a smooth nowhere-vanishing vector field on $S^2$.

But a closed surface carrying a nowhere-vanishing vector field has vanishing Euler characteristic: the zero section and a small perturbation by $V$ are disjoint, so the self-intersection number of the zero section of $TS^2$ is $0$, and that number equals $\chi(S^2)$. Since $\chi(S^2)=2\ne0$, no such $\Phi$ exists. Equivalently, the Euler number of $TS^2$ is $2$, while that of the trivial rank-two bundle is $0$.

This settles the bundle question. It does **not** by itself settle whether the underlying smooth $4$-manifolds are diffeomorphic, since a diffeomorphism of total spaces need not respect the projections or the fibres; the printed January 2006 statement, which is the one the card carries, is the dimension count of step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::
