::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the quotient of $S^2$ obtained by identifying the north and south poles to a single point. Carefully define a cell-complex structure on $X$, including the attaching maps, and use it to compute
\[
\pi_1(X).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
N,S\in S^2
$$
be the north and south poles, and choose an embedded arc
$$
e
\subset
S^2
$$
joining $N$ to $S$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The sphere $S^2$ admits a CW structure with:

$$
\text{two }0\text{-cells }N,S,
$$
$$
\text{one }1\text{-cell }e
\text{ joining }N\text{ to }S,
$$
and
$$
\text{one }2\text{-cell }D.
$$


::: pf-proof

The embedded arc
$$
e
$$
together with its endpoints is a closed interval in $S^2$.
Its complement is homeomorphic to an open disk.
Thus
$$
S^2
=
\{N,S\}
\cup
e^\circ
\cup
D^\circ
$$
is a CW decomposition with one open cell in dimensions $1$ and $2$ and two vertices.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The attaching map of the $2$-cell traverses the $1$-cell once from $N$ to $S$ and once back from $S$ to $N$.


::: pf-proof

Take a closed disk whose interior maps homeomorphically onto
$$
S^2\setminus e.
$$
The two semicircles of its boundary map to the two sides of the arc $e$.
With the induced boundary orientation, one side traverses $e$ from $N$ to $S$ and the other traverses it from $S$ to $N$.
Thus the attaching path is
$$
e\,e^{-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Form the quotient
$$
X
=
S^2/(N\sim S),
$$
and denote the identified point by
$$
v.
$$
Then $X$ inherits a CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$, one $1$-cell $a$, and one $2$-cell $D$.


::: pf-proof

Identifying the two $0$-cells
$$
N
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
S
$$
turns the interval cell $e$ into a loop based at the single vertex $v$.
Call the resulting $1$-cell $a$.
The open $2$-cell is unaffected by the quotient.
Thus the quotient has exactly the stated cells.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The attaching map of the $2$-cell in $X$ represents the word
$$
aa^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Before taking the quotient, the attaching path is
$$
ee^{-1}
$$
by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
Under the quotient map,
$$
e
$$
becomes the loop
$$
a.
$$
Therefore the attaching path becomes
$$
aa^{-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The $1$-skeleton of $X$ is a circle.


::: pf-proof

There is one vertex
$$
v
$$
and one $1$-cell
$$
a
$$
whose two endpoints are both attached to $v$.
Thus
$$
X^{(1)}
\cong
S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The fundamental group of the $1$-skeleton is
$$
\pi_1(X^{(1)},v)
\cong
\langle a\rangle
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the standard computation
$$
\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ.
$$
The loop $a$ is a generator.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Attaching the $2$-cell imposes the relation
$$
aa^{-1}=1,
$$
which is already trivial.


::: pf-proof

By the cellular form of van Kampen's theorem, attaching a $2$-cell along a loop representing a word $w$ quotients the fundamental group of the $1$-skeleton by the normal closure of $w$.

Here, by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
w
=
aa^{-1}
=
1
$$
already in
$$
\pi_1(X^{(1)}).
$$
Thus no nontrivial relation is added.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\ZZ.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a\mid aa^{-1}=1\rangle
\cong
\langle a\rangle
\cong
\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} give the requested CW structure and attaching map, and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives the fundamental group.

:::
