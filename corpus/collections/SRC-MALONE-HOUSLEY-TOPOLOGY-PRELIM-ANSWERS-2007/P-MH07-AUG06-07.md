::: {.problem}
Give an example of a closed $1$-form on
\[
\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}
\]
which is not exact, and prove both properties.
:::

Define
$$
\boxed{
\omega
\coloneqq
\frac{-y\,dx+x\,dy}{x^2+y^2}
}
$$
on
$$
\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The form $\omega$ is smooth on $\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}$.


::: pf-proof

The denominator
$$
x^2+y^2
$$
is strictly positive away from the origin.
Hence both coefficient functions
$$
\frac{-y}{x^2+y^2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{x}{x^2+y^2}
$$
are smooth on the punctured plane.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Write
$$
\omega
=
P\,dx+Q\,dy
$$
with
$$
P
=
\frac{-y}{x^2+y^2},
\qquad
Q
=
\frac{x}{x^2+y^2}.
$$
Then
$$
d\omega
=
\left(
\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}
-
\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}
\right)
dx\wedge dy.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a $1$-form
$$
P\,dx+Q\,dy,
$$
one has
$$
d(P\,dx+Q\,dy)
=
dP\wedge dx
+
dQ\wedge dy
=
\left(
\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}
-
\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}
\right)
dx\wedge dy.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}
=
\frac{y^2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using the quotient rule,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
\left(
\frac{x}{x^2+y^2}
\right)
&=
\frac{
(x^2+y^2)-2x^2
}{
(x^2+y^2)^2
}\\
&=
\frac{y^2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}
=
\frac{y^2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Again by the quotient rule,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
\left(
\frac{-y}{x^2+y^2}
\right)
&=
\frac{
-(x^2+y^2)+2y^2
}{
(x^2+y^2)^2
}\\
&=
\frac{y^2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
d\omega=0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps <1>3--<1>4 give
$$
\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}
-
\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}
=
0.
$$
Apply step <1>2.
Thus $\omega$ is closed.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
\gamma:[0,2\pi]\to\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}
$$
be the unit circle
$$
\gamma(t)
=
(\cos t,\sin t).
$$
Then
$$
\gamma^*\omega=dt.
$$


::: pf-proof

Along $\gamma$,
$$
x=\cos t,
\qquad
y=\sin t,
$$
so
$$
dx=-\sin t\,dt,
\qquad
dy=\cos t\,dt,
$$
and
$$
x^2+y^2=1.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^*\omega
&=
-\sin t(-\sin t\,dt)
+
\cos t(\cos t\,dt)\\
&=
(\sin^2t+\cos^2t)\,dt\\
&=
dt.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
\oint_\gamma\omega
=
2\pi.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>6,
$$
\oint_\gamma\omega
=
\int_0^{2\pi}dt
=
2\pi.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The form $\omega$ is not exact.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\omega=df
$$
for some smooth function
$$
f:
\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}
\to
\mathbb R.
$$
Then the integral of $\omega$ around every closed curve would vanish:
$$
\oint_\gamma df
=
f(\gamma(2\pi))-f(\gamma(0))
=
0.
$$
But step <1>7 gives
$$
\oint_\gamma\omega
=
2\pi
\ne
0.
$$
Contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\omega
=
\frac{-y\,dx+x\,dy}{x^2+y^2}
\text{ is closed but not exact on }
\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Closedness is step <1>5 and nonexactness is step <1>8.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>9 gives the required example and verifies both properties.

:::
