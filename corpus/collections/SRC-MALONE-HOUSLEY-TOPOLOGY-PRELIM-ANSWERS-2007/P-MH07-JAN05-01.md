::: {.problem}
Let
\[
X=\{(x,y)\in\RR^3\times\RR^3:x\cdot x=y\cdot y=1,\ x\cdot y=0\}.
\]
Show that $X$ is a submanifold of $\RR^3\times\RR^3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Define
$$
F:
\RR^3\times\RR^3
\to
\RR^3
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
F(x,y)
=
\left(
x\cdot x,
y\cdot y,
x\cdot y
\right).
}
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The set $X$ is the level set
$$
\boxed{
X
=
F^{-1}(1,1,0).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
(x,y)\in X
$$
exactly when
$$
x\cdot x=1,
\qquad
y\cdot y=1,
\qquad
x\cdot y=0.
$$
These are precisely the equations
$$
F(x,y)=(1,1,0).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $F$ is smooth.


::: pf-proof

Each component of $F$ is a polynomial in the coordinates of $x$ and $y$.
Hence $F$ is smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The differential of $F$ at $(x,y)$ is
$$
\boxed{
dF_{(x,y)}(u,v)
=
\left(
2x\cdot u,
2y\cdot v,
u\cdot y+x\cdot v
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For real $t$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+tu)\cdot(x+tu)
&=
x\cdot x
+
2t\,x\cdot u
+
t^2u\cdot u,\\
(y+tv)\cdot(y+tv)
&=
y\cdot y
+
2t\,y\cdot v
+
t^2v\cdot v,
\end{aligned}
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+tu)\cdot(y+tv)
&=
x\cdot y
+
t(u\cdot y+x\cdot v)
+
t^2u\cdot v.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the coefficients of $t$ gives the derivative.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Fix
$$
(x,y)\in X
$$
and let
$$
(a,b,c)\in\RR^3
$$
be arbitrary.
Define
$$
u
\coloneqq
\frac a2x+\frac c2y
$$
and
$$
v
\coloneqq
\frac b2y+\frac c2x.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is a choice of tangent vector
$$
(u,v)
\in
\RR^3\times\RR^3
$$
designed to produce the prescribed target vector
$$
(a,b,c).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For the vectors in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
dF_{(x,y)}(u,v)
=
(a,b,c).
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
(x,y)\in X,
$$
one has
$$
x\cdot x=1,
\qquad
y\cdot y=1,
\qquad
x\cdot y=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
2x\cdot u
&=
2x\cdot
\left(
\frac a2x+\frac c2y
\right)\\
&=
a,
\end{aligned}
$$
and similarly
$$
2y\cdot v=b.
$$

For the third component,
$$
\begin{aligned}
u\cdot y+x\cdot v
&=
\left(
\frac a2x+\frac c2y
\right)\cdot y
+
x\cdot
\left(
\frac b2y+\frac c2x
\right)\\
&=
\frac c2
+
\frac c2\\
&=
c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Apply the formula from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence
$$
dF_{(x,y)}
$$
is surjective for every
$$
(x,y)\in X.
$$


::: pf-proof

The vector
$$
(a,b,c)\in\RR^3
$$
was arbitrary in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} constructs a preimage under
$$
dF_{(x,y)}.
$$
Thus the differential is onto.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
(1,1,0)
$$
is a regular value of $F$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the preimage of
$$
(1,1,0)
$$
is $X$.
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives surjectivity of the derivative at every point of this preimage.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
X
\text{ is a smooth submanifold of }
\RR^3\times\RR^3
\cong
\RR^6.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the regular value theorem to the regular level set
$$
F^{-1}(1,1,0).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Moreover,
$$
\boxed{
\dim X
=
6-3
=
3.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The ambient space has dimension $6$, and the target of the regular-value map has dimension $3$.
Thus the level set has codimension $3$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves the submanifold statement, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} records its dimension.

:::
