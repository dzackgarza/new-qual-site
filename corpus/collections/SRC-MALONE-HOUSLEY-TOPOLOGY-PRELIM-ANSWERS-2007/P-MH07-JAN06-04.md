::: {.problem}
State the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem for Lefschetz maps. Compute the Lefschetz number of the identity map
$$
\operatorname{id}:\CP^2\to\CP^2
$$
by first perturbing it to a Lefschetz map and then applying the theorem.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Lefschetz fixed-point theorem for Lefschetz maps.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
f:M\to M
$$
be a smooth self-map of a compact manifold whose fixed points are isolated and nondegenerate, meaning
$$
\det
\left(
I-Df_p
\right)
\ne0
$$
at every fixed point $p$.
Then
$$
\boxed{
L(f)
=
\sum_{p\in\operatorname{Fix}(f)}
\operatorname{ind}_p(f),
}
$$
where
$$
\operatorname{ind}_p(f)
=
\operatorname{sgn}
\det_{\RR}
\left(
I-Df_p
\right).
$$
In particular, if
$$
L(f)\ne0,
$$
then $f$ has a fixed point.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Fix
$$
\varepsilon>0
$$
and define
$$
f_\varepsilon:
\CP^2
\to
\CP^2
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
f_\varepsilon
\bigl(
[z_0:z_1:z_2]
\bigr)
=
[z_0:(1+\varepsilon)z_1:(1+2\varepsilon)z_2].
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The diagonal linear map
$$
\operatorname{diag}
\left(
1,
1+\varepsilon,
1+2\varepsilon
\right)
$$
is invertible and therefore induces a smooth projective transformation of
$$
\CP^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The map $f_\varepsilon$ is homotopic to the identity.


::: pf-proof

For
$$
0\le t\le1,
$$
define
$$
H_t
\bigl(
[z_0:z_1:z_2]
\bigr)
\coloneqq
[z_0:(1+t\varepsilon)z_1:(1+2t\varepsilon)z_2].
$$
All diagonal coefficients are positive, so each $H_t$ is well-defined.
Moreover,
$$
H_0=\operatorname{id}
$$
and
$$
H_1=f_\varepsilon.
$$
Thus
$$
f_\varepsilon
\simeq
\operatorname{id}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The fixed points of $f_\varepsilon$ are exactly
$$
p_0=[1:0:0],
\qquad
p_1=[0:1:0],
\qquad
p_2=[0:0:1].
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\lambda_0=1,
\qquad
\lambda_1=1+\varepsilon,
\qquad
\lambda_2=1+2\varepsilon.
$$
A projective point
$$
[z_0:z_1:z_2]
$$
is fixed exactly when there is a nonzero scalar $\mu$ such that
$$
\lambda_i z_i
=
\mu z_i
$$
for
$$
i=0,1,2.
$$
Since the $\lambda_i$ are pairwise distinct, at most one coordinate can be nonzero.
Thus the only fixed points are the three coordinate lines.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In the affine chart
$$
z_i\ne0
$$
around $p_i$, the derivative of $f_\varepsilon$ has complex eigenvalues
$$
\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i},
\qquad
j\ne i.
$$


::: pf-proof

Normalize
$$
z_i=1
$$
and use local complex coordinates
$$
u_j
\coloneqq
\frac{z_j}{z_i},
\qquad
j\ne i.
$$
Then
$$
u_j
\longmapsto
\frac{\lambda_jz_j}{\lambda_i z_i}
=
\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i}u_j.
$$
Thus the derivative is complex diagonal with the stated eigenvalues.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every fixed point is nondegenerate.


::: pf-proof

At $p_i$, the eigenvalues of
$$
Df_\varepsilon
$$
are
$$
\lambda_j/\lambda_i
$$
with
$$
j\ne i.
$$
Because the $\lambda_i$ are pairwise distinct,
$$
\lambda_j/\lambda_i\ne1.
$$
Hence
$$
I-Df_{\varepsilon,p_i}
$$
is invertible.
Therefore every fixed point is nondegenerate.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The local Lefschetz index at each fixed point is $+1$.


::: pf-proof

At $p_i$, the complex eigenvalues of
$$
I-Df_{\varepsilon,p_i}
$$
are the real nonzero numbers
$$
1-\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i},
\qquad
j\ne i.
$$
Multiplication by a real scalar $c$ on one complex coordinate is, as a real linear map on
$$
\RR^2,
$$
represented by
$$
cI_2
$$
and has determinant
$$
c^2>0
$$
when
$$
c\ne0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\det_{\RR}
\left(
I-Df_{\varepsilon,p_i}
\right)
=
\prod_{j\ne i}
\left(
1-\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i}
\right)^2
>
0.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{ind}_{p_i}(f_\varepsilon)=+1
$$
for
$$
i=0,1,2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The Lefschetz number of $f_\varepsilon$ is
$$
L(f_\varepsilon)=3.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
L(f_\varepsilon)
&=
\sum_{p\in\operatorname{Fix}(f_\varepsilon)}
\operatorname{ind}_p(f_\varepsilon)\\
&=
1+1+1\\
&=
3.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Lefschetz number is invariant under homotopy.


::: pf-proof

The Lefschetz number is defined as an alternating sum of traces of the induced maps on homology:
$$
L(f)
=
\sum_i
(-1)^i
\operatorname{tr}(f_*\restrict H_i).
$$
Homotopic maps induce the same maps on homology, hence have the same Lefschetz number.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
L
\left(
\operatorname{id}_{\CP^2}
\right)
=
3.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
f_\varepsilon
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_{\CP^2}.
$$
Thus step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives
$$
L
\left(
\operatorname{id}_{\CP^2}
\right)
=
L(f_\varepsilon).
$$
Apply step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} states the theorem, and step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} computes the requested Lefschetz number via a Lefschetz perturbation.
