::: {.problem}
Give an example of an irregular (that is, non-normal) covering space, and prove that the covering is irregular.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
X
\coloneqq
\RP^2\vee\RP^2.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle
a,b
\mid
a^2=b^2=1
\rangle
\cong
C_2*C_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Each copy of
$$
\RP^2
$$
has fundamental group
$$
C_2.
$$
By van Kampen's theorem, the fundamental group of the wedge is the free product of the two groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Let
$$
H
\coloneqq
\langle b\rangle
=
\{1,b\}.
$$
Then $H$ is not normal in
$$
\pi_1(X).
$$


::: pf-proof

Conjugate
$$
b
$$
by
$$
a.
$$
Since
$$
a^{-1}=a,
$$
one gets
$$
aba^{-1}
=
aba.
$$
The word
$$
aba
$$
is reduced in the free product
$$
C_2*C_2.
$$
Therefore it is neither
$$
1
$$
nor
$$
b.
$$
Hence
$$
aba
\notin
H.
$$
Thus
$$
aHa^{-1}
\ne
H,
$$
so $H$ is not normal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

There exists a connected covering
$$
p:\widetilde X_H\to X
$$
corresponding to the subgroup $H$.


::: pf-proof

The space
$$
X
$$
is connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected.
Therefore the classification theorem for connected covering spaces applies.
It associates to
$$
H\le\pi_1(X)
$$
a connected covering whose induced subgroup is $H$ up to conjugacy.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

A connected covering is regular if and only if its corresponding subgroup is normal.


::: pf-proof

This is the regular-covering criterion from covering-space classification.
For a connected covering corresponding to
$$
H\le\pi_1(X),
$$
regularity is equivalent to
$$
H\trianglelefteq\pi_1(X).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The covering
$$
p:\widetilde X_H\to X
$$
is irregular.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
H
$$
is not normal.
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the corresponding connected covering cannot be regular.
Hence it is irregular.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde X_H\to
\RP^2\vee\RP^2
}
$$
is an example of an irregular covering.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} constructs the covering and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} proves irregularity.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} supplies the required example and proof.

:::
