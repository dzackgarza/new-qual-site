::: {.problem}
Show that the set of real $2\times2$ matrices of rank $1$ is a three-dimensional submanifold of
$$
M(2,2)\cong\RR^4.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Identify
$$
M(2,2)
\cong
\RR^4
$$
by
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix}
\longleftrightarrow
(a,b,c,d).
$$

Define
$$
F:M(2,2)\to\RR
$$
by
$$
F(A)
\coloneqq
\det A.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

On the open set
$$
U
\coloneqq
M(2,2)\setminus\{0\},
$$
the rank-one locus is exactly
$$
F^{-1}(0)\cap U.
$$


::: pf-proof

A real $2\times2$ matrix has rank less than $2$ exactly when its determinant is zero.
Thus
$$
F(A)=0
$$
means
$$
\operatorname{rank}A\le1.
$$
On $U$ the zero matrix has been excluded, so the rank cannot be $0$.
Therefore
$$
A\in F^{-1}(0)\cap U
\iff
\operatorname{rank}A=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix},
$$
then for
$$
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta
\end{pmatrix},
$$
$$
dF_A(H)
=
d\alpha
-
c\beta
-
b\gamma
+
a\delta.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
F(a,b,c,d)
=
ad-bc.
$$
Therefore
$$
dF
=
d\,da
-
c\,db
-
b\,dc
+
a\,dd.
$$
Evaluating this differential on
$$
H=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)
$$
gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If
$$
A\ne0,
$$
then
$$
dF_A
$$
is not the zero linear map.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the coefficients of the linear functional
$$
dF_A
$$
are
$$
(d,-c,-b,a).
$$
If
$$
dF_A=0,
$$
then all four coefficients vanish:
$$
a=b=c=d=0.
$$
This would give
$$
A=0,
$$
contrary to the assumption.
Hence
$$
dF_A\ne0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every rank-one matrix $A$, the differential
$$
dF_A:T_AM(2,2)\to\RR
$$
is surjective.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, a rank-one matrix is nonzero.
Thus step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
dF_A\ne0.
$$
A nonzero linear map from a real vector space to the one-dimensional space
$$
\RR
$$
is surjective.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore $0$ is a regular value of
$$
F\restrict_U:U\to\RR.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
A\in(F\restrict_U)^{-1}(0).
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $A$ has rank $1$.
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows
$$
d(F\restrict_U)_A
$$
is surjective.
Hence every point of the zero level is regular.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The rank-one locus is a smooth submanifold of codimension $1$ in $\RR^4$.


::: pf-proof

The set
$$
U=M(2,2)\setminus\{0\}
$$
is an open submanifold of
$$
\RR^4.
$$
By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, $0$ is a regular value of
$$
F\restrict_U.
$$
The regular value theorem therefore gives
$$
(F\restrict_U)^{-1}(0)
$$
as a smooth submanifold of $U$ of codimension
$$
\dim\RR=1.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, this level set is exactly the rank-one locus.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Consequently, the rank-one locus has dimension
$$
\boxed{3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The ambient manifold has dimension $4$, and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives codimension $1$.
Thus
$$
\dim
\{A\in M_2(\RR):\operatorname{rank}A=1\}
=
4-1
=
3.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} prove that the rank-one real $2\times2$ matrices form a three-dimensional submanifold of $M(2,2)\cong\RR^4$.

:::
