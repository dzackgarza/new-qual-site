::: {.problem}
Let $f:M\to N$ be a map of degree $1$ between smooth, closed, connected, oriented $n$-manifolds. Prove that
\[
f_*:\pi_1(M)\to\pi_1(N)
\]
is surjective.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix basepoints
$$
m_0\in M,
\qquad
n_0=f(m_0)\in N,
$$
and set
$$
H
\coloneqq
f_*\pi_1(M,m_0)
\le
\pi_1(N,n_0).
$$

<1>1. Let
$$
p:(\widetilde N_H,\widetilde n_0)\to(N,n_0)
$$
be the connected covering corresponding to the subgroup $H$.

::: {.proof}
The closed manifold $N$ is connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected.
Therefore the classification theorem for connected covering spaces assigns to
$$
H
\le
\pi_1(N,n_0)
$$
a connected covering with
$$
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde N_H,\widetilde n_0)=H.
$$
:::

<1>2. The map $f$ lifts to
$$
\widetilde f:
(M,m_0)\to
(\widetilde N_H,\widetilde n_0)
$$
such that
$$
p\circ\widetilde f=f.
$$

::: {.proof}
The lifting criterion states that a based map
$$
f:(M,m_0)\to(N,n_0)
$$
lifts to the covering $p$ exactly when
$$
f_*\pi_1(M,m_0)
\subseteq
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde N_H,\widetilde n_0).
$$
By definition, both groups are $H$.
Thus the lift exists.
:::

<1>3. The manifold $\widetilde N_H$ is orientable.

::: {.proof}
The covering map
$$
p:\widetilde N_H\to N
$$
is a local diffeomorphism.
Pull back the chosen orientation of $N$ through each local diffeomorphism chart.
These local orientations agree on overlaps because they all come from the same orientation of $N$.
Thus $\widetilde N_H$ is orientable.
:::

<1>4. If
$$
[\pi_1(N):H]
$$
were infinite, then
$$
\widetilde N_H
$$
would be noncompact.

::: {.proof}
The number of sheets of the connected covering $p$ equals the index
$$
[\pi_1(N):H].
$$
If the total space $\widetilde N_H$ were compact, then for every
$$
y\in N
$$
the discrete fiber
$$
p^{-1}(y)
$$
would be a compact discrete space and therefore finite.
Thus the covering would have finitely many sheets.
Hence an infinite-sheeted covering of the compact base $N$ must be noncompact.
:::

<1>5. If
$$
\widetilde N_H
$$
is noncompact, then
$$
H_n(\widetilde N_H;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The space $\widetilde N_H$ is a connected orientable noncompact $n$-manifold by steps <1>3--<1>4.
For any connected orientable noncompact $n$-manifold, the top-dimensional integral homology vanishes:
$$
H_n(-;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$
:::

<1>6. The index
$$
[\pi_1(N):H]
$$
is finite.

::: {.proof}
Suppose the index were infinite.
Then steps <1>4--<1>5 give
$$
H_n(\widetilde N_H;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\widetilde f_*[M]=0
$$
in top homology.
Using
$$
f=p\circ\widetilde f,
$$
one obtains
$$
f_*[M]
=
p_*\widetilde f_*[M]
=
0.
$$
But
$$
\deg f=1,
$$
so
$$
f_*[M]
=
[N]
\ne
0
$$
in
$$
H_n(N;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
Contradiction.
Thus the index is finite.
:::

<1>7. Let
$$
d
\coloneqq
[\pi_1(N):H].
$$
Then
$$
p:\widetilde N_H\to N
$$
is a $d$-sheeted covering.

::: {.proof}
For a connected covering, the number of sheets equals the index of the corresponding subgroup.
Apply step <1>6.
:::

<1>8. The covering map $p$ has degree
$$
\deg p=\pm d.
$$

::: {.proof}
A finite $d$-sheeted covering between closed connected oriented $n$-manifolds has degree
$$
\pm d.
$$
The total space is closed because it is a finite-sheeted cover of the compact manifold $N$.
Apply this covering-degree formula to $p$.
:::

<1>9. Degree is multiplicative under composition:
$$
\deg f
=
(\deg p)(\deg\widetilde f).
$$

::: {.proof}
For maps between closed connected oriented $n$-manifolds,
$$
(p\circ\widetilde f)_*
=
p_*\circ\widetilde f_*
$$
on
$$
H_n(-;\mathbb Z).
$$
If
$$
\widetilde f_*[M]
=
(\deg\widetilde f)[\widetilde N_H]
$$
and
$$
p_*[\widetilde N_H]
=
(\deg p)[N],
$$
then
$$
f_*[M]
=
(\deg p)(\deg\widetilde f)[N].
$$
Thus the displayed formula holds.
:::

<1>10. Since
$$
\deg f=1,
$$
one must have
$$
d=1.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>8--<1>9,
$$
1
=
\deg f
=
(\pm d)(\deg\widetilde f).
$$
Both
$$
d
$$
and
$$
\deg\widetilde f
$$
are integers.
Therefore the positive integer $d$ divides $1$, forcing
$$
d=1.
$$
:::

<1>11. Hence
$$
H=\pi_1(N,n_0).
$$

::: {.proof}
By definition,
$$
d
=
[\pi_1(N):H].
$$
Step <1>10 gives
$$
d=1.
$$
A subgroup of index $1$ is the whole group.
:::

<1>12. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
f_*:\pi_1(M)\to\pi_1(N)
\text{ is surjective}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The image of $f_*$ is $H$ by definition.
Step <1>11 identifies $H$ with all of
$$
\pi_1(N).
$$
:::

<1>13. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>12 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
