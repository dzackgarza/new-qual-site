::: {.problem}
Prove that the tangent bundle $TM$ of a smooth manifold $M$, viewed as a smooth manifold in its own right, is orientable.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\dim M=n.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
$$
(U,\varphi)
$$
be a coordinate chart on $M$, with
$$
\varphi
=
(x^1,\ldots,x^n):
U\to\RR^n.
$$
Then $TM|_U$ has induced coordinates
$$
\widetilde\varphi:
TM|_U
\to
\varphi(U)\times\RR^n
\subset
\RR^{2n}
$$
given by
$$
\widetilde\varphi
\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
v^i
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\bigg|_p
\right)
=
\left(
x^1(p),\ldots,x^n(p),
v^1,\ldots,v^n
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

Over a coordinate chart, the tangent bundle is trivialized by the coordinate frame
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^1},
\ldots,
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^n}.
$$
Thus each tangent vector is uniquely described by its base coordinates and its coefficients in this frame.
These are the standard smooth bundle coordinates on $TM$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
(V,\psi)
$$
be another chart with
$$
\psi
=
(y^1,\ldots,y^n).
$$
On
$$
U\cap V,
$$
write the base-coordinate transition as
$$
y
=
\Psi(x),
$$
where
$$
\Psi
\coloneqq
\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the usual coordinate transition on the base manifold.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The induced coordinate transition on $TM$ is
$$
\boxed{
(x,v)
\longmapsto
\left(
\Psi(x),
D\Psi_x\,v
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\xi
=
\sum_i
v^i
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\bigg|_p
$$
be a tangent vector.
Under the coordinate change
$$
y=\Psi(x),
$$
its coordinate vector transforms by the derivative:
$$
\left(
w^1,\ldots,w^n
\right)^T
=
D\Psi_x
\left(
v^1,\ldots,v^n
\right)^T.
$$
Thus the tangent-bundle chart transition is exactly the displayed map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The Jacobian matrix of the transition in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} has block form
$$
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
D\Psi_x & 0\\
* & D\Psi_x
\end{pmatrix}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
(x,v)
\longmapsto
\left(
\Psi(x),
D\Psi_xv
\right).
$$

The derivative of the first component with respect to $x$ is
$$
D\Psi_x,
$$
and with respect to $v$ is zero.

The derivative of the second component with respect to $v$ is again
$$
D\Psi_x.
$$
Its derivative with respect to $x$ involves the second derivatives of $\Psi$ applied to $v$; these entries form the lower-left block denoted by
$$
*.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The determinant of the tangent-bundle transition Jacobian is
$$
\boxed{
\det(D\Psi_x)^2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The matrix in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is block triangular.
Therefore its determinant is the product of the determinants of the two diagonal blocks:
$$
\det(D\Psi_x)
\det(D\Psi_x)
=
\det(D\Psi_x)^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Every tangent-bundle coordinate transition has positive Jacobian determinant.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
\Psi
$$
is a coordinate change on $M$,
$$
D\Psi_x
$$
is invertible.
Hence
$$
\det(D\Psi_x)\ne0.
$$
By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
\det
\left(
D(\widetilde\psi\circ\widetilde\varphi^{-1})
\right)
=
\det(D\Psi_x)^2
>
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Thus the tangent-bundle atlas
$$
\{
(TM|_U,\widetilde\varphi)
\}
$$
is an oriented atlas.


::: pf-proof

An atlas on a smooth manifold is oriented when every transition map between overlapping charts has positive Jacobian determinant.
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} verifies exactly this condition for the standard tangent-bundle atlas.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
TM
\text{ is orientable}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The manifold
$$
TM
$$
admits the oriented atlas from step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.
Thus it is orientable.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
