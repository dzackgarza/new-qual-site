::: {.problem}
Let $V,W:\mathbb R^4\to\mathbb R^4$ be the vector fields
\[
V(x,y,z,w)=(y,-x,w,-z),
\qquad
W(x,y,z,w)=(w,z,-y,-x).
\]
Is there a nonempty surface $\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^4$ such that
\[
V(p),W(p)\in T_p\Sigma
\]
for every $p\in\Sigma$? Find such a surface or prove that none exists.
:::

Write
$$
p=(x,y,z,w)\in\mathbb R^4.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\langle V(p),W(p)\rangle=0
$$
for every $p$.


::: pf-proof

Compute
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle V,W\rangle
&=
yw+(-x)z+w(-y)+(-z)(-x)\\
&=
yw-xz-wy+zx\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\|V(p)\|^2
=
\|W(p)\|^2
=
x^2+y^2+z^2+w^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Directly,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|V\|^2
&=
y^2+x^2+w^2+z^2,\\
\|W\|^2
&=
w^2+z^2+y^2+x^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If
$$
p\ne0,
$$
then
$$
V(p)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
W(p)
$$
are linearly independent.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>2, both vectors are nonzero when
$$
p\ne0.
$$
By step <1>1 they are orthogonal.
Two nonzero orthogonal vectors are linearly independent.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Lie bracket is
$$
\boxed{
[V,W]
=
2(-z,w,x,-y).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Using coordinates
$$
(x,y,z,w),
$$
the bracket components are
$$
[V,W]^i
=
V(W^i)-W(V^i).
$$

For the first component,
$$
V(w)-W(y)
=
(-z)-z
=
-2z.
$$

For the second,
$$
V(z)-W(-x)
=
w-(-w)
=
2w.
$$

For the third,
$$
V(-y)-W(w)
=
x-(-x)
=
2x.
$$

For the fourth,
$$
V(-x)-W(-z)
=
-y-y
=
-2y.
$$
Combining the components gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
U
\coloneqq
(-z,w,x,-y).
$$
Then
$$
\langle U,V\rangle
=
\langle U,W\rangle
=
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Compute
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle U,V\rangle
&=
(-z)y+w(-x)+xw+(-y)(-z)\\
&=
-zy-wx+xw+yz\\
&=
0,
\end{aligned}
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle U,W\rangle
&=
(-z)w+wz+x(-y)+(-y)(-x)\\
&=
-zw+wz-xy+xy\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If
$$
p\ne0,
$$
then
$$
[V,W](p)
\notin
\operatorname{span}
\{V(p),W(p)\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>4,
$$
[V,W](p)=2U(p).
$$
Moreover,
$$
\|U(p)\|^2
=
x^2+y^2+z^2+w^2.
$$
Thus
$$
U(p)\ne0
$$
when
$$
p\ne0.
$$

By step <1>5, $U(p)$ is orthogonal to both
$$
V(p)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
W(p).
$$
By step <1>3 these two vectors span a $2$-plane.
A nonzero vector orthogonal to that plane cannot lie in it.
Hence
$$
[V,W](p)
\notin
\operatorname{span}\{V(p),W(p)\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a nonempty smooth surface
$$
\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^4
$$
such that
$$
V(p),W(p)\in T_p\Sigma
$$
for every
$$
p\in\Sigma.
$$
Then $\Sigma$ contains a point
$$
p\ne0.
$$


::: pf-proof

A nonempty $2$-dimensional submanifold cannot be contained in the singleton
$$
\{0\}.
$$
Hence some point of $\Sigma$ is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

At such a nonzero point
$$
p\in\Sigma,
$$
one has
$$
T_p\Sigma
=
\operatorname{span}
\{V(p),W(p)\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By hypothesis,
$$
V(p),W(p)\in T_p\Sigma.
$$
By step <1>3 they are linearly independent.
Since
$$
\dim T_p\Sigma=2,
$$
they form a basis of the tangent plane.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Lie bracket
$$
[V,W](p)
$$
must belong to
$$
T_p\Sigma.
$$


::: pf-proof

The restrictions
$$
V|_\Sigma
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
W|_\Sigma
$$
are tangent vector fields on $\Sigma$.
The Lie bracket of two vector fields tangent to a submanifold is again tangent to that submanifold.
Therefore
$$
[V,W](p)\in T_p\Sigma.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Steps <1>6, <1>8, and <1>9 give a contradiction.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>8 says
$$
T_p\Sigma
=
\operatorname{span}\{V(p),W(p)\}.
$$
Step <1>9 says
$$
[V,W](p)\in T_p\Sigma.
$$
But step <1>6 says
$$
[V,W](p)
\notin
\operatorname{span}\{V(p),W(p)\}.
$$
Contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\text{there is no nonempty surface }
\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^4
\text{ tangent to both }V\text{ and }W.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The existence of such a surface leads to the contradiction in step <1>10.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>11 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
