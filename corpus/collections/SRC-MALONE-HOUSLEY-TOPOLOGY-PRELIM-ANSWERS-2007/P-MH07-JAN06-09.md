::: {.problem}
Two covering spaces
$$
p_0:X_0\to X,
\qquad
p_1:X_1\to X
$$
are called isomorphic if there is a homeomorphism $h:X_0\to X_1$ such that
$$
p_0=p_1\circ h.
$$
Assume throughout that all spaces are path-connected, locally path-connected,
and semilocally path-connected.

(a) Let
$$
x\in X,
\qquad
x_0\in X_0,
\qquad
x_1\in X_1
$$
be basepoints such that
$$
p_0(x_0)=p_1(x_1)=x.
$$
Prove that the covers are isomorphic if and only if
$$
(p_0)_*\pi_1(X_0,x_0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(p_1)_*\pi_1(X_1,x_1)
$$
are conjugate subgroups of
$$
\pi_1(X,x).
$$

(b) Show that there are non-isomorphic covers of a genus-two surface of every
degree.
:::

::: {.solution}

Put
$$
H_i
\coloneqq
(p_i)_*\pi_1(X_i,x_i)
\le
\pi_1(X,x),
\qquad
i=0,1.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Suppose first that
$$
h:X_0\to X_1
$$
is an isomorphism of covers:
$$
p_0=p_1\circ h.
$$
Set
$$
x_1'=h(x_0).
$$
Then
$$
(p_0)_*\pi_1(X_0,x_0)
=
(p_1)_*\pi_1(X_1,x_1').
$$


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
h:(X_0,x_0)\to(X_1,x_1')
$$
is a homeomorphism, hence
$$
h_*:
\pi_1(X_0,x_0)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\pi_1(X_1,x_1').
$$
Since
$$
p_0=p_1\circ h,
$$
one has
$$
(p_0)_*
=
(p_1)_*\circ h_*.
$$
Taking images gives the equality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The subgroups
$$
(p_1)_*\pi_1(X_1,x_1')
$$
and
$$
H_1
$$
are conjugate in
$$
\pi_1(X,x).
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose a path
$$
\eta
$$
in
$$
X_1
$$
from
$$
x_1
$$
to
$$
x_1'.
$$
Then
$$
\lambda
\coloneqq
p_1\circ\eta
$$
is a loop at
$$
x.
$$

Changing basepoint from
$$
x_1
$$
to
$$
x_1'
$$
by
$$
\eta
$$
conjugates fundamental-group classes.
After applying
$$
(p_1)_*,
$$
the conjugating element is
$$
[\lambda]\in\pi_1(X,x).
$$
Thus the two image subgroups are conjugate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Hence isomorphic covers determine conjugate subgroups
$$
H_0
$$
and
$$
H_1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} identifies
$$
H_0
$$
with the image subgroup based at
$$
x_1',
$$
and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that this subgroup is conjugate to
$$
H_1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Conversely, suppose
$$
H_0
$$
and
$$
H_1
$$
are conjugate.
Then there exists a point
$$
x_1'\in p_1^{-1}(x)
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{
(p_1)_*\pi_1(X_1,x_1')
=
H_0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose
$$
[\lambda]\in\pi_1(X,x)
$$
which realizes the conjugacy.
Lift a representative loop
$$
\lambda
$$
to a path
$$
\widetilde\lambda
$$
in
$$
X_1
$$
starting at
$$
x_1.
$$
Because
$$
\lambda
$$
is a loop,
$$
x_1'
\coloneqq
\widetilde\lambda(1)
$$
lies in the same fiber
$$
p_1^{-1}(x).
$$

The basepoint-change computation from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} says that the image subgroup
at
$$
x_1'
$$
is the corresponding conjugate of
$$
H_1.
$$
Choose the orientation of
$$
\lambda
$$
or its inverse so that this conjugate is exactly
$$
H_0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

There is a unique lift
$$
h:(X_0,x_0)\to(X_1,x_1')
$$
of
$$
p_0:X_0\to X
$$
through
$$
p_1,
$$
so that
$$
\boxed{
p_1\circ h=p_0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The lifting criterion for the covering
$$
p_1:(X_1,x_1')\to(X,x)
$$
requires
$$
(p_0)_*\pi_1(X_0,x_0)
\subseteq
(p_1)_*\pi_1(X_1,x_1').
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives equality of these two groups.
Therefore the lift exists.
Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of lifts on the path-connected space
$$
X_0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Similarly, there is a unique lift
$$
k:(X_1,x_1')\to(X_0,x_0)
$$
of
$$
p_1
$$
through
$$
p_0,
$$
with
$$
p_0\circ k=p_1.
$$


::: pf-proof

The same lifting criterion applies in the reverse direction because the two
image subgroups are equal by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The maps
$$
h
\quad\text{and}\quad
k
$$
are inverse homeomorphisms.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
p_0\circ(k\circ h)
=
p_1\circ h
=
p_0.
$$
Both
$$
k\circ h
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{id}_{X_0}
$$
are lifts of
$$
p_0
$$
through
$$
p_0
$$
which send
$$
x_0
$$
to
$$
x_0.
$$
Uniqueness of lifts gives
$$
k\circ h
=
\operatorname{id}_{X_0}.
$$

Likewise,
$$
h\circ k
=
\operatorname{id}_{X_1}.
$$
Thus
$$
h
$$
is a homeomorphism, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
p_0=p_1\circ h.
$$
So the covers are isomorphic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
p_0\text{ and }p_1\text{ are isomorphic}
\iff
H_0\text{ and }H_1\text{ are conjugate in }\pi_1(X,x).}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} prove the forward implication.
Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} prove the converse.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
\Sigma_2
$$
be a closed orientable surface of genus
$$
2.
$$
Its fundamental group has presentation
$$
\pi_1(\Sigma_2)
\cong
\left\langle
a,b,c,d
\mid
[a,b][c,d]=1
\right\rangle.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard polygonal presentation of the genus-two surface group.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every integer
$$
m\ge1,
$$
there is a surjective homomorphism
$$
\varphi_m:
\pi_1(\Sigma_2)
\twoheadrightarrow
\ZZ/m\ZZ
$$
defined by
$$
\varphi_m(a)=1,
\qquad
\varphi_m(b)=\varphi_m(c)=\varphi_m(d)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The target is abelian, so every commutator maps to
$$
0.
$$
Thus the relator
$$
[a,b][c,d]
$$
maps to
$$
0
$$
and the assignment defines a homomorphism.
It is surjective because
$$
1
$$
is in its image.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The kernel
$$
K_m
\coloneqq
\ker\varphi_m
$$
has index
$$
\boxed{m}
$$
in
$$
\pi_1(\Sigma_2).
$$


::: pf-proof

By the first isomorphism theorem,
$$
\pi_1(\Sigma_2)/K_m
\cong
\ZZ/m\ZZ.
$$
Hence
$$
[\pi_1(\Sigma_2):K_m]
=
m.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

For every
$$
m\ge1,
$$
there is a connected
$$
m
$$
-sheeted covering
$$
p_m:\widetilde\Sigma_m\to\Sigma_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The surface
$$
\Sigma_2
$$
is connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected.
The covering-space classification theorem therefore assigns a connected
cover to the subgroup
$$
K_m.
$$
The number of sheets equals the subgroup index, which is
$$
m
$$
by step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

If
$$
m\ne n,
$$
then the covers
$$
p_m
$$
and
$$
p_n
$$
are not isomorphic.


::: pf-proof

An isomorphism of covering spaces over
$$
\Sigma_2
$$
restricts to a bijection between the fibers over every basepoint.
The fibers of
$$
p_m
$$
and
$$
p_n
$$
have cardinalities
$$
m
$$
and
$$
n,
$$
respectively.
Thus an isomorphism is impossible when
$$
m\ne n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Consequently, a genus-two surface has pairwise non-isomorphic connected
covers of every positive degree.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} constructs a connected cover of every positive degree, and
step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} shows that covers of distinct degrees are non-isomorphic.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
