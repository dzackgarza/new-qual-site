::: {.problem}
Define the Lefschetz index of an isolated fixed point of a smooth map $f:M\to M$. Compute the Lefschetz index at $0$ of
$$
f:\CC\to\CC,
\qquad
f(z)=z+z^m.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume
$$
m\ge1.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let
$$
p
$$
be an isolated fixed point of a smooth map
$$
f:M^n\to M^n.
$$
Choose an oriented coordinate chart
$$
\phi:U\to\RR^n
$$
with
$$
\phi(p)=0
$$
and a sufficiently small closed ball
$$
\overline B_\varepsilon(0)
\subset
\phi(U)
$$
containing no fixed point of $f$ other than $p$.
The Lefschetz index of $f$ at $p$ is
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{ind}_p(f)
\coloneqq
\deg
\left(
S^{n-1}_\varepsilon
\to
S^{n-1},
\quad
x
\mapsto
\frac{x-\widetilde f(x)}
{\norm{x-\widetilde f(x)}}
\right),
}
$$
where
$$
\widetilde f
\coloneqq
\phi\circ f\circ\phi^{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $p$ is an isolated fixed point, after shrinking the chart one has
$$
x-\widetilde f(x)\ne0
$$
on the boundary sphere
$$
S^{n-1}_\varepsilon.
$$
Thus the normalized displacement map is well-defined.
Its topological degree is independent of all sufficiently small choices and is the local Lefschetz index.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For
$$
f(z)=z+z^m,
$$
the origin is an isolated fixed point.


::: pf-proof

A fixed point satisfies
$$
z+z^m=z,
$$
so
$$
z^m=0.
$$
Hence
$$
z=0.
$$
Thus $0$ is the unique fixed point of $f$ and in particular is isolated.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The displacement map is
$$
z-f(z)
=
-z^m.
$$


::: pf-proof

Directly,
$$
z-(z+z^m)
=
-z^m.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

On the circle
$$
\abs z=\varepsilon,
$$
the normalized displacement map is
$$
\frac{-z^m}{\abs{z^m}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
z-f(z)
=
-z^m.
$$
Since
$$
z\ne0
$$
on the boundary circle,
$$
\abs{z^m}>0,
$$
and normalization gives the displayed map.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Parametrize the boundary circle by
$$
z
=
\varepsilon e^{it},
\qquad
0\le t\le2\pi.
$$
Then the normalized displacement map is
$$
e^{it}
\longmapsto
e^{i(mt+\pi)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
-z^m
=
-\varepsilon^m e^{imt}
=
\varepsilon^m e^{i(mt+\pi)}.
$$
Dividing by its modulus
$$
\varepsilon^m
$$
gives
$$
e^{i(mt+\pi)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The map
$$
S^1\to S^1,
\qquad
e^{it}
\longmapsto
e^{i(mt+\pi)}
$$
has degree $m$.


::: pf-proof

The factor
$$
e^{i\pi}=-1
$$
is a rotation of the target circle and has degree $1$.
The map
$$
e^{it}
\longmapsto
e^{imt}
$$
winds the circle exactly $m$ times and has degree $m$.
Therefore their composition also has degree $m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Hence the Lefschetz index of $f$ at the origin is
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{ind}_0(f)=m.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the local Lefschetz index is the degree of the normalized displacement map.
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} computes this degree as $m$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives the definition, and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives the requested index.

:::
