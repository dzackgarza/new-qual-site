::: {.problem}
View $S^3$ as the unit sphere in $\CC^2$. Show that
\[
h:S^3\to\mathbb{CP}^1,
\qquad
h(x,y)=[x:y],
\]
is a submersion. Describe the point-inverses of $h$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
U_0
\coloneqq
\{[x:y]\in\mathbb{CP}^1:y\ne0\},
$$
and
$$
U_\infty
\coloneqq
\{[x:y]\in\mathbb{CP}^1:x\ne0\}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The sets $U_0$ and $U_\infty$ cover $\mathbb{CP}^1$.


::: pf-proof

For every projective point
$$
[x:y],
$$
at least one of $x,y$ is nonzero.
Hence every point lies in one of the two standard affine charts.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

On $U_0$, use the affine coordinate
$$
z=\frac{x}{y}.
$$
Define
$$
\Phi_0:
h^{-1}(U_0)
\to
U_0\times S^1
$$
by
$$
\Phi_0(x,y)
=
\left(
[x:y],
\frac{y}{|y|}
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
(x,y)\in h^{-1}(U_0),
$$
then
$$
y\ne0,
$$
so the phase
$$
y/|y|
\in
S^1
$$
is well-defined.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The map $\Phi_0$ is a diffeomorphism with inverse
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_0^{-1}
\left(
[z:1],\lambda
\right)
=
\left(
\frac{\lambda z}{\sqrt{1+|z|^2}},
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+|z|^2}}
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The displayed vector has norm
$$
\frac{|z|^2+1}{1+|z|^2}
=
1,
$$
so it lies in $S^3$.
Its projective class is
$$
[z:1],
$$
and its second-coordinate phase is $\lambda$.

Conversely, if
$$
z=x/y
$$
and
$$
\lambda=y/|y|,
$$
then
$$
|y|
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|z|^2}}
$$
because
$$
|x|^2+|y|^2=1.
$$
Thus the inverse formula reconstructs $(x,y)$.
Smoothness is immediate from the formulas.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In the coordinates given by $\Phi_0$, the map $h$ is projection onto the first factor:
$$
h
=
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\Phi_0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
\Phi_0(x,y)
=
\left(
h(x,y),
y/|y|
\right).
$$
Taking the first coordinate gives
$$
h(x,y).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore $h$ is a submersion at every point of $h^{-1}(U_0)$.


::: pf-proof

Under the diffeomorphism
$$
\Phi_0:
h^{-1}(U_0)
\cong
U_0\times S^1,
$$
the map $h$ is the projection
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1.
$$
The derivative of a projection
$$
T_{(p,\lambda)}(U_0\times S^1)
\to
T_pU_0
$$
is surjective.
Hence
$$
dh
$$
is surjective at every point lying over $U_0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

On $U_\infty$, define similarly
$$
\Phi_\infty:
h^{-1}(U_\infty)
\to
U_\infty\times S^1
$$
by
$$
\Phi_\infty(x,y)
=
\left(
[x:y],
\frac{x}{|x|}
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

On
$$
h^{-1}(U_\infty),
$$
one has
$$
x\ne0,
$$
so the phase
$$
x/|x|
$$
is well-defined.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The map $\Phi_\infty$ is a diffeomorphism, and under it $h$ is again projection onto the first factor.


::: pf-proof

Using the affine coordinate
$$
w=y/x,
$$
the inverse is
$$
\Phi_\infty^{-1}
\left(
[1:w],\lambda
\right)
=
\left(
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+|w|^2}},
\frac{\lambda w}{\sqrt{1+|w|^2}}
\right).
$$
The same calculation as in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} verifies the inverse identities.
By construction,
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\Phi_\infty=h.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence $h$ is a submersion on all of $S^3$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
U_0\cup U_\infty
=
\mathbb{CP}^1.
$$
Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives surjectivity of $dh$ over $U_0$, and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives surjectivity over $U_\infty$.
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
h:S^3\to\mathbb{CP}^1
\text{ is a submersion}.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Let
$$
[x:y]\in\mathbb{CP}^1
$$
and choose a unit representative
$$
(x,y)\in S^3.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
h^{-1}([x:y])
=
\{
(\lambda x,\lambda y)
:
\lambda\in S^1
\}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\lambda\in S^1,
$$
then
$$
h(\lambda x,\lambda y)
=
[\lambda x:\lambda y]
=
[x:y],
$$
and
$$
|\lambda x|^2+|\lambda y|^2
=
1.
$$

Conversely, if
$$
(x',y')\in S^3
$$
has
$$
[x':y']=[x:y],
$$
then
$$
(x',y')
=
\mu(x,y)
$$
for some
$$
\mu\in\CC^\times.
$$
Both vectors have norm $1$, so
$$
|\mu|=1.
$$
Thus
$$
\mu\in S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Each point-inverse is a circle.


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
S^1
\to
h^{-1}([x:y]),
\qquad
\lambda
\longmapsto
(\lambda x,\lambda y)
$$
is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse given by either nonzero coordinate ratio.
Hence
$$
h^{-1}([x:y])
\cong
S^1.
$$

:::

:::

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves that $h$ is a submersion, and steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} describe its point-inverses.
