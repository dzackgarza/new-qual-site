::: {.problem}
Give an example of a two-plane field on $\mathbb R^3$ that admits no nonempty integral manifolds, and prove that it has this property.
:::

Define vector fields
$$
X
\coloneqq
\frac{\partial}{\partial x},
\qquad
Y
\coloneqq
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
+
x\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.
$$
Let
$$
\xi
\coloneqq
\operatorname{span}\{X,Y\}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The vector fields $X$ and $Y$ are linearly independent at every point of $\mathbb R^3$.


::: pf-proof

At
$$
(x,y,z),
$$
one has
$$
X=(1,0,0),
\qquad
Y=(0,1,x).
$$
If
$$
aX+bY=0,
$$
then the first coordinate gives
$$
a=0,
$$
and the second coordinate gives
$$
b=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\xi_p
$$
is a $2$-dimensional plane for every point $p$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Lie bracket is
$$
\boxed{
[X,Y]
=
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
X=\partial_x
$$
and
$$
Y=\partial_y+x\partial_z,
$$
one has
$$
[X,\partial_y]=0
$$
and
$$
[X,x\partial_z]
=
X(x)\partial_z
+
x[X,\partial_z]
=
\partial_z.
$$
Therefore
$$
[X,Y]
=
\partial_z.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

At no point does
$$
\partial_z
$$
belong to
$$
\xi
=
\operatorname{span}\{X,Y\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
aX+bY
=
\partial_z.
$$
In coordinates this says
$$
a(1,0,0)
+
b(0,1,x)
=
(0,0,1).
$$
The first coordinate gives
$$
a=0,
$$
and the second gives
$$
b=0.
$$
The third coordinate would then give
$$
0=1,
$$
a contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence the plane field $\xi$ is not involutive on any nonempty open subset of $\mathbb R^3$.


::: pf-proof

The vector fields
$$
X,Y
$$
are sections of $\xi$.
By step <1>2,
$$
[X,Y]=\partial_z,
$$
and step <1>3 shows
$$
[X,Y]_p\notin\xi_p
$$
at every point.
Thus the bracket of two sections of $\xi$ fails to be a section of $\xi$ on every nonempty open set.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Suppose, for contradiction, that there were a nonempty immersed surface
$$
S\subset\mathbb R^3
$$
integral for $\xi$.
Then
$$
T_pS=\xi_p
$$
for every
$$
p\in S.
$$


::: pf-proof

An integral manifold for a rank-$2$ distribution is a $2$-dimensional immersed submanifold whose tangent space equals the prescribed plane field at every point.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The restrictions of $X$ and $Y$ to $S$ are tangent vector fields on $S$, so their bracket must also be tangent to $S$.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>5,
$$
X_p,Y_p\in T_pS
$$
for every $p\in S$.
Thus
$$
X|_S
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Y|_S
$$
are tangent vector fields on $S$.

The Lie bracket of two vector fields tangent to a submanifold is again tangent to that submanifold.
Hence
$$
[X,Y]_p\in T_pS
$$
for every
$$
p\in S.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

This contradicts step <1>3.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>5,
$$
T_pS=\xi_p.
$$
Step <1>6 gives
$$
[X,Y]_p\in\xi_p.
$$
But step <1>2 gives
$$
[X,Y]_p=\partial_z,
$$
and step <1>3 says
$$
\partial_z\notin\xi_p.
$$
Contradiction.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\xi
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
\partial_x,
\partial_y+x\partial_z
\right\}
\text{ admits no nonempty integral manifolds}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The existence of any nonempty integral surface leads to the contradiction in step <1>7.
Equivalently, this is the obstruction given by the Frobenius integrability theorem.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>8 gives the requested example and proof.

:::

:::
