::: {.problem}
Show that a $p$-sheeted covering map
\[
M\longrightarrow N
\]
between closed, connected, oriented smooth manifolds has degree $\pm p$.
:::

Let
$$
q:M\to N
$$
be the given $p$-sheeted covering.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map $q$ is a local diffeomorphism.


::: pf-proof

A smooth covering map is locally a diffeomorphism: every point
$$
y\in N
$$
has an evenly covered neighborhood $U$ such that each component of
$$
q^{-1}(U)
$$
maps diffeomorphically onto $U$.
Thus
$$
dq_x:T_xM\to T_{q(x)}N
$$
is an isomorphism for every $x\in M$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For each
$$
x\in M,
$$
define
$$
\sigma(x)
\in
\{+1,-1\}
$$
to be $+1$ if $dq_x$ preserves orientation and $-1$ if it reverses orientation.


::: pf-proof

Because $dq_x$ is an isomorphism by step <1>1, it either preserves or reverses the chosen orientations.
Thus the sign is well-defined.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The function
$$
\sigma:M\to\{+1,-1\}
$$
is locally constant.


::: pf-proof

In oriented local coordinates, the sign is the sign of the nonzero Jacobian determinant
$$
\det Dq.
$$
This determinant is continuous and never vanishes.
Hence its sign is constant on a sufficiently small neighborhood of every point.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $M$ is connected, $\sigma$ is constant on $M$.


::: pf-proof

A locally constant map from a connected space to a discrete space is constant.
Thus either
$$
\sigma(x)=+1
$$
for every $x\in M$, or
$$
\sigma(x)=-1
$$
for every $x\in M$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every point
$$
y\in N
$$
is a regular value of $q$, and
$$
\abs{q^{-1}(y)}=p.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>1 gives surjectivity of every differential, so every value is regular.
Because the covering has $p$ sheets, every fiber consists of exactly $p$ points.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For any
$$
y\in N,
$$
the degree is
$$
\deg q
=
\sum_{x\in q^{-1}(y)}
\sigma(x).
$$


::: pf-proof

The degree formula for a smooth map between closed connected oriented manifolds of the same dimension states that for any regular value $y$,
$$
\deg q
=
\sum_{x\in q^{-1}(y)}
\operatorname{sgn}\det(dq_x).
$$
The summand is exactly $\sigma(x)$ from step <1>2.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\deg q=\pm p.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>5, the fiber over $y$ has exactly $p$ points.
By step <1>4, all local signs in step <1>6 are equal.
Hence either
$$
\deg q
=
\underbrace{1+\cdots+1}_{p\text{ terms}}
=
p,
$$
or
$$
\deg q
=
\underbrace{-1-\cdots-1}_{p\text{ terms}}
=
-p.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>7 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
