::: {.problem}
Show that
\[
SL_n(\mathbb R)\subset M_n(\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^{n^2}
\]
is a submanifold.
:::

Define
$$
F:M_n(\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R
$$
by
$$
F(A)\coloneqq\det A.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

One has
$$
SL_n(\mathbb R)=F^{-1}(1).
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
SL_n(\mathbb R)
=
\{A\in M_n(\mathbb R):\det A=1\}.
$$
This is exactly the level set
$$
F^{-1}(1).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If
$$
A\in GL_n(\mathbb R),
$$
then
$$
\boxed{
dF_A(H)
=
\det(A)\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}H)
}
$$
for every
$$
H\in M_n(\mathbb R).
$$


::: pf-proof

For small real $t$,
$$
\det(A+tH)
=
\det(A)\det(I+tA^{-1}H).
$$
Using the first-order expansion
$$
\det(I+tB)
=
1+t\operatorname{tr}(B)+O(t^2),
$$
one gets
$$
\det(A+tH)
=
\det(A)
\,
t\det(A)\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}H)
+
O(t^2).
$$
Therefore the derivative at $t=0$ is the displayed linear functional.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If
$$
A\in SL_n(\mathbb R),
$$
then
$$
dF_A(AE_{11})=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

For
$$
A\in SL_n(\mathbb R),
$$
one has
$$
\det A=1.
$$
Apply step <1>2 with
$$
H=AE_{11}.
$$
Then
$$
A^{-1}H
=
E_{11},
$$
so
$$
dF_A(H)
=
\operatorname{tr}(E_{11})
=
1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus
$$
dF_A:M_n(\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R
$$
is surjective for every
$$
A\in F^{-1}(1).
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>3 shows that the linear map
$$
dF_A
$$
takes the value $1$ on the vector
$$
AE_{11}.
$$
Hence it is nonzero.
A nonzero linear map to the one-dimensional vector space
$$
\mathbb R
$$
is surjective.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
1
$$
is a regular value of $F$.


::: pf-proof

By definition, a value is regular if the differential is surjective at every point of its preimage.
This is exactly step <1>4.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
SL_n(\mathbb R)
\text{ is a smooth submanifold of }
M_n(\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^{n^2}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>1,
$$
SL_n(\mathbb R)=F^{-1}(1).
$$
By step <1>5, $1$ is a regular value.
The regular value theorem therefore implies that
$$
F^{-1}(1)
$$
is a smooth submanifold.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Its dimension is
$$
\boxed{n^2-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The ambient matrix space has dimension
$$
n^2.
$$
The regular level set of a smooth map to the one-dimensional manifold
$$
\mathbb R
$$
has codimension $1$.
Thus
$$
\dim SL_n(\mathbb R)
=
n^2-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps <1>6--<1>7 give the required submanifold statement and its dimension.

:::

:::
