::: {.problem}
Let
$$
h:S^3\to\CP^1,
\qquad
h(x,y)=[x:y],
$$
where $S^3=\{(x,y)\in\CC^2:\abs{x}^2+\abs{y}^2=1\}$. Prove that $h$ is a fiber bundle.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
U_0
\coloneqq
\{[x:y]\in\CP^1:y\ne0\},
$$
and
$$
U_\infty
\coloneqq
\{[x:y]\in\CP^1:x\ne0\}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The sets $U_0$ and $U_\infty$ form an open cover of $\CP^1$.


::: pf-proof

Every projective point
$$
[x:y]
$$
has
$$
(x,y)\ne(0,0).
$$
Thus at least one of $x,y$ is nonzero.
Hence every point lies in
$$
U_0\cup U_\infty.
$$
Each set is a standard affine chart and is open.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The chart
$$
U_0
$$
is identified with $\CC$ by
$$
[x:y]
\longmapsto
z
\coloneqq
\frac{x}{y}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
y\ne0,
$$
then
$$
[x:y]
=
\left[
\frac{x}{y}:1
\right].
$$
This representative is unique.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
\Phi_0:
h^{-1}(U_0)
\to
U_0\times S^1
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_0(x,y)
=
\left(
[x:y],
\frac{y}{\abs{y}}
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
(x,y)\in h^{-1}(U_0),
$$
then
$$
y\ne0.
$$
Thus
$$
y/\abs{y}
\in
S^1
$$
is well-defined.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The map $\Phi_0$ has inverse
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_0^{-1}
\left(
[z:1],\lambda
\right)
=
\left(
\frac{\lambda z}{\sqrt{1+\abs{z}^2}},
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\abs{z}^2}}
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The displayed vector lies on $S^3$ because
$$
\frac{\abs{z}^2}{1+\abs{z}^2}
+
\frac{1}{1+\abs{z}^2}
=
1.
$$
Its projective class is
$$
[z:1],
$$
and the phase of its second coordinate is $\lambda$.

Conversely, if
$$
(x,y)\in h^{-1}(U_0),
$$
put
$$
z=x/y,
\qquad
\lambda=y/\abs{y}.
$$
Because
$$
\abs{x}^2+\abs{y}^2=1,
$$
one has
$$
\abs{y}
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\abs{z}^2}}.
$$
Substitution into the inverse formula recovers $(x,y)$.
Thus the two maps are inverse.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The map $\Phi_0$ is a homeomorphism and satisfies
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\Phi_0=h.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both $\Phi_0$ and the inverse from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} are continuous.
The first coordinate of $\Phi_0(x,y)$ is
$$
[x:y]
=
h(x,y).
$$
Thus $\Phi_0$ is a fiber-preserving trivialization over $U_0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Similarly, the chart
$$
U_\infty
$$
is identified with $\CC$ by
$$
[x:y]
\longmapsto
w
\coloneqq
\frac{y}{x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
x\ne0,
$$
then
$$
[x:y]
=
[1:y/x].
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
\Phi_\infty:
h^{-1}(U_\infty)
\to
U_\infty\times S^1
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_\infty(x,y)
=
\left(
[x:y],
\frac{x}{\abs{x}}
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

On
$$
h^{-1}(U_\infty),
$$
one has
$$
x\ne0,
$$
so the phase
$$
x/\abs{x}
$$
is defined.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The inverse of $\Phi_\infty$ is
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_\infty^{-1}
\left(
[1:w],\lambda
\right)
=
\left(
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\abs{w}^2}},
\frac{\lambda w}{\sqrt{1+\abs{w}^2}}
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The verification is the same as in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.
The displayed vector has unit norm, projective class $[1:w]$, and first-coordinate phase $\lambda$.
Conversely, for a point $(x,y)$ with
$$
w=y/x,
$$
the equation
$$
\abs{x}^2+\abs{y}^2=1
$$
gives
$$
\abs{x}
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\abs{w}^2}}.
$$
Thus the inverse formula reconstructs $(x,y)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The map $\Phi_\infty$ is a fiber-preserving homeomorphism
$$
h^{-1}(U_\infty)
\cong
U_\infty\times S^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives a continuous inverse, and
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\Phi_\infty=h.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every fiber of $h$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
h(x,y)=h(x',y'),
$$
then
$$
(x',y')
=
\mu(x,y)
$$
for some
$$
\mu\in\CC^\times.
$$
Since both vectors have norm $1$,
$$
\abs{\mu}=1.
$$
Thus points in one fiber differ exactly by multiplication by an element of
$$
S^1.
$$
Hence each fiber is one $S^1$-orbit.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
S^1
\longrightarrow
S^3
\xrightarrow{\,h\,}
\CP^1
}
$$
is a fiber bundle.


::: pf-proof

The open sets
$$
U_0,U_\infty
$$
cover the base by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
Steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} give fiber-preserving homeomorphisms
$$
h^{-1}(U_0)
\cong
U_0\times S^1,
$$
and
$$
h^{-1}(U_\infty)
\cong
U_\infty\times S^1.
$$
Thus $h$ is locally trivial with fiber $S^1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} is the required fiber-bundle verification.
