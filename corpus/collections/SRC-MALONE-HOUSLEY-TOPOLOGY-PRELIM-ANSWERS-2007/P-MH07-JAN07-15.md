::: {.problem}
Let
\[
h:S^3\to\mathbb{CP}^1,
\qquad
h(x,y)=[x:y],
\]
where $S^3=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb C^2:|x|^2+|y|^2=1\}$. Prove that $h$ is a fiber bundle.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
U_0
\coloneqq
\{[x:y]\in\mathbb{CP}^1:y\ne0\},
$$
and
$$
U_\infty
\coloneqq
\{[x:y]\in\mathbb{CP}^1:x\ne0\}.
$$

<1>1. The sets $U_0$ and $U_\infty$ form an open cover of $\mathbb{CP}^1$.

::: {.proof}
Every projective point
$$
[x:y]
$$
has
$$
(x,y)\ne(0,0).
$$
Thus at least one of $x,y$ is nonzero.
Hence every point lies in
$$
U_0\cup U_\infty.
$$
Each set is a standard affine chart and is open.
:::

<1>2. The chart
$$
U_0
$$
is identified with $\mathbb C$ by
$$
[x:y]
\longmapsto
z
\coloneqq
\frac{x}{y}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
y\ne0,
$$
then
$$
[x:y]
=
\left[
\frac{x}{y}:1
\right].
$$
This representative is unique.
:::

<1>3. Define
$$
\Phi_0:
h^{-1}(U_0)
\to
U_0\times S^1
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_0(x,y)
=
\left(
[x:y],
\frac{y}{|y|}
\right).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
(x,y)\in h^{-1}(U_0),
$$
then
$$
y\ne0.
$$
Thus
$$
y/|y|
\in
S^1
$$
is well-defined.
:::

<1>4. The map $\Phi_0$ has inverse
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_0^{-1}
\left(
[z:1],\lambda
\right)
=
\left(
\frac{\lambda z}{\sqrt{1+|z|^2}},
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+|z|^2}}
\right).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The displayed vector lies on $S^3$ because
$$
\frac{|z|^2}{1+|z|^2}
+
\frac{1}{1+|z|^2}
=
1.
$$
Its projective class is
$$
[z:1],
$$
and the phase of its second coordinate is $\lambda$.

Conversely, if
$$
(x,y)\in h^{-1}(U_0),
$$
put
$$
z=x/y,
\qquad
\lambda=y/|y|.
$$
Because
$$
|x|^2+|y|^2=1,
$$
one has
$$
|y|
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|z|^2}}.
$$
Substitution into the inverse formula recovers $(x,y)$.
Thus the two maps are inverse.
:::

<1>5. The map $\Phi_0$ is a homeomorphism and satisfies
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\Phi_0=h.
$$

::: {.proof}
Both $\Phi_0$ and the inverse from step <1>4 are continuous.
The first coordinate of $\Phi_0(x,y)$ is
$$
[x:y]
=
h(x,y).
$$
Thus $\Phi_0$ is a fiber-preserving trivialization over $U_0$.
:::

<1>6. Similarly, the chart
$$
U_\infty
$$
is identified with $\mathbb C$ by
$$
[x:y]
\longmapsto
w
\coloneqq
\frac{y}{x}.
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
x\ne0,
$$
then
$$
[x:y]
=
[1:y/x].
$$
:::

<1>7. Define
$$
\Phi_\infty:
h^{-1}(U_\infty)
\to
U_\infty\times S^1
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_\infty(x,y)
=
\left(
[x:y],
\frac{x}{|x|}
\right).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
On
$$
h^{-1}(U_\infty),
$$
one has
$$
x\ne0,
$$
so the phase
$$
x/|x|
$$
is defined.
:::

<1>8. The inverse of $\Phi_\infty$ is
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_\infty^{-1}
\left(
[1:w],\lambda
\right)
=
\left(
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+|w|^2}},
\frac{\lambda w}{\sqrt{1+|w|^2}}
\right).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The verification is the same as in step <1>4.
The displayed vector has unit norm, projective class $[1:w]$, and first-coordinate phase $\lambda$.
Conversely, for a point $(x,y)$ with
$$
w=y/x,
$$
the equation
$$
|x|^2+|y|^2=1
$$
gives
$$
|x|
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|w|^2}}.
$$
Thus the inverse formula reconstructs $(x,y)$.
:::

<1>9. The map $\Phi_\infty$ is a fiber-preserving homeomorphism
$$
h^{-1}(U_\infty)
\cong
U_\infty\times S^1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 gives a continuous inverse, and
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\Phi_\infty=h.
$$
:::

<1>10. Every fiber of $h$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$.

::: {.proof}
If
$$
h(x,y)=h(x',y'),
$$
then
$$
(x',y')
=
\mu(x,y)
$$
for some
$$
\mu\in\mathbb C^\times.
$$
Since both vectors have norm $1$,
$$
|\mu|=1.
$$
Thus points in one fiber differ exactly by multiplication by an element of
$$
S^1.
$$
Hence each fiber is one $S^1$-orbit.
:::

<1>11. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
S^1
\longrightarrow
S^3
\xrightarrow{\,h\,}
\mathbb{CP}^1
}
$$
is a fiber bundle.

::: {.proof}
The open sets
$$
U_0,U_\infty
$$
cover the base by step <1>1.
Steps <1>5 and <1>9 give fiber-preserving homeomorphisms
$$
h^{-1}(U_0)
\cong
U_0\times S^1,
$$
and
$$
h^{-1}(U_\infty)
\cong
U_\infty\times S^1.
$$
Thus $h$ is locally trivial with fiber $S^1$.
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>11 is the required fiber-bundle verification.
:::
:::
