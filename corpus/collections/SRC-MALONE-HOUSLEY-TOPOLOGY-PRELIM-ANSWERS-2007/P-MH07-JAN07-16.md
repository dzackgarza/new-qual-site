::: {.problem}
Prove that
\[
\mathbb{RP}^3\not\simeq \mathbb{RP}^2\vee S^3.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Work with cohomology coefficients in
$$
\mathbb F_2
\coloneqq
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
$$

<1>1. The mod-$2$ cohomology ring of $\mathbb{RP}^3$ is
$$
\boxed{
H^*(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2[a]/(a^4),
\qquad
\deg a=1.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The standard mod-$2$ cohomology ring of real projective space is
$$
H^*(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2[a]/(a^{n+1}),
\qquad
\deg a=1.
$$
Taking
$$
n=3
$$
gives the displayed ring.
:::

<1>2. In particular,
$$
a^3\ne0
$$
in
$$
H^3(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb F_2).
$$

::: {.proof}
In the quotient ring
$$
\mathbb F_2[a]/(a^4),
$$
the first vanishing power of $a$ is
$$
a^4.
$$
Thus
$$
a^3
$$
is nonzero.
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
Y
\coloneqq
\mathbb{RP}^2\vee S^3.
$$
Then
$$
H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
H^1(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2.
$$

::: {.proof}
For positive degrees, reduced cohomology of a wedge splits as a direct sum:
$$
\widetilde H^k(X\vee Z;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\widetilde H^k(X;\mathbb F_2)
\oplus
\widetilde H^k(Z;\mathbb F_2).
$$
Since
$$
H^1(S^3;\mathbb F_2)=0,
$$
the degree-$1$ cohomology of $Y$ comes entirely from the $\mathbb{RP}^2$ summand.
:::

<1>4. If
$$
x\in H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2),
$$
then
$$
x^3=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, every degree-$1$ class is the pullback of a class
$$
\bar x
\in
H^1(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb F_2)
$$
under the retraction
$$
Y
\to
\mathbb{RP}^2.
$$

The mod-$2$ cohomology ring of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is
$$
H^*(\mathbb{RP}^2;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^3),
\qquad
\deg t=1.
$$
Thus every degree-$1$ class has cube zero.
Therefore
$$
x^3=0
$$
in $H^3(Y;\mathbb F_2)$.

Equivalently, cup products between positive-degree classes supported on different wedge summands vanish, and the $\mathbb{RP}^2$ summand has no degree-$3$ cohomology.
:::

<1>5. Suppose, for contradiction, that there were a homotopy equivalence
$$
f:
\mathbb{RP}^3
\xrightarrow{\simeq}
Y.
$$
Then
$$
f^*:
H^*(Y;\mathbb F_2)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H^*(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb F_2)
$$
would be an isomorphism of graded rings.

::: {.proof}
Singular cohomology is homotopy invariant, and the induced map of a continuous map preserves cup products.
A homotopy inverse to $f$ therefore makes
$$
f^*
$$
an isomorphism of graded cohomology rings.
:::

<1>6. Let
$$
x\in H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)
$$
be the unique nonzero degree-$1$ class.
Then
$$
f^*(x)=a.
$$

::: {.proof}
Both degree-$1$ groups are one-dimensional over $\mathbb F_2$:
$$
H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2
$$
by step <1>3, and
$$
H^1(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2
$$
by step <1>1.
Since
$$
f^*
$$
is an isomorphism, it sends the nonzero generator $x$ to the nonzero generator $a$.
:::

<1>7. Cup-product preservation gives
$$
a^3
=
f^*(x^3)
=
0,
$$
contradicting step <1>2.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4,
$$
x^3=0.
$$
Thus
$$
f^*(x^3)=0.
$$
On the other hand, by step <1>6 and multiplicativity of $f^*$,
$$
f^*(x^3)
=
f^*(x)^3
=
a^3.
$$
This contradicts
$$
a^3\ne0.
$$
:::

<1>8. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb{RP}^3
\not\simeq
\mathbb{RP}^2\vee S^3.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The existence of a homotopy equivalence leads to the contradiction in step <1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
