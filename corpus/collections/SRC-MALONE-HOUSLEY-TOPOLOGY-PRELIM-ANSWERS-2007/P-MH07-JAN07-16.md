::: {.problem}
Prove that
$$
\RP^3\not\simeq \RP^2\vee S^3.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

The problem is Problem 16 of the January 2007 examination in the retained Malone-Housley packet (`assets/attachments/Topology_Prelim_Answers_-_Unknown.pdf`). The packet prints the problem and no solution follows it, so nothing here is transcribed: the argument is the card's own, and it compares the mod-$2$ cohomology rings $\FF_2[a]/(a^4)$ of $\RP^3$ and that of $\RP^2\vee S^3$

Work with cohomology coefficients in
$$
\FF_2
\coloneqq
\ZZ/2\ZZ.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The mod-$2$ cohomology ring of $\RP^3$ is
$$
\boxed{
H^*(\RP^3;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2[a]/(a^4),
\qquad
\deg a=1.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The standard mod-$2$ cohomology ring of real projective space is
$$
H^*(\RP^n;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2[a]/(a^{n+1}),
\qquad
\deg a=1.
$$
Taking
$$
n=3
$$
gives the displayed ring.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

In particular,
$$
a^3\ne0
$$
in
$$
H^3(\RP^3;\FF_2).
$$


::: pf-proof

In the quotient ring
$$
\FF_2[a]/(a^4),
$$
the first vanishing power of $a$ is
$$
a^4.
$$
Thus
$$
a^3
$$
is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let
$$
Y
\coloneqq
\RP^2\vee S^3.
$$
Then
$$
H^1(Y;\FF_2)
\cong
H^1(\RP^2;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

For positive degrees, reduced cohomology of a wedge splits as a direct sum:
$$
\widetilde H^k(X\vee Z;\FF_2)
\cong
\widetilde H^k(X;\FF_2)
\oplus
\widetilde H^k(Z;\FF_2).
$$
Since
$$
H^1(S^3;\FF_2)=0,
$$
the degree-$1$ cohomology of $Y$ comes entirely from the $\RP^2$ summand.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If
$$
x\in H^1(Y;\FF_2),
$$
then
$$
x^3=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, every degree-$1$ class is the pullback of a class
$$
\bar x
\in
H^1(\RP^2;\FF_2)
$$
under the retraction
$$
Y
\to
\RP^2.
$$

The mod-$2$ cohomology ring of $\RP^2$ is
$$
H^*(\RP^2;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2[t]/(t^3),
\qquad
\deg t=1.
$$
Thus every degree-$1$ class has cube zero.
Therefore
$$
x^3=0
$$
in $H^3(Y;\FF_2)$.

Equivalently, cup products between positive-degree classes supported on different wedge summands vanish, and the $\RP^2$ summand has no degree-$3$ cohomology.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Suppose, for contradiction, that there were a homotopy equivalence
$$
f:
\RP^3
\xrightarrow{\simeq}
Y.
$$
Then
$$
f^*:
H^*(Y;\FF_2)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H^*(\RP^3;\FF_2)
$$
would be an isomorphism of graded rings.


::: pf-proof

Singular cohomology is homotopy invariant, and the induced map of a continuous map preserves cup products.
A homotopy inverse to $f$ therefore makes
$$
f^*
$$
an isomorphism of graded cohomology rings.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Let
$$
x\in H^1(Y;\FF_2)
$$
be the unique nonzero degree-$1$ class.
Then
$$
f^*(x)=a.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both degree-$1$ groups are one-dimensional over $\FF_2$:
$$
H^1(Y;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2
$$
by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, and
$$
H^1(\RP^3;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2
$$
by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
Since
$$
f^*
$$
is an isomorphism, it sends the nonzero generator $x$ to the nonzero generator $a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Cup-product preservation gives
$$
a^3
=
f^*(x^3)
=
0,
$$
contradicting step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
x^3=0.
$$
Thus
$$
f^*(x^3)=0.
$$
On the other hand, by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and multiplicativity of $f^*$,
$$
f^*(x^3)
=
f^*(x)^3
=
a^3.
$$
This contradicts
$$
a^3\ne0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\RP^3
\not\simeq
\RP^2\vee S^3.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The existence of a homotopy equivalence leads to the contradiction in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::

:::

:::
