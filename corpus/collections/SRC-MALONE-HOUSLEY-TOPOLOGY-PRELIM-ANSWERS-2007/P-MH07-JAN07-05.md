::: {.problem}
Let $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ be smooth. Show that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a vector $v\in\mathbb R^n$ with $\lVert v\rVert<\varepsilon$ such that
\[
g_v(x)=f(x)+v\cdot x
\]
is a Morse function.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define the gradient map
$$
h:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n,
\qquad
h(x)
\coloneqq
\nabla f(x).
$$

<1>1. For
$$
v\in\mathbb R^n,
$$
a point
$$
x\in\mathbb R^n
$$
is critical for $g_v$ if and only if
$$
h(x)=-v.
$$

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
g_v(x)
=
f(x)+v\cdot x.
$$
Therefore
$$
\nabla g_v(x)
=
\nabla f(x)+v
=
h(x)+v.
$$
Thus
$$
\nabla g_v(x)=0
\iff
h(x)=-v.
$$
:::

<1>2. The derivative of $h$ at $x$ is the Hessian of $f$ at $x$:
$$
dh_x
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f).
$$

::: {.proof}
In standard coordinates,
$$
h
=
\left(
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1},
\ldots,
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}
\right).
$$
Thus the Jacobian matrix of $h$ is
$$
\left(
\frac{\partial^2f}
{\partial x_i\partial x_j}
\right)_{i,j},
$$
which is exactly the Hessian matrix of $f$.
:::

<1>3. The Hessian of $g_v$ equals the Hessian of $f$:
$$
\operatorname{Hess}_x(g_v)
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f).
$$

::: {.proof}
The perturbing function
$$
x\longmapsto v\cdot x
$$
is linear.
All of its second derivatives vanish.
Therefore adding it does not change the Hessian.
:::

<1>4. If
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$, then every critical point of $g_v$ is nondegenerate.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
x
$$
be a critical point of $g_v$.
By step <1>1,
$$
h(x)=-v.
$$
If
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$, then
$$
dh_x:
\mathbb R^n
\to
\mathbb R^n
$$
is surjective.
Because domain and codomain both have dimension $n$, it is an isomorphism.

By step <1>2,
$$
dh_x
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f),
$$
and by step <1>3 this is also
$$
\operatorname{Hess}_x(g_v).
$$
Hence the Hessian of $g_v$ at $x$ is nonsingular.
Thus $x$ is nondegenerate.
:::

<1>5. If
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$, then $g_v$ is a Morse function.

::: {.proof}
A smooth function is Morse exactly when all of its critical points are nondegenerate.
Step <1>4 proves this condition for $g_v$.
If $g_v$ has no critical points, the condition is vacuously satisfied.
:::

<1>6. By Sard's theorem, the set of critical values of $h$ has measure zero in $\mathbb R^n$.

::: {.proof}
The map
$$
h=\nabla f
$$
is smooth because $f$ is smooth.
Sard's theorem states that the set of critical values of any smooth map has Lebesgue measure zero.
:::

<1>7. For every
$$
\varepsilon>0,
$$
the open ball
$$
B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
contains a regular value of $h$.

::: {.proof}
The ball
$$
B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
has positive Lebesgue measure.
By step <1>6, the critical values of $h$ form a measure-zero subset.
Therefore they cannot fill the ball.
Hence there exists
$$
w\in B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
that is not a critical value, i.e. $w$ is a regular value of $h$.
:::

<1>8. Choose such a regular value $w$ and set
$$
v
\coloneqq
-w.
$$
Then
$$
\|v\|<\varepsilon
$$
and
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$.

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
w\in B_\varepsilon(0),
$$
one has
$$
\|w\|<\varepsilon.
$$
Thus
$$
\|v\|
=
\|-w\|
=
\|w\|
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Also,
$$
-v=w,
$$
which is regular by construction.
:::

<1>9. For this vector $v$, the function
$$
g_v(x)
=
f(x)+v\cdot x
$$
is Morse.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 shows that
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$.
Apply step <1>5.
:::

<1>10. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\forall\varepsilon>0\quad
\exists v\in\mathbb R^n,\quad
\|v\|<\varepsilon,
\text{ such that }
f(x)+v\cdot x
\text{ is Morse}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>7--<1>9 construct the required vector $v$ for an arbitrary positive $\varepsilon$.
:::

<1>11. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>10 is the required statement.
:::
:::
