::: {.problem}
Let $f:\RR^n\to\RR$ be smooth. Show that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a vector $v\in\RR^n$ with $\lVert v\rVert<\varepsilon$ such that
$$
g_v(x)=f(x)+v\cdot x
$$
is a Morse function.
:::

::: {.solution}

Define the gradient map
$$
h:\RR^n\to\RR^n,
\qquad
h(x)
\coloneqq
\nabla f(x).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For
$$
v\in\RR^n,
$$
a point
$$
x\in\RR^n
$$
is critical for $g_v$ if and only if
$$
h(x)=-v.
$$


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
g_v(x)
=
f(x)+v\cdot x.
$$
Therefore
$$
\nabla g_v(x)
=
\nabla f(x)+v
=
h(x)+v.
$$
Thus
$$
\nabla g_v(x)=0
\iff
h(x)=-v.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The derivative of $h$ at $x$ is the Hessian of $f$ at $x$:
$$
dh_x
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f).
$$


::: pf-proof

In standard coordinates,
$$
h
=
\left(
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1},
\ldots,
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}
\right).
$$
Thus the Jacobian matrix of $h$ is
$$
\left(
\frac{\partial^2f}
{\partial x_i\partial x_j}
\right)_{i,j},
$$
which is exactly the Hessian matrix of $f$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Hessian of $g_v$ equals the Hessian of $f$:
$$
\operatorname{Hess}_x(g_v)
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f).
$$


::: pf-proof

The perturbing function
$$
x\longmapsto v\cdot x
$$
is linear.
All of its second derivatives vanish.
Therefore adding it does not change the Hessian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$, then every critical point of $g_v$ is nondegenerate.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
x
$$
be a critical point of $g_v$.
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
h(x)=-v.
$$
If
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$, then
$$
dh_x:
\RR^n
\to
\RR^n
$$
is surjective.
Because domain and codomain both have dimension $n$, it is an isomorphism.

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
dh_x
=
\operatorname{Hess}_x(f),
$$
and by step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} this is also
$$
\operatorname{Hess}_x(g_v).
$$
Hence the Hessian of $g_v$ at $x$ is nonsingular.
Thus $x$ is nondegenerate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$, then $g_v$ is a Morse function.


::: pf-proof

A smooth function is Morse exactly when all of its critical points are nondegenerate.
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves this condition for $g_v$.
If $g_v$ has no critical points, the condition is vacuously satisfied.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

By Sard's theorem, the set of critical values of $h$ has measure zero in $\RR^n$.


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
h=\nabla f
$$
is smooth because $f$ is smooth.
Sard's theorem states that the set of critical values of any smooth map has Lebesgue measure zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For every
$$
\varepsilon>0,
$$
the open ball
$$
B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
contains a regular value of $h$.


::: pf-proof

The ball
$$
B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
has positive Lebesgue measure.
By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, the critical values of $h$ form a measure-zero subset.
Therefore they cannot fill the ball.
Hence there exists
$$
w\in B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
that is not a critical value, i.e. $w$ is a regular value of $h$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Choose such a regular value $w$ and set
$$
v
\coloneqq
-w.
$$
Then
$$
\|v\|<\varepsilon
$$
and
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
w\in B_\varepsilon(0),
$$
one has
$$
\|w\|<\varepsilon.
$$
Thus
$$
\|v\|
=
\|-w\|
=
\|w\|
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Also,
$$
-v=w,
$$
which is regular by construction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For this vector $v$, the function
$$
g_v(x)
=
f(x)+v\cdot x
$$
is Morse.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} shows that
$$
-v
$$
is a regular value of $h$.
Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\forall\varepsilon>0\quad
\exists v\in\RR^n,\quad
\|v\|<\varepsilon,
\text{ such that }
f(x)+v\cdot x
\text{ is Morse}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} construct the required vector $v$ for an arbitrary positive $\varepsilon$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} is the required statement.

:::
