::: {.problem}
Give a proof that $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is nonorientable.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\pi:S^2\to\mathbb{RP}^2
$$
be the standard double covering, and let
$$
A:S^2\to S^2,
\qquad
A(x)=-x
$$
be the antipodal map.

<1>1. One has
$$
\pi\circ A=\pi.
$$

::: {.proof}
The point
$$
\pi(x)
$$
is the line through $x\in S^2$.
The vectors $x$ and $-x$ span the same line.
Hence
$$
\pi(-x)=\pi(x)
$$
for every $x\in S^2$.
:::

<1>2. The antipodal map $A:S^2\to S^2$ reverses orientation.

::: {.proof}
Give $S^2$ its standard boundary orientation as the boundary of the unit ball
$$
B^3\subset\mathbb R^3.
$$
The antipodal map is the restriction of the linear map
$$
-I_3:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3.
$$
Its determinant is
$$
\det(-I_3)=(-1)^3=-1.
$$
Thus
$$
-I_3
$$
reverses the orientation of $\mathbb R^3$.
Because it also sends the outward unit normal $x$ at a point $x\in S^2$ to the outward unit normal $-x$ at $-x$, the induced map on the oriented boundary reverses the orientation of $S^2$.

Equivalently,
$$
\deg A=-1.
$$
:::

<1>3. Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
\mathbb{RP}^2
$$
is orientable.
Then its orientation pulls back along $\pi$ to an orientation
$$
\omega
$$
of $S^2$.

::: {.proof}
The map
$$
\pi:S^2\to\mathbb{RP}^2
$$
is a covering map and hence a local diffeomorphism.
Therefore an orientation on the target determines, by pullback through the local differential
$$
d\pi_x,
$$
an orientation on every tangent space
$$
T_xS^2.
$$
These local orientations vary continuously, giving an orientation
$$
\omega=\pi^*(\text{orientation of }\mathbb{RP}^2).
$$
:::

<1>4. The equality
$$
\pi\circ A=\pi
$$
implies
$$
A^*\omega=\omega.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
\omega
=
\pi^*\eta,
$$
where
$$
\eta
$$
is the assumed orientation of
$$
\mathbb{RP}^2.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
A^*\omega
&=
A^*\pi^*\eta\\
&=
(\pi\circ A)^*\eta\\
&=
\pi^*\eta\\
&=
\omega,
\end{aligned}
$$
using step <1>1.
:::

<1>5. Since $A$ is orientation reversing,
$$
A^*\omega=-\omega.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 says that the antipodal map reverses any chosen orientation of $S^2$.
Therefore its pullback sends the orientation
$$
\omega
$$
to the opposite orientation.
:::

<1>6. Steps <1>4 and <1>5 give a contradiction.

::: {.proof}
They imply simultaneously
$$
A^*\omega=\omega
$$
and
$$
A^*\omega=-\omega.
$$
An orientation cannot equal its opposite.
Thus the assumption that
$$
\mathbb{RP}^2
$$
is orientable is impossible.
:::

<1>7. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb{RP}^2
\text{ is nonorientable}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the conclusion of step <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 is the required result.
:::
:::
