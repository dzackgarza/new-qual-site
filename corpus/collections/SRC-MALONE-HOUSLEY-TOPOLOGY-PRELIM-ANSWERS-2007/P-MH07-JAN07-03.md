::: {.problem}
Give a proof that $\RP^2$ is nonorientable.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\pi:S^2\to\RP^2
$$
be the standard double covering, and let
$$
A:S^2\to S^2,
\qquad
A(x)=-x
$$
be the antipodal map.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\pi\circ A=\pi.
$$


::: pf-proof

The point
$$
\pi(x)
$$
is the line through $x\in S^2$.
The vectors $x$ and $-x$ span the same line.
Hence
$$
\pi(-x)=\pi(x)
$$
for every $x\in S^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The antipodal map $A:S^2\to S^2$ reverses orientation.


::: pf-proof

Give $S^2$ its standard boundary orientation as the boundary of the unit ball
$$
B^3\subset\RR^3.
$$
The antipodal map is the restriction of the linear map
$$
-I_3:\RR^3\to\RR^3.
$$
Its determinant is
$$
\det(-I_3)=(-1)^3=-1.
$$
Thus
$$
-I_3
$$
reverses the orientation of $\RR^3$.
Because it also sends the outward unit normal $x$ at a point $x\in S^2$ to the outward unit normal $-x$ at $-x$, the induced map on the oriented boundary reverses the orientation of $S^2$.

Equivalently,
$$
\deg A=-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
\RP^2
$$
is orientable.
Then its orientation pulls back along $\pi$ to an orientation
$$
\omega
$$
of $S^2$.


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
\pi:S^2\to\RP^2
$$
is a covering map and hence a local diffeomorphism.
Therefore an orientation on the target determines, by pullback through the local differential
$$
d\pi_x,
$$
an orientation on every tangent space
$$
T_xS^2.
$$
These local orientations vary continuously, giving an orientation
$$
\omega=\pi^*(\text{orientation of }\RP^2).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The equality
$$
\pi\circ A=\pi
$$
implies
$$
A^*\omega=\omega.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\omega
=
\pi^*\eta,
$$
where
$$
\eta
$$
is the assumed orientation of
$$
\RP^2.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
A^*\omega
&=
A^*\pi^*\eta\\
&=
(\pi\circ A)^*\eta\\
&=
\pi^*\eta\\
&=
\omega,
\end{aligned}
$$
using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Since $A$ is orientation reversing,
$$
A^*\omega=-\omega.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} says that the antipodal map reverses any chosen orientation of $S^2$.
Therefore its pullback sends the orientation
$$
\omega
$$
to the opposite orientation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} give a contradiction.


::: pf-proof

They imply simultaneously
$$
A^*\omega=\omega
$$
and
$$
A^*\omega=-\omega.
$$
An orientation cannot equal its opposite.
Thus the assumption that
$$
\RP^2
$$
is orientable is impossible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\RP^2
\text{ is nonorientable}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the conclusion of step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is the required result.

:::
