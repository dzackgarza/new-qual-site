::: {.problem}
Let $S\subset\RP^2$ be an embedded circle that is not null-homotopic. Let $X$ be obtained by taking two copies of $\RP^2$ and identifying them along $S$.

Construct an explicit $\Delta$-complex structure on $X$ and use it to compute the homology and cohomology of $X$ with coefficients in $\ZZ$ and in $\ZZ/2\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Use the standard two-triangle $\Delta$-complex structure on each copy of $\RP^2$ in which the essential projective line is a one-simplex loop.
After gluing the two copies along that loop, take:

$$
\text{vertices }V,W,P,
$$
$$
\text{edges }A,B,C,D,E,
$$
and
$$
\text{faces }L,U,R,Z.
$$

The edges $A,B$ run from $V$ to $W$, the edges $D,E$ run from $V$ to $P$, and $C$ is the common loop at $V$ representing the identified essential circle.
Choose orientations so that
$$
\partial L=A-B+C,
$$
$$
\partial U=-A+B+C,
$$
$$
\partial R=C-D+E,
$$
and
$$
\partial Z=C+D-E.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

This gives a $\Delta$-complex structure on $X$.


::: pf-proof

The two faces $L,U$ with edges $A,B,C$ give the standard two-simplex model of one copy of $\RP^2$, with $C$ the essential projective line.
Likewise the faces $R,Z$ with edges $C,D,E$ give the second copy.
The two copies share exactly the edge $C$ and its vertex $V$, which realizes the prescribed identification along the embedded essential circle $S$.
Thus the resulting quotient is $X$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

With coefficients in an abelian group $G$, the chain groups are
$$
C_2(X;G)\cong G^4,
$$
$$
C_1(X;G)\cong G^5,
$$
and
$$
C_0(X;G)\cong G^3.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are four $2$-simplices, five $1$-simplices, and three vertices.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Relative to the ordered bases
$$
(L,U,R,Z),
$$
$$
(A,B,C,D,E),
$$
and
$$
(V,W,P),
$$
the boundary maps satisfy
$$
\partial_2(L)=A-B+C,
$$
$$
\partial_2(U)=-A+B+C,
$$
$$
\partial_2(R)=C-D+E,
$$
$$
\partial_2(Z)=C+D-E,
$$
and
$$
\partial_1(A)=\partial_1(B)=W-V,
$$
$$
\partial_1(C)=0,
$$
$$
\partial_1(D)=\partial_1(E)=P-V.
$$


::: pf-proof

The formulas for $\partial_2$ are the chosen oriented face boundaries.
The formulas for $\partial_1$ follow from the chosen edge orientations.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Over $\ZZ$,
$$
\ker\partial_1
$$
is freely generated by
$$
x\coloneqq A-B,
\qquad
c\coloneqq C,
\qquad
y\coloneqq D-E.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a $1$-chain
$$
aA+bB+cC+dD+eE,
$$
step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\partial_1
=
(a+b)(W-V)
+
(d+e)(P-V).
$$
Thus the cycle conditions are
$$
a+b=0,
\qquad
d+e=0.
$$
Hence every cycle is uniquely an integral combination of
$$
A-B,
\quad
C,
\quad
D-E.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

In the basis $(x,c,y)$ of $\ker\partial_1$, the image of $\partial_2$ is generated by
$$
x+c,
\qquad
-x+c,
\qquad
c-y,
\qquad
c+y.
$$


::: pf-proof

Rewrite the four boundary formulas from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}:
$$
\partial_2(L)=x+c,
$$
$$
\partial_2(U)=-x+c,
$$
$$
\partial_2(R)=c-y,
$$
and
$$
\partial_2(Z)=c+y.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
H_1(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The quotient
$$
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
$$
has generators
$$
x,c,y
$$
and relations
$$
x+c=0,
$$
$$
-x+c=0,
$$
$$
c-y=0,
$$
and
$$
c+y=0.
$$
The first two relations give
$$
x=-c
$$
and
$$
2c=0.
$$
The last two give
$$
y=c
$$
and again
$$
2c=0.
$$
Thus the quotient is generated by $c$ with the single relation
$$
2c=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_1(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/2.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Over $\ZZ$,
$$
\ker\partial_2
$$
is generated by
$$
L+U-R-Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\alpha L+\beta U+\gamma R+\delta Z
$$
lie in the kernel.
Using step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, its boundary has coefficients
$$
\alpha-\beta
$$
on $x$,
$$
\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta
$$
on $c$, and
$$
-\gamma+\delta
$$
on $y$.
Thus
$$
\alpha=\beta,
\qquad
\gamma=\delta,
$$
and
$$
2\alpha+2\gamma=0.
$$
Over $\ZZ$ this gives
$$
\gamma=-\alpha.
$$
Hence every element of the kernel is a multiple of
$$
L+U-R-Z.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence
$$
H_2(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-simplices, so
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_3=0.
$$
By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
\ker\partial_2
\cong
\ZZ.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_2(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The space $X$ is connected, so
$$
H_0(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

The two copies of $\RP^2$ meet along the nonempty connected circle $S$.
Hence their union $X$ is connected.
The zeroth homology of a connected space is $\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_k(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,2,\\
\ZZ/2,&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.
There are no cells in dimensions above $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

With coefficients in
$$
\FF_2
\coloneqq
\ZZ/2\ZZ,
$$
the kernel of $\partial_1$ is again $3$-dimensional, generated by
$$
x=A+B,
\qquad
c=C,
\qquad
y=D+E.
$$


::: pf-proof

Modulo $2$, subtraction equals addition.
The conditions from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} become
$$
a+b=0,
\qquad
d+e=0
$$
in $\FF_2$.
Thus the displayed three cycles form a basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Over $\FF_2$, the image of $\partial_2$ is generated by
$$
x+c
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c+y.
$$


::: pf-proof

Modulo $2$,
$$
\partial L
=
\partial U
=
x+c,
$$
and
$$
\partial R
=
\partial Z
=
c+y.
$$
The two displayed vectors are linearly independent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore
$$
H_1(X;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim_{\FF_2}\ker\partial_1=3.
$$
By step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim_{\FF_2}\operatorname{im}\partial_2=2.
$$
Hence
$$
\dim_{\FF_2}H_1
=
3-2
=
1.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_1(X;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Over $\FF_2$,
$$
\ker\partial_2
$$
has basis
$$
L+U,
\qquad
R+Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
\alpha L+\beta U+\gamma R+\delta Z
$$
be a $2$-cycle.
By step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}, its boundary is
$$
(\alpha+\beta)(x+c)
+
(\gamma+\delta)(c+y).
$$
Since the two displayed boundary vectors are independent, the cycle conditions are
$$
\alpha+\beta=0,
\qquad
\gamma+\delta=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\alpha=\beta,
\qquad
\gamma=\delta,
$$
giving the stated basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Hence
$$
H_2(X;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-simplices, so
$$
H_2
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Since $X$ is connected,
$$
H_0(X;\FF_2)
\cong
\FF_2.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the field-coefficient version of step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_k(X;\FF_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\FF_2,&k=0,1,\\
\FF_2^2,&k=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-18}

The integral cohomology is
$$
\boxed{
H^k(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
0,&k=1,\\
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2,&k=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology.

For degree $0$,
$$
H^0(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(H_0,\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$

For degree $1$,
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}(H_0,\ZZ)
\to
H^1(X;\ZZ)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}(H_1,\ZZ)
\to
0.
$$
Here
$$
\operatorname{Ext}(\ZZ,\ZZ)=0
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ/2,\ZZ)=0,
$$
so
$$
H^1=0.
$$

For degree $2$,
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}(\ZZ/2,\ZZ)
\to
H^2(X;\ZZ)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}(\ZZ,\ZZ)
\to
0.
$$
The outer groups are
$$
\ZZ/2
$$
and
$$
\ZZ.
$$
Since $\ZZ$ is free, the sequence splits, giving
$$
H^2(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-19}

The mod-$2$ cohomology is
$$
\boxed{
H^k(X;\FF_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\FF_2,&k=0,1,\\
\FF_2^2,&k=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Over the field $\FF_2$, the cochain complex is the dual of the finite-dimensional chain complex.
Therefore
$$
\dim_{\FF_2}H^k(X;\FF_2)
=
\dim_{\FF_2}H_k(X;\FF_2)
$$
for each $k$.
Apply step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref}.

:::
