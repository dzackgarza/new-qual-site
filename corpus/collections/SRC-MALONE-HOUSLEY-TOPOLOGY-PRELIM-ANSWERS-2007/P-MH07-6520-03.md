::: {.problem}
Let $S\subset\mathbb{RP}^2$ be an embedded circle that is not null-homotopic. Let $X$ be obtained by taking two copies of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ and identifying them along $S$.

Construct an explicit $\Delta$-complex structure on $X$ and use it to compute the homology and cohomology of $X$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Z$ and in $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the standard two-triangle $\Delta$-complex structure on each copy of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ in which the essential projective line is a one-simplex loop.
After gluing the two copies along that loop, take:

$$
\text{vertices }V,W,P,
$$
$$
\text{edges }A,B,C,D,E,
$$
and
$$
\text{faces }L,U,R,Z.
$$

The edges $A,B$ run from $V$ to $W$, the edges $D,E$ run from $V$ to $P$, and $C$ is the common loop at $V$ representing the identified essential circle.
Choose orientations so that
$$
\partial L=A-B+C,
$$
$$
\partial U=-A+B+C,
$$
$$
\partial R=C-D+E,
$$
and
$$
\partial Z=C+D-E.
$$

<1>1. This gives a $\Delta$-complex structure on $X$.

::: {.proof}
The two faces $L,U$ with edges $A,B,C$ give the standard two-simplex model of one copy of $\mathbb{RP}^2$, with $C$ the essential projective line.
Likewise the faces $R,Z$ with edges $C,D,E$ give the second copy.
The two copies share exactly the edge $C$ and its vertex $V$, which realizes the prescribed identification along the embedded essential circle $S$.
Thus the resulting quotient is $X$.
:::

<1>2. With coefficients in an abelian group $G$, the chain groups are
$$
C_2(X;G)\cong G^4,
$$
$$
C_1(X;G)\cong G^5,
$$
and
$$
C_0(X;G)\cong G^3.
$$

::: {.proof}
There are four $2$-simplices, five $1$-simplices, and three vertices.
:::

<1>3. Relative to the ordered bases
$$
(L,U,R,Z),
$$
$$
(A,B,C,D,E),
$$
and
$$
(V,W,P),
$$
the boundary maps satisfy
$$
\partial_2(L)=A-B+C,
$$
$$
\partial_2(U)=-A+B+C,
$$
$$
\partial_2(R)=C-D+E,
$$
$$
\partial_2(Z)=C+D-E,
$$
and
$$
\partial_1(A)=\partial_1(B)=W-V,
$$
$$
\partial_1(C)=0,
$$
$$
\partial_1(D)=\partial_1(E)=P-V.
$$

::: {.proof}
The formulas for $\partial_2$ are the chosen oriented face boundaries.
The formulas for $\partial_1$ follow from the chosen edge orientations.
:::

<1>4. Over $\mathbb Z$,
$$
\ker\partial_1
$$
is freely generated by
$$
x\coloneqq A-B,
\qquad
c\coloneqq C,
\qquad
y\coloneqq D-E.
$$

::: {.proof}
For a $1$-chain
$$
aA+bB+cC+dD+eE,
$$
step <1>3 gives
$$
\partial_1
=
(a+b)(W-V)
+
(d+e)(P-V).
$$
Thus the cycle conditions are
$$
a+b=0,
\qquad
d+e=0.
$$
Hence every cycle is uniquely an integral combination of
$$
A-B,
\quad
C,
\quad
D-E.
$$
:::

<1>5. In the basis $(x,c,y)$ of $\ker\partial_1$, the image of $\partial_2$ is generated by
$$
x+c,
\qquad
-x+c,
\qquad
c-y,
\qquad
c+y.
$$

::: {.proof}
Rewrite the four boundary formulas from step <1>3:
$$
\partial_2(L)=x+c,
$$
$$
\partial_2(U)=-x+c,
$$
$$
\partial_2(R)=c-y,
$$
and
$$
\partial_2(Z)=c+y.
$$
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2.
$$

::: {.proof}
The quotient
$$
\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2
$$
has generators
$$
x,c,y
$$
and relations
$$
x+c=0,
$$
$$
-x+c=0,
$$
$$
c-y=0,
$$
and
$$
c+y=0.
$$
The first two relations give
$$
x=-c
$$
and
$$
2c=0.
$$
The last two give
$$
y=c
$$
and again
$$
2c=0.
$$
Thus the quotient is generated by $c$ with the single relation
$$
2c=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z/2.
}
$$
:::

<1>7. Over $\mathbb Z$,
$$
\ker\partial_2
$$
is generated by
$$
L+U-R-Z.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\alpha L+\beta U+\gamma R+\delta Z
$$
lie in the kernel.
Using step <1>5, its boundary has coefficients
$$
\alpha-\beta
$$
on $x$,
$$
\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta
$$
on $c$, and
$$
-\gamma+\delta
$$
on $y$.
Thus
$$
\alpha=\beta,
\qquad
\gamma=\delta,
$$
and
$$
2\alpha+2\gamma=0.
$$
Over $\mathbb Z$ this gives
$$
\gamma=-\alpha.
$$
Hence every element of the kernel is a multiple of
$$
L+U-R-Z.
$$
:::

<1>8. Hence
$$
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$

::: {.proof}
There are no $3$-simplices, so
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_3=0.
$$
By step <1>7,
$$
\ker\partial_2
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
}
$$
:::

<1>9. The space $X$ is connected, so
$$
H_0(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$

::: {.proof}
The two copies of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ meet along the nonempty connected circle $S$.
Hence their union $X$ is connected.
The zeroth homology of a connected space is $\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>10. Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_k(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,2,\\
\mathbb Z/2,&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>6, <1>8, and <1>9.
There are no cells in dimensions above $2$.
:::

<1>11. With coefficients in
$$
\mathbb F_2
\coloneqq
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
$$
the kernel of $\partial_1$ is again $3$-dimensional, generated by
$$
x=A+B,
\qquad
c=C,
\qquad
y=D+E.
$$

::: {.proof}
Modulo $2$, subtraction equals addition.
The conditions from step <1>4 become
$$
a+b=0,
\qquad
d+e=0
$$
in $\mathbb F_2$.
Thus the displayed three cycles form a basis.
:::

<1>12. Over $\mathbb F_2$, the image of $\partial_2$ is generated by
$$
x+c
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c+y.
$$

::: {.proof}
Modulo $2$,
$$
\partial L
=
\partial U
=
x+c,
$$
and
$$
\partial R
=
\partial Z
=
c+y.
$$
The two displayed vectors are linearly independent.
:::

<1>13. Therefore
$$
H_1(X;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>11,
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\ker\partial_1=3.
$$
By step <1>12,
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{im}\partial_2=2.
$$
Hence
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_1
=
3-2
=
1.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_1(X;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2.
}
$$
:::

<1>14. Over $\mathbb F_2$,
$$
\ker\partial_2
$$
has basis
$$
L+U,
\qquad
R+Z.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\alpha L+\beta U+\gamma R+\delta Z
$$
be a $2$-cycle.
By step <1>12, its boundary is
$$
(\alpha+\beta)(x+c)
+
(\gamma+\delta)(c+y).
$$
Since the two displayed boundary vectors are independent, the cycle conditions are
$$
\alpha+\beta=0,
\qquad
\gamma+\delta=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\alpha=\beta,
\qquad
\gamma=\delta,
$$
giving the stated basis.
:::

<1>15. Hence
$$
H_2(X;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
There are no $3$-simplices, so
$$
H_2
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step <1>14.
:::

<1>16. Since $X$ is connected,
$$
H_0(X;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\mathbb F_2.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the field-coefficient version of step <1>9.
:::

<1>17. Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_k(X;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb F_2,&k=0,1,\\
\mathbb F_2^2,&k=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>13, <1>15, and <1>16.
:::

<1>18. The integral cohomology is
$$
\boxed{
H^k(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
0,&k=1,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2,&k=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology.

For degree $0$,
$$
H^0(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(H_0,\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z.
$$

For degree $1$,
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}(H_0,\mathbb Z)
\to
H^1(X;\mathbb Z)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}(H_1,\mathbb Z)
\to
0.
$$
Here
$$
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)=0
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb Z)=0,
$$
so
$$
H^1=0.
$$

For degree $2$,
$$
0
\to
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb Z)
\to
H^2(X;\mathbb Z)
\to
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)
\to
0.
$$
The outer groups are
$$
\mathbb Z/2
$$
and
$$
\mathbb Z.
$$
Since $\mathbb Z$ is free, the sequence splits, giving
$$
H^2(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
$$
:::

<1>19. The mod-$2$ cohomology is
$$
\boxed{
H^k(X;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb F_2,&k=0,1,\\
\mathbb F_2^2,&k=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Over the field $\mathbb F_2$, the cochain complex is the dual of the finite-dimensional chain complex.
Therefore
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H^k(X;\mathbb F_2)
=
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_k(X;\mathbb F_2)
$$
for each $k$.
Apply step <1>17.
:::

<1>20. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the explicit $\Delta$-complex.
Steps <1>10 and <1>17 compute homology with $\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb F_2$ coefficients.
Steps <1>18 and <1>19 compute the corresponding cohomology groups.
:::
