::: {.problem}
Show that the total space of the tangent bundle $TS^2$ is not diffeomorphic to
\[
S^2\times\RR^2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\pi:E\to S^2
$$
be an oriented real rank-$2$ vector bundle, and let
$$
u_E
\in
H_c^2(E;\ZZ)
$$
denote its Thom class.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Thom isomorphism gives
$$
H_c^k(E;\ZZ)
\cong
H^{k-2}(S^2;\ZZ).
$$


::: pf-proof

For an oriented rank-$2$ real vector bundle
$$
\pi:E\to S^2,
$$
cup product with the Thom class gives the Thom isomorphism
$$
\Phi_E:
H^j(S^2;\ZZ)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H_c^{j+2}(E;\ZZ),
$$
$$
\Phi_E(\alpha)
=
\pi^*\alpha\smile u_E.
$$
Reindex with
$$
k=j+2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

In particular,
$$
H_c^2(E;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ,
\qquad
H_c^4(E;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and use
$$
H^0(S^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H^2(S^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
$$
The degree-$2$ compactly supported group is generated by
$$
u_E.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
s:S^2\to E
$$
be the zero section.
Then
$$
s^*u_E
=
e(E),
$$
the Euler class of the bundle.


::: pf-proof

This is the defining relation between the Thom class and the Euler class:
the Euler class is the pullback of the Thom class by the zero section.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The square of the Thom class satisfies
$$
\boxed{
u_E\smile u_E
=
\pi^*e(E)\smile u_E.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By the Thom isomorphism in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, there is a unique class
$$
\alpha\in H^2(S^2;\ZZ)
$$
such that
$$
u_E^2
=
\pi^*\alpha\smile u_E.
$$
Pull this identity back along the zero section:
$$
\begin{aligned}
s^*(u_E^2)
&=
s^*(\pi^*\alpha\smile u_E)\\
&=
\alpha\smile s^*u_E\\
&=
\alpha\smile e(E).
\end{aligned}
$$
Also,
$$
s^*(u_E^2)
=
s^*u_E\smile s^*u_E
=
e(E)^2.
$$
Since the base has dimension $2$, both sides lie formally above the top degree after this pullback, so this pullback alone does not identify $\alpha$.
Instead use the standard Thom-module relation:
for any class $\beta$,
$$
u_E\smile \Phi_E(\beta)
=
\Phi_E(e(E)\smile\beta).
$$
Taking
$$
\beta=1
$$
gives
$$
u_E^2
=
\Phi_E(e(E))
=
\pi^*e(E)\smile u_E.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For the tangent bundle
$$
E=TS^2,
$$
the Euler class is
$$
e(TS^2)
=
2\eta,
$$
where
$$
\eta
$$
generates
$$
H^2(S^2;\ZZ).
$$


::: pf-proof

For a closed oriented manifold, the Euler class of its tangent bundle evaluates on the fundamental class as the Euler characteristic:
$$
\langle e(TS^2),[S^2]\rangle
=
\chi(S^2)
=
2.
$$
If
$$
\eta
$$
is chosen with
$$
\langle\eta,[S^2]\rangle=1,
$$
then
$$
e(TS^2)=2\eta.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If
$$
u
\in
H_c^2(TS^2;\ZZ)
$$
is the Thom generator, then
$$
\boxed{
u^2
=
2\,\pi^*\eta\smile u
\ne
0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} with
$$
E=TS^2
$$
and then step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}:
$$
u^2
=
\pi^*(2\eta)\smile u
=
2\,\pi^*\eta\smile u.
$$
By the Thom isomorphism,
$$
\pi^*\eta\smile u
$$
generates
$$
H_c^4(TS^2;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$
Therefore twice this generator is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For the trivial bundle
$$
E_0
\coloneqq
S^2\times\RR^2,
$$
one has
$$
e(E_0)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The trivial rank-$2$ bundle admits a nowhere-zero section.
The Euler class is the obstruction to such a section, so it vanishes.
Equivalently, all characteristic classes of a trivial bundle vanish in positive degree.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

If
$$
u_0
\in
H_c^2(S^2\times\RR^2;\ZZ)
$$
is its Thom generator, then
$$
\boxed{
u_0^2=0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}:
$$
u_0^2
=
\pi^*e(E_0)\smile u_0
=
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Compactly supported cohomology rings are invariant under diffeomorphism.


::: pf-proof

A diffeomorphism
$$
\Phi:E\to E'
$$
is a proper homeomorphism, so pullback sends compactly supported cochains to compactly supported cochains and induces a graded-ring isomorphism
$$
\Phi^*:
H_c^*(E';\ZZ)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H_c^*(E;\ZZ).
$$
It preserves cup products.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
\Phi:
TS^2
\xrightarrow{\cong}
S^2\times\RR^2
$$
were a diffeomorphism.
Then
$$
\Phi^*(u_0)
=
\pm u.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both compactly supported degree-$2$ groups are infinite cyclic by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
A group isomorphism between them sends a generator to a generator, hence to
$$
\pm u.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

This gives a contradiction:
$$
0
=
\Phi^*(u_0^2)
=
\Phi^*(u_0)^2
=
(\pm u)^2
=
u^2
\ne
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives
$$
u_0^2=0.
$$
Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives multiplicativity of pullback.
Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} identifies the pulled-back generator, and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
u^2\ne0.
$$
The resulting equality is impossible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
TS^2
\text{ is not diffeomorphic to }
S^2\times\RR^2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The existence of a diffeomorphism leads to the contradiction in step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
