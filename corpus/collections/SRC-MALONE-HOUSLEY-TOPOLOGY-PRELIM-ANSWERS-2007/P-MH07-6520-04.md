::: {.problem}
Give a definition of the Hopf map
$$
S^3\longrightarrow S^2
$$
and verify that it is a fiber bundle.
:::

::: {.solution}

Identify
$$
S^3
=
\{(z,w)\in\CC^2:\abs z^2+\abs w^2=1\}
$$
and
$$
S^2
\cong
\CP^1.
$$
Define
$$
\boxed{
h:S^3\to\CP^1,
\qquad
h(z,w)=[z:w].
}
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map $h$ is well-defined and continuous.


::: pf-proof

No point of $S^3$ is the zero vector, so
$$
[z:w]
$$
is a well-defined point of projective space.
The quotient map
$$
\CC^2\setminus\{0\}
\to
\CP^1
$$
is continuous, and $h$ is its restriction to $S^3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The circle
$$
S^1
=
\{\lambda\in\CC:\abs\lambda=1\}
$$
acts on $S^3$ by
$$
\lambda\cdot(z,w)
\coloneqq
(\lambda z,\lambda w),
$$
and $h$ is constant on every orbit.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\abs\lambda=1,
$$
then
$$
\abs{\lambda z}^2+\abs{\lambda w}^2
=
\abs z^2+\abs w^2
=
1,
$$
so the action preserves $S^3$.
Moreover,
$$
h(\lambda z,\lambda w)
=[\lambda z:\lambda w]
=[z:w].
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Every fiber of $h$ is one $S^1$-orbit and is therefore homeomorphic to $S^1$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
h(z,w)=h(z',w').
$$
Then
$$
(z',w')
=
\mu(z,w)
$$
for some
$$
\mu\in\CC^\times.
$$
Both vectors lie in $S^3$, so
$$
1
=
\abs{z'}^2+\abs{w'}^2
=
\abs\mu^2
\left(
\abs z^2+\abs w^2
\right)
=
\abs\mu^2.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs\mu=1,
$$
so
$$
\mu\in S^1.
$$
Thus two points lie in the same fiber exactly when they differ by the $S^1$-action from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
U_0
\coloneqq
\{[z:w]\in\CP^1:w\ne0\}.
$$
Then
$$
U_0
\cong
\CC
$$
via
$$
[z:w]
\longmapsto
u
\coloneqq
\frac zw.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every point with
$$
w\ne0
$$
has the unique representative
$$
[u:1],
\qquad
u=z/w.
$$
This is the standard affine chart on
$$
\CP^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Define
$$
\Phi_0:
h^{-1}(U_0)
\longrightarrow
U_0\times S^1
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_0(z,w)
=
\left(
[z:w],
\frac{w}{\abs w}
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
(z,w)\in h^{-1}(U_0),
$$
then
$$
w\ne0,
$$
so
$$
w/\abs w
\in
S^1
$$
is well-defined.
Continuity is immediate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The map $\Phi_0$ is a homeomorphism with inverse
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_0^{-1}
\bigl(
[u:1],\lambda
\bigr)
=
\left(
\frac{\lambda u}{\sqrt{1+\abs u^2}},
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\abs u^2}}
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The displayed vector lies on $S^3$ because
$$
\frac{\abs u^2}{1+\abs u^2}
+
\frac1{1+\abs u^2}
=
1.
$$
Its projective class is
$$
[u:1],
$$
and its second coordinate has phase
$$
\lambda.
$$
Thus
$$
\Phi_0\circ\Phi_0^{-1}
=
\operatorname{id}.
$$

Conversely, if
$$
(z,w)\in h^{-1}(U_0),
$$
set
$$
u=z/w,
\qquad
\lambda=w/\abs w.
$$
Since
$$
\abs z^2+\abs w^2=1,
$$
one has
$$
\abs w
=
\frac1{\sqrt{1+\abs u^2}}.
$$
Therefore the inverse formula reconstructs exactly $(z,w)$.
Both maps are continuous, so $\Phi_0$ is a homeomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
U_\infty
\coloneqq
\{[z:w]\in\CP^1:z\ne0\}.
$$
Then
$$
U_\infty
\cong
\CC
$$
via
$$
[z:w]
\longmapsto
v
\coloneqq
\frac wz.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the second standard affine chart:
every point with
$$
z\ne0
$$
has unique representative
$$
[1:v].
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
\Phi_\infty:
h^{-1}(U_\infty)
\longrightarrow
U_\infty\times S^1
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_\infty(z,w)
=
\left(
[z:w],
\frac z{\abs z}
\right).
}
$$
Then $\Phi_\infty$ is a homeomorphism.


::: pf-proof

The proof is identical to steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.
In the coordinate
$$
v=w/z,
$$
the inverse is
$$
\Phi_\infty^{-1}
\bigl(
[1:v],\lambda
\bigr)
=
\left(
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\abs v^2}},
\frac{\lambda v}{\sqrt{1+\abs v^2}}
\right).
$$
Direct substitution verifies the two inverse identities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The maps $\Phi_0$ and $\Phi_\infty$ are fiber-preserving local trivializations with fiber $S^1$.


::: pf-proof

For
$$
\Phi_0,
$$
projection onto the first factor gives
$$
\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\Phi_0(z,w)
=
[z:w]
=
h(z,w).
$$
The same identity holds for
$$
\Phi_\infty.
$$
Since
$$
U_0\cup U_\infty
=
\CP^1,
$$
these two trivializations cover the base.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
S^1
\longrightarrow
S^3
\xrightarrow{\,h\,}
S^2
}
$$
is a fiber bundle.


::: pf-proof

Under the identification
$$
\CP^1\cong S^2,
$$
the map $h$ is the Hopf map.
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} identifies each fiber with $S^1$, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives local trivializations over an open cover of the base.
Thus $h$ is an $S^1$-bundle over $S^2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives both the definition of the Hopf map and the required fiber-bundle verification.
