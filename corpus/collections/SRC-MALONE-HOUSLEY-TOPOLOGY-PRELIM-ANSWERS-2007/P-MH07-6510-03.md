::: {.problem}
Let $M,N\subset\RR^3$ be one-dimensional submanifolds. Show that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $v\in\RR^3$ such that
$$
\norm{v}<\varepsilon
$$
and
$$
(M+v)\cap N=\varnothing.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix
$$
\varepsilon>0.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Define
$$
F:
M\times\RR^3
\to
\RR^3
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
F(m,v)
\coloneqq
m+v.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Translation in Euclidean space is smooth, so $F$ is a smooth map.
For fixed $v$, the partial map
$$
F_v:M\to\RR^3
$$
is exactly the translated inclusion
$$
F_v(m)=m+v.
$$
Its image is
$$
M+v.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The differential
$$
dF_{(m,v)}:
T_mM\oplus\RR^3
\to
\RR^3
$$
is
$$
dF_{(m,v)}(\xi,w)
=
\xi+w.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
F(m,v)=m+v
$$
in the two variables.
The derivative in the $M$-direction is the inclusion
$$
T_mM\hookrightarrow\RR^3,
$$
and the derivative in the parameter direction is the identity on
$$
\RR^3.
$$
Thus the total derivative is the sum
$$
\xi+w.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $F$ is a submersion.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
u\in\RR^3.
$$
Take
$$
\xi=0,
\qquad
w=u.
$$
Then by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
dF_{(m,v)}(0,u)=u.
$$
Hence the differential is surjective at every point.
Thus $F$ is a submersion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
F
\pitchfork
N.
$$


::: pf-proof

A submersion is transverse to every submanifold of its target.
Indeed, at a point with
$$
F(m,v)\in N,
$$
one has
$$
dF_{(m,v)}
\left(
T_mM\oplus\RR^3
\right)
=
\RR^3,
$$
so
$$
dF_{(m,v)}
\left(
T_mM\oplus\RR^3
\right)
+
T_{F(m,v)}N
=
\RR^3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

By the parametric transversality theorem, the set
$$
\mathcal T
\coloneqq
\{
v\in\RR^3:
F_v\pitchfork N
\}
$$
has full measure in
$$
\RR^3.
$$


::: pf-proof

The parametric transversality theorem states:
if
$$
F:X\times V\to Y
$$
is smooth and transverse to a submanifold
$$
Z\subseteq Y,
$$
then for almost every parameter
$$
v\in V,
$$
the partial map
$$
F_v:X\to Y
$$
is transverse to $Z$.

Apply this theorem with
$$
X=M,
\qquad
V=\RR^3,
\qquad
Y=\RR^3,
\qquad
Z=N.
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the required transversality of $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If
$$
F_v\pitchfork N,
$$
then
$$
(M+v)\cap N=\varnothing.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
p
\in
(M+v)\cap N.
$$
Then for some
$$
m\in M,
$$
$$
p=m+v.
$$
Transversality of
$$
F_v
$$
to
$$
N
$$
at $m$ would require
$$
d(F_v)_m(T_mM)
+
T_pN
=
T_p\RR^3.
$$

But
$$
d(F_v)_m(T_mM)
$$
is one-dimensional because $M$ is a one-dimensional submanifold, and
$$
T_pN
$$
is also one-dimensional.
Thus the dimension of their sum is at most $2$:
$$
\dim
\left(
d(F_v)_m(T_mM)+T_pN
\right)
\le2.
$$
This cannot equal the three-dimensional space
$$
T_p\RR^3.
$$
Contradiction.

Therefore no intersection point can exist.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The open ball
$$
B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
contains some
$$
v\in\mathcal T.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
\RR^3\setminus\mathcal T
$$
has measure zero.
The ball
$$
B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
has positive measure.
Hence it cannot be contained in the measure-zero exceptional set.
Thus
$$
B_\varepsilon(0)\cap\mathcal T
\ne
\varnothing.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Choose
$$
v\in
B_\varepsilon(0)\cap\mathcal T.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
\norm{v}<\varepsilon
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(M+v)\cap N=\varnothing.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Membership in
$$
B_\varepsilon(0)
$$
gives
$$
\norm{v}<\varepsilon.
$$
Membership in
$$
\mathcal T
$$
gives
$$
F_v\pitchfork N,
$$
and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives
$$
(M+v)\cap N=\varnothing.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required translation.
