::: {.problem}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be closed oriented manifolds. Prove that
\[
\chi(X\times Y)=\chi(X)\chi(Y).
\]
Do not use the Künneth formula.
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

The closed manifolds
$$
X
\quad\text{and}\quad
Y
$$
admit finite CW decompositions.


::: pf-proof

Every compact smooth manifold admits a finite triangulation, hence a finite
CW structure.
Closed manifolds are compact, so choose finite CW decompositions of
$$
X
$$
and
$$
Y.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
c_p(X)
$$
denote the number of
$$
p
$$
-cells in the chosen CW decomposition of
$$
X,
$$
and similarly define
$$
c_q(Y).
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{
\chi(X)
=
\sum_p(-1)^p c_p(X)}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
\chi(Y)
=
\sum_q(-1)^q c_q(Y)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For a finite CW complex, Euler characteristic is the alternating sum of the
numbers of cells in any finite CW decomposition.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The product
$$
X\times Y
$$
has a finite CW structure whose cells are the products
$$
e^p\times e^q
$$
of cells of
$$
X
$$
and
$$
Y.
$$
The product cell
$$
e^p\times e^q
$$
has dimension
$$
p+q.
$$


::: pf-proof

The product of two CW complexes carries the standard product CW structure
when one factor is locally finite; in particular this applies to finite CW
complexes.

Since an open
$$
p
$$
-cell is homeomorphic to
$$
\mathbb R^p
$$
and an open
$$
q
$$
-cell is homeomorphic to
$$
\mathbb R^q,
$$
their product is homeomorphic to
$$
\mathbb R^{p+q}.
$$
Thus its dimension is
$$
p+q.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Consequently, the number of
$$
k
$$
-cells in
$$
X\times Y
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
c_k(X\times Y)
=
\sum_{p+q=k}
c_p(X)c_q(Y)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every pair consisting of a
$$
p
$$
-cell of
$$
X
$$
and a
$$
q
$$
-cell of
$$
Y,
$$
step <1>3 gives one
$$
(p+q)
$$
-cell of the product.

There are
$$
c_p(X)c_q(Y)
$$
such pairs.
Summing over all
$$
p+q=k
$$
gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi(X\times Y)
&=
\sum_k
(-1)^k c_k(X\times Y)\\
&=
\sum_{p,q}
(-1)^{p+q}
c_p(X)c_q(Y).
\end{aligned}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the finite-CW Euler-characteristic formula to the product and substitute
the cell count from step <1>4.
Because all sums are finite, the order of summation may be rearranged freely.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The double sum factors:
$$
\sum_{p,q}
(-1)^{p+q}
c_p(X)c_q(Y)
=
\left(
\sum_p(-1)^pc_p(X)
\right)
\left(
\sum_q(-1)^qc_q(Y)
\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

Use
$$
(-1)^{p+q}
=
(-1)^p(-1)^q
$$
and distribute the product of the two finite sums.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\chi(X\times Y)
=
\chi(X)\chi(Y)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>2, <1>5, and <1>6.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The argument does not use the Künneth formula.


::: pf-proof

The proof uses only finite CW decompositions, the product CW structure, and
the cellular definition of Euler characteristic.
No homology computation of a product is invoked.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>7 is the required identity, and step <1>8 verifies the stipulated
method.
