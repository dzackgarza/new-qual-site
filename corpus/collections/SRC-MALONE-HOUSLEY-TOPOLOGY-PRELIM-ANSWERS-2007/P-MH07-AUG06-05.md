::: {.problem}
Compute the Gaussian curvature of
$$
x^2+y^2-z^2=1
$$
at the point $(1,0,0)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Near the point
$$
(1,0,0),
$$
the surface can be written as the graph
$$
x=f(y,z)
\coloneqq
\sqrt{1-y^2+z^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The defining equation is
$$
x^2+y^2-z^2=1.
$$
Near the point
$$
(1,0,0),
$$
the coordinate $x$ is positive.
Thus one may solve uniquely for
$$
x
=
\sqrt{1-y^2+z^2}.
$$
The square root is smooth near
$$
(y,z)=(0,0)
$$
because its argument is positive there.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The first derivatives of $f$ are
$$
f_y
=
-
\frac{y}{\sqrt{1-y^2+z^2}},
$$
and
$$
f_z
=
\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-y^2+z^2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate
$$
f(y,z)
=
(1-y^2+z^2)^{1/2}
$$
with respect to $y$ and $z$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

At
$$
(0,0),
$$
one has
$$
f_y(0,0)=0,
\qquad
f_z(0,0)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute
$$
y=z=0
$$
into the formulas from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The second derivatives at
$$
(0,0)
$$
are
$$
f_{yy}(0,0)=-1,
\qquad
f_{zz}(0,0)=1,
\qquad
f_{yz}(0,0)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
f_y
=
-y(1-y^2+z^2)^{-1/2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
f_{yy}
=
-(1-y^2+z^2)^{-1/2}
-
y^2(1-y^2+z^2)^{-3/2},
$$
so
$$
f_{yy}(0,0)=-1.
$$

Similarly,
$$
f_z
=
z(1-y^2+z^2)^{-1/2},
$$
and hence
$$
f_{zz}(0,0)=1.
$$

Finally,
$$
f_{yz}
$$
has a factor
$$
yz,
$$
so
$$
f_{yz}(0,0)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Hessian matrix of $f$ at the origin is
$$
\operatorname{Hess}_{(0,0)}f
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the matrix of second derivatives from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For a graph
$$
x=f(y,z),
$$
the Gaussian curvature is
$$
K
=
\frac{
f_{yy}f_{zz}-f_{yz}^2
}{
\left(
1+f_y^2+f_z^2
\right)^2
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard Gaussian-curvature formula for a graph in Euclidean $3$-space.
The formula is invariant under which coordinate is chosen as the graph variable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

At
$$
(1,0,0),
$$
the Gaussian curvature is
$$
\boxed{-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
f_y=f_z=0
$$
at
$$
(0,0),
$$
so the denominator in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is $1$.
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
f_{yy}f_{zz}-f_{yz}^2
=
(-1)(1)-0
=
-1.
$$
Therefore
$$
K(1,0,0)=-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is the required Gaussian curvature.

:::
