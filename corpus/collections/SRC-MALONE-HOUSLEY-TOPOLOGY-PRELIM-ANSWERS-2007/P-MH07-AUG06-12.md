::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the quotient of $S^2$ obtained by imposing
$$
x\sim -x
$$
for $x$ on the equator $S^1$. Compute the homology groups
$$
H_i(X).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
E
\subset
S^2
$$
be the equator.
The quotient identifies antipodal points of $E$ and makes no other identifications.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The quotient
$$
E/(x\sim -x)
$$
is homeomorphic to
$$
S^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Identify
$$
E
\cong
S^1
$$
by
$$
e^{i\theta}.
$$
The antipodal relation is
$$
e^{i\theta}
\sim
e^{i(\theta+\pi)}.
$$
The map
$$
q:S^1\to S^1,
\qquad
q(z)=z^2
$$
is constant precisely on these antipodal pairs and induces a homeomorphism
$$
S^1/(z\sim-z)
\cong
S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The space $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell
$$
v,
$$
one $1$-cell
$$
a,
$$
and two $2$-cells
$$
e_N^2,
\qquad
e_S^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
S^2
=
D_N\cup_E D_S
$$
as the union of the closed northern and southern hemispheres.

After passing to the quotient, the common equator becomes
$$
E/(x\sim-x)
\cong
S^1
$$
by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.
Give this circle the CW structure with one vertex $v$ and one open edge $a$.

The interiors of the two hemispheres are unaffected by the quotient and become two open $2$-cells attached to this common $1$-skeleton.
Thus the stated CW structure results.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The attaching map of each $2$-cell has degree $2$ onto the $1$-skeleton, after choosing the orientation of each $2$-cell appropriately.


::: pf-proof

The boundary circle of either hemisphere is the original equator
$$
E\cong S^1.
$$
Its attaching map into the quotient equator is exactly the quotient map
$$
q:E\to E/(x\sim-x).
$$
Under the identification from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, this map is
$$
z\longmapsto z^2,
$$
which has degree $2$.

The orientation of each $2$-cell is arbitrary.
Choose it so that its cellular attaching degree is $+2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The cellular chain complex with integral coefficients is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\ZZ^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
\ZZ
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
\ZZ
\longrightarrow
0,
$$
with
$$
\partial_1=0
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
\partial_2(m,n)=2m+2n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

There are two $2$-cells, one $1$-cell, and one $0$-cell by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
The unique $1$-cell is a loop at the single vertex, so
$$
\partial_1=0.
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, each oriented $2$-cell contributes degree $2$ to the unique $1$-cell.
Hence
$$
\partial_2(1,0)=2
$$
and
$$
\partial_2(0,1)=2.
$$
Therefore
$$
\partial_2(m,n)=2m+2n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The kernel of $\partial_2$ is
$$
\ker\partial_2
=
\{(m,-m):m\in\ZZ\}
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

The equation
$$
\partial_2(m,n)=0
$$
is
$$
2m+2n=0.
$$
Over $\ZZ$, this is equivalent to
$$
m+n=0,
$$
so
$$
n=-m.
$$
Thus the kernel is generated by
$$
(1,-1).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_2(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-cells, so
$$
H_2(X;\ZZ)
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The image of $\partial_2$ is
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
2\ZZ
\subseteq
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\{2m+2n:m,n\in\ZZ\}
=
2\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_1(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ/2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\partial_1=0,
$$
one has
$$
\ker\partial_1
=
\ZZ.
$$
Therefore
$$
H_1(X;\ZZ)
=
\ZZ/2\ZZ
\cong
\ZZ/2
$$
by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Since $X$ is connected,
$$
\boxed{
H_0(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The quotient of a connected space is connected.
Since $S^2$ is connected, so is $X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

All homology groups in degrees above $2$ vanish.


::: pf-proof

The CW structure from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} has no cells in dimensions greater than $2$.
Hence the cellular chain groups and therefore the homology groups vanish in those degrees.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_i(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,2,\\
\ZZ/2,&i=1,\\
0,&i>2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives all homology groups.

:::
