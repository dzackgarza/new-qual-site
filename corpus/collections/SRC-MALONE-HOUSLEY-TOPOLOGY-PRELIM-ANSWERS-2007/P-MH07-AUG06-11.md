::: {.problem}
Define a CW-complex structure on the Klein bottle $K$ and use it to compute
\[
H_*(K;\ZZ),
\qquad
H_*(K;\ZZ/2),
\qquad
H_*(K;\QQ).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Represent the Klein bottle as the quotient of a square with boundary word
$$
aba^{-1}b.
$$
This gives a CW structure with one $0$-cell
$$
v,
$$
two $1$-cells
$$
a,b,
$$
and one $2$-cell
$$
e^2.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The cellular chain groups with coefficients in an abelian group $G$ are
$$
C_2(K;G)\cong G,
$$
$$
C_1(K;G)\cong G^2,
$$
and
$$
C_0(K;G)\cong G.
$$


::: pf-proof

The chosen CW structure has one cell in dimensions $0$ and $2$, and two cells in dimension $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The cellular boundary
$$
\partial_1:C_1(K;G)\to C_0(K;G)
$$
is zero.


::: pf-proof

Both $1$-cells are loops attached at the unique vertex $v$.
Hence each cellular $1$-boundary is
$$
v-v=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The attaching word of the $2$-cell is
$$
aba^{-1}b.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard polygon presentation of the Klein bottle:
one pair of opposite sides is identified in the same direction and the other pair in opposite directions.
Reading the oriented boundary of the square gives the word
$$
aba^{-1}b.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The cellular boundary of the $2$-cell is
$$
\boxed{
\partial_2(e^2)
=
2b.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For a CW complex with one vertex and $1$-cells corresponding to generators, the cellular boundary of a $2$-cell records the total exponent sums of the generators in the attaching word.

In
$$
aba^{-1}b,
$$
the total exponent of $a$ is
$$
1-1=0,
$$
while the total exponent of $b$ is
$$
1+1=2.
$$
Therefore
$$
\partial_2(e^2)
=
0\cdot a+2\cdot b
=
2b.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Over $\ZZ$, the cellular chain complex is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\ZZ
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
\ZZ^2
\xrightarrow{0}
\ZZ
\longrightarrow
0,
$$
with
$$
\partial_2(1)=(0,2)
$$
in the basis $(a,b)$.


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} with
$$
G=\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The map
$$
\partial_2:\ZZ\to\ZZ^2
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\partial_2(n)=0,
$$
then
$$
(0,2n)=(0,0).
$$
Thus
$$
2n=0
$$
in $\ZZ$, which implies
$$
n=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore
$$
H_2(K;\ZZ)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-cells, so
$$
H_2
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

One has
$$
H_1(K;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\ker\partial_1
=
\ZZ^2.
$$
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\langle(0,2)\rangle.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_1(K;\ZZ)
&=
\ZZ^2/\langle(0,2)\rangle\\
&\cong
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Since $K$ is connected,
$$
H_0(K;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

The quotient-square model is connected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_i(K;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=0,\\
\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/2,&i=1,\\
0,&i\ge2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.
There are no cells above dimension $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

With coefficients in
$$
\mathbb F_2
\coloneqq
\ZZ/2,
$$
the map $\partial_2$ is zero.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
\partial_2(e^2)
=
2b.
$$
But
$$
2=0
$$
in
$$
\mathbb F_2.
$$
Hence
$$
\partial_2=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_i(K;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb F_2,&i=0,2,\\
\mathbb F_2^2,&i=1,\\
0,&i>2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Over $\mathbb F_2$, both cellular boundary maps vanish by steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.
Thus the homology groups equal the chain groups in dimensions $0,1,2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

With coefficients in $\QQ$, the map
$$
\partial_2:\QQ\to\QQ^2
$$
is injective with image
$$
\QQ\cdot b.
$$


::: pf-proof

Again
$$
\partial_2(1)=2b.
$$
Since
$$
2
$$
is invertible in $\QQ$,
$$
\langle2b\rangle
=
\langle b\rangle
$$
as a $\QQ$-subspace.
The map is injective because
$$
2q=0
$$
implies
$$
q=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Hence
$$
H_2(K;\QQ)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref},
$$
\ker\partial_2=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

One has
$$
H_1(K;\QQ)
\cong
\QQ.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
\partial_1=0,
$$
$$
H_1(K;\QQ)
=
\QQ^2/\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
$$
By step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}, the image is the one-dimensional subspace spanned by $b$.
Thus the quotient is one-dimensional, generated by the class of $a$:
$$
H_1(K;\QQ)
\cong
\QQ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

Since $K$ is connected,
$$
H_0(K;\QQ)\cong\QQ.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the rational-coefficient version of step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_i(K;\QQ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\QQ,&i=0,1,\\
0,&i\ge2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives the integral homology, step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} the mod-$2$ homology, and step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref} the rational homology.

:::
