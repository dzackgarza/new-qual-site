::: {.problem}
Define a CW-complex structure on the Klein bottle $K$ and use it to compute
\[
H_*(K;\mathbb Z),
\qquad
H_*(K;\mathbb Z/2),
\qquad
H_*(K;\mathbb Q).
\]
:::

Represent the Klein bottle as the quotient of a square with boundary word
$$
aba^{-1}b.
$$
This gives a CW structure with one $0$-cell
$$
v,
$$
two $1$-cells
$$
a,b,
$$
and one $2$-cell
$$
e^2.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The cellular chain groups with coefficients in an abelian group $G$ are
$$
C_2(K;G)\cong G,
$$
$$
C_1(K;G)\cong G^2,
$$
and
$$
C_0(K;G)\cong G.
$$


::: pf-proof

The chosen CW structure has one cell in dimensions $0$ and $2$, and two cells in dimension $1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The cellular boundary
$$
\partial_1:C_1(K;G)\to C_0(K;G)
$$
is zero.


::: pf-proof

Both $1$-cells are loops attached at the unique vertex $v$.
Hence each cellular $1$-boundary is
$$
v-v=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The attaching word of the $2$-cell is
$$
aba^{-1}b.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the standard polygon presentation of the Klein bottle:
one pair of opposite sides is identified in the same direction and the other pair in opposite directions.
Reading the oriented boundary of the square gives the word
$$
aba^{-1}b.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The cellular boundary of the $2$-cell is
$$
\boxed{
\partial_2(e^2)
=
2b.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For a CW complex with one vertex and $1$-cells corresponding to generators, the cellular boundary of a $2$-cell records the total exponent sums of the generators in the attaching word.

In
$$
aba^{-1}b,
$$
the total exponent of $a$ is
$$
1-1=0,
$$
while the total exponent of $b$ is
$$
1+1=2.
$$
Therefore
$$
\partial_2(e^2)
=
0\cdot a+2\cdot b
=
2b.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Over $\mathbb Z$, the cellular chain complex is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{0}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow
0,
$$
with
$$
\partial_2(1)=(0,2)
$$
in the basis $(a,b)$.


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>1--<1>4 with
$$
G=\mathbb Z.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map
$$
\partial_2:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z^2
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\partial_2(n)=0,
$$
then
$$
(0,2n)=(0,0).
$$
Thus
$$
2n=0
$$
in $\mathbb Z$, which implies
$$
n=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

There are no $3$-cells, so
$$
H_2
=
\ker\partial_2.
$$
Apply step <1>6.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>2,
$$
\ker\partial_1
=
\mathbb Z^2.
$$
By step <1>4,
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_2
=
\langle(0,2)\rangle.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)
&=
\mathbb Z^2/\langle(0,2)\rangle\\
&\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $K$ is connected,
$$
H_0(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$


::: pf-proof

The quotient-square model is connected.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_i(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2,&i=1,\\
0,&i\ge2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>7--<1>9.
There are no cells above dimension $2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

With coefficients in
$$
\mathbb F_2
\coloneqq
\mathbb Z/2,
$$
the map $\partial_2$ is zero.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>4,
$$
\partial_2(e^2)
=
2b.
$$
But
$$
2=0
$$
in
$$
\mathbb F_2.
$$
Hence
$$
\partial_2=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_i(K;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb F_2,&i=0,2,\\
\mathbb F_2^2,&i=1,\\
0,&i>2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Over $\mathbb F_2$, both cellular boundary maps vanish by steps <1>2 and <1>11.
Thus the homology groups equal the chain groups in dimensions $0,1,2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

With coefficients in $\mathbb Q$, the map
$$
\partial_2:\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q^2
$$
is injective with image
$$
\mathbb Q\cdot b.
$$


::: pf-proof

Again
$$
\partial_2(1)=2b.
$$
Since
$$
2
$$
is invertible in $\mathbb Q$,
$$
\langle2b\rangle
=
\langle b\rangle
$$
as a $\mathbb Q$-subspace.
The map is injective because
$$
2q=0
$$
implies
$$
q=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
H_2(K;\mathbb Q)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>13,
$$
\ker\partial_2=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
H_1(K;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\mathbb Q.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
\partial_1=0,
$$
$$
H_1(K;\mathbb Q)
=
\mathbb Q^2/\operatorname{im}\partial_2.
$$
By step <1>13, the image is the one-dimensional subspace spanned by $b$.
Thus the quotient is one-dimensional, generated by the class of $a$:
$$
H_1(K;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\mathbb Q.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $K$ is connected,
$$
H_0(K;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the rational-coefficient version of step <1>9.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_i(K;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&i=0,1,\\
0,&i\ge2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps <1>14--<1>16.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>10 gives the integral homology, step <1>12 the mod-$2$ homology, and step <1>17 the rational homology.

:::

:::
