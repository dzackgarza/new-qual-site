::: {.problem}
Compute the fundamental group of the space obtained from the disjoint union of two $2$-tori by identifying them along a pair of points.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
T_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
T_2
$$
be the two tori.
Choose distinct points
$$
p_i,q_i\in T_i
$$
and form $X$ by identifying
$$
p_1\sim p_2
$$
and
$$
q_1\sim q_2.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The space $X$ is homotopy equivalent to a graph of spaces with vertex spaces $T_1,T_2$ and two edges joining the two vertices.


::: pf-proof

The two point-identifications may be replaced, up to homotopy, by attaching two intervals
$$
I_p
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
I_q
$$
between the corresponding points of
$$
T_1
$$
and
$$
T_2.
$$
Indeed, collapsing each interval to a point recovers the original quotient, and attaching an interval along its two endpoints gives the homotopy pushout of the two point inclusions.
Since inclusions of points in CW complexes are cofibrations, the ordinary quotient and this graph-of-spaces model have the same homotopy type.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Collapsing one of the two connecting intervals produces a homotopy-equivalent space
$$
T_1
\vee
T_2
\vee
S^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

In the graph with two vertices and two parallel edges, either one of the two edges is a maximal tree.
Collapse, say,
$$
I_p
$$
to a point.
Collapsing a contractible tree in a graph of spaces does not change the homotopy type.

After this collapse, the two vertex spaces
$$
T_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
T_2
$$
meet at the collapsed point, so they form the wedge
$$
T_1\vee T_2.
$$
The remaining edge
$$
I_q
$$
now has both endpoints at this same wedge point, so it becomes an additional circle.
Hence
$$
\boxed{
X\simeq T^2\vee T^2\vee S^1.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The fundamental group of the $2$-torus is
$$
\pi_1(T^2)
\cong
\ZZ^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
T^2=S^1\times S^1,
$$
one has
$$
\pi_1(T^2)
\cong
\pi_1(S^1)\times\pi_1(S^1)
\cong
\ZZ\times\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

By van Kampen's theorem,
$$
\pi_1
\left(
T^2\vee T^2\vee S^1
\right)
\cong
\pi_1(T^2)
*
\pi_1(T^2)
*
\pi_1(S^1).
$$


::: pf-proof

For path-connected CW complexes, the fundamental group of a wedge is the free product of the fundamental groups of the wedge summands.
Apply this twice.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\ZZ^2
*
\ZZ^2
*
\ZZ.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and use
$$
\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Equivalently,
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\left\langle
a,b,c,d,t
\ \presentationbar\
[a,b]=1,\ [c,d]=1
\right\rangle.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The first copy of
$$
\ZZ^2
$$
has presentation
$$
\langle a,b\mid[a,b]=1\rangle,
$$
the second has
$$
\langle c,d\mid[c,d]=1\rangle,
$$
and the free factor
$$
\ZZ
$$
is generated by $t$ with no additional relation.
The free product combines these presentations.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives the requested fundamental group.

:::
