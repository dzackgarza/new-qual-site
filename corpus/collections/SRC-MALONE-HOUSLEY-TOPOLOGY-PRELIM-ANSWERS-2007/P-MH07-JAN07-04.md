::: {.problem}
Compute the de Rham cohomology of the $2$-torus. You may use the Poincaré lemma and the Mayer--Vietoris sequence. For example, you may first compute $H^*(S^1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The de Rham cohomology of the circle is
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k>1.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

Cover $S^1$ by two connected open arcs
$$
A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B
$$
whose intersection has two connected components.
The arcs $A$ and $B$ are contractible, so
$$
H^0(A)
\cong
H^0(B)
\cong
\RR
$$
and
$$
H^k(A)
=
H^k(B)
=
0
$$
for $k>0$.

The intersection
$$
A\cap B
$$
is the disjoint union of two contractible arcs, so
$$
H^0(A\cap B)
\cong
\RR^2
$$
and higher cohomology vanishes.

The beginning of the Mayer--Vietoris sequence is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H^0(S^1)
\longrightarrow
\RR^2
\xrightarrow{\rho}
\RR^2
\longrightarrow
H^1(S^1)
\longrightarrow
0.
$$
Under the natural identifications,
$$
\rho(a,b)
=
(a-b,a-b).
$$
Thus
$$
\ker\rho
\cong
\RR
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{coker}\rho
\cong
\RR.
$$
Exactness gives
$$
H^0(S^1)
\cong
\RR
$$
and
$$
H^1(S^1)
\cong
\RR.
$$
There are no forms in degrees greater than $1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Write
$$
T^2
=
S^1\times S^1.
$$
Choose the same two-arc cover
$$
S^1=A\cup B
$$
from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, and set
$$
U
\coloneqq
A\times S^1,
\qquad
V
\coloneqq
B\times S^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Clearly
$$
U\cup V
=
(A\cup B)\times S^1
=
S^1\times S^1
=
T^2.
$$
Thus $U,V$ form an open cover of the torus.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Both $U$ and $V$ deformation retract onto $S^1$.


::: pf-proof

The arcs
$$
A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B
$$
are contractible.
Hence
$$
A\times S^1
\simeq
S^1
$$
and
$$
B\times S^1
\simeq
S^1
$$
by contracting the first factor to a point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U)
\cong
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k>1.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply homotopy invariance to step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and then use step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The intersection
$$
U\cap V
$$
is homotopy equivalent to the disjoint union of two circles.


::: pf-proof

One has
$$
U\cap V
=
(A\cap B)\times S^1.
$$
The intersection
$$
A\cap B
$$
has two contractible components.
Thus
$$
(A\cap B)\times S^1
$$
has two components, each homotopy equivalent to $S^1$.
Hence
$$
U\cap V
\simeq
S^1\sqcup S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Consequently,
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U\cap V)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR^2,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k>1.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

De Rham cohomology of a disjoint union is the direct sum of the cohomologies of its components.
Apply step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} to the two circle components from step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Under the identifications in steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, the Mayer--Vietoris restriction-difference map in degree $0$ is
$$
\rho_0:
\RR^2
\to
\RR^2,
\qquad
\rho_0(a,b)
=
(a-b,a-b).
$$


::: pf-proof

A class in
$$
H^0(U)\oplus H^0(V)
$$
is represented by a pair of constants $(a,b)$.
On each of the two connected components of
$$
U\cap V,
$$
the restriction from $U$ is the constant $a$ and the restriction from $V$ is the constant $b$.
The Mayer--Vietoris map is the difference of restrictions, giving the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The map $\rho_0$ has kernel and cokernel both isomorphic to $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Its image is the diagonal line
$$
\{(c,c):c\in\RR\}
\subset
\RR^2.
$$
Hence its rank is $1$.
Therefore
$$
\dim\ker\rho_0
=
1
$$
and
$$
\dim\operatorname{coker}\rho_0
=
1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Under the identifications in steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, the Mayer--Vietoris map in degree $1$ is
$$
\rho_1:
\RR^2
\to
\RR^2,
\qquad
\rho_1(a,b)
=
(a-b,a-b).
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose the standard generator
$$
\eta
\in
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)
\cong
\RR.
$$
Via the deformation retractions
$$
U\simeq S^1,
\qquad
V\simeq S^1,
$$
the degree-$1$ classes on $U$ and $V$ are scalar multiples of the pullback of $\eta$ from the second factor.

Each component of
$$
U\cap V
$$
has the same second-factor circle, and the restriction of this generator to either component is the standard generator there.
Thus the difference of restrictions sends
$$
(a,b)
$$
to
$$
(a-b,a-b).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The map $\rho_1$ has kernel and cokernel both isomorphic to $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

It has exactly the same matrix as $\rho_0$ in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.
Thus it has rank $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

The Mayer--Vietoris sequence gives
$$
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\RR.
$$


::: pf-proof

The beginning of the exact sequence is
$$
0
\to
H^0(T^2)
\to
\RR^2
\xrightarrow{\rho_0}
\RR^2.
$$
Hence
$$
H^0(T^2)
\cong
\ker\rho_0
\cong
\RR
$$
by step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

There is a short exact sequence
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{coker}\rho_0
\longrightarrow
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\longrightarrow
\ker\rho_1
\longrightarrow
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The relevant portion of Mayer--Vietoris is
$$
\RR^2
\xrightarrow{\rho_0}
\RR^2
\longrightarrow
H^1(T^2)
\longrightarrow
\RR^2
\xrightarrow{\rho_1}
\RR^2.
$$
Exactness identifies the kernel of the map into $H^1(T^2)$ with
$$
\operatorname{im}\rho_0,
$$
and the image of the map out of $H^1(T^2)$ with
$$
\ker\rho_1.
$$
Thus the displayed short exact sequence follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\RR^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{coker}\rho_0
\cong
\RR
$$
and
$$
\ker\rho_1
\cong
\RR.
$$
The short exact sequence in step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} is a short exact sequence of real vector spaces, so it splits.
Hence
$$
\boxed{
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\RR\oplus\RR
\cong
\RR^2.
}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

One has
$$
H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}\rho_1.
$$


::: pf-proof

The relevant tail of Mayer--Vietoris is
$$
\RR^2
\xrightarrow{\rho_1}
\RR^2
\longrightarrow
H^2(T^2)
\longrightarrow
H^2(U)\oplus H^2(V).
$$
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
H^2(U)=H^2(V)=0.
$$
Hence exactness gives
$$
H^2(T^2)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}\rho_1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Therefore
$$
H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\RR.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} to step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref}:
$$
\operatorname{coker}\rho_1
\cong
\RR.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-16}

For
$$
k>2,
$$
one has
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The torus is a $2$-dimensional manifold, so differential forms of degree greater than $2$ vanish identically.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-17}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&k=0,2,\\
\RR^2,&k=1,\\
0,&k>2.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}, [](#s1-13){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-15){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-16){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-17){.pf-ref} is the required de Rham cohomology of the $2$-torus.
