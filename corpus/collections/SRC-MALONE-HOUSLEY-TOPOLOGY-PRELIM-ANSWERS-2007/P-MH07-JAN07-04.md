::: {.problem}
Compute the de Rham cohomology of the $2$-torus. You may use the Poincaré lemma and the Mayer--Vietoris sequence. For example, you may first compute $H^*(S^1)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The de Rham cohomology of the circle is
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k>1.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
Cover $S^1$ by two connected open arcs
$$
A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B
$$
whose intersection has two connected components.
The arcs $A$ and $B$ are contractible, so
$$
H^0(A)
\cong
H^0(B)
\cong
\mathbb R
$$
and
$$
H^k(A)
=
H^k(B)
=
0
$$
for $k>0$.

The intersection
$$
A\cap B
$$
is the disjoint union of two contractible arcs, so
$$
H^0(A\cap B)
\cong
\mathbb R^2
$$
and higher cohomology vanishes.

The beginning of the Mayer--Vietoris sequence is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H^0(S^1)
\longrightarrow
\mathbb R^2
\xrightarrow{\rho}
\mathbb R^2
\longrightarrow
H^1(S^1)
\longrightarrow
0.
$$
Under the natural identifications,
$$
\rho(a,b)
=
(a-b,a-b).
$$
Thus
$$
\ker\rho
\cong
\mathbb R
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{coker}\rho
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$
Exactness gives
$$
H^0(S^1)
\cong
\mathbb R
$$
and
$$
H^1(S^1)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$
There are no forms in degrees greater than $1$.
:::

<1>2. Write
$$
T^2
=
S^1\times S^1.
$$
Choose the same two-arc cover
$$
S^1=A\cup B
$$
from step <1>1, and set
$$
U
\coloneqq
A\times S^1,
\qquad
V
\coloneqq
B\times S^1.
$$

::: {.proof}
Clearly
$$
U\cup V
=
(A\cup B)\times S^1
=
S^1\times S^1
=
T^2.
$$
Thus $U,V$ form an open cover of the torus.
:::

<1>3. Both $U$ and $V$ deformation retract onto $S^1$.

::: {.proof}
The arcs
$$
A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B
$$
are contractible.
Hence
$$
A\times S^1
\simeq
S^1
$$
and
$$
B\times S^1
\simeq
S^1
$$
by contracting the first factor to a point.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U)
\cong
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k>1.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply homotopy invariance to step <1>3 and then use step <1>1.
:::

<1>5. The intersection
$$
U\cap V
$$
is homotopy equivalent to the disjoint union of two circles.

::: {.proof}
One has
$$
U\cap V
=
(A\cap B)\times S^1.
$$
The intersection
$$
A\cap B
$$
has two contractible components.
Thus
$$
(A\cap B)\times S^1
$$
has two components, each homotopy equivalent to $S^1$.
Hence
$$
U\cap V
\simeq
S^1\sqcup S^1.
$$
:::

<1>6. Consequently,
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U\cap V)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R^2,&k=0,1,\\
0,&k>1.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
De Rham cohomology of a disjoint union is the direct sum of the cohomologies of its components.
Apply step <1>1 to the two circle components from step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Under the identifications in steps <1>4--<1>6, the Mayer--Vietoris restriction-difference map in degree $0$ is
$$
\rho_0:
\mathbb R^2
\to
\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
\rho_0(a,b)
=
(a-b,a-b).
$$

::: {.proof}
A class in
$$
H^0(U)\oplus H^0(V)
$$
is represented by a pair of constants $(a,b)$.
On each of the two connected components of
$$
U\cap V,
$$
the restriction from $U$ is the constant $a$ and the restriction from $V$ is the constant $b$.
The Mayer--Vietoris map is the difference of restrictions, giving the displayed formula.
:::

<1>8. The map $\rho_0$ has kernel and cokernel both isomorphic to $\mathbb R$.

::: {.proof}
Its image is the diagonal line
$$
\{(c,c):c\in\mathbb R\}
\subset
\mathbb R^2.
$$
Hence its rank is $1$.
Therefore
$$
\dim\ker\rho_0
=
1
$$
and
$$
\dim\operatorname{coker}\rho_0
=
1.
$$
:::

<1>9. Under the identifications in steps <1>4--<1>6, the Mayer--Vietoris map in degree $1$ is
$$
\rho_1:
\mathbb R^2
\to
\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
\rho_1(a,b)
=
(a-b,a-b).
$$

::: {.proof}
Choose the standard generator
$$
\eta
\in
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$
Via the deformation retractions
$$
U\simeq S^1,
\qquad
V\simeq S^1,
$$
the degree-$1$ classes on $U$ and $V$ are scalar multiples of the pullback of $\eta$ from the second factor.

Each component of
$$
U\cap V
$$
has the same second-factor circle, and the restriction of this generator to either component is the standard generator there.
Thus the difference of restrictions sends
$$
(a,b)
$$
to
$$
(a-b,a-b).
$$
:::

<1>10. The map $\rho_1$ has kernel and cokernel both isomorphic to $\mathbb R$.

::: {.proof}
It has exactly the same matrix as $\rho_0$ in step <1>7.
Thus it has rank $1$.
:::

<1>11. The Mayer--Vietoris sequence gives
$$
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$

::: {.proof}
The beginning of the exact sequence is
$$
0
\to
H^0(T^2)
\to
\mathbb R^2
\xrightarrow{\rho_0}
\mathbb R^2.
$$
Hence
$$
H^0(T^2)
\cong
\ker\rho_0
\cong
\mathbb R
$$
by step <1>8.
:::

<1>12. There is a short exact sequence
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{coker}\rho_0
\longrightarrow
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\longrightarrow
\ker\rho_1
\longrightarrow
0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The relevant portion of Mayer--Vietoris is
$$
\mathbb R^2
\xrightarrow{\rho_0}
\mathbb R^2
\longrightarrow
H^1(T^2)
\longrightarrow
\mathbb R^2
\xrightarrow{\rho_1}
\mathbb R^2.
$$
Exactness identifies the kernel of the map into $H^1(T^2)$ with
$$
\operatorname{im}\rho_0,
$$
and the image of the map out of $H^1(T^2)$ with
$$
\ker\rho_1.
$$
Thus the displayed short exact sequence follows.
:::

<1>13. Therefore
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\mathbb R^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>8 and <1>10,
$$
\operatorname{coker}\rho_0
\cong
\mathbb R
$$
and
$$
\ker\rho_1
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$
The short exact sequence in step <1>12 is a short exact sequence of real vector spaces, so it splits.
Hence
$$
\boxed{
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R
\cong
\mathbb R^2.
}
$$
:::

<1>14. One has
$$
H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}\rho_1.
$$

::: {.proof}
The relevant tail of Mayer--Vietoris is
$$
\mathbb R^2
\xrightarrow{\rho_1}
\mathbb R^2
\longrightarrow
H^2(T^2)
\longrightarrow
H^2(U)\oplus H^2(V).
$$
By step <1>4,
$$
H^2(U)=H^2(V)=0.
$$
Hence exactness gives
$$
H^2(T^2)
\cong
\operatorname{coker}\rho_1.
$$
:::

<1>15. Therefore
$$
H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>10 to step <1>14:
$$
\operatorname{coker}\rho_1
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$
:::

<1>16. For
$$
k>2,
$$
one has
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The torus is a $2$-dimensional manifold, so differential forms of degree greater than $2$ vanish identically.
:::

<1>17. Hence
$$
\boxed{
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(T^2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&k=0,2,\\
\mathbb R^2,&k=1,\\
0,&k>2.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>11, <1>13, <1>15, and <1>16.
:::

<1>18. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>17 is the required de Rham cohomology of the $2$-torus.
:::
