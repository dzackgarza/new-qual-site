::: {.problem}
For a connected CW complex $X$, call a connected covering space $\widetilde X\to X$ *abelian* if it is normal and its deck-transformation group is abelian.

Show that $X$ has an abelian covering space which covers every other abelian covering space of $X$, and that such a universal abelian covering is unique up to isomorphism. Describe it explicitly for
\[
X=S^1\vee S^1.
\]
Carefully state the theorems used.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix a basepoint
$$
x_0\in X
$$
and write
$$
G
\coloneqq
\pi_1(X,x_0).
$$

<1>1. Connected covering spaces of $X$ are classified by subgroups of $G$, up to conjugacy.

::: {.proof}
Because $X$ is a connected CW complex, it is locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected.
The classification theorem for covering spaces therefore gives a bijection between:

$$
\{\text{connected covers of }X\text{ up to isomorphism}\}
$$
and
$$
\{\text{conjugacy classes of subgroups }H\le G\}.
$$

For a based connected cover
$$
p:(Y,y_0)\to(X,x_0),
$$
the associated subgroup is
$$
H
=
p_*\pi_1(Y,y_0).
$$
:::

<1>2. A connected cover corresponding to a subgroup
$$
H\le G
$$
is normal if and only if
$$
H\trianglelefteq G.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the normal-covering criterion in the classification of covering spaces.
Equivalently, a connected covering is regular precisely when its subgroup is normal.
:::

<1>3. If
$$
H\trianglelefteq G,
$$
then the deck-transformation group of the corresponding connected cover is
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Deck}(Y/X)
\cong
G/H.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
For a connected regular covering with corresponding normal subgroup $H$, the deck group is canonically isomorphic to the quotient of the base fundamental group by $H$.
:::

<1>4. A connected cover corresponding to
$$
H\le G
$$
is abelian if and only if
$$
\boxed{
[G,G]\subseteq H.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Suppose first that the cover is abelian.
By definition it is normal, so
$$
H\trianglelefteq G.
$$
By step <1>3 its deck group is
$$
G/H,
$$
and this quotient is abelian.
The quotient
$$
G/H
$$
is abelian exactly when every commutator lies in $H$, that is,
$$
[G,G]\subseteq H.
$$

Conversely, suppose
$$
[G,G]\subseteq H.
$$
Because the commutator subgroup is normal, the quotient
$$
G/[G,G]
$$
is abelian.
The subgroup $H/[G,G]$ is therefore normal in this abelian quotient.
Hence its inverse image $H$ is normal in $G$.
Moreover,
$$
G/H
$$
is a quotient of the abelian group
$$
G/[G,G],
$$
so it is abelian.
Thus the corresponding cover is normal with abelian deck group.
:::

<1>5. Let
$$
p_{\mathrm{ab}}:
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}
\to
X
$$
be the connected covering corresponding to the commutator subgroup
$$
[G,G].
$$
Then $p_{\mathrm{ab}}$ is an abelian covering.

::: {.proof}
The subgroup
$$
[G,G]
$$
is normal in $G$, and
$$
G/[G,G]
$$
is the abelianization of $G$, hence abelian.
By steps <1>2--<1>3, the cover is normal and has abelian deck group.
:::

<1>6. Its deck-transformation group is
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Deck}
\left(
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}/X
\right)
\cong
G^{\mathrm{ab}}
\cong
H_1(X;\mathbb Z).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
\operatorname{Deck}
\left(
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}/X
\right)
\cong
G/[G,G]
=
G^{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$
The Hurewicz theorem in degree $1$ identifies
$$
G^{\mathrm{ab}}
\cong
H_1(X;\mathbb Z).
$$
:::

<1>7. Let
$$
q:Y\to X
$$
be any connected abelian covering, corresponding to
$$
H\le G.
$$
Then
$$
[G,G]\subseteq H.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is exactly step <1>4.
:::

<1>8. There exists a covering map
$$
r:
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}
\to
Y
$$
such that
$$
q\circ r
=
p_{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The lifting criterion for covering spaces says that a based map
$$
f:(Z,z_0)\to(X,x_0)
$$
lifts to
$$
(Y,y_0)
$$
if and only if
$$
f_*\pi_1(Z,z_0)
\subseteq
q_*\pi_1(Y,y_0).
$$

Apply this to
$$
f=p_{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$
The subgroup associated to
$$
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}
$$
is
$$
[G,G],
$$
while the subgroup associated to $Y$ is $H$.
Step <1>7 gives
$$
[G,G]\subseteq H.
$$
Hence
$$
p_{\mathrm{ab}}
$$
lifts to a map
$$
r:
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}\to Y.
$$

Because both
$$
p_{\mathrm{ab}}
$$
and
$$
q
$$
are covering maps and
$$
q\circ r=p_{\mathrm{ab}},
$$
the lift $r$ is itself a covering map.
:::

<1>9. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}\to X
}
$$
covers every connected abelian covering of $X$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 constructs the required intermediate covering map for an arbitrary connected abelian cover
$$
Y\to X.
$$
:::

<1>10. The universal abelian covering is unique up to isomorphism over $X$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
p:Z\to X
$$
is another connected abelian cover that covers every connected abelian cover of $X$.
Let its subgroup be
$$
K\le G.
$$

Because $Z\to X$ is abelian, step <1>4 gives
$$
[G,G]\subseteq K.
$$

Because $Z$ covers the abelian cover
$$
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}\to X,
$$
the subgroup-containment criterion for intermediate coverings gives
$$
K\subseteq[G,G].
$$

Thus
$$
K=[G,G].
$$
The classification theorem from step <1>1 then implies that
$$
Z\to X
$$
and
$$
\widetilde X_{\mathrm{ab}}\to X
$$
are isomorphic as coverings.
:::

<1>11. Now let
$$
X=S^1\vee S^1.
$$
Then
$$
G
\cong
F(a,b),
$$
the free group on two generators.

::: {.proof}
The fundamental group of a wedge of two circles is the free product
$$
\mathbb Z*\mathbb Z,
$$
which is the free group
$$
F_2=F(a,b).
$$
:::

<1>12. Its abelianization is
$$
\boxed{
G^{\mathrm{ab}}
\cong
\mathbb Z^2.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Abelianizing the free group on $a,b$ imposes the single requirement that the generators commute and introduces no torsion.
Hence
$$
F(a,b)^{\mathrm{ab}}
\cong
\mathbb Z\langle\bar a\rangle
\oplus
\mathbb Z\langle\bar b\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z^2.
$$
:::

<1>13. Define a graph
$$
\Gamma
$$
whose vertices are the lattice points
$$
\mathbb Z^2.
$$
For every
$$
(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2,
$$
include:

$$
\text{an }a\text{-edge from }(m,n)\text{ to }(m+1,n),
$$
and
$$
\text{a }b\text{-edge from }(m,n)\text{ to }(m,n+1).
$$

::: {.proof}
This defines the usual infinite square-grid graph, regarded as a $1$-dimensional CW complex.
The square regions are not filled in; only vertices and edges are included.
:::

<1>14. Define
$$
p:\Gamma\to S^1\vee S^1
$$
by sending every vertex to the wedge point, every $a$-edge homeomorphically to the first circle, and every $b$-edge homeomorphically to the second circle.

::: {.proof}
At each vertex of $\Gamma$, exactly four oriented edge germs occur:
one incoming and one outgoing $a$-edge, and one incoming and one outgoing $b$-edge.
These map bijectively to the four edge germs at the wedge point of
$$
S^1\vee S^1.
$$
Away from vertices, each edge maps homeomorphically onto the corresponding open circle edge.
Thus every point of the base has an evenly covered neighborhood.
Hence
$$
p:\Gamma\to S^1\vee S^1
$$
is a connected covering map.
:::

<1>15. The group
$$
\mathbb Z^2
$$
acts on $\Gamma$ by translations:
$$
(r,s)\cdot(m,n)
=
(m+r,n+s),
$$
and similarly on edges.

::: {.proof}
Translation preserves horizontal $a$-edges and vertical $b$-edges.
It also commutes with the covering map $p$, because $p$ depends only on the edge label, not on the lattice location.
Thus every translation is a deck transformation.
:::

<1>16. The deck-transformation group of
$$
\Gamma\to S^1\vee S^1
$$
is exactly
$$
\mathbb Z^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
The translation action from step <1>15 is free and transitive on the fiber over the wedge point, whose vertices are all of
$$
\mathbb Z^2.
$$
For a connected regular covering, the deck group acts freely and transitively on each fiber.
Hence all deck transformations are precisely these lattice translations.
Thus
$$
\operatorname{Deck}(\Gamma/X)
\cong
\mathbb Z^2.
$$
:::

<1>17. The covering
$$
\Gamma\to S^1\vee S^1
$$
corresponds to the commutator subgroup
$$
[F(a,b),F(a,b)].
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>16, its deck group is
$$
\mathbb Z^2.
$$
The quotient
$$
F(a,b)/[F(a,b),F(a,b)]
$$
is also
$$
\mathbb Z^2
$$
by step <1>12.

More explicitly, lifting a word in $a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}$ starting at $(0,0)$ ends at the lattice point
$$
\left(
\text{total exponent of }a,
\text{ total exponent of }b
\right).
$$
The lift closes exactly when both exponent sums vanish, which is precisely the kernel of the abelianization map
$$
F(a,b)\to\mathbb Z^2.
$$
That kernel is the commutator subgroup.
:::

<1>18. Therefore the universal abelian cover of
$$
S^1\vee S^1
$$
is the infinite square-grid graph:
$$
\boxed{
\Gamma
\longrightarrow
S^1\vee S^1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Deck}(\Gamma/X)
\cong
\mathbb Z^2.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>17 shows that this cover corresponds to
$$
[G,G].
$$
By steps <1>5--<1>10, that is exactly the universal abelian covering.
:::

<1>19. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>5--<1>10 establish existence, universality, and uniqueness in general, and step <1>18 gives the explicit cover for
$$
S^1\vee S^1.
$$
:::
