::: {.problem}
Show that $SL_n(\RR)$ is connected and that
\[
\pi_1\bigl(SL_n(\RR)\bigr)
\]
is cyclic for every $n\ge2$. You do not need to prove that any fiber bundles you use are indeed fiber bundles.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Every matrix
$$
A\in SL_n(\RR)
$$
has a polar decomposition
$$
A=QS
$$
with
$$
Q\in SO(n)
$$
and
$$
S
$$
positive-definite symmetric with
$$
\det S=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Set
$$
S
\coloneqq
(A^TA)^{1/2}.
$$
Then $S$ is positive-definite symmetric and
$$
\det S
=
\sqrt{\det(A^TA)}
=
\sqrt{(\det A)^2}
=
1.
$$
Define
$$
Q
\coloneqq
AS^{-1}.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q^TQ
&=
S^{-1}A^TAS^{-1}\\
&=
S^{-1}S^2S^{-1}\\
&=
I,
\end{aligned}
$$
so $Q\in O(n)$.
Also
$$
\det Q
=
\frac{\det A}{\det S}
=
1.
$$
Hence
$$
Q\in SO(n).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map
$$
H:
SL_n(\RR)\times[0,1]
\to
SL_n(\RR)
$$
defined by
$$
H(A,t)
\coloneqq
A(A^TA)^{-t/2}
$$
is a strong deformation retraction onto $SO(n)$.


::: pf-proof

Using the polar decomposition
$$
A=QS
$$
from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, one has
$$
A^TA=S^2,
$$
so
$$
H(A,t)
=
QS^{1-t}.
$$
Because $S$ is positive-definite symmetric, the real powers
$$
S^{1-t}
$$
depend continuously on $(S,t)$.
Moreover,
$$
\det H(A,t)
=
\det Q\,
\det(S)^{1-t}
=
1,
$$
so
$$
H(A,t)\in SL_n(\RR).
$$

At
$$
t=0,
$$
$$
H(A,0)=A.
$$
At
$$
t=1,
$$
$$
H(A,1)=Q\in SO(n).
$$
If
$$
A\in SO(n),
$$
then
$$
A^TA=I,
$$
so
$$
H(A,t)=A
$$
for all $t$.
Thus $H$ is a strong deformation retraction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
SL_n(\RR)
\simeq
SO(n).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the conclusion of step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
In particular, the two spaces have the same number of path components and isomorphic fundamental groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The group
$$
SO(2)
$$
is homeomorphic to
$$
S^1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every matrix in $SO(2)$ has the form
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The assignment
$$
e^{i\theta}
\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}
$$
is a homeomorphism
$$
S^1\cong SO(2).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence
$$
SO(2)
$$
is connected and
$$
\pi_1(SO(2))
\cong
\ZZ.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and the standard facts
$$
S^1
\text{ is connected}
$$
and
$$
\pi_1(S^1)\cong\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For
$$
n\ge3,
$$
there is a fiber bundle
$$
SO(n-1)
\longrightarrow
SO(n)
\xrightarrow{\,p_n\,}
S^{n-1},
$$
where
$$
p_n(A)
$$
is the first column of $A$.


::: pf-proof

The group
$$
SO(n)
$$
acts transitively on
$$
S^{n-1}.
$$
The stabilizer of
$$
e_1
$$
consists of block matrices
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&B
\end{pmatrix}
$$
with
$$
B\in SO(n-1).
$$
Hence
$$
SO(n)/SO(n-1)
\cong
S^{n-1},
$$
giving the stated fiber bundle.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The space
$$
SO(n)
$$
is connected for every
$$
n\ge2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The base case
$$
n=2
$$
is step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

Assume
$$
SO(n-1)
$$
is connected.
For
$$
n\ge3,
$$
the base
$$
S^{n-1}
$$
is connected, and the fiber
$$
SO(n-1)
$$
is connected by induction.
The total space of a fiber bundle with connected base and connected fiber is connected.
Thus
$$
SO(n)
$$
is connected.
Induction completes the proof.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
SL_n(\RR)
\text{ is connected for every }n\ge2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
SL_n(\RR)
$$
deformation-retracts onto
$$
SO(n).
$$
Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} shows
$$
SO(n)
$$
is connected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

One has
$$
\pi_1(SO(3))
\cong
\ZZ/2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The unit quaternions form
$$
S^3
$$
and act on the imaginary quaternions
$$
\operatorname{Im}\mathbb H
\cong
\RR^3
$$
by conjugation.
This gives a surjective homomorphism
$$
S^3
\to
SO(3)
$$
with kernel
$$
\{\pm1\}.
$$
Hence
$$
SO(3)
\cong
S^3/\{\pm1\}
\cong
\RP^3.
$$
The sphere
$$
S^3
$$
is simply connected, so the double covering
$$
S^3\to\RP^3
$$
is universal.
Therefore
$$
\pi_1(SO(3))
\cong
\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

For
$$
n\ge4,
$$
the bundle in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} induces an isomorphism
$$
\pi_1(SO(n-1))
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\pi_1(SO(n)).
$$


::: pf-proof

The relevant part of the long exact homotopy sequence is
$$
\pi_2(S^{n-1})
\longrightarrow
\pi_1(SO(n-1))
\longrightarrow
\pi_1(SO(n))
\longrightarrow
\pi_1(S^{n-1}).
$$
For
$$
n\ge4,
$$
one has
$$
n-1\ge3,
$$
so
$$
\pi_1(S^{n-1})=0
$$
and
$$
\pi_2(S^{n-1})=0.
$$
Exactness therefore gives the stated isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Hence
$$
\pi_1(SO(n))
\cong
\ZZ/2
$$
for every
$$
n\ge3.
$$


::: pf-proof

The case
$$
n=3
$$
is step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.
For
$$
n\ge4,
$$
apply the isomorphism in step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} repeatedly:
$$
\pi_1(SO(n))
\cong
\pi_1(SO(n-1))
\cong
\cdots
\cong
\pi_1(SO(3))
\cong
\ZZ/2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore
$$
\pi_1(SL_n(\RR))
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&n=2,\\
\ZZ/2,&n\ge3.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\pi_1(SL_n(\RR))
\cong
\pi_1(SO(n)).
$$
Use step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} for
$$
n=2
$$
and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} for
$$
n\ge3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

In particular,
$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(SL_n(\RR))
\text{ is cyclic for every }n\ge2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Both
$$
\ZZ
$$
and
$$
\ZZ/2
$$
are cyclic.
Apply step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves connectedness, and step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} proves cyclicity of the fundamental group.
