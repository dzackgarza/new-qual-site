::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be a manifold and let
$$
f:X\to\RR^k
$$
be continuous. Show that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a smooth function
$$
g:X\to\RR^k
$$
such that
$$
\norm{ g(x)-f(x)}<\varepsilon
$$
for every $x\in X$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix
$$
\varepsilon>0.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every point
$$
p\in X,
$$
there exists an open neighborhood
$$
U_p
\subseteq
X
$$
such that
$$
\norm{ f(x)-f(p)}<\varepsilon
$$
for every
$$
x\in U_p.
$$


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
f:X\to\RR^k
$$
is continuous.
Therefore the inverse image
$$
f^{-1}
\left(
B_\varepsilon(f(p))
\right)
$$
is an open neighborhood of $p$.
Take this to be $U_p$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The collection
$$
\mathcal U
\coloneqq
\{U_p:p\in X\}
$$
is an open cover of $X$.


::: pf-proof

Each point
$$
p\in X
$$
belongs to its own neighborhood
$$
U_p.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists a locally finite smooth partition of unity
$$
\{\rho_i\}_{i\in I}
$$
subordinate to a locally finite refinement
$$
\{V_i\}_{i\in I}
$$
of $\mathcal U$.


::: pf-proof

Every smooth manifold is paracompact.
Hence every open cover admits a locally finite open refinement and a smooth partition of unity subordinate to that refinement.
Thus there are open sets
$$
V_i
$$
and smooth functions
$$
\rho_i:X\to[0,1]
$$
such that:
$$
\operatorname{supp}\rho_i
\subseteq
V_i,
$$
the family
$$
\{\operatorname{supp}\rho_i\}
$$
is locally finite, and
$$
\sum_{i\in I}\rho_i(x)=1
$$
for every $x\in X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For each index
$$
i\in I,
$$
choose a point
$$
p_i\in X
$$
such that
$$
V_i\subseteq U_{p_i}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The family
$$
\{V_i\}
$$
refines
$$
\mathcal U.
$$
Therefore each
$$
V_i
$$
is contained in at least one member
$$
U_p
$$
of the original cover.
Choose one such $p$ and call it $p_i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Define
$$
g:X\to\RR^k
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
g(x)
\coloneqq
\sum_{i\in I}
\rho_i(x)f(p_i).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The family
$$
\{\rho_i\}
$$
is locally finite.
Thus near every point only finitely many terms are nonzero, so the displayed sum is well-defined pointwise.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The map $g$ is smooth.


::: pf-proof

Each vector
$$
f(p_i)\in\RR^k
$$
is constant, and each
$$
\rho_i
$$
is smooth.
Hence
$$
x\longmapsto \rho_i(x)f(p_i)
$$
is smooth.
By local finiteness, the sum defining $g$ is locally a finite sum of smooth maps.
Therefore $g$ is smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If
$$
\rho_i(x)\ne0,
$$
then
$$
\norm{ f(p_i)-f(x)}<\varepsilon.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\rho_i(x)\ne0,
$$
then
$$
x\in\operatorname{supp}\rho_i
\subseteq
V_i.
$$
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
V_i\subseteq U_{p_i}.
$$
Hence
$$
x\in U_{p_i}.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\norm{ f(x)-f(p_i)}<\varepsilon.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

For every
$$
x\in X,
$$
one has
$$
\norm{ g(x)-f(x)}<\varepsilon.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using
$$
\sum_i\rho_i(x)=1,
$$
one has
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x)-f(x)
&=
\sum_i
\rho_i(x)f(p_i)
-
\sum_i
\rho_i(x)f(x)\\
&=
\sum_i
\rho_i(x)
\bigl(
f(p_i)-f(x)
\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\norm{ g(x)-f(x)}
&\le
\sum_i
\rho_i(x)
\norm{ f(p_i)-f(x)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every term with
$$
\rho_i(x)=0
$$
vanishes.
For every remaining term, step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\norm{ f(p_i)-f(x)}<\varepsilon.
$$
Hence
$$
\norm{ g(x)-f(x)}
<
\sum_i
\rho_i(x)\varepsilon
=
\varepsilon.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\text{for every }\varepsilon>0
\text{ there exists smooth }g:X\to\RR^k
\text{ with }
\norm{ g(x)-f(x)}<\varepsilon
\text{ for all }x\in X.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The map $g$ constructed in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} is smooth by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and satisfies the uniform error bound by step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} is the required approximation statement.

:::
