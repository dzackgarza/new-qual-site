::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a connected orientable noncompact $n$-manifold. Prove that
\[
H_n(M;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Poincaré duality with compact supports gives
$$
H_n(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H_c^0(M;\mathbb Z).
$$

::: {.proof}
For an orientable $n$-manifold $M$, Poincaré duality in the noncompact case states that cap product with the orientation class gives natural isomorphisms
$$
H_c^k(M;\mathbb Z)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H_{n-k}(M;\mathbb Z).
$$
Taking
$$
k=0
$$
gives
$$
H_c^0(M;\mathbb Z)
\cong
H_n(M;\mathbb Z).
$$
:::

<1>2. A compactly supported $0$-cocycle on $M$ is a locally constant integer-valued function.

::: {.proof}
A singular $0$-cochain with integer coefficients may be viewed as an integer-valued function on points.
The cocycle condition says that for every singular $1$-simplex
$$
\sigma:[0,1]\to M,
$$
the values at the two endpoints agree.
Thus the function is constant along paths and hence locally constant.
Equivalently,
$$
H^0(M;\mathbb Z)
$$
is the group of locally constant integer-valued functions.
The compact-support condition additionally requires that the closure of the nonzero set be compact.
:::

<1>3. Since $M$ is connected, every locally constant integer-valued function on $M$ is constant.

::: {.proof}
For a locally constant function
$$
\phi:M\to\mathbb Z,
$$
each fiber
$$
\phi^{-1}(k)
$$
is open.
Its complement is the union of the other open fibers, so it is also closed.
If two distinct fibers were nonempty, they would separate the connected space $M$.
Hence exactly one value occurs.
:::

<1>4. A constant compactly supported function on the noncompact space $M$ must be zero.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
\phi\equiv c
$$
with
$$
c\ne0.
$$
Then the support of $\phi$ is all of $M$:
$$
\operatorname{supp}\phi=M.
$$
But $M$ is noncompact, so this support is not compact.
Therefore a compactly supported constant function must have
$$
c=0.
$$
:::

<1>5. Hence
$$
H_c^0(M;\mathbb Z)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, a class in
$$
H_c^0(M;\mathbb Z)
$$
is represented by a compactly supported locally constant integer-valued function.
Step <1>3 makes it constant, and step <1>4 makes that constant zero.
Thus the group is trivial.
:::

<1>6. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_n(M;\mathbb Z)=0.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine the duality isomorphism from step <1>1 with the vanishing in step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
