::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a connected orientable noncompact $n$-manifold. Prove that
$$
H_n(M;\ZZ)=0.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

The problem is Problem 6 of the same examination. The packet gives two proofs, one through the long exact sequence of the triple $(M,U\cup V,V)$ applied to a cycle with compact image and one through compactly supported cohomology with Poincaré duality. The card takes the second: a compactly supported $0$-cocycle is locally constant, hence constant on the connected $M$, hence zero because $M$ is not compact, so $H^0_c(M;\ZZ)=0$ and $H_n(M;\ZZ)=0$ by Poincaré duality. What it adds is the step structure and the details of the locally-constant argument.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Poincaré duality with compact supports gives
$$
H_n(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H_c^0(M;\ZZ).
$$


::: pf-proof

For an orientable $n$-manifold $M$, Poincaré duality in the noncompact case states that cap product with the orientation class gives natural isomorphisms
$$
H_c^k(M;\ZZ)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H_{n-k}(M;\ZZ).
$$
Taking
$$
k=0
$$
gives
$$
H_c^0(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H_n(M;\ZZ).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

A compactly supported $0$-cocycle on $M$ is a locally constant integer-valued function.


::: pf-proof

A singular $0$-cochain with integer coefficients may be viewed as an integer-valued function on points.
The cocycle condition says that for every singular $1$-simplex
$$
\sigma:[0,1]\to M,
$$
the values at the two endpoints agree.
Thus the function is constant along paths and hence locally constant.
Equivalently,
$$
H^0(M;\ZZ)
$$
is the group of locally constant integer-valued functions.
The compact-support condition additionally requires that the closure of the nonzero set be compact.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Since $M$ is connected, every locally constant integer-valued function on $M$ is constant.


::: pf-proof

For a locally constant function
$$
\phi:M\to\ZZ,
$$
each fiber
$$
\phi^{-1}(k)
$$
is open.
Its complement is the union of the other open fibers, so it is also closed.
If two distinct fibers were nonempty, they would separate the connected space $M$.
Hence exactly one value occurs.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

A constant compactly supported function on the noncompact space $M$ must be zero.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\phi\equiv c
$$
with
$$
c\ne0.
$$
Then the support of $\phi$ is all of $M$:
$$
\operatorname{supp}\phi=M.
$$
But $M$ is noncompact, so this support is not compact.
Therefore a compactly supported constant function must have
$$
c=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Hence
$$
H_c^0(M;\ZZ)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, a class in
$$
H_c^0(M;\ZZ)
$$
is represented by a compactly supported locally constant integer-valued function.
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} makes it constant, and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} makes that constant zero.
Thus the group is trivial.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_n(M;\ZZ)=0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine the duality isomorphism from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} with the vanishing in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
