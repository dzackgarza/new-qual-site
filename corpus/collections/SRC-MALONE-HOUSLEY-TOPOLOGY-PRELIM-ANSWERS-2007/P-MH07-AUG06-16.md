::: {.problem}
Let $M_g$ be the closed orientable surface of genus $g$. Show that if $M_g$ retracts onto a graph
\[
X\subset M_g,
\]
then $H_1(X)$ has rank at most $g$.

You may use the algebraic fact that a nonsingular skew-symmetric bilinear form on $\mathbb Q^n$ cannot vanish identically on $V\times V$ for a subspace $V\subseteq\mathbb Q^n$ of dimension greater than $n/2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
i:X\hookrightarrow M_g
$$
be the inclusion and let
$$
r:M_g\to X
$$
be a retraction, so that
$$
r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_X.
$$

<1>1. On cohomology,
$$
i^*\circ r^*
=
\operatorname{id}_{H^1(X;\mathbb Q)}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Cohomology is contravariant.
From
$$
r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_X
$$
one obtains
$$
(r\circ i)^*
=
i^*\circ r^*
=
\operatorname{id}_{H^1(X;\mathbb Q)}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Therefore
$$
r^*:
H^1(X;\mathbb Q)
\to
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
$$
is injective.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
r^*(\alpha)=0.
$$
Apply
$$
i^*
$$
and use step <1>1:
$$
\alpha
=
i^*r^*(\alpha)
=
0.
$$
Thus
$$
\ker r^*=0.
$$
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
V
\coloneqq
\operatorname{im}
\left(
r^*:H^1(X;\mathbb Q)\to H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
\right).
$$
Then
$$
\dim_{\mathbb Q}V
=
\dim_{\mathbb Q}H^1(X;\mathbb Q).
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, the map
$$
r^*
$$
is injective.
Thus it identifies
$$
H^1(X;\mathbb Q)
$$
with its image $V$.
:::

<1>4. Since $X$ is a graph,
$$
H^2(X;\mathbb Q)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
A graph is a $1$-dimensional CW complex.
Its cellular cochain groups vanish in degrees greater than $1$.
Hence its cohomology vanishes in degree $2$.
:::

<1>5. The cup product vanishes identically on
$$
V\times V.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
u=r^*(\alpha),
\qquad
v=r^*(\beta)
$$
with
$$
\alpha,\beta\in H^1(X;\mathbb Q).
$$
Naturality of the cup product gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
u\smile v
&=
r^*(\alpha)\smile r^*(\beta)\\
&=
r^*(\alpha\smile\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
But
$$
\alpha\smile\beta
\in
H^2(X;\mathbb Q)
=
0
$$
by step <1>4.
Therefore
$$
u\smile v=0.
$$
Thus cup product is identically zero on
$$
V\times V.
$$
:::

<1>6. The bilinear pairing
$$
\langle-,-\rangle:
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
\times
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
\to
\mathbb Q
$$
defined by
$$
\langle a,b\rangle
\coloneqq
\langle
a\smile b,
[M_g]
\rangle
$$
is nonsingular and skew-symmetric.

::: {.proof}
Because
$$
M_g
$$
is a closed orientable surface, Poincaré duality identifies
$$
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
$$
with the dual of
$$
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
$$
via cup product with the fundamental class.
Hence the pairing is nonsingular.

Since degree-$1$ classes satisfy graded commutativity,
$$
a\smile b
=
-b\smile a,
$$
so the pairing is skew-symmetric.
:::

<1>7. The vector space
$$
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
$$
has dimension
$$
2g.
$$

::: {.proof}
The first homology of a closed orientable genus-$g$ surface is
$$
H_1(M_g;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z^{2g}.
$$
Thus
$$
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\mathbb Q^{2g}.
$$
:::

<1>8. The subspace $V$ is isotropic for the pairing in step <1>6.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>5,
$$
u\smile v=0
$$
for all
$$
u,v\in V.
$$
Therefore
$$
\langle u,v\rangle
=
\langle0,[M_g]\rangle
=
0.
$$
Thus the pairing vanishes identically on
$$
V\times V.
$$
:::

<1>9. Therefore
$$
\dim_{\mathbb Q}V
\le
g.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6, the pairing on
$$
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q)
$$
is a nonsingular skew-symmetric bilinear form.
By step <1>7, the ambient vector space has dimension
$$
2g.
$$
By step <1>8, the pairing vanishes identically on
$$
V\times V.
$$

Apply the algebraic fact given in the problem:
an isotropic subspace of a nonsingular skew-symmetric vector space of dimension
$$
2g
$$
has dimension at most
$$
g.
$$
:::

<1>10. For a graph $X$,
$$
\dim_{\mathbb Q}H^1(X;\mathbb Q)
=
\operatorname{rank}H_1(X;\mathbb Z).
$$

::: {.proof}
The first homology of a graph is a free abelian group:
$$
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\mathbb Z^r
$$
for some cardinal rank $r$.
Because $X$ is a retract of the compact surface $M_g$, the injection on rational cohomology from step <1>2 places
$$
H^1(X;\mathbb Q)
$$
inside the finite-dimensional space
$$
H^1(M_g;\mathbb Q),
$$
so $r$ is finite.

The universal coefficient theorem over the field $\mathbb Q$ gives
$$
H^1(X;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
H_1(X;\mathbb Z),
\mathbb Q
\right)
\cong
\mathbb Q^r.
$$
Thus
$$
\dim_{\mathbb Q}H^1(X;\mathbb Q)=r.
$$
:::

<1>11. Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
\le
g.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3,
$$
\dim H^1(X;\mathbb Q)
=
\dim V.
$$
Step <1>9 gives
$$
\dim V\le g.
$$
Step <1>10 identifies
$$
\dim H^1(X;\mathbb Q)
$$
with
$$
\operatorname{rank}H_1(X;\mathbb Z).
$$
Combining these equalities and inequalities yields the result.
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>11 is the required rank bound.
:::
