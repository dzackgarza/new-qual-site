::: {.problem}
Let $M_g$ be the closed orientable surface of genus $g$. Show that if $M_g$ retracts onto a graph
$$
X\subset M_g,
$$
then $H_1(X)$ has rank at most $g$.

You may use the algebraic fact that a nonsingular skew-symmetric bilinear form on $\QQ^n$ cannot vanish identically on $V\times V$ for a subspace $V\subseteq\QQ^n$ of dimension greater than $n/2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
i:X\hookrightarrow M_g
$$
be the inclusion and let
$$
r:M_g\to X
$$
be a retraction, so that
$$
r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_X.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

On cohomology,
$$
i^*\circ r^*
=
\operatorname{id}_{H^1(X;\QQ)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Cohomology is contravariant.
From
$$
r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_X
$$
one obtains
$$
(r\circ i)^*
=
i^*\circ r^*
=
\operatorname{id}_{H^1(X;\QQ)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Therefore
$$
r^*:
H^1(X;\QQ)
\to
H^1(M_g;\QQ)
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
r^*(\alpha)=0.
$$
Apply
$$
i^*
$$
and use step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}:
$$
\alpha
=
i^*r^*(\alpha)
=
0.
$$
Thus
$$
\ker r^*=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let
$$
V
\coloneqq
\operatorname{im}
\left(
r^*:H^1(X;\QQ)\to H^1(M_g;\QQ)
\right).
$$
Then
$$
\dim_{\QQ}V
=
\dim_{\QQ}H^1(X;\QQ).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the map
$$
r^*
$$
is injective.
Thus it identifies
$$
H^1(X;\QQ)
$$
with its image $V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Since $X$ is a graph,
$$
H^2(X;\QQ)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

A graph is a $1$-dimensional CW complex.
Its cellular cochain groups vanish in degrees greater than $1$.
Hence its cohomology vanishes in degree $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The cup product vanishes identically on
$$
V\times V.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
u=r^*(\alpha),
\qquad
v=r^*(\beta)
$$
with
$$
\alpha,\beta\in H^1(X;\QQ).
$$
Naturality of the cup product gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
u\smile v
&=
r^*(\alpha)\smile r^*(\beta)\\
&=
r^*(\alpha\smile\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
But
$$
\alpha\smile\beta
\in
H^2(X;\QQ)
=
0
$$
by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.
Therefore
$$
u\smile v=0.
$$
Thus cup product is identically zero on
$$
V\times V.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The bilinear pairing
$$
\langle-,-\rangle:
H^1(M_g;\QQ)
\times
H^1(M_g;\QQ)
\to
\QQ
$$
defined by
$$
\langle a,b\rangle
\coloneqq
\langle
a\smile b,
[M_g]
\rangle
$$
is nonsingular and skew-symmetric.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
M_g
$$
is a closed orientable surface, Poincaré duality identifies
$$
H^1(M_g;\QQ)
$$
with the dual of
$$
H^1(M_g;\QQ)
$$
via cup product with the fundamental class.
Hence the pairing is nonsingular.

Since degree-$1$ classes satisfy graded commutativity,
$$
a\smile b
=
-b\smile a,
$$
so the pairing is skew-symmetric.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The vector space
$$
H^1(M_g;\QQ)
$$
has dimension
$$
2g.
$$


::: pf-proof

The first homology of a closed orientable genus-$g$ surface is
$$
H_1(M_g;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ^{2g}.
$$
Thus
$$
H^1(M_g;\QQ)
\cong
\QQ^{2g}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The subspace $V$ is isotropic for the pairing in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
u\smile v=0
$$
for all
$$
u,v\in V.
$$
Therefore
$$
\langle u,v\rangle
=
\langle0,[M_g]\rangle
=
0.
$$
Thus the pairing vanishes identically on
$$
V\times V.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\dim_{\QQ}V
\le
g.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, the pairing on
$$
H^1(M_g;\QQ)
$$
is a nonsingular skew-symmetric bilinear form.
By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, the ambient vector space has dimension
$$
2g.
$$
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, the pairing vanishes identically on
$$
V\times V.
$$

Apply the algebraic fact given in the problem:
an isotropic subspace of a nonsingular skew-symmetric vector space of dimension
$$
2g
$$
has dimension at most
$$
g.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

For a graph $X$,
$$
\dim_{\QQ}H^1(X;\QQ)
=
\operatorname{rank}H_1(X;\ZZ).
$$


::: pf-proof

The first homology of a graph is a free abelian group:
$$
H_1(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ^r
$$
for some cardinal rank $r$.
Because $X$ is a retract of the compact surface $M_g$, the injection on rational cohomology from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} places
$$
H^1(X;\QQ)
$$
inside the finite-dimensional space
$$
H^1(M_g;\QQ),
$$
so $r$ is finite.

The universal coefficient theorem over the field $\QQ$ gives
$$
H^1(X;\QQ)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
H_1(X;\ZZ),
\QQ
\right)
\cong
\QQ^r.
$$
Thus
$$
\dim_{\QQ}H^1(X;\QQ)=r.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}H_1(X;\ZZ)
\le
g.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim H^1(X;\QQ)
=
\dim V.
$$
Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\dim V\le g.
$$
Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} identifies
$$
\dim H^1(X;\QQ)
$$
with
$$
\operatorname{rank}H_1(X;\ZZ).
$$
Combining these equalities and inequalities yields the result.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} is the required rank bound.
