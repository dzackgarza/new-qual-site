::: {.problem}
Let $M_{n\times n}$ be the manifold of real $n\times n$ matrices, identified with $\RR^{n^2}$ by choosing an ordering of entries. Let
$$
O(n)=\{A\in M_{n\times n}:AA^T=I\}.
$$
Prove that $O(n)$ is a submanifold of $M_{n\times n}$. What is its dimension?
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\operatorname{Sym}_n(\RR)
\coloneqq
\{S\in M_n(\RR):S^T=S\}.
$$
Define
$$
F:M_n(\RR)\to\operatorname{Sym}_n(\RR)
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
F(A)=AA^T.
}
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map $F$ is smooth.


::: pf-proof

Matrix multiplication and transpose are polynomial operations in the matrix entries.
Thus each entry of
$$
AA^T
$$
is a polynomial in the entries of $A$.
Hence $F$ is smooth.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One has
$$
O(n)=F^{-1}(I).
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
A\in O(n)
\iff
AA^T=I.
$$
This is exactly the condition
$$
F(A)=I.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The differential of $F$ at $A$ is
$$
\boxed{
dF_A(H)
=
HA^T+AH^T.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For real $t$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(A+tH)
&=
(A+tH)(A+tH)^T\\
&=
AA^T
+tHA^T
+tAH^T
+t^2HH^T.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore the coefficient of $t$ is
$$
HA^T+AH^T,
$$
which is the derivative.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every
$$
A\in O(n),
$$
the differential
$$
dF_A:
M_n(\RR)\to\operatorname{Sym}_n(\RR)
$$
is surjective.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
S\in\operatorname{Sym}_n(\RR)
$$
be arbitrary.
Set
$$
H
\coloneqq
\frac12SA.
$$
Because
$$
A\in O(n),
$$
one has
$$
AA^T=I.
$$
Using step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
dF_A(H)
&=
\frac12SAA^T
+
A
\left(
\frac12SA
\right)^T\\
&=
\frac12S
+
\frac12AA^TS^T\\
&=
\frac12S+\frac12S\\
&=
S,
\end{aligned}
$$
because
$$
S^T=S.
$$
Thus every symmetric matrix lies in the image.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence
$$
I
$$
is a regular value of $F$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the preimage
$$
F^{-1}(I)
$$
is
$$
O(n).
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} shows that the derivative is surjective at every point of this preimage.
That is exactly the definition of a regular value.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
O(n)
\text{ is a smooth submanifold of }
M_n(\RR)\cong\RR^{n^2}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the regular value theorem to
$$
F^{-1}(I).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The vector space of symmetric matrices has dimension
$$
\boxed{
\dim\operatorname{Sym}_n(\RR)
=
\frac{n(n+1)}2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

A symmetric matrix is determined by its
$$
n
$$
diagonal entries and its
$$
\frac{n(n-1)}2
$$
entries strictly above the diagonal.
Thus
$$
n+\frac{n(n-1)}2
=
\frac{n(n+1)}2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\dim O(n)
=
n^2-\frac{n(n+1)}2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The regular level set has codimension equal to the dimension of the target
$$
\operatorname{Sym}_n(\RR).
$$
The ambient matrix space has dimension
$$
n^2.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\dim O(n)
=
\frac{n(n-1)}2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Simplify step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}:
$$
n^2-\frac{n(n+1)}2
=
\frac{2n^2-n^2-n}{2}
=
\frac{n(n-1)}2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves the submanifold statement, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives its dimension.
