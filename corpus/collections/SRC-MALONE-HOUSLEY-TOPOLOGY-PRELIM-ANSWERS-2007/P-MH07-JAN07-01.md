::: {.problem}
Let $S^n\subset\RR^{n+1}$ be the unit sphere, and let
\[
h:\RR^{n+1}\to\RR
\]
be projection onto the last coordinate. Prove that $h|_{S^n}$ is a Morse function, find all of its critical points, and compute their Morse indices.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
H
\coloneqq
h|_{S^n}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

A point
$$
p=(x_1,\ldots,x_{n+1})\in S^n
$$
is critical for $H$ if and only if
$$
e_{n+1}
$$
is normal to $S^n$ at $p$.


::: pf-proof

The differential of
$$
h(x_1,\ldots,x_{n+1})=x_{n+1}
$$
is the linear functional
$$
dh_p(v)=\langle e_{n+1},v\rangle.
$$
A point $p$ is critical for the restriction $H$ exactly when
$$
dh_p
$$
vanishes on
$$
T_pS^n.
$$
This is equivalent to
$$
e_{n+1}
\perp
T_pS^n,
$$
that is, $e_{n+1}$ is normal to the sphere at $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The normal line to $S^n$ at $p$ is spanned by $p$.


::: pf-proof

The sphere is the level set
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_i^2=1.
$$
Its gradient at $p$ is
$$
2p,
$$
so the normal line is
$$
\RR p.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore the only critical points of $H$ are
$$
\boxed{
N=(0,\ldots,0,1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
S=(0,\ldots,0,-1).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, a critical point satisfies
$$
e_{n+1}\in\RR p.
$$
Thus $p$ is a scalar multiple of $e_{n+1}$.
Because
$$
\|p\|=1,
$$
the only possibilities are
$$
p=\pm e_{n+1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Near the north pole $N$, use local coordinates
$$
u=(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
$$
with
$$
\phi_N(u)
=
\left(
u_1,\ldots,u_n,
\sqrt{1-\|u\|^2}
\right).
$$
Then
$$
H\circ\phi_N(u)
=
\sqrt{1-\|u\|^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $u$ sufficiently close to $0$, the displayed point lies on $S^n$ and has positive last coordinate, so it parametrizes a neighborhood of $N$.
The last coordinate is exactly the displayed square root.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The Hessian of $H$ at $N$ is
$$
-I_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
F_N(u)
\coloneqq
\sqrt{1-\sum_{i=1}^n u_i^2}.
$$
Then
$$
\frac{\partial F_N}{\partial u_i}
=
-
\frac{u_i}{
\sqrt{1-\|u\|^2}
}.
$$
At
$$
u=0,
$$
all first derivatives vanish.

For $i=j$,
$$
\left.
\frac{\partial^2F_N}{\partial u_i^2}
\right|_{u=0}
=
-1,
$$
while for $i\ne j$,
$$
\left.
\frac{\partial^2F_N}{\partial u_i\partial u_j}
\right|_{u=0}
=
0.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{Hess}_N(H)
=
-I_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The north pole is nondegenerate and has Morse index
$$
\boxed{n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Hessian
$$
-I_n
$$
is nonsingular and has $n$ negative eigenvalues.
Thus the dimension of its maximal negative subspace is $n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Near the south pole $S$, use local coordinates
$$
\phi_S(u)
=
\left(
u_1,\ldots,u_n,
-
\sqrt{1-\|u\|^2}
\right).
$$
Then
$$
H\circ\phi_S(u)
=
-
\sqrt{1-\|u\|^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the analogous lower-hemisphere graph chart near $S$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Hessian of $H$ at $S$ is
$$
I_n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The local representative at $S$ is
$$
F_S(u)
=
-F_N(u).
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Hess}_S(H)
=
-
\operatorname{Hess}_N(H)
=
I_n
$$
by step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The south pole is nondegenerate and has Morse index
$$
\boxed{0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Hessian
$$
I_n
$$
is nonsingular and has no negative eigenvalues.
Hence its Morse index is $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Therefore $H$ is a Morse function with exactly two critical points:
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{critical point} & H\text{-value} & \text{Morse index}\\
\hline
S=(0,\ldots,0,-1) & -1 & 0\\
N=(0,\ldots,0,1) & 1 & n.
\end{array}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the complete critical set.
Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} show that both critical points are nondegenerate and compute their indices.
Therefore $H$ is Morse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives all required conclusions.
