::: {.problem}
Show that for any two points $x,y\in\mathbb R^n$ there is a compactly supported isotopy $\phi_t$ such that
\[
\phi_0=\operatorname{id},
\qquad
\phi_1(x)=y.
\]
:::

Set
$$
v
\coloneqq
y-x.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Choose a compactly supported smooth function
$$
\rho:\mathbb R^n\to[0,1]
$$
that is identically $1$ on an open neighborhood of the line segment
$$
[x,y]
\coloneqq
\{x+tv:0\le t\le1\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The segment
$$
[x,y]
$$
is compact.
Choose open sets
$$
[x,y]
\subset
U
\Subset
V
\subset
\mathbb R^n,
$$
with $\overline U$ compact and contained in $V$.
By the smooth bump-function lemma, there exists
$$
\rho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)
$$
such that
$$
0\le\rho\le1,
\qquad
\rho|_U=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define the smooth vector field
$$
X(z)
\coloneqq
\rho(z)v.
$$
Then $X$ has compact support.


::: pf-proof

The vector $v$ is constant, and $\rho$ is smooth, so $X$ is smooth.
Moreover,
$$
X(z)=0
$$
whenever
$$
\rho(z)=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{supp}X
\subseteq
\operatorname{supp}\rho,
$$
which is compact.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The vector field $X$ is complete.


::: pf-proof

A smooth compactly supported vector field on
$$
\mathbb R^n
$$
is bounded and has bounded derivative on all of $\mathbb R^n$.
Its integral curves therefore cannot escape to infinity in finite time, and the standard existence theorem for ordinary differential equations extends every maximal integral curve to all
$$
t\in\mathbb R.
$$
Thus $X$ is complete.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
\phi_t:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n
$$
be the time-$t$ flow of $X$.
Then
$$
\phi_0=\operatorname{id}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every flow satisfies
$$
\phi_0(z)=z
$$
for all $z$ by definition.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every
$$
0\le t\le1,
$$
one has
$$
\phi_t(x)
=
x+tv.
$$


::: pf-proof

Consider the curve
$$
\gamma(t)
\coloneqq
x+tv.
$$
For
$$
0\le t\le1,
$$
the point $\gamma(t)$ lies on the segment
$$
[x,y],
$$
and hence inside the region where
$$
\rho=1
$$
by step <1>1.
Therefore
$$
X(\gamma(t))
=
\rho(\gamma(t))v
=
v.
$$
But
$$
\gamma'(t)=v.
$$
Thus $\gamma$ is an integral curve of $X$ through $x$.
Uniqueness of solutions to the flow equation gives
$$
\phi_t(x)=\gamma(t)=x+tv.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In particular,
$$
\phi_1(x)=y.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step <1>5,
$$
\phi_1(x)
=
x+v
=
x+(y-x)
=
y.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Each diffeomorphism $\phi_t$ is the identity outside the compact set
$$
\operatorname{supp}X.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
z\notin\operatorname{supp}X,
$$
then $X$ vanishes on a neighborhood of $z$.
The constant curve
$$
\gamma(t)=z
$$
is therefore the unique integral curve through $z$.
Hence
$$
\phi_t(z)=z
$$
for all $t$.
Thus
$$
\phi_t
$$
is the identity outside the fixed compact set
$$
\operatorname{supp}X.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\{\phi_t\}_{0\le t\le1}
\text{ is a compactly supported isotopy with }
\phi_0=\operatorname{id}
\text{ and }
\phi_1(x)=y.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The flow of a complete smooth vector field is a smooth one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms.
Step <1>4 gives the initial map, step <1>6 gives the prescribed endpoint motion, and step <1>7 gives compact support.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>8 is the required isotopy.

:::

:::
