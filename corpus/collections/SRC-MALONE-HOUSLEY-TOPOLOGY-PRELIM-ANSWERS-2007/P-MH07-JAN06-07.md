::: {.problem}
Let
\[
a:S^n\to S^n
\]
be the antipodal map, and let $\omega$ be a smooth form on $S^n$ satisfying $a^*\omega=\omega$.

Prove that if $\omega$ is exact, then there is a smooth form $\eta$ such that
\[
\omega=d\eta,
\qquad
a^*\eta=\eta.
\]
Use the previous problem and the fact that $H^k(S^n)=0$ for $0<k<n$ to deduce that
\[
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\RP^n)=0
\]
for $0<k<n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Suppose
$$
\omega=d\eta_0
$$
for some smooth form
$$
\eta_0
$$
on $S^n$.
Define
$$
\boxed{
\eta
\coloneqq
\frac12
\left(
\eta_0+a^*\eta_0
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The form $\eta$ is smooth because it is a linear combination of smooth forms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The form $\eta$ is antipodally invariant:
$$
a^*\eta=\eta.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
a\circ a=\operatorname{id}_{S^n},
$$
one has
$$
(a^*)^2=\operatorname{id}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
a^*\eta
&=
\frac12
\left(
a^*\eta_0
+
(a^*)^2\eta_0
\right)\\
&=
\frac12
\left(
a^*\eta_0+\eta_0
\right)\\
&=
\eta.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
d\eta=\omega.
$$


::: pf-proof

Exterior differentiation commutes with pullback, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\eta
&=
\frac12
\left(
d\eta_0+d(a^*\eta_0)
\right)\\
&=
\frac12
\left(
\omega+a^*(d\eta_0)
\right)\\
&=
\frac12
\left(
\omega+a^*\omega
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
By hypothesis,
$$
a^*\omega=\omega.
$$
Hence
$$
d\eta=\omega.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Therefore every exact antipodally invariant form on $S^n$ has an antipodally invariant primitive.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} construct the required primitive explicitly.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Let
$$
p:S^n\to\RP^n
$$
be the antipodal double covering.
If
$$
\alpha
$$
is a smooth form on $\RP^n$, then
$$
p^*\alpha
$$
is antipodally invariant:
$$
a^*(p^*\alpha)=p^*\alpha.
$$


::: pf-proof

The quotient map satisfies
$$
p\circ a=p.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
a^*(p^*\alpha)
&=
(p\circ a)^*\alpha\\
&=
p^*\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Conversely, every antipodally invariant smooth form on $S^n$ is the pullback of a unique smooth form on $\RP^n$.


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
p:S^n\to\RP^n
$$
is a covering map and hence a local diffeomorphism.
Let
$$
\eta
$$
be a smooth form with
$$
a^*\eta=\eta.
$$

For each evenly covered open set
$$
U\subset\RP^n,
$$
choose one sheet
$$
\widetilde U
\subset
S^n
$$
such that
$$
p|_{\widetilde U}:\widetilde U\to U
$$
is a diffeomorphism.
Define a form on $U$ by
$$
\beta_U
\coloneqq
\left(
(p|_{\widetilde U})^{-1}
\right)^*\eta.
$$

Choosing the other sheet replaces
$$
\widetilde U
$$
by
$$
a(\widetilde U).
$$
The invariance
$$
a^*\eta=\eta
$$
shows that the resulting form on $U$ is unchanged.
Thus the local forms glue to a global smooth form
$$
\beta
$$
on
$$
\RP^n
$$
with
$$
p^*\beta=\eta.
$$

Uniqueness follows because $p$ is surjective and a local diffeomorphism: if
$$
p^*\beta_1=p^*\beta_2,
$$
then on each evenly covered chart pullback by a local inverse gives
$$
\beta_1=\beta_2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Fix
$$
0<k<n,
$$
and let
$$
\alpha
\in
\Omega^k(\RP^n)
$$
be closed:
$$
d\alpha=0.
$$
Then
$$
p^*\alpha
$$
is a closed antipodally invariant $k$-form on $S^n$.


::: pf-proof

Antipodal invariance follows from step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.
Moreover,
$$
d(p^*\alpha)
=
p^*(d\alpha)
=
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Since
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)=0
$$
for
$$
0<k<n,
$$
the form
$$
p^*\alpha
$$
is exact.


::: pf-proof

Vanishing of
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
$$
means every closed $k$-form on
$$
S^n
$$
is exact.
Apply this to the closed form from step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

There exists an antipodally invariant
$$
(k-1)\text{-form }\eta
$$
on $S^n$ such that
$$
p^*\alpha=d\eta.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref},
$$
p^*\alpha
$$
is exact.
By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, it is antipodally invariant.
Apply step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

There exists a smooth
$$
(k-1)\text{-form }\beta
$$
on
$$
\RP^n
$$
such that
$$
p^*\beta=\eta.
$$


::: pf-proof

The form
$$
\eta
$$
is antipodally invariant by step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.
Apply the descent statement from step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

One has
$$
p^*(d\beta-\alpha)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using steps [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-10){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
p^*(d\beta-\alpha)
&=
d(p^*\beta)-p^*\alpha\\
&=
d\eta-d\eta\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Pullback
$$
p^*:
\Omega^k(\RP^n)
\to
\Omega^k(S^n)
$$
is injective.


::: pf-proof

The map $p$ is a surjective local diffeomorphism.
If
$$
p^*\gamma=0,
$$
then on any evenly covered open set
$$
U\subset\RP^n
$$
and any sheet
$$
\widetilde U
$$
over $U$, one has
$$
(p|_{\widetilde U})^*\gamma|_U=0.
$$
Pull back by the inverse diffeomorphism
$$
(p|_{\widetilde U})^{-1}
$$
to get
$$
\gamma|_U=0.
$$
Such sets $U$ cover the base, so
$$
\gamma=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

Therefore
$$
d\beta=\alpha.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives
$$
p^*(d\beta-\alpha)=0.
$$
By injectivity from step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref},
$$
d\beta-\alpha=0.
$$
Hence
$$
d\beta=\alpha.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

Every closed $k$-form on
$$
\RP^n
$$
is exact for
$$
0<k<n.
$$


::: pf-proof

The closed form
$$
\alpha
$$
in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} was arbitrary, and step [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} gives an explicit primitive on
$$
\RP^n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\RP^n)=0
\qquad
\text{for }0<k<n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition, de Rham cohomology in degree $k$ is closed $k$-forms modulo exact $k$-forms.
Step [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} says these two spaces coincide.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove the invariant-primitive statement, and steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} deduce the intermediate de Rham cohomology vanishing.
