::: {.problem}
Find a perturbation of the identity map
\[
\mathbb{RP}^3\to\mathbb{RP}^3
\]
which is a Lefschetz map, and compute its Lefschetz number.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix
$$
\varepsilon>0,
$$
and define
$$
f_\varepsilon:\mathbb{RP}^3\to\mathbb{RP}^3
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
f_\varepsilon
\bigl(
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]
\bigr)
=
[x_0:(1+\varepsilon)x_1:(1+2\varepsilon)x_2:(1+3\varepsilon)x_3].
}
$$

<1>1. The map $f_\varepsilon$ is well-defined on $\mathbb{RP}^3$.

::: {.proof}
A projective point is an equivalence class of nonzero vectors under multiplication by a nonzero scalar.
If
$$
(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)
\sim
\lambda(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3),
$$
then applying the diagonal linear map
$$
A_\varepsilon
\coloneqq
\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(
1,
1+\varepsilon,
1+2\varepsilon,
1+3\varepsilon
\bigr)
$$
gives vectors that still differ by the same scalar $\lambda$.
Thus $A_\varepsilon$ descends to a well-defined map on projective space.
:::

<1>2. The map $f_\varepsilon$ is homotopic to the identity.

::: {.proof}
For
$$
0\le t\le1,
$$
define
$$
H_t
\bigl(
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]
\bigr)
\coloneqq
[x_0:(1+t\varepsilon)x_1:(1+2t\varepsilon)x_2:(1+3t\varepsilon)x_3].
$$
Every diagonal coefficient is positive, so each diagonal linear map is invertible and induces a continuous map on $\mathbb{RP}^3$.
Moreover,
$$
H_0=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{RP}^3}
$$
and
$$
H_1=f_\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $H$ is a homotopy from the identity to $f_\varepsilon$.
:::

<1>3. For arbitrarily small $\varepsilon>0$, the map $f_\varepsilon$ is an arbitrarily small smooth perturbation of the identity.

::: {.proof}
The inducing matrices
$$
A_\varepsilon
=
\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(
1,
1+\varepsilon,
1+2\varepsilon,
1+3\varepsilon
\bigr)
$$
converge to the identity matrix as
$$
\varepsilon\to0.
$$
The induced projective transformations therefore converge smoothly to the identity map.
:::

<1>4. The fixed points of $f_\varepsilon$ are exactly
$$
[1:0:0:0],
\quad
[0:1:0:0],
\quad
[0:0:1:0],
\quad
[0:0:0:1].
$$

::: {.proof}
A projective point
$$
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]
$$
is fixed exactly when there exists a nonzero scalar $\lambda$ such that
$$
x_0
=
\lambda x_0,
$$
$$
(1+\varepsilon)x_1
=
\lambda x_1,
$$
$$
(1+2\varepsilon)x_2
=
\lambda x_2,
$$
and
$$
(1+3\varepsilon)x_3
=
\lambda x_3.
$$
The four diagonal coefficients
$$
1,
\quad
1+\varepsilon,
\quad
1+2\varepsilon,
\quad
1+3\varepsilon
$$
are distinct.
Therefore at most one homogeneous coordinate can be nonzero.
The four coordinate-axis points are plainly fixed.
Hence these are exactly the fixed points.
:::

<1>5. Let
$$
\lambda_0=1,
\quad
\lambda_1=1+\varepsilon,
\quad
\lambda_2=1+2\varepsilon,
\quad
\lambda_3=1+3\varepsilon.
$$
At the fixed point corresponding to the $i$th coordinate axis, the derivative of $f_\varepsilon$ has eigenvalues
$$
\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i},
\qquad
j\ne i.
$$

::: {.proof}
Work in the affine chart
$$
x_i\ne0
$$
and normalize
$$
x_i=1.
$$
The remaining affine coordinates are
$$
u_j
\coloneqq
\frac{x_j}{x_i},
\qquad
j\ne i.
$$
Under $f_\varepsilon$,
$$
u_j
\longmapsto
\frac{\lambda_jx_j}{\lambda_ix_i}
=
\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i}u_j.
$$
Thus the derivative is diagonal with precisely the displayed eigenvalues.
:::

<1>6. None of the derivatives at the fixed points has eigenvalue $1$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>5, an eigenvalue at the $i$th fixed point is
$$
\lambda_j/\lambda_i
$$
with
$$
j\ne i.
$$
Because the $\lambda_i$ are pairwise distinct,
$$
\lambda_j/\lambda_i\ne1.
$$
:::

<1>7. Therefore $f_\varepsilon$ is a Lefschetz map.

::: {.proof}
The fixed points are isolated by step <1>4.
Step <1>6 shows that at every fixed point
$$
\det
\bigl(
I-Df_\varepsilon
\bigr)
\ne0.
$$
Thus every fixed point is nondegenerate, which is the Lefschetz condition for this smooth map.
:::

<1>8. With rational coefficients,
$$
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&k=0,3,\\
0,&k=1,2.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
The manifold
$$
\mathbb{RP}^3
$$
is connected and orientable.
Its integral homology is
$$
H_0\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_1\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\qquad
H_2=0,
\qquad
H_3\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
Tensoring with $\mathbb Q$ kills the torsion in degree $1$ and gives the displayed rational homology.
:::

<1>9. The identity map on $\mathbb{RP}^3$ has Lefschetz number
$$
L(\operatorname{id})
=
1-1
=
0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By definition,
$$
L(\operatorname{id})
=
\sum_{k=0}^{3}
(-1)^k
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
\operatorname{id}_*
:
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb Q)
\to
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb Q)
\right).
$$
By step <1>8, only degrees
$$
0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
3
$$
contribute.
The identity has trace $1$ in each of these one-dimensional groups.
Hence
$$
L(\operatorname{id})
=
1+(-1)^3
=
0.
$$
:::

<1>10. The map $f_\varepsilon$ has Lefschetz number
$$
\boxed{L(f_\varepsilon)=0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The Lefschetz number is invariant under homotopy.
By step <1>2,
$$
f_\varepsilon
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{RP}^3}.
$$
Therefore
$$
L(f_\varepsilon)
=
L(\operatorname{id})
=
0
$$
by step <1>9.
:::

<1>11. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3 and <1>7 give an arbitrarily small perturbation of the identity that is a Lefschetz map, and step <1>10 computes its Lefschetz number.
:::
