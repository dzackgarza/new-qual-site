::: {.problem}
Find a perturbation of the identity map
$$
\RP^3\to\RP^3
$$
which is a Lefschetz map, and compute its Lefschetz number.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix
$$
\varepsilon>0,
$$
and define
$$
f_\varepsilon:\RP^3\to\RP^3
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
f_\varepsilon
\bigl(
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]
\bigr)
=
[x_0:(1+\varepsilon)x_1:(1+2\varepsilon)x_2:(1+3\varepsilon)x_3].
}
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The map $f_\varepsilon$ is well-defined on $\RP^3$.


::: pf-proof

A projective point is an equivalence class of nonzero vectors under multiplication by a nonzero scalar.
If
$$
(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)
\sim
\lambda(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3),
$$
then applying the diagonal linear map
$$
A_\varepsilon
\coloneqq
\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(
1,
1+\varepsilon,
1+2\varepsilon,
1+3\varepsilon
\bigr)
$$
gives vectors that still differ by the same scalar $\lambda$.
Thus $A_\varepsilon$ descends to a well-defined map on projective space.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map $f_\varepsilon$ is homotopic to the identity.


::: pf-proof

For
$$
0\le t\le1,
$$
define
$$
H_t
\bigl(
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]
\bigr)
\coloneqq
[x_0:(1+t\varepsilon)x_1:(1+2t\varepsilon)x_2:(1+3t\varepsilon)x_3].
$$
Every diagonal coefficient is positive, so each diagonal linear map is invertible and induces a continuous map on $\RP^3$.
Moreover,
$$
H_0=\operatorname{id}_{\RP^3}
$$
and
$$
H_1=f_\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $H$ is a homotopy from the identity to $f_\varepsilon$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For arbitrarily small $\varepsilon>0$, the map $f_\varepsilon$ is an arbitrarily small smooth perturbation of the identity.


::: pf-proof

The inducing matrices
$$
A_\varepsilon
=
\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(
1,
1+\varepsilon,
1+2\varepsilon,
1+3\varepsilon
\bigr)
$$
converge to the identity matrix as
$$
\varepsilon\to0.
$$
The induced projective transformations therefore converge smoothly to the identity map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The fixed points of $f_\varepsilon$ are exactly
$$
[1:0:0:0],
\quad
[0:1:0:0],
\quad
[0:0:1:0],
\quad
[0:0:0:1].
$$


::: pf-proof

A projective point
$$
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]
$$
is fixed exactly when there exists a nonzero scalar $\lambda$ such that
$$
x_0
=
\lambda x_0,
$$
$$
(1+\varepsilon)x_1
=
\lambda x_1,
$$
$$
(1+2\varepsilon)x_2
=
\lambda x_2,
$$
and
$$
(1+3\varepsilon)x_3
=
\lambda x_3.
$$
The four diagonal coefficients
$$
1,
\quad
1+\varepsilon,
\quad
1+2\varepsilon,
\quad
1+3\varepsilon
$$
are distinct.
Therefore at most one homogeneous coordinate can be nonzero.
The four coordinate-axis points are plainly fixed.
Hence these are exactly the fixed points.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Let
$$
\lambda_0=1,
\quad
\lambda_1=1+\varepsilon,
\quad
\lambda_2=1+2\varepsilon,
\quad
\lambda_3=1+3\varepsilon.
$$
At the fixed point corresponding to the $i$th coordinate axis, the derivative of $f_\varepsilon$ has eigenvalues
$$
\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i},
\qquad
j\ne i.
$$


::: pf-proof

Work in the affine chart
$$
x_i\ne0
$$
and normalize
$$
x_i=1.
$$
The remaining affine coordinates are
$$
u_j
\coloneqq
\frac{x_j}{x_i},
\qquad
j\ne i.
$$
Under $f_\varepsilon$,
$$
u_j
\longmapsto
\frac{\lambda_jx_j}{\lambda_ix_i}
=
\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i}u_j.
$$
Thus the derivative is diagonal with precisely the displayed eigenvalues.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

None of the derivatives at the fixed points has eigenvalue $1$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, an eigenvalue at the $i$th fixed point is
$$
\lambda_j/\lambda_i
$$
with
$$
j\ne i.
$$
Because the $\lambda_i$ are pairwise distinct,
$$
\lambda_j/\lambda_i\ne1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Therefore $f_\varepsilon$ is a Lefschetz map.


::: pf-proof

The fixed points are isolated by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} shows that at every fixed point
$$
\det
\bigl(
I-Df_\varepsilon
\bigr)
\ne0.
$$
Thus every fixed point is nondegenerate, which is the Lefschetz condition for this smooth map.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

With rational coefficients,
$$
H_k(\RP^3;\QQ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\QQ,&k=0,3,\\
0,&k=1,2.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

The manifold
$$
\RP^3
$$
is connected and orientable.
Its integral homology is
$$
H_0\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_1\cong\ZZ/2,
\qquad
H_2=0,
\qquad
H_3\cong\ZZ.
$$
Tensoring with $\QQ$ kills the torsion in degree $1$ and gives the displayed rational homology.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The identity map on $\RP^3$ has Lefschetz number
$$
L(\operatorname{id})
=
1-1
=
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
L(\operatorname{id})
=
\sum_{k=0}^{3}
(-1)^k
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
\operatorname{id}_*
:
H_k(\RP^3;\QQ)
\to
H_k(\RP^3;\QQ)
\right).
$$
By step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, only degrees
$$
0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
3
$$
contribute.
The identity has trace $1$ in each of these one-dimensional groups.
Hence
$$
L(\operatorname{id})
=
1+(-1)^3
=
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

The map $f_\varepsilon$ has Lefschetz number
$$
\boxed{L(f_\varepsilon)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Lefschetz number is invariant under homotopy.
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
f_\varepsilon
\simeq
\operatorname{id}_{\RP^3}.
$$
Therefore
$$
L(f_\varepsilon)
=
L(\operatorname{id})
=
0
$$
by step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} give an arbitrarily small perturbation of the identity that is a Lefschetz map, and step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} computes its Lefschetz number.
