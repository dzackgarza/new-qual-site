::: {.problem}
Define carefully a CW structure on $\mathbb{RP}^n$; you do not need to prove here that it is a CW structure. Use it to compute
\[
H_*(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Q)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H_*(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Use homogeneous coordinates
$$
[x_0:\cdots:x_n]
$$
on
$$
\mathbb{RP}^n.
$$
For
$$
0\le k\le n,
$$
let
$$
\mathbb{RP}^k
\coloneqq
\{
[x_0:\cdots:x_n]
:
x_{k+1}
=
\cdots
=
x_n
=
0
\}.
$$

<1>1. The inclusions
$$
\mathbb{RP}^0
\subset
\mathbb{RP}^1
\subset
\cdots
\subset
\mathbb{RP}^n
$$
give a CW structure with exactly one cell
$$
e^k
$$
in each dimension
$$
0\le k\le n.
$$

::: {.proof}
For each
$$
k\ge1,
$$
the quotient
$$
\mathbb{RP}^k/\mathbb{RP}^{k-1}
$$
is obtained from a closed $k$-disk by identifying antipodal points on its boundary.
Equivalently,
$$
\mathbb{RP}^k
$$
is obtained from
$$
\mathbb{RP}^{k-1}
$$
by attaching one $k$-cell via the antipodal quotient map
$$
S^{k-1}\to\mathbb{RP}^{k-1}.
$$
Thus there is precisely one open cell in each dimension.
:::

<1>2. With coefficients in any ring $R$, the cellular chain groups are
$$
C_k(\mathbb{RP}^n;R)
\cong
R
$$
for
$$
0\le k\le n,
$$
and zero otherwise.

::: {.proof}
There is one $k$-cell in each dimension by step <1>1.
The cellular chain group is the free $R$-module generated by the $k$-cells.
:::

<1>3. For
$$
1\le k\le n,
$$
the integral cellular boundary
$$
\partial_k:
C_k(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Z)
\to
C_{k-1}(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Z)
$$
is multiplication by
$$
\boxed{
1+(-1)^k.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Because there is only one cell in dimensions $k$ and $k-1$, the boundary map is multiplication by the degree of the composite
$$
S^{k-1}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb{RP}^{k-1}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb{RP}^{k-1}/\mathbb{RP}^{k-2}
\cong
S^{k-1},
$$
where the first map is the attaching map.

The sphere
$$
S^{k-1}
$$
is divided into two hemispheres exchanged by the antipodal map.
The first hemisphere maps with degree
$$
1,
$$
while the second contributes the degree of the antipodal map on
$$
S^{k-1},
$$
namely
$$
(-1)^k.
$$
Thus the total degree is
$$
1+(-1)^k.
$$
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\partial_k
=
\begin{cases}
0,&k\text{ odd},\\
\times2,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
If $k$ is odd, then
$$
(-1)^k=-1,
$$
so
$$
1+(-1)^k=0.
$$
If $k$ is even, then
$$
(-1)^k=1,
$$
so
$$
1+(-1)^k=2.
$$
Apply step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Over
$$
\mathbb Q,
$$
the cellular boundary is
$$
\partial_k
=
\begin{cases}
0,&k\text{ odd},\\
\text{an isomorphism},&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
Tensor the integral cellular chain complex with
$$
\mathbb Q.
$$
Multiplication by $2$ becomes an isomorphism because
$$
2
$$
is invertible in
$$
\mathbb Q.
$$
:::

<1>6. For
$$
0<k<n,
$$
one has
$$
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Q)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $k$ is even, then
$$
\partial_k
$$
is an isomorphism by step <1>5, so
$$
\ker\partial_k=0.
$$
Hence
$$
H_k=0.
$$

If $k$ is odd, then
$$
\partial_k=0,
$$
but
$$
\partial_{k+1}
$$
is an isomorphism because
$$
k+1
$$
is even.
Thus
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}
=
C_k,
$$
so again
$$
H_k=0.
$$
:::

<1>7. One has
$$
H_0(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\mathbb Q.
$$

::: {.proof}
The space
$$
\mathbb{RP}^n
$$
is connected.
Thus its zeroth homology over
$$
\mathbb Q
$$
is
$$
\mathbb Q.
$$
:::

<1>8. If $n$ is odd, then
$$
H_n(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\mathbb Q.
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
n
$$
is odd, then by step <1>5,
$$
\partial_n=0.
$$
There is no group
$$
C_{n+1},
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1}=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
H_n
=
\ker\partial_n
\cong
\mathbb Q.
$$
:::

<1>9. If $n$ is even, then
$$
H_n(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Q)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
n
$$
is even, then
$$
\partial_n
$$
is an isomorphism by step <1>5.
Hence
$$
\ker\partial_n=0.
$$
:::

<1>10. Thus
$$
\boxed{
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Q)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Q,&k=n\text{ and }n\text{ odd},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>6--<1>9.
:::

<1>11. Over
$$
\mathbb F_2
\coloneqq
\mathbb Z/2,
$$
every cellular boundary map is zero.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4, every integral boundary is either multiplication by $0$ or by $2$.
Modulo $2$, both maps become zero.
:::

<1>12. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb F_2)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb F_2,&0\le k\le n,\\
0,&k>n.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>11, the cellular differential is identically zero.
Hence in every degree with a cell,
$$
H_k
=
C_k
\cong
\mathbb F_2.
$$
There are no cells above dimension $n$.
:::

<1>13. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the requested CW structure.
Step <1>10 computes the rational homology, and step <1>12 computes the mod-$2$ homology.
:::
:::
