::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the standard middle-thirds Cantor set in $\mathbb R$, viewed as a subset of $\mathbb R^2$. Suppose $f:X\to\mathbb R$ has the following property: for every $x\in X$ there is an open neighborhood $U_x\subset\mathbb R^2$ of $x$ and a smooth function
\[
f_x:U_x\to\mathbb R
\]
such that
\[
f_x|_{U_x\cap X}=f|_{U_x\cap X}.
\]
Show that there is an open set $U\subset\mathbb R^2$ containing $X$ and a smooth function $g:U\to\mathbb R$ such that
\[
g|_X=f.
\]
:::

::: pf

::: pf-step

The Cantor set $X$ is compact.


::: pf-proof

The middle-thirds Cantor set is a closed subset of the compact interval
$$
[0,1].
$$
Hence it is compact.
Viewing it inside
$$
\mathbb R^2
$$
does not change compactness.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The neighborhoods
$$
\{U_x\}_{x\in X}
$$
form an open cover of $X$.


::: pf-proof

By hypothesis,
$$
x\in U_x
$$
for every
$$
x\in X.
$$
Thus every point of $X$ belongs to one member of the family.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Choose finitely many points
$$
x_1,\ldots,x_r
\in
X
$$
such that
$$
X
\subseteq
U_{x_1}
\cup
\cdots
\cup
U_{x_r}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply compactness from step <1>1 to the open cover in step <1>2.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Set
$$
U
\coloneqq
\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}
U_{x_i}.
$$
Then
$$
U
$$
is an open neighborhood of $X$ in
$$
\mathbb R^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Each
$$
U_{x_i}
$$
is open, so their finite union is open.
Step <1>3 gives
$$
X\subseteq U.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There exists a smooth partition of unity
$$
\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r
$$
on $U$ subordinate to the open cover
$$
\{U_{x_i}\}_{i=1}^{r}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The open set
$$
U\subset\mathbb R^2
$$
is a smooth manifold.
Every open cover of a smooth manifold admits a smooth partition of unity subordinate to it.
Hence there are smooth functions
$$
\phi_i:U\to[0,1]
$$
such that
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\phi_i=1
$$
on $U$ and
$$
\operatorname{supp}\phi_i
\subseteq
U_{x_i}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For each
$$
i,
$$
let
$$
f_i
\coloneqq
f_{x_i}
:
U_{x_i}
\to
\mathbb R.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is simply notation for the finitely many local smooth extensions supplied by the hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
\boxed{
g
\coloneqq
\sum_{i=1}^{r}
\phi_i f_i
}
$$
on $U$.


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
\operatorname{supp}\phi_i
\subseteq
U_{x_i},
$$
the product
$$
\phi_i f_i
$$
is well-defined on
$$
U_{x_i}.
$$
Extend it by zero to
$$
U\setminus U_{x_i}.
$$
This extension is smooth because
$$
\phi_i
$$
vanishes on a neighborhood of every point outside its support.
Thus each summand is a globally defined smooth function on $U$.
The sum is finite.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The function
$$
g:U\to\mathbb R
$$
is smooth.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>7, $g$ is a finite sum of smooth functions.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
$$
p\in X.
$$
If
$$
\phi_i(p)\ne0,
$$
then
$$
p\in U_{x_i}\cap X
$$
and therefore
$$
f_i(p)=f(p).
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
\phi_i(p)\ne0,
$$
then
$$
p\in\operatorname{supp}\phi_i
\subseteq
U_{x_i}.
$$
Since also
$$
p\in X,
$$
one has
$$
p\in U_{x_i}\cap X.
$$
The defining property of
$$
f_i=f_{x_i}
$$
then gives
$$
f_i(p)=f(p).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every
$$
p\in X,
$$
one has
$$
g(p)=f(p).
$$


::: pf-proof

Using step <1>9,
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(p)
&=
\sum_{i=1}^{r}
\phi_i(p)f_i(p)\\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^{r}
\phi_i(p)f(p)\\
&=
f(p)
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\phi_i(p)\\
&=
f(p),
\end{aligned}
$$
because
$$
\sum_i\phi_i(p)=1
$$
by step <1>5.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore there exists an open neighborhood
$$
U
\supseteq
X
$$
and a smooth function
$$
g:U\to\mathbb R
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{
g|_X=f.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The neighborhood $U$ is constructed in step <1>4, the smooth function $g$ in steps <1>7--<1>8, and the restriction identity in step <1>10.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>11 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
