::: {.problem}
Let $\omega$ be a $1$-form on $\mathbb R^n$ and let $X,Y$ be vector fields. Verify
\[
d\omega(X,Y)
=X(\omega(Y))-Y(\omega(X))-\omega([X,Y]).
\]
Use Spivak's normalization convention, in which
\[
(dx\wedge dy)(\partial_x,\partial_y)=1.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
\omega
=
\sum_{j=1}^{n}
a_j\,dx_j,
$$
$$
X
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
X^i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i},
\qquad
Y
=
\sum_{j=1}^{n}
Y^j\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}.
$$

<1>1. The exterior derivative of $\omega$ is
$$
\boxed{
d\omega
=
\sum_{1\le i<j\le n}
\left(
\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}
-
\frac{\partial a_i}{\partial x_j}
\right)
dx_i\wedge dx_j.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
d(dx_j)=0,
$$
one has
$$
d\omega
=
\sum_j
da_j\wedge dx_j.
$$
Also
$$
da_j
=
\sum_i
\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}
dx_i.
$$
Therefore
$$
d\omega
=
\sum_{i,j}
\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}
dx_i\wedge dx_j.
$$
Collecting the terms with
$$
i<j
$$
and using
$$
dx_j\wedge dx_i
=
-dx_i\wedge dx_j
$$
gives the displayed formula.
:::

<1>2. Evaluating step <1>1 on $(X,Y)$ gives
$$
d\omega(X,Y)
=
\sum_{i,j}
X^iY^j
\left(
\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}
-
\frac{\partial a_i}{\partial x_j}
\right).
$$

::: {.proof}
Under Spivak's normalization,
$$
(dx_i\wedge dx_j)(X,Y)
=
dx_i(X)dx_j(Y)
-
dx_i(Y)dx_j(X)
$$
for
$$
i<j.
$$
Thus
$$
(dx_i\wedge dx_j)(X,Y)
=
X^iY^j-Y^iX^j.
$$
Substituting this into step <1>1 and combining the two antisymmetric terms gives
$$
d\omega(X,Y)
=
\sum_{i,j}
X^iY^j
\left(
\partial_i a_j-\partial_j a_i
\right).
$$
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\omega(Y)
=
\sum_j a_jY^j
$$
and therefore
$$
X(\omega(Y))
=
\sum_{i,j}
X^i
\left(
\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}Y^j
+
a_j\frac{\partial Y^j}{\partial x_i}
\right).
$$

::: {.proof}
The first formula follows from
$$
dx_j(Y)=Y^j.
$$
Apply the vector field
$$
X=\sum_iX^i\partial_i
$$
and use the product rule.
:::

<1>4. Similarly,
$$
Y(\omega(X))
=
\sum_{i,j}
Y^i
\left(
\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}X^j
+
a_j\frac{\partial X^j}{\partial x_i}
\right).
$$

::: {.proof}
The calculation is identical to step <1>3 with $X$ and $Y$ interchanged.
:::

<1>5. The Lie bracket is
$$
[X,Y]
=
\sum_j
\left(
\sum_i
X^i\frac{\partial Y^j}{\partial x_i}
-
Y^i\frac{\partial X^j}{\partial x_i}
\right)
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the standard coordinate formula for the Lie bracket.
:::

<1>6. Hence
$$
\omega([X,Y])
=
\sum_{i,j}
a_j
\left(
X^i\frac{\partial Y^j}{\partial x_i}
-
Y^i\frac{\partial X^j}{\partial x_i}
\right).
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply the $1$-form
$$
\omega=\sum_j a_jdx_j
$$
to the vector field in step <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Subtracting the expressions from steps <1>3--<1>6 yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
&X(\omega(Y))
-Y(\omega(X))
-\omega([X,Y])\\
&\qquad=
\sum_{i,j}
\left(
X^iY^j\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}
-
Y^iX^j\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

::: {.proof}
The terms
$$
\sum_{i,j}
a_jX^i\frac{\partial Y^j}{\partial x_i}
$$
from step <1>3 cancel with the corresponding terms in step <1>6.
Likewise, the terms involving
$$
\partial_iX^j
$$
cancel between steps <1>4 and <1>6.
Only the derivatives of the coefficient functions $a_j$ remain.
:::

<1>8. Renaming the indices in the second sum of step <1>7 gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
&X(\omega(Y))
-Y(\omega(X))
-\omega([X,Y])\\
&\qquad=
\sum_{i,j}
X^iY^j
\left(
\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}
-
\frac{\partial a_i}{\partial x_j}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

::: {.proof}
In the second term of step <1>7,
replace the dummy indices
$$
i\leftrightarrow j.
$$
Then
$$
\sum_{i,j}
Y^iX^j
\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}
=
\sum_{i,j}
X^iY^j
\frac{\partial a_i}{\partial x_j}.
$$
Substitute this identity.
:::

<1>9. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
d\omega(X,Y)
=
X(\omega(Y))
-Y(\omega(X))
-\omega([X,Y]).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The right-hand side in step <1>8 is exactly the expression for
$$
d\omega(X,Y)
$$
obtained in step <1>2.
:::

<1>10. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>9 is the required formula.
:::
