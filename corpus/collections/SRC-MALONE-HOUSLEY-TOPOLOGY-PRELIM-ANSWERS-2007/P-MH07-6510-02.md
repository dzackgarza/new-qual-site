::: {.problem}
Using a Mayer–Vietoris argument, compute the de Rham cohomology of the spheres $S^n$. You may use that a contractible space has the cohomology of a point.
:::

::: {.solution}

We prove that
$$
\boxed{
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&k=0,n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let
$$
U
\coloneqq
S^n\setminus\{\text{south pole}\},
\qquad
V
\coloneqq
S^n\setminus\{\text{north pole}\}.
$$
Then $U$ and $V$ are contractible.


::: pf-proof

Stereographic projection gives diffeomorphisms
$$
U\cong\RR^n,
\qquad
V\cong\RR^n.
$$
Hence both are contractible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Therefore
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U)
\cong
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&k=0,\\
0,&k>0.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the de Rham cohomology of a point, using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and homotopy invariance.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The intersection
$$
U\cap V
$$
deformation retracts onto
$$
S^{n-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Geometrically,
$$
U\cap V
=
S^n
\setminus
\{\text{north pole},\text{south pole}\}.
$$
Using latitude and longitude coordinates, this space is diffeomorphic to
$$
S^{n-1}\times\RR.
$$
Projection onto the first factor is a deformation retraction.
Thus
$$
U\cap V
\simeq
S^{n-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Hence
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U\cap V)
\cong
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1}).
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply homotopy invariance to step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For
$$
k\ge2,
$$
the Mayer–Vietoris sequence gives an isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1})
\cong
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The relevant part of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence is
$$
H^{k-1}(U)\oplus H^{k-1}(V)
\longrightarrow
H^{k-1}(U\cap V)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H^k(S^n)
\longrightarrow
H^k(U)\oplus H^k(V).
$$
For
$$
k\ge2,
$$
step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
H^{k-1}(U)
=
H^{k-1}(V)
=
H^k(U)
=
H^k(V)
=
0.
$$
Exactness therefore makes
$$
\delta
:
H^{k-1}(U\cap V)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H^k(S^n).
$$
Apply step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For every
$$
n\ge1,
$$
one has
$$
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\RR.
$$


::: pf-proof

The sphere
$$
S^n
$$
is connected.
The zeroth de Rham cohomology of a connected manifold consists of locally constant functions modulo zero, hence constant functions:
$$
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\RR.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For
$$
n>1,
$$
the intersection
$$
U\cap V
\simeq
S^{n-1}
$$
is connected.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
n>1,
$$
then
$$
n-1\ge1,
$$
and
$$
S^{n-1}
$$
is connected.
Apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

For
$$
n>1,
$$
one has
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The beginning of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H^0(S^n)
\longrightarrow
H^0(U)\oplus H^0(V)
\xrightarrow{\rho}
H^0(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
H^1(S^n)
\longrightarrow
0,
$$
where the final zero comes from
$$
H^1(U)=H^1(V)=0
$$
by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref},
$$
H^0(S^n)
\cong
H^0(U)
\cong
H^0(V)
\cong
H^0(U\cap V)
\cong
\RR.
$$
Under these identifications,
$$
\rho(a,b)=a-b.
$$
This map is surjective.
Exactness therefore gives
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For
$$
n=1,
$$
one has
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)
\cong
\RR.
$$


::: pf-proof

Now
$$
U\cap V
$$
has two connected components.
Hence
$$
H^0(U\cap V)
\cong
\RR^2.
$$
The same Mayer–Vietoris segment becomes
$$
0
\to
\RR
\to
\RR^2
\xrightarrow{\rho}
\RR^2
\to
H^1(S^1)
\to
0.
$$
The restriction-difference map $\rho$ has image the diagonal one-dimensional subspace
$$
\{(c,c):c\in\RR\}
$$
after choosing compatible signs on the two components.
Thus its cokernel is one-dimensional:
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)
\cong
\RR.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Assume inductively that
$$
H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1})
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&j=0,n-1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
Then for
$$
2\le k\le n,
$$
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&k=n,\\
0,&2\le k<n.
\end{cases}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1}).
$$
Under the induction hypothesis, the right-hand side is nonzero exactly when
$$
k-1=n-1,
$$
that is,
$$
k=n.
$$
In that case it is isomorphic to
$$
\RR.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

For
$$
k>n,
$$
one has
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The manifold
$$
S^n
$$
has dimension $n$, so there are no nonzero differential $k$-forms for
$$
k>n.
$$
Hence the corresponding de Rham cohomology groups vanish.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore, for every
$$
n\ge1,
$$
$$
\boxed{
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\RR,&k=0,n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The base case
$$
n=1
$$
is steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-9){.pf-ref}.
For
$$
n>1,
$$
step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives degree $0$, step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives degree $1$, step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} gives degrees $2$ through $n$, and step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} gives degrees above $n$.
Induction on $n$ completes the computation.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} is the required de Rham cohomology of the spheres.

:::
