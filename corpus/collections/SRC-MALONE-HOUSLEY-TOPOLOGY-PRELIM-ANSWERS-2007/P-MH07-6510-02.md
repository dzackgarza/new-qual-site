::: {.problem}
Using a Mayer–Vietoris argument, compute the de Rham cohomology of the spheres $S^n$. You may use that a contractible space has the cohomology of a point.
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove that
$$
\boxed{
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&k=0,n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

<1>1. Let
$$
U
\coloneqq
S^n\setminus\{\text{south pole}\},
\qquad
V
\coloneqq
S^n\setminus\{\text{north pole}\}.
$$
Then $U$ and $V$ are contractible.

::: {.proof}
Stereographic projection gives diffeomorphisms
$$
U\cong\mathbb R^n,
\qquad
V\cong\mathbb R^n.
$$
Hence both are contractible.
:::

<1>2. Therefore
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U)
\cong
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&k=0,\\
0,&k>0.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
This is the de Rham cohomology of a point, using step <1>1 and homotopy invariance.
:::

<1>3. The intersection
$$
U\cap V
$$
deformation retracts onto
$$
S^{n-1}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Geometrically,
$$
U\cap V
=
S^n
\setminus
\{\text{north pole},\text{south pole}\}.
$$
Using latitude and longitude coordinates, this space is diffeomorphic to
$$
S^{n-1}\times\mathbb R.
$$
Projection onto the first factor is a deformation retraction.
Thus
$$
U\cap V
\simeq
S^{n-1}.
$$
:::

<1>4. Hence
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U\cap V)
\cong
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1}).
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply homotopy invariance to step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. For
$$
k\ge2,
$$
the Mayer–Vietoris sequence gives an isomorphism
$$
\boxed{
H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1})
\cong
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n).
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The relevant part of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence is
$$
H^{k-1}(U)\oplus H^{k-1}(V)
\longrightarrow
H^{k-1}(U\cap V)
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H^k(S^n)
\longrightarrow
H^k(U)\oplus H^k(V).
$$
For
$$
k\ge2,
$$
step <1>2 gives
$$
H^{k-1}(U)
=
H^{k-1}(V)
=
H^k(U)
=
H^k(V)
=
0.
$$
Exactness therefore makes
$$
\delta
:
H^{k-1}(U\cap V)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H^k(S^n).
$$
Apply step <1>4.
:::

<1>6. For every
$$
n\ge1,
$$
one has
$$
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$

::: {.proof}
The sphere
$$
S^n
$$
is connected.
The zeroth de Rham cohomology of a connected manifold consists of locally constant functions modulo zero, hence constant functions:
$$
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$
:::

<1>7. For
$$
n>1,
$$
the intersection
$$
U\cap V
\simeq
S^{n-1}
$$
is connected.

::: {.proof}
If
$$
n>1,
$$
then
$$
n-1\ge1,
$$
and
$$
S^{n-1}
$$
is connected.
Apply step <1>3.
:::

<1>8. For
$$
n>1,
$$
one has
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The beginning of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
H^0(S^n)
\longrightarrow
H^0(U)\oplus H^0(V)
\xrightarrow{\rho}
H^0(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
H^1(S^n)
\longrightarrow
0,
$$
where the final zero comes from
$$
H^1(U)=H^1(V)=0
$$
by step <1>2.

By steps <1>2, <1>6, and <1>7,
$$
H^0(S^n)
\cong
H^0(U)
\cong
H^0(V)
\cong
H^0(U\cap V)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$
Under these identifications,
$$
\rho(a,b)=a-b.
$$
This map is surjective.
Exactness therefore gives
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)=0.
$$
:::

<1>9. For
$$
n=1,
$$
one has
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$

::: {.proof}
Now
$$
U\cap V
$$
has two connected components.
Hence
$$
H^0(U\cap V)
\cong
\mathbb R^2.
$$
The same Mayer–Vietoris segment becomes
$$
0
\to
\mathbb R
\to
\mathbb R^2
\xrightarrow{\rho}
\mathbb R^2
\to
H^1(S^1)
\to
0.
$$
The restriction-difference map $\rho$ has image the diagonal one-dimensional subspace
$$
\{(c,c):c\in\mathbb R\}
$$
after choosing compatible signs on the two components.
Thus its cokernel is one-dimensional:
$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)
\cong
\mathbb R.
$$
:::

<1>10. Assume inductively that
$$
H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1})
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&j=0,n-1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
Then for
$$
2\le k\le n,
$$
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&k=n,\\
0,&2\le k<n.
\end{cases}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>5,
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^{n-1}).
$$
Under the induction hypothesis, the right-hand side is nonzero exactly when
$$
k-1=n-1,
$$
that is,
$$
k=n.
$$
In that case it is isomorphic to
$$
\mathbb R.
$$
:::

<1>11. For
$$
k>n,
$$
one has
$$
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The manifold
$$
S^n
$$
has dimension $n$, so there are no nonzero differential $k$-forms for
$$
k>n.
$$
Hence the corresponding de Rham cohomology groups vanish.
:::

<1>12. Therefore, for every
$$
n\ge1,
$$
$$
\boxed{
H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^n)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&k=0,n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The base case
$$
n=1
$$
is steps <1>6 and <1>9.
For
$$
n>1,
$$
step <1>6 gives degree $0$, step <1>8 gives degree $1$, step <1>10 gives degrees $2$ through $n$, and step <1>11 gives degrees above $n$.
Induction on $n$ completes the computation.
:::

<1>13. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>12 is the required de Rham cohomology of the spheres.
:::
