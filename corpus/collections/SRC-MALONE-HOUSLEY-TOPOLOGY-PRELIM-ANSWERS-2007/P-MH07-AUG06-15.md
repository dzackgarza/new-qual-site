::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a closed orientable manifold of dimension $2k$. Show that if
\[
H_{k-1}(M;\ZZ)
\]
is torsion-free, then
\[
H_k(M;\ZZ)
\]
is also torsion-free.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Every homology group
$$
H_i(M;\ZZ)
$$
is finitely generated.


::: pf-proof

A closed smooth manifold admits a finite CW structure.
The cellular chain groups are therefore finitely generated free abelian groups.
Homology groups of a finite chain complex of finitely generated abelian groups are finitely generated.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Since
$$
H_{k-1}(M;\ZZ)
$$
is finitely generated and torsion-free, it is free abelian.


::: pf-proof

By the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups, every such group is a direct sum of a free abelian part and a finite torsion part.
The torsion-free hypothesis removes the torsion part.
Hence
$$
H_{k-1}(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ^r
$$
for some
$$
r\ge0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Ext}
\left(
H_{k-1}(M;\ZZ),
\ZZ
\right)
=
0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
H_{k-1}(M;\ZZ)
$$
is free abelian.
The functor
$$
\operatorname{Ext}(-,\ZZ)
$$
vanishes on free abelian groups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The universal coefficient theorem gives
$$
\boxed{
H^k(M;\ZZ)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
H_k(M;\ZZ),
\ZZ
\right).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The cohomological universal coefficient sequence in degree $k$ is
$$
0
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ext}
\left(
H_{k-1}(M;\ZZ),
\ZZ
\right)
\longrightarrow
H^k(M;\ZZ)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
H_k(M;\ZZ),
\ZZ
\right)
\longrightarrow
0.
$$
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} kills the left-hand term.
Hence the right-hand map is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The group
$$
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
H_k(M;\ZZ),
\ZZ
\right)
$$
is torsion-free.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\phi
\in
\operatorname{Hom}
\left(
H_k(M;\ZZ),
\ZZ
\right)
$$
and
$$
n\phi=0
$$
for some nonzero integer $n$.
Then for every
$$
x\in H_k(M;\ZZ),
$$
$$
n\phi(x)=0
$$
in $\ZZ$.
Since $\ZZ$ is torsion-free,
$$
\phi(x)=0
$$
for every $x$.
Thus
$$
\phi=0.
$$
Hence the Hom group has no nonzero torsion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore
$$
H^k(M;\ZZ)
$$
is torsion-free.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
H^k(M;\ZZ)
$$
is isomorphic to the torsion-free group in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Poincaré duality gives
$$
\boxed{
H_k(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^k(M;\ZZ).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The manifold $M$ is closed, orientable, and has dimension
$$
2k.
$$
Poincaré duality gives
$$
H_i(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^{2k-i}(M;\ZZ).
$$
Setting
$$
i=k
$$
gives
$$
H_k(M;\ZZ)
\cong
H^k(M;\ZZ).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
H_k(M;\ZZ)
\text{ is torsion-free}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
H^k(M;\ZZ)
$$
is torsion-free.
Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} identifies
$$
H_k(M;\ZZ)
$$
with this group.
Therefore
$$
H_k(M;\ZZ)
$$
is torsion-free.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required conclusion.

:::
