::: {.problem}
Is there a smooth embedding of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ into an orientable $3$-manifold? Give a proof or an example.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Define
$$
\iota:\mathbb{RP}^2\to\mathbb{RP}^3
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\iota([x:y:z])
=
[x:y:z:0].
}
$$

::: {.proof}
If
$$
[x:y:z]
=
[\lambda x:\lambda y:\lambda z]
$$
for some nonzero scalar $\lambda$, then
$$
[x:y:z:0]
=
[\lambda x:\lambda y:\lambda z:0].
$$
Thus the formula is well-defined on projective equivalence classes.
:::

<1>2. The map $\iota$ is injective.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
\iota([x:y:z])
=
\iota([x':y':z']).
$$
Then
$$
[x:y:z:0]
=
[x':y':z':0].
$$
Hence there exists
$$
\lambda\ne0
$$
such that
$$
(x',y',z',0)
=
\lambda(x,y,z,0).
$$
Therefore
$$
(x',y',z')
=
\lambda(x,y,z),
$$
so
$$
[x:y:z]
=
[x':y':z'].
$$
:::

<1>3. The image of $\iota$ is the projective hyperplane
$$
H
\coloneqq
\{
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]
\in
\mathbb{RP}^3
:
x_3=0
\}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Every point in the image has last homogeneous coordinate $0$ by definition.
Conversely, every point of $H$ has the form
$$
[x:y:z:0]
$$
with
$$
(x,y,z)\ne(0,0,0),
$$
and is therefore
$$
\iota([x:y:z]).
$$
:::

<1>4. The subset $H$ is a smooth embedded $2$-dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{RP}^3$.

::: {.proof}
Use the standard affine charts
$$
U_i
\coloneqq
\{x_i\ne0\}
\subset
\mathbb{RP}^3.
$$
On a chart
$$
U_i
$$
with
$$
i\in\{0,1,2\},
$$
normalize
$$
x_i=1.
$$
Then the chart is diffeomorphic to
$$
\mathbb R^3,
$$
and the equation
$$
x_3=0
$$
becomes the coordinate equation
$$
u_3=0.
$$
Thus
$$
H\cap U_i
$$
is a coordinate plane
$$
\mathbb R^2\subset\mathbb R^3.
$$
These charts cover $H$, so $H$ is a smooth embedded surface.
:::

<1>5. The map
$$
\iota:\mathbb{RP}^2\to H
$$
is a diffeomorphism.

::: {.proof}
The inverse is
$$
[x:y:z:0]
\longmapsto
[x:y:z].
$$
Both maps are smooth in the standard projective charts.
Hence $\iota$ is a smooth embedding of
$$
\mathbb{RP}^2
$$
into
$$
\mathbb{RP}^3.
$$
:::

<1>6. The manifold
$$
\mathbb{RP}^3
$$
is orientable.

::: {.proof}
The standard double covering
$$
\pi:S^3\to\mathbb{RP}^3
$$
has deck transformation the antipodal map
$$
A:S^3\to S^3,
\qquad
A(x)=-x.
$$
The antipodal map on
$$
S^n
$$
has degree
$$
(-1)^{n+1}.
$$
For
$$
n=3,
$$
this degree is
$$
(-1)^4=1.
$$
Thus the deck transformation preserves the standard orientation of
$$
S^3.
$$
Therefore that orientation descends through the covering to an orientation of
$$
\mathbb{RP}^3.
$$
:::

<1>7. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb{RP}^2
\text{ embeds smoothly in the orientable }3\text{-manifold }
\mathbb{RP}^3.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the smooth embedding, and step <1>6 shows that the ambient $3$-manifold is orientable.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 gives the requested example.
:::
