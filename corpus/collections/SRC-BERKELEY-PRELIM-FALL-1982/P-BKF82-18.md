::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be continuous on $[0,1]^2$ with $|K(x,y)|<1$. Show that there is a continuous $f$ on $[0,1]$ such that
\[
f(x)+\int_0^1K(x,y)f(y)\,dy=e^{x^2}.
\]
Can there be more than one such $f$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
X\coloneqq C([0,1])
$$
with the supremum norm, and define
$$
(Tf)(x)
\coloneqq
\int_0^1K(x,y)f(y)\,dy.
$$
Also put
$$
g(x)\coloneqq e^{x^2}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

There is a number $q<1$ such that
$$
\abs{K(x,y)}\le q
$$
for all $(x,y)\in[0,1]^2$.

::: pf-proof

The continuous function $|K|$ attains a maximum on the compact square
$[0,1]^2$. By hypothesis, its value is strictly less than $1$ at every
point. Hence
$$
q
\coloneqq
\max_{[0,1]^2}|K|
<1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The operator $T:X\to X$ is linear and satisfies
$$
\norm{Tf}_\infty
\le
q\norm f_\infty
$$
for every $f\in X$.

::: pf-proof

Continuity of $K$ and $f$ implies that $Tf$ is continuous in $x$, so
$T$ maps $X$ to itself. Linearity follows from linearity of the integral.
For every $x\in[0,1]$, step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{(Tf)(x)}
&\le
\int_0^1
\abs{K(x,y)}\abs{f(y)}\,dy\\
&\le
\int_0^1
q\norm f_\infty\,dy\\
&=
q\norm f_\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the supremum over $x$ proves the estimate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The series
$$
f
\coloneqq
\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-T)^n g
$$
converges in $X$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\norm{T^ng}_\infty
\le
q^n\norm g_\infty.
$$
Since $0\le q<1$, the geometric series
$$
\sum_{n=0}^\infty q^n\norm g_\infty
$$
converges. Hence the displayed series converges absolutely in the Banach
space $X=C([0,1])$. Its sum $f$ is therefore continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The function $f$ from step [](#s3){.pf-ref} satisfies
$$
f+Tf=g.
$$

::: pf-proof

Because $T$ is bounded, it may be applied termwise to the norm-convergent
series:
$$
Tf
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty T(-T)^ng
=
-\sum_{n=1}^\infty(-T)^ng.
$$
Therefore
$$
f+Tf
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-T)^ng
-\sum_{n=1}^\infty(-T)^ng
=g.
$$
In pointwise form, this is
$$
f(x)
+
\int_0^1K(x,y)f(y)\,dy
=
e^{x^2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The solution is unique.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $f_1,f_2\in X$ are two solutions, and put
$$
h\coloneqq f_1-f_2.
$$
Subtracting the two equations gives
$$
h+Th=0.
$$
Thus, by step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\norm h_\infty
=
\norm{Th}_\infty
\le
q\norm h_\infty.
$$
Since $q<1$, this forces
$$
\norm h_\infty=0,
$$
so $h=0$ and $f_1=f_2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Hence there exists exactly one continuous solution.

::: pf-proof

Existence is step [](#s4){.pf-ref} and uniqueness is step [](#s5){.pf-ref}. Thus there cannot be
more than one such function.

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s6){.pf-ref} answers both questions.
