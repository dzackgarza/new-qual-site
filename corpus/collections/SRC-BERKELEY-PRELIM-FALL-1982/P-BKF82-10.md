::: problem
Let $a,b\in\mathbb C$ have nonpositive real parts. Prove
\[
|e^a-e^b|\le|a-b|.
\]
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Express the difference by integrating along the line segment from $b$ to $a$.
::: proof
Define
$$
\gamma(t)=b+t(a-b),
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
$$
Then
$$
\frac{d}{dt}e^{\gamma(t)}
=(a-b)e^{\gamma(t)}.
$$
Therefore the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
$$
e^a-e^b
=(a-b)\int_0^1 e^{b+t(a-b)}\,dt.
$$
:::

<1>2. Bound the exponential along the segment.
::: proof
For $0\le t\le1$,
$$
\operatorname{Re}(b+t(a-b))
=(1-t)\operatorname{Re}b+t\operatorname{Re}a
\le0.
$$
Hence
$$
\left|e^{b+t(a-b)}\right|
=e^{\operatorname{Re}(b+t(a-b))}
\le1.
$$
Using step <1>1,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|e^a-e^b|
&\le |a-b|\int_0^1\left|e^{b+t(a-b)}\right|\,dt\\
&\le |a-b|\int_0^1 1\,dt\\
&=|a-b|.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{|e^a-e^b|\le|a-b|.}
$$
:::
:::
