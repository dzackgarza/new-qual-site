::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a complex $n\times n$ matrix and $B=A^*$ its Hermitian transpose. Suppose $AB=BA$. Let $\alpha,\beta$ be the corresponding operators on $\mathbb C^n$. Prove that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ have the same image and the same kernel.
:::

::: {.solution}
Equip $\mathbb C^n$ with its standard Hermitian inner product.

<1>1. For every $v\in\mathbb C^n$,
$$
\norm{Av}^2=\norm{Bv}^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $B=A^*$ and $B^*=A$,
$$
\norm{Av}^2
=
v^*A^*Av
=
v^*BAv.
$$
The hypothesis $AB=BA$ gives
$$
v^*BAv
=
v^*ABv
=
v^*B^*Bv
=
\norm{Bv}^2.
$$
:::

<1>2. The two operators have the same kernel:
$$
\ker\alpha=\ker\beta.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $Av=0$, then step <1>1 gives
$$
\norm{Bv}^2=\norm{Av}^2=0,
$$
so $Bv=0$. Thus $\ker A\subseteq\ker B$. The same norm identity gives
the reverse inclusion, hence
$$
\ker A=\ker B.
$$
These are precisely the kernels of $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
:::

<1>3. For any linear operator $T$ on $\mathbb C^n$,
$$
\operatorname{im}T
=
(\ker T^*)^\perp.
$$

::: {.proof}
A vector $w$ lies in $(\operatorname{im}T)^\perp$ exactly when
$$
\langle Tv,w\rangle=0
$$
for every $v$. By the defining property of the adjoint, this is equivalent
to
$$
\langle v,T^*w\rangle=0
$$
for every $v$, hence to $T^*w=0$. Therefore
$$
(\operatorname{im}T)^\perp=\ker T^*.
$$
Taking orthogonal complements in finite dimension gives the claim.
:::

<1>4. The two operators have the same image:
$$
\operatorname{im}\alpha
=
\operatorname{im}\beta.
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply step <1>3 to $A$ and $B$. Since $A^*=B$ and $B^*=A$,
$$
\operatorname{im}A
=
(\ker B)^\perp
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{im}B
=
(\ker A)^\perp.
$$
Step <1>2 gives $\ker A=\ker B$, so the two images are equal.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives equality of the kernels, and step <1>4 gives equality of
the images.
:::
:::
