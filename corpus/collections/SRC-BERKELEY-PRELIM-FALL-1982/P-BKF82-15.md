::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a subgroup of an abelian group $B$ and suppose $A$ is a direct summand of $B$. If $A\subset C\subset B$, is $A$ necessarily a direct summand of $C$? Prove or disprove.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The answer is yes. Since $A$ is a direct summand of $B$, there is a
subgroup $D\le B$ such that
$$
B=A\oplus D.
$$

::: {.proof}
This is exactly the definition of $A$ being a direct summand of $B$.
:::

<1>2. Every element of $C$ belongs to
$$
A+(C\cap D).
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $c\in C$. By step <1>1, there are unique elements
$$
a\in A,
\qquad
d\in D
$$
such that
$$
c=a+d.
$$
Because $A\subset C$, one has $a\in C$. Since also $c\in C$,
$$
d=c-a\in C.
$$
Thus $d\in C\cap D$, and therefore
$$
c\in A+(C\cap D).
$$
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
C=A\oplus(C\cap D).
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives
$$
C\subseteq A+(C\cap D).
$$
The reverse inclusion is immediate because both $A$ and $C\cap D$ are
subgroups of $C$. Hence
$$
C=A+(C\cap D).
$$
Moreover,
$$
A\cap(C\cap D)
=
(A\cap D)\cap C
=
\{0\},
$$
because $B=A\oplus D$. Thus the sum is direct.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $A$ is a direct summand of $C$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 exhibits $C\cap D$ as an explicit complement to $A$ in $C$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 answers the question affirmatively.
:::
:::
