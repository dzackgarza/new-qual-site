::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the abelian group generated by $x,y,z$ with relations
\[
2x+4y+6z=0,\qquad 8x+4z=4y,\qquad 6x=8y+2z.
\]
Write $G$ as a product of cyclic groups. How many elements of $G$ have order $2$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. With respect to the ordered generators $(x,y,z)$, the relation
matrix is
$$
M
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&4&6\\
8&-4&4\\
6&-8&-2
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and
$$
G\cong\mathbb Z^3/\operatorname{rowspan}_{\mathbb Z}(M).
$$

::: {.proof}
The first relation gives the row $(2,4,6)$. Rewriting the second and third
relations as
$$
8x-4y+4z=0
$$
and
$$
6x-8y-2z=0
$$
gives the other two rows. The standard presentation of an abelian group
from an integer relation matrix gives the stated quotient.
:::

<1>2. The matrix $M$ has rank $2$ over $\mathbb Q$.

::: {.proof}
Its determinant is
$$
\det M=0.
$$
On the other hand, the upper-left $2\times2$ minor is
$$
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
2&4\\
8&-4
\end{pmatrix}
=
-40\ne0.
$$
Hence the rank is exactly $2$.
:::

<1>3. The first two determinantal divisors of $M$ are
$$
\Delta_1=2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Delta_2=40.
$$

::: {.proof}
The greatest common divisor of all entries of $M$ is $2$, so
$$
\Delta_1=2.
$$
The nine $2\times2$ minors of $M$ are
$$
-40,-40,40,
\qquad
-40,-40,40,
\qquad
-40,-40,40.
$$
Therefore their greatest common divisor is
$$
\Delta_2=40.
$$
:::

<1>4. The Smith normal form of $M$ is
$$
\operatorname{diag}(2,20,0),
$$
and consequently
$$
\boxed{
G
\cong
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
\oplus
\mathbb Z/20\mathbb Z
\oplus
\mathbb Z
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For a rank-two integer matrix, the nonzero Smith invariant factors
$d_1,d_2$ satisfy
$$
d_1=\Delta_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
d_1d_2=\Delta_2.
$$
By step <1>3,
$$
d_1=2,
\qquad
d_2=\frac{40}{2}=20.
$$
Step <1>2 gives one zero diagonal entry, hence one free cyclic factor.
This yields the displayed decomposition.
:::

<1>5. Exactly three elements of $G$ have order $2$.

::: {.proof}
An element of finite order in the decomposition from step <1>4 must have
zero component in the free factor $\mathbb Z$. The elements annihilated
by $2$ in the two finite factors are
$$
\{0,1\}\subset\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
$$
and
$$
\{0,10\}\subset\mathbb Z/20\mathbb Z.
$$
Thus the subgroup of elements annihilated by $2$ has
$$
2\cdot2=4
$$
elements. One is the identity, whose order is $1$; each of the other three
has order exactly $2$. Therefore the requested number is
$$
\boxed{3}.
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4 and <1>5 give the requested decomposition and count.
:::
