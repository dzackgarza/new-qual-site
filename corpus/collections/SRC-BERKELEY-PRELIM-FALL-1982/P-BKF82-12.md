::: {.problem}
Let $A,B$ be complex $n\times n$ matrices with the same rank and satisfying $A^2=A$, $B^2=B$. Prove that $A$ and $B$ are similar.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
r\coloneqq\operatorname{rank}A
=
\operatorname{rank}B.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For any idempotent linear operator $T$ on $\mathbb C^n$,
$$
\mathbb C^n
=
\operatorname{im}T
\oplus
\ker T.
$$

::: pf-proof

For every $v\in\mathbb C^n$,
$$
v
=
Tv+(v-Tv).
$$
The first summand lies in $\operatorname{im}T$, while
$$
T(v-Tv)
=
Tv-T^2v
=0,
$$
so the second lies in $\ker T$. Thus the two subspaces span
$\mathbb C^n$.

If $w\in\operatorname{im}T\cap\ker T$, write $w=Tu$. Then
$$
w=Tu=T^2u=Tw=0.
$$
Hence the intersection is trivial and the sum is direct.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

In a basis adapted to the decomposition in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, an idempotent
$T$ of rank $r$ has matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
I_r&0\\
0&0_{n-r}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

If $w\in\operatorname{im}T$, then $w=Tu$ for some $u$, so
$$
Tw=T^2u=Tu=w.
$$
Thus $T$ is the identity on $\operatorname{im}T$ and zero on
$\ker T$. Since
$$
\dim\operatorname{im}T=r,
$$
choosing a basis first of the image and then of the kernel gives the stated
matrix.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Both $A$ and $B$ are similar to the same matrix
$$
D
\coloneqq
\begin{pmatrix}
I_r&0\\
0&0_{n-r}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s2){.pf-ref} to $A$ and to $B$. Their ranks are both $r$, so the same
diagonal matrix $D$ occurs in each case. Hence there are invertible matrices
$P,Q$ such that
$$
P^{-1}AP=D
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Q^{-1}BQ=D.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The matrices $A$ and $B$ are similar.

::: pf-proof

From step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
A=PDP^{-1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B=QDQ^{-1}.
$$
Therefore
$$
B
=
(QP^{-1})A(PQ^{-1}),
$$
and $PQ^{-1}=(QP^{-1})^{-1}$. Thus $A$ and $B$ are similar.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} proves the claim.

:::
