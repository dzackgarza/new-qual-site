::: {.problem}
Find all $C^\infty$ pairs $x(t),y(t)$ on $\mathbb R$ satisfying
\[
x'=2x-y,\qquad y'=x.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every solution satisfies
$$
y''-2y'+y=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The second equation gives
$$
x=y'.
$$
Differentiating gives $x'=y''$. Substituting these identities into
$$
x'=2x-y
$$
yields
$$
y''=2y'-y,
$$
which is the stated equation.
:::

<1>2. The general solution of the scalar equation in step <1>1 is
$$
y(t)=(a+bt)e^t,
\qquad
a,b\in\mathbb R.
$$

::: {.proof}
The characteristic polynomial is
$$
r^2-2r+1=(r-1)^2.
$$
Thus $r=1$ is a repeated root of multiplicity $2$, so the general real
solution is
$$
y(t)=(a+bt)e^t.
$$
:::

<1>3. For such a solution,
$$
x(t)=\bigl(a+b+bt\bigr)e^t.
$$

::: {.proof}
The original system requires $x=y'$. Differentiating the expression in
step <1>2 gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
y'(t)
&=
b e^t+(a+bt)e^t\\
&=
(a+b+bt)e^t.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore $x$ has the displayed form.
:::

<1>4. Every pair
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
x(t)&=(a+b+bt)e^t,\\
y(t)&=(a+bt)e^t,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
a,b\in\mathbb R
}
$$
satisfies the system.

::: {.proof}
For the displayed pair,
$$
y'(t)=(a+b+bt)e^t=x(t).
$$
Also
$$
x'(t)
=
(a+2b+bt)e^t,
$$
while
$$
2x(t)-y(t)
=
\bigl(2a+2b+2bt-a-bt\bigr)e^t
=
(a+2b+bt)e^t.
$$
Hence $x'=2x-y$ and $y'=x$.
:::

<1>5. These are all solutions.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 shows that every solution of the system produces a solution of
the scalar equation. Step <1>2 lists every solution of that scalar equation,
and step <1>3 then forces $x=y'$. Thus no other pairs are possible.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4 and <1>5 give exactly the full solution set.
:::
:::
