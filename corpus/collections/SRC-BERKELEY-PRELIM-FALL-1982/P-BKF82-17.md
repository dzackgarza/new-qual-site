::: {.problem}
Evaluate
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
I
\coloneqq
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\,dx.
$$

<1>1. The integral defining $I$ converges absolutely, and
$$
I
=
2\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
Near $0$,
$$
\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\longrightarrow1,
$$
so there is no singularity in the improper-integral sense. For large
$\abs x$,
$$
\abs{
\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}
}
\le
\frac1{\abs x^3},
$$
which is integrable. Thus the integral is absolutely convergent. The
integrand is even, giving the displayed identity.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\,dx
=
\frac32
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\sin^2x\cos x}{x^2}\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
Apply integration by parts first on $[\varepsilon,R]$ with
$$
u=\sin^3x,
\qquad
dv=\frac{dx}{x^3}.
$$
Then
$$
du=3\sin^2x\cos x\,dx,
\qquad
v=-\frac1{2x^2},
$$
so
$$
\int_\varepsilon^R
\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\,dx
=
\left[
-\frac{\sin^3x}{2x^2}
\right]_{\varepsilon}^{R}
+
\frac32
\int_\varepsilon^R
\frac{\sin^2x\cos x}{x^2}\,dx.
$$
The boundary term tends to $0$ as $R\to\infty$ because it is
$O(R^{-2})$, and it tends to $0$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ because
$\sin^3\varepsilon/\varepsilon^2=O(\varepsilon)$. Passing to the
limits gives the identity.
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\sin^2x\cos x}{x^2}\,dx
=
\frac14
\int_0^\infty
\frac{-\sin x+3\sin3x}{x}\,dx.
$$

::: {.proof}
Set
$$
u(x)=\sin^2x\cos x.
$$
Integration by parts on $[\varepsilon,R]$ with
$dv=x^{-2}dx$ gives
$$
\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{u(x)}{x^2}\,dx
=
\left[
-\frac{u(x)}x
\right]_{\varepsilon}^{R}
+
\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{u'(x)}x\,dx.
$$
The boundary term tends to $0$ at both endpoints: $u$ is bounded at
infinity, while $u(x)=O(x^2)$ near $0$.

The identity
$$
u(x)
=
\frac14(\cos x-\cos3x)
$$
gives
$$
u'(x)
=
\frac14(-\sin x+3\sin3x).
$$
Passing to the improper limits yields the displayed formula.
:::

<1>4. The half-line integral equals
$$
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\,dx
=
\frac{3\pi}{8}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By the standard Dirichlet integral,
$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin(ax)}x\,dx
=
\frac\pi2
$$
for every $a>0$. Combining steps <1>2 and <1>3 therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\,dx
&=
\frac38
\left(
-\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
+
3\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin3x}{x}\,dx
\right)\\
&=
\frac38
\left(
-\frac\pi2+3\frac\pi2
\right)\\
&=
\frac{3\pi}{8}.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>5. Hence
$$
\boxed{
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin^3x}{x^3}\,dx
=
\frac{3\pi}{4}
}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine step <1>1 with step <1>4:
$$
I=2\cdot\frac{3\pi}{8}=\frac{3\pi}{4}.
$$
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the requested evaluation.
