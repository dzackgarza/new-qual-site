::: {.problem}
Let $f_1\ge f_2\ge\cdots$ be continuous on $[0,1]$ and suppose $f_n(x)\to0$ for every $x$. Must $f_n\to0$ uniformly on $[0,1]$? Prove your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $n$ and every $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
f_n(x)\ge0.
$$

::: pf-proof

For fixed $x$, the real sequence
$$
f_1(x)\ge f_2(x)\ge\cdots
$$
decreases to $0$. Every term of a decreasing sequence is at least its
limit, so $f_n(x)\ge0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and define
$$
U_n
\coloneqq
\{x\in[0,1]:f_n(x)<\varepsilon\}.
$$
Then each $U_n$ is open in $[0,1]$, the sets satisfy
$$
U_1\subseteq U_2\subseteq\cdots,
$$
and
$$
\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty U_n=[0,1].
$$

::: pf-proof

Continuity of $f_n$ makes $U_n$ open. Since
$f_{n+1}\le f_n$, the inequality $f_n(x)<\varepsilon$ implies
$f_{n+1}(x)<\varepsilon$, so $U_n\subseteq U_{n+1}$.

Finally, for every fixed $x$ one has $f_n(x)\to0$, so
$f_n(x)<\varepsilon$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Thus every point
belongs to some $U_n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

There is an $N$ such that
$$
U_N=[0,1].
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the sets $U_n$ form an open cover of the compact interval
$[0,1]$. Choose a finite subcover
$$
U_{n_1},\ldots,U_{n_k}.
$$
Let
$$
N\coloneqq\max\{n_1,\ldots,n_k\}.
$$
Since the sets are increasing, every $U_{n_j}$ is contained in $U_N$.
Hence $U_N$ already contains the whole interval.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

For every $n\ge N$ and every $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
\abs{f_n(x)}<\varepsilon.
$$

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
0\le f_n(x).
$$
Since the sequence is decreasing,
$$
f_n(x)\le f_N(x).
$$
Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives $f_N(x)<\varepsilon$ for every $x$. Therefore
$$
0\le f_n(x)\le f_N(x)<\varepsilon,
$$
which is the stated estimate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{f_n\longrightarrow0\text{ uniformly on }[0,1]}.
$$

::: pf-proof

For every $\varepsilon>0$, steps [](#s2){.pf-ref}, [](#s3){.pf-ref} and [](#s4){.pf-ref} produce an $N$ such that
$$
\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\abs{f_n(x)}<\varepsilon
$$
for all $n\ge N$. This is uniform convergence.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} answers the question affirmatively.
