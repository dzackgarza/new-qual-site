::: {.problem}
a) Let $f$ be a function defined on an interval $(a,b)$ and let $c \in (a,b)$.
Define what it means to say that $f$ is continuous at $c$.

b) Use this definition to show that $f(x) = 1/x$ is continuous at $x = 1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A function $f:(a,b)\to\RR$ is continuous at $c\in(a,b)$ if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that whenever $x\in(a,b)$ and $\abs{x-c}<\delta$, one has
$$
\abs{f(x)-f(c)}<\varepsilon.
$$

For $f(x)=1/x$ at $c=1$, let $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
$$
\delta=\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right\}.
$$
If $\abs{x-1}<\delta$, then $x>1/2$, so $\abs{x}>1/2$. Hence
$$
\abs{\frac1x-1}
=\frac{\abs{x-1}}{\abs{x}}
<2\abs{x-1}
<2\delta
\le\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $1/x$ is continuous at $1$.
:::
