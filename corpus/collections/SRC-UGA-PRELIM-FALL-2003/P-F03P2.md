::: {.problem}
Find a $2 \times 2$ matrix $P$ such that $P \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ \frac{2}{9} & 1 \end{pmatrix} P^{-1}$ is diagonal.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2/9 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$; its eigenvalues are $5/3$ and $1/3$.

::: pf-proof

$\det(A - \lambda I) = (1-\lambda)^2 - \tfrac{4}{9}$, which vanishes exactly when $1 - \lambda = \pm\tfrac{2}{3}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If the rows of an invertible $P$ are left eigenvectors of $A$, then $P A P^{-1}$ is diagonal.

::: pf-proof

If the rows $p_1, p_2$ of $P$ satisfy $p_i A = \lambda_i p_i$, then $PA = DP$ with $D = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2)$, so $PAP^{-1} = D$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The row $(1, 3)$ is a left eigenvector for $5/3$, and the row $(1, -3)$ is a left eigenvector for $1/3$.

::: pf-proof

$(1, 3)A = (1 + \tfrac{6}{9},\ 2 + 3) = (\tfrac{5}{3}, 5) = \tfrac{5}{3}(1, 3)$ and $(1, -3)A = (1 - \tfrac{6}{9},\ 2 - 3) = (\tfrac{1}{3}, -1) = \tfrac{1}{3}(1, -3)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Let $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$. Then $P A P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 5/3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1/3 \end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

$\det P = -6 \neq 0$, so $P$ is invertible. Its rows are the left eigenvectors of the previous step, so the second step gives the diagonal form.

:::
