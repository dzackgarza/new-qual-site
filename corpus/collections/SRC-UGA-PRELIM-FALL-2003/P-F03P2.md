::: {.problem}
Find a $2 \times 2$ matrix $P$ such that $P \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ \frac{1}{2} & 1 \end{pmatrix} P^{-1}$ is diagonal.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1/2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$; its eigenvalues are $0$ and $2$.

::: pf-proof

$\det(A - \lambda I) = (1-\lambda)^2 - 1 = \lambda^2 - 2\lambda = \lambda(\lambda - 2)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

An eigenvector for $\lambda = 0$ is $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

$A\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 + 2 \\ -1 + 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

An eigenvector for $\lambda = 2$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

$A\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 + 2 \\ 1 + 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = 2\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For $P A P^{-1}$ to be diagonal, the rows of $P$ must be left eigenvectors of $A$ (i.e. eigenvectors of $A^T$).

::: pf-proof

$P A P^{-1}$ is diagonal iff the rows of $P$ are left eigenvectors of $A$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Left eigenvectors: for $\lambda = 0$, solve $(x, y)A = 0$, i.e. $x + y/2 = 0$ and $2x + y = 0$, giving $(x, y) = (-1, 2)$; for $\lambda = 2$, solve $(x,y)A = 2(x,y)$, giving $(x, y) = (1, 2)$.

::: pf-proof

direct computation.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let $P = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ (rows are the left eigenvectors).

::: pf-proof

definition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

Then $P A P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

the rows of $P$ are left eigenvectors with eigenvalues $0$ and $2$, so $P A P^{-1}$ is diagonal with diagonal entries $0$ and $2$.

:::
