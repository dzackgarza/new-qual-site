::: problem
Suppose that $f\in L^p(\mathbb{R})$ for all $p\in(1,2)$ and that $\sup_{p\in(1,2)}\|f\|_p<\infty$.
Prove that $f\in L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and that $$\lim_{p\to 2^-}\|f\|_p=\|f\|_2.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $M = \sup_{p \in (1,2)} \|f\|_p < \infty$.
Proof: hypothesis.

<1>2. $f \in L^2(\RR)$.
<2>1. On $\{|f| > 1\}$, $|f|^p \uparrow |f|^2$ as $p \uparrow 2$ (monotone increasing).
Proof: for $|f| > 1$, $|f|^p$ increases with $p$.
<2>2. By the monotone convergence theorem, $\int_{\{|f|>1\}} |f|^2 = \lim_{p \to 2^-} \int_{\{|f|>1\}} |f|^p \le \limsup_{p \to 2^-} \|f\|_p^p \le M^2 < \infty$.
Proof: monotone convergence and the bound $\|f\|_p \le M$.
<2>3. On $\{|f| \le 1\}$, $|f|^2 \le |f|^p$ for $p < 2$, so $\int_{\{|f|\le 1\}} |f|^2 \le \int |f|^p \le M^p < \infty$.
Proof: for $|f| \le 1$ and $p < 2$, $|f|^2 \le |f|^p$.
<2>4. Hence $\int |f|^2 = \int_{\{|f|>1\}} |f|^2 + \int_{\{|f|\le 1\}} |f|^2 < \infty$, so $f \in L^2$.
Proof: <2>2 and <2>3.

<1>3. $\lim_{p \to 2^-} \|f\|_p = \|f\|_2$.
<2>1. On $\{|f| > 1\}$, $|f|^p \uparrow |f|^2$ as $p \uparrow 2$, so by monotone convergence $\int_{\{|f|>1\}} |f|^p \to \int_{\{|f|>1\}} |f|^2$.
Proof: monotone convergence theorem.
<2>2. On $\{|f| \le 1\}$, $|f|^p \downarrow |f|^2$ as $p \uparrow 2$, so by monotone convergence $\int_{\{|f|\le 1\}} |f|^p \to \int_{\{|f|\le 1\}} |f|^2$.
Proof: monotone convergence theorem (decreasing version).
<2>3. Hence $\int |f|^p \to \int |f|^2$, i.e. $\|f\|_p^p \to \|f\|_2^2$.
Proof: <2>1 and <2>2.
<2>4. Therefore $\|f\|_p \to \|f\|_2$.
Proof: <2>3 and continuity of the $p$-th root.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>3.
:::
