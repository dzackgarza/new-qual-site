::: {.problem}
If $f\in L^2(\mathbb{R})$, $g\in L^3(\mathbb{R})$, and $h\in L^6(\mathbb{R})$ then prove that the product $fgh$ is in $L^1(\mathbb{R})$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Since
\[
\frac12+\frac13+\frac16=1,
\]
the generalized Hölder inequality with exponents $2,3,6$ gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|fgh|\,dx
\le
\left(\int_{\mathbb R}|f|^2\,dx\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_{\mathbb R}|g|^3\,dx\right)^{1/3}
\left(\int_{\mathbb R}|h|^6\,dx\right)^{1/6}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\|fgh\|_1\le \|f\|_2\,\|g\|_3\,\|h\|_6.
\]
The right-hand side is finite by hypothesis. Therefore
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|f(x)g(x)h(x)|\,dx<\infty,
\]
so $fgh\in L^1(\mathbb R)$.
:::
