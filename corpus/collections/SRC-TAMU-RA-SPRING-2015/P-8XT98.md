::: {.problem}
If $f\in L^2(\RR)$, $g\in L^3(\RR)$, and $h\in L^6(\RR)$ then prove that the product $fgh$ is in $L^1(\RR)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

$\norm{fgh}_1 \le \norm{f}_2\norm{g}_3\norm{h}_6 < \infty$, so $fgh \in L^1(\RR)$.

::: pf-proof

Since $\frac12+\frac13+\frac16=1$, Hölder's inequality with exponents $2$ and $2$ applied to $f$ and $gh$, followed by Hölder's inequality with exponents $\frac32$ and $3$ applied to $\abs{g}^2$ and $\abs{h}^2$, gives
$$
\norm{fgh}_1 \le \norm{f}_2\norm{gh}_2 = \norm{f}_2\left(\int_{\RR}\abs{g}^2\abs{h}^2\right)^{1/2} \le \norm{f}_2\left(\norm{g}_3^{2}\norm{h}_6^{2}\right)^{1/2} = \norm{f}_2\norm{g}_3\norm{h}_6.
$$
The right side is finite by hypothesis.
