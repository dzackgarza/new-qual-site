::: {.problem}
Let $X = S^1 \vee S^1$ be the figure-of-eight space, and let $F_n$ denote the free group on $n$ generators.
By considering covering spaces of $X$, show that $F_2$ contains subgroups isomorphic to $F_n$, for arbitrary $n \geq 3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $X = S^{1}_{a} \vee S^{1}_{b}$ for the figure of eight, so that $\pi_1(X,x_{0}) = F(a,b) \cong F_{2}$ is free on the two loops, and $F_{n}$ for the free group on $n$ generators. The collection's `provenance` is the UCSD qualifying examination compilation, with no solutions sheet, so the argument below is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cover}
There is a covering map $p\colon \tilde{X} \to X$ whose total space $\tilde{X}$ is connected and has $\pi_1(\tilde{X})$ free of countably infinite rank.

::: pf-proof
Let $\tilde{X}$ be the quotient of the disjoint union of $\RR$ and circles $S^{1}_{n}$, $n \in \ZZ$, that identifies the point $n$ of $\RR$ with a chosen point $q_{n}$ of $S^{1}_{n}$, so that $\tilde{X}$ is the real line with a circle attached at each integer. Let $p$ carry $\RR$ onto $S^{1}_{a}$ by $t \mapsto e^{2\pi it}$ and carry $S^{1}_{n}$ onto $S^{1}_{b}$ by a homeomorphism sending $q_{n}$ to $x_{0}$. Each point of $\tilde{X}$ other than the integers has a neighbourhood mapped homeomorphically onto an open arc of $X$ missing the wedge point, and at an integer $n$ the neighbourhood consisting of a small arc about $n$ together with a small arc of $S^{1}_{n}$ about $q_{n}$ is mapped homeomorphically onto a neighbourhood of $x_{0}$ in $X$; since the arcs of $\RR$ about the integers are the whole preimage of the wedge point, $p$ is a covering map. For the rank: $\tilde{X}$ is homotopy equivalent to the graph with vertex set $\ZZ$ together with one vertex on each circle and edge set the segments $[n,n+1]$ together with one loop $e_{n}$ at each integer; a maximal tree is the line formed by the segments, and the remaining edges are the loops $e_{n}$, so a connected graph with a spanning tree and countably many leftover edges has free fundamental group on those edges.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inject}
$p_{*}$ is injective, so $\pi_1(\tilde{X})$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $F_{2}$.

::: pf-proof
For a covering map $p$, the induced homomorphism $p_{*}\colon\pi_1(\tilde{X},\tilde{x}_{0}) \to \pi_1(X,x_{0})$ is injective: the lift of a homotopy of loops starting at $\tilde{x}_{0}$ is a homotopy of loops starting at $\tilde{x}_{0}$, so a loop in $\tilde{X}$ that is null-homotopic downstairs is null-homotopic upstairs. The target is $F(a,b)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #countable}
$\pi_1(\tilde{X})$ is a free group of countably infinite rank, and its image under $p_{*}$ is generated by the loops $a^{n}ba^{-n}$, $n \in \ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
The generators $e_{n}$ of step [](#cover){.pf-ref} map under $p$ to a loop that runs from $x_{0}$ along $\RR$ to the integer $n$, around the $n$-th circle, and back, which is the conjugate $a^{n}ba^{-n}$; conversely each such conjugate arises this way. Since $p_{*}$ is injective by step [](#inject){.pf-ref}, $\pi_1(\tilde{X})$ is isomorphic to $\langle a^{n}ba^{-n} \mid n \in \ZZ\rangle$, which as a set of words on the free basis $\\{a,b\\}$ is a free generating set, so the rank is countably infinite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subgroups}
For every $n \ge 3$ the group $F_{n}$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $F_{2}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#countable){.pf-ref} the group $F_{2} = F(a,b)$ contains the subgroup $H = \langle a^{k}ba^{-k} \mid k \in \ZZ \rangle$, and $H$ is free with the displayed set as a free basis, one generator for each integer. A subset of a free basis is again a free basis of the subgroup it generates, so the first $n$ of these generators span a subgroup isomorphic to $F_{n}$; and a subgroup of a subgroup is a subgroup, so $F_{n} \cong \langle a^{k}ba^{-k} \mid 0 \le k \le n-1\rangle \le F_{2}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step
$F_{2}$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $\boxed{F_{n}}$ for every $n \ge 3$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#subgroups){.pf-ref} produces the subgroup for arbitrary $n$, which is the assertion; the covering space of $X$ that realises it is the one of step [](#cover){.pf-ref}, restricted to the subgraph spanned by the chosen $n$ generators.

:::
