For each ordered pair of distinct spaces among $S^2$, $T^2$, and the Klein bottle $K$, determine whether a covering map from the first space to the second can exist.

:::{.solution}
<1>1. None of the four covering maps involving $S^2$ and one of $T^2,K$ can exist.
::: {.proof}
A covering map induces an isomorphism on $\pi_n$ for every $n\ge2$.
But
\[
\pi_2(S^2)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
\pi_2(T^2)=\pi_2(K)=0,
\]
because the universal covers of $T^2$ and $K$ are contractible planes.
Thus no covering can exist in either direction between $S^2$ and either aspherical surface.
:::

<1>2. There is no covering map $K\to T^2$.
::: {.proof}
A covering $K\to T^2$ would induce an injection
\[
\pi_1(K)\hookrightarrow\pi_1(T^2)\cong\ZZ^2.
\]
Every subgroup of the abelian group $\ZZ^2$ is abelian, whereas
\[
\pi_1(K)\cong\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}=b^{-1}\rangle
\]
is nonabelian.
:::

<1>3. There is a two-sheeted covering map $T^2\to K$.
::: {.proof}
Realize the universal cover of the Klein bottle as $\RR^2$ with deck group generated by
\[
a(x,y)=(x+1,y),
\qquad
b(x,y)=(-x,y+1).
\]
The subgroup generated by $a$ and $b^2$ consists of translations
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(x+m,y+2n),
\qquad m,n\in\ZZ,
\]
and has index $2$ in the Klein-bottle deck group.
Its quotient of $\RR^2$ is a torus, so the subgroup inclusion induces a two-sheeted covering
\[
T^2\longrightarrow K.
\]
This is the orientation double cover of the Klein bottle.
:::

<1>4. Among the six directed pairs of distinct spaces, $T^2\to K$ is the only possible covering direction.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>1--<1>3.
:::
