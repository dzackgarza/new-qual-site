Show that any homotopy equivalence from $\mathbb{CP}^{2n}$ to itself is orientation-preserving, that is has degree +1.

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $H^*(\CP^{2n};\ZZ) = \ZZ[x]/(x^{2n+1})$ with $|x| = 2$, so $H^{4n}(\CP^{2n};\ZZ) = \ZZ$ generated by $x^{2n}$.
Proof: standard computation of the cohomology ring of complex projective space.

<1>2. Let $f: \CP^{2n} \to \CP^{2n}$ be a homotopy equivalence; then $f^*: H^2 \to H^2$ is an isomorphism, so $f^*(x) = \pm x$.
Proof: $f^*$ is a ring isomorphism, so it sends the generator $x$ of $H^2 = \ZZ$ to $\pm x$.

<1>3. $f^*(x^{2n}) = f^*(x)^{2n} = (\pm x)^{2n} = x^{2n}$ (since $2n$ is even).
Proof: $f^*$ is a ring homomorphism, and $(\pm x)^{2n} = x^{2n}$.

<1>4. Hence $f^*$ is the identity on $H^{4n}(\CP^{2n};\ZZ) = \ZZ$.
Proof: <1>3.

<1>5. The degree of $f$ is the integer $d$ such that $f_*$ is multiplication by $d$ on $H_{4n}(\CP^{2n};\ZZ) = \ZZ$.
Proof: definition of degree.

<1>6. By Poincaré duality (or the universal coefficient theorem), $f^*$ on $H^{4n}$ is multiplication by the same integer $d$ as $f_*$ on $H_{4n}$.
Proof: the induced maps on cohomology and homology are dual.

<1>7. Hence $d = 1$, so $f$ has degree $+1$ and is orientation-preserving.
Proof: <1>4 and <1>6.

<1>8. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>7.
:::
