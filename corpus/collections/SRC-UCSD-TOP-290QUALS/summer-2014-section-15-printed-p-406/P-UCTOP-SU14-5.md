Show that there is no compact 4-manifold, with or without boundary, which is homotopy-equivalent to $S^2 \vee S^4$.

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose a compact connected $4$-manifold $M$ were homotopy-equivalent to $S^2\vee S^4$.
::: {.proof}
We derive a contradiction.
:::

<1>2. Then $H_4(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$, so $M$ must be closed and orientable.
::: {.proof}
A compact connected $4$-manifold with nonempty boundary has $H_4(M;\mathbb Z)=0$. A closed connected nonorientable $4$-manifold also has zero integral top homology. Since $H_4(S^2\vee S^4)\cong\mathbb Z$, neither case is possible.
:::

<1>3. The group $H^2(M;\mathbb Z)$ is infinite cyclic, generated by a class $u$, and $u^2=0$.
::: {.proof}
Homotopy equivalence preserves the cohomology ring. For $S^2\vee S^4$, the degree-$2$ cohomology is generated by the $S^2$ summand, while its square vanishes because the cup product between positive-degree classes supported on distinct wedge summands is zero and $H^4(S^2)=0$.
:::

<1>4. Poincaré duality forces the intersection pairing
$$
H^2(M;\mathbb Z)/\text{torsion}\times H^2(M;\mathbb Z)/\text{torsion}\to\mathbb Z
$$
to be unimodular.
::: {.proof}
For a closed oriented $4$-manifold, the pairing $(x,y)\mapsto\langle x\smile y,[M]\rangle$ is a unimodular bilinear form on the free part of $H^2$.
:::

<1>5. Since this free part has rank one, unimodularity requires
$$
\langle u^2,[M]\rangle=\pm1,
$$
contradicting $u^2=0$ from <1>3.
::: {.proof}
A rank-one integral bilinear form is represented by the $1\times1$ matrix $(q)$ and is unimodular exactly when $q=\pm1$.
:::

<1>6. Hence no compact $4$-manifold, with or without boundary, is homotopy-equivalent to $S^2\vee S^4$.
::: {.proof}
The assumption in <1>1 leads to the contradiction in <1>5.
:::
:::
