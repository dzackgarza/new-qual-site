::: {.problem}
Show that there is no self-map of $\mathbb{CP}^2 \times \mathbb{CP}^2$ having degree $-1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
Write
$$
H^*(\mathbb{CP}^2\times\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb Z)
\cong\mathbb Z[x,y]/(x^3,y^3),
\qquad |x|=|y|=2.
$$

::: pf-proof
This follows from the cohomology ring of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and the Künneth theorem.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
For any self-map $f$, write
$$
f^*x=ax+by,\qquad f^*y=cx+dy
$$
with $a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z$.

::: pf-proof
The degree-$2$ cohomology is freely generated by $x,y$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ab-cd-zero}
The relations $x^3=y^3=0$ force
$$
ab=0,\qquad cd=0.
$$

::: pf-proof
Since $f^*(x^3)=0$,
$$
0=(ax+by)^3=3a^2b\,x^2y+3ab^2\,xy^2.
$$
The classes $x^2y$ and $xy^2$ freely generate $H^6$, so $a^2b=ab^2=0$, hence $ab=0$. The same argument applied to $f^*(y^3)$ gives $cd=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #degree-formula}
The degree of $f$ is
$$
\deg f=a^2d^2+b^2c^2.
$$

::: pf-proof
The top cohomology is generated by $x^2y^2$. Thus
$$
f^*(x^2y^2)=(ax+by)^2(cx+dy)^2.
$$
The coefficient of $x^2y^2$ is
$$
a^2d^2+4abcd+b^2c^2.
$$
By step [](#ab-cd-zero){.pf-ref}, $abcd=0$, leaving $a^2d^2+b^2c^2$. This coefficient is the degree.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #degree-nonnegative}
In particular $\deg f\ge0$ for every self-map.

::: pf-proof
The expression in step [](#degree-formula){.pf-ref} is a sum of two integer squares.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore no self-map of $\mathbb{CP}^2\times\mathbb{CP}^2$ can have degree $-1$.

::: pf-proof
Immediate from step [](#degree-nonnegative){.pf-ref}.
:::
