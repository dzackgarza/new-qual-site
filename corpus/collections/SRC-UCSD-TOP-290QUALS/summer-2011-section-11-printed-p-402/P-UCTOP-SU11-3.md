Let $n \geq 2$ be a positive integer, and let $k$ be in the range $0 < k < n$.
Let $X = \mathbb{CP}^n / \mathbb{CP}^k$ be the quotient space obtained from $\mathbb{CP}^n$ by identifying its subspace $\mathbb{CP}^k$ to a point.
Calculate the integral cohomology ring of $X$.

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n) = \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{n+1})$, $|x|=2$.
Proof: cohomology of $\mathbb{CP}^n$.

<1>2. $\mathbb{CP}^k \subset \mathbb{CP}^n$ corresponds to the ideal $(x^{k+1})$? Actually $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n,\mathbb{CP}^k) \cong \tilde H^*(X)$.
Proof: long exact sequence of pair.

<1>3. As abelian groups, $H^{2i}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ for $i=0$ and $k< i \le n$, and $0$ otherwise (reduced cohomology $\tilde H^{2i}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ for $k< i \le n$).
Proof: cellular cohomology (one cell in each even dimension $0,2k+2,2k+4,\dots,2n$).

<1>4. The quotient map $q : \mathbb{CP}^n \to X$ induces $q^* : \tilde H^*(X) \hookrightarrow H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n)$ identifying $\tilde H^{2i}(X)$ with $\mathbb{Z} x^i$ for $k< i \le n$.
Proof: $q$ collapses the $2k$-skeleton, so in cohomology it is injective on the remaining degrees.

<1>5. Hence for $a \in H^{2i}(X)$, $b \in H^{2j}(X)$ with $i,j>k$, $q^*(a\smile b) = q^*(a)\smile q^*(b) = x^{i+j}$ if $i+j \le n$ and $0$ otherwise.
Proof: <1>4 and ring structure of $\mathbb{CP}^n$.

<1>6. Therefore $a\smile b$ is a generator of $H^{2(i+j)}(X)$ when $i,j>k$ and $i+j \le n$, and $0$ when $i+j>n$.
Proof: <1>5 (injectivity of $q^*$).

<1>7. In particular $H^*(X)$ is generated by $y = x^{k+1} \in H^{2k+2}(X)$ with relation $y^{\lceil (n+1)/(k+1)\rceil}=0$, and as a ring $H^*(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}[y]/(y^m)$ truncated where products exceeding degree $2n$ vanish.
Proof: <1>6.

<1>8. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>6.
:::
