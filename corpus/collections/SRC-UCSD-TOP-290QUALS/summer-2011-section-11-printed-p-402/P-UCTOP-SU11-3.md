Let $n \geq 2$ be a positive integer, and let $k$ be in the range $0 < k < n$.
Let $X = \mathbb{CP}^n / \mathbb{CP}^k$ be the quotient space obtained from $\mathbb{CP}^n$ by identifying its subspace $\mathbb{CP}^k$ to a point.
Calculate the integral cohomology ring of $X$.

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For the quotient map $q:\mathbb{CP}^n\to X=\mathbb{CP}^n/\mathbb{CP}^k$, there is a natural identification
$$
\widetilde H^*(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n,\mathbb{CP}^k;\mathbb Z).
$$
::: {.proof}
Reduced cohomology of a quotient by a subcomplex agrees with the relative cohomology of the pair.
:::

<1>2. As an abelian group,
$$
H^r(X;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&r=0,\\
\mathbb Z,&r=2i\text{ with }k+1\le i\le n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
The inclusion $\mathbb{CP}^k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^n$ identifies the cellular cochain complexes through degree $2k$. Equivalently, the relative CW complex has one cell in each even dimension $2k+2,2k+4,\dots,2n$ and no odd-dimensional cells, so all relative coboundaries vanish.
:::

<1>3. Let $u_i\in H^{2i}(X;\mathbb Z)$, $k+1\le i\le n$, be the unique generator characterized by
$$
q^*(u_i)=x^i,
$$
where $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[x]/(x^{n+1})$, $|x|=2$.
::: {.proof}
From the long exact sequence of the pair, the map $q^*:H^{2i}(X)\to H^{2i}(\mathbb{CP}^n)$ is an isomorphism for $i>k$ because $H^{2i}(\mathbb{CP}^k)=0$ there.
:::

<1>4. The products of positive-degree generators are
$$
u_i u_j=
\begin{cases}
u_{i+j},&i+j\le n,\\
0,&i+j>n.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
Naturality of cup product gives
$$
q^*(u_i u_j)=q^*(u_i)q^*(u_j)=x^{i+j}.
$$
If $i+j\le n$, then $q^*:H^{2(i+j)}(X)\to H^{2(i+j)}(\mathbb{CP}^n)$ is an isomorphism, so $u_i u_j=u_{i+j}$. If $i+j>n$, the target cohomology group is zero.
:::

<1>5. Therefore, as a graded ring,
$$
H^*(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\cdot1\oplus\bigoplus_{i=k+1}^{n}\mathbb Z u_i,
$$
with $|u_i|=2i$ and multiplication given by <1>4.
::: {.proof}
The additive groups are those of <1>2, the unit is the degree-$0$ generator, and <1>4 determines every product of positive-degree classes.
:::

<1>6. In general this ring is not generated by $u_{k+1}$ alone.
::: {.proof}
For example, if $n\ge k+2$, the nonzero class $u_{k+2}$ has degree $2k+4$, whereas every positive power of $u_{k+1}$ has degree divisible by $2k+2$. Thus the earlier single-generator description is false except in degenerate short ranges.
:::
:::
