How many distinct double covers does $\mathbb{RP}^3 \times S^1$ have?
Can you identify any of them?

::: {.solution}
<1>1. We have
$$
\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^3\times S^1)\cong\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z.
$$
::: {.proof}
Use $\pi_1(X\times Y)\cong\pi_1(X)\times\pi_1(Y)$ together with $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^3)=\mathbb Z/2$ and $\pi_1(S^1)=\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>2. Connected double covers correspond to index-$2$ subgroups, equivalently kernels of nonzero homomorphisms
$$
\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2.
$$
There are exactly three.
::: {.proof}
Every index-$2$ subgroup is normal, so it is the kernel of the quotient map to $\mathbb Z/2$. Conversely, every nonzero homomorphism to $\mathbb Z/2$ is surjective and has index-$2$ kernel. Since
$$
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/2)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2,
$$
there are three nonzero homomorphisms.
:::

<1>3. Their kernels are
$$
0\oplus\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\mathbb Z/2\oplus2\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\{(\epsilon,m):\epsilon\equiv m\pmod2\}.
$$
::: {.proof}
These are the kernels of the three maps $(\epsilon,m)\mapsto\epsilon$, $(\epsilon,m)\mapsto m\bmod2$, and $(\epsilon,m)\mapsto\epsilon+m\bmod2$.
:::

<1>4. The first cover is $S^3\times S^1\to\mathbb{RP}^3\times S^1$, and the second is the product of the identity on $\mathbb{RP}^3$ with the degree-$2$ circle cover.
::: {.proof}
The first replaces the $\mathbb{RP}^3$ factor by its universal double cover $S^3$. The second replaces only the $S^1$ factor by $z\mapsto z^2$.
:::

<1>5. The third cover is the mapping torus of the antipodal map on $S^3$; in particular its total space is homeomorphic to $S^3\times S^1$.
::: {.proof}
Its fundamental group is the diagonal infinite cyclic subgroup generated by $(1,1)$. One model is
$$
S^3\times[0,1]/(x,1)\sim(-x,0).
$$
The antipodal map on $S^3$ lies in $SO(4)$ and is isotopic to the identity, so its mapping torus is homeomorphic to the product $S^3\times S^1$.
:::

<1>6. Hence there are exactly three connected double covers up to equivalence over the base.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>2--<1>5. If disconnected double covers are also allowed, one additionally has the trivial cover consisting of two disjoint copies of the base.
:::
:::
