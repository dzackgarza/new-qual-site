The connect-sum (#) of two oriented 4-manifolds is defined by removing an open 4-ball from each, and gluing the resulting manifolds using a homeomorphism between their boundary 3-spheres, in such a way that the orientations match to make a new oriented manifold.
Compute the cohomology ring of the connect-sum $X = \mathbb{CP}^2 \# (S^2 \times S^2)$.

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The additive cohomology groups of
$$
X=\mathbb{CP}^2\#(S^2\times S^2)
$$
are
$$
H^0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H^4(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H^2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^3,
$$
and all other groups vanish.
::: {.proof}
For connected sums of closed oriented $4$-manifolds, the middle cohomology is the direct sum of the middle cohomologies of the two summands, while degrees $0$ and $4$ remain $\mathbb Z$. This follows from Mayer--Vietoris applied after removing an open $4$-ball from each summand; the common boundary is $S^3$.
:::

<1>2. Choose degree-$2$ classes $x,u,v$ so that $x$ comes from $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and $u,v$ come from the two sphere factors of $S^2\times S^2$.
::: {.proof}
Thus $x$ generates $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2)$ and $u,v$ are the pullbacks of the fundamental classes of the two $S^2$ factors.
:::

<1>3. Let $\omega\in H^4(X;\mathbb Z)$ be the orientation class. Then
$$
x^2=\omega,
\qquad
uv=\omega,
\qquad
u^2=v^2=0.
$$
::: {.proof}
On $\mathbb{CP}^2$, the standard generator satisfies $x^2=[\mathbb{CP}^2]^*$. On $S^2\times S^2$, the two factor classes square to zero and their product is the orientation class. Under the oriented connected sum, the two top classes are identified with the common orientation class $\omega$.
:::

<1>4. The mixed products satisfy
$$
xu=xv=0.
$$
::: {.proof}
Represent the degree-$2$ classes from the two summands by cocycles supported away from the gluing sphere and inside their respective punctured summands. Supports from distinct summands are disjoint, so mixed cup products vanish. Equivalently, the intersection form of an oriented connected sum is the orthogonal direct sum of the intersection forms of its summands.
:::

<1>5. Thus the cohomology ring is generated by $x,u,v$ in degree $2$ and $\omega$ in degree $4$, with
$$
x^2=uv=\omega,\qquad xu=xv=u^2=v^2=0,
$$
and every product of total degree $>4$ equal to zero.
::: {.proof}
The additive groups are given in <1>1, and <1>3--<1>4 determine every product of positive-degree generators. Together with the unit in $H^0$, this determines the graded ring.
:::
:::
