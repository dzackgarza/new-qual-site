::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be the result of gluing up the edges of two solid pentagons in pairs, according to the picture shown in the source.
Compute the fundamental group and the homology groups of $X$.
Is it a manifold?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
Reading the arrows in the source figure clockwise, the two pentagon boundary words are
$$
abcde \qquad\text{and}\qquad adbec.
$$

::: pf-proof
For each of the ten drawn edges, the arrowhead points in the clockwise direction around its pentagon. The labels around the left pentagon are $a,b,c,d,e$, and those around the right pentagon are $a,d,b,e,c$. Hence the two attaching words are exactly the displayed positive words.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #vertices-identified}
All ten polygon vertices are identified to a single vertex in the quotient.

::: pf-proof
Write the first pentagon vertices clockwise as $v_0,\dots,v_4$, with edges
$$
a:v_0\to v_1,\ b:v_1\to v_2,\ c:v_2\to v_3,\ d:v_3\to v_4,\ e:v_4\to v_0,
$$
and the second as $w_0,\dots,w_4$, with edges
$$
a:w_0\to w_1,\ d:w_1\to w_2,\ b:w_2\to w_3,\ e:w_3\to w_4,\ c:w_4\to w_0.
$$
Matching equally labelled arrows identifies initial endpoints with initial endpoints and terminal endpoints with terminal endpoints. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
a:&\ v_0\sim w_0,\ v_1\sim w_1,\\
b:&\ v_1\sim w_2,\ v_2\sim w_3,\\
c:&\ v_2\sim w_4,\ v_3\sim w_0,\\
d:&\ v_3\sim w_1,\ v_4\sim w_2,\\
e:&\ v_4\sim w_3,\ v_0\sim w_4.
\end{aligned}
$$
These relations place all $v_i$ and $w_j$ in one equivalence class.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Hence $X$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, five $1$-cells $a,b,c,d,e$, and two $2$-cells attached by $abcde$ and $adbec$.

::: pf-proof
The five edge-pairs give the five $1$-cells, step [](#vertices-identified){.pf-ref} gives one vertex, and the two polygon interiors give the two $2$-cells.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pi1-presentation}
Therefore
$$
\pi_1(X)\cong
\langle a,b,c,d,e\mid abcde=1,\ adbec=1\rangle.
$$

::: pf-proof
Apply the standard CW presentation of the fundamental group: one generator for each $1$-cell and one relator for each $2$-cell attaching word.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The cellular chain complex in positive degrees is
$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
\mathbb Z^5
\xrightarrow{0}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0,
$$
where both basis vectors of $\mathbb Z^2$ map to $(1,1,1,1,1)$.

::: pf-proof
Since there is one vertex, $\partial_1=0$. The cellular boundary of a $2$-cell records the exponent sum of each oriented $1$-cell in its attaching word. Each of $a,b,c,d,e$ occurs exactly once with positive exponent in each relation.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Thus
$$
H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4,\qquad
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
$$
and $H_i(X;\mathbb Z)=0$ for $i\ge3$.

::: pf-proof
The image of $\partial_2$ is the primitive rank-one subgroup generated by $(1,1,1,1,1)$, so its cokernel is $\mathbb Z^4$. Its kernel is generated by $(1,-1)$, hence is $\mathbb Z$. Connectedness gives $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #is-2-manifold}
The quotient is a closed connected $2$-manifold.

::: pf-proof
Interior points of either pentagon have disk neighborhoods, and interior points of paired edges have disk neighborhoods because exactly two edge neighborhoods are glued there. At the unique vertex, the ten polygon corners contribute ten intervals to the link. Matching endpoints according to the five edge pairings gives a $2$-regular link graph, and tracing the pairings shows that all ten corner intervals lie in one cycle. Hence the vertex link is a single circle, so the vertex also has a disk neighborhood. All edges are paired, so there is no boundary.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #orientable-genus-2}
The surface is orientable of genus $2$.

::: pf-proof
Give the two pentagons opposite orientations. Because corresponding arrows point in the same direction in the drawing, each edge identification reverses the induced boundary orientations, so these polygon orientations glue to an orientation of $X$. Its Euler characteristic is
$$
\chi(X)=1-5+2=-2.
$$
For a closed connected orientable surface, $\chi=2-2g$, so $g=2$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Consequently
$$
\pi_1(X)\cong
\langle x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2\mid [x_1,y_1][x_2,y_2]=1\rangle.
$$

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#is-2-manifold){.pf-ref} and [](#orientable-genus-2){.pf-ref}, $X$ is homeomorphic to the closed orientable genus-$2$ surface. The displayed presentation is its standard surface-group presentation and is therefore isomorphic to the presentation in step [](#pi1-presentation){.pf-ref}.
:::
