Consider a solid tetrahedron $ABCD$.
The face $ABC$ is glued to $ABD$ by an affine map preserving the order of vertices (i.e. $A$ goes to $A$, $B$ goes to $B$, $C$ goes to $D$.)
Similarly, $BCD$ is glued to $ACD$.
Compute the fundamental group of the resulting quotient space.

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The face identifications leave two vertex classes, namely $A\sim B$ and $C\sim D$.
::: {.proof}
The gluing $ABC\to ABD$ sends $C$ to $D$, while the gluing $BCD\to ACD$ sends $B$ to $A$. No further vertex identifications are needed.
:::

<1>2. In the quotient $1$-skeleton, the edges $AC,AD,BC,BD$ all become one edge $e$ joining the two vertex classes, while $AB$ and $CD$ become loops $a$ and $c$ at the two vertices.
::: {.proof}
The first face gluing identifies $AC$ with $AD$ and $BC$ with $BD$. The second identifies $BC$ with $AC$ and $BD$ with $AD$. Hence all four cross-edges form one class. The edges $AB$ and $CD$ are each fixed by the relevant face pairing.
:::

<1>3. The quotient of the face $ABC$ is a $2$-cell whose attaching loop is homotopic to $a$.
::: {.proof}
Traversing $ABC$ gives the edge path
$$
AB\cdot BC\cdot CA=aee^{-1}=a.
$$
Thus this $2$-cell kills the loop $a$.
:::

<1>4. The quotient of the face $BCD$ is a $2$-cell whose attaching loop is $ece^{-1}$.
::: {.proof}
Traversing $BCD$ gives
$$
BC\cdot CD\cdot DB=ece^{-1}.
$$
Thus this $2$-cell kills the conjugate of $c$, hence kills $c$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore the quotient of the boundary tetrahedron is simply connected, and attaching the interior $3$-cell does not change the fundamental group.
::: {.proof}
Choose a maximal tree consisting of the edge $e$. Before attaching the $2$-cells, the fundamental group is freely generated by the two loops $a,c$. By <1>3--<1>4 both generators are killed. Cells of dimension at least $3$ do not affect $\pi_1$.
:::

<1>6. Hence the resulting quotient space has
$$
\boxed{\pi_1=1}.
$$
:::
::: {.proof}
This is the conclusion of <1>5.
:::
:::
