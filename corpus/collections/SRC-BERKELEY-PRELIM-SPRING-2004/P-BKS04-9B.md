::: {.problem}
Let $S_4$ be the group of permutations of $\{1,2,3,4\}$.
Determine the order of $\operatorname{Aut}(S_4)$.
Justify your answer.
:::

::: {.solution}
The published solution is the collection's own retained sheet, **Spring 2004 prelim solutions, Problem 9B, PDF p. 9** (`assets/attachments/s04solution.pdf`, the file named in this collection's `provenance`): the counting of the four subgroups of order $3$, the reduction by an inner automorphism, the characterization of transpositions through the $H_k$, and the conclusion that every automorphism is inner are that solution's. The card supplies the two facts the sheet invokes without proof — that inner automorphisms permute the four subgroups arbitrarily, and that an automorphism carries a conjugacy class onto a conjugacy class — and it records why the subgroups of order $3$ are exactly those $H_i$.

Throughout $H_i$ is the subgroup of $S_4$ consisting of the identity and the two $3$-cycles fixing $i$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #center}
Conjugation gives an injective homomorphism $S_4\to\operatorname{Aut}(S_4)$, so $\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(S_4)}\ge24$ with equality iff every automorphism is inner.

::: pf-proof
$Z(S_4)=1$: an element commuting with every permutation is a product of disjoint cycles of equal length, and in $S_4$ such an element is a $4$-cycle, which does not commute with the transposition $(12)$. So the kernel of $S_4\to\operatorname{Aut}(S_4)$, $g\mapsto(g\mapsto gxg^{-1})$, is trivial, and the inner automorphisms realize $S_4$ inside $\operatorname{Aut}(S_4)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #four}
$S_4$ has exactly four subgroups of order $3$, namely $H_1,\dots,H_4$; they are distinct and every automorphism permutes them.

::: pf-proof
A group of order $3$ is cyclic, generated by an element of order $3$, and the elements of order $3$ in $S_4$ are the eight $3$-cycles; the subgroup generated by a $3$-cycle contains its inverse, so the eight $3$-cycles fall into four subgroups, each $\theset{1,\gamma,\gamma^2}$ for the two $3$-cycles fixing one point $i$ — that is $H_i$. There are no other subgroups of order $3$. An automorphism carries a subgroup of order $3$ to one of order $3$, so it permutes these four.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inner-realizes}
Inner automorphisms realize every permutation of $H_1,\dots,H_4$.

::: pf-proof
Conjugation by $\sigma\in S_4$ carries a $3$-cycle to a $3$-cycle, and the $3$-cycles of $H_i$ are exactly those fixing $i$; so $\sigma H_i\sigma^{-1}=H_{\sigma(i)}$, since $\sigma\gamma\sigma^{-1}$ fixes $\sigma(i)$ whenever $\gamma$ fixes $i$. Hence the action of $S_4$ on $\theset{H_1,\dots,H_4}$ by conjugation is the natural action on $\theset{1,2,3,4}$, which is onto $S_4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #reduce}
After composing a given $\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(S_4)$ with an inner automorphism, one may assume $\sigma$ fixes every $H_i$.

::: pf-proof
$\sigma$ induces a permutation $\pi$ of $\theset{H_1,\dots,H_4}$ by step [](#four){.pf-ref}; by step [](#inner-realizes){.pf-ref} there is $\tau\in S_4$ with conjugation by $\tau$ inducing $\pi^{-1}$; then $\sigma'=\mathrm{conj}_\tau\circ\sigma$ fixes each $H_i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #transpositions}
$\sigma$ fixes every transposition.

::: pf-proof
An automorphism preserves element orders and carries a conjugacy class onto a conjugacy class, since $\sigma(g^h)=\sigma(g)^{\sigma(h)}$. The elements of order $2$ in $S_4$ are the six transpositions and the three double transpositions, which form two conjugacy classes of sizes $6$ and $3$; so the class of transpositions is the unique class of size $6$ among elements of order $2$, and $\sigma$ preserves it. Within transpositions, $(ij)$ is characterized by: for which $k$ does $\langle(ij),H_k\rangle$ equal $S_4$? For $k\notin\theset{i,j}$, both $(ij)$ and $H_k$ lie in the stabilizer in $S_4$ of the remaining point, a copy of $S_3$, so the subgroup is proper; for $k\in\theset{i,j}$, say $k=i$, the subgroup contains the transposition $(ij)$ and the $3$-cycles on the other three points, hence the transpositions $(jl)$ for those points, hence all transpositions, hence $S_4$. Since $\sigma$ fixes each $H_k$ and carries transpositions to transpositions, it preserves this characterization and so fixes $(ij)$ for every transposition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
Every automorphism of $S_4$ is inner, so $\abs{\operatorname{Aut}(S_4)}=\abs{S_4}=24$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#reduce){.pf-ref} and step [](#transpositions){.pf-ref}, an arbitrary automorphism becomes the identity after composition with an inner automorphism; the transpositions generate $S_4$, so an automorphism fixing them is the identity. Hence $\operatorname{Aut}(S_4)=\operatorname{Inn}(S_4)\cong S_4/Z(S_4)\cong S_4$, of order $24$.

:::
