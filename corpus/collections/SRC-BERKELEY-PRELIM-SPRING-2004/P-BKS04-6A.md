::: {.problem}
For which pairs of integers $(a,b)$ is the quotient ring $\ZZ[x]/(x^2+ax+b)$ isomorphic (as a ring) to the direct product of rings $\ZZ\times\ZZ$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $A=\ZZ[x]/(x^2+ax+b)$ and $B=\ZZ\times\ZZ$. If $x^2+ax+b$ is irreducible in the UFD $\ZZ[x]$, then $(x^2+ax+b)$ is a prime ideal, so $A$ is a domain.
But $B$ is not a domain.
Thus we may assume $x^2+ax+b=(x-c)(x-d)$ for some $c,d\in\ZZ$.

Suppose $p$ is a prime integer dividing $c-d$. Then $A\cong B$ implies $A/pA\cong B/pB$; that is, $\FF_p[x]/(x-\bar c)^2\cong\FF_p\times\FF_p$, where $\bar c=\bar d$ is the image of $c$ in $\FF_p$. The ring on the left has a nonzero element with square $0$, namely $x-\bar c$, whereas the right hand side has no such element.
This contradiction shows that $c-d$ is divisible by no primes, so $c-d=\pm1$.

Conversely, if $c-d=\pm1$, then the sum of the ideals $(x-c)$ and $(x-d)$ in $\ZZ[x]$ is the unit ideal, and their product equals their intersection (since they are generated by non-associate irreducible elements), so the Chinese remainder theorem gives

$$
\frac{\ZZ[x]}{((x-c)(x-d))}\cong\frac{\ZZ[x]}{(x-c)}\times\frac{\ZZ[x]}{(x-d)}.
$$

Each factor on the right is isomorphic to $\ZZ$, because each polynomial in $\ZZ[x]$ is uniquely expressible as $q(x)(x-c)+r$ with $q(x)\in\ZZ[x]$ and $r\in\ZZ$. Thus $c-d=\pm1$ implies $A\cong B$. In other words, the answer is the set of $(a,b)$ such that $x^2+ax+b$ has the form $(x-n)(x-(n+1))$; that is,

$$
\{(-(2n+1),n(n+1)):n\in\ZZ\}.
$$
:::
