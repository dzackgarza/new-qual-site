::: problem
Let $p$ be a prime and let $H$ be a subgroup of the symmetric group $S_p$ such that $|H| = p$.

(a) Show that the centralizer of $H$ in $S_p$ is $H$; that is, $C_{S_p}(H) = H$.

(b) Suppose that $H \subseteq N \subseteq S_p$ where $N$ is a nilpotent group.
Prove that $H = N$.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Since \(|H|=p\), the group \(H\) is generated by a \(p\)-cycle \(\sigma\in S_p\).
::: {.proof}
A group of prime order is cyclic. A nonidentity element of order \(p\) in \(S_p\) must be a single \(p\)-cycle, since the cycle lengths divide \(p\) and their sum is \(p\).
:::

<1>2. The centralizer of \(\sigma\) in \(S_p\) is \(\langle\sigma\rangle\). Hence \(C_{S_p}(H)=H\).
::: {.proof}
Choose a point \(i\). Since \(\sigma\) is a \(p\)-cycle, every point is \(\sigma^k(i)\) for a unique \(k\pmod p\). If \(\tau\sigma=\sigma\tau\), then
\[
\tau(\sigma^k(i))=\sigma^k(\tau(i)).
\]
Thus \(\tau\) is completely determined by \(\tau(i)\). Write \(\tau(i)=\sigma^j(i)\). Then the displayed identity gives \(\tau(\sigma^k(i))=\sigma^{k+j}(i)\) for every \(k\), so \(\tau=\sigma^j\). Therefore \(C_{S_p}(\sigma)=\langle\sigma\rangle=H\). Since \(H\) is generated by \(\sigma\), its centralizer is the same.
:::

<1>3. Now suppose \(H\le N\le S_p\) and \(N\) is nilpotent. The Sylow \(p\)-subgroup of \(N\) is exactly \(H\).
::: {.proof}
A \(p\)-subgroup of \(S_p\) has order dividing the \(p\)-part of \(p!\). Since \(v_p(p!)=1\), every such subgroup has order at most \(p\). Because \(H\le N\) already has order \(p\), it is a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup of \(N\). In a finite nilpotent group every Sylow subgroup is normal, so \(H\trianglelefteq N\).
:::

<1>4. Every Sylow \(q\)-subgroup of \(N\) with \(q\ne p\) is trivial.
::: {.proof}
Let \(Q\) be a Sylow \(q\)-subgroup of \(N\), \(q\ne p\). In a finite nilpotent group distinct Sylow subgroups commute elementwise, so \(Q\le C_N(H)\le C_{S_p}(H)=H\) by part (a). But \(|Q|\) is a power of \(q\) while \(|H|=p\), so \(Q=1\).
:::

<1>5. Therefore \(N=H\).
::: {.proof}
A finite nilpotent group is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups. By <1>3 its Sylow \(p\)-subgroup is \(H\), and by <1>4 all other Sylow subgroups are trivial.
:::
:::
