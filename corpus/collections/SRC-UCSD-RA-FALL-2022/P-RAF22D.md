::: problem
Let $(X, d)$ be a compact metric space.

1. State the definition of what it means for a metric space to be separable, and what it means for a topological space to be second countable.

2. Prove that $X$ is second countable.

3. Prove that $C(X, \mathbb{R})$ equipped with the uniform norm is separable.
   (Hint: Use (2) and the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem.)
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Recall the definitions.
::: proof
A metric space is **separable** if it contains a countable dense subset. A topological space is **second countable** if its topology has a countable base.
:::

<1>2. Construct a countable base for $X$.
::: proof
For every $n\ge1$, compactness gives a finite set
\[
D_n=\{x_{n,1},\dots,x_{n,N_n}\}\subset X
\]
such that the balls of radius $1/n$ centered at points of $D_n$ cover $X$. Put
\[
D:=\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty D_n.
\]
Then $D$ is countable and dense in $X$.

Consider
\[
\mathcal B:=\{B(q,r):q\in D,\ r\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\}.
\]
This family is countable. To see that it is a base, let $U$ be open and let $x\in U$. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ with
\[
B(x,\varepsilon)\subset U.
\]
Choose $q\in D$ with $d(x,q)<\varepsilon/4$, and then choose rational $r$ with
\[
d(x,q)<r<\varepsilon-d(x,q).
\]
Then
\[
x\in B(q,r)\subset B(x,\varepsilon)\subset U.
\]
Hence $X$ is second countable.
:::

<1>3. Build a countable dense subset of $C(X,\mathbb R)$.
::: proof
For each $q\in D$, define
\[
\rho_q(x):=d(x,q).
\]
Let $\mathcal A$ be the real algebra generated by the constants and the functions $\rho_q$, $q\in D$. It contains the constants. It also separates points: if $x\ne y$, choose $q\in D$ sufficiently close to $x$ that
\[
d(x,q)<\frac{d(x,y)}3.
\]
Then
\[
d(y,q)>\frac{2d(x,y)}3>d(x,q),
\]
so $\rho_q(x)\ne\rho_q(y)$.

By the real Stone--Weierstrass theorem, $\mathcal A$ is uniformly dense in $C(X,\mathbb R)$.

Now let $\mathcal A_{\mathbb Q}$ consist of all polynomials with rational coefficients in finitely many of the functions $\rho_q$. Since $D$ is countable, $\mathcal A_{\mathbb Q}$ is countable. Every element of $\mathcal A$ involves only finitely many real coefficients, which can be approximated by rational coefficients; since each $\rho_q$ is bounded on compact $X$, this approximation is uniform. Thus $\mathcal A_{\mathbb Q}$ is uniformly dense in $\mathcal A$, hence in $C(X,\mathbb R)$.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{C(X,\mathbb R)\text{ is separable in the uniform norm}.}
\]
:::
:::
