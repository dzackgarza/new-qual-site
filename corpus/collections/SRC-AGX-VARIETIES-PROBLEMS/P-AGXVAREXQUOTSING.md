::: {.problem}
Let $n>1$ and let
$$
\mu_n
=
\{\zeta\in\CC^\times:\zeta^n=1\}
$$
act on $\AA^2_\CC$ by
$$
\zeta\cdot(x,y)=(\zeta x,\zeta y).
$$
Define
$$
X
=
\operatorname{Specm}\CC[x,y]^{\mu_n}
=
\AA^2_\CC/\mu_n.
$$
Show that $X$ is a normal affine variety with a unique singular point.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
S=\CC[x,y],
\qquad
R=S^{\mu_n},
\qquad
X=\operatorname{Specm}R.
$$
For $0\leq i\leq n$, set
$$
z_i=x^{n-i}y^i.
$$

<1>1. The invariant ring is
$$
\boxed{
R
=
\CC[z_0,z_1,\ldots,z_n]
\subseteq
\CC[x,y].
}
$$
Moreover, $S$ is integral over $R$ and
$$
\dim X=2.
$$

::: {.proof}
For a monomial $x^ay^b$,
$$
\zeta\cdot x^ay^b
=
\zeta^{a+b}x^ay^b.
$$
Hence the monomial is $\mu_n$-invariant exactly when
$$
a+b\equiv0\pmod n.
$$
Because the monomials form a simultaneous eigenbasis for the scalar action,
an invariant polynomial is a linear combination of invariant monomials.

Every invariant monomial has total degree $qn$ for some $q\geq0$, and every
pair of nonnegative integers $(a,b)$ with $a+b=qn$ can be decomposed as a sum
of $q$ pairs whose coordinates sum to $n$. Thus every invariant monomial is a
product of degree-$n$ invariant monomials
$$
z_i=x^{n-i}y^i.
$$
This proves the displayed generating set.

Since
$$
x^n=z_0\in R,
\qquad
y^n=z_n\in R,
$$
the elements $x$ and $y$ satisfy the monic equations
$$
T^n-z_0=0,
\qquad
T^n-z_n=0
$$
over $R$. Hence $S$ is integral over $R$. Integral extensions preserve
Krull dimension, so
$$
\dim R=\dim S=2.
$$
:::

<1>2. The ring $R$ is normal.

::: {.proof}
Let
$$
K=\Frac R
$$
and suppose
$$
u\in K
$$
is integral over $R$. Since $R\subseteq S$, the same monic equation shows
that $u$ is integral over $S$. The polynomial ring $S=\CC[x,y]$ is a UFD,
hence integrally closed in its fraction field, so
$$
u\in S.
$$

But $u\in\Frac R$, and every element of $\Frac R$ is fixed by $\mu_n$ because
both numerator and denominator are invariant. Therefore
$$
u\in S^{\mu_n}=R.
$$
Thus $R$ is integrally closed in its fraction field and is normal.
:::

<1>3. Let
$$
\mfm=(z_0,z_1,\ldots,z_n)\subseteq R.
$$
Then $\mfm$ is the point $\bar0\in X$ which is the image of the origin, and
$$
X\setminus\{\bar0\}
=
D(z_0)\cup D(z_n).
$$

::: {.proof}
The quotient map
$$
\AA^2_\CC\longrightarrow X
$$
is induced by the inclusion $R\hookrightarrow S$. All positive-degree
invariants vanish at $(0,0)$, so the origin maps to $\mfm$.

Now let $\mathfrak p$ be a maximal ideal of $R$ containing both
$$
z_0=x^n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
z_n=y^n.
$$
For every $i$,
$$
z_i^n
=
(x^n)^{n-i}(y^n)^i
=
z_0^{\,n-i}z_n^{\,i}
\in\mathfrak p.
$$
Since $\mathfrak p$ is radical,
$$
z_i\in\mathfrak p
$$
for every $i$. Hence $\mathfrak p=\mfm$. Therefore the only point outside
$D(z_0)\cup D(z_n)$ is $\bar0$.
:::

<1>4. The open set $D(z_0)$ is smooth and has coordinate ring
$$
\boxed{
R_{z_0}
\cong
\CC[a,a^{-1},t],
\qquad
a=x^n,
\quad
t=\frac yx.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
In $R_{z_0}$ one has
$$
t
=
\frac{z_1}{z_0}
=
\frac{x^{n-1}y}{x^n}
=
\frac yx.
$$
Every generator satisfies
$$
z_i
=
x^{n-i}y^i
=
x^n\left(\frac yx\right)^i
=
a t^i.
$$
Thus $R_{z_0}$ is generated by
$$
a,\ a^{-1},\ t.
$$

These elements are algebraically independent except for the invertibility of
$a$: distinct Laurent monomials
$$
a^jt^i
$$
map to distinct monomials
$$
x^{nj-i}y^i
$$
in $\CC[x,x^{-1},y]$. Hence there is no further relation, giving
$$
R_{z_0}\cong\CC[a,a^{-1},t].
$$
This Laurent polynomial ring is regular, so $D(z_0)$ is smooth.
:::

<1>5. The open set $D(z_n)$ is smooth.

::: {.proof}
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ in step <1>4 gives
$$
R_{z_n}
\cong
\CC[b,b^{-1},s],
\qquad
b=y^n,
\quad
s=\frac xy.
$$
This is again a regular Laurent polynomial ring. Hence $D(z_n)$ is smooth.
:::

<1>6. The point $\bar0$ is singular, with
$$
\boxed{
\dim_\CC T_{\bar0}X=n+1.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The invariant ring inherits the usual grading from $\CC[x,y]$. Its positive
degrees are multiples of $n$, and the degree-$n$ piece has basis
$$
z_0,z_1,\ldots,z_n.
$$
The maximal ideal $\mfm$ consists of the positive-degree elements. Therefore
every element of $\mfm^2$ has degree at least $2n$. It follows that the
classes of
$$
z_0,\ldots,z_n
$$
are linearly independent in
$$
\mfm/\mfm^2,
$$
and step <1>1 shows that every invariant of degree $qn$ with $q\geq2$ is a
product of degree-$n$ invariants, hence lies in $\mfm^2$. Thus these classes
also generate $\mfm/\mfm^2$. Hence
$$
\dim_\CC\mfm/\mfm^2=n+1.
$$
Thus
$$
\dim_\CC T_{\bar0}X
=
\dim_\CC(\mfm/\mfm^2)^\vee
=
n+1.
$$

By step <1>1,
$$
\dim X=2,
$$
while $n+1>2$ because $n>1$. Therefore $\bar0$ is singular.
:::

<1>7. The quotient has exactly one singular point:
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Sing}(X)=\{\bar0\}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, every point other than $\bar0$ lies in $D(z_0)$ or $D(z_n)$.
Steps <1>4--<1>5 show that both opens are smooth. Step <1>6 shows that
$\bar0$ is singular. Hence it is the unique singular point.

Step <1>2 proves at the same time that $X$ is normal, so $(X,\bar0)$ is the
cyclic quotient singularity described in the source.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves normality, and step <1>7 proves uniqueness of the singular
point.
:::
