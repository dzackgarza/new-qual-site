::: {.problem}
With $\PP^n(\CC)$ given its classical topology, compute the integral singular
cohomology ring $H^*(\PP^n(\CC);\ZZ)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
X=\PP^n(\CC).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cohomology-n-zero}
If $n=0$, then
$$
H^*(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ\cong\ZZ[h]/(h).
$$

::: pf-proof
In this case
$$
X=\PP^0(\CC)
$$
is a point. Its integral cohomology is $\ZZ$ in degree $0$ and vanishes in
positive degrees, which is the displayed ring. Hence assume $n\geq1$ below.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cw-decomposition-even-cells}
The space $X$ has a CW decomposition with one cell in each real
dimension
$$
0,2,\ldots,2n
$$
and no cells in odd dimensions.

::: pf-proof
For $0\leq r\leq n$, let
$$
X_r
=
\{[z_0:\cdots:z_n]\in X:
z_{r+1}=\cdots=z_n=0\}.
$$
Then
$$
X_r\cong\PP^r(\CC),
\qquad
X_0\subset X_1\subset\cdots\subset X_n=X.
$$
Moreover,
$$
X_r\setminus X_{r-1}
=
\{[z_0:\cdots:z_r:0:\cdots:0]:z_r\neq0\}
\cong
\CC^r
\cong
\RR^{2r},
$$
where the affine coordinates are
$$
\left(\frac{z_0}{z_r},\ldots,\frac{z_{r-1}}{z_r}\right).
$$
Thus $X_r$ is obtained from $X_{r-1}$ by attaching one $2r$-cell, which
gives the asserted CW decomposition.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #additive-cohomology-groups}
Additively,
$$
H^q(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&q=0,2,\ldots,2n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#cw-decomposition-even-cells){.pf-ref}, the cellular cochain group is
$$
C^q_{\mathrm{cell}}(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&q=0,2,\ldots,2n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
Every cellular differential has either zero source or zero target because
there are no odd-dimensional cells. Hence all cellular differentials vanish,
so cellular cohomology gives exactly the displayed groups.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hyperplane-class-generates}
Let
$$
H\cong\PP^{n-1}(\CC)\subseteq X
$$
be a hyperplane and let
$$
h=\operatorname{PD}[H]\in H^2(X;\ZZ)
$$
be its Poincare dual. For every $0\leq r\leq n$,
$$
h^r
=
\operatorname{PD}[\PP^{n-r}(\CC)],
$$
and $h^r$ generates $H^{2r}(X;\ZZ)$.

::: pf-proof
Choose $r$ hyperplanes
$$
H_1,\ldots,H_r\subseteq X
$$
defined by linearly independent linear forms. They meet transversely, and
their intersection is a linear subspace
$$
H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_r
\cong
\PP^{n-r}(\CC).
$$
All hyperplanes represent the same homology class. For oriented transverse
submanifolds, cup product of Poincare duals is the Poincare dual of their
intersection. With the complex orientations on the hyperplanes and their
intersection, the intersection sign is positive. Therefore
$$
h^r
=
\operatorname{PD}[H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_r]
=
\operatorname{PD}[\PP^{n-r}(\CC)].
$$

The standard linear $\PP^{n-r}(\CC)$ is the closure of the unique
$2(n-r)$-cell in the filtration from step [](#cw-decomposition-even-cells){.pf-ref}, so its fundamental class
generates
$$
H_{2(n-r)}(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
$$
Poincare duality therefore shows that $h^r$ generates
$$
H^{2r}(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cohomology-ring-formula}
The cohomology ring is
$$
\boxed{
H^*(\PP^n(\CC);\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ[h]/(h^{n+1}),
\qquad
\deg h=2.
}
$$

::: pf-proof
For $n=0$, this is step [](#cohomology-n-zero){.pf-ref}. Assume $n\geq1$. By step [](#hyperplane-class-generates){.pf-ref}, the powers
$$
1,h,\ldots,h^n
$$
generate every nonzero cohomology group from step [](#additive-cohomology-groups){.pf-ref}. Since $X$ has real
dimension $2n$,
$$
H^{2n+2}(X;\ZZ)=0,
$$
so
$$
h^{n+1}=0.
$$
Hence the homomorphism of graded rings
$$
\ZZ[h]/(h^{n+1})
\longrightarrow
H^*(X;\ZZ)
$$
sending $h$ to the hyperplane class is surjective. In each even degree
$0,2,\ldots,2n$, both sides are free of rank one and the image of $h^r$ is
a generator by step [](#hyperplane-class-generates){.pf-ref}; both sides vanish in every other degree. Therefore
the homomorphism is an isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#cohomology-ring-formula){.pf-ref} gives the requested integral singular cohomology ring.
:::
