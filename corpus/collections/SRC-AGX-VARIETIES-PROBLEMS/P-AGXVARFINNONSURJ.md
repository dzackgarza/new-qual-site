::: {.problem}
Can a non-surjective finite morphism exist?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let
$$
i:\{0\}\longrightarrow\AA^1_k
$$
be the inclusion of the origin. Then $i$ is finite.

::: {.proof}
The comorphism is the quotient map
$$
i^*:k[t]\longrightarrow k,
\qquad
t\longmapsto0,
$$
or equivalently
$$
k[t]\longrightarrow k[t]/(t).
$$
As a $k[t]$-module,
$$
k[t]/(t)
$$
is generated by the class of $1$. Hence it is a finite $k[t]$-module, so
$i$ is finite by [[D-MORFIN|the standard definition]].
:::

<1>2. The finite morphism $i$ is not surjective.

::: {.proof}
Its image is
$$
i(\{0\})=\{0\}\subsetneq\AA^1_k.
$$
Thus a finite morphism need not be surjective when its comorphism is not
injective. In this example
$$
\dim\{0\}=0<1=\dim\AA^1,
$$
so there is no conflict with
[[P-AGXVARFINCLOSED|the equal-dimension surjectivity statement]].
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>2 give a non-surjective finite morphism.
:::
:::
