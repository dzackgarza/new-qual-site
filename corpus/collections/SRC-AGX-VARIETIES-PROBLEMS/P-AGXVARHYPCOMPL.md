::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]
$$
be nonconstant. Why is the complement
$$
\AA^n\sm V(f)
$$
an affine variety?

What is its coordinate ring?
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]
$$
and
$$
D(f)=\AA^n\sm V(f).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #y-affine-coord-ring}
The hypersurface
$$
Y
=
V(tf-1)
\subseteq
\AA^{n+1}_{x_1,\ldots,x_n,t}
$$
is an affine variety with coordinate ring
$$
\boxed{
\mco(Y)
\cong
A_f.
}
$$

::: pf-proof
The coordinate ring of $Y$ is
$$
\mco(Y)
=
A[t]/(tf-1).
$$
There is a homomorphism
$$
\Phi:A[t]/(tf-1)\longrightarrow A_f
$$
which is the identity on $A$ and sends
$$
t\longmapsto f^{-1}.
$$
It is well-defined because $f^{-1}f=1$, and it is surjective because $A_f$
is generated by $A$ and $f^{-1}$.

Conversely, in $A[t]/(tf-1)$ the image of $f$ is invertible with inverse
$t$. By the universal property of localization, the quotient map
$$
A\longrightarrow A[t]/(tf-1)
$$
extends uniquely to
$$
\Psi:A_f\longrightarrow A[t]/(tf-1).
$$
The maps $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are inverse because both fix $A$ and interchange
$t$ with $f^{-1}$. Hence
$$
A[t]/(tf-1)\cong A_f.
$$

Since $A$ is a domain, its localization $A_f$ is a domain. Thus $(tf-1)$ is
prime and $Y$ is an irreducible affine variety.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pi-isomorphism}
Projection onto the first $n$ coordinates restricts to an isomorphism
$$
\pi:Y\xrightarrow{\sim}D(f).
$$

::: pf-proof
If
$$
(a_1,\ldots,a_n,t)\in Y,
$$
then
$$
t\,f(a_1,\ldots,a_n)=1,
$$
so
$$
f(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\ne0.
$$
Hence $\pi(Y)\subseteq D(f)$.

Conversely, if
$$
a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in D(f),
$$
then
$$
\left(a_1,\ldots,a_n,\frac1{f(a)}\right)\in Y.
$$
Thus $\pi$ is bijective, with inverse
$$
a
\longmapsto
\left(a,\frac1{f(a)}\right).
$$
The projection is regular, and its inverse is regular on $D(f)$ because
$f$ is nowhere zero there. Therefore $\pi$ is an isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #complement-affine-coord-ring}
The hypersurface complement is affine and
$$
\boxed{
\mco\bigl(\AA^n\sm V(f)\bigr)
\cong
k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]_f.
}
$$

::: pf-proof
By step [](#pi-isomorphism){.pf-ref},
$$
D(f)\cong Y.
$$
Step [](#y-affine-coord-ring){.pf-ref} shows that $Y$ is affine and computes its coordinate ring as
$A_f$. Transporting this structure across the isomorphism gives the result;
see also [[PR-WZGOQ]].
:::
