::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and let
$$
X=V(xy-z^2)\subseteq\AA^3_k.
$$
Compute the divisor class group $\Cl(X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
A=k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2),
\qquad
X=\Spec A,
$$
and let
$$
P=V(x,z)\subseteq X.
$$

<1>1. The ring $A$ is a normal domain of dimension $2$, and $P$ is a prime
divisor on $X$.

::: {.proof}
The polynomial
$$
xy-z^2
$$
is irreducible, so $A$ is a domain. Since $A$ is a hypersurface in a
three-dimensional polynomial ring,
$$
\dim A=2,
$$
and $A$ is Cohen--Macaulay, hence satisfies Serre's condition $S_2$.

The partial derivatives of $xy-z^2$ are
$$
y,\qquad x,\qquad -2z.
$$
Because $\operatorname{char}k=0$, they vanish simultaneously only at the
vertex
$$
\mfm=(x,y,z).
$$
Thus the singular locus has codimension $2$ in $X$, so $A$ is regular in
codimension $1$, i.e. it satisfies $R_1$. Serre's criterion $R_1+S_2$ now
shows that $A$ is normal.

Finally,
$$
A/(x,z)\cong k[y],
$$
so $(x,z)$ is a height-one prime. Hence $P$ is a prime divisor.
:::

<1>2. The class group $\Cl(X)$ is generated by $[P]$.

::: {.proof}
On the distinguished open set
$$
D(x)\subseteq X,
$$
the relation $xy=z^2$ gives
$$
A_x
\cong
k[x,x^{-1},z],
\qquad
y=\frac{z^2}{x}.
$$
This is a UFD, so every Weil divisor on $D(x)$ is principal.

Let $D$ be any Weil divisor on $X$. Its restriction to $D(x)$ is therefore
the divisor of some rational function $g\in k(X)^\times$. Hence
$$
D-\div_X(g)
$$
is supported on
$$
V(x).
$$
But
$$
\sqrt{(x)}=(x,z),
$$
so $P$ is the only prime divisor contained in $V(x)$. Consequently
$$
D\sim nP
$$
for some $n\in\ZZ$. Thus $[P]$ generates $\Cl(X)$.
:::

<1>3. The principal divisor of $x$ is
$$
\boxed{\div_X(x)=2P.}
$$

::: {.proof}
At the generic point of $P$, the element $y$ is a unit. Localizing first at
$y$ eliminates $x$:
$$
A_y
\cong
k[y,y^{-1},z],
\qquad
x=\frac{z^2}{y}.
$$
The prime $P$ becomes the principal prime $(z)$ in this ring. Hence $z$ is a
uniformizer at $P$, so
$$
\operatorname{ord}_P(z)=1
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{ord}_P(x)
=
\operatorname{ord}_P(z^2/y)
=2.
$$
Step <1>2 showed that $P$ is the only prime divisor in $V(x)$, so $x$ has no
other divisorial zeros. Therefore $\div_X(x)=2P$.
:::

<1>4. The divisor $P$ is not Cartier at the vertex
$$
\mfm=(x,y,z).
$$

::: {.proof}
The ideal of $P$ is
$$
I=(x,z)\subseteq A.
$$
If $P$ were Cartier at the vertex, the localized ideal $I_\mfm$ would be
principal.

The ring $A$ is standard graded with
$$
\deg x=\deg y=\deg z=1.
$$
Every element of $\mfm I$ has degree at least $2$. Hence the degree-one
classes of $x$ and $z$ are linearly independent in
$$
I/\mfm I,
$$
and they span it. Thus
$$
\dim_k I/\mfm I=2.
$$
By Nakayama's lemma, $I_\mfm$ needs at least two generators and is not
principal. Therefore $P$ is not Cartier at the vertex.
:::

<1>5. The class $[P]$ is nonzero and has order exactly $2$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives
$$
2[P]=0
$$
because $2P$ is principal.

If $[P]=0$, then $P$ would be a principal divisor. Every principal divisor is
Cartier, contradicting step <1>4. Hence
$$
[P]\neq0.
$$
Therefore $[P]$ has order exactly $2$.
:::

<1>6. Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\Cl(X)\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ,
}
$$
generated by the class of the ruling $P=V(x,z)$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $[P]$ generates the class group. By step <1>5, it has order
exactly $2$. This gives the displayed isomorphism.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 is the requested class-group computation.
