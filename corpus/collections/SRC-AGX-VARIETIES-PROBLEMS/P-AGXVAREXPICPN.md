::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let
$n\geq1$. Compute $\Pic(\PP^n\slice{k})$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
X=\PP^n_k
$$
and let
$$
H=V(x_0)\subseteq X
$$
be a hyperplane.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #pic-equals-cl}
Since $X$ is smooth,
$$
\Pic(X)\cong\Cl(X).
$$

::: pf-proof
Projective space is smooth over $k$. Hence every local ring of $X$ is regular
and every regular local ring is a UFD. Thus $X$ is locally factorial, every
Weil divisor is Cartier, and the natural map from Cartier divisor classes to
Weil divisor classes is an isomorphism:
$$
\Pic(X)\cong\Cl(X).
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Every prime divisor $D\subseteq X$ is a hypersurface
$$
D=V(F)
$$
for an irreducible homogeneous polynomial
$$
F\in k[x_0,\ldots,x_n].
$$

::: pf-proof
A prime divisor has codimension one. Its homogeneous ideal
$$
I(D)\subseteq k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]
$$
is therefore a homogeneous prime ideal of height one. The polynomial ring is
a UFD, so every height-one prime ideal is principal:
$$
I(D)=(F)
$$
for an irreducible polynomial $F$. Since the ideal is homogeneous, $F$ may
be chosen homogeneous.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #divisor-equiv-dh}
If
$$
D=V(F)
$$
with
$$
d=\deg F,
$$
then
$$
\boxed{D\sim dH.}
$$

::: pf-proof
The rational function
$$
\frac{F}{x_0^d}
$$
on projective space is well defined because numerator and denominator are
homogeneous of the same degree. Its divisor is
$$
\div\left(\frac{F}{x_0^d}\right)
=
D-dH.
$$
Thus $D-dH$ is principal, so
$$
D\sim dH.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #h-generates-cl}
The class $[H]$ generates $\Cl(X)$.

::: pf-proof
Every Weil divisor is a finite integral linear combination of prime divisors.
By step [](#divisor-equiv-dh){.pf-ref}, each prime divisor is linearly equivalent to an integral
multiple of $H$. Hence every divisor class is an integral multiple of
$[H]$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #degree-homomorphism}
Degree defines a homomorphism
$$
\deg:\Cl(X)\longrightarrow\ZZ
$$
satisfying
$$
\deg[H]=1.
$$

::: pf-proof
For a prime divisor
$$
D=V(F),
$$
define
$$
\deg D=\deg F,
$$
and extend additively to all Weil divisors.

It remains to check that principal divisors have degree zero. Let
$$
r\in k(X)^\times.
$$
Write
$$
r=\frac{F}{G}
$$
with $F$ and $G$ homogeneous of the same degree $d$. Factoring them into
irreducible homogeneous polynomials gives
$$
\deg\div(F)=d
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\deg\div(G)=d.
$$
Therefore
$$
\deg\div(r)
=
\deg\div(F)-\deg\div(G)
=0.
$$
So degree descends to $\Cl(X)$.

Since $H=V(x_0)$ is a degree-one hypersurface,
$$
\deg[H]=1.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cl-is-z}
The divisor class group is
$$
\boxed{
\Cl(\PP^n_k)\cong\ZZ,
}
$$
generated by $[H]$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#h-generates-cl){.pf-ref}, the homomorphism
$$
\ZZ\longrightarrow\Cl(X),
\qquad
m\longmapsto m[H]
$$
is surjective. By step [](#degree-homomorphism){.pf-ref}, composing it with degree gives the identity on
$\ZZ$. Hence the homomorphism is also injective and therefore an
isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pic-is-z}
Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\Pic(\PP^n_k)
\cong
\ZZ,
\qquad
1\longleftrightarrow\OO_{\PP^n}(1).
}
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#pic-equals-cl){.pf-ref} identifies $\Pic(X)$ with $\Cl(X)$, and step [](#cl-is-z){.pf-ref} identifies the
latter with $\ZZ$ generated by the hyperplane class. The line bundle
associated with the hyperplane divisor is
$$
\OO_{\PP^n}(H)=\OO_{\PP^n}(1).
$$
Thus every line bundle on $\PP^n_k$ is uniquely of the form
$$
\OO_{\PP^n}(m),
\qquad
m\in\ZZ.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#pic-is-z){.pf-ref} is the requested Picard-group computation.
