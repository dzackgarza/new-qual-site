::: {.problem}
Show that $\Aut \PP^n\slice{k} = \PGL_{n+1}\slice{k} = \GL_{n+1}\slice{k} /\GG_m$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume $n\ge1$ and write
$$
V=k^{n+1},
\qquad
\PP^n_k=\PP(V).
$$
For $n=0$ both sides are the trivial group.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #gl-acts-on-pn}
Every element of $\GL(V)$ induces an automorphism of $\PP(V)$.

::: pf-proof
For
$$
A\in\GL(V),
$$
define
$$
\overline A:\PP(V)\longrightarrow\PP(V),
\qquad
[v]\longmapsto[Av].
$$
This is a projective linear morphism, and
$$
\overline{A^{-1}}
$$
is its inverse. Thus there is a group homomorphism
$$
\GL(V)\longrightarrow\Aut_k(\PP(V)).
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #kernel-is-scalars}
The kernel of
$$
\GL(V)\longrightarrow\Aut_k(\PP(V))
$$
is the scalar subgroup
$$
k^\times I\cong\GG_m(k).
$$

::: pf-proof
Every scalar matrix acts trivially on one-dimensional subspaces, so
$$
k^\times I
$$
lies in the kernel.

Conversely, suppose $A\in\GL(V)$ acts trivially on $\PP(V)$. Then every
nonzero vector spans an $A$-stable line. For a basis
$$
e_0,\ldots,e_n
$$
write
$$
Ae_i=\lambda_i e_i.
$$
Since the line spanned by $e_i+e_j$ is also fixed,
$$
\lambda_i e_i+\lambda_j e_j
$$
must be a scalar multiple of
$$
e_i+e_j.
$$
Hence
$$
\lambda_i=\lambda_j
$$
for every $i,j$. Thus $A=\lambda I$ is scalar.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pgl-injects}
Therefore the projective linear action gives an injective
homomorphism
$$
\PGL(V)=\GL(V)/k^\times
\injects
\Aut_k(\PP(V)).
$$

::: pf-proof
This is the first isomorphism theorem applied to steps [](#gl-acts-on-pn){.pf-ref} and [](#kernel-is-scalars){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pullback-o1-trivial}
Let
$$
f\in\Aut_k(\PP^n).
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{f^*\OO_{\PP^n}(1)\cong\OO_{\PP^n}(1)}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The Picard group is
$$
\Pic(\PP^n_k)\cong\ZZ,
$$
generated by $\OO(1)$ [[D-CB9XS]]. Since $f$ is an isomorphism,
$$
f^*:\Pic(\PP^n)\longrightarrow\Pic(\PP^n)
$$
is a group automorphism. Therefore
$$
f^*\OO(1)\cong\OO(\varepsilon),
\qquad
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}.
$$

The pullback of an ample line bundle by an isomorphism is ample.
The bundle $\OO(1)$ is ample, whereas $\OO(-1)$ is not ample. Hence
$$
\varepsilon=1.
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #invertible-linear-map-t}
After choosing an isomorphism
$$
\alpha:f^*\OO(1)\xrightarrow{\sim}\OO(1),
$$
pullback of sections gives an invertible linear map
$$
T:
H^0(\PP^n,\OO(1))
\longrightarrow
H^0(\PP^n,\OO(1)).
$$

::: pf-proof
Pullback along $f$ gives an isomorphism
$$
H^0(\PP^n,\OO(1))
\xrightarrow{\sim}
H^0(\PP^n,f^*\OO(1)).
$$
Composing with the isomorphism on global sections induced by $\alpha$
gives $T$. Both maps are invertible, so $T$ is an invertible
$k$-linear transformation.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #f-is-projective-linear}
The automorphism $f$ is induced by an element of $\GL_{n+1}(k)$.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
x_0,\ldots,x_n
$$
be the standard basis of
$$
H^0(\PP^n,\OO(1)).
$$
Put
$$
\ell_i=T(x_i).
$$
Since $T$ is invertible, the $\ell_i$ form a basis of the space of linear
forms. Hence there is an invertible matrix
$$
A\in\GL_{n+1}(k)
$$
whose coordinate linear forms are $\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_n$.

The morphism defined by this basis is
$$
\overline A:
[v]\longmapsto
[\ell_0(v):\cdots:\ell_n(v)].
$$
For each homogeneous coordinate section $x_i$, its pullback along $f$,
after the chosen identification $\alpha$, is exactly $\ell_i$. On the
open set where $\ell_j\ne0$, both $f$ and $\overline A$ land in the
standard chart $D_+(x_j)$, and the pullback of its affine coordinate
$x_i/x_j$ is
$$
\frac{\ell_i}{\ell_j}
$$
for both maps. The opens $\{\ell_j\ne0\}$ cover $\PP^n$ because the
$\ell_j$ form a basis and hence have no common projective zero. Therefore
the two morphisms agree on an open cover:
$$
f=\overline A.
$$
Therefore every automorphism of $\PP^n$ is projective linear.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equivalence-statement}
Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\Aut_k(\PP^n)
\cong
\PGL_{n+1}(k)
\cong
\GL_{n+1}(k)/k^\times.
}
$$

::: pf-proof
Step [](#pgl-injects){.pf-ref} gives injectivity of the projective linear action, and step
[](#f-is-projective-linear){.pf-ref} gives surjectivity. The quotient description is the definition of
$\PGL_{n+1}(k)$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#equivalence-statement){.pf-ref} is the required identification.
:::
