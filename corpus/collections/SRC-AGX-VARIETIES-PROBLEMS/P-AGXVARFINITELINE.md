::: {.problem}
Show that any non-constant morphism $\AA^1\to \AA^1$ or
$\AA^1\smz\to \AA^1\smz$ is finite.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $k$ be the algebraically closed ground field of the source. We use the
standard definition [[D-MORFIN|of a finite morphism]].

<1>1. Every non-constant morphism
$$
f:\AA^1\longrightarrow\AA^1
$$
is finite.

::: {.proof}
Choose coordinates $t$ on the target and $x$ on the source. The comorphism is
$$
f^*:k[t]\longrightarrow k[x],
\qquad
t\longmapsto p(x)
$$
for some polynomial
$$
p(x)=a_dx^d+a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0.
$$
Non-constancy means $d\geq1$ and $a_d\ne0$.

In the $k[t]$-algebra $k[x]$, the element $x$ satisfies the monic equation
$$
T^d
+\frac{a_{d-1}}{a_d}T^{d-1}
+\cdots
+\frac{a_1}{a_d}T
+\frac{a_0-t}{a_d}
=0,
$$
because substituting $T=x$ and $t=p(x)$ gives zero. Thus $x$ is integral over
the image $k[p(x)]$.

Since
$$
k[x]=k[p(x)][x],
$$
the ring $k[x]$ is generated as a $k[p(x)]$-module by
$$
1,x,\ldots,x^{d-1}.
$$
Hence $f$ is finite.
:::

<1>2. The units of the Laurent polynomial ring are exactly
$$
k[x,x^{-1}]^\times
=
\{c x^n:c\in k^\times,\ n\in\ZZ\}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For a nonzero Laurent polynomial
$$
u=\sum_{i=r}^s a_ix^i,
\qquad
a_r,a_s\ne0,
$$
define its width by
$$
w(u)=s-r.
$$
If $u,v\ne0$, the lowest and highest nonzero terms of $uv$ are obtained by
multiplying the corresponding lowest and highest terms, so
$$
w(uv)=w(u)+w(v).
$$

If $u$ is a unit, choose $v$ with $uv=1$. Then
$$
0=w(1)=w(u)+w(v),
$$
and both widths are nonnegative. Hence $w(u)=0$, so $u$ has exactly one
nonzero term:
$$
u=cx^n.
$$
Conversely every such Laurent monomial is a unit.
:::

<1>3. Every non-constant morphism
$$
g:\AA^1\smz\longrightarrow\AA^1\smz
$$
is finite.

::: {.proof}
The punctured affine line has coordinate ring
$$
k[x,x^{-1}].
$$
A morphism to $\AA^1\smz$ is determined by the image of the target coordinate
$t$, which must be a unit. By step <1>2,
$$
g^*(t)=c x^n
$$
for some $c\in k^\times$ and $n\in\ZZ$.

The morphism is constant exactly when $n=0$, so non-constancy gives
$$
r:=|n|\geq1.
$$
The image of the target coordinate ring is
$$
k[cx^n,c^{-1}x^{-n}]
=
k[x^r,x^{-r}].
$$
Put
$$
R=k[x^r,x^{-r}].
$$
Every Laurent monomial $x^m$ can be written uniquely as
$$
x^m=(x^r)^q x^i
$$
with $q\in\ZZ$ and $0\leq i<r$. Therefore
$$
k[x,x^{-1}]
=
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}R x^i.
$$
Thus $k[x,x^{-1}]$ is a finite, indeed free, $R$-module of rank $r$. Hence
$g$ is finite.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves the affine-line case, and step <1>3 proves the punctured-line
case after the unit classification in step <1>2.
:::
