::: {.problem}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Classify the
nonempty irreducible Zariski-closed subvarieties of
$$
\AA^2_k.
$$
Show that they are exactly:

- $\AA^2_k$ itself;
- irreducible plane curves $V(f)$ for irreducible nonconstant
  $f\in k[x,y]$;
- points.

Show moreover that every irreducible plane curve $V(f)$ has infinitely many
$k$-points.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
$$
A=k[x,y].
$$
Irreducible Zariski-closed subsets of $\AA^2_k$ correspond to prime ideals
of $A$.

<1>1. The prime ideal $(0)$ corresponds to the whole affine plane
$$
V(0)=\AA^2_k.
$$

::: {.proof}
The ring $A=k[x,y]$ is a domain, so $(0)$ is prime. Its zero set is all of
$\AA^2_k$.
:::

<1>2. Every height-one prime ideal of $A$ has the form
$$
(f)
$$
for an irreducible nonconstant polynomial $f\in k[x,y]$.

::: {.proof}
The polynomial ring
$$
k[x,y]
$$
is a UFD. In a Noetherian UFD, every height-one prime ideal is principal and
generated by an irreducible element [[PR-VARHT]].

Thus every height-one prime is
$$
\mathfrak p=(f)
$$
with $f$ irreducible and nonconstant. Conversely, an irreducible polynomial
is prime in the UFD $A$, so $(f)$ is prime; Krull's principal ideal theorem
gives it height one.
:::

<1>3. The height-one primes correspond exactly to irreducible plane curves
$$
V(f)\subseteq\AA^2_k.
$$

::: {.proof}
If $\mathfrak p=(f)$ is a height-one prime as in step <1>2, then
$$
A/\mathfrak p
=
k[x,y]/(f)
$$
is a domain, so $V(f)$ is irreducible. The affine dimension formula gives
$$
\dim V(f)
=
\dim A-\height(f)
=
2-1
=
1.
$$
Thus it is a curve.

Conversely, every irreducible closed curve in $\AA^2_k$ has a prime
vanishing ideal of height one, hence is of this form by step <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Every height-two prime ideal of $A$ is the maximal ideal of a point:
$$
\mathfrak m_{(a,b)}
=
(x-a,y-b).
$$

::: {.proof}
The ring $A$ has Krull dimension $2$. A prime ideal of height $2$ is
therefore maximal. By the weak Nullstellensatz over the algebraically closed
field $k$, every maximal ideal of $k[x,y]$ is
$$
(x-a,y-b)
$$
for a unique point
$$
(a,b)\in\AA^2_k.
$$
Its zero set is the singleton
$$
\{(a,b)\}.
$$
:::

<1>5. There are no other nonempty irreducible closed subvarieties of
$\AA^2_k$.

::: {.proof}
Every prime ideal of the two-dimensional domain $A$ has height
$$
0,\ 1,\ \text{or }2.
$$
Step <1>1 classifies height zero, steps <1>2--<1>3 classify height one, and
step <1>4 classifies height two. These exhaust all prime ideals and hence all
nonempty irreducible closed subvarieties.

An irreducible topological space is nonempty by definition, and
$$
\varnothing=V(1)
$$
corresponds to the unit ideal, which is not prime.
:::

<1>6. Every irreducible plane curve
$$
C=V(f)
$$
has infinitely many $k$-points.

::: {.proof}
Write $f$ as a polynomial in $y$:
$$
f(x,y)
=
a_r(x)y^r+\cdots+a_0(x),
\qquad
a_r\ne0.
$$

If $r=0$, then $f\in k[x]$. Since $k$ is algebraically closed and $f$ is
irreducible nonconstant, $f$ is linear:
$$
f=c(x-a).
$$
Then
$$
C=\{a\}\cross\AA^1_k,
$$
which has infinitely many $k$-points because $k$ is infinite.

Suppose $r>0$. The nonzero polynomial $a_r(x)$ has only finitely many roots.
Hence there are infinitely many
$$
c\in k
$$
with
$$
a_r(c)\ne0.
$$
For each such $c$, the specialization
$$
f(c,y)\in k[y]
$$
has degree $r>0$. Since $k$ is algebraically closed, it has a root
$$
b_c\in k.
$$
Thus
$$
(c,b_c)\in C.
$$
Distinct values of $c$ give distinct points, so $C(k)$ is infinite.
:::

<1>7. Therefore the complete classification is
$$
\boxed{
\AA^2_k,\quad
V(f)\text{ for irreducible nonconstant }f,\quad
\text{and points}.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 gives the exhaustive classification, and step <1>6 proves the
additional cardinality assertion for the curve case.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>7 is the required conclusion.
:::
