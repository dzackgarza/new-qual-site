::: {.problem}
Show that $2 \ZZ \cup 3 \ZZ$ is not a subring of $\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the union fails closure under addition, which a subring must have.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #members}

$2\in2\ZZ\cup3\ZZ$ and $3\in2\ZZ\cup3\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

$2=2\cdot1\in2\ZZ$ and $3=3\cdot1\in3\ZZ$, so each lies in one of the two sets and hence in their union.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sum}

$2+3=5\notin2\ZZ\cup3\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

An element of $2\ZZ$ is even and an element of $3\ZZ$ is divisible by $3$; $5$ is neither even nor divisible by $3$, and the only multiple of $6$ in $\{2,3\}$ — the intersection $2\ZZ\cap3\ZZ=6\ZZ$ — is not $5$. So $5$ lies in neither set.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$2\ZZ\cup3\ZZ$ is not a subring of $\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

A subring is closed under addition, but steps [](#members){.pf-ref} and [](#sum){.pf-ref} exhibit two elements of the set whose sum is outside it:
$$
\boxed{2,3\in2\ZZ\cup3\ZZ,\quad 2+3=5\notin2\ZZ\cup3\ZZ.}
$$

:::
