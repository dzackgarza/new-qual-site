::: {.problem}
Let $R = \mathbb { Z } \oplus \mathbb { Z } \oplus \mathbb { Z }$ and $S = \{ ( a , b , c ) \in R | a + b = c \}$ . Prove or disprove that S is a subring of R.
:::

::: {.solution}
First, it is clear that S is contained in R and that S is not empty (S contains $( 0 , 0 , 0 )$ ). Let $x = ( a , b , c )$ and $\boldsymbol { y } = ( d , f , g )$ be elements of S. Then $x - y = ( a , b , c ) - ( d , f , g ) =$ $( a - d , b - f , c - g )$ . Since $x , y \in S , a + b = c$ and $d + f = g$ . Now, $( a - d ) + ( b - f ) =$ $( a + b ) - ( d + f ) = c - g$ so $x - y \in S$ . Now, consider the condition for $x y = ( a d , b f , c g )$ We have that $c g = ( a + b ) ( d + f ) = a d + b f + a f + b d$ so $x y$ is in S only if $a f + b d = 0$ . But this equality is not always true.
For example, let $x = ( 1 , 2 , 3 )$ and $y = ( 4 , 5 , 9 )$ . Then $x y = ( 4 , 1 0 , 2 7 )$ but $4 + 1 0 \neq 2 7$ . Hence we can not say that $x y \in S$ . Thus, S is not a subring of R.
:::
