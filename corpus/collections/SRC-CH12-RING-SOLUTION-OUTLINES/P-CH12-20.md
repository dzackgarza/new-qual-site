::: {.problem}
Describe the elements of $M_{2}(\ZZ)$ that have multiplicative inverses.
:::

::: {.solution}

The exercise is Gallian, *Contemporary Abstract Algebra* (7th ed.), Chapter 12 exercise 20,
printed p. 243, and is item 20 of the retained Chapter 12 solution outline
(`assets/attachments/Ch12Sltns.pdf`, PDF p. 3). Its answer is kept; both implications are
proved below.

Write $M_{2}(\ZZ)$ for the ring of $2\times2$ matrices with integer entries under the usual
sum and product, and for $A=\matt{a}{b}{c}{d}$ put $\det(A)=ad-bc$ and
$\operatorname{adj}(A)=\matt{d}{-b}{-c}{a}$. A matrix $A$ has a multiplicative inverse in
$M_{2}(\ZZ)$ when there is $B\in M_{2}(\ZZ)$ with $AB=BA=I_{2}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #adjugate}

$\operatorname{adj}(A)\in M_{2}(\ZZ)$ and
$A\operatorname{adj}(A)=\operatorname{adj}(A)A=\det(A)I_{2}$.

::: pf-proof

The entries of $\operatorname{adj}(A)$ are $d,-b,-c,a$, which are integers, so
$\operatorname{adj}(A)\in M_{2}(\ZZ)$. Multiplying out,
$A\operatorname{adj}(A)=\matt{ad-bc}{-ab+ba}{cd-dc}{-bc+ad}=\det(A)I_{2}$ and
$\operatorname{adj}(A)A=\matt{da-bc}{db-bd}{-ca+ac}{-cb+da}=\det(A)I_{2}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #necessary}

If $A$ has a multiplicative inverse in $M_{2}(\ZZ)$, then $\det(A)=\pm1$.

::: pf-proof

Let $B\in M_{2}(\ZZ)$ with $AB=I_{2}$. The determinant is multiplicative, so
$\det(A)\det(B)=\det(AB)=\det(I_{2})=1$. Both $\det(A)$ and $\det(B)$ are integers, and two
integers with product $1$ are both $1$ or both $-1$; in particular $\det(A)=\pm1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sufficient}

If $\det(A)=\pm1$, then $A$ has a multiplicative inverse in $M_{2}(\ZZ)$, namely
$\det(A)\operatorname{adj}(A)$.

::: pf-proof

Since $\det(A)=\pm1$ is its own inverse and $\operatorname{adj}(A)$ has integer entries, the
matrix $B=\det(A)\operatorname{adj}(A)$ lies in $M_{2}(\ZZ)$. By step [](#adjugate){.pf-ref},
$AB=\det(A)\det(A)I_{2}=\det(A)^{2}I_{2}=I_{2}$ and $BA=I_{2}$ likewise, since
$(\pm1)^{2}=1$. So $B$ is a two-sided inverse of $A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #description}

The elements of $M_{2}(\ZZ)$ with a multiplicative inverse are exactly the matrices
$\matt{a}{b}{c}{d}$ with $ad-bc=\pm1$; they are closed under multiplication and form a group.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#necessary){.pf-ref} and [](#sufficient){.pf-ref} give the two implications. If
$A,A'$ have determinant $\pm1$ then $\det(AA')=\det(A)\det(A')=\pm1$ and also
$\det(I_{2})=1$, so the set is closed under multiplication and contains the identity; each
of its members has its inverse in the set by step [](#sufficient){.pf-ref}, so it is a group
of units.

:::
