::: {.problem}
Suppose that $R$ is a ring such that $x^{3} = x$ for all $x$ in $R$. Prove that $6x = 0$ for all $x$ in $R$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is that of the retained solution outline for Chapter 12, Exercise 33, PDF p. 2 of `assets/attachments/Ch12Sltns.pdf`, restructured into numbered steps. Here $R$ is a ring, not assumed commutative or unital, with $x^{3}=x$ for every $x\in R$; for $n\in\ZZ$ the notation $nx$ means the $n$-fold sum of $x$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cube}

$(2x)^{3}=8x^{3}$ for every $x\in R$.

::: pf-proof

$2x=x+x$, so $(2x)^{3}=(x+x)(x+x)(x+x)$; expanding and using that powers of the single element $x$ commute with each other gives $8$ copies of $x^{3}$. The integer $8$ is central, so no commutativity of $R$ is used.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #hyp}

$(2x)^{3}=2x$ and $8x^{3}=8x$.

::: pf-proof

The first is the hypothesis $y^{3}=y$ applied to $y=2x$; the second is $x^{3}=x$ multiplied by the integer $8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$2x=8x$, hence $6x=0$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#cube){.pf-ref} and [](#hyp){.pf-ref} give $2x=(2x)^{3}=8x^{3}=8x$; subtracting $2x$ from both sides, that is adding $-(2x)$, gives $0=6x$.

:::
