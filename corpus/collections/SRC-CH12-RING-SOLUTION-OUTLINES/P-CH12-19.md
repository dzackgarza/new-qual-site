::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a ring.
The center of $R$ is the set $\{ x \in R \mid ax = xa \text{ for all } a \in R \}$. Prove that the center of a ring is a subring.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the retained solution outline for this chapter, **item 19, PDF p. 1** (`assets/attachments/Ch12Sltns.pdf`), restructured into steps.

Throughout, $R$ is a ring and $Z(R)=\{x\in R : ax=xa \text{ for all } a\in R\}$ is its center.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nonempty}
$Z(R)$ is nonempty: $0\in Z(R)$.

::: pf-proof
By the rules of multiplication in a ring (Theorem 12.1), $a\cdot0=0$ and $0\cdot a=0$ for every $a\in R$. Hence $a0=0=0a$ for every $a$, which says $0\in Z(R)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subtraction}
If $a,b\in Z(R)$ then $a-b\in Z(R)$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x\in R$. Using distributivity and the definition of the difference, $(a-b)x=ax-bx$. Since $a$ and $b$ lie in the center, $ax=xa$ and $bx=xb$, so $ax-bx=xa-xb$. By distributivity again, $xa-xb=x(a-b)$. Chaining, $(a-b)x=x(a-b)$; as $x$ was arbitrary, $a-b$ commutes with every element of $R$, that is $a-b\in Z(R)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiplication}
If $a,b\in Z(R)$ then $ab\in Z(R)$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x\in R$. Using associativity of multiplication and the centrality of $b$, then of $a$,
$(ab)x=a(bx)=a(xb)=(ax)b=(xa)b$.
By associativity again, $(xa)b=x(ab)$. Hence $(ab)x=x(ab)$ for every $x\in R$, that is $ab\in Z(R)$. The computation uses both hypotheses: $bx=xb$ and $ax=xa$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subring}
$Z(R)$ is a subring of $R$.

::: pf-proof
$Z(R)$ is a nonempty subset of $R$ by step [](#nonempty){.pf-ref}, closed under subtraction by step [](#subtraction){.pf-ref} and under multiplication by step [](#multiplication){.pf-ref}. The Subring Test (Theorem 12.3) then says $Z(R)$ is a subring of $R$: those two closures make it an abelian group under addition, and multiplication in $R$ restricts to a binary operation on it, associative and distributive because it is so in $R$. So the center of a ring is a subring.
:::
