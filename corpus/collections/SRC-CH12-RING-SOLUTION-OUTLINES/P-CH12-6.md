::: {.problem}
Find an integer $n$ that shows that the rings $\mathbb{Z}_n$ need not have the following properties that the ring of integers has.
Then answer: Is the $n$ you found prime?

(a) $a^2 = a$ implies $a = 0$ or $a = 1$.

(b) $ab = 0$ implies $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.

(c) $ab = ac$ and $a \neq 0$ implies $b = c$.
:::

::: {.solution}
(a) Let n = 6. Then $3 ^ { 2 } = 3$ but 3 is neither 0 nor 1.

(b) Let n = 6. Then $2 \cdot 3 = 0$ but $2 \neq 0$ and $3 \neq 0$ .

(c) Let n = 6. $3 \cdot 2 = 0 = 3 \cdot 4$ but $3 \neq 0$ and $2 \neq 4 .$
:::
