::: {.problem}
Find an integer $n$ that shows that the rings $\ZZ_n$ need not have the following properties that the ring of integers has.
Then answer: Is the $n$ you found prime?

(a) $a^2 = a$ implies $a = 0$ or $a = 1$.

(b) $ab = 0$ implies $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.

(c) $ab = ac$ and $a \neq 0$ implies $b = c$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: $n=6$ is composite, and in $\ZZ_{6}$ each of the three integral-domain properties fails by an explicit computation.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a}

(a) In $\ZZ_{6}$, $3^{2}=3$ while $3\ne0$ and $3\ne1$.

::: pf-proof

$3\cdot3=9\equiv3\pmod 6$. The residue $3$ is neither the zero class nor the class of $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}

(b) In $\ZZ_{6}$, $2\cdot3=0$ while $2\ne0$ and $3\ne0$.

::: pf-proof

$2\cdot3=6\equiv0\pmod6$, and neither factor is divisible by $6$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c}

(c) In $\ZZ_{6}$, $3\cdot2=3\cdot4$ and $3\ne0$ while $2\ne4$.

::: pf-proof

$3\cdot2=6\equiv0$ and $3\cdot4=12\equiv0\pmod6$, so the two products are equal and both vanish; the multiplier $3$ is not the zero class, and the classes $2$ and $4$ are different since $4-2=2\not\equiv0\pmod6$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #prime}

The integer $n=6$ is not prime.

::: pf-proof

$6=2\cdot3$ with $1<2,3<6$; equivalently $\ZZ_{6}$ has zero divisors by step [](#b){.pf-ref}, whereas $\ZZ_{p}$ for prime $p$ is a field and has none:
$$
\boxed{n=6\ \text{is composite, so } \ZZ_{6}\ \text{fails all three properties.}}
$$

:::
