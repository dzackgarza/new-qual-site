::: {.problem}
Let $R = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} a & a \\ b & b \end{bmatrix} \,\st\, a, b \in \ZZ \right\}$.
Prove or disprove that $R$ is a subring of $M_2(\ZZ)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection retains its solution outline as `assets/attachments/Ch12Sltns.pdf`, and entry "# 42" of that outline covers Gallian 7e Chapter 12 Exercise 42. The outline checks nonemptiness, closure under subtraction, and closure under multiplication. Those computations are retained below and reorganized under the subring test.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nonempty}
The set $R$ is a nonempty subset of $M_2(\ZZ)$.

::: pf-proof
Every displayed matrix has integer entries, so $R\subseteq M_2(\ZZ)$. Taking $a=b=0$ gives the zero matrix, which therefore belongs to $R$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #closure}
For every $A,C\in R$, both $A-C$ and $AC$ belong to $R$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #subtraction}
The difference $A-C$ belongs to $R$.

::: pf-proof
Write $A=\begin{bmatrix}a&a\\b&b\end{bmatrix}$ and $C=\begin{bmatrix}c&c\\d&d\end{bmatrix}$ with $a,b,c,d\in\ZZ$. Matrix subtraction gives
$$
A-C=\begin{bmatrix}a-c&a-c\\b-d&b-d\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Since $a-c,b-d\in\ZZ$ and the two columns are equal, $A-C\in R$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiplication}
The product $AC$ belongs to $R$.

::: pf-proof
With the same notation as in step [](#subtraction){.pf-ref}, matrix multiplication gives
$$
AC=\begin{bmatrix}ac+ad&ac+ad\\bc+bd&bc+bd\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}a(c+d)&a(c+d)\\b(c+d)&b(c+d)\end{bmatrix}.
$$
The integers $a(c+d)$ and $b(c+d)$ supply the two row parameters in the definition of $R$, and the columns are equal. Hence $AC\in R$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\boxed{R\text{ is a subring of }M_2(\ZZ)}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#nonempty){.pf-ref} shows that $R$ is a nonempty subset of $M_2(\ZZ)$, and step [](#closure){.pf-ref} shows that it is closed under subtraction and multiplication. These are exactly the conditions of the subring test, so $R$ is a subring. Its failure to contain the identity matrix does not affect that test.
:::
