::: {.problem}
Is $\ZZ_{6}$ a subring of $\ZZ_{12}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own: the collection's provenance is a set of solution outlines. The question is whether the set $S=\{0,1,\dots,5\}\subseteq\ZZ_{12}$ is a subring, that is a subset closed under the addition and multiplication of $\ZZ_{12}$ and containing $1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #closure}
$S$ is not closed under the addition of $\ZZ_{12}$.

::: pf-proof
In $\ZZ_{12}$ one has $3+3=6$, and $6\notin S$ since $S$ consists of the residues $0,\dots,5$. So the sum of two elements of $S$ leaves $S$, which is exactly what closure forbids.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$\ZZ_6$ is not a subring of $\ZZ_{12}$.

::: pf-proof
A subring must be closed under the operations it inherits; by step [](#closure){.pf-ref} the set $S$ fails that for addition, so no ring structure on $S$ equal to the restriction of the operations of $\ZZ_{12}$ exists. The reason is additive rather than multiplicative: $S$ is not a subgroup of $(\ZZ_{12},+)$. The additive group $\ZZ_{12}$ does have a subgroup of order $6$, namely $\langle2\rangle=\{0,2,4,6,8,10\}$, but that is not $S$; the failure is one of elements, not of orders, as $3+3=6\notin S$ shows. So $\ZZ_6$ is not a subring of $\ZZ_{12}$, although both are rings of order $6$ and $12$.
:::
