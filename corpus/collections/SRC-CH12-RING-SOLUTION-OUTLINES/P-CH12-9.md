::: {.problem}
Prove that the intersection of any collection of subrings of a ring R is a subring of R.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a collection of subrings of $R$ and $S=\bigcap_{T\in\mathcal{S}}T$; if the collection is empty, $S=R$ by the convention that the intersection of an empty family is the whole set, and $R$ is a subring, so assume the collection nonempty.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #zero}
$0\in S$.

::: pf-proof
Every subring of $R$ contains $0$, since it is an additive subgroup; so $0$ lies in every $T\in\mathcal{S}$ and hence in $S$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subtraction}
For all $a,b\in S$ the difference $a-b$ lies in $S$.

::: pf-proof
Fix $T\in\mathcal{S}$. Both $a$ and $b$ lie in $T$ because $S$ is the intersection, and a subring is closed under subtraction, so $a-b\in T$. This holds for every $T\in\mathcal{S}$, hence $a-b\in S$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiplication}
For all $a,b\in S$ the product $ab$ lies in $S$.

::: pf-proof
The same argument with multiplication in place of subtraction: for each $T\in\mathcal{S}$ the elements $a,b$ lie in $T$, which is closed under multiplication, so $ab\in T$ and therefore $ab\in S$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$S$ is a subring of $R$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#zero){.pf-ref} makes $S$ nonempty, steps [](#subtraction){.pf-ref} and [](#multiplication){.pf-ref} make it closed under subtraction and under multiplication, and by the subring test a nonempty subset of a ring with those two closure properties is a subring. The collection was arbitrary.
:::
