::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be the set of all $2 \times 2$ complex Hermitian matrices, that is, the set of $2 \times 2$ complex matrices $A$ such that $A_{ij} = \overline{A_{ji}}$ (the bar denoting complex conjugation).
As we pointed out in Example 6 of Chapter 2, $W$ is a vector space over the field of real numbers, under the usual operations.
Verify that
$$
(x, y, z, t) \rightarrow \begin{bmatrix} t + x & y + iz \\ y - iz & t - x \end{bmatrix}
$$
is an isomorphism of $\RR^4$ onto $W$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.3 Exercise 3.

Let $W=\{A\in M_{2}(\CC)\mid A_{ij}=\overline{A_{ji}}\}$ be the set of Hermitian matrices, regarded as a vector space over $\RR$, and define
$$
\Phi\colon\RR^{4}\to W,\qquad \Phi(x,y,z,t)=\begin{bmatrix}t+x&y+iz\\ y-iz&t-x\end{bmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #linear}
$\Phi$ is well defined, its values are Hermitian, and it is $\RR$-linear.

::: pf-proof
Each of the four entries is a real-linear function of $(x,y,z,t)$ with complex coefficients, so $\Phi$ is a map into $M_{2}(\CC)$ and $\Phi\bigl(a(x,y,z,t)+b(x',y',z',t')\bigr)=a\Phi(x,y,z,t)+b\Phi(x',y',z',t')$ for real $a,b$, since complex conjugation fixes real scalars. For $A=\Phi(x,y,z,t)$: $A_{11}=t+x$ and $A_{22}=t-x$ are real, so $A_{11}=\overline{A_{11}}$ and $A_{22}=\overline{A_{22}}$; and $A_{12}=y+iz$, $A_{21}=y-iz=\overline{y+iz}=\overline{A_{12}}$. So $A\in W$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #injective}
$\Phi(x,y,z,t)=0$ forces $x=y=z=t=0$.

::: pf-proof
Setting the four entries to zero gives $t+x=0$, $y+iz=0$, $y-iz=0$ and $t-x=0$. Adding the first and fourth gives $2t=0$ and subtracting them gives $2x=0$, so $t=x=0$ in $\RR$; adding the second and third gives $2y=0$ and subtracting gives $2iz=0$, so $y=z=0$ in $\RR$. A linear map with trivial kernel is injective.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #surjective}
Every $A\in W$ equals $\Phi(x,y,z,t)$ for some real $x,y,z,t$.

::: pf-proof
Hermitianity gives $A_{11}=\overline{A_{11}}$, so $a\coloneqq A_{11}\in\RR$, and likewise $b\coloneqq A_{22}\in\RR$; write $A_{12}=c+id$ with $c,d\in\RR$, which is possible for any complex number, and then $A_{21}=\overline{A_{12}}=c-id$. Put
$$
t=\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad x=\frac{a-b}{2},\qquad y=c,\qquad z=d .
$$
These are real, and $t+x=a$, $t-x=b$, $y+iz=c+id=A_{12}$, $y-iz=c-id=A_{21}$, so $\Phi(x,y,z,t)=A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\Phi$ is an isomorphism of $\RR^{4}$ onto $W$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#linear){.pf-ref} makes $\Phi$ a real-linear map into $W$, steps [](#injective){.pf-ref} and [](#surjective){.pf-ref} make it injective and surjective. An invertible linear map is an isomorphism.
:::
