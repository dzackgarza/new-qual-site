::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ and $W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces over the field $F$.
Prove that $V$ and $W$ are isomorphic if and only if $\dim V = \dim W$.
:::

::: solution
Suppose first that $V$ and $W$ are isomorphic, and let $T:V\to W$ be an isomorphism. If $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ is a basis of $V$, then $Tv_1,\ldots,Tv_n$ is linearly independent because $T$ is injective, and it spans $W$ because $T$ is surjective. Hence it is a basis of $W$, so
\[
\dim V=\dim W.
\]

Conversely, suppose $\dim V=\dim W=n$. Choose bases $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ of $V$ and $w_1,\ldots,w_n$ of $W$. There is a unique linear map $T:V\to W$ with
\[
T(v_i)=w_i\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
It sends a basis to a basis, hence is both injective and surjective. Therefore $T$ is an isomorphism.
:::
