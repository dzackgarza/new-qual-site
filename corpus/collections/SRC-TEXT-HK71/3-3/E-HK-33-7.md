::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over the field $F$ and let $U$ be an isomorphism of $V$ onto $W$.
Prove that $T \rightarrow UTU^{-1}$ is an isomorphism of $L(V, V)$ onto $L(W, W)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.3 Exercise 7. Here $U:V\to W$ is an isomorphism of vector spaces over $F$, so it is linear, bijective, and has an inverse $U^{-1}:W\to V$ that is linear.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #linear}

$\Phi(T)\coloneqq UTU^{-1}$ defines a map $L(V,V)\to L(W,W)$, and $\Phi$ is linear.

::: pf-proof

A composite of linear maps is linear, so $UTU^{-1}$ is linear from $W$ to $W$. For $S,T\in L(V,V)$ and $a,b\in F$, $\Phi(aS+bT)=U(aS+bT)U^{-1}=aUSU^{-1}+bUTU^{-1}=a\Phi(S)+b\Phi(T)$, using linearity of $U$ and $U^{-1}$ and distributivity of composition over addition of maps.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inverse}

$\Psi(R)\coloneqq U^{-1}RU$ is a map $L(W,W)\to L(V,V)$ inverse to $\Phi$.

::: pf-proof

For $T\in L(V,V)$, $\Psi(\Phi(T))=U^{-1}(UTU^{-1})U=(U^{-1}U)T(U^{-1}U)=T$ by associativity of composition and $U^{-1}U=\mathrm{id}_V$; for $R\in L(W,W)$, $\Phi(\Psi(R))=U(U^{-1}RU)U^{-1}=(UU^{-1})R(UU^{-1})=R$. Hence $\Phi$ is bijective with inverse $\Psi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #iso}

$\Phi$ is an isomorphism of $L(V,V)$ onto $L(W,W)$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#linear){.pf-ref} it is linear and by step [](#inverse){.pf-ref} it is bijective; a bijective linear map of vector spaces is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #mult}

$\Phi$ also preserves composition: $\Phi(ST)=\Phi(S)\Phi(T)$.

::: pf-proof

$\Phi(S)\Phi(T)=(USU^{-1})(UTU^{-1})=US(U^{-1}U)TU^{-1}=USTU^{-1}=\Phi(ST)$, by associativity and $U^{-1}U=\mathrm{id}_V$.

:::
