::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a vector space over the field of complex numbers, and suppose there is an isomorphism $T$ of $V$ onto $\CC^3$.
Let $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3, \alpha_4$ be vectors in $V$ such that
$$
T\alpha_1 = (1, 0, i), \quad T\alpha_2 = (-2, 1 + i, 0),
$$
$$
T\alpha_3 = (-1, 1, 1), \quad T\alpha_4 = (\sqrt{2}, i, 3).
$$

(a) Is $\alpha_1$ in the subspace spanned by $\alpha_2$ and $\alpha_3$?

(b) Let $W_1$ be the subspace spanned by $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$, and let $W_2$ be the subspace spanned by $\alpha_3$ and $\alpha_4$.
What is the intersection of $W_1$ and $W_2$?

(c) Find a basis for the subspace of $V$ spanned by the four vectors $\alpha_i$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and each computation below was redone for this pass.

Since $T$ is an isomorphism of $V$ onto $\CC^{3}$, a linear relation among the $\alpha_{i}$ holds in $V$ exactly when the same relation among their images $T\alpha_{i}$ holds in $\CC^{3}$: applying $T$ carries one to the other, and applying $T^{-1}$ carries it back.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) $\alpha_{1}\in\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\}$, namely $\alpha_{1}=-\frac{1+i}{2}\alpha_{2}+i\alpha_{3}$.

::: pf-proof
Apply $T$ to the right-hand side:
$$
-\frac{1+i}{2}(-2,1+i,0)+i(-1,1,1)=\bigl(1+i,-(1+i)^{2}/2,0\bigr)+(-i,i,i).
$$
Since $(1+i)^{2}=2i$, the second coordinate is $-i$, and the sum is $\bigl(1+i-i,\,-i+i,\,i\bigr)=(1,0,i)=T\alpha_{1}$. So the relation holds among the images, hence among the $\alpha_{i}$, and $\alpha_{1}$ is in the span of $\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b}
(b) $W_{1}\cap W_{2}=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{3}\}$.

::: pf-proof
One inclusion, then the elimination that gives the other.

::: {.pf-step #b-contains}
$\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{3}\}\subseteq W_{1}\cap W_{2}$.

::: pf-proof
$\alpha_{3}$ lies in $W_{2}$ by the definition of $W_{2}$, and it lies in $W_{1}$ because $-i\alpha_{1}+\frac{1-i}{2}\alpha_{2}$ maps under $T$ to
$$
-i(1,0,i)+\frac{1-i}{2}(-2,1+i,0)=(-i,0,1)+(-1+i,1,0)=(-1,1,1)=T\alpha_{3},
$$
using $(1-i)(1+i)=2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-eliminate}
If $a\alpha_{1}+b\alpha_{2}=c\alpha_{3}+d\alpha_{4}$ then $d=0$, $c=ia$ and $b=\frac{1+i}{2}a$.

::: pf-proof
Apply $T$ and compare coordinates. The three coordinates of the two sides give
$$
a-2b=-c+\sqrt{2}d,\qquad (1+i)b=c+id,\qquad ia=c+3d.
$$
The third gives $c=ia-3d$; substituting in the second gives $(1+i)b=ia-(3-i)d$, that is $b=\frac{(1+i)a-(2-4i)d}{2}$, since $(1-i)i=1+i$ and $(1-i)(3-i)=2-4i$. Substituting both in the first equation and collecting the coefficients of $a$ gives $a-(1+i)a+ia=0$, so what remains is
$$
(2-4i)d-3d-\sqrt{2}d=0,
$$
that is $(-1-\sqrt{2}-4i)d=0$. The complex number $-1-\sqrt{2}-4i$ is nonzero, so $d=0$; then $c=ia$ and $b=\frac{1+i}{2}a$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-equals}
$W_{1}\cap W_{2}=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{3}\}$.

::: pf-proof
A vector of $W_{1}\cap W_{2}$ has the form $a\alpha_{1}+b\alpha_{2}=c\alpha_{3}+d\alpha_{4}$, and by step [](#b-eliminate){.pf-ref} its coefficients satisfy $d=0$ and $c=ia$, so the vector equals $ia\,\alpha_{3}$. Hence every element of the intersection lies in $\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{3}\}$, and step [](#b-contains){.pf-ref} gives the reverse inclusion. Therefore the intersection is exactly the one-dimensional subspace $\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{3}\}$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(c) $\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{4}\}$ is a basis for the subspace spanned by $\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}$.

::: pf-proof
The matrix with columns $T\alpha_{1},T\alpha_{2},T\alpha_{4}$ has determinant
$$
\det\begin{bmatrix}1&-2&\sqrt{2}\\ 0&1+i&i\\ i&0&3\end{bmatrix}
=1\cdot\bigl(3(1+i)\bigr)+2\cdot\bigl(0-i^{2}\bigr)+\sqrt{2}\cdot\bigl(0-i(1+i)\bigr)
=5+\sqrt{2}+i(3-\sqrt{2}),
$$
expanding along the first row. Its real part $5+\sqrt{2}$ is nonzero, so the determinant is nonzero and the three images are linearly independent in $\CC^{3}$; hence $\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{4}$ are linearly independent in $V$. By step [](#b-contains){.pf-ref} $\alpha_{3}\in\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}$, so the span of the four vectors is already the span of $\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{4}$, which is therefore a basis of it.
:::
