::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the set of complex numbers regarded as a vector space over the field of real numbers (Exercise 1). We define a function $T$ from $V$ into the space of $2 \times 2$ real matrices, as follows.
If $z = x + iy$ with $x$ and $y$ real numbers, then
$$
T(z) = \begin{bmatrix} x + 7y & 5y \\ -10y & x - 7y \end{bmatrix}.
$$

(a) Verify that $T$ is a one-one (real) linear transformation of $V$ into the space of $2 \times 2$ real matrices.

(b) Verify that $T(z_1 z_2) = T(z_1) T(z_2)$.

(c) How would you describe the range of $T$?

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise and no text version of Hoffman and Kunze in the checkout; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $V=\CC$ is regarded as a real vector space with basis $1,i$, so $z=x+iy$ with $x,y\in\RR$ is unique, and
$$
J=\begin{bmatrix}7&5\\-10&-7\end{bmatrix},\qquad T(x+iy)=xI_2+yJ=\begin{bmatrix}x+7y&5y\\-10y&x-7y\end{bmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #linear}
(a) $T$ is real-linear and one-to-one.

::: pf-proof
Real-linearity: $T$ is the map $x+iy\mapsto xI_2+yJ$, which is the composite of the real-linear identification $x+iy\mapsto(x,y)$ with the linear map $(x,y)\mapsto xI_2+yJ$ into $M_2(\RR)$; explicitly $T$ preserves sums and real multiples term by term in $xI_2+yJ$. Injectivity: if $T(x+iy)=0$ then the $(1,2)$ entry gives $5y=0$, so $y=0$, and then the $(1,1)$ entry gives $x=0$; hence $x+iy=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square}
$J^2=-I_2$.

::: pf-proof
$\begin{bmatrix}7&5\\-10&-7\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}7&5\\-10&-7\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}49-50&35-35\\-70+70&-50+49\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiplicative}
(b) $T(z_1z_2)=T(z_1)T(z_2)$ for all $z_1,z_2$.

::: pf-proof
With $z_1=x+iy$ and $z_2=u+iv$ we have $z_1z_2=(xu-yv)+i(xv+yu)$, and by step [](#square){.pf-ref}
$$
(xI_2+yJ)(uI_2+vJ)=xu\,I_2+xv\,J+yu\,J+yv\,J^2=(xu-yv)I_2+(xv+yu)J=T(z_1z_2),
$$
using that $I_2$ commutes with $J$ and that the multiplication in $M_2(\RR)$ is bilinear.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #range}
(c) $\im T=\{xI_2+yJ:x,y\in\RR\}$ is a two-dimensional real subspace of $M_2(\RR)$, consisting of the matrices $\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}$ with $c=-2b$ and $5(a-d)=14b$, and it is a subfield isomorphic to $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
The image is the image of the two-dimensional space $V$ under the injective linear map $T$, so it equals $\{xI_2+yJ\}$ and has dimension $2$ by rank-nullity together with injectivity. Writing $a=x+7y$, $b=5y$, $c=-10y$, $d=x-7y$: from $b=5y$ we get $y=b/5$, hence $c=-10y=-2b$ and $a-d=14y=14b/5$, that is $5(a-d)=14b$; conversely if $c=-2b$ and $5(a-d)=14b$, put $y=b/5$ and $x=(a+d)/2$, and then $a=x+7y$, $d=x-7y$, $b=5y$, $c=-10y$, so the matrix is $xI_2+yJ$. By (b) the map $T$ is a ring homomorphism onto its image, injective by (a), and $z\mapsto xI_2+yJ$ carries $i$ to $J$ with $J^2=-I_2$; so the image is a subfield of $M_2(\RR)$ isomorphic to $\CC$.

:::
