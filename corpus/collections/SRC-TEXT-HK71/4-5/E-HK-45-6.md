::: {.exercise}
Suppose $f \equiv g \bmod p$ and $f_1 \equiv g_1 \bmod p$.

(a) Prove that $f + f_1 \equiv g + g_1 \bmod p$.

(b) Prove that $ff_1 \equiv gg_1 \bmod p$.
:::

::: solution
Assume
\[
p\mid(f-g),\qquad p\mid(f_1-g_1).
\]
For sums,
\[
(f+f_1)-(g+g_1)=(f-g)+(f_1-g_1),
\]
which is divisible by $p$. Hence
\[
f+f_1\equiv g+g_1\pmod p.
\]

For products,
\[
ff_1-gg_1=f(f_1-g_1)+g_1(f-g).
\]
Both terms on the right are divisible by $p$, so
\[
ff_1\equiv gg_1\pmod p.
\]
:::
