::: {.exercise}
Suppose $f \equiv g \bmod p$ and $f_1 \equiv g_1 \bmod p$.

(a) Prove that $f + f_1 \equiv g + g_1 \bmod p$.

(b) Prove that $ff_1 \equiv gg_1 \bmod p$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: congruence modulo $p$ is divisibility of the difference by $p$, and sums of multiples of $p$ are multiples of $p$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #hypothesis}

$f\equiv g$ and $f_{1}\equiv g_{1}\pmod p$ mean $p$ divides $f-g$ and $p$ divides $f_{1}-g_{1}$.

::: pf-proof

For polynomials, $a\equiv b\pmod p$ is defined as $p\mid(a-b)$, that is, $a-b=pq$ for some $q\in F[x]$. This is exactly the hypothesis in the two instances.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sums}

(a) $f+f_{1}\equiv g+g_{1}\pmod p$.

::: pf-proof

$$
(f+f_{1})-(g+g_{1})=(f-g)+(f_{1}-g_{1})=p(q+q_{1}),
$$
with $f-g=pq$ and $f_{1}-g_{1}=pq_{1}$ as step [](#hypothesis){.pf-ref} allows. So $p$ divides the difference and $f+f_{1}\equiv g+g_{1}\pmod p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #products}

(b) $ff_{1}\equiv gg_{1}\pmod p$.

::: pf-proof

Adding and subtracting $gf_{1}$,
$$
ff_{1}-gg_{1}=f(f_{1}-g_{1})+g_{1}(f-g)=f\,p q_{1}+g_{1}\,p q=p\big(fq_{1}+g_{1}q\big),
$$
again using step [](#hypothesis){.pf-ref}. So $p$ divides $ff_{1}-gg_{1}$, and
$$
\boxed{f+f_{1}\equiv g+g_{1}\pmod p,\qquad ff_{1}\equiv gg_{1}\pmod p.}
$$

:::
