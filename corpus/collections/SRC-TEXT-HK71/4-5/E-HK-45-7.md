::: {.exercise}
Use Exercise 6 to prove the following.
If $f$, $g$, $h$, and $p$ are polynomials over the field $F$ and $p \neq 0$, and if $f \equiv g \bmod p$, then $h(f) \equiv h(g) \bmod p$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §4.5 Exercise 7. Here $f\equiv g\pmod p$ means $p$ divides $f-g$ in $F[x]$, and Exercise 6 of the same section gives that congruence modulo a fixed nonzero $p$ is compatible with addition and multiplication: from $f\equiv f'$ and $g\equiv g'$ one gets $f+g\equiv f'+g'$ and $fg\equiv f'g'$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #powers}

$f^{k}\equiv g^{k}\pmod p$ for every $k\geq0$.

::: pf-proof

Induction on $k$: for $k=0$ both sides are $1$; if $f^{k}\equiv g^{k}$ then multiplying the congruences $f\equiv g$ and $f^{k}\equiv g^{k}$ with Exercise 6 gives $f^{k+1}\equiv g^{k+1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #scalars}

$a_{k}f^{k}\equiv a_{k}g^{k}\pmod p$ for each $k$ and each $a_{k}\in F$.

::: pf-proof

A constant $c$ satisfies $c\equiv c\pmod p$, so Exercise 6 applied to $c\equiv c$ and $f^{k}\equiv g^{k}$ gives $cf^{k}\equiv cg^{k}$; alternatively $p$ divides $f^{k}-g^{k}$, hence also $a_{k}(f^{k}-g^{k})$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sum}

$\sum_{k=0}^{r}a_{k}f^{k}\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{r}a_{k}g^{k}\pmod p$.

::: pf-proof

Repeated application of the additive half of Exercise 6 to the congruences of step [](#scalars){.pf-ref}, starting from $0\equiv0$; a finite sum of congruences is a congruence, since $p$ divides each difference and therefore their sum.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$h(f)\equiv h(g)\pmod p$ for $h(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_rx^{r}$.

::: pf-proof

$h(f)=\sum_{k}a_{k}f^{k}$ and $h(g)=\sum_{k}a_{k}g^{k}$ by definition of substitution, so step [](#sum){.pf-ref} is the assertion.

:::
