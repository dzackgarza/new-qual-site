::: {.exercise}
Prove the following generalization of Taylor's formula.
Let $f$, $g$, and $h$ be polynomials over a subfield of the complex numbers, with $\deg f \leq n$.
Then
\[
f(g) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{1}{k!} f^{(k)}(h)(g - h)^k.
\]
(Here $f(g)$ denotes 'f of g.')
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $R$ be the given subfield of $\CC$. Introduce an indeterminate $t$ and consider the polynomial
\[
q(t)=f(h+t)\in R[x][t].
\]
Since $\deg f\le n$, Taylor's formula for the polynomial $q$ at $t=0$ is an exact identity:
\[
q(t)=\sum_{k=0}^n \frac{q^{(k)}(0)}{k!}t^k.
\]
Differentiating with respect to $t$ gives
\[
q^{(k)}(0)=f^{(k)}(h).
\]
Therefore
\[
f(h+t)=\sum_{k=0}^n \frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(h)t^k.
\]
Now substitute $t=g-h$. Since polynomials commute under multiplication,
\[
f(g)=\sum_{k=0}^n \frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(h)(g-h)^k.
\]
:::
