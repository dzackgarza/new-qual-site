::: {.exercise}
Prove the following generalization of Taylor's formula.
Let $f$, $g$, and $h$ be polynomials over a subfield of the complex numbers, with $\deg f \leq n$.
Then
$$
f(g) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{1}{k!} f^{(k)}(h)(g - h)^k.
$$
(Here $f(g)$ denotes 'f of g.')
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Let $R\subset\CC$ be the subfield, and note that $R$ then has characteristic $0$, so $k!$ is invertible in $R$ for every $k\ge0$. Fix $f,g,h\in R[y]$ with $\deg f\le n$, and write $f(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_jy^j$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #taylor}
For any $q\in R[t]$ with $\deg q\le n$ one has $q(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{q^{(k)}(0)}{k!}t^k$.

::: pf-proof
Write $q(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}b_jt^j$. The $k$-th formal derivative is $q^{(k)}(t)=\sum_{j\ge k}b_j\,j(j-1)\cdots(j-k+1)t^{j-k}$, so $q^{(k)}(0)=b_k\,k!$ for $k\le n$, and substituting gives $\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{b_kk!}{k!}t^k=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_kt^k=q(t)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #shift}
Set $q(t)=f(h+t)\in R[y][t]$. Then $\deg_tq\le n$ and $q^{(k)}(0)=f^{(k)}(h)$ for $k\le n$.

::: pf-proof
Expanding, $f(h+t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_j(h+t)^j$, a polynomial in $t$ of degree at most $n$ whose coefficients are polynomials in $h$ with coefficients in $R$; so $\deg_tq\le n$. For the derivatives: formal differentiation in $t$ commutes with the coefficients, which lie in $R[h]$, and $\frac{d}{dt}(h+t)^j=j(h+t)^{j-1}$; hence $\frac{d^k}{dt^k}f(h+t)=f^{(k)}(h+t)$ by induction on $k$, and setting $t=0$ gives $q^{(k)}(0)=f^{(k)}(h)$. (Formally, $f^{(k)}$ denotes the $k$-th formal derivative of $f$ with respect to its variable, which is a polynomial in $R[y]$, evaluated at $h$.)
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #substitute}
The evaluation map $\varepsilon:R[t]\to R[y]$, $t\mapsto g-h$, is a homomorphism of $R$-algebras, and $\varepsilon\bigl(f(h+t)\bigr)=f(g)$.

::: pf-proof
Evaluation at a point of an $R$-algebra is a ring homomorphism: $\varepsilon(\sum c_kt^k)=\sum c_k(g-h)^k$ is additive and multiplicative, and fixes $R$. Since $f(h+t)=\sum_ja_j(h+t)^j$, applying $\varepsilon$ gives $\sum_ja_j\bigl(h+(g-h)\bigr)^j=\sum_ja_jg^j=f(g)$, using that $h+(g-h)=g$ and that polynomials in $g$ and $h$ obey the usual algebra because $R[y]$ is commutative.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$f(g)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(h)(g-h)^k$.

::: pf-proof
Apply $\varepsilon$ to step [](#taylor){.pf-ref} with $q=f(h+t)$, which is legitimate since $\deg_tq\le n$ by step [](#shift){.pf-ref}: the left side becomes $f(g)$ by step [](#substitute){.pf-ref}, and the right side becomes $\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{q^{(k)}(0)}{k!}(g-h)^k=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(h)(g-h)^k$ by step [](#shift){.pf-ref}. The coefficients $1/k!$ exist in $R$ because $\operatorname{char}R=0$.
:::
