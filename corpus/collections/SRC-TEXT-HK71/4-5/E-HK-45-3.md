::: {.exercise}
Let $D$ be the differentiation operator on the space of polynomials over the field of complex numbers.
Let $f$ be a monic polynomial over the field of complex numbers.
Prove that
\[
f = (x - c_1) \cdots (x - c_k)
\]
where $c_1, \ldots, c_k$ are distinct complex numbers if and only if $f$ and $Df$ are relatively prime.
In other words, $f$ has no repeated root if and only if $f$ and $Df$ have no common root.
(Assume the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra.)
:::

::: solution
By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, write
\[
f(x)=\prod_{j=1}^r (x-c_j)^{m_j}
\]
with distinct $c_j$ and positive integers $m_j$.

If some $m_j\ge2$, then $(x-c_j)$ divides both $f$ and $Df$: differentiating the displayed factorization shows every term of $Df$ still contains at least one factor $(x-c_j)$. Hence $f$ and $Df$ are not relatively prime.

Conversely, suppose all $m_j=1$. Fix a root $c_j$. Writing
\[
f(x)=(x-c_j)q_j(x)
\]
with $q_j(c_j)\ne0$, we have
\[
f'(c_j)=q_j(c_j)\ne0.
\]
Thus no root of $f$ is a root of $Df$. Over $\CC$, two nonconstant polynomials have a nonconstant common divisor exactly when they have a common root. Therefore $f$ and $Df$ are relatively prime.

Hence $f$ has distinct roots if and only if $\gcd(f,Df)=1$.
:::
