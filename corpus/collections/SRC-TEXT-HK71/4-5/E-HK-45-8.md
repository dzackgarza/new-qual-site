::: {.exercise}
If $p$ is an irreducible polynomial and $fg \equiv 0 \bmod p$, prove that either $f \equiv 0 \bmod p$ or $g \equiv 0 \bmod p$.
Give an example which shows that this is false if $p$ is not irreducible.
:::

::: {.solution}
The congruence
\[
fg\equiv0\pmod p
\]
means $p\mid fg$. Since $p$ is irreducible in the PID $F[x]$, it is prime. Hence
\[
p\mid f\quad\text{or}\quad p\mid g.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
f\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\text{or}\quad
g\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Irreducibility is necessary. For example, take
\[
p=x^2,\qquad f=g=x.
\]
Then $fg=x^2\equiv0\pmod{x^2}$, but neither $f$ nor $g$ is congruent to $0$ modulo $x^2$.
:::
