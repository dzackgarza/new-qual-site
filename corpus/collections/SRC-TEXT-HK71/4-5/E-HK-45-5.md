::: {.exercise}
Prove, for any non-zero polynomial $p$, that congruence modulo $p$ is an equivalence relation.

(a) It is reflexive: $f \equiv f \bmod p$.

(b) It is symmetric: if $f \equiv g \bmod p$, then $g \equiv f \bmod p$.

(c) It is transitive: if $f \equiv g \bmod p$ and $g \equiv h \bmod p$, then $f \equiv h \bmod p$.
:::

::: {.solution}
By definition,
\[
f\equiv g\pmod p
\]
means that $p$ divides $f-g$.

(a) Since $f-f=0$ and $p\mid0$, we have $f\equiv f\pmod p$.

(b) If $p\mid(f-g)$, then
\[
g-f=-(f-g),
\]
so $p\mid(g-f)$ and hence $g\equiv f\pmod p$.

(c) If $p\mid(f-g)$ and $p\mid(g-h)$, then
\[
f-h=(f-g)+(g-h),
\]
so $p\mid(f-h)$. Hence $f\equiv h\pmod p$.

Thus congruence modulo $p$ is an equivalence relation.
:::
