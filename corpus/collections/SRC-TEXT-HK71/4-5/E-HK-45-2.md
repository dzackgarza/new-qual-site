::: {.exercise}
Assuming the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, prove the following.
If $f$ and $g$ are polynomials over the field of complex numbers, then g.c.d.$(f, g) = 1$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ have no common root.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that for polynomials $f, g \in \mathbb{C}[x]$, $\gcd(f, g) = 1$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ have no common root in $\mathbb{C}$.

<1>1. Forward direction: $\gcd(f, g) = 1 \implies f$ and $g$ have no common root.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Assume $\gcd(f, g) = 1$.
    <2>2. Suppose for contradiction that there exists $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ such that $f(\alpha) = 0$ and $g(\alpha) = 0$.
    <2>3. By the Factor Theorem, the linear polynomial $(x - \alpha) \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ divides both $f(x)$ and $g(x)$.
    <2>4. Thus $(x - \alpha)$ is a common divisor of $f$ and $g$.
    <2>5. By definition of the greatest common divisor in a PID $\mathbb{C}[x]$, $(x - \alpha)$ must divide $\gcd(f, g) = 1$.
    <2>6. But $\deg(x - \alpha) = 1 > 0 = \deg(1)$, so $(x - \alpha)$ cannot divide 1 in $\mathbb{C}[x]$, a contradiction.
    <2>7. Therefore $f$ and $g$ share no common roots in $\mathbb{C}$.

<1>2. Backward direction: $f$ and $g$ have no common root $\implies \gcd(f, g) = 1$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Assume $f$ and $g$ have no common root in $\mathbb{C}$.
    <2>2. Let $d(x) = \gcd(f, g)$ denote the unique monic greatest common divisor of $f$ and $g$ in $\mathbb{C}[x]$.
    <2>3. Suppose for contradiction that $\deg d(x) \ge 1$.
    <2>4. By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, every non-constant polynomial in $\mathbb{C}[x]$ has at least one root in $\mathbb{C}$.
    <2>5. Thus there exists $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ such that $d(\alpha) = 0$.
    <2>6. Because $d(x)$ divides $f(x)$ and $g(x)$, there exist polynomials $q_1, q_2 \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ such that $f(x) = d(x) q_1(x)$ and $g(x) = d(x) q_2(x)$.
    <2>7. Evaluating at $\alpha$ gives $f(\alpha) = d(\alpha) q_1(\alpha) = 0$ and $g(\alpha) = d(\alpha) q_2(\alpha) = 0$.
    <2>8. This means $\alpha$ is a common root of $f$ and $g$, contradicting the hypothesis.
    <2>9. Hence $\deg d(x) = 0$. Since $d(x)$ is monic, $d(x) = 1$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    *Proof:*
    By <1>1 and <1>2, $\gcd(f, g) = 1$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ have no common root in $\mathbb{C}$.
:::
