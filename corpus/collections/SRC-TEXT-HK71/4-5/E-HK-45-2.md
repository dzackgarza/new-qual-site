::: {.exercise}
Assuming the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, prove the following.
If $f$ and $g$ are polynomials over the field of complex numbers, then g.c.d.$(f, g) = 1$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ have no common root.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Prove that for polynomials $f, g \in \mathbb{C}[x]$, $\gcd(f, g) = 1$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ have no common root in $\mathbb{C}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
Forward direction: $\gcd(f, g) = 1 \implies f$ and $g$ have no common root.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Assume $\gcd(f, g) = 1$.
:::

::: pf-step
Suppose for contradiction that there exists $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ such that $f(\alpha) = 0$ and $g(\alpha) = 0$.
:::

::: pf-step
By the Factor Theorem, the linear polynomial $(x - \alpha) \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ divides both $f(x)$ and $g(x)$.
:::

::: pf-step
Thus $(x - \alpha)$ is a common divisor of $f$ and $g$.
:::

::: pf-step
By definition of the greatest common divisor in a PID $\mathbb{C}[x]$, $(x - \alpha)$ must divide $\gcd(f, g) = 1$.
:::

::: pf-step
But $\deg(x - \alpha) = 1 > 0 = \deg(1)$, so $(x - \alpha)$ cannot divide 1 in $\mathbb{C}[x]$, a contradiction.
:::

::: pf-step
Therefore $f$ and $g$ share no common roots in $\mathbb{C}$.
:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}
Backward direction: $f$ and $g$ have no common root $\implies \gcd(f, g) = 1$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Assume $f$ and $g$ have no common root in $\mathbb{C}$.
:::

::: pf-step
Let $d(x) = \gcd(f, g)$ denote the unique monic greatest common divisor of $f$ and $g$ in $\mathbb{C}[x]$.
:::

::: pf-step
Suppose for contradiction that $\deg d(x) \ge 1$.
:::

::: pf-step
By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, every non-constant polynomial in $\mathbb{C}[x]$ has at least one root in $\mathbb{C}$.
:::

::: pf-step
Thus there exists $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ such that $d(\alpha) = 0$.
:::

::: pf-step
Because $d(x)$ divides $f(x)$ and $g(x)$, there exist polynomials $q_1, q_2 \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ such that $f(x) = d(x) q_1(x)$ and $g(x) = d(x) q_2(x)$.
:::

::: pf-step
Evaluating at $\alpha$ gives $f(\alpha) = d(\alpha) q_1(\alpha) = 0$ and $g(\alpha) = d(\alpha) q_2(\alpha) = 0$.
:::

::: pf-step
This means $\alpha$ is a common root of $f$ and $g$, contradicting the hypothesis.
:::

::: pf-step
Hence $\deg d(x) = 0$. Since $d(x)$ is monic, $d(x) = 1$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Conclusion:

::: pf-proof
By step [](#s1){.pf-ref} and step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, $\gcd(f, g) = 1$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ have no common root in $\mathbb{C}$.
:::
