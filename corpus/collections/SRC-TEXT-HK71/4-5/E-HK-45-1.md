::: {.exercise}
Let $p$ be a monic polynomial over the field $F$, and let $f$ and $g$ be relatively prime polynomials over $F$.
Prove that the g.c.d. of $pf$ and $pg$ is $p$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §4.5 Exercise 1, p. 139. The three steps are the Bezout identity for relatively prime polynomials, the multiplication by $p$, and the two divisibilities that make the monic common divisor greatest.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #bezout}
There are $a,b\in F[x]$ with $af+bg=1$.

::: pf-proof
Two polynomials over a field are relatively prime exactly when the ideal they generate is the whole ring, which here means that $1$ is a combination $af+bg$; the Euclidean algorithm produces such $a$ and $b$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #divides}
Every common divisor of $pf$ and $pg$ divides $p$.

::: pf-proof
Multiply the identity of step [](#bezout){.pf-ref} by $p$: $a(pf)+b(pg)=p$. If $d$ divides both $pf$ and $pg$, then $d$ divides both terms on the left, hence $d$ divides their sum $p$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #common}
$p$ is a common divisor of $pf$ and $pg$, and it is monic.

::: pf-proof
$pf=p\cdot f$ and $pg=p\cdot g$, and $p$ is monic by hypothesis.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #gcd}
The greatest common divisor of $pf$ and $pg$ is $p$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#common){.pf-ref} $p$ is a monic common divisor, and by step [](#divides){.pf-ref} every common divisor divides it. A monic common divisor divisible by every common divisor is the greatest common divisor, which is unique.
:::
:::
