::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be a symmetric bilinear form on $\RR^n$.
Use the result of Exercise 8 to prove the existence of an ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$ such that $[f]_{\mathfrak{B}}$ is diagonal.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
q(v)=f(v,v)
$$
be the quadratic form associated with the symmetric bilinear form $f$.

<1>1. Apply Exercise 8 to $q$.
::: {.proof}
Exercise 8 provides an invertible linear operator
$$
U:\mathbb R^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R^n
$$
and scalars
$$
c_1,\ldots,c_n\in\{-1,0,1\}
$$
such that
$$
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
=q(Ux)
=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i x_i^2.
$$
:::

<1>2. Use the columns of $U$ as the desired basis.
::: {.proof}
Let $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ be the standard basis and set
$$
u_i=Ue_i.
$$
Since $U$ is invertible,
$$
\mathfrak B=(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
$$
is an ordered basis.

The symmetric bilinear form associated with the pulled-back quadratic form
$U^\dagger q$ is
$$
h(x,y)=f(Ux,Uy).
$$
By polarization of
$$
U^\dagger q(x)=\sum_i c_i x_i^2,
$$
we obtain
$$
h(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i x_i y_i.
$$
Hence
$$
h(e_i,e_j)=c_i\delta_{ij}.
$$
But
$$
h(e_i,e_j)=f(Ue_i,Ue_j)=f(u_i,u_j).
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
[f]_{\mathfrak B}
=\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_n),}
$$
which is diagonal.
:::
:::
