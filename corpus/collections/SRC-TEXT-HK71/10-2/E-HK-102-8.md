::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be a symmetric $n \times n$ matrix over $\RR$, and let $q$ be the quadratic form on $\RR^n$ given by
\[
q(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = \sum_{i,j} A_{ij}x_ix_j.
\]
Generalize the method used in Exercise 7 to show that there is an invertible linear operator $U$ on $\RR^n$ such that
\[
(U^{\dagger}q)(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} c_ix_i^2
\]
where $c_i$ is $1$, $-1$, or $0$, $i = 1, \ldots, n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $f$ be the symmetric bilinear form associated with $q$, so
$$
q(v)=f(v,v).
$$
We prove by induction on $n=\dim\mathbb R^n$ that there is a basis
$u_1,\ldots,u_n$ satisfying
$$
f(u_i,u_j)=0\quad(i\ne j),
\qquad
f(u_i,u_i)\in\{-1,0,1\}.
$$

<1>1. The assertion holds in dimension zero, and also when $q$ is identically zero.
::: {.proof}
If $n=0$ there is nothing to prove. For arbitrary $n$, if $q=0$, then
polarization gives
$$
f(v,w)=\frac12\bigl(q(v+w)-q(v)-q(w)\bigr)=0
$$
for all $v,w$. Thus every basis has Gram matrix zero, and we may take
$c_1=\cdots=c_n=0$.
:::

<1>2. If $q$ is not zero, one can split off a vector of value $1$ or $-1$.
::: {.proof}
Choose $v\in V$ with
$$
q(v)=f(v,v)\ne0.
$$
Let
$$
c=\operatorname{sgn}(q(v))\in\{-1,1\},
\qquad
u_1=\frac{v}{\sqrt{|q(v)|}}.
$$
Then
$$
f(u_1,u_1)=c.
$$

Define
$$
W=\{w\in V:f(u_1,w)=0\}.
$$
For any $x\in V$, put
$$
w=x-c\,f(u_1,x)u_1.
$$
Since $c^2=1$,
$$
f(u_1,w)
=f(u_1,x)-c f(u_1,x)f(u_1,u_1)
=0.
$$
Hence
$$
x=c f(u_1,x)u_1+w
$$
with $w\in W$. Also
$$
\mathbb R u_1\cap W=0
$$
because $f(u_1,u_1)=c\ne0$. Therefore
$$
V=\mathbb R u_1\oplus W,
\qquad
\dim W=n-1.
$$
:::

<1>3. Apply induction to the orthogonal complement.
::: {.proof}
The restriction of $f$ to $W$ is again a symmetric bilinear form, with
quadratic form $q|_W$. By the induction hypothesis, $W$ has a basis
$$
u_2,\ldots,u_n
$$
such that
$$
f(u_i,u_j)=0\quad(2\le i\ne j\le n)
$$
and
$$
f(u_i,u_i)=c_i\in\{-1,0,1\}
\qquad(2\le i\le n).
$$
Since every $u_i$ with $i\ge2$ lies in $W$,
$$
f(u_1,u_i)=0.
$$
Thus $u_1,\ldots,u_n$ is a basis of $V$ with diagonal Gram matrix
$$
\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_n),
\qquad c_i\in\{-1,0,1\},
$$
where $c_1=c$.
:::

<1>4. Convert the constructed basis into the required operator $U$.
::: {.proof}
Let $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ be the standard basis and define the invertible linear
operator $U$ by
$$
Ue_i=u_i.
$$
For
$$
x=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i e_i,
$$
we have
$$
Ux=\sum_i x_i u_i.
$$
Using orthogonality of the $u_i$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(U^\dagger q)(x)
&=q(Ux)\\
&=f\left(\sum_i x_i u_i,\sum_j x_j u_j\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i x_i^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i x_i^2,
\qquad c_i\in\{-1,0,1\}.}
$$
:::
