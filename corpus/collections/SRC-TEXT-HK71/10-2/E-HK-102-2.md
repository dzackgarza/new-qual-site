::: {.problem}
Exercise 1 lists the following quadratic forms $q(x) = q(x_1, x_2)$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and asks for the symmetric bilinear form $f$ corresponding to each:

(a) $q(x) = a x_1^2$;

(b) $q(x) = b x_1 x_2$;

(c) $q(x) = c x_2^2$;

(d) $q(x) = 2 x_1^2 - \frac{1}{3} x_1 x_2$;

(e) $q(x) = x_1^2 + 9 x_2^2$;

(f) $q(x) = 3 x_1 x_2 - x_2^2$;

(g) $q(x) = 4 x_1^2 + 6 x_1 x_2 - 3 x_2^2$.

Find the matrix, in the standard ordered basis, and the rank of each of the bilinear forms determined in Exercise 1.
Indicate which forms are non-degenerate.
:::

::: solution
For a quadratic form
$$
q(x_1,x_2)=A x_1^2+2B x_1x_2+C x_2^2,
$$
the associated symmetric bilinear form has standard matrix
$$
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&B\\
B&C
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
On $\mathbb R^2$, the form is non-degenerate exactly when
$\det M\ne0$, equivalently when $M$ has rank two.

<1>1. Part (a): $q=a x_1^2$.
::: proof
The matrix is
$$
M_a=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&0\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{rank}M_a
=\begin{cases}
1,&a\ne0,\\
0,&a=0.
\end{cases}
$$
In either case the form is degenerate.
:::

<1>2. Part (b): $q=b x_1x_2$.
::: proof
Here
$$
M_b=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&b/2\\
b/2&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det M_b=-\frac{b^2}{4}.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{rank}M_b
=\begin{cases}
2,&b\ne0,\\
0,&b=0,
\end{cases}
$$
and the form is non-degenerate exactly when $b\ne0$.
:::

<1>3. Part (c): $q=c x_2^2$.
::: proof
The matrix is
$$
M_c=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\
0&c
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{rank}M_c
=\begin{cases}
1,&c\ne0,\\
0,&c=0,
\end{cases}
$$
and the form is always degenerate.
:::

<1>4. Part (d): $q=2x_1^2-\frac13x_1x_2$.
::: proof
The matrix is
$$
M_d=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1/6\\
-1/6&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and
$$
\det M_d=-\frac1{36}\ne0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{rank}M_d=2,
$$
so the form is non-degenerate.
:::

<1>5. Part (e): $q=x_1^2+9x_2^2$.
::: proof
The matrix is
$$
M_e=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&9
\end{pmatrix},
$$
with determinant $9$. Hence its rank is two and the form is non-degenerate.
:::

<1>6. Part (f): $q=3x_1x_2-x_2^2$.
::: proof
The matrix is
$$
M_f=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&3/2\\
3/2&-1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and
$$
\det M_f=-\frac94\ne0.
$$
Thus its rank is two and the form is non-degenerate.
:::

<1>7. Part (g): $q=4x_1^2+6x_1x_2-3x_2^2$.
::: proof
The matrix is
$$
M_g=
\begin{pmatrix}
4&3\\
3&-3
\end{pmatrix},
$$
with
$$
\det M_g=4(-3)-9=-21\ne0.
$$
Therefore its rank is two and the form is non-degenerate.
:::

In summary, (d), (e), (f), and (g) are always non-degenerate; (b) is
non-degenerate exactly when $b\ne0$; and (a), (c) are always degenerate.
:::
