::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the real vector space of all $2 \times 2$ (complex) Hermitian matrices, that is, $2 \times 2$ complex matrices $A$ which satisfy $A_{ij} = \overline{A_{ji}}$.

(a) Show that the equation $q(A) = \det A$ defines a quadratic form $q$ on $V$.

(b) Let $W$ be the subspace of $V$ of matrices of trace 0. Show that the bilinear form $f$ determined by $q$ is negative definite on the subspace $W$.
:::


::: solution
Every Hermitian matrix in $V$ has a unique expression
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
 a&z\\
 \overline z&d
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,d\in\mathbb R,\quad z=x+iy\in\mathbb C.
$$

<1>1. The determinant is a quadratic form on the real vector space $V$.
::: proof
In the real coordinates $(a,d,x,y)$,
$$
q(A)=\det A=ad-|z|^2=ad-x^2-y^2.
$$
Thus $q$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree two in the real coordinates
of $V$. Equivalently, its polarization
$$
f(A,B)=\frac12\bigl(q(A+B)-q(A)-q(B)\bigr)
$$
is bilinear over $\mathbb R$ and satisfies $q(A)=f(A,A)$. Hence $q$ is a
quadratic form on $V$.

More explicitly, if
$$
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
 b&w\\
 \overline w&e
\end{pmatrix},
$$
then direct expansion gives
$$
\boxed{
 f(A,B)=\frac12(ae+db)-\operatorname{Re}(z\overline w).}
$$
This formula is visibly real-bilinear and symmetric.
:::

<1>2. On the trace-zero subspace, the quadratic form is strictly negative away from zero.
::: proof
A matrix $A\in W$ has trace zero, so $d=-a$. Therefore
$$
q(A)=a(-a)-|z|^2=-a^2-|z|^2.
$$
If $A\ne0$, then either $a\ne0$ or $z\ne0$, and hence
$$
q(A)=-a^2-|z|^2<0.
$$
Since $q(A)=f(A,A)$, it follows that
$$
\boxed{f(A,A)<0\qquad\text{for every }0\ne A\in W.}
$$
Thus the restriction of $f$ to $W$ is negative definite.
:::
:::
