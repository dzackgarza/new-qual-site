::: {.exercise}
Let $q$ be the quadratic form on $\RR^2$ given by
$$
q(x_1, x_2) = ax_1^2 + 2bx_1x_2 + cx_2^2, \quad a \neq 0.
$$
Find an invertible linear operator $U$ on $\RR^2$ such that
$$
(U^{\dagger}q)(x_1, x_2) = ax_1^2 + \left(c - \frac{b^2}{a}\right)x_2^2.
$$
(Hint: To find $U^{-1}$ (and hence $U$), complete the square.
For the definition of $U^{\dagger}$, see part (e) of Exercise 4.)
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §10.2 Exercise 5 on printed p. 372. The argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Complete the square in the original variables.

::: pf-proof

Because $a\ne0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x_1,x_2)
&=a x_1^2+2b x_1x_2+c x_2^2\\
&=a\left(x_1+\frac ba x_2\right)^2
+\left(c-\frac{b^2}{a}\right)x_2^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the coordinates
$$
y_1=x_1+\frac ba x_2,
\qquad
y_2=x_2
$$
diagonalize the quadratic form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Invert the coordinate change to obtain $U$.

::: pf-proof

Solving the equations in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} for $(x_1,x_2)$ gives
$$
x_1=y_1-\frac ba y_2,
\qquad
x_2=y_2.
$$
Hence define
$$
U(y_1,y_2)
=\left(y_1-\frac ba y_2,\ y_2\right),
$$
so that
$$
[U]=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-b/a\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is $1$, so $U$ is invertible.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Verify the requested pullback formula.

::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2)=q(U(x_1,x_2)).
$$
Substituting the formula from step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
q\left(x_1-\frac ba x_2,x_2\right)
={}&a\left(x_1-\frac ba x_2\right)^2
+2b\left(x_1-\frac ba x_2\right)x_2
+cx_2^2\\
={}&a x_1^2
+\left(c-\frac{b^2}{a}\right)x_2^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2)
=a x_1^2+\left(c-\frac{b^2}{a}\right)x_2^2.}
$$

:::
