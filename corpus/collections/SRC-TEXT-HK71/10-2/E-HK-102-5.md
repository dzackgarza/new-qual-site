::: {.exercise}
Let $q$ be the quadratic form on $\RR^2$ given by
\[
q(x_1, x_2) = ax_1^2 + 2bx_1x_2 + cx_2^2, \quad a \neq 0.
\]
Find an invertible linear operator $U$ on $\RR^2$ such that
\[
(U^{\dagger}q)(x_1, x_2) = ax_1^2 + \left(c - \frac{b^2}{a}\right)x_2^2.
\]
(Hint: To find $U^{-1}$ (and hence $U$), complete the square.
For the definition of $U^{\dagger}$, see part (e) of Exercise 4.)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Complete the square in the original variables.
::: {.proof}
Because $a\ne0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x_1,x_2)
&=a x_1^2+2b x_1x_2+c x_2^2\\
&=a\left(x_1+\frac ba x_2\right)^2
+\left(c-\frac{b^2}{a}\right)x_2^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the coordinates
$$
y_1=x_1+\frac ba x_2,
\qquad
y_2=x_2
$$
diagonalize the quadratic form.
:::

<1>2. Invert the coordinate change to obtain $U$.
::: {.proof}
Solving the equations in step <1>1 for $(x_1,x_2)$ gives
$$
x_1=y_1-\frac ba y_2,
\qquad
x_2=y_2.
$$
Hence define
$$
U(y_1,y_2)
=\left(y_1-\frac ba y_2,\ y_2\right),
$$
so that
$$
[U]=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-b/a\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is $1$, so $U$ is invertible.
:::

<1>3. Verify the requested pullback formula.
::: {.proof}
By definition,
$$
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2)=q(U(x_1,x_2)).
$$
Substituting the formula from step <1>2,
$$
\begin{aligned}
q\left(x_1-\frac ba x_2,x_2\right)
={}&a\left(x_1-\frac ba x_2\right)^2
+2b\left(x_1-\frac ba x_2\right)x_2
+cx_2^2\\
={}&a x_1^2
+\left(c-\frac{b^2}{a}\right)x_2^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2)
=a x_1^2+\left(c-\frac{b^2}{a}\right)x_2^2.}
$$
:::
:::
