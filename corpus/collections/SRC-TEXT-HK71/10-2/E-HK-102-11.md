::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and let $f$ be a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on $V$.
Show that for each linear operator $T$ on $V$ there is a unique linear operator $T'$ on $V$ such that
\[
f(T\alpha,\beta)=f(\alpha,T'\beta)
\]
for all $\alpha,\beta\in V$.
Also show that
\[
(T')'=T.
\]
How much of the above remains valid without the assumption that $f$ is non-degenerate?
:::

::: solution
Define
$$
L_f:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
L_f(\gamma)(\alpha)=f(\alpha,\gamma).
$$
Because $f$ is non-degenerate and $V$ is finite-dimensional, $L_f$ is an
isomorphism.

<1>1. Every operator has a unique adjoint relative to $f$.
::: proof
Fix $\beta\in V$. The map
$$
\alpha\longmapsto f(T\alpha,\beta)
$$
is a linear functional on $V$. Since $L_f$ is onto, there is a unique vector,
denoted $T'\beta$, such that
$$
f(T\alpha,\beta)=f(\alpha,T'\beta)
$$
for every $\alpha$.

To see that $T'$ is linear, let $a,b$ be scalars. For all $\alpha$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha,T'(a\beta+b\gamma))
&=f(T\alpha,a\beta+b\gamma)\\
&=a f(T\alpha,\beta)+b f(T\alpha,\gamma)\\
&=f(\alpha,aT'\beta+bT'\gamma).
\end{aligned}
$$
Non-degeneracy in the second variable gives
$$
T'(a\beta+b\gamma)=aT'\beta+bT'\gamma.
$$

If $S$ also satisfies the adjoint identity, then
$$
f(\alpha,(S-T')\beta)=0
$$
for all $\alpha$, so non-degeneracy implies $(S-T')\beta=0$ for every
$\beta$. Thus $S=T'$.
:::

<1>2. The adjoint operation is an involution.
::: proof
Symmetry gives, for all $\alpha,\beta$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(T'\beta,\alpha)
&=f(\alpha,T'\beta)\\
&=f(T\alpha,\beta)\\
&=f(\beta,T\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $T$ is an adjoint of $T'$. By uniqueness from step <1>1,
$$
\boxed{(T')'=T.}
$$
:::

<1>3. Describe the degenerate case.
::: proof
Let
$$
R=V^\perp=\{r\in V:f(r,v)=0\text{ for every }v\in V\}
$$
be the radical of the symmetric form.

An adjoint of $T$ exists if and only if
$$
T(R)\subseteq R.
$$
Indeed, the image of $L_f$ is the annihilator
$$
R^0=\{\lambda\in V^*: \lambda(r)=0\text{ for all }r\in R\}.
$$
For fixed $\beta$, the functional
$\alpha\mapsto f(T\alpha,\beta)$ lies in $R^0$ exactly when
$$
f(Tr,\beta)=0
$$
for every $r\in R$. Requiring this for all $\beta$ is exactly
$Tr\in R$ for all $r\in R$.

When an adjoint exists it need not be unique. If $S_1,S_2$ are adjoints, then
$$
f(\alpha,(S_1-S_2)\beta)=0
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$, so
$$
\operatorname{im}(S_1-S_2)\subseteq R.
$$
Conversely, adding any operator with image in $R$ to an adjoint gives another
adjoint.

Finally, symmetry still shows that if $S$ is an adjoint of $T$, then $T$ is
an adjoint of $S$. Thus the adjoint relation remains symmetric, but without
non-degeneracy there is generally neither existence for every $T$ nor
uniqueness, so one cannot define a single-valued involution $T\mapsto T'$ on
all operators.
:::
:::
