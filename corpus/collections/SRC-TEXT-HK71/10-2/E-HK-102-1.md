::: {.exercise}
The following expressions define quadratic forms $q(x) = q(x_1, x_2)$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$.
Find the symmetric bilinear form $f(x, y)$ corresponding to each quadratic form $q$:

(a) $q(x) = a x_1^2$
(b) $q(x) = b x_1 x_2$
(c) $q(x) = c x_2^2$
(d) $q(x) = 2 x_1^2 - \frac{1}{3} x_1 x_2$
(e) $q(x) = x_1^2 + 9 x_2^2$
(f) $q(x) = 3 x_1 x_2 - x_2^2$
(g) $q(x) = 4 x_1^2 + 6 x_1 x_2 - 3 x_2^2$
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Compute the unique symmetric bilinear form $f(x, y)$ from a quadratic form $q(x)$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ using polarization: $f(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}[q(x + y) - q(x) - q(y)]$.

<1>1. Polarization Identity on $\mathbb{R}^2$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. A general quadratic form on $\mathbb{R}^2$ is given by:
        $$q(x) = A x_1^2 + 2B x_1 x_2 + C x_2^2 = x^T \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ B & C \end{bmatrix} x.$$
    <2>2. The unique symmetric bilinear form $f: \mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying $f(x, x) = q(x)$ is given by:
        $$f(x, y) = x^T \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ B & C \end{bmatrix} y = A x_1 y_1 + B(x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1) + C x_2 y_2.$$
    <2>3. In particular, pure quadratic terms $x_i^2$ polarize to $x_i y_i$, and cross terms $x_1 x_2$ polarize to $\frac{1}{2}(x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1)$.

<1>2. Computations for Each Part:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Part (a): $q(x) = a x_1^2$**
        - Matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
        - Symmetric form: $f(x, y) = a x_1 y_1$.
    <2>2. **Part (b): $q(x) = b x_1 x_2$**
        - Matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & b/2 \\ b/2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
        - Symmetric form: $f(x, y) = \frac{b}{2}(x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1)$.
    <2>3. **Part (c): $q(x) = c x_2^2$**
        - Matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & c \end{bmatrix}$.
        - Symmetric form: $f(x, y) = c x_2 y_2$.
    <2>4. **Part (d): $q(x) = 2 x_1^2 - \frac{1}{3} x_1 x_2$**
        - Matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1/6 \\ -1/6 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
        - Symmetric form: $f(x, y) = 2 x_1 y_1 - \frac{1}{6}(x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1)$.
    <2>5. **Part (e): $q(x) = x_1^2 + 9 x_2^2$**
        - Matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$.
        - Symmetric form: $f(x, y) = x_1 y_1 + 9 x_2 y_2$.
    <2>6. **Part (f): $q(x) = 3 x_1 x_2 - x_2^2$**
        - Matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 3/2 \\ 3/2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$.
        - Symmetric form: $f(x, y) = \frac{3}{2}(x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1) - x_2 y_2$.
    <2>7. **Part (g): $q(x) = 4 x_1^2 + 6 x_1 x_2 - 3 x_2^2$**
        - Matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 \\ 3 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$.
        - Symmetric form: $f(x, y) = 4 x_1 y_1 + 3(x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1) - 3 x_2 y_2$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    The symmetric bilinear forms are obtained by polarizing $x_i^2 \mapsto x_i y_i$ and $x_1 x_2 \mapsto \frac{1}{2}(x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1)$. Q.E.D.
