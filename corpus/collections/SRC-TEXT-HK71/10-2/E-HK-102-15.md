::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form $f$.
Let
$$
\mathfrak B=\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\}
$$
be a basis, let
$$
\mathfrak B'=\{\alpha'_1,\ldots,\alpha'_n\}
$$
be its $f$-dual basis, and let
$$
A=[f]_{\mathfrak B}.
$$
Show that
$$
\alpha'_i=\sum_{j=1}^n (A^{-1})_{ij}\alpha_j=\sum_{j=1}^n (A^{-1})_{ji}\alpha_j.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
\alpha'_j=\sum_{k=1}^n c_{kj}\alpha_k,
$$
and let
$$
C=(c_{kj})
$$
be the matrix whose $j$th column is the coordinate vector of $\alpha'_j$ in
$\mathfrak B$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Translate the dual-basis conditions into a matrix equation.

::: pf-proof

By definition of the Gram matrix,
$$
A_{ik}=f(\alpha_i,\alpha_k).
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha_i,\alpha'_j)
&=f\left(\alpha_i,\sum_k c_{kj}\alpha_k\right)\\
&=\sum_k A_{ik}c_{kj}\\
&=(AC)_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f$-dual-basis relation says
$$
f(\alpha_i,\alpha'_j)=\delta_{ij},
$$
so
$$
AC=I.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Solve for the coordinate matrix of the $f$-dual basis.

::: pf-proof

Non-degeneracy of $f$ implies that its Gram matrix $A$ is invertible. Hence
$$
C=A^{-1}.
$$
Thus the coefficient of $\alpha_j$ in $\alpha'_i$ is $(A^{-1})_{ji}$. Since $A$
is symmetric, $A^{-1}$ is symmetric as well, so $(A^{-1})_{ji}=(A^{-1})_{ij}$ and
$$
\boxed{
\alpha'_i=\sum_{j=1}^n(A^{-1})_{ij}\alpha_j=\sum_{j=1}^n(A^{-1})_{ji}\alpha_j.}
$$

:::
