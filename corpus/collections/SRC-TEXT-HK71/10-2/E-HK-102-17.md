::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and let $f$ be a symmetric bilinear form on $V$.
If $W$ is a subspace of $V$, define
\[
W^{\perp}=\{\alpha\in V:f(\alpha,\beta)=0\text{ for every }\beta\in W\}.
\]
Prove the following.

(a) $W^{\perp}$ is a subspace of $V$.

(b) $\{0\}^{\perp}=V$.

(c) $V^{\perp}=\{0\}$ if and only if $f$ is non-degenerate.

(d) $\operatorname{rank}f=\dim V-\dim V^{\perp}$.

(e) If $\dim V=n$ and $\dim W=m$, then
\[
\dim W^{\perp}\ge n-m.
\]

(f) The restriction of $f$ to $W$ is non-degenerate if and only if
\[
W\cap W^{\perp}=\{0\}.
\]

(g) One has
\[
V=W\oplus W^{\perp}
\]
if and only if the restriction of $f$ to $W$ is non-degenerate.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Prove that $W^\perp$ is a subspace and compute $\{0\}^\perp$.
::: {.proof}
The zero vector lies in $W^\perp$. If $x,y\in W^\perp$ and $a,b$ are
scalars, then for every $w\in W$,
$$
f(ax+by,w)=a f(x,w)+b f(y,w)=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{W^\perp\le V,}
$$
which proves (a).

For $W=\{0\}$, every vector is orthogonal to its only element, so
$$
\boxed{\{0\}^\perp=V.}
$$
This proves (b).
:::

<1>2. Identify $V^\perp$ with the radical and prove (c)--(d).
::: {.proof}
Define
$$
L_f:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
L_f(x)(y)=f(x,y).
$$
Since $f$ is symmetric,
$$
\ker L_f
=\{x:f(x,y)=0\text{ for all }y\in V\}
=V^\perp.
$$
Hence $f$ is non-degenerate exactly when $L_f$ is injective, i.e. exactly
when
$$
\boxed{V^\perp=\{0\}.}
$$
This is (c).

By definition $\operatorname{rank}f=\operatorname{rank}L_f$. Rank-nullity
therefore gives
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}f
=\dim V-\dim V^\perp.}
$$
This proves (d).
:::

<1>3. Prove the lower bound on $\dim W^\perp$.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\Phi:V\longrightarrow W^*,
\qquad
\Phi(x)(w)=f(x,w).
$$
Then
$$
\ker\Phi=W^\perp.
$$
Since $\dim W^*=m$,
$$
\operatorname{rank}\Phi\le m.
$$
Rank-nullity yields
$$
\dim W^\perp
=n-\operatorname{rank}\Phi
\ge n-m.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\dim W^\perp\ge n-m,}
$$
proving (e).
:::

<1>4. Characterize non-degeneracy of the restriction to $W$.
::: {.proof}
Let $f_W$ denote the restriction of $f$ to $W\times W$. Its radical is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rad}(f_W)
&=\{w\in W:f(w,u)=0\text{ for every }u\in W\}\\
&=W\cap W^\perp.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore $f_W$ is non-degenerate exactly when its radical is zero, i.e.
exactly when
$$
\boxed{W\cap W^\perp=\{0\}.}
$$
This proves (f).
:::

<1>5. Prove the direct-sum criterion.
::: {.proof}
Assume first that $f_W$ is non-degenerate. By step <1>4,
$$
W\cap W^\perp=\{0\}.
$$
Thus
$$
\dim(W+W^\perp)=\dim W+\dim W^\perp.
$$
Step <1>3 gives
$$
\dim W^\perp\ge n-m,
$$
so
$$
\dim(W+W^\perp)\ge m+(n-m)=n.
$$
But $W+W^\perp\le V$ and $\dim V=n$, hence equality holds and
$$
V=W+W^\perp.
$$
Together with the zero intersection this gives
$$
V=W\oplus W^\perp.
$$

Conversely, if
$$
V=W\oplus W^\perp,
$$
then necessarily
$$
W\cap W^\perp=\{0\}.
$$
Step <1>4 then shows that the restriction $f_W$ is non-degenerate.
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
V=W\oplus W^\perp
\iff
f|_{W\times W}\text{ is non-degenerate}.}
$$
This proves (g).
:::
