::: {.exercise}
Let $q$ be the quadratic form on $\RR^2$ given by
\[
q(x_1, x_2) = 2bx_1x_2.
\]
Find an invertible linear operator $U$ on $\RR^2$ such that
\[
(U^{\dagger}q)(x_1, x_2) = 2bx_1^2 - 2bx_2^2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Define
$$
U(x_1,x_2)=(x_1+x_2,\ x_1-x_2).
$$

<1>1. The operator $U$ is invertible.
::: {.proof}
Its matrix in the standard basis is
$$
[U]=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose determinant is
$$
-2\ne0.
$$
Therefore $U$ is invertible.
:::

<1>2. Compute the pulled-back quadratic form.
::: {.proof}
By definition,
$$
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2)
=q(U(x_1,x_2)).
$$
Since
$$
q(z_1,z_2)=2bz_1z_2,
$$
we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2)
&=2b(x_1+x_2)(x_1-x_2)\\
&=2b(x_1^2-x_2^2)\\
&=2bx_1^2-2bx_2^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2)=2bx_1^2-2bx_2^2.}
$$
:::
:::
