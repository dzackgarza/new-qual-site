::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field, let $V$ be the space of $n\times1$ matrices over $F$, and let
\[
f(X,Y)=X^tAY
\]
be a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form, where $A$ is an invertible symmetric $n\times n$ matrix.
Let $P$ be an invertible $n\times n$ matrix whose columns form an ordered basis
\[
\mathfrak B=\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\}
\]
of $V$.
Find the matrix whose columns are the vectors of the $f$-dual basis
\[
\mathfrak B'=\{\alpha'_1,\ldots,\alpha'_n\}.
\]
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
Q=
\begin{pmatrix}
|&&|\\
\alpha'_1&\cdots&\alpha'_n\\
|&&|
\end{pmatrix}
$$
be the unknown matrix whose columns form the $f$-dual basis.

<1>1. Express the duality equations in matrix form.
::: proof
The $(i,j)$ entry of $P^tAQ$ is
$$
\alpha_i^tA\alpha'_j
=f(\alpha_i,\alpha'_j).
$$
By definition of the $f$-dual basis, this equals $\delta_{ij}$. Therefore
$$
P^tAQ=I.
$$
:::

<1>2. Solve for the dual-basis matrix.
::: proof
Both $P$ and $A$ are invertible, so the equation from step <1>1 gives
$$
AQ=(P^t)^{-1}
$$
and hence
$$
\boxed{Q=A^{-1}(P^t)^{-1}.}
$$

Conversely, this matrix satisfies
$$
P^tA\bigl(A^{-1}(P^t)^{-1}\bigr)=I,
$$
so its columns have exactly the required pairings with the columns of $P$.
Thus it is the unique matrix of the $f$-dual basis.
:::
:::
