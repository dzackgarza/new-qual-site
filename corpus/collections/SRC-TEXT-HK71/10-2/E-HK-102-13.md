::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and let $f$ be a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on $V$.
Associated with $f$ is the natural isomorphism
\[
L_f:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
L_f(\alpha)(\beta)=f(\alpha,\beta).
\]
Using $L_f$, show that for each basis
\[
\mathfrak B=\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\}
\]
of $V$ there exists a unique basis
\[
\mathfrak B'=\{\alpha'_1,\ldots,\alpha'_n\}
\]
such that
\[
f(\alpha_i,\alpha'_j)=\delta_{ij}.
\]
Then show that every $\alpha\in V$ satisfies
\[
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^n f(\alpha,\alpha'_i)\alpha_i
=\sum_{i=1}^n f(\alpha_i,\alpha)\alpha'_i.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\mathfrak B^*=\{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\}
$$
be the ordinary dual basis in $V^*$, so
$$
\lambda_j(\alpha_i)=\delta_{ij}.
$$

<1>1. Construct the unique $f$-dual basis.
::: {.proof}
Since $L_f$ is an isomorphism, for each $j$ there is a unique vector
$\alpha'_j$ such that
$$
L_f(\alpha'_j)=\lambda_j.
$$
By symmetry of $f$,
$$
f(\alpha_i,\alpha'_j)
=f(\alpha'_j,\alpha_i)
=L_f(\alpha'_j)(\alpha_i)
=\lambda_j(\alpha_i)
=\delta_{ij}.
$$

The vectors $\alpha'_1,\ldots,\alpha'_n$ form a basis because their images
under the isomorphism $L_f$ form the basis $\mathfrak B^*$ of $V^*$.

If another basis $(\beta'_1,\ldots,\beta'_n)$ had the same pairings, then
for each $j$ the two functionals
$L_f(\beta'_j)$ and $L_f(\alpha'_j)$ agree on every basis vector
$\alpha_i$, hence are equal. Injectivity of $L_f$ gives
$\beta'_j=\alpha'_j$. Thus the $f$-dual basis is unique.
:::

<1>2. Expand a vector in the original basis using the $f$-dual basis.
::: {.proof}
Write
$$
\alpha=\sum_i c_i\alpha_i.
$$
Pairing with $\alpha'_j$ gives
$$
f(\alpha,\alpha'_j)
=\sum_i c_i f(\alpha_i,\alpha'_j)
=c_j.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^n f(\alpha,\alpha'_i)\alpha_i.}
$$
:::

<1>3. Expand a vector in the $f$-dual basis.
::: {.proof}
Write
$$
\alpha=\sum_i d_i\alpha'_i.
$$
Pairing on the left with $\alpha_j$ gives
$$
f(\alpha_j,\alpha)
=\sum_i d_i f(\alpha_j,\alpha'_i)
=d_j.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^n f(\alpha_i,\alpha)\alpha'_i.}
$$
:::
:::
