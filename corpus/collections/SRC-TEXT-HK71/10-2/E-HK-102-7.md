::: {.exercise}
Let $q$ be the quadratic form on $\RR^3$ given by
\[
q(x_1, x_2, x_3) = x_1x_2 + 2x_1x_3 + x_3^2.
\]
Find an invertible linear operator $U$ on $\RR^3$ such that
\[
(U^{\dagger}q)(x_1, x_2, x_3) = x_1^2 - x_2^2 + x_3^2.
\]
(Hint: Express $U$ as a product of operators similar to those used in Exercises 5 and 6.)
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Rewrite the quadratic form as one square plus a pure cross term.

::: pf-proof

For variables $z_1,z_2,z_3$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
q(z_1,z_2,z_3)
&=z_1z_2+2z_1z_3+z_3^2\\
&=(z_3+z_1)^2+z_1(z_2-z_1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Set
$$
a=z_1,
\qquad
b=z_2-z_1.
$$
Then
$$
ab
=\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^2
-\left(\frac{a-b}{2}\right)^2.
$$
Hence the new coordinates
$$
y_1=\frac{z_2}{2},
\qquad
y_2=z_1-\frac{z_2}{2},
\qquad
y_3=z_3+z_1
$$
give
$$
q(z_1,z_2,z_3)=y_1^2-y_2^2+y_3^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Invert the coordinate change and define $U$.

::: pf-proof

Solving the equations in step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
z_1=y_1+y_2,
\qquad
z_2=2y_1,
\qquad
z_3=y_3-y_1-y_2.
$$
Therefore define
$$
U(x_1,x_2,x_3)
=\bigl(x_1+x_2,\ 2x_1,\ x_3-x_1-x_2\bigr).
$$
Its standard matrix is
$$
[U]=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0\\
2&0&0\\
-1&-1&1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose determinant is $-2$. Hence $U$ is invertible.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Verify the required diagonal form.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
z_1=x_1+x_2,
\qquad
z_2=2x_1,
\qquad
z_3=x_3-x_1-x_2.
$$
Then
$$
z_3+z_1=x_3,
\qquad
z_2-z_1=x_1-x_2.
$$
Using the decomposition from step [](#s1){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2,x_3)
&=q(z_1,z_2,z_3)\\
&=(z_3+z_1)^2+z_1(z_2-z_1)\\
&=x_3^2+(x_1+x_2)(x_1-x_2)\\
&=x_1^2-x_2^2+x_3^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{(U^\dagger q)(x_1,x_2,x_3)=x_1^2-x_2^2+x_3^2.}
$$

:::
