::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a vector space over a subfield $F$ of the complex numbers.
Suppose $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ are linearly independent vectors in $V$.
Prove that $(\alpha + \beta)$, $(\beta + \gamma)$, and $(\gamma + \alpha)$ are linearly independent.
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §2.3 Exercise 9, p. 49.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #relation}
For $a,b,c\in F$, $a(\alpha+\beta)+b(\beta+\gamma)+c(\gamma+\alpha)=(a+c)\alpha+(a+b)\beta+(b+c)\gamma$.

::: pf-proof
Expand and collect: the coefficient of $\alpha$ is $a+c$, that of $\beta$ is $a+b$, and that of $\gamma$ is $b+c$, using distributivity of scalar multiplication over addition and commutativity of addition in $V$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #system}
If the left side of step [](#relation){.pf-ref} is $0$, then $a+c=0$, $a+b=0$ and $b+c=0$.

::: pf-proof
$\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ are linearly independent, and a linear relation among them has all its coefficients zero; step [](#relation){.pf-ref} writes the relation in the form with coefficients $a+c$, $a+b$, $b+c$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #charzero}
$2a=0$ forces $a=0$.

::: pf-proof
$F$ is a subfield of $\CC$ and so contains $\QQ$; in particular $2\neq0$ in $F$ and $2$ has an inverse, so $2a=0$ gives $a=2^{-1}\cdot0=0$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
$a=b=c=0$.

::: pf-proof
From $a+b=0$ and $a+c=0$ we get $b=-a$ and $c=-a$; then $b+c=-2a=0$, and step [](#charzero){.pf-ref} gives $a=0$, whence $b=0$ and $c=0$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
$(\alpha+\beta)$, $(\beta+\gamma)$ and $(\gamma+\alpha)$ are linearly independent.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#relation){.pf-ref} and [](#system){.pf-ref} turn an arbitrary linear relation among the three vectors into the system solved in the preceding step, so every such relation has all coefficients zero, which is linear independence.
:::
:::
