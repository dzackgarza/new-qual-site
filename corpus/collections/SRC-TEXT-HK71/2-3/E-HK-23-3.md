::: {.exercise}
Find a basis for the subspace of $\RR^4$ spanned by the four vectors of Exercise 2.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The four vectors are $\alpha_1 = (1,1,2,4)$, $\alpha_2 = (2,-1,-5,2)$, $\alpha_3 = (1,-1,-4,0)$, $\alpha_4 = (2,1,1,6)$.
Proof: from Exercise 2.

<1>2. Row-reduce the matrix with these vectors as rows:
$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 2 & -1 & -5 & 2 \\ 1 & -1 & -4 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 & 6 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Proof: set up the matrix.

<1>3. Row-reducing gives the row echelon form
$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 0 & -3 & -9 & -6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
so the rank is $2$.
Proof: $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$ gives $(0, -3, -9, -6)$; $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_1$ gives $(0, -2, -6, -4) = \frac{2}{3}(0,-3,-9,-6)$; $R_4 \leftarrow R_4 - 2R_1$ gives $(0, -1, -3, -2) = \frac{1}{3}(0,-3,-9,-6)$.

<1>4. Hence the span has dimension $2$, and a basis is $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2\}$.
Proof: <1>3 (the first two rows are linearly independent and span the row space).

<1>5. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>4.
:::
