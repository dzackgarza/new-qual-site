::: {.exercise}
Discuss Exercise 9 when $V$ is a vector space over the field with two elements described in Exercise 5, Section 1.1.
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §2.3 Exercise 13. The reference is to Exercise 2.3.9 of the same section, which asks whether $\alpha+\beta$, $\beta+\gamma$, $\gamma+\alpha$ are independent when $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ are; over $\FF_2$ they are not, and the computation below shows exactly how the answer changes.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #sumzero}

$(\alpha+\beta)+(\beta+\gamma)+(\gamma+\alpha)=0$.

::: pf-proof

In any vector space the sum expands to $2\alpha+2\beta+2\gamma$, and $2=1+1=0$ in $\FF_2$, so each coefficient vanishes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dep}

The three pairwise sums are linearly dependent.

::: pf-proof

A finite set of vectors is independent exactly when every linear combination of its members with coefficients not all zero is nonzero. Step [](#sumzero){.pf-ref} exhibits such a combination, with all three coefficients equal to $1\neq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pairs}

Any two of the pairwise sums are linearly independent.

::: pf-proof

By symmetry of the three expressions it suffices to treat $\alpha+\beta$ and $\beta+\gamma$. Let $a,b\in\FF_2$ with $a(\alpha+\beta)+b(\beta+\gamma)=0$. Expanding gives $a\alpha+(a+b)\beta+b\gamma=0$, and independence of $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ forces $a=0$, $a+b=0$ and $b=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

Over $\FF_2$ the answer to Exercise 2.3.9 is negative: three independent vectors can have dependent pairwise sums, while no two of the three sums are dependent.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#dep){.pf-ref} and [](#pairs){.pf-ref}.

:::
