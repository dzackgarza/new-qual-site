::: {.exercise}
Prove that the space of all $m \times n$ matrices over the field $F$ has dimension $mn$, by exhibiting a basis for this space.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #unit-matrices}
For $1\le i\le m$ and $1\le j\le n$ let $E_{ij}\in F^{m\times n}$ be the matrix whose $(i,j)$ entry is $1$ and whose other entries are $0$; the family $\{E_{ij}\}$ has exactly $mn$ members.

::: pf-proof
The pair $(i,j)$ ranges over a set with $m$ choices for $i$ and $n$ choices for $j$, hence $mn$ pairs, and each pair determines exactly one matrix, because a matrix is determined by its entries in all $mn$ positions.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #spanning}
Every $A\in F^{m\times n}$ satisfies $A=\sum_{i,j}A(i,j)E_{ij}$.

::: pf-proof
Fix $(p,q)$. By the definitions of matrix addition and of scalar multiplication, the $(p,q)$ entry of $\sum_{i,j}A(i,j)E_{ij}$ is $\sum_{i,j}A(i,j)E_{ij}(p,q)$, and here $E_{ij}(p,q)$ is $1$ when $(i,j)=(p,q)$ and $0$ otherwise, so the sum reduces to the single term $A(p,q)$. Two $m\times n$ matrices whose entries agree in every position are equal, so the displayed identity holds.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #independent}
If $\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}E_{ij}=0$ with $c_{ij}\in F$, then $c_{pq}=0$ for every pair $(p,q)$.

::: pf-proof
Fix $(p,q)$ and read the $(p,q)$ entry of the identity. On the right is the zero matrix, whose $(p,q)$ entry is $0$; on the left the $(p,q)$ entry is $c_{pq}$, by the entry computation of step [](#spanning){.pf-ref}. Hence $c_{pq}=0$, and since $(p,q)$ was arbitrary the claim follows.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis}
$\{E_{ij}\}$ is a basis of $F^{m\times n}$, and $\dim F^{m\times n}=mn$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#spanning){.pf-ref} makes the family a spanning set and step [](#independent){.pf-ref} makes it linearly independent, which is what a basis is; the dimension of a space with a finite basis is the number of members in such a basis, and step [](#unit-matrices){.pf-ref} counts $mn$ members in this one.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Steps [](#spanning){.pf-ref} and [](#independent){.pf-ref} exhibit the basis the exercise asks for, and step [](#basis){.pf-ref} reads off its size.
:::
