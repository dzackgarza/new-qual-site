::: {.exercise}
Prove that if two vectors are linearly dependent, one of them is a scalar multiple of the other.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $u, v$ be linearly dependent.
Proof: setup.

<1>2. Then there exist scalars $a, b$, not both zero, with $au + bv = 0$.
Proof: definition of linear dependence.

<1>3. If $a \neq 0$, then $u = -\frac{b}{a} v$, so $u$ is a scalar multiple of $v$.
Proof: solve $au + bv = 0$ for $u$.

<1>4. If $b \neq 0$, then $v = -\frac{a}{b} u$, so $v$ is a scalar multiple of $u$.
Proof: solve $au + bv = 0$ for $v$.

<1>5. Hence one of $u, v$ is a scalar multiple of the other.
Proof: <1>3 and <1>4 cover the two cases (at least one of $a, b$ is nonzero).

<1>6. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>5.
:::
