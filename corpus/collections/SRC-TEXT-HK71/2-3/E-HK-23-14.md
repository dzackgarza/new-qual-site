::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the set of real numbers.
Regard $V$ as a vector space over the field of rational numbers, with the usual operations.
Prove that this vector space is not finite-dimensional.
:::


::: solution
Suppose for contradiction that $\RR$ were finite-dimensional over $\QQ$, say with basis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$. Then every real number would have the form
\[
q_1v_1+\cdots+q_nv_n,
\qquad q_i\in\QQ.
\]
Thus the map
\[
\QQ^n\longrightarrow\RR,
\qquad
(q_1,\ldots,q_n)\longmapsto \sum_{i=1}^n q_iv_i
\]
would be surjective. But $\QQ$ is countable, so the finite Cartesian product $\QQ^n$ is countable; hence its image would be countable. This would make $\RR$ countable, contradicting the uncountability of the real numbers. Therefore $\RR$ is not finite-dimensional as a vector space over $\QQ$.
:::
