::: {.exercise}
Show that the vectors
$$
\alpha_1 = (1, 0, -1), \quad \alpha_2 = (1, 2, 1), \quad \alpha_3 = (0, -3, 2)
$$
form a basis for $\mathbb{R}^3$.
Express each of the standard basis vectors $e_1, e_2, e_3$ as linear combinations of $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove linear independence by computing the non-zero determinant of the matrix formed by $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$, and invert the matrix to express standard basis vectors.

<1>1. Basis Verification via Determinant:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Form the $3 \times 3$ matrix $A = [\alpha_1 \mid \alpha_2 \mid \alpha_3]$ whose columns are the vectors:
        $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & -3 \\ -1 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.$$
    <2>2. Compute the determinant of $A$ along the first row:
        $$\det(A) = 1 \cdot \det\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} - 1 \cdot \det\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -3 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} + 0$$
        $$\det(A) = 1(4 - (-3)) - 1(0 - 3) = 1(7) - 1(-3) = 7 + 3 = 10.$$
    <2>3. Since $\det(A) = 10 \ne 0$, the vectors $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$ are linearly independent.
    <2>4. Since $\dim(\mathbb{R}^3) = 3$, three linearly independent vectors form a **basis for $\mathbb{R}^3$**.

<1>2. Matrix Inversion to Express $e_1, e_2, e_3$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. To express $e_j = c_{1j}\alpha_1 + c_{2j}\alpha_2 + c_{3j}\alpha_3$, we find the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$:
        $$[\alpha_1 \mid \alpha_2 \mid \alpha_3] A^{-1} = I_3 \implies e_j = A (A^{-1} e_j).$$
    <2>2. We compute the matrix of cofactors of $A$:
        - $C_{11} = +(4 - (-3)) = 7$
        - $C_{12} = -(0 - 3) = 3$
        - $C_{13} = +(0 - (-2)) = 2$
        - $C_{21} = -(2 - 0) = -2$
        - $C_{22} = +(2 - 0) = 2$
        - $C_{23} = -(1 - (-1)) = -2$
        - $C_{31} = +(-3 - 0) = -3$
        - $C_{32} = -(-3 - 0) = 3$
        - $C_{33} = +(2 - 0) = 2$
    <2>3. The adjugate matrix $\operatorname{adj}(A) = C^T$:
        $$\operatorname{adj}(A) = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -2 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & -2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.$$
    <2>4. The inverse matrix is $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)} \operatorname{adj}(A)$:
        $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{10} \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -2 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & -2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.$$

<1>3. Linear Combinations for Standard Basis Vectors:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **For $e_1 = (1, 0, 0)$ (first column of $A^{-1}$):**
        $$e_1 = \frac{7}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{3}{10} \alpha_2 + \frac{2}{10} \alpha_3 = \frac{7}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{3}{10} \alpha_2 + \frac{1}{5} \alpha_3.$$
        *Check:* $\frac{7}{10}(1, 0, -1) + \frac{3}{10}(1, 2, 1) + \frac{2}{10}(0, -3, 2) = (\frac{10}{10}, \frac{6-6}{10}, \frac{-7+3+4}{10}) = (1, 0, 0)$.
    <2>2. **For $e_2 = (0, 1, 0)$ (second column of $A^{-1}$):**
        $$e_2 = -\frac{2}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{2}{10} \alpha_2 - \frac{2}{10} \alpha_3 = -\frac{1}{5} \alpha_1 + \frac{1}{5} \alpha_2 - \frac{1}{5} \alpha_3.$$
        *Check:* $-\frac{2}{10}(1, 0, -1) + \frac{2}{10}(1, 2, 1) - \frac{2}{10}(0, -3, 2) = (0, \frac{4+6}{10}, \frac{2+2-4}{10}) = (0, 1, 0)$.
    <2>3. **For $e_3 = (0, 0, 1)$ (third column of $A^{-1}$):**
        $$e_3 = -\frac{3}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{3}{10} \alpha_2 + \frac{2}{10} \alpha_3 = -\frac{3}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{3}{10} \alpha_2 + \frac{1}{5} \alpha_3.$$
        *Check:* $-\frac{3}{10}(1, 0, -1) + \frac{3}{10}(1, 2, 1) + \frac{2}{10}(0, -3, 2) = (0, \frac{6-6}{10}, \frac{3+3+4}{10}) = (0, 0, 1)$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $\det[\alpha_1 \mid \alpha_2 \mid \alpha_3] = 10 \ne 0$ confirms the basis; the standard vectors are expressed via the columns of $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{10}\begin{bmatrix} 7 & -2 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & -2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$. Q.E.D.
:::
