::: {.exercise}
Show that the vectors
$$
\alpha_1 = (1, 0, -1), \quad \alpha_2 = (1, 2, 1), \quad \alpha_3 = (0, -3, 2)
$$
form a basis for $\RR^3$.
Express each of the standard basis vectors $e_1, e_2, e_3$ as linear combinations of $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Prove linear independence by computing the non-zero determinant of the matrix formed by $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$, and invert the matrix to express standard basis vectors.

::: pf

::: pf-step
Basis Verification via Determinant:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Form the $3 \times 3$ matrix $A = [\alpha_1 \mid \alpha_2 \mid \alpha_3]$ whose columns are the vectors:
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & -3 \\ -1 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.$$

::: pf-proof
This is a definition: the columns of $A$ are the three given vectors.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Compute the determinant of $A$ along the first row:
$$\det(A) = 1 \cdot \det\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} - 1 \cdot \det\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -3 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} + 0$$
$$\det(A) = 1(4 - (-3)) - 1(0 - 3) = 1(7) - 1(-3) = 7 + 3 = 10.$$

::: pf-proof
Expanding along the first row: $1\cdot(4+3)-1\cdot(0-3)+0=7+3=10$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since $\det(A) = 10 \ne 0$, the vectors $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$ are linearly independent.

::: pf-proof
A square matrix with non-zero determinant has linearly independent columns.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since $\dim(\RR^3) = 3$, three linearly independent vectors form a **basis for $\RR^3$**.

::: pf-proof
In a space of dimension $n$, any $n$ linearly independent vectors form a basis.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Matrix Inversion to Express $e_1, e_2, e_3$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
To express $e_j = c_{1j}\alpha_1 + c_{2j}\alpha_2 + c_{3j}\alpha_3$, we find the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$:
$$[\alpha_1 \mid \alpha_2 \mid \alpha_3] A^{-1} = I_3 \implies e_j = A (A^{-1} e_j).$$

::: pf-proof
The coefficients expressing $e_j$ in the basis $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ are the entries of the $j$-th column of $A^{-1}$, since $A$ times that column is $e_j$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
We compute the matrix of cofactors of $A$:
- $C_{11} = +(4 - (-3)) = 7$
- $C_{12} = -(0 - 3) = 3$
- $C_{13} = +(0 - (-2)) = 2$
- $C_{21} = -(2 - 0) = -2$
- $C_{22} = +(2 - 0) = 2$
- $C_{23} = -(1 - (-1)) = -2$
- $C_{31} = +(-3 - 0) = -3$
- $C_{32} = -(-3 - 0) = 3$
- $C_{33} = +(2 - 0) = 2$

::: pf-proof
Each cofactor is the signed determinant of the $2\times2$ minor obtained by deleting its row and column, and the listed values are those determinants.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The adjugate matrix $\operatorname{adj}(A) = C^T$:
$$\operatorname{adj}(A) = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -2 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & -2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.$$

::: pf-proof
The adjugate is by definition the transpose of the cofactor matrix.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The inverse matrix is $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)} \operatorname{adj}(A)$:
$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{10} \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -2 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & -2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.$$

::: pf-proof
This is the standard formula expressing the inverse through the adjugate and the determinant.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Linear Combinations for Standard Basis Vectors:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
**For $e_1 = (1, 0, 0)$ (first column of $A^{-1}$):**
$$e_1 = \frac{7}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{3}{10} \alpha_2 + \frac{2}{10} \alpha_3 = \frac{7}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{3}{10} \alpha_2 + \frac{1}{5} \alpha_3.$$
*Check:* $\frac{7}{10}(1, 0, -1) + \frac{3}{10}(1, 2, 1) + \frac{2}{10}(0, -3, 2) = (\frac{10}{10}, \frac{6-6}{10}, \frac{-7+3+4}{10}) = (1, 0, 0)$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting the first column of $A^{-1}$ as the coefficients of $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
**For $e_2 = (0, 1, 0)$ (second column of $A^{-1}$):**
$$e_2 = -\frac{2}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{2}{10} \alpha_2 - \frac{2}{10} \alpha_3 = -\frac{1}{5} \alpha_1 + \frac{1}{5} \alpha_2 - \frac{1}{5} \alpha_3.$$
*Check:* $-\frac{2}{10}(1, 0, -1) + \frac{2}{10}(1, 2, 1) - \frac{2}{10}(0, -3, 2) = (0, \frac{4+6}{10}, \frac{2+2-4}{10}) = (0, 1, 0)$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting the second column of $A^{-1}$ as the coefficients.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
**For $e_3 = (0, 0, 1)$ (third column of $A^{-1}$):**
$$e_3 = -\frac{3}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{3}{10} \alpha_2 + \frac{2}{10} \alpha_3 = -\frac{3}{10} \alpha_1 + \frac{3}{10} \alpha_2 + \frac{1}{5} \alpha_3.$$
*Check:* $-\frac{3}{10}(1, 0, -1) + \frac{3}{10}(1, 2, 1) + \frac{2}{10}(0, -3, 2) = (0, \frac{6-6}{10}, \frac{3+3+4}{10}) = (0, 0, 1)$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting the third column of $A^{-1}$ as the coefficients.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Conclusion:
$\det[\alpha_1 \mid \alpha_2 \mid \alpha_3] = 10 \ne 0$ confirms the basis; the standard vectors are expressed via the columns of $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{10}\begin{bmatrix} 7 & -2 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & -2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
The non-zero determinant makes the three vectors a basis, and the three displayed combinations express $e_1,e_2,e_3$ in it.
:::
