::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the set of all $2 \times 2$ matrices $A$ with complex entries which satisfy $A_{11} + A_{22} = 0$.

(a) Show that $V$ is a vector space over the field of real numbers, with the usual operations of matrix addition and multiplication of a matrix by a scalar.

(b) Find a basis for this vector space.

(c) Let $W$ be the set of all matrices $A$ in $V$ such that $A_{21} = -\overline{A}_{12}$ (the bar denotes complex conjugation).
Prove that $W$ is a subspace of $V$ and find a basis for $W$.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. $V$ is a real vector space.
::: proof
The condition $A_{11}+A_{22}=0$ is preserved under matrix addition and multiplication by real scalars, and the zero matrix satisfies it. Thus $V$ is a real subspace of $M_2(\CC)$.
:::

<1>2. A real basis of $V$ is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}i&0\\0&-i\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}0&i\\0&0\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\i&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
::: proof
Every $A\in V$ has the form
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&-a\end{bmatrix},
\qquad a,b,c\in\CC.
\]
Writing $a,b,c$ in real and imaginary parts gives a unique real linear combination of the six displayed matrices. Hence they form a basis and $\dim_{\RR}V=6$.
:::

<1>3. $W$ is a real subspace of $V$.
::: proof
If $A,B\in W$, then
\[
(A+B)_{21}=A_{21}+B_{21}
=-\overline{A_{12}}-\overline{B_{12}}
=-\overline{(A+B)_{12}}.
\]
For $r\in\RR$,
\[
(rA)_{21}=rA_{21}=-r\overline{A_{12}}=-\overline{rA_{12}}.
\]
Thus the defining condition is preserved.
:::

<1>4. A real basis of $W$ is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}i&0\\0&-i\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}0&i\\i&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
::: proof
Write $a=x+iy$ and $b=u+iv$ with $x,y,u,v\in\RR$. Since $c=-\overline b=-u+iv$, every matrix in $W$ is uniquely
\[
x\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}
+y\begin{bmatrix}i&0\\0&-i\end{bmatrix}
+u\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{bmatrix}
+v\begin{bmatrix}0&i\\i&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Hence the four matrices form a basis.
:::
:::
