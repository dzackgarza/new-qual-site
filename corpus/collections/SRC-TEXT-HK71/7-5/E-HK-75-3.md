::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional space over a subfield of $C$.
Prove that $T$ is semi-simple if and only if the following is true: If $f$ is a polynomial and $f(T)$ is nilpotent, then $f(T) = 0$.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. If $T$ is semisimple, every nilpotent polynomial in $T$ is zero.
::: proof
By Exercise 2, $f(T)$ is semisimple for every polynomial $f$ [@HK71]. If in
addition $f(T)$ is nilpotent, then its minimal polynomial divides both a
squarefree polynomial and a power of $x$. Hence its minimal polynomial divides
$x$, so $f(T)=0$.
:::

<1>2. Conversely, the stated property forces $T$ to be semisimple.
::: proof
Let
$$
T=D+N,
\qquad DN=ND,
$$
be the semisimple--nilpotent decomposition. Hoffman--Kunze's decomposition
 theorem gives $D=g(T)$ for some polynomial $g$ [@HK71]. Therefore
$$
N=T-D=(x-g(x))(T)
$$
is itself a polynomial in $T$. By construction $N$ is nilpotent. The assumed
property therefore implies $N=0$. Hence $T=D$ is semisimple.
:::

Thus the two conditions are equivalent.
:::
