::: {.exercise}
If $N$ is a nilpotent linear operator on $V$, show that for any polynomial $f$ the semi-simple part of $f(N)$ is a scalar multiple of the identity operator ($F$ a subfield of $C$).
:::


::: solution
Write
$$
f(x)=f(0)+xq(x).
$$
Then
$$
f(N)=f(0)I+Nq(N).
$$
The scalar operator $f(0)I$ is semisimple. Also $Nq(N)$ is nilpotent: if
$N^r=0$, then, since $N$ commutes with $q(N)$,
$$
(Nq(N))^r=N^rq(N)^r=0.
$$
The two summands commute. Hence this is a semisimple--nilpotent decomposition
of $f(N)$. By uniqueness of that decomposition [@HK71], the semisimple part of
$f(N)$ is exactly
$$
\boxed{f(0)I}.
$$
:::
