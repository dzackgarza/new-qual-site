::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the complex numbers, $V$ a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and $T$ a semi-simple linear operator on $V$.
If $f$ is any polynomial over $F$, prove that $f(T)$ is semi-simple.
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. It uses the semisimplicity criterion for an operator on a finite-dimensional space over a subfield of $\CC$: $T$ is semisimple exactly when its minimal polynomial has no repeated irreducible factor.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #minimal}
$m_{T}=p_{1}\cdots p_{r}$ with $p_{1},\dots,p_{r}$ distinct monic irreducibles in $F[x]$.

::: pf-proof
$T$ is semisimple, so by the criterion its minimal polynomial is squarefree; factoring it into monic irreducibles over $F$ therefore gives a product in which each irreducible occurs to the first power. (Over a subfield of $\CC$ every irreducible factor is separable, so squarefree means exactly what is written.)
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #primary}
$V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{r}$ with $V_{i}=\ker p_{i}(T)$, and $F[\restrictionof{T}{V_{i}}]\cong F[x]/(p_{i})$ is a field.

::: pf-proof
The $p_{i}$ are pairwise coprime, so the primary decomposition theorem applies to the factorization of $m_{T}$ and gives the direct sum decomposition with $V_{i}=\ker p_{i}(T)$. Since $p_{i}$ is irreducible, $(p_{i})$ is a maximal ideal of $F[x]$ and $F[x]/(p_{i})$ is a field; the map sending a polynomial $g$ to $\restrictionof{g(T)}{V_{i}}$ has kernel $(p_{i})$, because $g(T)$ vanishes on $V_{i}=\ker p_{i}(T)$ exactly when $p_{i}\mid g$, and its image is the algebra generated by $\restrictionof{T}{V_{i}}$. So that algebra is isomorphic to the field $F[x]/(p_{i})$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #restriction}
On each $V_{i}$, the minimal polynomial of $\restrictionof{S}{V_{i}}$, where $S=f(T)$, is irreducible over $F$.

::: pf-proof
Under the isomorphism of step [](#primary){.pf-ref}, the operator $\restrictionof{S}{V_{i}}$ corresponds to multiplication by the class $\bar f$, an element of the field $F[x]/(p_{i})$; for an element $\alpha$ of a field extension of $F$, multiplication by $\alpha$ on the span of the powers of $\alpha$ has minimal polynomial equal to the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$, and $F[x]/(p_{i})$ is spanned by the powers of $\bar f$; that minimal polynomial is irreducible because it generates the kernel of the evaluation map $F[x]\to F[x]/(p_{i})$, $g\mapsto g(\bar f)$, which is a prime ideal of $F[x]$. Hence each $\restrictionof{S}{V_{i}}$ has an irreducible minimal polynomial.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lcm}
The minimal polynomial of $S=f(T)$ is the least common multiple of the minimal polynomials of the restrictions, hence is squarefree.

::: pf-proof
$V$ is the direct sum of the $V_{i}$, each invariant under $S$ because it is invariant under $T$, so a polynomial annihilates $S$ exactly when it annihilates every $\restrictionof{S}{V_{i}}$. The monic polynomial of least degree with that property is the least common multiple of the finitely many minimal polynomials of the restrictions. Each of those is irreducible by step [](#restriction){.pf-ref}, so each is squarefree, and a least common multiple of squarefree polynomials is a product of distinct irreducibles — distinct indices $i$ may give the same irreducible, or different ones, and in either case each irreducible appears to the first power only. So $m_{S}$ is squarefree.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$f(T)$ is semisimple.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#lcm){.pf-ref} the minimal polynomial of $f(T)$ has no repeated irreducible factor, so the semisimplicity criterion gives that $f(T)$ is semisimple, which is the assertion of the exercise. Nothing in the argument uses more than that $F$ is a subfield of $\CC$.
:::
