::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the complex numbers, $V$ a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and $T$ a semi-simple linear operator on $V$.
If $f$ is any polynomial over $F$, prove that $f(T)$ is semi-simple.
:::


::: {.solution}
Because $T$ is semisimple, its minimal polynomial is squarefree [@HK71]. Over
the given subfield of $\mathbb C$, write
$$
m_T=p_1\cdots p_r
$$
with the $p_i$ distinct monic irreducibles. The primary decomposition gives
$$
V=V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_r,
\qquad V_i=\ker p_i(T),
$$
and on each $V_i$ the algebra $F[T|_{V_i}]$ is a field isomorphic to
$F[x]/(p_i)$.

Let $S=f(T)$. On $V_i$, the operator $S$ is multiplication by the class of
$f$ in the field $F[x]/(p_i)$. Therefore the minimal polynomial of
$S|_{V_i}$ is the irreducible minimal polynomial over $F$ of that field
 element, hence is squarefree. The minimal polynomial of $S$ is the least
common multiple of these finitely many irreducible polynomials, so it is also
squarefree. By the semisimplicity criterion [@HK71], $S=f(T)$ is semisimple.
:::
