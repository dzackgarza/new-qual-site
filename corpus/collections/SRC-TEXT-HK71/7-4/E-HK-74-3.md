::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with entries in the field $F$ and let $f_1, \ldots, f_n$ be the diagonal entries of the normal form of $xI - A$.
For which matrices $A$ is $f_1 \neq 1$?
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §7.4 Exercise 3.

Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ matrix over $F$ with $n\geq1$, and let $f_{1},\dots,f_{n}$ be the invariant factors of $xI-A$, so that $f_{1}$ is the monic generator of the ideal of $F[x]$ generated by all the entries of $xI-A$ (the first determinantal divisor). Write $a_{ij}$ for the entries of $A$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #d1}
$f_{1}$ is the monic generator of the ideal generated by the entries $(xI-A)_{ij}=-a_{ij}$ for $i\neq j$ and $(xI-A)_{ii}=x-a_{ii}$.

::: pf-proof
In the Smith normal form of $xI-A$ the first diagonal entry $f_{1}$ is the $1$-st determinantal divisor, the monic generator of the ideal generated by all $1\times1$ minors. The $1\times1$ minors are exactly the entries of $xI-A$, and those are as displayed since $xI$ contributes $x$ on the diagonal and $0$ off it.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nondiagonal}
If $A$ has a nonzero off-diagonal entry then $f_{1}=1$.

::: pf-proof
Let $a_{ij}\neq0$ with $i\neq j$. Then $xI-A$ has the entry $-a_{ij}$, a nonzero element of $F$, hence a unit of $F[x]$ (its inverse is $-a_{ij}^{-1}$). The ideal generated by the entries then contains a unit, so it is all of $F[x]$, whose monic generator is $1$; by step [](#d1){.pf-ref}, $f_{1}=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #diagonal}
If $A$ is diagonal with entries $a_{1},\dots,a_{n}$, then $f_{1}\neq1$ exactly when $a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n}$.

::: pf-proof
Here the entries of $xI-A$ are $x-a_{1},\dots,x-a_{n}$ together with zeros, so $f_{1}$ is the monic gcd of $x-a_{1},\dots,x-a_{n}$ by step [](#d1){.pf-ref}. If $a_{i}\neq a_{j}$ then $(x-a_{i})-(x-a_{j})=a_{j}-a_{i}$ is a nonzero constant in the ideal generated by those two, so that ideal is all of $F[x]$ and the gcd of the whole family is $1$. If instead all $a_{i}=c$, the family consists of copies of $x-c$, whose monic gcd is $x-c\neq1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #forward}
If $f_{1}\neq1$ then $A=cI$ for some $c\in F$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#nondiagonal){.pf-ref}, $f_{1}\neq1$ forces every off-diagonal entry of $A$ to vanish, so $A$ is diagonal with entries $a_{1},\dots,a_{n}$; by step [](#diagonal){.pf-ref} $f_{1}\neq1$ then forces $a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n}=c$, that is $A=cI$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #backward}
If $A=cI$ then $f_{1}=x-c\neq1$.

::: pf-proof
Then $xI-A=(x-c)I$ has every entry off the diagonal equal to $0$ and every diagonal entry equal to $x-c$, so by step [](#d1){.pf-ref} $f_{1}$ is the monic generator of the ideal generated by $x-c$, namely $x-c$. This is not the constant $1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$f_{1}\neq1$ if and only if $A$ is a scalar matrix.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#forward){.pf-ref} and [](#backward){.pf-ref} are the two implications; the answer to the exercise is the class of scalar matrices, with $f_{1}=x-c$ for $A=cI$.
:::
