::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with entries in the field $F$ and let $f_1, \ldots, f_n$ be the diagonal entries of the normal form of $xI - A$.
For which matrices $A$ is $f_1 \neq 1$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Exactly the scalar matrices have $f_1\ne1$:
$$
\boxed{f_1\ne1\iff A=cI\text{ for some }c\in F.}
$$

Indeed, the first determinantal divisor is the gcd of the $1\times1$ minors,
i.e. of all entries of $xI-A$, and in Smith normal form this gcd is $f_1$ up to
a unit [@HK71]. If some off-diagonal entry $a_{ij}$ is nonzero, then
$xI-A$ contains the nonzero constant $-a_{ij}$, a unit of $F[x]$, so
$f_1=1$. Hence $f_1\ne1$ forces $A$ to be diagonal.

For a diagonal matrix $A=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$, the nonzero
entries of $xI-A$ are $x-a_1,\ldots,x-a_n$. Their gcd is nonconstant only if
all these monic linear polynomials coincide, i.e. only if
$a_1=\cdots=a_n=c$. In that case $A=cI$ and every nonzero entry of
$xI-A$ equals $x-c$, so $f_1=x-c\ne1$. This proves both directions.
:::
