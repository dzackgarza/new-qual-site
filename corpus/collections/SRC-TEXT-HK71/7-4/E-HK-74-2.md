::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space and let $A$ be the matrix of $T$ in some ordered basis.
Then $T$ has a cyclic vector if and only if the determinants of the $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ submatrices of $xI - A$ are relatively prime.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. It uses the Smith normal form of the polynomial matrix $xI-A$ over $F[x]$, whose diagonal entries are the invariant factors $f_{1}\mid f_{2}\mid\cdots\mid f_{n}$, taken monic, with $f_{n}$ the minimal polynomial of $T$ and $f_{1}\cdots f_{n}$ the characteristic polynomial. These facts are standard for the matrix $xI-A$ over a Euclidean domain and are not re-derived here.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #divisor}
The gcd of the determinants of the $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ submatrices of $xI-A$ is, up to a nonzero scalar, $f_{1}\cdots f_{n-1}$.

::: pf-proof
For a polynomial matrix in Smith form with diagonal entries $f_{1},\dots,f_{n}$ and invariant factors in a divisibility chain, the determinantal divisors satisfy $\Delta_{k}=f_{1}\cdots f_{k}$ up to a unit, where $\Delta_{k}$ is the gcd of the $k\times k$ minors. Taking $k=n-1$ gives the displayed identity, and multiplication by an invertible matrix over $F[x]$, which is how $xI-A$ is reduced to Smith form, multiplies the $k\times k$ minors by units only, so the gcd is unchanged up to a unit.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #units}
The $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ minors are relatively prime exactly when $f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n-1}=1$.

::: pf-proof
Their gcd is $f_{1}\cdots f_{n-1}$ up to a nonzero scalar by step [](#divisor){.pf-ref}, so they are relatively prime exactly when $f_{1}\cdots f_{n-1}$ is a nonzero constant, that is a unit of $F[x]$. Since the $f_{i}$ are monic and $f_{1}\mid\cdots\mid f_{n-1}$, the product is a unit exactly when each factor is a unit, and a monic unit is $1$; so the condition is $f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n-1}=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cyclic}
$T$ has a cyclic vector exactly when $f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n-1}=1$.

::: pf-proof
By the rational canonical form, $V$ decomposes as a direct sum of $T$-cyclic subspaces whose annihilators are the nonunit invariant factors, and $f_{n}$ is the minimal polynomial. If some $f_{j}$ with $j<n$ were a nonunit, the decomposition would have at least two summands, so no single vector could generate $V$; if instead $f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n-1}=1$, there is a single summand, of dimension $\deg f_{n}=n$, and any vector generating it is cyclic for $T$. So a cyclic vector exists exactly when the only possible nonunit invariant factor is $f_{n}$, that is exactly when $f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n-1}=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$T$ has a cyclic vector if and only if the determinants of the $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ submatrices of $xI-A$ are relatively prime.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#units){.pf-ref} and [](#cyclic){.pf-ref} give, for the same condition $f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n-1}=1$, both the relative primality of those minors and the existence of a cyclic vector; hence the two conditions are equivalent, which is the assertion. The matrix $A$ enters only through $xI-A$, so the criterion is independent of the ordered basis chosen for $T$.
:::
