::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space and let $A$ be the matrix of $T$ in some ordered basis.
Then $T$ has a cyclic vector if and only if the determinants of the $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ submatrices of $xI - A$ are relatively prime.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
f_1\mid f_2\mid\cdots\mid f_n
$$
be the invariant factors of the polynomial matrix $xI-A$, with units included
when necessary. The Smith-normal-form determinantal-divisor theorem gives
$$
\Delta_{n-1}:=\gcd\{\det M:M\text{ an }(n-1)\times(n-1)
\text{ submatrix of }xI-A\}
=f_1\cdots f_{n-1}
$$
up to a nonzero scalar [@HK71]. Therefore the indicated minors are relatively
prime exactly when
$$
f_1\cdots f_{n-1}=1.
$$
Because the $f_i$ are monic and satisfy the divisibility chain, this is
equivalent to
$$
f_1=\cdots=f_{n-1}=1.
$$

By the rational canonical form, the nonunit invariant factors are precisely the
annihilators of the cyclic summands in the cyclic decomposition of $V$ under
$T$ [@HK71]. Thus $T$ has a cyclic vector exactly when there is a single
nonunit invariant factor, necessarily $f_n$; equivalently, exactly when
$f_1=\cdots=f_{n-1}=1$. Combining the two equivalences proves the claim.
:::
