::: {.exercise}
True or false?
Every matrix in $F[x]^{n \times n}$ is row-equivalent to an upper-triangular matrix.
:::

::: {.solution}
The assertion is $\boxed{\text{true}}$.

We prove it by induction on $n$. The case $n=1$ is immediate. Let
$A\in M_n(F[x])$. Since $F[x]$ is a Euclidean domain, elementary row
operations can replace the entries in the first column by their greatest common
divisor $d$ in the first position and zeros below it: repeated Euclidean
division on pairs of entries gives exactly the usual gcd algorithm, implemented
by invertible elementary row operations [@HK71]. Thus $A$ is row-equivalent to
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
d&*\\
0&B
\end{pmatrix},
$$
where $B$ is an $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ matrix over $F[x]$. By the induction
hypothesis there is an invertible product of elementary row operations on the
last $n-1$ rows taking $B$ to an upper-triangular matrix $U$. Applying those
same operations to the full matrix leaves the zeros in the first column below
$d$ unchanged and gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
d&*\\
0&U
\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is upper triangular. Hence every matrix over $F[x]$ is row-equivalent to
an upper-triangular one.
:::
