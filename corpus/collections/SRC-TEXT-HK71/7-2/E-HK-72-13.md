::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with complex entries.
Prove that if every characteristic value of $A$ is real, then $A$ is similar to a matrix with real entries.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
R=\operatorname{diag}(C(p_1),\ldots,C(p_r))
$$
be the rational canonical form of $A$ over $\mathbb C$, with invariant factors
$p_i\in\mathbb C[x]$ [@HK71].

<1>1. Every invariant factor has real coefficients.
::: {.proof}
Each invariant factor $p_i$ divides the characteristic polynomial of $A$.
Over $\mathbb C$, it therefore factors as
$$
p_i(x)=\prod_j(x-\lambda_j)^{m_j}
$$
for characteristic values $\lambda_j$ of $A$. By hypothesis every
$\lambda_j$ is real. Since $p_i$ is monic, the displayed product has real
coefficients. Hence
$$
p_i\in\mathbb R[x]
$$
for every $i$.
:::

<1>2. The rational form is a real matrix.
::: {.proof}
The companion matrix of a monic polynomial has entries consisting only of
$0$, $1$, and the negatives of its coefficients. Since every $p_i$ has real
coefficients, every block $C(p_i)$ has real entries. Therefore
$$
R\in M_n(\mathbb R).
$$
By definition of rational form there is some $P\in GL_n(\mathbb C)$ such that
$$
P^{-1}AP=R.
$$
Thus $A$ is similar over $\mathbb C$ to the real matrix $R$, proving the
claim.
:::
:::
