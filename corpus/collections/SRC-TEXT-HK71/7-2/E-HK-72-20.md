::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over the field $F$, and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
We ask when it is true that every non-zero vector in $V$ is a cyclic vector for $T$.
Prove that this is the case if and only if the characteristic polynomial for $T$ is irreducible over $F$.
:::

::: solution
Write $n=\dim V$ and let $\chi_T$ be the characteristic polynomial.

<1>1. If $\chi_T$ is irreducible, every nonzero vector is cyclic.
::: proof
Let $0\ne\alpha\in V$, and let $p_\alpha$ be its monic $T$-annihilator.
The minimal polynomial $m_T$ annihilates $\alpha$, so
$$
p_\alpha\mid m_T\mid\chi_T.
$$
The polynomial $p_\alpha$ is nonconstant, since the constant polynomial $1$
does not annihilate a nonzero vector. Because $\chi_T$ is irreducible, the
divisibility chain forces
$$
p_\alpha=\chi_T.
$$
Thus
$$
\dim Z(\alpha;T)=\deg p_\alpha=\deg\chi_T=n
$$
[@HK71]. Hence $Z(\alpha;T)=V$, so $\alpha$ is cyclic.
:::

<1>2. If every nonzero vector is cyclic, $\chi_T$ is irreducible.
::: proof
Choose any nonzero $\alpha$. By hypothesis it is cyclic, so its annihilator is
the minimal polynomial and has degree $n$. Since the characteristic polynomial
is also monic of degree $n$ and is divisible by the minimal polynomial,
$$
m_T=\chi_T.
$$

Suppose $\chi_T$ were reducible. Write
$$
\chi_T=fg
$$
with monic polynomials $f,g$ of positive degree strictly less than $n$.
Because the annihilator of the cyclic vector $\alpha$ is exactly $\chi_T$,
$$
\beta=f(T)\alpha\ne0;
$$
otherwise the lower-degree polynomial $f$ would annihilate $\alpha$.
But
$$
g(T)\beta=(gf)(T)\alpha=\chi_T(T)\alpha=0.
$$
Thus the annihilator of $\beta$ has degree at most $\deg g<n$, so
$$
\dim Z(\beta;T)<n.
$$
Therefore the nonzero vector $\beta$ is not cyclic, contradicting the
hypothesis. Hence $\chi_T$ is irreducible.
:::

<1>3. Conclusion.
::: proof
The two steps prove
$$
\boxed{\text{every nonzero vector is cyclic}
\iff \chi_T\text{ is irreducible over }F.}
$$
:::
:::
