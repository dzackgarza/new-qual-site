::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the field of complex numbers, and let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $F$.
Let $p$ be the minimal polynomial for $A$.
If we regard $A$ as a matrix over $C$, then $A$ has a minimal polynomial $f$ as an $n \times n$ matrix over $C$.
Use a theorem on linear equations to prove $p = f$.
Can you also see how this follows from the cyclic decomposition theorem?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $p\in F[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $A$ over $F$, and let
$f\in\mathbb C[x]$ be its minimal polynomial after scalar extension.

<1>1. A theorem on linear equations forces $\deg f\geq\deg p$.
::: {.proof}
Since $p(A)=0$ remains true over $\mathbb C$, minimality of $f$ gives
$$
f\mid p
$$
in $\mathbb C[x]$. Hence $\deg f\leq\deg p$.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $m=\deg f<d=\deg p$. Since $f$ is
monic, write
$$
f(x)=x^m+c_{m-1}x^{m-1}+\cdots+c_0.
$$
The equation $f(A)=0$ says that the entries of the matrices
$I,A,\ldots,A^m$ satisfy a finite system of linear equations over $F$ for the
unknowns $c_0,\ldots,c_{m-1}$, with right side $-A^m$. This system has a
solution over $\mathbb C$.

Row reduction of an augmented matrix with entries in $F$ uses only field
operations in $F$. Therefore a linear system over $F$ that is consistent over
an extension field is already consistent over $F$ [@HK71]. Hence there exist
$b_0,\ldots,b_{m-1}\in F$ such that
$$
A^m+b_{m-1}A^{m-1}+\cdots+b_0I=0.
$$
This gives a monic annihilating polynomial over $F$ of degree $m<d$,
contradicting minimality of $p$. Thus $\deg f\ge d$.
:::

<1>2. The two monic minimal polynomials are equal.
::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 and the divisibility $f\mid p$ give
$$
\deg f=\deg p.
$$
Both are monic, so divisibility at equal degree forces
$$
\boxed{f=p}.
$$
In particular the complex minimal polynomial actually has coefficients in
$F$.
:::

<1>3. The cyclic decomposition theorem gives the same conclusion.
::: {.proof}
Apply the cyclic decomposition theorem over $F$. In a suitable $F$-basis, the
matrix of $A$ is the rational form
$$
\operatorname{diag}(C(p_1),\ldots,C(p_r)),
\qquad p_r\mid\cdots\mid p_1,
$$
and the largest invariant factor $p_1$ is the minimal polynomial $p$ [@HK71].
The same basis and same block matrix remain valid over $\mathbb C$, and the
same divisibility relations hold in $\mathbb C[x]$. Hence this is also a
rational form over $\mathbb C$. Its largest invariant factor is still $p_1$,
so the complex minimal polynomial is $p_1=p$.
:::
