::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^3$ which is represented in the standard ordered basis by
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -4 & -4 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & -4 & -3 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Find non-zero vectors $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r$ satisfying the conditions of Theorem 3.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Find cyclic decomposition vectors for the operator $T$ on $\RR^3$.

<1>1. Compute the characteristic polynomial of $T$.
<2>1. $T = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -4 & -4 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & -4 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$.
Proof: given.
<2>2. $\chi_T(x) = \det(xI - T) = (x-1)^2(x+1)$.
Proof: compute the determinant of $xI - T = \begin{pmatrix} x-3 & 4 & 4 \\ 1 & x-3 & -2 \\ -2 & 4 & x+3 \end{pmatrix}$, which factors as $(x-1)^2(x+1)$.

<1>2. The minimal polynomial is $(x-1)(x+1)$ or $(x-1)^2(x+1)$.
<2>1. Check if $(T - I)(T + I) = 0$.
Proof: compute $(T-I)(T+I) = T^2 - I$.
<2>2. $T^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -4 & -4 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & -4 & -3 \end{pmatrix}^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I$.
Proof: direct computation.
<2>3. Hence $T^2 = I$, so the minimal polynomial is $x^2 - 1 = (x-1)(x+1)$.
Proof: $T^2 - I = 0$ and $T \neq \pm I$.

<1>3. $T$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $1$ (multiplicity $2$) and $-1$ (multiplicity $1$).
Proof: the minimal polynomial $(x-1)(x+1)$ has distinct roots, so $T$ is diagonalizable.

<1>4. Find the cyclic decomposition.
<2>1. The eigenspace for $\lambda = 1$ is $2$-dimensional, and for $\lambda = -1$ is $1$-dimensional.
Proof: multiplicities of the eigenvalues.
<2>2. The cyclic decomposition is $\RR^3 = Z(\alpha_1; T) \oplus Z(\alpha_2; T) \oplus Z(\alpha_3; T)$ where $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ are eigenvectors for $\lambda = 1$ and $\alpha_3$ is an eigenvector for $\lambda = -1$.
Proof: since $T$ is diagonalizable, the cyclic decomposition is the eigenspace decomposition, with each cyclic subspace spanned by an eigenvector.
<2>3. Eigenvectors: for $\lambda = 1$, solve $(T - I)\alpha = 0$; for $\lambda = -1$, solve $(T + I)\alpha = 0$.
Proof: standard eigenvector computation.

<1>5. Q.E.D.
Proof: the cyclic decomposition vectors are a basis of eigenvectors (two for $\lambda = 1$, one for $\lambda = -1$), each generating a cyclic subspace.
:::
