::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^3$ which is represented in the standard ordered basis by
$$
\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -4 & -4 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & -4 & -3 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Find non-zero vectors $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r$ satisfying the conditions of Theorem 3.
:::

::: {.solution}
Theorem 3 asks for nonzero vectors $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r$ with
$\RR^3=Z(\alpha_1;T)\oplus\cdots\oplus Z(\alpha_r;T)$ whose $T$-annihilators
$p_i$ satisfy $p_{i+1}\mid p_i$ [@HK71].

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$\chi_T=(x-1)^3$ and $m_T=(x-1)^2$.

::: pf-proof

Put $N=T-I$. Then
$$
N=\begin{pmatrix}2&-4&-4\\-1&2&2\\2&-4&-4\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Every row of $N$ is a multiple of $(1,-2,-2)$, so $N$ has rank one and
$N\ne0$. Its image is spanned by $(2,-1,2)$, and
$(1,-2,-2)\cdot(2,-1,2)=2+2-4=0$, so $N$ kills its own image: $N^2=0$.
Hence $m_T=(x-1)^2$. The only characteristic value is $1$, so
$\chi_T=(x-1)^3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

$\alpha_1=\epsilon_1$ has $T$-annihilator $(x-1)^2$.

::: pf-proof

$N\epsilon_1=(2,-1,2)\neq0$ and $N^2=0$, so the $T$-annihilator of
$\epsilon_1$ is $(x-1)^2$ and
$Z(\epsilon_1;T)=\operatorname{span}\{\epsilon_1,(2,-1,2)\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

$\alpha_2=(2,1,0)$ has $T$-annihilator $x-1$ and completes the decomposition.

::: pf-proof

$N(2,1,0)=(4-4,-2+2,4-4)=0$, so $T\alpha_2=\alpha_2$ and
$Z(\alpha_2;T)=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_2\}$. The vectors
$\epsilon_1,(2,-1,2),(2,1,0)$ form a basis, since
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2\\0&-1&1\\0&2&0\end{pmatrix}=-2\neq0.
$$
Hence $\RR^3=Z(\alpha_1;T)\oplus Z(\alpha_2;T)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $r=2$, $\alpha_1=\epsilon_1$, $\alpha_2=(2,1,0)$ satisfy Theorem 3, with
$p_1=(x-1)^2$ and $p_2=x-1\mid p_1$.

:::
