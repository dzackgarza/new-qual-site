::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Suppose that

(a) the minimal polynomial for $T$ is a power of an irreducible polynomial;

(b) the minimal polynomial is equal to the characteristic polynomial.

Show that no non-trivial $T$-invariant subspace has a complementary $T$-invariant subspace.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let the common minimal and characteristic polynomial be
$$
m_T(x)=\chi_T(x)=p(x)^s,
$$
where $p$ is monic irreducible. Since the minimal and characteristic
polynomials coincide, $T$ has a cyclic vector $\alpha$ [@HK71]. Thus
$$
V=Z(\alpha;T)
$$
and the $T$-annihilator of $\alpha$ is $p^s$.

<1>1. Every $T$-invariant subspace is a power-of-$p$ submodule.
::: {.proof}
Let $W\subseteq V$ be $T$-invariant and define
$$
I_W=\{f\in F[x]:f(T)\alpha\in W\}.
$$
Because $W$ is a subspace invariant under $T$, it is invariant under every
polynomial in $T$. Hence $I_W$ is an ideal of $F[x]$. It contains
$(p^s)$, because $p(T)^s\alpha=0\in W$.

Since $F[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, write $I_W=(q)$. The containment
$(p^s)\subseteq(q)$ means $q\mid p^s$. Irreducibility of $p$ gives, after
choosing the monic generator,
$$
q=p^j
$$
for some $0\le j\le s$. Moreover every vector in $V$ is $f(T)\alpha$, so by
definition of $I_W$,
$$
W=\{f(T)\alpha:f\in I_W\}=p(T)^jV.
$$
Thus the invariant subspaces are exactly the spaces $p(T)^jV$.
:::

<1>2. Any two nonzero invariant subspaces have nonzero intersection.
::: {.proof}
The subspace $p(T)^jV$ is zero exactly when $j=s$. Indeed
$p(T)^{s-1}\alpha\ne0$, because the annihilator of $\alpha$ is exactly
$p^s$.

Take nonzero invariant subspaces
$$
W=p(T)^jV,
\qquad
W'=p(T)^kV,
$$
so $j,k<s$. Then
$$
p(T)^{\max(j,k)}V\subseteq W\cap W'.
$$
Because $\max(j,k)<s$, this contained subspace is nonzero. Hence
$$
W\cap W'\ne\{0\}.
$$
:::

<1>3. No proper nonzero invariant subspace has an invariant complement.
::: {.proof}
Let $0\ne W\ne V$ be $T$-invariant. If
$$
V=W\oplus W'
$$
with $W'$ also invariant, then $W'\ne0$ because $W\ne V$. But step <1>2
gives $W\cap W'\ne0$, contradicting directness. Therefore no nontrivial
$T$-invariant subspace admits a complementary $T$-invariant subspace.
:::
:::
