::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with real entries.
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^n$ which is represented by $A$ in the standard ordered basis, and let $U$ be the linear operator on $C^n$ which is represented by $A$ in the standard ordered basis.
Use the result of Exercise 20 to prove the following: If the only subspaces invariant under $T$ are $\RR^n$ and the zero subspace, then $U$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Every nonzero real vector is cyclic for $T$.
::: proof
Let $0\ne v\in\mathbb R^n$. Its cyclic subspace
$$
Z(v;T)=\operatorname{span}\{v,Tv,T^2v,\ldots\}
$$
is nonzero and $T$-invariant. By hypothesis the only such subspaces are
$0$ and $\mathbb R^n$. Therefore
$$
Z(v;T)=\mathbb R^n.
$$
So every nonzero vector is cyclic.
:::

<1>2. The real characteristic polynomial is irreducible and has degree at most two.
::: proof
By Exercise 20, step <1>1 implies that the characteristic polynomial
$\chi_A\in\mathbb R[x]$ is irreducible [@HK71]. Every irreducible polynomial
over $\mathbb R$ has degree one or two: a polynomial of odd degree has a real
root, while after removing real linear factors any nonreal complex root occurs
with its conjugate and gives a real quadratic factor. Hence
$$
n=\deg\chi_A\le2.
$$
:::

<1>3. Over $\mathbb C$, the characteristic polynomial has distinct roots.
::: proof
If $n=1$, the complex operator $U$ is already represented by a $1\times1$
matrix and is diagonal.

If $n=2$, irreducibility over $\mathbb R$ means $\chi_A$ has no real root.
Its two complex roots are therefore a nonreal conjugate pair
$$
\lambda,\overline\lambda,
$$
which are distinct. Thus $U$ has two distinct characteristic values on the
two-dimensional complex vector space $\mathbb C^2$. Eigenvectors belonging to
distinct characteristic values are linearly independent, so they form a basis
of $\mathbb C^2$ [@HK71]. Hence $U$ is diagonalizable.

Thus in all cases
$$
\boxed{U\text{ is diagonalizable over }\mathbb C.}
$$
:::
:::
