::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the field of complex numbers, and let $A$ and $B$ be $n \times n$ matrices over $F$.
Prove that if $A$ and $B$ are similar over the field of complex numbers, then they are similar over $F$.
(Hint: Prove that the rational form of $A$ is the same whether $A$ is viewed as a matrix over $F$ or a matrix over $C$; likewise for $B$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Rational form is unchanged by extending scalars from $F$ to $\mathbb C$.
::: {.proof}
Let $A\in M_n(F)$. Its rational canonical form over $F$ is a block diagonal
matrix
$$
R_A=\operatorname{diag}(C(p_1),\ldots,C(p_r)),
$$
where the monic invariant factors satisfy
$$
p_r\mid p_{r-1}\mid\cdots\mid p_1
$$
in the convention of Hoffman--Kunze, and $C(p_i)$ denotes the companion matrix
of $p_i$ [@HK71]. There is $P\in GL_n(F)$ with
$$
P^{-1}AP=R_A.
$$

Regard the same equality over $\mathbb C$. The polynomials $p_i$ still have
the same divisibility relations in $\mathbb C[x]$, and the same companion
blocks therefore satisfy the defining conditions for rational form over
$\mathbb C$. By uniqueness of the rational canonical form, $R_A$ is also the
rational form of $A$ when $A$ is viewed as a complex matrix.
:::

<1>2. Complex similarity forces equality of the two $F$-rational forms.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $A$ and $B$ are similar over $\mathbb C$. Similar matrices have the
same rational canonical form over the ground field [@HK71]. Hence their
complex rational forms coincide. By step <1>1, those are exactly the rational
forms $R_A$ and $R_B$ already obtained over $F$. Thus
$$
R_A=R_B.
$$
Choose $P,Q\in GL_n(F)$ with
$$
P^{-1}AP=R_A,
\qquad
Q^{-1}BQ=R_B.
$$
Then
$$
B=Q R_AQ^{-1}=QP^{-1}APQ^{-1}.
$$
Since $QP^{-1}\in GL_n(F)$, this is a similarity over $F$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{A\sim_{\mathbb C}B\Longrightarrow A\sim_F B.}
$$
:::
