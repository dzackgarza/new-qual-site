::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the complex numbers and let $T$ be the linear operator on $F^4$ which is represented in the standard ordered basis by the matrix
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & a & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & b & 2 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Find the characteristic polynomial for $T$.
Consider the cases $a = b = 1$; $a = b = 0$; $a = 0$, $b = 1$.
In each of these cases, find the minimal polynomial for $T$ and non-zero vectors $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r$ which satisfy the conditions of Theorem 3.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
N=T-2I.
$$
For the standard basis $e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4$,
$$
Ne_1=e_2,
\qquad
Ne_2=ae_3,
\qquad
Ne_3=be_4,
\qquad
Ne_4=0.
$$
Because the matrix of $T$ is triangular with diagonal entries all equal to
$2$,
$$
\boxed{\chi_T(x)=(x-2)^4}
$$
in every case [@HK71].

Theorem 3 asks for nonzero vectors $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r$ such that
$$
F^4=Z(\alpha_1;T)\oplus\cdots\oplus Z(\alpha_r;T)
$$
and, if $p_i$ is the $T$-annihilator of $\alpha_i$, then
$p_{i+1}\mid p_i$ [@HK71].

<1>1. Case $a=b=1$.
::: {.proof}
Here
$$
Ne_1=e_2,
\quad N^2e_1=e_3,
\quad N^3e_1=e_4,
\quad N^4e_1=0.
$$
Thus
$$
Z(e_1;T)=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\}=F^4.
$$
Since $N^3e_1\ne0$ but $N^4e_1=0$, the $T$-annihilator of $e_1$ is
$(x-2)^4$. Hence
$$
\boxed{m_T(x)=(x-2)^4,\qquad r=1,\qquad \alpha_1=e_1.}
$$
:::

<1>2. Case $a=b=0$.
::: {.proof}
Now
$$
Ne_1=e_2,
\qquad
Ne_2=Ne_3=Ne_4=0.
$$
Therefore $N^2=0$ but $N\ne0$, so
$$
\boxed{m_T(x)=(x-2)^2.}
$$
Take
$$
\alpha_1=e_1,
\qquad
\alpha_2=e_3,
\qquad
\alpha_3=e_4.
$$
Then
$$
Z(e_1;T)=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\},
\qquad
Z(e_3;T)=\operatorname{span}\{e_3\},
\qquad
Z(e_4;T)=\operatorname{span}\{e_4\}.
$$
These subspaces form a direct sum equal to $F^4$. Their annihilators are
$$
p_1=(x-2)^2,
\qquad
p_2=p_3=x-2,
$$
and indeed $p_2\mid p_1$ and $p_3\mid p_2$.
:::

<1>3. Case $a=0$, $b=1$.
::: {.proof}
Here
$$
Ne_1=e_2,
\qquad
Ne_2=0,
\qquad
Ne_3=e_4,
\qquad
Ne_4=0.
$$
Again $N^2=0$ and $N\ne0$, so
$$
\boxed{m_T(x)=(x-2)^2.}
$$
Choose
$$
\alpha_1=e_1,
\qquad
\alpha_2=e_3.
$$
Then
$$
Z(e_1;T)=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\},
\qquad
Z(e_3;T)=\operatorname{span}\{e_3,e_4\},
$$
so
$$
F^4=Z(e_1;T)\oplus Z(e_3;T).
$$
Both vectors have $T$-annihilator $(x-2)^2$, so the divisibility condition is
also satisfied. Hence one may take
$$
\boxed{r=2,\qquad \alpha_1=e_1,\ \alpha_2=e_3.}
$$
:::
