::: {.exercise}
Prove that if $A$ and $B$ are $3 \times 3$ matrices over the field $F$, a necessary and sufficient condition that $A$ and $B$ be similar over $F$ is that they have the same characteristic polynomial and the same minimal polynomial.
Give an example which shows that this is false for $4 \times 4$ matrices.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

($\Rightarrow$) If $A$ and $B$ are similar, they have the same characteristic and minimal polynomials.

::: pf-proof

similar matrices have the same characteristic polynomial and the same minimal polynomial (both are similarity invariants).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

($\Leftarrow$) For $3 \times 3$ matrices, the characteristic and minimal polynomials together determine the rational canonical form.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

The rational canonical form is determined by the invariant factors $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$ with $d_1 \cdots d_k = \chi$ (the characteristic polynomial) and $d_k = m$ (the minimal polynomial).

::: pf-proof

the invariant factors determine the rational canonical form, and their product is the characteristic polynomial while the largest is the minimal polynomial.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For a $3 \times 3$ matrix, $\deg \chi = 3$, so the invariant factors have degrees summing to $3$; the minimal polynomial $m$ is the largest invariant factor.

::: pf-proof

degree count.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-3}

Given $\chi$ and $m$ (with $m \mid \chi$), the invariant factors are uniquely determined: if $\deg m = 3$, then the single invariant factor is $m = \chi$; if $\deg m = 2$, then the invariant factors are $d_1 = \chi/m$ (degree $1$) and $d_2 = m$ (degree $2$); if $\deg m = 1$, then $m = x - \lambda$ and the invariant factors are three linear factors (determined by $\chi$).

::: pf-proof

enumerate the possible degree decompositions of $3$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $A$ and $B$ have the same invariant factors, so the same rational canonical form, so they are similar.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Counterexample for $4 \times 4$: let $A$ have invariant factors $(x, x, x^2)$ and $B$ have invariant factors $(x^2, x^2)$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

Both have characteristic polynomial $x^4$ and minimal polynomial $x^2$.

::: pf-proof

the product of invariant factors is $x^4$ in both cases, and the largest invariant factor is $x^2$ in both cases.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

But $A$ and $B$ are not similar.

::: pf-proof

they have different invariant factors ($(x, x, x^2)$ versus $(x^2, x^2)$), hence different rational canonical forms.

:::
