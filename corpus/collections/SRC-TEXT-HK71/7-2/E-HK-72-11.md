::: {.exercise}
Prove that if $A$ and $B$ are $3 \times 3$ matrices over the field $F$, a necessary and sufficient condition that $A$ and $B$ be similar over $F$ is that they have the same characteristic polynomial and the same minimal polynomial.
Give an example which shows that this is false for $4 \times 4$ matrices.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. ($\Rightarrow$) If $A$ and $B$ are similar, they have the same characteristic and minimal polynomials.
::: {.proof}
similar matrices have the same characteristic polynomial and the same minimal polynomial (both are similarity invariants).
:::

<1>2. ($\Leftarrow$) For $3 \times 3$ matrices, the characteristic and minimal polynomials together determine the rational canonical form.
<2>1. The rational canonical form is determined by the invariant factors $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$ with $d_1 \cdots d_k = \chi$ (the characteristic polynomial) and $d_k = m$ (the minimal polynomial).
::: {.proof}
the invariant factors determine the rational canonical form, and their product is the characteristic polynomial while the largest is the minimal polynomial.
:::
<2>2. For a $3 \times 3$ matrix, $\deg \chi = 3$, so the invariant factors have degrees summing to $3$; the minimal polynomial $m$ is the largest invariant factor.
::: {.proof}
degree count.
:::
<2>3. Given $\chi$ and $m$ (with $m \mid \chi$), the invariant factors are uniquely determined: if $\deg m = 3$, then the single invariant factor is $m = \chi$; if $\deg m = 2$, then the invariant factors are $d_1 = \chi/m$ (degree $1$) and $d_2 = m$ (degree $2$); if $\deg m = 1$, then $m = x - \lambda$ and the invariant factors are three linear factors (determined by $\chi$).
::: {.proof}
enumerate the possible degree decompositions of $3$.
:::
<2>4. Hence $A$ and $B$ have the same invariant factors, so the same rational canonical form, so they are similar.
::: {.proof}
<2>3.
:::

<1>3. Counterexample for $4 \times 4$: let $A$ have invariant factors $(x, x, x^2)$ and $B$ have invariant factors $(x^2, x^2)$.
<2>1. Both have characteristic polynomial $x^4$ and minimal polynomial $x^2$.
::: {.proof}
the product of invariant factors is $x^4$ in both cases, and the largest invariant factor is $x^2$ in both cases.
:::
<2>2. But $A$ and $B$ are not similar.
::: {.proof}
they have different invariant factors ($(x, x, x^2)$ versus $(x^2, x^2)$), hence different rational canonical forms.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1, <1>2, and <1>3.
:::
:::
