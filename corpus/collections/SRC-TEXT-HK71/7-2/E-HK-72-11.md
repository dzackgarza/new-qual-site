::: {.exercise}
Prove that if $A$ and $B$ are $3 \times 3$ matrices over the field $F$, a necessary and sufficient condition that $A$ and $B$ be similar over $F$ is that they have the same characteristic polynomial and the same minimal polynomial.
Give an example which shows that this is false for $4 \times 4$ matrices.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §7.2 Exercise 11 on printed p. 242. The argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #forward}
If $A$ and $B$ are similar, they have the same characteristic and minimal polynomials.

::: pf-proof
Let $B=P^{-1}AP$. Then $xI-B=P^{-1}(xI-A)P$, so $\det(xI-B)=\det(xI-A)$; and for every polynomial $f$, $f(B)=P^{-1}f(A)P$, so $f(B)=0$ if and only if $f(A)=0$, and the monic polynomials of least degree with this property coincide.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #invariants}
The invariant factors $d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_k$ determine the rational canonical form, their product is $\chi$, and $d_k=m$.

::: pf-proof
The rational canonical form is the direct sum of the companion matrices of the invariant factors, so the factors determine it and it determines them. The characteristic polynomial of a direct sum of companion matrices is the product of the factors, and the minimal polynomial of a companion matrix $C(d)$ is $d$, so the minimal polynomial of the sum is the least common multiple of the $d_i$, which is $d_k$ because $d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_k$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #determined}
For a $3\times3$ matrix, $\chi$ and $m$ determine the invariant factors: if $\deg m=3$ the only factor is $m=\chi$; if $\deg m=2$ the factors are $\chi/m$ and $m$; if $\deg m=1$ the factors are $m,m,m$. Hence $A$ and $B$ have the same rational canonical form and are similar.

::: pf-proof
Here $\deg\chi=3$ and $d_k=m$, so $k$ and the degrees are constrained: if $\deg m=3$ then $k=1$ and $d_1=m=\chi$; if $\deg m=2$ then $k=2$, and $d_1=\chi/m$ is forced because $d_1d_2=\chi$ and $d_2=m$; if $\deg m=1$ then every $d_i$ divides the monic linear $m$ and is monic, so every $d_i=m$ and $k=3$. In each case the invariant factors are functions of $\chi$ and $m$ alone, so two $3\times3$ matrices with the same $\chi$ and $m$ have the same rational canonical form, hence are similar by step [](#invariants){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #counterexample}
For $4\times4$ matrices the statement fails: $A=J_2(0)\oplus0\oplus0$ and $B=J_2(0)\oplus J_2(0)$ both have $\chi=x^4$ and $m=x^2$, but they are not similar.

::: pf-proof
$A$ has invariant factors $(x,x,x^2)$ and $B$ has $(x^2,x^2)$, so both have characteristic polynomial $x^4$ and minimal polynomial $x^2$; but the invariant factors, hence the rational canonical forms, differ. Directly: $A$ has rank $1$ and $B$ has rank $2$, and rank is preserved by similarity.
:::
