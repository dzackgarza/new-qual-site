::: {.exercise}
Let $T$, $V$, and $F$ be as in Exercise 5. Suppose $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are vectors in $V$ which have the same $T$-annihilator.
Prove that, for any polynomial $f$, the vectors $f\alpha$ and $f\beta$ have the same $T$-annihilator.
:::

::: solution
In the section's notation, $f\alpha$ means $f(T)\alpha$ [@HK71]. Let
$$
I=\operatorname{Ann}_T(\alpha)=\operatorname{Ann}_T(\beta)
=\{h\in F[x]:h(T)\alpha=0\}
=\{h\in F[x]:h(T)\beta=0\}.
$$

<1>1. Compute the annihilator of $f(T)\alpha$ from $I$.
::: proof
For any polynomial $g$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
g\in\operatorname{Ann}_T(f(T)\alpha)
&\iff g(T)f(T)\alpha=0\\
&\iff (gf)(T)\alpha=0\\
&\iff gf\in I.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Ann}_T(f(T)\alpha)
=\{g\in F[x]:gf\in I\}.
$$
:::

<1>2. The same description holds for $f(T)\beta$.
::: proof
Using $\operatorname{Ann}_T(\beta)=I$ in exactly the same calculation gives
$$
\operatorname{Ann}_T(f(T)\beta)
=\{g\in F[x]:gf\in I\}.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Ann}_T(f(T)\alpha)
=\operatorname{Ann}_T(f(T)\beta).}
$$
Thus applying any polynomial in $T$ preserves equality of the two
$T$-annihilators.
:::
:::
