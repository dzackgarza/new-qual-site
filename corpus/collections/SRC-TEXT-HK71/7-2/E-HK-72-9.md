::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be the real matrix
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 & 3 \\ -3 & -3 & -5 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Find an invertible $3 \times 3$ real matrix $P$ such that $P^{-1}AP$ is in rational form.
:::

::: solution
One valid choice is
$$
\boxed{
P=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1\\
0&0&3\\
-1&0&-3
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P^{-1}AP=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&0&0\\
0&0&2\\
0&1&-1
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
The lower-right block is the companion matrix of
$x^2+x-2=(x+2)(x-1)$ in Hoffman--Kunze's convention.

<1>1. Determine the invariant factors.
::: proof
A direct determinant calculation gives
$$
\chi_A(x)=\det(xI-A)=(x-1)(x+2)^2.
$$
Also
$$
A+2I=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&3&3\\3&3&3\\-3&-3&-3
\end{pmatrix}
$$
has rank one, so the $-2$ eigenspace has dimension two. The $1$ eigenspace
is one-dimensional, for example spanned by $(1,1,-1)$. Thus $A$ is
diagonalizable and its minimal polynomial is
$$
m_A(x)=(x+2)(x-1)=x^2+x-2.
$$
The invariant factors multiply to the characteristic polynomial and the last
one is the minimal polynomial [@HK71]. Hence they are
$$
x+2,
\qquad
(x+2)(x-1).
$$
:::

<1>2. Choose cyclic generators for the two invariant-factor summands.
::: proof
Let
$$
u=(1,0,-1),
\qquad
v=(1,0,0).
$$
Then
$$
Au=-2u,
$$
so $Z(u;A)=\operatorname{span}\{u\}$ has annihilator $x+2$.
Moreover
$$
Av=(1,3,-3).
$$
The three vectors
$$
u,\quad v,\quad Av
$$
form a basis because the matrix with these columns is
$$
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1\\
0&0&3\\
-1&0&-3
\end{pmatrix}
$$
and $\det P=-3\ne0$. Thus
$V=Z(u;A)\oplus Z(v;A)$, with $Z(v;A)$ two-dimensional.

Since $m_A(A)=0$,
$$
(A^2+A-2I)v=0,
$$
so
$$
A(Av)=2v-Av.
$$
Thus relative to the ordered cyclic basis $(v,Av)$, the restriction of $A$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},
$$
the companion matrix of $x^2+x-2$ [@HK71].
:::

<1>3. Assemble the rational form.
::: proof
Relative to the ordered basis $(u,v,Av)$, the first summand is multiplication
by $-2$ and the second has the companion matrix computed above. Therefore
$$
P^{-1}AP
=\operatorname{diag}\left(-2,
\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}\right)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&0&0\\
0&0&2\\
0&1&-1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is the rational canonical form corresponding to the invariant factors
$x+2\mid(x+2)(x-1)$.
:::
:::
