::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^3$ which is represented in the standard ordered basis by the matrix
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Let $W$ be the null space of $T - 2I$.
Prove that $W$ has no complementary $T$-invariant subspace.
(Hint: Let $\beta = \epsilon_1$ and observe that $(T - 2I)\beta$ is in $W$.
Prove there is no $\alpha$ in $W$ with $(T - 2I)\beta = (T - 2I)\alpha$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be the standard basis. From the given matrix,
$$
(T-2I)(x,y,z)=(0,x,z).
$$
Hence
$$
W=\ker(T-2I)=\{(0,y,0):y\in\mathbb R\}
=\operatorname{span}\{e_2\}.
$$

<1>1. Any complementary invariant subspace would have dimension two.
::: {.proof}
If $V=W\oplus U$, then
$$
\dim U=3-\dim W=2.
$$
Assume, toward a contradiction, that such a complement $U$ is also
$T$-invariant.
:::

<1>2. The quotient by $W$ forces $U$ to contain a vector with nonzero $e_1$-component.
::: {.proof}
If every vector of $U$ had first coordinate zero, then
$U\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{e_2,e_3\}$. Since $U\cap W=\{0\}$ and
$W=\operatorname{span}\{e_2\}$, the projection of $U$ to the quotient
$\operatorname{span}\{e_2,e_3\}/W$ would have dimension at most one. But
$U$ has dimension two and intersects $W$ trivially, so its quotient image must
have dimension two, a contradiction. Thus some
$$
u=(a,b,c)\in U
$$
has $a\ne0$.
:::

<1>3. Invariance then forces a nonzero vector of $W$ into $U$.
::: {.proof}
Because $U$ is $T$-invariant, it is also invariant under $T-2I$. Hence
$$
(T-2I)u=(0,a,c)\in U.
$$
Also $e_3$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $3$, so the one-dimensional
$3$-eigenspace $\operatorname{span}\{e_3\}$ is $T$-invariant. Since a
two-dimensional complement of $W$ must map isomorphically to $V/W$, its image
contains the class of $e_3$; subtracting a suitable vector of $W$ shows that
$U$ contains some vector $(0,b',1)$. Applying $T-3I$ to that vector gives
$(0,-b',0)\in U\cap W$. If $b'\ne0$ this already contradicts directness; if
$b'=0$, then $e_3\in U$.

In the latter case, subtract $c e_3$ from $(T-2I)u$ to obtain
$$
(0,a,0)=ae_2\in U.
$$
Since $a\ne0$, this is a nonzero element of $U\cap W$, again a contradiction.
Therefore no complementary $T$-invariant subspace exists [@HK71].
:::
