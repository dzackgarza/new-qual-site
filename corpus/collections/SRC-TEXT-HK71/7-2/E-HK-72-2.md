::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on the finite-dimensional space $V$, and let $R$ be the range of $T$.

(a) Prove that $R$ has a complementary $T$-invariant subspace if and only if $R$ is independent of the null space $N$ of $T$.

(b) If $R$ and $N$ are independent, prove that $N$ is the unique $T$-invariant subspace complementary to $R$.
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
R=\operatorname{range}T,
\qquad
N=\ker T.
$$
Recall that $R$ is $T$-invariant because $T(R)=T(T(V))\subseteq T(V)=R$,
and $N$ is $T$-invariant because $T(N)=\{0\}\subseteq N$.

<1>1. If $R$ has a complementary $T$-invariant subspace, then $R\cap N=\{0\}$.
::: proof
Suppose
$$
V=R\oplus W
$$
with $W$ invariant under $T$. Since $T(W)\subseteq W$ and every value of $T$
lies in $R$, one has
$$
T(W)\subseteq R\cap W=\{0\}.
$$
Hence $W\subseteq N$.

Rank-nullity gives
$$
\dim N=\dim V-\dim R=\dim W.
$$
Thus the inclusion $W\subseteq N$ is equality. Consequently
$$
V=R\oplus N,
$$
and in particular $R\cap N=\{0\}$.
:::

<1>2. If $R$ and $N$ are independent, then $N$ is a complementary invariant subspace.
::: proof
Independence means $R\cap N=\{0\}$. Rank-nullity gives
$$
\dim R+\dim N=\dim V.
$$
Therefore the direct sum $R\oplus N$ has dimension $\dim V$, so
$$
V=R\oplus N.
$$
As observed above, $N$ is $T$-invariant. Hence $R$ has a complementary
$T$-invariant subspace. This proves the equivalence in part (a) [@HK71].
:::

<1>3. The invariant complement is unique.
::: proof
Assume $R\cap N=\{0\}$, and let $W$ be any $T$-invariant complement of $R$.
The argument in step <1>1 applies verbatim and yields $W\subseteq N$. Again
$$
\dim W=\dim V-\dim R=\dim N,
$$
so $W=N$. Thus $N$ is the unique $T$-invariant subspace complementary to
$R$, as required in part (b).
:::
:::
