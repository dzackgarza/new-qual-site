::: {.exercise}
For the matrix $A$ of Exercise 2, find elementary matrices $E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_k$ such that
$$
E_k \cdots E_2E_1A = I.
$$
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise, and the section's textbook is not in the checkout (`SRC-TEXT-HK71`'s provenance is the archive.org record and the publisher's table of contents; `tmp/sources` holds no Hoffman--Kunze text). The argument is the card's own: the row reduction below was performed and checked in exact rational arithmetic, and the matrices $E_1,\dots,E_8$ were multiplied to confirm that their product reduces $A$ to $I_3$.

For
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&1\\2&0&1\\3&0&1\end{bmatrix},
$$
perform the row operations
$$
R_2\leftarrow R_2-2R_1,\quad R_3\leftarrow R_3-3R_1,\quad R_2\leftarrow\tfrac12R_2,\quad R_3\leftarrow R_3-3R_2,
$$
$$
R_3\leftarrow -2R_3,\quad R_2\leftarrow R_2+\tfrac12R_3,\quad R_1\leftarrow R_1-R_3,\quad R_1\leftarrow R_1+R_2 .
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #reduction}
The eight operations take $A$ to $I_3$.

::: pf-proof
Carrying them out in order on the rows of $A$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&1\\0&2&-1\\0&3&-2\end{bmatrix},
&&\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&1\\0&1&-\tfrac12\\0&0&-\tfrac12\end{bmatrix},
&&\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&1\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix},
&&\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix},
\end{aligned}
$$
the first matrix being $A$ after the first two operations, the second after $R_2\leftarrow\tfrac12R_2$ and $R_3\leftarrow-2R_3$, the third after $R_3\leftarrow R_3-3R_2$ and $R_2\leftarrow R_2+\tfrac12R_3$, and the fourth after the last two.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrices}
The elementary matrices of the eight operations are the displayed $E_1,\dots,E_8$, in that order.

::: pf-proof
An elementary operation on rows is left multiplication by the matrix obtained by applying it to the identity, so $R_2\leftarrow R_2-2R_1$ gives $E_1$ with $-2$ in position $(2,1)$, $R_3\leftarrow R_3-3R_1$ gives $E_2$ with $-3$ in position $(3,1)$, $R_2\leftarrow\tfrac12R_2$ gives $E_3$ with $\tfrac12$ in position $(2,2)$, $R_3\leftarrow R_3-3R_2$ gives $E_4$ with $-3$ in position $(3,2)$, $R_3\leftarrow-2R_3$ gives $E_5$ with $-2$ in position $(3,3)$, $R_2\leftarrow R_2+\tfrac12R_3$ gives $E_6$ with $\tfrac12$ in position $(2,3)$, $R_1\leftarrow R_1-R_3$ gives $E_7$ with $-1$ in position $(1,3)$, and $R_1\leftarrow R_1+R_2$ gives $E_8$ with $1$ in position $(1,2)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product}
$E_8E_7E_6E_5E_4E_3E_2E_1A=I_3$.

::: pf-proof
Left multiplication by $E_1$, then $E_2$, and so on, performs the eight operations in order, so by step [](#reduction){.pf-ref} the composite carries $A$ to $I_3$; and left multiplication by a product is the composite of left multiplications. So the displayed product is $I_3$, and consequently $E_8\cdots E_1=A^{-1}$, which for this $A$ is
$$
\begin{bmatrix}0&-1&1\\-1&2&-1\\0&3&-2\end{bmatrix}.
$$
:::
