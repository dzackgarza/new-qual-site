::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ and $B$ be $2 \times 2$ matrices such that $AB = I$.
Prove that $BA = I$.
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §1.5 Exercise 7. Both matrices are $2\times2$ over a field, and the determinant is the $2\times2$ determinant, which is multiplicative and satisfies $\det I_2=1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #det}

$\det(A)\det(B)=1$.

::: pf-proof

Taking determinants of $AB=I_2$ gives $\det(AB)=\det(I_2)$, and $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$ while $\det(I_2)=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inv}

$\det(A)\neq 0$, so $A$ is invertible.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#det){.pf-ref} reads $\det(A)\det(B)=1$ in the field, so $\det(A)$ has the inverse $\det(B)$ and is nonzero; a square matrix over a field is invertible exactly when its determinant is nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}

$B=A^{-1}$.

::: pf-proof

Multiply $AB=I_2$ on the left by $A^{-1}$, which exists by step [](#inv){.pf-ref}: $A^{-1}(AB)=A^{-1}I_2$, that is, $(A^{-1}A)B=A^{-1}$, that is, $B=A^{-1}$, using associativity and $A^{-1}A=I_2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$BA=I_2$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#b){.pf-ref}, $BA=A^{-1}A=I_2$.

:::
