::: {.exercise}
Let
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 3 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 4 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Verify directly that $A(AB) = A^2B$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and the products below are computed from the statement by the row-column rule.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ab}
$AB=\begin{bmatrix}5&-1\\8&0\\10&-2\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
The $(i,j)$ entry of $AB$ is the dot product of row $i$ of $A$ with column $j$ of $B$. Row $1$ of $A$ is $(1,-1,1)$: against column $1$, $(1)(2)+(-1)(1)+(1)(4)=5$, and against column $2$, $(1)(-2)+(-1)(3)+(1)(4)=-1$. Row $2$ is $(2,0,1)$: $4+0+4=8$ and $-4+0+4=0$. Row $3$ is $(3,0,1)$: $6+0+4=10$ and $-6+0+4=-2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a2}
$A^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}2&-1&1\\5&-2&3\\6&-3&4\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Again by the row-column rule. Row $1$ of $A$ against the three columns of $A$ gives $1-2+3=2$, $-1+0+0=-1$ and $1-1+1=1$. Row $2$ gives $2+0+3=5$, $-2+0+0=-2$ and $2+0+1=3$. Row $3$ gives $3+0+3=6$, $-3+0+0=-3$ and $3+0+1=4$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #left}
$A(AB)=\begin{bmatrix}7&-3\\20&-4\\25&-5\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Row $1$ of $A$ against the columns of $AB$ from step [](#ab){.pf-ref}: $(1)(5)+(-1)(8)+(1)(10)=7$ and $(1)(-1)+(-1)(0)+(1)(-2)=-3$. Row $2$: $(2)(5)+0+(1)(10)=20$ and $(2)(-1)+0+(1)(-2)=-4$. Row $3$: $(3)(5)+0+(1)(10)=25$ and $(3)(-1)+0+(1)(-2)=-5$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #right}
$A^{2}B=\begin{bmatrix}7&-3\\20&-4\\25&-5\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Row $1$ of $A^{2}$ from step [](#a2){.pf-ref} against $B$: $(2)(2)+(-1)(1)+(1)(4)=7$ and $(2)(-2)+(-1)(3)+(1)(4)=-3$. Row $2$: $(5)(2)+(-2)(1)+(3)(4)=20$ and $(5)(-2)+(-2)(3)+(3)(4)=-4$. Row $3$: $(6)(2)+(-3)(1)+(4)(4)=25$ and $(6)(-2)+(-3)(3)+(4)(4)=-5$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$A(AB)=A^{2}B$, as required.

::: pf-proof
The two matrices of steps [](#left){.pf-ref} and [](#right){.pf-ref} have the same six entries in the same positions, so they are equal. The matrix product is associative, so this also illustrates that associativity directly, with both products of the triple $A,A,B$ computed independently.
:::
