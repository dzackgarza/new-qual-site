::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix and $B$ an $n \times k$ matrix.
Show that the columns of $C = AB$ are linear combinations of the columns of $A$.
If $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$ are the columns of $A$ and $\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_k$ are the columns of $C$, then
\[
\gamma_j = \sum_{r=1}^{n} B_{rj}\alpha_r.
\]
:::
\n::: solution\nFix $1\le j\le k$. The $i$th entry of the $j$th column $\gamma_j$ of $C=AB$ is\n\[\n(C)_{ij}=\sum_{r=1}^n A_{ir}B_{rj}.\n\]\nOn the other hand, the $i$th entry of\n\[\n\sum_{r=1}^n B_{rj}\alpha_r\n\]\nis exactly the same sum, since the $i$th entry of $\alpha_r$ is $A_{ir}$. Hence the two column vectors are equal:\n\[\n\gamma_j=\sum_{r=1}^n B_{rj}\alpha_r.\n\]\nThus every column of $AB$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$.\n:::\n
