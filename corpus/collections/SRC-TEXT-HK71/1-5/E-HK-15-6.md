::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix and $B$ an $n \times k$ matrix.
Show that the columns of $C = AB$ are linear combinations of the columns of $A$.
If $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$ are the columns of $A$ and $\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_k$ are the columns of $C$, then
$$
\gamma_j = \sum_{r=1}^{n} B_{rj}\alpha_r.
$$
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: it is the entry-by-entry form of the definition of matrix multiplication.

Let $A$ be $m\times n$ with entries $A_{ir}$ and columns $\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}$, let $B$ be $n\times k$ with entries $B_{rj}$, and let $C=AB$ have entries $C_{ij}$ and columns $\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #entry}

$C_{ij}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}A_{ir}B_{rj}$ for all $1\le i\le m$ and $1\le j\le k$.

::: pf-proof

By the definition of matrix multiplication the $(i,j)$ entry of $AB$ is the dot product of row $i$ of $A$ with column $j$ of $B$, namely $\sum_{r=1}^{n}A_{ir}B_{rj}$. In particular $C$ has $m$ rows and $k$ columns, so $\gamma_{j}$ is defined for each $1\le j\le k$ and has height $m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #combination}

The $i$-th entry of $\sum_{r=1}^{n}B_{rj}\alpha_{r}$ equals $\sum_{r=1}^{n}B_{rj}A_{ir}$.

::: pf-proof

The $i$-th entry of the column $\alpha_{r}$ is $A_{ir}$ by the definition of the columns of $A$. A linear combination of columns of a fixed height is formed entry by entry, so the $i$-th entry of $\sum_{r}B_{rj}\alpha_{r}$ is $\sum_{r}B_{rj}A_{ir}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #equals}

$\gamma_{j}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}B_{rj}\alpha_{r}$ for each $1\le j\le k$.

::: pf-proof

Both sides are columns of height $m$, and two columns of the same height are equal exactly when their entries agree in every position $i$. At position $i$ the left side has $C_{ij}=\sum_{r}A_{ir}B_{rj}$ by step [](#entry){.pf-ref} and the right side has $\sum_{r}B_{rj}A_{ir}$ by step [](#combination){.pf-ref}; the two sums differ only in the order of the factors of each term, and the scalars commute, so they are equal. Hence the columns agree.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclude}

Every column of $C$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$, and the coefficients of $\gamma_{j}$ are $B_{1j},\dots,B_{nj}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#equals){.pf-ref}, $\gamma_{j}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}B_{rj}\alpha_{r}$, which is by definition a linear combination of $\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}$ with coefficients $B_{1j},\dots,B_{nj}$. This holds for each $j=1,\dots,k$, so
$$
\boxed{\gamma_{j}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}B_{rj}\alpha_{r}\ \text{ for all } j,\ \text{so each column of } C=AB \text{ is a linear combination of the columns of } A.}
$$

:::
