::: {.exercise}
Find two different $2 \times 2$ matrices $A$ such that $A^2 = 0$ but $A \neq 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The examples are the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §1.5 Exercise 3.

Work in $M_{2}(\RR)$; the same two matrices lie in $M_{2}(F)$ for every field $F$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #matrices}
$A_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$ and $A_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$ are nonzero.

::: pf-proof
$A_{1}$ has $1$ in position $(1,2)$ and $A_{2}$ has $1$ in position $(2,1)$, so neither is the zero matrix.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square}
$A_{1}^{2}=0$ and $A_{2}^{2}=0$.

::: pf-proof
For a $2\times2$ matrix $A=(a_{ij})$ the $(i,j)$ entry of $A^{2}$ is $a_{i1}a_{1j}+a_{i2}a_{2j}$. For $A_{1}$ the only nonzero entry is $a_{12}=1$. Its four entries are computed in turn: the $(1,1)$ entry is $a_{11}a_{11}+a_{12}a_{21}=0\cdot0+1\cdot0=0$; the $(1,2)$ entry is $a_{11}a_{12}+a_{12}a_{22}=0\cdot1+1\cdot0=0$; the $(2,1)$ entry is $a_{21}a_{11}+a_{22}a_{21}=0$; the $(2,2)$ entry is $a_{21}a_{12}+a_{22}a_{22}=0\cdot1+0\cdot0=0$. Each entry vanishes, so $A_{1}^{2}=0$. The same computation with rows and columns interchanged gives $A_{2}^{2}=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #different}
$A_{1}\neq A_{2}$.

::: pf-proof
Their $(1,2)$ entries are $1$ and $0$ respectively, so the matrices differ.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ are two different nonzero $2\times2$ matrices with square zero.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#matrices){.pf-ref}, [](#square){.pf-ref} and [](#different){.pf-ref} give the three required properties; the exercise asks only for existence of two such matrices, so the example is complete.
:::
