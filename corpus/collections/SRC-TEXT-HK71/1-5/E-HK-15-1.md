::: {.exercise}
Let
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad C = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Compute $ABC$ and $CAB$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §1.5 Exercise 1.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #product-ab}
$AB=\begin{pmatrix}4\\4\end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
The $i$-th entry of $AB$ is the dot product of row $i$ of $A$ with $B$: the first row gives $2\cdot3+(-1)\cdot1+1\cdot(-1)=4$ and the second gives $1\cdot3+2\cdot1+1\cdot(-1)=4$. Since $A$ is $2\times3$ and $B$ is $3\times1$, the product is $2\times1$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #product-abc}
$ABC=\begin{pmatrix}4&-4\\4&-4\end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Matrix multiplication is associative, so $ABC=(AB)C$. With $AB=(4,4)^t$ from step [](#product-ab){.pf-ref} and $C=(1,-1)$, the product is the $2\times1$ column $(4,4)^t$ times the $1\times2$ row $(1,-1)$, whose $(i,j)$ entry is the $i$-th entry of $AB$ times the $j$-th entry of $C$: $4\cdot1=4$ and $4\cdot(-1)=-4$ in each row.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #product-cab}
$CAB=(0)$, the $1\times1$ zero matrix.

::: pf-proof
Again by associativity $CAB=C(AB)$. The $1\times2$ row $(1,-1)$ times the $2\times1$ column $(4,4)^t$ is $1\cdot4+(-1)\cdot4=0$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
The two products are $ABC=\begin{pmatrix}4&-4\\4&-4\end{pmatrix}$ and $CAB=(0)$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#product-abc){.pf-ref} and [](#product-cab){.pf-ref} give the two values. They are matrices of different sizes, $2\times2$ and $1\times1$, so the exercise is not asserting that they agree.
:::
:::
