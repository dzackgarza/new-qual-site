::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Suppose $V = W_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus W_k$, where each $W_i$ is invariant under $T$.
Let $T_i$ be the induced (restriction) operator on $W_i$.

(a) Prove that $\det(T) = \det(T_1) \cdots \det(T_k)$.

(b) Prove that the characteristic polynomial for $T$ is the product of the characteristic polynomials for $T_1, \ldots, T_k$.

(c) Prove that the minimal polynomial for $T$ is the least common multiple of the minimal polynomials for $T_1, \ldots, T_k$.
(Hint: Prove and then use the corresponding facts about direct sums of matrices.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose an ordered basis $\mathfrak B_i$ of each $W_i$ and concatenate them to
an ordered basis $\mathfrak B$ of $V$. Since every $W_i$ is $T$-invariant, the
matrix of $T$ in this basis is block diagonal:
$$
[T]_{\mathfrak B}
=\operatorname{diag}([T_1]_{\mathfrak B_1},\ldots,[T_k]_{\mathfrak B_k}).
$$

<1>1. Determinants multiply across the invariant direct sum.
::: {.proof}
The determinant of a block diagonal matrix is the product of the determinants
of its diagonal blocks [@HK71]. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\det T=\prod_{i=1}^k\det T_i}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Characteristic polynomials multiply across the invariant direct sum.
::: {.proof}
For an indeterminate $x$,
$$
xI-[T]_{\mathfrak B}
=\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(xI-[T_1]_{\mathfrak B_1},\ldots,
xI-[T_k]_{\mathfrak B_k}\bigr).
$$
Taking determinants and using step <1>1 for the block matrix gives
$$
\chi_T(x)
=\prod_{i=1}^k\det(xI-[T_i])
=\boxed{\prod_{i=1}^k\chi_{T_i}(x)}.
$$
:::

<1>3. The minimal polynomial is the least common multiple of the block minimal polynomials.
::: {.proof}
Let $m_i$ be the minimal polynomial of $T_i$, and let
$$
\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(m_1,\ldots,m_k),
$$
taken monic. Since each $m_i$ divides $\ell$,
$$
\ell(T_i)=0
$$
for every $i$. Polynomial evaluation preserves the block diagonal form, so
$$
\ell(T)
=\operatorname{diag}(\ell(T_1),\ldots,\ell(T_k))=0.
$$
Hence the minimal polynomial $m_T$ divides $\ell$.

Conversely, if a polynomial $p$ satisfies $p(T)=0$, then restricting this
identity to the invariant subspace $W_i$ gives
$$
p(T_i)=0.
$$
By the defining property of the minimal polynomial, $m_i$ divides $p$ for
every $i$. Therefore their least common multiple $\ell$ divides $p$.
Applying this to $p=m_T$ gives $\ell\mid m_T$.

Both $m_T$ and $\ell$ are monic, and each divides the other, so
$$
\boxed{m_T=\operatorname{lcm}(m_1,\ldots,m_k)}.
$$
:::
