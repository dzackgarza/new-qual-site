::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the vector space of continuous real-valued functions on the interval $[-1, 1]$ of the real line.
Let $W_e$ be the subspace of even functions, $f(-x) = f(x)$, and let $W_o$ be the subspace of odd functions, $f(-x) = -f(x)$.

(a) Show that $V = W_e \oplus W_o$.

(b) If $T$ is the indefinite integral operator
\[
(Tf)(x) = \int_0^x f(t)\,dt
\]
are $W_e$ and $W_o$ invariant under $T$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every continuous function is uniquely a sum of an even and an odd function.
::: {.proof}
For $f\in V$, define
$$
f_e(x)=\frac{f(x)+f(-x)}2,
\qquad
f_o(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(-x)}2.
$$
Both functions are continuous, and direct substitution gives
$$
f_e(-x)=f_e(x),
\qquad
f_o(-x)=-f_o(x).
$$
Thus $f_e\in W_e$, $f_o\in W_o$, and
$$
f=f_e+f_o.
$$
Hence $V=W_e+W_o$. If $h\in W_e\cap W_o$, then
$$
h(x)=h(-x)=-h(x),
$$
so $2h(x)=0$ for every $x$. Over $\mathbb R$, this implies $h=0$.
Therefore
$$
\boxed{V=W_e\oplus W_o}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Integration interchanges parity rather than preserving either summand.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
(Tf)(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt.
$$
If $f$ is even, then using $t=-u$ gives
$$
(Tf)(-x)
=-\int_0^x f(-u)\,du
=-\int_0^x f(u)\,du
=-(Tf)(x).
$$
Thus $T(W_e)\subseteq W_o$. If $f$ is odd, the same substitution gives
$$
(Tf)(-x)
=-\int_0^x f(-u)\,du
=\int_0^x f(u)\,du
=(Tf)(x),
$$
so $T(W_o)\subseteq W_e$ [@HK71].

Neither subspace is invariant. Indeed, $f(x)=1$ lies in $W_e$ but
$$
Tf(x)=x\notin W_e,
$$
while $f(x)=x$ lies in $W_o$ but
$$
Tf(x)=\frac{x^2}{2}\notin W_o.
$$
Therefore the answer to both invariance questions is
$\boxed{\text{no}}$.
:::
:::
