::: {.exercise}
In Exercises 5 and 6, notice that (for each $i$) the space of characteristic vectors associated with the characteristic value $c_i$ is spanned by the column vectors of the various matrices $E_j$ with $j \neq i$.
Is that a coincidence?
:::

::: remark
The printed observation has the indices reversed. In the spectral resolution,
the columns of $E_i$ span the characteristic space for $c_i$. The columns of
the matrices $E_j$ with $j\ne i$ span the complementary sum of the other
characteristic spaces.
:::

::: solution
It is not a coincidence; the corrected statement is a general property of
spectral projections.

Let $A$ be diagonalizable with distinct characteristic values
$c_1,\ldots,c_r$, and define the Lagrange polynomials
$$
p_i(x)=\prod_{j\ne i}\frac{x-c_j}{c_i-c_j},
\qquad
E_i=p_i(A).
$$
Then $p_i(c_i)=1$ and $p_i(c_j)=0$ for $j\ne i$ [@HK71].

<1>1. The range of $E_i$ is exactly the $c_i$-characteristic space.
::: proof
If $v$ is a characteristic vector for $c_j$, then
$$
E_i v=p_i(A)v=p_i(c_j)v
=\begin{cases}
v,&j=i,\\
0,&j\ne i.
\end{cases}
$$
Because $A$ is diagonalizable, the characteristic spaces span the whole
space. Thus $E_i$ acts as the identity on the $c_i$-space and as zero on all
the others. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{range}E_i=W_i,
$$
where $W_i$ denotes the characteristic space for $c_i$.
:::

<1>2. The columns of $E_i$ span that characteristic space.
::: proof
For any matrix representing a linear map, its column space is the range of the
map. Hence the columns of $E_i$ span
$$
\operatorname{range}E_i=W_i.
$$
Likewise, for $j\ne i$, the columns of $E_j$ span $W_j$, so the columns of all
the other $E_j$ together span
$$
\bigoplus_{j\ne i}W_j,
$$
the complementary spectral subspace. Thus the printed statement is false as
written, and the corrected column-space phenomenon follows directly from the
spectral-projection construction.
:::
:::
