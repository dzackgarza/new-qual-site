::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^2$, the matrix of which in the standard ordered basis is
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Let $W_1$ be the subspace of $\RR^2$ spanned by the vector $\epsilon_1 = (1, 0)$.

(a) Prove that $W_1$ is invariant under $T$.

(b) Prove that there is no subspace $W_2$ which is invariant under $T$ and which is complementary to $W_1$:
\[
\RR^2 = W_1 \oplus W_2.
\]
(Compare with Exercise 1 of Section 6.5.)
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix},
$$
so $T(x,y)=(2x+y,2y)$.

<1>1. The line $W_1=\operatorname{span}(1,0)$ is $T$-invariant.
::: proof
For every scalar $a$,
$$
T(a,0)=(2a,0)=2(a,0)\in W_1.
$$
Thus $T(W_1)\subseteq W_1$.
:::

<1>2. No complementary line can be invariant.
::: proof
Any one-dimensional complement $W_2$ of $W_1$ is spanned by a vector
$$
v=(x,y)
$$
with $y\ne0$; otherwise $v\in W_1$. If $W_2$ were $T$-invariant, then because
it is one-dimensional there would be a scalar $\lambda$ with
$$
Tv=\lambda v.
$$
Thus $v$ would be an eigenvector of $A$. The characteristic polynomial is
$(t-2)^2$, so necessarily $\lambda=2$. But
$$
(A-2I)v
=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}y\\0\end{pmatrix},
$$
which vanishes only when $y=0$. Hence every eigenvector lies in $W_1$.
Therefore no line complementary to $W_1$ is invariant under $T$ [@HK71].
:::
:::
