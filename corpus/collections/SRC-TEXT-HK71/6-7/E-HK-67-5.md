::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the diagonalizable linear operator on $\RR^3$ which we discussed in Example 3 of Section 6.2. Use the Lagrange polynomials to write the representing matrix $A$ in the form $A = E_1 + 2E_2$, $E_1 + E_2 = I$, $E_1E_2 = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §6.7 Exercise 5 on printed p. 218. The argument is the card's own.

In the referenced example, the distinct characteristic values of $T$ are
$1$ and $2$ [@HK71]. The corresponding Lagrange polynomials are
$$
p_1(x)=\frac{x-2}{1-2}=2-x,
\qquad
p_2(x)=\frac{x-1}{2-1}=x-1.
$$
Therefore define
$$
\boxed{E_1=p_1(A)=2I-A,
\qquad
E_2=p_2(A)=A-I.}
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

These matrices resolve the identity and recover $A$.

::: pf-proof

Directly,
$$
E_1+E_2=(2I-A)+(A-I)=I,
$$
and
$$
E_1+2E_2=(2I-A)+2(A-I)=A.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The two spectral projections annihilate one another.

::: pf-proof

Since the only characteristic values are $1$ and $2$ and $A$ is
diagonalizable, its minimal polynomial is
$$
m_A(x)=(x-1)(x-2).
$$
Hence
$$
(A-I)(A-2I)=0
$$
[@HK71]. Therefore
$$
E_1E_2=(2I-A)(A-I)=-(A-2I)(A-I)=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{A=E_1+2E_2,\qquad E_1+E_2=I,\qquad E_1E_2=0},
$$
as required.

:::
