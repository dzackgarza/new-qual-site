::: {.exercise}
Let $E$ be a projection of $V$ and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Prove that the range of $E$ is invariant under $T$ if and only if $ETE = TE$.
Prove that both the range and null space of $E$ are invariant under $T$ if and only if $ET = TE$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Part 1: Show $\operatorname{im}(E)$ is $T$-invariant if and only if $ETE = TE$:
<2>1. Recall that for a projection $E^2 = E$, the range is characterized by $\operatorname{im}(E) = \{v \in V \mid Ev = v\} = \{Ev \mid v \in V\}$.
Proof: $E(Ev) = E^2 v = Ev$.
<2>2. **Forward direction ($\implies$):** Assume $T(\operatorname{im}(E)) \subseteq \operatorname{im}(E)$.
For any $v \in V$, $Ev \in \operatorname{im}(E)$.
By $T$-invariance, $T(Ev) \in \operatorname{im}(E)$.
Since $E$ acts as the identity on $\operatorname{im}(E)$, $E(TEv) = TEv$.
Thus $ETE v = TE v$ for all $v \in V$, so $ETE = TE$.
Proof: $E|_{{\operatorname{im}(E)}} = \operatorname{id}$.
<2>3. **Reverse direction ($\impliedby$):** Assume $ETE = TE$.
For any $w \in \operatorname{im}(E)$, $Ew = w$.
Then $Tw = T(Ew) = (TE)w = (ETE)w = E(TEw) = E(Tw) \in \operatorname{im}(E)$.
Thus $T(\operatorname{im}(E)) \subseteq \operatorname{im}(E)$.
Proof: $\operatorname{im}(E)$ is the image of $E$.

<1>2. Part 2: Show $\operatorname{im}(E)$ and $\ker(E)$ are both $T$-invariant if and only if $ET = TE$:
<2>1. The complementary projection is $I - E$, which satisfies $(I - E)^2 = I - E$, $\operatorname{im}(I - E) = \ker(E)$, and $\ker(I - E) = \operatorname{im}(E)$.
Proof: properties of complementary projections.
<2>2. Applying Part 1 to $I - E$, $\ker(E) = \operatorname{im}(I - E)$ is $T$-invariant if and only if:
\[
(I - E)T(I - E) = T(I - E).
\]
Proof: <1>1 applied to the projection $I - E$.
<2>3. Expand both sides of the condition in <2>2:
\[
(I - E)(T - TE) = T - TE - ET + ETE.
\]
Thus:
\[
T - TE - ET + ETE = T - TE \iff ETE = ET.
\]
Therefore $\ker(E)$ is $T$-invariant if and only if $ETE = ET$.
Proof: algebraic expansion and cancellation of $T - TE$.
<2>4. Combining the two invariance conditions:
- $\operatorname{im}(E)$ is $T$-invariant $\iff ETE = TE$.
- $\ker(E)$ is $T$-invariant $\iff ETE = ET$.
Therefore, both $\operatorname{im}(E)$ and $\ker(E)$ are $T$-invariant if and only if $TE = ETE = ET \iff ET = TE$.
Proof: <1>1 and <2>3.

<1>3. Conclusion:
$\operatorname{im}(E)$ is $T$-invariant $\iff ETE = TE$, and both $\operatorname{im}(E)$ and $\ker(E)$ are $T$-invariant $\iff ET = TE$. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.
:::
