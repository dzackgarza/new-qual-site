::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$ which commutes with every projection operator on $V$.
What can you say about $T$?
:::

::: {.solution}
One must have
$$
\boxed{T=cI}
$$
for some scalar $c$.

<1>1. Every one-dimensional subspace is $T$-invariant.
::: {.proof}
Let $0\ne v\in V$ and put $L=\operatorname{span}(v)$. Extend $v$ to a basis
of $V$ and let $M$ be the span of the remaining basis vectors. Then
$$
V=L\oplus M,
$$
so there is a projection $E$ onto $L$ along $M$ [@HK71]. Since $TE=ET$,
for any $x\in L$ one has $Ex=x$ and therefore
$$
E(Tx)=T(Ex)=Tx.
$$
Thus $Tx\in\operatorname{range}E=L$. In particular there is a scalar
$c_v$ such that
$$
Tv=c_vv.
$$
Hence every nonzero vector is an eigenvector of $T$.
:::

<1>2. All these eigenvalues are equal.
::: {.proof}
If $\dim V\le1$, every linear operator already has the form $cI$. Assume
$\dim V\ge2$ and take linearly independent vectors $v,w$. By step <1>1,
$$
Tv=av,
\qquad
Tw=bw,
\qquad
T(v+w)=c(v+w)
$$
for some scalars $a,b,c$. Linearity also gives
$$
T(v+w)=av+bw.
$$
Therefore
$$
(a-c)v+(b-c)w=0.
$$
Independence of $v,w$ implies $a=b=c$.

Now fix one nonzero vector $v_0$. For any nonzero $u$, either $u$ is a scalar
multiple of $v_0$, in which case its eigenvalue is the same by linearity, or
$u$ is independent of $v_0$, in which case the preceding argument gives the
same value. Thus a single scalar $c$ satisfies $Tv=cv$ for every $v\in V$.
Hence $T=cI$.
:::
:::
