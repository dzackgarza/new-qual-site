::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Let $R$ be the range of $T$ and let $N$ be the null space of $T$.
Prove that $R$ and $N$ are independent if and only if $V = R \oplus N$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed, and the two quoted facts are those this card already cited. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §6.7 Exercise 3.

Let $T$ be a linear operator on the finite-dimensional space $V$, let $R=T(V)$ be its range and $N=\ker T$ its null space; both are subspaces of $V$ because $T$ is linear. Two subspaces are called independent when their intersection is $\{0\}$, and $V=R\oplus N$ means $R+N=V$ together with $R\cap N=\{0\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #rank-nullity}
$\dim R+\dim N=\dim V$.

::: pf-proof
The dimension theorem for a linear map of a finite-dimensional space (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* [@HK71], §3.2) states $\dim V=\dim\ker T+\dim T(V)$; substituting $N=\ker T$ and $R=T(V)$ gives the identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dimension-formula}
$\dim(R+N)=\dim R+\dim N-\dim(R\cap N)$.

::: pf-proof
The dimension formula for the sum of two finite-dimensional subspaces: the map $R\times N\to R+N$, $(r,n)\mapsto r+n$, is linear and surjective with kernel $\{(s,-s)\mid s\in R\cap N\}$, which is isomorphic to $R\cap N$; rank-nullity for that map gives $\dim(R\times N)=\dim(R+N)+\dim(R\cap N)$, and $\dim(R\times N)=\dim R+\dim N$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #combined}
$\dim(R+N)=\dim V-\dim(R\cap N)$.

::: pf-proof
Substitute step [](#rank-nullity){.pf-ref} into step [](#dimension-formula){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #forward}
If $R$ and $N$ are independent, then $V=R\oplus N$.

::: pf-proof
Independence means $R\cap N=\{0\}$, so $\dim(R\cap N)=0$ and step [](#combined){.pf-ref} gives $\dim(R+N)=\dim V$. Since $R+N\subset V$ is a subspace of the finite-dimensional space $V$ with the same dimension, $R+N=V$. Together with $R\cap N=\{0\}$ this is the definition of the internal direct sum $V=R\oplus N$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #backward}
If $V=R\oplus N$, then $R$ and $N$ are independent.

::: pf-proof
The definition of the internal direct sum includes $R\cap N=\{0\}$, which is exactly independence.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$R$ and $N$ are independent if and only if $V=R\oplus N$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#forward){.pf-ref} and [](#backward){.pf-ref} are the two implications.
:::
