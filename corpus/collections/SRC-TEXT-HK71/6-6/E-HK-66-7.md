::: {.exercise}
Prove that if $E$ is the projection on $R$ along $N$, then $(I - E)$ is the projection on $N$ along $R$.
:::

::: solution
Because $E$ is the projection onto $R$ along $N$, one has
$$
V=R\oplus N,
$$
and every $v\in V$ has a unique decomposition
$$
v=r+n,
\qquad r\in R,\ n\in N,
$$
with
$$
E(v)=r
$$
[@HK71]. Therefore
$$
(I-E)(v)=v-E(v)=(r+n)-r=n.
$$
Thus $I-E$ fixes every vector of $N$ and kills every vector of $R$.
Consequently
$$
\operatorname{range}(I-E)=N,
\qquad
\ker(I-E)=R.
$$
Moreover,
$$
(I-E)^2=I-2E+E^2=I-E
$$
since $E^2=E$. Hence $I-E$ is precisely the projection onto $N$ along $R$.
:::
