::: {.exercise}
Prove that if $E$ is the projection on $R$ along $N$, then $(I - E)$ is the projection on $N$ along $R$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §6.6 Exercise 7. Here $E$ is the projection on $R$ along $N$: $V=R\oplus N$, $E$ is the identity on $R$, and $\ker E=N$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #decomp}

$V=R\oplus N$ and every $v\in V$ is uniquely $v=r+n$ with $r\in R$, $n\in N$, and $E(v)=r$.

::: pf-proof

This is the definition of the projection on $R$ along $N$: the range is $R$, the kernel is $N$, and $Ev=v$ for $v\in R$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #value}

$(I-E)(v)=n$ for $v=r+n$.

::: pf-proof

$(I-E)(v)=v-E(v)=r+n-r=n$ by step [](#decomp){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rangekernel}

$\operatorname{range}(I-E)=N$ and $\ker(I-E)=R$.

::: pf-proof

Every value of $I-E$ is some $n\in N$ by step [](#value){.pf-ref}, and every $n\in N$ is $n=(I-E)(0+n)$, so the range is $N$. If $(I-E)(v)=0$ then $n=0$ in the decomposition of step [](#decomp){.pf-ref} and $v=r\in R$; conversely $(I-E)(r)=r-r=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #idem}

$(I-E)^{2}=I-E$.

::: pf-proof

$(I-E)^{2}=I^{2}-E-E+E^{2}=I-2E+E^{2}=I-E$, using $E^{2}=E$ and linearity of composition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$I-E$ is the projection on $N$ along $R$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#rangekernel){.pf-ref} and [](#idem){.pf-ref}, $I-E$ is a linear operator with $(I-E)^{2}=I-E$, range $N$ and kernel $R$; that is exactly the projection on its range along its kernel.

:::
