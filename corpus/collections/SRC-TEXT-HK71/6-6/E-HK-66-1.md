::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and let $W_1$ be any subspace of $V$.
Prove that there is a subspace $W_2$ of $V$ such that $V = W_1 \oplus W_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Extend a basis of $W_1$ to a basis of $V$.
::: {.proof}
Choose a basis
$$
w_1,\ldots,w_r
$$
of $W_1$. Since $V$ is finite dimensional, the basis-extension theorem gives
vectors
$$
v_{r+1},\ldots,v_n
$$
such that
$$
w_1,\ldots,w_r,v_{r+1},\ldots,v_n
$$
is a basis of $V$ [@HK71]. Define
$$
W_2=\operatorname{span}\{v_{r+1},\ldots,v_n\}.
$$
:::

<1>2. The sum $W_1+W_2$ is all of $V$ and is direct.
::: {.proof}
The basis from step <1>1 spans $V$, so every vector in $V$ is a sum of a
linear combination of the $w_i$ and a linear combination of the $v_j$.
Hence
$$
V=W_1+W_2.
$$

If $x\in W_1\cap W_2$, then for some scalars $a_i,b_j$,
$$
x=\sum_{i=1}^r a_iw_i
=\sum_{j=r+1}^n b_jv_j.
$$
Thus
$$
\sum_{i=1}^r a_iw_i-\sum_{j=r+1}^n b_jv_j=0.
$$
Linear independence of the extended basis forces all $a_i$ and $b_j$ to be
zero. Therefore $x=0$ and
$$
W_1\cap W_2=\{0\}.
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{V=W_1\oplus W_2}.
$$
:::
:::
