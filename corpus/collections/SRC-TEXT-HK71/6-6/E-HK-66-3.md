::: {.exercise}
Find a projection $E$ which projects $\RR^2$ onto the subspace spanned by $(1, -1)$ along the subspace spanned by $(1, 2)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The required projection is
$$
\boxed{
E(x,y)=\left(\frac{2x-y}{3},\frac{-2x+y}{3}\right),
\qquad
[E]=\frac13\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

<1>1. Decompose an arbitrary vector along the two prescribed lines.
::: {.proof}
The vectors
$$
u=(1,-1),
\qquad
v=(1,2)
$$
are linearly independent, since
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}=3\ne0.
$$
Thus they form a basis of $\mathbb R^2$. Write
$$
(x,y)=au+bv=(a+b,-a+2b).
$$
Then
$$
x=a+b,
\qquad
y=-a+2b.
$$
Adding the equations gives $x+y=3b$, so
$$
b=\frac{x+y}{3},
\qquad
a=x-b=\frac{2x-y}{3}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Keep the component on $\operatorname{span}(1,-1)$.
::: {.proof}
Projection onto $\operatorname{span}(u)$ along $\operatorname{span}(v)$ means
$$
E(au+bv)=au
$$
[@HK71]. Using the coefficient $a$ from step <1>1,
$$
E(x,y)
=\frac{2x-y}{3}(1,-1)
=\left(\frac{2x-y}{3},\frac{-2x+y}{3}\right).
$$
This formula is linear and has the displayed matrix.

It also verifies the required properties directly:
$$
E(u)=u,
\qquad
E(v)=0.
$$
Hence the image is $\operatorname{span}(u)$ and the kernel is
$\operatorname{span}(v)$. Since $E$ fixes its image, $E^2=E$, so it is indeed
the desired projection.
:::
:::
