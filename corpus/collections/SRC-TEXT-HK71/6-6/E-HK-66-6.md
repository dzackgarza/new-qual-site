::: {.exercise}
True or false?
If a diagonalizable operator has only the characteristic values 0 and 1, it is a projection.
:::

::: {.solution}

The assertion is true, and the argument is the card's own: on a basis of eigenvectors with eigenvalues in $\{0,1\}$ an operator agrees with its square.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis}

A diagonalizable operator $T$ with only the characteristic values $0$ and $1$ has a basis of eigenvectors with eigenvalues in $\{0,1\}$.

::: pf-proof

Diagonalizability means $V$ has a basis $v_{1},\dots,v_{n}$ of eigenvectors of $T$ [@HK71]; let $\lambda_{j}$ with $Tv_{j}=\lambda_{j}v_{j}$. The characteristic values of $T$ are exactly the eigenvalues that occur, so each $\lambda_{j}\in\{0,1\}$ by hypothesis. Every eigenvalue occurs among the $\lambda_{j}$, since an eigenvector is a nonzero vector and hence has a nonzero coordinate in some $v_{j}$, forcing its eigenvalue to be $\lambda_{j}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square}

$T^{2}v_{j}=Tv_{j}$ for every $j$.

::: pf-proof

For an eigenvector, $T^{2}v_{j}=T(\lambda_{j}v_{j})=\lambda_{j}^{2}v_{j}$, and $\lambda_{j}^{2}=\lambda_{j}$ because $\lambda_{j}$ is $0$ or $1$ (step [](#basis){.pf-ref}). Since $Tv_{j}=\lambda_{j}v_{j}$ as well, $T^{2}v_{j}=Tv_{j}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #idempotent}

$T^{2}=T$.

::: pf-proof

Two linear operators that agree on a basis are equal. By step [](#square){.pf-ref} the operators $T^{2}$ and $T$ agree on each $v_{j}$, and the $v_{j}$ form a basis by step [](#basis){.pf-ref}. Hence $T^{2}=T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #projection}

$T$ is a projection.

::: pf-proof

A projection is by definition an idempotent linear operator, $T^{2}=T$, which is step [](#idempotent){.pf-ref}. So
$$
\boxed{T^{2}=T,\ \text{and } T \text{ is a projection.}}
$$
The hypothesis of diagonalizability is needed: the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$ has characteristic values $0$ only, so its spectrum is contained in $\{0,1\}$, and it is not a projection since its square is $0\ne$ itself.

:::
