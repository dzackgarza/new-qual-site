::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the complex numbers (or, a field of characteristic zero).
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$.
Suppose that $E_1, \ldots, E_k$ are projections of $V$ and that $E_1 + \cdots + E_k = I$.
Prove that $E_iE_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$ (Hint: Use the trace function and ask yourself what the trace of a projection is.)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For any projection $E$ on a finite-dimensional space $V$, $\operatorname{tr}(E) = \dim \operatorname{im}(E)$: <2>1. A projection satisfies $E^2 = E$, so its minimal polynomial divides $x(x - 1)$.
::: {.proof}
definition of projection operator.
:::
<2>2. Hence $E$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues in $\{0, 1\}$, and $V = \operatorname{im}(E) \oplus \ker(E)$.
::: {.proof}
direct sum decomposition into eigenspaces.
:::
<2>3. In an eigenbasis, the diagonal entries are $1$ on $\operatorname{im}(E)$ and $0$ on $\ker(E)$, so $\operatorname{tr}(E) = \dim \operatorname{im}(E)$.
::: {.proof}
trace is the sum of eigenvalues with multiplicity.
:::

<1>2. Show that $V = \operatorname{im}(E_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus \operatorname{im}(E_k)$ is an internal direct sum: <2>1. Taking the trace of the identity $I = \sum_{i=1}^k E_i$:
\[
\dim V = \operatorname{tr}(I) = \operatorname{tr}\left(\sum_{i=1}^k E_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^k \operatorname{tr}(E_i) = \sum_{i=1}^k \dim \operatorname{im}(E_i).
\]
::: {.proof}
linearity of the trace functional and <1>1. <2>2. For any $v \in V$, $v = I(v) = E_1(v) + \cdots + E_k(v) \in \operatorname{im}(E_1) + \cdots + \operatorname{im}(E_k)$, so $V = \sum_{i=1}^k \operatorname{im}(E_i)$.
:::
::: {.proof}
range of linear maps.
:::
<2>3. For any collection of subspaces $\{W_i\}$, $\dim(\sum W_i) \le \sum \dim W_i$, with equality if and only if the sum is direct.
::: {.proof}
standard dimension formula for subspace sums.
:::
<2>4. Since $\dim V = \sum_{i=1}^k \dim \operatorname{im}(E_i)$, the sum is a direct sum:
\[
V = \operatorname{im}(E_1) \oplus \operatorname{im}(E_2) \oplus \cdots \oplus \operatorname{im}(E_k).
\]
::: {.proof}
<2>1, <2>2, and <2>3.
:::

<1>3. Prove $E_i E_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$: <2>1. Let $j \in \{1, \dots, k\}$ and let $w \in \operatorname{im}(E_j)$.
::: {.proof}
setup.
:::
<2>2. Since $w \in \operatorname{im}(E_j)$, the unique representation of $w$ in the direct sum $V = \bigoplus_{r=1}^k \operatorname{im}(E_r)$ is $w = 0 + \cdots + w + \cdots + 0$ (with $w$ in the $j$-th slot and $0$ elsewhere).
::: {.proof}
definition of direct sum.
:::
<2>3. On the other hand, $w = I(w) = E_1(w) + \cdots + E_k(w)$, where $E_r(w) \in \operatorname{im}(E_r)$ for each $r$.
::: {.proof}
definition of image.
:::
<2>4. By uniqueness of the direct sum decomposition, comparing <2>2 and <2>3 yields:
\[
E_i(w) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i \neq j.
\]
::: {.proof}
uniqueness of coordinates in direct sum.
:::
<2>5. For any $v \in V$, the vector $w = E_j(v) \in \operatorname{im}(E_j)$.
::: {.proof}
definition of image.
:::
<2>6. Applying <2>4 to $w = E_j(v)$ gives:
\[
(E_i E_j)(v) = E_i(E_j(v)) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i \neq j \text{ and all } v \in V.
\]
::: {.proof}
<2>4 applied to $E_j(v)$.
:::
<2>7. Thus $E_i E_j = 0$ for all $i \neq j$.
::: {.proof}
<2>6.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion: $E_i E_j = 0$ for all $i \neq j$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and <1>3.
:::
Q.E.D.
:::
