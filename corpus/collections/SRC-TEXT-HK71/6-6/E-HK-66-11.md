::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a vector space, let $W_1, \ldots, W_k$ be subspaces of $V$, and let
\[
V_j = W_1 + \cdots + W_{j-1} + W_{j+1} + \cdots + W_k.
\]
Suppose that $V = W_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus W_k$.
Prove that the dual space $V^*$ has the direct-sum decomposition $V^* = V_1^0 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_k^0$.
:::

::: solution
For each $j$, let
$$
E_j:V\to W_j
$$
be the projection associated to the direct-sum decomposition
$V=W_1\oplus\cdots\oplus W_k$; thus if
$v=w_1+\cdots+w_k$ with $w_i\in W_i$, then $E_jv=w_j$ [@HK71].

<1>1. Every functional on $V$ is a sum of elements of the annihilators $V_j^0$.
::: proof
Let $f\in V^*$ and define
$$
f_j=f\circ E_j.
$$
If $v\in V_j=\sum_{i\ne j}W_i$, then its $W_j$-component is zero, so
$E_jv=0$. Hence $f_j(v)=0$, and therefore
$$
f_j\in V_j^0.
$$
Also
$$
E_1+\cdots+E_k=I,
$$
so for every $v\in V$,
$$
(f_1+\cdots+f_k)(v)
=f((E_1+\cdots+E_k)v)=f(v).
$$
Thus
$$
V^*=V_1^0+\cdots+V_k^0.
$$
:::

<1>2. The sum is direct.
::: proof
Suppose
$$
g_1+\cdots+g_k=0,
\qquad g_j\in V_j^0.
$$
Fix $i$ and take $w\in W_i$. For $j\ne i$, one has
$W_i\subseteq V_j$, so $g_j(w)=0$. Evaluating the displayed zero sum at $w$
therefore gives
$$
g_i(w)=0.
$$
Hence $g_i$ vanishes on $W_i$. By definition it also vanishes on $V_i$, and
$$
V=W_i\oplus V_i.
$$
Therefore $g_i=0$ on all of $V$. Since $i$ was arbitrary, every $g_i=0$.
Consequently the sum is direct, and
$$
\boxed{V^*=V_1^0\oplus\cdots\oplus V_k^0}.
$$
:::
:::
