::: {.exercise}
Let $E_1, \ldots, E_k$ be linear operators on the space $V$ such that $E_1 + \cdots + E_k = I$.

(a) Prove that if $E_iE_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$, then $E_i^2 = E_i$ for each $i$.

(b) In the case $k = 2$, prove the converse of (a). That is, if $E_1 + E_2 = I$ and $E_1^2 = E_1$, $E_2^2 = E_2$, then $E_1E_2 = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Pairwise annihilation implies each $E_i$ is idempotent.
::: {.proof}
Fix $i$. From
$$
E_1+\cdots+E_k=I
$$
we get, by multiplying on the left by $E_i$,
$$
E_i=E_iI=E_i(E_1+\cdots+E_k)
=E_i^2+\sum_{j\ne i}E_iE_j.
$$
The hypothesis gives $E_iE_j=0$ for $j\ne i$, so
$$
E_i=E_i^2.
$$
Thus every $E_i$ is a projection [@HK71].
:::

<1>2. For two summands, idempotence implies the cross-products vanish.
::: {.proof}
Assume $k=2$, $E_1+E_2=I$, and $E_1^2=E_1$, $E_2^2=E_2$.
Then
$$
E_2=I-E_1,
$$
so
$$
E_1E_2=E_1(I-E_1)=E_1-E_1^2=0.
$$
Similarly,
$$
E_2E_1=(I-E_1)E_1=E_1-E_1^2=0.
$$
Hence the two projections annihilate one another, proving the converse in the
case $k=2$.
:::
:::
