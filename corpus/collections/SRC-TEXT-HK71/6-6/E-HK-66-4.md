::: {.exercise}
If $E_1$ and $E_2$ are projections onto independent subspaces, then $E_1 + E_2$ is a projection.
True or false?
:::

::: {.solution}
The assertion is $\boxed{\text{false}}$.

<1>1. Construct projections with independent ranges.
::: {.proof}
On $\mathbb R^2$, define
$$
E_1(x,y)=(x,0),
\qquad
E_2(x,y)=(0,y-x).
$$
Their matrices in the standard basis are
$$
[E_1]=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
[E_2]=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Direct multiplication gives
$$
E_1^2=E_1,
\qquad
E_2^2=E_2,
$$
so both are projections [@HK71]. Their ranges are
$$
\operatorname{range}E_1=\operatorname{span}(1,0),
\qquad
\operatorname{range}E_2=\operatorname{span}(0,1),
$$
which are independent subspaces.
:::

<1>2. Their sum is not a projection.
::: {.proof}
We have
$$
[E_1+E_2]
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
But
$$
[E_1+E_2]^2
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}
\ne
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus $E_1+E_2$ is not idempotent and hence is not a projection. Therefore
independence of the ranges alone does not make the sum of two projections a
projection.
:::
:::
