::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and let $W_1, \ldots, W_k$ be subspaces of $V$ such that
\[
V = W_1 + \cdots + W_k \quad \text{and} \quad \dim V = \dim W_1 + \cdots + \dim W_k.
\]
Prove that $V = W_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus W_k$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. The union of bases of the $W_i$ is a basis of $V$.
::: proof
For each $i$, choose a basis $\mathcal B_i$ of $W_i$. Since
$$
V=W_1+\cdots+W_k,
$$
the concatenated list
$$
\mathcal B_1,\ldots,\mathcal B_k
$$
spans $V$. Its total number of vectors is
$$
\sum_{i=1}^k|\mathcal B_i|
=\sum_{i=1}^k\dim W_i
=\dim V.
$$
A spanning list in a finite-dimensional vector space whose length equals the
dimension is a basis [@HK71]. Hence the concatenated list is linearly
independent.
:::

<1>2. Linear independence gives uniqueness of the decomposition.
::: proof
Suppose
$$
w_1+\cdots+w_k=0,
\qquad w_i\in W_i.
$$
Expand each $w_i$ in the basis $\mathcal B_i$. The resulting equation is a
linear relation among the vectors in the concatenated basis from step <1>1.
Linear independence forces every coefficient to vanish. Hence
$$
w_1=\cdots=w_k=0.
$$
Therefore the representation of a vector as a sum of vectors from the $W_i$
is unique. Together with the given equality $V=W_1+\cdots+W_k$, this is
exactly
$$
\boxed{V=W_1\oplus\cdots\oplus W_k}.
$$
:::
:::
