::: {.exercise}
If $E$ is a projection and $f$ is a polynomial, then $f(E) = aI + bE$.
What are $a$ and $b$ in terms of the coefficients of $f$?

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise ; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §6.6 Exercise 5 on printed p. 213; the computation is the card's own.

Throughout, $E$ is a projection, that is $E^{2}=E$, and $f(t)=c_0+c_1t+\cdots+c_mt^{m}$ is a polynomial over the field, so $f(E)=c_0I+c_1E+\cdots+c_mE^{m}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #powers}
$E^{k}=E$ for every integer $k\ge1$.

::: pf-proof
Induction on $k$, with $k=1$ trivial. If $E^{k}=E$ then $E^{k+1}=E^{k}E=EE=E^{2}=E$, using the hypothesis $E^{2}=E$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #form}
$f(E)=c_0I+(c_1+\cdots+c_m)E$.

::: pf-proof
By linearity of the map $p\mapsto p(E)$ and step [](#powers){.pf-ref},
$$
f(E)=c_0I+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_kE^{k}=c_0I+\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_k\Big)E.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coefficients}
With $a=c_0=f(0)$ and $b=c_1+\cdots+c_m=f(1)-f(0)$ the identity $f(E)=aI+bE$ holds, and these are the only coefficients with that property when $E$ is neither $0$ nor $I$.

::: pf-proof
$a=c_0=f(0)$ is immediate, and $f(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}c_k$, so $f(1)-f(0)=c_1+\cdots+c_m=b$; the identity is step [](#form){.pf-ref}. If $E$ is neither the zero nor the identity operator, then $I$ and $E$ are linearly independent in the space of operators: $I=\lambda E$ would make every vector an eigenvector of $E$ with the single eigenvalue $\lambda$, forcing $\lambda\in\{0,1\}$ and hence $E=0$ or $E=I$. So the expression of $f(E)$ as $aI+bE$ is unique for such $E$.

:::
