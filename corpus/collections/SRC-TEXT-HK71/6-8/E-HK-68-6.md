::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over the field $F$, and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$ such that $\operatorname{rank}(T) = 1$.
Prove that either $T$ is diagonalizable or $T$ is nilpotent, not both.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $\dim \operatorname{im} T = 1$, so $\operatorname{im} T = \operatorname{span}\{v\}$ for some nonzero $v \in V$.
::: {.proof}
rank $1$.
:::

<1>2. $T$ has at most one nonzero eigenvalue.
<2>1. If $T$ has a nonzero eigenvalue $\lambda$ with eigenvector $u$, then $Tu = \lambda u \in \operatorname{im} T$, so $u \in \operatorname{im} T = \operatorname{span}\{v\}$.
::: {.proof}
$\lambda \neq 0$ and $Tu = \lambda u$ implies $u = \lambda^{-1} Tu \in \operatorname{im} T$.
:::
<2>2. Hence the eigenspace for any nonzero eigenvalue is contained in the $1$-dimensional space $\operatorname{span}\{v\}$.
::: {.proof}
<2>1.
:::
<2>3. Therefore there is at most one nonzero eigenvalue.
::: {.proof}
two distinct nonzero eigenvalues would give two linearly independent eigenvectors, contradicting <2>2.
:::

<1>3. Case 1: $T$ has a nonzero eigenvalue $\lambda$.
<2>1. Then $T$ is diagonalizable.
::: {.proof}
$T$ has eigenvalues $\lambda$ (with multiplicity $1$) and $0$ (with multiplicity $n-1$); the geometric multiplicity of $0$ is $\dim \ker T = n - 1$ (by rank–nullity), and of $\lambda$ is $1$, so the geometric multiplicities sum to $n$, hence $T$ is diagonalizable.
:::
<2>2. $T$ is not nilpotent.
::: {.proof}
a nilpotent operator has all eigenvalues $0$, but $T$ has the nonzero eigenvalue $\lambda$.
:::

<1>4. Case 2: $T$ has no nonzero eigenvalue.
<2>1. Then all eigenvalues of $T$ are $0$, so $T$ is nilpotent.
::: {.proof}
the characteristic polynomial is $t^n$, so by Cayley–Hamilton $T^n = 0$.
:::
<2>2. $T$ is not diagonalizable (unless $T = 0$).
::: {.proof}
if $T$ were diagonalizable with all eigenvalues $0$, then $T = 0$, contradicting $\operatorname{rank} T = 1$.
:::

<1>5. Hence exactly one of "diagonalizable" or "nilpotent" holds.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and <1>4 are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>5.
:::
:::
