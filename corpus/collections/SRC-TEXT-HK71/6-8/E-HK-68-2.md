::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^3$ which is represented by the matrix
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 2 & 2 & -1 \\ 2 & 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
\]
in the standard ordered basis.
Show that there is a diagonalizable operator $D$ on $\RR^3$ and a nilpotent operator $N$ on $\RR^3$ such that $T = D + N$ and $DN = ND$.
Find the matrices of $D$ and $N$ in the standard basis.
(Just repeat the proof of Theorem 12 for this special case.)
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}
3&1&-1\\
2&2&-1\\
2&2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The required matrices are
$$
\boxed{
D=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0\\
0&2&0\\
-2&2&2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
N=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0&-1\\
2&0&-1\\
4&0&-2
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

<1>1. Determine the polynomial relations of $A$.
::: proof
A direct determinant computation gives
$$
\chi_A(x)=(x-1)(x-2)^2.
$$
The matrix is not diagonalizable at the eigenvalue $2$: for example,
$$
A-2I=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&-1\\
2&0&-1\\
2&2&-2
\end{pmatrix}
$$
has rank $2$, so the $2$-eigenspace is one-dimensional although $2$ has
algebraic multiplicity two. Hence the minimal polynomial is
$$
m_A(x)=(x-1)(x-2)^2
$$
[@HK71].
:::

<1>2. Construct the diagonalizable part as a polynomial in $A$.
::: proof
We seek a polynomial $q$ that equals $1$ at the eigenvalue $1$, equals $2$ at
the eigenvalue $2$, and is constant to first order on the repeated primary
factor at $2$. Thus impose
$$
q(1)=1,
\qquad
q(2)=2,
\qquad
q'(2)=0.
$$
The unique polynomial of degree at most two satisfying these conditions is
$$
q(x)=-x^2+4x-2.
$$
Set
$$
D=q(A)=-A^2+4A-2I.
$$
Direct multiplication gives
$$
A^2=
\begin{pmatrix}
9&3&-4\\
8&4&-4\\
10&6&-4
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and hence the displayed matrix for $D$.

On the generalized $1$-eigenspace, $D$ acts as multiplication by $1$; on the
generalized $2$-eigenspace, the congruence
$$
q(x)\equiv2\pmod{(x-2)^2}
$$
shows that $D$ acts as multiplication by $2$. Therefore $D$ is diagonalizable,
with minimal polynomial dividing $(x-1)(x-2)$ [@HK71].
:::

<1>3. The remainder is nilpotent and commutes with $D$.
::: proof
Define
$$
N=A-D=A^2-3A+2I=(A-I)(A-2I).
$$
This gives the displayed matrix for $N$. Since $D$ and $N$ are polynomials in
$A$, they commute. Moreover,
$$
N^2=(A-I)^2(A-2I)^2.
$$
The minimal polynomial
$(A-I)(A-2I)^2=0$ implies
$$
N^2=(A-I)\bigl((A-I)(A-2I)^2\bigr)=0.
$$
Thus $N$ is nilpotent. Finally its definition gives
$$
A=D+N,
$$
so the corresponding operators satisfy
$$
\boxed{T=D+N,\qquad DN=ND,}
$$
with $D$ diagonalizable and $N$ nilpotent.
:::
:::
