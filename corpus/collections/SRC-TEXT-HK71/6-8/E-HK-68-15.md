::: {.exercise}
If $N$ is a nilpotent linear operator on an $n$-dimensional vector space $V$, then the characteristic polynomial for $N$ is $x^n$.
:::

::: solution
Choose $m$ with $N^m=0$. Consider the increasing chain
$$
\{0\}=\ker N^0\subseteq\ker N\subseteq\ker N^2\subseteq\cdots
\subseteq\ker N^m=V.
$$
Choose a basis of $\ker N$, extend it to a basis of $\ker N^2$, continue in
this way, and finally obtain a basis of $V$ adapted to this flag [@HK71].

If $v\in\ker N^j$, then
$$
Nv\in\ker N^{j-1}.
$$
Therefore, in the adapted basis, the matrix of $N$ is strictly upper
triangular: every basis vector is sent into the span of vectors occurring in
earlier stages of the flag, and all diagonal entries are zero.

Hence $xI-[N]$ is upper triangular with every diagonal entry equal to $x$.
Its determinant is therefore
$$
\chi_N(x)=\det(xI-[N])=x^n.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\chi_N(x)=x^n}.
$$
:::
