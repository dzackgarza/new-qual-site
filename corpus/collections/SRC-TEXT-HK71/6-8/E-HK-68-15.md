::: {.exercise}
If $N$ is a nilpotent linear operator on an $n$-dimensional vector space $V$, then the characteristic polynomial for $N$ is $x^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §6.8 Exercise 15, p. 226. The eigenvector argument used here avoids choosing a basis adapted to the flag of kernels: over the algebraic closure the roots of the characteristic polynomial are exactly the eigenvalues.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #eigenvalue}
If $c$ lies in a field $K\supseteq F$ and $Nv=cv$ for some $v\neq0$, then $c=0$.

::: pf-proof
Induction gives $N^mv=c^mv$ for every $m\geq0$. Choose $m$ with $N^m=0$; then $c^mv=0$, and since $v\neq0$ the scalar $c^m$ is $0$, hence $c=0$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #roots}
Over an algebraic closure $\overline F$ of $F$, the only root of the characteristic polynomial $\chi_N$ is $0$.

::: pf-proof
$\chi_N$ splits over $\overline F$, and its roots there are exactly the eigenvalues of $N$ acting on $\overline F\otimes_FV$, that is, the scalars $c$ for which $Nv=cv$ has a nonzero solution. All such $c$ are $0$ by step [](#eigenvalue){.pf-ref}.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #degree}
$\chi_N(x)=x^n$.

::: pf-proof
$\chi_N$ is monic of degree $n=\dim V$ with coefficients in $F$, and by step [](#roots){.pf-ref} its only root in $\overline F$ is $0$. A monic polynomial of degree $n$ whose only root is $0$ is $x^n$, and since $x^n$ has coefficients in $F$ this is the characteristic polynomial over $F$ as well.
:::
:::
