::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Suppose that $T$ commutes with every diagonalizable linear operator on $V$.
Prove that $T$ is a scalar multiple of the identity operator.
:::

::: {.solution}
Every projection $E$ is diagonalizable: since $E^2=E$, one has
$$
V=\operatorname{range}E\oplus\ker E,
$$
and $E$ acts by $1$ on its range and by $0$ on its kernel [@HK71]. Therefore
the hypothesis implies that $T$ commutes with every projection.

Fix a nonzero vector $v\in V$. Extend $v$ to a basis and let $E_v$ be the
projection onto the line $Fv$ along the span of the remaining basis vectors.
Since $TE_v=E_vT$,
$$
E_v(Tv)=T(E_vv)=Tv,
$$
so $Tv\in Fv$. Thus every nonzero vector is an eigenvector of $T$.

If $v,w$ are linearly independent, write
$$
Tv=av,
\qquad Tw=bw,
\qquad T(v+w)=c(v+w).
$$
Linearity gives
$$
av+bw=c(v+w),
$$
and independence implies $a=b=c$. Comparing every vector with one fixed
nonzero vector shows that one scalar $c$ satisfies $Tv=cv$ for all $v\in V$.
Hence
$$
\boxed{T=cI}.
$$
:::
