::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Suppose that $T$ commutes with every diagonalizable linear operator on $V$.
Prove that $T$ is a scalar multiple of the identity operator.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §6.8 Exercise 7. Here $V$ is finite-dimensional over $F$ and $T$ commutes with every diagonalizable operator on $V$; a projection is an operator $E$ with $E^{2}=E$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #proj}

Every projection is diagonalizable.

::: pf-proof

For a projection $E$, every $v$ is $Ev+(v-Ev)$ with $Ev\in\operatorname{range}E$ and $v-Ev\in\ker E$, and $\operatorname{range}E\cap\ker E=\theset{0}$; so $V=\operatorname{range}E\oplus\ker E$ and $E$ acts as $1$ on its range and $0$ on its kernel, hence is diagonal in a basis adapted to the decomposition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #commute}

$T$ commutes with every projection.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#proj){.pf-ref} projections are diagonalizable, and $T$ commutes with every diagonalizable operator by hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eigen}

$Tv\in Fv$ for every nonzero $v\in V$.

::: pf-proof

Let $E_{v}$ be the projection onto the line $Fv$ along a complement of $Fv$, which exists since $v\neq0$. Then $E_{v}v=v$ and $TE_{v}=E_{v}T$ by step [](#commute){.pf-ref}, so $E_{v}(Tv)=T(E_{v}v)=Tv$, and a vector fixed by $E_{v}$ lies in its range $Fv$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #indep}

If $v,w$ are linearly independent then the scalars $a,b$ with $Tv=av$, $Tw=bw$ agree.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#eigen){.pf-ref} write $Tv=av$, $Tw=bw$, and $T(v+w)=c(v+w)$ with $v+w\neq0$; linearity gives $av+bw=cv+cw$, so $(a-c)v+(b-c)w=0$, and independence forces $a=b=c$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$T=cI$ for a single scalar $c$.

::: pf-proof

If $V=\theset{0}$ take $c=0$; if $\dim V=1$ every operator is a scalar multiple of the identity. Otherwise fix $0\neq v\in V$, let $c$ be the scalar with $Tv=cv$ from step [](#eigen){.pf-ref}; for $w$ independent of $v$ step [](#indep){.pf-ref} gives $Tw=cw$, and for $w=\lambda v$ one has $Tw=\lambda Tv=c\,w$. So $Tw=cw$ for all $w$, that is, $T=cI$.

:::
