::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the space of $n \times n$ matrices over a field $F$, and let $A$ be a fixed $n \times n$ matrix over $F$.
Define a linear operator $T$ on $V$ by $T(B) = AB - BA$.
Prove that if $A$ is a nilpotent matrix, then $T$ is a nilpotent operator.
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
L(B)=AB,
\qquad
R(B)=BA.
$$
Then
$$
T=L-R.
$$

<1>1. The operators $L$ and $R$ commute and are nilpotent when $A$ is nilpotent.
::: proof
For every $B\in V$,
$$
LR(B)=A(BA)=(AB)A=RL(B),
$$
so $LR=RL$. If $A^m=0$, then
$$
L^m(B)=A^mB=0,
\qquad
R^m(B)=BA^m=0
$$
for every $B$. Hence $L^m=R^m=0$ [@HK71].
:::

<1>2. A sufficiently high power of $T=L-R$ vanishes.
::: proof
Because $L$ and $R$ commute, the binomial theorem gives
$$
T^{2m-1}
=(L-R)^{2m-1}
=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}(-1)^j
\binom{2m-1}{j}L^{2m-1-j}R^j.
$$
For each summand, either $j\ge m$ or $2m-1-j\ge m$; otherwise both would be
at most $m-1$ and their sum would be at most $2m-2$. Thus every summand
contains either $R^m$ or $L^m$ as a factor, and hence is zero by step <1>1.
Therefore
$$
T^{2m-1}=0.
$$
So $T$ is nilpotent.
:::
:::
