::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$ with minimal polynomial of the form $p^n$, where $p$ is irreducible over the scalar field.
Show that there is a vector $\alpha$ in $V$ such that the $T$-annihilator of $\alpha$ is $p^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let the minimal polynomial of $T$ be
$$
m_T=p^n.
$$
Because $p^{n-1}$ is a proper divisor of the minimal polynomial,
$$
p(T)^{n-1}\ne0.
$$
Hence there exists a vector $\alpha\in V$ such that
$$
p(T)^{n-1}\alpha\ne0.
$$

Let $m_\alpha$ be the monic generator of the ideal
$$
\{f\in F[x]:f(T)\alpha=0\},
$$
the $T$-annihilator of $\alpha$ [@HK71]. Since
$p(T)^n=0$ on all of $V$, one has
$$
m_\alpha\mid p^n.
$$
The polynomial $p$ is irreducible, so every monic divisor of $p^n$ has the
form $p^r$ for some $0\le r\le n$. Thus
$$
m_\alpha=p^r.
$$
If $r\le n-1$, then $m_\alpha$ divides $p^{n-1}$, so
$$
p(T)^{n-1}\alpha=0,
$$
contrary to the choice of $\alpha$. Therefore $r=n$, and
$$
\boxed{m_\alpha=p^n}.
$$
This is the required vector.
:::
