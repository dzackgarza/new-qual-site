::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$ with minimal polynomial of the form $p^n$, where $p$ is irreducible over the scalar field.
Show that there is a vector $\alpha$ in $V$ such that the $T$-annihilator of $\alpha$ is $p^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §6.8 Exercise 13. Here $T$ is a linear operator on $V$ with minimal polynomial $p^{n}$, $p$ irreducible over the scalar field $F$, and the $T$-annihilator of $\alpha$ is the monic generator $m_{\alpha}$ of the ideal $\theset{g\in F[x]\mid g(T)\alpha=0}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nonzero}

$p(T)^{n-1}\neq0$.

::: pf-proof

If $p(T)^{n-1}=0$ then $p^{n-1}$ annihilates $T$, so the minimal polynomial $p^{n}$ divides $p^{n-1}$, which is impossible since degrees of nonzero divisors cannot exceed the dividend's.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #alpha}

There is $\alpha\in V$ with $p(T)^{n-1}\alpha\neq0$.

::: pf-proof

An operator that is not the zero operator takes a nonzero value at some vector, by step [](#nonzero){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #divides}

$m_{\alpha}$ divides $p^{n}$.

::: pf-proof

$p(T)^{n}=0$ on all of $V$ because $p^{n}$ is the minimal polynomial, so $p^{n}$ lies in the ideal of polynomials annihilating $\alpha$, and $m_{\alpha}$, the monic generator of that ideal, divides its every element.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #form}

$m_{\alpha}=p^{r}$ for some $0\leq r\leq n$.

::: pf-proof

A monic divisor of $p^{n}$ is a product of powers of the monic irreducible factors of $p^{n}$; $p^{n}$ has the single irreducible factor $p$, so every monic divisor is $p^{r}$ with $0\leq r\leq n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #exponent}

$r=n$.

::: pf-proof

If $r\leq n-1$ then $p^{r}$ divides $p^{n-1}$, and since $m_{\alpha}=p^{r}$ annihilates $\alpha$, so does every multiple of $p^{r}$, in particular $p^{n-1}$; then $p(T)^{n-1}\alpha=0$, contradicting step [](#alpha){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

The $T$-annihilator of $\alpha$ is $p^{n}$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#form){.pf-ref} and [](#exponent){.pf-ref}.

:::
