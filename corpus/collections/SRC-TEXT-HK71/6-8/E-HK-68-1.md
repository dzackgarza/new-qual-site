::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $\RR^3$ which is represented in the standard ordered basis by the matrix
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 6 & -3 & -2 \\ 4 & -1 & -2 \\ 10 & -5 & -3 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Express the minimal polynomial $p$ for $T$ in the form $p = p_1p_2$, where $p_1$ and $p_2$ are monic and irreducible over the field of real numbers.
Let $W_i$ be the null space of $p_i(T)$.
Find bases $B_i$ for the spaces $W_1$ and $W_2$.
If $T_i$ is the operator induced on $W_i$ by $T$, find the matrix of $T_i$ in the basis $B_i$ (above).
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}
6&-3&-2\\
4&-1&-2\\
10&-5&-3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The answer is
$$
\boxed{p(x)=(x-2)(x^2+1),}
$$
with
$$
\boxed{B_1=((1,0,2)),
\qquad
B_2=((1,1,0),(0,0,1)),}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{[T_1]_{B_1}=(2),
\qquad
[T_2]_{B_2}=\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\5&-3\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

<1>1. Determine the minimal polynomial.
::: {.proof}
A direct determinant computation gives
$$
\chi_T(x)=\det(xI-A)=(x-2)(x^2+1).
$$
The two factors are monic and irreducible over $\mathbb R$. The characteristic
polynomial is squarefree, so the minimal polynomial, which has the same
irreducible factors as the characteristic polynomial and divides it, must be
$$
p(x)=(x-2)(x^2+1)
$$
[@HK71]. Thus take
$$
p_1(x)=x-2,
\qquad
p_2(x)=x^2+1.
$$
:::

<1>2. Compute the two primary subspaces.
::: {.proof}
Row reduction of
$$
A-2I=
\begin{pmatrix}
4&-3&-2\\
4&-3&-2\\
10&-5&-5
\end{pmatrix}
$$
gives a one-dimensional kernel spanned by $(1,0,2)$. Hence
$$
W_1=\ker(A-2I)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0,2)\}.
$$

Also
$$
A^2+I=
\begin{pmatrix}
5&-5&0\\
4&-4&0\\
10&-10&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose kernel is
$$
W_2=\{(x,x,z):x,z\in\mathbb R\}
=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1,0),(0,0,1)\}.
$$
Thus the displayed $B_1,B_2$ are bases.
:::

<1>3. Compute the induced matrices.
::: {.proof}
For $v_1=(1,0,2)$,
$$
Av_1=(2,0,4)=2v_1,
$$
so $[T_1]_{B_1}=(2)$.

For $u_1=(1,1,0)$ and $u_2=(0,0,1)$,
$$
Au_1=(3,3,5)=3u_1+5u_2,
$$
and
$$
Au_2=(-2,-2,-3)=-2u_1-3u_2.
$$
Therefore
$$
[T_2]_{B_2}
=\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\5&-3\end{pmatrix}.
$$
As a check,
$$
\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\5&-3\end{pmatrix}^2=-I_2,
$$
which is exactly the relation $p_2(T_2)=0$ on the second primary component.
:::
