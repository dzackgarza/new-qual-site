::: {.exercise}
Use the primary decomposition theorem and the result of Exercise 13 to prove the following.
If $T$ is any linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$, then there is a vector $\alpha$ in $V$ with $T$-annihilator equal to the minimal polynomial for $T$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Factor the minimal polynomial as
$$
m_T=p_1^{r_1}\cdots p_k^{r_k},
$$
where the $p_i$ are distinct monic irreducible polynomials. By the primary
decomposition theorem,
$$
V=W_1\oplus\cdots\oplus W_k,
\qquad
W_i=\ker p_i(T)^{r_i},
$$
and the restriction $T_i=T|_{W_i}$ has minimal polynomial
$p_i^{r_i}$ [@HK71].

<1>1. Choose one vector realizing each primary power.
::: {.proof}
Exercise 13, applied to $T_i$ on $W_i$, gives a vector
$\alpha_i\in W_i$ whose $T_i$-annihilator is exactly
$$
p_i^{r_i}.
$$
Set
$$
\alpha=\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_k.
$$
:::

<1>2. The annihilator of $\alpha$ is the least common multiple of the component annihilators.
::: {.proof}
For any polynomial $f$,
$$
f(T)\alpha
=f(T)\alpha_1+\cdots+f(T)\alpha_k,
$$
and the $i$th summand lies in $W_i$ because each $W_i$ is $T$-invariant.
The sum $V=W_1\oplus\cdots\oplus W_k$ is direct, so
$$
f(T)\alpha=0
$$
if and only if
$$
f(T_i)\alpha_i=0
$$
for every $i$. By the choice of $\alpha_i$, this is equivalent to
$$
p_i^{r_i}\mid f
$$
for every $i$. Since the distinct irreducibles are pairwise relatively prime,
that is equivalent to
$$
m_T=p_1^{r_1}\cdots p_k^{r_k}\mid f.
$$
Thus the annihilator ideal of $\alpha$ is generated by $m_T$, and the monic
$T$-annihilator of $\alpha$ is
$$
\boxed{m_T}.
$$
:::
:::
