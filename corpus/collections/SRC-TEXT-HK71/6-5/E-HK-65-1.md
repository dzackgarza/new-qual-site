::: {.exercise}
Find an invertible real matrix $P$ such that $P^{-1}AP$ and $P^{-1}BP$ are both diagonal, where $A$ and $B$ are the real matrices

(a)
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -8 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}
\]

(b)
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & a \\ a & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #p1-1}
$A$ has eigenvalues $1$ and $2$ (it is upper-triangular), with eigenvectors $e_1 = (1,0)$ and $(2,1)$.

::: pf-proof
$A e_1 = e_1$, and $A(2,1) = (4, 2) = 2(2,1)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p1-2}
$B$ has eigenvalues $3$ and $-1$, with eigenvectors $e_1 = (1,0)$ and $(2,1)$.

::: pf-proof
$B e_1 = 3e_1$, and $B(2,1) = (-2, -1) = -1(2,1)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p1-3}
Hence $A$ and $B$ are simultaneously diagonalized by $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ (whose columns are the common eigenvectors).

::: pf-proof
steps [](#p1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#p1-2){.pf-ref} (both matrices share the eigenvectors $e_1$ and $(2,1)$).
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p1-4}
Indeed $P^{-1}AP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $P^{-1}BP = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
step [](#p1-3){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

:::

**(b).**

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #p2-1}
$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues $0$ and $2$, with eigenvectors $(1,-1)$ and $(1,1)$.

::: pf-proof
$A(1,-1) = (0,0)$ and $A(1,1) = (2,2) = 2(1,1)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-2}
$B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a \\ a & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues $1 + a$ and $1 - a$, with eigenvectors $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$.

::: pf-proof
$B(1,1) = (1+a, 1+a) = (1+a)(1,1)$ and $B(1,-1) = (1-a, a-1) = (1-a)(1,-1)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-3}
Hence $A$ and $B$ are simultaneously diagonalized by $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ (columns are the common eigenvectors $(1,-1)$ and $(1,1)$).

::: pf-proof
steps [](#p2-1){.pf-ref} and [](#p2-2){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-4}
Indeed $P^{-1}AP = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $P^{-1}BP = \begin{pmatrix} 1-a & 0 \\ 0 & 1+a \end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
step [](#p2-3){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

Steps [](#p1-4){.pf-ref} (a) and [](#p2-4){.pf-ref} (b).
:::
