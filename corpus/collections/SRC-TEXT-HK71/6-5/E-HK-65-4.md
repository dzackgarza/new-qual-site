::: {.exercise}
Let $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$ be $n \times n$ complex matrices which commute.
Let $E$ be the $2n \times 2n$ matrix
\[
E = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Prove that $\operatorname{det} E = \operatorname{det}(AD - BC)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Prove the formula when the upper-left block is invertible.
::: {.proof}
Assume first that $A$ is invertible. Since $A$ commutes with $B,C,D$, its
inverse also commutes with them. Multiply $E$ on the left by
$$
L=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\-CA^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}.
$$
This block lower-triangular matrix has determinant $1$, and
$$
LE
=
\begin{bmatrix}
A&B\\
0&D-CA^{-1}B
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Hence
$$
\det E
=\det A\,\det(D-CA^{-1}B).
$$
Because all relevant factors commute,
$$
A(D-CA^{-1}B)=AD-CB=AD-BC.
$$
Taking determinants and using multiplicativity gives
$$
\det E
=\det\bigl(A(D-CA^{-1}B)\bigr)
=\det(AD-BC).
$$
Thus the formula holds whenever $A$ is invertible [@HK71].
:::

<1>2. Perturb $A$ by a scalar to remove the invertibility hypothesis.
::: {.proof}
For $t\in\mathbb C$, define
$$
E(t)=
\begin{bmatrix}
A+tI&B\\
C&D
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
The four blocks still commute. The polynomial
$$
q(t)=\det(A+tI)
$$
is monic of degree $n$, so it has only finitely many zeros. Therefore
$A+tI$ is invertible for infinitely many complex numbers $t$. For every such
$t$, step <1>1 gives
$$
\det E(t)
=\det\bigl((A+tI)D-BC\bigr).
$$

Both sides are polynomials in $t$: determinants are polynomial expressions in
their matrix entries, and every entry of the two displayed matrices is affine
in $t$. Since two complex polynomials agreeing at infinitely many values are
identical, the equality holds for every $t\in\mathbb C$. Setting $t=0$ yields
$$
\boxed{
\det\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix}
=\det(AD-BC).}
$$
:::
:::
