::: {.exercise}
Let
\[
\alpha_1 = (1, 1, -2, 1), \quad \alpha_2 = (3, 0, 4, -1), \quad \alpha_3 = (-1, 2, 5, 2).
\]
Let
\[
\alpha = (4, -5, 9, -7), \quad \beta = (3, 1, -4, 4), \quad \gamma = (-1, 1, 0, 1).
\]

(a) Which of the vectors $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ are in the subspace of $\RR^4$ spanned by the $\alpha_i$?

(b) Which of the vectors $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ are in the subspace of $C^4$ spanned by the $\alpha_i$?

(c) Does this suggest a theorem?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The $\alpha_i$ are linearly independent, so they span a $3$-dimensional subspace $W$.
Proof: the matrix with columns $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$ has rank $3$ (its three columns are independent).

<1>2. A vector $v$ lies in $W$ iff the matrix $[\alpha_1\ \alpha_2\ \alpha_3\ v]$ has rank $3$ (i.e. $v$ does not increase the rank).
Proof: $v \in W$ iff $v$ is a linear combination of the $\alpha_i$, iff the augmented matrix has the same rank as $[\alpha_1\ \alpha_2\ \alpha_3]$.

<1>3. $\alpha = (4,-5,9,-7)$ is in $W$.
Proof: $\operatorname{rank}[\alpha_1\ \alpha_2\ \alpha_3\ \alpha] = 3 = \operatorname{rank}[\alpha_1\ \alpha_2\ \alpha_3]$; indeed $\alpha = -3\alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2 - \alpha_3$.

<1>4. $\beta = (3,1,-4,4)$ is not in $W$.
Proof: $\operatorname{rank}[\alpha_1\ \alpha_2\ \alpha_3\ \beta] = 4 > 3$.

<1>5. $\gamma = (-1,1,0,1)$ is not in $W$.
Proof: $\operatorname{rank}[\alpha_1\ \alpha_2\ \alpha_3\ \gamma] = 4 > 3$.

<1>6. Since the $\alpha_i$ have real entries, the real span and the complex span coincide on real vectors: a real vector is in the complex span iff it is in the real span.
Proof: if $v = \sum c_i \alpha_i$ with $c_i \in \CC$ and $v, \alpha_i$ all real, then taking real parts gives $v = \sum \operatorname{Re}(c_i) \alpha_i$ with real coefficients.

<1>7. Hence the answers to (a) and (b) are identical: $\alpha$ is in the span, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are not.
Proof: <1>3–<1>6.

<1>8. Theorem suggested: if $v_1, \ldots, v_k \in \RR^n$ and $v \in \RR^n$, then $v$ is in the $\CC$-span of the $v_i$ iff $v$ is in the $\RR$-span of the $v_i$.
Proof: <1>6.

<1>9. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>7 and <1>8.
:::
