::: {.exercise}
Consider the vectors in $\RR^4$ defined by
\[
\alpha_1 = (-1, 0, 1, 2), \quad \alpha_2 = (3, 4, -2, 5), \quad \alpha_3 = (1, 4, 0, 9).
\]
Find a system of homogeneous linear equations for which the space of solutions is exactly the subspace of $\RR^4$ spanned by the three given vectors.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Find homogeneous linear equations whose solution space is $W = \operatorname{span}\theset{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3}$.

<1>1. Compute the dimension of $W$.
<2>1. Form the matrix $A$ with rows $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$ and row-reduce.
::: {.proof}
row-reduce to find the rank.
:::
<2>2. $\operatorname{rank} A = 2$.
::: {.proof}
row-reducing $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 & -2 & 5 \\ 1 & 4 & 0 & 9 \end{pmatrix}$ gives rank $2$ (the three vectors are linearly dependent, spanning a $2$-dimensional subspace).
:::
<2>3. Hence $\dim W = 2$, so $W$ is cut out by $4 - 2 = 2$ independent linear equations.
::: {.proof}
the solution space of a system of rank $r$ in $\RR^4$ has dimension $4 - r$, so we need $r = 2$.
:::

<1>2. Find a basis for $W^\perp$.
<2>1. $W^\perp$ is the null space of $A$ (vectors $n$ with $n \cdot \alpha_i = 0$ for all $i$).
::: {.proof}
$W^\perp = \theset{n : n \cdot \alpha_i = 0 \text{ for } i = 1,2,3}$.
:::
<2>2. A basis for $W^\perp$ is $\theset{n_1, n_2}$ with $n_1 = (4, -1, 4, 0)$ and $n_2 = (8, -11, 0, 4)$.
::: {.proof}
solving $A n = 0$ gives the null space spanned by $(1, -1/4, 1, 0)$ and $(2, -11/4, 0, 1)$; clearing denominators gives $n_1 = (4, -1, 4, 0)$ and $n_2 = (8, -11, 0, 4)$.
:::

<1>3. The system is $n_1 \cdot x = 0$ and $n_2 \cdot x = 0$.
<2>1. $W = \theset{x \in \RR^4 : n_1 \cdot x = 0 \text{ and } n_2 \cdot x = 0}$.
::: {.proof}
$W$ is $2$-dimensional, and the two equations $n_1 \cdot x = 0$, $n_2 \cdot x = 0$ cut out a $2$-dimensional subspace containing $W$ (since $n_1, n_2 \perp W$), so they are equal.
:::
<2>2. Explicitly, the system is
$$
\begin{cases}
4x_1 - x_2 + 4x_3 = 0, \\
8x_1 - 11x_2 + 4x_4 = 0.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
expand $n_1 \cdot x = 0$ and $n_2 \cdot x = 0$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>3.2 gives the homogeneous system whose solution space is exactly $W$.
:::
:::
