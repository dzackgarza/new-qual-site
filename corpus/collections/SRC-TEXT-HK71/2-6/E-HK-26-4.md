::: {.exercise}
In $C^3$, let
\[
\alpha_1 = (1, 0, -i), \quad \alpha_2 = (1 + i, 1 - i, 1), \quad \alpha_3 = (i, i, i).
\]
Prove that these vectors form a basis for $C^3$.
What are the coordinates of the vector $(a, b, c)$ in this basis?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Prove that $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$ forms a basis of $\mathbb{C}^3$:
<2>1. Form the matrix $A$ having $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$ as columns:
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1+i & i \\
0 & 1-i & i \\
-i & 1 & i
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
matrix representation of column vectors.
:::
<2>2. Compute the determinant of $A$ by cofactor expansion along the first column:
\[
\det(A) = 1 \cdot \det\begin{pmatrix} 1-i & i \\ 1 & i \end{pmatrix} - i \cdot \det\begin{pmatrix} 1+i & i \\ 1-i & i \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Compute the $2 \times 2$ determinants:
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix} 1-i & i \\ 1 & i \end{pmatrix} = (1-i)i - i = i + 1 - i = 1,
\]
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix} 1+i & i \\ 1-i & i \end{pmatrix} = (1+i)i - i(1-i) = i - 1 - i - 1 = -2.
\]
Thus:
\[
\det(A) = 1(1) - i(-2) = 1 + 2i.
\]
::: {.proof}
cofactor expansion.
:::
<2>3. Since $\det(A) = 1 + 2i \neq 0$, the matrix $A$ is invertible, so the vectors $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$ are linearly independent.
Since $\dim(\mathbb{C}^3) = 3$, they form a basis for $\mathbb{C}^3$.
::: {.proof}
non-zero determinant characterization of bases.
:::

<1>2. Compute the coordinates $(z_1, z_2, z_3)$ of $(a, b, c)$ in the basis:
<2>1. The coordinates satisfy $A \begin{pmatrix} z_1 \\ z_2 \\ z_3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix}$, so $\begin{pmatrix} z_1 \\ z_2 \\ z_3 \end{pmatrix} = A^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix}$.
::: {.proof}
definition of coordinate vector.
:::
<2>2. Compute the inverse matrix $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)} \operatorname{adj}(A) = \frac{1-2i}{5} C^T$:
The cofactor matrix of $A$ is:
\[
C = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1+i \\
1 & i-1 & -i \\
-2 & -i & 1-i
\end{pmatrix}
\implies
\operatorname{adj}(A) = C^T = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & -2 \\
1 & i-1 & -i \\
1+i & -i & 1-i
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
computation of cofactor transpose.
:::
<2>3. Multiply $A^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1-2i}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & -2 \\ 1 & i-1 & -i \\ 1+i & -i & 1-i \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix}$:
\[
z_1 = \frac{1-2i}{5}(a + b - 2c),
\]
\[
z_2 = \frac{1-2i}{5}\big(a + (i-1)b - ic\big) = \frac{(1-2i)a + (1+3i)b - (2+i)c}{5},
\]
\[
z_3 = \frac{1-2i}{5}\big((1+i)a - ib + (1-i)c\big) = \frac{(3-i)a - (2+i)b - (1+3i)c}{5}.
\]
::: {.proof}
matrix-vector multiplication.
:::

<1>3. Conclusion:
$\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$ is a basis, and the coordinates are $(z_1, z_2, z_3)$ as given above. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2.
:::
