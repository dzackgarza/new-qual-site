::: {.exercise}
Let $s < n$ and $A$ an $s \times n$ matrix with entries in the field $F$.
Use Theorem 4 (not its proof) to show that there is a non-zero $X$ in $F^{n \times 1}$ such that $AX = 0$.
:::


::: {.solution}
Let $A_1, \ldots, A_n$ be the columns of $A$; they are $n$ vectors in $F^{s \times 1}$. The space $F^{s \times 1}$ is spanned by the $s$ standard basis columns. By Theorem 4 of Section 2.3 [@HK71], every subset of $F^{s \times 1}$ with more than $s$ vectors is linearly dependent. Since $n > s$, there are scalars $x_1, \ldots, x_n$, not all $0$, with
$$
x_1A_1 + \cdots + x_nA_n = 0.
$$
Let $X$ be the column with entries $x_1, \ldots, x_n$. Then $X \neq 0$ and $AX = x_1A_1 + \cdots + x_nA_n = 0$.
:::
