::: {.exercise}
Let $s < n$ and $A$ an $s \times n$ matrix with entries in the field $F$.
Use Theorem 4 (not its proof) to show that there is a non-zero $X$ in $F^{n \times 1}$ such that $AX = 0$.
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. It uses Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), Theorem 4 of Chapter 2, p. 44, as the exercise directs: a vector space spanned by $m$ vectors contains no independent set of more than $m$ elements.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #columns}
The $n$ columns $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ of $A$ are vectors in $F^{s\times1}$, which is spanned by the $s$ standard vectors $e_1,\ldots,e_s$.

::: pf-proof
$A$ has $s$ rows and $n$ columns, so each column has its $s$ entries in $F$. The vector $e_k$ is the column with $1$ in row $k$ and $0$ elsewhere, and a column $v=(v_1,\ldots,v_s)^t$ is $\sum_{k=1}^s v_k e_k$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #dependent}
The columns $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ are linearly dependent.

::: pf-proof
Were they independent, Theorem 4 of Chapter 2 (p. 44) applied to the spanning set $e_1,\ldots,e_s$ of step [](#columns){.pf-ref} would give $n\le s$, contrary to the hypothesis $s<n$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
There is a non-zero $X\in F^{n\times1}$ with $AX=0$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#dependent){.pf-ref} there are $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in F$, not all zero, with $\sum_{j=1}^n x_jA_j=0$. Put $X\coloneqq(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^t$, a non-zero column. The $k$-th entry of $AX$ is $\sum_{j=1}^n a_{kj}x_j$, which is the $k$-th entry of $\sum_j x_jA_j$, so $AX=0$.
:::
:::
