::: {.exercise}
Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be subspaces of a vector space $V$ such that $W_1 + W_2 = V$ and $W_1 \cap W_2 = \{0\}$.
Prove that for each vector $\alpha$ in $V$ there are unique vectors $\alpha_1$ in $W_1$ and $\alpha_2$ in $W_2$ such that $\alpha = \alpha_1 + \alpha_2$.
:::


::: solution
Existence follows from $W_1+W_2=V$: for every $\alpha\in V$ there are $\alpha_1\in W_1$ and $\alpha_2\in W_2$ with
\[
\alpha=\alpha_1+\alpha_2.
\]
For uniqueness, suppose also
\[
\alpha=\beta_1+\beta_2,
\qquad
\beta_1\in W_1,
\quad
\beta_2\in W_2.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha_1-\beta_1=\beta_2-\alpha_2.
\]
The left side lies in $W_1$ and the right side lies in $W_2$, so their common value lies in $W_1\cap W_2=\{0\}$. Hence
\[
\alpha_1=\beta_1,
\qquad
\alpha_2=\beta_2.
\]
Thus the decomposition is unique.
:::
