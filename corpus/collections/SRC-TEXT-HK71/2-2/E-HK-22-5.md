::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field and let $n$ be a positive integer ($n \geq 2$). Let $V$ be the vector space of all $n \times n$ matrices over $F$.
Which of the following sets of matrices $A$ in $V$ are subspaces of $V$?

(a) all invertible $A$;

(b) all non-invertible $A$;

(c) all $A$ such that $AB = BA$, where $B$ is some fixed matrix in $V$;

(d) all $A$ such that $A^2 = A$.
:::


::: {.solution}
Only (c) is always a subspace.

::: pf

::: pf-step
The invertible matrices are not a subspace.

::: pf-proof
The zero matrix is not invertible, so the set does not contain the zero vector of $V$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The non-invertible matrices are not a subspace when $n\ge2$.

::: pf-proof
Let
$$
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,1,\ldots,1),
\qquad
C=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0,\ldots,0).
$$
Both are singular, but $A+C=I_n$ is invertible.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The matrices commuting with a fixed matrix $B$ form a subspace.

::: pf-proof
If $AB=BA$ and $CB=BC$, then for scalars $r,s$,
$$
(rA+sC)B=rAB+sCB=rBA+sBC=B(rA+sC).
$$
The zero matrix also commutes with $B$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The idempotent matrices are not a subspace.

::: pf-proof
Let $A$ and $C$ be the $n\times n$ matrices whose upper-left $2\times2$ blocks are
$$
\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&1\end{bmatrix},
$$
with all other entries $0$. Direct multiplication gives $A^2=A$ and $C^2=C$. The sum $S=A+C$ has upper-left block of all ones, so $S^2=2S$. Since $S\ne0$ and $2\ne1$ in every field, $S^2\ne S$. Thus the set is not closed under addition, over any field.
:::
