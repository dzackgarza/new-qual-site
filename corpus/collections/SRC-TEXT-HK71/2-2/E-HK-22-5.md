::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field and let $n$ be a positive integer ($n \geq 2$). Let $V$ be the vector space of all $n \times n$ matrices over $F$.
Which of the following sets of matrices $A$ in $V$ are subspaces of $V$?

(a) all invertible $A$;

(b) all non-invertible $A$;

(c) all $A$ such that $AB = BA$, where $B$ is some fixed matrix in $V$;

(d) all $A$ such that $A^2 = A$.
:::


::: solution
Only (c) is always a subspace.

<1>1. The invertible matrices are not a subspace.
::: proof
The zero matrix is not invertible, so the set does not contain the zero vector of $V$.
:::

<1>2. The non-invertible matrices are not a subspace when $n\ge2$.
::: proof
Let
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,1,\ldots,1),
\qquad
C=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0,\ldots,0).
\]
Both are singular, but $A+C=I_n$ is invertible.
:::

<1>3. The matrices commuting with a fixed matrix $B$ form a subspace.
::: proof
If $AB=BA$ and $CB=BC$, then for scalars $r,s$,
\[
(rA+sC)B=rAB+sCB=rBA+sBC=B(rA+sC).
\]
The zero matrix also commutes with $B$.
:::

<1>4. The idempotent matrices are not a subspace.
::: proof
Both $0$ and $I_n$ are idempotent, but for a scalar $c$ with $c^2\ne c$ the matrix $cI_n$ is not idempotent. Over any field with such a scalar this already disproves closure under scalar multiplication. In the field with two elements, take the two idempotents
\[
E_{11},\qquad E_{22};
\]
for $n\ge2$; their sum is idempotent, so instead use
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
C=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&1\end{bmatrix}
\]
inside the upper-left $2\times2$ block. Both satisfy $A^2=A$ and $C^2=C$, while $(A+C)^2=0\ne A+C$ in characteristic $2$. Thus the set is not a subspace over any field.
:::
:::
