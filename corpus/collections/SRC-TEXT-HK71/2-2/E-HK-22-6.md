::: {.exercise}

(a) Prove that the only subspaces of $\RR^1$ are $\RR^1$ and the zero subspace.

(b) Prove that a subspace of $\RR^2$ is $\RR^2$, or the zero subspace, or consists of all scalar multiples of some fixed vector in $\RR^2$.
(The last type of subspace is, intuitively, a straight line through the origin.)

(c) Can you describe the subspaces of $\RR^3$?
:::


::: solution
<1>1. The only subspaces of $\RR$ are $\{0\}$ and $\RR$.
::: proof
Let $W\le\RR$. If $W=\{0\}$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose $0\ne a\in W$. For every $x\in\RR$,
\[
x=\frac{x}{a}a\in W,
\]
so $W=\RR$.
:::

<1>2. Every subspace of $\RR^2$ is either $\{0\}$, a line through the origin, or all of $\RR^2$.
::: proof
Let $W\le\RR^2$. If $W=\{0\}$, done. Otherwise choose $0\ne v\in W$. If every vector of $W$ is a scalar multiple of $v$, then $W=\operatorname{span}\{v\}$, a line through the origin. Otherwise choose $w\in W$ not proportional to $v$. Then $v,w$ are linearly independent and hence form a basis of $\RR^2$, so $W=\RR^2$.
:::

<1>3. Every subspace of $\RR^3$ is one of $\{0\}$, a line through the origin, a plane through the origin, or all of $\RR^3$.
::: proof
A subspace has dimension $0,1,2,$ or $3$. Dimension $0$ gives $\{0\}$; dimension $1$ gives the span of one nonzero vector, hence a line through the origin; dimension $2$ gives the span of two independent vectors, hence a plane through the origin; dimension $3$ gives $\RR^3$.
:::
:::
