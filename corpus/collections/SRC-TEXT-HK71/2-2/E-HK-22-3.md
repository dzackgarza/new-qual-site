::: {.exercise}
Is the vector $(3, -1, 0, -1)$ in the subspace of $\RR^4$ spanned by the vectors $(2, -1, 3, 2)$, $(-1, 1, 1, -3)$, and $(1, 1, 9, -5)$?
:::


::: {.solution}

The computation is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §2.2 Exercise 3.

Let $u_{1}=(2,-1,3,2)$, $u_{2}=(-1,1,1,-3)$, $u_{3}=(1,1,9,-5)$ and $w=(3,-1,0,-1)$, and write $W=\operatorname{span}\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #equivalence}
$w\in W$ if and only if the system
$$
2a-b+c=3,\qquad -a+b+c=-1,\qquad 3a+b+9c=0,\qquad 2a-3b-5c=-1
$$
has a solution $(a,b,c)\in\RR^{3}$.

::: pf-proof
$w\in W$ means $w=au_{1}+bu_{2}+cu_{3}$ for some scalars, and comparing the four coordinates of the two sides gives exactly the four displayed equations.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #first-two}
The first two equations imply $a+2c=2$.

::: pf-proof
Adding $(2a-b+c)+(-a+b+c)$ gives $a+2c$, and adding the right-hand sides $3+(-1)$ gives $2$; the sum of two true equations is a true equation.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #last-two}
The second and third equations imply $a+2c=\tfrac14$.

::: pf-proof
The second equation gives $b=a-c-1$. Substituting into the third: $3a+(a-c-1)+9c=0$, that is $4a+8c=1$, and dividing by $4$ gives $a+2c=\tfrac14$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inconsistent}
The three equations $2a-b+c=3$, $-a+b+c=-1$, $3a+b+9c=0$ have no common solution.

::: pf-proof
A solution would satisfy both $a+2c=2$ (step [](#first-two){.pf-ref}) and $a+2c=\tfrac14$ (step [](#last-two){.pf-ref}); subtracting gives $0=2-\tfrac14=\tfrac74$, which is false. So no triple of real numbers satisfies the three equations, and a fortiori none satisfies the four equations of step [](#equivalence){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$(3,-1,0,-1)$ is not in the span of $(2,-1,3,2)$, $(-1,1,1,-3)$ and $(1,1,9,-5)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#inconsistent){.pf-ref} the system of step [](#equivalence){.pf-ref} has no solution, so by that step the vector is not a combination of the three.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The three given vectors are themselves dependent, $u_{3}=2u_{1}+3u_{2}$, so $W$ is the plane spanned by $u_{1}$ and $u_{2}$.

::: pf-proof
$2u_{1}+3u_{2}=(4-3,\ -2+3,\ 6+3,\ 4-9)=(1,1,9,-5)=u_{3}$. Hence $u_{1},u_{2}$ span $W$; they are independent because their entries in the first two coordinates form the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\ -1&1\end{bmatrix}$ of determinant $2-1=1\neq0$, so $\dim W=2$. This observation is not needed for the answer, which step [](#inconsistent){.pf-ref} settles, but it explains why a vector of $\RR^{4}$ can miss $W$.
:::
