::: {.exercise}
Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be subspaces of a vector space $V$ such that the set-theoretic union of $W_1$ and $W_2$ is also a subspace.
Prove that one of the spaces $W_i$ is contained in the other.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #assume-neither-contains}
Suppose for contradiction that neither $W_1 \subseteq W_2$ nor $W_2 \subseteq W_1$.

::: pf-proof
assume the conclusion fails.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Then there exist $w_1 \in W_1 \setminus W_2$ and $w_2 \in W_2 \setminus W_1$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#assume-neither-contains){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sum-in-union}
Since $W_1 \cup W_2$ is a subspace, $w_1 + w_2 \in W_1 \cup W_2$.

::: pf-proof
closure under addition.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #case-sum-in-w1}
If $w_1 + w_2 \in W_1$, then $w_2 = (w_1 + w_2) - w_1 \in W_1$, contradicting $w_2 \notin W_1$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#sum-in-union){.pf-ref} and closure of $W_1$ under subtraction.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #case-sum-in-w2}
If $w_1 + w_2 \in W_2$, then $w_1 = (w_1 + w_2) - w_2 \in W_2$, contradicting $w_1 \notin W_2$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#sum-in-union){.pf-ref} and closure of $W_2$ under subtraction.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #containment-established}
Both cases contradict, so one of $W_1, W_2$ is contained in the other.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#case-sum-in-w1){.pf-ref} and [](#case-sum-in-w2){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Step [](#containment-established){.pf-ref}.
:::
