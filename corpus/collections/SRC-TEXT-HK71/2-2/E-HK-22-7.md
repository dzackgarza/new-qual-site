::: {.exercise}
Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be subspaces of a vector space $V$ such that the set-theoretic union of $W_1$ and $W_2$ is also a subspace.
Prove that one of the spaces $W_i$ is contained in the other.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose for contradiction that neither $W_1 \subseteq W_2$ nor $W_2 \subseteq W_1$.
::: {.proof}
assume the conclusion fails.
:::

<1>2. Then there exist $w_1 \in W_1 \setminus W_2$ and $w_2 \in W_2 \setminus W_1$.
::: {.proof}
<1>1.
:::

<1>3. Since $W_1 \cup W_2$ is a subspace, $w_1 + w_2 \in W_1 \cup W_2$.
::: {.proof}
closure under addition.
:::

<1>4. If $w_1 + w_2 \in W_1$, then $w_2 = (w_1 + w_2) - w_1 \in W_1$, contradicting $w_2 \notin W_1$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and closure of $W_1$ under subtraction.
:::

<1>5. If $w_1 + w_2 \in W_2$, then $w_1 = (w_1 + w_2) - w_2 \in W_2$, contradicting $w_1 \notin W_2$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and closure of $W_2$ under subtraction.
:::

<1>6. Both cases contradict, so one of $W_1, W_2$ is contained in the other.
::: {.proof}
<1>4 and <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>6.
:::
:::
