::: {.exercise}
In each of the following cases, let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^2$ which is represented by the matrix $A$ in the standard ordered basis for $\RR^2$, and let $U$ be the linear operator on $\CC^2$ represented by $A$ in the standard ordered basis.
Find the characteristic polynomial for $T$ and that for $U$, find the characteristic values of each operator, and for each such characteristic value $c$ find a basis for the corresponding space of characteristic vectors.
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §6.2 Exercise 1, p. 189, the three matrices matching the printed ones. The characteristic polynomials, the eigenvalues over $\RR$ and $\CC$ and the eigenvector bases were all recomputed.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #first}
For $A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$: $\chi=x(x-1)$, the characteristic values are $0$ and $1$ over both $\RR$ and $\CC$, and the eigenspaces are $\operatorname{span}\{(0,1)\}$ and $\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}$.

::: pf-proof
$\det(xI-A_1)=\det\begin{pmatrix}x-1&0\\0&x\end{pmatrix}=x(x-1)$, whose roots $0,1$ are real. Solving $A_1v=0$ gives $v_1=0$, so the $0$-eigenspace is the second coordinate axis; solving $A_1v=v$ gives $v_2=0$, so the $1$-eigenspace is the first.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #second}
For $A_2=\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}$: $\chi=x^2-3x+5$, there are no real characteristic values, and over $\CC$ the values are $\tfrac{3\pm i\sqrt{11}}{2}$ with eigenvectors $(3,\lambda-2)$.

::: pf-proof
$\det(xI-A_2)=(x-2)(x-1)+3=x^2-3x+5$, whose discriminant is $9-20=-11<0$, so no real roots; over $\CC$ the roots are $\tfrac{3\pm i\sqrt{11}}{2}$. For such a root $\lambda$, $(A_2-\lambda I)(3,\lambda-2)^t=\bigl((2-\lambda)3+3(\lambda-2),\ -3+(1-\lambda)(\lambda-2)\bigr)=(0,0)$ because $(\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)=-3$ at a root of $\lambda^2-3\lambda+5$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #third}
For $A_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$: $\chi=x(x-2)$, the characteristic values are $0$ and $2$ over both fields, and the eigenspaces are $\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)\}$ and $\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}$.

::: pf-proof
$\det(xI-A_3)=(x-1)^2-1=x(x-2)$. Solving $A_3v=0$ gives $v_1+v_2=0$, so the $0$-eigenspace is spanned by $(1,-1)$; solving $A_3v=2v$ gives $v_1=v_2$, so the $2$-eigenspace is spanned by $(1,1)$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
The characteristic polynomials of $T$ and $U$ coincide for each of the three matrices; the characteristic values differ only in that $A_2$ has none in $\RR$ and two in $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
The polynomial $\det(xI-A)$ depends on the matrix and not on the field of scalars, which is steps [](#first){.pf-ref}, [](#second){.pf-ref} and [](#third){.pf-ref} in each case; the values are the roots lying in the field, and the eigenspaces are computed over $\CC$, hence over $\RR$ where the roots are real.
:::
