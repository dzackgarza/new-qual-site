::: {.exercise}
Let
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -3 & -2 \\ 4 & -1 & -2 \\ 10 & -5 & -3 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Is $A$ similar over the field $\RR$ to a diagonal matrix?
Is $A$ similar over the field $C$ to a diagonal matrix?
:::

::: {.solution}
For
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}6&-3&-2\\4&-1&-2\\10&-5&-3\end{bmatrix},
\]
a determinant computation gives
\[
\chi_A(x)=(x-2)(x^2+1).
\]
Over $\mathbb R$, the only characteristic value is $2$. A real $3\times3$ matrix similar over $\mathbb R$ to a diagonal matrix must have three real characteristic values counted with multiplicity, namely its diagonal entries. Since $x^2+1$ has no real root, $A$ is not similar over $\mathbb R$ to a diagonal matrix.

Over $\mathbb C$, the characteristic values are $2,i,-i$, which are distinct. Corresponding characteristic vectors are, for example,
\[
(1,0,2),\qquad (3+i,3+i,5),\qquad (3-i,3-i,5).
\]
Eigenvectors belonging to distinct characteristic values are linearly independent. Hence these three vectors form a basis of $\mathbb C^3$, so $A$ is similar over $\mathbb C$ to
\[
\operatorname{diag}(2,i,-i).
\]
:::
