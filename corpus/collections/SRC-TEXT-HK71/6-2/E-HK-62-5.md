::: {.exercise}
Let
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -3 & -2 \\ 4 & -1 & -2 \\ 10 & -5 & -3 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Is $A$ similar over the field $\RR$ to a diagonal matrix?
Is $A$ similar over the field $\CC$ to a diagonal matrix?

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise ; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §6.2 Exercise 5 on printed p. 189; the computation below is the card's own.

Throughout, $A=\begin{bmatrix}6&-3&-2\\4&-1&-2\\10&-5&-3\end{bmatrix}$, and $\chi_A(x)=\det(xI-A)$ is its characteristic polynomial.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #characteristic}
$\chi_A(x)=x^{3}-2x^{2}+x-2=(x-2)(x^{2}+1)$.

::: pf-proof
For a $3\times3$ matrix, $\chi_A(x)=x^{3}-(\operatorname{tr}A)x^{2}+s_1x-\det A$, where $s_1$ is the sum of the three principal $2\times2$ minors, since both sides are obtained from the same expansion of $\det(xI-A)$ (the constant term is $\det(-A)=-\det A$ and the leading terms are $x^{3}$ and $x^{2}$ times the trace). Here $\operatorname{tr}A=6-1-3=2$, and the minors are $\det\begin{bmatrix}-1&-2\\-5&-3\end{bmatrix}=3-10=-7$, $\det\begin{bmatrix}6&-2\\10&-3\end{bmatrix}=-18+20=2$, $\det\begin{bmatrix}6&-3\\4&-1\end{bmatrix}=-6+12=6$, so $s_1=-7+2+6=1$; and $\det A=2$ (expanding along the first row: $6(3-10)+3(-12+20)-2(-20+10)=-42+24+20=2$). Hence $\chi_A(x)=x^{3}-2x^{2}+x-2$, and this factors as $(x-2)(x^{2}+1)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #over-r}
$A$ is not similar over $\RR$ to a diagonal matrix.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $P^{-1}AP=D$ with $D$ diagonal and $P$ real and invertible. Similar matrices have the same characteristic polynomial, and $\chi_D(x)=\prod_i(x-d_{ii})$ with all $d_{ii}\in\RR$; so $\chi_A$ would be a product of linear factors over $\RR$. But by step [](#characteristic){.pf-ref} $\chi_A(x)=(x-2)(x^{2}+1)$, and $x^{2}+1$ has no real root, so it is not a product of linear factors over $\RR$. That contradicts the equality of characteristic polynomials; hence no such $P$ exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #vectors}
Over $\CC$ the three vectors $(1,0,2)$, $(3+i,3+i,5)$ and $(3-i,3-i,5)$ are characteristic vectors of $A$ for the characteristic values $2$, $i$ and $-i$.

::: pf-proof
Direct multiplication:
$$
A\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}6-4\\4-4\\10-6\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2\\0\\4\end{bmatrix}=2\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\2\end{bmatrix},
$$
$$
A\begin{bmatrix}3+i\\3+i\\5\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}6(3+i)-3(3+i)-10\\4(3+i)-(3+i)-10\\10(3+i)-5(3+i)-15\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-1+3i\\-1+3i\\5i\end{bmatrix}=i\begin{bmatrix}3+i\\3+i\\5\end{bmatrix},
$$
since $i(3+i)=-1+3i$ and $i\cdot5=5i$, so the third coordinate $10(3+i)-5(3+i)-15=5(3+i)-15=5i$ matches; and $A(3-i,3-i,5)=(-1-3i,-1-3i,-5i)=(-i)(3-i,3-i,5)$ by the same computation with $i$ replaced by $-i$, using $(-i)(3-i)=-1-3i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #over-c}
$A$ is similar over $\CC$ to $\operatorname{diag}(2,i,-i)$.

::: pf-proof
The characteristic values $2,i,-i$ are distinct. Characteristic vectors belonging to distinct characteristic values of a matrix are linearly independent: if a nontrivial relation $\sum_ja_jv_j=0$ held among vectors $v_j$ with $Av_j=\lambda_jv_j$ and the $\lambda_j$ distinct, choose one of minimal length and let $\lambda_k$ be the value of one of the vectors appearing; applying $A$ gives $\sum_ja_j\lambda_jv_j=0$, and subtracting $\lambda_k$ times the original relation gives $\sum_j a_j(\lambda_j-\lambda_k)v_j=0$, a shorter nontrivial relation because the $k$-th coefficient vanishes and every other one is nonzero, contradicting minimality. So the three vectors of step [](#vectors){.pf-ref}, being nonzero and belonging to distinct characteristic values, form a basis of $\CC^{3}$. The matrix with these vectors as columns is therefore invertible and conjugates $A$ to the diagonal matrix of the corresponding characteristic values.

:::
