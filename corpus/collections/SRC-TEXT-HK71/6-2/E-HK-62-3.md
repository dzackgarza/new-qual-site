::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ triangular matrix over the field $F$.
Prove that the characteristic values of $A$ are the diagonal entries of $A$, i.e., the scalars $A_{ii}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Since $A$ is triangular, so is $xI-A$, and its diagonal entries are
\[
x-A_{11},\ldots,x-A_{nn}.
\]
The determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries. Therefore
\[
\chi_A(x)=\det(xI-A)=\prod_{i=1}^n (x-A_{ii}).
\]
A scalar $c\in F$ is a characteristic value exactly when $\chi_A(c)=0$. Since $F$ is a field,
\[
\prod_{i=1}^n(c-A_{ii})=0
\]
if and only if $c=A_{ii}$ for at least one $i$. Hence the characteristic values are exactly the diagonal entries of $A$.
:::
