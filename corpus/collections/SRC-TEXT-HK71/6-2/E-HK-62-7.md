::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on the $n$-dimensional vector space $V$, and suppose that $T$ has $n$ distinct characteristic values.
Prove that $T$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose nonzero eigenvectors $v_i$ with $Tv_i=c_i v_i$, where $c_1,\ldots,c_n$ are the distinct characteristic values. Eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent: if a shortest nontrivial relation $\sum_{i=1}^r a_i v_i=0$ existed, applying $T-c_rI$ would give
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}a_i(c_i-c_r)v_i=0,
\]
a shorter nontrivial relation, since $c_i-c_r\ne0$. Thus $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ are linearly independent. Since $\dim V=n$, they form a basis, and the matrix of $T$ in this basis is diagonal. Hence $T$ is diagonalizable.
:::
