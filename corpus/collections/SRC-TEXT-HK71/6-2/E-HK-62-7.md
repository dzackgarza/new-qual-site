::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on the $n$-dimensional vector space $V$, and suppose that $T$ has $n$ distinct characteristic values.
Prove that $T$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §6.2 Exercise 7. Here $V$ is $n$-dimensional over a field and $T$ has $n$ distinct characteristic values, that is, $n$ distinct eigenvalues in the field.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #indep}

Eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent, and more generally any finite set of nonzero eigenvectors with pairwise distinct eigenvalues is independent.

::: pf-proof

Suppose a nontrivial relation with the fewest terms, $\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}v_{i}=0$ with all $a_{i}\neq0$ and the $c_{i}$ pairwise distinct; then $r\geq2$. Applying $T-c_{r}I$ gives $\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}a_{i}(c_{i}-c_{r})v_{i}=0$, a relation with fewer terms whose coefficients $a_{i}(c_{i}-c_{r})$ are nonzero because $c_{i}\neq c_{r}$ and $a_i\neq0$ — contradicting minimality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #choose}

Choose $v_{i}\neq0$ with $Tv_{i}=c_{i}v_{i}$ for the $n$ distinct eigenvalues $c_{1},\dots,c_{n}$.

::: pf-proof

A characteristic value is by definition an eigenvalue, so an eigenvector for it exists, and eigenvectors are nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis}

$v_{1},\dots,v_{n}$ is a basis of $V$.

::: pf-proof

They are $n$ vectors, independent by steps [](#choose){.pf-ref} and [](#indep){.pf-ref}, in a space of dimension $n$; an independent set of $\dim V$ vectors is a basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$T$ is diagonalizable.

::: pf-proof

In the basis of step [](#basis){.pf-ref} the matrix of $T$ is diagonal, with diagonal entries $c_{1},\dots,c_{n}$, since $Tv_{i}=c_{i}v_{i}$.

:::
