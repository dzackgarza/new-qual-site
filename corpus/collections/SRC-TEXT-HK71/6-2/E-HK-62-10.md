::: {.exercise}
Suppose that $A$ is a $2 \times 2$ matrix with real entries which is symmetric ($A^t = A$). Prove that $A$ is similar over $\RR$ to a diagonal matrix.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\b&d\end{bmatrix}$. Its characteristic polynomial is
\[
x^2-(a+d)x+(ad-b^2),
\]
with discriminant
\[
(a-d)^2+4b^2\ge0.
\]
Thus it splits over $\mathbb R$. If the roots are distinct, eigenvectors for them are linearly independent and diagonalize $A$. If the root is repeated, the discriminant is zero, so $a=d$ and $b=0$; hence $A=aI$ is already diagonal. Therefore $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb R$.
:::
