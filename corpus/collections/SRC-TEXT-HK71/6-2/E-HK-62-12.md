::: {.exercise}
Use the result of Exercise 11 to prove the following: If $A$ is a $2 \times 2$ matrix with complex entries, then $A$ is similar over $C$ to a matrix of one of the two types
\[
\begin{bmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & b \end{bmatrix} \qquad \begin{bmatrix} a & 0 \\ 1 & a \end{bmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The characteristic polynomial of $A$ splits over $\mathbb C$. If it has two distinct roots $a,b$, eigenvectors for those roots form a basis and $A$ is similar to $\operatorname{diag}(a,b)$.

Otherwise the characteristic polynomial is $(x-a)^2$. Put $N=A-aI$. Then the characteristic polynomial of $N$ is $x^2$, so Cayley--Hamilton gives $N^2=0$. If $N=0$, then $A=aI$. If $N\ne0$, Exercise 11 gives a basis in which
\[
N=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix},
\]
and hence
\[
A=aI+N=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\1&a\end{bmatrix}.
\]
These are exactly the two stated types.
:::
