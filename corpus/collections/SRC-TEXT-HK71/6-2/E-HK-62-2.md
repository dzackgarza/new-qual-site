::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space over $F$.
What is the characteristic polynomial of the identity operator on $V$?
What is the characteristic polynomial for the zero operator?
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. The characteristic polynomial of an operator $T$ on an $n$-dimensional space is $\det(xI-[T])$, where $[T]$ is the matrix of $T$ in any ordered basis, since the determinant is unchanged under change of basis.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #identity-matrix}
In any ordered basis, the identity operator $I_{V}$ has matrix $I_{n}$.

::: pf-proof
The $j$-th column of the matrix is the coordinate matrix of $I_{V}\epsilon_{j}=\epsilon_{j}$, which is the $j$-th standard column; so the matrix is the $n\times n$ identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #zero-matrix}
In any ordered basis, the zero operator has matrix $0$.

::: pf-proof
Its $j$-th column is the coordinate matrix of the zero vector, all of whose coordinates are $0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #identity}
The characteristic polynomial of the identity operator is $(x-1)^{n}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#identity-matrix){.pf-ref},
$$
\chi_{I_{V}}(x)=\det(xI_{n}-I_{n})=\det\bigl((x-1)I_{n}\bigr)=(x-1)^{n},
$$
the last equality because the determinant of a diagonal matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, here $n$ copies of $x-1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The characteristic polynomial of the zero operator is $x^{n}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#zero-matrix){.pf-ref}, $\chi_{0}(x)=\det(xI_{n}-0)=\det(xI_{n})=x^{n}$, again because the determinant of the diagonal matrix $xI_{n}$ is the product of its $n$ diagonal entries. So the answers are $(x-1)^{n}$ for the identity operator and $x^{n}$ for the zero operator.
:::
