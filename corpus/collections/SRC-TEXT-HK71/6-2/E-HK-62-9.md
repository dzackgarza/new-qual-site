::: {.exercise}
Use the result of Exercise 8 to prove that, if $A$ and $B$ are $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$, then $AB$ and $BA$ have precisely the same characteristic values in $F$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $c\ne0$. Then $cI-AB$ is invertible exactly when $I-c^{-1}AB$ is invertible. Exercise 8, applied to $c^{-1}A$ and $B$, says this is equivalent to invertibility of $I-c^{-1}BA$, hence of $cI-BA$. Thus $AB$ and $BA$ have the same nonzero characteristic values.

For $c=0$, $AB$ is invertible iff both $A$ and $B$ are invertible. Indeed, invertibility of $AB$ makes $B$ injective and $A$ surjective; for square matrices these imply invertibility. The same criterion holds for $BA$. Hence $AB$ is singular iff $BA$ is singular. Therefore the characteristic-value sets agree completely.
:::
