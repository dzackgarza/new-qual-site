::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ and $B$ be $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$.
Prove that if $(I - AB)$ is invertible, then $I - BA$ is invertible and
$$
(I - BA)^{-1} = I + B(I - AB)^{-1}A.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §6.2 Exercise 8.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #intertwine}
$(I-BA)B=B(I-AB)$ and $A(I-BA)=(I-AB)A$.

::: pf-proof
Expand: $(I-BA)B=B-BAB$ and $B(I-AB)=B-BAB$; and $A(I-BA)=A-ABA=(I-AB)A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #candidate}
Let $C=I+B(I-AB)^{-1}A$.

::: pf-proof
This is a definition; it exists because $I-AB$ is invertible by hypothesis.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #right}
$(I-BA)C=I$.

::: pf-proof
$(I-BA)C=(I-BA)+(I-BA)B(I-AB)^{-1}A=(I-BA)+B(I-AB)(I-AB)^{-1}A$ by step [](#intertwine){.pf-ref}, and the middle product is $I$, so the sum is $(I-BA)+BA=I$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #left}
$C(I-BA)=I$.

::: pf-proof
$C(I-BA)=(I-BA)+B(I-AB)^{-1}A(I-BA)=(I-BA)+B(I-AB)^{-1}(I-AB)A$ by step [](#intertwine){.pf-ref}, and the last product is $A$, so the sum is $(I-BA)+BA=I$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$I-BA$ is invertible with inverse $C=I+B(I-AB)^{-1}A$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#right){.pf-ref} and [](#left){.pf-ref} exhibit a matrix $C$ with $(I-BA)C=I=C(I-BA)$. For square matrices a right inverse is a two-sided inverse, since a matrix with a right inverse is surjective and hence invertible, its inverse being $C$. So
$$
\boxed{(I-BA)^{-1}=I+B(I-AB)^{-1}A.}
$$
No commutativity of $A$ and $B$ is used; only the two intertwining identities of step [](#intertwine){.pf-ref}, each of which follows by expansion.
:::
