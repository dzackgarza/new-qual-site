::: {.exercise}
Let $N$ be a $2 \times 2$ complex matrix such that $N^2 = 0$.
Prove that either $N = 0$ or $N$ is similar over $\CC$ to
$$
\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §6.2 Exercise 11; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $N$ is a $2\times2$ complex matrix with $N^{2}=0$, regarded as an operator on $\CC^{2}$, and "similar" means conjugate by an invertible matrix, equivalently: the matrix of the same operator in another ordered basis.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #zero-case}
If $N=0$ there is nothing to prove, so assume $N\neq0$.

::: pf-proof
$N=0$ is one of the two alternatives of the statement.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #vector}
There is $v\in\CC^{2}$ with $Nv\neq0$, and for $w=Nv$ we have $w\neq0$ and $Nw=0$.

::: pf-proof
$N\neq0$ means some entry of $N$ is nonzero, so $N$ is not the zero map and some $v$ has $Nv\neq0$. Put $w=Nv$; then $w\neq0$ by the choice of $v$, and $Nw=N(Nv)=N^{2}v=0$ because $N^{2}=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #independent}
$v$ and $w$ are linearly independent.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $v=\lambda w$ for some $\lambda\in\CC$. Then $Nv=\lambda Nw=\lambda\cdot0=0$, contradicting $Nv=w\neq0$. A two-element set is dependent exactly when one member is a multiple of the other, so $\{v,w\}$ is independent; as $\CC^{2}$ has dimension $2$, $(v,w)$ is an ordered basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix}
In the ordered basis $(v,w)$ the matrix of $N$ is $\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
The $j$-th column of the matrix of $N$ in the basis $(v,w)$ is the coordinate column of the image of the $j$-th basis vector. Here $Nv=w$, whose coordinates in $(v,w)$ are $(0,1)$, and $Nw=0$, whose coordinates are $(0,0)$. So the columns are $\binom{0}{1}$ and $\binom{0}{0}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
Either $N=0$ or $N$ is similar over $\CC$ to $\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
If $N\neq0$, steps [](#vector){.pf-ref}, [](#independent){.pf-ref} and [](#matrix){.pf-ref} exhibit a basis in which the matrix of $N$ is $\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$; the change-of-basis matrix is invertible, which is the asserted similarity.

:::
