::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ and $W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces over the field $F$ and let $T$ be a linear transformation from $V$ into $W$.
If
$$
\mathcal{B} = \{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n\} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{B}' = \{\beta_1, \ldots, \beta_m\}
$$
are ordered bases for $V$ and $W$, respectively, define the linear transformations $E^{p,q}$ as in the proof of Theorem 5: $E^{p,q}(\alpha_i) = \delta_{iq}\beta_p$.
Then the $E^{p,q}$, $1 \leq p \leq m$, $1 \leq q \leq n$, form a basis for $L(V, W)$, and so
$$
T = \sum_{p=1}^{m} \sum_{q=1}^{n} A_{pq} E^{p,q}
$$
for certain scalars $A_{pq}$ (the coordinates of $T$ in this basis for $L(V, W)$). Show that the matrix $A$ with entries $A(p, q) = A_{pq}$ is precisely the matrix of $T$ relative to the pair $\mathfrak{B}, \mathfrak{B}'$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.4 Exercise 13. Here $V$ has ordered basis $\mathcal{B}=(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$, $W$ has ordered basis $\mathcal{B}'=(\beta_1,\dots,\beta_m)$, and $E^{p,q}\in L(V,W)$ is defined by $E^{p,q}(\alpha_i)=\delta_{iq}\beta_p$, where $\delta_{iq}=1$ if $i=q$ and $0$ otherwise.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #expand}

$T\alpha_q=\sum_{p=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{pj}E^{p,j}(\alpha_q)$ for each $q$.

::: pf-proof

Substitute $T=\sum_{p}\sum_{j}A_{pj}E^{p,j}$ and apply both sides to $\alpha_q$; evaluation at a vector is linear.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #delta}

$E^{p,q}(\alpha_q)=\beta_p$ and $E^{p,j}(\alpha_q)=0$ for $j\neq q$.

::: pf-proof

Both are the definition of $E^{p,j}$ evaluated at $\alpha_i$ with $i=q$: the value is $\delta_{qj}\beta_p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #column}

$T\alpha_q=\sum_{p=1}^{m}A_{pq}\beta_p$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#delta){.pf-ref} every term of step [](#expand){.pf-ref} with $j\neq q$ vanishes, leaving $\sum_{p}A_{pq}\beta_p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #colcoord}

The $q$-th column of the matrix of $T$ relative to $\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}'$ is $(A_{1q},\dots,A_{mq})^t$.

::: pf-proof

The matrix of $T$ relative to the pair is the matrix whose $q$-th column is the coordinate column of $T\alpha_q$ in $\mathcal{B}'$, and step [](#column){.pf-ref} exhibits $T\alpha_q$ as the combination of the basis vectors $\beta_p$ with coefficients $A_{pq}$; coordinates in a basis are unique.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

The matrix $A=(A_{pq})$ is the matrix of $T$ relative to $\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}'$.

::: pf-proof

Two $m\times n$ matrices with the same columns agree, and step [](#colcoord){.pf-ref} identifies the $q$-th column of each as $(A_{1q},\dots,A_{mq})^t$; so the coordinates $A_{pq}$ of $T$ in the basis $\theset{E^{p,q}}$ are exactly the entries of that matrix.

:::
