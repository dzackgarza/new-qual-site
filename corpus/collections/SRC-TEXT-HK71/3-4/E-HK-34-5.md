::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^3$, the matrix of which in the standard ordered basis is
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Find a basis for the range of $T$ and a basis for the null space of $T$.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise and no text version of Hoffman and Kunze in the checkout; the reduction below is the card's own.

Throughout, $A$ is the matrix of $T$ in the standard ordered basis of $\RR^3$, so that the range of $T$ is the column space of $A$ and the null space of $T$ is the solution space of $AX=0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #reduction}
$\rref(A)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-1\\0&1&1\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
With rows $R_1=(1,2,1)$, $R_2=(0,1,1)$, $R_3=(-1,3,4)$, the replacement $R_3\mapsto R_3+R_1$ gives $(0,5,5)=5R_2$, so $R_3$ becomes $0$; then $R_1\mapsto R_1-2R_2=(1,0,-1)$. Both operations are elementary row operations of the third kind, which preserve the solution space (and the column dependencies).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #range}
$\{(1,0,-1),(2,1,3)\}$ is a basis of the range of $T$.

::: pf-proof
The pivot columns of the reduced matrix are the first and second, and row operations do not change which columns of the original matrix are independent: the reduced columns $\binom{1}{0},\binom{0}{1},\binom{-1}{1}$ show that the first two are independent and the third is minus the first plus the second. Hence the first two columns of $A$, namely $(1,0,-1)$ and $(2,1,3)$, are independent and span the column space, which is the range of $T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #null}
$\{(1,-1,1)\}$ is a basis of the null space of $T$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#reduction){.pf-ref} the solutions of $AX=0$ are the solutions of the reduced system $x_1-x_3=0$, $x_2+x_3=0$, that is $x_1=x_3$ and $x_2=-x_3$ with $x_3$ free. So the solution set is $\{t(1,-1,1):t\in\RR\}$, the span of the nonzero vector $(1,-1,1)$, which is therefore a basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #count}
The two dimensions agree with rank-nullity.

::: pf-proof
$\dim\im T=2$ by step [](#range){.pf-ref} and $\dim\ker T=1$ by step [](#null){.pf-ref}, and $2+1=3=\dim\RR^3$, as rank-nullity requires; this is a check on both bases, and it confirms that $A$ has rank $2$.

:::
