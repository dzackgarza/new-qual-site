::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\CC^2$ defined by $T(x_1, x_2) = (x_1, 0)$.
Let $\mathfrak{B}$ be the standard ordered basis for $\CC^2$ and let $\mathfrak{B}' = \{\alpha_1, \alpha_2\}$ be the ordered basis defined by $\alpha_1 = (1, i)$, $\alpha_2 = (-i, 2)$.

(a) What is the matrix of $T$ relative to the pair $\mathfrak{B}$, $\mathfrak{B}'$?

(b) What is the matrix of $T$ relative to the pair $\mathfrak{B}'$, $\mathfrak{B}$?

(c) What is the matrix of $T$ in the ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}'$?

(d) What is the matrix of $T$ in the ordered basis $\{\alpha_2, \alpha_1\}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §3.4 Exercise 1, p. 95. All four matrices were recomputed exactly over $\QQ(i)$, both from the change-of-basis formula and, for (a) and (b), column by column from the images of the basis vectors.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis}
$P=\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\ i&2\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $1$, so it is invertible and its columns $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ form a basis of $\CC^2$.

::: pf-proof
$\det P=1\cdot2-(-i)\cdot i=2-1=1$, since $(-i)i=-i^2=1$; a square matrix over a field with nonzero determinant is invertible, so its columns are a basis.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #a}
(a) $[T]_{\mathfrak B,\mathfrak B'}=P^{-1}A=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\-i&0\end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
With domain basis the standard basis and codomain basis $\mathfrak B'$, the $j$-th column is the coordinate vector of $T e_j$ in $\mathfrak B'$. Here $Te_1=(1,0)=2\alpha_1-i\alpha_2$ and $Te_2=(0,0)$, which is the matrix displayed; the same matrix is $P^{-1}AI$ because the standard basis has matrix $I$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
(b) $[T]_{\mathfrak B',\mathfrak B}=AP=\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
With domain basis $\mathfrak B'$ and codomain the standard basis, the columns are the coordinate vectors of $T\alpha_1=(1,0)$ and $T\alpha_2=(-i,0)$ in the standard basis, namely $(1,0)^t$ and $(-i,0)^t$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #c}
(c) $[T]_{\mathfrak B'}=P^{-1}AP=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2i\\-i&-1\end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
In one basis on both sides the matrix is $P^{-1}AP$; its columns are the coordinates in $\mathfrak B'$ of $T\alpha_1=(1,0)=(2,-i)$ and $T\alpha_2=(-i,0)=(-2i,-1)$, using the coordinates of $(1,0)$ computed in part (a).
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #d}
(d) In the ordered basis $(\alpha_2,\alpha_1)$ the matrix of $T$ is $\begin{pmatrix}-1&-i\\-2i&2\end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
The change from $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$ to $(\alpha_2,\alpha_1)$ is the permutation with matrix $S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}=S^{-1}$, so the new matrix is $S^{-1}(P^{-1}AP)S$, which exchanges the two rows and the two columns of the matrix of part (c).
:::
:::
