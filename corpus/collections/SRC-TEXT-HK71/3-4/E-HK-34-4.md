::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a two-dimensional vector space over the field $F$, and let $\mathfrak{B}$ be an ordered basis for $V$.
If $T$ is a linear operator on $V$ and
$$
[T]_{\mathfrak{B}} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}
$$
prove that $T^2 - (a + d)T + (ad - bc)I = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Write $\mathfrak B=(v_1,v_2)$ and
$$
A=[T]_{\mathfrak B}=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{bmatrix},
$$
so that $Tv_1=av_1+cv_2$ and $Tv_2=bv_1+dv_2$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #matrix}
$A^2-(a+d)A+(ad-bc)I_2=0$, entry by entry.

::: pf-proof
Multiply out with $A^2=\begin{bmatrix}a^2+bc&ab+bd\\ ac+cd&bc+d^2\end{bmatrix}$. The four entries of $A^2-(a+d)A+(ad-bc)I_2$ are
$$
(1,1):\ a^2+bc-(a+d)a+(ad-bc)=a^2+bc-a^2-ad+ad-bc=0,
$$
$$
(2,2):\ bc+d^2-(a+d)d+(ad-bc)=bc+d^2-ad-d^2+ad-bc=0,
$$
where the constant $ad-bc$ is what the identity matrix contributes and is what cancels the surviving terms;
$$
(1,2):\ ab+bd-(a+d)b+0=ab+bd-ab-db=0,\qquad (2,1):\ ac+cd-(a+d)c+0=0 .
$$
So all four entries vanish.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #represent}
For any two linear operators $S,U$ on $V$ and scalars $\lambda,\mu$,
$$
[\lambda S+\mu U]_{\mathfrak B}=\lambda[S]_{\mathfrak B}+\mu[U]_{\mathfrak B},\qquad [SU]_{\mathfrak B}=[S]_{\mathfrak B}[U]_{\mathfrak B},\qquad [I]_{\mathfrak B}=I_2,
$$
and $[R]_{\mathfrak B}=0$ implies $R=0$.

::: pf-proof
The first and third identities hold because the $j$-th column of $[S]_{\mathfrak B}$ is the coordinate vector of $Sv_j$, coordinates being taken in $\mathfrak B$: coordinates of a sum add, and coordinates of a scalar multiple scale. For the second, $[SU]_{\mathfrak B}$ has $j$-th column equal to the coordinate vector of $S(Uv_j)=S\bigl(\sum_k[U]_{kj}v_k\bigr)=\sum_k[U]_{kj}(Sv_k)$, whose coordinates are $\sum_k[S]_{ik}[U]_{kj}$, the $(i,j)$ entry of the matrix product. For injectivity: if $[R]_{\mathfrak B}=0$ then every $Rv_j=0$, and since the $v_j$ span $V$, $R=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #apply}
$\bigl[T^2-(a+d)T+(ad-bc)I\bigr]_{\mathfrak B}=A^2-(a+d)A+(ad-bc)I_2$.

::: pf-proof
Apply step [](#represent){.pf-ref} to the composite $T^2=TT$ and to the scalar multiples in turn: the representation of a polynomial expression in $T$ is the same expression evaluated at $A$, because the representation is additive, respects multiplication and fixes the identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$T^2-(a+d)T+(ad-bc)I=0$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#apply){.pf-ref} the operator in question is represented by $A^2-(a+d)A+(ad-bc)I_2$, which is the zero matrix by step [](#matrix){.pf-ref}; by injectivity in step [](#represent){.pf-ref} the operator itself is zero. This is the identity asserted in the exercise, for every linear operator on a two-dimensional space with matrix of the displayed shape.
:::
