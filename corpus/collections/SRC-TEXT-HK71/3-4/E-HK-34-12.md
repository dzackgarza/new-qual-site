::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space over the field $F$, and let $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n\}$ be an ordered basis for $V$.

(a) According to Theorem 1, there is a unique linear operator $T$ on $V$ such that
\[
T\alpha_j = \alpha_{j+1}, \quad j = 1, \ldots, n-1, \quad T\alpha_n = 0.
\]
What is the matrix $A$ of $T$ in the ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$?

(b) Prove that $T^n = 0$ but $T^{n-1} \neq 0$.

(c) Let $S$ be any linear operator on $V$ such that $S^n = 0$ but $S^{n-1} \neq 0$.
Prove that there is an ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}'$ for $V$ such that the matrix of $S$ in the ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}'$ is the matrix $A$ of part (a).

(d) Prove that if $M$ and $N$ are $n \times n$ matrices over $F$ such that $M^n = N^n = 0$ but $M^{n-1} \neq 0 \neq N^{n-1}$, then $M$ and $N$ are similar.
:::

::: {.solution}
For part (a), the $j$th column of the matrix is the coordinate vector of $T\alpha_j$. Hence
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}
0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&\ddots&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
It has ones immediately below the main diagonal.

For part (b), $T^k\alpha_j=\alpha_{j+k}$ when $j+k\le n$ and is zero otherwise. Hence $T^n=0$, while
\[
T^{n-1}\alpha_1=\alpha_n\ne0.
\]

For part (c), choose $v\in V$ with $S^{n-1}v\ne0$. We claim
\[
\mathfrak B'=\{v,Sv,\ldots,S^{n-1}v\}
\]
is a basis. If
\[
c_1v+c_2Sv+\cdots+c_nS^{n-1}v=0,
\]
apply $S^{n-1}$ to obtain $c_1S^{n-1}v=0$, so $c_1=0$. Applying successively $S^{n-2},S^{n-3},\ldots$ shows every $c_j=0$. Thus these $n$ vectors are independent and form a basis. In this basis $S$ sends each basis vector to the next and the last to zero, so its matrix is $A$.

For part (d), the operators defined by $M$ and $N$ both admit a basis in which their matrices are $A$. Therefore $M$ and $N$ are each similar to $A$, hence similar to each other.
:::
