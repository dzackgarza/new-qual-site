::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^3$ defined by
$$
T(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (3x_1 + x_3,\; -2x_1 + x_2,\; -x_1 + 2x_2 + 4x_3).
$$

(a) What is the matrix of $T$ in the standard ordered basis for $\RR^3$?

(b) What is the matrix of $T$ in the ordered basis
$$
\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}
$$
where $\alpha_1 = (1, 0, 1)$, $\alpha_2 = (-1, 2, 1)$, and $\alpha_3 = (2, 1, 1)$?

(c) Prove that $T$ is invertible and give a rule for $T^{-1}$ like the one which defines $T$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.4 Exercise 7. For a linear operator the matrix in an ordered basis has as its $j$-th column the coordinate column of the image of the $j$-th basis vector, and a change of basis matrix $P$ whose columns are the new basis vectors satisfies $[T]_{\mathfrak{B}}=P^{-1}[T]P$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #std}

(a) The matrix of $T$ in the standard basis is $A=\begin{bmatrix}3&0&1\\-2&1&0\\-1&2&4\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

Evaluate $T$ on the standard basis: $T(1,0,0)=(3,-2,-1)$, $T(0,1,0)=(0,1,2)$, $T(0,0,1)=(1,0,4)$, and these are the three columns.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dets}

$\det A=9$ and $\det P=-4$ for $P=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&2\\0&2&1\\1&1&1\end{bmatrix}$, so both are invertible.

::: pf-proof

The determinant of $A$ is $3(1\cdot4-0\cdot2)-0+1((-2)\cdot2-1\cdot(-1))=12-3=9$, and that of $P$ is $1(2\cdot1-1\cdot1)+1(0\cdot1-1\cdot1)+2(0\cdot1-2\cdot1)=1-1-4=-4$, both nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #change}

(b) The matrix of $T$ in $\mathfrak{B}$ is $P^{-1}AP=M\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}\frac{17}{4}&\frac{35}{4}&\frac{11}{2}\\-\frac34&\frac{15}{4}&-\frac32\\-\frac12&-\frac72&0\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

The columns of $P$ are $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$, so $P$ is the change-of-basis matrix from $\mathfrak{B}$ to the standard basis and $[T]_{\mathfrak{B}}=P^{-1}AP$. Direct multiplication gives $AP=PM$ entry by entry, and $P$ is invertible by step [](#dets){.pf-ref}, so $M=P^{-1}AP$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inverse}

(c) $T$ is invertible, with $A^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac49&\frac29&-\frac19\\\frac89&\frac{13}{9}&-\frac29\\-\frac13&-\frac23&\frac13\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

$\det A=9\neq0$ by step [](#dets){.pf-ref}, so $A$, the matrix of $T$ in a basis, is invertible and hence so is $T$. Multiplying the displayed matrix by $A$ on either side gives $I_3$, which identifies it as $A^{-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rule}

(c) $T^{-1}(y_1,y_2,y_3)=\left(\frac{4y_1+2y_2-y_3}{9},\frac{8y_1+13y_2-2y_3}{9},\frac{-y_1-2y_2+y_3}{3}\right)$.

::: pf-proof

The inverse operator acts by $y\mapsto A^{-1}y$ in the standard coordinates; multiplying $A^{-1}$ by the column $(y_1,y_2,y_3)^t$ gives the three displayed entries.

:::
