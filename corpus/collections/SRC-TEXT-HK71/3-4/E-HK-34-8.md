::: {.exercise}
Let $\theta$ be a real number.
Prove that the following two matrices are similar over the field of complex numbers:
$$
\begin{bmatrix} \cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{bmatrix} e^{i\theta} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{-i\theta} \end{bmatrix}
$$
(Hint: Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\CC^2$ which is represented by the first matrix in the standard ordered basis.
Then find vectors $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ such that $T\alpha_1 = e^{i\theta}\alpha_1$, $T\alpha_2 = e^{-i\theta}\alpha_2$, and $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2\}$ is a basis.)
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §3.4 Exercise 8, and follows the printed hint.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #setup}
Let $R=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{bmatrix}$ and let $T$ be the operator on $\CC^2$ represented by $R$ in the standard basis.

::: pf-proof
This is a definition; the entries of $R$ are real, hence complex numbers, so $R$ defines a $\CC$-linear operator.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #vectors}
The vectors $\alpha_1=\binom{1}{-i}$ and $\alpha_2=\binom{1}{i}$ are linearly independent over $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
The matrix $P$ with these as columns has determinant $1\cdot i-1\cdot(-i)=2i\ne0$, so its columns form a basis of $\CC^2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eigen}
$T\alpha_1=e^{i\theta}\alpha_1$ and $T\alpha_2=e^{-i\theta}\alpha_2$.

::: pf-proof
Compute $R\alpha_1=\binom{\cos\theta+i\sin\theta}{\sin\theta-i\cos\theta}=\binom{e^{i\theta}}{-ie^{i\theta}}=e^{i\theta}\alpha_1$, using $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ and $-ie^{i\theta}=\sin\theta-i\cos\theta$. Similarly $R\alpha_2=\binom{\cos\theta-i\sin\theta}{\sin\theta+i\cos\theta}=\binom{e^{-i\theta}}{ie^{-i\theta}}=e^{-i\theta}\alpha_2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #similar}
$P^{-1}RP=\begin{bmatrix}e^{i\theta}&0\\0&e^{-i\theta}\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
$P$ is invertible by step [](#vectors){.pf-ref}, and its columns are the eigenvectors of steps [](#eigen){.pf-ref} with eigenvalues $e^{i\theta}$ and $e^{-i\theta}$. The matrix of $T$ in the basis $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$ is therefore diagonal with those entries, and that matrix is $P^{-1}RP$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The two displayed matrices are similar over $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#similar){.pf-ref} exhibits an invertible $P$ over $\CC$ conjugating the rotation matrix to the diagonal matrix with entries $e^{i\theta}$ and $e^{-i\theta}$:
$$
\boxed{\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{bmatrix}\sim_{\CC}\begin{bmatrix}e^{i\theta}&0\\0&e^{-i\theta}\end{bmatrix}.}
$$
The same $P$ works for every $\theta$, including $\theta\in\pi\ZZ$, where the two eigenvalues coincide and $P$ is still invertible.
:::
