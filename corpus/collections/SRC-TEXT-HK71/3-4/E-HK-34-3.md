::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $F^n$, let $A$ be the matrix of $T$ in the standard ordered basis for $F^n$, and let $W$ be the subspace of $F^n$ spanned by the column vectors of $A$.
What does $W$ have to do with $T$?
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.4 Exercise 3.

Let $e_{1},\dots,e_{n}$ be the standard ordered basis of $F^{n}$, let $T$ be a linear operator on $F^{n}$, let $A$ be its matrix in that basis, and let $W$ be the span of the columns of $A$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #columns}
The $j$-th column of $A$ is the vector $T(e_{j})$.

::: pf-proof
By the definition of the matrix of $T$ in the standard basis, the $j$-th column of $A$ is the coordinate matrix of $T(e_{j})$ with respect to the standard basis. The coordinate matrix of a vector $v$ in the standard basis is $v$ itself, since $v=\sum_{i}v_{i}e_{i}$ displays the coordinates directly. Hence the $j$-th column is $T(e_{j})$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inclusion-one}
$W\subset\operatorname{range}T$.

::: pf-proof
$W$ is the set of all combinations $\sum_{j}c_{j}T(e_{j})$ by step [](#columns){.pf-ref}. Such a combination equals $T\bigl(\sum_{j}c_{j}e_{j}\bigr)$ by linearity, hence lies in the range of $T$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inclusion-two}
$\operatorname{range}T\subset W$.

::: pf-proof
Any $x\in F^{n}$ is $\sum_{j}x_{j}e_{j}$ for its coordinates $x_{j}$, so $T(x)=\sum_{j}x_{j}T(e_{j})$ by linearity, a combination of the columns and therefore an element of $W$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$W=\operatorname{range}T$: the column space of the standard matrix is the range of the operator.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#inclusion-one){.pf-ref} and [](#inclusion-two){.pf-ref} give the two inclusions.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The column rank of $A$ equals the rank of $T$.

::: pf-proof
The column rank is by definition $\dim W$, and the rank of $T$ is $\dim\operatorname{range}T$; by step [](#columns){.pf-ref} the spaces are equal, so their dimensions are.
:::
