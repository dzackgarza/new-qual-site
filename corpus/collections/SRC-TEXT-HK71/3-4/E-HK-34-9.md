::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over the field $F$ and let $S$ and $T$ be linear operators on $V$.
We ask: When do there exist ordered bases $\mathfrak{B}$ and $\mathfrak{B}'$ for $V$ such that $[S]_{\mathfrak{B}} = [T]_{\mathfrak{B}'}$?
Prove that such bases exist if and only if there is an invertible linear operator $U$ on $V$ such that $T = USU^{-1}$.
(Outline of proof: If $[S]_{\mathfrak{B}} = [T]_{\mathfrak{B}'}$, let $U$ be the operator which carries $\mathfrak{B}$ onto $\mathfrak{B}'$ and show that $T = USU^{-1}$.
Conversely, if $T = USU^{-1}$ for some invertible $U$, let $\mathfrak{B}$ be any ordered basis for $V$ and let $\mathfrak{B}'$ be its image under $U$.
Then show that $[S]_{\mathfrak{B}} = [T]_{\mathfrak{B}'}$.)
:::

::: {.remark}
The printed outline has $S=UTU^{-1}$ in its first direction while defining
$U$ to carry $\mathfrak B$ onto $\mathfrak B'$. For that choice of $U$, the
correct identity is $T=USU^{-1}$, as in the theorem statement.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Equal matrices in suitable bases imply conjugacy.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\mathfrak B=(v_1,\ldots,v_n),
\qquad
\mathfrak B'=(w_1,\ldots,w_n),
$$
and suppose
$$
[S]_{\mathfrak B}=[T]_{\mathfrak B'}=(a_{ij}).
$$
Define $U:V\to V$ by $U(v_j)=w_j$ for every $j$ and extend linearly. Since
$U$ sends a basis to a basis, it is invertible. By the definition of the
matrix of a linear operator,
$$
S(v_j)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ij}v_i,
\qquad
T(w_j)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ij}w_i
$$
for every $j$ [@HK71]. Hence
$$
US(v_j)=\sum_i a_{ij}U(v_i)
=\sum_i a_{ij}w_i
=T(w_j)=TU(v_j).
$$
Thus $US=TU$ on a basis, hence on all of $V$. Multiplying on the right by
$U^{-1}$ gives
$$
T=USU^{-1}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Conjugacy produces bases with the same matrix.
::: {.proof}
Conversely, suppose $U$ is invertible and $T=USU^{-1}$. Choose any ordered
basis
$$
\mathfrak B=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)
$$
and put
$$
\mathfrak B'=(Uv_1,\ldots,Uv_n).
$$
This is an ordered basis because $U$ is invertible. Write
$$
S(v_j)=\sum_i a_{ij}v_i.
$$
Then
$$
T(Uv_j)=USU^{-1}(Uv_j)=US(v_j)
=\sum_i a_{ij}Uv_i.
$$
Therefore the $j$th column of $[T]_{\mathfrak B'}$ is the same as the $j$th
column of $[S]_{\mathfrak B}$ for every $j$. Hence
$$
[T]_{\mathfrak B'}=[S]_{\mathfrak B}.
$$
This proves the converse and completes the equivalence.
:::
