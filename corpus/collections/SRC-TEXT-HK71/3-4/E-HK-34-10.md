::: {.exercise title="Hoffman and Kunze 3.4.10"}
We have seen that the linear operator $T$ on $\RR^2$ defined by $T(x_1, x_2) = (x_1, 0)$ is represented in the standard ordered basis by the matrix
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
This operator satisfies $T^2 = T$.
Prove that if $S$ is a linear operator on $\RR^2$ such that $S^2 = S$, then $S = 0$, or $S = I$, or there is an ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$ for $\RR^2$ such that $[S]_{\mathfrak{B}} = A$ (above).
:::
