::: {.exercise}
We have seen that the linear operator $T$ on $\RR^2$ defined by $T(x_1, x_2) = (x_1, 0)$ is represented in the standard ordered basis by the matrix
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
This operator satisfies $T^2 = T$.
Prove that if $S$ is a linear operator on $\RR^2$ such that $S^2 = S$, then $S = 0$, or $S = I$, or there is an ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$ for $\RR^2$ such that $[S]_{\mathfrak{B}} = A$ (above).
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume $S^2=S$. For every $v\in\mathbb R^2$,
\[
v=Sv+(v-Sv),
\]
where $Sv\in\operatorname{range}S$ and
\[
S(v-Sv)=Sv-S^2v=0,
\]
so $v-Sv\in\ker S$. Also, if $w\in\operatorname{range}S\cap\ker S$, write $w=Su$; then
\[
w=Su=S^2u=S(w)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb R^2=\operatorname{range}S\oplus\ker S.
\]
If the range is $0$, then $S=0$. If the kernel is $0$, then the direct-sum decomposition forces the range to be all of $\mathbb R^2$, and $S^2=S$ with invertible $S$ gives $S=I$.

Otherwise both range and kernel are one-dimensional. Choose nonzero $u\in\operatorname{range}S$ and nonzero $v\in\ker S$. Then $Su=u$ and $Sv=0$, and $\{u,v\}$ is a basis. In this basis
\[
[S]=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
:::
