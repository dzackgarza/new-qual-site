::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a vector space and $T$ a linear transformation from $V$ into $V$.
Prove that the following two statements about $T$ are equivalent.

(a) The intersection of the range of $T$ and the null space of $T$ is the zero subspace of $V$.

(b) If $T(T\alpha) = 0$, then $T\alpha = 0$.
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.1 Exercise 13. Here $T:V\to V$ is linear, $\operatorname{range}T=\theset{T\alpha\mid \alpha\in V}$ and $\ker T=\theset{\alpha\mid T\alpha=0}$; both contain $0$ and are subspaces.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ab}

(a) implies (b).

::: pf-proof

Assume (a), i.e. $\operatorname{range}T\cap\ker T=\theset{0}$, and let $T(T\alpha)=0$. Then $T\alpha\in\ker T$ by definition of the kernel, and $T\alpha\in\operatorname{range}T$ because it is the image of $\alpha$; so $T\alpha\in\operatorname{range}T\cap\ker T=\theset{0}$ and $T\alpha=0$, which is (b).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ba}

(b) implies (a).

::: pf-proof

Assume (b) and let $v\in\operatorname{range}T\cap\ker T$. Since $v\in\operatorname{range}T$ there is $\alpha$ with $v=T\alpha$; then $T(T\alpha)=Tv=0$ because $v\in\ker T$. By (b), $T\alpha=0$, that is, $v=0$. Hence $\operatorname{range}T\cap\ker T=\theset{0}$, since $0$ lies in both subspaces, which is (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

(a) and (b) are equivalent.

::: pf-proof
