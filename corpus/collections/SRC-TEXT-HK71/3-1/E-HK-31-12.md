::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space over the field $F$ and let $T$ be a linear transformation from $V$ into $V$ such that the range and null space of $T$ are identical.
Prove that $n$ is even.
(Can you give an example of such a linear transformation $T$?)
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: rank-nullity applied to the hypothesis $\operatorname{range}T=\ker T$, then a concrete map on $F^{2}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #equal}

$\operatorname{rank}T=\operatorname{nullity}T$.

::: pf-proof

By hypothesis $\operatorname{range}T$ and $\ker T$ are the same subspace $R$ of $V$. The rank of $T$ is by definition $\dim\operatorname{range}T$ and the nullity is by definition $\dim\ker T$, so both equal the single number $\dim R$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ranknullity}

$n=\operatorname{rank}T+\operatorname{nullity}T=2\dim R$.

::: pf-proof

For a linear transformation from an $n$-dimensional space into itself, rank-nullity gives $n=\operatorname{rank}T+\operatorname{nullity}T$: the restriction of $T$ to a complement of $\ker T$ is an isomorphism onto $\operatorname{range}T$, so the dimensions of the two subspaces add to $n$. Substituting step [](#equal){.pf-ref},
$$
n=\operatorname{rank}T+\operatorname{nullity}T=\dim R+\dim R=2\dim R.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #even}

$n$ is even.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#ranknullity){.pf-ref}, $n=2\dim R$ with $\dim R$ an integer (the dimension of a subspace of the finite-dimensional space $V$ is an integer), so
$$
\boxed{n=2\dim R\ \text{is even.}}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #example}

On $F^{2}$, $T(x,y)=(y,0)$ is linear and satisfies $\operatorname{range}T=\ker T$.

::: pf-proof

$T$ is linear: $T((x,y)+(x',y'))=(y+y',0)=T(x,y)+T(x',y')$ and $T(c(x,y))=(cy,0)=cT(x,y)$ for $c\in F$. Its range is $\{(y,0):y\in F\}=F(1,0)$, and $(y,0)=(0,0)$ exactly when $y=0$, so its kernel is $\{(x,0):x\in F\}=F(1,0)$ as well. Thus range and null space coincide, and indeed $2=\dim F^{2}$ is even.

:::
