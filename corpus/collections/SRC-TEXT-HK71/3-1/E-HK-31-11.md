::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the space of $n \times 1$ matrices over $F$ and let $W$ be the space of $m \times 1$ matrices over $F$.
Let $A$ be a fixed $m \times n$ matrix over $F$ and let $T$ be the linear transformation from $V$ into $W$ defined by $T(X) = AX$.
Prove that $T$ is the zero transformation if and only if $A$ is the zero matrix.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise and no text version of Hoffman and Kunze in the checkout; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $F$ is a field, $V=F^{n\times1}$, $W=F^{m\times1}$, $A\in F^{m\times n}$, and $T(X)=AX$; the zero transformation sends every vector of $V$ to the zero vector of $W$, and $0$ denotes a zero matrix of whatever size the context fixes.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #forward}
If $A=0$ then $T$ is the zero transformation.

::: pf-proof
For every $X\in V$ the product $AX$ has entries $\sum_ka_{ik}x_{kj}$ with every $a_{ik}=0$, hence all entries $0$; so $T(X)=0$ for every $X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #columns}
$Ae_j$ is the $j$-th column of $A$, and the $e_j$ span $V$.

::: pf-proof
The $j$-th standard basis column $e_j$ has a single nonzero entry, $1$ in the $j$-th place, so $(Ae_j)_i=\sum_ka_{ik}(e_j)_{k1}=a_{ij}$: the $i$-th entry of the $j$-th column of $A$. Every $X\in V$ is $\sum_jx_{j1}e_j$, so the $e_j$ span $V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #converse}
If $T$ is the zero transformation then $A=0$.

::: pf-proof
For each $j$ the hypothesis gives $Ae_j=T(e_j)=0$, and by step [](#columns){.pf-ref} $Ae_j$ is the $j$-th column of $A$; so every column of $A$ vanishes and $A=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
$T$ is the zero transformation if and only if $A$ is the zero matrix.

::: pf-proof
The two implications are steps [](#forward){.pf-ref} and [](#converse){.pf-ref}.

:::
