::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the set of all complex numbers regarded as a vector space over the field of real numbers (usual operations).
Find a function from $V$ into $V$ which is a linear transformation on the above vector space, but which is not a linear transformation on $\CC^1$, i.e., which is not complex linear.
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise, and the section's textbook is not in the checkout (`SRC-TEXT-HK71`'s provenance is the archive.org record and the publisher's table of contents; `tmp/sources` holds no Hoffman--Kunze text). The argument is the card's own; the two properties below were checked directly from the definitions.

Take complex conjugation,
$$
T(z)=\overline z .
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #real-linear}
$T$ is linear over $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
For real $a,b$ and complex $z,w$,
$$
T(az+bw)=\overline{az+bw}=a\overline z+b\overline w=aT(z)+bT(w),
$$
the middle equality because conjugation is additive and fixes real scalars.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not-complex-linear}
$T$ is not linear over $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
$T(i\cdot1)=T(i)=-i$, whereas complex linearity would require $T(i\cdot1)=i\,T(1)=i$. Since $-i\ne i$, the scalar $i$ is not carried through, so $T$ is not a linear transformation of $\CC^1$. Equivalently, a map of one-dimensional complex spaces is complex linear precisely when it is multiplication by a complex number, and conjugation is not.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$T(z)=\overline z$ is a function $V\to V$ that is linear on $V$ as a real vector space but not on $\CC^1$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#real-linear){.pf-ref} and [](#not-complex-linear){.pf-ref} give the two properties, and $T$ maps $V$ into $V$ because conjugation of a complex number is a complex number. This answers the exercise; the same phenomenon shows that a complex-linear map is a special case of a real-linear one, the real dimension being twice the complex dimension.
:::
