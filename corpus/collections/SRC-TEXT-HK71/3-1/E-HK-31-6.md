::: {.exercise}
Describe explicitly (as in Exercises 1 and 2) the linear transformation $T$ from $F^2$ into $F^2$ such that $T\epsilon_1 = (a, b)$, $T\epsilon_2 = (c, d)$.
:::


::: solution
Every $(x,y)\in F^2$ has the basis expansion
\[
(x,y)=x\epsilon_1+y\epsilon_2.
\]
By linearity,
\[
T(x,y)=xT\epsilon_1+yT\epsilon_2
=x(a,b)+y(c,d).
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{T(x,y)=(ax+cy,\;bx+dy)}.
\]
Equivalently, relative to the standard bases,
\[
[T]=\begin{bmatrix}a&c\\b&d\end{bmatrix}.
\]
:::
