::: {.exercise}
Describe explicitly (as in Exercises 1 and 2) the linear transformation $T$ from $F^2$ into $F^2$ such that $T\epsilon_1 = (a, b)$, $T\epsilon_2 = (c, d)$.
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: it is the computation of the map from its values on a basis, as in Hoffman and Kunze Exercises 1 and 2 of the same section.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #expansion}

$\{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\}$ is a basis of $F^{2}$ and $(x,y)=x\epsilon_{1}+y\epsilon_{2}$.

::: pf-proof

$\epsilon_{1}=(1,0)$ and $\epsilon_{2}=(0,1)$ are the standard basis vectors of $F^{2}$, which are linearly independent and span $F^{2}$: $x\epsilon_{1}+y\epsilon_{2}=(x,y)$ for every $(x,y)\in F^{2}$, and this combination vanishes only when $x=y=0$. So the coordinates of $(x,y)$ in this basis are exactly $x$ and $y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #linear}

$T(x,y)=xT\epsilon_{1}+yT\epsilon_{2}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#expansion){.pf-ref}, $(x,y)=x\epsilon_{1}+y\epsilon_{2}$, an equality in $F^{2}$ with scalars $x,y\in F$. A linear transformation preserves sums and scalar multiples, so
$$
T(x,y)=T(x\epsilon_{1}+y\epsilon_{2})=x\,T(\epsilon_{1})+y\,T(\epsilon_{2}).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #values}

$xT\epsilon_{1}+yT\epsilon_{2}=(ax+cy,\ bx+dy)$.

::: pf-proof

By hypothesis $T\epsilon_{1}=(a,b)$ and $T\epsilon_{2}=(c,d)$, and scalar multiplication and addition in $F^{2}$ are componentwise, so
$$
x(a,b)+y(c,d)=(ax,bx)+(cy,dy)=(ax+cy,\ bx+dy).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$T(x,y)=(ax+cy,\ bx+dy)$ for every $(x,y)\in F^{2}$, and the matrix of $T$ in the standard basis is $\begin{bmatrix}a&c\\b&d\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

Combining steps [](#linear){.pf-ref} and [](#values){.pf-ref},
$$
\boxed{T(x,y)=(ax+cy,\ bx+dy).}
$$
The matrix of $T$ relative to the standard bases has as its $j$-th column the coordinate tuple of $T\epsilon_{j}$, that is $(a,b)^{t}$ and $(c,d)^{t}$, giving $\begin{bmatrix}a&c\\b&d\end{bmatrix}$. Conversely, this formula defines a linear map, and it takes $\epsilon_{1}$ to $(a,b)$ and $\epsilon_{2}$ to $(c,d)$, so it is the unique map with the required values.

:::
