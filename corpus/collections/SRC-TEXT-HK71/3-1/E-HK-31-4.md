::: {.exercise}
Is there a linear transformation $T$ from $\RR^3$ into $\RR^2$ such that $T(1, -1, 1) = (1, 0)$ and $T(1, 1, 1) = (0, 1)$?
:::


::: solution
Yes. The vectors
\[
v_1=(1,-1,1),
\qquad
v_2=(1,1,1)
\]
are linearly independent. Extend them to a basis $v_1,v_2,v_3$ of $\RR^3$. Define $T$ on this basis by
\[
T(v_1)=(1,0),
\qquad
T(v_2)=(0,1),
\qquad
T(v_3)=(0,0),
\]
and extend linearly. This produces a linear transformation $T:\RR^3\to\RR^2$ with the required values.
:::
