::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear transformation from $\RR^3$ into $\RR^2$, and let $U$ be a linear transformation from $\RR^2$ into $\RR^3$.
Prove that the transformation $UT$ is not invertible.
Generalize the theorem.
:::

::: solution
The composite
\[
UT:\mathbb R^3\longrightarrow\mathbb R^3
\]
factors through $\mathbb R^2$. Hence
\[
\operatorname{rank}(UT)\le \operatorname{rank}(T)\le 2<3.
\]
An invertible operator on $\mathbb R^3$ must have rank $3$, so $UT$ is not invertible.

More generally, let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces with
$\dim W<\dim V$, and let
\[
T:V\to W,\qquad U:W\to V
\]
be linear. Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}(UT)\le \dim W<\dim V,
\]
so $UT$ cannot be invertible on $V$.
:::
