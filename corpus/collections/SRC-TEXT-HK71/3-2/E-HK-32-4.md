::: {.exercise}
For the linear operator $T$ of Exercise 3, prove that
\[
(T^2 - I)(T - 3I) = 0.
\]
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Show that the operator in Exercise 3 satisfies $(T^2-I)(T-3I)=0$.

<1>1. Read $T$ from Exercise 3:
    for $x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in\mathbb R^3$,
    $$T(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(3x_1,\;x_1-x_2,\;2x_1+x_2+x_3).$$
    In matrix form on the standard basis,
    $$M=\begin{pmatrix}3&0&0\\1&-1&0\\2&1&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

<1>2. Compute $T^2$.
    <2>1. A direct product gives
    $$M^2=\begin{pmatrix}
    9&0&0\\2&1&0\\9&0&1
    \end{pmatrix},$$
    so
    $$T^2(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(9x_1,\;2x_1+x_2,\;9x_1+x_3).$$

<1>3. Compute $T-3I$ and $T^2-I$.
    <2>1. Since
    $$M-3I=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\1&-4&0\\2&1&-2\end{pmatrix},\quad
    M^2-I=\begin{pmatrix}8&0&0\\2&0&0\\9&0&0\end{pmatrix},$$
    every column of $M^2-I$ except the first is zero.

<1>4. Multiply $(M^2-I)(M-3I)$.
    <2>1. The second and third columns of $M^2-I$ are zero, so only the first column can contribute.
    <2>2. The first column of $M-3I$ is $(0,1,2)^T$, and row arithmetic gives
    $$\begin{aligned}
    [8,0,0]\cdot(0,1,2)&=0,\\
    [2,0,0]\cdot(0,1,2)&=0,\\
    [9,0,0]\cdot(0,1,2)&=0.
    \end{aligned}$$
    Hence $(M^2-I)(M-3I)=0$, and therefore
    <2>3. $(T^2-I)(T-3I)=0$.
:::
