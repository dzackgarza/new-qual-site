::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with entries in $F$ and let $T$ be the linear transformation from $F^{n \times 1}$ into $F^{m \times 1}$ defined by $T(X) = AX$.
Show that if $m < n$ it may happen that $T$ is onto without being non-singular.
Similarly, show that if $m > n$ we may have $T$ non-singular but not onto.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise, and the section's textbook is not in the checkout (`SRC-TEXT-HK71`'s provenance is the archive.org record and the publisher's table of contents; `tmp/sources` holds no Hoffman--Kunze text). The argument is the card's own, and each of the two examples is checked against the definitions of onto and of non-singular (injective) rather than by appeal to dimension.

Let $A$ be an $m\times n$ matrix over $F$ and $T(X)=AX$ from $F^{n\times1}$ to $F^{m\times1}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #wide}
If $m<n$, the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}I_m&0\end{bmatrix}$ gives a $T$ that is onto but not non-singular.

::: pf-proof
$A$ acts by $A(x_1,\dots,x_n)^t=(x_1,\dots,x_m)^t$, so every vector of $F^{m\times1}$ is the image of a vector with the last $n-m$ coordinates zero: $T$ is onto. It is not non-singular, because $e_{m+1}\ne0$ in $F^{n\times1}$ yet $T(e_{m+1})=0$, and a non-singular (injective) map has kernel $\{0\}$. This is the first assertion of the exercise, that surjectivity does not force injectivity when $m<n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #tall}
If $m>n$, the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}I_n\\0\end{bmatrix}$ gives a $T$ that is non-singular but not onto.

::: pf-proof
$T(x_1,\dots,x_n)^t=(x_1,\dots,x_n,0,\dots,0)^t$, so $T(X)=0$ forces $X=0$: $T$ is non-singular. It is not onto, since a vector whose last $m-n$ coordinates are not all zero is not of that form; for instance $e_m$ is not in the range when $m>n$. This is the second assertion, that injectivity does not force surjectivity when $m>n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #why}
Both phenomena are failures of the dimension count, and they explain why 'non-singular and onto' coincide only for square matrices.

::: pf-proof
For $T(X)=AX$, the rank-nullity relation for the linear map on the $n$-dimensional space $F^{n\times1}$ gives $\operatorname{rank}T+\dim\ker T=n$, and $T$ is injective exactly when $\dim\ker T=0$ while $T$ is onto exactly when $\operatorname{rank}T=m$. So injectivity alone pins the rank at $n$ and surjectivity alone pins it at $m$; both can hold simultaneously only when $m=n$. The two examples above realise each single condition without the other, and the matrix pencils $\begin{bmatrix}I_m&0\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}I_n\\0\end{bmatrix}$ are the standard witnesses.
:::
