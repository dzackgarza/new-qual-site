::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with entries in $F$ and let $T$ be the linear transformation from $F^{n \times 1}$ into $F^{m \times 1}$ defined by $T(X) = AX$.
Show that if $m < n$ it may happen that $T$ is onto without being non-singular.
Similarly, show that if $m > n$ we may have $T$ non-singular but not onto.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $m<n$, take
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}I_m&0\end{bmatrix},
\]
an $m\times n$ matrix. Then
\[
T(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=(x_1,\ldots,x_m),
\]
so $T$ is onto. But $T$ is not non-singular: for example,
$e_{m+1}\ne0$ and $T(e_{m+1})=0$.

If $m>n$, take
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}I_n\\0\end{bmatrix},
\]
an $m\times n$ matrix. Then
\[
T(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=(x_1,\ldots,x_n,0,\ldots,0),
\]
so $T$ is non-singular. It is not onto, since any vector whose last
$m-n$ coordinates are not all zero is outside its range.
:::
