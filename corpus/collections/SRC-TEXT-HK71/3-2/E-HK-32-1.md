::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ and $U$ be the linear operators on $\RR^2$ defined by
$$
T(x_1, x_2) = (x_2, x_1) \quad \text{and} \quad U(x_1, x_2) = (x_1, 0).
$$

(a) How would you describe $T$ and $U$ geometrically?

(b) Give rules like the ones defining $T$ and $U$ for each of the transformations $(U + T)$, $UT$, $TU$, $T^2$, $U^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §3.2 Exercise 1, p. 83.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #geometry}
$T$ is the reflection in the line $x_1=x_2$ and $U$ is the projection onto the $x_1$-axis along the $x_2$-axis.

::: pf-proof
$T$ interchanges the two coordinates, so it fixes exactly the vectors with $x_1=x_2$ and negates the component along $(1,-1)$: it is the reflection in that line. $U$ keeps the first coordinate and sets the second to $0$, so it is the projection onto $\{(x_1,0)\}$ with kernel $\{(0,x_2)\}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #rules}
The five transformations are given by $(U+T)(x_1,x_2)=(x_1+x_2,x_1)$, $UT(x_1,x_2)=(x_2,0)$, $TU(x_1,x_2)=(0,x_1)$, $T^2(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,x_2)$ and $U^2(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,0)$.

::: pf-proof
Compute from the definitions, where a product of operators is composition with the right factor applied first. $(U+T)(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,0)+(x_2,x_1)=(x_1+x_2,x_1)$. $UT(x_1,x_2)=U(x_2,x_1)=(x_2,0)$. $TU(x_1,x_2)=T(x_1,0)=(0,x_1)$. $T^2(x_1,x_2)=T(x_2,x_1)=(x_1,x_2)$. $U^2(x_1,x_2)=U(x_1,0)=(x_1,0)$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #compare}
$UT$ and $TU$ are different operators, and $U^2=U$, $T^2=I$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#rules){.pf-ref} $UT(1,1)=(1,0)$ while $TU(1,1)=(0,1)$, so the two compositions differ; and the rules for $T^2$ and $U^2$ are those of the identity and of $U$ itself.
:::
:::
