::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on the finite-dimensional space $V$.
Suppose there is a linear operator $U$ on $V$ such that $TU = I$.
Prove that $T$ is invertible and $U = T^{-1}$.
Give an example which shows that this is false when $V$ is not finite-dimensional.
(Hint: Let $T = D$, the differentiation operator on the space of polynomial functions.)
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §3.2 Exercise 9, p. 84, whose hint the example follows.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #onto}
$T$ is surjective.

::: pf-proof
For $\beta\in V$ put $\alpha\coloneqq U\beta\in V$. Then $T\alpha=T(U\beta)=(TU)\beta=I\beta=\beta$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #injective}
$T$ is injective.

::: pf-proof
$V$ is finite-dimensional, so $\dim V=\dim\ker T+\dim\operatorname{im}T$. By step [](#onto){.pf-ref} $\operatorname{im}T=V$, hence $\dim\ker T=0$ and $\ker T=\{0\}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #inverse}
$T$ is invertible and $U=T^{-1}$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#onto){.pf-ref} and [](#injective){.pf-ref} make $T$ an invertible linear operator on $V$. Applying $T^{-1}$ on the left to $TU=I$ gives $T^{-1}TU=T^{-1}$, that is $U=T^{-1}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #example}
The finiteness hypothesis cannot be dropped: on $V=\RR[x]$ let $D$ be differentiation and $S(p)(x)\coloneqq\int_0^x p(t)\,dt$. Then $DS=I$ while $D$ is not invertible.

::: pf-proof
$D$ and $S$ are linear and $S$ maps polynomials to polynomials, so both are operators on $V$. For a monomial, $\frac{d}{dx}\int_0^x t^n\,dt=\frac{d}{dx}\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}=x^n$, so $DS=I$ on a basis of $V$ and hence on $V$. But $D1=0$, so $\ker D\neq\{0\}$ and $D$ is not injective, hence not invertible; and $SD\neq I$ because $SD(x)=\int_0^x t\,dt=\frac{x^2}{2}\neq x$. Thus $TU=I$ holds for $T=D$, $U=S$, and $T$ is not invertible.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
The assertion holds under the stated hypothesis and fails without it.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#inverse){.pf-ref} proves it for finite-dimensional $V$ and step [](#example){.pf-ref} exhibits an infinite-dimensional $V$ where $TU=I$ holds and $T$ is not invertible.
:::
:::
