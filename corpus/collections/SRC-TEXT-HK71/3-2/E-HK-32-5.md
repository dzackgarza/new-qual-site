::: {.exercise}
Let $\CC^{2 \times 2}$ be the complex vector space of $2 \times 2$ matrices with complex entries.
Let
$$
B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -4 & 4 \end{bmatrix}
$$
and let $T$ be the linear operator on $\CC^{2 \times 2}$ defined by $T(A) = BA$.
What is the rank of $T$?
Can you describe $T^2$?

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise and no text version of Hoffman and Kunze in the checkout; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $B=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-4&4\end{bmatrix}$ acts on $\CC^2$ by left multiplication, $T(A)=BA$ on $\CC^{2\times2}$, and for $A$ with columns $a_1,a_2$ the columns of $T(A)$ are $Ba_1,Ba_2$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #rank-b}
$B$ has rank $1$ and $\im B$ is the line spanned by $\binom{1}{-4}$.

::: pf-proof
The second row of $B$ is $-4$ times the first, and the first row $(1,-1)$ is nonzero, so the row space of $B$ is one dimensional and $\rank B=1$; the columns of $B$ are $\binom{1}{-4}$ and $\binom{-1}{4}=-\binom{1}{-4}$, so the column space, which is $\im B$, is the span of $\binom{1}{-4}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #range}
$\range T=\{[u\ v]:u,v\in\im B\}$, of dimension $2$.

::: pf-proof
For $A=[a_1\ a_2]$ the product rule for block multiplication gives $T(A)=[Ba_1\ Ba_2]$, so every element of $\range T$ has both columns in $\im B$. Conversely, given $u,v\in\im B$, say $u=Ba_1$ and $v=Ba_2$ for some $a_1,a_2\in\CC^2$, the matrix $A=[a_1\ a_2]$ satisfies $T(A)=[u\ v]$. The map $[u\ v]\mapsto(u,v)$ is an isomorphism of $\{[u\ v]:u,v\in\im B\}$ with $(\im B)\times(\im B)$, whose dimension is $1+1=2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rank-t}
$\rank T=2$.

::: pf-proof
The rank of $T$ is the dimension of its range, which is $2$ by step [](#range){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square}
$B^2=5B$ and hence $T^2=5T$.

::: pf-proof
$B^2=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-4&4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-4&4\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1+4&-1-4\\-4-16&4+16\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}5&-5\\-20&20\end{bmatrix}=5B$. So for every $A$, $T^2(A)=B(BA)=B^2A=5BA=5T(A)$, that is $T^2=5T$.

:::
