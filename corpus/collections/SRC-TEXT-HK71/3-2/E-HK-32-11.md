::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Suppose that $\operatorname{rank}(T^2) = \operatorname{rank}(T)$.
Prove that the range and null space of $T$ are disjoint, i.e., have only the zero vector in common.
:::

::: solution
Since $\ker T\subseteq\ker T^2$, rank-nullity and the hypothesis give
\[
\dim\ker T
=\dim V-\operatorname{rank}T
=\dim V-\operatorname{rank}T^2
=\dim\ker T^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\ker T=\ker T^2.
\]
Now let $y\in\operatorname{range}T\cap\ker T$. Write $y=T\alpha$. Since
$T(y)=0$,
\[
T^2\alpha=0,
\]
so $\alpha\in\ker T^2=\ker T$. Hence $y=T\alpha=0$. Thus
\[
\operatorname{range}T\cap\ker T=\{0\}.
\]
:::
