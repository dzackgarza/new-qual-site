::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Suppose that $\operatorname{rank}(T^2) = \operatorname{rank}(T)$.
Prove that the range and null space of $T$ are disjoint, i.e., have only the zero vector in common.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise; the book's text is retained in the checkout as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §3.2 Exercise 11 on printed p. 84. The argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $V$ is finite dimensional, $T$ is a linear operator on $V$, and rank-nullity says $\dim\ker S+\rank S=\dim V$ for every linear $S:V\to V$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nested}
$\ker T\subseteq\ker T^2$, and the hypothesis forces equality.

::: pf-proof
If $T\alpha=0$ then $T^2\alpha=T(0)=0$, so $\ker T\subseteq\ker T^2$. Applying rank-nullity to $T$ and to $T^2$ and using $\rank T^2=\rank T$,
$$
\dim\ker T=\dim V-\rank T=\dim V-\rank T^{2}=\dim\ker T^{2}.
$$
A subspace with the dimension of the whole is the whole, so $\ker T=\ker T^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #disjoint}
$\im T\cap\ker T=\{0\}$; that is, the range and the null space of $T$ have only the
zero vector in common.

::: pf-proof
The zero vector lies in both spaces. Conversely let $y\in\im T\cap\ker T$, say $y=T\alpha$ with $\alpha\in V$. Then $T^2\alpha=T(y)=0$, so $\alpha\in\ker T^2=\ker T$ by step [](#nested){.pf-ref}, and hence $y=T\alpha=0$.

:::
