::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a vector space over the field $F$ and $T$ a linear operator on $V$.
If $T^2 = 0$, what can you say about the relation of the range of $T$ to the null space of $T$?
Give an example of a linear operator $T$ on $\RR^2$ such that $T^2 = 0$ but $T \neq 0$.
:::

::: solution
If $T^2=0$ and $y\in\operatorname{range}T$, then $y=T\alpha$ for some
$\alpha$, and
\[
T(y)=T(T\alpha)=0.
\]
Thus every vector in the range of $T$ lies in the null space:
\[
\operatorname{range}T\subseteq\ker T.
\]

For example, on $\mathbb R^2$ define
\[
T(x,y)=(y,0).
\]
Then $T\ne0$, since $T(0,1)=(1,0)$, while
\[
T^2(x,y)=T(y,0)=(0,0).
\]
Hence $T^2=0$ but $T\ne0$.
:::
