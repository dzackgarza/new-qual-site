::: {.exercise}
Use the Lagrange interpolation formula to find a polynomial $f$ with real coefficients such that $f$ has degree $\leq 3$ and $f(-1) = -6$, $f(0) = 2$, $f(1) = -2$, $f(2) = 6$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §4.3 Exercise 1, p. 126. The interpolation polynomial was recomputed and its four prescribed values were then verified by substitution.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #formula}
The Lagrange interpolation formula gives $f=\sum_{i}y_iL_i$ with $L_i(x)=\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{x-x_j}{x_i-x_j}$, and $\deg f\leq3$.

::: pf-proof
Each $L_i$ has degree $3$ and satisfies $L_i(x_j)=\delta_{ij}$, so $\sum_iy_iL_i$ is a polynomial of degree at most $3$ taking the value $y_i$ at $x_i$; this is the Lagrange formula of §4.3.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #terms}
With the nodes $-1,0,1,2$ and values $-6,2,-2,6$ the denominators are $-6,2,-2,6$ respectively.

::: pf-proof
For $x_i=-1$: $(-1-0)(-1-1)(-1-2)=(-1)(-2)(-3)=-6$. For $x_i=0$: $(1)(-1)(-2)=2$. For $x_i=1$: $(2)(1)(-1)=-2$. For $x_i=2$: $(3)(2)(1)=6$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #polynomial}
$f(x)=4x^3-6x^2-2x+2$.

::: pf-proof
With the denominators of step [](#terms){.pf-ref} the four terms simplify to $x(x-1)(x-2)$, $(x+1)(x-1)(x-2)$, $(x+1)x(x-2)$ and $(x+1)x(x-1)$. These expand to $x^3-3x^2+2x$, $x^3-2x^2-x+2$, $x^3-x^2-2x$ and $x^3-x$, whose sum is $4x^3-6x^2-2x+2$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #check}
$f(-1)=-6$, $f(0)=2$, $f(1)=-2$ and $f(2)=6$.

::: pf-proof
$4(-1)^3-6(1)-2(-1)+2=-4-6+2+2=-6$; $f(0)=2$; $4-6-2+2=-2$; $32-24-4+2=6$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
The polynomial of degree at most $3$ with the four prescribed values is $4x^3-6x^2-2x+2$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#formula){.pf-ref} produces a polynomial of degree at most $3$ with those values and step [](#check){.pf-ref} confirms them; by step [](#polynomial){.pf-ref} that polynomial is $4x^3-6x^2-2x+2$, and it is unique because the difference of two such polynomials would have four roots and degree at most $3$.
:::
:::
