::: {.exercise}
Let $n$ be a positive integer and $F$ a field.
Suppose $A$ is an $n \times n$ matrix over $F$ and $P$ is an invertible $n \times n$ matrix over $F$.
If $f$ is any polynomial over $F$, prove that
$$
f(P^{-1}AP) = P^{-1}f(A)P.
$$

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise; the book's text is retained in the checkout as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §4.3 Exercise 5 on printed p. 127. The argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $A$ and $P$ are $n\times n$ matrices over $F$ with $P$ invertible, $f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{r}c_kx^{k}$ is a polynomial over $F$, and $f(A)$ denotes $\sum_{k=0}^{r}c_kA^{k}$ with $A^{0}=I$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #powers}
$(P^{-1}AP)^{k}=P^{-1}A^{k}P$ for every $k\ge0$.

::: pf-proof
Induction on $k$. For $k=0$ both sides are $P^{-1}IP=I$. If the identity holds for $k$, then
$$
(P^{-1}AP)^{k+1}=(P^{-1}A^{k}P)(P^{-1}AP)=P^{-1}A^{k}(PP^{-1})AP=P^{-1}A^{k+1}P,
$$
using associativity of matrix multiplication and $PP^{-1}=I$; so it holds for $k+1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #linearity}
For any matrices $B_0,B_1$ and scalars $c_0,c_1$, $c_0P^{-1}B_0P+c_1P^{-1}B_1P=P^{-1}(c_0B_0+c_1B_1)P$.

::: pf-proof
Scalars commute with matrices and matrix multiplication is distributive and associative, so both sides equal $c_0P^{-1}B_0P+c_1P^{-1}B_1P$ term by term; iterating gives the same for any finite sum.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #main}
$f(P^{-1}AP)=P^{-1}f(A)P$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#powers){.pf-ref} and step [](#linearity){.pf-ref},
$$
f(P^{-1}AP)=\sum_{k=0}^{r}c_k(P^{-1}AP)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}c_kP^{-1}A^{k}P=P^{-1}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{r}c_kA^{k}\Big)P=P^{-1}f(A)P.
$$

:::
