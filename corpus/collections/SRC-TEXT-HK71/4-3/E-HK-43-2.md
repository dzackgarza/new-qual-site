::: {.exercise}
Let $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ be real numbers.
We ask when it is possible to find a polynomial $f$ over $\RR$, of degree not more than 2, such that $f(-1) = \alpha$, $f(1) = \beta$, $f(3) = \gamma$ and $f(0) = \delta$.
Prove that this is possible if and only if
$$
3\alpha + 6\beta - \gamma - 8\delta = 0.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and the computation below was redone for this pass. The four points $-1,0,1,3$ are distinct, so a polynomial of degree at most $2$ is determined by any three of its values.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #candidate}
If $f(x)=ax^{2}+bx+c$ satisfies $f(0)=\delta$, $f(-1)=\alpha$ and $f(1)=\beta$, then $c=\delta$, $a=\frac{\alpha+\beta-2\delta}{2}$ and $b=\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}$.

::: pf-proof
$f(0)=c$ gives $c=\delta$. The other two conditions read $a-b+\delta=\alpha$ and $a+b+\delta=\beta$. Adding them gives $2a+2\delta=\alpha+\beta$, so $a=\frac{\alpha+\beta-2\delta}{2}$; subtracting the first from the second gives $2b=\beta-\alpha$, so $b=\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #at-three}
With those coefficients, $f(3)=3\alpha+6\beta-8\delta$.

::: pf-proof
$f(3)=9a+3b+\delta=\frac{9(\alpha+\beta-2\delta)}{2}+\frac{3(\beta-\alpha)}{2}+\delta=\frac{9\alpha+9\beta-18\delta+3\beta-3\alpha}{2}+\delta=\frac{6\alpha+12\beta-18\delta}{2}+\delta=3\alpha+6\beta-9\delta+\delta$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #necessity}
If such an $f$ exists, then $3\alpha+6\beta-\gamma-8\delta=0$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#candidate){.pf-ref} the coefficients of $f$ are forced by the values at $-1,0,1$, so $f$ is the polynomial displayed there; by step [](#at-three){.pf-ref} its value at $3$ is $3\alpha+6\beta-8\delta$. The fourth condition demands $f(3)=\gamma$, hence $\gamma=3\alpha+6\beta-8\delta$, which is the stated equation.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Conversely, if $3\alpha+6\beta-\gamma-8\delta=0$, such a polynomial exists; so the interpolation is possible exactly under that condition.

::: pf-proof
Assume the condition and let $f(x)=ax^{2}+bx+c$ with the three coefficients of step [](#candidate){.pf-ref}. Then $f(0)=\delta$, $f(-1)=\alpha$ and $f(1)=\beta$ by construction, $f$ has degree at most $2$, and by step [](#at-three){.pf-ref} $f(3)=3\alpha+6\beta-8\delta=\gamma$ by the assumed condition. So $f$ has all four prescribed values. Together with step [](#necessity){.pf-ref} this proves the equivalence: the polynomial exists if and only if $3\alpha+6\beta-\gamma-8\delta=0$.
:::
