::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be the field of real numbers,
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad p = (x - 2)(x - 3)(x - 1).
$$

(a) Show that $p(A) = 0$.

(b) Let $P_1, P_2, P_3$ be the Lagrange polynomials for $t_1 = 2$, $t_2 = 3$, $t_3 = 1$.
Compute $E_i = P_i(A)$, $i = 1, 2, 3$.

(c) Show that $E_1 + E_2 + E_3 = I$, $E_iE_j = 0$ if $i \neq j$, $E_i^2 = E_i$.

(d) Show that $A = 2E_1 + 3E_2 + E_3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §4.3 Exercise 3.

Let
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&0&0&0\\0&2&0&0\\0&0&3&0\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,3,1),\qquad p(x)=(x-2)(x-3)(x-1),
$$
and let $t_{1}=2$, $t_{2}=3$, $t_{3}=1$. The Lagrange polynomial for the node $t_{i}$ among these three nodes is
$$
P_{i}(x)=\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{x-t_{j}}{t_{i}-t_{j}} .
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #diagonal}
For a diagonal matrix $D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})$ and a polynomial $f$, $f(D)=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(f(\lambda_{1}),\dots,f(\lambda_{n})\bigr)$.

::: pf-proof
$D^{k}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1}^{k},\dots,\lambda_{n}^{k})$ by induction on $k$, since multiplying diagonal matrices multiplies corresponding entries. Hence for $f=\sum_{k}c_{k}x^{k}$, $f(D)=\sum_{k}c_{k}D^{k}$ is diagonal with $i$-th entry $\sum_{k}c_{k}\lambda_{i}^{k}=f(\lambda_{i})$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pa}
$p(A)=0$.

::: pf-proof
$p(2)=(2-2)(2-3)(2-1)=0$, $p(3)=(3-2)(3-3)(3-1)=0$ and $p(1)=(1-2)(1-3)(1-1)=0$. By step [](#diagonal){.pf-ref}, $p(A)=\operatorname{diag}(p(2),p(2),p(3),p(1))=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,0,0)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lagrange}
$P_{1}(x)=-(x-3)(x-1)$, $P_{2}(x)=\tfrac{(x-2)(x-1)}{2}$, $P_{3}(x)=\tfrac{(x-2)(x-3)}{2}$, and $P_{i}(t_{j})$ is $1$ for $i=j$ and $0$ for $i\neq j$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting the nodes into the product formula: $P_{1}$ has denominator $(2-3)(2-1)=-1$, $P_{2}$ has denominator $(3-2)(3-1)=2$, and $P_{3}$ has denominator $(1-2)(1-3)=2$. For $i=j$ every factor of the product equals $1$; for $i\neq j$ one factor has numerator $t_{j}-t_{j}=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #E}
$E_{1}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0)$, $E_{2}=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1,0)$, $E_{3}=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,0,1)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#diagonal){.pf-ref}, $E_{i}=P_{i}(A)=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(P_{i}(2),P_{i}(2),P_{i}(3),P_{i}(1)\bigr)$. For $i=1$ the values at the nodes $(2,3,1)$ are $(1,0,0)$, giving $\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0)$ after reading the first entry twice; for $i=2$ they are $(0,1,0)$, giving $\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1,0)$; for $i=3$ they are $(0,0,1)$, giving $\operatorname{diag}(0,0,0,1)$. Step [](#lagrange){.pf-ref} supplies each value.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c}
$E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}=I$, $E_{i}E_{j}=0$ for $i\neq j$, and $E_{i}^{2}=E_{i}$.

::: pf-proof
Addition and multiplication of diagonal matrices act entry by entry. At each of the four positions, exactly one of $E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}$ has entry $1$ and the others $0$, by step [](#E){.pf-ref}: so the sum has $1$ in every position, which is $I$; a product $E_{i}E_{j}$ with $i\neq j$ has $1\cdot0=0$ in the position where $E_{i}$ is $1$ and $0$ elsewhere; and $E_{i}^{2}$ has $1^{2}=1$ and $0^{2}=0$ where $E_{i}$ has $1$ and $0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #d}
$A=2E_{1}+3E_{2}+E_{3}$.

::: pf-proof
$2E_{1}+3E_{2}+E_{3}$ is diagonal with entries $2,2,3,1$ by step [](#E){.pf-ref} and entrywise operations, and that is $A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Parts (a), (b), (c) and (d) hold.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#pa){.pf-ref}, [](#E){.pf-ref}, [](#c){.pf-ref} and [](#d){.pf-ref} are the four parts.
:::
