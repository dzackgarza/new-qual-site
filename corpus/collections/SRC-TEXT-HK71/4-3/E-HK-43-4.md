::: {.exercise}
Let $p = (x - 2)(x - 3)(x - 1)$ and let $T$ be any linear operator on $\RR^4$ such that $p(T) = 0$.
Let $P_1, P_2, P_3$ be the Lagrange polynomials of Exercise 3, and let $E_i = P_i(T)$, $i = 1, 2, 3$.
Prove that
$$
E_1 + E_2 + E_3 = I, \qquad E_iE_j = 0 \quad \text{if} \quad i \neq j,
$$
$$
E_i^2 = E_i, \quad \text{and} \quad T = 2E_1 + 3E_2 + E_3.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Write $p(x)=(x-2)(x-3)(x-1)$ and $t_1=2$, $t_2=3$, $t_3=1$, let $P_i$ be the Lagrange polynomial of degree at most $2$ with $P_i(t_j)=\delta_{ij}$, and let $T$ be a linear operator on $\RR^4$ with $p(T)=0$, $E_i=P_i(T)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #homomorphism}
Evaluation at $T$, $\varphi:F[x]\to\operatorname{End}(\RR^4)$, $\varphi(f)=f(T)$, is a ring homomorphism fixing the constants, and $\varphi(p)=0$; hence $\varphi(pq)=0$ for every polynomial $q$.

::: pf-proof
$\varphi$ is additive and multiplicative: $(\lambda f+\mu g)(T)=\lambda f(T)+\mu g(T)$ and $(fg)(T)=f(T)g(T)$, because $T$ commutes with each of its own powers; it sends the constant $1$ to $I$. So $\varphi(pq)=\varphi(p)\varphi(q)=0\cdot\varphi(q)=0$ by the hypothesis $p(T)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lagrange}
$\deg P_i\le2$ and $P_i(t_j)=\delta_{ij}$.

::: pf-proof
This is the defining property of the Lagrange polynomials for the three distinct nodes $t_1,t_2,t_3$, which exist because the nodes are pairwise distinct: $P_i(x)=\prod_{k\ne i}\frac{x-t_k}{t_i-t_k}$, of degree $2$, and each factor of the numerator vanishes at $t_k$ for $k\ne i$ while the denominator normalizes the value at $t_i$ to $1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sum}
$P_1+P_2+P_3=1$ and $E_1+E_2+E_3=I$.

::: pf-proof
The polynomial $P_1+P_2+P_3-1$ has degree at most $2$ and its values at $t_1,t_2,t_3$ are $1-1=0$ by step [](#lagrange){.pf-ref}. A polynomial of degree at most $2$ with three distinct roots is zero: otherwise it would be divisible by $(x-t_1)(x-t_2)(x-t_3)$, of degree $3$. Applying $\varphi$ and step [](#homomorphism){.pf-ref} gives the operator identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #decomposition}
$T=2E_1+3E_2+E_3$.

::: pf-proof
The polynomial $q(x)=x-\bigl(t_1P_1(x)+t_2P_2(x)+t_3P_3(x)\bigr)$ has degree at most $2$, and $q(t_j)=t_j-t_j\cdot1=0$ for $j=1,2,3$ by step [](#lagrange){.pf-ref}; so $q=0$ by the same root count as in step [](#sum){.pf-ref}. Since $t_1=2$, $t_2=3$, $t_3=1$, applying $\varphi$ gives $T=2E_1+3E_2+E_3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #orthogonal}
$E_iE_j=0$ for $i\ne j$.

::: pf-proof
For $i\ne j$ and every $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, $(P_iP_j)(t_k)=P_i(t_k)P_j(t_k)=0$ by step [](#lagrange){.pf-ref}, since at least one of the two factors vanishes at $t_k$. So $P_iP_j$ is divisible by $(x-t_1)(x-t_2)(x-t_3)=p$ and equals $p\cdot q_{ij}$ for some polynomial $q_{ij}$; then $E_iE_j=\varphi(P_iP_j)=\varphi(p\,q_{ij})=0$ by step [](#homomorphism){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #idempotent}
$E_i^2=E_i$.

::: pf-proof
$P_i(x)^2-P_i(x)$ takes the values $1-1=0$ at $x=t_i$ and $0-0=0$ at $x=t_j$, $j\ne i$, by step [](#lagrange){.pf-ref}; so it is divisible by $p$ and equals $p\,r_i$ for some polynomial $r_i$. Hence $E_i^2-E_i=\varphi(P_i^2-P_i)=\varphi(p\,r_i)=0$, again by step [](#homomorphism){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$E_1+E_2+E_3=I$, $E_iE_j=0$ for $i\ne j$, $E_i^2=E_i$, and $T=2E_1+3E_2+E_3$.

::: pf-proof
The four identities are steps [](#sum){.pf-ref}, [](#orthogonal){.pf-ref}, [](#idempotent){.pf-ref} and [](#decomposition){.pf-ref}. Only $p(T)=0$ and the distinctness of $1,2,3$ were used; no property of the particular operator $T$ and no dimension count entered, which is why the argument does not use $\dim=4$ beyond having an operator to evaluate at.
:::
