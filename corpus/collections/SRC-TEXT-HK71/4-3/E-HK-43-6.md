::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field.
We have considered certain special linear functionals on $F[x]$ obtained via 'evaluation at $t$':
$$
L(f) = f(t).
$$
Such functionals are not only linear but also have the property that $L(fg) = L(f)L(g)$.
Prove that if $L$ is any linear functional on $F[x]$ such that
$$
L(fg) = L(f)L(g)
$$
for all $f$ and $g$, then either $L = 0$ or there is a $t$ in $F$ such that $L(f) = f(t)$ for all $f$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume $L \neq 0$.

Since $L(1) = L(1 \cdot 1) = L(1)^2$, we have $L(1) \in \{0, 1\}$.
If $L(1) = 0$, then $L(f) = L(f \cdot 1) = L(f)L(1) = 0$ for every $f$, so $L = 0$, a contradiction.
Hence $L(1) = 1$.

Let $t = L(x) \in F$.
For every polynomial $g$,
$$
L\big((x - t)g\big) = L(x)L(g) - tL(g) = 0.
$$

Let $f \in F[x]$.
Division by $x - t$ gives $f = (x - t)q + c$ with $q \in F[x]$ and $c = f(t) \in F$.
Then
$$
L(f) = L\big((x - t)q\big) + cL(1) = 0 + c = f(t).
$$
Hence $L(f) = f(t)$ for all $f$.
:::
