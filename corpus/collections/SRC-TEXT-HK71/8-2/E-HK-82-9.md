::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the subspace of $R[x]$ of polynomials of degree at most 3. Equip $V$ with the inner product
\[
(f \mid g) = \int_0^1 f(t)g(t)\,dt.
\]

(a) Find the orthogonal complement of the subspace of scalar polynomials.

(b) Apply the Gram-Schmidt process to the basis $\{1, x, x^2, x^3\}$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. The orthogonal complement of the scalar polynomials is the zero-integral hyperplane.
::: proof
The scalar polynomials form $\operatorname{span}\{1\}$. Thus
$$
f\perp1
\iff (f\mid1)=0
\iff \int_0^1f(t)\,dt=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
(\operatorname{span}\{1\})^\perp
=\left\{a+bx+cx^2+dx^3:
a+\frac b2+\frac c3+\frac d4=0\right\}.}
$$
For example, a convenient basis is
$$
\boxed{
x-\frac12,
\quad x^2-\frac13,
\quad x^3-\frac14.}
$$
These three vectors satisfy the zero-integral condition and are linearly
independent, so they form a basis of the three-dimensional hyperplane.
:::

<1>2. Gram--Schmidt on $(1,x,x^2,x^3)$ yields the shifted Legendre basis.
::: proof
Starting with $q_0=1$, one has $\|q_0\|=1$. The next residual is
$$
q_1=x-\frac12,
$$
with
$$
\|q_1\|^2=\frac1{12}.
$$
For $x^2$, subtracting its projections onto $q_0$ and $q_1$ gives
$$
q_2=x^2-x+\frac16,
\qquad
\|q_2\|^2=\frac1{180}.
$$
For $x^3$, the next Gram--Schmidt residual is
$$
q_3=x^3-\frac32x^2+\frac35x-\frac1{20},
\qquad
\|q_3\|^2=\frac1{2800}.
$$
Each norm follows by integrating the square on $[0,1]$, and direct integration
shows $(q_i\mid q_j)=0$ for $i\ne j$ [@HK71].

Normalizing gives the orthonormal basis
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u_0&=1,\\
u_1&=\sqrt3(2x-1),\\
u_2&=\sqrt5(6x^2-6x+1),\\
u_3&=\sqrt7(20x^3-30x^2+12x-1).
\end{aligned}}
$$
These are exactly the vectors obtained by Gram--Schmidt from the prescribed
ordered basis.
:::
:::
