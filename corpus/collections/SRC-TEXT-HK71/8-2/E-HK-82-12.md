::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional subspace of an inner product space $V$, and let $E$ be the orthogonal projection of $V$ on $W$.
Prove that $(E\alpha \mid \beta) = (\alpha \mid E\beta)$ for all $\alpha, \beta$ in $V$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the cross terms of the two orthogonal decompositions vanish, so both sides collapse to the inner product of the $W$-components.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #decomposition}

Every $\alpha\in V$ is uniquely $\alpha=w+u$ with $w\in W$, $u\in W^{\perp}$, and $E\alpha=w$.

::: pf-proof

$W$ is a finite-dimensional subspace of the inner product space $V$, so $V=W\oplus W^{\perp}$: every $\alpha$ has such an expression, and the expression is unique because $W\cap W^{\perp}=0$ (a vector orthogonal to itself is $0$ by positive definiteness). The orthogonal projection $E$ of $V$ on $W$ is defined by $E\alpha=w$ for this decomposition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #left}

$(E\alpha\mid\beta)=(w\mid w')$ when $\beta=w'+u'$ with $w'\in W$, $u'\in W^{\perp}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#decomposition){.pf-ref} $E\alpha=w$. Additivity in the second argument gives
$$
(w\mid w'+u')=(w\mid w')+(w\mid u')=(w\mid w'),
$$
since $w\in W$, $u'\in W^{\perp}$, and so $(w\mid u')=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #right}

$(\alpha\mid E\beta)=(w\mid w')$ for the same decompositions.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#decomposition){.pf-ref} $E\beta=w'$, and additivity (and conjugate-homogeneity) in the first argument gives
$$
(w+u\mid w')=(w\mid w')+(u\mid w')=(w\mid w'),
$$
since $u\in W^{\perp}$ and $w'\in W$ give $(u\mid w')=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$(E\alpha\mid\beta)=(\alpha\mid E\beta)$ for all $\alpha,\beta\in V$; that is, $E$ is self-adjoint.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#left){.pf-ref} and [](#right){.pf-ref} compute the two sides as the same number $(w\mid w')$, where $\alpha=w+u$ and $\beta=w'+u'$ are the orthogonal decompositions of steps [](#decomposition){.pf-ref}. Hence
$$
\boxed{(E\alpha\mid\beta)=(\alpha\mid E\beta)\qquad(\alpha,\beta\in V),}
$$
which is the statement that the orthogonal projection is self-adjoint.

:::
