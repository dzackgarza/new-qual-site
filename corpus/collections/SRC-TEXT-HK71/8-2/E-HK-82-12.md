::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional subspace of an inner product space $V$, and let $E$ be the orthogonal projection of $V$ on $W$.
Prove that $(E\alpha \mid \beta) = (\alpha \mid E\beta)$ for all $\alpha, \beta$ in $V$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For arbitrary $\alpha,\beta\in V$, write their orthogonal decompositions as
$$
\alpha=w+u,
\qquad
\beta=w'+u',
$$
where
$$
w,w'\in W,
\qquad
u,u'\in W^\perp.
$$
By definition of the orthogonal projection,
$$
E\alpha=w,
\qquad
E\beta=w'.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
(E\alpha\mid\beta)
&=(w\mid w'+u')\\
&=(w\mid w')
\end{aligned}
$$
because $w\perp u'$. Similarly,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\alpha\mid E\beta)
&=(w+u\mid w')\\
&=(w\mid w')
\end{aligned}
$$
because $u\perp w'$. Hence
$$
\boxed{(E\alpha\mid\beta)=(\alpha\mid E\beta)}
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$, so the orthogonal projection is self-adjoint
[@HK71].
:::
