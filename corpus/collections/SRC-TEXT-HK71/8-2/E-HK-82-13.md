::: {.exercise}
Let $S$ be a subset of an inner product space $V$.
Show that $(S^{\perp})^{\perp}$ contains the subspace spanned by $S$.
When $V$ is finite-dimensional, show that $(S^{\perp})^{\perp}$ is the subspace spanned by $S$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
W=\operatorname{span}S.
$$
First observe that
$$
S^\perp=W^\perp,
$$
because being orthogonal to every vector of $S$ is equivalent, by linearity,
to being orthogonal to every linear combination of vectors of $S$.

<1>1. One always has
$$
\boxed{W\subseteq(S^\perp)^\perp.}
$$
::: {.proof}
Take $w\in W$ and $u\in S^\perp=W^\perp$. By definition of $W^\perp$,
$$
(w\mid u)=0.
$$
Thus $w$ is orthogonal to every element of $S^\perp$, which means
$$
w\in(S^\perp)^\perp.
$$
:::

<1>2. If $V$ is finite dimensional, equality holds.
::: {.proof}
In finite dimension the orthogonal-decomposition theorem gives
$$
V=W\oplus W^\perp
$$
[@HK71]. Apply it again to the subspace $W^\perp$:
$$
V=W^\perp\oplus(W^\perp)^\perp.
$$
The first decomposition shows that $W$ is a complementary subspace to
$W^\perp$ and is itself orthogonal to $W^\perp$. The orthogonal complement is
unique, hence
$$
(W^\perp)^\perp=W.
$$
Since $S^\perp=W^\perp$,
$$
\boxed{(S^\perp)^\perp=\operatorname{span}S.}
$$
:::
:::
