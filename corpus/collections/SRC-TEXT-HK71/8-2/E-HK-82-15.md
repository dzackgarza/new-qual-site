::: {.exercise}
Suppose $V = W_1 \oplus W_2$ and that $f_1$ and $f_2$ are inner products on $W_1$ and $W_2$, respectively.
Show that there is a unique inner product $f$ on $V$ such that

(a) $W_2 = W_1^{\perp}$;

(b) $f(\alpha, \beta) = f_k(\alpha, \beta)$, when $\alpha, \beta$ are in $W_k$, $k = 1, 2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** For $V = W_1 \oplus W_2$ with inner products $f_1, f_2$ on $W_1, W_2$, construct the unique inner product $f$ on $V$ with $W_2 = W_1^\perp$ and $f|_{W_k} = f_k$.

<1>1. Define $f$ on $V$.
<2>1. Every $v \in V$ writes uniquely as $v = w_1 + w_2$ with $w_k \in W_k$.
Proof: $V = W_1 \oplus W_2$ is a direct sum.
<2>2. Define $f(w_1 + w_2, w_1' + w_2') \definedas f_1(w_1, w_1') + f_2(w_2, w_2')$.
Proof: this is the orthogonal direct sum of the two inner products.

<1>2. $f$ is an inner product.
<2>1. $f$ is bilinear and symmetric.
Proof: each $f_k$ is bilinear and symmetric, and the sum is componentwise.
<2>2. $f$ is positive definite.
Proof: $f(v, v) = f_1(w_1, w_1) + f_2(w_2, w_2) \ge 0$, and equality forces $f_1(w_1, w_1) = f_2(w_2, w_2) = 0$, hence $w_1 = w_2 = 0$ by positive definiteness of each $f_k$.

<1>3. $W_2 = W_1^\perp$.
<2>1. $W_2 \subseteq W_1^\perp$.
Proof: for $w_1 \in W_1$ and $w_2 \in W_2$, $f(w_1, w_2) = f_1(w_1, 0) + f_2(0, w_2) = 0$.
<2>2. $W_1^\perp \subseteq W_2$.
Proof: if $v = w_1 + w_2 \in W_1^\perp$, then for all $w_1' \in W_1$, $0 = f(v, w_1') = f_1(w_1, w_1')$, so $w_1 = 0$ (since $f_1$ is nondegenerate), hence $v = w_2 \in W_2$.

<1>4. $f|_{W_k} = f_k$.
Proof: for $\alpha, \beta \in W_1$, $f(\alpha, \beta) = f_1(\alpha, \beta) + f_2(0, 0) = f_1(\alpha, \beta)$; similarly for $W_2$.

<1>5. Uniqueness.
<2>1. Any inner product satisfying (a) and (b) must equal $f$.
Proof: (b) fixes $f$ on each $W_k$; (a) forces $f(w_1, w_2) = 0$ for $w_1 \in W_1, w_2 \in W_2$; bilinearity then determines $f$ on all of $V = W_1 \oplus W_2$.

<1>6. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>2, <1>3, <1>4 give existence; <1>5 gives uniqueness.
:::
