::: {.exercise}
Find an inner product on $\RR^2$ such that $(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2) = 2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §8.2 Exercise 8 [@HK71].

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #definition}
Define
$$
\bigl((x_1,x_2)\mid(y_1,y_2)\bigr)=x_1y_1+2x_1y_2+2x_2y_1+5x_2y_2,
$$
with Gram matrix $G=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&5\end{pmatrix}$ in the standard basis.

::: pf-proof
This is a definition. The coefficient of $x_iy_j$ in the displayed form is $G_{ij}$, that is, $G_{11}=1$, $G_{12}=G_{21}=2$ and $G_{22}=5$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #bilinear}
The form is bilinear and symmetric.

::: pf-proof
It is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $2$ in the eight variables $x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2$ that is linear in $x$ for fixed $y$ and linear in $y$ for fixed $x$, which is bilinearity; and $G$ is symmetric, so interchanging the two arguments (which transposes $G$) does not change the form.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #positive}
$\bigl(x\mid x\bigr)>0$ for every $x\ne0$.

::: pf-proof
$\bigl(x\mid x\bigr)=x_1^{2}+4x_1x_2+5x_2^{2}=(x_1+2x_2)^{2}+x_2^{2}$, a sum of two squares of the real numbers $x_1+2x_2$ and $x_2$. If the sum is $0$ then $x_2=0$ and $x_1+2x_2=x_1=0$, so $x=0$; hence it is strictly positive for $x\ne0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #value}
$\bigl(\epsilon_1\mid\epsilon_2\bigr)=2$.

::: pf-proof
$\epsilon_1=(1,0)$ and $\epsilon_2=(0,1)$, so the form evaluates to $x_1y_1$ with $x=(1,0)$, $y=(0,1)$: the only surviving term is $2x_1y_2=2\cdot1\cdot1=2$, which is $G_{12}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The displayed form is an inner product on $\RR^2$ with $\bigl(\epsilon_1\mid\epsilon_2\bigr)=2$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#bilinear){.pf-ref}, [](#positive){.pf-ref} and [](#value){.pf-ref} give the three defining properties and the required value:
$$
\boxed{\bigl((x_1,x_2)\mid(y_1,y_2)\bigr)=x_1y_1+2x_1y_2+2x_2y_1+5x_2y_2,\qquad \bigl(\epsilon_1\mid\epsilon_2\bigr)=2.}
$$
Positive definiteness is exactly the positive definiteness of $G$, which the completion of squares in step [](#positive){.pf-ref} exhibits: $G=L^{t}L$ with $L=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.
:::
