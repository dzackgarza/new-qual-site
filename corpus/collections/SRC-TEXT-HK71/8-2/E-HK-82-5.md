::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an inner product space, and let $\alpha, \beta$ be vectors in $V$.
Show that $\alpha = \beta$ if and only if $(\alpha \mid \gamma) = (\beta \mid \gamma)$ for every $\gamma$ in $V$.
:::

::: {.solution}
If $\alpha=\beta$, then of course
$$
(\alpha\mid\gamma)=(\beta\mid\gamma)
$$
for every $\gamma\in V$.

Conversely, suppose
$$
(\alpha\mid\gamma)=(\beta\mid\gamma)
$$
for every $\gamma$. By linearity in the first argument,
$$
(\alpha-\beta\mid\gamma)=0
$$
for every $\gamma\in V$. Taking
$$
\gamma=\alpha-\beta
$$
gives
$$
\|\alpha-\beta\|^2
=(\alpha-\beta\mid\alpha-\beta)=0.
$$
Positive definiteness therefore implies $\alpha-\beta=0$, so
$$
\boxed{\alpha=\beta.}
$$
This proves both directions [@HK71].
:::
