::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the vector space of all $n \times n$ matrices over $\CC$, with the inner product $(A \mid B) = \operatorname{tr}(AB^*)$.
Find the orthogonal complement of the subspace of diagonal matrices.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Let $\mathcal D\le M_n(\CC)$ be the subspace of diagonal matrices, and let $A=(a_{ij})$ range over $M_n(\CC)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #trace}
For $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_n)$ one has $(A\mid D)=\operatorname{tr}(AD^{*})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}\overline{d_i}$.

::: pf-proof
$D^{*}=\operatorname{diag}(\overline{d_1},\dots,\overline{d_n})$. The $(i,i)$ entry of $AD^{*}$ is $\sum_j a_{ij}(D^{*})_{ji}$, and $(D^{*})_{ji}=\overline{d_i}$ when $j=i$ and $0$ otherwise, so the sum reduces to $a_{ii}\overline{d_i}$. Summing the diagonal entries gives $\operatorname{tr}(AD^{*})=\sum_ia_{ii}\overline{d_i}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #one_way}
If $a_{ii}=0$ for every $i$ then $(A\mid D)=0$ for every $D\in\mathcal D$, that is $A\in\mathcal D^{\perp}$.

::: pf-proof
Under the hypothesis each summand $a_{ii}\overline{d_i}$ of step [](#trace){.pf-ref} is $0$, whatever the $d_i$ are; so the inner product vanishes against every diagonal matrix, which is the definition of membership in the orthogonal complement.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #other_way}
If $A\in\mathcal D^{\perp}$ then $a_{kk}=0$ for every $k$.

::: pf-proof
Fix $k$ and take $D=E_{kk}$, the diagonal matrix with $1$ in position $kk$ and $0$ elsewhere, which lies in $\mathcal D$. Membership in $\mathcal D^{\perp}$ gives $0=(A\mid E_{kk})$, and by step [](#trace){.pf-ref} that number is $\sum_ia_{ii}\overline{(E_{kk})_{ii}}=a_{kk}\cdot1=a_{kk}$. So $a_{kk}=0$, and $k$ was arbitrary.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\mathcal D^{\perp}=\{A=(a_{ij})\in M_n(\CC):a_{11}=\cdots=a_{nn}=0\}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#one_way){.pf-ref} shows that every matrix with zero diagonal lies in $\mathcal D^{\perp}$, and step [](#other_way){.pf-ref} that every element of $\mathcal D^{\perp}$ has zero diagonal. The two inclusions give the displayed equality, which is the answer. Note that no condition at all is imposed on the off-diagonal entries $a_{ij}$ with $i\ne j$.
:::
