::: {.exercise}
Apply the Gram-Schmidt process to the vectors $\beta_1 = (1, 0, 1)$, $\beta_2 = (1, 0, -1)$, $\beta_3 = (0, 3, 4)$, to obtain an orthonormal basis for $\RR^3$ with the standard inner product.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and each inner product below was recomputed for this pass. Gram--Schmidt in the order $\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}$ produces $u_{j}=v_{j}/\norm{v_{j}}$ from $v_{j}=\beta_{j}-\sum_{i<j}(\beta_{j}\mid u_{i})u_{i}$, with $(\cdot\mid\cdot)$ the standard inner product on $\RR^{3}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #first-two}
$\beta_{1}\cdot\beta_{2}=0$ and $\norm{\beta_{1}}=\norm{\beta_{2}}=\sqrt2$, so $u_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,0,1)$ and $u_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,0,-1)$.

::: pf-proof
$\beta_{1}\cdot\beta_{2}=1\cdot1+0\cdot0+1\cdot(-1)=0$, so $\beta_{1},\beta_{2}$ are already orthogonal and $v_{1}=\beta_{1}$, $v_{2}=\beta_{2}$. Also $\beta_{1}\cdot\beta_{1}=1+0+1=2$ and $\beta_{2}\cdot\beta_{2}=1+0+1=2$, so both norms are $\sqrt2$ and dividing by them gives $u_{1},u_{2}$ as displayed, each of norm $1$ and orthogonal to the other.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inner-products}
$(\beta_{3}\mid u_{1})=2\sqrt2$ and $(\beta_{3}\mid u_{2})=-2\sqrt2$.

::: pf-proof
$\beta_{3}\cdot\beta_{1}=0\cdot1+3\cdot0+4\cdot1=4$, so $(\beta_{3}\mid u_{1})=4/\sqrt2=2\sqrt2$; and $\beta_{3}\cdot\beta_{2}=0\cdot1+3\cdot0+4\cdot(-1)=-4$, so $(\beta_{3}\mid u_{2})=-4/\sqrt2=-2\sqrt2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #residual}
The residual is $v_{3}=(0,3,0)$, so $u_{3}=(0,1,0)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#inner-products){.pf-ref},
$$
v_{3}=\beta_{3}-2\sqrt2\,u_{1}+2\sqrt2\,u_{2}=(0,3,4)-2(1,0,1)+2(1,0,-1)=(0,3,4)+(-2,0,-2)+(2,0,-2)=(0,3,0),
$$
where $2\sqrt2\,u_{1}=2(1,0,1)$ and $2\sqrt2\,u_{2}=2(1,0,-1)$. Its norm is $3$, so $u_{3}=v_{3}/3=(0,1,0)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,0,1),\ \frac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,0,-1),\ (0,1,0)\right\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\RR^{3}$ for the given order.

::: pf-proof
Each of the three has norm $1$ by construction, being a vector divided by its norm. Their pairwise inner products vanish: $u_{1}\cdot u_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-1)=0$ from step [](#first-two){.pf-ref}, and $u_{1}\cdot u_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(0+0+0)=0$, $u_{2}\cdot u_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(0+0+0)=0$. So the three are orthonormal; being three orthonormal vectors in the three-dimensional space $\RR^{3}$, they form a basis. This is the orthonormal basis the Gram--Schmidt process yields from $\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}$ in the prescribed order.
:::
