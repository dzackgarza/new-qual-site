::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space, and let $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n\}$ be an orthonormal basis for $V$.
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$ and $A$ the matrix of $T$ in the ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$.
Prove that
$$
A_{ij} = (T\alpha_j \mid \alpha_i).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §8.2 Exercise 14 [@HK71].

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #columns}
$T\alpha_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{kj}\alpha_{k}$ for each $j$.

::: pf-proof
The $j$th column of the matrix $A=[T]_{\mathfrak B}$ is by definition the coordinate vector of $T\alpha_{j}$ with respect to $\mathfrak B$, and coordinates are the coefficients in the expansion of a vector in the basis.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pairing}
$\bigl(T\alpha_{j}\mid\alpha_{i}\bigr)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{kj}\bigl(\alpha_{k}\mid\alpha_{i}\bigr)$.

::: pf-proof
The inner product is linear in its first variable, so the inner product with $\alpha_{i}$ of the finite sum of step [](#columns){.pf-ref} is the corresponding sum of inner products.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #orthonormal}
$\bigl(\alpha_{k}\mid\alpha_{i}\bigr)=\delta_{ki}$.

::: pf-proof
This is the definition of an orthonormal basis: the vectors have norm $1$ and are pairwise orthogonal, so the inner product is $1$ for $k=i$ and $0$ otherwise.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$A_{ij}=\bigl(T\alpha_{j}\mid\alpha_{i}\bigr)$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting step [](#orthonormal){.pf-ref} into step [](#pairing){.pf-ref} leaves the single term with $k=i$:
$$
\boxed{A_{ij}=\bigl(T\alpha_{j}\mid\alpha_{i}\bigr).}
$$
The formula fails for a basis that is merely orthogonal, where the surviving term is $A_{ij}\norm{\alpha_{i}}^{2}$ instead; orthonormality is what removes the factor.
:::
