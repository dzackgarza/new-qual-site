::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an inner product space and $W$ a finite-dimensional subspace of $V$.
There are (in general) many projections which have $W$ as their range.
One of these, the orthogonal projection on $W$, has the property that $||E\alpha|| \leq ||\alpha||$ for every $\alpha$ in $V$.
Prove that if $E$ is a projection with range $W$, such that $||E\alpha|| \leq ||\alpha||$ for all $\alpha$ in $V$, then $E$ is the orthogonal projection on $W$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $E$ be a projection with range $W$ and $\|E\alpha\| \le \|\alpha\|$ for all $\alpha$.
Proof: hypotheses.

<1>2. It suffices to show $\ker E = W^\perp$.
Proof: a projection with range $W$ is the orthogonal projection iff its kernel is $W^\perp$.

<1>3. Let $\alpha \in \ker E$ and $w \in W$. For any $t \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$\|w\|^2 = \|E(w + t\alpha)\|^2 \le \|w + t\alpha\|^2 = \|w\|^2 + 2t\operatorname{Re}\langle w, \alpha \rangle + t^2\|\alpha\|^2.$$
Proof: $E(w) = w$ (since $w \in W = \operatorname{range} E$) and $E\alpha = 0$, so $E(w + t\alpha) = w$; then apply the norm bound and expand.

<1>4. Hence $2t\operatorname{Re}\langle w, \alpha \rangle + t^2\|\alpha\|^2 \ge 0$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
Proof: <1>3, subtracting $\|w\|^2$.

<1>5. This forces $\operatorname{Re}\langle w, \alpha \rangle = 0$ (otherwise choosing $t$ of the appropriate small sign makes the left side negative).
Proof: <1>4 (a linear term in $t$ with nonzero coefficient dominates the quadratic term for small $t$).

<1>6. Replacing $\alpha$ by $i\alpha$ (in the complex case) gives $\operatorname{Im}\langle w, \alpha \rangle = 0$ as well, so $\langle w, \alpha \rangle = 0$.
Proof: <1>5 applied to $i\alpha$.

<1>7. Hence $\alpha \in W^\perp$, so $\ker E \subseteq W^\perp$.
Proof: <1>6 (for all $w \in W$).

<1>8. Conversely, if $\alpha \in W^\perp$ then $E\alpha \in W$ and $\langle E\alpha, w \rangle = \langle \alpha, E^* w \rangle$; more directly, $\|E\alpha\| \le \|\alpha\|$ and $E\alpha \in W$ with $\alpha - E\alpha \in \ker E$; since $\ker E \subseteq W^\perp$ and $\alpha \in W^\perp$, we get $E\alpha = 0$.
Proof: <1>7 and the fact that $E\alpha \in W$ while $\alpha \in W^\perp$ forces $E\alpha = 0$ (as $E\alpha = \alpha - (\alpha - E\alpha)$ with $\alpha - E\alpha \in \ker E \subseteq W^\perp$).

<1>9. Hence $\ker E = W^\perp$, so $E$ is the orthogonal projection onto $W$.
Proof: <1>7, <1>8, and <1>2.

<1>10. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>9.
:::
