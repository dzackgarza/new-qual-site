::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an inner product space and $W$ a finite-dimensional subspace of $V$.
There are (in general) many projections which have $W$ as their range.
One of these, the orthogonal projection on $W$, has the property that $||E\alpha|| \leq ||\alpha||$ for every $\alpha$ in $V$.
Prove that if $E$ is a projection with range $W$, such that $||E\alpha|| \leq ||\alpha||$ for all $\alpha$ in $V$, then $E$ is the orthogonal projection on $W$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let $E$ be a projection with range $W$ and $\|E\alpha\| \le \|\alpha\|$ for all $\alpha$.

::: pf-proof

hypotheses.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

It suffices to show $\ker E = W^\perp$.

::: pf-proof

a projection with range $W$ is the orthogonal projection iff its kernel is $W^\perp$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Let $\alpha \in \ker E$ and $w \in W$. For any $t \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$\|w\|^2 = \|E(w + t\alpha)\|^2 \le \|w + t\alpha\|^2 = \|w\|^2 + 2t\operatorname{Re}\langle w, \alpha \rangle + t^2\|\alpha\|^2.$$

::: pf-proof

$E(w) = w$ (since $w \in W = \operatorname{range} E$) and $E\alpha = 0$, so $E(w + t\alpha) = w$; then apply the norm bound and expand.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Hence $2t\operatorname{Re}\langle w, \alpha \rangle + t^2\|\alpha\|^2 \ge 0$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, subtracting $\|w\|^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

This forces $\operatorname{Re}\langle w, \alpha \rangle = 0$ (otherwise choosing $t$ of the appropriate small sign makes the left side negative).

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} (a linear term in $t$ with nonzero coefficient dominates the quadratic term for small $t$).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Replacing $\alpha$ by $i\alpha$ (in the complex case) gives $\operatorname{Im}\langle w, \alpha \rangle = 0$ as well, so $\langle w, \alpha \rangle = 0$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s5){.pf-ref} applied to $i\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

Hence $\alpha \in W^\perp$, so $\ker E \subseteq W^\perp$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s6){.pf-ref} (for all $w \in W$).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s8}

Conversely, if $\alpha \in W^\perp$ then $E\alpha \in W$ and $\langle E\alpha, w \rangle = \langle \alpha, E^* w \rangle$; more directly, $\|E\alpha\| \le \|\alpha\|$ and $E\alpha \in W$ with $\alpha - E\alpha \in \ker E$; since $\ker E \subseteq W^\perp$ and $\alpha \in W^\perp$, we get $E\alpha = 0$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s7){.pf-ref} and the fact that $E\alpha \in W$ while $\alpha \in W^\perp$ forces $E\alpha = 0$ (as $E\alpha = \alpha - (\alpha - E\alpha)$ with $\alpha - E\alpha \in \ker E \subseteq W^\perp$).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s9}

Hence $\ker E = W^\perp$, so $E$ is the orthogonal projection onto $W$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s7){.pf-ref}, [](#s8){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::
