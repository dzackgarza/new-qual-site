::: {.exercise}
Prove that a real symmetric matrix has a real symmetric cube root; i.e., if $A$ is real symmetric, there is a real symmetric $B$ such that $B^3 = A$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $A$ be a real symmetric $n\times n$ matrix. By the real spectral theorem,
there is a real orthogonal matrix $P$ such that
$$
P^tAP=D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n),
$$
where every $\lambda_i\in\mathbb R$.

For each $i$, let
$$
\mu_i=\sqrt[3]{\lambda_i}
$$
be the real cube root, and put
$$
C=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n).
$$
Then $C$ is real symmetric and
$$
C^3=D.
$$
Define
$$
B=PCP^t.
$$
Since $P$ and $C$ are real, $B$ is real. Also
$$
B^t=(PCP^t)^t=PC^tP^t=PCP^t=B,
$$
so $B$ is symmetric. Finally, using $P^tP=I$,
$$
B^3
=PCP^tPCP^tPCP^t
=PC^3P^t
=PDP^t
=A.
$$
Thus every real symmetric matrix has a real symmetric cube root.
:::
