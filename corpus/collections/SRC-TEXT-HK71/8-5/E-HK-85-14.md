::: {.exercise}
If two normal operators commute, prove that their product is normal.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $S$ and $T$ be normal operators satisfying
$$
ST=TS.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Commutation with a normal operator implies commutation with its adjoint.

::: pf-proof

By Exercise 13, there is a polynomial $f$ such that
$$
T^*=f(T).
$$
Since $S$ commutes with $T$, it commutes with every polynomial in $T$.
Therefore
$$
ST^*=T^*S.
$$
Likewise, since $S^*$ is a polynomial in $S$ and $T$ commutes with $S$, we
have
$$
TS^*=S^*T.
$$
Taking adjoints also gives the remaining corresponding commutation relations.
Thus the factors $S,S^*,T,T^*$ commute whenever they come from different
operators.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The product is normal.

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
(ST)^*=T^*S^*,
$$
we obtain, using step [](#s1){.pf-ref} and normality of $S$ and $T$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(ST)(ST)^*
&=STT^*S^*\\
&=SS^*TT^*\\
&=S^*ST^*T\\
&=T^*S^*ST\\
&=(ST)^*(ST).
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence $ST$ commutes with its adjoint, so
$$
\boxed{ST\text{ is normal}.}
$$

:::
