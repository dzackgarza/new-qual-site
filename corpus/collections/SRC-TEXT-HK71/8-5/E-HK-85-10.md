::: {.exercise}
Prove that every positive matrix is the square of a positive matrix.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $A$ be a positive (positive-definite Hermitian) matrix.
::: {.proof}
hypothesis.
:::

<1>2. $A$ is Hermitian, so it is unitarily diagonalizable: $A = U D U^*$ with $U$ unitary and $D = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n)$.
::: {.proof}
spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices.
:::

<1>3. Since $A$ is positive, each $\lambda_i > 0$.
::: {.proof}
the eigenvalues of a positive matrix are positive.
:::

<1>4. Define $B = U \operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda_1}, \ldots, \sqrt{\lambda_n}) U^*$.
::: {.proof}
definition.
:::

<1>5. $B$ is positive (its eigenvalues $\sqrt{\lambda_i} > 0$).
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and <1>4.
:::

<1>6. $B^2 = U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n) U^* = A$.
::: {.proof}
<1>4 (squaring the diagonal matrix squares the entries).
:::

<1>7. Hence $A$ is the square of the positive matrix $B$.
::: {.proof}
<1>5 and <1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>7.
:::
:::
