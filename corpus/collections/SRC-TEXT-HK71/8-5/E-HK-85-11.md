::: {.exercise}
Prove that a normal and nilpotent operator is the zero operator.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $T$ be normal and nilpotent on a finite-dimensional inner product space $V$.
Proof: setup.

<1>2. A normal operator is diagonalizable (over $\CC$).
Proof: the spectral theorem for normal operators.

<1>3. A nilpotent operator has all eigenvalues equal to $0$.
Proof: if $Tv = \lambda v$ with $\lambda \neq 0$, then $T^n v = \lambda^n v \neq 0$ for all $n$, contradicting nilpotency.

<1>4. Hence $T$ is diagonalizable with all eigenvalues $0$.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>3.

<1>5. Therefore $T = 0$.
Proof: a diagonalizable operator with all eigenvalues $0$ is the zero operator.

<1>6. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>5.
:::
