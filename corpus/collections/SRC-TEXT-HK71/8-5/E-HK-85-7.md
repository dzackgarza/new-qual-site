::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on the finite-dimensional inner product space $V$, and suppose $T$ is both positive and unitary.
Prove $T = I$.
:::

::: solution
Since $T$ is positive, it is self-adjoint. Hence the spectral theorem gives an
orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of $T$, and every eigenvalue is a positive
real number.

On the other hand, $T$ is unitary. If
$$
Tv=\lambda v
$$
for a nonzero eigenvector $v$, then
$$
\|v\|=\|Tv\|=|\lambda|\,\|v\|,
$$
so $|\lambda|=1$. The only positive real number of absolute value one is
$1$. Thus every eigenvalue of $T$ is $1$.

Because the orthonormal eigenbasis spans $V$, the operator acts as the identity
on a basis. Therefore
$$
\boxed{T=I}.
$$
:::
