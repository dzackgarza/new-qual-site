::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on the finite-dimensional inner product space $V$, and suppose $T$ is both positive and unitary.
Prove $T = I$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §8.5 Exercise 7. Here $V$ is a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, $T$ is positive, that is, self-adjoint with $\langle Tv,v\rangle>0$ for $v\neq0$, and unitary, that is, invertible with $T^{*}=T^{-1}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #spectral}

$T$ is self-adjoint and has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors with real eigenvalues.

::: pf-proof

A positive operator is self-adjoint, and the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators gives the orthonormal eigenbasis and the reality of the eigenvalues.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pos}

Every eigenvalue of $T$ is positive.

::: pf-proof

If $Tv=\lambda v$ with $v\neq0$ then $\lambda\norm{v}^{2}=\langle Tv,v\rangle>0$, so $\lambda>0$ since $\norm{v}^{2}>0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #unit}

Every eigenvalue of $T$ has absolute value $1$.

::: pf-proof

If $Tv=\lambda v$ with $v\neq0$ then $\norm{v}=\norm{Tv}=\abs{\lambda}\norm{v}$ because a unitary operator preserves norms, and $\norm{v}\neq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #one}

Every eigenvalue of $T$ equals $1$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#pos){.pf-ref} and [](#unit){.pf-ref}: a positive real number of absolute value $1$ is $1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$T=I$.

::: pf-proof

The orthonormal eigenbasis of step [](#spectral){.pf-ref} consists of eigenvectors, all with eigenvalue $1$ by step [](#one){.pf-ref}; so $T$ fixes every vector of a basis, hence $T=I$.

:::
