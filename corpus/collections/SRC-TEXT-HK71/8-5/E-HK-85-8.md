::: {.exercise}
Prove $T$ is normal if and only if $T = T_1 + iT_2$, where $T_1$ and $T_2$ are self-adjoint operators which commute.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Every operator has canonical self-adjoint real and imaginary parts.
::: proof
Define
$$
T_1=\frac{T+T^*}{2},
\qquad
T_2=\frac{T-T^*}{2i}.
$$
Then
$$
T_1^*=T_1,
$$
and, since conjugating $1/(2i)$ changes its sign,
$$
T_2^*=\frac{T^*-T}{-2i}=T_2.
$$
Moreover
$$
T_1+iT_2
=\frac{T+T^*}{2}+\frac{T-T^*}{2}
=T.
$$
:::

<1>2. For these canonical parts, commutation is equivalent to normality.
::: proof
A direct calculation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_1T_2-T_2T_1
&=\frac1{4i}
\bigl((T+T^*)(T-T^*)-(T-T^*)(T+T^*)\bigr)\\
&=\frac1{2i}(T^*T-TT^*).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
T_1T_2=T_2T_1
\iff T^*T=TT^*.
$$
So if $T$ is normal, the self-adjoint operators in step <1>1 commute.
:::

<1>3. Conversely, a sum of commuting self-adjoint real and imaginary parts is normal.
::: proof
Suppose
$$
T=T_1+iT_2,
$$
where $T_1^*=T_1$, $T_2^*=T_2$, and $T_1T_2=T_2T_1$. Then
$$
T^*=T_1-iT_2.
$$
Hence
$$
TT^*
=(T_1+iT_2)(T_1-iT_2)
=T_1^2+T_2^2,
$$
because the cross terms cancel by commutativity. Similarly
$$
T^*T=T_1^2+T_2^2.
$$
Thus $TT^*=T^*T$, so $T$ is normal.
:::
:::
