::: {.exercise}
If $T$ is a normal operator, prove that characteristic vectors for $T$ which are associated with distinct characteristic values are orthogonal.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
Tv=\lambda v,
\qquad
Tw=\mu w,
$$
with $v,w\ne0$ and $\lambda\ne\mu$.

<1>1. An eigenvector of a normal operator is an eigenvector of the adjoint with conjugate eigenvalue.
::: {.proof}
Since $T$ is normal, so is $T-\lambda I$, and therefore
$$
\|(T-\lambda I)x\|
=\|(T^*-\overline\lambda I)x\|
$$
for every $x$. Taking $x=v$ gives
$$
0
=\|(T-\lambda I)v\|
=\|(T^*-\overline\lambda I)v\|,
$$
hence
$$
T^*v=\overline\lambda v.
$$
Similarly,
$$
T^*w=\overline\mu w.
$$
:::

<1>2. Distinct eigenvalues force orthogonality.
::: {.proof}
Using the adjoint identity and the eigenvector equations,
$$
\lambda(v\mid w)
=(Tv\mid w)
=(v\mid T^*w).
$$
Since $T^*w=\overline\mu w$ and the inner product is conjugate-linear in its
second slot,
$$
(v\mid T^*w)
=(v\mid\overline\mu w)
=\mu(v\mid w).
$$
Therefore
$$
(\lambda-\mu)(v\mid w)=0.
$$
Because $\lambda\ne\mu$, we conclude
$$
\boxed{(v\mid w)=0}.
$$
Thus characteristic vectors belonging to distinct characteristic values are
orthogonal.
:::
:::
