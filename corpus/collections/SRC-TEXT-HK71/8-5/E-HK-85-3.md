::: {.exercise}
For
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 3 & 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}
\]
there is a real orthogonal matrix $P$ such that $P^tAP = D$ is diagonal.
Find such a diagonal matrix $D$.
:::

::: solution
Because $A$ is real symmetric, the spectral theorem guarantees a real
orthogonal matrix $P$ for which $P^tAP$ is diagonal. Its diagonal entries are
the eigenvalues of $A$.

<1>1. Compute the characteristic polynomial.
::: proof
For
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\
2&3&4\\
3&4&5
\end{pmatrix},
$$
we have
$$
\operatorname{tr}(A)=9,
$$
and the sum of the three principal $2\times2$ minors is
$$
(3-4)+(5-9)+(15-16)=-1-4-1=-6.
$$
Also $\det A=0$, since the row differences satisfy
$$
(2,3,4)-(1,2,3)=(1,1,1)
$$
and
$$
(3,4,5)-(2,3,4)=(1,1,1).
$$
Therefore
$$
\chi_A(x)
=x^3-9x^2-6x
=x(x^2-9x-6).
$$
:::

<1>2. Read off the diagonal form.
::: proof
The roots are
$$
0,
\qquad
\frac{9+\sqrt{105}}2,
\qquad
\frac{9-\sqrt{105}}2.
$$
Thus one valid diagonal matrix is
$$
\boxed{
D=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{9+\sqrt{105}}2&0&0\\
0&\frac{9-\sqrt{105}}2&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
Any permutation of these three diagonal entries is equally valid, with the
corresponding permutation of the orthonormal eigenbasis.
:::
:::
