::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a normal operator on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space.
Prove that there is a polynomial $f$, with complex coefficients, such that $T^* = f(T)$.
(Represent $T$ by a diagonal matrix, and see what $f$ must be.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Let the distinct characteristic values of $T$ be
$$
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r.
$$
Since $T$ is normal, the spectral theorem gives an orthonormal basis in which
$$
[T]=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{i_1},\ldots,\lambda_{i_n}),
$$
where each diagonal entry is one of the distinct $\lambda_j$. In the same
basis,
$$
[T^*]=\operatorname{diag}(\overline{\lambda_{i_1}},\ldots,
\overline{\lambda_{i_n}}).
$$

Choose the Lagrange interpolation polynomial
$$
f(x)=
\sum_{j=1}^r
\overline{\lambda_j}
\prod_{k\ne j}\frac{x-\lambda_k}{\lambda_j-\lambda_k}.
$$
The eigenvalues are distinct, so the denominators are nonzero, and
$$
f(\lambda_j)=\overline{\lambda_j}
$$
for every $j$.

Therefore, in the chosen orthonormal eigenbasis,
$$
[f(T)]
=\operatorname{diag}(f(\lambda_{i_1}),\ldots,f(\lambda_{i_n}))
=\operatorname{diag}(\overline{\lambda_{i_1}},\ldots,
\overline{\lambda_{i_n}})
=[T^*].
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{T^*=f(T)}.
$$
:::
