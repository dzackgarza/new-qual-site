::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a normal operator on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space.
Prove that there is a polynomial $f$, with complex coefficients, such that $T^* = f(T)$.
(Represent $T$ by a diagonal matrix, and see what $f$ must be.)
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §8.5 Exercise 13. Here $T$ is normal on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, so $TT^{*}=T^{*}T$, and the spectral theorem for normal operators applies; $\overline{\lambda}$ is the complex conjugate.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #spectral}

$T$ has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors, with eigenvalues $\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\CC$.

::: pf-proof

This is the spectral theorem for a normal operator on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #adjoint}

In that basis $[T]=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})$ and $[T^{*}]=\operatorname{diag}(\overline{\lambda_{1}},\dots,\overline{\lambda_{n}})$.

::: pf-proof

The first is step [](#spectral){.pf-ref}. For the second, the adjoint of a diagonal matrix in an orthonormal basis is the conjugate transpose, which for a diagonal matrix is the diagonal of conjugates.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lagrange}

There is a polynomial $f\in\CC[x]$ with $f(\lambda_{i})=\overline{\lambda_{i}}$ for $i=1,\dots,n$.

::: pf-proof

If $\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}$ are the distinct numbers among the $\lambda_{i}$, the Lagrange polynomial $f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\overline{\mu_{j}}\prod_{l\neq j}\frac{x-\mu_{l}}{\mu_{j}-\mu_{l}}$ is defined — the denominators are nonzero because the $\mu_{j}$ are distinct — and satisfies $f(\mu_{j})=\overline{\mu_{j}}$; in particular $f(\lambda_{i})=\overline{\lambda_{i}}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$[f(T)]=[T^{*}]$ in that basis, hence $T^{*}=f(T)$.

::: pf-proof

A polynomial in a diagonal matrix is diagonal, with the polynomial applied to each diagonal entry, so $[f(T)]=\operatorname{diag}(f(\lambda_{1}),\dots,f(\lambda_{n}))=\operatorname{diag}(\overline{\lambda_{1}},\dots,\overline{\lambda_{n}})=[T^{*}]$ by steps [](#adjoint){.pf-ref} and [](#lagrange){.pf-ref}. Two operators with the same matrix in a basis are equal.

:::
