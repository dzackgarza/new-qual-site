::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be $\CC^2$, with the standard inner product.
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $V$ which is represented in the standard ordered basis by the matrix
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Show that $T$ is normal, and find an orthonormal basis for $V$, consisting of characteristic vectors for $T$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Prove normality of $A$ and compute its spectral decomposition into an orthonormal eigenbasis.

::: pf

::: pf-step
Part 1: Verification of Normality:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
The conjugate transpose (adjoint) of $A$ is:
$$A^* = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix}^* = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \bar{i} \\ \bar{i} & 1 \end{bmatrix}^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

::: pf-proof
The adjoint is the conjugate transpose: conjugating $i$ gives $-i$, and the real diagonal entries are fixed.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
We compute the product $A A^*$:
$$A A^* = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - i^2 & -i + i \\ i - i & -i^2 + 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = 2 I_2.$$

::: pf-proof
Matrix multiplication: the diagonal entries are $1\cdot1+i\overline{i}=1+1=2$, and the off-diagonal entries cancel.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
We compute the product $A^* A$:
$$A^* A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - i^2 & i - i \\ -i + i & -i^2 + 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = 2 I_2.$$

::: pf-proof
The same computation with the factors in the other order.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since $A A^* = A^* A = 2 I_2$, $T$ is a **normal operator**.

::: pf-proof
This is the definition of a normal operator: it commutes with its adjoint.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Part 2: Eigenvalues of $A$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
The characteristic polynomial of $A$ is:
$$p_A(\lambda) = \det(\lambda I - A) = \det \begin{bmatrix} \lambda - 1 & -i \\ -i & \lambda - 1 \end{bmatrix} = (\lambda - 1)^2 - (-i)^2 = (\lambda - 1)^2 - (-1) = (\lambda - 1)^2 + 1.$$

::: pf-proof
The determinant of $\lambda I-A$ is $(\lambda-1)^2-i\cdot i=(\lambda-1)^2+1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Setting $p_A(\lambda) = 0$:
$$(\lambda - 1)^2 = -1 \implies \lambda - 1 = \pm i \implies \lambda_1 = 1 + i, \quad \lambda_2 = 1 - i.$$

::: pf-proof
Solving $(\lambda-1)^2=-1$ in $\CC$ gives $\lambda-1=\pm i$, hence the two eigenvalues.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since the two eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2$ are distinct, their eigenspaces are orthogonal.

::: pf-proof
Eigenvectors of a normal operator belonging to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Part 3: Eigenvectors and Orthonormal Basis:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
**For $\lambda_1 = 1 + i$:**
$$(A - (1+i)I) v = \begin{bmatrix} -i & i \\ i & -i \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies -i x + i y = 0 \implies x = y.$$
An eigenvector is $v_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
Normalizing to unit length:
$$u_1 = \frac{1}{\norm{v_1}} v_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

::: pf-proof
The displayed matrix forces $v_1=v_2$, so the eigenspace is spanned by $(1,1)$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
**For $\lambda_2 = 1 - i$:**
$$(A - (1-i)I) v = \begin{bmatrix} i & i \\ i & i \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies i x + i y = 0 \implies x = -y.$$
An eigenvector is $v_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$.
Normalizing to unit length:
$$u_2 = \frac{1}{\norm{v_2}} v_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

::: pf-proof
The displayed matrix forces $v_1+v_2=0$, so the eigenspace is spanned by $(1,-1)$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
**Inner product verification:**
$$\langle u_1, u_2 \rangle = \frac{1}{2} (1 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot (-1)) = 0.$$

::: pf-proof
The normalized vectors $(1,1)/\sqrt2$ and $(1,-1)/\sqrt2$ have inner product $(1-1)/2=0$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Conclusion:
$T$ is normal since $A A^* = A^* A = 2 I_2$; an orthonormal eigenbasis is $\mathcal{B} = \left\{ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}$ with eigenvalues $1+i$ and $1-i$.

::: pf-proof
The computation $AA^*=A^*A$ gives normality, and the two displayed vectors form an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.
:::
