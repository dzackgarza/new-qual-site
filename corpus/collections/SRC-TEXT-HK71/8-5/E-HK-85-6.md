::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a normal operator on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space.
Prove that $T$ is self-adjoint, positive, or unitary according as every characteristic value of $T$ is real, positive, or of absolute value 1. (Use Theorem 22 to reduce to a similar question about diagonal matrices.)
:::

::: {.solution}
Since $T$ is normal on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, the
spectral theorem gives a unitary operator $U$ and scalars
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$ such that
$$
U^*TU=D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n).
$$
The $\lambda_i$ are exactly the characteristic values of $T$.

<1>1. Real characteristic values give a self-adjoint operator.
::: {.proof}
If every $\lambda_i$ is real, then
$$
D^*=\operatorname{diag}(\overline{\lambda_1},\ldots,\overline{\lambda_n})=D.
$$
Hence
$$
T^*=(UDU^*)^*=UD^*U^*=UDU^*=T.
$$
Thus $T$ is self-adjoint.
:::

<1>2. Positive characteristic values give a positive operator.
::: {.proof}
Assume every $\lambda_i>0$. For any nonzero vector $x$, write
$y=U^*x$. Since $U$ is unitary, $y\ne0$, and
$$
(Tx\mid x)
=(Dy\mid y)
=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i|y_i|^2>0.
$$
Therefore $T$ is positive.
:::

<1>3. Characteristic values of absolute value one give a unitary operator.
::: {.proof}
If $|\lambda_i|=1$ for every $i$, then
$$
D^*D
=\operatorname{diag}(|\lambda_1|^2,\ldots,|\lambda_n|^2)
=I.
$$
Consequently
$$
T^*T
=UD^*U^*UDU^*
=U(D^*D)U^*
=I.
$$
Thus $T$ is unitary.
:::
:::
