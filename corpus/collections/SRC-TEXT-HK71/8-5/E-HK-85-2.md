::: {.exercise}
Is a complex symmetric matrix self-adjoint?
Is it normal?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A complex matrix $A$ is symmetric if $A^t = A$, and self-adjoint (Hermitian) if $A^* = \overline{A}^t = A$.
::: {.proof}
definitions.
:::

<1>2. A complex symmetric matrix need not be self-adjoint.
::: {.proof}
e.g. $A = \begin{pmatrix} i & 0 \\ 0 & i \end{pmatrix}$ is symmetric ($A^t = A$) but $A^* = \begin{pmatrix} -i & 0 \\ 0 & -i \end{pmatrix} \neq A$.
:::

<1>3. A complex symmetric matrix need not be normal.
::: {.proof}
e.g. $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is symmetric, but $A A^* \neq A^* A$ (direct computation shows $AA^* = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -i \\ i & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ while $A^*A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & i \\ -i & 1 \end{pmatrix}$).
:::

<1>4. Hence the answer to both questions is: not necessarily.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>4.
:::
:::
