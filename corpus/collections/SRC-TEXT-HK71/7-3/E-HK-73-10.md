::: {.exercise}
Let $n$ be a positive integer, $n \geq 2$, and let $N$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over the field $F$ such that $N^n = 0$ but $N^{n-1} \neq 0$.
Prove that $N$ has no square root, i.e., that there is no $n \times n$ matrix $A$ such that $A^2 = N$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $N$ is nilpotent of index $n$ (i.e. $N^n = 0$ but $N^{n-1} \neq 0$), so its Jordan form is a single Jordan block $J_n(0)$ of size $n$.
Proof: a nilpotent matrix of index $n$ on an $n$-dimensional space has a single Jordan block of size $n$.

<1>2. Suppose for contradiction that $A^2 = N$.
Proof: assume a square root exists.

<1>3. Then $A^{2n} = N^n = 0$, so $A$ is nilpotent.
Proof: $A^{2n} = (A^2)^n = N^n = 0$.

<1>4. A nilpotent $n \times n$ matrix has nilpotency index at most $n$, so $A^n = 0$.
Proof: the nilpotency index of an $n \times n$ nilpotent matrix is at most $n$.

<1>5. Hence $N^{n-1} = (A^2)^{n-1} = A^{2n-2} = 0$ (since $2n - 2 \ge n$ for $n \ge 2$).
Proof: $A^{2n-2} = A^n A^{n-2} = 0 \cdot A^{n-2} = 0$ (using $A^n = 0$ from <1>4).

<1>6. This contradicts $N^{n-1} \neq 0$.
Proof: <1>5 and the hypothesis.

<1>7. Hence no such $A$ exists.
Proof: <1>2–<1>6.

<1>8. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>7.
:::
