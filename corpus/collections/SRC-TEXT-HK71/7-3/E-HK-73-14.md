::: {.exercise}
What's wrong with the following proof?
If $A$ is a complex $n \times n$ matrix such that $A^t = -A$, then $A$ is 0. (Proof: Let $J$ be the Jordan form of $A$.
Since $A^t = -A$, $J^t = -J$.
But $J$ is triangular so that $J^t = -J$ implies that every entry of $J$ is zero.
Since $J = 0$ and $A$ is similar to $J$, we see that $A = 0$.)
(Give an example of a non-zero $A$ such that $A^t = -A$.)
:::

::: solution
The error is the assertion
$$
J^t=-J.
$$
If $J=P^{-1}AP$, then
$$
J^t=P^tA^t(P^{-1})^t=-P^tA(P^{-1})^t,
$$
which need not equal
$$
-P^{-1}AP=-J.
$$
Transpose does not preserve the same conjugating matrix. What follows from
$A^t=-A$ is only that $J^t$ is similar to $-J$, not that the two matrices are
equal [@HK71]. Therefore the triangularity argument cannot be applied.

A concrete counterexample to the claimed theorem is
$$
\boxed{
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
It is nonzero and
$$
A^t=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}=-A.
$$
Over $\mathbb C$ its eigenvalues are $i$ and $-i$, so its Jordan form is
$\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)$, which indeed is not skew-symmetric even though it
is similar to the skew-symmetric matrix $A$.
:::
