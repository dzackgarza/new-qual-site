::: {.exercise}
Classify up to similarity all $n \times n$ complex matrices $A$ such that $A^n = I$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}.
$$
The polynomial
$$
x^n-1=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(x-\zeta^j)
$$
has distinct roots over $\mathbb C$, since its derivative $nx^{n-1}$ has no
common root with it. If $A^n=I$, then the minimal polynomial of $A$ divides
$x^n-1$, hence is squarefree. Therefore $A$ is diagonalizable and every
eigenvalue is an $n$th root of unity [@HK71].

Thus the similarity classes are precisely
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{diag}
(I_{m_0},\zeta I_{m_1},\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}I_{m_{n-1}}),
\qquad
m_j\ge0,\quad \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}m_j=n.}
$$
The multiplicity vector $(m_0,\ldots,m_{n-1})$ is determined by the
characteristic polynomial and hence by the similarity class, so the list is
irredundant. Conversely every displayed diagonal matrix has $n$th power equal
to $I$. In particular, the number of classes is
$$
\binom{2n-1}{n}.
$$
:::
