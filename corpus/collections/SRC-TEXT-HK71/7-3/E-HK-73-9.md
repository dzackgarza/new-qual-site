::: {.exercise}
Classify up to similarity all $n \times n$ complex matrices $A$ such that $A^n = I$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §7.3 Exercise 9, p. 250. The classification is the one the rational canonical form gives for a squarefree annihilating polynomial: $A$ is diagonalisable and its eigenvalues are $n$-th roots of unity, so a similarity class is a multiplicity function on the roots.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #roots}
$x^n-1$ has $n$ distinct roots in $\CC$, namely $\zeta^{j}$ for $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}$ and $j=0,\dots,n-1$.

::: pf-proof
$\zeta^{j}$ satisfies $(\zeta^{j})^n=1$, and the $n$ numbers are distinct because $\zeta$ has order $n$; a polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ roots, so these are all of them, and they are simple.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #minpoly}
If $A^n=I$ then the minimal polynomial $m_A$ divides $x^n-1$, hence is a product of distinct linear factors over $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
The minimal polynomial divides every polynomial annihilating $A$, and $x^n-1$ annihilates $A$; a divisor of a polynomial with distinct roots has distinct roots.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #diagonal}
$A$ is diagonalisable over $\CC$.

::: pf-proof
A matrix is diagonalisable exactly when its minimal polynomial is a product of distinct linear factors; step [](#minpoly){.pf-ref} gives that.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #values}
Every eigenvalue of $A$ is an $n$-th root of unity.

::: pf-proof
If $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq0$ then $v=A^nv=\lambda^nv$, so $\lambda^n=1$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #converse}
For any choice of $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ among the $n$-th roots of unity, the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$ satisfies $A^n=I$.

::: pf-proof
The $n$-th power of a diagonal matrix is the diagonal matrix of the $n$-th powers, and $\lambda_i^n=1$ for every $i$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #classes}
The similarity classes of complex $n\times n$ matrices with $A^n=I$ correspond to the functions $m$ from the $n$ roots of unity to $\{0,1,\dots,n\}$ with $\sum m(\lambda)=n$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#diagonal){.pf-ref} and [](#values){.pf-ref} such an $A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are $n$-th roots of unity, and two diagonal matrices are similar exactly when they have the same multiset of diagonal entries. The multiset is recorded by the multiplicities $m(\lambda)$, which are nonnegative integers summing to $n$, and step [](#converse){.pf-ref} shows every such multiset occurs.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
The matrices $A$ with $A^n=I$ are, up to similarity, exactly the diagonal matrices $\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{j_1},\dots,\zeta^{j_n})$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#diagonal){.pf-ref}, [](#values){.pf-ref} and [](#classes){.pf-ref} give the classification and step [](#converse){.pf-ref} shows each listed matrix satisfies $A^n=I$.
:::
:::
