::: {.exercise}
If $A$ is a complex $5 \times 5$ matrix with characteristic polynomial
$$
f = (x - 2)^3(x + 7)^2
$$
and minimal polynomial $p = (x - 2)^2(x + 7)$, what is the Jordan form for $A$?
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §7.3 Exercise 3. Write $J_{m}(\lambda)$ for the $m\times m$ Jordan block with eigenvalue $\lambda$.

The exercise uses three facts about Jordan form over $\CC$, stated for a matrix $A$ with distinct eigenvalues $\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}$: the multiset of sizes of the Jordan blocks belonging to $\lambda$ is a partition of the algebraic multiplicity $a_{\lambda}$ of $\lambda$ in the characteristic polynomial; the exponent $e_{\lambda}$ of $(x-\lambda)$ in the minimal polynomial is the largest of those sizes; and the characteristic polynomial is $\prod_{\lambda}(x-\lambda)^{a_{\lambda}}$ while the minimal polynomial is $\prod_{\lambda}(x-\lambda)^{e_{\lambda}}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #blocks-kill}
$(x-\lambda)^{m}$ annihilates $J_{m}(\lambda)$, and $(x-\lambda)^{m-1}$ does not.

::: pf-proof
Write $J_{m}(\lambda)=\lambda I+N$ with $N$ the $m\times m$ matrix whose only nonzero entries are $1$ in positions $(i,i+1)$. Then $J_{m}(\lambda)-\lambda I=N$ and $N^{m}=0$ while $N^{m-1}\neq0$, since $N^{k}$ has $1$ in positions $(i,i+k)$ for $i+k\leq m$ and is zero otherwise. Substituting, $(J_{m}(\lambda)-\lambda I)^{m}=N^{m}=0$ and $(J_{m}(\lambda)-\lambda I)^{m-1}=N^{m-1}\neq0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #min-poly-sum}
For a direct sum of Jordan blocks $J=\bigoplus_{t}J_{m_{t}}(\lambda_{t})$, the minimal polynomial of $J$ is $\prod_{\lambda}(x-\lambda)^{e_{\lambda}}$ with $e_{\lambda}=\max\{m_{t}\mid\lambda_{t}=\lambda\}$, and the characteristic polynomial is $\prod_{t}(x-\lambda_{t})^{m_{t}}$.

::: pf-proof
A polynomial $g$ kills a direct sum exactly when it kills each summand; by step [](#blocks-kill){.pf-ref}, $g$ kills $J_{m}(\lambda)$ exactly when $(x-\lambda)^{m}$ divides $g$, that is when the multiplicity of $x-\lambda$ in $g$ is at least $m$. So the monic polynomial of least degree killing $J$ has multiplicity $\max_{t:\lambda_{t}=\lambda}m_{t}$ at $x-\lambda$. The characteristic polynomial of a direct sum is the product of those of the summands, with multiplicity $m_{t}$ at $\lambda_{t}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #multiplicities}
The characteristic polynomial of $A$ gives algebraic multiplicities $a_{2}=3$ and $a_{-7}=2$, and the minimal polynomial gives $e_{2}=2$ and $e_{-7}=1$.

::: pf-proof
The characteristic polynomial is $f=(x-2)^{3}(x+7)^{2}$, so the exponents are the multiplicities; the minimal polynomial is $p=(x-2)^{2}(x+7)$ with exponents $2$ and $1$. Both factor completely over $\CC$ with these two roots, so these are all the eigenvalues.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lambda2}
The Jordan blocks for the eigenvalue $2$ have sizes $2$ and $1$.

::: pf-proof
The sizes form a partition of $a_{2}=3$ whose largest part is $e_{2}=2$, by the three facts quoted above. The partitions of $3$ are $3$, $2+1$ and $1+1+1$, and only $2+1$ has largest part $2$. Hence the sizes are $2$ and $1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lambda7}
The Jordan blocks for the eigenvalue $-7$ have sizes $1$ and $1$.

::: pf-proof
The sizes form a partition of $a_{-7}=2$ with largest part $e_{-7}=1$; the only partition of $2$ with largest part $1$ is $1+1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #form}
Up to order of the blocks, $J(A)=J_{2}(2)\oplus J_{1}(2)\oplus J_{1}(-7)\oplus J_{1}(-7)$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#lambda2){.pf-ref} and [](#lambda7){.pf-ref} list all the blocks, and the Jordan form of a matrix is the direct sum of its Jordan blocks, determined up to permutation of the blocks.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #check}
The matrix $J=J_{2}(2)\oplus J_{1}(2)\oplus J_{1}(-7)\oplus J_{1}(-7)$ has characteristic polynomial $(x-2)^{3}(x+7)^{2}$ and minimal polynomial $(x-2)^{2}(x+7)$, so it is a valid Jordan form for $A$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#min-poly-sum){.pf-ref} the characteristic polynomial is $(x-2)^{2}(x-2)(x+7)(x+7)$ and the minimal polynomial is $(x-2)^{\max(2,1)}(x+7)^{\max(1,1)}=(x-2)^{2}(x+7)$. These are $f$ and $p$, so a matrix with these Jordan blocks has exactly the invariants prescribed; matrices with the same Jordan form are similar, so $J$ is the Jordan form of $A$ up to ordering of the blocks. In coordinates ordered so that the blocks appear in the order $2,2,-7,-7$, $J$ is the matrix displayed in the card's statement.
:::
