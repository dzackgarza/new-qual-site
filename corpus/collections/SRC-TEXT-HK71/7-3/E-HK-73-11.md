::: {.exercise}
Let $N_1$ and $N_2$ be $6 \times 6$ nilpotent matrices over the field $F$.
Suppose that $N_1$ and $N_2$ have the same minimal polynomial and the same nullity.
Prove that $N_1$ and $N_2$ are similar.
Show that this is not true for $7 \times 7$ nilpotent matrices.
:::

::: {.solution}
For a nilpotent matrix, the Jordan block sizes form a partition of the
dimension. The exponent of $x$ in the minimal polynomial is the largest block
size, while the nullity is the number of Jordan blocks [@HK71].

<1>1. In dimension six these two data determine the partition uniquely.
::: {.proof}
The partitions of $6$, recorded as
$(\text{largest part},\text{number of parts})$, are
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
6&(6,1)\\
5+1&(5,2)\\
4+2&(4,2)\\
4+1+1&(4,3)\\
3+3&(3,2)\\
3+2+1&(3,3)\\
3+1+1+1&(3,4)\\
2+2+2&(2,3)\\
2+2+1+1&(2,4)\\
2+1+1+1+1&(2,5)\\
1+1+1+1+1+1&(1,6).
\end{array}
$$
No two rows have the same ordered pair. Hence if two nilpotent $6\times6$
matrices have the same minimal polynomial and nullity, they have the same
Jordan block partition, hence the same Jordan form and are similar.
:::

<1>2. Dimension seven admits two distinct partitions with the same data.
::: {.proof}
Consider the nilpotent Jordan matrices
$$
N_1=J_3(0)\oplus J_3(0)\oplus J_1(0),
$$
and
$$
N_2=J_3(0)\oplus J_2(0)\oplus J_2(0).
$$
Both are $7\times7$, both have minimal polynomial $x^3$, and both have
nullity $3$ because each has three Jordan blocks. But their Jordan block
partitions, $3+3+1$ and $3+2+2$, are different. Therefore they are not
similar. This proves the asserted failure in dimension seven.
:::
:::
