::: {.exercise}
If $A$ is an $n \times n$ matrix over the field $F$ with characteristic polynomial
$$
f = (x - c_1)^{d_1} \cdots (x - c_k)^{d_k}
$$
what is the trace of $A$?
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §7.3 Exercise 7. Here $A$ is $n\times n$ over $F$ with characteristic polynomial $f=(x-c_{1})^{d_{1}}\cdots(x-c_{k})^{d_{k}}$, the $c_{i}$ distinct elements of $F$ and $d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}=n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #triangular}

$A$ is similar over $F$ to an upper triangular matrix whose diagonal carries $c_{i}$ exactly $d_{i}$ times, for each $i$.

::: pf-proof

The characteristic polynomial of $A$ splits into linear factors over $F$ by hypothesis; for a matrix whose characteristic polynomial is a product of linear factors, induction on $n$ produces an eigenvector, a complementary invariant subspace, and an upper triangular form, and the diagonal entries of that form are the roots of the characteristic polynomial with their multiplicities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #trace}

Similar matrices have equal trace, and the trace of an upper triangular matrix is the sum of its diagonal entries.

::: pf-proof

The trace is the sum of the diagonal entries; $\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}AP)=\operatorname{tr}(APP^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(A)$ because $\operatorname{tr}(XY)=\operatorname{tr}(YX)$, and for an upper triangular matrix the diagonal entries are unchanged by the definition of trace.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #formula}

$\operatorname{tr}(A)=d_{1}c_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}c_{k}$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#triangular){.pf-ref} and [](#trace){.pf-ref}, the trace of $A$ is the sum of the diagonal entries of the triangular form, in which $c_{i}$ occurs $d_{i}$ times.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coeff}

Equivalently, $\operatorname{tr}(A)$ is minus the coefficient of $x^{n-1}$ in $f$.

::: pf-proof

Expanding $\prod_{i}(x-c_{i})^{d_{i}}$, the coefficient of $x^{n-1}$ is $-\sum_{i}d_{i}c_{i}$, since the product of the roots counted with multiplicity is the sum of the $c_{i}$ each taken $d_{i}$ times.

:::
