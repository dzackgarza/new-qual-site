::: {.exercise}
Classify up to similarity all $3 \times 3$ complex matrices $A$ such that $A^3 = I$.
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
$$
Then
$$
x^3-1=(x-1)(x-\omega)(x-\omega^2)
$$
has three distinct roots. Since $A^3=I$, the minimal polynomial of $A$ divides
$x^3-1$. Hence it has no repeated root, so $A$ is diagonalizable [@HK71].
Its eigenvalues can only be $1,\omega,\omega^2$.

Therefore every such matrix is similar to exactly one matrix
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{diag}(I_a,\omega I_b,\omega^2 I_c),
\qquad a,b,c\ge0,\quad a+b+c=3.}
$$
Two such diagonal matrices are similar if and only if the three eigenvalue
multiplicities agree. Thus this parameterization is a complete and irredundant
classification. There are
$$
\binom{3+3-1}{3}=10
$$
similarity classes.
:::
