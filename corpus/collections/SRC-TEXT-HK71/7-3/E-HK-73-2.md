::: {.exercise}
Use the result of Exercise 1 and the Jordan form to prove the following.
Let $A$ and $B$ be $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$ which have the same characteristic polynomial
\[
f = (x - c_1)^{d_1} \cdots (x - c_k)^{d_k}
\]
and the same minimal polynomial.
If no $d_i$ is greater than 3, then $A$ and $B$ are similar.
:::

::: solution
For each eigenvalue $c_i$, let the exponent of $(x-c_i)$ in the common
minimal polynomial be $e_i$. In Jordan form, the $c_i$-primary component has
total dimension $d_i$, and its largest Jordan block has size $e_i$ [@HK71].

For $d_i\le3$, the pair $(d_i,e_i)$ determines the partition of $d_i$ into
Jordan block sizes uniquely:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
d_i,e_i&\text{block sizes}\\ \hline
1,1&1\\
2,1&1+1\\
2,2&2\\
3,1&1+1+1\\
3,2&2+1\\
3,3&3.
\end{array}
$$
Thus the common characteristic polynomial determines each total multiplicity
$d_i$, while the common minimal polynomial determines each maximal block size
$e_i$; together they determine every Jordan block for every eigenvalue.
Therefore $A$ and $B$ have identical Jordan forms up to block order, and hence
$$
\boxed{A\sim B.}
$$
This is precisely the dimension-at-most-three nilpotent classification from
Exercise 1 applied separately to the operators $A-c_iI$ and $B-c_iI$ on their
primary components.
:::
