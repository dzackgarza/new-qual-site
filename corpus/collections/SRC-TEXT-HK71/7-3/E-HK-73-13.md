::: {.exercise}
If $N$ is a $k \times k$ elementary nilpotent matrix, i.e., $N^k = 0$ but $N^{k-1} \neq 0$, show that $N^t$ is similar to $N$.
Now use the Jordan form to prove that every complex $n \times n$ matrix is similar to its transpose.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. An elementary nilpotent matrix is similar to its transpose.
::: proof
If $N$ is $k\times k$, nilpotent, and $N^{k-1}\ne0$, then its nilpotency
index is $k$. Hence its Jordan form consists of one block $J_k(0)$ [@HK71].
It therefore suffices to compare $J_k(0)$ with its transpose.

Let $R$ be the permutation matrix reversing the standard basis:
$$
Re_j=e_{k+1-j}.
$$
Then $R^{-1}=R$, and direct inspection gives
$$
R J_k(0)R=J_k(0)^t.
$$
Thus $J_k(0)^t\sim J_k(0)$, and hence $N^t\sim N$.
:::

<1>2. Every Jordan block is similar to its transpose.
::: proof
For any $c\in\mathbb C$,
$$
J_k(c)=cI+J_k(0).
$$
Using the same reversal matrix,
$$
R J_k(c)R
=cI+R J_k(0)R
=J_k(c)^t.
$$
So each Jordan block is similar to its transpose.
:::

<1>3. Apply the blockwise similarity to an arbitrary complex matrix.
::: proof
Let $A=PJP^{-1}$ be a Jordan form. Then
$$
A^t=(P^{-1})^tJ^tP^t,
$$
so $A^t$ is similar to $J^t$. By step <1>2, each block of $J^t$ is similar
to the corresponding block of $J$, hence their direct sums are similar:
$J^t\sim J$. Therefore
$$
A^t\sim J^t\sim J\sim A.
$$
Thus every complex square matrix is similar to its transpose.
:::
:::
