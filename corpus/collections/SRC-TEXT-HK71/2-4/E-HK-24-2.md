::: {.exercise}
Find the coordinate matrix of the vector $(1, 0, 1)$ in the basis of $\CC^3$ consisting of the vectors $(2i, 1, 0)$, $(2, -1, 1)$, $(0, 1+i, 1-i)$, in that order.
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and the three coordinates below were recomputed for this pass. Since the three given vectors form an ordered basis, the coordinate matrix is unique, so solving the linear system determines it.

The basis of $\CC^{3}$ is $\alpha_{1}=(2i,1,0)$, $\alpha_{2}=(2,-1,1)$, $\alpha_{3}=(0,1+i,1-i)$, and the vector is $\gamma=(1,0,1)$. Its coordinate matrix is the column $(a,b,c)$ with $\gamma=a\alpha_{1}+b\alpha_{2}+c\alpha_{3}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #system}
The coordinates satisfy $2ia+2b=1$, $a-b+(1+i)c=0$ and $b+(1-i)c=1$.

::: pf-proof
Compute the combination coordinatewise: the first coordinate of $a\alpha_{1}+b\alpha_{2}+c\alpha_{3}$ is $2ia+2b+0$, the second is $a-b+(1+i)c$, and the third is $0\cdot a+b+(1-i)c$. Setting each equal to the corresponding coordinate $1,0,1$ of $\gamma$ gives the three equations.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eliminate}
$b=1-(1-i)c$ and $a=1-2c$.

::: pf-proof
The third equation reads $b+(1-i)c=1$, so $b=1-(1-i)c$. Substituting in the second gives $a-\bigl(1-(1-i)c\bigr)+(1+i)c=0$, that is $a=1-(1-i)c-(1+i)c=1-2c$, since $(1-i)+(1+i)=2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #solve}
$c=\frac{3+i}{4}$, $a=-\frac{1+i}{2}$ and $b=\frac{i}{2}$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting the expressions of step [](#eliminate){.pf-ref} in the first equation,
$$
2i(1-2c)+2\bigl(1-(1-i)c\bigr)=1,
$$
that is $2i-4ic+2-2c+2ic=1$, so $2c(1+i)=1+2i$ and
$$
c=\frac{1+2i}{2(1+i)}=\frac{(1+2i)(1-i)}{2\cdot2}=\frac{3+i}{4}.
$$
Then $a=1-2c=1-\frac{3+i}{2}=-\frac{1+i}{2}$ and $b=1-(1-i)\frac{3+i}{4}=1-\frac{4-2i}{4}=\frac{i}{2}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The coordinate matrix of $(1,0,1)$ in the given ordered basis is $\begin{bmatrix}-\frac12-\frac{i}{2}\\ \frac{i}{2}\\ \frac34+\frac{i}{4}\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting the values of step [](#solve){.pf-ref} back into the three equations of step [](#system){.pf-ref}: the first coordinate is $2i\bigl(-\frac{1+i}{2}\bigr)+\frac{2i}{2}=-i(1+i)+i=-i+1+i=1$; the second is $-\frac{1+i}{2}-\frac{i}{2}+\frac{(1+i)(3+i)}{4}=-\frac{1+2i}{2}+\frac{2+4i}{4}=0$; the third is $\frac{i}{2}+\frac{(1-i)(3+i)}{4}=\frac{i}{2}+\frac{4-2i}{4}=1$. All three hold, so the vector $(a,b,c)$ found does satisfy the equation, and by uniqueness of coordinates in a basis it is the coordinate matrix.
:::
