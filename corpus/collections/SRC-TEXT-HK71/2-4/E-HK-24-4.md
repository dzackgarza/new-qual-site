::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be the subspace of $C^3$ spanned by $\alpha_1 = (1, 0, i)$ and $\alpha_2 = (1 + i, 1, -1)$.

(a) Show that $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ form a basis for $W$.

(b) Show that the vectors $\beta_1 = (1, 1, 0)$ and $\beta_2 = (1, i, 1 + i)$ are in $W$ and form another basis for $W$.

(c) Find the coordinates of each of $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ in the ordered basis $\{\beta_1, \beta_2\}$.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ form a basis of $W$.
::: proof
They span $W$ by definition. They are linearly independent because if
\[
a\alpha_1+b\alpha_2=0,
\]
the second coordinate gives $b=0$, and then the first coordinate gives $a=0$.
:::

<1>2. $\beta_1,\beta_2$ lie in $W$ and form another basis.
::: proof
Directly,
\[
\beta_1=-i\alpha_1+\alpha_2,
\]
and
\[
\beta_2=(2-i)\alpha_1+i\alpha_2.
\]
Thus both are in $W$. If $a\beta_1+b\beta_2=0$, then using their first two coordinates gives
\[
a+b=0,
\qquad
a+ib=0.
\]
Hence $(i-1)b=0$, so $b=0$ and then $a=0$. Thus they are independent. Since $W$ has dimension $2$, they form a basis.
:::

<1>3. The coordinates of $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ in the ordered basis $\{\beta_1,\beta_2\}$ are
\[
[\alpha_1]_{\beta}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\frac12-\frac{i}{2}\\
\frac12+\frac{i}{2}
\end{bmatrix},
\]
\[
[\alpha_2]_{\beta}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\frac32+\frac{i}{2}\\
-\frac12+\frac{i}{2}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
::: proof
Substitution of these coefficients into the displayed expressions for $\beta_1,\beta_2$ gives $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$, respectively; uniqueness follows because $\beta_1,\beta_2$ are a basis.
:::
:::
