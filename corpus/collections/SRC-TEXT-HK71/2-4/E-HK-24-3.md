::: {.exercise}
Let $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$ be the ordered basis for $\RR^3$ consisting of
$$
\alpha_1 = (1, 0, -1), \quad \alpha_2 = (1, 1, 1), \quad \alpha_3 = (1, 0, 0).
$$
What are the coordinates of the vector $(a, b, c)$ in the ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$?
:::


::: {.solution}

The computation is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §2.4 Exercise 3.

Let $\mathfrak{B}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})$ with $\alpha_{1}=(1,0,-1)$, $\alpha_{2}=(1,1,1)$, $\alpha_{3}=(1,0,0)$, an ordered basis of $\RR^{3}$. The coordinates of $v=(a,b,c)$ in $\mathfrak{B}$ are the unique scalars $x,y,z$ with $v=x\alpha_{1}+y\alpha_{2}+z\alpha_{3}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #combine}
$x\alpha_{1}+y\alpha_{2}+z\alpha_{3}=(x+y+z,\ y,\ -x+y)$.

::: pf-proof
By the definition of scalar multiplication and addition of triples, $x(1,0,-1)+y(1,1,1)+z(1,0,0)=(x+y+z,\ 0+y+0,\ -x+y+0)$, collecting first, second and third coordinates.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #system}
$v=x\alpha_{1}+y\alpha_{2}+z\alpha_{3}$ holds exactly when
$$
x+y+z=a,\qquad y=b,\qquad -x+y=c .
$$

::: pf-proof
Two triples are equal exactly when their coordinates agree; applying that to step [](#combine){.pf-ref} and $(a,b,c)$ gives the three equations. The coordinates $x,y,z$ exist and are unique because $\mathfrak{B}$ is a basis, so the system has exactly one solution.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #solve}
The system of step [](#system){.pf-ref} has the unique solution $y=b$, $x=b-c$, $z=a-2b+c$.

::: pf-proof
The second equation gives $y=b$ at once. The third then gives $-x+b=c$, that is $x=b-c$. The first then gives $z=a-x-y=a-(b-c)-b=a-2b+c$. Uniqueness is step [](#system){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #check}
$(b-c)\alpha_{1}+b\alpha_{2}+(a-2b+c)\alpha_{3}=(a,b,c)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#combine){.pf-ref} the combination has coordinates
$$
x+y+z=(b-c)+b+(a-2b+c)=a,\qquad y=b,\qquad -x+y=-(b-c)+b=c,
$$
so the resulting triple is $(a,b,c)$; this is the substitution check that the solved values do satisfy the original vector equation.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\bigl[(a,b,c)\bigr]_{\mathfrak{B}}=\begin{bmatrix}b-c\\ b\\ a-2b+c\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
By definition the coordinate matrix collects the coefficients of the unique expression of the vector in the ordered basis; steps [](#solve){.pf-ref} and [](#check){.pf-ref} give those coefficients.
:::
