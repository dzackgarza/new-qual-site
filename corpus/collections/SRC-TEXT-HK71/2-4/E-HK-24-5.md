::: {.exercise}
Let $\alpha = (x_1, x_2)$ and $\beta = (y_1, y_2)$ be vectors in $\RR^2$ such that
\[
x_1 y_1 + x_2 y_2 = 0, \quad x_1^2 + x_2^2 = y_1^2 + y_2^2 = 1.
\]
Prove that $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha, \beta\}$ is a basis for $\RR^2$.
Find the coordinates of the vector $(a, b)$ in the ordered basis $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha, \beta\}$.
(The conditions on $\alpha$ and $\beta$ say, geometrically, that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are perpendicular and each has length 1.)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Proof that $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha, \beta\}$ is a basis for $\mathbb{R}^2$:
<2>1. Endow $\mathbb{R}^2$ with the standard Euclidean inner product $\langle u, v \rangle = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2$.
The given conditions state that $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha, \beta\}$ is an orthonormal set:
\[
\langle \alpha, \alpha \rangle = 1, \qquad \langle \beta, \beta \rangle = 1, \qquad \langle \alpha, \beta \rangle = 0.
\]
<2>2. Suppose $c_1 \alpha + c_2 \beta = (0, 0)$ for scalars $c_1, c_2 \in \mathbb{R}$.
Taking the inner product with $\alpha$:
\[
0 = \langle (0, 0), \alpha \rangle = \langle c_1 \alpha + c_2 \beta, \alpha \rangle = c_1 \langle \alpha, \alpha \rangle + c_2 \langle \beta, \alpha \rangle = c_1(1) + c_2(0) = c_1.
\]
Similarly, taking the inner product with $\beta$:
\[
0 = \langle (0, 0), \beta \rangle = \langle c_1 \alpha + c_2 \beta, \beta \rangle = c_1 \langle \alpha, \beta \rangle + c_2 \langle \beta, \beta \rangle = c_1(0) + c_2(1) = c_2.
\]
Thus $c_1 = c_2 = 0$, so $\mathfrak{B}$ is linearly independent.
<2>3. Since $\dim(\mathbb{R}^2) = 2$ and $\mathfrak{B}$ consists of 2 linearly independent vectors, $\mathfrak{B}$ is a basis for $\mathbb{R}^2$.

<1>2. Coordinates of $(a, b)$ with respect to $\mathfrak{B}$:
<2>1. Let $v = (a, b) \in \mathbb{R}^2$, and write $v = c_1 \alpha + c_2 \beta$.
Taking the inner product of $v$ with $\alpha$:
\[
c_1 = \langle v, \alpha \rangle = a x_1 + b x_2.
\]
<2>2. Taking the inner product of $v$ with $\beta$:
\[
c_2 = \langle v, \beta \rangle = a y_1 + b y_2.
\]
<2>3. Thus $(a, b) = (a x_1 + b x_2)\alpha + (a y_1 + b y_2)\beta$, so the coordinate vector is:
\[
[(a, b)]_\mathfrak{B} = \begin{pmatrix} a x_1 + b x_2 \\ a y_1 + b y_2 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

<1>3. Conclusion:
$\mathfrak{B}$ is an orthonormal basis, and the coordinates of $(a, b)$ are $(a x_1 + b x_2, a y_1 + b y_2)$. Q.E.D.
:::
