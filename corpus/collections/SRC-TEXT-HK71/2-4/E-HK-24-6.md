::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the vector space over the complex numbers of all functions from $\RR$ into $C$, i.e., the space of all complex-valued functions on the real line.
Let $f_1(x) = 1$, $f_2(x) = e^{ix}$, $f_3(x) = e^{-ix}$.

(a) Prove that $f_1$, $f_2$, and $f_3$ are linearly independent.

(b) Let $g_1(x) = 1$, $g_2(x) = \cos x$, $g_3(x) = \sin x$.
Find an invertible $3 \times 3$ matrix $P$ such that
\[
g_j = \sum_{i=1}^{3} P_{ij} f_i.
\]
:::


::: solution
<1>1. The functions $f_1,f_2,f_3$ are linearly independent.
::: proof
Suppose
\[
a+b e^{ix}+c e^{-ix}=0
\]
for all real $x$. Evaluating at $x=0$ and $x=\pi$ gives
\[
a+b+c=0,
\qquad
a-b-c=0.
\]
Hence $a=0$ and $b+c=0$. Evaluating at $x=\pi/2$ then gives
\[
ib-ic=0,
\]
so $b=c$. Together with $b+c=0$, this yields $2b=0$, hence $b=c=0$. Thus the three functions are independent.
:::

<1>2. The required matrix is
\[
P=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&\frac12&-\frac{i}{2}\\
0&\frac12&\frac{i}{2}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
::: proof
Euler's formulas give
\[
1=f_1,
\qquad
\cos x=\frac12 f_2+\frac12 f_3,
\]
\[
\sin x=-\frac{i}{2}f_2+\frac{i}{2}f_3.
\]
These are exactly the column relations $g_j=\sum_iP_{ij}f_i$. Moreover
\[
\det P=\frac{i}{2}\ne0,
\]
so $P$ is invertible.
:::
:::
