::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the (real) vector space of all polynomial functions from $\RR$ into $\RR$ of degree 2 or less, i.e., the space of all functions $f$ of the form
$$
f(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2.
$$
Let $t$ be a fixed real number and define
$$
g_1(x) = 1, \quad g_2(x) = x + t, \quad g_3(x) = (x + t)^2.
$$
Prove that $\mathfrak{B} = \{g_1, g_2, g_3\}$ is a basis for $V$.
If
$$
f(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2,
$$
what are the coordinates of $f$ in this ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$?
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §2.4 Exercise 7. Here $V$ is the space of polynomial functions $f(x)=c_0+c_1x+c_2x^2$ of degree at most $2$, $t\in\RR$ is fixed, and $\mathfrak{B}=\theset{g_1,g_2,g_3}$ with $g_1(x)=1$, $g_2(x)=x+t$, $g_3(x)=(x+t)^2$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #expand}

For all $a,b,c$ one has $a g_1+b g_2+c g_3=f$ where $f(x)=(a+bt+ct^2)+(b+2ct)x+cx^2$.

::: pf-proof

Expand $a+b(x+t)+c(x+t)^2=a+bt+ct^2+(b+2ct)x+cx^2$ and collect by powers of $x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #span}

Every $f\in V$ is a linear combination of $g_1,g_2,g_3$.

::: pf-proof

Given $f(x)=c_0+c_1x+c_2x^2$, put $c\coloneqq c_2$, $b\coloneqq c_1-2tc_2$ and $a\coloneqq c_0-tc_1+t^2c_2$. Then $a+bt+ct^2=c_0$, $b+2ct=c_1$ and $c=c_2$, so step [](#expand){.pf-ref} gives $a g_1+b g_2+c g_3=f$. Thus $\mathfrak{B}$ spans $V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #unique}

The scalars $a,b,c$ of step [](#expand){.pf-ref} are determined by $f$.

::: pf-proof

The coefficient of $x^2$ is $c=c_2$, then the coefficient of $x$ gives $b=c_1-2c_2t$, and the constant term gives $a=c_0-bt-ct^2$; since a nonzero polynomial function has a nonzero coefficient, two triples giving the same $f$ agree.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis}

$\mathfrak{B}$ is a basis of $V$ and $[f]_{\mathfrak{B}}=(a,b,c)^t$ is the vector of step [](#span){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof

A spanning set of $V$ with three elements, $V$ having dimension $3$ (the monomials $1,x,x^2$ are a basis), is a basis: a spanning set of a finite-dimensional space that is not a basis has more elements than the dimension. Coordinates in an ordered basis are the unique scalars of step [](#unique){.pf-ref}, and here they are $a=c_0-tc_1+t^2c_2$, $b=c_1-2tc_2$, $c=c_2$.

:::
