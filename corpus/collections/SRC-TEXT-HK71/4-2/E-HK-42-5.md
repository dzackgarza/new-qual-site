::: {.exercise}
If $F$ is a field, show that the product of two non-zero elements of $F^{\infty}$ is non-zero.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise; the book's text is retained in the checkout as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §4.2 Exercise 5 on printed p. 123. The argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $F^{\infty}$ is the set of sequences $f=(f_0,f_1,f_2,\dots)$ over $F$ with the operations of termwise addition and scalar multiplication and the convolution product $(fg)_k=\sum_{i=0}^{k}f_ig_{k-i}$, and $0$ is the sequence all of whose terms are $0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #indices}
For nonzero $f$ and $g$ there are least indices $m$ and $n$ with $f_m\neq0$ and $g_n\neq0$.

::: pf-proof
$f\neq0$ means some $f_i\neq0$; the set of such $i$ is a nonempty subset of the well-ordered set $\{0,1,2,\dots\}$, hence has a least element $m$, and $f_i=0$ for $i<m$ by minimality. The same for $g$, with least index $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #convolution-term}
$(fg)_{m+n}=f_mg_n$.

::: pf-proof
By the convolution formula $(fg)_{m+n}=\sum_{i=0}^{m+n}f_ig_{m+n-i}$. If $i<m$ then $f_i=0$ by step [](#indices){.pf-ref}. If $i>m$ then $m+n-i<n$, so $g_{m+n-i}=0$ again by minimality of $n$. So every term of the sum vanishes except the term $i=m$, which is $f_mg_{m+n-m}=f_mg_n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nonzero}
$fg\neq0$.

::: pf-proof
$f_m\neq0$ and $g_n\neq0$, and $F$ is a field, so it has no zero divisors: $f_mg_n\neq0$. By step [](#convolution-term){.pf-ref} this is the $(m+n)$-th term of $fg$, which is therefore not the zero sequence.

:::
