::: {.exercise}
If $F$ is a field and $h$ is a polynomial over $F$ of degree $\geq 1$, show that the mapping $f \to f(h)$ is a one-one linear transformation of $F[x]$ into $F[x]$.
Show that this transformation is an isomorphism of $F[x]$ onto $F[x]$ if and only if $\deg h = 1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The map $\phi: F[x] \to F[x]$, $\phi(f) = f(h)$, is linear.
Proof: $(f + g)(h) = f(h) + g(h)$ and $(cf)(h) = c f(h)$ for $c \in F$.

<1>2. $\phi$ is injective.
<2>1. If $f \neq 0$ has degree $d$, then $f(h)$ has degree $d \cdot \deg h \ge d \ge 0$, so $f(h) \neq 0$.
Proof: the leading term of $f(h)$ is $a_d h^d$, whose degree is $d \cdot \deg h$ (since $\deg h \ge 1$), so $f(h)$ is nonzero.
<2>2. Hence $\ker \phi = 0$, so $\phi$ is injective.
Proof: <2>1.

<1>3. $\phi$ is surjective iff $\deg h = 1$.
<2>1. If $\deg h = 1$, then $h = ax + b$ with $a \neq 0$, and $\phi$ is surjective.
Proof: the inverse map is $g \mapsto g((x - b)/a)$, i.e. $\phi$ has inverse $\psi(g) = g(h^{-1})$ where $h^{-1} = (x-b)/a$; indeed $\phi(\psi(g)) = g(h(h^{-1})) = g(x) = g$.
<2>2. If $\deg h > 1$, then $\phi$ is not surjective.
Proof: the image of $\phi$ consists of polynomials $f(h)$ whose degree is a multiple of $\deg h$ (or $0$); in particular, the polynomial $x$ is not in the image (since $x = f(h)$ would require $\deg f \cdot \deg h = 1$, impossible when $\deg h > 1$).

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1, <1>2, and <1>3.
:::
