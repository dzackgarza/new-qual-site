::: {.exercise}
Let $S$ be a set of non-zero polynomials over a field $F$.
If no two elements of $S$ have the same degree, show that $S$ is an independent set in $F[x]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: in a finite linear relation among polynomials of pairwise distinct degrees, the term of largest degree cannot be cancelled.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #relation}

It suffices to show that any finite linear relation among distinct members of $S$ has all coefficients zero.

::: pf-proof

A subset of a vector space is independent when every finite linear relation among its elements has all coefficients zero, which is the definition of independence for an arbitrary (possibly infinite) set: a relation involves only finitely many vectors by definition. So it is enough to treat a relation $c_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}f_{r}=0$ with $f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in S$ distinct and $c_{i}\in F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #leading}

In such a relation, if $f_{k}$ has strictly larger degree than every other $f_{i}$, then $c_{k}=0$.

::: pf-proof

Let $d=\deg f_{k}$ and let $a_{k}\ne0$ be the coefficient of $x^{d}$ in $f_{k}$. For $i\ne k$ we have $\deg f_{i}<d$, so $f_{i}$ contributes nothing to the coefficient of $x^{d}$, and the coefficient of $x^{d}$ in $\sum_{i}c_{i}f_{i}$ is $c_{k}a_{k}$. That sum is the zero polynomial, whose coefficients all vanish, so $c_{k}a_{k}=0$; since $a_{k}\ne0$ and $F$ is a field, $c_{k}=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #induction}

Every coefficient $c_{i}$ in such a relation is zero.

::: pf-proof

Induction on $r$. For $r=0$ there is nothing to prove. For $r\ge1$, the degrees $\deg f_{1},\dots,\deg f_{r}$ are pairwise distinct, since $S$ has no two elements of the same degree and the $f_{i}$ are distinct elements of $S$; hence some $f_{k}$ has strictly the largest degree. By step [](#leading){.pf-ref} $c_{k}=0$, so the relation reduces to one among the remaining $r-1$ polynomials, still with pairwise distinct degrees, and the induction hypothesis gives $c_{i}=0$ for all $i\ne k$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$S$ is linearly independent in $F[x]$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#relation){.pf-ref} and [](#induction){.pf-ref}, every finite linear relation among elements of $S$ has all coefficients zero, so
$$
\boxed{S\ \text{is an independent set in } F[x].}
$$

:::
