::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^3$ defined by
$$
T(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (x_1,\; x_3,\; -2x_2 - x_3).
$$
Let $f$ be the polynomial over $\RR$ defined by $f = -x^3 + 2$.
Find $f(T)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and the matrices below were recomputed for this pass.

Relative to the standard ordered basis of $\RR^{3}$ the operator $T$ is represented by a matrix, and $f(T)$ is represented by $f$ evaluated at that matrix, $-[T]^{3}+2I$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #matrix}
$[T]=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&-2&-1\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
The $j$-th column of $[T]$ is the coordinate matrix of $T\epsilon_{j}$. Now $T(1,0,0)=(1,0,0)$, $T(0,1,0)=(0,0,-2)$ and $T(0,0,1)=(0,1,-1)$, which are the three columns displayed.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square}
$[T]^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&-2&-1\\0&2&-1\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Multiply $[T]$ by itself. The first row is the first row of $[T]$, namely $(1,0,0)$. The second row of the product is row $2$ of $[T]$ times $[T]$, which is row $3$ of $[T]$, namely $(0,-2,-1)$. The third row is row $3$ times $[T]$, that is $-2$ times row $2$ of $[T]$ minus row $3$ of $[T]$: $-2(0,0,1)-(0,-2,-1)=(0,2,-1)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cube}
$[T]^{3}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&2&-1\\0&2&3\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Multiply the matrix of step [](#square){.pf-ref} by $[T]$. The first row stays $(1,0,0)$. The second row is $(0,-2,-1)$ times $[T]$, that is $-2$ times row $2$ of $[T]$ minus row $3$ of $[T]$, giving $(0,2,-1)$. The third row is $(0,2,-1)$ times $[T]$, that is $2$ times row $2$ of $[T]$ minus row $3$ of $[T]$, giving $(0,2,2+1)=(0,2,3)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$f(T)=T$, since $-[T]^{3}+2I=[T]$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#cube){.pf-ref},
$$
-[T]^{3}+2I=-\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&2&-1\\0&2&3\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}2&0&0\\0&2&0\\0&0&2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&-2&-1\end{bmatrix},
$$
which is $[T]$ by step [](#matrix){.pf-ref}. So the matrix of $-T^{3}+2I$ equals the matrix of $T$, and two operators with the same matrix in a basis are equal; hence $f(T)=T$.
:::
