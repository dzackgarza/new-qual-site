::: {.exercise}
If $f$ and $g$ are independent polynomials over a field $F$ and $h$ is a non-zero polynomial over $F$, show that $fh$ and $gh$ are independent.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Let $f,g\in F[x]$ be linearly independent and let $h\in F[x]$ with $h\ne0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #combination}
If $a(fh)+b(gh)=0$ in $F[x]$ with $a,b\in F$, then $(af+bg)h=0$.

::: pf-proof
Rearranging the left side by commutativity and distributivity in the ring $F[x]$: $a(fh)+b(gh)=(af)h+(bg)h=(af+bg)h$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #domain}
$(af+bg)h=0$ with $h\ne0$ implies $af+bg=0$.

::: pf-proof
For a field $F$ the ring $F[x]$ is an integral domain. The degree of a product of nonzero polynomials is the sum of the degrees, computed from the leading coefficients, and the leading coefficient of such a product is a product of two nonzero elements of the field $F$, hence nonzero; so a product of nonzero polynomials in $F[x]$ is nonzero. Equivalently $F[x]$ has no zerodivisors, and from $(af+bg)h=0$ with $h\ne0$ it follows that $af+bg=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #independence}
$af+bg=0$ implies $a=b=0$.

::: pf-proof
This is the definition of linear independence of the two elements $f,g$ of the $F$-vector space $F[x]$, applied to the combination with coefficients $a,b$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$fh$ and $gh$ are linearly independent.

::: pf-proof
Let $a,b\in F$ with $a(fh)+b(gh)=0$. Steps [](#combination){.pf-ref}, [](#domain){.pf-ref} and [](#independence){.pf-ref}, in that order, give $a=b=0$, which is the independence of $fh$ and $gh$. No property of $f$ and $g$ beyond independence was used, and $h\ne0$ was used only in step [](#domain){.pf-ref}.
:::
