::: {.exercise}
If $a$ and $b$ are elements of a field $F$ and $a \neq 0$, show that the polynomials $1$, $ax + b$, $(ax + b)^2$, $(ax + b)^3$, $\ldots$ form a basis of $F[x]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §4.2 Exercise 7. Here $F$ is a field, $a\in F^{\times}$ and $b\in F$; $F[x]$ is the space of all polynomials over $F$, whose standard basis is $1,x,x^{2},\dots$, and the span of an infinite family is the set of finite linear combinations of its members.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #degree}

$(ax+b)^{k}$ has degree $k$ and leading coefficient $a^{k}\neq0$.

::: pf-proof

Induction on $k$: the case $k=0$ is the constant $1$; multiplying a polynomial of degree $k$ with leading coefficient $a^{k}$ by $ax+b$ gives degree $k+1$ with leading coefficient $a^{k}\cdot a=a^{k+1}$, and $a\neq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #indep}

Every finite subfamily of $1,ax+b,(ax+b)^{2},\dots$ is linearly independent.

::: pf-proof

In a relation $\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k}(ax+b)^{k}=0$, the term of largest $k$ with $c_{k}\neq0$ has degree $k$ by step [](#degree){.pf-ref}, while all lower terms have smaller degree; so the sum has that degree and cannot vanish, whence every $c_k=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #x}

$x$ lies in the span of the family.

::: pf-proof

$x=\frac{(ax+b)-b}{a}=a^{-1}(ax+b)-a^{-1}b\cdot1$, a combination of $1$ and $ax+b$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #powers}

$x^{n}$ lies in the span of the family for every $n\geq0$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#x){.pf-ref}, $x$ is a linear combination of the family, say $x=\sum_{k=0}^{1}c_{k}(ax+b)^{k}$; then $x^{n}=(\sum_k c_k(ax+b)^{k})^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}d_{k}(ax+b)^{k}$ for suitable $d_k\in F$, a finite combination of the family. The same is immediate from $x^{n}=a^{-n}((ax+b)-b)^{n}$ by the binomial theorem.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #span}

The family spans $F[x]$.

::: pf-proof

Every element of $F[x]$ is a finite sum $\sum_{n}\beta_{n}x^{n}$, so it lies in the span of the vectors $x^{n}$, each of which lies in the span of the family by step [](#powers){.pf-ref}; a span is a subspace and contains the span of any subset.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis}

$1,ax+b,(ax+b)^{2},\dots$ is a basis of $F[x]$.

::: pf-proof

It spans by step [](#span){.pf-ref} and every finite subfamily is independent by step [](#indep){.pf-ref}, which is the definition of linear independence for an infinite family; a linearly independent spanning family is a basis.

:::
