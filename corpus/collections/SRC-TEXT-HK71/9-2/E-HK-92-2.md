::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be the form on $\RR^2$ defined by
\[
f((x_1, x_2), (y_1, y_2)) = x_1y_1 + x_2y_2.
\]
Find the matrix of $f$ in each of the following bases:
\[
\{(1, 0), (0, 1)\}, \quad \{(1, -1), (1, 1)\}, \quad \{(1, 2), (3, 4)\}.
\]
:::

::: remark
The previous transcription swapped the second coordinate of the first vector
with the first coordinate of the second vector, producing a formula that was
not bilinear. The displayed statement restores the source's coordinate pairs.
:::

::: solution
For an ordered basis $\mathfrak B=(v_1,v_2)$, the matrix of $f$ is the Gram
matrix
$$
[f]_{\mathfrak B}
=\begin{pmatrix}
f(v_1,v_1)&f(v_1,v_2)\\
f(v_2,v_1)&f(v_2,v_2)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Here $f$ is the standard dot product on $\mathbb R^2$.

<1>1. Standard basis.
::: proof
For
$$
v_1=(1,0),\qquad v_2=(0,1),
$$
the basis is orthonormal. Hence
$$
\boxed{[f]_{(v_1,v_2)}=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
:::

<1>2. Basis $((1,-1),(1,1))$.
::: proof
Let
$$
v_1=(1,-1),\qquad v_2=(1,1).
$$
Then
$$
v_1\cdot v_1=2,
\qquad
v_2\cdot v_2=2,
\qquad
v_1\cdot v_2=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{[f]_{(v_1,v_2)}=
\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
:::

<1>3. Basis $((1,2),(3,4))$.
::: proof
Let
$$
v_1=(1,2),\qquad v_2=(3,4).
$$
Their pairwise dot products are
$$
v_1\cdot v_1=5,
\qquad
v_1\cdot v_2=11,
\qquad
v_2\cdot v_2=25.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{[f]_{(v_1,v_2)}=
\begin{pmatrix}5&11\\11&25\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
:::
:::
