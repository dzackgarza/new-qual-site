::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be a non-degenerate form (Exercises 6 and 7) on a finite-dimensional space $V$.
Let $L$ be a linear functional on $V$.
Show that there exists one and only one vector $\beta$ in $V$ such that $L(\alpha) = f(\alpha, \beta)$ for all $\alpha$.
:::

::: solution
Choose an ordered basis of $V$. Let $A$ be the matrix of $f$ in this basis,
so for coordinate columns $X,Y$,
$$
f(X,Y)=Y^*AX.
$$
Since $f$ is non-degenerate, Exercise 7 shows it is both left and right
non-degenerate. Equivalently, the square matrix $A$ is invertible.

Write the linear functional $L$ in coordinates as
$$
L(X)=\ell^tX
$$
for a unique coefficient column $\ell$.

<1>1. Existence.
::: proof
Set the row vector
$$
r=\ell^tA^{-1}.
$$
There is a unique column $Y$ such that
$$
Y^*=r.
$$
Let $\beta$ be the vector with coordinate column $Y$. Then for every
$\alpha$ with coordinate column $X$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha,\beta)
&=Y^*AX\\
&=rAX\\
&=\ell^tA^{-1}AX\\
&=\ell^tX\\
&=L(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus such a representing vector exists.
:::

<1>2. Uniqueness.
::: proof
Suppose $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ both represent $L$. Then for every $\alpha$,
$$
f(\alpha,\beta_1-\beta_2)=0.
$$
By right non-degeneracy of $f$,
$$
\beta_1-\beta_2=0.
$$
Hence $\beta_1=\beta_2$.

Therefore every linear functional has a unique representation
$$
\boxed{L(\alpha)=f(\alpha,\beta)}
$$
through the non-degenerate form $f$.
:::
:::
