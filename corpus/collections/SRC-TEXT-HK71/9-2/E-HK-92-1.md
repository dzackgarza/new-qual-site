::: {.exercise}
Which of the following functions $f$, defined on vectors $\alpha = (x_1, x_2)$ and $\beta = (y_1, y_2)$ in $C^2$, are (sesqui-linear) forms on $C^2$?

(a) $f(\alpha, \beta) = 1$.

(b) $f(\alpha, \beta) = (x_1 - \bar{y}_1)^2 + x_2\bar{y}_2$.

(c) $f(\alpha, \beta) = (x_1 + \overline{y}_1)^2 - (x_1 - \overline{y}_1)^2$.

(d) $f(\alpha, \beta) = x_1\overline{y}_2 - \overline{x}_2 y_1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A sesquilinear form on $\mathbb C^2$ must be linear in $\alpha$ and
conjugate-linear in $\beta$.

<1>1. Part (a) is not a sesquilinear form.
::: {.proof}
The constant function
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=1
$$
does not satisfy $f(0,\beta)=0$, which every linear function of the first
variable must satisfy.
:::

<1>2. Part (b) is not a sesquilinear form.
::: {.proof}
Expanding gives
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)
=x_1^2-2x_1\overline{y}_1+\overline{y}_1^{\,2}
+x_2\overline{y}_2.
$$
The terms $x_1^2$ and $\overline{y}_1^{\,2}$ violate linearity in the first
variable and conjugate-linearity in the second, respectively.
:::

<1>3. Part (c) is a sesquilinear form.
::: {.proof}
Using $(a+b)^2-(a-b)^2=4ab$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha,\beta)
&=(x_1+\overline y_1)^2-(x_1-\overline y_1)^2\\
&=4x_1\overline y_1.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is linear in $\alpha$ and conjugate-linear in $\beta$. Hence it is a
sesquilinear form.
:::

<1>4. Part (d) is not a sesquilinear form.
::: {.proof}
The first term
$$
x_1\overline y_2
$$
has the correct sesquilinearity, but the second term
$$
-\overline x_2y_1
$$
is conjugate-linear in the first variable and linear in the second. For
example, scaling $\alpha$ by $i$ changes this second term by $-i$ rather than
by $i$. Thus the whole expression is not linear in the first variable.
:::

Therefore the only sesquilinear form in the list is
$$
\boxed{\text{(c)}}.
$$
:::
