::: {.exercise}
Call the form $f$ (left) non-degenerate if 0 is the only vector $\alpha$ such that $f(\alpha, \beta) = 0$ for all $\beta$.
Let $f$ be a form on an inner product space $V$.
Prove that $f$ is non-degenerate if and only if the associated linear operator $T_f$ (Theorem 1) is non-singular.
:::

::: {.solution}
By definition of the associated operator,
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=(T_f\alpha\mid\beta)
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta\in V$.

<1>1. The left radical of $f$ is exactly $\ker T_f$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose first that $\alpha\in\ker T_f$. Then $T_f\alpha=0$, so
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)
=(T_f\alpha\mid\beta)
=0
$$
for every $\beta$.

Conversely, suppose
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=0
$$
for every $\beta$. Then
$$
(T_f\alpha\mid\beta)=0
$$
for every $\beta$. Taking $\beta=T_f\alpha$ gives
$$
\|T_f\alpha\|^2=0,
$$
hence $T_f\alpha=0$. Thus
$$
\{\alpha:f(\alpha,\beta)=0\text{ for all }\beta\}
=\ker T_f.
$$
:::

<1>2. Non-degeneracy is equivalent to nonsingularity of $T_f$.
::: {.proof}
The form $f$ is left non-degenerate exactly when its left radical is
$\{0\}$. By step <1>1 this is equivalent to
$$
\ker T_f=\{0\},
$$
so $T_f$ is injective. Since $V$ is finite-dimensional, an injective linear
operator $V\to V$ is invertible. Therefore
$$
\boxed{f\text{ is non-degenerate}\iff T_f\text{ is nonsingular}.}
$$
:::
:::
