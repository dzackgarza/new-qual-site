::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a complex vector space and let $f$ be a (sesqui-linear) form on $V$ which is symmetric: $f(\alpha, \beta) = f(\beta, \alpha)$.
What is $f$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Definition of sesquilinear form:
<2>1. By convention (Hoffman–Kunze), a sesquilinear form $f: V \times V \to \mathbb{C}$ on a complex vector space $V$ is linear in the first argument and conjugate-linear in the second argument:
\[
f(c\alpha, \beta) = c f(\alpha, \beta), \quad f(\alpha, c\beta) = \overline{c} f(\alpha, \beta) \quad \text{for all } c \in \mathbb{C}, \, \alpha, \beta \in V.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of sesquilinear form.
:::

<1>2. Combine symmetry with scalar multiplication:
<2>1. Assume $f$ is symmetric: $f(\alpha, \beta) = f(\beta, \alpha)$ for all $\alpha, \beta \in V$.
::: {.proof}
hypothesis.
:::
<2>2. For any $c \in \mathbb{C}$ and any $\alpha, \beta \in V$, compute $f(c\alpha, \beta)$ in two ways:
- By linearity in the first argument:
\[
f(c\alpha, \beta) = c f(\alpha, \beta).
\]
- By symmetry and conjugate-linearity in the second argument:
\[
f(c\alpha, \beta) = f(\beta, c\alpha) = \overline{c} f(\beta, \alpha) = \overline{c} f(\alpha, \beta).
\]
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <2>1.
:::
<2>3. Equating both expressions gives:
\[
(c - \overline{c}) f(\alpha, \beta) = 0 \quad \text{for all } c \in \mathbb{C}, \, \alpha, \beta \in V.
\]
::: {.proof}
subtraction of equal quantities.
:::

<1>3. Deduce that $f$ is identically zero:
<2>1. Choose $c = i \in \mathbb{C}$.
Then $c - \overline{c} = i - (-i) = 2i \neq 0$.
::: {.proof}
properties of the imaginary unit $i$.
:::
<2>2. Substituting $c = i$ into <2>3 yields:
\[
2i f(\alpha, \beta) = 0 \implies f(\alpha, \beta) = 0.
\]
Since this holds for all $\alpha, \beta \in V$, $f \equiv 0$ is the zero form.
::: {.proof}
division by $2i \neq 0$.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
$f$ is the identically zero form ($f = 0$). Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and <1>3.
:::
:::
