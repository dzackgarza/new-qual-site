::: {.exercise}
Let
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ -i & 2 \end{bmatrix}
\]
and let $g$ be the form (on the space of $2 \times 1$ complex matrices) defined by $g(X, Y) = Y^*AX$.
Is $g$ an inner product?
:::

::: {.solution}
Yes. The form $g$ is an inner product.

<1>1. The matrix $A$ is Hermitian.
::: {.proof}
We have
$$
A^*
=\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\-i&2\end{pmatrix}
=A.
$$
Therefore
$$
g(Y,X)=X^*AY
=\overline{Y^*A^*X}
=\overline{g(X,Y)},
$$
so $g$ is Hermitian. Sesquilinearity follows directly from the matrix formula
$g(X,Y)=Y^*AX$.
:::

<1>2. The form is positive definite.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
X=\binom{z_1}{z_2}.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(X,X)
&=X^*AX\\
&=|z_1|^2+2|z_2|^2
+i\overline{z_1}z_2-i\overline{z_2}z_1\\
&=|z_1+iz_2|^2+|z_2|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is nonnegative. If it is zero, then $z_2=0$ and
$z_1+iz_2=z_1=0$, so $X=0$. Hence
$$
g(X,X)>0
$$
for every nonzero $X$.

Thus $g$ is a positive Hermitian sesquilinear form, i.e. an inner product.
:::
:::
