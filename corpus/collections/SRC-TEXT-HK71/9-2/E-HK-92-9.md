::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be a non-degenerate form on a finite-dimensional space $V$.
Show that each linear operator $S$ has an 'adjoint relative to $f$,' i.e., an operator $S'$ such that $f(S\alpha, \beta) = f(\alpha, S'\beta)$ for all $\alpha, \beta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix a linear operator $S$ on $V$.

<1>1. For each $\beta$, construct a unique vector $S'\beta$.
::: {.proof}
For fixed $\beta\in V$, define
$$
L_\beta(\alpha)=f(S\alpha,\beta).
$$
Because $S$ is linear and $f$ is linear in its first variable, $L_\beta$ is a
linear functional on $V$. By Exercise 8, since $f$ is non-degenerate, there is
a unique vector, which we denote by $S'\beta$, such that
$$
L_\beta(\alpha)=f(\alpha,S'\beta)
$$
for every $\alpha$. Therefore
$$
f(S\alpha,\beta)=f(\alpha,S'\beta)
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$.
:::

<1>2. The assignment $\beta\mapsto S'\beta$ is linear.
::: {.proof}
Let $c$ be a scalar and let $\beta,\gamma\in V$. Since a sesquilinear form is
conjugate-linear in its second variable,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(S\alpha,c\beta+\gamma)
&=\overline c\,f(S\alpha,\beta)+f(S\alpha,\gamma)\\
&=\overline c\,f(\alpha,S'\beta)+f(\alpha,S'\gamma)\\
&=f(\alpha,cS'\beta+S'\gamma).
\end{aligned}
$$
But by the defining property of $S'$ applied to $c\beta+\gamma$,
$$
f(S\alpha,c\beta+\gamma)=f(\alpha,S'(c\beta+\gamma)).
$$
Thus
$$
f\bigl(\alpha,S'(c\beta+\gamma)-cS'\beta-S'\gamma\bigr)=0
$$
for every $\alpha$. Right non-degeneracy implies
$$
S'(c\beta+\gamma)=cS'\beta+S'\gamma.
$$
Hence $S'$ is a linear operator.
:::

Thus every linear operator $S$ has a relative adjoint $S'$ satisfying
$$
\boxed{f(S\alpha,\beta)=f(\alpha,S'\beta)}
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta\in V$.
:::
