::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be a self-adjoint $n \times n$ matrix.
Prove that there is a real number $c$ such that the matrix $cI + A$ is positive.
:::

::: {.solution}
Because $A$ is self-adjoint, the spectral theorem gives a unitary matrix $U$
and real eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$ such that
$$
U^*AU=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n).
$$
Let
$$
m=\min_i\lambda_i
$$
and choose any real number $c>-m$; for example,
$$
c=1-m.
$$
Then every number
$$
c+\lambda_i>0.
$$
Therefore
$$
U^*(cI+A)U
=\operatorname{diag}(c+\lambda_1,\ldots,c+\lambda_n)
$$
is a positive diagonal matrix. Unitary conjugation preserves positivity, so
$$
\boxed{cI+A\text{ is positive}.}
$$
:::
