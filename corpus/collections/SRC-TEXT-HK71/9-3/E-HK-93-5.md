::: {.exercise}
Let
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \frac{1}{2} \\ \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{3} \end{bmatrix}.
$$

(a) Show that $A$ is positive.

(b) Let $V$ be the space of $2 \times 1$ real matrices, with the inner product $(X \mid Y) = Y^tAX$.
Find an orthonormal basis for $V$, by applying the Gram-Schmidt process to the basis $\{X_1, X_2\}$ defined by
$$
X_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad X_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

(c) Find an invertible $2 \times 2$ real matrix $P$ such that $A = P^tP$.
:::

No retained solution set covers this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §9.3 Exercise 5 on printed p. 330. The argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The matrix $A$ is positive.

::: pf-proof

The matrix is symmetric. Its leading principal minors are
$$
\Delta_1=1>0
$$
and
$$
\Delta_2
=\det A
=\frac13-\frac14
=\frac1{12}>0.
$$
By Sylvester's criterion, $A$ is positive definite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Apply Gram--Schmidt to $X_1,X_2$.

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\norm{X_1}^2=X_1^tAX_1=1,
$$
the first orthonormal vector is
$$
E_1=X_1=\binom10.
$$
Also
$$
(X_2\mid E_1)=E_1^tAX_2=\frac12.
$$
Thus
$$
U_2=X_2-\frac12E_1
=\binom{-1/2}{1}.
$$
A direct calculation gives
$$
\norm{U_2}^2=U_2^tAU_2=\frac1{12}.
$$
Hence
$$
E_2=\frac{U_2}{\norm{U_2}}
=\binom{-\sqrt3}{2\sqrt3}.
$$
Therefore an orthonormal basis is
$$
\boxed{
\left\{
\binom10,
\binom{-\sqrt3}{2\sqrt3}
\right\}.}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Construct $P$ with $A=P^tP$.

::: pf-proof

Let $Q$ be the matrix whose columns are the orthonormal basis from step [](#s2){.pf-ref}:
$$
Q=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-\sqrt3\\
0&2\sqrt3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The orthonormality relation for the $A$-inner product is
$$
Q^tAQ=I.
$$
Thus
$$
A=(Q^{-1})^tQ^{-1}.
$$
Now
$$
Q^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/2\\
0&1/(2\sqrt3)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Therefore one valid choice is
$$
\boxed{
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/2\\
0&1/(2\sqrt3)
\end{pmatrix},}
$$
and direct multiplication gives
$$
P^tP=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/2\\
1/2&1/3
\end{pmatrix}=A.
$$

:::
