::: {.exercise}
Prove that the product of two positive linear operators is positive if and only if they commute.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $S$ and $T$ be positive operators.

<1>1. If $ST$ is positive, then $S$ and $T$ commute.
::: {.proof}
Since $S$ and $T$ are positive, they are self-adjoint. If $ST$ is positive,
then $ST$ is also self-adjoint. Therefore
$$
ST=(ST)^*=T^*S^*=TS.
$$
Hence $S$ and $T$ commute.
:::

<1>2. If $S$ and $T$ commute, then $ST$ is positive.
::: {.proof}
Because $S$ and $T$ are commuting self-adjoint operators, they can be
simultaneously diagonalized by an orthonormal basis. Thus in some orthonormal
basis,
$$
[S]=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_n),
\qquad
[T]=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_n),
$$
with every $s_i>0$ and every $t_i>0$. Hence
$$
[ST]=\operatorname{diag}(s_1t_1,\ldots,s_nt_n),
$$
whose diagonal entries are all positive. Therefore $ST$ is positive.
:::

Thus
$$
\boxed{ST\text{ is positive}\iff ST=TS.}
$$
:::
