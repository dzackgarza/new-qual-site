::: {.exercise}
Let $S$ and $T$ be positive operators.
Prove that every characteristic value of $ST$ is positive.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A positive operator has an invertible positive square root.
::: {.proof}
Since $S$ is positive, the spectral theorem gives an orthonormal basis in
which $S$ is diagonal with strictly positive diagonal entries
$s_1,\ldots,s_n$. Define
$$
R=S^{1/2}
$$
by replacing each $s_i$ with its positive square root $\sqrt{s_i}$. Then
$R$ is positive, invertible, and
$$
R^2=S.
$$
:::

<1>2. The operator $ST$ is similar to the positive operator $RTR$.
::: {.proof}
Using $S=R^2$ and the invertibility of $R$,
$$
R^{-1}(ST)R
=R^{-1}R^2TR
=RTR.
$$
Thus $ST$ and $RTR$ have the same characteristic values.

The operator $RTR$ is self-adjoint because $R$ and $T$ are self-adjoint:
$$
(RTR)^*=R^*T^*R^*=RTR.
$$
Moreover, for every nonzero $v$,
$$
(RTRv\mid v)
=(TRv\mid Rv)>0,
$$
since $Rv\ne0$ and $T$ is positive. Hence $RTR$ is positive.
:::

<1>3. Every characteristic value of $ST$ is positive.
::: {.proof}
A positive self-adjoint operator has only positive characteristic values: if
$Aw=\lambda w$ with $w\ne0$, then
$$
\lambda\|w\|^2=(Aw\mid w)>0,
$$
so $\lambda>0$. Applying this to $A=RTR$ and using the similarity from step
<1>2 yields
$$
\boxed{\text{every characteristic value of $ST$ is positive}.}
$$
:::
:::
