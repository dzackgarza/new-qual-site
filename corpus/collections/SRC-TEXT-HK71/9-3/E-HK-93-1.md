::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be $C^2$, with the standard inner product.
For which vectors $\alpha$ in $V$ is there a positive linear operator $T$ such that $\alpha = T\epsilon_1$?
:::

::: solution
Write
$$
\alpha=\binom ab\in\mathbb C^2.
$$
We claim that such a positive operator exists exactly when
$$
\boxed{a\in\mathbb R_{>0},\qquad b\in\mathbb C\text{ arbitrary}.}
$$

<1>1. Positivity forces the first coordinate to be positive real.
::: proof
Suppose $T$ is positive and $T\epsilon_1=\alpha$. Then
$$
a=(T\epsilon_1\mid\epsilon_1).
$$
Since $T$ is positive and $\epsilon_1\ne0$, this scalar is strictly positive.
In particular $a$ is real and $a>0$.
:::

<1>2. Every vector with positive real first coordinate occurs.
::: proof
Assume $a>0$ is real and $b\in\mathbb C$. Choose a real number $d$ satisfying
$$
d>\frac{|b|^2}{a},
$$
and define
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}
a&\overline b\\
b&d
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
This matrix is Hermitian, its first leading principal minor is $a>0$, and
$$
\det A=ad-|b|^2>0.
$$
Hence $A$ is positive definite. Let $T$ be the operator with standard matrix
$A$. Then $T$ is positive and its first column is
$$
A\epsilon_1=\binom ab=\alpha.
$$
Thus every such $\alpha$ is realized.
:::
:::
