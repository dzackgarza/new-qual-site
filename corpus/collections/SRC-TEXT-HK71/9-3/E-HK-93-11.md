::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space and $E$ the orthogonal projection of $V$ onto some subspace.

(a) Prove that, for any positive number $c$, the operator $cI + E$ is positive.

(b) Express in terms of $E$ a self-adjoint linear operator $T$ such that $T^2 = I + E$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For every $c>0$, the operator $cI+E$ is positive.
::: {.proof}
Because $E$ is an orthogonal projection,
$$
E^*=E.
$$
Hence $cI+E$ is self-adjoint. Also, for every nonzero $x$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
((cI+E)x\mid x)
&=c\|x\|^2+(Ex\mid x)\\
&=c\|x\|^2+\|Ex\|^2\\
&>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $cI+E$ is positive.
:::

<1>2. Construct a self-adjoint square root of $I+E$.
::: {.proof}
Seek a polynomial in $E$ of the form
$$
T=I+bE
$$
with real $b$. Since $E^2=E$,
$$
T^2
=I+(2b+b^2)E.
$$
We want $T^2=I+E$, so $b$ must satisfy
$$
b^2+2b=1.
$$
Taking
$$
b=\sqrt2-1
$$
gives the desired coefficient. Therefore
$$
\boxed{T=I+(\sqrt2-1)E}.
$$
Since $E^*=E$ and the coefficient is real, $T$ is self-adjoint. Finally,
$$
T^2
=I+\bigl(2(\sqrt2-1)+(\sqrt2-1)^2\bigr)E
=I+E.
$$
:::
