::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the space of $n \times 1$ matrices over $C$, with the inner product $(X \mid Y) = Y^*GX$ (where $G$ is an $n \times n$ matrix such that this is an inner product).
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix and $T$ the linear operator $T(X) = AX$.
Find $T^*$.
If $Y$ is a fixed element of $V$, find the element $Z$ of $V$ which determines the linear functional $X \to Y^*X$.
In other words, find $Z$ such that $Y^*X = (X \mid Z)$ for all $X$ in $V$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Since
$$
(X\mid Y)=Y^*GX
$$
is an inner product, $G$ is Hermitian positive definite and in particular
invertible.

<1>1. Compute the adjoint of $T(X)=AX$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $T^*$ is represented by a matrix $B$. The defining identity is
$$
(AX\mid Y)=(X\mid BY)
$$
for all $X,Y$. The two sides are
$$
Y^*GAX
$$
and
$$
(BY)^*GX=Y^*B^*GX.
$$
Thus
$$
B^*G=GA.
$$
Multiplying on the right by $G^{-1}$ gives
$$
B^*=GAG^{-1}.
$$
Taking adjoints and using $G^*=G$ yields
$$
\boxed{B=G^{-1}A^*G.}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{T^*(X)=G^{-1}A^*GX.}
$$
:::

<1>2. Represent the functional $X\mapsto Y^*X$.
::: {.proof}
We seek $Z$ such that
$$
Y^*X=(X\mid Z)=Z^*GX
$$
for every $X$. Hence
$$
Z^*G=Y^*.
$$
Taking adjoints gives
$$
GZ=Y,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{Z=G^{-1}Y.}
$$
Indeed,
$$
(X\mid G^{-1}Y)
=(G^{-1}Y)^*GX
=Y^*X.
$$
:::
:::
