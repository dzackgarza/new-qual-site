::: {.exercise}
Does $((x_1, x_2) \mid (y_1, y_2)) = x_1\bar{y}_1 + 2x_2\bar{y}_1 + 2x_1\bar{y}_2 + x_2\bar{y}_2$ define an inner product on $C^2$?
:::

::: {.solution}
No. The form can be written as
$$
(X\mid Y)=Y^*AX,
\qquad
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
2&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The matrix $A$ is Hermitian, so the form has the required conjugate symmetry.
However it is not positive definite.

Take
$$
X=\binom1{-1}.
$$
Then
$$
AX=\binom{-1}{1},
$$
and therefore
$$
(X\mid X)
=X^*AX
=(1,-1)\binom{-1}{1}
=-2<0.
$$
An inner product must satisfy $(X\mid X)>0$ for every nonzero $X$. Hence the
displayed form does **not** define an inner product on $\mathbb C^2$.
:::
