::: {.exercise}
Give an example of an $n \times n$ matrix which has all its principal minors positive, but which is not a positive matrix.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §9.3 Exercise 7. Here a positive matrix is a self-adjoint matrix all of whose eigenvalues are positive, and the principal minors of $A$ are the determinants of the square submatrices cut out by the same set of rows and columns.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #minors}

$A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$ has all principal minors positive.

::: pf-proof

The principal $1\times1$ minors are the diagonal entries $1$ and $1$, and the principal $2\times2$ minor is $\det A=1\cdot1-1\cdot0=1$; all are positive.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #notself}

$A$ is not self-adjoint.

::: pf-proof

$A^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\neq A$, since the $(1,2)$ entry is $1$ in $A$ and $0$ in $A^{*}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$A$ is not positive.

::: pf-proof

A positive matrix is self-adjoint by definition, and $A$ is not by step [](#notself){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

Positivity of all principal minors does not imply positivity of the matrix.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#minors){.pf-ref} and [](#done){.pf-ref} give an $n=2$ example, and padding it with an identity block gives an example in every size $n\geq2$.

:::
