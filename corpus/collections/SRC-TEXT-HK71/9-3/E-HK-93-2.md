::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be $\RR^2$, with the standard inner product.
If $\theta$ is a real number, let $T$ be the linear operator 'rotation through $\theta$,'
\[
T_\theta(x_1, x_2) = (x_1\cos\theta - x_2\sin\theta,\; x_1\sin\theta + x_2\cos\theta).
\]
For which values of $\theta$ is $T_\theta$ a positive operator?
:::

::: solution
The matrix of $T_\theta$ in the standard orthonormal basis is
$$
R_\theta=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

<1>1. Positivity forces $R_\theta$ to be symmetric.
::: proof
A positive operator is self-adjoint. Over $\mathbb R$ in an orthonormal basis,
self-adjointness is equivalent to symmetry of the matrix. Thus
$$
R_\theta^t=R_\theta.
$$
Comparing the off-diagonal entries gives
$$
-\sin\theta=\sin\theta,
$$
hence
$$
\sin\theta=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\theta\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad\pi\pmod{2\pi}.
$$
:::

<1>2. Only the identity rotation is positive.
::: proof
If $\theta\equiv0\pmod{2\pi}$, then $T_\theta=I$, which is positive.

If $\theta\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}$, then $T_\theta=-I$, and for every nonzero
$x$,
$$
(T_\theta x\mid x)=-\|x\|^2<0.
$$
So this case is not positive.

Hence
$$
\boxed{T_\theta\text{ is positive}\iff \theta\in2\pi\mathbb Z.}
$$
:::
:::
