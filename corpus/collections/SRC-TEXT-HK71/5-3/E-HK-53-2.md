::: {.exercise}
Prove that the determinant of the Vandermonde matrix
$$
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & a & a^2 \\ 1 & b & b^2 \\ 1 & c & c^2 \end{bmatrix}
$$
is $(b - a)(c - a)(c - b)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and it was checked for this pass against the values $a=0$, $b=1$, $c=2$, where both sides equal $2$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #rowops}
Subtracting the first row from the second and third rows does not change the determinant, so
$$
\det\begin{bmatrix}1&a&a^{2}\\1&b&b^{2}\\1&c&c^{2}\end{bmatrix}=\det\begin{bmatrix}1&a&a^{2}\\0&b-a&b^{2}-a^{2}\\0&c-a&c^{2}-a^{2}\end{bmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Adding a multiple of one row to another leaves the determinant unchanged, and subtracting the first row from the second means adding $-1$ times the first row to it, and likewise for the third. The entries of the new second row are $1-1=0$, $b-a$ and $b^{2}-a^{2}$, and those of the third are $0$, $c-a$ and $c^{2}-a^{2}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #factor}
That determinant equals $(b-a)(c-a)\det\begin{bmatrix}1&a&a^{2}\\0&1&b+a\\0&1&c+a\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
In the second row every entry is a multiple of $b-a$, since $b^{2}-a^{2}=(b-a)(b+a)$, so the factor $b-a$ may be taken out of that row; in the third row the factor $c-a$ extracts by $c^{2}-a^{2}=(c-a)(c+a)$. A common factor of one row can be pulled out of a determinant, and doing so twice gives the displayed product.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The determinant is $(b-a)(c-a)(c-b)$.

::: pf-proof
Expanding the last matrix along its first column, in which only the entry $1$ is nonzero, leaves the $2\times2$ determinant of the rows and columns $2,3$:
$$
(b-a)(c-a)\det\begin{bmatrix}1&b+a\\1&c+a\end{bmatrix}=(b-a)(c-a)\bigl((c+a)-(b+a)\bigr)=(b-a)(c-a)(c-b),
$$
since $ad-bc$ for this $2\times2$ matrix is $1\cdot(c+a)-(b+a)\cdot1$. That is the asserted value of the Vandermonde determinant.
:::
