::: {.exercise}
Prove that the determinant of the Vandermonde matrix
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & a & a^2 \\ 1 & b & b^2 \\ 1 & c & c^2 \end{bmatrix}
\]
is $(b - a)(c - a)(c - b)$.
:::

::: solution
Subtract the first row from the second and third rows. This does not change the determinant:
\[
\det\begin{bmatrix}1&a&a^2\\1&b&b^2\\1&c&c^2\end{bmatrix}
=
\det\begin{bmatrix}1&a&a^2\\0&b-a&b^2-a^2\\0&c-a&c^2-a^2\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Factor $b-a$ from the second row and $c-a$ from the third, using
$b^2-a^2=(b-a)(b+a)$ and $c^2-a^2=(c-a)(c+a)$:
\[
=(b-a)(c-a)
\det\begin{bmatrix}1&a&a^2\\0&1&b+a\\0&1&c+a\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Expanding along the first column leaves
\[
(b-a)(c-a)\det\begin{bmatrix}1&b+a\\1&c+a\end{bmatrix}
=(b-a)(c-a)(c-b).
\]
:::
