::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be a $3 \times 3$ matrix over the field of complex numbers.
We form the matrix $xI - A$ with polynomial entries, the $i$, $j$ entry of this matrix being the polynomial $\delta_{ij}x - A_{ij}$.
If $f = \det(xI - A)$, show that $f$ is a monic polynomial of degree 3. If we write
$$
f = (x - c_1)(x - c_2)(x - c_3)
$$
with complex numbers $c_1$, $c_2$, and $c_3$, prove that
$$
c_1 + c_2 + c_3 = \operatorname{trace}(A) \quad \text{and} \quad c_1 c_2 c_3 = \det A.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §5.3 Exercise 8.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #monic}
$f(x)=\det(xI-A)$ is monic of degree $3$.

::: pf-proof
Expanding the determinant over the six permutations of three letters, the identity permutation contributes $(x-A_{11})(x-A_{22})(x-A_{33})$, whose leading term is $x^3$, and every other permutation includes at least two off-diagonal entries $A_{ij}$ with $i\ne j$, hence contributes a polynomial of degree at most $1$ (each off-diagonal entry is a constant, and a permutation with exactly one off-diagonal entry is impossible for $n=3$). The degree three coefficient is therefore $1$: $f$ is monic of degree $3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #xtwo}
The coefficient of $x^2$ in $f$ is $-\operatorname{trace}(A)$.

::: pf-proof
Only the identity permutation reaches degree $2$, since the others have degree at most $1$ by step [](#monic){.pf-ref}. In $(x-A_{11})(x-A_{22})(x-A_{33})$ the coefficient of $x^2$ is $-(A_{11}+A_{22}+A_{33})=-\operatorname{trace}(A)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rootssum}
$c_1+c_2+c_3=\operatorname{trace}(A)$.

::: pf-proof
$(x-c_1)(x-c_2)(x-c_3)=x^3-(c_1+c_2+c_3)x^2+\cdots$, so the coefficient of $x^2$ in $f$ is also $-(c_1+c_2+c_3)$; two expressions for the same polynomial have equal coefficients, so $-(c_1+c_2+c_3)=-\operatorname{trace}(A)$ by step [](#xtwo){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #constant}
$f(0)=-\det A$ and $f(0)=-c_1c_2c_3$.

::: pf-proof
$f(0)=\det(0\cdot I-A)=\det(-A)=(-1)^3\det A=-\det A$ because $A$ is $3\times3$ and the determinant is multiplicative in scalar multiples. From the factorisation, $f(0)=(-c_1)(-c_2)(-c_3)=-c_1c_2c_3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$c_1c_2c_3=\det A$.

::: pf-proof
Comparing the two expressions for $f(0)$ in step [](#constant){.pf-ref} gives $-c_1c_2c_3=-\det A$, hence $c_1c_2c_3=\det A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$f$ is monic of degree $3$ with $c_1+c_2+c_3=\operatorname{trace}(A)$ and $c_1c_2c_3=\det A$.

::: pf-proof
Collecting steps [](#monic){.pf-ref}, [](#rootssum){.pf-ref} and [](#constant){.pf-ref}:
$$
\boxed{f=\det(xI-A)\text{ is monic of degree }3,\quad \sum_i c_i=\operatorname{trace}(A),\quad \prod_i c_i=\det A.}
$$
The roots exist because the coefficients are complex and $f$ has degree $3$; nothing is claimed about the middle elementary symmetric function, which is not asked for.
:::
