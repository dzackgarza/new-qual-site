::: {.exercise}
If $K$ is a commutative ring with identity and $A$ is the matrix over $K$ given by
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & a & b \\ -a & 0 & c \\ -b & -c & 0 \end{bmatrix}
\]
show that $\det A = 0$.
:::

::: solution
Because the determinant is unchanged by transpose,
\[
\det A=\det(A^t).
\]
Here $A^t=-A$. By multilinearity in the three rows,
\[
\det(-A)=(-1)^3\det A=-\det A.
\]
Thus $\det A=-\det A$, so $2\det A=0$. This argument alone is insufficient in characteristic $2$, so compute without division: expansion along the first row gives
\[
\det A=-a\begin{vmatrix}-a&c\\-b&0\end{vmatrix}
+b\begin{vmatrix}-a&0\\-b&-c\end{vmatrix}
=-abc+abc=0.
\]
Hence $\det A=0$ over every commutative ring $K$.
:::
