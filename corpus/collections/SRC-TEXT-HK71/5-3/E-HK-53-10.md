::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field, $n$ a positive integer, and $S$ the set of $n \times n$ matrices over $F$.
Let $V$ be the vector space of all functions from $S$ into $F$.
Let $W$ be the set of alternating $n$-linear functions on $S$.
Prove that $W$ is a subspace of $V$.
What is the dimension of $W$?
:::

::: {.solution}
If $D,E\in W$ and $a,b\in F$, then $aD+bE$ is $n$-linear because linear combinations preserve linearity in each row variable. If two row arguments are equal, both $D$ and $E$ vanish, so
\[
(aD+bE)(A)=aD(A)+bE(A)=0.
\]
Thus $aD+bE$ is alternating. The zero function is alternating and $n$-linear, so $W$ is a subspace of $V$.

Let $D\in W$, and let the rows of $A$ be $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$. Writing each row as
\[
\alpha_i=\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}e_j
\]
and expanding by multilinearity gives a sum indexed by $j_1,\ldots,j_n$. Every term with two equal $j_i$ vanishes by alternation. Hence only permutations survive, and moving the rows $(e_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,e_{\sigma(n)})$ back to $(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ gives
\[
D(A)=D(I)\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
\prod_{i=1}^n A_{i,\sigma(i)}
=D(I)\det A.
\]
Thus every element of $W$ is a scalar multiple of the determinant. Conversely, the determinant is a nonzero alternating $n$-linear function, since $\det I=1$. Therefore
\[
W=F\det,
\qquad \dim W=1.
\]
:::
