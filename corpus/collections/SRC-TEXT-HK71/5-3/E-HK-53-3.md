::: {.exercise}
List explicitly the six permutations of degree 3, state which are odd and which are even, and use this to give the complete formula (5-15) for the determinant of a $3 \times 3$ matrix.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §5.3 Exercise 3, and the expansion used is the permutation formula $\det A=\sum_{\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{3}A_{i,\sigma(i)}$ of that section.

Write each permutation of $\{1,2,3\}$ as the sequence $\bigl(\sigma(1),\sigma(2),\sigma(3)\bigr)$, and let an inversion be a pair $i<j$ with $\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)$; then $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=(-1)^{\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)}$, where $\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)$ is the number of inversions.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #six}
The permutations of $\{1,2,3\}$ are exactly
$$
\operatorname{id}=(1,2,3),\quad (123)=(2,3,1),\quad (132)=(3,1,2),\quad (12)=(2,1,3),\quad (13)=(3,2,1),\quad (23)=(1,3,2).
$$

::: pf-proof
There are $3!=6$ permutations in all, and the six listed are distinct: the first three fix no point except that the identity fixes all three, $(123)$ and $(132)$ move all three, and the last three are the three choices of the two points that a transposition interchanges while fixing the third. Six distinct permutations of a six-element set exhaust it.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #parity}
The identity and the two $3$-cycles are even; the three transpositions are odd.

::: pf-proof
Count inversions in the sequences of step [](#six){.pf-ref}. The identity $(1,2,3)$ has none. $(2,3,1)$ has the inversions $(2,1)$ and $(3,1)$, so $\operatorname{inv}=2$. $(3,1,2)$ has the inversions $(3,1)$ and $(3,2)$, so $\operatorname{inv}=2$. $(2,1,3)$ has the single inversion $(2,1)$, $(3,2,1)$ has the three inversions $(3,2),(3,1),(2,1)$, and $(1,3,2)$ has the single inversion $(3,2)$. Hence the signs are $+1,+1,+1$ for the first three and $-1,-1,-1$ for the last three; equivalently each $3$-cycle is a product of two transpositions, $(123)=(12)(23)$ and $(132)=(23)(12)$, while the last three are transpositions themselves.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #terms-even}
The three even permutations contribute $+A_{11}A_{22}A_{33}+A_{12}A_{23}A_{31}+A_{13}A_{21}A_{32}$.

::: pf-proof
For each permutation the summand is $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i}A_{i,\sigma(i)}$, the product running over $i=1,2,3$ with the second index $\sigma(i)$ read from the sequence: for the identity, $A_{11}A_{22}A_{33}$; for $(123)$, with $\sigma(1)=2$, $\sigma(2)=3$, $\sigma(3)=1$, the product $A_{12}A_{23}A_{31}$; for $(132)$, the product $A_{13}A_{21}A_{32}$. By step [](#parity){.pf-ref} all three have sign $+1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #terms-odd}
The three transpositions contribute $-A_{12}A_{21}A_{33}-A_{13}A_{22}A_{31}-A_{11}A_{23}A_{32}$.

::: pf-proof
Reading the second indices from the sequences of step [](#six){.pf-ref}: $(12)$ gives $A_{12}A_{21}A_{33}$, $(13)$ gives $A_{13}A_{22}A_{31}$, and $(23)$ gives $A_{11}A_{23}A_{32}$. By step [](#parity){.pf-ref} each has sign $-1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\det A=\sum_{\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)A_{1,\sigma(1)}A_{2,\sigma(2)}A_{3,\sigma(3)}$ equals
$$
A_{11}A_{22}A_{33}+A_{12}A_{23}A_{31}+A_{13}A_{21}A_{32}-A_{12}A_{21}A_{33}-A_{13}A_{22}A_{31}-A_{11}A_{23}A_{32}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The determinant of a $3\times3$ matrix is the sum of the six summands, one for each permutation of $\{1,2,3\}$; steps [](#six){.pf-ref} lists them, [](#terms-even){.pf-ref} and [](#terms-odd){.pf-ref} compute the two groups. That is the complete formula.
:::
