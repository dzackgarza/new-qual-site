::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be a $2 \times 2$ matrix over a field.
Prove that $\det(I + A) = 1 + \det A$ if and only if $\operatorname{trace}(A) = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: for $2\times2$ matrices $\det(I+A)$ is computed in terms of the trace and the determinant.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #expansion}

For $A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}$, $\det(I+A)=1+\operatorname{trace}(A)+\det A$.

::: pf-proof

$I+A=\begin{bmatrix}1+a&b\\c&1+d\end{bmatrix}$, whose determinant is the product of the diagonal entries minus the product of the off-diagonal ones:
$$
\det(I+A)=(1+a)(1+d)-bc=1+(a+d)+(ad-bc).
$$
Now $a+d=\operatorname{trace}(A)$ and $ad-bc=\det A$ by the definitions of the trace and the determinant of a $2\times2$ matrix.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #forward}

If $\det(I+A)=1+\det A$ then $\operatorname{trace}(A)=0$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#expansion){.pf-ref}, $\det(I+A)-1-\det A=\operatorname{trace}(A)$. The hypothesis says the left side is $0$, so the trace is $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #backward}

If $\operatorname{trace}(A)=0$ then $\det(I+A)=1+\det A$.

::: pf-proof

Substituting $\operatorname{trace}(A)=0$ into step [](#expansion){.pf-ref},
$$
\det(I+A)=1+0+\det A=1+\det A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$\det(I+A)=1+\det A$ if and only if $\operatorname{trace}(A)=0$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#forward){.pf-ref} and [](#backward){.pf-ref} give the two implications over any field $F$, since no division is used:
$$
\boxed{\det(I+A)=1+\det A\iff\operatorname{trace}(A)=0\quad(A\in M_{2}(F)).}
$$

:::
