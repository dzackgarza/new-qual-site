::: {.exercise}
If $\sigma$ is a permutation of degree $n$ and $A$ is an $n \times n$ matrix over the field $F$ with row vectors $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$, let $\sigma(A)$ denote the $n \times n$ matrix with row vectors $\alpha_{\sigma 1}, \ldots, \alpha_{\sigma n}$.

(a) Prove that $\sigma(AB) = \sigma(A)B$, and in particular that $\sigma(A) = \sigma(I)A$.

(b) If $T$ is the linear operator of Exercise 9, prove that the matrix of $T$ in the standard ordered basis is $\sigma(I)$.

(c) Is $\sigma^{-1}(I)$ the inverse matrix of $\sigma(I)$?

(d) Is it true that $\sigma(A)$ is similar to $A$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let the rows of $A$ be $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$.

For (a), the $i$-th row of $\sigma(AB)$ is the $\sigma(i)$-th row of $AB$, namely $\alpha_{\sigma(i)}B$. This is also the $i$-th row of $\sigma(A)B$. Hence
\[
\sigma(AB)=\sigma(A)B.
\]
Taking $A=I$ in the identity $\sigma(IA)=\sigma(I)A$ gives
\[
\sigma(A)=\sigma(I)A.
\]

For (b), the operator of Exercise 9 sends
\[
(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\longmapsto(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}).
\]
For a column vector $x$, the $i$-th coordinate of $\sigma(I)x$ is precisely $x_{\sigma(i)}$. Therefore its standard matrix is $\sigma(I)$.

For (c), apply (a):
\[
\sigma^{-1}(\sigma(I))=\sigma^{-1}(I)\sigma(I).
\]
The left side is $I$, since applying the inverse row permutation undoes $\sigma$. Thus $\sigma^{-1}(I)\sigma(I)=I$; similarly the reverse product is $I$. Hence $\sigma^{-1}(I)=\sigma(I)^{-1}$.

For (d), not in general. Take $A=I$ and any nonidentity permutation $\sigma$. Then
\[
\sigma(A)=\sigma(I)\ne I.
\]
But every matrix similar to $I$ equals $I$, since $P^{-1}IP=I$. Hence $\sigma(A)$ need not be similar to $A$.
:::
