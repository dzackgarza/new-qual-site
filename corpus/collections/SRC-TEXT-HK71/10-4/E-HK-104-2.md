::: {.exercise}
Suppose $M$ belongs to $O(n, C)$ and that $M'$ is similar to $M$.
Does $M'$ also belong to $O(n, C)$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The answer is **no**: a matrix similar to a complex orthogonal matrix is not necessarily orthogonal.
::: {.proof}
statement of claim.
:::

<1>2. Counterexample in $n = 2$: <2>1. Consider the matrix:
\[
M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Compute $M^t M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I_2$.
Thus $M \in O(2, \mathbb{C})$.
::: {.proof}
definition of the complex orthogonal group $O(n, \mathbb{C}) = \{A \in M_n(\mathbb{C}) : A^t A = I_n\}$.
:::
<2>2. Choose the invertible matrix $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \in \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})$, with inverse $P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
::: {.proof}
$\det(P) = 1 \neq 0$.
:::
<2>3. Define $M' = P M P^{-1}$, which is similar to $M$ by construction:
\[
M' = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
matrix multiplication.
:::
<2>4. Compute $(M')^t M'$:
\[
(M')^t M' = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 5 \end{pmatrix} \neq I_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
matrix multiplication.
:::
<2>5. Since $(M')^t M' \neq I_2$, $M' \notin O(2, \mathbb{C})$.
::: {.proof}
failure of the orthogonality condition.
:::

<1>3. Conclusion: $M'$ does not necessarily belong to $O(n, \mathbb{C})$.
Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2.
:::
:::
