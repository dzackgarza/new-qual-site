::: {.exercise}
Let
$$
y_j = \sum_{k=1}^{n} M_{jk}x_k
$$
where $M$ is a member of $O(n, \CC)$.
Show that
$$
\sum_j y_j^2 = \sum_j x_j^2.
$$
:::


::: {.solution}

The computation is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed, and the statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §10.4 Exercise 3.

Let $M\in O(n,\CC)$, that is $M^{t}M=I$ with the plain transpose and no complex conjugation, and let $y=Mx$ mean $y_{j}=\sum_{k}M_{jk}x_{k}$ for $j=1,\dots,n$. Write $x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{t}$ and $y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})^{t}$ as column vectors.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #matrix-form}
The equations $y_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}M_{jk}x_{k}$ say exactly that $y=Mx$.

::: pf-proof
The $j$-th entry of the matrix product $Mx$ is by definition $\sum_{k}M_{jk}x_{k}$, so the system of $n$ equations is the single equation $y=Mx$ written coordinate by coordinate.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #square}
$\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{2}=y^{t}y$.

::: pf-proof
For column vectors $u,v$ the product $u^{t}v$ is the $1\times1$ matrix $\sum_{j}u_{j}v_{j}$, by the definition of matrix multiplication; taking $u=v=y$ gives $\sum_{j}y_{j}^{2}$, and a $1\times1$ matrix is identified with its entry.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #transpose}
$(Mx)^{t}=x^{t}M^{t}$.

::: pf-proof
The $(i,j)$ entry of $(Mx)^{t}$ is the $(j,i)$ entry of $Mx$, namely $\sum_{k}M_{jk}x_{k}$; the $(i,j)$ entry of $x^{t}M^{t}$ is $\sum_{k}(x^{t})_{ik}(M^{t})_{kj}=\sum_{k}x_{k}M_{jk}$, the same. Hence the two agree in every entry.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #compute}
$y^{t}y=x^{t}M^{t}Mx=x^{t}x$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#matrix-form){.pf-ref} and [](#transpose){.pf-ref}, $y^{t}y=(Mx)^{t}(Mx)=x^{t}M^{t}Mx$. The hypothesis $M\in O(n,\CC)$ is $M^{t}M=I$, so $x^{t}M^{t}Mx=x^{t}Ix=x^{t}x$, using associativity of matrix multiplication.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\sum_{j}y_{j}^{2}=\sum_{j}x_{j}^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
$\sum_{j}y_{j}^{2}=y^{t}y=x^{t}x=\sum_{j}x_{j}^{2}$ by steps [](#square){.pf-ref} and [](#compute){.pf-ref}. Over $\CC$ no conjugation enters, which is exactly why the complex orthogonal group, and not the unitary group, is the group that preserves this quadratic form.
:::
