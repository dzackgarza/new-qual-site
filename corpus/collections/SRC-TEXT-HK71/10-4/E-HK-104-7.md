::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the space of all $n \times 1$ matrices over $\CC$ and $f$ the bilinear form on $V$ given by $f(X, Y) = X^tY$.
Let $M$ belong to $O(n, \CC)$.
What is the matrix of $f$ in the basis of $V$ consisting of the columns $M_1, M_2, \ldots, M_n$ of $M$?
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §10.4 Exercise 7. Here $V=\CC^{n}$ is the space of $n\times1$ matrices, $f(X,Y)=X^{t}Y$ is the bilinear form (transpose, no conjugation), and $O(n,\CC)=\theset{M\mid M^{t}M=I}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis}

$M\in O(n,\CC)$ is invertible, so its columns $M_{1},\dots,M_{n}$ form a basis of $V$.

::: pf-proof

$M^{t}M=I$ exhibits $M^{t}$ as a left inverse and hence $M$ as invertible: a square matrix with a one-sided inverse is invertible and the inverse is two-sided. The columns of an invertible $n\times n$ matrix are linearly independent, and $n$ of them in an $n$-dimensional space form a basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #entry}

The $(j,k)$ entry of the matrix of $f$ in the basis $\mathfrak{B}=(M_{1},\dots,M_{n})$ is $f(M_{j},M_{k})=M_{j}^{t}M_{k}$.

::: pf-proof

The matrix of a bilinear form in a basis has $(j,k)$ entry $f$ evaluated at the $j$-th and $k$-th basis vectors, and $f(X,Y)=X^{t}Y$ by definition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #delta}

$M_{j}^{t}M_{k}=\delta_{jk}$.

::: pf-proof

The $(j,k)$ entry of $M^{t}M$ is the product of the $j$-th row of $M^{t}$, which is $M_{j}^{t}$, with the $k$-th column of $M$, which is $M_{k}$; and $M^{t}M=I$ has entries $\delta_{jk}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

The matrix of $f$ in $\mathfrak{B}$ is $I_{n}$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#entry){.pf-ref} and [](#delta){.pf-ref} its entries are $\delta_{jk}$, which are the entries of the identity matrix.

:::
