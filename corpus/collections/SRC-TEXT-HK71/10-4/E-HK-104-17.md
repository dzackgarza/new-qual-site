::: {.exercise}
Find all bilinear forms on the space of $n \times 1$ matrices over $\RR$ which are invariant under $O(n, R)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A bilinear form on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is given by a matrix $B$: $f(x, y) = x^t B y$.
::: {.proof}
bilinear forms correspond to matrices.
:::

<1>2. Invariance under $O(n)$ means $f(Ax, Ay) = f(x, y)$ for all $A \in O(n)$, i.e. $(Ax)^t B (Ay) = x^t B y$ for all $A \in O(n)$.
::: {.proof}
definition of invariance.
:::

<1>3. Hence $A^t B A = B$ for all $A \in O(n)$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2.
:::

<1>4. Taking $A$ to be a reflection (or any $A \in O(n)$), this forces $B$ to commute with every $A \in O(n)$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 (since $A^t = A^{-1}$ for $A \in O(n)$, we get $A^{-1} B A = B$, i.e. $AB = BA$).
:::

<1>5. The only matrices commuting with all of $O(n)$ are the scalar matrices $cI$.
::: {.proof}
$O(n)$ acts irreducibly on $\mathbb{R}^n$ (for $n \ge 2$), so by Schur's lemma the only endomorphisms commuting with the action are scalars.
:::

<1>6. Hence $B = cI$ for some $c \in \mathbb{R}$.
::: {.proof}
<1>4 and <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Therefore the invariant bilinear forms are exactly $f(x, y) = c\,x^t y = c\langle x, y \rangle$, the scalar multiples of the standard inner product.
::: {.proof}
<1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>7.
:::
:::
