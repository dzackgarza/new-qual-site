::: {.exercise}
Let $M$ be a member of the complex orthogonal group, $O(n, C)$.
Show that $M^t$, $\overline{M}$, and $M^* = \overline{M}^t$ also belong to $O(n, C)$.
:::


::: {.solution}
For $M\in O(n,\mathbb C)$,
$$
M^tM=I.
$$

<1>1. The transpose $M^t$ is orthogonal.
::: {.proof}
The equation $M^tM=I$ shows that $M$ is invertible and
$$
M^{-1}=M^t.
$$
Hence also
$$
MM^t=I.
$$
Therefore
$$
(M^t)^tM^t=MM^t=I,
$$
so
$$
M^t\in O(n,\mathbb C).
$$
:::

<1>2. The entrywise conjugate $\overline M$ is orthogonal.
::: {.proof}
Take complex conjugates of the defining equation:
$$
\overline{M^tM}=\overline I.
$$
Because conjugation commutes with transpose and multiplication,
$$
(\overline M)^t\overline M=I.
$$
Thus
$$
\overline M\in O(n,\mathbb C).
$$
:::

<1>3. The adjoint $M^*=\overline M^t$ is orthogonal.
::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $\overline M$ belongs to the orthogonal group. Applying step
<1>1 to $\overline M$ shows that
$$
\overline M^t=M^*
$$
also lies in $O(n,\mathbb C)$.
:::

Hence
$$
\boxed{M^t,\ \overline M,\ M^*\in O(n,\mathbb C).}
$$
:::
