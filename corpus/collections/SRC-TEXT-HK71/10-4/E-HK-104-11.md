::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of $\CC$, $V$ a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, and $f$ a non-singular bilinear form on $V$.
If $T$ is a linear operator on $V$ preserving $f$, prove that $\det T = \pm 1$.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise and no text version of Hoffman and Kunze in the checkout; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $F$ is a subfield of $\CC$, $V$ is finite dimensional over $F$, $f$ is a non-singular bilinear form on $V$ (so its matrix in any basis is invertible), and $T$ preserves $f$, that is $f(T\alpha,T\beta)=f(\alpha,\beta)$ for all $\alpha,\beta\in V$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #matrix-form}
For any ordered basis with matrix $A$ of $f$ and matrix $P$ of $T$, preservation of $f$ is $P^{t}AP=A$, and $\det A\neq0$.

::: pf-proof
Writing $\alpha,\beta$ as coordinate columns $u,w$, we have $f(\alpha,\beta)=u^{t}Aw$ and $f(T\alpha,T\beta)=(Pu)^{t}A(Pw)=u^{t}(P^{t}AP)w$. The two agree for all $u,w$ exactly when $P^{t}AP=A$: if they agree then $u^{t}(P^{t}AP-A)w=0$ for all $u,w$, and choosing $u,w$ to be standard basis vectors makes every entry of the difference $0$. Finally $\det A\neq0$ because $f$ is non-singular.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #determinants}
$(\det P)^{2}\det A=\det A$.

::: pf-proof
Taking determinants in $P^{t}AP=A$ and using multiplicativity and $\det(P^{t})=\det P$,
$$
\det(P^{t})\det A\det P=(\det P)^{2}\det A=\det A.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #squares}
$(\det P)^{2}=1$.

::: pf-proof
$\det A\neq0$ by step [](#matrix-form){.pf-ref}, so it may be cancelled in step [](#determinants){.pf-ref} to give $(\det P)^{2}=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pm-one}
$\det T=\det P=\pm1$.

::: pf-proof
$(\det P)^{2}-1=0$, that is $(\det P-1)(\det P+1)=0$. Since $F\subseteq\CC$, $F$ is a field and hence an integral domain, so one factor is $0$: $\det P=1$ or $\det P=-1$. The determinant of $T$ is by definition the determinant of its matrix in any basis, so $\det T=\pm1$.

:::
