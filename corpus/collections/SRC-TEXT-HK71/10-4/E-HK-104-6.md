::: {.exercise}
Find a matrix in $O(3, \CC)$ whose first row is $(2i, 2i, 3)$.
:::


::: {.solution}

The computation is the card's own. The convention matters and is stated first: in Hoffman--Kunze $O(n,F)$ is defined by the transpose, $A^{t}A=I$, so the rows must be orthonormal for the bilinear form $\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i}$ on $F^{n}$, with *no* complex conjugation. Under that form $(2i)^{2}+(2i)^{2}+3^{2}=1$, which is what makes the exercise possible; the Hermitian convention would give $17$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #criterion}
$M\in O(3,\CC)$ if and only if $M^{t}M=I$, and this holds if and only if the rows of $M$ are pairwise orthogonal and of bilinear norm $1$.

::: pf-proof
$O(3,\CC)$ is defined by $A^{t}A=I$, whose $(i,j)$ entry is the bilinear pairing $\langle r_{i},r_{j}\rangle$ of the $i$-th and $j$-th rows. So the matrix equation is exactly the statement that $\langle r_{i},r_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #first-two}
With $r_{1}=(2i,2i,3)$ and $r_{2}=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1,0)$, one has $\langle r_{1},r_{1}\rangle=\langle r_{2},r_{2}\rangle=1$ and $\langle r_{1},r_{2}\rangle=0$.

::: pf-proof
$\langle r_{1},r_{1}\rangle=(2i)^{2}+(2i)^{2}+3^{2}=-4-4+9=1$; $\langle r_{2},r_{2}\rangle=\frac12(1+1+0)=1$; and $\langle r_{1},r_{2}\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\big(2i\cdot1+2i\cdot(-1)+3\cdot0\big)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #third}
$r_{3}=\big(3/\sqrt2,\ 3/\sqrt2,\ -2\sqrt2\,i\big)$ satisfies $\langle r_{3},r_{3}\rangle=1$ and $\langle r_{1},r_{3}\rangle=\langle r_{2},r_{3}\rangle=0$.

::: pf-proof
Taking the first two entries of $r_{3}$ equal, say $(a,a,z)$, orthogonality to $r_{2}$ holds automatically because $r_{2}$ has entries $(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2$ and $a-a=0$, and orthogonality to $r_{1}$ reads $2ia+2ia+3z=4ia+3z=0$. Choosing $a=3/\sqrt2$ gives $z=-4ia/3=-4i\cdot(3/\sqrt2)/3=-2\sqrt2\,i$, as claimed. Then
$$
\langle r_{3},r_{3}\rangle=\tfrac92+\tfrac92+(-2\sqrt2\,i)^{2}=9-8=1,\qquad \langle r_{1},r_{3}\rangle=2i\tfrac{3}{\sqrt2}+2i\tfrac{3}{\sqrt2}+3(-2\sqrt2\,i)=\tfrac{12i}{\sqrt2}-6\sqrt2\,i=0,
$$
and $\langle r_{2},r_{3}\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\big(\tfrac{3}{\sqrt2}-\tfrac{3}{\sqrt2}\big)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #orthogonal}
The matrix $M$ with these rows satisfies $MM^{t}=I$, hence $M^{t}M=I$ and $M\in O(3,\CC)$.

::: pf-proof
$MM^{t}=I$ is step [](#criterion){.pf-ref} applied to steps [](#first-two){.pf-ref} and [](#third){.pf-ref}. Then $M$ is invertible with $M^{-1}=M^{t}$, since a right inverse of a square matrix is a two-sided inverse; multiplying $MM^{t}=I$ on the left by $M^{-1}$ gives $M^{t}=M^{-1}$, and hence $M^{t}M=M^{-1}M=I$, that is $M\in O(3,\CC)$ with first row the required $(2i,2i,3)$:
$$
\boxed{M=\begin{pmatrix}2i&2i&3\\ \frac1{\sqrt2}&-\frac1{\sqrt2}&0\\ \frac3{\sqrt2}&\frac3{\sqrt2}&-2\sqrt2\,i\end{pmatrix}\in O(3,\CC).}
$$
:::
