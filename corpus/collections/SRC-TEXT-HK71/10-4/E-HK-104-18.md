::: {.exercise}
Find all bilinear forms on the space of $n \times 1$ matrices over $C$ which are invariant under $O(n, C)$.
:::


::: solution
Every bilinear form on $V=\mathbb C^n$ has a unique representing matrix
$A\in M_n(\mathbb C)$ such that
$$
f(X,Y)=X^tAY.
$$
By Exercise 9, $f$ is invariant under $O(n,\mathbb C)$ if and only if
$$
AM=MA
$$
for every $M\in O(n,\mathbb C)$. We therefore determine this commutant.

<1>1. A matrix commuting with every orthogonal sign matrix is diagonal.
::: proof
For each $k$, let
$$
D_k=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1,1,\ldots,1),
$$
with the $-1$ in the $k$th position. Since
$$
D_k^tD_k=I,
$$
we have $D_k\in O(n,\mathbb C)$.

Suppose $A=(a_{ij})$ commutes with every $D_k$. For $i\ne j$, choose
$k=i$. Comparing the $(i,j)$ entries of
$$
AD_i=D_iA
$$
gives
$$
a_{ij}= -a_{ij},
$$
up to the same conclusion after tracking the two sign factors explicitly;
thus
$$
2a_{ij}=0.
$$
Over $\mathbb C$, this implies $a_{ij}=0$. Hence $A$ is diagonal.
:::

<1>2. A diagonal matrix commuting with every orthogonal permutation matrix is scalar.
::: proof
Every permutation matrix $P$ satisfies
$$
P^tP=I,
$$
so $P\in O(n,\mathbb C)$. Write
$$
A=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n).
$$
For any transposition matrix exchanging the $i$th and $j$th coordinate,
commutation
$$
AP=PA
$$
forces
$$
a_i=a_j.
$$
Since $i,j$ are arbitrary, all diagonal entries are equal. Therefore
$$
A=cI
$$
for some $c\in\mathbb C$.
:::

<1>3. Scalar multiples of the standard form are invariant.
::: proof
If $A=cI$, then for $M\in O(n,\mathbb C)$,
$$
M^tAM=cM^tM=cI=A.
$$
Hence
$$
f(X,Y)=cX^tY
$$
is invariant under the whole orthogonal group.
:::

Thus the invariant bilinear forms are exactly
$$
\boxed{f_c(X,Y)=cX^tY\qquad(c\in\mathbb C).}
$$
:::
