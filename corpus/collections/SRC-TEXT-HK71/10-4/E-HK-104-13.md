::: {.exercise}
Let $g$ be a non-singular bilinear form on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Suppose $T$ is an invertible linear operator on $V$ and that $f$ is the bilinear form on $V$ given by $f(\alpha, \beta) = g(\alpha, T\beta)$.
If $U$ is a linear operator on $V$, find necessary and sufficient conditions for $U$ to preserve $f$.
:::


::: {.solution}
Because $g$ is non-singular, every linear operator $U$ has a unique adjoint
$U^\sharp$ relative to $g$, characterized by
$$
g(U\alpha,\gamma)=g(\alpha,U^\sharp\gamma)
$$
for all $\alpha,\gamma\in V$.

<1>1. Express preservation of $f$ through the $g$-adjoint.
::: {.proof}
For arbitrary $\alpha,\beta$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(U\alpha,U\beta)
&=g(U\alpha,TU\beta)\\
&=g(\alpha,U^\sharp T U\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
On the other hand,
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=g(\alpha,T\beta).
$$
Therefore $U$ preserves $f$ exactly when
$$
g(\alpha,(U^\sharp T U-T)\beta)=0
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$.
:::

<1>2. Use non-singularity of $g$ to obtain the operator criterion.
::: {.proof}
Since $g$ is non-singular, the condition
$$
g(\alpha,\gamma)=0
$$
for every $\alpha$ forces $\gamma=0$. Hence the condition from step <1>1 is
equivalent to
$$
\boxed{U^\sharp T U=T.}
$$
Thus this equation is necessary and sufficient for $U$ to preserve $f$.
:::

<1>3. Give the equivalent inverse form of the condition.
::: {.proof}
If
$$
U^\sharp T U=T,
$$
then the left-hand side is invertible because the right-hand side $T$ is
invertible. In particular $U$ is invertible. Multiplying on the right by
$U^{-1}$ and then by $T^{-1}$ gives
$$
U^\sharp=T U^{-1}T^{-1}.
$$
Conversely this identity immediately implies $U^\sharp T U=T$.
Therefore one may equivalently state the answer as
$$
\boxed{
U\text{ preserves }f
\iff
U^\sharp T U=T
\iff
U\in GL(V)\text{ and }U^\sharp=T U^{-1}T^{-1}.}
$$
:::
:::
