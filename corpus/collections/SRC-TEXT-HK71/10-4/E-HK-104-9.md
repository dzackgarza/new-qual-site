::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the space of all $n \times 1$ matrices over $\CC$, $A$ an $n \times n$ matrix over $\CC$, and $f$ the bilinear form on $V$ given by $f(X, Y) = X^tAY$.
Show that $f$ is invariant under $O(n, \CC)$, i.e., $f(MX, MY) = f(X, Y)$ for all $X, Y$ in $V$ and $M$ in $O(n, \CC)$, if and only if $A$ commutes with each member of $O(n, \CC)$.
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §10.4 Exercise 9, p. 383. The three steps are the computation of $f(MX,MY)$, the passage from the identity of bilinear forms to the matrix identity, and the use of $M^{t}M=I=M M^{t}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #compute}
$f(MX,MY)=X^{t}M^{t}AMY$ for $M\in O(n,\CC)$ and $X,Y\in V$.

::: pf-proof
$f(U,W)=U^{t}AW$ by definition, and $(MX)^{t}=X^{t}M^{t}$, so $f(MX,MY)=(MX)^{t}A(MY)=X^{t}M^{t}AMY$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix}
$f(MX,MY)=f(X,Y)$ for all $X,Y\in V$ if and only if $M^{t}AM=A$.

::: pf-proof
The difference $f(MX,MY)-f(X,Y)=X^{t}(M^{t}AM-A)Y$ is the bilinear form of the matrix $M^{t}AM-A$. A bilinear form on $V$ that vanishes identically has zero matrix, since its value on the standard basis vectors $E_i,E_j$ is the $(i,j)$ entry; conversely a zero matrix gives the zero form.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #commute}
For $M\in O(n,\CC)$, $M^{t}AM=A$ if and only if $AM=MA$.

::: pf-proof
If $M^{t}AM=A$, multiply on the left by $M$ and use $MM^{t}=I$ to get $AM=MA$. If $AM=MA$, then $M^{t}AM=M^{t}MA=A$ using $M^{t}M=I$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
$f$ is invariant under $O(n,\CC)$ if and only if $A$ commutes with every member of $O(n,\CC)$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#compute){.pf-ref} and [](#matrix){.pf-ref} reduce invariance for a fixed $M$ to $M^{t}AM=A$, and step [](#commute){.pf-ref} identifies that with $AM=MA$. Quantifying over all $M\in O(n,\CC)$ gives the assertion.
:::
:::
