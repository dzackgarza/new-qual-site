::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on $C^2$ which preserves the quadratic form $x_1^2 - x_2^2$.
Show that

(a) $\det(T) = \pm 1$.

(b) If $M$ is the matrix of $T$ in the standard basis, then $M_{22} = \pm M_{11}$, $M_{21} = \pm M_{12}$, $M_{11}^2 - M_{12}^2 = 1$.

(c) If $\det M = 1$, then there is a non-zero complex number $c$ such that
\[
M = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} c + \frac{1}{c} & c - \frac{1}{c} \\ c - \frac{1}{c} & c + \frac{1}{c} \end{bmatrix}.
\]

(d) If $\det M = -1$ then there is a non-zero complex number $c$ such that
\[
M = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} c + \frac{1}{c} & c - \frac{1}{c} \\ -c + \frac{1}{c} & -c - \frac{1}{c} \end{bmatrix}.
\]
:::


::: {.remark}
In the printed source, part (d) says only “there is a complex number $c$,”
but the displayed formula contains $1/c$. The parameter must be nonzero, as
stated here.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ d&e\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Preservation of $x_1^2-x_2^2$ is equivalent to
$$
M^tJM=J.
$$

<1>1. The determinant of $M$ is $\pm1$.
::: {.proof}
Taking determinants in
$$
M^tJM=J
$$
gives
$$
(\det M)^2\det J=\det J.
$$
Since $\det J=-1\ne0$,
$$
(\det M)^2=1,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{\det M=\pm1.}
$$
:::

<1>2. Relate the second row of $M$ to the first row.
::: {.proof}
Put
$$
\Delta=\det M\in\{1,-1\}.
$$
Because $J^{-1}=J$, the preservation equation can be rewritten as
$$
JM^tJ=M^{-1}.
$$
Now
$$
JM^tJ
=\begin{pmatrix}a&-d\\-b&e\end{pmatrix},
$$
while
$$
M^{-1}
=\frac1\Delta
\begin{pmatrix}e&-b\\-d&a\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Equating entries gives
$$
e=\Delta a,
\qquad
d=\Delta b.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{M_{22}=\Delta M_{11},\qquad M_{21}=\Delta M_{12}.}
$$
In particular each is equal to the corresponding entry up to the same sign.

The $(1,1)$ entry of $M^tJM=J$ is
$$
a^2-d^2=1.
$$
Since $d=\Delta b$ and $\Delta^2=1$,
$$
\boxed{a^2-b^2=1,}
$$
which is the asserted relation
$M_{11}^2-M_{12}^2=1$.
:::

<1>3. Parametrize the component with determinant $1$.
::: {.proof}
If $\Delta=1$, step <1>2 gives
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
a^2-b^2=1.
$$
Thus
$$
(a+b)(a-b)=1.
$$
Set
$$
c=a+b.
$$
Then $c\ne0$ and
$$
\frac1c=a-b.
$$
Adding and subtracting these equations gives
$$
a=\frac12\left(c+\frac1c\right),
\qquad
b=\frac12\left(c-\frac1c\right).
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
M=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
c+\frac1c&c-\frac1c\\
c-\frac1c&c+\frac1c
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
:::

<1>4. Parametrize the component with determinant $-1$.
::: {.proof}
If $\Delta=-1$, step <1>2 gives
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-b&-a\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
a^2-b^2=1.
$$
Again set
$$
c=a+b\ne0,
\qquad
\frac1c=a-b.
$$
Then the first row is as in step <1>3, while
$$
-b=\frac12\left(-c+\frac1c\right),
\qquad
-a=\frac12\left(-c-\frac1c\right).
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
M=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
c+\frac1c&c-\frac1c\\
-c+\frac1c&-c-\frac1c
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
:::
