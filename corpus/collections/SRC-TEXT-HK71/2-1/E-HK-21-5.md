::: {.exercise}
On $\RR^n$, define two operations
\[
\alpha \oplus \beta = \alpha - \beta, \qquad c \cdot \alpha = -c\alpha.
\]
The operations on the right are the usual ones.
Which of the axioms for a vector space are satisfied by $(\RR^n, \oplus, \cdot)$?
:::


::: solution
The operation $\oplus$ is not a vector addition. For instance,
\[
\alpha\oplus\beta=\alpha-\beta,
\qquad
\beta\oplus\alpha=\beta-\alpha,
\]
which are generally unequal, so commutativity fails. Associativity also fails:
\[
(\alpha\oplus\beta)\oplus\gamma=\alpha-\beta-\gamma,
\]
while
\[
\alpha\oplus(\beta\oplus\gamma)=\alpha-\beta+\gamma.
\]
There is a right identity $0$ because $\alpha\oplus0=\alpha$, but no two-sided additive identity since $0\oplus\alpha=-\alpha$ in general.

For scalar multiplication, one has
\[
1\cdot\alpha=-\alpha,
\]
so the axiom $1\cdot\alpha=\alpha$ fails. Also
\[
(ab)\cdot\alpha=-ab\alpha,
\qquad
a\cdot(b\cdot\alpha)=ab\alpha,
\]
so scalar associativity fails in general.

Some distributive identities do hold accidentally, but the vector-space axioms as a whole fail; in particular $(\RR^n,\oplus,\cdot)$ is not a vector space.
:::
