::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the set of all pairs $(x, y)$ of real numbers, and let $F$ be the field of real numbers.
Define
\[
(x, y) + (x_1, y_1) = (x + x_1,\; y + y_1), \qquad c(x, y) = (cx,\; y).
\]
Is $V$, with these operations, a vector space over the field of real numbers?
:::


::: solution
No. The scalar-multiplication axiom
\[
1\cdot v=v
\]
fails in general. Indeed, for $v=(x,y)$ the defined operation gives
\[
1\cdot(x,y)=(x,y),
\]
so this axiom does hold; however distributivity over scalar addition fails. Take $v=(0,1)$ and scalars $1,1$. Then
\[
(1+1)v=2v=(0,1),
\]
whereas
\[
1v+1v=(0,1)+(0,1)=(0,2).
\]
Thus $(a+b)v=av+bv$ is false, so these operations do not make $V$ a real vector space.
:::
