::: {.exercise}
If $F$ is a field, verify that $F^n$ is a vector space over $F$, where $F^n$ is the set of $n$-tuples of elements of $F$ with componentwise addition and scalar multiplication.
:::


::: solution
Let $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n)$, $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$ in $F^n$ and $a,b\in F$. Every vector-space axiom follows coordinatewise from the corresponding field axiom in $F$. For example,
\[
x+y=(x_1+y_1,\ldots,x_n+y_n),
\]
so addition is associative and commutative because addition in $F$ is; the zero vector is $(0,\ldots,0)$ and $-x=(-x_1,\ldots,-x_n)$. Likewise
\[
a(x+y)=ax+ay,
\qquad
(a+b)x=ax+bx,
\qquad
(ab)x=a(bx),
\qquad
1x=x
\]
hold coordinatewise. Hence $F^n$ is a vector space over $F$.
:::
