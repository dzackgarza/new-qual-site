::: {.exercise}
If $F$ is a field, verify that $F^n$ is a vector space over $F$, where $F^n$ is the set of $n$-tuples of elements of $F$ with componentwise addition and scalar multiplication.
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §2.1 Exercise 1, p. 33.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #closure}
For $x,y\in F^n$ and $a\in F$ the tuples $x+y$ and $ax$ are again elements of $F^n$.

::: pf-proof
By definition $x+y\coloneqq(x_1+y_1,\ldots,x_n+y_n)$ and $ax\coloneqq(ax_1,\ldots,ax_n)$. Each entry is a sum of two elements of $F$, or a product of two elements of $F$, so lies in $F$; the tuple has $n$ entries. Thus addition and scalar multiplication are operations on $F^n$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #group}
$(F^n,+)$ is an abelian group.

::: pf-proof
Let $x,y,z\in F^n$. In every coordinate $i$ the field laws give $(x_i+y_i)+z_i=x_i+(y_i+z_i)$ and $x_i+y_i=y_i+x_i$, and two $n$-tuples are equal exactly when they agree coordinatewise; hence $(x+y)+z=x+(y+z)$ and $x+y=y+x$. The tuple $0\coloneqq(0,\ldots,0)$ lies in $F^n$ and satisfies $x+0=x$ coordinatewise, and $-x\coloneqq(-x_1,\ldots,-x_n)$ lies in $F^n$ and satisfies $x+(-x)=0$ coordinatewise.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #scalars}
For $x,y\in F^n$ and $a,b\in F$, $a(x+y)=ax+ay$, $(a+b)x=ax+bx$, $(ab)x=a(bx)$ and $1x=x$.

::: pf-proof
In coordinate $i$ these read $a(x_i+y_i)=ax_i+ay_i$, $(a+b)x_i=ax_i+bx_i$, $(ab)x_i=a(bx_i)$ and $1x_i=x_i$, which are the distributive, associative and unital laws of the field $F$. Each identity of tuples holds because it holds in every coordinate.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
$F^n$ is a vector space over $F$.

::: pf-proof
A vector space over $F$ is an abelian group under addition carrying a scalar multiplication that satisfies the four identities of step [](#scalars){.pf-ref}. Steps [](#closure){.pf-ref} and [](#group){.pf-ref} supply the abelian group and step [](#scalars){.pf-ref} the identities.
:::
:::
