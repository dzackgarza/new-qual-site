::: {.exercise}
Which vectors in $\CC^3$ are linear combinations of $(1, 0, -1)$, $(0, 1, 1)$, and $(1, 1, 1)$?
:::


::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §2.1 Exercise 3.

Let $v_{1}=(1,0,-1)$, $v_{2}=(0,1,1)$, $v_{3}=(1,1,1)$ and let $M$ be the $3\times3$ matrix whose columns are $v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}$:
$$
M=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\ 0&1&1\\ -1&1&1\end{bmatrix}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #combination}
$c_{1}v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2}+c_{3}v_{3}=M\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}\\ c_{2}\\ c_{3}\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
The $i$-th entry of the right-hand side is $\sum_{j}M_{ij}c_{j}=\sum_{j}(v_{j})_{i}c_{j}$, which is the $i$-th coordinate of the combination on the left, by the definition of scalar multiplication and addition in $\CC^{3}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #det}
$\det M=1\neq0$.

::: pf-proof
Expanding along the first row,
$$
\det M=1\cdot\det\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\ 1&1\end{bmatrix}-0\cdot\det\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\ -1&1\end{bmatrix}+1\cdot\det\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\ -1&1\end{bmatrix}=1\cdot(1\cdot1-1\cdot1)+1\cdot\bigl(0\cdot1-1\cdot(-1)\bigr)=0+1=1 .
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #independent}
$v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}$ are linearly independent.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#combination){.pf-ref} a relation $c_{1}v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2}+c_{3}v_{3}=0$ is $Mc=0$ for the column vector $c$ of coefficients. Since $\det M\neq0$ by step [](#det){.pf-ref}, $M$ is invertible and $c=M^{-1}0=0$, so all three coefficients vanish.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis}
$\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}$ is a basis of $\CC^{3}$.

::: pf-proof
Three linearly independent vectors in the three-dimensional space $\CC^{3}$ form a basis: a linearly independent set can be extended to a basis, and no extension by further vectors is possible in a space of dimension $3=\#$ of the vectors, so the set is already maximal and spans.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Every vector of $\CC^{3}$ is a linear combination of $(1,0,-1)$, $(0,1,1)$ and $(1,1,1)$, and no further restriction applies.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#basis){.pf-ref} the three vectors span $\CC^{3}$, so each vector of $\CC^{3}$ is a combination of them; conversely every combination lies in $\CC^{3}$. Hence the answer to the exercise is: all vectors of $\CC^{3}$, and the coefficients of a given vector $w$ are $M^{-1}w$.
:::
