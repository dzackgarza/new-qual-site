::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space, and let $W$ be a subspace of $V$.
Then $V = W \oplus W^{\perp}$, that is, each $\alpha$ in $V$ is uniquely expressible in the form $\alpha = \beta + \gamma$, with $\beta$ in $W$ and $\gamma$ in $W^{\perp}$.
Define a linear operator $U$ by $U\alpha = \beta - \gamma$.

(a) Prove that $U$ is both self-adjoint and unitary.

(b) If $V$ is $\RR^3$ with the standard inner product and $W$ is the subspace spanned by $(1, 0, 1)$, find the matrix of $U$ in the standard ordered basis.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Show the reflection across $W$ is orthogonal and write its $\RR^3$ matrix.

<1> Let $\alpha=\beta+\gamma$ and $\delta=\beta'+\gamma'$ with $\beta,\beta'\in W$, $\gamma,\gamma'\in W^\perp$.
    *Proof of (a):*
    <2>1. Linearity of projections gives linearity of $U$.
    <2>2. For self-adjointness:
        $$
        \langle U\alpha,\delta\rangle
        =\langle \beta-\gamma,\beta'+\gamma'\rangle
        =\langle\beta,\beta'\rangle-\langle\gamma,\gamma'\rangle
        =\langle\beta+\gamma,\beta'-\gamma'\rangle
        =\langle\alpha,U\delta\rangle.
        $$
        So $\langle U\alpha,\delta\rangle=\langle\alpha,U\delta\rangle$.
    <2>3. For unitarity:
        $$
        \langle U\alpha,U\delta\rangle
        =\langle \beta-\gamma,\beta'-\gamma'\rangle
        =\langle\beta,\beta'\rangle+\langle\gamma,\gamma'\rangle
        =\langle\beta+\gamma,\beta'+\gamma'\rangle
        =\langle\alpha,\delta\rangle.
        $$
        Hence $\|U\alpha\|=\|\alpha\|$, so $U$ is unitary.

<1> (b) Set $W=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0,1)\}$, so
    $W^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0,-1),(0,1,0)\}$.
    Write $\alpha=(x,y,z)$ as
    $$
    \beta=\frac{x+z}{2}(1,0,1),\qquad
    \gamma=\left(\frac{x-z}{2},y,\,-\frac{x-z}{2}\right).
    $$
    Then
    $$
    U(x,y,z)=\beta-\gamma=(z,-y,x),
    $$
    so the matrix is
    $$
    \begin{pmatrix}
    0&0&1\\
    0&-1&0\\
    1&0&0
    \end{pmatrix}.
    $$
