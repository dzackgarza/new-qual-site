::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be $\RR^3$, with the standard inner product.
Let $W$ be the plane spanned by $\alpha = (1, 1, 1)$ and $\beta = (1, 1, -2)$.
Let $U$ be the linear operator defined, geometrically, as follows: $U$ is rotation through the angle $\theta$, about the straight line through the origin which is orthogonal to $W$.
There are actually two such rotations—choose one.
Find the matrix of $U$ in the standard ordered basis.
(Here is one way you might proceed.
Find $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ which form an orthonormal basis for $W$.
Let $\alpha_3$ be a vector of norm 1 which is orthogonal to $W$.
Find the matrix of $U$ in the basis $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$.
Perform a change of basis.)
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §8.4 Exercise 5 on printed p. 308. The argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
The axis of the rotation is the line $W^{\perp}$, spanned by $\alpha\times\beta$.

::: pf-proof
By the exercise's definition, $U$ is a rotation about the line through the origin orthogonal to $W$, that is, about $W^{\perp}$. Since $\alpha=(1,1,1)$ and $\beta=(1,1,-2)$ are independent, $W$ is a plane and $W^{\perp}$ is a line; $\alpha\times\beta$ is nonzero and orthogonal to both, so it spans $W^{\perp}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}
$\alpha\times\beta=(-3,3,0)$, so a unit vector along the axis is $u=\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt2},\frac{1}{\sqrt2},0\right)$.

::: pf-proof
Expanding the determinant, $\alpha\times\beta=(1\cdot(-2)-1\cdot1,\ 1\cdot1-1\cdot(-2),\ 1\cdot1-1\cdot1)=(-3,3,0)$, whose norm is $\sqrt{9+9}=3\sqrt2$; dividing by it gives $u$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}
For a unit vector $u$, the rotation by $\theta$ about $u$ is
$$
U=\cos\theta\,I+(1-\cos\theta)\,uu^{T}+\sin\theta\,[u]_\times .
$$

::: pf-proof
Write $v=v_\parallel+v_\perp$ with $v_\parallel=(v\mid u)u$ and $v_\perp=v-v_\parallel$. The rotation fixes $v_\parallel$ and sends $v_\perp$ to $\cos\theta\,v_\perp+\sin\theta\,(u\times v_\perp)$; since $u\times v=u\times v_\perp$, this is $\cos\theta\,v+(1-\cos\theta)(v\mid u)u+\sin\theta\,(u\times v)$. The three terms are the operators $\cos\theta\,I$, $(1-\cos\theta)uu^{T}$ and $\sin\theta\,[u]_\times$, since $(v\mid u)u=uu^{T}v$ and $[u]_\times v=u\times v$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}
With $u=\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt2},\frac{1}{\sqrt2},0\right)$ the matrix is
$$
U=\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}&\frac{\cos\theta-1}{2}&\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sin\theta\\[2pt]
\frac{\cos\theta-1}{2}&\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}&\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sin\theta\\[2pt]
-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sin\theta&-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Here $uu^{T}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $[u]_\times=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&1\\-1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Substituting these in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} and collecting coefficients of $\cos\theta$, $\sin\theta$ and $1$ gives the displayed entries. The result is orthogonal with determinant $1$ and satisfies $Uu=u$, as a rotation about $u$ must.
:::
