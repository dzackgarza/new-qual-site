::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be $\RR^3$, with the standard inner product.
Let $W$ be the plane spanned by $\alpha = (1, 1, 1)$ and $\beta = (1, 1, -2)$.
Let $U$ be the linear operator defined, geometrically, as follows: $U$ is rotation through the angle $\theta$, about the straight line through the origin which is orthogonal to $W$.
There are actually two such rotations—choose one.
Find the matrix of $U$ in the standard ordered basis.
(Here is one way you might proceed.
Find $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ which form an orthonormal basis for $W$.
Let $\alpha_3$ be a vector of norm 1 which is orthogonal to $W$.
Find the matrix of $U$ in the basis $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$.
Perform a change of basis.)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The axis of rotation is the line orthogonal to $W$, spanned by $\alpha \times \beta$.
::: {.proof}
the axis is the orthogonal complement of $W = \operatorname{span}\{\alpha, \beta\}$.
:::

<1>2. $\alpha \times \beta = (1,1,1) \times (1,1,-2) = (-3, 3, 0)$, with norm $3\sqrt{2}$, so a unit vector along the axis is $u = \left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}, \frac{1}{\sqrt2}, 0\right)$.
::: {.proof}
cross product and normalization.
:::

<1>3. By Rodrigues' rotation formula, the matrix of $U$ (rotation by $\theta$ about the unit axis $u$) is
$$U = \cos\theta\, I + (1 - \cos\theta)\, uu^T + \sin\theta\, [u]_\times,$$
where $[u]_\times$ is the cross-product matrix of $u$.
::: {.proof}
Rodrigues' formula.
:::

<1>4. Computing with $u = \left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}, \frac{1}{\sqrt2}, 0\right)$ gives
$$U = \begin{pmatrix}
\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2} & \frac{\cos\theta - 1}{2} & \frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sin\theta \\[2pt]
\frac{\cos\theta - 1}{2} & \frac{1+\cos\theta}{2} & \frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sin\theta \\[2pt]
-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sin\theta & -\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sin\theta & \cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}.$$
::: {.proof}
substitute $u$ into <1>3 and simplify.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>4.
:::
:::
