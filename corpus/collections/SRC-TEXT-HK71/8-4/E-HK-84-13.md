::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ and $W$ be finite-dimensional inner product spaces having the same dimension.
Let $U$ be an isomorphism of $V$ onto $W$.
Show that:

(a) The mapping $T \to UTU^{-1}$ is an isomorphism of the vector space $L(V, V)$ onto the vector space $L(W, W)$.

(b) $\operatorname{trace}(UTU^{-1}) = \operatorname{trace}(T)$ for each $T$ in $L(V, V)$.

(c) $UT_{\alpha,\beta}U^{-1} = T_{U\alpha, U\beta}$ ($T_{\alpha,\beta}$ defined in Exercise 10).

(d) $(UTU^{-1})^* = UT^*U^{-1}$.

(e) If we equip $L(V, V)$ with inner product $(T_1 \mid T_2) = \operatorname{trace}(T_1T_2^*)$, and similarly for $L(W, W)$, then $T \to UTU^{-1}$ is an inner product space isomorphism.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
$\Phi(T) = UTU^{-1}$ is an isomorphism of vector spaces from $L(V, V)$ onto $L(W, W)$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Define $\Phi(T) = U T U^{-1}$ for $T \in L(V, V)$.
For any $T_1, T_2 \in L(V, V)$ and $c \in F$:
$$
\Phi(c T_1 + T_2) = U(c T_1 + T_2)U^{-1} = c U T_1 U^{-1} + U T_2 U^{-1} = c \Phi(T_1) + \Phi(T_2).
$$
Thus $\Phi$ is a linear map.

::: pf-proof
For $T_1, T_2 \in L(V,V)$ and a scalar $c$, composition is linear in each argument, so $U(cT_1 + T_2)U^{-1} = cUT_1U^{-1} + UT_2U^{-1}$; this is additivity and homogeneity of $\Phi$, and $\Phi(T)$ is a linear operator on $W$ because it is a composite of linear maps, so $\Phi$ maps $L(V,V)$ into $L(W,W)$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Define $\Psi: L(W, W) \to L(V, V)$ by $\Psi(S) = U^{-1} S U$.
Then $\Psi(\Phi(T)) = U^{-1}(U T U^{-1})U = T$ and $\Phi(\Psi(S)) = U(U^{-1} S U)U^{-1} = S$, so $\Psi = \Phi^{-1}$.
Thus $\Phi$ is a vector space isomorphism.

::: pf-proof
Associativity of composition and $U^{-1}U = \id_V$, $UU^{-1} = \id_W$ give $\Psi(\Phi(T)) = U^{-1}(UTU^{-1})U = T$ and $\Phi(\Psi(S)) = U(U^{-1}SU)U^{-1} = S$. A map with a two-sided inverse is a bijection, and $\Phi$ is linear by the previous step, so it is an isomorphism of vector spaces.
:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b}
$\operatorname{tr}(UTU^{-1}) = \operatorname{tr}(T)$ for every $T \in L(V, V)$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
By the cyclic property of the trace functional on linear operators $\operatorname{tr}(AB) = \operatorname{tr}(BA)$:
$$
\operatorname{tr}\left(U T U^{-1}\right) = \operatorname{tr}\left((UT) U^{-1}\right) = \operatorname{tr}\left(U^{-1} (UT)\right) = \operatorname{tr}\left(\operatorname{id}_V T\right) = \operatorname{tr}(T).
$$

::: pf-proof
The trace of a product of two operators on a finite-dimensional space satisfies $\operatorname{tr}(AB) = \operatorname{tr}(BA)$; apply this with $A = UT$ and $B = U^{-1}$, use associativity to read $\operatorname{tr}((UT)U^{-1})$ as one side and $\operatorname{tr}(U^{-1}(UT))$ as the other, and $U^{-1}U = \id_V$ to reduce the second to $\operatorname{tr}(T)$.
:::

::: {.pf-step #part-c}
$UT_{\alpha, \beta}U^{-1} = T_{U\alpha, U\beta}$ for all $\alpha \in V$ and $\beta \in V$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s3-1}
The operator $T_{\alpha, \beta} \in L(V, V)$ is defined by $T_{\alpha, \beta}(v) = \langle v, \beta \rangle_V \alpha$.
For any vector $w \in W$:
$$
\left(U T_{\alpha, \beta} U^{-1}\right)(w) = U\left(T_{\alpha, \beta}\left(U^{-1}w\right)\right) = U\left( \langle U^{-1}w, \beta \rangle_V \alpha \right) = \langle U^{-1}w, \beta \rangle_V (U\alpha).
$$

::: pf-proof
By definition of composition $(UT_{\alpha,\beta}U^{-1})(w) = U\left(T_{\alpha,\beta}(U^{-1}w)\right)$, and the definition of $T_{\alpha,\beta}$ with $v = U^{-1}w$ gives $T_{\alpha,\beta}(U^{-1}w) = \langle U^{-1}w,\beta\rangle_V \alpha$; applying the linear map $U$ and pulling the scalar out gives $\langle U^{-1}w,\beta\rangle_V (U\alpha)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #unitary-inner}
Since $U: V \to W$ is an inner product space isomorphism (unitary map), it preserves inner products:
$$
\langle U^{-1}w, \beta \rangle_V = \langle U(U^{-1}w), U\beta \rangle_W = \langle w, U\beta \rangle_W.
$$

::: pf-proof
An isomorphism of inner product spaces satisfies $\langle Uv, Uw\rangle_W = \langle v,w\rangle_V$ for all $v, w$; applying it to $v = U^{-1}w$ and to $\beta$ gives $\langle U(U^{-1}w), U\beta\rangle_W = \langle U^{-1}w,\beta\rangle_V$, and $U(U^{-1}w) = w$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Substituting this into step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref}:
$$
\left(U T_{\alpha, \beta} U^{-1}\right)(w) = \langle w, U\beta \rangle_W (U\alpha) = T_{U\alpha, U\beta}(w).
$$
Since this holds for all $w \in W$, $U T_{\alpha, \beta} U^{-1} = T_{U\alpha, U\beta}$.

::: pf-proof
Substituting the identity of step [](#unitary-inner){.pf-ref} into the computation of step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref} gives the first equality, and the definition of $T_{U\alpha,U\beta}$ applied to $w \in W$ gives $\langle w, U\beta\rangle_W (U\alpha)$, which is the second. Two linear maps that agree at every vector of $W$ are equal.
:::

::: {.pf-step #part-d}
$(UTU^{-1})^* = UT^*U^{-1}$ for every $T \in L(V, V)$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #unitary}
Because $U$ is an inner product space isomorphism, $U$ is unitary, which means $U^* = U^{-1}$ and $(U^{-1})^* = (U^*)^* = U$.

::: pf-proof
Since $U$ preserves inner products, $\langle U^*Uv, w\rangle_V = \langle Uv, Uw\rangle_W = \langle v, w\rangle_V$ for all $v, w$, so $U^*U = \id_V$. As $U$ is invertible, multiplying on the right by $U^{-1}$ gives $U^* = U^{-1}$, and taking adjoints of that identity gives $(U^{-1})^* = (U^*)^* = U$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Applying the property of adjoints on composite operators $(ABC)^* = C^* B^* A^*$:
$$
\left(U T U^{-1}\right)^* = \left(U^{-1}\right)^* T^* U^* = U T^* U^{-1}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The adjoint of a composite reverses the order, $(ABC)^* = C^*B^*A^*$; with $A = U$, $B = T$ and $C = U^{-1}$ this gives $(UTU^{-1})^* = (U^{-1})^* T^* U^*$, and substituting $U^* = U^{-1}$ and $(U^{-1})^* = U$ from step [](#unitary){.pf-ref} gives the displayed identity.
:::

::: {.pf-step #part-e}
$\left(UT_1U^{-1} \mid UT_2U^{-1}\right) = (T_1 \mid T_2)$ for all $T_1, T_2 \in L(V, V)$, so $\Phi$ is an inner product space isomorphism.

::: pf-proof

For $T_1, T_2 \in L(V, V)$,
$$
\left(\Phi(T_1) \mid \Phi(T_2)\right)
= \operatorname{tr}\left(U T_1 U^{-1} (U T_2 U^{-1})^*\right)
= \operatorname{tr}\left(U T_1 U^{-1} U T_2^* U^{-1}\right)
= \operatorname{tr}\left(U T_1 T_2^* U^{-1}\right)
= \operatorname{tr}(T_1 T_2^*)
= (T_1 \mid T_2),
$$
by part (d) and then part (b) applied to $T_1 T_2^*$. With part (a), $\Phi$ is an inner product space isomorphism.

:::
