::: {.exercise}
A *rigid motion* of an inner product space $V$ is a function $T:V\to V$, not necessarily linear, such that $\norm{T\alpha-T\beta}=\norm{\alpha-\beta}$ for every $\alpha,\beta\in V$. Linear unitary operators and translations are examples.

(a) Let $V=\RR^2$ with its standard inner product. If $T$ is a rigid motion with $T(0)=0$, prove that $T$ is linear and unitary.

(b) Deduce that every rigid motion of $\RR^2$ is a translation followed by a unitary operator.

(c) Show that every rigid motion of $\RR^2$ is either a translation followed by a rotation, or a translation followed by a reflection followed by a rotation.
:::
