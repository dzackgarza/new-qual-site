::: {.exercise}
A *rigid motion* of an inner product space $V$ is a function $T:V\to V$, not necessarily linear, such that $\norm{T\alpha-T\beta}=\norm{\alpha-\beta}$ for every $\alpha,\beta\in V$. Linear unitary operators and translations are examples.

(a) Let $V=\RR^2$ with its standard inner product. If $T$ is a rigid motion with $T(0)=0$, prove that $T$ is linear and unitary.

(b) Deduce that every rigid motion of $\RR^2$ is a translation followed by a unitary operator.

(c) Show that every rigid motion of $\RR^2$ is either a translation followed by a rotation, or a translation followed by a reflection followed by a rotation.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $e_1,e_2$ for the standard basis, and let $F(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,-x_2)$ be reflection in the first coordinate axis. In the compositions below, the rightmost map acts first.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #origin-fixed}
If $T(0)=0$, then $T$ is linear and unitary.

::: pf-proof
For $x,y\in\RR^2$, polarization and distance preservation give
$$
\langle Tx,Ty\rangle
=\frac{\norm{Tx}^2+\norm{Ty}^2-\norm{Tx-Ty}^2}{2}
=\frac{\norm{x}^2+\norm{y}^2-\norm{x-y}^2}{2}
=\langle x,y\rangle.
$$
Thus $Te_1,Te_2$ are an orthonormal basis of $\RR^2$. If $x=x_1e_1+x_2e_2$, then $\langle Tx,Te_i\rangle=x_i$ for $i=1,2$, so $Tx=x_1Te_1+x_2Te_2$. This formula is linear in $x$ and preserves the inner product; its orthonormal image basis also makes it onto. Hence $T$ is unitary.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #translation}
Every rigid motion $T$ is a translation followed by a unitary operator.

::: pf-proof
Let $b=T(0)$ and $U(x)=T(x)-b$. Then $U$ preserves distances and $U(0)=0$, so step [](#origin-fixed){.pf-ref} makes $U$ unitary. Set $a=U^{-1}b$ and $\tau_a(x)=x+a$. For every $x\in\RR^2$,
$$
(U\circ\tau_a)(x)=Ux+Ua=Ux+b=T(x).
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #orthogonal-forms}
Every unitary operator on $\RR^2$ is a rotation or a reflection followed by a rotation.

::: pf-proof
Let $A$ be its matrix in the standard basis. Since $A^{\mathsf T}A=I$, its determinant is $1$ or $-1$. If $\det A=1$, write the first column as $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^{\mathsf T}$. Orthogonality and the positive determinant force the second column to be $(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^{\mathsf T}$, so the operator is $R_\theta$. If $\det A=-1$, then the operator composed with $F$ is orthogonal with determinant $1$. The first case makes this composition $R_\theta$, and the operator is $R_\theta\circ F$ because $F^2=I$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Q.E.D. Every rigid motion has one of the forms $R_\theta\circ\tau_a$ or $R_\theta\circ F\circ\tau_a$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#translation){.pf-ref}, write $T=U\circ\tau_a$. Apply step [](#orthogonal-forms){.pf-ref} to $U$. These are respectively a translation followed by a rotation, and a translation followed by a reflection followed by a rotation.
:::
