::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space.
In Exercise 6, we showed how to construct some linear operators on $V$ which are both self-adjoint and unitary.
Now prove that there are no others, i.e., that every self-adjoint unitary operator arises from some subspace $W$ as we described in Exercise 6.
:::

::: solution
Let $U$ be both self-adjoint and unitary. Then
$$
U^*=U
$$
and
$$
U^*U=I,
$$
so
$$
U^2=I.
$$

<1>1. Construct a projection from $U$.
::: proof
Define
$$
E=\frac12(I+U).
$$
Using $U^2=I$,
$$
E^2
=\frac14(I+2U+U^2)
=\frac12(I+U)
=E.
$$
Also, since $U^*=U$,
$$
E^*=\frac12(I+U^*)=E.
$$
Thus $E$ is an idempotent self-adjoint operator.
:::

<1>2. A self-adjoint projection is the orthogonal projection onto its range.
::: proof
Put
$$
W=\operatorname{range}E.
$$
Because $E^2=E$, the operator $E$ acts as the identity on $W$. If
$x\in\ker E$ and $w=Ey\in W$, then
$$
(x\mid w)
=(x\mid Ey)
=(E^*x\mid y)
=(Ex\mid y)
=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\ker E\subseteq W^\perp.
$$
Since an idempotent gives the direct sum
$$
V=\operatorname{range}E\oplus\ker E,
$$
the dimensions force
$$
\ker E=W^\perp.
$$
Therefore $E$ is exactly the orthogonal projection onto $W$.
:::

<1>3. Recover $U$ as the reflection determined by $W$.
::: proof
From the definition of $E$,
$$
U=2E-I.
$$
If $w\in W$, then $Ew=w$, so
$$
Uw=(2E-I)w=w.
$$
If $z\in W^\perp=\ker E$, then
$$
Uz=(2E-I)z=-z.
$$
Thus $U$ fixes $W$ pointwise and changes the sign on $W^\perp$. Equivalently,
if $v=w+z$ with $w\in W$ and $z\in W^\perp$, then
$$
Uv=w-z.
$$
This is precisely the reflection across $W$ constructed in Exercise 6.
Hence every self-adjoint unitary operator arises from such a subspace.
:::
:::
