::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be $\RR^2$, with the standard inner product.
If $U$ is a unitary operator on $V$, show that the matrix of $U$ in the standard ordered basis is either
\[
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{or} \quad \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & -\cos\theta \end{bmatrix}
\]
for some real $\theta$, $0 \leq \theta < 2\pi$.
Let $U_\theta$ be the linear operator corresponding to the first matrix, i.e., $U_\theta$ is rotation through the angle $\theta$.
Now convince yourself that every unitary operator on $V$ is either a rotation, or reflection about the $\epsilon_1$-axis followed by a rotation.

(a) What is $U_\theta U_\phi$?

(b) Show that $U_\theta^* = U_{-\theta}$.

(c) Let $\phi$ be a fixed real number, and let $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha_1, \alpha_2\}$ be the orthonormal basis obtained by rotating $\{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2\}$ through the angle $\phi$, i.e., $\alpha_j = U_\phi\epsilon_j$.
If $\theta$ is another real number, what is the matrix of $U_\theta$ in the ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Classify all unitary operators on the real Euclidean plane.
::: {.proof}
Over $\mathbb R$, a unitary operator is an orthogonal operator. Let the first
column of its standard matrix be
$$
\binom ac.
$$
It has Euclidean norm one, so for a unique
$\theta\in[0,2\pi)$,
$$
a=\cos\theta,
\qquad
c=\sin\theta.
$$
The second column must be a unit vector perpendicular to the first. There are
exactly two such vectors:
$$
\binom{-\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\binom{\sin\theta}{-\cos\theta}.
$$
Thus the matrix is exactly one of
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&-\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

If
$$
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
$$
is reflection in the $\epsilon_1$-axis, then the second matrix is
$U_\theta R$. Hence every orthogonal operator is either a rotation or a
reflection in the $\epsilon_1$-axis followed by a rotation.
:::

<1>2. The product of two rotations adds their angles.
::: {.proof}
Direct multiplication and the angle-addition identities give
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_\theta U_\phi
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\phi&-\sin\phi\\
\sin\phi&\cos\phi
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos(\theta+\phi)&-\sin(\theta+\phi)\\
\sin(\theta+\phi)&\cos(\theta+\phi)
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=U_{\theta+\phi}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Angles may of course be read modulo $2\pi$.
:::

<1>3. The adjoint of a rotation is rotation through the opposite angle.
::: {.proof}
Since the standard basis is orthonormal and the matrix is real,
$$
[U_\theta^*]=[U_\theta]^t.
$$
But
$$
[U_\theta]^t
=\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}
=[U_{-\theta}].
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{U_\theta^*=U_{-\theta}}.
$$
:::

<1>4. Rotating the orthonormal basis does not change the matrix of a rotation.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
\mathfrak B=(U_\phi\epsilon_1,U_\phi\epsilon_2).
$$
The change-of-basis matrix from $\mathfrak B$ to the standard basis is
$U_\phi$. Hence
$$
[U_\theta]_{\mathfrak B}
=U_\phi^{-1}U_\theta U_\phi.
$$
By steps <1>2--<1>3,
$$
U_\phi^{-1}U_\theta U_\phi
=U_{-\phi}U_\theta U_\phi
=U_{-\phi+\theta+\phi}
=U_\theta.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{
[U_\theta]_{\mathfrak B}
=\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
:::
