::: {.exercise}
Find a unitary matrix which is not orthogonal, and find an orthogonal matrix which is not unitary.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A unitary matrix need not be orthogonal.
::: {.proof}
Take the $1\times1$ complex matrix
$$
U=(i).
$$
Its conjugate transpose is $U^*=(-i)$, so
$$
U^*U=(-i)i=1.
$$
Thus $U$ is unitary. But orthogonality uses transpose rather than conjugate
transpose, and
$$
U^tU=i^2=-1\ne1.
$$
Hence $U$ is not orthogonal.
:::

<1>2. An orthogonal complex matrix need not be unitary.
::: {.proof}
Consider
$$
Q=
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt2&-i\\
i&\sqrt2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its two columns have transpose-dot-products
$$
2+i^2=1,
\qquad
(-i)^2+2=1,
$$
and their mutual transpose-dot-product is
$$
\sqrt2(-i)+i\sqrt2=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
Q^tQ=I,
$$
so $Q$ is orthogonal.

On the other hand, the Hermitian squared norm of its first column is
$$
|\sqrt2|^2+|i|^2=2+1=3,
$$
so its columns are not orthonormal for the Hermitian inner product. Thus
$$
Q^*Q\ne I,
$$
and $Q$ is not unitary.
:::
:::
