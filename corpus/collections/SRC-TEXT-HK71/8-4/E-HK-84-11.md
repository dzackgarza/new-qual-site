::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional inner product space over the field $F$, and let $L(V, V)$ be the space of linear operators on $V$.
Show that there is a unique inner product on $L(V, V)$ with the property that $||T_{\alpha, \beta}||^2 = ||\alpha||^2 ||\beta||^2$ for all $\alpha, \beta$ in $V$.
($T_{\alpha, \beta}$ is the operator defined in Exercise 10.) Find an isomorphism between $L(V, V)$ with this inner product and the space of $n \times n$ matrices over $F$, with the inner product $(A \mid B) = \operatorname{tr}(AB^*)$.
:::

::: solution
Define
$$
(S\mid T)_L=\operatorname{tr}(ST^*)
$$
for $S,T\in L(V,V)$.

<1>1. This formula defines an inner product on $L(V,V)$.
::: proof
Choose an orthonormal basis of $V$. If $S=(s_{ij})$ and $T=(t_{ij})$ in
that basis, then
$$
\operatorname{tr}(ST^*)
=\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}\overline{t_{ij}}.
$$
Thus $(\cdot\mid\cdot)_L$ is the standard inner product on the $n^2$ matrix
entries. In particular it is sesquilinear, conjugate-symmetric, and
$$
(T\mid T)_L=\sum_{i,j}|t_{ij}|^2>0
$$
for every nonzero $T$.
:::

<1>2. Rank-one operators have the required norm.
::: proof
Exercise 10 gives
$$
T_{\alpha,\beta}^*=T_{\beta,\alpha}.
$$
Hence
$$
T_{\alpha,\beta}T_{\alpha,\beta}^*
=T_{\alpha,\beta}T_{\beta,\alpha}
=\|\beta\|^2T_{\alpha,\alpha}.
$$
Taking traces and again using Exercise 10,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|T_{\alpha,\beta}\|_L^2
&=\operatorname{tr}(T_{\alpha,\beta}T_{\alpha,\beta}^*)\\
&=\|\beta\|^2\operatorname{tr}(T_{\alpha,\alpha})\\
&=\|\beta\|^2\|\alpha\|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus this inner product has the stated property.
:::

<1>3. The norm condition determines the inner product uniquely.
::: proof
Suppose $[\cdot\mid\cdot]$ is any inner product on $L(V,V)$ satisfying
$$
[T_{\alpha,\beta}\mid T_{\alpha,\beta}]
=\|\alpha\|^2\|\beta\|^2
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$.

Fix $\alpha$. The map $\beta\mapsto T_{\alpha,\beta}$ is conjugate-linear in
$\beta$. Polarizing the displayed norm identity in the $\beta$ variable
therefore determines
$$
[T_{\alpha,\beta}\mid T_{\alpha,\delta}]
=\|\alpha\|^2(\delta\mid\beta).
$$
Now fix $\beta,\delta$. The map $\alpha\mapsto T_{\alpha,\beta}$ is linear.
Polarizing the last identity in the $\alpha$ variable gives
$$
[T_{\alpha,\beta}\mid T_{\gamma,\delta}]
=(\alpha\mid\gamma)(\delta\mid\beta).
$$
Hence the inner product is uniquely determined on every pair of rank-one
operators.

Rank-one operators span $L(V,V)$: if $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is an orthonormal basis,
then $T_{e_i,e_j}$ is the matrix unit sending $e_j$ to $e_i$ and all other
$e_k$ to zero. Sesquilinearity therefore determines the inner product uniquely
on all of $L(V,V)$.
:::

<1>4. Matrix representation in an orthonormal basis is an isometric isomorphism.
::: proof
Fix an orthonormal basis $\mathfrak B$ of $V$ and define
$$
\Phi:L(V,V)\longrightarrow M_n(F),
\qquad
\Phi(T)=[T]_{\mathfrak B}.
$$
This is a linear isomorphism. Since $\mathfrak B$ is orthonormal,
$$
[T^*]_{\mathfrak B}=[T]_{\mathfrak B}^*.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
(S\mid T)_L
&=\operatorname{tr}(ST^*)\\
&=\operatorname{tr}\bigl([S]_{\mathfrak B}[T]_{\mathfrak B}^*\bigr)\\
&=(\Phi(S)\mid\Phi(T))_{M_n(F)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $\Phi$ is the required inner-product-space isomorphism.
:::
