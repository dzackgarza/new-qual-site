::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space and $T$ a positive linear operator on $V$.
Let $p_T$ be the inner product on $V$ defined by $p_T(\alpha, \beta) = (T\alpha \mid \beta)$.
Let $U$ be a linear operator on $V$ and $U^*$ its adjoint with respect to $( \mid )$.
Prove that $U$ is unitary with respect to the inner product $p_T$ if and only if $T = U^*TU$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Express preservation of $p_T$ in terms of the original inner product.
::: proof
By definition, $U$ is unitary for $p_T$ exactly when
$$
p_T(U\alpha,U\beta)=p_T(\alpha,\beta)
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta\in V$. Using
$p_T(\alpha,\beta)=(T\alpha\mid\beta)$, this is
$$
(TU\alpha\mid U\beta)=(T\alpha\mid\beta)
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$.
:::

<1>2. Move $U$ from the second slot by means of the adjoint.
::: proof
For arbitrary $x,y\in V$,
$$
(x\mid Uy)=(U^*x\mid y).
$$
Apply this with $x=TU\alpha$ and $y=\beta$. The condition from step <1>1
becomes
$$
(U^*TU\alpha\mid\beta)=(T\alpha\mid\beta)
$$
for every $\alpha,\beta$.
:::

<1>3. Equality of all matrix coefficients is equivalent to equality of the operators.
::: proof
If
$$
(U^*TU\alpha-T\alpha\mid\beta)=0
$$
for every $\beta$, then taking
$\beta=U^*TU\alpha-T\alpha$ gives
$$
\|U^*TU\alpha-T\alpha\|^2=0.
$$
Hence
$$
U^*TU\alpha=T\alpha
$$
for every $\alpha$, so
$$
U^*TU=T.
$$
The argument reverses verbatim: if $U^*TU=T$, then step <1>2 gives
$p_T(U\alpha,U\beta)=p_T(\alpha,\beta)$ for all $\alpha,\beta$.

Therefore
$$
\boxed{U\text{ is unitary for }p_T\iff T=U^*TU.}
$$
:::
:::
