::: {.exercise}
If $\theta$ is a real number, prove that the following matrices are unitarily equivalent
\[
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{bmatrix} e^{i\theta} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{-i\theta} \end{bmatrix}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
R_\theta=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

<1>1. Find an orthonormal eigenbasis over $\mathbb C$.
::: {.proof}
Put
$$
v_+=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom{1}{-i},
\qquad
v_-=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom{1}{i}.
$$
Then
$$
R_\theta v_+
=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\binom{\cos\theta+i\sin\theta}
{\sin\theta-i\cos\theta}
=e^{i\theta}v_+,
$$
and similarly
$$
R_\theta v_-=e^{-i\theta}v_-.
$$
Moreover
$$
v_+^*v_+=v_-^*v_-=1,
\qquad
v_+^*v_-=0.
$$
Hence $(v_+,v_-)$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb C^2$.
:::

<1>2. Use that eigenbasis to obtain a unitary equivalence.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
U=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
-i&i
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its columns are the orthonormal vectors from step <1>1, so $U$ is unitary.
The two eigenvector equations are exactly
$$
R_\theta U
=U
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i\theta}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Multiplying on the left by $U^*$ gives
$$
\boxed{
U^*R_\theta U
=\begin{pmatrix}
e^{i\theta}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta}
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
Thus the two matrices are unitarily equivalent.
:::
:::
