::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over the field $F$.
Show that $T \to T^t$ is an isomorphism of $L(V, V)$ onto $L(V^*, V^*)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.7 Exercise 7. Here $T^{t}\in L(V^{*},V^{*})$ is the transpose of $T$, defined by $(T^{t}g)(v)=g(Tv)$ for $g\in V^{*}$ and $v\in V$; $\mathcal{B}^{*}$ is the basis of $V^{*}$ dual to an ordered basis $\mathcal{B}=(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ of $V$, and $[S]$ denotes the matrix of an operator in the basis named.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #linear}

$T\mapsto T^{t}$ is a linear map $L(V,V)\to L(V^{*},V^{*})$.

::: pf-proof

For $g\in V^{*}$ and $v\in V$, $((aS+bT)^{t}g)(v)=g((aS+bT)v)=ag(Sv)+bg(Tv)=(aS^{t}g+bT^{t}g)(v)$, so $(aS+bT)^{t}=aS^{t}+bT^{t}$; and $T^{t}$ is linear on $V^{*}$ because $g\mapsto g\circ T$ is.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix}

$[T^{t}]_{\mathcal{B}^{*}}=[T]_{\mathcal{B}}^{t}$.

::: pf-proof

Write $A=[T]_{\mathcal{B}}$, so $Tv_j=\sum_i A_{ij}v_i$, and let $f_1,\dots,f_n$ be the dual basis, $f_i(v_j)=\delta_{ij}$. Then $(T^{t}f_j)(v_i)=f_j(Tv_i)=f_j(\sum_k A_{ki}v_k)=A_{ji}$, so $T^{t}f_j=\sum_i A_{ji}f_i$, whose coordinate column is the $j$-th column of $A^{t}$, namely $(A_{j1},\dots,A_{jn})^{t}$; that is the definition of $[T^{t}]_{\mathcal{B}^{*}}=A^{t}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #correspond}

Under the isomorphisms $L(V,V)\cong M_n(F)$, $S\mapsto[S]_{\mathcal{B}}$, and $L(V^{*},V^{*})\cong M_n(F)$, $R\mapsto[R]_{\mathcal{B}^{*}}$, the map $T\mapsto T^{t}$ corresponds to $A\mapsto A^{t}$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#matrix){.pf-ref} says exactly that the second isomorphism carries $T^{t}$ to the transpose of the image of $T$ under the first.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #iso}

$T\mapsto T^{t}$ is an isomorphism of $L(V,V)$ onto $L(V^{*},V^{*})$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#linear){.pf-ref} it is linear. Under the two isomorphisms of step [](#correspond){.pf-ref} it corresponds to transposition of $n\times n$ matrices, which is linear and satisfies $(A^{t})^{t}=A$; hence it is bijective with inverse $R\mapsto R^{t}$. A linear bijection of vector spaces is an isomorphism.

:::
