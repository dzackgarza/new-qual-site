::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the vector space of all polynomial functions over the field of real numbers.
Let $a$ and $b$ be fixed real numbers and let $f$ be the linear functional on $V$ defined by
$$
f(p) = \int_a^b p(x)\,dx.
$$
If $D$ is the differentiation operator on $V$, what is $D^t f$?
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and the functional below was recomputed for this pass.

Here $V$ is the space of all polynomial functions over $\RR$, $D$ is differentiation, and the transpose $D^{t}$ of the linear operator $D$ is defined by $(D^{t}f)(p)=f(Dp)$ for $f\in V^{*}$ and $p\in V$; the functional $f$ is fixed by $f(p)=\int_{a}^{b}p(x)\,dx$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #transpose}
$(D^{t}f)(p)=f(Dp)=\int_{a}^{b}p'(x)\,dx$.

::: pf-proof
The transpose acts by precomposition with $D$, so $(D^{t}f)(p)=f(Dp)$; the definition of $f$ applied to the polynomial $Dp=p'$ gives $\int_{a}^{b}p'(x)\,dx$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #evaluate}
$\int_{a}^{b}p'(x)\,dx=p(b)-p(a)$.

::: pf-proof
A polynomial has a derivative everywhere, hence so does $p$ on $[a,b]$, and $p$ is an antiderivative of $p'$ because differentiating a polynomial restores $p'$. The fundamental theorem of calculus therefore gives $\int_{a}^{b}p'(x)\,dx=p(b)-p(a)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$D^{t}f$ is the linear functional $p\mapsto p(b)-p(a)$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#transpose){.pf-ref} and [](#evaluate){.pf-ref}, $(D^{t}f)(p)=p(b)-p(a)$ for every $p\in V$, so $D^{t}f$ is evaluation at the two points $b$ and $a$ and subtracting; and that is linear, since $(p+q)(b)-(p+q)(a)=\bigl(p(b)-p(a)\bigr)+\bigl(q(b)-q(a)\bigr)$ and $(cp)(b)-(cp)(a)=c\bigl(p(b)-p(a)\bigr)$ for $c\in\RR$. Hence $D^{t}f\in V^{*}$ and is the functional displayed.
:::
