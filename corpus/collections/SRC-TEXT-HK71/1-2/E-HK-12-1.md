::: {.exercise}
Verify that the set of complex numbers of the form $x + y\sqrt{2}$, where $x$ and $y$ are rational, is a subfield of $\CC$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #definition-and-identities}
Definition and identities:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Let $F = \{x + y\sqrt{2} \mid x, y \in \QQ\} \subset \CC$.

::: pf-proof
definition of subset.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$0 = 0 + 0\sqrt{2} \in F$ and $1 = 1 + 0\sqrt{2} \in F$ since $0, 1 \in \QQ$.
Thus $F$ contains the additive and multiplicative identities.

::: pf-proof
$0, 1 \in \QQ$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Closure under subtraction and multiplication:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Let $\alpha = x_1 + y_1\sqrt{2} \in F$ and $\beta = x_2 + y_2\sqrt{2} \in F$ with $x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2 \in \QQ$.

::: pf-proof
setup.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The difference is:
$$
\alpha - \beta = (x_1 - x_2) + (y_1 - y_2)\sqrt{2}.
$$
Since $\QQ$ is a field, $x_1 - x_2 \in \QQ$ and $y_1 - y_2 \in \QQ$, so $\alpha - \beta \in F$.

::: pf-proof
closure of $\QQ$ under subtraction.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The product is:
$$
\alpha \beta = (x_1 + y_1\sqrt{2})(x_2 + y_2\sqrt{2}) = (x_1 x_2 + 2 y_1 y_2) + (x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1)\sqrt{2}.
$$
Since $x_1 x_2 + 2 y_1 y_2 \in \QQ$ and $x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1 \in \QQ$, we have $\alpha \beta \in F$.

::: pf-proof
ring operations in $\QQ$ and $(\sqrt{2})^2 = 2 \in \QQ$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #existence-of-inverses}
Existence of multiplicative inverses for non-zero elements:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Let $\alpha = x + y\sqrt{2} \in F \setminus \{0\}$.

::: pf-proof
non-zero element setup.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
We claim $x^2 - 2y^2 \neq 0$:
If $y = 0$, then $x \neq 0$, so $x^2 - 2y^2 = x^2 \neq 0$.
If $y \neq 0$, then $x^2 - 2y^2 = 0 \implies \sqrt{2} = \abs{x/y} \in \QQ$, contradicting the irrationality of $\sqrt{2}$.
Thus $x^2 - 2y^2 \in \QQ \setminus \{0\}$.

::: pf-proof
irrationality of $\sqrt{2}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Multiply numerator and denominator by the algebraic conjugate $x - y\sqrt{2}$:
$$
\frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{1}{x + y\sqrt{2}} = \frac{x - y\sqrt{2}}{(x + y\sqrt{2})(x - y\sqrt{2})} = \left(\frac{x}{x^2 - 2y^2}\right) + \left(\frac{-y}{x^2 - 2y^2}\right)\sqrt{2}.
$$
Since $\frac{x}{x^2 - 2y^2} \in \QQ$ and $\frac{-y}{x^2 - 2y^2} \in \QQ$, $\alpha^{-1} \in F$.

::: pf-proof
field axioms of $\QQ$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-qed
$F$ contains 1, is closed under subtraction, multiplication, and taking inverses of non-zero elements; hence $F = \QQ(\sqrt{2})$ is a subfield of $\CC$.

:::
