::: {.exercise}
Verify that the set of complex numbers of the form $x + y\sqrt{2}$, where $x$ and $y$ are rational, is a subfield of $\CC$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Definition and identities:
<2>1. Let $F = \{x + y\sqrt{2} \mid x, y \in \mathbb{Q}\} \subset \mathbb{C}$.
::: {.proof}
definition of subset.
:::
<2>2. $0 = 0 + 0\sqrt{2} \in F$ and $1 = 1 + 0\sqrt{2} \in F$ since $0, 1 \in \mathbb{Q}$.
Thus $F$ contains the additive and multiplicative identities.
::: {.proof}
$0, 1 \in \mathbb{Q}$.
:::

<1>2. Closure under subtraction and multiplication:
<2>1. Let $\alpha = x_1 + y_1\sqrt{2} \in F$ and $\beta = x_2 + y_2\sqrt{2} \in F$ with $x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2 \in \mathbb{Q}$.
::: {.proof}
setup.
:::
<2>2. The difference is:
\[
\alpha - \beta = (x_1 - x_2) + (y_1 - y_2)\sqrt{2}.
\]
Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is a field, $x_1 - x_2 \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $y_1 - y_2 \in \mathbb{Q}$, so $\alpha - \beta \in F$.
::: {.proof}
closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ under subtraction.
:::
<2>3. The product is:
\[
\alpha \beta = (x_1 + y_1\sqrt{2})(x_2 + y_2\sqrt{2}) = (x_1 x_2 + 2 y_1 y_2) + (x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1)\sqrt{2}.
\]
Since $x_1 x_2 + 2 y_1 y_2 \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1 \in \mathbb{Q}$, we have $\alpha \beta \in F$.
::: {.proof}
ring operations in $\mathbb{Q}$ and $(\sqrt{2})^2 = 2 \in \mathbb{Q}$.
:::

<1>3. Existence of multiplicative inverses for non-zero elements:
<2>1. Let $\alpha = x + y\sqrt{2} \in F \setminus \{0\}$.
::: {.proof}
non-zero element setup.
:::
<2>2. We claim $x^2 - 2y^2 \neq 0$:
If $y = 0$, then $x \neq 0$, so $x^2 - 2y^2 = x^2 \neq 0$.
If $y \neq 0$, then $x^2 - 2y^2 = 0 \implies \sqrt{2} = |x/y| \in \mathbb{Q}$, contradicting the irrationality of $\sqrt{2}$.
Thus $x^2 - 2y^2 \in \mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0\}$.
::: {.proof}
irrationality of $\sqrt{2}$.
:::
<2>3. Multiply numerator and denominator by the algebraic conjugate $x - y\sqrt{2}$:
\[
\frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{1}{x + y\sqrt{2}} = \frac{x - y\sqrt{2}}{(x + y\sqrt{2})(x - y\sqrt{2})} = \left(\frac{x}{x^2 - 2y^2}\right) + \left(\frac{-y}{x^2 - 2y^2}\right)\sqrt{2}.
\]
Since $\frac{x}{x^2 - 2y^2} \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $\frac{-y}{x^2 - 2y^2} \in \mathbb{Q}$, $\alpha^{-1} \in F$.
::: {.proof}
field axioms of $\mathbb{Q}$.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
$F$ contains 1, is closed under subtraction, multiplication, and taking inverses of non-zero elements; hence $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>3 (Subfield Criterion).
:::
:::
