::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a set which contains exactly two elements, $0$ and $1$. Define an addition and multiplication by the tables
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
+ & 0 & 1 \\
\hline
0 & 0 & 1 \\
1 & 1 & 0
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\cdot & 0 & 1 \\
\hline
0 & 0 & 0 \\
1 & 0 & 1
\end{array}
.\]
Verify that the set $F$, together with these two operations, is a field.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Addition is commutative and associative, with identity $0$ and each element its own inverse.
::: {.proof}
the table is symmetric; $0 + a = a$; $1 + 1 = 0$ so $1$ is its own inverse.
:::

<1>2. Multiplication is commutative and associative, with identity $1$.
::: {.proof}
the table is symmetric; $1 \cdot a = a$; associativity is checked directly (the only nontrivial case is $1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = 1$).
:::

<1>3. Multiplication distributes over addition.
::: {.proof}
$a(b + c) = ab + ac$ holds for all $a, b, c \in \{0, 1\}$ by direct check (the only case needing care is $1 \cdot (1 + 1) = 1 \cdot 0 = 0 = 1 + 1 = 1 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 1$).
:::

<1>4. Every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse.
::: {.proof}
the only nonzero element is $1$, and $1 \cdot 1 = 1$.
:::

<1>5. Hence $F$ is a field.
::: {.proof}
<1>1–<1>4 verify the field axioms; this is the field $\FF_2$ with two elements.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>5.
:::
:::
