::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a complex vector space and $W$ a subset of $V$ with the following properties:

(a) $W$ is a real vector space with respect to the operations defined in $V$.

(b) For each $\alpha$ in $V$ there exist unique vectors $\beta, \gamma$ in $W$ such that $\alpha = \beta + i\gamma$.

Show that the equation $J\alpha = \beta - i\gamma$ defines a conjugation on $V$ such that $J\alpha = \alpha$ if and only if $\alpha$ belongs to $W$, and show also that $J$ is the only conjugation on $V$ with this property.
:::

::: solution
For each $\alpha\in V$, let
$$
\alpha=\beta+i\gamma,
\qquad
\beta,\gamma\in W
$$
be the unique decomposition supplied by the hypothesis, and define
$$
J\alpha=\beta-i\gamma.
$$

<1>1. The map $J$ is additive and conjugate homogeneous.
::: proof
If
$$
\alpha=\beta+i\gamma,
\qquad
\alpha'=\beta'+i\gamma',
$$
with all four vectors in $W$, then
$$
\alpha+\alpha'
=(\beta+\beta')+i(\gamma+\gamma').
$$
Since $W$ is a real vector space, uniqueness of the decomposition gives
$$
J(\alpha+\alpha')
=(\beta+\beta')-i(\gamma+\gamma')
=J\alpha+J\alpha'.
$$

Now let $c=a+ib\in\mathbb C$, with $a,b\in\mathbb R$. Then
$$
c\alpha
=(a\beta-b\gamma)+i(b\beta+a\gamma),
$$
and both displayed components lie in $W$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
J(c\alpha)
&=(a\beta-b\gamma)-i(b\beta+a\gamma)\\
&=(a-ib)(\beta-i\gamma)\\
&=\overline c\,J\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. The map is an involution and its fixed set is exactly $W$.
::: proof
If $\alpha=\beta+i\gamma$, then
$$
J\alpha=\beta-i\gamma=\beta+i(-\gamma),
$$
so applying $J$ again gives
$$
J(J\alpha)=\beta+i\gamma=\alpha.
$$
Hence $J^2=I$.

If $\alpha\in W$, its unique decomposition is $\alpha=\alpha+i0$, so
$J\alpha=\alpha$. Conversely, if $J\alpha=\alpha$ and
$\alpha=\beta+i\gamma$, then
$$
\beta-i\gamma=\beta+i\gamma,
$$
so $2i\gamma=0$, hence $\gamma=0$ and $\alpha=\beta\in W$. Thus
$$
\boxed{W=\{\alpha:J\alpha=\alpha\}.}
$$
:::

<1>3. This conjugation is unique with fixed set $W$.
::: proof
Let $K$ be any conjugation whose fixed set is $W$. For
$\alpha=\beta+i\gamma$ with $\beta,\gamma\in W$,
$$
K\alpha=K\beta+K(i\gamma)
=\beta-i\gamma
=J\alpha.
$$
Thus $K=J$. Hence the displayed construction is the unique conjugation with
fixed set $W$ [@HK71].
:::
:::
