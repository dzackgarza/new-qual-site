::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional real subspace of a complex vector space $V$.
Show that $W$ satisfies condition (b) of Exercise 14 if and only if every basis of $W$ is also a basis of $V$.
:::

::: solution
Condition (b) of Exercise 14 says that every $\alpha\in V$ has a unique
expression
$$
\alpha=\beta+i\gamma,
\qquad \beta,\gamma\in W.
$$

<1>1. If condition (b) holds, every real basis of $W$ is a complex basis of $V$.
::: proof
Let
$$
\mathcal B=(w_1,\ldots,w_m)
$$
be any basis of the finite-dimensional real vector space $W$. Given
$\alpha\in V$, write uniquely
$$
\alpha=\beta+i\gamma,
\qquad
\beta,\gamma\in W.
$$
Expanding
$$
\beta=\sum_j a_jw_j,
\qquad
\gamma=\sum_j b_jw_j,
\qquad a_j,b_j\in\mathbb R,
$$
gives
$$
\alpha=\sum_j(a_j+ib_j)w_j.
$$
Thus $\mathcal B$ spans $V$ over $\mathbb C$.

For complex linear independence, suppose
$$
\sum_j(a_j+ib_j)w_j=0.
$$
Then
$$
\sum_j a_jw_j+i\sum_jb_jw_j=0.
$$
By uniqueness in condition (b), both real components vanish. Since
$\mathcal B$ is real-linearly independent, all $a_j$ and $b_j$ are zero.
Hence $\mathcal B$ is a complex basis of $V$.
:::

<1>2. If every real basis of $W$ is a complex basis of $V$, then condition (b) holds.
::: proof
Choose a real basis
$$
\mathcal B=(w_1,\ldots,w_m)
$$
of $W$. By hypothesis it is also a complex basis of $V$. Therefore every
$\alpha\in V$ has unique complex coordinates
$$
\alpha=\sum_j z_jw_j,
\qquad
z_j=a_j+ib_j,\quad a_j,b_j\in\mathbb R.
$$
Set
$$
\beta=\sum_j a_jw_j,
\qquad
\gamma=\sum_j b_jw_j.
$$
Then $\beta,\gamma\in W$ and
$$
\alpha=\beta+i\gamma.
$$

If also $\alpha=\beta'+i\gamma'$ with $\beta',\gamma'\in W$, expand
$\beta',\gamma'$ in the same real basis. Equality of the resulting complex
coordinates forces equality of all real and imaginary coefficients, so
$\beta'=\beta$ and $\gamma'=\gamma$. Thus the decomposition is unique.

Hence $W$ satisfies condition (b) if and only if every real basis of $W$ is a
complex basis of $V$ [@HK71].
:::
:::
