::: {.exercise}
Describe explicitly all inner products on $\RR^1$ and on $C^1$.
:::


::: solution
All inner products are obtained from a single positive real constant $c$:
$$
\boxed{(x\mid y)=cxy\quad\text{on }\mathbb R,}
$$
and, with Hoffman--Kunze's convention of linearity in the first variable,
$$
\boxed{(z\mid w)=c z\overline w\quad\text{on }\mathbb C,}
$$
where in both cases $c>0$.

<1>1. Real one-dimensional case.
::: proof
Let $e=1$ and put $c=(e\mid e)$. Positive definiteness gives $c>0$. Since
$x=xe$ and $y=ye$, bilinearity gives
$$
(x\mid y)=xy(e\mid e)=cxy.
$$
Conversely, for any $c>0$, this formula is bilinear, symmetric, and satisfies
$(x\mid x)=cx^2>0$ for $x\ne0$, so it is an inner product.
:::

<1>2. Complex one-dimensional case.
::: proof
Again set $e=1$ and $c=(e\mid e)>0$. For $z,w\in\mathbb C$, linearity in the
first variable and conjugate linearity in the second give
$$
(z\mid w)=(ze\mid we)=z\overline w(e\mid e)=cz\overline w.
$$
Conversely this formula has the required sesquilinearity and conjugate symmetry,
and
$$
(z\mid z)=c|z|^2>0
$$
for $z\ne0$. Hence these are exactly all inner products on $\mathbb C^1$ [@HK71].
:::
:::
