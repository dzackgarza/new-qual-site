::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and let $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n\}$ be a basis for $V$.
Let $(\quad \diracbar \quad)$ be an inner product on $V$.
If $c_1, \ldots, c_n$ are any $n$ scalars, show that there is exactly one vector $\alpha$ in $V$ such that $(\alpha \mid \alpha_j) = c_j$, $j = 1, \ldots, n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the coordinate map with respect to a basis is an injective linear map between spaces of equal finite dimension, hence bijective.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #linear}

$\Phi(\alpha)=\big((\alpha\mid\alpha_{1}),\dots,(\alpha\mid\alpha_{n})\big)$ is a linear map $V\to F^{n}$.

::: pf-proof

The inner product of Hoffman and Kunze is linear in its first argument, so $(\alpha+c\beta\mid\alpha_{j})=(\alpha\mid\alpha_{j})+c(\beta\mid\alpha_{j})$ for all $j$, and $F^{n}$ carries the componentwise operations. Hence $\Phi(\alpha+c\beta)=\Phi(\alpha)+c\,\Phi(\beta)$, and $\Phi$ takes values in $F^{n}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #injective}

$\Phi$ is injective.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $\Phi(\alpha)=0$, that is $(\alpha\mid\alpha_{j})=0$ for every $j$. An arbitrary $\beta\in V$ is $\beta=\sum_{j}c_{j}\alpha_{j}$ because the $\alpha_{j}$ form a basis, so, by additivity and conjugate-homogeneity of the inner product in its second argument,
$$
(\alpha\mid\beta)=\sum_{j}\overline{c_{j}}\,(\alpha\mid\alpha_{j})=0.
$$
Taking $\beta=\alpha$ gives $(\alpha\mid\alpha)$ $=0$, and positive definiteness of an inner product then forces $\alpha=0$. So $\ker\Phi=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #surjective}

$\Phi$ is bijective.

::: pf-proof

$V$ is finite-dimensional with basis $\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}$, so $\dim_{F}V=n=\dim_{F}F^{n}$. A linear map between finite-dimensional spaces of equal dimension is injective exactly when it is surjective: by rank-nullity, $\dim\ker\Phi+\dim\operatorname{range}\Phi=n$, and injectivity makes $\dim\ker\Phi=0$, so $\dim\operatorname{range}\Phi=n$ and the range is all of $F^{n}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

For prescribed $c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\in F$ there is exactly one $\alpha\in V$ with $(\alpha\mid\alpha_{j})=c_{j}$ for all $j$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#surjective){.pf-ref} the tuple $(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})$ has a preimage $\alpha$ under $\Phi$, which is the required system of equations; and by step [](#injective){.pf-ref} a preimage is unique, since two preimages differ by an element of $\ker\Phi=0$. So
$$
\boxed{\exists!\,\alpha\in V:\ (\alpha\mid\alpha_{j})=c_{j},\quad j=1,\dots,n.}
$$

:::
