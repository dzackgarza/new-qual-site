::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be a $2 \times 2$ matrix with real entries.
For $X$, $Y$ in $\RR^{2 \times 1}$ let
\[
f_A(X, Y) = Y^t A X.
\]
Show that $f_A$ is an inner product on $\RR^{2 \times 1}$ if and only if $A = A^t$, $A_{11} > 0$, $A_{22} > 0$, and $\det A > 0$.
:::


::: solution
Write
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The form $f_A(X,Y)=Y^tAX$ is an inner product exactly when
$$
\boxed{A=A^t,\qquad a>0,\qquad d>0,\qquad \det A>0.}
$$

<1>1. Symmetry of the form is equivalent to symmetry of $A$.
::: proof
For all column vectors $X,Y$,
$$
f_A(X,Y)=Y^tAX,
\qquad
f_A(Y,X)=X^tAY=Y^tA^tX.
$$
Thus $f_A(X,Y)=f_A(Y,X)$ for every $X,Y$ if and only if
$Y^t(A-A^t)X=0$ for every $X,Y$, which is equivalent to $A=A^t$.
Bilinearity is automatic for every real matrix $A$.
:::

<1>2. Positive definiteness implies the three numerical inequalities.
::: proof
Assume $f_A$ is an inner product. By step <1>1 write
$A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&d\end{pmatrix}$. Testing the nonzero standard basis
vectors gives $a>0$ and $d>0$. For every $t\in\mathbb R$,
$$
0<f_A((t,1)^t,(t,1)^t)=at^2+2bt+d.
$$
A real quadratic with positive leading coefficient that is strictly positive
for every $t$ has negative discriminant, so
$$
4b^2-4ad<0,
$$
i.e. $ad-b^2=\det A>0$.
:::

<1>3. The stated conditions imply positive definiteness.
::: proof
Conversely assume $A=A^t$, $a>0$, $d>0$, and $ad-b^2>0$. For
$X=(x,y)^t$,
$$
X^tAX=ax^2+2bxy+dy^2
=a\left(x+\frac ba y\right)^2+\frac{ad-b^2}{a}y^2.
$$
Both coefficients are positive. Hence this expression is nonnegative and can
vanish only when $y=0$ and then $x=0$. Therefore $f_A$ is positive definite.
Together with bilinearity and symmetry, it is an inner product [@HK71].
:::
:::
