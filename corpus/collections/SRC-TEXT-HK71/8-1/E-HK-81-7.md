::: {.exercise}
Let $( \mid )$ be the standard inner product on $C^2$.
Prove that there is no non-zero linear operator on $C^2$ such that $(\alpha \mid T\alpha) = 0$ for every $\alpha$ in $C^2$.
Generalize.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The Complex Polarization Identity:
<2>1. Let $V$ be an inner product space over the complex field $\mathbb{C}$ with inner product $(\cdot \mid \cdot)$, and let $T: V \to V$ be a linear operator.
::: {.proof}
setup.
:::
<2>2. For any $\alpha, \beta \in V$ and each $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, expand the quadratic form at $\alpha + i^k \beta$:
\[
\big(\alpha + i^k \beta \mid T(\alpha + i^k \beta)\big) = (\alpha \mid T\alpha) + i^k (\alpha \mid T\beta) + \overline{i^k} (\beta \mid T\alpha) + (\beta \mid T\beta).
\]
::: {.proof}
conjugate-linearity in the first argument and linearity in the second argument.
:::
<2>3. Multiply by $i^k$ and sum over $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$:
- $\sum_{k=0}^3 i^k = 1 + i - 1 - i = 0$,
- $\sum_{k=0}^3 i^{2k} = 1 - 1 + 1 - 1 = 0$,
- $\sum_{k=0}^3 i^k \overline{i^k} = \sum_{k=0}^3 |i^k|^2 = 4$.
Thus:
\[
\sum_{k=0}^3 i^k \big(\alpha + i^k \beta \mid T(\alpha + i^k \beta)\big) = 4 (\alpha \mid T\beta).
\]
::: {.proof}
sum of powers of $i$.
:::

<1>2. Proof that $(v \mid Tv) = 0$ for all $v$ forces $T = 0$:
<2>1. By hypothesis, $(v \mid Tv) = 0$ for all $v \in V$.
Applying this to the four vectors $v_k = \alpha + i^k \beta$ gives:
\[
(\alpha \mid T\beta) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{k=0}^3 i^k (v_k \mid T v_k) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{k=0}^3 i^k \cdot 0 = 0 \quad \text{for all } \alpha, \beta \in V.
\]
::: {.proof}
polarization identity from <1>1.
:::
<2>2. For each $\beta \in V$, choose $\alpha = T\beta$:
\[
\|T\beta\|^2 = (T\beta \mid T\beta) = 0 \implies T\beta = 0.
\]
::: {.proof}
positive definiteness of inner products.
:::
<2>3. Since $T\beta = 0$ for every $\beta \in V$, $T$ is the zero operator ($T = 0$).
::: {.proof}
definition of the zero operator.
:::

<1>3. Generalization and Comparison with Real Spaces:
<2>1. **Generalization:** The result holds for any complex inner product space $V$ (of arbitrary dimension):
If $(v \mid Tv) = 0$ for all $v \in V$, then $T = 0$.
::: {.proof}
polarization holds over any complex inner product space.
:::
<2>2. **Real vs Complex Distinction:** Over a real inner product space, this property fails:
For example, on $\mathbb{R}^2$ with standard inner product, the $90^\circ$ rotation $T(x, y) = (-y, x)$ satisfies $(v \mid Tv) = -xy + yx = 0$ for all $v \in \mathbb{R}^2$, yet $T \neq 0$.
::: {.proof}
skew-symmetric operators over $\mathbb{R}$.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
There is no non-zero linear operator on $\mathbb{C}^2$ (or any complex inner product space) such that $(\alpha \mid T\alpha) = 0$ for all $\alpha$. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>3.
:::
