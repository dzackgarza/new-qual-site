::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a real or complex vector space with an inner product.
Show that the quadratic form determined by the inner product satisfies the parallelogram law
$$
\norm{\alpha + \beta}^2 + \norm{\alpha - \beta}^2 = 2\norm{\alpha}^2 + 2\norm{\beta}^2.
$$
:::


::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §8.1 Exercise 9, p. 276. The two expansions and their sum are the whole proof; both hold over $\RR$ and over $\CC$ because only additivity in each variable and conjugate symmetry are used.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #plus}
$\norm{\alpha+\beta}^2=\inner{\alpha}{\alpha}+\inner{\alpha}{\beta}+\inner{\beta}{\alpha}+\inner{\beta}{\beta}$.

::: pf-proof
$\norm{\gamma}^2=\inner{\gamma}{\gamma}$ by definition, and expanding $\inner{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha+\beta}$ by additivity in each argument gives the four terms.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #minus}
$\norm{\alpha-\beta}^2=\inner{\alpha}{\alpha}-\inner{\alpha}{\beta}-\inner{\beta}{\alpha}+\inner{\beta}{\beta}$.

::: pf-proof
Same expansion with $\beta$ replaced by $-\beta$, using that the inner product is additive and that scalars pull out of the first argument and their conjugates out of the second; for the real scalar $-1$ both give a factor $-1$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #sum}
Adding the two identities gives $\norm{\alpha+\beta}^2+\norm{\alpha-\beta}^2=2\norm{\alpha}^2+2\norm{\beta}^2$.

::: pf-proof
The mixed terms $\inner{\alpha}{\beta}$ and $\inner{\beta}{\alpha}$ occur with opposite signs in the two expansions and cancel; what remains is twice $\inner{\alpha}{\alpha}$ plus twice $\inner{\beta}{\beta}$, that is $2\norm{\alpha}^2+2\norm{\beta}^2$.
:::
:::
