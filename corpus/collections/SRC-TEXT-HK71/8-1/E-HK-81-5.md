::: {.exercise}
Let $( \mid )$ be the standard inner product on $\RR^2$.

(a) Let $\alpha = (1, 2)$, $\beta = (-1, 1)$.
If $\gamma$ is a vector such that $(\alpha \mid \gamma) = -1$ and $(\beta \mid \gamma) = 3$, find $\gamma$.

(b) Show that for any $\alpha$ in $\RR^2$ we have $\alpha = (\alpha \mid \epsilon_1)\epsilon_1 + (\alpha \mid \epsilon_2)\epsilon_2$.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. The vector in part (a) is $\gamma=(-7/3,2/3)$.
::: proof
Write $\gamma=(x,y)$. Under the standard inner product,
$$
(\alpha\mid\gamma)=x+2y=-1,
\qquad
(\beta\mid\gamma)=-x+y=3.
$$
Adding these equations gives $3y=2$, so $y=2/3$, and then
$x=-1-4/3=-7/3$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\gamma=\left(-\frac73,\frac23\right)}.
$$
Direct substitution checks both prescribed inner products.
:::

<1>2. Every vector expands in the standard orthonormal basis by its inner products.
::: proof
If $\alpha=(a,b)$, then for $\epsilon_1=(1,0)$ and
$\epsilon_2=(0,1)$,
$$
(\alpha\mid\epsilon_1)=a,
\qquad
(\alpha\mid\epsilon_2)=b.
$$
Therefore
$$
(\alpha\mid\epsilon_1)\epsilon_1+(\alpha\mid\epsilon_2)\epsilon_2
=a(1,0)+b(0,1)=(a,b)=\alpha,
$$
as required [@HK71].
:::
:::
