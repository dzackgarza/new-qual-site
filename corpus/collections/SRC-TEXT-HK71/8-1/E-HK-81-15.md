::: {.exercise}
Find all conjugations on $C^1$ and $C^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. All conjugations on $\mathbb C^1$ are
$$
\boxed{J_u(z)=u\overline z,\qquad |u|=1.}
$$
::: {.proof}
Let $J$ be a conjugation of $\mathbb C$. Put $u=J(1)$. Conjugate homogeneity
gives
$$
J(z)=J(z\cdot1)=\overline z\,u.
$$
The condition $J^2=I$ gives
$$
1=J^2(1)=J(u)=\overline u\,u=|u|^2,
$$
so $|u|=1$.

Conversely, if $|u|=1$, then $J_u(z)=u\overline z$ is additive and conjugate
linear, and
$$
J_u^2(z)
=u\overline{u\overline z}
=u\overline u\,z
=z.
$$
Thus every such $J_u$ is a conjugation, and the list is complete.
:::

<1>2. All conjugations on $\mathbb C^2$ have the form
$$
\boxed{J_P(z)=P\,\overline{P^{-1}z},\qquad P\in GL_2(\mathbb C).}
$$
::: {.proof}
Fix $P\in GL_2(\mathbb C)$. The map $J_P$ is additive and conjugate linear,
and
$$
J_P^2(z)
=P\,\overline{P^{-1}P\,\overline{P^{-1}z}}
=P\,\overline{\overline{P^{-1}z}}
=z.
$$
So $J_P$ is a conjugation.

Conversely, let $J$ be any conjugation. By Exercise 13, its fixed-point set
$W$ is a real form of $\mathbb C^2$: every vector is uniquely
$\beta+i\gamma$ with $\beta,\gamma\in W$ [@HK71]. Thus
$\dim_{\mathbb R}W=2$. Choose a real basis $w_1,w_2$ of $W$ and let
$P$ be the complex matrix whose columns are $w_1,w_2$. Exercise 16, or the
same uniqueness argument, shows that $w_1,w_2$ are a complex basis, so
$P\in GL_2(\mathbb C)$.

If $z=P\xi$, with $\xi\in\mathbb C^2$, then writing the coordinates of
$\xi$ into real and imaginary parts shows
$$
J(z)=P\overline\xi
=P\,\overline{P^{-1}z}
=J_P(z).
$$
Hence every conjugation has the displayed form.
:::

<1>3. The parameterization by $P$ is unique up to a real change of basis.
::: {.proof}
The fixed-point set of $J_P$ is
$$
P\mathbb R^2.
$$
Therefore $J_P=J_Q$ if and only if
$$
P\mathbb R^2=Q\mathbb R^2.
$$
This holds exactly when
$$
Q=PR
$$
for some $R\in GL_2(\mathbb R)$: such an $R$ merely changes the real basis of
the same fixed real form. Thus the family above lists all conjugations without
ambiguity once one identifies $P$ modulo right multiplication by
$GL_2(\mathbb R)$.
:::
:::
