::: {.exercise}
In Example 2 the inner product on $\RR^2$ is
$$
((x_1,x_2)\mid(y_1,y_2))
=x_1y_1-x_2y_1-x_1y_2+4x_2y_2.
$$
Let $B$ be the standard ordered basis for $\RR^2$.
Find the matrix of this inner product relative to $B$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Let $e_1=(1,0)$ and $e_2=(0,1)$, and write
$$
((x_1,x_2)\mid(y_1,y_2))=x_1y_1-x_2y_1-x_1y_2+4x_2y_2
$$
for the inner product of the exercise.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #definition}
The matrix of the inner product relative to $B=(e_1,e_2)$ is $G=\bigl((e_i\mid e_j)\bigr)_{i,j=1,2}$, and for column vectors $X,Y$ of coordinates one has $(X\mid Y)=X^{t}GY$.

::: pf-proof
The matrix of a bilinear form in an ordered basis is defined by $G_{ij}=(e_i\mid e_j)$; then for $X=\sum_ix_ie_i$ and $Y=\sum_jy_je_j$, bilinearity gives $(X\mid Y)=\sum_{i,j}x_iy_j(e_i\mid e_j)=X^{t}GY$. This is the definition being applied; the point of the exercise is to compute the entries.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #entries}
$(e_1\mid e_1)=1$, $(e_1\mid e_2)=-1$, $(e_2\mid e_1)=-1$, $(e_2\mid e_2)=4$.

::: pf-proof
Substitute into the displayed formula. With $(x_1,x_2)=(1,0)$ and $(y_1,y_2)=(1,0)$: $1\cdot1-0\cdot1-1\cdot0+4\cdot0\cdot0=1$. With $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$: $1\cdot0-0\cdot0-1\cdot1+4\cdot0\cdot1=-1$. With $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$: $0\cdot1-1\cdot1-0\cdot0+4\cdot1\cdot0=-1$. With $(0,1)$ and $(0,1)$: $0\cdot0-1\cdot0-0\cdot1+4\cdot1\cdot1=4$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix}
$G=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\ -1&4\end{pmatrix}$, and $X^{t}GY=x_1y_1-x_1y_2-x_2y_1+4x_2y_2$.

::: pf-proof
Assemble the four values of step [](#entries){.pf-ref} into $G$ by step [](#definition){.pf-ref}. Then
$$
X^{t}GY=x_1(1\cdot y_1+(-1)\cdot y_2)+x_2((-1)\cdot y_1+4\cdot y_2)=x_1y_1-x_1y_2-x_2y_1+4x_2y_2,
$$
which is the given inner product, so the matrix does represent it. Notice $G^{t}=G$, as it must be for a symmetric bilinear form, and $G$ is invertible with determinant $4-1=3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The matrix of the inner product relative to the standard ordered basis is $\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\ -1&4\end{pmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#matrix){.pf-ref} exhibits the matrix and verifies that it reproduces the inner product, which is the answer to the exercise. The computation used only the definition of the matrix of a bilinear form and the four values of the given formula.
:::
