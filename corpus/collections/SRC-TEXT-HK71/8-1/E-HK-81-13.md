::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a complex vector space.
A function $J$ from $V$ into $V$ is called a conjugation if $J(\alpha + \beta) = J(\alpha) + J(\beta)$, $J(c\alpha) = \overline{c}J(\alpha)$, and $J(J(\alpha)) = \alpha$, for all scalars $c$ and all $\alpha, \beta$ in $V$.
If $J$ is a conjugation show that:

(a) The set $W$ of all $\alpha$ in $V$ such that $J\alpha = \alpha$ is a vector space over $\RR$ with respect to the operations defined in $V$.

(b) For each $\alpha$ in $V$ there exist unique vectors $\beta, \gamma$ in $W$ such that $\alpha = \beta + i\gamma$.
:::
