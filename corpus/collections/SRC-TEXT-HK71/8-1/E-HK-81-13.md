::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a complex vector space.
A function $J$ from $V$ into $V$ is called a conjugation if $J(\alpha + \beta) = J(\alpha) + J(\beta)$, $J(c\alpha) = \overline{c}J(\alpha)$, and $J(J(\alpha)) = \alpha$, for all scalars $c$ and all $\alpha, \beta$ in $V$.
If $J$ is a conjugation show that:

(a) The set $W$ of all $\alpha$ in $V$ such that $J\alpha = \alpha$ is a vector space over $\RR$ with respect to the operations defined in $V$.

(b) For each $\alpha$ in $V$ there exist unique vectors $\beta, \gamma$ in $W$ such that $\alpha = \beta + i\gamma$.
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
W=\{\alpha\in V:J\alpha=\alpha\}.
$$

<1>1. The fixed-point set $W$ is a real vector subspace of $V$.
::: proof
Since $J(0)=0$, one has $0\in W$. If $\alpha,\beta\in W$, then
$$
J(\alpha+\beta)=J\alpha+J\beta=\alpha+\beta,
$$
so $\alpha+\beta\in W$.

If $r\in\mathbb R$ and $\alpha\in W$, then $\overline r=r$, hence
$$
J(r\alpha)=\overline r\,J\alpha=r\alpha.
$$
Thus $r\alpha\in W$. Therefore $W$ is a vector space over $\mathbb R$.
:::

<1>2. Every vector has a decomposition into fixed real and imaginary parts.
::: proof
For $\alpha\in V$, define
$$
\beta=\frac{\alpha+J\alpha}{2},
\qquad
\gamma=\frac{\alpha-J\alpha}{2i}.
$$
Using conjugate linearity and $J^2=I$,
$$
J\beta
=\frac{J\alpha+\alpha}{2}
=\beta.
$$
Also, because $\overline{1/(2i)}=-1/(2i)$,
$$
J\gamma
=-\frac{J\alpha-\alpha}{2i}
=\frac{\alpha-J\alpha}{2i}
=\gamma.
$$
Thus $\beta,\gamma\in W$. Directly,
$$
\beta+i\gamma
=\frac{\alpha+J\alpha}{2}
+\frac{\alpha-J\alpha}{2}
=\alpha.
$$
:::

<1>3. The decomposition is unique.
::: proof
Suppose
$$
\alpha=\beta+i\gamma
=\beta'+i\gamma',
\qquad
\beta,\gamma,\beta',\gamma'\in W.
$$
Applying $J$ gives
$$
J\alpha=\beta-i\gamma=\beta'-i\gamma'.
$$
Adding this equality to the original one gives $2\beta=2\beta'$, hence
$\beta=\beta'$. Subtracting gives $2i\gamma=2i\gamma'$, hence
$\gamma=\gamma'$. Therefore the representation
$$
\boxed{\alpha=\beta+i\gamma,\qquad \beta,\gamma\in W}
$$
is unique [@HK71].
:::
:::
