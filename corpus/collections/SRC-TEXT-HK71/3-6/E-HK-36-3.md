::: {.exercise}
Let $S$ be a set, $F$ a field, and $V(S; F)$ the space of all functions from $S$ into $F$:
$$
(f + g)(x) = f(x) + g(x), \qquad (cf)(x) = cf(x).
$$
Let $W$ be any $n$-dimensional subspace of $V(S; F)$.
Show that there exist points $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ in $S$ and functions $f_1, \ldots, f_n$ in $W$ such that $f_i(x_j) = \delta_{ij}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.6 Exercise 3.

Let $S$ be a set, $F$ a field, $V(S;F)$ the space of all functions $S\to F$ with pointwise operations, and $W\subset V(S;F)$ a subspace with $\dim W=n<\infty$. For $x\in S$ let $e_{x}\colon W\to F$ be evaluation at $x$, $e_{x}(f)=f(x)$; it is linear because the operations on $V(S;F)$ are pointwise.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #double-dual}
For each nonzero $f\in W$ there is a linear functional $\lambda\in W^{*}$ with $\lambda(f)\neq0$; equivalently, the map $W\to W^{**}$, $f\mapsto\bigl(\lambda\mapsto\lambda(f)\bigr)$, is an isomorphism.

::: pf-proof
The map displayed is linear and injective: if $\lambda(f)=0$ for every $\lambda\in W^{*}$ then $f=0$, since otherwise $f$ could be extended to a basis of $W$ and the coordinate functional at $f$ would be nonzero on it. Both $W$ and $W^{**}$ have dimension $n<\infty$, so an injective linear map between them is an isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #spanning}
The evaluation functionals $\{e_{x}\mid x\in S\}$ span $W^{*}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $U$ be their span and suppose $U\neq W^{*}$. Extend a basis of $U$ to a basis of $W^{*}$; the linear functional on $W^{*}$ that is $1$ on the added basis vectors and $0$ on the basis of $U$ is a nonzero element $\psi\in W^{**}$ vanishing on $U$. By step [](#double-dual){.pf-ref} there is a nonzero $f\in W$ with $\psi(\lambda)=\lambda(f)$ for every $\lambda\in W^{*}$. In particular, for every $x\in S$, $0=\psi(e_{x})=e_{x}(f)=f(x)$; so $f$ is the zero function on $S$, that is $f=0$ in $W$, contradicting $f\neq0$. Hence $U=W^{*}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #choose-points}
There are $x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in S$ such that $e_{x_{1}},\dots,e_{x_{n}}$ is a basis of $W^{*}$.

::: pf-proof
A spanning family of a finite-dimensional space contains a basis: discard, one at a time, any member that lies in the span of the others, until none does; the remaining family still spans and is independent. Since $\dim W^{*}=\dim W=n$, the resulting basis has exactly $n$ elements, each of the form $e_{x}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dual-basis}
There are $f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in W$ with $e_{x_{j}}(f_{i})=\delta_{ij}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n}\in W^{**}$ be the basis dual to $e_{x_{1}},\dots,e_{x_{n}}$, that is $\ell_{i}(e_{x_{j}})=\delta_{ij}$. By step [](#double-dual){.pf-ref} the map $f\mapsto\bigl(\lambda\mapsto\lambda(f)\bigr)$ is a bijection onto $W^{**}$, so for each $i$ there is $f_{i}\in W$ with $\lambda(f_{i})=\ell_{i}(\lambda)$ for all $\lambda\in W^{*}$. Evaluating at $\lambda=e_{x_{j}}$ gives $f_{i}(x_{j})=\ell_{i}(e_{x_{j}})=\delta_{ij}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The points $x_{1},\dots,x_{n}$ and the functions $f_{1},\dots,f_{n}$ satisfy $f_{i}(x_{j})=\delta_{ij}$, as required.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#dual-basis){.pf-ref} gives the identity, and step [](#choose-points){.pf-ref} gives the points in $S$; step [](#dual-basis){.pf-ref} also shows $f_{1},\dots,f_{n}$ lie in $W$, and being dual to a basis of $W^{*}$ they are a basis of $W$.
:::
