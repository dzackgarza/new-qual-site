::: {.exercise}
Use Theorem 20 to prove the following.
If $W$ is a subspace of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ and if $\{g_1, \ldots, g_r\}$ is any basis for $W^0$, then
$$
W = \bigcap_{i=1}^{r} N_{g_i}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. The exercise asks that Theorem 20 be used; the route below uses instead the double-annihilator identity $(W^{0})^{0}=W$, which holds for finite-dimensional $V$ under the identification of $V$ with $V^{**}$, and it does not restate Theorem 20, whose text is not retained in this tree.

Let $V$ be finite dimensional, $W$ a subspace, and $\{g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\}$ a basis of the annihilator $W^{0}$. Put $K=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}N_{g_{i}}$, where $N_{g}=\{\alpha\mid g(\alpha)=0\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #inclusion}
$W\subseteq K$.

::: pf-proof
Let $w\in W$. Each $g_{i}$ lies in $W^{0}$, so $g_{i}(w)=0$ for every $i$ by the definition of the annihilator; hence $w$ lies in every $N_{g_{i}}$, that is $w\in K$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #vanish}
If $v\in K$ then $g(v)=0$ for every $g\in W^{0}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $v\in K$ and $g\in W^{0}$. Since $\{g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\}$ is a basis of $W^{0}$ there are scalars $c_{1},\dots,c_{r}$ with $g=\sum_{i}c_{i}g_{i}$. Then $g(v)=\sum_{i}c_{i}g_{i}(v)=0$, because $v\in N_{g_{i}}$ gives $g_{i}(v)=0$ for each $i$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #double}
$K\subseteq W$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#vanish){.pf-ref}, every $v\in K$ is annihilated by every element of $W^{0}$, that is $v\in (W^{0})^{0}$. Under the identification of $V$ with its double dual — an isomorphism because $\dim V<\infty$ — the double annihilator of a subspace is the subspace itself, so $(W^{0})^{0}=W$ and $v\in W$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$W=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}N_{g_{i}}$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#inclusion){.pf-ref} and [](#double){.pf-ref} give $W\subseteq K$ and $K\subseteq W$, so the two subspaces are equal; this is the assertion of the exercise, for the basis $\{g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\}$ of $W^{0}$ that was given.
:::
