::: {.exercise}
Let $n$ be a positive integer and $F$ a field.
Let $W$ be the set of all vectors $(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ in $F^n$ such that $x_1 + \cdots + x_n = 0$.

(a) Prove that $W^0$ consists of all linear functionals $f$ of the form
\[
f(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = c \sum_{j=1}^{n} x_j.
\]

(b) Show that the dual space $W^*$ of $W$ can be 'naturally' identified with the linear functionals
\[
f(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = c_1 x_1 + \cdots + c_n x_n
\]
on $F^n$ which satisfy $c_1 + \cdots + c_n = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ for the standard basis of $F^n$ and define
$$
\sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_{j=1}^n x_j.
$$
Then $W=\ker\sigma$.

<1>1. The annihilator $W^0$ is the one-dimensional space spanned by $\sigma$.
::: {.proof}
Every linear functional on $F^n$ has a unique expression
$$
f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_{j=1}^n c_jx_j.
$$
Suppose $f\in W^0$. For each $i<n$, the vector $e_i-e_n$ belongs to $W$.
Therefore
$$
0=f(e_i-e_n)=c_i-c_n,
$$
so $c_i=c_n$ for every $i<n$. Thus all coefficients are equal to some scalar
$c$, and
$$
f=c\sigma.
$$
Conversely, every $c\sigma$ vanishes on $W=\ker\sigma$. Hence
$$
W^0=\{c\sigma:c\in F\},
$$
which is exactly the description in part (a) [@HK71].
:::

<1>2. Every functional on $W$ is the restriction of a functional on $F^n$ whose coefficients sum to zero.
::: {.proof}
Let $\lambda\in W^*$. Since $W$ is a subspace of the finite-dimensional
space $F^n$, extend a basis of $W$ to a basis of $F^n$ and assign any value to
the additional basis vector. This extends $\lambda$ to some
$$
f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^n c_jx_j
$$
on $F^n$.

Put
$$
s=\sum_{j=1}^n c_j,
\qquad
d_j=c_j-\frac{s}{n}.
$$
Hoffman--Kunze's scalar fields have characteristic zero, so $n$ is invertible
in $F$ [@HK71]. Then
$$
\sum_{j=1}^n d_j=s-n\frac{s}{n}=0.
$$
The functional
$$
g(x)=\sum_{j=1}^n d_jx_j
$$
differs from $f$ by
$$
g-f=-\frac{s}{n}\sigma,
$$
which lies in $W^0$ by part (a). Therefore $g|_W=f|_W=\lambda$.
Thus every element of $W^*$ has a representative with coefficient sum zero.
:::

<1>3. That representative is unique, giving the natural identification.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
L=\left\{f_c(x)=\sum_{j=1}^n c_jx_j:\sum_{j=1}^n c_j=0\right\}
\subseteq(F^n)^*.
$$
Consider the restriction map
$$
\rho:L\longrightarrow W^*,\qquad \rho(f)=f|_W.
$$
Step <1>2 proves surjectivity. If $f_c\in\ker\rho$, then $f_c\in W^0$.
By part (a), all its coefficients equal a common scalar $a$. Since their sum
is zero,
$$
na=0.
$$
Characteristic zero gives $a=0$, hence $f_c=0$. Therefore $\rho$ is injective.

So restriction is a linear isomorphism
$$
L\cong W^*,
$$
which is the claimed natural identification.
:::
:::
