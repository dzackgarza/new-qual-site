::: {.exercise}
Give a direct proof that if $A$ is the companion matrix of the monic polynomial $p$, then $p$ is the characteristic polynomial for $A$.
:::

::: solution
Write
$$
p(x)=x^n+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_1x+c_0.
$$
With the companion-matrix convention of this section,
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&\cdots&0&-c_0\\
1&0&\cdots&0&-c_1\\
0&1&\ddots&0&-c_2\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&1&-c_{n-1}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
[@HK71]. We prove directly that $\det(xI-A)=p(x)$.

<1>1. The determinant satisfies the companion recurrence.
::: proof
One has
$$
xI-A=
\begin{pmatrix}
x&0&\cdots&0&c_0\\
-1&x&\cdots&0&c_1\\
0&-1&\ddots&0&c_2\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&x&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&-1&x+c_{n-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Expand along the first row. The cofactor of the entry $x$ is the same
determinant of size $n-1$ with coefficient list
$c_1,\ldots,c_{n-1}$. The minor of the entry $c_0$ is upper triangular with
all diagonal entries $-1$, so its determinant is $(-1)^{n-1}$. Its cofactor
sign is $(-1)^{1+n}$, and therefore the total sign multiplying $c_0$ is
$$
(-1)^{n+1}(-1)^{n-1}=1.
$$
Hence, if $D_n$ denotes this determinant,
$$
D_n=xD_{n-1}+c_0.
$$
:::

<1>2. Induction gives the polynomial $p$.
::: proof
For $n=1$,
$$
D_1=x+c_0.
$$
Assume the formula for size $n-1$. By step <1>1,
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_n
&=x\bigl(x^{n-1}+c_{n-1}x^{n-2}+\cdots+c_2x+c_1\bigr)+c_0\\
&=x^n+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_1x+c_0\\
&=p(x).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the characteristic polynomial of the companion matrix is exactly
$$
\boxed{\chi_A=p}.
$$
:::
:::
