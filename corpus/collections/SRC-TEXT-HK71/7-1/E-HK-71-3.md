::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $C^3$ which is represented in the standard ordered basis by the matrix
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & i & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -i \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Find the $T$-annihilator of the vector $(1, 0, 0)$.
Find the $T$-annihilator of $(1, 0, i)$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Determine the $T$-annihilator polynomials $p_{v_1}(x)$ and $p_{v_2}(x)$ for the vectors $v_1 = (1, 0, 0)^T$ and $v_2 = (1, 0, i)^T$ under the linear operator $T$.

<1>1. Definition of the $T$-annihilator:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For a linear operator $T$ on a vector space $V$ and a vector $v \in V$, the $T$-annihilator of $v$ (denoted $p_v(x)$) is the unique monic polynomial of minimal degree in $F[x]$ such that $p_v(T)v = 0$.
    <2>2. The degree of $p_v(x)$ equals the dimension of the $T$-cyclic subspace $Z(v; T) = \operatorname{span}\{v, Tv, T^2v, \dots\}$.

<1>2. $T$-annihilator of $v_1 = (1, 0, 0)^T$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Compute the powers of $T$ acting on $v_1$:
    $$v_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad Tv_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -i \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix},$$
    $$T^2 v_1 = T(Tv_1) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -i \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - i \\ -3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}.$$
    <2>2. Place the vectors $v_1, Tv_1, T^2v_1$ as columns of a matrix and compute the determinant:
    $$\det \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1-i \\ 0 & -1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = 1 \cdot (-1) \cdot (-1) = 1 \neq 0.$$
    <2>3. Because the determinant is non-zero, the set $\{v_1, Tv_1, T^2v_1\}$ is linearly independent in $\mathbb{C}^3$.
    <2>4. Thus $\dim Z(v_1; T) = 3 = \dim \mathbb{C}^3$, which implies $\deg p_{v_1}(x) = 3$.
    <2>5. Consequently, $p_{v_1}(x)$ must equal the characteristic polynomial $\det(xI - T)$ of $T$:
    $$\det(xI - T) = \det \begin{bmatrix} x - 1 & -i & 0 \\ 1 & x - 2 & i \\ 0 & -1 & x - 1 \end{bmatrix} = (x - 1)[(x - 2)(x - 1) + i] + i(x - 1) = (x - 1)(x^2 - 3x + 2 + 2i).$$
    <2>6. Expanding gives
    $$p_{v_1}(x) = x^3 - 4x^2 + (5 + 2i)x - (2 + 2i).$$

<1>3. $T$-annihilator of $v_2 = (1, 0, i)^T$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Compute $Tv_2$:
    $$Tv_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -i \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ i \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 + 0 + 0 \\ -1 + 0 - i^2 \\ 0 + 0 + i \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ i \end{bmatrix} = v_2.$$
    <2>2. Thus $(T - I)v_2 = 0$.
    <2>3. Since $v_2 \neq 0$, the degree of the $T$-annihilator is at least 1, so the monic polynomial of degree 1 annihilating $v_2$ is
    $$p_{v_2}(x) = x - 1.$$

<1>4. Conclusion:
    *Proof:*
    The $T$-annihilator of $(1, 0, 0)$ is $x^3 - 4x^2 + (5 + 2i)x - (2 + 2i)$, and the $T$-annihilator of $(1, 0, i)$ is $x - 1$.
:::
