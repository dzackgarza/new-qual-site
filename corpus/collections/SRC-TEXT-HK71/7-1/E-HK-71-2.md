::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^3$ which is represented in the standard ordered basis by the matrix
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Prove that $T$ has no cyclic vector.
What is the $T$-cyclic subspace generated by the vector $(1, -1, 3)$?
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
A=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,-1).
$$
The polynomial
$$
q(x)=(x-2)(x+1)=x^2-x-2
$$
annihilates $T$, since $q(2)=q(-1)=0$. Thus
$$
T^2=T+2I.
$$
It follows inductively that every power $T^k$ lies in the span of $I$ and
$T$. Hence, for every vector $v$,
$$
Z(v;T)=\operatorname{span}\{v,Tv,T^2v,\ldots\}
=\operatorname{span}\{v,Tv\},
$$
so every cyclic subspace has dimension at most two. Since
$\dim\mathbb R^3=3$, no vector can be cyclic for $T$ [@HK71].

For
$$
v=(1,-1,3),
$$
one has
$$
Tv=(2,-2,-3).
$$
These two vectors are linearly independent, because they are not scalar
multiples. Therefore
$$
\boxed{Z(v;T)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1,3),(2,-2,-3)\}.}
$$
Equivalently, adding and subtracting suitable multiples gives
$$
Z(v;T)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1,0),(0,0,1)\}.
$$
:::
