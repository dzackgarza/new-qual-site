::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space, and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Suppose that $T$ is diagonalizable.

(a) If $T$ has a cyclic vector, show that $T$ has $n$ distinct characteristic values.

(b) If $T$ has $n$ distinct characteristic values, and if $\{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n\}$ is a basis of characteristic vectors for $T$, show that $\alpha = \alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n$ is a cyclic vector for $T$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A diagonalizable operator with a cyclic vector must have $n$ distinct characteristic values.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $T$ has a cyclic vector $\alpha$. Then
$$
\alpha,T\alpha,\ldots,T^{n-1}\alpha
$$
span the $n$-dimensional space $V$ and hence form a basis. Therefore no
nonzero polynomial of degree less than $n$ can annihilate $\alpha$; such a
relation would be a nontrivial linear dependence among these basis vectors.
On the other hand, $T^n\alpha$ is a linear combination of the displayed
basis, so some monic polynomial of degree $n$ annihilates $\alpha$. Hence the
$T$-annihilator of $\alpha$ has degree $n$.

For a cyclic vector its annihilator equals the minimal polynomial of $T$
[@HK71], so
$$
\deg m_T=n.
$$
If $T$ is diagonalizable and its distinct characteristic values are
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$, then
$$
m_T(x)=\prod_{i=1}^r(x-\lambda_i).
$$
Thus $r=\deg m_T=n$. Therefore $T$ has $n$ distinct characteristic values.
:::

<1>2. The sum of an eigenbasis is cyclic when the eigenvalues are distinct.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
T\alpha_i=\lambda_i\alpha_i,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
$$
where the $\lambda_i$ are distinct, and put
$$
\alpha=\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_n.
$$
Then for $0\le k\le n-1$,
$$
T^k\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i^k\alpha_i.
$$
Relative to the eigenbasis $(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)$, the coordinate
columns of
$$
\alpha,T\alpha,\ldots,T^{n-1}\alpha
$$
form the Vandermonde matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\lambda_1&\lambda_1^2&\cdots&\lambda_1^{n-1}\\
1&\lambda_2&\lambda_2^2&\cdots&\lambda_2^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
1&\lambda_n&\lambda_n^2&\cdots&\lambda_n^{n-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is
$$
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)\ne0
$$
because the characteristic values are distinct. Hence those $n$ vectors form
a basis of $V$. Therefore $\alpha$ is a cyclic vector for $T$.
:::
