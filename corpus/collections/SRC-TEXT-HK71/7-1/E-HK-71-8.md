::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on the finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Suppose $T$ has a cyclic vector.
Prove that if $U$ is any linear operator which commutes with $T$, then $U$ is a polynomial in $T$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\alpha$ be a cyclic vector for $T$, and put $n=\dim V$. Then
$$
\alpha,T\alpha,\ldots,T^{n-1}\alpha
$$
is a basis of $V$ [@HK71].

<1>1. Express $U\alpha$ as a polynomial in $T$ applied to $\alpha$.
::: {.proof}
Because the cyclic vectors above form a basis, there are scalars
$a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1}$ such that
$$
U\alpha=a_0\alpha+a_1T\alpha+\cdots+a_{n-1}T^{n-1}\alpha.
$$
Define
$$
p(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}.
$$
Then
$$
U\alpha=p(T)\alpha.
$$
:::

<1>2. Commutation propagates this equality to every cyclic-basis vector.
::: {.proof}
Since $UT=TU$, induction gives
$$
UT^k=T^kU
$$
for every integer $k\ge0$. Hence, for $0\le k<n$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
U(T^k\alpha)
&=T^kU\alpha\\
&=T^kp(T)\alpha\\
&=p(T)T^k\alpha,
\end{aligned}
$$
because $p(T)$ commutes with $T$.
Thus $U$ and $p(T)$ agree on each vector of the cyclic basis.
:::

<1>3. Conclude equality of the operators.
::: {.proof}
Two linear operators that agree on a basis agree on all of $V$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{U=p(T)}.
$$
So every operator commuting with a cyclic operator is a polynomial in that
operator.
:::
:::
