::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the vector space of all $n \times n$ matrices over the field of complex numbers, and let $B$ be a fixed $n \times n$ matrix over $C$.
Define a linear operator $M_B$ on $V$ by $M_B(A) = BAB^*$, where $B^* = \overline{B^t}$.
Show that
\[
\det M_B = |\det B|^{2n}.
\]
Now let $H$ be the set of all Hermitian matrices in $V$, $A$ being Hermitian if $A = A^*$.
Then $H$ is a vector space over the field of real numbers.
Show that the function $T_B$ defined by $T_B(A) = BAB^*$ is a linear operator on the real vector space $H$, and then show that $\det T_B = |\det B|^{2n}$.
(Hint: In computing $\det T_B$, show that $V$ has a basis consisting of Hermitian matrices and then show that $\det T_B = \det M_B$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Show $\det M_B = |\det B|^{2n}$ on $V$, and $\det T_B = |\det B|^{2n}$ on the real space $H$ of Hermitian matrices.

<1>1. $\det M_B = |\det B|^{2n}$.
<2>1. $M_B = L_B \circ R_{B^*}$, where $L_B(A) = BA$ and $R_{B^*}(A) = AB^*$.
::: {.proof}
$M_B(A) = BAB^* = L_B(R_{B^*}(A))$.
:::
<2>2. $\det L_B = (\det B)^n$ and $\det R_{B^*} = (\det B^*)^n = \overline{\det B}^n$.
::: {.proof}
$L_B$ acts as $B$ on each of the $n$ columns, so $\det L_B = (\det B)^n$; similarly $R_{B^*}$ acts as $B^*$ on each row, so $\det R_{B^*} = (\det B^*)^n$.
:::
<2>3. Hence $\det M_B = \det L_B \cdot \det R_{B^*} = (\det B)^n \overline{\det B}^n = |\det B|^{2n}$.
::: {.proof}
determinant is multiplicative.
:::

<1>2. $H$ is a real vector space, and $T_B$ is a linear operator on it.
<2>1. $H = \theset{A : A = A^*}$ is closed under real linear combinations.
::: {.proof}
if $A, C$ are Hermitian and $r \in \RR$, then $(A + C)^* = A + C$ and $(rA)^* = rA$.
:::
<2>2. $T_B(A) = BAB^*$ is Hermitian when $A$ is.
::: {.proof}
$(BAB^*)^* = B A^* B^* = B A B^*$ (since $A^* = A$).
:::
<2>3. Hence $T_B$ is a linear operator on the real vector space $H$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2.1 and <1>2.2.
:::

<1>3. $\det T_B = |\det B|^{2n}$.
<2>1. $V$ has a basis consisting of Hermitian matrices.
::: {.proof}
the matrices $E_{jj}$ (diagonal) and $E_{jk} + E_{kj}$, $i(E_{jk} - E_{kj})$ (off-diagonal) form a basis of $V$ over $\CC$, and each is Hermitian; this gives a real basis of $H$ and a complex basis of $V$.
:::
<2>2. $M_B$ and $T_B$ have the same matrix (in the appropriate bases), so $\det T_B = \det M_B$.
::: {.proof}
$M_B$ and $T_B$ are the same map $A \mapsto BAB^*$; on the Hermitian basis, the real matrix of $T_B$ and the complex matrix of $M_B$ agree, so their determinants agree.
:::
<2>3. Hence $\det T_B = \det M_B = |\det B|^{2n}$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3.2 and <1>1.3.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1.3 and <1>3.3 give the two results.
:::
