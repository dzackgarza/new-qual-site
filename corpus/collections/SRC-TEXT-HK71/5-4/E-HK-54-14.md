::: {.exercise}
Let $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ be commuting $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$.
Show that the determinant of the $2n \times 2n$ matrix
$$
\begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{bmatrix}
$$
is $\det(AD - BC)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §5.4 Exercise 14.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #setup}
Let $M=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix}$ with $A,B,C,D$ commuting $n\times n$ matrices over $F$.

::: pf-proof
This is the matrix of the statement; the commutativity hypothesis is used in steps [](#factor){.pf-ref} and [](#inverse){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #invertiblecase}
If $A$ is invertible then $\det M=\det(AD-BC)$.

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #factor}
$\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\C&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&A^{-1}B\\0&D-CA^{-1}B\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Multiply the right-hand side blockwise: the top-left block is $A\cdot I=A$, the top-right is $A\,A^{-1}B+0=B$, the bottom-left is $C\cdot I+I\cdot0=C$, and the bottom-right is $C A^{-1}B+I\cdot(D-CA^{-1}B)=D$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #determinants}
$\det M=\det A\cdot\det(D-CA^{-1}B)$.

::: pf-proof
Both factors on the right of step [](#factor){.pf-ref} are block triangular with identity or zero blocks in complementary corners, so each determinant is the product of the determinants of its diagonal blocks: $\det A\cdot\det I$ and $\det I\cdot\det(D-CA^{-1}B)$. The determinant is multiplicative.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inverse}
$D-CA^{-1}B=A^{-1}(AD-BC)$.

::: pf-proof
Since $A$ commutes with $B,C,D$, its inverse commutes with them too, so $A^{-1}(AD-BC)=D-A^{-1}BC=D-CA^{-1}B$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\det M=\det(AD-BC)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#inverse){.pf-ref}, $\det(D-CA^{-1}B)=\det A^{-1}\det(AD-BC)$, and $\det A\det A^{-1}=1$; substituting into step [](#determinants){.pf-ref} gives the claim.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #generalcase}
The formula holds without assuming $A$ invertible.

::: pf-proof
Work over the field $F(t)$ of rational functions. The polynomial $\det(A+tI)$ is monic of degree $n$, hence nonzero, so $A+tI$ is invertible over $F(t)$; the four matrices $A+tI,B,C,D$ commute. By step [](#invertiblecase){.pf-ref} applied over $F(t)$,
$$
\det\begin{bmatrix}A+tI&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix}=\det\bigl((A+tI)D-BC\bigr)
$$
holds in $F(t)$. Both sides are determinants of matrices with polynomial entries, hence lie in $F[t]$, and two polynomials equal as rational functions are equal in $F[t]$; evaluating the identity at $t=0$ gives the formula for $M$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\det\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix}=\det(AD-BC)$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#invertiblecase){.pf-ref} and [](#generalcase){.pf-ref} together cover every case:
$$
\boxed{\det\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix}=\det(AD-BC).}
$$
Commutativity is used twice: in step [](#inverse){.pf-ref} to move $A^{-1}$ past $B$ and $C$, and in step [](#generalcase){.pf-ref} to keep the hypothesis for $A+tI$. Commutativity enters exactly at those two points; the card does not claim any formula for non-commuting blocks.
:::
