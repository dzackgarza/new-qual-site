::: {.exercise}
Let $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ be commuting $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$.
Show that the determinant of the $2n \times 2n$ matrix
\[
\begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{bmatrix}
\]
is $\det(AD - BC)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
M=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\ C&D\end{bmatrix}.
\]
We first prove the formula when $A$ is invertible. Since $A,B,C,D$ commute, $A^{-1}$ also commutes with $B,C,D$. The block factorization
\[
\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\ C&D\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\ C&I\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}I&A^{-1}B\\ 0&D-CA^{-1}B\end{bmatrix}
\]
gives
\[
\det M
=\det A\,\det(D-CA^{-1}B).
\]
Because the blocks commute,
\[
D-CA^{-1}B=A^{-1}(AD-CB)=A^{-1}(AD-BC).
\]
Thus
\[
\det M
=\det A\,\det(A^{-1})\det(AD-BC)
=\det(AD-BC).
\]

Now remove the assumption that $A$ is invertible. Work over the rational-function field $F(t)$. The matrix $A+tI$ is invertible over $F(t)$ because
\[
\det(A+tI)
\]
is a nonzero polynomial in $t$ with leading term $t^n$. The matrices $A+tI,B,C,D$ still commute, so the already proved case yields
\[
\det\begin{bmatrix}A+tI&B\\ C&D\end{bmatrix}
=
\det\bigl((A+tI)D-BC\bigr)
\]
as an identity in $F(t)$. Both sides are in fact polynomials in $t$, so this is an identity in $F[t]$. Evaluating at $t=0$ gives
\[
\det\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\ C&D\end{bmatrix}
=\det(AD-BC),
\]
as required.
:::
