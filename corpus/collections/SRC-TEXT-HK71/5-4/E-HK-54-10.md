::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over the field $F$.
Prove that there are at most $n$ distinct scalars $c$ in $F$ such that $\det(cI - A) = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Let $A$ be $n\times n$ over the field $F$ and put
$$
p(x)=\det(xI-A)\in F[x].
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #monic}
$p$ is a monic polynomial of degree $n$; in particular $p\ne0$.

::: pf-proof
By the Leibniz formula, $p(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x\delta_{i,\sigma(i)}-a_{i,\sigma(i)})$. For the identity permutation every factor is $x-a_{ii}$, so that term is $\prod_i(x-a_{ii})$, of degree $n$ with leading coefficient $1$. For any other $\sigma$ there is at least one $i$ with $\sigma(i)\ne i$, so at least one factor is the constant $-a_{i,\sigma(i)}$ and the term has degree at most $n-1$. Summing, the coefficient of $x^n$ is $1$ and all other terms have degree at most $n-1$, so $p$ is monic of degree $n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #evaluate}
For $c\in F$ one has $\det(cI-A)=p(c)$.

::: pf-proof
$p$ is defined by a polynomial expression in the variable $x$ whose coefficients are polynomials in the entries of $A$; substituting the scalar $c$ for $x$ gives the determinant of the matrix obtained by substituting $c$ for $x$ in $xI-A$, which is $cI-A$. So evaluating the polynomial at $c$ computes the determinant of $cI-A$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #roots}
A nonzero polynomial $p\in F[x]$ of degree $n$ has at most $n$ distinct roots in $F$.

::: pf-proof
Let $c_1,\dots,c_m$ be distinct roots of $p$. Since $c_i$ is a root, $x-c_i$ divides $p$ (division by the linear polynomial $x-c_i$ leaves a constant remainder, which must be $0$). As the $c_i$ are distinct, the factors $x-c_i$ are pairwise coprime in $F[x]$, so their product $\prod_{i=1}^m(x-c_i)$ divides $p$. Comparing degrees, $m\le\deg p=n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
There are at most $n$ distinct scalars $c\in F$ with $\det(cI-A)=0$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#evaluate){.pf-ref} the scalars $c$ in question are exactly the roots of $p$, and $p$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree $n$ by step [](#monic){.pf-ref}; by step [](#roots){.pf-ref} it has at most $n$ distinct roots in $F$. So the set of such $c$ has at most $n$ elements, which is the assertion.
:::
