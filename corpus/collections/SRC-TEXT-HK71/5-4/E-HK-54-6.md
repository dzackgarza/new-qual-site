::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ and $U$ be linear operators on the finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Prove

(a) $\det(TU) = (\det T)(\det U)$;

(b) $T$ is invertible if and only if $\det T \neq 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: both statements are transported from matrices to operators through an ordered basis, using that $[\cdot]$ is multiplicative and injective.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis}

Fix an ordered basis $\mathfrak{B}$ of $V$ and put $A=[T]_{\mathfrak{B}}$, $B=[U]_{\mathfrak{B}}$, so that $[TU]_{\mathfrak{B}}=AB$.

::: pf-proof

The matrix of a composite is the product of the matrices: the $j$-th column of $[TU]$ is the coordinate tuple of $TU(\beta_{j})=T\big(U\beta_{j}\big)$, which is $[T]$ applied to the coordinate tuple of $U\beta_{j}$, i.e. the $j$-th column of $[T][U]$. This holds in any ordered basis, and $V$ is finite-dimensional, so a basis of $V$ exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #parta}

(a) $\det(TU)=(\det T)(\det U)$, where $\det T$ means $\det$ of the matrix of $T$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#basis){.pf-ref} and the multiplicativity of the matrix determinant,
$$
\det(TU)=\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)=(\det T)(\det U),
$$
using the definition $\det T=\det[T]_{\mathfrak{B}}$ and $\det U=\det[U]_{\mathfrak{B}}$. The number obtained does not depend on the basis chosen, since similar matrices have equal determinants.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix}

(b) $T$ is invertible if and only if its matrix $A$ is invertible.

::: pf-proof

If $T$ has an inverse $T^{-1}$, then $[T][T^{-1}]=[TT^{-1}]=[I]=I$ and likewise on the other side by step [](#basis){.pf-ref}, so $[T]$ is invertible with inverse $[T^{-1}]$. Conversely if $A=[T]$ is invertible with inverse $C$, the linear operator $S$ with $[S]_{\mathfrak{B}}=C$ satisfies $[TS]=AC=I=[I]$ and $[ST]=I$, and since the map $T\mapsto[T]_{\mathfrak{B}}$ is injective ($T=0$ exactly when all its columns vanish) we get $TS=ST=I$, so $T$ is invertible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

(b) $T$ is invertible if and only if $\det T\ne0$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#matrix){.pf-ref}, invertibility of $T$ is invertibility of the square matrix $A$ over the field $F$, and a square matrix over a field is invertible exactly when its determinant is nonzero (its inverse is $\det(A)^{-1}$ times the adjugate when $\det A\ne0$, while $\det A\det A^{-1}=1$ forbids $\det A=0$ when $A$ is invertible). Since $\det T=\det A$,
$$
\boxed{\det(TU)=(\det T)(\det U),\qquad T\ \text{invertible}\iff\det T\ne0.}
$$

:::
