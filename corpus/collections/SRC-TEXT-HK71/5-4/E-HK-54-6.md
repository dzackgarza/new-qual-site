::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ and $U$ be linear operators on the finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Prove

(a) $\det(TU) = (\det T)(\det U)$;

(b) $T$ is invertible if and only if $\det T \neq 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Choose an ordered basis of $V$, and let $A=[T]$ and $B=[U]$ be the corresponding matrices. Then
\[
[TU]=AB.
\]
By multiplicativity of the matrix determinant,
\[
\det(TU)=\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)=(\det T)(\det U).
\]
This proves (a).

For (b), $T$ is invertible exactly when its matrix $A$ is invertible. For a square matrix over a field,
\[
A\text{ is invertible}\iff \det A\ne0.
\]
Since $\det T$ is defined as $\det A$, this is exactly
\[
T\text{ is invertible}\iff \det T\ne0.
\]
:::
