::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ and $B$ be $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$.
Show that if $A$ is invertible there are at most $n$ scalars $c$ in $F$ for which the matrix $cA + B$ is not invertible.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §5.4 Exercise 11; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $A,B$ are $n\times n$ matrices over the field $F$ with $A$ invertible, and $\chi_M(x)=\det(xI-M)$ is the characteristic polynomial of $M$, a monic polynomial of degree $n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #factor}
$cA+B=A(cI+A^{-1}B)$, so $\det(cA+B)=\det A\cdot\chi_{-A^{-1}B}(c)$.

::: pf-proof
$A(cI+A^{-1}B)=cA+AA^{-1}B=cA+B$ by distributivity and $AA^{-1}=I$. Taking determinants and using multiplicativity,
$$
\det(cA+B)=\det A\cdot\det(cI+A^{-1}B),
$$
and $\det(cI+A^{-1}B)=\det(cI-(-A^{-1}B))=\chi_{-A^{-1}B}(c)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #singular-iff}
$cA+B$ is not invertible if and only if $\chi_{-A^{-1}B}(c)=0$.

::: pf-proof
A square matrix over a field is invertible exactly when its determinant is nonzero. Since $\det A\neq0$ by step [](#factor){.pf-ref} and invertibility of $A$, $\det(cA+B)=0$ exactly when $\det(cI+A^{-1}B)=0$, which by step [](#factor){.pf-ref} is the condition $\chi_{-A^{-1}B}(c)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #at-most}
A nonzero polynomial of degree $n$ over a field has at most $n$ roots in the field.

::: pf-proof
Induction on $n$. For $n=0$ the polynomial is a nonzero constant and has no root. For $n\ge1$, if $p$ has no root there is nothing to prove; if $c$ is a root then $p=(x-c)q$ with $\deg q=n-1$ by the factor theorem, and every root of $p$ is $c$ or a root of $q$, of which there are at most $n-1$ by the induction hypothesis; so $p$ has at most $n$ roots.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
At most $n$ scalars $c\in F$ make $cA+B$ noninvertible.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#singular-iff){.pf-ref} those $c$ are exactly the roots in $F$ of $\chi_{-A^{-1}B}$, which is monic of degree $n$ and hence nonzero; by step [](#at-most){.pf-ref} it has at most $n$ roots, so there are at most $n$ such scalars.

:::
