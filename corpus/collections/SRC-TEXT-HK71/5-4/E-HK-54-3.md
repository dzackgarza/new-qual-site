::: {.exercise}
An $n \times n$ matrix $A$ over a field $F$ is skew-symmetric if $A^t = -A$.
If $A$ is a skew-symmetric $n \times n$ matrix with complex entries and $n$ is odd, prove that $\det A = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection `SRC-TEXT-HK71` cites only a scanned copy on archive.org, no text or solutions sheet for Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so neither the printed page nor a published solution could be re-checked in this session. The statement is the stored one for §5.4 Exercise 3, and the argument below is the card's own.

Throughout, $A$ is an $n\times n$ matrix with complex entries satisfying $A^{t}=-A$, and $n$ is odd.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #transpose}
$\det A^{t}=\det A$.

::: pf-proof
The determinant of a matrix equals that of its transpose: expanding along a row of $A$ gives the same sum of signed products as expanding along the corresponding column of $A^{t}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #scaling}
$\det(-A)=(-1)^{n}\det A$.

::: pf-proof
$-A$ is obtained from $A$ by multiplying each of the $n$ rows by $-1$, and the determinant is multilinear in the rows, so each of the $n$ operations contributes a factor $-1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore $\det A=0$.

::: pf-proof
Combining the hypothesis $A^{t}=-A$ with steps [](#transpose){.pf-ref} and [](#scaling){.pf-ref},
$$
\det A=\det A^{t}=\det(-A)=(-1)^{n}\det A=-\det A,
$$
because $n$ is odd. Hence $2\det A=0$ in $\CC$, and $2\neq0$ there, so $\det A=0$. The only field-theoretic input is $2\neq0$: over a field of characteristic $2$ the argument gives nothing, and the statement itself fails there, as the identity matrix of odd order satisfies $A^{t}=A=-A$ and has determinant $1$.
:::
