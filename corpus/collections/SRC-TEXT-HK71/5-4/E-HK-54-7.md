::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over $K$, a commutative ring with identity.
Suppose $A$ has the block form
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix}
A_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
0 & A_2 & \cdots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \cdots & A_k
\end{bmatrix}
\]
where $A_j$ is an $r_j \times r_j$ matrix.
Prove
\[
\det A = (\det A_1)(\det A_2) \cdots (\det A_k).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Two-block case:
<2>1. Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} B & 0 \\ 0 & C \end{pmatrix}$, where $B \in M_r(K)$ and $C \in M_s(K)$ with $r + s = n$.
By the Leibniz formula for the determinant of $A = (a_{ij})$:
\[
\det A = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) \prod_{i=1}^n a_{i, \sigma(i)}.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of determinant.
:::
<2>2. The matrix entries satisfy $a_{i, j} = 0$ whenever $1 \le i \le r < j \le n$ or $1 \le j \le r < i \le n$.
Thus a product $\prod_{i=1}^n a_{i, \sigma(i)}$ is non-zero only if $\sigma(\{1, \dots, r\}) = \{1, \dots, r\}$ and $\sigma(\{r+1, \dots, n\}) = \{r+1, \dots, n\}$.
::: {.proof}
block-diagonal structure has zero off-diagonal blocks.
:::
<2>3. Such permutations $\sigma \in S_n$ are precisely the direct product $\sigma = \tau \times \pi$ where $\tau \in S_r$ and $\pi \in S_s$.
Because there are no inversions between $\{1, \dots, r\}$ and $\{r+1, \dots, n\}$ (since $\tau(i) \le r < \pi(j)$ for all $i \le r < j$), the sign decomposes as:
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = \operatorname{sgn}(\tau) \operatorname{sgn}(\pi).
\]
::: {.proof}
parity of disjoint block permutations.
:::
<2>4. Factoring the summation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det A &= \sum_{\tau \in S_r} \sum_{\pi \in S_s} \operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\operatorname{sgn}(\pi) \left( \prod_{i=1}^r B_{i, \tau(i)} \right) \left( \prod_{j=1}^s C_{j, \pi(j)} \right) \\
&= \left( \sum_{\tau \in S_r} \operatorname{sgn}(\tau) \prod_{i=1}^r B_{i, \tau(i)} \right) \left( \sum_{\pi \in S_s} \operatorname{sgn}(\pi) \prod_{j=1}^s C_{j, \pi(j)} \right) \\
&= (\det B)(\det C).
\end{aligned}
\]
::: {.proof}
distributivity in commutative ring $K$.
:::

<1>2. General $k$-block case by induction:
<2>1. For $k = 1$, the statement is the identity $\det(A_1) = \det(A_1)$.
::: {.proof}
base case.
:::
<2>2. Assume the result holds for $k - 1$ diagonal blocks.
Write $A = \operatorname{diag}(A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_k)$ as a $2 \times 2$ block-diagonal matrix:
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix} A_1 & 0 \\ 0 & \widetilde{A} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \text{where } \widetilde{A} = \operatorname{diag}(A_2, \ldots, A_k).
\]
Applying <1>1 and the induction hypothesis:
\[
\det A = (\det A_1)(\det \widetilde{A}) = (\det A_1) \prod_{j=2}^k \det A_j = \prod_{j=1}^k \det A_j.
\]
::: {.proof}
induction step using <1>1.
:::

<1>3. Conclusion:
$\det A = (\det A_1)(\det A_2) \cdots (\det A_k)$. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2.
:::
:::
