::: {.exercise}
Use Cramer's rule to solve each of the following systems of linear equations over the field of rational numbers.

(a) $x + y + z = 11$ $\phantom{(a)} 2x - 6y - z = 0$ $\phantom{(a)} 3x + 4y + 2z = 0$.

(b) $3x - 2y = 7$ $\phantom{(b)} 3y - 2z = 6$ $\phantom{(b)} 3z - 2x = -1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For (a), the coefficient determinant is
\[
\Delta=\det\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\\2&-6&-1\\3&4&2\end{bmatrix}=-9.
\]
Replacing the first, second, and third columns by $(11,0,0)^t$ gives respectively
\[
\Delta_x=72,\qquad \Delta_y=63,\qquad \Delta_z=-234.
\]
Cramer's rule therefore gives
\[
(x,y,z)=(-8,-7,26).
\]

For (b),
\[
\Delta=\det\begin{bmatrix}3&-2&0\\0&3&-2\\-2&0&3\end{bmatrix}=19.
\]
The three replaced-column determinants are $95,76,57$, so
\[
(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{95}{19},\frac{76}{19},\frac{57}{19}\right)=(5,4,3).
\]
:::
