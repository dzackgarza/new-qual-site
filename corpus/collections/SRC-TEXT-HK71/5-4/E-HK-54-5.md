::: {.exercise}
An $n \times n$ matrix $A$ over the field of complex numbers is said to be unitary if $AA^* = I$ ($A^*$ denotes the conjugate transpose of $A$). If $A$ is unitary, show that $\abs{\det A} = 1$.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise ; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §5.4 Exercise 5 on printed p. 163; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $A$ is an $n\times n$ matrix over $\CC$, $A^{*}=\overline{A}^{\,t}$ is its conjugate transpose, $A$ is unitary when $AA^{*}=I$, and $\abs{z}$ denotes the modulus of a complex number. The facts used about the determinant are that it is multiplicative, that $\det I=1$, that $\det(A^{t})=\det A$, and that conjugation carries determinants to determinants.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #conjugate}
$\det(A^{*})=\overline{\det A}$.

::: pf-proof
$\det(A^{*})=\det(\overline{A}^{\,t})=\det(\overline{A})$, since transposition does not change the determinant: the Leibniz expansion of $\det(A^{t})$ runs over the same products of entries, one from each row and column, as that of $\det A$. And $\det(\overline{A})=\overline{\det A}$, because every product $\prod_ia_{i\sigma(i)}$ in the expansion of $\det(\overline{A})$ is the conjugate of the corresponding product in the expansion of $\det A$, and conjugation is a field automorphism, so it commutes with the finite sum with its signs.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #product}
$(\det A)\overline{\det A}=1$.

::: pf-proof
By multiplicativity and $AA^{*}=I$,
$$
(\det A)\det(A^{*})=\det(AA^{*})=\det I=1,
$$
and $\det(A^{*})=\overline{\det A}$ by step [](#conjugate){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #modulus}
$\abs{\det A}=1$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#product){.pf-ref}, $\abs{\det A}^{2}=(\det A)\overline{\det A}=1$, using $z\bar z=\abs{z}^{2}$ for complex $z$. The modulus $\abs{\det A}$ is a nonnegative real number whose square is $1$, and the only such number is $1$; hence $\abs{\det A}=1$, and in particular $\det A\neq0$.

:::
