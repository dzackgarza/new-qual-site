::: {.exercise}
If $V$ is the vector space of $n \times n$ matrices over $F$ and $B$ is a fixed $n \times n$ matrix over $F$, let $L_B$ and $R_B$ be the linear operators on $V$ defined by $L_B(A) = BA$ and $R_B(A) = AB$.
Show that

(a) $\det L_B = (\det B)^n$;

(b) $\det R_B = (\det B)^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Part (a): Determinant of left multiplication $L_B$:
<2>1. Decompose the vector space $V = M_n(F)$ as a direct sum of its $n$ column subspaces:
\[
V = V_1 \oplus V_2 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_n,
\]
where $V_j = \{A \in M_n(F) \mid \text{column } k \text{ of } A \text{ is } 0 \text{ for all } k \neq j\} \cong F^n$.
Proof: every matrix is uniquely the sum of its single-column components.
<2>2. For any $A = [A_1 \mid A_2 \mid \cdots \mid A_n] \in V$:
\[
L_B(A) = BA = [B A_1 \mid B A_2 \mid \cdots \mid B A_n].
\]
Thus each subspace $V_j$ is an invariant subspace under $L_B$, and the restriction $L_B|_{V_j}: V_j \to V_j$ has matrix representation $B$ with respect to the standard basis of $V_j \cong F^n$.
Proof: definition of matrix multiplication on column vectors.
<2>3. With respect to the ordered basis $\mathcal{B} = (E_{11}, E_{21}, \dots, E_{n1}, E_{12}, \dots, E_{nn})$, the matrix of $L_B$ is block diagonal:
\[
[L_B]_\mathcal{B} = \begin{pmatrix}
B & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
0 & B & \cdots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \cdots & B
\end{pmatrix} = I_n \otimes B.
\]
Proof: invariant direct sum decomposition into $n$ identical blocks.
<2>4. The determinant of a block diagonal matrix is the product of the determinants of the diagonal blocks:
\[
\det L_B = \prod_{j=1}^n \det B = (\det B)^n.
\]
Proof: determinant of block diagonal matrix.

<1>2. Part (b): Determinant of right multiplication $R_B$:
<2>1. Let $\tau: V \to V$ be the matrix transposition operator $\tau(A) = A^T$.
$\tau$ is an invertible linear operator on $V$ with $\tau^2 = \operatorname{id}_V$.
Proof: $(A^T)^T = A$ and linearity of transpose.
<2>2. For any $A \in V$:
\[
(\tau \circ R_B \circ \tau)(A) = \tau\big(R_B(A^T)\big) = \tau(A^T B) = (A^T B)^T = B^T A = L_{B^T}(A).
\]
Thus $R_B = \tau^{-1} \circ L_{B^T} \circ \tau$.
Proof: anti-automorphism property of transpose $(XY)^T = Y^T X^T$.
<2>3. Taking the determinant of both sides:
\[
\det R_B = \det(\tau^{-1} \circ L_{B^T} \circ \tau) = \det(L_{B^T}) = (\det B^T)^n = (\det B)^n.
\]
Proof: multiplicative property of determinants, Part (a) applied to $B^T$, and $\det B^T = \det B$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
$\det L_B = (\det B)^n$ and $\det R_B = (\det B)^n$. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.
