::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the vector space of $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$.
Let $B$ be a fixed element of $V$ and let $T_B$ be the linear operator on $V$ defined by $T_B(A) = AB - BA$.
Show that $\det T_B = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §5.4 Exercise 8, and assumes $n\ge1$, without which $V$ is the zero space and $T_B$ the identity operator on it.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #kernel}
$T_B(I)=0$ while $I\ne0$, so $T_B$ has nonzero kernel.

::: pf-proof
$T_B(I)=I\cdot B-B\cdot I=B-B=0$, and the identity matrix is a nonzero matrix when $n\ge1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #notinvertible}
$T_B$ is not invertible.

::: pf-proof
An invertible linear map is injective, and $T_B$ is not injective because it kills the nonzero vector $I$ by step [](#kernel){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #det}
$\det T_B=0$.

::: pf-proof
For an operator on a finite-dimensional space, $\det T_B\ne0$ exactly when $T_B$ is invertible: the determinant is the product of the eigenvalues, and it is also the constant term of the characteristic polynomial up to sign, so it vanishes exactly when $0$ is an eigenvalue, that is, when the operator has nontrivial kernel. Step [](#notinvertible){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\boxed{\det T_B=0.}
$$
:::
