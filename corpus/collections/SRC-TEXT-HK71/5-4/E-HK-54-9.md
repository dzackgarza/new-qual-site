::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over a field, $A \neq 0$.
If $r$ is any positive integer between 1 and $n$, an $r \times r$ submatrix of $A$ is any $r \times r$ matrix obtained by deleting $(n - r)$ rows and $(n - r)$ columns of $A$.
The determinant rank of $A$ is the largest positive integer $r$ such that some $r \times r$ submatrix of $A$ has a non-zero determinant.
Prove that the determinant rank of $A$ is equal to the row rank of $A$ (= column rank $A$).
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\rho(A)$ denote the ordinary rank of $A$ and let $d(A)$ denote its determinant rank.

If an $s\times s$ submatrix $M$ has nonzero determinant, then the $s$ selected columns of $M$ are linearly independent. Any linear relation among the corresponding columns of $A$ would restrict to the same relation among the columns of $M$, so those columns of $A$ are linearly independent. Hence
\[
s\le \rho(A).
\]
Taking the largest such $s$ gives
\[
d(A)\le \rho(A).
\]

Conversely, put $r=\rho(A)$. Choose $r$ linearly independent columns of $A$ and form the resulting $n\times r$ matrix $C$. Then $C$ has rank $r$. Since row rank equals column rank, $C$ has $r$ linearly independent rows. Restricting $C$ to those $r$ rows gives an $r\times r$ matrix $M$ whose rows are linearly independent; therefore $\det M\ne0$. This $M$ is an $r\times r$ submatrix of $A$, so
\[
d(A)\ge r=\rho(A).
\]
Therefore
\[
d(A)=\rho(A),
\]
and this equals both the row rank and the column rank.
:::
