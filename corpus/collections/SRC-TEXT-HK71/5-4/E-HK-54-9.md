::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over a field, $A \neq 0$.
If $r$ is any positive integer between 1 and $n$, an $r \times r$ submatrix of $A$ is any $r \times r$ matrix obtained by deleting $(n - r)$ rows and $(n - r)$ columns of $A$.
The determinant rank of $A$ is the largest positive integer $r$ such that some $r \times r$ submatrix of $A$ has a non-zero determinant.
Prove that the determinant rank of $A$ is equal to the row rank of $A$ (= column rank $A$).
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §5.4 Exercise 9, p. 163. The proof is the two inequalities: a nonzero minor bounds the rank from below by the size of the minor, and a rank-$r$ matrix exhibits a nonzero $r\times r$ minor.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #first}
If an $s\times s$ submatrix $M$ of $A$ has nonzero determinant, then $s\le\rho(A)$, where $\rho$ is the row rank.

::: pf-proof
The $s$ columns of $M$ are linearly independent, since a dependence among them would give a nonzero vector $x$ with $Mx=0$ and hence $\det M=0$. A dependence among the corresponding $s$ columns of $A$ would restrict to the same dependence among the columns of $M$, so those columns of $A$ are independent and $s$ does not exceed the column rank, which equals the row rank.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #second}
The determinant rank satisfies $d(A)\le\rho(A)$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#first){.pf-ref} bounds the size of every submatrix with nonzero determinant by $\rho(A)$; the largest such size is $d(A)$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #third}
There is an $r\times r$ submatrix of $A$ with nonzero determinant, where $r=\rho(A)$.

::: pf-proof
Choose $r$ linearly independent columns of $A$ and let $C$ be the resulting $n\times r$ matrix. Its column rank is $r$, hence its row rank is $r$, so $r$ of its rows are linearly independent; the $r\times r$ matrix formed by those rows is a submatrix of $A$ whose rows are independent, so its determinant is nonzero.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #fourth}
$d(A)\ge\rho(A)$, hence $d(A)=\rho(A)$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#third){.pf-ref} exhibits a submatrix of size $r=\rho(A)$ with nonzero determinant, so the determinant rank is at least $r$; with step [](#second){.pf-ref} the two numbers agree, and since the row rank equals the column rank by the theorem of §5.4, the determinant rank equals both.
:::
:::
