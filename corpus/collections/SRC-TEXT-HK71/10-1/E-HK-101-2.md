::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be the bilinear form on $\RR^2$ defined by
\[
f((x_1, x_2), (y_1, y_2)) = x_1y_1 + x_2y_2.
\]
Find the matrix of $f$ in each of the following bases:
\[
\{(1, 0), (0, 1)\}, \quad \{(1, -1), (1, 1)\}, \quad \{(1, 2), (3, 4)\}.
\]
:::

::: remark
The printed exercise groups the coordinates as $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$
while retaining the dot-product formula $x_1y_1+x_2y_2$. Taken literally,
that expression is not bilinear in the two displayed vector arguments. The
preceding dot-product example makes the intended grouping
$(x_1,x_2)$ and $(y_1,y_2)$ clear; that corrected statement is used here.
:::

::: solution
If $\mathfrak B=(v_1,v_2)$ is an ordered basis and $P$ is the matrix whose
columns are $v_1,v_2$ in the standard basis, then the matrix of the standard
dot-product form in $\mathfrak B$ is
$$
[f]_{\mathfrak B}=P^tIP=P^tP.
$$

<1>1. Compute the matrix in the standard basis.
::: proof
For
$$
\mathfrak B_1=((1,0),(0,1)),
$$
the change-of-basis matrix is $P_1=I$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
[f]_{\mathfrak B_1}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
:::

<1>2. Compute the matrix in the basis $((1,-1),(1,1))$.
::: proof
Here
$$
P_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
-1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Hence
$$
[f]_{\mathfrak B_2}
=P_2^tP_2
=\begin{pmatrix}
2&0\\
0&2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{[f]_{\mathfrak B_2}=2I_2.}
$$
:::

<1>3. Compute the matrix in the basis $((1,2),(3,4))$.
::: proof
The change-of-basis matrix is
$$
P_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&3\\
2&4
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
[f]_{\mathfrak B_3}
&=P_3^tP_3\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
3&4
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&3\\
2&4
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
5&11\\
11&25
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
[f]_{\mathfrak B_3}
=\begin{pmatrix}5&11\\11&25\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
:::
:::
