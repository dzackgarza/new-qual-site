::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be a bilinear form on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Suppose $V_1$ is a subspace of $V$ with the property that the restriction of $f$ to $V_1$ is non-degenerate.
Show that $\operatorname{rank} f \geq \dim V_1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Define the linear map associated with $f$ by
$$
L_f:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
L_f(\alpha)(\beta)=f(\alpha,\beta).
$$
By definition,
$$
\operatorname{rank}f=\operatorname{rank}L_f.
$$

<1>1. The restriction of $f$ to $V_1$ defines an injective map $V_1\to V_1^*$.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
L_{f_1}:V_1\longrightarrow V_1^*,
\qquad
L_{f_1}(\alpha)(\beta)=f(\alpha,\beta)
$$
for $\alpha,\beta\in V_1$.
If
$$
L_{f_1}(\alpha)=0,
$$
then
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=0
$$
for every $\beta\in V_1$. Since the restriction $f_1$ is non-degenerate,
this forces $\alpha=0$. Hence $L_{f_1}$ is injective, and therefore
$$
\operatorname{rank}L_{f_1}=\dim V_1.
$$
:::

<1>2. The restricted map factors through $L_f$.
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
i:V_1\hookrightarrow V
$$
be inclusion and let
$$
\rho:V^*\longrightarrow V_1^*
$$
be restriction of linear functionals. For $\alpha\in V_1$ and
$\beta\in V_1$,
$$
(\rho L_f i)(\alpha)(\beta)
=L_f(\alpha)(\beta)
=f(\alpha,\beta).
$$
Thus
$$
L_{f_1}=\rho\circ L_f\circ i.
$$
Consequently
$$
\operatorname{rank}L_{f_1}
\le \operatorname{rank}L_f.
$$
:::

<1>3. Conclude the rank inequality.
::: {.proof}
Combining steps <1>1 and <1>2 gives
$$
\dim V_1
=\operatorname{rank}L_{f_1}
\le\operatorname{rank}L_f
=\operatorname{rank}f.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}f\ge\dim V_1.}
$$
:::
:::
