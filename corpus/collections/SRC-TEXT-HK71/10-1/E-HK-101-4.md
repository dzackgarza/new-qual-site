::: {.exercise}
Describe explicitly all bilinear forms $f$ on $\RR^3$ with the property that $f(\alpha, \beta) = f(\beta, \alpha)$ for all $\alpha, \beta$.
:::

::: solution
Let
$$
\alpha=(x_1,x_2,x_3),
\qquad
\beta=(y_1,y_2,y_3).
$$

<1>1. A bilinear form is determined by its matrix in the standard basis.
::: proof
Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be the standard basis and put
$$
a_{ij}=f(e_i,e_j).
$$
By bilinearity,
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)
=\sum_{i,j=1}^3 a_{ij}x_i y_j.
$$
Thus every bilinear form is represented uniquely by the matrix
$A=(a_{ij})$ through
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha^tA\beta.
$$
:::

<1>2. Symmetry of the form is exactly symmetry of its matrix.
::: proof
If
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=f(\beta,\alpha)
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$, then in particular
$$
a_{ij}=f(e_i,e_j)=f(e_j,e_i)=a_{ji}.
$$
Hence $A=A^t$.

Conversely, if $A=A^t$, then
$$
f(\beta,\alpha)
=\beta^tA\alpha
=\alpha^tA^t\beta
=\alpha^tA\beta
=f(\alpha,\beta).
$$
So the form is symmetric.
:::

<1>3. Write all such forms explicitly.
::: proof
An arbitrary symmetric $3\times3$ real matrix has the form
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&d&e\\
d&b&g\\
e&g&c
\end{pmatrix},
$$
with arbitrary real parameters $a,b,c,d,e,g$. Therefore the symmetric
bilinear forms on $\mathbb R^3$ are exactly
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha,\beta)
={}&a x_1y_1+b x_2y_2+c x_3y_3\\
&+d(x_1y_2+x_2y_1)
+e(x_1y_3+x_3y_1)\\
&+g(x_2y_3+x_3y_2),
\end{aligned}}
$$
where $a,b,c,d,e,g\in\mathbb R$ are arbitrary. Conversely every expression
of this form is visibly bilinear and symmetric.
:::
:::
