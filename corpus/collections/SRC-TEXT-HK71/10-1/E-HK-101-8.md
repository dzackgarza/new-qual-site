::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be the bilinear form defined in Exercise 6, and let $V_2$ be the subspace of $V$ consisting of all matrices $A$ such that $\operatorname{trace}(A) = 0$ and $A^* = -A$ ($A^*$ is the conjugate transpose of $A$). Denote by $f_2$ the restriction of $f$ to $V_2$.
Show that $f_2$ is negative definite, i.e., that $f_2(A, A) < 0$ for each non-zero $A$ in $V_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

From Exercise 6, $f(A, B) = n\operatorname{tr}(AB) - \operatorname{tr}(A)\operatorname{tr}(B)$.

::: pf-proof

the definition of $f$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For $A \in V_2$, $\operatorname{tr}(A) = 0$, so $f(A, A) = n\operatorname{tr}(A^2)$.

::: pf-proof

substitute $\operatorname{tr}(A) = 0$ into step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $A^* = -A$, $A$ is skew-Hermitian, so $A$ is diagonalizable with purely imaginary eigenvalues $i\lambda_1, \ldots, i\lambda_n$ (with $\lambda_j \in \RR$).

::: pf-proof

a skew-Hermitian matrix is normal, hence unitarily diagonalizable, and its eigenvalues are purely imaginary.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

$\operatorname{tr}(A^2) = \sum_j (i\lambda_j)^2 = -\sum_j \lambda_j^2 \le 0$.

::: pf-proof

the trace is the sum of the eigenvalues of $A^2$, which are $(i\lambda_j)^2 = -\lambda_j^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

$\operatorname{tr}(A^2) < 0$ for $A \neq 0$.

::: pf-proof

if $A \neq 0$, then at least one eigenvalue $i\lambda_j \neq 0$, so $\lambda_j \neq 0$ and $-\sum_j \lambda_j^2 < 0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Hence $f(A, A) = n\operatorname{tr}(A^2) < 0$ for every nonzero $A \in V_2$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s2){.pf-ref}, [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

:::
