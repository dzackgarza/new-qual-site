::: {.exercise}
Describe the bilinear forms on $\RR^3$ which satisfy $f(\alpha, \beta) = -f(\beta, \alpha)$ for all $\alpha, \beta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\alpha=(x_1,x_2,x_3),
\qquad
\beta=(y_1,y_2,y_3).
$$

<1>1. The matrix of such a form must be skew-symmetric.
::: {.proof}
Write
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha^tA\beta,
\qquad A=(a_{ij}),
$$
where
$$
a_{ij}=f(e_i,e_j).
$$
The hypothesis gives
$$
a_{ij}=f(e_i,e_j)=-f(e_j,e_i)=-a_{ji}.
$$
In particular, taking $i=j$ yields
$$
a_{ii}=-a_{ii},
$$
and since the scalar field is $\mathbb R$,
$$
a_{ii}=0.
$$
Thus
$$
A^t=-A.
$$
:::

<1>2. Conversely, every skew-symmetric matrix defines such a form.
::: {.proof}
If $A^t=-A$, then
$$
f(\beta,\alpha)
=\beta^tA\alpha
=\alpha^tA^t\beta
=-\alpha^tA\beta
=-f(\alpha,\beta).
$$
Hence skew-symmetry of the matrix is equivalent to the required identity.
:::

<1>3. Write all such forms explicitly.
::: {.proof}
An arbitrary real skew-symmetric $3\times3$ matrix is
$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&a&b\\
-a&0&c\\
-b&-c&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
with $a,b,c\in\mathbb R$. Therefore the desired bilinear forms are exactly
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha,\beta)
={}&a(x_1y_2-x_2y_1)\\
&+b(x_1y_3-x_3y_1)\\
&+c(x_2y_3-x_3y_2),
\end{aligned}}
$$
where $a,b,c$ are arbitrary real scalars.
:::
:::
