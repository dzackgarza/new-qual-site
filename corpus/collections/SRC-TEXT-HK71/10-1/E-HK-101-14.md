::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be a nonzero bilinear form on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Show that $f$ can be expressed as a product of two linear functionals (i.e., $f(\alpha, \beta) = L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta)$ for $L_1, L_2$ in $V^*$) if and only if $f$ has rank 1.
:::

::: remark
The printed exercise does not say that $f$ is nonzero. Without that
qualification the assertion is false: the zero form is
$0(\alpha)L_2(\beta)$ for any $L_2$, but has rank zero. Equivalently one
could leave $f$ arbitrary and require both factor functionals to be nonzero.
:::

::: solution
Define
$$
L_f:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
L_f(\alpha)(\beta)=f(\alpha,\beta).
$$
Then
$$
\operatorname{rank}f=\dim\operatorname{im}L_f.
$$

<1>1. A product of two functionals has rank one when the form is nonzero.
::: proof
Suppose
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta).
$$
Because $f\ne0$, neither $L_1$ nor $L_2$ is zero. For every $\alpha$,
$$
L_f(\alpha)=L_1(\alpha)L_2,
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{im}L_f\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{L_2\}.
$$
Since $L_1\ne0$, choose $\alpha_0$ with $L_1(\alpha_0)\ne0$. Then
$$
L_f(\alpha_0)=L_1(\alpha_0)L_2\ne0,
$$
so the image is not zero. Hence
$$
\operatorname{im}L_f=\operatorname{span}\{L_2\}
$$
is one-dimensional, and
$$
\operatorname{rank}f=1.
$$
:::

<1>2. Every rank-one bilinear form factors as a product of two functionals.
::: proof
Assume
$$
\operatorname{rank}f=1.
$$
Then $\operatorname{im}L_f$ is a one-dimensional subspace of $V^*$. Choose
a nonzero functional $L_2$ spanning it. For each $\alpha\in V$ there is a
unique scalar $L_1(\alpha)$ such that
$$
L_f(\alpha)=L_1(\alpha)L_2.
$$

We claim that $L_1$ is linear. If $a,b$ are scalars and
$\alpha,\gamma\in V$, linearity of $L_f$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_1(a\alpha+b\gamma)L_2
&=L_f(a\alpha+b\gamma)\\
&=aL_f(\alpha)+bL_f(\gamma)\\
&=(aL_1(\alpha)+bL_1(\gamma))L_2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $L_2\ne0$, equality of these scalar multiples implies
$$
L_1(a\alpha+b\gamma)
=aL_1(\alpha)+bL_1(\gamma).
$$
Thus $L_1\in V^*$.

Finally, evaluating the identity
$L_f(\alpha)=L_1(\alpha)L_2$ at $\beta$ gives
$$
\boxed{
f(\alpha,\beta)=L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta)}
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta\in V$.
:::
:::
