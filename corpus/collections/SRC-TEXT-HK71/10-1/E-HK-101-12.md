::: {.exercise}
Let $f, g$ be bilinear forms on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$.
Suppose $g$ is non-singular.
Show that there exist unique linear operators $T_1, T_2$ on $V$ such that
\[
f(\alpha, \beta) = g(T_1\alpha, \beta) = g(\alpha, T_2\beta)
\]
for all $\alpha, \beta$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For any bilinear form $h$ on $V$, define
$$
L_h:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
L_h(\alpha)(\beta)=h(\alpha,\beta),
$$
and
$$
R_h:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
R_h(\beta)(\alpha)=h(\alpha,\beta).
$$

<1>1. Nonsingularity of $g$ makes both $L_g$ and $R_g$ isomorphisms.
::: {.proof}
If $L_g(\alpha)=0$, then
$$
g(\alpha,\beta)=0
$$
for every $\beta\in V$. Nonsingularity of $g$ forces $\alpha=0$, so $L_g$
is injective. Because $V$ and $V^*$ have the same finite dimension, $L_g$ is
an isomorphism.

For a bilinear form on a finite-dimensional space, left and right
nonsingularity are equivalent. Hence the same argument shows that $R_g$ is
also injective and therefore an isomorphism.
:::

<1>2. Construct $T_1$ and verify the first factorization.
::: {.proof}
Define
$$
T_1=L_g^{-1}L_f.
$$
This is a linear operator on $V$. For every $\alpha,\beta\in V$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(T_1\alpha,\beta)
&=L_g(T_1\alpha)(\beta)\\
&=L_f(\alpha)(\beta)\\
&=f(\alpha,\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=g(T_1\alpha,\beta).
$$
:::

<1>3. Construct $T_2$ and verify the second factorization.
::: {.proof}
Define
$$
T_2=R_g^{-1}R_f.
$$
Then for every $\alpha,\beta$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(\alpha,T_2\beta)
&=R_g(T_2\beta)(\alpha)\\
&=R_f(\beta)(\alpha)\\
&=f(\alpha,\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=g(\alpha,T_2\beta).
$$
:::

<1>4. Prove uniqueness of $T_1$ and $T_2$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $S_1$ also satisfies
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=g(S_1\alpha,\beta)
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$. Then
$$
L_gS_1=L_f=L_gT_1.
$$
Since $L_g$ is injective,
$$
S_1=T_1.
$$

Similarly, if $S_2$ satisfies
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=g(\alpha,S_2\beta),
$$
then
$$
R_gS_2=R_f=R_gT_2,
$$
and injectivity of $R_g$ gives
$$
S_2=T_2.
$$
Therefore the two required operators exist uniquely.
:::
:::
