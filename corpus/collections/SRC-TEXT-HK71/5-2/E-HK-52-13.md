::: {.exercise}
Let $\RR$ be the field of real numbers, and let $D$ be a function on $2 \times 2$ matrices over $\RR$, with values in $\RR$, such that $D(AB) = D(A)D(B)$ for all $A, B$.
Suppose also that
\[
D\left(\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\right) \neq D\left(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\right).
\]
Prove the following.

(a) $D(0) = 0$;

(b) $D(A) = 0$ if $A^2 = 0$;

(c) $D(B) = -D(A)$ if $B$ is obtained by interchanging the rows (or columns) of $A$;

(d) $D(A) = 0$ if one row (or one column) of $A$ is 0;

(e) $D(A) = 0$ whenever $A$ is singular.
:::

::: solution
Let
\[
S=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The hypothesis $D(S)\ne D(I)$ rules out the identically zero function. By the preceding multiplicativity argument, $D(I)=1$. Since $S^2=I$,
\[
D(S)^2=D(I)=1.
\]
Over $\mathbb R$ this gives $D(S)=\pm1$, and the hypothesis excludes $+1$. Hence
\[
D(S)=-1.
\]

For (a), multiplicativity gives $D(0)=D(0)^2$, so $D(0)\in\{0,1\}$. If $D(0)=1$, then for every $A$,
\[
1=D(0)=D(0A)=D(0)D(A)=D(A),
\]
contradicting $D(S)=-1$. Thus $D(0)=0$.

For (b), if $A^2=0$, then
\[
D(A)^2=D(A^2)=D(0)=0,
\]
so $D(A)=0$.

For (c), interchanging the rows of $A$ replaces $A$ by $SA$, while interchanging its columns replaces it by $AS$. In either case multiplicativity and $D(S)=-1$ give
\[
D(B)=-D(A).
\]

For (d), suppose first that the first row of $A$ is zero, so
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\a&b\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\0&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The first factor squares to zero, so by (b) its $D$-value is zero; hence $D(A)=0$. If the second row is zero, interchange the rows and use (c). The column statement follows identically, or by the analogous factorization.

For (e), let $A$ be singular. If a row is zero, (d) applies. Otherwise its two rows are linearly dependent, so the second row is $\lambda$ times the first for some $\lambda\in\mathbb R$. Writing the first row as $(a,b)$,
\[
A=
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\\lambda&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\0&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The second factor has a zero row, so (d) gives it $D$-value zero. Multiplicativity therefore gives $D(A)=0$.
:::
