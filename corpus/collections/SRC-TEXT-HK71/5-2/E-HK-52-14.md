::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be a $2 \times 2$ matrix over a field $F$.
Then the set of all matrices of the form $f(A)$, where $f$ is a polynomial over $F$, is a commutative ring $K$ with identity.
If $B$ is a $2 \times 2$ matrix over $K$, the determinant of $B$ is then a $2 \times 2$ matrix over $F$, of the form $f(A)$.
Suppose $I$ is the $2 \times 2$ identity matrix over $F$ and that $B$ is the $2 \times 2$ matrix over $K$
\[
B = \begin{bmatrix} A - A_{11}I & -A_{12}I \\ -A_{21}I & A - A_{22}I \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Show that $\det B = f(A)$, where $f = x^2 - (A_{11} + A_{22})x + \det A$, and also that $f(A) = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $a=A_{11}$, $b=A_{12}$, $c=A_{21}$, and $d=A_{22}$. Since every element of $K$ is a polynomial in $A$, the entries of $B$ commute. Therefore the $2\times2$ determinant over $K$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det B
&=(A-aI)(A-dI)-(-bI)(-cI)\\
&=A^2-(a+d)A+(ad-bc)I\\
&=f(A),
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
f(x)=x^2-(a+d)x+(ad-bc).
\]
Since $ad-bc=\det A$, this is the polynomial asserted in the exercise.

It remains to show that $f(A)=0$. Direct multiplication gives
\[
A^2=
\begin{bmatrix}
a^2+bc&ab+bd\\
ac+cd&bc+d^2
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Hence
\[
A^2-(a+d)A
=
\begin{bmatrix}
bc-ad&0\\0&bc-ad
\end{bmatrix}
=-(ad-bc)I.
\]
Consequently
\[
f(A)=A^2-(a+d)A+(ad-bc)I=0.
\]
:::
