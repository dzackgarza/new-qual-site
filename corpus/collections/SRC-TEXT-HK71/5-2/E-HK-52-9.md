::: {.exercise}
Let $K$ be a commutative ring with identity and $D$ an alternating $n$-linear function on $n \times n$ matrices over $K$.
Show that

(a) $D(A) = 0$, if one of the rows of $A$ is 0.

(b) $D(B) = D(A)$, if $B$ is obtained from $A$ by adding a scalar multiple of one row of $A$ to another.
:::

::: solution
Regard $D(A)$ as a function of the ordered rows $r_1,\dots,r_n$.

For (a), suppose $r_i=0$. By linearity in the $i$th row,
\[
D(r_1,\dots,0,\dots,r_n)
=D(r_1,\dots,0+0,\dots,r_n)
=2D(r_1,\dots,0,\dots,r_n).
\]
Subtracting gives $D(A)=0$.

For (b), suppose $B$ is obtained by replacing $r_j$ by $r_j+\lambda r_i$, with $i\ne j$. Multilinearity gives
\[
D(B)
=D(r_1,\dots,r_j,\dots,r_n)
+\lambda D(r_1,\dots,\underset{j}{r_i},\dots,r_n).
\]
In the second term the $i$th and $j$th rows are equal, so alternation makes that term zero. Hence $D(B)=D(A)$.
:::
