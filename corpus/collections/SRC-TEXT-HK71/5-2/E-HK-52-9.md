::: {.exercise}
Let $K$ be a commutative ring with identity and $D$ an alternating $n$-linear function on $n \times n$ matrices over $K$.
Show that

(a) $D(A) = 0$, if one of the rows of $A$ is 0.

(b) $D(B) = D(A)$, if $B$ is obtained from $A$ by adding a scalar multiple of one row of $A$ to another.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §5.2 Exercise 9, p. 149. Both parts use only the two defining properties of an alternating $n$-linear function: linearity in each row, and vanishing when two rows agree.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #a}
(a) If the $i$-th row of $A$ is $0$, then $D(A)=0$.

::: pf-proof
Regard $D$ as a function of the ordered rows. By linearity in the $i$-th row, $D(r_1,\dots,0,\dots,r_n)=c\,D(r_1,\dots,0,\dots,r_n)$ for every scalar $c$, taking the row $0=c\cdot0$; with $c=0$ the right side is $0$, so $D(A)=0$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #expansion}
(b) If $B$ is obtained from $A$ by adding $\lambda$ times row $i$ to row $j$ with $i\neq j$, then $D(B)=D(A)+\lambda D(A')$, where $A'$ is $A$ with row $j$ replaced by row $i$.

::: pf-proof
This is linearity of $D$ in the $j$-th row: the row $r_j+\lambda r_i$ splits into the two summands, and $D$ is additive and homogeneous in that argument.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #vanish}
For $A'$ as above, $D(A')=0$.

::: pf-proof
In $A'$ the rows in positions $i$ and $j$ are both $r_i$, so two arguments of the alternating function agree; an alternating function vanishes whenever two of its arguments are equal.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
(b) $D(B)=D(A)$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#expansion){.pf-ref} and [](#vanish){.pf-ref} give $D(B)=D(A)+\lambda\cdot0=D(A)$.
:::
:::
