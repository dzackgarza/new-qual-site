::: {.exercise}
Let $K$ be a commutative ring with identity.
Define a function $D$ on $3 \times 3$ matrices over $K$ by Laplace cofactor expansion along the first row:
\[
D(A) = A_{11} \det \begin{bmatrix} A_{22} & A_{23} \\ A_{32} & A_{33} \end{bmatrix} - A_{12} \det \begin{bmatrix} A_{21} & A_{23} \\ A_{31} & A_{33} \end{bmatrix} + A_{13} \det \begin{bmatrix} A_{21} & A_{22} \\ A_{31} & A_{32} \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Show that $D$ is **alternating** and **3-linear** as a function of the columns of $A$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that the Laplace expansion $D(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ on $3 \times 3$ matrices is multilinear (3-linear) and alternating in its column vectors.

<1>1. Setting and Column Representation:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let the columns of $A \in M_3(K)$ be $c_1 = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} \\ A_{21} \\ A_{31} \end{bmatrix}$, $c_2 = \begin{bmatrix} A_{12} \\ A_{22} \\ A_{32} \end{bmatrix}$, $c_3 = \begin{bmatrix} A_{13} \\ A_{23} \\ A_{33} \end{bmatrix}$.
    <2>2. The formula for $D(A) = D(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ expands as:
        $$D(c_1, c_2, c_3) = A_{11}(A_{22} A_{33} - A_{32} A_{23}) - A_{12}(A_{21} A_{33} - A_{31} A_{23}) + A_{13}(A_{21} A_{32} - A_{31} A_{22}).$$
    <2>3. Expanding the terms fully:
        $$D(A) = A_{11} A_{22} A_{33} - A_{11} A_{32} A_{23} - A_{12} A_{21} A_{33} + A_{12} A_{31} A_{23} + A_{13} A_{21} A_{32} - A_{13} A_{31} A_{22}.$$
    <2>4. In permutation notation:
        $$D(A) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_3} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) A_{\sigma(1), 1} A_{\sigma(2), 2} A_{\sigma(3), 3} = \sum_{\tau \in S_3} \operatorname{sgn}(\tau) A_{1, \tau(1)} A_{2, \tau(2)} A_{3, \tau(3)}.$$

<1>2. Proof of 3-Linearity:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. In the fully expanded sum, each monomial contains exactly one entry from column 1 ($A_{i1}$), exactly one entry from column 2 ($A_{j2}$), and exactly one entry from column 3 ($A_{k3}$).
    <2>2. If column $c_1$ is replaced by $\alpha u + \beta v$, then for each row $i$, $A_{i1} = \alpha u_i + \beta v_i$.
    <2>3. By distributivity of multiplication over addition in $K$:
        $$D(\alpha u + \beta v, c_2, c_3) = \alpha D(u, c_2, c_3) + \beta D(v, c_2, c_3).$$
    <2>4. By identical reasoning on columns 2 and 3, $D$ is linear in $c_2$ and linear in $c_3$.
    <2>5. Therefore, $D$ is a **3-linear function** of the columns.

<1>3. Proof that $D$ is Alternating:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. A multilinear function is alternating if $D(A) = 0$ whenever two adjacent columns are equal.
    <2>2. **Case 1: $c_1 = c_2$.**
        - Then $A_{i1} = A_{i2}$ for all $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
        - In the definition of $D(A)$:
          - The first two terms are $A_{11}(A_{21} A_{33} - A_{31} A_{23}) - A_{11}(A_{21} A_{33} - A_{31} A_{23}) = 0$.
          - The third minor is $\det \begin{bmatrix} A_{21} & A_{21} \\ A_{31} & A_{31} \end{bmatrix} = A_{21} A_{31} - A_{31} A_{21} = 0$.
        - Thus $D(c_1, c_1, c_3) = 0 - 0 + A_{13}(0) = 0$.
    <2>3. **Case 2: $c_2 = c_3$.**
        - Then $A_{i2} = A_{i3}$ for all $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
        - The first minor is $\det \begin{bmatrix} A_{22} & A_{22} \\ A_{32} & A_{32} \end{bmatrix} = 0$.
        - The second and third terms are:
          $$-A_{12}(A_{21} A_{32} - A_{31} A_{22}) + A_{12}(A_{21} A_{32} - A_{31} A_{22}) = 0.$$
        - Thus $D(c_1, c_2, c_2) = A_{11}(0) - 0 = 0$.
    <2>4. **Case 3: $c_1 = c_3$.**
        - By swapping columns, $D(c_1, c_2, c_1) = 0$.
    <2>5. Since $D(A)$ vanishes whenever any two columns coincide, $D$ is **alternating**.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $D$ is linear in each column entry and vanishes when any two columns are equal, so $D$ is an alternating 3-linear function. Q.E.D.
:::
