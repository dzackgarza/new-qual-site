::: {.exercise}
Let $K$ be a commutative ring with identity.
Define a function $D$ on $3 \times 3$ matrices over $K$ by Laplace cofactor expansion along the first row:
$$
D(A) = A_{11} \det \begin{bmatrix} A_{22} & A_{23} \\ A_{32} & A_{33} \end{bmatrix} - A_{12} \det \begin{bmatrix} A_{21} & A_{23} \\ A_{31} & A_{33} \end{bmatrix} + A_{13} \det \begin{bmatrix} A_{21} & A_{22} \\ A_{31} & A_{32} \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Show that $D$ is **alternating** and **3-linear** as a function of the columns of $A$.
:::

::: {.solution}

$D$ is a polynomial of degree at most one in each column, and it vanishes on repeated columns; steps 1--9 verify the first property from the cofactor formula and steps 10--15 the second.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Setting and column representation:

::: pf-proof

Steps 1.1--1.4 rewrite the cofactor expansion as a sum over permutations, in which each monomial visibly carries one entry from each column; that is the form used in both halves of the proof.

::: pf-step

Let the columns of $A \in M_3(K)$ be $c_1 = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} \\ A_{21} \\ A_{31} \end{bmatrix}$, $c_2 = \begin{bmatrix} A_{12} \\ A_{22} \\ A_{32} \end{bmatrix}$, $c_3 = \begin{bmatrix} A_{13} \\ A_{23} \\ A_{33} \end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof

The columns of $A$ are its three vertical slices: the $j$-th column has entries $A_{1j},A_{2j},A_{3j}$ in rows $1,2,3$. The nine entries of $A$ are exactly the nine entries of $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$, so $A$ is recovered from the triple of columns and $D(A)$ may be written $D(c_1,c_2,c_3)$ as a function of the three vectors.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The formula for $D(A) = D(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ expands as:
$$D(c_1, c_2, c_3) = A_{11}(A_{22} A_{33} - A_{32} A_{23}) - A_{12}(A_{21} A_{33} - A_{31} A_{23}) + A_{13}(A_{21} A_{32} - A_{31} A_{22}).$$

::: pf-proof

Each $2\times2$ determinant is $ad-bc$: the first is $A_{22}A_{33}-A_{23}A_{32}$, the second $A_{21}A_{33}-A_{23}A_{31}$, the third $A_{21}A_{32}-A_{22}A_{31}$. Substituting these three values into the cofactor expansion displayed in the statement gives the formula, the signs $+,-,+$ being those already printed there.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Expanding the terms fully:
$$D(A) = A_{11} A_{22} A_{33} - A_{11} A_{32} A_{23} - A_{12} A_{21} A_{33} + A_{12} A_{31} A_{23} + A_{13} A_{21} A_{32} - A_{13} A_{31} A_{22}.$$

::: pf-proof

Multiply out the three products of step 1.2 with the distributive law: $A_{11}(A_{22}A_{33}-A_{32}A_{23})=A_{11}A_{22}A_{33}-A_{11}A_{32}A_{23}$; $-A_{12}(A_{21}A_{33}-A_{31}A_{23})=-A_{12}A_{21}A_{33}+A_{12}A_{31}A_{23}$; $A_{13}(A_{21}A_{32}-A_{31}A_{22})=A_{13}A_{21}A_{32}-A_{13}A_{31}A_{22}$. The order of the factors is immaterial, $K$ being commutative.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

In permutation notation:
$$D(A) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_3} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) A_{\sigma(1), 1} A_{\sigma(2), 2} A_{\sigma(3), 3} = \sum_{\tau \in S_3} \operatorname{sgn}(\tau) A_{1, \tau(1)} A_{2, \tau(2)} A_{3, \tau(3)}.$$

::: pf-proof

The six monomials of step 1.3 are the six permutations of the row indices: the identity contributes $A_{11}A_{22}A_{33}$ with sign $+1$; $(23)$ contributes $A_{11}A_{32}A_{23}$ with sign $-1$; $(12)$ contributes $A_{21}A_{12}A_{33}$ with sign $-1$; $(132)$ contributes $A_{31}A_{12}A_{23}$ with sign $+1$; $(123)$ contributes $A_{21}A_{32}A_{13}$ with sign $+1$; $(13)$ contributes $A_{31}A_{22}A_{13}$ with sign $-1$. These are the six terms of step 1.3 with their signs, which gives the first sum. The second sum is the first reindexed by $\tau=\sigma^{-1}$: with $j=\sigma(i)$ the monomial $\prod_i A_{\sigma(i),i}$ becomes $\prod_j A_{j,\tau(j)}$, and $\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)$.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Proof of 3-linearity:

::: pf-proof

Steps 2.1--2.4 show that $D$ is linear in each column with the other two held fixed; step 2.5 assembles the three into 3-linearity.

::: pf-step

In the fully expanded sum, each monomial contains exactly one entry from column 1 ($A_{i1}$), exactly one entry from column 2 ($A_{j2}$), and exactly one entry from column 3 ($A_{k3}$).

::: pf-proof

In the display of step 1.4 every monomial is $A_{1,\tau(1)}A_{2,\tau(2)}A_{3,\tau(3)}$ with $\tau$ a permutation of $\{1,2,3\}$: the first factor carries second index $1$, the second second index $2$ and the third second index $3$, and the three first indices $\tau(1),\tau(2),\tau(3)$ are distinct. So exactly one factor lies in each column, and no column occurs twice.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If column $c_1$ is replaced by $\alpha u + \beta v$, then for each row $i$, $A_{i1} = \alpha u_i + \beta v_i$.

::: pf-proof

Replacing the first column of $A$ by the vector $\alpha u+\beta v$ leaves the other eight entries unchanged and makes the three entries of the new first column $\alpha u_i+\beta v_i$, $i=1,2,3$; that is the definition of the column operation.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

By distributivity of multiplication over addition in $K$:
$$D(\alpha u + \beta v, c_2, c_3) = \alpha D(u, c_2, c_3) + \beta D(v, c_2, c_3).$$

::: pf-proof

By step 2.1, $D(c_1,c_2,c_3)=\sum_m A_{i_m 1}C_m$, where $C_m$ is the product of the two remaining factors of the $m$-th monomial of step 1.4, one from column 2 and one from column 3, and so does not involve column 1. Substituting $A_{i_m1}=\alpha u_{i_m}+\beta v_{i_m}$ and using distributivity gives $\sum_m(\alpha u_{i_m}+\beta v_{i_m})C_m=\alpha\sum_m u_{i_m}C_m+\beta\sum_m v_{i_m}C_m$, and the two sums are $D(u,c_2,c_3)$ and $D(v,c_2,c_3)$ because the coefficients $C_m$ of columns 2 and 3 are unchanged.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

By identical reasoning on columns 2 and 3, $D$ is linear in $c_2$ and linear in $c_3$.

::: pf-proof

Step 2.1 is symmetric in the three columns: every monomial has exactly one factor in column 2 and one in column 3 as well. So the computation of step 2.3, with column 2 or column 3 in place of column 1 and the coefficient $C_m$ read off the other two columns, gives $D(c_1,\alpha u+\beta v,c_3)=\alpha D(c_1,u,c_3)+\beta D(c_1,v,c_3)$ and the same identity in the third slot.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore, $D$ is a **3-linear function** of the columns.

::: pf-proof

Linearity in the first slot is step 2.3, in the second and third the two identities of step 2.4; that is precisely the definition of a 3-linear function of $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Proof that $D$ is alternating:

::: pf-proof

Hoffman and Kunze define an $n$-linear $D$ to be alternating when (a) $D(A)=0$ whenever two rows of $A$ are equal and (b) $D(B)=-D(A)$ when $B$ is obtained from $A$ by interchanging two rows (§5.2, printed p. 142), and they prove that (a) implies (b). Steps 3.2--3.4 verify (a) in the three cases of two equal columns; step 3.5 derives (b) from (a) and concludes.

::: pf-step

We show that $D(A) = 0$ whenever two columns of $A$ are equal. For a 3-linear $D$ this implies that interchanging two columns changes the sign, so $D$ is alternating.

::: pf-proof

By step 2.5, $D$ is 3-linear. A 3-linear $D$ that vanishes on every triple with two equal arguments satisfies condition (a) of the definition, and (a) implies (b) by the argument of step 3.5, so such a $D$ is alternating. It remains to check the three possible coincidences of columns, which steps 3.2--3.4 do.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

**Case 1: $c_1 = c_2$.**
- Then $A_{i1} = A_{i2}$ for all $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
- In the definition of $D(A)$:
  - The first two terms are $A_{11}(A_{21} A_{33} - A_{31} A_{23}) - A_{11}(A_{21} A_{33} - A_{31} A_{23}) = 0$.
  - The third minor is $\det \begin{bmatrix} A_{21} & A_{21} \\ A_{31} & A_{31} \end{bmatrix} = A_{21} A_{31} - A_{31} A_{21} = 0$.
- Thus $D(c_1, c_1, c_3) = 0 - 0 + A_{13}(0) = 0$.

::: pf-proof

$c_1=c_2$ means $A_{i1}=A_{i2}$ for $i=1,2,3$, that is $A_{12}=A_{11}$, $A_{22}=A_{21}$ and $A_{32}=A_{31}$. Then the first term of step 1.2 is $A_{11}(A_{22}A_{33}-A_{32}A_{23})=A_{11}(A_{21}A_{33}-A_{31}A_{23})$ and the second is its negative, so they cancel; the third is $A_{13}(A_{21}A_{32}-A_{31}A_{22})=A_{13}(A_{21}A_{31}-A_{31}A_{21})=0$. The sum is $0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

**Case 2: $c_2 = c_3$.**
- Then $A_{i2} = A_{i3}$ for all $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
- The first minor is $\det \begin{bmatrix} A_{22} & A_{22} \\ A_{32} & A_{32} \end{bmatrix} = 0$.
- The second and third terms are:
  $$-A_{12}(A_{21} A_{32} - A_{31} A_{22}) + A_{12}(A_{21} A_{32} - A_{31} A_{22}) = 0.$$
- Thus $D(c_1, c_2, c_2) = A_{11}(0) - 0 = 0$.

::: pf-proof

$c_2=c_3$ means $A_{i2}=A_{i3}$ for $i=1,2,3$, that is $A_{13}=A_{12}$, $A_{23}=A_{22}$ and $A_{33}=A_{32}$. The first minor of step 1.2 is $A_{22}A_{33}-A_{32}A_{23}=A_{22}A_{32}-A_{32}A_{22}=0$; the second and third terms are $-A_{12}(A_{21}A_{33}-A_{31}A_{23})=-A_{12}(A_{21}A_{32}-A_{31}A_{22})$ and $A_{13}(A_{21}A_{32}-A_{31}A_{22})=A_{12}(A_{21}A_{32}-A_{31}A_{22})$, which cancel. The sum is $0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

**Case 3: $c_1 = c_3$.**
- Then $A_{i1} = A_{i3}$ for all $i$, and
  $$D(A) = A_{11}(A_{22}A_{31} - A_{21}A_{32}) - A_{12}(A_{21}A_{31} - A_{21}A_{31}) + A_{11}(A_{21}A_{32} - A_{31}A_{22}) = 0.$$

::: pf-proof

$c_1=c_3$ means $A_{i1}=A_{i3}$ for $i=1,2,3$, that is $A_{13}=A_{11}$, $A_{23}=A_{21}$ and $A_{33}=A_{31}$. Substituting in step 1.2 gives $A_{11}(A_{22}A_{31}-A_{32}A_{21})$, then $-A_{12}(A_{21}A_{31}-A_{31}A_{21})=0$, then $A_{11}(A_{21}A_{32}-A_{31}A_{22})$; the first and third sum to $A_{11}(A_{22}A_{31}-A_{31}A_{22}+A_{21}A_{32}-A_{32}A_{21})=0$, since $K$ is commutative.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Since $D(A)$ vanishes whenever any two columns coincide, $D$ is **alternating**.

::: pf-proof

Cases 3.2--3.4 cover the three pairs of equal columns, so $D$ satisfies condition (a). For condition (b), let $B$ be obtained from $A$ by interchanging two columns, say the first two; by step 2.5, $0=D(c_1+c_2,c_1+c_2,c_3)=D(c_1,c_1,c_3)+D(c_1,c_2,c_3)+D(c_2,c_1,c_3)+D(c_2,c_2,c_3)=D(c_1,c_2,c_3)+D(c_2,c_1,c_3)$, the two repeated triples vanishing by (a), so $D(c_2,c_1,c_3)=-D(c_1,c_2,c_3)$; the same computation in the other two slots gives the other transpositions. Both conditions of the definition hold, so $D$ is alternating.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Conclusion:
$D$ is linear in each column entry and vanishes when any two columns are equal, so $D$ is an alternating 3-linear function.

::: pf-proof

Linearity in each column is step 2.5 and vanishing on coincident columns is step 3.5, which also derives the sign rule. That is exactly the statement that $D$ is an alternating 3-linear function of the columns of $A$, and it is what the exercise asks for.
