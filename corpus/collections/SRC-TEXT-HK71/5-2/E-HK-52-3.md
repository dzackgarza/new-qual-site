::: {.exercise}
Let $K$ be a commutative ring with identity.
If $A$ is a $2 \times 2$ matrix over $K$, the classical adjoint of $A$ is the $2 \times 2$ matrix $\operatorname{adj} A$ defined by
$$
\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} A_{22} & -A_{12} \\ -A_{21} & A_{11} \end{bmatrix}.
$$
If $\det$ denotes the unique determinant function on $2 \times 2$ matrices over $K$, show that

(a) $(\operatorname{adj} A)A = A(\operatorname{adj} A) = (\det A)I$;

(b) $\det(\operatorname{adj} A) = \det(A)$;

(c) $\operatorname{adj}(A^t) = (\operatorname{adj} A)^t$.

($A^t$ denotes the transpose of $A$.)
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §5.2 Exercise 3. Every product below is computed entry by entry, using only that $K$ is a commutative ring with identity.

Write
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix},
$$
so that $\det A=ad-bc$. Recall that the determinant on $2\times2$ matrices over $K$ is the unique function that is bilinear, alternating in the two columns and normalized by $\det I=1$; it follows that $\det\begin{bmatrix}p&q\\r&s\end{bmatrix}=ps-qr$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #right}
$(\operatorname{adj}A)A=(\det A)I$.

::: pf-proof
By the definition of the matrix product, the $(1,1)$ entry of the product is $da+(-b)c=da-bc=\det A$; the $(2,2)$ entry is $(-c)b+ad=ad-cb=\det A$; and the off-diagonal entries are $(1,2)$: $db+(-b)d=db-bd=0$ and $(2,1)$: $(-c)a+ac=-ca+ac=0$, both using commutativity of multiplication in $K$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #left}
$A(\operatorname{adj}A)=(\det A)I$.

::: pf-proof
The $(1,1)$ entry is $ad+b(-c)=ad-bc=\det A$, the $(2,2)$ entry is $c(-b)+da=da-cb=\det A$, and the off-diagonal entries are $(1,2)$: $a(-b)+bd=-ab+bd=0$ and $(2,1)$: $cd+d(-c)=cd-dc=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a}
Part (a) holds: $(\operatorname{adj}A)A=A(\operatorname{adj}A)=(\det A)I$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#right){.pf-ref} and [](#left){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
$\det(\operatorname{adj}A)=\det A$.

::: pf-proof
The matrix $\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix}$ has determinant $d\cdot a-(-b)(-c)=da-bc$, since $(-b)(-c)=bc$; and $da-bc=ad-cb=\det A$ because multiplication is commutative.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c}
$\operatorname{adj}(A^{t})=(\operatorname{adj}A)^{t}$.

::: pf-proof
$A^{t}=\begin{bmatrix}a&c\\b&d\end{bmatrix}$, so $\operatorname{adj}(A^{t})=\begin{bmatrix}d&-c\\-b&a\end{bmatrix}$. On the other hand $(\operatorname{adj}A)^{t}=\begin{bmatrix}d&-c\\-b&a\end{bmatrix}$, the transpose of $\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix}$. The two matrices have equal entries in every position.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Parts (a), (b) and (c) all hold, so the three identities are established for every $2\times 2$ matrix over a commutative ring with identity.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#a){.pf-ref}, [](#b){.pf-ref} and [](#c){.pf-ref} are the three assertions. No step divides by an element of $K$ or uses the existence of an inverse, so commutativity with identity is the whole hypothesis.
:::
