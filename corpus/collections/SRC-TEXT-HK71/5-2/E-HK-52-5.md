::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be a $2 \times 2$ matrix over a field $F$, and suppose that $A^2 = 0$.
Show for each scalar $c$ that $\det(cI - A) = c^2$.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise and no text version of Hoffman and Kunze in the checkout; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}$ is a $2\times2$ matrix over the field $F$ with $A^{2}=0$, and the scalar of the exercise is written $c_0$ below, to keep it apart from the entry $A_{21}=c$. We use the characteristic identity of a $2\times2$ matrix, $\det(c_0I-A)=c_0^{2}-c_0(a+d)+\det A$, which is the expansion of $\det\begin{bmatrix}c_0-a&-b\\-c&c_0-d\end{bmatrix}=(c_0-a)(c_0-d)-bc$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #equations}
The entries of $A^{2}$ satisfy $a^{2}+bc=0$, $b(a+d)=0$, $c(a+d)=0$ and $d^{2}+bc=0$.

::: pf-proof
$A^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}a^{2}+bc&ab+bd\\ca+dc&cb+d^{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}a^{2}+bc&b(a+d)\\c(a+d)&bc+d^{2}\end{bmatrix}$, and $A^{2}=0$ says all four entries vanish.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #trace}
$a+d=0$.

::: pf-proof
If $b\neq0$ then $b(a+d)=0$ gives $a+d=0$ because $F$ is a field and so has no zero divisors; if $c\neq0$ the same follows from $c(a+d)=0$. If $b=c=0$ then $a^{2}+bc=a^{2}=0$ and $d^{2}+bc=d^{2}=0$, and again $a=0$ and $d=0$ since a field has no nonzero nilpotent element, so $a+d=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #determinant}
$\det A=0$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#trace){.pf-ref} $d=-a$, so $\det A=ad-bc=-a^{2}-bc=-(a^{2}+bc)=0$ by the first equation of step [](#equations){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
$\det(c_0I-A)=c_0^{2}$ for every scalar $c_0$.

::: pf-proof
By the characteristic identity and steps [](#trace){.pf-ref} and [](#determinant){.pf-ref},
$$
\det(c_0I-A)=c_0^{2}-c_0(a+d)+\det A=c_0^{2}-0+0=c_0^{2}.
$$

:::
