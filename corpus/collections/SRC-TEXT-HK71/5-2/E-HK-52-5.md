::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be a $2 \times 2$ matrix over a field $F$, and suppose that $A^2 = 0$.
Show for each scalar $c$ that $\det(cI - A) = c^2$.
:::

::: solution
Write
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The equation $A^2=0$ gives
\[
a^2+bc=0,\qquad b(a+d)=0,\qquad c(a+d)=0,\qquad d^2+bc=0.
\]
If either $b$ or $c$ is nonzero, then $a+d=0$. If $b=c=0$, then $a^2=d^2=0$; since $F$ is a field, $a=d=0$. Thus in every case
\[
a+d=0.
\]
Using $d=-a$ and $a^2+bc=0$,
\[
\det A=ad-bc=-a^2-bc=0.
\]
Therefore, for every scalar $c_0\in F$,
\[
\det(c_0I-A)
=c_0^2-c_0(a+d)+\det A
=c_0^2.
\]
(The symbol $c_0$ here denotes the scalar called $c$ in the exercise, to distinguish it from the matrix entry $A_{21}$.)
:::
