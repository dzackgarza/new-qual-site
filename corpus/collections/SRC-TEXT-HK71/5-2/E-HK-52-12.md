::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field and $D$ a function on $n \times n$ matrices over $F$ (with values in $F$). Suppose $D(AB) = D(A)D(B)$ for all $A, B$.
Show that either $D(A) = 0$ for all $A$, or $D(I) = 1$.
In the latter case show that $D(A) \neq 0$ whenever $A$ is invertible.
:::

::: {.solution}
Putting $A=B=I$ gives
\[
D(I)=D(I)^2.
\]
Since $F$ is a field, $D(I)$ is therefore either $0$ or $1$.

If $D(I)=0$, then for every matrix $A$,
\[
D(A)=D(IA)=D(I)D(A)=0.
\]
Thus either $D$ is identically zero or $D(I)=1$.

Assume now that $D(I)=1$, and let $A$ be invertible. Then
\[
1=D(I)=D(AA^{-1})=D(A)D(A^{-1}).
\]
Thus $D(A)$ has a multiplicative inverse in $F$, so in particular $D(A)\ne0$.
:::
