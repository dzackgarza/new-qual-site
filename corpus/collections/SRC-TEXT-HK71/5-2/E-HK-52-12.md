::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field and $D$ a function on $n \times n$ matrices over $F$ (with values in $F$). Suppose $D(AB) = D(A)D(B)$ for all $A, B$.
Show that either $D(A) = 0$ for all $A$, or $D(I) = 1$.
In the latter case show that $D(A) \neq 0$ whenever $A$ is invertible.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: multiplicativity at the identity forces $D(I)$ into $\{0,1\}$, and multiplicativity at an inverse makes $D$ nonzero on invertible matrices.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #idempotent}

$D(I)=D(I)^{2}$, hence $D(I)\in\{0,1\}$.

::: pf-proof

Putting $A=B=I$ in $D(AB)=D(A)D(B)$ gives $D(I)=D(I)D(I)$. So $D(I)\big(D(I)-1\big)=0$ in the field $F$, which has no zero divisors, and therefore $D(I)=0$ or $D(I)=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #zero}

If $D(I)=0$ then $D(A)=0$ for every $A$.

::: pf-proof

For an arbitrary $n\times n$ matrix $A$ over $F$, multiplicativity with $A$ and $I$ gives
$$
D(A)=D(AI)=D(A)D(I)=D(A)\cdot0=0.
$$
So $D$ is identically zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nonzero}

If $D(I)=1$ then $D(A)\ne0$ for every invertible $A$.

::: pf-proof

Let $A$ be invertible, with inverse $A^{-1}$. Then, by multiplicativity and step [](#idempotent){.pf-ref},
$$
1=D(I)=D\big(AA^{-1}\big)=D(A)D\big(A^{-1}\big).
$$
A product equal to $1$ has both factors nonzero, so $D(A)\ne0$; explicitly, $D(A)$ has the multiplicative inverse $D(A^{-1})$ in the field $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

Either $D$ vanishes identically, or $D(I)=1$ and $D$ is nonzero on invertible matrices.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#idempotent){.pf-ref} either $D(I)=0$, in which case step [](#zero){.pf-ref} makes $D$ identically zero, or $D(I)=1$, in which case step [](#nonzero){.pf-ref} gives $D(A)\ne0$ for every invertible $A$:
$$
\boxed{D\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad\big(D(I)=1\ \text{and}\ D(A)\ne0\ \text{for invertible } A\big).}
$$

:::
