::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be the space of $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$, and let $W_0$ be the subspace spanned by the matrices $C$ of the form $C = AB - BA$.
Prove that $W_0$ is exactly the subspace of matrices which have trace zero.
(Hint: What is the dimension of the space of matrices of trace zero?
Use the matrix 'units,' i.e., matrices with exactly one non-zero entry, to construct enough linearly independent matrices of the form $AB - BA$.)
:::

::: solution
Every commutator has trace zero, since
\[
\operatorname{tr}(AB-BA)=0.
\]
Hence $W_0$ is contained in the trace-zero subspace
\[
Z=\{A\in W:\operatorname{tr}A=0\}.
\]

Let $E_{ij}$ denote the matrix units. If $i\ne j$, then
\[
[E_{ii},E_{ij}]=E_{ij},
\]
so every off-diagonal matrix unit lies in $W_0$. Also, for $i\ne j$,
\[
[E_{ij},E_{ji}]=E_{ii}-E_{jj}.
\]
Thus $W_0$ contains all off-diagonal $E_{ij}$ and the $n-1$ diagonal differences
\[
E_{11}-E_{nn},\ldots,E_{n-1,n-1}-E_{nn}.
\]
These $n(n-1)+(n-1)=n^2-1$ matrices are linearly independent. Therefore
\[
\dim W_0\ge n^2-1.
\]
The trace map $W\to F$ is a nonzero linear functional, so its kernel $Z$ has dimension $n^2-1$. Since $W_0\subseteq Z$, equality of dimensions gives
\[
W_0=Z.
\]
Thus the span of the commutators is exactly the subspace of trace-zero matrices.
:::
