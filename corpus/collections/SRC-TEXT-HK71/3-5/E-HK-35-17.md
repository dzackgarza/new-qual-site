::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be the space of $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$, and let $W_0$ be the subspace spanned by the matrices $C$ of the form $C = AB - BA$.
Prove that $W_0$ is exactly the subspace of matrices which have trace zero.
(Hint: What is the dimension of the space of matrices of trace zero?
Use the matrix 'units,' i.e., matrices with exactly one non-zero entry, to construct enough linearly independent matrices of the form $AB - BA$.)
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.5 Exercise 17, and the route is the one its hint prescribes.

Let $W$ be the space of $n\times n$ matrices over $F$, let $E_{ij}$ be the matrix with $1$ in position $(i,j)$ and $0$ elsewhere, let $W_{0}$ be the span of the commutators $AB-BA$, and let $Z=\{A\in W\mid\operatorname{tr}A=0\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #trace-linear}
The trace is linear, and $\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)$ for all $A,B\in W$.

::: pf-proof
$\operatorname{tr}C=\sum_{i}C_{ii}$ is a sum of linear functions of the entries, hence linear. For the second identity, $\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}A_{ij}B_{ji}=\sum_{j}\sum_{i}B_{ji}A_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(BA)$, the middle equality being commutativity of addition in $F$ and a reindexing of the finite double sum.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subset}
$W_{0}\subset Z$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#trace-linear){.pf-ref}, $\operatorname{tr}(AB-BA)=\operatorname{tr}(AB)-\operatorname{tr}(BA)=0$, so every commutator lies in $Z$; and $Z$ is a subspace, being the kernel of the linear functional $\operatorname{tr}$. A subspace containing every commutator contains the span of the commutators, that is $W_{0}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #offdiagonal}
For $i\neq j$, $E_{ii}E_{ij}=E_{ij}$ and $E_{ij}E_{ii}=0$, so $E_{ij}=E_{ii}E_{ij}-E_{ij}E_{ii}\in W_{0}$.

::: pf-proof
$E_{ab}E_{cd}=\delta_{bc}E_{ad}$ by the definition of matrix multiplication, where $\delta$ is $1$ when the two indices agree and $0$ otherwise. In the product $E_{ii}E_{ij}$ the indices are $a=b=i$, $c=i$, $d=j$, so the middle indices agree and the product is $E_{ij}$. In the product $E_{ij}E_{ii}$ they are $a=i$, $b=j$, $c=i$, $d=i$, and the middle indices $j$ and $i$ differ because $i\neq j$, so the product is $0$. The difference is therefore $E_{ij}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #diagonal-differences}
For $i\neq j$, $E_{ij}E_{ji}-E_{ji}E_{ij}=E_{ii}-E_{jj}\in W_{0}$.

::: pf-proof
By the multiplication rule of step [](#offdiagonal){.pf-ref}, $E_{ij}E_{ji}=E_{ii}$ (middle indices agree) and $E_{ji}E_{ij}=E_{jj}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #independent}
The $n(n-1)+(n-1)=n^{2}-1$ matrices $S=\{E_{ij}\mid i\neq j\}\cup\{E_{ii}-E_{nn}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}$ are linearly independent.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $\sum_{i\neq j}c_{ij}E_{ij}+\sum_{i<n}d_{i}(E_{ii}-E_{nn})=0$. Every listed matrix other than $E_{ij}$ has zero in position $(i,j)$ when $i\neq j$, so the $(i,j)$ entry of the combination is $c_{ij}$, giving $c_{ij}=0$ for all $i\neq j$. What remains is the diagonal matrix $\sum_{i<n}d_{i}(E_{ii}-E_{nn})$, whose $(i,i)$ entry is $d_{i}$ for $i<n$; hence $d_{i}=0$ for every $i<n$. All coefficients vanish, which is independence. When $n=1$ the set $S$ is empty and the assertion is vacuous.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lower-bound}
$\dim W_{0}\geq n^{2}-1$.

::: pf-proof
The matrices of step [](#offdiagonal){.pf-ref} and of step [](#diagonal-differences){.pf-ref} lie in $W_{0}$, so by step [](#independent){.pf-ref} the subspace $W_{0}$ contains $n^{2}-1$ linearly independent vectors.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #codim}
$\dim Z=n^{2}-1$.

::: pf-proof
$\operatorname{tr}$ is a linear functional on the $n^{2}$-dimensional space $W$ and is nonzero, since $\operatorname{tr}E_{11}=1$; a nonzero functional is surjective onto the one-dimensional space $F$, so by rank-nullity $\dim\ker\operatorname{tr}=n^{2}-1$, and $\ker\operatorname{tr}=Z$ by definition.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$W_{0}=Z$: the span of the commutators is exactly the space of trace-zero matrices.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#subset){.pf-ref} gives $W_{0}\subset Z$; steps [](#lower-bound){.pf-ref} and [](#codim){.pf-ref} give $\dim W_{0}\geq n^{2}-1=\dim Z$. A subspace contained in another of the same finite dimension is equal to it, so $W_{0}=Z$.
:::
