::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the field of complex numbers and let $V$ be any vector space over $F$.
Suppose that $f$ and $g$ are linear functionals on $V$ such that the function $h$ defined by $h(\alpha) = f(\alpha)g(\alpha)$ is also a linear functional on $V$.
Prove that either $f = 0$ or $g = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose for contradiction that $f \neq 0$ and $g \neq 0$.
Proof: assume both are nonzero.

<1>2. Since $h$ is linear, $h(c\alpha) = c\,h(\alpha)$ for every scalar $c$ and every $\alpha \in V$.
Proof: linearity of $h$.

<1>3. Expanding: $f(c\alpha)g(c\alpha) = c f(\alpha)g(\alpha)$, i.e. $c^2 f(\alpha)g(\alpha) = c f(\alpha)g(\alpha)$.
Proof: <1>2 and linearity of $f, g$.

<1>4. Hence $(c^2 - c) f(\alpha)g(\alpha) = 0$ for all scalars $c$ and all $\alpha \in V$.
Proof: <1>3.

<1>5. Taking $c = 2$ (so $c^2 - c = 2 \neq 0$), we get $f(\alpha)g(\alpha) = 0$ for all $\alpha \in V$.
Proof: <1>4.

<1>6. Hence $h \equiv 0$, i.e. $f(\alpha)g(\alpha) = 0$ for every $\alpha$.
Proof: <1>5.

<1>7. Since $f \neq 0$, there is $\alpha_0$ with $f(\alpha_0) \neq 0$; then $g(\alpha_0) = 0$ by <1>6.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>6.

<1>8. For any $\beta \in V$, $f(\alpha_0 + \beta)g(\alpha_0 + \beta) = 0$ by <1>6.
Proof: <1>6 applied to $\alpha_0 + \beta$.

<1>9. Expanding: $(f(\alpha_0) + f(\beta))(g(\alpha_0) + g(\beta)) = 0$, and since $g(\alpha_0) = 0$, this gives $(f(\alpha_0) + f(\beta))g(\beta) = 0$.
Proof: <1>7 and <1>8.

<1>10. Hence $f(\alpha_0)g(\beta) + f(\beta)g(\beta) = 0$, and $f(\beta)g(\beta) = 0$ by <1>6, so $f(\alpha_0)g(\beta) = 0$.
Proof: <1>9 and <1>6.

<1>11. Since $f(\alpha_0) \neq 0$, this forces $g(\beta) = 0$ for every $\beta \in V$.
Proof: <1>10.

<1>12. Hence $g = 0$, contradicting <1>1.
Proof: <1>11.

<1>13. Therefore $f = 0$ or $g = 0$.
Proof: <1>12.

<1>14. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>13.
:::
