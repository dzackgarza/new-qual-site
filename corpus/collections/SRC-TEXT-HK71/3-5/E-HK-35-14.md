::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$.
If $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_m$ are finitely many vectors in $V$, each different from the zero vector, prove that there is a linear functional $f$ on $V$ such that
\[
f(\alpha_i) \neq 0, \quad i = 1, \ldots, m.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove the statement by induction on $m$. For $m=1$, extend $\alpha_1$ to a basis of $V$ and choose a coordinate functional taking value $1$ on $\alpha_1$.

Assume the result for $m-1$. Choose $f\in V^*$ such that
\[
f(\alpha_i)\ne0\qquad(1\le i<m).
\]
Choose $g\in V^*$ with $g(\alpha_m)\ne0$; such a $g$ exists by extending $\alpha_m$ to a basis.

For $t\in F$, put $f_t=f+t g$. For each fixed $i$, the equation
\[
f_t(\alpha_i)=0
\]
excludes at most one value of $t$; for $i=m$ this is true because $g(\alpha_m)\ne0$, and for $i<m$ it is either one excluded value or none. Thus only finitely many scalars $t$ are forbidden.

Since $F$ has characteristic zero, it is infinite. Choose $t$ outside this finite set. Then
\[
f_t(\alpha_i)\ne0\qquad(1\le i\le m),
\]
as required.
:::
