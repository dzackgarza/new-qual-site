::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the complex numbers.
We define $n$ linear functionals on $F^n$ ($n \geq 2$) by
$$
f_k(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} (k - j)x_j, \quad 1 \leq k \leq n.
$$
What is the dimension of the subspace annihilated by $f_1, \ldots, f_n$?
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Let $n\ge2$ and let $W=\{x\in F^n:f_k(x)=0\text{ for }1\le k\le n\}$ be the common annihilator, where $f_k(x)=\sum_j(k-j)x_j$. Write
$$
S(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_j,\qquad T(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}jx_j .
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #split}
$f_k(x)=kS(x)-T(x)$ for every $k$ and every $x$.

::: pf-proof
Expand the definition: $\sum_j(k-j)x_j=\sum_jkx_j-\sum_jjx_j=k\sum_jx_j-\sum_jjx_j=kS(x)-T(x)$, using that $k$ is a scalar and summation is linear.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #implication}
If $x\in W$ then $S(x)=0$ and $T(x)=0$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#split){.pf-ref}, $0=f_k(x)=kS(x)-T(x)$ for every $k=1,\dots,n$. Take two distinct indices $k\ne l$, possible because $n\ge2$, and subtract the two equations: $0=(k-l)S(x)$. Since $F$ is a subfield of $\CC$, it has characteristic $0$, so $k-l\ne0$ and $S(x)=0$. Substituting back into $0=kS(x)-T(x)$ gives $T(x)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #converse}
If $S(x)=0$ and $T(x)=0$ then $x\in W$.

::: pf-proof
Under these hypotheses $f_k(x)=k\cdot0-0=0$ for every $k$ by step [](#split){.pf-ref}, which is membership in $W$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rank}
The linear map $\varphi:F^n\to F^2$, $\varphi(x)=\bigl(S(x),T(x)\bigr)$, has rank $2$.

::: pf-proof
$\varphi$ is linear because $S$ and $T$ are linear functionals. Its image is the span of the two vectors $u=(1,\dots,1)$ and $w=(1,2,\dots,n)$ in the dual sense: $S$ and $T$ are the coordinate functionals. They are linearly independent: if $w=\lambda u$ for some $\lambda\in F$, then comparing the $j$-th entries gives $j=\lambda$ for every $j$, hence $1=2$ in $F$, which fails because $\operatorname{char}F=0$. So $\dim\operatorname{im}\varphi=2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dimension}
$\dim W=n-2$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#implication){.pf-ref} and [](#converse){.pf-ref} give $W=\ker\varphi$. By rank-nullity for the map $\varphi$ on the $n$-dimensional space $F^n$, $\dim\ker\varphi=n-\operatorname{rank}\varphi=n-2$ by step [](#rank){.pf-ref}. So the subspace annihilated by $f_1,\dots,f_n$ has dimension $n-2$, which is the answer to the exercise; in particular the $n$ functionals span only a two-dimensional space of functionals.
:::
