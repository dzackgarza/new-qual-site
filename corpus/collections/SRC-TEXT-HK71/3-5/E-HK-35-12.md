::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over the field $F$ and let $W$ be a subspace of $V$.
If $f$ is a linear functional on $W$, prove that there is a linear functional $g$ on $V$ such that $g(\alpha) = f(\alpha)$ for each $\alpha$ in the subspace $W$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: a linear functional is prescribed freely on a basis, and a basis of a subspace extends to a basis of the space.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #extend}

There are $\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}$ spanning $W$ and $\alpha_{r+1},\dots,\alpha_{n}$ completing them to a basis of $V$.

::: pf-proof

$W$ is a subspace of the finite-dimensional space $V$, hence finite-dimensional, so it has a basis $\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}$ with $r=\dim W$. The extension theorem for bases says a linearly independent list in a finite-dimensional space can be enlarged to a basis, so there are $\alpha_{r+1},\dots,\alpha_{n}$ with $(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})$ a basis of $V$ and $n=\dim V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #define}

Setting $g(\alpha_{i})=f(\alpha_{i})$ for $i\le r$ and $g(\alpha_{i})=0$ for $i>r$ extends uniquely to a linear functional $g$ on $V$.

::: pf-proof

A linear map is determined by its values on a basis, and any prescribed values on a basis extend to exactly one linear map: for $\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\alpha_{i}$ one must set $g(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}g(\alpha_{i})$, and this formula is linear in $\alpha$. Its values are in $F$, so $g$ is a linear functional on $V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #restricts}

$g(\alpha)=f(\alpha)$ for every $\alpha\in W$.

::: pf-proof

An element of $W$ is $\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\alpha_{i}$, a combination of the first $r$ basis vectors only, by step [](#extend){.pf-ref}. Then
$$
g(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}g(\alpha_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}f(\alpha_{i})=f(\alpha),
$$
using step [](#define){.pf-ref} for the first equality and the linearity of $f$ on $W$ for the last.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

Every linear functional on $W$ extends to one on $V$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#define){.pf-ref} and [](#restricts){.pf-ref} produce, for the given $f$, a linear functional $g$ on $V$ whose restriction to $W$ is $f$:
$$
\boxed{g\in V^{*},\qquad \restrictionof{g}{W}=f.}
$$

:::
