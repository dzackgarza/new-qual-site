::: {.exercise}
Let $m$ and $n$ be positive integers and $F$ a field.
Let $f_1, \ldots, f_m$ be linear functionals on $F^n$.
For $\alpha$ in $F^n$ define
$$
T\alpha = (f_1(\alpha), \ldots, f_m(\alpha)).
$$
Show that $T$ is a linear transformation from $F^n$ into $F^m$.
Then show that every linear transformation from $F^n$ into $F^m$ is of the above form, for some $f_1, \ldots, f_m$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: componentwise formation of a tuple is linear exactly when each component functional is, and the coordinate functionals recover the components.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #linear}

The map $T$ defined by $T\alpha=(f_{1}(\alpha),\dots,f_{m}(\alpha))$ is linear.

::: pf-proof

Let $\alpha,\beta\in F^{n}$ and $c\in F$. Addition and scalar multiplication in $F^{m}$ are componentwise, and each $f_{i}$ is linear, so the $i$-th components of the two sides of $T(\alpha+c\beta)=T\alpha+cT\beta$ agree for every $i$:
$$
f_{i}(\alpha+c\beta)=f_{i}(\alpha)+c\,f_{i}(\beta).
$$
Two vectors of $F^{m}$ with equal components in every position are equal, so $T(\alpha+c\beta)=T\alpha+cT\beta$. Also $T$ takes values in $F^{m}$ since each $f_{i}(\alpha)\in F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #coordinates}

Each $\pi_{i}:F^{m}\to F$, the $i$-th coordinate functional $\pi_{i}(\gamma)=\gamma_{i}$, is linear.

::: pf-proof

$\pi_{i}(\gamma+c\delta)=\gamma_{i}+c\delta_{i}=\pi_{i}(\gamma)+c\,\pi_{i}(\delta)$ for $\gamma,\delta\in F^{m}$ and $c\in F$, by the componentwise definitions of addition and scalar multiplication in $F^{m}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #converse}

Every linear $T:F^{n}\to F^{m}$ has the stated form with $f_{i}=\pi_{i}\circ T$.

::: pf-proof

Let $T$ be linear and put $f_{i}=\pi_{i}\circ T$ for $1\le i\le m$. A composite of linear maps is linear, so each $f_{i}$ is a linear functional on $F^{n}$. For $\alpha\in F^{n}$, the tuple $(f_{1}(\alpha),\dots,f_{m}(\alpha))$ has $i$-th component $\pi_{i}(T\alpha)$, which is the $i$-th component of $T\alpha$; hence the tuple equals $T\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

The transformations $F^{n}\to F^{m}$ of the form $T\alpha=(f_{1}(\alpha),\dots,f_{m}(\alpha))$ are exactly the linear ones.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#linear){.pf-ref} gives that every such tuple of functionals produces a linear $T$, and step [](#converse){.pf-ref} gives that every linear $T$ arises from the functionals $f_{i}=\pi_{i}\circ T$, using step [](#coordinates){.pf-ref} for their linearity. So
$$
\boxed{T\alpha=(f_{1}(\alpha),\dots,f_{m}(\alpha))\ \text{for linear functionals } f_{i}\iff T \text{ is linear.}}
$$

:::
