::: {.exercise}
Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be subspaces of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$.

(a) Prove that $(W_1 + W_2)^0 = W_1^0 \cap W_2^0$.

(b) Prove that $(W_1 \cap W_2)^0 = W_1^0 + W_2^0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For (a), a functional $f$ annihilates $W_1+W_2$ exactly when it annihilates every vector $w_1+w_2$ with $w_i\in W_i$. This is equivalent to annihilating both $W_1$ and $W_2$. Hence
\[
(W_1+W_2)^0=W_1^0\cap W_2^0.
\]

For (b), every $f_1\in W_1^0$ and $f_2\in W_2^0$ satisfies
\[
(f_1+f_2)(w)=0
\]
for every $w\in W_1\cap W_2$, so
\[
W_1^0+W_2^0\subseteq (W_1\cap W_2)^0.
\]
Let $n=\dim V$ and $d_i=\dim W_i$, $d=\dim(W_1\cap W_2)$. By part (a),
\[
\dim(W_1^0\cap W_2^0)
=\dim (W_1+W_2)^0
=n-(d_1+d_2-d).
\]
Therefore
\[
\dim(W_1^0+W_2^0)
=(n-d_1)+(n-d_2)-[n-d_1-d_2+d]
=n-d.
\]
But $\dim (W_1\cap W_2)^0=n-d$. Since the former is contained in the latter and they have equal dimension,
\[
(W_1\cap W_2)^0=W_1^0+W_2^0.
\]
:::
