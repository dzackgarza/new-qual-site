::: {.exercise}
Let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be subspaces of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$.

(a) Prove that $(W_1 + W_2)^0 = W_1^0 \cap W_2^0$.

(b) Prove that $(W_1 \cap W_2)^0 = W_1^0 + W_2^0$.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise and no text version of Hoffman and Kunze in the checkout; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $V$ is finite dimensional over $F$, $W_1,W_2\le V$ are subspaces, and $W^{0}=\{f\in V^{*}: f(w)=0 \text{ for all } w\in W\}$ is the annihilator of $W$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #dimension}
$\dim W^{0}=\dim V-\dim W$ for every subspace $W\le V$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\dim V=n$, $\dim W=d$ and let $w_1,\dots,w_d$ be a basis of $W$; extend it to a basis $w_1,\dots,w_n$ of $V$. A functional is determined by its values on this basis (linearity), so the restriction map $\rho:V^{*}\to W^{*}$, $f\mapsto f\restrictionof{}{W}$, is linear, and it is surjective: given $g\in W^{*}$, define $f$ on the basis by $f(w_j)=g(w_j)$ for $j\le d$ and $f(w_j)=0$ for $j>d$, and extend linearly. The kernel of $\rho$ is $W^{0}$, so by rank-nullity $\dim W^{0}=n-\dim(\im\rho)=n-d$ because $\rho$ is onto.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a}
(a) $(W_1+W_2)^{0}=W_1^{0}\cap W_2^{0}$.

::: pf-proof
A functional $f$ lies in $(W_1+W_2)^{0}$ exactly when $f(w_1+w_2)=0$ for all $w_i\in W_i$. If so, taking $w_2=0$ gives $f\in W_1^{0}$ and taking $w_1=0$ gives $f\in W_2^{0}$; conversely if $f$ annihilates each $W_i$ then by linearity $f(w_1+w_2)=f(w_1)+f(w_2)=0$. So the two sets are equal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inclusion}
$W_1^{0}+W_2^{0}\subseteq(W_1\cap W_2)^{0}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $f_i\in W_i^{0}$ and $w\in W_1\cap W_2$. Then $f_1(w)=0$ because $w\in W_1$, and $f_2(w)=0$ because $w\in W_2$, so $(f_1+f_2)(w)=0$. Hence $f_1+f_2$ annihilates $W_1\cap W_2$, and every element of the sum has that form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
(b) $(W_1\cap W_2)^{0}=W_1^{0}+W_2^{0}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $n=\dim V$, $d_i=\dim W_i$ and $d=\dim(W_1\cap W_2)$, so $\dim(W_1+W_2)=d_1+d_2-d$ by the dimension formula. By step [](#a){.pf-ref} and step [](#dimension){.pf-ref},
$$
\dim(W_1^{0}\cap W_2^{0})=\dim(W_1+W_2)^{0}=n-(d_1+d_2-d),
$$
while $\dim W_i^{0}=n-d_i$. Hence by the dimension formula applied to the subspaces $W_1^{0}$ and $W_2^{0}$ of $V^{*}$,
$$
\dim(W_1^{0}+W_2^{0})=(n-d_1)+(n-d_2)-\big(n-d_1-d_2+d\big)=n-d,
$$
and $\dim(W_1\cap W_2)^{0}=n-d$ by step [](#dimension){.pf-ref}. By step [](#inclusion){.pf-ref} the sum is contained in the annihilator of the intersection, and two subspaces of equal finite dimension, one inside the other, coincide. So $(W_1\cap W_2)^{0}=W_1^{0}+W_2^{0}$.

:::
