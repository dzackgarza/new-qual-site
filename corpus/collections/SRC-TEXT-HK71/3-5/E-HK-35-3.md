::: {.exercise}
If $A$ and $B$ are $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$, show that $\operatorname{trace}(AB) = \operatorname{trace}(BA)$.
Now show that similar matrices have the same trace.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §3.5 Exercise 3. Only finite sums are taken, and the only field facts used are commutativity of multiplication, distributivity and associativity.

Fix $n\geq 1$ and write $C=(c_{ij})$ for an $n\times n$ matrix over a field $F$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #entries}
For $n\times n$ matrices $A=(a_{ij})$ and $B=(b_{ij})$ the $(i,i)$ entry of $AB$ is $(AB)_{ii}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}b_{ji}$.

::: pf-proof
By the definition of the matrix product, the $(i,k)$ entry of $AB$ is $\sum_{j}a_{ij}b_{jk}$; put $k=i$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #twosums}
$\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}b_{ji}$ and $\operatorname{tr}(BA)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij}a_{ji}$.

::: pf-proof
The trace of an $n\times n$ matrix $C$ is by definition $\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{ii}$. Substituting the diagonal entries of step [](#entries){.pf-ref} gives the first identity, and the same computation with the roles of $A$ and $B$ interchanged gives the second, the $(i,i)$ entry of $BA$ being $\sum_{j}b_{ij}a_{ji}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #reindex}
$\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}b_{ji}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij}a_{ji}$.

::: pf-proof
Both sides are sums over the index set $\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}$, namely $\sum_{(i,j)}a_{ij}b_{ji}$ and $\sum_{(i,j)}b_{ij}a_{ji}$. A finite sum is unchanged by reindexing along a bijection of its index set, and $\sigma(i,j)\coloneqq(j,i)$ is a bijection of that set onto itself, so
$$
\sum_{(i,j)}a_{ij}b_{ji}=\sum_{(i,j)}a_{\sigma(i,j)}b_{\sigma(j,i)}=\sum_{(i,j)}a_{ji}b_{ij}=\sum_{(i,j)}b_{ij}a_{ji},
$$
the last equality by commutativity of multiplication in $F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)$.

::: pf-proof
Immediate from the two identities of step [](#twosums){.pf-ref} and the equality of step [](#reindex){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rotate}
For arbitrary $n\times n$ matrices $X$ and $Y$, $\operatorname{tr}(XY)=\operatorname{tr}(YX)$.

::: pf-proof
This is the preceding step applied to the pair $(X,Y)$ in place of $(A,B)$; nothing in its proof used more than squareness of the two factors.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #similar}
If $B=P^{-1}AP$ with $P$ invertible, then $\operatorname{tr}(B)=\operatorname{tr}(A)$.

:::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #associate}
$\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}AP)=\operatorname{tr}(APP^{-1})$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#rotate){.pf-ref} with $X\coloneqq P^{-1}$ and $Y\coloneqq AP$ gives $\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}(AP))=\operatorname{tr}((AP)P^{-1})$, and $P^{-1}(AP)=(P^{-1}A)P=P^{-1}AP$ by associativity of matrix multiplication.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cancel}
$APP^{-1}=A$, hence $\operatorname{tr}(APP^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(A)$.

::: pf-proof
$PP^{-1}=I$ because $P$ is invertible, associativity gives $A(PP^{-1})=AI$, and $AI=A$; equal matrices have equal trace.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\operatorname{tr}(B)=\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}AP)=\operatorname{tr}(APP^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(A)$.

::: pf-proof
The first equality is the hypothesis $B=P^{-1}AP$, the second is step [](#associate){.pf-ref}, and the third is step [](#cancel){.pf-ref}.
:::
