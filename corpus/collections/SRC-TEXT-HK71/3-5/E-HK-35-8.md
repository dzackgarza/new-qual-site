::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be the subspace of $\RR^5$ which is spanned by the vectors
$$
\alpha_1 = \epsilon_1 + 2\epsilon_2 + \epsilon_3, \quad \alpha_2 = \epsilon_2 + 3\epsilon_3 + 3\epsilon_4 + \epsilon_5,
$$
$$
\alpha_3 = \epsilon_1 + 4\epsilon_2 + 6\epsilon_3 + 4\epsilon_4 + \epsilon_5.
$$
Find a basis for $W^0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §3.5 Exercise 8.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #setup}
Identify a linear functional on $\RR^5$ with its coefficient vector $c=(c_1,\dots,c_5)$, so that $c\in W^0$ exactly when $c\cdot\alpha_j=0$ for $j=1,2,3$.

::: pf-proof
A functional is $x\mapsto c\cdot x$ for a unique $c$; it annihilates $W$ exactly when it annihilates the three spanning vectors.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #system}
The condition is the homogeneous system with coefficient matrix
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&1&0&0\\0&1&3&3&1\\1&4&6&4&1\end{bmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The rows are the coordinate vectors $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ read off from the statement: $\alpha_1=\epsilon_1+2\epsilon_2+\epsilon_3=(1,2,1,0,0)$, $\alpha_2=(0,1,3,3,1)$ and $\alpha_3=(1,4,6,4,1)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #rank}
The three vectors $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ are linearly independent, so $\dim W=3$ and $\dim W^0=2$.

::: pf-proof
$\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ are independent because $\alpha_1$ has a nonzero first coordinate and $\alpha_2$ has zero first coordinate. If $\alpha_3=a\alpha_1+b\alpha_2$, comparison of coordinates gives $a=1$ from the first, $b=1$ from the fifth, and then the fourth coordinate would be $3b=3$, whereas $\alpha_3$ has fourth coordinate $4$; so no such $a,b$ exist. Hence $\dim W=3$, and $\dim W^0=5-3=2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #candidates}
The two coefficient vectors
$$
v=(-4,3,-2,1,0),\qquad w=(-3,2,-1,0,1)
$$
are independent and lie in $W^0$.

::: pf-proof
Independence is clear from the last two coordinates. For $v$: $v\cdot\alpha_1=-4+6-2+0+0=0$, $v\cdot\alpha_2=0+3-6+3+0=0$, $v\cdot\alpha_3=-4+12-12+4+0=0$. For $w$: $w\cdot\alpha_1=-3+4-1+0+0=0$, $w\cdot\alpha_2=0+2-3+0+1=0$, $w\cdot\alpha_3=-3+8-6+0+1=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
A basis of $W^0$ is given by $f_1(x)=-4x_1+3x_2-2x_3+x_4$ and $f_2(x)=-3x_1+2x_2-x_3+x_5$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#rank){.pf-ref} and [](#candidates){.pf-ref} exhibit two independent elements of a two-dimensional space, so they form a basis:
$$
\boxed{W^0=\operatorname{span}\{(-4,3,-2,1,0),\,(-3,2,-1,0,1)\}.}
$$
:::
