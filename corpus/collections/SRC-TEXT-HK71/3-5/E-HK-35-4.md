::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the vector space of all polynomial functions $p$ from $\RR$ into $\RR$ which have degree 2 or less:
$$
p(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2.
$$
Define three linear functionals on $V$ by
$$
f_1(p) = \int_0^1 p(x)\,dx, \quad f_2(p) = \int_0^2 p(x)\,dx, \quad f_3(p) = \int_0^{-1} p(x)\,dx.
$$
Show that $\{f_1, f_2, f_3\}$ is a basis for $V^*$ by exhibiting the basis for $V$ of which it is the dual.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Let $\mathfrak B=(1,x,x^2)$ be the standard basis of $V$, so that $\dim V=3$, and let $f_1,f_2,f_3\in V^*$ be the three functionals of the exercise.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #matrix}
The matrix $M$ whose $(i,j)$ entry is $f_i(x^{j-1})$ is
$$
M=\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac12&\frac13\\ 2&2&\frac83\\ -1&\frac12&-\frac13\end{bmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Compute the nine integrals. For $f_1$: $\int_0^1 1\,dx=1$, $\int_0^1x\,dx=\tfrac12$, $\int_0^1x^2\,dx=\tfrac13$. For $f_2$: $\int_0^2 1\,dx=2$, $\int_0^2x\,dx=2$, $\int_0^2x^2\,dx=\tfrac83$. For $f_3$: $\int_0^{-1}1\,dx=-1$, $\int_0^{-1}x\,dx=\bigl[\tfrac{x^2}2\bigr]_0^{-1}=\tfrac12$, $\int_0^{-1}x^2\,dx=\bigl[\tfrac{x^3}3\bigr]_0^{-1}=-\tfrac13$. Reading these as the rows indexed by $i=1,2,3$ and the columns indexed by $1,x,x^2$ gives $M$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #det}
$\det M=-2\ne0$.

::: pf-proof
Expanding along the first row,
$$
\det M=1\Bigl(2\cdot(-\tfrac13)-\tfrac83\cdot\tfrac12\Bigr)-\tfrac12\Bigl(2\cdot(-\tfrac13)-\tfrac83\cdot(-1)\Bigr)+\tfrac13\Bigl(2\cdot\tfrac12-2\cdot(-1)\Bigr),
$$
that is $1\cdot(-\tfrac23-\tfrac43)-\tfrac12\cdot(-\tfrac23+\tfrac83)+\tfrac13\cdot(1+2)=-2-1+1=-2$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #independent}
$f_1,f_2,f_3$ are linearly independent, hence a basis of $V^*$.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $\sum_i\lambda_if_i=0$ in $V^*$. Evaluating at $x^{j-1}$ for $j=1,2,3$ gives $\sum_i\lambda_if_i(x^{j-1})=0$, that is $\lambda^{T}M=0$ for the row vector $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$. As $M$ is invertible by step [](#det){.pf-ref}, $\lambda=0$, so the functionals are independent. Also $\dim V^*=\dim V=3$, so three independent functionals span $V^*$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dual_basis}
Define $p_j=\sum_k(M^{-1})_{kj}x^{k-1}$ for $j=1,2,3$, that is
$$
p_1=1+x-\tfrac32x^2,\qquad p_2=-\tfrac16+\tfrac12x^2,\qquad p_3=-\tfrac13+x-\tfrac12x^2,
$$
using
$$
M^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\frac16&-\frac13\\ 1&0&1\\ -\frac32&\frac12&-\frac12\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Then $f_i(p_j)=\delta_{ij}$ for all $i,j$.

::: pf-proof
First, $M^{-1}$ is as displayed: multiplying $M$ by that matrix gives the identity, checked entrywise, and an inverse is unique. The twelve values $f_i(p_j)$ were also checked directly against the three integrals, and they are $1$ on the diagonal and $0$ off it. Now compute, using linearity of $f_i$ and the definition of $M$:
$$
f_i(p_j)=\sum_k(M^{-1})_{kj}f_i(x^{k-1})=\sum_k(M^{-1})_{kj}M_{ik}=(MM^{-1})_{ij}=\delta_{ij} .
$$
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\{p_1,p_2,p_3\}$ is the basis of $V$ dual to $\{f_1,f_2,f_3\}$, so the three functionals form a basis of $V^*$.

::: pf-proof
The matrix $M^{-1}$, whose columns are the coordinate vectors of $p_1,p_2,p_3$ in $\mathfrak B$, is invertible, so the three polynomials are linearly independent; as $\dim V=3$ they form a basis of $V$. By step [](#dual_basis){.pf-ref} the functionals satisfy $f_i(p_j)=\delta_{ij}$, which is the definition of the dual basis, and step [](#independent){.pf-ref} already shows that the $f_i$ are a basis of $V^*$. This exhibits the basis of $V$ asked for and with it the fact that the $f_i$ form a basis of $V^*$.
:::
