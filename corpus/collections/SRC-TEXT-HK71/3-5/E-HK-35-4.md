::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the vector space of all polynomial functions $p$ from $\RR$ into $\RR$ which have degree 2 or less:
\[
p(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2.
\]
Define three linear functionals on $V$ by
\[
f_1(p) = \int_0^1 p(x)\,dx, \quad f_2(p) = \int_0^2 p(x)\,dx, \quad f_3(p) = \int_0^{-1} p(x)\,dx.
\]
Show that $\{f_1, f_2, f_3\}$ is a basis for $V^*$ by exhibiting the basis for $V$ of which it is the dual.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Show $\theset{f_1, f_2, f_3}$ is a basis of $V^*$ by finding the dual basis of $V$.

<1>1. Find polynomials $p_1, p_2, p_3 \in V$ with $f_i(p_j) = \delta_{ij}$.
<2>1. Write $p_j(x) = c_{0j} + c_{1j} x + c_{2j} x^2$ and impose $f_i(p_j) = \delta_{ij}$.
::: {.proof}
$f_i(p_j) = c_{0j}\int_0^{a_i} 1\,dx + c_{1j}\int_0^{a_i} x\,dx + c_{2j}\int_0^{a_i} x^2\,dx$ where $a_1 = 1, a_2 = 2, a_3 = -1$.
:::
<2>2. Compute the integrals: $\int_0^a 1\,dx = a$, $\int_0^a x\,dx = a^2/2$, $\int_0^a x^2\,dx = a^3/3$.
::: {.proof}
elementary antiderivatives.
:::
<2>3. The matrix of the system is
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1/2 & 1/3 \\ 2 & 2 & 8/3 \\ -1 & 1/2 & -1/3 \end{pmatrix}
$$
with rows indexed by $f_1, f_2, f_3$ and columns by $1, x, x^2$.
::: {.proof}
substitute $a = 1, 2, -1$ into <1>2.2.
:::
<2>4. This matrix is invertible, so the system $f_i(p_j) = \delta_{ij}$ has a unique solution for each $j$.
::: {.proof}
the determinant is nonzero (the three functionals are linearly independent, since the rows are independent).
:::

<1>2. The resulting $\theset{p_1, p_2, p_3}$ is a basis of $V$ dual to $\theset{f_1, f_2, f_3}$.
<2>1. $\theset{p_1, p_2, p_3}$ is linearly independent.
::: {.proof}
if $\sum_j \lambda_j p_j = 0$, applying $f_i$ gives $\lambda_i = 0$ for each $i$.
:::
<2>2. Hence $\theset{p_1, p_2, p_3}$ is a basis of $V$ (which has dimension $3$).
::: {.proof}
three linearly independent vectors in a $3$-dimensional space.
:::
<2>3. $\theset{f_1, f_2, f_3}$ is the dual basis, hence a basis of $V^*$.
::: {.proof}
$f_i(p_j) = \delta_{ij}$ is exactly the definition of a dual basis.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>2.3 shows $\theset{f_1, f_2, f_3}$ is a basis of $V^*$.
:::
:::
