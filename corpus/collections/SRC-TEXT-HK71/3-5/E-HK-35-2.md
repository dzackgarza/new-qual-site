::: {.exercise}
Let $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$ be the basis for $C^3$ defined by
\[
\alpha_1 = (1, 0, -1), \quad \alpha_2 = (1, 1, 1), \quad \alpha_3 = (2, 2, 0).
\]
Find the dual basis of $\mathfrak{B}$.
:::

::: solution
Let $A$ be the matrix whose columns are the basis vectors:
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&2\\0&1&2\\-1&1&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The coordinate functionals are the rows of $A^{-1}$. Direct computation gives
\[
A^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}
1&-1&0\\
1&-1&1\\
-\frac12&1&-\frac12
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Hence the dual basis $\{f_1,f_2,f_3\}$ is
\[
f_1(x,y,z)=x-y,
\]
\[
f_2(x,y,z)=x-y+z,
\]
\[
f_3(x,y,z)=-\frac12x+y-\frac12z.
\]
Indeed, the matrix identity $A^{-1}A=I$ says exactly that
$f_i(\alpha_j)=\delta_{ij}$.
:::
