::: {.exercise}
Let $\mathfrak{B} = \{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3\}$ be the basis for $\CC^3$ defined by
$$
\alpha_1 = (1, 0, -1), \quad \alpha_2 = (1, 1, 1), \quad \alpha_3 = (2, 2, 0).
$$
Find the dual basis of $\mathfrak{B}$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and the three functionals were recomputed for this pass.

Let $A$ be the matrix whose columns are the basis vectors $\alpha_{1}=(1,0,-1)$, $\alpha_{2}=(1,1,1)$, $\alpha_{3}=(2,2,0)$ of $\CC^{3}$, and let $f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}$ be the dual basis, so that $f_{i}(\alpha_{j})=\delta_{ij}$. A functional $g$ is written $g(x,y,z)=a x+b y+c z$ with coefficient row $(a,b,c)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #rows}
$f_{i}$ is the functional whose coefficient row is the $i$-th row of $A^{-1}$.

::: pf-proof
For a coefficient row $u$, the vector of values $\bigl(g(\alpha_{1}),g(\alpha_{2}),g(\alpha_{3})\bigr)$ is $uA$, because the columns of $A$ are the $\alpha_{j}$. Taking $u$ to be the $i$-th row of $A^{-1}$ gives $uA$ equal to the $i$-th row of $A^{-1}A=I$, that is $(0,\dots,1,\dots,0)$ with the $1$ in position $i$; so $g(\alpha_{j})=\delta_{ij}$ and $g=f_{i}$. Since the dual basis is unique, this identifies the three functionals.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inverse}
$A^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\\1&-1&1\\-\frac12&1&-\frac12\end{bmatrix}$.

::: pf-proof
Multiply the displayed matrix into $A$. First row against the columns $(1,0,-1)$, $(1,1,1)$, $(2,2,0)$: $1-0+0=1$, $1-1+0=0$, $2-2+0=0$. Second row: $1+0-1=0$, $1-1+1=1$, $2-2+0=0$. Third row: $-\frac12+0+\frac12=0$, $-\frac12+1-\frac12=0$, $-1+2+0=1$. So the product is $I$, and since $\det A=-2\ne0$ the matrix is invertible with the displayed inverse.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The dual basis of $\mathfrak{B}$ is $f_{1}(x,y,z)=x-y$, $f_{2}(x,y,z)=x-y+z$, $f_{3}(x,y,z)=-\frac12x+y-\frac12z$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#rows){.pf-ref} and [](#inverse){.pf-ref} the three functionals are the rows of $A^{-1}$, written out with coefficients as displayed. Checking the values directly: $f_{1}(\alpha_{1})=1-0=1$, $f_{1}(\alpha_{2})=1-1=0$, $f_{1}(\alpha_{3})=2-2=0$; $f_{2}(\alpha_{1})=1-0-1=0$, $f_{2}(\alpha_{2})=1-1+1=1$, $f_{2}(\alpha_{3})=2-2+0=0$; and $f_{3}(\alpha_{1})=-\frac12+0+\frac12=0$, $f_{3}(\alpha_{2})=-\frac12+1-\frac12=0$, $f_{3}(\alpha_{3})=-1+2+0=1$. So $f_{i}(\alpha_{j})=\delta_{ij}$ for all $i,j$, which is exactly the defining property of the dual basis.
:::
