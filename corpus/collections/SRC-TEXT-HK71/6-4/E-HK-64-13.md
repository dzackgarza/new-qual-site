::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the space of $n \times n$ matrices over $F$.
Let $A$ be a fixed $n \times n$ matrix over $F$.
Let $T$ and $U$ be the linear operators on $V$ defined by
\[
T(B) = AB, \qquad U(B) = AB - BA.
\]

(a) True or false?
If $A$ is diagonalizable (over $F$), then $T$ is diagonalizable.

(b) True or false?
If $A$ is diagonalizable, then $U$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: solution
Both assertions are $\boxed{\text{true}}$.

Let $P$ be invertible with
$$
D=P^{-1}AP=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n),
$$
and define the invertible linear map
$$
\Phi:V\to V,
\qquad
\Phi(B)=P^{-1}BP.
$$

<1>1. Left multiplication by $A$ is similar to left multiplication by $D$.
::: proof
For $C\in V$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\Phi T\Phi^{-1})(C)
&=\Phi\bigl(A(PCP^{-1})\bigr)\\
&=P^{-1}APC\\
&=DC.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $T$ is similar, as an operator on the $n^2$-dimensional space $V$, to
the operator $L_D(C)=DC$.

For the standard matrix units $E_{ij}$,
$$
L_D(E_{ij})=DE_{ij}=\lambda_iE_{ij}.
$$
The $n^2$ matrices $E_{ij}$ form a basis of $V$, so they form an eigenbasis
for $L_D$. Hence $L_D$ is diagonalizable, and similarity preserves
diagonalizability. Therefore $T$ is diagonalizable [@HK71].
:::

<1>2. The commutator operator is similar to commutation with $D$.
::: proof
For $C\in V$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\Phi U\Phi^{-1})(C)
&=\Phi\bigl(A(PCP^{-1})-(PCP^{-1})A\bigr)\\
&=DC-CD.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $U$ is similar to $\operatorname{ad}_D(C)=DC-CD$. Again using the matrix
units,
$$
DE_{ij}=\lambda_iE_{ij},
\qquad
E_{ij}D=\lambda_jE_{ij},
$$
and therefore
$$
\operatorname{ad}_D(E_{ij})=(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)E_{ij}.
$$
Hence the same basis $\{E_{ij}\}$ is an eigenbasis for $\operatorname{ad}_D$.
So $\operatorname{ad}_D$, and consequently $U$, is diagonalizable.
:::
:::
