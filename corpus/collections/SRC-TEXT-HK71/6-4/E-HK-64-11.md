::: {.exercise}
True or false?
If the triangular matrix $A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix, then $A$ is already diagonal.
:::

::: solution
The assertion is $\boxed{\text{false}}$.

<1>1. A concrete counterexample is
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
::: proof
The matrix is upper triangular but is not diagonal because its $(1,2)$ entry
is $1$. Its two eigenvalues are the distinct diagonal entries $1$ and $2$.
Corresponding eigenvectors are
$$
v_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v_2=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix},
$$
because $Av_1=v_1$ and $Av_2=2v_2$.

Let
$$
P=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its columns are $v_1,v_2$, so $P$ is invertible, and the two eigenvector
identities give
$$
AP=P\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Multiplying by $P^{-1}$ on the left yields
$$
P^{-1}AP=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus $A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix although $A$ itself is not diagonal,
which disproves the assertion [@HK71].
:::
:::
