::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space over the field of complex numbers.
Prove that $T$ is diagonalizable if and only if $T$ is annihilated by some polynomial over $C$ which has distinct roots.
:::

::: {.solution}
If \(T\) is diagonalizable, let \(c_1,\ldots,c_r\) be its distinct characteristic values. Then in an eigenbasis the polynomial
\[
f(x)=\prod_{j=1}^r(x-c_j)
\]
vanishes on every diagonal entry, so \(f(T)=0\). Its roots are distinct.

Conversely, suppose \(f(T)=0\) for a polynomial \(f\in\mathbb C[x]\) with distinct roots. The minimal polynomial \(m_T\) divides every annihilating polynomial, hence \(m_T\mid f\). Because \(f\) has no repeated root, neither does \(m_T\); and because the base field is \(\mathbb C\), \(m_T\) splits into linear factors. The minimal-polynomial criterion therefore implies that \(T\) is diagonalizable.
:::
