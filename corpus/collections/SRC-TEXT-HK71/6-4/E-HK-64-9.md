::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the indefinite integral operator
\[
(Tf)(x) = \int_0^x f(t)\,dt
\]
on the space of continuous functions on the interval $[0, 1]$.
Is the space of polynomial functions invariant under $T$?
The space of differentiable functions?
The space of functions which vanish at $x = \frac{1}{2}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The answers are, respectively,
$$
\boxed{\text{yes},\qquad \text{yes},\qquad \text{no}.}
$$

<1>1. Polynomial functions form a $T$-invariant subspace.
::: {.proof}
If
$$
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^m a_kx^k,
$$
then direct integration gives
$$
(Tf)(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt
=\sum_{k=0}^m\frac{a_k}{k+1}x^{k+1},
$$
which is again a polynomial function. Thus $T$ maps the polynomial subspace
into itself, so that subspace is invariant [@HK71].
:::

<1>2. Differentiable functions form a $T$-invariant subspace.
::: {.proof}
Let $f$ be differentiable on $[0,1]$. In particular $f$ is continuous, so the
fundamental theorem of calculus gives
$$
(Tf)'(x)=f(x)
$$
for $0<x<1$, with the corresponding one-sided derivatives at the endpoints.
Hence $Tf$ is differentiable on $[0,1]$. Therefore the differentiable
functions are invariant under $T$.
:::

<1>3. Functions vanishing at $1/2$ do not form a $T$-invariant subspace.
::: {.proof}
Take
$$
f(x)=x-\frac12.
$$
Then $f(1/2)=0$, so $f$ belongs to the stated subspace. But
$$
(Tf)\left(\frac12\right)
=\int_0^{1/2}\left(t-\frac12\right)dt
=\left[\frac{t^2}{2}-\frac{t}{2}\right]_0^{1/2}
=-\frac18\ne0.
$$
Thus $Tf$ does not belong to the subspace. Hence that subspace is not
$T$-invariant.
:::
:::
