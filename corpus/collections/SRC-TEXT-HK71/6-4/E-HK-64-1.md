::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^2$, the matrix of which in the standard ordered basis is
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

(a) Prove that the only subspaces of $\RR^2$ invariant under $T$ are $\RR^2$ and the zero subspace.

(b) If $U$ is the linear operator on $\CC^2$, the matrix of which in the standard ordered basis is $A$, show that $U$ has 1-dimensional invariant subspaces.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, checked against Hoffman--Kunze, *Linear Algebra* (1971), §6.4 Exercise 1. The characteristic polynomial, its discriminant and the two complex roots were recomputed; the eigenvectors are given explicitly in the complex case.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #charpoly}
$\det(xI-A)=x^2-3x+4$, whose discriminant is $-7$.

::: pf-proof
$xI-A=\begin{pmatrix}x-1&1\\-2&x-2\end{pmatrix}$, so the determinant is $(x-1)(x-2)+2=x^2-3x+4$; the discriminant is $9-16=-7$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #no-real}
$A$ has no real characteristic value.

::: pf-proof
The characteristic values are the roots of $x^2-3x+4$, that is $\tfrac12\left(3\pm\sqrt{-7}\right)$, and $\sqrt{-7}$ is not real; so no real number is a root.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #a}
(a) The only subspaces of $\RR^2$ invariant under $T$ are $\{0\}$ and $\RR^2$.

::: pf-proof
A proper nonzero subspace of $\RR^2$ is a line $\RR v$ with $v\neq0$. If it were invariant then $Tv\in\RR v$, so $Tv=cv$ for some real $c$, and $c$ would be a real characteristic value of $T$ with eigenvector $v$, which step [](#no-real){.pf-ref} excludes. The two remaining subspaces are $\{0\}$ and $\RR^2$, and both are invariant.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #b}
(b) The operator $U$ on $\CC^2$ with the same matrix has one-dimensional invariant subspaces.

::: pf-proof
Over $\CC$ the polynomial factors as $x^2-3x+4=\left(x-\tfrac{3+i\sqrt7}{2}\right)\left(x-\tfrac{3-i\sqrt7}{2}\right)$, so each root $\lambda$ is a characteristic value. For $v_\lambda=(1,\,1-\lambda)$ we have $(A-\lambda I)v_\lambda=\bigl((1-\lambda)\cdot1-1\cdot(1-\lambda),\ 2\cdot1+(2-\lambda)(1-\lambda)\bigr)$, which is $(0,0)$ because $(2-\lambda)(1-\lambda)=-2$ for either root of $x^2-3x+4$. So $v_\lambda\neq0$ spans a one-dimensional $U$-invariant subspace.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step}
Over $\RR$ the operator has no proper nonzero invariant subspace, and over $\CC$ it has one-dimensional invariant subspaces.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#a){.pf-ref} and [](#b){.pf-ref} give the two assertions.
:::
:::
