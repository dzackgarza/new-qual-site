::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $\RR^2$, the matrix of which in the standard ordered basis is
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}.
\]

(a) Prove that the only subspaces of $\RR^2$ invariant under $T$ are $\RR^2$ and the zero subspace.

(b) If $U$ is the linear operator on $C^2$, the matrix of which in the standard ordered basis is $A$, show that $U$ has 1-dimensional invariant subspaces.
:::

::: {.solution}
The characteristic polynomial of
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\2&2\end{bmatrix}
\]
is
\[
p(x)=\det(xI-A)=x^2-3x+4.
\]
Its discriminant is \(9-16=-7\), so it has no real root.

For (a), every proper nonzero subspace of \(\mathbb R^2\) is one-dimensional. If such a line \(W=\mathbb Rv\) were \(T\)-invariant, then \(Tv\in W\), so \(Tv=cv\) for some real \(c\). This would make \(c\) a real characteristic value, impossible. Hence the only invariant subspaces are \(0\) and \(\mathbb R^2\).

For (b), over \(\mathbb C\) the polynomial factors as
\[
p(x)=\left(x-\frac{3+i\sqrt7}{2}\right)
\left(x-\frac{3-i\sqrt7}{2}\right).
\]
Thus \(U\) has complex eigenvectors for these two eigenvalues, and the span of any such eigenvector is a one-dimensional invariant subspace.
:::
