::: {.exercise}
Let $W$ be an invariant subspace for $T$.
Prove that the minimal polynomial for the restriction operator $T_W$ divides the minimal polynomial for $T$, without referring to matrices.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. The minimal polynomial $m_{T}$ of an operator $T$ is the monic polynomial of least degree with $m_{T}(T)=0$, and it divides every polynomial that annihilates $T$; that divisibility is the property used below.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #powers}
If $W$ is $T$-invariant then $T_{W}^{k}=\restrictionof{T^{k}}{W}$ for every $k\ge0$, where $T_{W}$ is the restriction of $T$ to $W$.

::: pf-proof
Induction on $k$. For $k=0$ both sides are the identity on $W$. If $T_{W}^{k}=\restrictionof{T^{k}}{W}$, then for $w\in W$ one has $T_{W}^{k+1}w=T_{W}\bigl(T^{k}w\bigr)$; now $T^{k}w\in W$ because $W$ is invariant, so $T_{W}$ of it equals $Tw$, giving $T_{W}^{k+1}w=T^{k+1}w$, that is $T_{W}^{k+1}=\restrictionof{T^{k+1}}{W}$. No matrix of $T$ or of $T_W$ enters.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #annihilates}
$m_{T}(T_{W})=0$; that is, $m_{T}$ annihilates the restriction.

::: pf-proof
Write $m_{T}(x)=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{d}x^{d}$. For $w\in W$, step [](#powers){.pf-ref} gives
$$
m_{T}(T_{W})w=c_{0}w+c_{1}T_{W}w+\cdots+c_{d}T_{W}^{d}w=c_{0}w+c_{1}Tw+\cdots+c_{d}T^{d}w=m_{T}(T)w=0,
$$
the last equality because $m_{T}(T)=0$ as an operator on the whole space. So $m_{T}(T_{W})$ kills every $w\in W$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$m_{T_{W}}$ divides $m_{T}$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#annihilates){.pf-ref} the polynomial $m_{T}$ annihilates $T_{W}$. Dividing $m_{T}$ by the monic polynomial $m_{T_{W}}$ with remainder gives $m_{T}=q\,m_{T_{W}}+r$ with $\deg r<\deg m_{T_{W}}$. Substituting $T_{W}$ gives $0=m_{T}(T_{W})=q(T_{W})m_{T_{W}}(T_{W})+r(T_{W})=r(T_{W})$, so $r$ annihilates $T_{W}$; by the minimality of $\deg m_{T_{W}}$ among such polynomials, $r$ must be $0$. Hence $m_{T}=q\,m_{T_{W}}$, that is $m_{T_{W}}\mid m_{T}$, which is the assertion of the exercise. The argument uses only invariance and the two defining properties of the minimal polynomial, and no matrices.
:::
