::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space over an algebraically closed field $F$.
Let $f$ be a polynomial over $F$.
Prove that $c$ is a characteristic value of $f(T)$ if and only if $c = f(t)$, where $t$ is a characteristic value of $T$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Triangularize $T$ and identify its characteristic values on the diagonal.
::: proof
Because $F$ is algebraically closed, the characteristic polynomial of $T$
splits over $F$. Hence $T$ is triangularizable [@HK71]. Choose a basis in
which
$$
[T]=A=
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda_1&*&\cdots&*\\
0&\lambda_2&\cdots&*\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&\lambda_n
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The characteristic polynomial of a triangular matrix is
$$
\prod_{i=1}^n(x-\lambda_i),
$$
so the characteristic values of $T$ are exactly the numbers
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$, with repetitions according to algebraic
multiplicity.
:::

<1>2. The diagonal entries of $f(A)$ are $f(\lambda_1),\ldots,f(\lambda_n)$.
::: proof
Products and sums of upper-triangular matrices are upper triangular, and the
diagonal of a product is the pointwise product of the diagonals. Hence, for
every integer $k\geq0$, the matrix $A^k$ is upper triangular with diagonal
$$
\lambda_1^k,\ldots,\lambda_n^k.
$$
If
$$
f(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_mx^m,
$$
then
$$
f(A)=a_0I+a_1A+\cdots+a_mA^m
$$
is upper triangular with diagonal
$$
f(\lambda_1),\ldots,f(\lambda_n).
$$
Moreover, matrix representation commutes with evaluating a polynomial in an
operator, so $[f(T)]=f(A)$ in this basis.
:::

<1>3. Read off the characteristic values of $f(T)$.
::: proof
Since $f(A)$ is triangular, its characteristic values are exactly its diagonal
entries. By step <1>2 these are
$$
f(\lambda_1),\ldots,f(\lambda_n).
$$
Combining this with step <1>1, a scalar $c$ is a characteristic value of
$f(T)$ if and only if
$$
c=f(\lambda_i)
$$
for some characteristic value $\lambda_i$ of $T$. Renaming
$t=\lambda_i$ gives precisely
$$
c=f(t),
$$
as required.
:::
:::
