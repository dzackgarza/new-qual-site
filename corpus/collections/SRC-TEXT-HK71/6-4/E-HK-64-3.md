::: {.exercise}
Let $c$ be a characteristic value of $T$ and let $W$ be the space of characteristic vectors associated with the characteristic value $c$.
What is the restriction operator $T_W$?
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing here is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §6.4 Exercise 3.

Let $T$ be a linear operator on a vector space $V$ over $F$, let $c\in F$ be a characteristic value of $T$, and let $W$ be the space of characteristic vectors of $T$ associated with $c$; write $I_{W}$ for the identity operator on $W$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #eigenspace}
$W=\ker(T-cI)=\{v\in V\mid Tv=cv\}$.

::: pf-proof
A characteristic vector associated with $c$ is by definition a nonzero vector $v$ with $Tv=cv$, and $Tv=cv$ is the same equation as $(T-cI)v=0$, that is $v\in\ker(T-cI)$. The set of all such $v$, together with $0$ (which satisfies the equation but is excluded from the characteristic vectors), is the subspace $\ker(T-cI)$, and that is $W$ as the exercise uses the term.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nonempty}
$W\neq\{0\}$.

::: pf-proof
$c$ is a characteristic value of $T$, which means by definition that some nonzero $v\in V$ satisfies $Tv=cv$; by step [](#eigenspace){.pf-ref} that $v$ lies in $W$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #invariant}
$W$ is $T$-invariant, so $T$ restricts to an operator $T_{W}\colon W\to W$.

::: pf-proof
If $w\in W$ then $Tw=cw$ by step [](#eigenspace){.pf-ref}, and $cw\in W$ because $W$ is a subspace and closed under scalar multiplication. So $T(W)\subset W$, which is invariance, and the restriction $T_{W}$ is again linear.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #scalar}
For every $w\in W$, $T_{W}(w)=cw$; that is, $T_{W}=cI_{W}$.

::: pf-proof
$T_{W}(w)=Tw=cw$ by step [](#eigenspace){.pf-ref}, and $(cI_{W})(w)=cw$ by the definition of a scalar multiple of an operator; two operators agreeing on every vector of $W$ are equal.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The restriction operator is the scalar operator $cI_{W}$ on the eigenspace $W$; it is not merely scalar on a subspace, and $W\neq\{0\}$ by step [](#nonempty){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#scalar){.pf-ref} identifies $T_{W}$, and step [](#invariant){.pf-ref} shows it is an operator on $W$. Consequently every nonzero vector of $W$ is a characteristic vector of $T_{W}$ with the same value $c$, and the characteristic polynomial of $T_{W}$ is $(x-c)^{\dim W}$.
:::
