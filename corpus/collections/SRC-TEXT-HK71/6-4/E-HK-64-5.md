::: {.exercise}
Every matrix $A$ such that $A^2 = A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix.

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise ; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §6.4 Exercise 5 on printed p. 205; the argument is the card's own.

Throughout, $V$ is a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$, $T$ is the linear operator with $T^{2}=T$ (an idempotent), $\ker T$ is its null space and $\im T$ its range.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #decomposition}
$V=\ker T+\im T$.

::: pf-proof
For every $v\in V$,
$$
v=(v-Tv)+Tv,
$$
and $T(v-Tv)=Tv-T^{2}v=Tv-Tv=0$ by $T^{2}=T$, so $v-Tv\in\ker T$; the second summand $Tv$ lies in $\im T$ by definition. Hence every $v$ is a sum of a vector in $\ker T$ and a vector in $\im T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #direct}
$\ker T\cap\im T=\{0\}$.

::: pf-proof
Let $w\in\ker T\cap\im T$. Since $w\in\im T$ there is $v$ with $w=Tv$; then $w=Tv=T^{2}v=T(Tv)=Tw$, and $w\in\ker T$ gives $Tw=0$. Hence $w=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #acts}
$T$ acts as $0$ on $\ker T$ and as the identity on $\im T$.

::: pf-proof
On $\ker T$ this is the definition. If $w\in\im T$, say $w=Tv$, then $Tw=T^{2}v=Tv=w$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #basis}
The union of a basis of $\ker T$ and a basis of $\im T$ is a basis of $V$ in which the matrix of $T$ is diagonal with entries $0$ and $1$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#decomposition){.pf-ref} and [](#direct){.pf-ref}, $V$ is the direct sum of $\ker T$ and $\im T$, so the union of bases of the two summands is a basis of $V$ (the coordinate description is unique across a direct sum). In that ordered basis, step [](#acts){.pf-ref} says that each basis vector of $\ker T$ is sent to $0$ and each basis vector of $\im T$ to itself; so the columns of the matrix of $T$ are the corresponding standard columns scaled by $0$ or $1$, that is the matrix is diagonal with diagonal entries $0$ on the first block and $1$ on the second.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #similar}
Every matrix $A$ with $A^{2}=A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix.

::: pf-proof
Let $A$ be such a matrix and $T$ the operator it represents in some ordered basis. By step [](#basis){.pf-ref} there is a basis of $V$ in which the matrix of $T$ is diagonal; the change-of-basis matrix between the two ordered bases is invertible and conjugates $A$ to that diagonal matrix. So $A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix, namely to one with only $0$'s and $1$'s on its diagonal.

:::
