::: {.exercise}
Every matrix $A$ such that $A^2 = A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let \(T\) be the operator represented by \(A\), so \(T^2=T\). For every \(v\),
\[
v=(v-Tv)+Tv,
\]
with
\[
T(v-Tv)=Tv-T^2v=0,
\]
so \(v-Tv\in\ker T\), while \(Tv\in\operatorname{im}T\). Thus
\[
V=\ker T+\operatorname{im}T.
\]
If \(w\in\ker T\cap\operatorname{im}T\), write \(w=Tv\). Then
\[
w=Tv=T^2v=Tw=0,
\]
so the sum is direct. Choose bases of \(\ker T\) and \(\operatorname{im}T\); their union is a basis of \(V\). In that basis, \(T\) is diagonal with only \(0\)'s and \(1\)'s on the diagonal. Hence \(A\) is similar to a diagonal matrix.
:::
