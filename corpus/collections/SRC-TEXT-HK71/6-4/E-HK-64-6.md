::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a diagonalizable linear operator on the $n$-dimensional vector space $V$, and let $W$ be a subspace which is invariant under $T$.
Prove that the restriction operator $T_W$ is diagonalizable.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$ be the distinct eigenvalues of $T$, and let
$$
E_i=\ker(T-\lambda_iI).
$$
Since $T$ is diagonalizable,
$$
V=E_1\oplus\cdots\oplus E_r
$$
[@HK71].

<1>1. Each spectral projection is a polynomial in $T$ and preserves $W$.
::: {.proof}
For each $i$, define the Lagrange polynomial
$$
p_i(t)=\prod_{j\ne i}\frac{t-\lambda_j}{\lambda_i-\lambda_j}.
$$
The denominators are nonzero because the eigenvalues are distinct. For
$v\in E_k$,
$$
p_i(T)v=p_i(\lambda_k)v
=\begin{cases}v,&k=i,\\0,&k\ne i.\end{cases}
$$
Thus $p_i(T)$ is precisely the projection of $V$ onto $E_i$ along the direct
sum of the other eigenspaces.

Because $W$ is $T$-invariant, it is invariant under every power $T^m$, hence
under every polynomial in $T$. Therefore
$$
p_i(T)(W)\subseteq W
$$
for every $i$.
:::

<1>2. The invariant subspace is the direct sum of its intersections with the eigenspaces.
::: {.proof}
Take $w\in W$. Its unique eigenspace decomposition is
$$
w=w_1+\cdots+w_r,
\qquad w_i\in E_i.
$$
By step <1>1,
$$
w_i=p_i(T)w\in W.
$$
Hence $w_i\in W\cap E_i$ for every $i$, so
$$
W\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^r(W\cap E_i).
$$
The reverse inclusion is immediate, and the sum is direct because the $E_i$
already form a direct sum. Therefore
$$
W=(W\cap E_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus(W\cap E_r).
$$
:::

<1>3. A basis adapted to this decomposition diagonalizes $T_W$.
::: {.proof}
Choose a basis of each subspace $W\cap E_i$ and concatenate these bases.
Step <1>2 shows that the resulting list is a basis of $W$. Every vector in the
basis belonging to $W\cap E_i$ satisfies
$$
T_Wv=Tv=\lambda_i v.
$$
Thus this is a basis of $W$ consisting entirely of eigenvectors of $T_W$.
Consequently $T_W$ is diagonalizable.
:::
:::
