::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Prove that $T$ is self-adjoint if and only if $(T\alpha \mid \alpha)$ is real for every $\alpha$ in $V$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. If $T$ is self-adjoint, then $(T\alpha\mid\alpha)$ is real.
::: proof
Assume $T=T^*$. Then
$$
(T\alpha\mid\alpha)
=(\alpha\mid T^*\alpha)
=(\alpha\mid T\alpha).
$$
By conjugate symmetry,
$$
(\alpha\mid T\alpha)=\overline{(T\alpha\mid\alpha)}.
$$
Thus $(T\alpha\mid\alpha)$ equals its complex conjugate and is therefore real.
:::

<1>2. Under the reality hypothesis, $S=T-T^*$ has zero quadratic form.
::: proof
Assume $(T\alpha\mid\alpha)$ is real for every $\alpha$. Put
$$
S=T-T^*.
$$
Then $S^*=-S$. Moreover,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(S\alpha\mid\alpha)
&=(T\alpha\mid\alpha)-(T^*\alpha\mid\alpha)\\
&=(T\alpha\mid\alpha)-(\alpha\mid T\alpha)\\
&=(T\alpha\mid\alpha)-\overline{(T\alpha\mid\alpha)}\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
So
$$
q(\alpha):=(S\alpha\mid\alpha)=0
$$
for every $\alpha\in V$.
:::

<1>3. The vanishing quadratic form forces every mixed value $(S\alpha\mid\beta)$ to vanish.
::: proof
Fix $\alpha,\beta\in V$ and write
$$
a=(S\alpha\mid\beta).
$$
Since $S^*=-S$,
$$
(S\beta\mid\alpha)
=(\beta\mid S^*\alpha)
=-(\beta\mid S\alpha)
=-\overline{a}.
$$

Now expand $q(\alpha+\beta)=0$. The diagonal terms vanish by step <1>2, so
$$
0=a-\overline a.
$$
Thus $a$ is real.

Next expand $q(\alpha+i\beta)=0$. Again the diagonal terms vanish, while the
cross terms are
$$
(S\alpha\mid i\beta)+i(S\beta\mid\alpha)
=-ia-i\overline a.
$$
Hence
$$
a+\overline a=0.
$$
Thus $a$ is purely imaginary. Being both real and purely imaginary, $a=0$.
Therefore
$$
(S\alpha\mid\beta)=0
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $T$ is self-adjoint.
::: proof
Fix $\alpha$. Step <1>3 says that $S\alpha$ is orthogonal to every vector in
$V$. Non-degeneracy of the inner product gives $S\alpha=0$. Hence $S=0$, so
$$
T-T^*=0.
$$
Thus $T=T^*$ [@HK71]. Combining with step <1>1 proves
$$
\boxed{T\text{ is self-adjoint}\iff (T\alpha\mid\alpha)\in\mathbb R
\text{ for every }\alpha.}
$$
:::
:::
