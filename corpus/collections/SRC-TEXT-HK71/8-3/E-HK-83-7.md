::: {.exercise}
Show that the product of two self-adjoint operators is self-adjoint if and only if the two operators commute.
:::

::: solution
Let $S$ and $T$ be self-adjoint. Then
$$
S^*=S,
\qquad
T^*=T.
$$
For adjoints of products,
$$
(ST)^*=T^*S^*
$$
[@HK71]. Therefore
$$
(ST)^*=TS.
$$

<1>1. If $S$ and $T$ commute, then $ST$ is self-adjoint.
::: proof
If $ST=TS$, then
$$
(ST)^*=TS=ST,
$$
which is exactly the definition of self-adjointness.
:::

<1>2. If $ST$ is self-adjoint, then $S$ and $T$ commute.
::: proof
If $ST$ is self-adjoint, then
$$
ST=(ST)^*.
$$
But the displayed identity above gives $(ST)^*=TS$. Hence
$$
ST=TS.
$$
Thus the two operators commute.
:::

Consequently
$$
\boxed{ST\text{ is self-adjoint}\iff ST=TS.}
$$
:::
