::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be the linear operator on $C^2$ defined by $T\epsilon_1 = (1 + i, 2)$, $T\epsilon_2 = (i, i)$.
Using the standard inner product, find the matrix of $T^*$ in the standard ordered basis.
Does $T$ commute with $T^*$?
:::

::: solution
The matrix of $T$ in the standard ordered basis is obtained from the two given
images as its columns:
$$
[T]=A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1+i&i\\
2&i
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
For the standard inner product on $\mathbb C^2$, the matrix of the adjoint is
the conjugate transpose [@HK71]. Hence
$$
\boxed{
[T^*]=A^*=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-i&2\\
-i&-i
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

<1>1. Compute the two products.
::: proof
Direct multiplication gives
$$
AA^*=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&3+2i\\
3-2i&5
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whereas
$$
A^*A=
\begin{pmatrix}
6&1+3i\\
1-3i&2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
These matrices are different; for example, their $(1,1)$ entries are $3$ and
$6$.
:::

<1>2. Therefore $T$ does not commute with its adjoint.
::: proof
Matrix multiplication represents composition in the standard basis, so
$TT^*=T^*T$ would force $AA^*=A^*A$. Step <1>1 shows this fails. Thus
$$
\boxed{TT^*\ne T^*T.}
$$
In particular, $T$ is not normal.
:::
:::
