::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional inner product space, and let $E$ be an idempotent linear operator on $V$, i.e., $E^2 = E$.
Prove that $E$ is self-adjoint if and only if $EE^* = E^*E$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §8.3 Exercise 11 on printed p. 298. The argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

A self-adjoint idempotent commutes with its adjoint.

::: pf-proof

If $E=E^*$, then
$$
EE^*=E^2=E^*E.
$$
Thus the forward implication is immediate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $EE^*=E^*E$, then $\ker E=\ker E^*$.

::: pf-proof

Assume $E$ commutes with $E^*$. For any $v\in V$,
$$
\norm{E^*v}^2
=(E^*v\mid E^*v)
=(EE^*v\mid v),
$$
while
$$
\norm{Ev}^2
=(Ev\mid Ev)
=(E^*Ev\mid v).
$$
Because $EE^*=E^*E$, these norms are equal. Hence
$$
Ev=0\iff E^*v=0,
$$
so
$$
\ker E=\ker E^*.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The range of $E$ is orthogonal to its kernel.

::: pf-proof

From the general adjoint identity proved in Exercise 4,
$$
\operatorname{range}E=(\ker E^*)^\perp.
$$
Using step [](#s2){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{range}E=(\ker E)^\perp.
$$
Thus the range and kernel are orthogonal complements.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $E$ is self-adjoint.

::: pf-proof

Since $E^2=E$, every vector has the decomposition
$$
v=Ev+(v-Ev),
$$
with $Ev\in\operatorname{range}E$ and
$$
E(v-Ev)=Ev-E^2v=0,
$$
so $v-Ev\in\ker E$. Hence $E$ is the projection onto
$\operatorname{range}E$ along $\ker E$.

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows this is in fact an orthogonal projection. Orthogonal
projections are self-adjoint [@HK71]. Therefore
$$
E=E^*.
$$
Combining with step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\boxed{E=E^*\iff EE^*=E^*E.}
$$

:::
