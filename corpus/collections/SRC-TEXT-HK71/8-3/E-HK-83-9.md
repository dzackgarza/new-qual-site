::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the inner product space of Exercise 8, and let $D$ be the differentiation operator on $V$.
Find $D^*$.
:::

::: solution
Exercise 8 uses
$$
V=\{a_0+a_1x+a_2x^2+a_3x^3:a_i\in\mathbb R\}
$$
with
$$
(f\mid g)=\int_0^1 f(t)g(t)\,dt.
$$
Let
$$
\mathfrak B=(1,x,x^2,x^3).
$$

<1>1. Write the Gram matrix and the matrix of differentiation in $\mathfrak B$.
::: proof
The Gram matrix is the $4\times4$ Hilbert matrix
$$
G=\left(\frac1{i+j+1}\right)_{0\le i,j\le3}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/2&1/3&1/4\\
1/2&1/3&1/4&1/5\\
1/3&1/4&1/5&1/6\\
1/4&1/5&1/6&1/7
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Since
$$
D(1)=0,\quad D(x)=1,\quad D(x^2)=2x,\quad D(x^3)=3x^2,
$$
we have
$$
[D]_{\mathfrak B}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0&0\\
0&0&2&0\\
0&0&0&3\\
0&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
:::

<1>2. The adjoint matrix is $G^{-1}[D]^tG$.
::: proof
For coordinate columns $u,v$, the inner product is
$$
(u\mid v)=u^tGv.
$$
If $A=[D]_{\mathfrak B}$ and $B=[D^*]_{\mathfrak B}$, then
$$
(Au)^tGv=u^tGBv
$$
for all $u,v$. Hence
$$
A^tG=GB,
\qquad
B=G^{-1}A^tG.
$$
Direct exact arithmetic gives
$$
\boxed{
[D^*]_{\mathfrak B}=
\begin{pmatrix}
-20&-5&-4&-4\\
180&60&58&60\\
-420&-180&-180&-183\\
280&140&140&140
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
This is the adjoint matrix relative to the monomial basis [@HK71].
:::

<1>3. Equivalently, $D^*$ is given on the basis vectors by the four columns above.
::: proof
Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
D^*(1)&=-20+180x-420x^2+280x^3,\\
D^*(x)&=-5+60x-180x^2+140x^3,\\
D^*(x^2)&=-4+58x-180x^2+140x^3,\\
D^*(x^3)&=-4+60x-183x^2+140x^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
These formulas determine $D^*$ uniquely on all of $V$.
:::
:::
