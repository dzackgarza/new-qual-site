::: {.exercise}
Let $n$ be a positive integer, and let $V$ be the space of polynomials over $\RR$ which have degree at most $n$ (throw in the 0-polynomial).
Let $D$ be the differentiation operator on $V$.
What is the minimal polynomial for $D$?
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: no copy of Hoffman and Kunze and no published solution set for it is retained in `tmp/sources`, so nothing below is transcribed. The statement is the exercise the collection index records as Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra* §6.3 Exercise 7. Here $V$ is the space of polynomials over $\RR$ of degree at most $n$, together with $0$, and $D$ is differentiation; $\dim V=n+1$, and the minimal polynomial of an operator is the monic generator of the ideal of polynomials annihilating it.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nilpotent}

$D^{n+1}=0$.

::: pf-proof

Differentiating a polynomial of degree at most $n$ strictly more than $n$ times leaves $0$: each differentiation drops the degree of a nonzero polynomial by exactly one, and the derivative of a constant is $0$. So $D^{n+1}f=0$ for every $f\in V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #divides}

The minimal polynomial $m_{D}$ divides $x^{n+1}$.

::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial divides every polynomial that annihilates $D$, and $x^{n+1}$ annihilates $D$ by step [](#nilpotent){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #nonzero}

$D^{n}\neq0$.

::: pf-proof

$D^{n}(x^{n})=n!\neq0$ in $\RR[x]_{\leq n}$, so $D^{n}$ is not the zero operator.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #done}

$m_{D}=x^{n+1}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#divides){.pf-ref} $m_{D}=x^{k}$ for some $0\leq k\leq n+1$. If $k\leq n$ then $x^{k}$ annihilates $D$, so $D^{k}=0$ and hence $D^{n}=D^{n-k}D^{k}=0$, contradicting step [](#nonzero){.pf-ref}. Therefore $k=n+1$.

:::
