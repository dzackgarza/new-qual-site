::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ and $B$ be $n \times n$ matrices over the field $F$.
According to Exercise 9 of Section 6.1, the matrices $AB$ and $BA$ have the same characteristic values.
Do they have the same characteristic polynomial?
Do they have the same minimal polynomial?

:::

::: {.solution}
There is no retained solution set for this exercise and no text version of Hoffman and Kunze in the checkout; the argument and the counterexample are the card's own.

Throughout, $A,B$ are $n\times n$ matrices over the field $F$, $F(x)$ is the field of rational functions over $F$, and $m_M$ denotes the minimal polynomial of $M$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #block-identity}
$\det(I-CD)=\det(I-DC)$ for square matrices $C,D$ of the same size over any field.

::: pf-proof
Consider the block matrix $M=\begin{bmatrix}I&C\\D&I\end{bmatrix}$ and the block matrices $L_1=\begin{bmatrix}I&-C\\0&I\end{bmatrix}$ and $L_2=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\-D&I\end{bmatrix}$. Multiplying blocks,
$$
L_1M=\begin{bmatrix}I-CD&0\\D&I\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
L_2M=\begin{bmatrix}I&C\\0&I-DC\end{bmatrix}.
$$
$L_1$ and $L_2$ are block triangular with identity diagonal blocks, so $\det L_1=\det L_2=1$; the two products are block triangular as displayed, so their determinants are $\det(I-CD)$ and $\det(I-DC)$. Since $\det(L_iM)=\det L_i\det M=\det M$ for $i=1,2$, the two determinants are both $\det M$ and hence equal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #characteristic}
$\det(xI-AB)=\det(xI-BA)$ in $F[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Over the field $F(x)$ the matrix $xI-AB=x(I-x^{-1}AB)$ is invertible-scalar times a matrix, so $\det(xI-AB)=x^{n}\det(I-x^{-1}AB)$ because $x\neq0$ in $F(x)$. Applying step [](#block-identity){.pf-ref} with $C=x^{-1}A$ and $D=B$, which are square of the same size over $F(x)$, gives $\det(I-x^{-1}AB)=\det(I-Bx^{-1}A)=\det(I-x^{-1}BA)$, the last equality because the scalar $x^{-1}$ commutes with $B$. Hence $\det(xI-AB)=x^{n}\det(I-x^{-1}BA)=\det(xI-BA)$. Both sides are polynomials in $F[x]$, so this is an identity of characteristic polynomials.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #minimal-fails}
$AB$ and $BA$ need not have the same minimal polynomial.

::: pf-proof
Take $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$ and $B=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$. Then $AB=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\neq0$ while $(AB)^{2}=0$, so the least-degree monic polynomial annihilating $AB$ is $x^{2}$, that is $m_{AB}=x^{2}$; and $BA=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$, whose minimal polynomial is $x$ because $x$ annihilates it and $1$ does not. Hence $m_{AB}\neq m_{BA}$, although the characteristic polynomials agree by step [](#characteristic){.pf-ref}.

:::
