::: {.exercise}
Find a $3 \times 3$ matrix for which the minimal polynomial is $x^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: an explicit matrix whose square is zero but which is not zero has minimal polynomial exactly $x^{2}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #candidate}

$A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}$ satisfies $A\ne0$ and $A^{2}=0$.

::: pf-proof

The $(1,2)$ entry of $A$ is $1$, so $A\ne0$. Squaring, the $(1,2)$ entry of $A^{2}$ is $\sum_{k}A_{1k}A_{k2}=A_{11}A_{12}+A_{12}A_{22}+A_{13}A_{32}=0\cdot1+1\cdot0+0\cdot0=0$, and every other entry of $A^{2}$ is a sum of products in which one factor is a zero entry of $A$; explicitly $A$ has nonzero entries only in position $(1,2)$, and there is no index $k$ with $A_{2k}\ne0$, so every entry of $A^{2}$ vanishes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #annihilates}

$x^{2}$ annihilates $A$, while $1$ and $x$ do not.

::: pf-proof

The polynomial $x^{2}$ evaluated at $A$ is $A^{2}=0$, so $x^{2}$ lies in the annihilator of $A$. The constant polynomial $1$ evaluates to $I\ne0$, and $x$ evaluates to $A\ne0$ by step [](#candidate){.pf-ref}; so neither annihilates $A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #minimal}

The minimal polynomial of $A$ is $x^{2}$.

::: pf-proof

The minimal polynomial is the monic generator of the annihilator ideal of $A$, so it divides every annihilating polynomial and is itself annihilating. By step [](#annihilates){.pf-ref} it divides $x^{2}$, so it is $1$, $x$ or $x^{2}$; it is not $1$ or $x$, and $x^{2}$ is monic and annihilates $A$. Hence
$$
\boxed{m_{A}(x)=x^{2},\qquad A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}.}
$$

:::
