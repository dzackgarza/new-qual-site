::: {.exercise}
Let $P$ be the operator on $\RR^2$ which projects each vector onto the $x$-axis, parallel to the $y$-axis: $P(x, y) = (x, 0)$.
Show that $P$ is linear.
What is the minimal polynomial for $P$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The argument is the card's own; no retained solution set is cited on the card, and no printed copy of Hoffman and Kunze is retained in `tmp/sources`, so the exercise is cited by section and number rather than by page. It is checked against Hoffman and Kunze, Linear Algebra (1971), §6.3 Exercise 8.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #linear}
$P(x,y)=(x,0)$ is linear.

::: pf-proof
$P$ is given by the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$ acting on column vectors, and matrix multiplication is linear.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #idempotent}
$P^2=P$.

::: pf-proof
$P^2(x,y)=P(x,0)=(x,0)=P(x,y)$ for every $(x,y)$, so the two operators agree.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #divides}
The minimal polynomial of $P$ divides $x(x-1)$.

::: pf-proof
$P^2=P$ says $P^2-P=0$, that is, the polynomial $x^2-x=x(x-1)$ annihilates $P$. The minimal polynomial divides every polynomial that annihilates $P$, so it divides $x(x-1)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #notsmaller}
Neither $x$ nor $x-1$ annihilates $P$.

::: pf-proof
$P\ne0$, since $P(1,0)=(1,0)$, so $x$ does not annihilate $P$; and $P\ne I$, since $P(0,1)=(0,0)\ne(0,1)$, so $x-1$ does not annihilate $P$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The minimal polynomial of $P$ is $x(x-1)$.

::: pf-proof
The minimal polynomial is monic and divides $x(x-1)$, whose monic divisors are $1$, $x$, $x-1$ and $x(x-1)$. The constant $1$ does not annihilate $P$ because $P\ne0$, and neither do $x$ and $x-1$ by step [](#notsmaller){.pf-ref}. Hence
$$
\boxed{m_P(x)=x(x-1).}
$$
Equivalently, $P$ is diagonalisable with eigenvalues $1$ (on the $x$-axis) and $0$ (on the $y$-axis), and its minimal polynomial is the product of the distinct linear factors.
:::
