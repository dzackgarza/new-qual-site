::: {.exercise}
Let $a$, $b$, and $c$ be elements of a field $F$, and let $A$ be the following $3 \times 3$ matrix over $F$:
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & c \\ 1 & 0 & b \\ 0 & 1 & a \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Prove that the characteristic polynomial for $A$ is $x^3 - ax^2 - bx - c$ and that this is also the minimal polynomial for $A$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Computation of the characteristic polynomial:
<2>1. The characteristic matrix $xI - A$ is:
\[
xI - A = \begin{pmatrix}
x & 0 & -c \\
-1 & x & -b \\
0 & -1 & x - a
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
matrix subtraction $xI_3 - A$.
:::
<2>2. Compute the determinant $\det(xI - A)$ by cofactor expansion along the first row:
\[
\det(xI - A) = x \cdot \det\begin{pmatrix} x & -b \\ -1 & x - a \end{pmatrix} - c \cdot \det\begin{pmatrix} -1 & x \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Laplace expansion along row 1.
:::
<2>3. Evaluating the $2 \times 2$ determinants:
\[
\det(xI - A) = x\big(x(x - a) - b\big) - c(1 - 0) = x(x^2 - ax - b) - c = x^3 - ax^2 - bx - c.
\]
Thus the characteristic polynomial is $p(x) = x^3 - ax^2 - bx - c$.
::: {.proof}
polynomial expansion.
:::

<1>2. Proof that the minimal polynomial is $p(x)$:
<2>1. By the Cayley–Hamilton Theorem, the minimal polynomial $m(x)$ divides the characteristic polynomial $p(x)$, so $\deg(m) \le 3$.
::: {.proof}
Cayley–Hamilton Theorem.
:::
<2>2. Consider the first standard basis vector $e_1 = (1, 0, 0)^T \in F^3$.
Compute the successive applications of $A$:
\[
A e_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = e_2, \qquad A^2 e_1 = A e_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = e_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
matrix-vector multiplication.
:::
<2>3. The set $\{e_1, A e_1, A^2 e_1\} = \{e_1, e_2, e_3\}$ is the standard basis of $F^3$, hence linearly independent over $F$.
::: {.proof}
standard unit vectors are linearly independent.
:::
<2>4. For any non-zero polynomial $g(x) = d_0 + d_1 x + d_2 x^2 \in F[x]$ of degree at most 2:
\[
g(A) e_1 = d_0 e_1 + d_1 A e_1 + d_2 A^2 e_1 = (d_0, d_1, d_2)^T \neq (0, 0, 0)^T.
\]
Thus $g(A) \neq 0$ for all non-zero polynomials of degree $< 3$.
::: {.proof}
linear independence of $\{e_1, Ae_1, A^2e_1\}$.
:::
<2>5. Therefore $\deg(m) \ge 3$.
Since $m(x)$ is monic, divides $p(x)$, and has degree 3, we must have $m(x) = p(x) = x^3 - ax^2 - bx - c$.
::: {.proof}
uniqueness of monic divisor of equal degree.
:::

<1>3. Conclusion:
Both the characteristic polynomial and minimal polynomial of $A$ equal $x^3 - ax^2 - bx - c$. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2.
:::
:::
