::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space.
What is the minimal polynomial for the identity operator on $V$?
What is the minimal polynomial for the zero operator?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The identity operator $I$ satisfies $I - I = 0$, i.e. the polynomial $x - 1$ annihilates $I$.
::: {.proof}
$(x-1)(I) = I - I = 0$.
:::

<1>2. No nonzero polynomial of degree $0$ annihilates $I$.
::: {.proof}
a constant $c$ annihilates $I$ only if $cI = 0$, forcing $c = 0$.
:::

<1>3. Hence the minimal polynomial of $I$ is $x - 1$.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2 (the minimal polynomial is the monic annihilating polynomial of least degree).
:::

<1>4. The zero operator $0$ satisfies $x(0) = 0$, i.e. the polynomial $x$ annihilates it.
::: {.proof}
$x(0) = 0$.
:::

<1>5. No nonzero constant annihilates the zero operator.
::: {.proof}
$c \cdot 0 = 0$ for all $c$, but the minimal polynomial must be monic and nonzero; the least-degree monic annihilating polynomial is $x$.
:::

<1>6. Hence the minimal polynomial of the zero operator is $x$.
::: {.proof}
<1>4 and <1>5.
:::

<1>7. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and <1>6.
:::
:::
