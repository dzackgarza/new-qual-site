::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space.
What is the minimal polynomial for the identity operator on $V$?
What is the minimal polynomial for the zero operator?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The identity operator $I$ satisfies $I - I = 0$, i.e. the polynomial $x - 1$ annihilates $I$.

::: pf-proof

$(x-1)(I) = I - I = 0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

No nonzero polynomial of degree $0$ annihilates $I$.

::: pf-proof

a constant $c$ annihilates $I$ only if $cI = 0$, forcing $c = 0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Hence the minimal polynomial of $I$ is $x - 1$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref} (the minimal polynomial is the monic annihilating polynomial of least degree).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The zero operator $0$ satisfies $x(0) = 0$, i.e. the polynomial $x$ annihilates it.

::: pf-proof

$x(0) = 0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

No nonzero constant annihilates the zero operator.

::: pf-proof

$c \cdot 0 = 0$ for all $c$, but the minimal polynomial must be monic and nonzero; the least-degree monic annihilating polynomial is $x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

Hence the minimal polynomial of the zero operator is $x$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

:::
