::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space and let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$.
Suppose that there exists some positive integer $k$ so that $T^k = 0$.
Prove that $T^n = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let \(m_T\) be the minimal polynomial of \(T\). Since \(T^k=0\), the polynomial \(x^k\) annihilates \(T\), so
\[
m_T(x)\mid x^k.
\]
Thus \(m_T=x^r\) for some \(r\ge1\). By the Cayley--Hamilton theorem, \(m_T\) divides the characteristic polynomial of \(T\), which has degree \(n\). Hence \(r\le n\). Therefore
\[
T^n=T^{n-r}T^r=0.
\]
:::
