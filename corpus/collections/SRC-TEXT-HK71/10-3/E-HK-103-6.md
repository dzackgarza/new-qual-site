::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a subfield $F$ of $\CC$, and let $f$ be a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form on $V$.
Prove the following skew-symmetric analogue of the adjoint theorem.

For each linear operator $T$ on $V$, there is a unique linear operator $T'$ such that
\[
f(T\alpha,\beta)=f(\alpha,T'\beta)
\]
for all $\alpha,\beta\in V$. Moreover,
\[
(T_1T_2)'=T_2'T_1',\qquad
(c_1T_1+c_2T_2)'=c_1T_1'+c_2T_2',\qquad
(T')'=T.
\]
:::


::: solution
Define
$$
L_f:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
L_f(\alpha)(\beta)=f(\beta,\alpha).
$$
Because $f$ is non-degenerate and $V$ is finite-dimensional, $L_f$ is an
isomorphism.

<1>1. Every operator $T$ has a unique adjoint $T'$ relative to $f$.
::: proof
For fixed $\beta\in V$, the map
$$
\alpha\longmapsto f(T\alpha,\beta)
$$
is a linear functional on $V$. Since $L_f$ is onto, there is a unique vector
$T'\beta$ such that
$$
f(T\alpha,\beta)=f(\alpha,T'\beta)
$$
for every $\alpha$.

It remains to see that $\beta\mapsto T'\beta$ is linear. Let $a,b\in F$. For
all $\alpha$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha,T'(a\beta+b\gamma))
&=f(T\alpha,a\beta+b\gamma)\\
&=a f(T\alpha,\beta)+b f(T\alpha,\gamma)\\
&=f(\alpha,aT'\beta+bT'\gamma).
\end{aligned}
$$
Non-degeneracy in the second variable therefore gives
$$
T'(a\beta+b\gamma)=aT'\beta+bT'\gamma.
$$
Thus $T'$ is linear. Uniqueness follows from the same argument: if $S$ also
satisfies the adjoint identity, then
$$
f(\alpha,(T'-S)\beta)=0
$$
for all $\alpha$, hence $(T'-S)\beta=0$ for every $\beta$.
:::

<1>2. Taking adjoints reverses products.
::: proof
For all $\alpha,\beta$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(T_1T_2\alpha,\beta)
&=f(T_2\alpha,T_1'\beta)\\
&=f(\alpha,T_2'T_1'\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
By uniqueness from step <1>1,
$$
\boxed{(T_1T_2)'=T_2'T_1'.}
$$
:::

<1>3. Taking adjoints is linear.
::: proof
For scalars $c_1,c_2$ and all $\alpha,\beta$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f((c_1T_1+c_2T_2)\alpha,\beta)
&=c_1f(T_1\alpha,\beta)+c_2f(T_2\alpha,\beta)\\
&=f(\alpha,(c_1T_1'+c_2T_2')\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence uniqueness gives
$$
\boxed{(c_1T_1+c_2T_2)'=c_1T_1'+c_2T_2'.}
$$
:::

<1>4. The adjoint operation is an involution.
::: proof
Apply the defining identity to $T'$:
$$
f(T'\beta,\alpha)=f(\beta,T''\alpha).
$$
Using skew-symmetry and then the defining identity for $T$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(T'\beta,\alpha)
&=-f(\alpha,T'\beta)\\
&=-f(T\alpha,\beta)\\
&=f(\beta,T\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
f(\beta,T''\alpha)=f(\beta,T\alpha)
$$
for every $\alpha,\beta$. Non-degeneracy implies
$$
T''\alpha=T\alpha
$$
for every $\alpha$, so
$$
\boxed{(T')'=T.}
$$
:::
