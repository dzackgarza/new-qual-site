::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be a symmetric bilinear form on $C^n$ and $g$ a skew-symmetric bilinear form on $C^n$.
Suppose $f + g = 0$.
Show that $f = g = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Because $f+g=0$, one has $g=-f$. Hence $f$ is both symmetric and
skew-symmetric.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The form $f$ vanishes identically.

::: pf-proof

For arbitrary $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C^n$, symmetry gives
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=f(\beta,\alpha),
$$
while skew-symmetry of $f=-g$ gives
$$
f(\beta,\alpha)=-f(\alpha,\beta).
$$
Therefore
$$
2f(\alpha,\beta)=0.
$$
Since the scalar field is $\mathbb C$, $2
e0$ and is invertible. Thus
$$
f(\alpha,\beta)=0
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$, so $f=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The form $g$ also vanishes.

::: pf-proof

Since $g=-f$ and $f=0$ by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, one has $g=0$.

:::

:::

:::

Hence
$$
\boxed{f=g=0}.
$$
:::
