::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $F$.
Show that the set of all skew-symmetric bilinear forms on $V$ is a subspace of $L(V, V, F)$.
:::


::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\mathcal K=\{f\in L(V,V,F): f(\alpha,\beta)=-f(\beta,\alpha)
	ext{ for all }\alpha,\beta\in V\}.
$$

<1>1. The zero form belongs to $\mathcal K$.
::: {.proof}
For all $\alpha,\beta\in V$,
$$
0(\alpha,\beta)=0=-0(\beta,\alpha).
$$
Hence the zero bilinear form is skew-symmetric.
:::

<1>2. The set $\mathcal K$ is closed under addition.
::: {.proof}
Let $f,g\in\mathcal K$. Then, for all $\alpha,\beta$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f+g)(\alpha,\beta)
&=f(\alpha,\beta)+g(\alpha,\beta)\\
&=-f(\beta,\alpha)-g(\beta,\alpha)\\
&=-(f+g)(\beta,\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $f+g$ is skew-symmetric and lies in $\mathcal K$.
:::

<1>3. The set $\mathcal K$ is closed under scalar multiplication.
::: {.proof}
Let $c\in F$ and $f\in\mathcal K$. Then
$$
(cf)(\alpha,\beta)
=c f(\alpha,\beta)
=-c f(\beta,\alpha)
=-(cf)(\beta,\alpha)
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$. Hence $cf\in\mathcal K$.
:::

By steps <1>1--<1>3, $\mathcal K$ is a vector subspace of $L(V,V,F)$.
:::
