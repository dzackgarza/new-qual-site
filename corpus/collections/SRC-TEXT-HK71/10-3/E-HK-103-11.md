::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be any skew-symmetric bilinear form on $\RR^3$.
Prove that there are linear functionals $L_1, L_2$ such that
$$
f(\alpha, \beta) = L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta) - L_1(\beta)L_2(\alpha).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the book's text is retained as `assets/attachments/Hoffman-Kunze-Linear-Algebra.pdf` (Hoffman and Kunze, *Linear Algebra*, 2nd ed., 1971), where it is §10.3 Exercise 11. The argument is the card's own.

Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be the standard basis and put
$$
a=f(e_1,e_2),\qquad
b=f(e_1,e_3),\qquad
c=f(e_2,e_3).
$$
Skew-symmetry gives, for
$\alpha=(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ and $\beta=(y_1,y_2,y_3)$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha,\beta)
={}&a(x_1y_2-x_2y_1)
+b(x_1y_3-x_3y_1)\\
&+c(x_2y_3-x_3y_2).
\end{aligned}
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

If $a\ne0$, construct the required functionals.

::: pf-proof

Define
$$
L_1(x_1,x_2,x_3)=x_1-\frac ca x_3,
$$
and
$$
L_2(x_1,x_2,x_3)=a x_2+b x_3.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
&L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta)-L_1(\beta)L_2(\alpha)\\
&=a(x_1y_2-x_2y_1)
+b(x_1y_3-x_3y_1)
+c(x_2y_3-x_3y_2)\\
&=f(\alpha,\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $a=0$ but $b\ne0$, construct the required functionals.

::: pf-proof

Define
$$
L_1(x_1,x_2,x_3)=x_1+\frac cb x_2,
\qquad
L_2(x_1,x_2,x_3)=b x_3.
$$
Then
$$
L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta)-L_1(\beta)L_2(\alpha)
=b(x_1y_3-x_3y_1)+c(x_2y_3-x_3y_2),
$$
which is $f(\alpha,\beta)$ because $a=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $a=b=0$, factor the remaining term.

::: pf-proof

If $c\ne0$, take
$$
L_1(x_1,x_2,x_3)=x_2,
\qquad
L_2(x_1,x_2,x_3)=c x_3.
$$
Then their alternating product is
$$
c(x_2y_3-x_3y_2)=f(\alpha,\beta).
$$
If also $c=0$, then $f=0$ and one may take $L_1=0$ and $L_2=0$.

:::

:::

:::

Thus in every case there exist $L_1,L_2\in(\RR^3)^*$ with
$$
\boxed{
f(\alpha,\beta)
=L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta)-L_1(\beta)L_2(\alpha).}
$$
:::
