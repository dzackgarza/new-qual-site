::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space over a subfield $F$ of $C$.
Prove the following.

(a) The equation $(Pf)(\alpha, \beta) = \frac{1}{2}f(\alpha, \beta) - \frac{1}{2}f(\beta, \alpha)$ defines a linear operator $P$ on $L(V, V, F)$.

(b) $P^2 = P$, i.e., $P$ is a projection.

(c) $\operatorname{rank} P = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$; $\operatorname{nullity} P = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.

(d) If $U$ is a linear operator on $V$, the equation $(U^{\dagger}f)(\alpha, \beta) = f(U\alpha, U\beta)$ defines a linear operator $U^{\dagger}$ on $L(V, V, F)$.

(e) For every linear operator $U$, the projection $P$ commutes with $U^{\dagger}$.
:::


::: solution
Write $\mathcal B=L(V,V,F)$ for the vector space of bilinear forms on $V$.

<1>1. The formula in part (a) defines a linear operator $P:\mathcal B\to\mathcal B$.
::: proof
For $f\in\mathcal B$, define
$$
(Pf)(\alpha,eta)
=\frac12\bigl(f(\alpha,eta)-f(eta,\alpha)\bigr).
$$
Because $f$ is bilinear, this expression is bilinear in $(\alpha,\beta)$, so
$Pf\in\mathcal B$. If $c,d\in F$ and $f,g\in\mathcal B$, then
$$
P(cf+dg)=cPf+dPg
$$
by direct expansion. Hence $P$ is linear.
:::

<1>2. The operator $P$ is a projection.
::: proof
For every $f$,
$$
(Pf)(eta,\alpha)=-(Pf)(\alpha,eta),
$$
so $Pf$ is skew-symmetric. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
(P^2f)(\alpha,eta)
&=\frac12\bigl((Pf)(\alpha,eta)-(Pf)(eta,\alpha)\bigr)\\
&=\frac12\bigl((Pf)(\alpha,eta)+(Pf)(\alpha,eta)\bigr)\\
&=(Pf)(\alpha,eta).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $P^2=P$.
:::

<1>3. The image of $P$ is the space of skew-symmetric forms and the kernel is the space of symmetric forms.
::: proof
Step <1>2 already shows that every $Pf$ is skew-symmetric. Conversely, if
$g$ is skew-symmetric, then
$$
(Pg)(\alpha,eta)
=\frac12(g(\alpha,eta)-g(eta,\alpha))
=g(\alpha,eta),
$$
so $g\in\operatorname{im}P$. Hence
$$
\operatorname{im}P=\{\text{skew-symmetric forms}\}.
$$

Also,
$$
Pf=0
$$
if and only if
$$
f(\alpha,eta)=f(eta,\alpha)
$$
for all $\alpha,eta$, i.e. exactly when $f$ is symmetric. Thus
$$
\ker P=\{\text{symmetric forms}\}.
$$
:::

<1>4. Compute the rank and nullity of $P$.
::: proof
Choose a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $V$. A bilinear form is determined by its
matrix $(f(e_i,e_j))$, so $\mathcal B$ has dimension $n^2$.

A skew-symmetric form has zero diagonal entries and is determined freely by the
entries with $i<j$; the entries with $j<i$ are their negatives. Hence the space
of skew-symmetric forms has dimension
$$
\binom n2=\frac{n(n-1)}2.
$$
By step <1>3,
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}P=\frac{n(n-1)}2}.
$$

Likewise, a symmetric form is determined freely by the diagonal entries and the
entries above the diagonal, giving
$$
n+\binom n2=\frac{n(n+1)}2
$$
parameters. Thus
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{nullity}P=\frac{n(n+1)}2}.
$$
:::

<1>5. Pullback by $U$ defines a linear operator $U^\dagger$ on $\mathcal B$.
::: proof
For $f\in\mathcal B$, set
$$
(U^\dagger f)(\alpha,\beta)=f(U\alpha,U\beta).
$$
Since $U$ is linear and $f$ is bilinear, $U^\dagger f$ is bilinear. Moreover
for scalars $c,d$ and forms $f,g$,
$$
U^\dagger(cf+dg)=cU^\dagger f+dU^\dagger g.
$$
Hence $U^\dagger$ is a linear operator on $\mathcal B$.
:::

<1>6. The operators $P$ and $U^\dagger$ commute.
::: proof
For every $f\in\mathcal B$ and $\alpha,\beta\in V$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(PU^\dagger f)(\alpha,\beta)
&=\frac12\bigl(f(U\alpha,U\beta)-f(U\beta,U\alpha)\bigr)\\
&=(Pf)(U\alpha,U\beta)\\
&=(U^\dagger Pf)(\alpha,\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{PU^\dagger=U^\dagger P}.
$$
:::
