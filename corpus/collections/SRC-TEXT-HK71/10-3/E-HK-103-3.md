::: {.exercise}
Find a basis for the space of all skew-symmetric bilinear forms on $\RR^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A skew-symmetric bilinear form $f$ on $\RR^n$ is determined by its matrix $A = (a_{ij})$ with $a_{ij} = f(e_i, e_j)$, where $A$ is skew-symmetric ($A^t = -A$).
::: {.proof}
bilinear forms correspond to matrices, and skew-symmetry means $a_{ij} = -a_{ji}$.
:::

<1>2. Hence the space of skew-symmetric forms is isomorphic to the space of skew-symmetric $n \times n$ matrices, of dimension $\binom{n}{2}$.
::: {.proof}
a skew-symmetric matrix is determined by its strictly upper-triangular entries $a_{ij}$ for $i < j$, of which there are $\binom{n}{2}$.
:::

<1>3. For each pair $1 \le i < j \le n$, define the form $f_{ij}$ by
$$f_{ij}(x, y) = x_i y_j - x_j y_i.$$
::: {.proof}
definition.
:::

<1>4. The matrix of $f_{ij}$ is $E_{ij} - E_{ji}$ (where $E_{ij}$ is the matrix with a $1$ in position $(i,j)$ and $0$ elsewhere).
::: {.proof}
$f_{ij}(e_p, e_q) = \delta_{pi}\delta_{qj} - \delta_{pj}\delta_{qi}$.
:::

<1>5. The set $\{f_{ij} : 1 \le i < j \le n\}$ is a basis.
::: {.proof}
the matrices $E_{ij} - E_{ji}$ (for $i < j$) form a basis of the skew-symmetric matrices, and <1>2 gives the dimension $\binom{n}{2}$, matching the number of these forms.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>5.
