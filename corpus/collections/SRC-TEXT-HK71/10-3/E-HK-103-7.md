::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be a bilinear form on a vector space $V$.
Let $L_f$ and $R_f$ be the mappings of $V$ into $V^*$ associated with $f$ in Section 10.1. Prove that $f$ is skew-symmetric if and only if $L_f = -R_f$.
:::


::: solution
Recall the two associated maps
$$
L_f,R_f:V\longrightarrow V^*
$$
defined by
$$
(L_f\alpha)(\beta)=f(\alpha,\beta),
\qquad
(R_f\alpha)(\beta)=f(\beta,\alpha).
$$

<1>1. If $f$ is skew-symmetric, then $L_f=-R_f$.
::: proof
For every $\alpha,\beta\in V$,
$$
(L_f\alpha)(\beta)
=f(\alpha,\beta)
=-f(\beta,\alpha)
=-(R_f\alpha)(\beta).
$$
Thus the functionals $L_f\alpha$ and $-R_f\alpha$ agree on every $\beta$,
so
$$
L_f\alpha=-R_f\alpha
$$
for every $\alpha$. Hence
$$
L_f=-R_f.
$$
:::

<1>2. If $L_f=-R_f$, then $f$ is skew-symmetric.
::: proof
Assume $L_f=-R_f$. For arbitrary $\alpha,\beta\in V$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(\alpha,\beta)
&=(L_f\alpha)(\beta)\\
&=-(R_f\alpha)(\beta)\\
&=-f(\beta,\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore $f$ is skew-symmetric.
:::

Hence
$$
\boxed{f\text{ is skew-symmetric}\iff L_f=-R_f.}
$$
:::
