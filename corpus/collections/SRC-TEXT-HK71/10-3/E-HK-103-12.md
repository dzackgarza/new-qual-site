::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a subfield of the complex numbers, and let $f$, $g$ be skew-symmetric bilinear forms on $V$.
Show that there is an invertible linear operator $T$ on $V$ such that $f(T\alpha, T\beta) = g(\alpha, \beta)$ for all $\alpha$, $\beta$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ have the same rank.
:::


::: {.solution}
We first establish the canonical form needed for the converse.

<1>1. Every skew-symmetric form has a basis in which its matrix is a direct sum of standard $2\times2$ skew blocks and zeros.
::: {.proof}
Let $h$ be a skew-symmetric bilinear form on an $n$-dimensional space.
If $h=0$, every basis gives the zero matrix, so there is nothing to prove.

Suppose $h\ne0$. Choose $u,v$ with $h(u,v)\ne0$, and rescale $v$ so that
$$
h(u,v)=1.
$$
Let
$$
H=\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}.
$$
In the ordered basis $(u,v)$, the restriction of $h$ to $H$ has matrix
$$
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
so it is non-degenerate on $H$.

Define
$$
H^\perp=\{x\in V:h(x,H)=0\}.
$$
For any $x\in V$, put
$$
h_x=h(x,v)u-h(x,u)v.
$$
Then
$$
h(x-h_x,u)=0,
\qquad
h(x-h_x,v)=0,
$$
so $x-h_x\in H^\perp$. Hence
$$
V=H+H^\perp.
$$
The sum is direct: if $au+bv\in H^\perp$, pairing with $u$ and $v$ gives
$b=0$ and $a=0$. Thus
$$
V=H\oplus H^\perp,
$$
and the matrix of $h$ is block diagonal with the block $J$ and the matrix of
$h|_{H^\perp}$.

Repeat the construction on $H^\perp$. After finitely many steps we obtain a
basis
$$
u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_m,v_m,z_1,\ldots,z_s
$$
for which
$$
[h]=J^{\oplus m}\oplus 0_s.
$$
The remaining restriction is zero, for otherwise another nondegenerate
$2$-plane could be split off. Consequently
$$
\operatorname{rank}h=2m.
$$
:::

<1>2. Congruent forms have the same rank.
::: {.proof}
Assume there is an invertible $T$ such that
$$
f(T\alpha,T\beta)=g(\alpha,\beta)
$$
for all $\alpha,\beta$. Choose any basis and let $P$ be the matrix of $T$ in
that basis. If $A$ and $B$ are the matrices of $f$ and $g$, respectively, then
$$
B=P^tAP.
$$
Because $P$ and $P^t$ are invertible,
$$
\operatorname{rank}B=\operatorname{rank}A.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{rank}g=\operatorname{rank}f.
$$
:::

<1>3. Skew-symmetric forms of the same rank are congruent.
::: {.proof}
Assume
$$
\operatorname{rank}f=\operatorname{rank}g=2m.
$$
By step <1>1, choose an ordered basis
$$
\mathfrak B_f=(u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_m,v_m,z_1,\ldots,z_s)
$$
for which
$$
[f]_{\mathfrak B_f}=J^{\oplus m}\oplus0_s,
$$
and an ordered basis
$$
\mathfrak B_g=(u'_1,v'_1,\ldots,u'_m,v'_m,z'_1,\ldots,z'_s)
$$
for which
$$
[g]_{\mathfrak B_g}=J^{\oplus m}\oplus0_s.
$$
The same value of $s=n-2m$ occurs because the two forms live on the same
space and have equal rank.

Define $T$ by sending the $g$-canonical basis to the $f$-canonical basis:
$$
T(u'_i)=u_i,\qquad T(v'_i)=v_i,\qquad T(z'_j)=z_j.
$$
This maps a basis to a basis, so $T$ is invertible. If $\alpha,\beta$ have
coordinate columns $x,y$ in $\mathfrak B_g$, then $T\alpha,T\beta$ have the
same coordinate columns $x,y$ in $\mathfrak B_f$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(T\alpha,T\beta)
&=x^t(J^{\oplus m}\oplus0_s)y\\
&=g(\alpha,\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the required congruence exists.
:::

Combining steps <1>2 and <1>3,
$$
\boxed{
\exists\,T\in GL(V): f(T\alpha,T\beta)=g(\alpha,\beta)
\iff
\operatorname{rank}f=\operatorname{rank}g.}
$$
:::
