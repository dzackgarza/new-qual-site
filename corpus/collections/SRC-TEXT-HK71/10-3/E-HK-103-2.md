::: {.exercise}
Find all skew-symmetric bilinear forms on $\RR^3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

A skew-symmetric bilinear form $f$ on $\RR^3$ has matrix $A = (a_{ij})$ with $a_{ij} = f(e_i, e_j)$ and $A^t = -A$.

::: pf-proof

skew-symmetry means $a_{ij} = -a_{ji}$, so $a_{ii} = 0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Hence $A$ has the form
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & a & b \\ -a & 0 & c \\ -b & -c & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
for arbitrary real $a, b, c$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} (the diagonal is zero and the upper triangle determines the lower triangle).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Therefore every skew-symmetric form on $\RR^3$ is
$$f(x, y) = a(x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1) + b(x_1 y_3 - x_3 y_1) + c(x_2 y_3 - x_3 y_2)$$
for some $a, b, c \in \RR$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, expanding $x^t A y$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Equivalently, $f(x, y) = \det\begin{pmatrix} a & b & c \\ x_1 & x_2 & x_3 \\ y_1 & y_2 & y_3 \end{pmatrix}$-type expression: $f(x,y) = (a, b, c) \cdot (x \times y)$, the dot product of the vector $(a,b,c)$ with the cross product $x \times y$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, recognizing the cross product components.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Hence the space of skew-symmetric forms on $\RR^3$ is $3$-dimensional, parametrized by $(a, b, c) \in \RR^3$.

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::
