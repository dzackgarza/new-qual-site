::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a subfield of the complex numbers and $f$ a skew-symmetric bilinear form on $V$.
Show that $f$ has rank 2 if and only if there exist linearly independent linear functionals $L_1$, $L_2$ on $V$ such that
\[
f(\alpha, \beta) = L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta) - L_1(\beta)L_2(\alpha).
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. ($\Leftarrow$) If $f(\alpha, \beta) = L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta) - L_1(\beta)L_2(\alpha)$ with $L_1, L_2$ linearly independent, then $f$ has rank $2$.
<2>1. $f$ is skew-symmetric.
::: {.proof}
$f(\beta, \alpha) = L_1(\beta)L_2(\alpha) - L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta) = -f(\alpha, \beta)$.
:::
<2>2. The matrix of $f$ (in a basis extending a basis of $\ker L_1 \cap \ker L_2$) has rank $2$.
::: {.proof}
$f$ is the wedge $L_1 \wedge L_2$; since $L_1, L_2$ are linearly independent, the associated alternating form has rank $2$ (its radical is $\ker L_1 \cap \ker L_2$, of codimension $2$).
:::
<2>3. Hence $f$ has rank $2$.
::: {.proof}
<2>2.
:::

<1>2. ($\Rightarrow$) If $f$ has rank $2$, then $f = L_1 \wedge L_2$ for linearly independent $L_1, L_2$.
<2>1. A skew-symmetric form of rank $2$ has a matrix (in a suitable basis) of the form $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ in a $2$-dimensional block, with zeros elsewhere.
::: {.proof}
the standard normal form of an alternating form: it is a direct sum of hyperbolic planes $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and a zero block; rank $2$ means exactly one hyperbolic plane.
:::
<2>2. In this basis, $f(\alpha, \beta) = \alpha_1 \beta_2 - \alpha_2 \beta_1$.
::: {.proof}
read off the matrix.
:::
<2>3. Define $L_1(\alpha) = \alpha_1$ and $L_2(\alpha) = \alpha_2$ (the coordinate functionals); these are linearly independent.
::: {.proof}
they are distinct coordinate functionals.
:::
<2>4. Then $f(\alpha, \beta) = L_1(\alpha)L_2(\beta) - L_1(\beta)L_2(\alpha)$.
::: {.proof}
<2>2 and <2>3.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>2.
:::
:::
