::: {.exercise}
Show that the result of Exercise 12 is valid for symmetric bilinear forms on a complex vector space, but is not valid for symmetric bilinear forms on a real vector space.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. Over $\mathbb C$, every symmetric bilinear form is congruent to
$\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)$, where $r$ is its rank.
::: proof
Let $f$ be a symmetric bilinear form on a finite-dimensional complex vector
space $V$.

If $f=0$, the assertion is immediate. Otherwise there is a vector $u$ with
$f(u,u)\ne0$. Indeed, if $f(v,v)=0$ for every $v$, then symmetry and
polarization give
$$
2f(x,y)=f(x+y,x+y)-f(x,x)-f(y,y)=0
$$
for all $x,y$, forcing $f=0$.

Choose such a vector $u$. Its one-dimensional span is non-degenerate, and
$$
V=\mathbb C u\oplus u^\perp,
\qquad
u^\perp=\{x:f(x,u)=0\}.
$$
To see the decomposition explicitly, for $x\in V$ put
$$
a=\frac{f(x,u)}{f(u,u)}.
$$
Then $x-au\in u^\perp$, while the intersection
$\mathbb C u\cap u^\perp$ is zero. Repeating this construction on the
orthogonal complement yields a basis in which
$$
[f]=\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_r,0,\ldots,0),
$$
where each $c_i\ne0$ and $r=\operatorname{rank}f$.

Because the field is $\mathbb C$, choose $s_i\in\mathbb C$ with
$$
s_i^2=c_i.
$$
Rescaling the corresponding basis vector by $1/s_i$ changes the $i$th diagonal
entry from $c_i$ to $1$. Hence there is a basis in which
$$
\boxed{[f]=\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0).}
$$
:::

<1>2. Complex symmetric forms are congruent if and only if they have the same rank.
::: proof
Congruence by an invertible operator preserves rank because, in matrices, it
has the form
$$
B=P^tAP
$$
with $P$ invertible.

Conversely, let $f$ and $g$ be complex symmetric forms of the same rank $r$.
By step <1>1, choose bases $\mathfrak B_f$ and $\mathfrak B_g$ in which both
forms have matrix
$$
\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0).
$$
Define $T$ to map the $g$-basis to the $f$-basis. Then $T$ is invertible and,
for all $\alpha,\beta$,
$$
f(T\alpha,T\beta)=g(\alpha,\beta).
$$
Thus the analogue of Exercise 12 is valid over $\mathbb C$.
:::

<1>3. Rank does not classify real symmetric bilinear forms up to congruence.
::: proof
On the one-dimensional real vector space $\mathbb R$, consider
$$
f(x,y)=xy,
\qquad
g(x,y)=-xy.
$$
Both forms have rank $1$.

Every invertible real linear operator has the form
$$
T(x)=tx
$$
for some $t\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$. Then
$$
f(Tx,Ty)=t^2xy.
$$
If this were equal to $g(x,y)=-xy$ for all $x,y$, we would have
$$
t^2=-1,
$$
which has no real solution. Hence $f$ and $g$ have the same rank but are not
congruent over $\mathbb R$.

Therefore the rank-only classification fails over the reals; the signs of the
nonzero diagonal terms carry additional information.
:::
:::
