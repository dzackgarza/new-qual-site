::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space and $U$ a unitary operator on $V$ such that $U\alpha = \alpha$ implies $\alpha = 0$.
Let
$$
f(z) = i\frac{(1 + z)}{(1 - z)}, \quad z \neq 1
$$
and show that

(a) $f(U) = i(I + U)(I - U)^{-1}$;

(b) $f(U)$ is self-adjoint;

(c) for every self-adjoint operator $T$ on $V$, the operator
$$
U = (T - iI)(T + iI)^{-1}
$$
is unitary and such that $T = f(U)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
(a) $I-U$ is invertible and $f(U)=i(I+U)(I-U)^{-1}$.

::: pf-proof
The hypothesis says $\ker(I-U)=\{0\}$, so $I-U$ is invertible on the finite-dimensional space $V$. The function $f$ is defined on the spectrum of the normal operator $U$, which omits $1$, and $f(U)$ is the operator obtained by substituting $U$ for $z$. Since $I+U$ and $(I-U)^{-1}$ commute, this is $i(I+U)(I-U)^{-1}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
(b) $f(U)$ is self-adjoint.

::: pf-proof
Because $U^*=U^{-1}$,
$$
I+U^*=U^{-1}(U+I),
\qquad
I-U^*=U^{-1}(U-I)=-U^{-1}(I-U).
$$
Hence $(I-U^*)^{-1}=-(I-U)^{-1}U$, and
$$
f(U)^*
=-i\,(I-U^*)^{-1}(I+U^*)
=i\,(I-U)^{-1}UU^{-1}(I+U)
=i\,(I-U)^{-1}(I+U)
=f(U),
$$
the last equality because $(I-U)^{-1}$ commutes with $I+U$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
(c) For self-adjoint $T$, $U=(T-iI)(T+iI)^{-1}$ is unitary and $f(U)=T$.

::: pf-proof
The characteristic values of $T$ are real, so $T\pm iI$ are invertible, and all of $T$, $T\pm iI$ and their inverses commute. Since $(T+iI)^*=T-iI$,
$$
U^*U=(T-iI)^{-1}(T+iI)(T-iI)(T+iI)^{-1}=I,
$$
so $U$ is unitary. Next,
$$
I-U=\bigl((T+iI)-(T-iI)\bigr)(T+iI)^{-1}=2i\,(T+iI)^{-1},
$$
which is invertible, so $U\alpha=\alpha$ implies $\alpha=0$ and (a) applies. Also
$$
I+U=2T\,(T+iI)^{-1}.
$$
Therefore
$$
f(U)=i\cdot 2T(T+iI)^{-1}\cdot\frac{1}{2i}(T+iI)=T.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
The three steps prove (a), (b) and (c).
:::
