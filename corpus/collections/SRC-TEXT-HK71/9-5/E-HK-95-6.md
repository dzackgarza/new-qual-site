::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space and $U$ a unitary operator on $V$ such that $U\alpha = \alpha$ implies $\alpha = 0$.
Let
\[
f(z) = i\frac{(1 + z)}{(1 - z)}, \quad z \neq 1
\]
and show that

(a) $f(U) = i(I + U)(I - U)^{-1}$;

(b) $f(U)$ is self-adjoint;

(c) for every self-adjoint operator $T$ on $V$, the operator
\[
U = (T - iI)(T + iI)^{-1}
\]
is unitary and such that $T = f(U)$.
:::

::: solution
**Theorem.**  
For the map $f(z)=i(1+z)(1-z)^{-1}$, the identities in (a),(b),(c) hold.

1. For unitary $U$ with no fixed vector at eigenvalue $1$, $I-U$ is invertible, so $f(U)=i(I+U)(I-U)^{-1}$ is well-defined.
   This is exactly part (a).

2. Since $U^*=U^{-1}$ and $I$ commutes with $U$,
   \[
   f(U)^*=-i(I-U)^{-1}(I+U)=i(I+U)(I-U)^{-1}=f(U),
   \]
   so $f(U)$ is self-adjoint.

3. Let $T$ be self-adjoint and define $U=(T-iI)(T+iI)^{-1}$.
   Because $T+iI$ is invertible, this is a valid operator.
4. We compute
   \[
   U^*=(T+iI)^{-1}(T-iI)=U^{-1},
   \]
   hence $U$ is unitary.
5. Also
   \[
   I+U=\frac{2T}{T+iI},\qquad
   I-U=\frac{2iI}{T+iI}.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   i(I+U)(I-U)^{-1}=i\left(\frac{2T}{T+iI}\right)\left(\frac{2iI}{T+iI}\right)^{-1}=T.
   \]

Hence $T=f(U)$, proving part (c).
:::
