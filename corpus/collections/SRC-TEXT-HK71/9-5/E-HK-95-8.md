::: {.exercise}
If $B$ is an arbitrary member of the inner product space in Exercise 7, show that $L_B$ is unitarily equivalent to $R_{B^t}$.
:::

::: solution
Define
$$
U:V\longrightarrow V,
\qquad
U(A)=A^t.
$$

<1>1. The transpose map $U$ is unitary.
::: proof
The matrix units $E_{ij}$ form an orthonormal basis of $V$ for the inner
product
$$
(A\mid C)=\operatorname{tr}(AC^*).
$$
Since
$$
U(E_{ij})=E_{ji},
$$
$U$ merely permutes this orthonormal basis. Hence it preserves the inner
product and is unitary. Also
$$
U^2=I,
$$
so $U^{-1}=U$.
:::

<1>2. Conjugating $L_B$ by $U$ gives $R_{B^t}$.
::: proof
For every $A\in V$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(UL_BU^{-1})(A)
&=U\bigl(B A^t\bigr)\\
&=(BA^t)^t\\
&=AB^t\\
&=R_{B^t}(A).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{UL_BU^{-1}=R_{B^t}.}
$$
Since $U$ is unitary, $L_B$ and $R_{B^t}$ are unitarily equivalent.
:::
:::
