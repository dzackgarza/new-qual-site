::: {.exercise}
Let $\mathfrak{F}$ be a commuting family of diagonalizable normal operators on a finite-dimensional inner product space $V$ and $\mathfrak{A}$ the self-adjoint algebra generated by $\mathfrak{F}$.
Let $\mathfrak{Q}$ be the self-adjoint algebra generated by $\mathfrak{F}$ and the identity operator $I$.
Show that

(a) $\mathfrak{Q}$ is the set of all operators on $V$ of the form $cI + T$ where $c$ is a scalar and $T$ an operator in $\mathfrak{A}$;

(b) There is at most one root $r$ of $\mathfrak{Q}$ such that $r(T) = 0$ for all $T$ in $\mathfrak{A}$.

(c) If one of the roots of $\mathfrak{Q}$ is 0 on $\mathfrak{A}$, the projections $P_1, \ldots, P_k$ in the resolution of the identity defined by $\mathfrak{F}$ may be indexed in such a way that $\mathfrak{A}$ consists of all operators on $V$ of the form
\[
T = \sum_{j=2}^{k} c_jP_j
\]
where $c_2, \ldots, c_k$ are arbitrary scalars.

(d) $\mathfrak{Q} = \mathfrak{A}$ if and only if for each root $r$ of $\mathfrak{Q}$ there exists an operator $T$ in $\mathfrak{A}$ such that $r(T) \neq 0$.
:::

::: solution
Let $r_1,\ldots,r_k$ be the roots of $\mathfrak Q$, and let
$P_1,\ldots,P_k$ be the corresponding projections in the joint spectral
resolution. Thus
$$
I=P_1+\cdots+P_k,
$$
the $P_j$ are mutually orthogonal self-adjoint projections, and every
$S\in\mathfrak Q$ has the spectral expansion
$$
S=\sum_{j=1}^k r_j(S)P_j
$$
[@HK71].

<1>1. Prove part (a): $\mathfrak Q=\mathbb F I+\mathfrak A$.
::: proof
Let
$$
\mathfrak B=\{cI+T:c\in\mathbb F,\ T\in\mathfrak A\}.
$$
Since $\mathfrak A$ is an algebra,
$$
(cI+T)(dI+U)=cdI+cU+dT+TU\in\mathfrak B.
$$
It is plainly a vector space, and because $\mathfrak A$ is self-adjoint,
$$
(cI+T)^*=\overline c I+T^*\in\mathfrak B.
$$
Thus $\mathfrak B$ is a self-adjoint algebra containing $\mathfrak F$ and
$I$. By minimality of $\mathfrak Q$,
$$
\mathfrak Q\subseteq\mathfrak B.
$$
Conversely, $I\in\mathfrak Q$ and
$\mathfrak A\subseteq\mathfrak Q$, so $\mathfrak B\subseteq\mathfrak Q$.
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathfrak Q=\{cI+T:c\in\mathbb F,\ T\in\mathfrak A\}.}
$$
:::

<1>2. The algebra $\mathfrak A$ is an ideal in $\mathfrak Q$.
::: proof
Let $S=cI+T\in\mathfrak Q$ as in step <1>1 and let $U\in\mathfrak A$.
Then
$$
SU=cU+TU\in\mathfrak A,
$$
because $\mathfrak A$ is an algebra. Since $\mathfrak Q$ is commutative,
also $US\in\mathfrak A$. Thus $\mathfrak A$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak Q$.
:::

<1>3. A spectral projection $P_j$ belongs to $\mathfrak A$ exactly when the root $r_j$ is nonzero somewhere on $\mathfrak A$.
::: proof
If $P_j\in\mathfrak A$, then
$$
r_j(P_j)=1,
$$
so $r_j$ is certainly nonzero on $\mathfrak A$.

Conversely, suppose there is $T\in\mathfrak A$ with
$$
r_j(T)\ne0.
$$
Since $P_j\in\mathfrak Q$ and $\mathfrak A$ is an ideal by step <1>2,
$$
P_jT\in\mathfrak A.
$$
But the spectral expansion gives
$$
P_jT=r_j(T)P_j.
$$
Because $r_j(T)\ne0$ and $\mathfrak A$ is a vector space,
$$
P_j\in\mathfrak A.
$$
:::

<1>4. Prove part (b): at most one root of $\mathfrak Q$ can vanish identically on $\mathfrak A$.
::: proof
Suppose roots $r$ and $s$ both satisfy
$$
r(T)=s(T)=0
\qquad(T\in\mathfrak A).
$$
Every root of $\mathfrak Q$ takes the value $1$ on $I$. Hence, using part
(a), for every $cI+T\in\mathfrak Q$,
$$
r(cI+T)=c=s(cI+T).
$$
Therefore $r=s$. So there is at most one such root.
:::

<1>5. Prove part (c).
::: proof
Assume one root of $\mathfrak Q$ vanishes on $\mathfrak A$ and index it as
$r_1$. Then
$$
r_1(T)=0
\qquad(T\in\mathfrak A).
$$
By part (b), no root $r_j$ with $j\ge2$ vanishes identically on
$\mathfrak A$. Hence for every $j\ge2$ there exists $T_j\in\mathfrak A$ with
$$
r_j(T_j)\ne0.
$$
Step <1>3 then gives
$$
P_2,\ldots,P_k\in\mathfrak A.
$$

On the other hand, if $T\in\mathfrak A$, its spectral expansion is
$$
T=\sum_{j=1}^k r_j(T)P_j,
$$
and the coefficient of $P_1$ is zero. Thus
$$
T\in\operatorname{span}\{P_2,\ldots,P_k\}.
$$
The reverse inclusion holds because each $P_j$, $j\ge2$, belongs to
$\mathfrak A$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\mathfrak A
=\left\{\sum_{j=2}^k c_jP_j:c_2,\ldots,c_k\text{ arbitrary}\right\}.}
$$
:::

<1>6. Prove the forward implication in part (d).
::: proof
If $\mathfrak Q=\mathfrak A$, then $I\in\mathfrak A$. For every root $r$ of
$\mathfrak Q$,
$$
r(I)=1\ne0.
$$
Thus the required operator may simply be chosen to be $T=I$.
:::

<1>7. Prove the reverse implication in part (d).
::: proof
Assume that for every root $r_j$ of $\mathfrak Q$ there is some
$T_j\in\mathfrak A$ with
$$
r_j(T_j)\ne0.
$$
By step <1>3,
$$
P_j\in\mathfrak A
\qquad(1\le j\le k).
$$
Hence
$$
I=P_1+\cdots+P_k\in\mathfrak A.
$$
Part (a) now yields
$$
\mathfrak Q=\mathbb F I+\mathfrak A=\mathfrak A.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\mathfrak Q=\mathfrak A
\iff
\text{every root of $\mathfrak Q$ is nonzero on some element of $\mathfrak A$}.}
$$
:::
:::
