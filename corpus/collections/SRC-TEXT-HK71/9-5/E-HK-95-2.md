::: {.exercise}
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with complex entries such that $A^* = -A$, and let $B = e^A$.
Show that

(a) $\det B = e^{\operatorname{tr} A}$;

(b) $B^* = e^{-A}$;

(c) $B$ is unitary.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

A skew-adjoint matrix is unitarily diagonalizable with purely imaginary characteristic values.

::: pf-proof

Since
$$
A^*=-A,
$$
one has
$$
AA^*=A(-A)=-A^2=A^*A,
$$
so $A$ is normal. By the spectral theorem there is a unitary matrix $U$ and
scalars $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$ such that
$$
A=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)U^*.
$$
If $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\ne0$, then
$$
\lambda\|v\|^2=(Av\mid v)
=-(v\mid Av)
=-\overline\lambda\|v\|^2,
$$
so $\lambda=-\overline\lambda$. Thus every $\lambda_j$ is purely imaginary.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Prove $\det(e^A)=e^{\operatorname{tr}A}$.

::: pf-proof

The matrix exponential respects similarity, and for a diagonal matrix it is
computed entrywise. Hence step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
e^A
=U\operatorname{diag}(e^{\lambda_1},\ldots,e^{\lambda_n})U^*.
$$
Therefore
$$
\det(e^A)
=\prod_{j=1}^n e^{\lambda_j}
=e^{\sum_j\lambda_j}.
$$
But
$$
\operatorname{tr}A=\sum_j\lambda_j,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{\det B=e^{\operatorname{tr}A}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Prove $B^*=e^{-A}$.

::: pf-proof

From the absolutely convergent power series
$$
e^A=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{A^k}{k!}
$$
and continuity of adjoint in finite dimensions,
$$
\begin{aligned}
B^*
&=\left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{A^k}{k!}\right)^*\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(A^*)^k}{k!}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(-A)^k}{k!}\\
&=e^{-A}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{B^*=e^{-A}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Conclude that $B$ is unitary.

::: pf-proof

Because $A$ commutes with $-A$,
$$
e^{-A}e^A=e^0=I.
$$
Using step [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
B^*B=e^{-A}e^A=I.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{B\text{ is unitary}.}
$$

:::
