::: {.exercise}
Let $\mathfrak{F}$ be any family of linear operators on a finite-dimensional inner product space $V$ and $\mathfrak{A}$ the self-adjoint algebra generated by $\mathfrak{F}$.
Show that

(a) each root of $\mathfrak{A}$ defines a root of $\mathfrak{F}$;

(b) each root $r$ of $\mathfrak{A}$ is a multiplicative linear function on $\mathfrak{A}$, i.e.,
\[
r(TU) = r(T)r(U), \qquad r(cT + U) = cr(T) + r(U)
\]
for all $T$ and $U$ in $\mathfrak{A}$ and all scalars $c$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Recall that a root $r$ of a family $\mathfrak G$ is a function assigning to
each $T\in\mathfrak G$ a scalar $r(T)$ for which there exists a nonzero vector
$\alpha$ satisfying
$$
T\alpha=r(T)\alpha
\qquad\text{for every }T\in\mathfrak G.
$$
Such an $\alpha$ is a common characteristic vector belonging to the root.

<1>1. A root of $\mathfrak A$ restricts to a root of $\mathfrak F$.
::: {.proof}
Let $r$ be a root of $\mathfrak A$ and choose a nonzero vector $\alpha$ such
that
$$
T\alpha=r(T)\alpha
$$
for every $T\in\mathfrak A$. Since
$$
\mathfrak F\subseteq\mathfrak A,
$$
the same vector satisfies this identity for every $T\in\mathfrak F$.
Therefore the restriction
$$
r|_{\mathfrak F}
$$
is a root of $\mathfrak F$.
:::

<1>2. Every root of $\mathfrak A$ is multiplicative.
::: {.proof}
Let $T,U\in\mathfrak A$. Acting on the common characteristic vector
$\alpha$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
(TU)\alpha
&=T(U\alpha)\\
&=T(r(U)\alpha)\\
&=r(U)r(T)\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
But $TU\in\mathfrak A$, so also
$$
(TU)\alpha=r(TU)\alpha.
$$
Since $\alpha\ne0$,
$$
\boxed{r(TU)=r(T)r(U)}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Every root of $\mathfrak A$ is linear.
::: {.proof}
For any scalar $c$ and $T,U\in\mathfrak A$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(cT+U)\alpha
&=cT\alpha+U\alpha\\
&=(cr(T)+r(U))\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because $cT+U\in\mathfrak A$, the left side is also
$$
r(cT+U)\alpha.
$$
Again $\alpha\ne0$, hence
$$
\boxed{r(cT+U)=cr(T)+r(U)}.
$$
Together with step <1>2, this proves that every root of $\mathfrak A$ is a
multiplicative linear function.
:::
:::
