::: {.exercise}
If $U$ and $T$ are normal operators which commute, prove that $U + T$ and $UT$ are normal.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The four operators $U,T,U^*,T^*$ commute pairwise.
::: {.proof}
Because $T$ is normal on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space,
its adjoint is a polynomial in $T$: there is a polynomial $f$ such that
$$
T^*=f(T)
$$
[@HK71]. Since $UT=TU$, the operator $U$ commutes with every polynomial in
$T$, hence
$$
UT^*=T^*U.
$$
Similarly, because $U$ is normal, $U^*$ is a polynomial in $U$, so
$$
U^*T=TU^*.
$$
Taking adjoints of either of these identities also gives
$$
U^*T^*=T^*U^*.
$$
Normality itself supplies
$$
UU^*=U^*U,
\qquad
TT^*=T^*T.
$$
Thus the four operators commute pairwise.
:::

<1>2. The sum $U+T$ is normal.
::: {.proof}
Using step <1>1,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(U+T)(U+T)^*
&=(U+T)(U^*+T^*)\\
&=UU^*+UT^*+TU^*+TT^*\\
&=U^*U+T^*U+U^*T+T^*T\\
&=(U^*+T^*)(U+T)\\
&=(U+T)^*(U+T).
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence $U+T$ is normal.
:::

<1>3. The product $UT$ is normal.
::: {.proof}
Again using pairwise commutativity,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(UT)(UT)^*
&=UTT^*U^*\\
&=UU^*TT^*\\
&=U^*UT^*T\\
&=T^*U^*UT\\
&=(UT)^*(UT).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{U+T\text{ and }UT\text{ are normal}.}
$$
:::
