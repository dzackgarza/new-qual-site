::: {.exercise}
Use Exercise 3 to show that any commuting family of normal operators (not necessarily diagonalizable ones) on a finite-dimensional inner product space generates a commutative self-adjoint algebra of normal operators.
:::

::: solution
Let $\mathcal F$ be a commuting family of normal operators on $V$, and let
$\mathcal A$ be the algebra it generates.

<1>1. Every adjoint of a generator belongs to the algebra generated by that generator.
::: proof
First suppose the scalar field is $\mathbb C$. If $T\in\mathcal F$ is normal,
the spectral theorem gives its distinct characteristic values
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$. By Lagrange interpolation there is a polynomial
$p\in\mathbb C[x]$ such that
$$
p(\lambda_j)=\overline{\lambda_j}
$$
for every $j$. Since $T^*$ acts by $\overline{\lambda_j}$ on the
$\lambda_j$-eigenspace,
$$
T^*=p(T).
$$

Now suppose the scalar field is $\mathbb R$. Complexify $V$ and $T$. The
complexified operator $T_{\mathbb C}$ is normal, so it is unitarily
diagonalizable, and its finite set of characteristic values is stable under
complex conjugation. Interpolate a polynomial $p\in\mathbb C[x]$ satisfying
$$
p(\lambda)=\overline\lambda
$$
on that set. Replacing $p$ by
$$
q(x)=\frac{p(x)+\overline{p(\overline x)}}2
$$
produces a polynomial with real coefficients and the same prescribed values:
for every characteristic value $\lambda$,
$$
q(\lambda)
=\frac{\overline\lambda+\overline{p(\overline\lambda)}}2
=\frac{\overline\lambda+\overline\lambda}2
=\overline\lambda.
$$
Thus
$$
T_{\mathbb C}^*=q(T_{\mathbb C}),
$$
and restricting to the original real space gives
$$
T^*=q(T).
$$

Therefore, over either scalar field, $T^*$ belongs to the algebra generated
by $T$, and hence to $\mathcal A$.
:::

<1>2. The algebra $\mathcal A$ is self-adjoint and commutative.
::: proof
An element of $\mathcal A$ is a finite linear combination of monomials in
members of $\mathcal F$. Because the generators commute, all such monomials
commute, so $\mathcal A$ is commutative.

By step <1>1, the adjoint of each generator is in $\mathcal A$. Taking the
adjoint of a monomial reverses its factors, but the factors and their adjoints
all belong to the same commutative algebra. Hence the adjoint of every
monomial is in $\mathcal A$, and by conjugate-linearity the adjoint of every
finite linear combination is also in $\mathcal A$. Therefore
$$
\mathcal A^*=\mathcal A,
$$
so $\mathcal A$ is self-adjoint.
:::

<1>3. Every element of $\mathcal A$ is normal.
::: proof
Every generator is normal by hypothesis. Exercise 3 shows that the product of
two commuting normal operators is normal, so by induction every monomial in
the commuting generators is normal. Any two such monomials commute.

Exercise 3 also shows that the sum of two commuting normal operators is
normal. Scalar multiples of normal operators are normal. Hence, by induction
on the number of terms, every finite linear combination of the commuting
normal monomials is normal. These finite linear combinations are precisely
the elements of $\mathcal A$.

Thus
$$
\boxed{\mathcal A\text{ is a commutative self-adjoint algebra consisting of
normal operators}.}
$$
:::
:::
