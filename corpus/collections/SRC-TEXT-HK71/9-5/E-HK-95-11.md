::: {.exercise}
Let $\mathfrak{F}$ be a commuting family of diagonalizable normal operators on a finite-dimensional inner product space $V$; and let $\mathfrak{Q}$ be the self-adjoint algebra generated by $\mathfrak{F}$ and the identity operator $I$.
Show that each root of $\mathfrak{Q}$ is different from 0, and that for each root $r$ of $\mathfrak{F}$ there is a unique root $s$ of $\mathfrak{Q}$ such that $s(T) = r(T)$ for all $T$ in $\mathfrak{F}$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. No root of $\mathfrak Q$ is the zero function.
::: proof
Let $s$ be a root of $\mathfrak Q$, and let $\alpha\ne0$ be a common
characteristic vector belonging to $s$. Since the identity operator belongs to
$\mathfrak Q$,
$$
I\alpha=s(I)\alpha.
$$
But $I\alpha=\alpha$, so
$$
s(I)=1.
$$
Hence $s$ is not the zero function.
:::

<1>2. A root of $\mathfrak F$ extends to a root of $\mathfrak Q$.
::: proof
Let $r$ be a root of $\mathfrak F$. Choose a nonzero vector $\alpha$ such
that
$$
T\alpha=r(T)\alpha
\qquad(T\in\mathfrak F).
$$
Because every $T\in\mathfrak F$ is normal, a characteristic vector for $T$
with characteristic value $r(T)$ is also a characteristic vector for $T^*$
with characteristic value $\overline{r(T)}$. Thus
$$
T^*\alpha=\overline{r(T)}\alpha
\qquad(T\in\mathfrak F).
$$

The self-adjoint algebra $\mathfrak Q$ is generated by the operators in
$\mathfrak F$, their adjoints, and $I$. Every finite product of these generators
therefore sends $\alpha$ to a scalar multiple of $\alpha$, and so does every
finite linear combination of such products. Hence for every
$A\in\mathfrak Q$ there is a scalar $s(A)$ such that
$$
A\alpha=s(A)\alpha.
$$
Because $\alpha\ne0$, this scalar is unique. Therefore $s$ is a root of
$\mathfrak Q$. For $T\in\mathfrak F$ the defining equation gives
$$
s(T)=r(T),
$$
so $s$ extends $r$.
:::

<1>3. The extension is unique.
::: proof
Suppose $s_1$ and $s_2$ are roots of $\mathfrak Q$ whose restrictions to
$\mathfrak F$ both equal $r$. By Exercise 10, each $s_j$ is a multiplicative
linear function on $\mathfrak Q$.

For every $T\in\mathfrak F$, normality and the root relation imply
$$
s_j(T^*)=\overline{s_j(T)}=\overline{r(T)}.
$$
Also, by step <1>1,
$$
s_j(I)=1.
$$
Thus $s_1$ and $s_2$ agree on every generator of the self-adjoint algebra
$\mathfrak Q$: they agree on $\mathfrak F$, on all adjoints of elements of
$\mathfrak F$, and on $I$. Since both functions are multiplicative and linear,
they agree on every finite linear combination of finite products of these
generators. Those are exactly the elements of $\mathfrak Q$.

Therefore
$$
\boxed{s_1=s_2},
$$
and the extension of $r$ is unique.
:::
:::
