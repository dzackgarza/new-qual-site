::: {.exercise}
Let $T$ be a linear operator on the finite-dimensional complex inner product space $V$.
Prove that the following ten statements about $T$ are equivalent.

(a) $T$ is normal.

(b) $||T\alpha|| = ||T^*\alpha||$ for every $\alpha$ in $V$.

(c) $T = T_1 + iT_2$, where $T_1$ and $T_2$ are self-adjoint and $T_1T_2 = T_2T_1$.

(d) If $\alpha$ is a vector and $c$ a scalar such that $T\alpha = c\alpha$, then $T^*\alpha = \overline{c}\alpha$.

(e) There is an orthonormal basis for $V$ consisting of characteristic vectors for $T$.

(f) There is an orthonormal basis $\mathfrak{B}$ such that $[T]_{\mathfrak{B}}$ is diagonal.

(g) There is a polynomial $g$ with complex coefficients such that $T^* = g(T)$.

(h) Every subspace which is invariant under $T$ is also invariant under $T^*$.

(i) $T = NU$, where $N$ is non-negative, $U$ is unitary, and $N$ commutes with $U$.

(j) $T = c_1E_1 + \cdots + c_kE_k$, where $I = E_1 + \cdots + E_k$, $E_iE_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$, and $E_i^2 = E_i = E_i^*$.
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove that each condition is equivalent to (a).

<1>1. Conditions (a) and (b) are equivalent.
::: {.proof}
If $T$ is normal, then for every $\alpha$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|T\alpha\|^2
&=(\alpha\mid T^*T\alpha)\\
&=(\alpha\mid TT^*\alpha)\\
&=\|T^*\alpha\|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus (a) implies (b).

Conversely assume (b) and put
$$
H=T^*T-TT^*.
$$
The operator $H$ is self-adjoint, and the norm identity says
$$
(\alpha\mid H\alpha)=0
$$
for every $\alpha$. By the polarization identity for Hermitian forms, the
sesquilinear form
$$
(\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto(\alpha\mid H\beta)
$$
is identically zero. Hence $H=0$, so $T^*T=TT^*$ and $T$ is normal.
:::

<1>2. Conditions (a) and (c) are equivalent.
::: {.proof}
For every operator define
$$
T_1=\frac{T+T^*}{2},
\qquad
T_2=\frac{T-T^*}{2i}.
$$
Then $T_1,T_2$ are self-adjoint and
$$
T=T_1+iT_2.
$$
A direct calculation gives
$$
T_1T_2-T_2T_1
=\frac{1}{2i}(T^*T-TT^*).
$$
Thus $T$ is normal exactly when $T_1$ and $T_2$ commute, proving
(a)$\iff$(c).
:::

<1>3. Condition (a) implies (e), and (e) is equivalent to (f).
::: {.proof}
The finite-dimensional spectral theorem says precisely that a normal operator
on a complex inner-product space has an orthonormal basis of characteristic
vectors. Hence (a) implies (e) [@HK71].

Given an ordered orthonormal basis, its vectors are characteristic vectors for
$T$ exactly when the matrix of $T$ in that basis is diagonal. Thus (e) and
(f) are equivalent. Conversely, a diagonal matrix commutes with its adjoint,
so (f) implies (a).
:::

<1>4. Condition (a) implies (d).
::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, decompose $V$ as the orthogonal direct sum of the eigenspaces
of $T$. On the eigenspace $E_c=\ker(T-cI)$, the adjoint is multiplication by
$\overline c$. Therefore
$$
T\alpha=c\alpha
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T^*\alpha=\overline c\,\alpha.
$$
Thus (d) holds.
:::

<1>5. Condition (d) implies (e), hence implies normality.
::: {.proof}
We argue by induction on $\dim V$. The claim is trivial in dimension zero.
Since the scalar field is $\mathbb C$, a nonzero finite-dimensional space has
a characteristic vector $\alpha$ for $T$; normalize it so that
$\|\alpha\|=1$, and write
$$
T\alpha=c\alpha.
$$
By (d),
$$
T^*\alpha=\overline c\,\alpha.
$$

Let $W=\alpha^\perp$. If $x\in W$, then
$$
(Tx\mid\alpha)=(x\mid T^*\alpha)=0,
$$
so $W$ is $T$-invariant. It is also $T^*$-invariant, since
$$
(T^*x\mid\alpha)=(x\mid T\alpha)=0.
$$
The restriction $T|_W$ again satisfies (d): every characteristic vector of
$T|_W$ is one for $T$, and the corresponding adjoint action remains inside
$W$. By induction, $W$ has an orthonormal basis of characteristic vectors for
$T|_W$. Adjoining $\alpha$ gives an orthonormal basis of characteristic
vectors for $T$. Thus (e), and hence (a), holds.
:::

<1>6. Conditions (a) and (g) are equivalent.
::: {.proof}
Assume $T$ is normal and let
$c_1,\ldots,c_r$ be its distinct characteristic values. By Lagrange
interpolation there is a polynomial $g\in\mathbb C[x]$ such that
$$
g(c_j)=\overline{c_j}
\qquad(1\le j\le r).
$$
On each eigenspace $E_{c_j}$, the operator $g(T)$ acts by
$\overline{c_j}$, which is exactly the action of $T^*$. Hence
$$
T^*=g(T).
$$
Thus (a) implies (g).

Conversely, if $T^*=g(T)$, then $T$ commutes with $T^*$ because every
operator commutes with every polynomial in itself. Therefore $T$ is normal.
:::

<1>7. Conditions (a) and (h) are equivalent.
::: {.proof}
If $T$ is normal, step <1>6 gives $T^*=g(T)$. Every $T$-invariant subspace
is invariant under every polynomial in $T$, hence under $T^*$. Thus (a)
implies (h).

Conversely assume (h). Let $T\alpha=c\alpha$ with $\alpha\ne0$. The line
$L=\mathbb C\alpha$ is $T$-invariant, hence by (h) it is $T^*$-invariant.
So
$$
T^*\alpha=d\alpha
$$
for some $d$. The adjoint identity gives
$$
c\|\alpha\|^2
=(T\alpha\mid\alpha)
=(\alpha\mid T^*\alpha)
=\overline d\,\|\alpha\|^2,
$$
and therefore $d=\overline c$. Thus (d) holds, and step <1>5 implies that
$T$ is normal.
:::

<1>8. Condition (a) implies (i).
::: {.proof}
Let
$$
T=\sum_{j=1}^r c_jE_j
$$
be the spectral resolution of the normal operator $T$, where the $E_j$ are
the mutually orthogonal self-adjoint projections onto its distinct
eigenspaces. Define
$$
N=\sum_{j=1}^r |c_j|E_j
$$
and choose
$$
u_j=
\begin{cases}
c_j/|c_j|,&c_j\ne0,\\
1,&c_j=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
U=\sum_{j=1}^r u_jE_j.
$$
Then $N$ is non-negative, $U$ is unitary, and both are linear combinations
of the same mutually orthogonal projections, so they commute. Moreover
$$
NU=\sum_j |c_j|u_jE_j
=\sum_j c_jE_j
=T.
$$
Hence (i) holds.
:::

<1>9. Condition (i) implies normality.
::: {.proof}
Assume
$$
T=NU,
$$
where $N=N^*$ is non-negative, $U$ is unitary, and $NU=UN$. Since $U$ is
invertible, $N$ also commutes with $U^*$. Therefore
$$
T^*=U^*N,
$$
and
$$
TT^*=NUU^*N=N^2,
$$
while
$$
T^*T=U^*N^2U=N^2.
$$
Thus $T$ is normal.
:::

<1>10. Conditions (a) and (j) are equivalent.
::: {.proof}
If $T$ is normal, take the distinct characteristic values
$c_1,\ldots,c_k$ and let $E_i$ be the orthogonal projection onto the
$c_i$-eigenspace. The spectral theorem gives
$$
I=E_1+\cdots+E_k,
\qquad
E_iE_j=0\quad(i\ne j),
$$
with
$$
E_i^2=E_i=E_i^*,
$$
and
$$
T=c_1E_1+\cdots+c_kE_k.
$$
Thus (j) holds.

Conversely, from (j),
$$
T^*=\overline{c_1}E_1+\cdots+\overline{c_k}E_k.
$$
Using the orthogonality of the projections,
$$
TT^*=\sum_i |c_i|^2E_i=T^*T.
$$
Hence $T$ is normal.
:::

Since each of (b)--(j) is equivalent to (a), all ten statements are
equivalent.
:::
