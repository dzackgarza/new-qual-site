::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the inner product space in Exercise 7 and $G$ the group of unitary matrices in $V$.
If $B$ is in $G$, let $C_B$ denote the linear operator on $V$ defined by
\[
C_B(A) = BAB^{-1}.
\]
Show that

(a) $C_B$ is a unitary operator on $V$;

(b) $C_{B_1B_2} = C_{B_1}C_{B_2}$;

(c) there is no unitary transformation $U$ on $V$ such that
\[
UL_BU^{-1} = C_B
\]
for all $B$ in $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** Prove the three facts about the conjugation operators $C_B$.

<1>1. (a) $C_B$ is unitary.
<2>1. $C_B(A) = BAB^{-1} = BAB^*$ (since $B$ is unitary, $B^{-1} = B^*$).
Proof: $B$ is unitary.
<2>2. $\|C_B(A)\| = \|BAB^*\| = \|A\|$.
Proof: the Frobenius (Hilbert–Schmidt) norm is invariant under left and right multiplication by unitaries: $\|BAB^*\|^2 = \operatorname{tr}((BAB^*)^*(BAB^*)) = \operatorname{tr}(BA^*B^*BAB^*) = \operatorname{tr}(BA^*AB^*) = \operatorname{tr}(A^*A) = \|A\|^2$.
<2>3. Hence $C_B$ is an isometry, so it is unitary.
Proof: a linear isometry on a finite-dimensional inner product space is unitary.

<1>2. (b) $C_{B_1 B_2} = C_{B_1} C_{B_2}$.
<2>1. $C_{B_1 B_2}(A) = (B_1 B_2) A (B_1 B_2)^{-1} = B_1 B_2 A B_2^{-1} B_1^{-1}$.
Proof: definition.
<2>2. $C_{B_1}(C_{B_2}(A)) = C_{B_1}(B_2 A B_2^{-1}) = B_1 B_2 A B_2^{-1} B_1^{-1}$.
Proof: apply $C_{B_2}$ then $C_{B_1}$.
<2>3. Hence $C_{B_1 B_2} = C_{B_1} C_{B_2}$.
Proof: <1>2.1 and <1>2.2 agree.

<1>3. (c) No unitary $U$ satisfies $U L_B U^{-1} = C_B$ for all $B$.
<2>1. $L_B$ is left multiplication by $B$: $L_B(A) = BA$.
Proof: definition (from Exercise 7).
<2>2. $L_B$ and $C_B$ are not conjugate by a single $U$ for all $B$.
Proof: the map $B \mapsto L_B$ is a representation of $G$ (left regular), while $B \mapsto C_B$ is the conjugation representation; these are not equivalent as representations of $G$ (the left regular representation and the conjugation representation have different characters — e.g. the trace of $L_B$ is $n \operatorname{tr}(B)$ while the trace of $C_B$ is $|\operatorname{tr}(B)|^2$).
<2>3. Hence no such $U$ exists.
Proof: if $U L_B U^{-1} = C_B$ for all $B$, then $L$ and $C$ would be equivalent representations, contradicting <1>3.2.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1, <1>2, <1>3 prove (a), (b), (c).
:::
