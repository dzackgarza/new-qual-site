::: {.exercise}
Let $V$ be the space of complex $n \times n$ matrices equipped with the inner product
\[
(A \mid B) = \operatorname{tr}(AB^*).
\]
If $B$ is an element of $V$, let $L_B$, $R_B$, and $T_B$ denote the linear operators on $V$ defined by

(a) $L_B(A) = BA$.

(b) $R_B(A) = AB$.

(c) $T_B(A) = BA - AB$.

Consider the three families of operators obtained by letting $B$ vary over all diagonal matrices.
For the first two families, show that each is a commutative self-adjoint algebra and find its spectral resolution.
For the commutator family, determine what fails in the printed algebra assertion, then show that the family is commuting and self-adjoint, that the algebra it generates is commutative and self-adjoint, and find its joint spectral resolution.
:::

::: remark
The printed exercise says that all three raw families are algebras. The third
assertion is false. For example, when
$$
B=\operatorname{diag}(0,1),
$$
the operator $T_B^2$ acts as $+1$ on both $E_{12}$ and $E_{21}$, whereas every
$T_D$ with diagonal $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2)$ acts on those two lines
by the opposite scalars $d_1-d_2$ and $d_2-d_1$. Thus $T_B^2$ is not another
member of the raw commutator family. The generated algebra is the natural
corrected object.
:::

::: solution
Let $E_{ij}$ be the standard matrix units. They form an orthonormal basis of
$V$, because
$$
(E_{ij}\mid E_{k\ell})
=\operatorname{tr}(E_{ij}E_{\ell k})
=\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}.
$$
Write
$$
B=\operatorname{diag}(b_1,\ldots,b_n).
$$
Then
$$
L_B(E_{ij})=b_iE_{ij},
\qquad
R_B(E_{ij})=b_jE_{ij},
\qquad
T_B(E_{ij})=(b_i-b_j)E_{ij}.
$$

<1>1. The left-multiplication family is a commutative self-adjoint algebra.
::: proof
For diagonal $B,C$,
$$
L_B+L_C=L_{B+C},
\qquad
L_BL_C=L_{BC}=L_{CB}=L_CL_B,
$$
and scalar multiples remain of the same form. Also $L_I$ is the identity on
$V$. Finally,
$$
L_B^*=L_{B^*},
$$
because for $A,C\in V$,
$$
(L_BA\mid C)
=\operatorname{tr}(BAC^*)
=\operatorname{tr}(AC^*B)
=(A\mid L_{B^*}C).
$$
Since $B^*$ is diagonal, the family is self-adjoint.
:::

<1>2. Its spectral resolution is by row subspaces.
::: proof
For $1\le i\le n$, put
$$
P_i^L=L_{E_{ii}}.
$$
These are mutually orthogonal self-adjoint projections and
$$
I_V=P_1^L+\cdots+P_n^L.
$$
Their ranges are
$$
\operatorname{ran}P_i^L
=\operatorname{span}\{E_{i1},\ldots,E_{in}\}.
$$
Since $L_B$ acts on this range by the scalar $b_i$,
$$
\boxed{L_B=\sum_{i=1}^n b_iP_i^L.}
$$
This is the joint spectral resolution of the left family.
:::

<1>3. The right-multiplication family is a commutative self-adjoint algebra, with the analogous column resolution.
::: proof
For diagonal $B,C$,
$$
R_B+R_C=R_{B+C},
\qquad
R_BR_C=R_{CB}=R_{BC}=R_CR_B,
$$
and
$$
R_B^*=R_{B^*}.
$$
Thus this family is again a commutative self-adjoint algebra.

Put
$$
P_j^R=R_{E_{jj}}.
$$
Then
$$
I_V=\sum_{j=1}^nP_j^R,
$$
the range of $P_j^R$ is
$$
\operatorname{span}\{E_{1j},\ldots,E_{nj}\},
$$
and $R_B$ acts there by $b_j$. Hence
$$
\boxed{R_B=\sum_{j=1}^n b_jP_j^R.}
$$
:::

<1>4. The raw commutator family is commuting and self-adjoint but is not, in general, an algebra.
::: proof
Since
$$
T_B=L_B-R_B,
$$
and left and right multiplications commute with one another, the operators
$T_B$ commute when the diagonal matrices $B$ commute. Moreover
$$
T_B^*=T_{B^*},
$$
so the family is closed under adjoints.

The failure of multiplicative closure is already visible for $n=2$. With
$B=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$,
$$
T_B(E_{12})=-E_{12},
\qquad
T_B(E_{21})=E_{21},
$$
so
$$
T_B^2(E_{12})=E_{12},
\qquad
T_B^2(E_{21})=E_{21}.
$$
But for every diagonal $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2)$,
$$
T_D(E_{12})=(d_1-d_2)E_{12},
\qquad
T_D(E_{21})=(d_2-d_1)E_{21}.
$$
Those two eigenvalues are negatives of one another, so they cannot both equal
$1$. Thus $T_B^2$ is not of the form $T_D$.
:::

<1>5. The algebra generated by the commutator family is commutative and self-adjoint.
::: proof
The generators commute and are closed under adjoint by step <1>4. Therefore
every polynomial expression in finitely many generators commutes with every
other such expression, and taking adjoints stays inside the generated
algebra. Hence the generated algebra is commutative and self-adjoint.
:::

<1>6. The joint spectral resolution of the commutator family is given by the diagonal subspace and the individual off-diagonal matrix-unit lines.
::: proof
For all $i,j$, define the rank-one orthogonal projection
$$
P_{ij}=L_{E_{ii}}R_{E_{jj}},
$$
so that
$$
P_{ij}(A)=a_{ij}E_{ij}.
$$
Set
$$
P_0=\sum_{i=1}^nP_{ii},
$$
the orthogonal projection onto the diagonal matrices. Then
$$
I_V=P_0+\sum_{i\ne j}P_{ij},
$$
and the displayed matrix-unit calculation at the start gives
$$
\boxed{
T_B=\sum_{i\ne j}(b_i-b_j)P_{ij},}
$$
while $T_B$ is zero on $\operatorname{ran}P_0$.

These joint eigenspaces are maximal. Indeed choose
$$
B_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,4,\ldots,2^{n-1}).
$$
The nonzero numbers $2^{i-1}-2^{j-1}$ for $i\ne j$ are pairwise distinct:
after changing sign if necessary, the lower power of $2$ is recovered from
the exact power of $2$ dividing the difference, and then the higher exponent
is determined. Thus $T_{B_0}$ has eigenvalue $0$ exactly on the diagonal
subspace and a different nonzero eigenvalue on every off-diagonal line
$\mathbb CE_{ij}$.

Consequently polynomial interpolation in $T_{B_0}$ produces each $P_{ij}$
for $i\ne j$ using a polynomial that also vanishes at $0$. Hence the
non-unital algebra generated by the commutator family is exactly
$$
\boxed{
\left\{\sum_{i\ne j}c_{ij}P_{ij}:c_{ij}\in\mathbb C\right\}.}
$$
The projection $P_0$ corresponds to the zero joint root and belongs to the
resolution of the identity even though it is not in this non-unital generated
algebra.
:::
