::: {.exercise}
Let $K$ be a subfield of a field $F$, and suppose $f$, $g$ are polynomials in $K[x]$.
Let $M_K$ be the ideal generated by $f$ and $g$ in $K[x]$ and $M_F$ be the ideal they generate in $F[x]$.
Show that $M_K$ and $M_F$ have the same monic generator.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}
$M_K$ is generated by the monic greatest common divisor $d \in K[x]$ of $f$ and $g$, and the Euclidean algorithm in $K[x]$ computes it.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Since $K$ is a field, the polynomial ring $K[x]$ is a principal ideal domain (PID).
The ideal $M_K = \langle f, g \rangle_{K[x]}$ is principal, generated by the unique monic greatest common divisor $d(x) = \gcd_{K[x]}(f, g)$.

::: pf-proof
$K[x]$ is a Euclidean domain with the degree as norm, since $K$ is a field and division with remainder by a nonzero polynomial is possible, and a Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain, so $M_K = \langle h \rangle$. Replacing $h$ by $h$ divided by its leading coefficient gives a monic generator, and a monic generator of a nonzero ideal is unique. That generator divides $f$ and $g$, and every common divisor divides each $af + bg$, hence divides $h$, so it is the greatest common divisor.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The polynomial $d(x)$ is computed by the Euclidean algorithm in $K[x]$:
$$
r_0 = f, \quad r_1 = g, \quad r_{i-1} = q_i r_i + r_{i+1} \quad \text{with } \deg(r_{i+1}) < \deg(r_i),
$$
terminating with the last non-zero remainder $r_k(x) \in K[x]$, and $d(x) = c^{-1} r_k(x)$ where $c \in K^\times$ is the leading coefficient of $r_k$.

::: pf-proof
The divisions are possible at each stage because $K$ is a field and the divisor is nonzero, and the degree drops strictly, so the sequence terminates at a last nonzero remainder $r_k$. That remainder divides all its predecessors, since $r_{i-1} = q_i r_i + r_{i+1}$ shows a common divisor of $r_i$ and $r_{i+1}$ divides $r_{i-1}$, so it divides $f$ and $g$; conversely every common divisor of $f$ and $g$ divides every remainder, so $r_k$ is a greatest common divisor, and multiplying by $c^{-1}$ makes it monic, which gives $d$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Division with remainder of two polynomials of $K[x]$, carried out in $F[x]$, returns the same quotient and remainder, so the Euclidean algorithm for $f$ and $g$ gives the same $d$ in either ring.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #same-division}
By the uniqueness of quotient and remainder in the polynomial division algorithm, performing polynomial division on elements of $K[x]$ inside $F[x]$ yields the exact same quotient $q_i \in K[x] \subseteq F[x]$ and remainder $r_{i+1} \in K[x] \subseteq F[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Let $a, b \in K[x]$ with $b \neq 0$, and let $a = qb + r$ with $\deg r < \deg b$ be the division carried out in $F[x]$. The division algorithm in $K[x]$ supplies a quotient and remainder in $K[x]$ satisfying the same two conditions, and both are polynomials in $F[x]$; by uniqueness of quotient and remainder in $F[x]$ the two pairs coincide, so $q$ and $r$ lie in $K[x]$ and are the same polynomials in either ring.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore the Euclidean algorithm for $f, g$ in $F[x]$ produces the exact same sequence of remainders, terminating at the same monic polynomial $d(x) \in K[x] \subseteq F[x]$.

::: pf-proof
Induction on the index: $r_0 = f$ and $r_1 = g$ lie in $K[x]$, and if $r_{i-1}$ and $r_i$ lie in $K[x]$ then dividing $r_{i-1}$ by $r_i$ in $F[x]$ gives the quotient and remainder of the division in $K[x]$, by step [](#same-division){.pf-ref}. So the sequence computed in $F[x]$ is the sequence computed in $K[x]$, the algorithm stops at the same index, the last nonzero remainder and its leading coefficient are the same, and so is the resulting monic $d$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}
$\langle d \rangle_{F[x]} = M_F$.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #bezout}
By Bézout's identity in $K[x]$, there exist polynomials $a(x), b(x) \in K[x]$ such that:
$$
d(x) = a(x) f(x) + b(x) g(x).
$$
Since $K[x] \subseteq F[x]$, $a, b \in F[x]$, so $d(x) \in M_F = \langle f, g \rangle_{F[x]}$, which implies $\langle d \rangle_{F[x]} \subseteq M_F$.

::: pf-proof
The ideal $M_K = \langle f, g \rangle_{K[x]}$ consists of the combinations $af + bg$ with $a, b \in K[x]$, and it is also generated by $d$, so such $a$ and $b$ exist; being elements of $K[x] \subseteq F[x]$, they are also coefficients over $F$. Hence $d \in M_F$, and an ideal containing $d$ contains all of its multiples, which is $\langle d \rangle_{F[x]} \subseteq M_F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #divisibility}
Conversely, because $d \mid f$ and $d \mid g$ in $K[x]$, there exist $u, v \in K[x] \subseteq F[x]$ such that $f = u d$ and $g = v d$.
For any element $h = A f + B g \in M_F$ with $A, B \in F[x]$:
$$
h = A(u d) + B(v d) = (A u + B v) d \in \langle d \rangle_{F[x]}.
$$
Thus $M_F \subseteq \langle d \rangle_{F[x]}$.

::: pf-proof
Since $d$ generates $M_K$ and $f, g$ are elements of $M_K$, there are $u, v \in K[x]$ with $f = ud$ and $g = vd$, and these coefficients lie in $F[x]$ as well. For $h = Af + Bg$ with $A, B \in F[x]$ one then has $h = (Au + Bv)d$, a multiple of $d$ in $F[x]$, so $M_F \subseteq \langle d \rangle_{F[x]}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Combining both inclusions yields $M_F = \langle d \rangle_{F[x]}$.
Since $d(x)$ is monic, $d(x)$ is the unique monic generator of $M_F$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#bezout){.pf-ref} and [](#divisibility){.pf-ref} give the two inclusions, hence $\langle d \rangle_{F[x]} = M_F$. A monic generator of a nonzero ideal is unique: if $\langle d \rangle = \langle e \rangle$ with both monic, then each divides the other, so they differ by a unit of $F[x]$, that is by a nonzero constant, and the only nonzero constant multiplying a monic polynomial to a monic one is $1$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
$M_K$ and $M_F$ have the same monic generator $d(x) = \gcd(f, g)$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} names $d$ as the monic generator of $M_K$ and step [](#s3){.pf-ref} names the same $d$ as the monic generator of $M_F$, so the two ideals have the same monic generator.
:::
