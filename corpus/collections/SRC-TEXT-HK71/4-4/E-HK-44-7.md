::: {.exercise}
Let $K$ be a subfield of a field $F$, and suppose $f$, $g$ are polynomials in $K[x]$.
Let $M_K$ be the ideal generated by $f$ and $g$ in $K[x]$ and $M_F$ be the ideal they generate in $F[x]$.
Show that $M_K$ and $M_F$ have the same monic generator.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Computation of the monic generator in $K[x]$ via the Euclidean algorithm:
<2>1. Since $K$ is a field, the polynomial ring $K[x]$ is a principal ideal domain (PID).
The ideal $M_K = \langle f, g \rangle_{K[x]}$ is principal, generated by the unique monic greatest common divisor $d(x) = \gcd_{K[x]}(f, g)$.
<2>2. The polynomial $d(x)$ is computed by the Euclidean algorithm in $K[x]$:
\[
r_0 = f, \quad r_1 = g, \quad r_{i-1} = q_i r_i + r_{i+1} \quad \text{with } \deg(r_{i+1}) < \deg(r_i),
\]
terminating with the last non-zero remainder $r_k(x) \in K[x]$, and $d(x) = c^{-1} r_k(x)$ where $c \in K^\times$ is the leading coefficient of $r_k$.

<1>2. Invariance of the Euclidean algorithm under field extensions:
<2>1. By the uniqueness of quotient and remainder in the polynomial division algorithm, performing polynomial division on elements of $K[x]$ inside $F[x]$ yields the exact same quotient $q_i \in K[x] \subseteq F[x]$ and remainder $r_{i+1} \in K[x] \subseteq F[x]$.
<2>2. Therefore the Euclidean algorithm for $f, g$ in $F[x]$ produces the exact same sequence of remainders, terminating at the same monic polynomial $d(x) \in K[x] \subseteq F[x]$.

<1>3. Proof that $d(x)$ generates $M_F$ in $F[x]$:
<2>1. By Bézout's identity in $K[x]$, there exist polynomials $a(x), b(x) \in K[x]$ such that:
\[
d(x) = a(x) f(x) + b(x) g(x).
\]
Since $K[x] \subseteq F[x]$, $a, b \in F[x]$, so $d(x) \in M_F = \langle f, g \rangle_{F[x]}$, which implies $\langle d \rangle_{F[x]} \subseteq M_F$.
<2>2. Conversely, because $d \mid f$ and $d \mid g$ in $K[x]$, there exist $u, v \in K[x] \subseteq F[x]$ such that $f = u d$ and $g = v d$.
For any element $h = A f + B g \in M_F$ with $A, B \in F[x]$:
\[
h = A(u d) + B(v d) = (A u + B v) d \in \langle d \rangle_{F[x]}.
\]
Thus $M_F \subseteq \langle d \rangle_{F[x]}$.
<2>3. Combining both inclusions yields $M_F = \langle d \rangle_{F[x]}$.
Since $d(x)$ is monic, $d(x)$ is the unique monic generator of $M_F$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
$M_K$ and $M_F$ have the exact same monic generator $d(x) = \gcd(f, g)$. Q.E.D.
:::
