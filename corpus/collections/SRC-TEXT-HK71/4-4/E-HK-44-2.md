::: {.exercise}
Find the g.c.d. of each of the following pairs of polynomials

(a) $2x^5 - x^3 - 3x^2 - 6x + 4$, $\;x^4 + x^3 - x^2 - 2x - 2$;

(b) $3x^4 + 8x^2 - 3$, $\;x^3 + 2x^2 + 3x + 6$;

(c) $x^4 - 2x^3 - 2x^2 - 2x - 3$, $\;x^3 + 6x^2 + 7x + 1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own, and each Euclidean chain below was recomputed for this pass. The gcd of two polynomials over $\RR$ is the monic polynomial of largest degree dividing both, so nonzero scalar factors of the remainders do not affect it.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) $\gcd(2x^{5}-x^{3}-3x^{2}-6x+4,\; x^{4}+x^{3}-x^{2}-2x-2)=1$.

::: pf-proof
The Euclidean chain, and a nonzero constant as the last remainder.

::: {.pf-step #a-chain}
With $f=2x^{5}-x^{3}-3x^{2}-6x+4$ and $g=x^{4}+x^{3}-x^{2}-2x-2$,
$$
f=(2x-2)g+(3x^{3}-x^{2}-6x),\qquad g=\Bigl(\frac{x}{3}+\frac{4}{9}\Bigr)(3x^{3}-x^{2}-6x)+\frac{13x^{2}+6x-18}{9},
$$
and dividing $13x^{2}+6x-18$ into $3x^{3}-x^{2}-6x$ leaves $-\frac{126}{169}x-\frac{558}{169}=-18\cdot\frac{7x+31}{169}$.

::: pf-proof
Each assertion is an identity checked by expanding. For the first, $(2x-2)g=2x^{5}+2x^{4}-2x^{3}-4x^{2}-4x-2x^{4}-2x^{3}+2x^{2}+4x+4=2x^{5}-4x^{3}-2x^{2}+4$, and adding $3x^{3}-x^{2}-6x$ gives $f$. For the second, $\bigl(\frac{x}{3}+\frac{4}{9}\bigr)(3x^{3}-x^{2}-6x)=x^{4}-\frac{x^{3}}{3}-2x^{2}+\frac{4x^{3}}{3}-\frac{4x^{2}}{9}-\frac{8x}{3}=x^{4}+x^{3}-\frac{22x^{2}}{9}-\frac{8x}{3}$, and adding $\frac{13x^{2}+6x-18}{9}$ gives $x^{4}+x^{3}-x^{2}-2x-2=g$. The third is the division of the cubic by the quadratic, whose remainder is the displayed linear polynomial.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-constant}
No polynomial of positive degree divides both $13x^{2}+6x-18$ and $7x+31$.

::: pf-proof
A common divisor of positive degree would share a root with the linear polynomial $7x+31$, whose only root is $-\frac{31}{7}$. At that point
$$
13\Bigl(-\frac{31}{7}\Bigr)^{2}+6\Bigl(-\frac{31}{7}\Bigr)-18=\frac{12493-1302-882}{49}=\frac{10309}{49}\ne0,
$$
so $13x^{2}+6x-18$ and $7x+31$ have no common root and hence no common factor of positive degree.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #a-result}
The gcd is $1$.

::: pf-proof
The Euclidean algorithm preserves the gcd at each step up to a nonzero scalar, so $\gcd(f,g)$ is, up to a scalar, the gcd of the last two remainders $13x^{2}+6x-18$ and $7x+31$; by step [](#a-constant){.pf-ref} that gcd is a nonzero constant, and normalized to be monic it is $1$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b}
(b) $\gcd(3x^{4}+8x^{2}-3,\; x^{3}+2x^{2}+3x+6)=x^{2}+3$.

::: pf-proof
A common factor is exhibited, and the cofactors are coprime.

::: {.pf-step #b-chain}
With $f=3x^{4}+8x^{2}-3$ and $g=x^{3}+2x^{2}+3x+6$, one has $f=(3x-6)g+11(x^{2}+3)$ and $g=(x+2)(x^{2}+3)$.

::: pf-proof
Expand $(3x-6)g=3x^{4}+6x^{3}+9x^{2}+18x-6x^{3}-12x^{2}-18x-36=3x^{4}-3x^{2}-36$, and adding $11x^{2}+33$ gives $3x^{4}+8x^{2}-3=f$. The second identity is the expansion of the product $(x+2)(x^{2}+3)=x^{3}+3x+2x^{2}+6=g$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #b-result}
The gcd is $x^{2}+3$.

::: pf-proof
From the two identities, $f=(x^{2}+3)\bigl[(3x-6)(x+2)+11\bigr]=(x^{2}+3)(3x^{2}-1)$ and $g=(x^{2}+3)(x+2)$, so $x^{2}+3$ divides both. The remaining cofactors $3x^{2}-1$ and $x+2$ are coprime: any common root would be a root $-2$ of $x+2$, and $3(-2)^{2}-1=11\ne0$. Hence no larger common factor exists, and $x^{2}+3$ is already monic.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(c) $\gcd(x^{4}-2x^{3}-2x^{2}-2x-3,\; x^{3}+6x^{2}+7x+1)=1$, and the three monic gcds are $1$, $x^{2}+3$, $1$.

::: pf-proof
The Euclidean chain for the last pair, then the summary.

::: {.pf-step #c-chain}
With $f=x^{4}-2x^{3}-2x^{2}-2x-3$ and $g=x^{3}+6x^{2}+7x+1$,
$$
f=(x-8)g+(39x^{2}+53x+5),\qquad g=\Bigl(\frac{x}{39}+\frac{181}{1521}\Bigr)(39x^{2}+53x+5)+\frac{859x+616}{1521}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For the first, $(x-8)g=x^{4}+6x^{3}+7x^{2}+x-8x^{3}-48x^{2}-56x-8=x^{4}-2x^{3}-41x^{2}-55x-8$, and adding $39x^{2}+53x+5$ gives $f$. The second is the division of the cubic by the quadratic; its remainder has the displayed coefficients $859$ and $616$ over the leading coefficient $1521=39^{2}$ of the divisor after clearing denominators.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-constant}
No polynomial of positive degree divides both $39x^{2}+53x+5$ and $859x+616$.

::: pf-proof
The only root of the linear polynomial is $-\frac{616}{859}$, and there
$$
39\Bigl(-\frac{616}{859}\Bigr)^{2}+53\Bigl(-\frac{616}{859}\Bigr)+5=-\frac{9556443}{737881}\ne0,
$$
as one checks by clearing the denominator $859^{2}$: $39\cdot616^{2}-53\cdot616\cdot859+5\cdot859^{2}=14790624-28044152+3689405=-9556443\ne0$ over $737881$. So the two polynomials have no common root, hence no common factor of positive degree.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c-result}
The gcd is $1$, so the three monic gcds are $1,\ x^{2}+3,\ 1$.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#c-chain){.pf-ref} and [](#c-constant){.pf-ref} the Euclidean algorithm for the third pair terminates in a nonzero constant, so its gcd is $1$. With the results of parts (a) and (b) — steps [](#a-result){.pf-ref} and [](#b-result){.pf-ref} — the three gcds are $1$, $x^{2}+3$ and $1$, as claimed.
:::
