::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a subfield of the complex numbers, and let
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}.
$$
Find the monic generator of the ideal of all polynomials $f$ in $F[x]$ such that $f(A) = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is an archive.org record, so the argument is the card's own. Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-2\\ 0&3\end{bmatrix}$ over a subfield $F\subset\CC$, and let $\mathfrak a=\{f\in F[x]:f(A)=0\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ideal}
$\mathfrak a$ is an ideal of $F[x]$, nonempty and proper, and $F[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, so $\mathfrak a=(g)$ for a unique monic $g$.

::: pf-proof
Evaluation at $A$ is a ring homomorphism $\varphi:F[x]\to M_2(F)$, so if $f_1,f_2\in\mathfrak a$ and $h\in F[x]$ then $(f_1+hf_2)(A)=f_1(A)+h(A)f_2(A)=0$; thus $\mathfrak a$ is an ideal. It is nonempty since $0\in\mathfrak a$, and proper since $1\notin\mathfrak a$. The polynomial ring over a field is a principal ideal domain, so $\mathfrak a=(g)$ for some $g$, and multiplying $g$ by a nonzero scalar makes it monic without changing the ideal; a monic generator is unique because two monic generators of the same nonzero ideal divide each other and have equal degree.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #eigenvectors}
$Ae_1=e_1$ and $A(1,-1)^{T}=3(1,-1)^{T}$, so $1$ and $3$ are eigenvalues of $A$; and $1\ne3$ in $F$.

::: pf-proof
$A(1,0)^{T}=(1,0)^{T}$ and $A(1,-1)^{T}=(1-2\cdot(-1),\,0+3\cdot(-1))^{T}=(3,-3)^{T}=3(1,-1)^{T}$, and $(1,-1)^{T}\ne0$. So both are eigenvalues with the displayed eigenvectors. Since $F\subset\CC$, the characteristic of $F$ is $0$, so $1\ne3$ and $2\ne0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #divides}
If $f\in\mathfrak a$ then $(x-1)(x-3)$ divides $f$.

::: pf-proof
$f(A)(v)=f(1)v$ for an eigenvector $v$ with eigenvalue $1$, and $f(A)(1,-1)^{T}=f(3)(1,-1)^{T}$ for eigenvalue $3$: for $f=\sum_kc_kx^k$ one has $f(A)v=\sum_kc_kA^kv=\sum_kc_k\lambda^kv=f(\lambda)v$. So $f(A)=0$ forces $f(1)=0$ and $f(3)=0$, and a polynomial vanishing at $1$ and at $3$ is divisible by $(x-1)(x-3)$ since $x-1$ and $x-3$ are coprime linear factors in $F[x]$, where $1\ne3$ by step [](#eigenvectors){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #annihilates}
$(A-I)(A-3I)=0$, so $(x-1)(x-3)\in\mathfrak a$.

::: pf-proof
$A-I=\begin{bmatrix}0&-2\\ 0&2\end{bmatrix}$ and $A-3I=\begin{bmatrix}-2&-2\\ 0&0\end{bmatrix}$; the product is $\begin{bmatrix}0&-2\\ 0&2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2&-2\\ 0&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\ 0&0\end{bmatrix}$. Hence $(x-1)(x-3)$ annihilates $A$, and it is monic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
The monic generator of $\mathfrak a$ is $(x-1)(x-3)$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#divides){.pf-ref} and [](#annihilates){.pf-ref} say that the monic polynomial $(x-1)(x-3)$ generates $\mathfrak a$: it lies in $\mathfrak a$, and every element of $\mathfrak a$ is a multiple of it. By the uniqueness in step [](#ideal){.pf-ref} it is the monic generator, so the answer is $(x-1)(x-3)$.
:::
