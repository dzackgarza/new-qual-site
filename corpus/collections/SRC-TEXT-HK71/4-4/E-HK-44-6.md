::: {.exercise}
Let $F$ be a field.
Show that the ideal generated by a finite number of polynomials $f_1, \ldots, f_n$ in $F[x]$ is the intersection of all ideals containing $f_1, \ldots, f_n$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the ideal generated by a set is the smallest ideal containing it, and the explicit description by finite linear combinations makes that minimality immediate.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ideal}

$J=\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}f_{i}: h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in F[x]\big\}$ is an ideal of $F[x]$.

::: pf-proof

$J$ is nonempty (take all $h_{i}=0$) and is closed under subtraction: the difference of $\sum_{i}h_{i}f_{i}$ and $\sum_{i}k_{i}f_{i}$ is $\sum_{i}(h_{i}-k_{i})f_{i}\in J$. It is closed under multiplication by an arbitrary $q\in F[x]$, since $q\sum_{i}h_{i}f_{i}=\sum_{i}(qh_{i})f_{i}\in J$. So $J$ is an ideal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #contains}

$f_{i}\in J$ for every $i$.

::: pf-proof

Take $h_{i}=1$ and $h_{j}=0$ for $j\ne i$; the corresponding sum is $1\cdot f_{i}=f_{i}$, which lies in $J$ by step [](#ideal){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #smallest}

Every ideal $I$ of $F[x]$ containing $f_{1},\dots,f_{n}$ contains $J$.

::: pf-proof

Let $I$ be such an ideal and let $h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in F[x]$. For each $i$, $h_{i}f_{i}\in I$ because $f_{i}\in I$ and $I$ is closed under multiplication by elements of $F[x]$; and the sum of these $n$ elements of $I$ lies in $I$ because $I$ is closed under addition. Hence every element of $J$ lies in $I$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$J=\bigcap\{I: I \text{ an ideal of } F[x] \text{ containing } f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#smallest){.pf-ref}, $J$ is contained in every ideal containing the $f_{i}$, hence in their intersection. By steps [](#ideal){.pf-ref} and [](#contains){.pf-ref}, $J$ is itself an ideal containing the $f_{i}$, so the intersection is contained in $J$. Two inclusions give
$$
\boxed{(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})=\bigcap\{I: f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in I,\ I \text{ an ideal of } F[x]\}.}
$$

:::
