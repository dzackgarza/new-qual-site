::: problem
Given two spaces $X$ and $Y$, let $\pi_X:X\times Y\to X$ and $\pi_Y:X\times Y\to Y$ be the two standard projection maps.

(a) Show that, for any other space $Z$, a function $f:Z\to X\times Y$ is continuous if and only if $\pi_X\circ f$ and $\pi_Y\circ f$ are continuous.

(b) Use this to show that the cartesian product of two path-connected topological spaces is path-connected.
:::

::: {.solution}
**(a).**

<1>1. ($\Rightarrow$) If $f$ is continuous, then $\pi_X \circ f$ and $\pi_Y \circ f$ are continuous.
::: {.proof}
composition of continuous maps is continuous, and the projections are continuous.
:::

<1>2. ($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $\pi_X \circ f$ and $\pi_Y \circ f$ are continuous.
::: {.proof}
assume the coordinate maps are continuous.
:::

<1>3. A map into a product is continuous iff each coordinate is continuous.
::: {.proof}
the product topology is the coarsest topology making the projections continuous (the universal property of the product).
:::

<1>4. Hence $f$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and <1>3.
:::

**(b).**

<1>1. Let $X, Y$ be path-connected, and take $(x_0, y_0), (x_1, y_1) \in X \times Y$.
::: {.proof}
fix two points.
:::

<1>2. Since $X$ is path-connected, there is a path $\gamma_X : [0,1] \to X$ with $\gamma_X(0) = x_0$, $\gamma_X(1) = x_1$.
::: {.proof}
path-connectedness of $X$.
:::

<1>3. Since $Y$ is path-connected, there is a path $\gamma_Y : [0,1] \to Y$ with $\gamma_Y(0) = y_0$, $\gamma_Y(1) = y_1$.
::: {.proof}
path-connectedness of $Y$.
:::

<1>4. Define $\gamma : [0,1] \to X \times Y$ by $\gamma(t) = (\gamma_X(t), \gamma_Y(t))$.
::: {.proof}
definition.
:::

<1>5. $\gamma$ is continuous.
::: {.proof}
<1>4 and part (a) (its coordinates $\pi_X \circ \gamma = \gamma_X$ and $\pi_Y \circ \gamma = \gamma_Y$ are continuous).
:::

<1>6. $\gamma(0) = (x_0, y_0)$ and $\gamma(1) = (x_1, y_1)$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>7. Hence $X \times Y$ is path-connected.
::: {.proof}
<1>5 and <1>6 (any two points are joined by a path).
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>4 (a) and <1>7 (b).
:::
:::
