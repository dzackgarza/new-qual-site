::: {.problem}
Given two spaces $X$ and $Y$, let $\pi_X:X\times Y\to X$ and $\pi_Y:X\times Y\to Y$ be the two standard projection maps.

(a) Show that, for any other space $Z$, a function $f:Z\to X\times Y$ is continuous if and only if $\pi_X\circ f$ and $\pi_Y\circ f$ are continuous.

(b) Use this to show that the cartesian product of two path-connected topological spaces is path-connected.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $f:Z\to X\times Y$ is continuous, then both coordinate maps $\pi_X\circ f$ and $\pi_Y\circ f$ are continuous.

::: pf-proof

The projection maps
\[
\pi_X:X\times Y\to X,
\qquad
\pi_Y:X\times Y\to Y
\]
are continuous for the product topology.
Therefore each composite of a projection with the continuous map $f$ is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Conversely, if both coordinate maps are continuous, then $f$ is continuous.

::: pf-proof

Assume
\[
f_X=\pi_X\circ f:Z\to X,
\qquad
f_Y=\pi_Y\circ f:Z\to Y
\]
are continuous.
A basis for the product topology on $X\times Y$ consists of sets $U\times V$ with $U\subseteq X$ open and $V\subseteq Y$ open.
For every such basic open set,
\[
f^{-1}(U\times V)
=f_X^{-1}(U)\cap f_Y^{-1}(V).
\]
Both sets on the right are open in $Z$, so their intersection is open.
Thus the inverse image under $f$ of every basic open subset of $X\times Y$ is open, which proves that $f$ is continuous.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Therefore a map into $X\times Y$ is continuous if and only if both of its coordinate maps are continuous.

::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $X$ and $Y$ are path-connected, then $X\times Y$ is path-connected.

::: pf-proof

Take arbitrary points
\[
(x_0,y_0),(x_1,y_1)\in X\times Y.
\]
Since $X$ is path-connected, there is a continuous path
\[
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
\]
with $\alpha(0)=x_0$ and $\alpha(1)=x_1$.
Likewise, path-connectedness of $Y$ gives a continuous path
\[
\beta:[0,1]\to Y
\]
with $\beta(0)=y_0$ and $\beta(1)=y_1$.
Define
\[
\gamma:[0,1]\longrightarrow X\times Y,
\qquad
\gamma(t)=(\alpha(t),\beta(t)).
\]
Its coordinate maps are
\[
\pi_X\circ\gamma=\alpha,
\qquad
\pi_Y\circ\gamma=\beta,
\]
so step [](#s3){.pf-ref} shows that $\gamma$ is continuous.
Moreover,
\[
\gamma(0)=(x_0,y_0),
\qquad
\gamma(1)=(x_1,y_1).
\]
Thus every two points of $X\times Y$ can be joined by a path, and $X\times Y$ is path-connected.

:::
