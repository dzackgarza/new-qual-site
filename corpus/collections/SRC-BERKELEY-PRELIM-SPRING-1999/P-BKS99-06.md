::: {.problem}
Let $p,q,r,s$ be polynomials of degree at most $3$. Which, if either, of the following conditions is sufficient to imply that $p,q,r,s$ are linearly dependent?

1. Each polynomial has value $0$ at $1$.
2. Each polynomial has value $1$ at $0$.

Prove your answers.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $V$ be the vector space of real or complex polynomials of degree at most $3$. Then $\dim V=4$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Condition 1 is sufficient.


::: pf-proof

Consider the linear functional
\[
\operatorname{ev}_1:V\to\FF,
\qquad
h\longmapsto h(1),
\]
where $\FF$ is the coefficient field. This functional is nonzero, so its kernel has dimension $3$. Under condition 1, all four polynomials $p,q,r,s$ lie in
\[
\ker(\operatorname{ev}_1)=\{h\in V:h(1)=0\}.
\]
Four vectors in a $3$-dimensional vector space are linearly dependent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Condition 2 is not sufficient.


::: pf-proof

The four polynomials
\[
1,
\qquad
1+x,
\qquad
1+x^2,
\qquad
1+x^3
\]
all have value $1$ at $0$. They are linearly independent: if
\[
a_0+a_1(1+x)+a_2(1+x^2)+a_3(1+x^3)=0,
\]
then comparison of the coefficients of $x^3,x^2,x$ gives
\[
a_3=a_2=a_1=0,
\]
and the constant coefficient then gives $a_0=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves that condition 1 is sufficient, while step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives a counterexample to condition 2.

:::
