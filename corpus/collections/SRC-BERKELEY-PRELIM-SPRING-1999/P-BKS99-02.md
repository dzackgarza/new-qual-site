::: {.problem}
Suppose $f_n:\RR\to\RR$ converges uniformly on $\RR$ to $f:\RR\to\RR$, and that for every positive integer $n$ the limit
$$
c_n=\lim_{x\to\infty}f_n(x)
$$
exists.
Prove that both
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)
$$
exist and are equal.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cauchy-sequence}
The sequence $(c_n)$ is Cauchy.

::: pf-proof
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $f_n\to f$ uniformly, the sequence $(f_n)$ is uniformly Cauchy. Hence there is $N$ such that for all $m,n\geq N$,
$$
\abs{f_n(x)-f_m(x)}<\varepsilon
$$
for every $x\in\RR$. Taking $x\to\infty$ and using the existence of $c_n$ and $c_m$ gives
$$
\abs{c_n-c_m}
=\lim_{x\to\infty}\abs{f_n(x)-f_m(x)}
\leq\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $(c_n)$ is Cauchy.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #limit-of-constants}
The limit
$$
c:=\lim_{n\to\infty}c_n
$$
exists.

::: pf-proof
By [](#cauchy-sequence){.pf-ref}, $(c_n)$ is a Cauchy sequence in $\RR$, and $\RR$ is complete.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
One has
$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=c.
$$

::: pf-proof
Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $n$ so large that
$$
\sup_{x\in\RR}\abs{f(x)-f_n(x)}<\frac\varepsilon3
$$
and, by [](#limit-of-constants){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{c_n-c}<\frac\varepsilon3.
$$
Since $f_n(x)\to c_n$ as $x\to\infty$, there is $R$ such that $x>R$ implies
$$
\abs{f_n(x)-c_n}<\frac\varepsilon3.
$$
Then for $x>R$,
$$
\abs{f(x)-c}
\leq \abs{f(x)-f_n(x)}+\abs{f_n(x)-c_n}+\abs{c_n-c}
<\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $f(x)\to c$ as $x\to\infty$.
:::
