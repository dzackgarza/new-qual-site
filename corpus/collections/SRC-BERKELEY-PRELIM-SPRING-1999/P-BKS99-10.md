::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be twice differentiable on $[a,b]$ and assume
$$
f''(x)>0\qquad(x\in[a,b]).
$$
For $c\in[a,b]$, consider the region between the graph $y=f(x)$, the tangent line to the graph at $(c,f(c))$, and the vertical lines $x=a$ and $x=b$.

Determine all $c\in[a,b]$ for which the area of this region is minimal.
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume first that $a<b$, and write
$$
m:=\frac{a+b}{2}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For each $c\in[a,b]$, the tangent line
$$
\ell_c(x):=f(c)+f'(c)(x-c)
$$
lies below the graph of $f$ on $[a,b]$, with equality only at $x=c$.


::: pf-proof

Since $f''>0$, the derivative $f'$ is strictly increasing. If $x>c$, the mean value theorem gives
$$
f(x)-f(c)=f'(\xi)(x-c)
$$
for some $\xi\in(c,x)$, so $f'(\xi)>f'(c)$ and hence $f(x)>\ell_c(x)$. If $x<c$, the same theorem gives such a $\xi\in(x,c)$; now $f'(\xi)<f'(c)$ and $x-c<0$, again yielding
$$
f(x)-f(c)>f'(c)(x-c).
$$
Thus the claim holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The area is
$$
A(c)=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx-(b-a)f(c)-(b-a)(m-c)f'(c).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the area is the integral of the nonnegative vertical difference:
$$
A(c)=\int_a^b\bigl(f(x)-\ell_c(x)\bigr)\,dx.
$$
Since
$$
\int_a^b(x-c)\,dx
=(b-a)\left(\frac{a+b}{2}-c\right)
=(b-a)(m-c),
$$
expanding the integral gives the stated formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For $c\in(a,b)$,
$$
A'(c)=(b-a)f''(c)(c-m).
$$


::: pf-proof

Differentiate the expression in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A'(c)
&=-(b-a)f'(c)
-(b-a)\frac{d}{dc}\bigl((m-c)f'(c)\bigr)\\
&=-(b-a)f'(c)
-(b-a)\bigl(-f'(c)+(m-c)f''(c)\bigr)\\
&=(b-a)f''(c)(c-m).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $a<b$, the unique minimizer is
$$
\boxed{c=\frac{a+b}{2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and the hypothesis $f''(c)>0$, one has $A'(c)<0$ for $a<c<m$ and $A'(c)>0$ for $m<c<b$. Hence $A$ is strictly decreasing up to $m$ and strictly increasing after $m$, so its unique minimum occurs at $c=m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $a=b$, the unique possible value is $c=a=b$, and its area is $0$.


::: pf-proof

The interval $[a,b]$ then consists of the single point $a$, so there is only one admissible $c$ and the enclosed area is zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} cover all intervals $[a,b]$.

:::
