::: {.problem}
For each real $c\in(0,1)$, evaluate
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{x^c(1+x)}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
\[
I_c:=\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{-c}}{1+x}\,dx.
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

On the slit plane $\CC\setminus[0,\infty)$, choose the branch
\[
z^{-c}:=e^{-c(\log|z|+i\arg z)},
\qquad 0<\arg z<2\pi.
\]
For the corresponding keyhole contour about the positive real axis,
\[
\lim_{\varepsilon\to0,\ R\to\infty}
\int_\Gamma \frac{z^{-c}}{1+z}\,dz
=(1-e^{-2\pi ic})I_c.
\]


::: pf-proof

On the upper side of the positive axis, $z^{-c}=x^{-c}$, giving
\[
\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{x^{-c}}{1+x}\,dx.
\]
On the lower side, where $\arg z=2\pi$, one has $z^{-c}=e^{-2\pi ic}x^{-c}$ and the orientation runs from $R$ to $\varepsilon$, giving
\[
-e^{-2\pi ic}\int_\varepsilon^R\frac{x^{-c}}{1+x}\,dx.
\]
The outer circular arc contributes $O(R^{-c})$ and the inner circular arc contributes $O(\varepsilon^{1-c})$. Since $0<c<1$, both tend to $0$. Taking the two limits proves the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The same contour integral tends to
\[
2\pi i e^{-\pi ic}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The only pole of $z^{-c}/(1+z)$ inside the keyhole contour is the simple pole at $z=-1$. On the chosen branch, $\arg(-1)=\pi$, so
\[
\operatorname*{Res}_{z=-1}\frac{z^{-c}}{1+z}
=(-1)^{-c}
=e^{-\pi ic}.
\]
The residue theorem therefore gives the stated limit.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
\[
I_c=\boxed{\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi c)}}.
\]


::: pf-proof

Equating steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
\[
(1-e^{-2\pi ic})I_c=2\pi i e^{-\pi ic}.
\]
Since
\[
1-e^{-2\pi ic}
=2i e^{-\pi ic}\sin(\pi c),
\]
and $\sin(\pi c)>0$ for $0<c<1$, division yields
\[
I_c=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi c)}.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the requested evaluation.
