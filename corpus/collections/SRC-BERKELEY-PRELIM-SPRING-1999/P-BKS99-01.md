::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a metric space and, for nonempty subsets $X,Y\subseteq M$, write
\[
d(X,Y)=\inf\{d(x,y):x\in X,\ y\in Y\}.
\]
For disjoint subsets $X,Y\subseteq M$, prove or give a counterexample to each assertion:

1. If $X$ and $Y$ are closed in $M$, then $d(X,Y)>0$.
2. If $X$ and $Y$ are compact, then $d(X,Y)>0$.
3. If $X$ is closed and $Y$ is compact, then $d(X,Y)>0$.
:::
