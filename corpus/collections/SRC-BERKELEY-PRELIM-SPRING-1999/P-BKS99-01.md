::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a metric space and, for nonempty subsets $X,Y\subseteq M$, write
$$
d(X,Y)=\inf\{d(x,y):x\in X,\ y\in Y\}.
$$
For disjoint subsets $X,Y\subseteq M$, prove or give a counterexample to each assertion:

(a) If $X$ and $Y$ are closed in $M$, then $d(X,Y)>0$.

(b) If $X$ and $Y$ are compact, then $d(X,Y)>0$.

(c) If $X$ is closed and $Y$ is compact, then $d(X,Y)>0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: pf-step
Assertion (a) is false.

::: pf-proof
Take $M=\RR^2$ with the Euclidean metric and let
$$
X:=\{(x,0):x\geq0\},
\qquad
Y:=\left\{\left(x,\frac1{x+1}\right):x\geq0\right\}.
$$
The set $X$ is closed. The set $Y$ is also closed: if
$$
\left(x_j,\frac1{x_j+1}\right)\to(u,v),
$$
then $x_j\to u\geq0$ and continuity gives $v=1/(u+1)$, so $(u,v)\in Y$. The sets are disjoint because every point of $Y$ has positive second coordinate. However,
$$
d(X,Y)
\leq d\left((n,0),\left(n,\frac1{n+1}\right)\right)
=\frac1{n+1}
$$
for every positive integer $n$. Hence $d(X,Y)=0$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Assertion (b) is true.

::: pf-proof
If $X$ and $Y$ are compact, then $X\times Y$ is compact and the function
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto d(x,y)
$$
is continuous. It therefore attains a minimum at some $(x_0,y_0)\in X\times Y$. Since $X$ and $Y$ are disjoint, $x_0\neq y_0$, so
$$
d(X,Y)=d(x_0,y_0)>0.
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Assertion (c) is true.

::: pf-proof
Suppose instead that $d(X,Y)=0$. For every positive integer $n$, choose $x_n\in X$ and $y_n\in Y$ with
$$
d(x_n,y_n)<\frac1n.
$$
Because $Y$ is compact, some subsequence $(y_{n_j})$ converges to a point $y\in Y$. Then
$$
d(x_{n_j},y)
\leq d(x_{n_j},y_{n_j})+d(y_{n_j},y)
\longrightarrow0,
$$
so $x_{n_j}\to y$. Since $X$ is closed, $y\in X$, contradicting $X\cap Y=\varnothing$. Therefore $d(X,Y)>0$.
:::
