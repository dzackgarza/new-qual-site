::: {.problem}
Let $f:\RR\to\RR$ be twice differentiable and suppose
$$
f(0)=0,\qquad f'(0)>0,
$$
and
$$
f''(x)\ge f(x)\qquad(x\ge0).
$$
Prove that $f(x)>0$ for every $x>0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The function
$$
u(x):=e^{-x}\bigl(f'(x)+f(x)\bigr)
$$
is nondecreasing on $[0,\infty)$.


::: pf-proof

Differentiate:
$$
u'(x)
=e^{-x}\bigl(f''(x)-f(x)\bigr).
$$
By hypothesis $f''(x)\geq f(x)$ for $x\geq0$, so $u'(x)\geq0$ there.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $x\geq0$,
$$
f'(x)+f(x)>0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
e^{-x}\bigl(f'(x)+f(x)\bigr)
=u(x)
\geq u(0)
=f'(0)+f(0)
=f'(0)>0.
$$
Multiplying by $e^x>0$ gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every $x>0$, one has $f(x)>0$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(e^x f(x)\bigr)
=e^x\bigl(f'(x)+f(x)\bigr)>0
$$
for $x\geq0$. Hence $e^x f(x)$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\infty)$. Since $f(0)=0$,
$$
e^x f(x)>e^0f(0)=0
$$
for every $x>0$. Because $e^x>0$, this implies $f(x)>0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves the required positivity.

:::
