::: {.problem}
Let $f(x)\in\mathbb Q[x]$ be irreducible of degree $n\ge3$, let $L$ be its splitting field, and let $\alpha\in L$ be a root of $f$.
Assume
\[
[L:\mathbb Q]=n!.
\]
Prove that
\[
\mathbb Q(\alpha^4)=\mathbb Q(\alpha).
\]
:::

Let
\[
\Gamma:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q).
\]

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Via its action on the $n$ roots of $f$, the group $\Gamma$ is the full symmetric group $S_n$.


::: pf-proof

Because $\mathbb Q$ has characteristic $0$, the irreducible polynomial $f$ is separable and has $n$ distinct roots in $L$. An automorphism of the splitting field is determined by its action on those roots, so the permutation action gives an injection
\[
\Gamma\hookrightarrow S_n.
\]
Since $L/\mathbb Q$ is Galois,
\[
|\Gamma|=[L:\mathbb Q]=n!=|S_n|.
\]
Hence the injection is an isomorphism onto $S_n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If
\[
H:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q(\alpha)),
\qquad
J:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q(\alpha^4)),
\]
then $H\subseteq J$, and under step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} the subgroup $H$ is a point stabilizer $S_{n-1}$.


::: pf-proof

The inclusion
\[
\mathbb Q(\alpha^4)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)
\]
reverses under Galois correspondence, giving $H\subseteq J$. An automorphism of $L$ lies in $H$ exactly when it fixes the root $\alpha$. Thus, in the permutation action on the roots, $H$ is exactly the stabilizer of the point $\alpha$, hence is isomorphic to $S_{n-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Any subgroup of $S_n$ containing $H$ is either $H$ or all of $S_n$.


::: pf-proof

Let $K$ be a subgroup with $H\subsetneq K\subseteq S_n$. Since $K$ properly contains the stabilizer of $\alpha$, some element of $K$ moves $\alpha$ to another root. The subgroup $H$ acts transitively on the other $n-1$ roots, so the $K$-orbit of $\alpha$ is all $n$ roots. Thus $K$ is transitive.

The stabilizer of $\alpha$ inside $K$ is
\[
K_\alpha=K\cap(S_n)_\alpha=K\cap H=H.
\]
By orbit--stabilizer,
\[
|K|=n|H|=n(n-1)!=n!,
\]
so $K=S_n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has $\alpha^4\notin\mathbb Q$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose instead that
\[
q:=\alpha^4\in\mathbb Q.
\]
Then the minimal polynomial $f$ of $\alpha$ divides $x^4-q$, so $n\leq4$. Since $n\geq3$, only $n=3$ or $n=4$ are possible.

If $n=3$, the quotient of $x^4-q$ by the irreducible cubic $f$ is linear over $\mathbb Q$, so $x^4-q$ has a rational root $r$. Then $q=r^4$ and
\[
x^4-q=(x-r)(x+r)(x^2+r^2),
\]
which has no irreducible cubic factor, a contradiction.

If $n=4$, the monic minimal polynomial is
\[
f(x)=x^4-q.
\]
Its roots are
\[
\alpha,\ -\alpha,\ i\alpha,\ -i\alpha,
\]
so its splitting field is contained in $\mathbb Q(\alpha,i)$. Hence
\[
[L:\mathbb Q]
\leq[\mathbb Q(\alpha,i):\mathbb Q]
\leq4\cdot2=8,
\]
contradicting the hypothesis
\[
[L:\mathbb Q]=4!=24.
\]
Thus $\alpha^4\notin\mathbb Q$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

$J=H$.


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, either $J=H$ or $J=S_n=\Gamma$. If $J=\Gamma$, its fixed field would be $\mathbb Q$, so
\[
\mathbb Q(\alpha^4)=\mathbb Q,
\]
contradicting step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. Therefore $J=H$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By the Galois correspondence, equal subgroups have equal fixed fields. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} therefore gives
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Q(\alpha^4)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)}.
\]

:::
