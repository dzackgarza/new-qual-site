::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a real $m\times n$ matrix and let $\norm{\cdot}$ denote the Euclidean norm.
Show that there is a unique nonnegative scalar $\sigma$ and possibly nonunique unit vectors
$$
u\in\RR^n,
\qquad
v\in\RR^m
$$
such that

1. $$
   \norm{Mx}\le\sigma\norm{x}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\RR^n;
   $$
2. $$
   Mu=\sigma v;
   $$
3. $$
   M^Tv=\sigma u.
   $$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The number
$$
\sigma:=\max_{\norm{x}=1}\norm{Mx}
$$
exists and is nonnegative.


::: pf-proof

The unit sphere in $\RR^n$ is compact, and the function
$$
x\longmapsto\norm{Mx}
$$
is continuous. Hence it attains a maximum there. Norms are nonnegative, so $\sigma\geq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $x\in\RR^n$,
$$
\norm{Mx}\leq\sigma\norm{x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The inequality is trivial for $x=0$. If $x\neq0$, then $x/\norm{x}$ is a unit vector, so by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\norm{M\frac{x}{\norm{x}}}\leq\sigma.
$$
Multiplying by $\norm{x}$ gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $\sigma>0$, there are unit vectors $u\in\RR^n$ and $v\in\RR^m$ such that
$$
Mu=\sigma v
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
M^Tv=\sigma u.
$$


::: pf-proof

Choose a unit vector $u$ at which the maximum in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} is attained, so
$$
\norm{Mu}=\sigma.
$$
Define
$$
v:=\frac{Mu}{\sigma}.
$$
Then $\norm{v}=1$ and $Mu=\sigma v$. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the operator norm of $M$ is at most $\sigma$, and therefore for every unit $y\in\RR^m$,
$$
\norm{M^Ty}
=\sup_{\norm{x}=1}\abs{\langle M^Ty,x\rangle}
=\sup_{\norm{x}=1}\abs{\langle y,Mx\rangle}
\leq\sigma.
$$
In particular $\norm{M^Tv}\leq\sigma$. On the other hand,
$$
\langle M^Tv,u\rangle
=\langle v,Mu\rangle
=\langle v,\sigma v\rangle
=\sigma.
$$
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
\sigma
=\langle M^Tv,u\rangle
\leq\norm{M^Tv}\,\norm{u}
\leq\sigma.
$$
Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz therefore holds, and since the inner product is positive, $M^Tv$ is the positive scalar multiple $\sigma u$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $\sigma=0$, any unit vectors $u\in\RR^n$ and $v\in\RR^m$ satisfy
$$
Mu=\sigma v,
\qquad
M^Tv=\sigma u.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $\norm{Mx}\leq0$ for every $x$, so $M=0$. Hence also $M^T=0$, and both displayed equations reduce to $0=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The scalar $\sigma$ is unique.


::: pf-proof

Suppose a nonnegative number $\tau$ and unit vectors $u,v$ satisfy the three conditions in the problem. Condition 1 implies
$$
\max_{\norm{x}=1}\norm{Mx}\leq\tau.
$$
Condition 2 gives
$$
\tau=\norm{\tau v}=\norm{Mu}
\leq\max_{\norm{x}=1}\norm{Mx}.
$$
Thus
$$
\tau=\max_{\norm{x}=1}\norm{Mx}=\sigma.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} establish existence of $\sigma,u,v$ with all three properties, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} establishes uniqueness of $\sigma$.

:::
