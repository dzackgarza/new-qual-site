::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a real $m\times n$ matrix and let $\|\cdot\|$ denote the Euclidean norm.
Show that there is a unique nonnegative scalar $\sigma$ and possibly nonunique unit vectors
\[
u\in\mathbb R^n,
\qquad
v\in\mathbb R^m
\]
such that

1. \[
   \|Mx\|\le\sigma\|x\|\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb R^n;
   \]
2. \[
   Mu=\sigma v;
   \]
3. \[
   M^Tv=\sigma u.
   \]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The number
\[
\sigma:=\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|
\]
exists and is nonnegative.

::: {.proof}
The unit sphere in $\mathbb R^n$ is compact, and the function
\[
x\longmapsto\|Mx\|
\]
is continuous. Hence it attains a maximum there. Norms are nonnegative, so $\sigma\geq0$.
:::

<1>2. For every $x\in\mathbb R^n$,
\[
\|Mx\|\leq\sigma\|x\|.
\]

::: {.proof}
The inequality is trivial for $x=0$. If $x\neq0$, then $x/\|x\|$ is a unit vector, so by step <1>1,
\[
\left\|M\frac{x}{\|x\|}\right\|\leq\sigma.
\]
Multiplying by $\|x\|$ gives the claim.
:::

<1>3. If $\sigma>0$, there are unit vectors $u\in\mathbb R^n$ and $v\in\mathbb R^m$ such that
\[
Mu=\sigma v
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
M^Tv=\sigma u.
\]

::: {.proof}
Choose a unit vector $u$ at which the maximum in step <1>1 is attained, so
\[
\|Mu\|=\sigma.
\]
Define
\[
v:=\frac{Mu}{\sigma}.
\]
Then $\|v\|=1$ and $Mu=\sigma v$. By step <1>2, the operator norm of $M$ is at most $\sigma$, and therefore for every unit $y\in\mathbb R^m$,
\[
\|M^Ty\|
=\sup_{\|x\|=1}|\langle M^Ty,x\rangle|
=\sup_{\|x\|=1}|\langle y,Mx\rangle|
\leq\sigma.
\]
In particular $\|M^Tv\|\leq\sigma$. On the other hand,
\[
\langle M^Tv,u\rangle
=\langle v,Mu\rangle
=\langle v,\sigma v\rangle
=\sigma.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\sigma
=\langle M^Tv,u\rangle
\leq\|M^Tv\|\,\|u\|
\leq\sigma.
\]
Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz therefore holds, and since the inner product is positive, $M^Tv$ is the positive scalar multiple $\sigma u$.
:::

<1>4. If $\sigma=0$, any unit vectors $u\in\mathbb R^n$ and $v\in\mathbb R^m$ satisfy
\[
Mu=\sigma v,
\qquad
M^Tv=\sigma u.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $\|Mx\|\leq0$ for every $x$, so $M=0$. Hence also $M^T=0$, and both displayed equations reduce to $0=0$.
:::

<1>5. The scalar $\sigma$ is unique.

::: {.proof}
Suppose a nonnegative number $\tau$ and unit vectors $u,v$ satisfy the three conditions in the problem. Condition 1 implies
\[
\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|\leq\tau.
\]
Condition 2 gives
\[
\tau=\|\tau v\|=\|Mu\|
\leq\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|.
\]
Thus
\[
\tau=\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|=\sigma.
\]
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 through <1>4 establish existence of $\sigma,u,v$ with all three properties, and step <1>5 establishes uniqueness of $\sigma$.
:::
:::
