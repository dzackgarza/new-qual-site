::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a real $m\times n$ matrix and let $\|\cdot\|$ denote the Euclidean norm.
Show that there is a unique nonnegative scalar $\sigma$ and possibly nonunique unit vectors
\[
u\in\RR^n,
\qquad
v\in\RR^m
\]
such that

1. \[
   \|Mx\|\le\sigma\|x\|\qquad\text{for all }x\in\RR^n;
   \]
2. \[
   Mu=\sigma v;
   \]
3. \[
   M^Tv=\sigma u.
   \]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The number
\[
\sigma:=\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|
\]
exists and is nonnegative.


::: pf-proof

The unit sphere in $\RR^n$ is compact, and the function
\[
x\longmapsto\|Mx\|
\]
is continuous. Hence it attains a maximum there. Norms are nonnegative, so $\sigma\geq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $x\in\RR^n$,
\[
\|Mx\|\leq\sigma\|x\|.
\]


::: pf-proof

The inequality is trivial for $x=0$. If $x\neq0$, then $x/\|x\|$ is a unit vector, so by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
\[
\left\|M\frac{x}{\|x\|}\right\|\leq\sigma.
\]
Multiplying by $\|x\|$ gives the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $\sigma>0$, there are unit vectors $u\in\RR^n$ and $v\in\RR^m$ such that
\[
Mu=\sigma v
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
M^Tv=\sigma u.
\]


::: pf-proof

Choose a unit vector $u$ at which the maximum in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} is attained, so
\[
\|Mu\|=\sigma.
\]
Define
\[
v:=\frac{Mu}{\sigma}.
\]
Then $\|v\|=1$ and $Mu=\sigma v$. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the operator norm of $M$ is at most $\sigma$, and therefore for every unit $y\in\RR^m$,
\[
\|M^Ty\|
=\sup_{\|x\|=1}|\langle M^Ty,x\rangle|
=\sup_{\|x\|=1}|\langle y,Mx\rangle|
\leq\sigma.
\]
In particular $\|M^Tv\|\leq\sigma$. On the other hand,
\[
\langle M^Tv,u\rangle
=\langle v,Mu\rangle
=\langle v,\sigma v\rangle
=\sigma.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\sigma
=\langle M^Tv,u\rangle
\leq\|M^Tv\|\,\|u\|
\leq\sigma.
\]
Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz therefore holds, and since the inner product is positive, $M^Tv$ is the positive scalar multiple $\sigma u$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $\sigma=0$, any unit vectors $u\in\RR^n$ and $v\in\RR^m$ satisfy
\[
Mu=\sigma v,
\qquad
M^Tv=\sigma u.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $\|Mx\|\leq0$ for every $x$, so $M=0$. Hence also $M^T=0$, and both displayed equations reduce to $0=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The scalar $\sigma$ is unique.


::: pf-proof

Suppose a nonnegative number $\tau$ and unit vectors $u,v$ satisfy the three conditions in the problem. Condition 1 implies
\[
\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|\leq\tau.
\]
Condition 2 gives
\[
\tau=\|\tau v\|=\|Mu\|
\leq\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|.
\]
Thus
\[
\tau=\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|=\sigma.
\]

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} establish existence of $\sigma,u,v$ with all three properties, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} establishes uniqueness of $\sigma$.

:::
