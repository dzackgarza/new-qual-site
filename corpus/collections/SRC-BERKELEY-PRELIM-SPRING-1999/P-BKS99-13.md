::: {.problem}
Let $(b_k)_{k\ge1}$ be a nonincreasing sequence of real numbers such that
$$
b_k\longrightarrow0.
$$
Prove that
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty b_k z^k
$$
converges for every complex number $z$ satisfying
$$
\abs{z}\le1,
\qquad z\ne1.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Every $b_k$ is nonnegative, and the sequence $(b_k)$ is bounded.


::: pf-proof

If $b_j<0$ for some $j$, then monotonicity would give
$$
b_k\leq b_j<0
$$
for every $k\geq j$, contradicting $b_k\to0$. Hence $b_k\geq0$ for all $k$. Since the sequence is nonincreasing,
$$
0\leq b_k\leq b_1,
$$
so it is bounded.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $\abs{z}<1$, then
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty b_k z^k
$$
converges absolutely.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \abs{b_k z^k}
\leq b_1\sum_{k=1}^\infty \abs{z}^k,
$$
and the geometric series on the right converges for $\abs{z}<1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $\abs{z}=1$ and $z\neq1$, then for every $N\leq M$,
$$
\abs{\sum_{k=N}^M b_kz^k}
\leq \frac{2b_N}{\abs{1-z}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $j\geq N$, set
$$
S_j:=\sum_{k=N}^j z^k.
$$
Since $z\neq1$ and $\abs{z}=1$,
$$
S_j
=z^N\frac{1-z^{j-N+1}}{1-z},
\qquad
\abs{S_j}\leq\frac2{\abs{1-z}}.
$$
Summation by parts gives
$$
\sum_{k=N}^M b_kz^k
=b_M S_M
+\sum_{k=N}^{M-1}(b_k-b_{k+1})S_k.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, all coefficients in the second term are nonnegative. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{\sum_{k=N}^M b_kz^k}
&\leq
\frac2{\abs{1-z}}
\left(
b_M+\sum_{k=N}^{M-1}(b_k-b_{k+1})
\right)\\
&=\frac{2b_N}{\abs{1-z}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $\abs{z}=1$ and $z\neq1$, the series converges.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, every tail from $N$ to $M$ has absolute value at most
$$
\frac{2b_N}{\abs{1-z}},
$$
which tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$. Hence the partial sums satisfy the Cauchy criterion.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} handles $\abs{z}<1$, and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} handles the remaining case $\abs{z}=1$, $z\neq1$.

:::
