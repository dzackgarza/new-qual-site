::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood of $a\in\mathbb C$.
Prove that either $f$ is constant on some neighborhood of $a$, or there exist an integer $n>0$ and real numbers $\delta,\varepsilon>0$ such that, for every $b\in\mathbb C$ satisfying
\[
0<|b-f(a)|<\varepsilon,
\]
the equation
\[
f(z)=b
\]
has exactly $n$ roots in
\[
\{z\in\mathbb C:|z-a|<\delta\}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Set
\[
h(z):=f(z)-f(a).
\]

<1>1. Either $f$ is constant on a neighborhood of $a$, or there are an integer $n\geq1$ and a holomorphic function $g$ near $a$ such that
\[
h(z)=(z-a)^n g(z),
\qquad
g(a)\neq0.
\]

::: {.proof}
Expand $h$ in its Taylor series at $a$. If every positive-degree coefficient vanishes, then $h=0$ on a disk about $a$, so $f$ is constant there. Otherwise let $n\geq1$ be the least index with nonzero Taylor coefficient. Factoring $(z-a)^n$ from the Taylor series gives the stated holomorphic function $g$, whose value at $a$ is precisely that first nonzero coefficient.
:::

<1>2. In the nonconstant case, there is $\delta>0$ such that on the closed disk
\[
\overline D_\delta:=\{z:|z-a|\leq\delta\},
\]
one has
\[
g(z)\neq0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
ng(z)+(z-a)g'(z)\neq0.
\]

::: {.proof}
At $z=a$ the two displayed quantities are $g(a)$ and $ng(a)$, both nonzero by step <1>1. By continuity, both remain nonzero on some sufficiently small closed disk centered at $a$, which may also be chosen inside the neighborhood on which $f$ is holomorphic.
:::

<1>3. On $\overline D_\delta$, the only zero of $h$ is $a$, of multiplicity $n$, and the only zero of $f'$ is $a$.

::: {.proof}
The factorization from step <1>1 and the first nonvanishing condition in step <1>2 show that $h$ vanishes only at $a$, with multiplicity $n$. Differentiating gives
\[
f'(z)=h'(z)
=(z-a)^{n-1}\bigl(ng(z)+(z-a)g'(z)\bigr).
\]
The second nonvanishing condition in step <1>2 therefore shows that $f'$ has no zero in $\overline D_\delta\setminus\{a\}$.
:::

<1>4. Let
\[
\varepsilon:=
\min_{|z-a|=\delta}|h(z)|.
\]
Then $\varepsilon>0$, and for every $b$ with
\[
0<|b-f(a)|<\varepsilon,
\]
the equation $f(z)=b$ has exactly $n$ roots in $D_\delta$, counted with multiplicity.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, $h$ has no zero on the boundary circle, so compactness gives $\varepsilon>0$. Put
\[
w:=b-f(a).
\]
On $|z-a|=\delta$ one has
\[
|w|<\varepsilon\leq|h(z)|.
\]
Rouché's theorem therefore implies that $h$ and $h-w$ have the same number of zeros in $D_\delta$, counted with multiplicity. By step <1>3, $h$ has exactly one zero there, namely $a$ with multiplicity $n$. Since
\[
h(z)-w=f(z)-b,
\]
the equation $f(z)=b$ has exactly $n$ roots counted with multiplicity.
:::

<1>5. The $n$ roots in step <1>4 are all simple, hence there are exactly $n$ distinct roots.

::: {.proof}
Because $w\neq0$, none of the roots of $h-w$ is $a$. By step <1>3, $f'(z)\neq0$ at every point of $D_\delta\setminus\{a\}$. Thus every root of $f(z)-b$ is simple. The multiplicity count in step <1>4 is therefore also the number of distinct roots.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the locally constant alternative. In the other case, steps <1>2 through <1>5 provide $n$, $\delta$, and $\varepsilon$ with the required property.
:::
