::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood of $a\in\CC$.
Prove that either $f$ is constant on some neighborhood of $a$, or there exist an integer $n>0$ and real numbers $\delta,\varepsilon>0$ such that, for every $b\in\CC$ satisfying
$$
0<\abs{b-f(a)}<\varepsilon,
$$
the equation
$$
f(z)=b
$$
has exactly $n$ roots in
$$
\{z\in\CC:\abs{z-a}<\delta\}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Set
$$
h(z):=f(z)-f(a).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Either $f$ is constant on a neighborhood of $a$, or there are an integer $n\geq1$ and a holomorphic function $g$ near $a$ such that
$$
h(z)=(z-a)^n g(z),
\qquad
g(a)\neq0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Expand $h$ in its Taylor series at $a$. If every positive-degree coefficient vanishes, then $h=0$ on a disk about $a$, so $f$ is constant there. Otherwise let $n\geq1$ be the least index with nonzero Taylor coefficient. Factoring $(z-a)^n$ from the Taylor series gives the stated holomorphic function $g$, whose value at $a$ is precisely that first nonzero coefficient.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

In the nonconstant case, there is $\delta>0$ such that on the closed disk
$$
\overline D_\delta:=\{z:\abs{z-a}\leq\delta\},
$$
one has
$$
g(z)\neq0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
ng(z)+(z-a)g'(z)\neq0.
$$


::: pf-proof

At $z=a$ the two displayed quantities are $g(a)$ and $ng(a)$, both nonzero by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. By continuity, both remain nonzero on some sufficiently small closed disk centered at $a$, which may also be chosen inside the neighborhood on which $f$ is holomorphic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

On $\overline D_\delta$, the only zero of $h$ is $a$, of multiplicity $n$, and the only zero of $f'$ is $a$.


::: pf-proof

The factorization from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and the first nonvanishing condition in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} show that $h$ vanishes only at $a$, with multiplicity $n$. Differentiating gives
$$
f'(z)=h'(z)
=(z-a)^{n-1}\bigl(ng(z)+(z-a)g'(z)\bigr).
$$
The second nonvanishing condition in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} therefore shows that $f'$ has no zero in $\overline D_\delta\setminus\{a\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Let
$$
\varepsilon:=
\min_{\abs{z-a}=\delta}\abs{h(z)}.
$$
Then $\varepsilon>0$, and for every $b$ with
$$
0<\abs{b-f(a)}<\varepsilon,
$$
the equation $f(z)=b$ has exactly $n$ roots in $D_\delta$, counted with multiplicity.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $h$ has no zero on the boundary circle, so compactness gives $\varepsilon>0$. Put
$$
w:=b-f(a).
$$
On $\abs{z-a}=\delta$ one has
$$
\abs{w}<\varepsilon\leq\abs{h(z)}.
$$
Rouché's theorem therefore implies that $h$ and $h-w$ have the same number of zeros in $D_\delta$, counted with multiplicity. By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $h$ has exactly one zero there, namely $a$ with multiplicity $n$. Since
$$
h(z)-w=f(z)-b,
$$
the equation $f(z)=b$ has exactly $n$ roots counted with multiplicity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The $n$ roots in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} are all simple, hence there are exactly $n$ distinct roots.


::: pf-proof

Because $w\neq0$, none of the roots of $h-w$ is $a$. By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $f'(z)\neq0$ at every point of $D_\delta\setminus\{a\}$. Thus every root of $f(z)-b$ is simple. The multiplicity count in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is therefore also the number of distinct roots.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives the locally constant alternative. In the other case, steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} provide $n$, $\delta$, and $\varepsilon$ with the required property.

:::
