::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite group with identity $e$. Suppose that for every pair $a,b\in G\setminus\{e\}$ there is an automorphism $\sigma$ of $G$ such that
$$
\sigma(a)=b.
$$
Prove that $G$ is abelian.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $G\neq\{e\}$, then every nonidentity element of $G$ has the same prime order $p$.


::: pf-proof

Automorphisms preserve element order. By the transitivity hypothesis, all elements of $G\setminus\{e\}$ therefore have one common order, say $m$. If $m$ were composite, choose a prime divisor $p$ of $m$ and an element $a\neq e$. Then
$$
a^{m/p}\neq e
$$
and has order $p<m$, contradicting the common-order property. Hence $m=p$ is prime.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

$G$ is a finite $p$-group.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, every nonidentity element has order $p$. If a prime $q\neq p$ divided $\cardinality{G}$, Cauchy's theorem would produce an element of order $q$, a contradiction. Thus every prime divisor of $\cardinality{G}$ is $p$, so $\cardinality{G}$ is a power of $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Every nonidentity element of $G$ lies in $Z(G)$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the finite $p$-group $G$ has nontrivial center. Choose
$$
z\in Z(G)\setminus\{e\}.
$$
Let $a\in G\setminus\{e\}$ be arbitrary. By hypothesis, some automorphism $\sigma$ satisfies $\sigma(z)=a$. The center is characteristic: every automorphism sends $Z(G)$ to itself. Hence
$$
a=\sigma(z)\in Z(G).
$$
Thus every nonidentity element is central.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

If $G$ is trivial, it is abelian. Otherwise step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows that every element of $G$ lies in its center, so $G=Z(G)$ and $G$ is abelian.

:::
