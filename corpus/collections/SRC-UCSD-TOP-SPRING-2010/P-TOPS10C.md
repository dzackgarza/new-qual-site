::: {.problem}
Let $n \geq 2$ be a positive integer, and let $k$ be in the range $0 < k < n$.
Let $X = \mathbb{CP}^n / \mathbb{CP}^k$ be the quotient space obtained from $\mathbb{CP}^n$ by identifying its subspace $\mathbb{CP}^k$ to a point.
Calculate the integral cohomology ring of $X$.
(You may assume the cohomology ring of $\mathbb{CP}^n$.)
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $q:\mathbb{CP}^n\to X=\mathbb{CP}^n/\mathbb{CP}^k$ be the quotient map, and let $u\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)$ be the standard generator.
::: {.proof}
Recall $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[u]/(u^{n+1})$.
:::

<1>2. For positive degrees,
$$
\widetilde H^*(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n,\mathbb{CP}^k;\mathbb Z).
$$
Hence
$$
H^{2j}(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\quad(k+1\le j\le n),
$$
and all other positive-degree groups vanish.
::: {.proof}
Reduced cohomology of the quotient is relative cohomology of the pair. In the long exact sequence, restriction $H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^n)\to H^{2j}(\mathbb{CP}^k)$ is an isomorphism for $j\le k$ and the target is zero for $j>k$.
:::

<1>3. For $j=k+1,\ldots,n$, let $x_j\in H^{2j}(X)$ be the unique class satisfying
$$
q^*x_j=u^j.
$$
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, $q^*$ identifies the positive-degree cohomology of $X$ with the ideal generated by $u^{k+1}$ inside $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n)$.
:::

<1>4. The products are
$$
x_i x_j=
\begin{cases}
x_{i+j},&i+j\le n,\\0,&i+j>n.
\end{cases}
$$
::: {.proof}
Pulling back by $q$ gives $u^iu^j=u^{i+j}$, and $q^*$ is injective in every positive degree. Powers beyond $u^n$ vanish.
:::

<1>5. Therefore the cohomology ring is
$$
\boxed{H^*(X;\mathbb Z)\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus u^{k+1}\mathbb Z[u]/(u^{n+1}),}
$$
where the second summand is viewed as a nonunital ideal and the first summand supplies the unit.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the additive description in <1>2 together with the multiplication in <1>4.
:::
:::
