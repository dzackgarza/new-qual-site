::: problem
How many distinct double covers does $\mathbb{RP}^3 \times S^1$ have?
Can you identify any of them?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. We have
$$
\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^3\times S^1)\cong \mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z.
$$
::: {.proof}
Use the product formula for fundamental groups, with $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^3)=\mathbb Z/2$ and $\pi_1(S^1)=\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>2. Connected double covers correspond to index-$2$ subgroups, equivalently kernels of nonzero homomorphisms
$$
\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2.
$$
There are exactly three.
::: {.proof}
A homomorphism is determined by the images of the two standard generators, each independently in $\mathbb Z/2$. Of the four possibilities, exactly three are nonzero; every nonzero map onto $\mathbb Z/2$ has index-$2$ kernel.
:::

<1>3. The three kernels are those of
$$
(\epsilon,n)\mapsto\epsilon,\qquad
(\epsilon,n)\mapsto n\bmod2,\qquad
(\epsilon,n)\mapsto\epsilon+n\bmod2.
$$
::: {.proof}
These are the three nonzero elements of $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/2)$.
:::

<1>4. The first gives the product covering
$$
S^3\times S^1\longrightarrow\mathbb{RP}^3\times S^1,
$$
and the second gives
$$
\mathbb{RP}^3\times S^1\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\times(z\mapsto z^2)}\mathbb{RP}^3\times S^1.
$$
::: {.proof}
Their induced subgroups are respectively $0\oplus\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Z/2\oplus2\mathbb Z$.
:::

<1>5. The third is the mixed double cover associated to the subgroup
$$
\{(\epsilon,n):\epsilon\equiv n\pmod2\}\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
It can be modeled as the mapping torus of the antipodal map on $S^3$, with its evident projection to $\mathbb{RP}^3\times S^1$.
::: {.proof}
The subgroup is generated by $(1,1)$. Pulling the universal cover $S^3\times\mathbb R$ down by this subgroup identifies $(x,t)$ with $(-x,t+1)$, which is precisely that mapping torus.
:::

<1>6. Thus there are
$$
\boxed{3\text{ connected double covers}.}
$$
If disconnected covers are allowed, there is one additional trivial double cover $X\sqcup X\to X$, for a total of $4$.
::: {.proof}
The connected classification is <1>2--<1>5. The zero homomorphism corresponds to the disconnected trivial two-sheeted covering.
:::
:::
