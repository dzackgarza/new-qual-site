    Pairwise surjectivity lets one lift x in G_1 with second coordinate
    1 and y in G_1 with third coordinate 1. Their coordinatewise
    commutator is ([x,y],1,1). Since G_1 is generated by commutators,
    A contains the pure G_1 factor; cyclically it contains the pure
    G_2 and G_3 factors, hence the whole direct product.

::: {.problem}
Let $G_1,G_2,G_3$ be finite groups, each generated by its commutators. Let
\[
A\le G_1\times G_2\times G_3
\]
be a subgroup whose natural projections onto each of
\[
G_1\times G_2,\qquad G_1\times G_3,\qquad G_2\times G_3
\]
are surjective.
Prove that
\[
A=G_1\times G_2\times G_3.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. For every commutator $[x,y]$ in $G_1$,
$$
([x,y],1,1)\in A.
$$

::: {.proof}
By surjectivity of the projection
$$
A\longrightarrow G_1\times G_2,
$$
there is an element
$$
u=(x,1,u_3)\in A.
$$
By surjectivity of the projection
$$
A\longrightarrow G_1\times G_3,
$$
there is an element
$$
v=(y,v_2,1)\in A.
$$
Since $A$ is a subgroup, it contains their commutator. Commutators in a
direct product are computed coordinatewise, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
[u,v]
&=
([x,y],[1,v_2],[u_3,1])\\
&=
([x,y],1,1).
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
G_1\times\{1\}\times\{1\}\leq A.
$$

::: {.proof}
By hypothesis, $G_1$ is generated by its commutators. Step <1>1 shows
that the pure first-coordinate copy in $A$ contains every such
commutator. Since $A$ is a subgroup, it therefore contains every finite
product of these elements and hence every element of
$$
G_1\times\{1\}\times\{1\}.
$$
:::

<1>3. One has
$$
\{1\}\times G_2\times\{1\}\leq A.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $x,y\in G_2$. Surjectivity onto $G_1\times G_2$ gives
$$
u=(1,x,u_3)\in A,
$$
and surjectivity onto $G_2\times G_3$ gives
$$
v=(v_1,y,1)\in A.
$$
Then
$$
[u,v]=(1,[x,y],1).
$$
Because $G_2$ is generated by its commutators, the same argument as in
step <1>2 gives the claimed inclusion.
:::

<1>4. One has
$$
\{1\}\times\{1\}\times G_3\leq A.
$$

::: {.proof}
Let $x,y\in G_3$. Surjectivity onto $G_1\times G_3$ gives
$$
u=(1,u_2,x)\in A,
$$
and surjectivity onto $G_2\times G_3$ gives
$$
v=(v_1,1,y)\in A.
$$
Then
$$
[u,v]=(1,1,[x,y]).
$$
Since $G_3$ is generated by its commutators, this implies the claimed
inclusion.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
$$
\boxed{A=G_1\times G_2\times G_3}.
$$

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>2--<1>4, for arbitrary
$$
(g_1,g_2,g_3)\in G_1\times G_2\times G_3,
$$
the three elements
$$
(g_1,1,1),
\qquad
(1,g_2,1),
\qquad
(1,1,g_3)
$$
all belong to $A$. Their product is $(g_1,g_2,g_3)$, so the whole
direct product is contained in $A$. The reverse inclusion is part of
the hypothesis that $A$ is a subgroup of that direct product.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required equality.
:::
