::: problem
Let $M_g^2$ be the compact oriented surface of genus $g$.

Show that there exists a continuous map $f : M_g^2 \to S^2$ which is not homotopic to a constant map.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Choose an embedded closed disk $D\subset M_g^2$.
::: {.proof}
Because $M_g^2$ is a surface, every point has a coordinate neighborhood homeomorphic to an open disk.
Taking a smaller closed disk inside such a coordinate neighborhood gives an embedded copy
\[
D\cong D^2.
\]
:::

<1>2. Collapse the complement of the interior of $D$ to a point.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
A=M_g^2\setminus \operatorname{int}(D)
\]
and let
\[
q:M_g^2\longrightarrow M_g^2/A
\]
be the quotient map.
Since every point of $A$ becomes one point and the only part left uncollapsed is $\operatorname{int}(D)$, there is a natural homeomorphism
\[
M_g^2/A\cong D/\partial D.
\]
The quotient $D^2/\partial D^2$ is homeomorphic to $S^2$.
Choose such a homeomorphism
\[
h:M_g^2/A\xrightarrow{\cong}S^2
\]
and define
\[
f=h\circ q:M_g^2\longrightarrow S^2.
\]
:::

<1>3. The map $f$ has degree $\pm1$.
::: {.proof}
Let $y\in S^2$ be different from the point corresponding to the collapsed set $A$.
Then
\[
f^{-1}(y)
\]
consists of exactly one point $x\in\operatorname{int}(D)$, because $q$ restricts to a homeomorphism
\[
\operatorname{int}(D)
\xrightarrow{\cong}
(M_g^2/A)\setminus\{q(A)\}.
\]
Choose the orientation of the homeomorphism $h$ on this disk either way.
The local degree of $f$ at the unique point $x$ is then $+1$ or $-1$, and therefore
\[
\deg f=\pm1.
\]
In particular,
\[
\deg f\ne0.
\]
:::

<1>4. Hence $f$ is not homotopic to a constant map.
::: {.proof}
For a continuous map between closed oriented connected surfaces, the induced map on top homology is multiplication by the degree.
Thus <1>3 gives
\[
f_*:H_2(M_g^2;\ZZ)\longrightarrow H_2(S^2;\ZZ),
\qquad
f_*([M_g^2])=(\deg f)[S^2]\ne0.
\]
A constant map induces the zero map on $H_2$, and homotopic maps induce the same map on homology.
Therefore $f$ cannot be homotopic to a constant map.
:::
:::
