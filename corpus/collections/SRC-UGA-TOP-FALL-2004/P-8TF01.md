::: problem
Let $f,g:S^n\to S^n$, where $f(x)\neq g(x)$ for all $x$.
Show that $f$ is homotopic to $a\circ g$, where $a(x)=-x$ is the antipodal map.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Define the straight-line interpolation from $f$ to $-g$ in $\RR^{n+1}$ by
\[
F(x,t)=(1-t)f(x)-tg(x).
\]
::: {.proof}
Here
\[
F:S^n\times[0,1]\longrightarrow\RR^{n+1}
\]
is continuous, and
\[
F(x,0)=f(x),
\qquad
F(x,1)=-g(x).
\]
To obtain a homotopy through maps into $S^n$, it remains only to prove that $F$ never vanishes.
:::

<1>2. For every $(x,t)\in S^n\times[0,1]$, one has $F(x,t)\neq0$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
(1-t)f(x)-tg(x)=0.
\]
At $t=0$ or $t=1$ this is impossible because $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ lie on the unit sphere.
Hence $0<t<1$, and
\[
(1-t)f(x)=tg(x).
\]
Taking Euclidean norms and using
\[
\|f(x)\|=\|g(x)\|=1
\]
gives
\[
1-t=t,
\]
so $t=\frac12$.
The displayed vector equation then becomes
\[
f(x)=g(x),
\]
contrary to the hypothesis.
Thus $F(x,t)$ is always nonzero.
:::

<1>3. Normalize $F$ to obtain the required homotopy.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, the formula
\[
H(x,t)
=
\frac{(1-t)f(x)-tg(x)}
{\|(1-t)f(x)-tg(x)\|}
\]
defines a continuous map
\[
H:S^n\times[0,1]\longrightarrow S^n.
\]
At the endpoints,
\[
H(x,0)=f(x)
\]
and
\[
H(x,1)=-g(x)=a(g(x)).
\]
Therefore
\[
f\simeq a\circ g.
\]
:::
:::
