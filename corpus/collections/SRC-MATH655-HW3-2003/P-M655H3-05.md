::: {.problem}
Let f be an entire function such that

$$
| f ( z ) | \leq A | z | ^ { k }
$$

for all $z \in \CC$ when $k\ge0$, and for all $z\ne0$ when $k<0$, for some constant $A>0$ and integer $k$. Show that $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most $\max\{0,k\}$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The source says “degree $\max\{0,k\}$,” but the valid conclusion is “degree at most $\max\{0,k\}$”: lower-degree polynomials also satisfy such a growth bound. For $k<0$, the source's expression $|z|^k$ is undefined at $z=0$, so the bound is interpreted on $\CC\setminus\{0\}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $k=0$, then $f$ is constant.


::: pf-proof

The hypothesis becomes
$$
|f(z)|\le A
$$
for every $z\in\CC$. Thus $f$ is a bounded entire function. Liouville's
theorem implies that $f$ is constant.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $k<0$, then $f\equiv0$.


::: pf-proof

For $|z|\ge1$,
$$
|f(z)|\le A|z|^k\le A
$$
because $k<0$. On the compact disk $|z|\le1$, continuity of $f$ makes
$|f|$ bounded. Hence $f$ is bounded on all of $\CC$, so Liouville's theorem
shows that $f$ is constant, say $f\equiv c$.

Letting $|z|\to\infty$ in
$$
|c|\le A|z|^k
$$
gives $|c|=0$, since $|z|^k\to0$. Thus $c=0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

It remains to consider $k>0$. Write the entire power series
$$
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n z^n.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every entire function has a globally convergent Taylor series centered at
$0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every $R>0$ and every $n\ge0$,
$$
|a_n|
\le
A R^{k-n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Cauchy's estimate on the circle $|z|=R$ gives
$$
|a_n|
\le
\frac{1}{R^n}
\max_{|z|=R}|f(z)|.
$$
By the growth hypothesis,
$$
\max_{|z|=R}|f(z)|
\le
A R^k.
$$
Combining the two inequalities gives
$$
|a_n|
\le
A R^{k-n}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $n>k$, then
$$
a_n=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Fix $n>k$. The exponent $k-n$ is negative. Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} holds for every
$R>0$, so letting $R\to\infty$ gives
$$
|a_n|
\le
A R^{k-n}
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Hence $a_n=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If $k>0$, then $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, all Taylor coefficients $a_n$ with $n>k$ vanish. Therefore
$$
f(z)=a_0+a_1z+\cdots+a_kz^k.
$$
Some of the displayed coefficients may also vanish, so the degree is at most
$k$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

In all cases, $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most
$$
\boxed{\max\{0,k\}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} handles $k=0$, step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} handles $k<0$, and step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} handles
$k>0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is the corrected conclusion.

:::
