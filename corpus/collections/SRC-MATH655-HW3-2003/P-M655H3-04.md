::: {.problem}
Show that if
\[
P(z)=z^n+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0
\]
is a polynomial of degree $n\ge1$, then $|P(z)|\to\infty$ as $|z|\to\infty$.
In fact, show that if
\[
|z|\ge\max\{1,2n|a_{n-1}|,\ldots,2n|a_0|\},
\]
then
\[
|P(z)|\ge\frac{|z|^n}{2}.
\]
:::

Let
$$
M=
\max\{
1,
2n|a_{n-1}|,
\ldots,
2n|a_0|
\}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

If $|z|\geq M$, then for every $k=1,\ldots,n$,
$$
\frac{|a_{n-k}|}{|z|^k}
\leq
\frac1{2n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $|z|\geq M\geq1$,
$$
|z|^k\geq|z|.
$$
Also,
$$
|z|
\geq
M
\geq
2n|a_{n-k}|.
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{|a_{n-k}|}{|z|^k}
\leq
\frac{|a_{n-k}|}{|z|}
\leq
\frac1{2n}.
$$
If $a_{n-k}=0$, the same conclusion is immediate.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $|z|\geq M$, then
$$
\left|
\frac{a_{n-1}}z
+
\frac{a_{n-2}}{z^2}
+
\cdots
+
\frac{a_0}{z^n}
\right|
\leq
\frac12.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the triangle inequality and step <1>1,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{a_{n-k}}{z^k}
\right|
&\leq
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{|a_{n-k}|}{|z|^k}\\
&\leq
n\cdot\frac1{2n}\\
&=
\frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $|z|\geq M$, then
$$
\boxed{
|P(z)|
\geq
\frac{|z|^n}{2}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Factor
$$
P(z)
=
z^n
\left(
1
+
\frac{a_{n-1}}z
+
\cdots
+
\frac{a_0}{z^n}
\right).
$$
Hence by the reverse triangle inequality and step <1>2,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|P(z)|
&=
|z|^n
\left|
1
+
\frac{a_{n-1}}z
+
\cdots
+
\frac{a_0}{z^n}
\right|\\
&\geq
|z|^n
\left(
1
-
\left|
\frac{a_{n-1}}z
+
\cdots
+
\frac{a_0}{z^n}
\right|
\right)\\
&\geq
\frac{|z|^n}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
|P(z)|\longrightarrow\infty
$$
as $|z|\to\infty$.


::: pf-proof

For all sufficiently large $|z|$, step <1>3 gives
$$
|P(z)|
\geq
\frac{|z|^n}{2}.
$$
Since $n\geq1$,
$$
\frac{|z|^n}{2}\longrightarrow\infty
$$
as $|z|\to\infty$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>3 proves the quantitative estimate, and step <1>4 gives the stated
growth at infinity.

:::

:::
