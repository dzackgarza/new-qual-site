::: {.problem}
Let f be an analytic function on a connected open set $U \subset \mathbf { C }$

(1) Show that if f is real valued, then f is constant on U .

(2) Show that if f has constant absolute value, then f is constant on $U .$
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
f=u+iv,
$$
where $u$ and $v$ are real-valued functions on $U$.

<1>1. Suppose $f$ is real-valued. Then
$$
v\equiv0
$$
on $U$.

::: {.proof}
The imaginary part of a real-valued complex number is zero.
:::

<1>2. Under the hypothesis of step <1>1,
$$
u_x=u_y=0
$$
throughout $U$.

::: {.proof}
Since $v\equiv0$,
$$
v_x=v_y=0.
$$
The Cauchy--Riemann equations give
$$
u_x=v_y=0
$$
and
$$
u_y=-v_x=0.
$$
:::

<1>3. Hence $f$ is constant on $U$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 gives
$$
f'(z)=0
$$
for every $z\in U$. A holomorphic function with identically zero derivative
is constant on each connected component of its domain. Since $U$ is
connected, $f$ is constant on all of $U$.
:::

<1>4. Part (1) follows.

::: {.proof}
This is step <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Suppose now that
$$
|f|\equiv c
$$
for some constant $c\geq0$. If $c=0$, then $f\equiv0$.

::: {.proof}
If $|f(z)|=0$ for every $z$, then $f(z)=0$ for every $z$.
:::

<1>6. Assume $c>0$. Then
$$
u^2+v^2=c^2
$$
throughout $U$, and therefore
$$
u u_x+v v_x=0,
\qquad
u u_y+v v_y=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The identity
$$
|f|^2=u^2+v^2=c^2
$$
holds pointwise. Differentiate it with respect to $x$ and $y$, then divide
the resulting equations by $2$.
:::

<1>7. Using the Cauchy--Riemann equations, the relations in step <1>6 become
$$
u u_x-v u_y=0,
\qquad
v u_x+u u_y=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
The Cauchy--Riemann equations give
$$
v_x=-u_y,
\qquad
v_y=u_x.
$$
Substitute these into the two equations from step <1>6.
:::

<1>8. One has
$$
u_x=u_y=0
$$
throughout $U$.

::: {.proof}
At each point of $U$, step <1>7 is the linear system
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
u&-v\\
v&u
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
u_x\\
u_y
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\
0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is
$$
u^2+v^2=c^2>0.
$$
Hence the coefficient matrix is invertible, so
$$
u_x=u_y=0.
$$
:::

<1>9. Hence $f$ is constant on $U$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 and the Cauchy--Riemann equations imply all first partial
derivatives of $u$ and $v$ vanish. Thus
$$
f'\equiv0.
$$
Since $U$ is connected, $f$ is constant.
:::

<1>10. Part (2) follows.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 handles $c=0$, and steps <1>6--<1>9 handle $c>0$.
:::

<1>11. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>4 and <1>10 establish parts (1) and (2), respectively.
:::
:::
