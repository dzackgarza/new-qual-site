::: {.problem}
Let f be an analytic function on a connected open set $U \subset \CC$

(1) Show that if f is real valued, then f is constant on U .

(2) Show that if f has constant absolute value, then f is constant on $U .$
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
f=u+iv,
$$
where $u$ and $v$ are real-valued functions on $U$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Suppose $f$ is real-valued. Then
$$
v\equiv0
$$
on $U$.


::: pf-proof

The imaginary part of a real-valued complex number is zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Under the hypothesis of step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
u_x=u_y=0
$$
throughout $U$.


::: pf-proof

Since $v\equiv0$,
$$
v_x=v_y=0.
$$
The Cauchy--Riemann equations give
$$
u_x=v_y=0
$$
and
$$
u_y=-v_x=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Hence $f$ is constant on $U$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
f'(z)=0
$$
for every $z\in U$. A holomorphic function with identically zero derivative
is constant on each connected component of its domain. Since $U$ is
connected, $f$ is constant on all of $U$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Part (1) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Suppose now that
$$
|f|\equiv c
$$
for some constant $c\geq0$. If $c=0$, then $f\equiv0$.


::: pf-proof

If $|f(z)|=0$ for every $z$, then $f(z)=0$ for every $z$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Assume $c>0$. Then
$$
u^2+v^2=c^2
$$
throughout $U$, and therefore
$$
u u_x+v v_x=0,
\qquad
u u_y+v v_y=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The identity
$$
|f|^2=u^2+v^2=c^2
$$
holds pointwise. Differentiate it with respect to $x$ and $y$, then divide
the resulting equations by $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Using the Cauchy--Riemann equations, the relations in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} become
$$
u u_x-v u_y=0,
\qquad
v u_x+u u_y=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Cauchy--Riemann equations give
$$
v_x=-u_y,
\qquad
v_y=u_x.
$$
Substitute these into the two equations from step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

One has
$$
u_x=u_y=0
$$
throughout $U$.


::: pf-proof

At each point of $U$, step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} is the linear system
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
u&-v\\
v&u
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
u_x\\
u_y
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\
0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is
$$
u^2+v^2=c^2>0.
$$
Hence the coefficient matrix is invertible, so
$$
u_x=u_y=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Hence $f$ is constant on $U$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} and the Cauchy--Riemann equations imply all first partial
derivatives of $u$ and $v$ vanish. Thus
$$
f'\equiv0.
$$
Since $U$ is connected, $f$ is constant.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Part (2) follows.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} handles $c=0$, and steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} handle $c>0$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} establish parts (1) and (2), respectively.

:::
