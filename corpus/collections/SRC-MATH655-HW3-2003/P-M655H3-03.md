::: {.problem}
Let

$$
f(z) = \int_{[1, z]} \frac{1}{w} \, dw
$$

where $[1, z]$ is the line segment from $1$ to $z$ in $\CC$. Show that $f$ is a well-defined analytic function on $\CC \setminus \{ z = x + iy \mid x \le 0 \}$, and compute its power series expansion centered at the point $z_{0} = 1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
U=
\{z\in\CC:\operatorname{Re}z>0\}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $z\in U$, the line segment $[1,z]$ is contained in $U$.


::: pf-proof

The right half-plane $U$ is convex. Indeed, if
$$
w=(1-t)\cdot1+t z,
\qquad
0\leq t\leq1,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{Re}w
=(1-t)+t\operatorname{Re}z
>0.
$$
Thus the entire segment from $1$ to $z$ lies in $U$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The integral
$$
f(z)=\int_{[1,z]}\frac{dw}{w}
$$
is well-defined for every $z\in U$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the integration path lies in $U$, which does not contain $0$.
Hence $w\mapsto1/w$ is continuous along the segment and the line integral is
defined.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Fix $z_0\in U$. For all sufficiently small $h$, the triangle with
vertices
$$
1,quad z_0,quad z_0+h
$$
lies in $U$.


::: pf-proof

Since $U$ is open, choose $r>0$ with
$$
B(z_0,r)\subseteq U.
$$
If $\abs{h}<r$, then $z_0+h\in U$. Because $U$ is convex, the convex hull of
the three vertices, hence the entire triangle, lies in $U$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For sufficiently small $h$,
$$
f(z_0+h)-f(z_0)
=
\int_{[z_0,z_0+h]}\frac{dw}{w}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the triangle lies in $U$, and $1/w$ is holomorphic there.
Cauchy's theorem gives zero integral around its boundary:
$$
\int_{[1,z_0]}\frac{dw}{w}
+
\int_{[z_0,z_0+h]}\frac{dw}{w}
+
\int_{[z_0+h,1]}\frac{dw}{w}
=0.
$$
Since
$$
\int_{[z_0+h,1]}\frac{dw}{w}
=
-f(z_0+h),
$$
the displayed identity rearranges to the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

One has
$$
f'(z_0)=\frac1{z_0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
\frac{f(z_0+h)-f(z_0)}h
-\frac1{z_0}
=
\frac1h
\int_{[z_0,z_0+h]}
\left(
\frac1w-\frac1{z_0}
\right)dw.
$$
Hence
$$
\abs{
\frac{f(z_0+h)-f(z_0)}h
-\frac1{z_0}
}
\leq
\max_{w\in[z_0,z_0+h]}
\abs{
\frac1w-\frac1{z_0}
}.
$$
As $h\to0$, the segment shrinks to $z_0$, and continuity of $1/w$ at
$z_0$ makes the right-hand side tend to $0$. Thus the derivative exists and
equals $1/z_0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The function $f$ is analytic on $U$.


::: pf-proof

The point $z_0\in U$ in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} was arbitrary.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If $\abs{w-1}<1$, then
$$
\frac1w
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^k(w-1)^k.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
w=1+(w-1).
$$
Then
$$
\frac1w
=
\frac1{1+(w-1)},
$$
and the geometric-series formula applies because $\abs{w-1}<1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

If $\abs{z-1}<1$, then the geometric series in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} converges
uniformly on the segment $[1,z]$.


::: pf-proof

For $w\in[1,z]$,
$$
\abs{w-1}
\leq
\abs{z-1}
=
r
<
1.
$$
Thus
$$
\abs{(-1)^k(w-1)^k}
\leq
r^k,
$$
and
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r^k
$$
converges. The Weierstrass $M$-test gives uniform convergence on the segment.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

For $\abs{z-1}<1$,
$$
\boxed{
f(z)
=
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}(z-1)^n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}, termwise integration along $[1,z]$ is valid:
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(z)
&=
\int_{[1,z]}
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
(-1)^k(w-1)^k,dw\\
&=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
(-1)^k
\int_{[1,z]}
(w-1)^k,dw\\
&=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^k}{k+1}(z-1)^{k+1}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Relabel $n=k+1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} proves analyticity on the stated domain, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives the
power series centered at $1$.

:::
