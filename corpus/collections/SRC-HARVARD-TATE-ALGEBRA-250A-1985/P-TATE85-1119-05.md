::: {.problem}
(5) Let a and b be positive integers such that ab is square free$> 1$, and let$E = \QQ ( { \sqrt [ { 3 } ] { a b ^ { 2 } } } )$Let$\alpha = \sqrt [ 3 ] { a b ^ { 2 } }$, and$\beta = \sqrt [ 3 ] { a ^ { 2 } b } = a b / \alpha$Show that if$a ^ { 2 } \not = b ^ { 2 }$(mod 9), then the integral closure of Z in E is$\ZZ + \ZZ \alpha + \ZZ \beta$, and the discriminant of the field E is$- 2 7 a ^ { 2 } b ^ { 2 }$. What if$a ^ { 2 } \equiv b ^ { 2 } ( { \mathrm { m o d } } \ 9 ) ?$
:::

::: {.solution}

Put $d=ab^2$ and
$$
A=\ZZ+\ZZ\alpha+\ZZ\beta\subset E.
$$
Since $ab$ is squarefree, $a$ and $b$ are squarefree and coprime. In particular,
$$
\alpha^3=ab^2,\qquad
\beta^3=a^2b,\qquad
\alpha\beta=ab,\qquad
\alpha^2=b\beta,\qquad
\beta^2=a\alpha.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

$A$ is an order in $E$ with
$$
\operatorname{disc}(A)=\boxed{-27a^2b^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The displayed multiplication relations show that $A$ is a subring. Since $ab>1$ is squarefree, $ab^2$ is not a cube, so $X^3-ab^2$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ and $1,\alpha,\beta$ is a $\QQ$-basis of $E$.

If $\omega$ is a primitive cube root of unity, the three embeddings of $E$ send
$$
(\alpha,\beta)
\quad\hbox{to}\quad
(\alpha,\beta),\ (\omega\alpha,\omega^2\beta),\ (\omega^2\alpha,\omega\beta).
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(1)=3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)=\operatorname{Tr}(\beta)
=\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(\beta^2)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha\beta)=3ab.
$$
Thus the trace matrix of the basis $1,\alpha,\beta$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
3&0&0\\
0&0&3ab\\
0&3ab&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose determinant is $-27a^2b^2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For integers $x,y,z$, if $\gamma=x+y\alpha+z\beta$, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma)&=3x,\\
s_2(\gamma)&=3(x^2-ab yz),\\
N_{E/\QQ}(\gamma)&=x^3+ab^2y^3+a^2bz^3-3abxyz,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $s_2$ is the second elementary symmetric function of the three conjugates.


::: pf-proof

The trace formula follows from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Squaring $\gamma$ and taking traces gives
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^2)=3x^2+6ab yz,
$$
so
$$
s_2(\gamma)
=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma)^2-\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^2)}2
=3(x^2-ab yz).
$$
For the norm, apply the identity
$$
X^3+Y^3+Z^3-3XYZ
=(X+Y+Z)(X+\omega Y+\omega^2Z)(X+\omega^2Y+\omega Z)
$$
with $X=x$, $Y=y\alpha$, and $Z=z\beta$, using $\alpha\beta=ab$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If a prime $p\ne3$ divides $ab$, then $A$ is maximal at $p$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $v_p(\operatorname{disc}(A))=2$. If $A$ were not maximal at $p$, the $p$-primary part of the quotient of the full ring of integers by $A$ would contain an element of order $p$. Thus there would be an algebraic integer
$$
\theta=\frac{x+y\alpha+z\beta}{p}\notin A
$$
with $x,y,z\in\ZZ$.

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, integrality of the trace gives $p\mid x$, while integrality of $s_2(\theta)$ gives
$$
p^2\mid x^2-ab yz.
$$
Since $ab$ is squarefree, $v_p(ab)=1$, and therefore $p\mid yz$.

Suppose first that $p\mid a$. Then $v_p(ab^2)=1$ and $v_p(a^2b)=2$. If $p\nmid y$, the divisibility $p\mid yz$ gives $p\mid z$; in the norm formula of step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} every term except $ab^2y^3$ is then divisible by $p^3$, whereas $ab^2y^3$ has valuation exactly $1$, contradicting integrality of $N(\theta)$. Hence $p\mid y$. The same norm formula then forces $p\mid z$, because the only term that can have valuation exactly $2$ is $a^2bz^3$. Thus $p$ divides $x,y,z$, contrary to $\theta\notin A$.

If $p\mid b$, the same argument with $y$ and $z$ interchanged gives the same contradiction. Therefore no such $\theta$ exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $3\mid ab$, then $A$ is also maximal at $3$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose that the $3$-primary index were nontrivial. Then the quotient of the full ring of integers by $A$ would contain an element of order $3$, so there would be an algebraic integer
$$
\theta=\frac{x+y\alpha+z\beta}{3}\notin A.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, integrality of $s_2(\theta)$ implies
$$
3\mid x^2-ab yz.
$$
Because $3\mid ab$, this gives $3\mid x$.

Assume $3\mid a$; then $3\nmid b$ and $v_3(a)=1$. Integrality of the norm says that
$$
27\mid x^3+ab^2y^3+a^2bz^3-3abxyz.
$$
The first and last terms are divisible by $27$, and $a^2bz^3$ is divisible by $9$. Reduction modulo $9$ therefore forces $3\mid y$. After this, every term except possibly $a^2bz^3$ is divisible by $27$, so the same norm divisibility forces $3\mid z$. Hence $3$ divides $x,y,z$, contradicting $\theta\notin A$. The case $3\mid b$ is symmetric.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Assume $3\nmid ab$. Then $A$ can fail to be maximal only at $3$, and the only possible nontrivial $3$-overorder is represented by
$$
\eta_\delta=\frac{b+\delta\alpha+\beta}{3},
$$
where $\delta\in\{1,-1\}$ is the unique sign satisfying $\delta\equiv ab\pmod3$.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} leave only the case $3\nmid ab$. Here step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives $v_3(\operatorname{disc}(A))=3$, so the $3$-part of the index of $A$ in the full ring of integers has exponent at most $1$. If it is nontrivial, the local quotient is one-dimensional over $\FF_3$.

Let an integral element outside $A$ be represented by
$$
\theta=\frac{x+y\alpha+z\beta}{3}.
$$
Multiplication by $\alpha$ preserves the one-dimensional quotient. Modulo $3A$, multiplication by $\alpha$ sends the coordinate vector $(x,y,z)$ to
$$
(abz,x,by).
$$
Thus $(x,y,z)$ is an eigenvector. Since multiplication by $\alpha$ satisfies $T^3-ab^2$ and $b^2=1$ in $\FF_3$, its eigenvalue is $a$. The eigenvector equations then give
$$
y=ax,\qquad z=bx
\qquad\text{in }\FF_3.
$$
The class is nonzero, so $x\ne0$. Rescaling the generator of the one-dimensional quotient and adding an element of $A$ therefore gives exactly the representative $(b,\delta,1)$, where $\delta\equiv ab\pmod3$. Hence the only possible overorder class is represented by $\eta_\delta$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The element $\eta_\delta$ is integral if and only if
$$
a\equiv\delta b\pmod9,
$$
equivalently if and only if $a^2\equiv b^2\pmod9$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} applied to $b+\delta\alpha+\beta$ gives
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\eta_\delta)=b,
\qquad
s_2(\eta_\delta)=\frac{b(b-\delta a)}3,
$$
and
$$
N(\eta_\delta)
=\frac{b^3+\delta ab^2+a^2b-3\delta ab^2}{27}
=\frac{b(a-\delta b)^2}{27}.
$$
The choice $\delta\equiv ab\pmod3$ implies $b-\delta a\equiv0\pmod3$, so the trace and $s_2$ are integers. Therefore $\eta_\delta$ is integral exactly when its norm is an integer. Since $3\nmid b$, this is equivalent to
$$
27\mid(a-\delta b)^2,
$$
which is equivalent to $9\mid a-\delta b$. For units modulo $9$, the congruence $a^2\equiv b^2\pmod9$ is equivalent to $a\equiv b\pmod9$ or $a\equiv-b\pmod9$, giving precisely the stated condition with the chosen sign $\delta$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If $a^2\not\equiv b^2\pmod9$, then the full ring of integers is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_E=A=\ZZ+\ZZ\alpha+\ZZ\beta},
$$
and
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{disc}(E)=-27a^2b^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} show that $A$ is maximal at every prime dividing $ab$. If $3\nmid ab$, steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} show that a nontrivial $3$-overorder exists only when $a^2\equiv b^2\pmod9$; if $3\mid ab$, step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} already gives maximality at $3$. Any prime dividing the index of $A$ must divide its discriminant, so step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} accounts for every possible prime. Thus $A=\mathcal O_E$, and its discriminant is the field discriminant.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

If $a^2\equiv b^2\pmod9$, let $\delta\in\{1,-1\}$ satisfy $a\equiv\delta b\pmod9$. Then
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal O_E
=\ZZ+\ZZ\alpha
+\ZZ\frac{b+\delta\alpha+\beta}{3}}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{disc}(E)=-3a^2b^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, $\eta_\delta=(b+\delta\alpha+\beta)/3$ is integral. Let
$$
B=\ZZ+\ZZ\alpha+\ZZ\eta_\delta.
$$
Because $a-\delta b$ is divisible by $9$, direct multiplication gives
$$
\alpha\eta_\delta
=\delta b\eta_\delta+\frac{b(a-\delta b)}3
\in B
$$
and
$$
\eta_\delta^2
=b\eta_\delta
+\frac{2b(\delta a-b)+(a-\delta b)\alpha}{9}
\in B.
$$
Thus $B$ is an order. Since
$$
\beta=3\eta_\delta-b-\delta\alpha,
$$
we have $A\subset B$ with $[B:A]=3$. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{disc}(B)
=\frac{\operatorname{disc}(A)}{3^2}
=-3a^2b^2.
$$

The congruence $a^2\equiv b^2\pmod9$ implies $3\nmid ab$. For every prime $p\mid ab$, the element $3$ is a unit in $\ZZ_{(p)}$, so $A$ and $B$ have the same localization; step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} shows it is already maximal. At $3$, the discriminant of $B$ has $3$-adic valuation $1$, so no further index divisible by $3$ is possible because the square of the index divides the discriminant of any order. No other prime can divide the index. Hence $B=\mathcal O_E$ and the displayed discriminant is the field discriminant.

:::
