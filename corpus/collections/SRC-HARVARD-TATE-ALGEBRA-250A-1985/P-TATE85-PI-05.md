::: {.problem}
(5) If $\CC$ is the field of complex numbers (or any algebraically closed field), apply (4) to $B = \CC[Y]$ to show that the prime ideals $P$ in the ring $A = \CC[X, Y]$ are of three distinct types:

(I) $P = (0)$.

(II) and (III) $P = f(X, Y) A$ where $f(X, Y)$ is an irreducible polynomial in two variables with complex coefficients, uniquely determined by $P$ up to a nonzero constant factor.

(IV) $P = (X - x_{0}) A + (Y - y_{0}) A$, where $x_{0}$ and $y_{0}$ are complex numbers uniquely determined by $P$.

The only maximal ideals are those of type IV, and the ideals of type IV containing a given $f(X, Y) A$ are those for which $f(x_{0}, y_{0}) = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Apply [[P-TATE85-PI-04]] with
$$
B=\CC[Y],
\qquad
K=\CC(Y),
\qquad
A=B[X]=\CC[X,Y].
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

A type-I prime is $(0)$.


::: pf-proof

This is exactly type I in [[P-TATE85-PI-04]].

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The primes of types II and III are precisely the principal ideals
$$
\boxed{P=f(X,Y)A}
$$
with $f\in\CC[X,Y]$ irreducible.


::: pf-proof

For type II, [[P-TATE85-PI-04]] gives a primitive polynomial
$$
f\in\CC[Y][X]
$$
irreducible in $\CC(Y)[X]$. By Gauss's lemma, this is equivalent to irreducibility in $\CC[Y][X]=\CC[X,Y]$.

For type III, the ideal has the form $p(Y)A$ with $p(Y)$ irreducible in $\CC[Y]$. Since $\CC$ is algebraically closed,
$$
p(Y)=c(Y-y_0)
$$
for some $c\in\CC^\times$ and $y_0\in\CC$, so it is again generated by an irreducible polynomial in $\CC[X,Y]$.

Conversely, $\CC[X,Y]$ is a UFD, so every irreducible polynomial is prime and therefore generates a prime ideal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

In step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the irreducible generator $f$ is determined by $P$ up to a nonzero constant factor.


::: pf-proof

If $(f)=(f')$, then $f'=uf$ for a unit $u$ of $\CC[X,Y]$. The only units of $\CC[X,Y]$ are the nonzero constants, so
$$
u\in\CC^\times.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The type-IV primes are exactly
$$
\boxed{
(X-x_0,\ Y-y_0)
}
$$
with $x_0,y_0\in\CC$.


::: pf-proof

An irreducible element of $B=\CC[Y]$ is associated to $Y-y_0$ for a unique $y_0\in\CC$. Reducing modulo $Y-y_0$ identifies
$$
B/(Y-y_0)\cong\CC.
$$
The type-IV classification from [[P-TATE85-PI-04]] therefore gives
$$
P=(Y-y_0,\ g(X)),
$$
where the reduction of $g$ is irreducible in $\CC[X]$. Because $\CC$ is algebraically closed, that irreducible polynomial is linear and is associated to $X-x_0$ for a unique $x_0\in\CC$. Hence
$$
P=(X-x_0,Y-y_0).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The pair $(x_0,y_0)$ in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is uniquely determined by the ideal.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
(X-x_0,Y-y_0)=(X-x_1,Y-y_1),
$$
then contracting both sides to $\CC[Y]$ gives
$$
(Y-y_0)=(Y-y_1),
$$
so $y_0=y_1$. Passing to the quotient by $(Y-y_0)$ then gives
$$
(X-x_0)=(X-x_1)
$$
in $\CC[X]$, hence $x_0=x_1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The only maximal ideals are the type-IV ideals.


::: pf-proof

By [[P-TATE85-PI-04]], every type-IV ideal is maximal. Type I is not maximal. Types II and III are principal prime ideals generated by an irreducible polynomial. Since $\CC[Y]$ has infinitely many maximal ideals, [[P-TATE85-PI-04]] shows that type-II ideals are not maximal; type III is properly contained, for example, in
$$
(Y-y_0,X)
$$
when its generator is associated to $Y-y_0$. Thus no prime of types I--III is maximal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

A type-IV ideal
$$
\mathfrak m_{(x_0,y_0)}=(X-x_0,Y-y_0)
$$
contains $(f)$ if and only if
$$
\boxed{f(x_0,y_0)=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Evaluation at $(x_0,y_0)$ defines a surjective homomorphism
$$
\operatorname{ev}_{(x_0,y_0)}:\CC[X,Y]\to\CC
$$
whose kernel is
$$
(X-x_0,Y-y_0).
$$
Therefore
$$
f\in\mathfrak m_{(x_0,y_0)}
\iff
f(x_0,y_0)=0.
$$
Since $(f)\subseteq\mathfrak m_{(x_0,y_0)}$ is equivalent to $f\in\mathfrak m_{(x_0,y_0)}$, the claim follows.

:::

:::
