::: {.problem}
(4)

(a) A ring of the form $\ZZ[\alpha]$ has at most two homomorphisms into $\FF_2$.
Why?

(b) Let $A$ be the integral closure of $\ZZ$ in the field $\QQ(\sqrt{-7},\sqrt{17})$.
Find a $\ZZ$-basis for $A$ (cf.
class discussion on October 31).

(c) Show that $A$ has four distinct homomorphisms into $\FF_2$ (and consequently there does not exist $\alpha\in A$ such that $A=\ZZ[\alpha]$).
:::

::: {.solution}

All ring homomorphisms below are unital. Put
$$
K=\QQ(\sqrt{-7},\sqrt{17}),
\qquad
\omega=\frac{1+\sqrt{-7}}2,
\qquad
\eta=\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}2.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $R=\ZZ[\alpha]$, then there are at most two ring homomorphisms $R\to\FF_2$.


::: pf-proof

Every unital ring homomorphism $\varphi:R\to\FF_2$ restricts on $\ZZ$ to reduction modulo $2$. Since every element of $R$ is a polynomial in $\alpha$ with integer coefficients, $\varphi$ is therefore completely determined by the single value $\varphi(\alpha)$. There are only two possible values, $0$ and $1$, in $\FF_2$. Hence there are at most two such homomorphisms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The quadratic subfields $K_1=\QQ(\sqrt{-7})$ and $K_2=\QQ(\sqrt{17})$ have rings of integers
$$
\mathcal O_{K_1}=\ZZ[\omega],
\qquad
\mathcal O_{K_2}=\ZZ[\eta],
$$
with discriminants $-7$ and $17$, respectively.


::: pf-proof

The elements $\omega$ and $\eta$ satisfy
$$
\omega^2-\omega+2=0,
\qquad
\eta^2-\eta-4=0.
$$
Thus they are algebraic integers. The discriminants of the bases $1,\omega$ and $1,\eta$ are
$$
(\omega-\bar\omega)^2=-7,
\qquad
(\eta-\bar\eta)^2=17.
$$
If either order were properly contained in the corresponding quadratic ring of integers with index $m>1$, its discriminant would equal $m^2$ times the field discriminant. Since $7$ and $17$ are squarefree, no integer $m>1$ can satisfy this divisibility. Hence both displayed orders are already maximal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The order
$$
B=\ZZ[\omega,\eta]
$$
has $\ZZ$-basis
$$
1,\quad \omega,\quad \eta,\quad \omega\eta
$$
and discriminant
$$
\operatorname{disc}(B)=(-7)^2\,17^2=119^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The quadratic fields $K_1$ and $K_2$ are distinct, so $K_1\cap K_2=\QQ$ and therefore $[K:\QQ]=4$. Hence the four displayed elements are $\QQ$-linearly independent and form a $\ZZ$-basis of $B$.

The four embeddings of $K$ are obtained by choosing independently the two conjugates of $\omega$ and the two conjugates of $\eta$. Consequently the embedding matrix for the product basis $1,\omega,\eta,\omega\eta$ is the Kronecker product of the two quadratic embedding matrices. Its squared determinant is therefore
$$
\operatorname{disc}(1,\omega)^2\operatorname{disc}(1,\eta)^2
=(-7)^2 17^2
=119^2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The integral closure $A$ of $\ZZ$ in $K$ is
$$
\boxed{A=\ZZ[\omega,\eta]},
$$
so a $\ZZ$-basis is
$$
\boxed{1,\ \frac{1+\sqrt{-7}}2,\ \frac{1+\sqrt{17}}2,\ \frac{(1+\sqrt{-7})(1+\sqrt{17})}{4}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $B$ is generated by algebraic integers, $B\subseteq A$. The discriminant-in-a-tower formula applied to the quadratic subfield $K_1\subset K$ gives
$$
\operatorname{disc}(K)
=\operatorname{disc}(K_1)^2
N_{K_1/\QQ}(\mathfrak D_{K/K_1}),
$$
so $7^2$ divides $\operatorname{disc}(K)$. Applying the same formula to $K_2\subset K$ shows that $17^2$ divides $\operatorname{disc}(K)$. Since $7$ and $17$ are coprime,
$$
119^2\mid \operatorname{disc}(K).
$$

On the other hand, for the order $B\subseteq A$,
$$
\operatorname{disc}(B)=[A:B]^2\operatorname{disc}(K).
$$
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives $\operatorname{disc}(B)=119^2$. Hence $\operatorname{disc}(K)$ is both divisible by $119^2$ and a divisor of $119^2$. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{disc}(K)=119^2,
\qquad
[A:B]=1,
$$
which proves the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

There are four distinct ring homomorphisms $A\to\FF_2$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and the two monic relations in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
A\cong
\ZZ[U,V]/(U^2-U+2,\ V^2-V-4),
$$
where $U$ maps to $\omega$ and $V$ maps to $\eta$: the quotient is free on $1,U,V,UV$, and these four elements map to the $\ZZ$-basis from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. Reducing modulo $2$ gives
$$
A/2A
\cong
\FF_2[U,V]/(U^2-U,\ V^2-V).
$$
For each pair $(\varepsilon,\delta)\in\FF_2^2$, evaluation
$$
U\longmapsto\varepsilon,
\qquad
V\longmapsto\delta
$$
respects the two relations because every element of $\FF_2$ satisfies $x^2-x=0$. Thus evaluation gives a homomorphism
$$
A/2A\longrightarrow\FF_2,
$$
and composing with $A\to A/2A$ gives a homomorphism $\varphi_{\varepsilon,\delta}:A\to\FF_2$. The four pairs
$$
(0,0),\quad(0,1),\quad(1,0),\quad(1,1)
$$
give four distinct maps because their values on $\omega$ and $\eta$ are different.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

There is no $\alpha\in A$ with $A=\ZZ[\alpha]$.


::: pf-proof

If $A=\ZZ[\alpha]$, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} would imply that $A$ has at most two homomorphisms to $\FF_2$. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} constructs four distinct such homomorphisms, a contradiction.

:::
