::: {.problem}
(1) Let $A$ be a u.f.d. (unique factorization domain, e.g., $A = \ZZ[x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}]$ or $A = K[x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}]$, $K$ a field) and let $\pi$ be a prime element in $A$. Show:

(a) The principal ideal $\pi A$ is a prime ideal.

(b) Every nonzero prime ideal contains one of the form $\pi A$.

(c) The ideals of the form $\pi A$ are the minimal elements in the set of nonzero prime ideals, ordered by inclusion, and they are the only nonzero principal prime ideals.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $\pi$ is a prime element of $A$, then $\pi A$ is a prime ideal.


::: pf-proof

The ideal $\pi A$ is proper because a prime element is not a unit. Suppose
$$
xy\in\pi A.
$$
Then $\pi\mid xy$. Since $\pi$ is prime, $\pi\mid x$ or $\pi\mid y$, so
$$
x\in\pi A
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
y\in\pi A.
$$
Thus $\pi A$ is prime.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every nonzero prime ideal $P\subset A$ contains $\pi A$ for some prime element $\pi$.


::: pf-proof

Choose $0\ne a\in P$. Since $P$ is proper, $a$ is not a unit. Factor $a$ in the UFD $A$:
$$
a=u\pi_1\cdots\pi_r,
$$
where $u$ is a unit and each $\pi_i$ is irreducible. In a UFD every irreducible element is prime. Since
$$
a\in P
$$
and $P$ is prime, repeated application of the defining property of a prime ideal gives
$$
\pi_i\in P
$$
for some $i$. Therefore
$$
\pi_iA\subseteq P.
$$
Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that $\pi_iA$ is itself a nonzero prime ideal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every prime element $\pi$, the prime ideal $\pi A$ is minimal among the nonzero prime ideals.


::: pf-proof

Let $Q$ be a nonzero prime ideal with
$$
Q\subseteq\pi A.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} there is a prime element $\rho$ such that
$$
\rho A\subseteq Q.
$$
Hence
$$
\rho\in Q\subseteq\pi A,
$$
so $\pi\mid\rho$. Because $\rho$ is prime, hence irreducible, this divisibility forces $\pi$ and $\rho$ to be associates. Therefore
$$
\rho A=\pi A.
$$
Consequently
$$
\pi A=\rho A\subseteq Q\subseteq\pi A,
$$
and hence $Q=\pi A$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The ideals $\pi A$, with $\pi$ prime, are exactly the nonzero principal prime ideals.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves that every ideal of the form $\pi A$ is a nonzero principal prime ideal.

Conversely, let $aA$ be a nonzero principal prime ideal. Since it is proper, $a$ is a nonzero nonunit. If
$$
a\mid xy,
$$
then $xy\in aA$. Since $aA$ is prime,
$$
x\in aA
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
y\in aA,
$$
so $a\mid x$ or $a\mid y$. Thus $a$ is a prime element. Therefore the ideal is of the required form.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves part (b), and steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove part (c).

:::
