::: {.problem}
(4) Let $k$ be a field ($\CC$ or $\RR$ if you wish) and let $f(X, Y)$ be an irreducible polynomial in two variables over $k$, i.e., a prime element in the U.F.D. $k[X, Y]$. Let $A = k[X, Y]/(f)$. Then $A$ is noetherian, and the nonzero prime ideals of $A$ are maximal. Can you show this? Anyway, taking that for granted, let $(x_{0}, y_{0}) \in k \times k$ be a point on the curve $f(X, Y) = 0$, i.e., be such that $f(x_{0}, y_{0}) = 0$, and let $P$ be the corresponding maximal ideal of $A$, consisting of the polynomials $p(X, Y)$ such that $p(x_{0}, y_{0}) = 0$, modulo $(f)$. Prove that $P$ is an invertible ideal in $A$ if and only if the point $(x_{0}, y_{0})$ is a “non-singular” point of the curve, in the sense that not both partial derivatives $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ and $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ vanish at $(x_{0}, y_{0})$. (Suggestion: Note that the translation $(X, Y) \mapsto (X - x_{0}, Y - y_{0})$, which is an automorphism of $k[X, Y]$, allows you to assume $(x_{0}, y_{0}) = (0, 0)$ without loss of generality.)
:::

::: {.solution}

Put
$$
R=k[X,Y],
\qquad
A=R/(f).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

$A$ is a Noetherian domain of Krull dimension $1$, and every nonzero prime ideal of $A$ is maximal.


::: pf-proof

The polynomial ring $R=k[X,Y]$ is Noetherian, so its quotient $A$ is Noetherian. Since $R$ is a UFD and $f$ is irreducible, $f$ is prime; hence $(f)$ is a prime ideal and $A$ is a domain.

The ring $R$ has dimension $2$. The nonzero principal prime $(f)$ has height $1$: the principal ideal theorem gives height at most $1$, while $(0)\subsetneq(f)$ gives height at least $1$. Hence
$$
\dim A=\dim R/(f)=1.
$$
Therefore every nonzero prime of the one-dimensional domain $A$ is maximal.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

After translating coordinates, it is enough to treat the point $(0,0)$, with
$$
\mathfrak q=(X,Y)\subset R,
\qquad
P=\mathfrak q/(f)\subset A.
$$


::: pf-proof

Replace the coordinates by
$$
U=X-x_0,
\qquad
V=Y-y_0.
$$
This is an automorphism of the polynomial ring. It sends the maximal ideal of the point $(x_0,y_0)$ to $(U,V)$ and carries $f$ to
$$
g(U,V)=f(U+x_0,V+y_0).
$$
Moreover,
$$
g_U(0,0)=f_X(x_0,y_0),
\qquad
g_V(0,0)=f_Y(x_0,y_0).
$$
Thus both invertibility of the point ideal and the nonsingularity condition are preserved. Renaming $U,V,g$ as $X,Y,f$ gives the stated reduction.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For a finitely generated nonzero ideal $I$ in a domain, $I$ is invertible if and only if $I_{\mathfrak m}$ is principal for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$.


::: pf-proof

If $I$ is invertible, let $J$ be a fractional inverse, so $IJ=A$. Localizing gives
$$
I_{\mathfrak m}J_{\mathfrak m}=A_{\mathfrak m}.
$$
Write $1=\sum_i x_i y_i$ with $x_i\in I_{\mathfrak m}$ and $y_i\in J_{\mathfrak m}$. Since $A_{\mathfrak m}$ is local, some product $x_i y_i$ is a unit. For that index, every $x\in I_{\mathfrak m}$ satisfies
$$
\frac{x}{x_i}
=\frac{x y_i}{x_i y_i}
\in A_{\mathfrak m},
$$
so $I_{\mathfrak m}=x_iA_{\mathfrak m}$ is principal.

Conversely, suppose every $I_{\mathfrak m}$ is principal. Let
$$
I^{-1}=\{z\in\operatorname{Frac}(A):zI\subseteq A\}.
$$
Because $I$ is finitely generated, localization commutes with forming this inverse, so
$$
(I^{-1})_{\mathfrak m}=(I_{\mathfrak m})^{-1}.
$$
Thus
$$
(II^{-1})_{\mathfrak m}=A_{\mathfrak m}
$$
for every maximal $\mathfrak m$. If $II^{-1}$ were a proper ideal of $A$, it would lie in some maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and then its localization at $\mathfrak m$ would be proper, a contradiction. Hence $II^{-1}=A$, so $I$ is invertible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For the maximal ideal $P$ of the chosen point,
$$
P\text{ is invertible}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P_P\text{ is principal}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The ideal $P$ is finitely generated because $A$ is Noetherian. If $\mathfrak m\ne P$ is another maximal ideal, choose $u\in P\setminus\mathfrak m$. Then $u$ becomes a unit in $A_{\mathfrak m}$, so
$$
P_{\mathfrak m}=A_{\mathfrak m}.
$$
Thus the local criterion of step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is automatic at every maximal ideal except $P$, and the displayed equivalence follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

With $\mathfrak m=P_P$ in the local ring $A_P$,
$$
P_P\text{ is principal}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\dim_k P/P^2=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $A/P\cong k$, the residue field of $A_P$ is $k$. Also,
the maximal ideal $P$ is nonzero: otherwise the domain $A$ would itself be a field, contradicting $\dim A=1$ from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Thus $\mathfrak m=P_P$ is nonzero. Moreover,
$$
\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2
\cong
(P/P^2)_P
\cong
P/P^2,
$$
because $P/P^2$ is already a vector space over $A/P=k$.

If $\mathfrak m=(t)$ is principal, then $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ is generated by the class of $t$. This class is nonzero: if $t\in(t^2)$, then $t=t^2a$ for some $a\in A_P$, and since $A_P$ is a domain and $t\ne0$, one gets $ta=1$, contradicting $t\in\mathfrak m$. Hence the quotient has dimension $1$ over $k$. Conversely, if this dimension is $1$, choose $t\in\mathfrak m$ whose class spans $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$. Then
$$
\mathfrak m=(t)+\mathfrak m^2.
$$
The module $\mathfrak m/(t)$ is finitely generated and satisfies
$$
\mathfrak m\bigl(\mathfrak m/(t)\bigr)=\mathfrak m/(t).
$$
Nakayama's lemma gives $\mathfrak m/(t)=0$, hence $\mathfrak m=(t)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

There is a natural isomorphism
$$
P/P^2
\cong
\mathfrak q/(\mathfrak q^2+(f)),
$$
and the image of $f$ in the two-dimensional vector space
$$
\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q^2
$$
is
$$
f_X(0,0)X+f_Y(0,0)Y.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $f(0,0)=0$, we have $f\in\mathfrak q$. Under the quotient map $R\to A$, the ideal $\mathfrak q$ maps onto $P$, and
$$
P^2=(\mathfrak q^2+(f))/(f).
$$
The third isomorphism theorem gives
$$
P/P^2
\cong
\mathfrak q/(\mathfrak q^2+(f)).
$$

Modulo $\mathfrak q^2$, only the linear part of $f$ remains. The coefficient of $X$ in that linear part is $f_X(0,0)$ and the coefficient of $Y$ is $f_Y(0,0)$, giving the displayed class.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The point $(0,0)$ is nonsingular if and only if
$$
\dim_k P/P^2=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

The vector space $\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q^2$ has basis given by the classes of $X$ and $Y$, so it has dimension $2$. By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, passing to $P/P^2$ imposes exactly the linear relation represented by
$$
f_X(0,0)X+f_Y(0,0)Y.
$$
If at least one partial derivative is nonzero, this is one nonzero relation, so
$$
\dim_k P/P^2=1.
$$
If both partial derivatives vanish, then $f\in\mathfrak q^2$, so no linear relation is imposed and
$$
\dim_k P/P^2=2.
$$
Thus dimension $1$ is equivalent to nonsingularity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The point ideal $P$ is invertible if and only if the point is nonsingular.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $P$ is invertible exactly when $P_P$ is principal. By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, this is equivalent to
$$
\dim_k P/P^2=1.
$$
By step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, that dimension condition is equivalent to at least one of
$$
f_X(x_0,y_0),
\qquad
f_Y(x_0,y_0)
$$
being nonzero. This is exactly the stated nonsingularity condition.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves the preliminary ring-theoretic assertions, and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves the required equivalence.
