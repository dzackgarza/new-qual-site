::: {.problem}
(1) Let $e$ be an idempotent ($e^{2} = e$) in a local ring $A$ (a ring with a unique maximal ideal). Show that $e = 0$ or $1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\mathfrak m$ be the unique maximal ideal of $A$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

At least one of $e$ and $1-e$ is a unit.


::: pf-proof

In a local ring the nonunits are exactly the elements of the unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. If both $e$ and $1-e$ were nonunits, then both would lie in $\mathfrak m$, and hence
$$
1=e+(1-e)\in\mathfrak m,
$$
which is impossible for a proper ideal. Thus at least one of $e$ and $1-e$ is a unit.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

$e=0$ or $e=1$.


::: pf-proof

The idempotent relation gives
$$
e(1-e)=0.
$$
If $e$ is a unit, multiply by $e^{-1}$ to obtain $1-e=0$, so $e=1$. If $1-e$ is a unit, multiply by $(1-e)^{-1}$ to obtain $e=0$. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that one of these two cases occurs.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves the claim.

:::
