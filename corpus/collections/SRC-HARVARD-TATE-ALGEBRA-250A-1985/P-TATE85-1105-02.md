::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be the fraction field of an integral domain $A$. Prove that $A$ is integrally closed (in $F$) $\iff$ $A$ has the following property: if $f(X)$ and $g(X) \in F[X]$ are monic and $f(X)\cdot g(X) \in A[X]$, then $f(X)$ and $g(X) \in A[X]$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $(\star)$ for the property that whenever monic $f,g\in F[X]$ satisfy $fg\in A[X]$, one has $f,g\in A[X]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $A$ is integrally closed in $F$, then $A$ satisfies $(\star)$.


::: pf-proof

Let $f,g\in F[X]$ be monic and suppose
$$
h=fg\in A[X].
$$
Because $f$ and $g$ are monic, $h$ is monic. Let $K$ be a splitting field of $h$ over $F$. Every root of $f$ or $g$ is then a root of the monic polynomial $h\in A[X]$, so every such root is integral over $A$.

The elements of $K$ integral over $A$ form a subring. Therefore every elementary symmetric polynomial in the roots of $f$ is integral over $A$. Since the coefficients of the monic polynomial $f$ are, up to sign, precisely these elementary symmetric polynomials, every coefficient of $f$ is integral over $A$. The coefficients of $f$ lie in $F$, and $A$ is integrally closed in $F$, so every coefficient of $f$ lies in $A$. Hence $f\in A[X]$.

The same argument applied to the roots of $g$ gives $g\in A[X]$. Thus $A$ satisfies $(\star)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $A$ satisfies $(\star)$, then $A$ is integrally closed in $F$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\alpha\in F$ be integral over $A$. Then there is a monic polynomial
$$
h(X)=X^n+a_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0\in A[X]
$$
such that $h(\alpha)=0$. Since $\alpha\in F$, polynomial division in $F[X]$ gives
$$
h(X)=(X-\alpha)q(X)
$$
for some $q(X)\in F[X]$. Because both $h$ and $X-\alpha$ are monic, $q$ is monic as well.

Property $(\star)$ applied to the two monic factors $X-\alpha$ and $q(X)$ yields
$$
X-\alpha\in A[X].
$$
Its constant coefficient is $-\alpha$, so $\alpha\in A$. Thus every element of $F$ integral over $A$ already lies in $A$, which is exactly that $A$ is integrally closed in $F$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The two conditions are equivalent.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves that integral closedness implies $(\star)$, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves the converse.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the required equivalence.

:::
