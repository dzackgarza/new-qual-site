::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(X) = X^4 - a_1 X^3 + a_2 X^2 - a_3 X + a_4 = \prod_{i=1}^{4} (X - \alpha_i)
$$
with $a_i \in F$, $F$ a field, $\alpha_i \in K = F(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_4)$, the splitting field.
Put
$$
\beta_1 = \alpha_1\alpha_2 + \alpha_3\alpha_4, \quad \beta_2 = \alpha_1\alpha_3 + \alpha_2\alpha_4, \quad \beta_3 = \alpha_1\alpha_4 + \alpha_2\alpha_3,
$$
and let
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(X) &= (X - \beta_1)(X - \beta_2)(X - \beta_3) \\
&= X^3 - a_2 X^2 + (a_1 a_3 - 4a_4) X + (a_1^2 a_4 + a_3^2 - 4 a_2 a_4)
\end{aligned}
$$
be the "cubic resolvent" of $f$.
Prove that $d_f = d_g$ (discriminants).
Suppose $d_f \neq 0$, and $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$ when necessary.
Assume also that $f(X)$ has no root in $F$.

(a) Show that $f$ has a quadratic factor in $F[X]$ if and only if, for some $i$, $\beta_i \in F$ and both $a_1^2 - 4a_2 + 4\beta_i$ and $\beta_i^2 - 4a_4$ are squares in $F$.

(b) $G_f = S_4 \iff g$ has no root in $F$ and $d_f$ not a square in $F$; $G_f = A_4 \iff g$ has no root in $F$ and $d_f$ is a square in $F$.

Suppose from now on, that $f$ is irreducible in $F[X]$ and $g$ has a root, say $\beta_1$, in $F$.

(c) Show that $G_f$ is a group of order a power of $2$, so is contained in a $2$-Sylow subgroup of $S_4$.

(d) Show $G_f = V \stackrel{\mathrm{defn}}{=} \{(1), (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$ if and only if $g$ has three roots in $f$, if and only if $d_f$ is a square in $F$.

(e) Suppose $G_f$ has exactly one root in $F$. Show that $G_f$ is cyclic of order $4$, or is dihedral of order $8$, and give a criterion to decide which.

(f) Find $G_f$'s for the following five quartic $f$'s:

(i) $x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1$;

(ii) $x^4 + x + 1$;

(iii) $x^4 + 2$;

(iv) $x^4 + 8x + 12$;

(v) $x^4 - 2x^2 + 9$.
:::

::: {.remark}
Three misprints in the source statement are kept above.
In the displayed expansion of $g$ the constant term should be $-(a_1^2 a_4 + a_3^2 - 4a_2 a_4)$, since it equals $-\beta_1\beta_2\beta_3$: for $f(X) = (X-1)^3(X-2)$ we have $a_1 = 5$, $a_2 = 9$, $a_3 = 7$, $a_4 = 2$ and $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = \beta_3 = 3$, so $g(X) = (X-3)^3$ has constant term $-27$, while $a_1^2 a_4 + a_3^2 - 4a_2 a_4 = 27$.
In (d), "three roots in $f$" means three roots in $F$.
In (e), the hypothesis is that $g$ has exactly one root in $F$.
:::
