::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(X) = X^4 - a_1 X^3 + a_2 X^2 - a_3 X + a_4 = \prod_{i=1}^{4} (X - \alpha_i)
$$
with $a_i \in F$, $F$ a field, $\alpha_i \in K = F(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_4)$, the splitting field.
Put
$$
\beta_1 = \alpha_1\alpha_2 + \alpha_3\alpha_4, \quad \beta_2 = \alpha_1\alpha_3 + \alpha_2\alpha_4, \quad \beta_3 = \alpha_1\alpha_4 + \alpha_2\alpha_3,
$$
and let
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(X) &= (X - \beta_1)(X - \beta_2)(X - \beta_3) \\
&= X^3 - a_2 X^2 + (a_1 a_3 - 4a_4) X + (a_1^2 a_4 + a_3^2 - 4 a_2 a_4)
\end{aligned}
$$
be the "cubic resolvent" of $f$.
Prove that $d_f = d_g$ (discriminants).
Suppose $d_f \neq 0$, and $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$ when necessary.
Assume also that $f(X)$ has no root in $F$.

(a) Show that $f$ has a quadratic factor in $F[X]$ if and only if, for some $i$, $\beta_i \in F$ and both $a_1^2 - 4a_2 + 4\beta_i$ and $\beta_i^2 - 4a_4$ are squares in $F$.

(b) $G_f = S_4 \iff g$ has no root in $F$ and $d_f$ not a square in $F$; $G_f = A_4 \iff g$ has no root in $F$ and $d_f$ is a square in $F$.

Suppose from now on, that $f$ is irreducible in $F[X]$ and $g$ has a root, say $\beta_1$, in $F$.

(c) Show that $G_f$ is a group of order a power of $2$, so is contained in a $2$-Sylow subgroup of $S_4$.

(d) Show $G_f = V \stackrel{\mathrm{defn}}{=} \{(1), (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$ if and only if $g$ has three roots in $f$, if and only if $d_f$ is a square in $F$.

(e) Suppose $G_f$ has exactly one root in $F$. Show that $G_f$ is cyclic of order $4$, or is dihedral of order $8$, and give a criterion to decide which.

(f) Find $G_f$'s for the following five quartic $f$'s:

(i) $x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1$;

(ii) $x^4 + x + 1$;

(iii) $x^4 + 2$;

(iv) $x^4 + 8x + 12$;

(v) $x^4 - 2x^2 + 9$.
:::

::: {.remark}
Errata to the statement.
In the displayed expansion of $g$ the constant term should be $-(a_1^2 a_4 + a_3^2 - 4a_2 a_4)$, since it equals $-\beta_1\beta_2\beta_3$: for $f(X) = (X-1)^3(X-2)$ we have $a_1 = 5$, $a_2 = 9$, $a_3 = 7$, $a_4 = 2$ and $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = \beta_3 = 3$, so $g(X) = (X-3)^3$ has constant term $-27$, while $a_1^2 a_4 + a_3^2 - 4a_2 a_4 = 27$.
In (d), "three roots in $f$" means three roots in $F$.
In (e), the hypothesis is that $g$ has exactly one root in $F$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Throughout, $g$ denotes the root-defined resolvent
\[
g(X)=\prod_{i=1}^3(X-\beta_i),
\]
so its constant term is the corrected value
\[
-(a_1^2a_4+a_3^2-4a_2a_4).
\]
Let
\[
V=\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}\subset S_4,
\]
and let
\[
\pi:S_4\longrightarrow S_3
\]
be the permutation action on the three pairings
\[
12|34,\qquad 13|24,\qquad 14|23,
\]
equivalently on $\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3$. Then $\ker\pi=V$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The discriminants satisfy $\boxed{d_f=d_g}$.


::: pf-proof

Direct subtraction gives
\[
\beta_1-\beta_2=(\alpha_1-\alpha_4)(\alpha_2-\alpha_3),
\]
\[
\beta_1-\beta_3=(\alpha_1-\alpha_3)(\alpha_2-\alpha_4),
\]
and
\[
\beta_2-\beta_3=(\alpha_1-\alpha_2)(\alpha_3-\alpha_4).
\]
Hence
\[
d_g=\prod_{i<j}(\beta_i-\beta_j)^2
=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2
=d_f.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Part (a) holds: $f$ has a quadratic factor in $F[X]$ if and only if for some $i$, $\beta_i\in F$ and both
\[
a_1^2-4a_2+4\beta_i,
\qquad
\beta_i^2-4a_4
\]
are squares in $F$.


::: pf-proof

It is enough to treat $i=1$. Put
\[
S=\alpha_1+\alpha_2,
\quad
T=\alpha_3+\alpha_4,
\quad
P=\alpha_1\alpha_2,
\quad
Q=\alpha_3\alpha_4.
\]
Then
\[
S+T=a_1,
\qquad
P+Q=\beta_1,
\qquad
ST=a_2-\beta_1,
\qquad
PQ=a_4,
\]
and therefore
\[
(S-T)^2=a_1^2-4a_2+4\beta_1,
\qquad
(P-Q)^2=\beta_1^2-4a_4.
\]

If $f$ has a quadratic factor over $F$, its roots can be partitioned into two Galois-stable pairs, which after relabeling are $\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\}$ and $\{\alpha_3,\alpha_4\}$. Then $S,T,P,Q\in F$, so $\beta_1=P+Q\in F$ and the two displayed quantities are squares in $F$.

Conversely, suppose $\beta_1\in F$ and choose $s,t\in F$ with
\[
s^2=a_1^2-4a_2+4\beta_1,
\qquad
t^2=\beta_1^2-4a_4.
\]
From the identities above,
\[
s^2t^2=(a_1\beta_1-2a_3)^2,
\]
because
\[
(S-T)(P-Q)=a_1\beta_1-2a_3.
\]
Changing the sign of $t$ if necessary, take $st=a_1\beta_1-2a_3$. Define
\[
S_0=\frac{a_1+s}{2},
\quad
T_0=\frac{a_1-s}{2},
\quad
P_0=\frac{\beta_1+t}{2},
\quad
Q_0=\frac{\beta_1-t}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
S_0+T_0=a_1,
\quad
P_0+Q_0+S_0T_0=a_2,
\quad
P_0Q_0=a_4,
\]
and
\[
S_0Q_0+T_0P_0
=\frac{a_1\beta_1-st}{2}
=a_3.
\]
Therefore
\[
f(X)=(X^2-S_0X+P_0)(X^2-T_0X+Q_0)
\]
in $F[X]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Part (b) holds:
\[
\boxed{G_f=S_4}
\iff
g\text{ has no root in }F\text{ and }d_f\notin F^{\times2},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{G_f=A_4}
\iff
g\text{ has no root in }F\text{ and }d_f\in F^{\times2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and $d_f\ne0$, the three $\beta_i$ are distinct. If $g$ has no root in $F$, then $\pi(G_f)$ has no fixed point on the three $\beta_i$, so it is transitive. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, a quadratic factor of $f$ would force some $\beta_i\in F$; together with the standing assumption that $f$ has no root in $F$, this shows that $f$ is irreducible. Hence $G_f$ is transitive on the four roots.

The Vandermonde element
\[
\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)
\]
satisfies $\Delta^2=d_f$ and $\sigma(\Delta)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\Delta$. Thus $d_f$ is a square in $F$ exactly when $G_f\subseteq A_4$.

If $d_f$ is a square, then $G_f\subseteq A_4$ and $\pi(G_f)=A_3$. The kernel $G_f\cap V$ is normal in $G_f$, and conjugation by a lift of a $3$-cycle cyclically permutes the three nonidentity elements of $V$. Hence $G_f\cap V$ is either trivial or all of $V$. In the first case $|G_f|=3$, so $G_f$ fixes one of the four roots, contrary to irreducibility. Therefore $G_f\cap V=V$, whence $|G_f|=12$ and $G_f=A_4$.

If $d_f$ is not a square, $G_f$ contains an odd permutation. The quotient map $\pi:S_4\to S_3$ satisfies $\pi^{-1}(A_3)=A_4$, because both groups have index $2$ and $V\subset A_4$. Hence the image of an odd permutation is odd, so the transitive group $\pi(G_f)$ is all of $S_3$. If $G_f\cap V$ were trivial, then $G_f\cong S_3$, and every $S_3$ subgroup of $S_4$ fixes one of the four letters, contrary to irreducibility. If $|G_f\cap V|=2$, then $|G_f|=12$, but the unique index-$2$ subgroup of $S_4$ is $A_4$, contrary to the presence of an odd permutation. Hence $G_f\cap V=V$, so $|G_f|=24$ and $G_f=S_4$.

The converse directions are immediate: $\pi(S_4)=S_3$ and $\pi(A_4)=A_3$, so neither group fixes a $\beta_i$, while $S_4\not\subset A_4$ and $A_4\subset A_4$ give the stated square-discriminant conditions.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Under the hypotheses of part (c), $G_f$ has order a power of $2$ and is contained in a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_4$.


::: pf-proof

Since $\beta_1\in F$, every element of $G_f$ fixes the pairing $12|34$. Its stabilizer in $S_4$ has order $8$ and is a Sylow $2$-subgroup, isomorphic to the dihedral group $D_8$. Thus $G_f$ is a subgroup of a group of order $8$, so $|G_f|$ is a power of $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Part (d) holds:
\[
\boxed{G_f=V}
\iff
g\text{ has three roots in }F
\iff
d_f\in F^{\times2}.
\]


::: pf-proof

The polynomial $g$ has all three roots in $F$ exactly when $G_f$ fixes every $\beta_i$, equivalently when $G_f\subseteq\ker\pi=V$. Since $f$ is irreducible, $G_f$ is transitive on four letters; no proper subgroup of $V$ is transitive. Hence this condition is equivalent to $G_f=V$.

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $G_f$ lies in the order-$8$ stabilizer of $\beta_1$. Its intersection with $A_4$ is exactly $V$. By the Vandermonde criterion from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $d_f$ is a square exactly when $G_f\subseteq A_4$, hence exactly when $G_f\subseteq V$, which by transitivity is exactly when $G_f=V$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Suppose $g$ has exactly one root $\beta_1\in F$. Then
\[
\boxed{G_f\cong C_4\text{ or }D_8}.
\]
More precisely, set
\[
u=a_1^2-4a_2+4\beta_1,
\qquad
v=\beta_1^2-4a_4,
\]
and choose a nonzero $c$ among $u,v$. Then
\[
\boxed{G_f\cong C_4\iff c\,d_f\in F^{\times2}},
\qquad
\boxed{G_f\cong D_8\iff c\,d_f\notin F^{\times2}}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $G_f$ is a transitive subgroup of the dihedral stabilizer $D_8$ of the pairing $12|34$. Since $g$ does not split completely, step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} excludes the transitive Klein four subgroup $V$. A transitive subgroup has order divisible by $4$. If its order is $8$, it is all of $D_8$; if its order is $4$, the only remaining transitive order-$4$ subgroup is the cyclic rotation subgroup $C_4$, since the other Klein four subgroup preserves the two sets $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4\}$. Thus $G_f\cong C_4$ or $D_8$.

With the notation of step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $u=(S-T)^2$ and $v=(P-Q)^2$, and
\[
uv=(a_1\beta_1-2a_3)^2.
\]
Hence if both $u$ and $v$ are nonzero they have the same square class. They cannot both be squares in $F$, since step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} would then make $f$ reducible. Thus any nonzero choice $c$ is a nonsquare.

If $c=u$, put $w=S-T$; if $c=v$, put $w=P-Q$. In either case $w^2=c$. Inside the full stabilizer $D_8$, the subgroup fixing $w$ is
\[
H=\{1,(12),(34),(12)(34)\},
\]
after the chosen labeling: permutations internal to the two pairs fix $w$, while an element interchanging the two pairs sends $w$ to $-w$. Thus $F(w)$ is the quadratic subfield associated with $G_f\cap H$.

The quadratic field $F(\sqrt{d_f})$ is associated with the even subgroup $G_f\cap A_4$. If $G_f=C_4$, both $G_f\cap H$ and $G_f\cap A_4$ are the unique subgroup of order $2$ in $C_4$, so
\[
F(w)=F(\sqrt{d_f}).
\]
Equivalently, $c$ and $d_f$ have the same square class, so $c\,d_f$ is a square.

If $G_f=D_8$, then $H$ and $D_8\cap A_4=V$ are distinct index-$2$ subgroups. Their fixed quadratic fields are therefore distinct, so $c$ and $d_f$ have different square classes and $c\,d_f$ is not a square. This proves the criterion.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For the five quartics in part (f), the Galois groups are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
f(X) & G_f\\
\hline
X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1 & C_4\\
X^4+X+1 & S_4\\
X^4+2 & D_8\\
X^4+8X+12 & A_4\\
X^4-2X^2+9 & V
\end{array}}
\]


::: pf-proof

For (i), $f=\Phi_5$ is irreducible and
\[
g(X)=X^3-X^2-3X+2=(X-2)(X^2+X-1),
\qquad
d_f=d_g=125.
\]
Thus $g$ has exactly one rational root $\beta_1=2$. Here
\[
u=5,
\qquad
u\,d_f=625=25^2,
\]
so step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives $G_f=C_4$.

For (ii), the only possible rational roots of $f=X^4+X+1$ are $\pm1$, and neither is a root. Its resolvent is
\[
g(X)=X^3-4X-1,
\]
which has no rational root, and
\[
d_f=d_g=229
\]
is not a square. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives $G_f=S_4$.

For (iii), $f=X^4+2$ is Eisenstein at $2$, and
\[
g(X)=X(X^2-8),
\qquad
d_f=d_g=2048.
\]
The unique rational resolvent root is $\beta_1=0$. Here $u=0$ and
\[
v=-8,
\qquad
v\,d_f=-16384,
\]
which is not a square in $\QQ$. Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives $G_f=D_8$.

For (iv),
\[
X^4+8X+12=(X^2-2)^2+4(X+1)^2+4>0
\]
for every real $X$, so $f$ has no rational root. Its resolvent is
\[
g(X)=X^3-48X-64.
\]
Modulo $5$ this is $X^3+2X+1$, which has no root in $\FF_5$, so $g$ has no rational root. Moreover
\[
d_f=d_g=331776=576^2.
\]
Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives $G_f=A_4$.

For (v), the quartic $f=X^4-2X^2+9$ is irreducible over $\QQ$. Indeed, if it factored into monic quadratics, the vanishing cubic coefficient would give a factorization
\[
(X^2+aX+b)(X^2-aX+d).
\]
The linear coefficient gives $a(d-b)=0$ and the constant term gives $bd=9$. If $a=0$, then $b+d=-2$ and $bd=9$, impossible over $\QQ$ because $T^2+2T+9$ has discriminant $-32$. If $b=d$, then $b=\pm3$; the quadratic coefficient would require $a^2=8$ or $a^2=-4$, again impossible in $\QQ$. Thus $f$ is irreducible. Its resolvent is
\[
g(X)=X^3+2X^2-36X-72=(X+2)(X-6)(X+6),
\]
so $g$ has three rational roots. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives $G_f=V$.

:::
