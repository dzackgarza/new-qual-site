::: {.problem}
(6) Let $A = \CC[X, Y, Z]$. What are the minimal non-zero prime ideals of $A$? Try to prove that the only maximal ideals of $A$ are those of the form $(X - x_{0}, Y - y_{0}, Z - z_{0})$ (special case of Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz). The prime ideals of $A$ which are neither maximal nor minimal nonzero are harder to describe. One such is $P = (X, Y)$. But not all of them can be generated by two elements. For example, let $\varphi \colon A \to \CC[T]$ be the homomorphism defined by $\varphi(f(X, Y, Z)) = f(T^{3}, T^{4}, T^{5})$, and let $P$ be the kernel of $\varphi$. Try to show that $P$ is generated by the three elements $Y^{2} - XZ$, $X^{3} - YZ$, $Z^{2} - X^{2} Y$, but on the other hand, $P$ cannot be generated by two elements.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
A=\CC[X,Y,Z].
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The minimal nonzero prime ideals of $A$ are exactly the ideals
$$
\boxed{(f)}
$$
with $f\in A$ irreducible.


::: pf-proof

The ring $A$ is a UFD. Hence every irreducible element is prime, so $(f)$ is a nonzero prime ideal when $f$ is irreducible.

Conversely, let $P$ be a nonzero prime ideal. Choose $0\ne h\in P$ and factor $h$ into irreducibles. Since $P$ is prime, some irreducible factor $f$ lies in $P$, so
$$
(f)\subseteq P.
$$
Therefore a nonzero prime minimal under inclusion must equal $(f)$ for some irreducible $f$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The maximal ideals of $A$ are exactly
$$
\boxed{(X-x_0,Y-y_0,Z-z_0)}
$$
with $x_0,y_0,z_0\in\CC$.


::: pf-proof

Each displayed ideal is maximal because evaluation at $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ gives a surjection
$$
A\longrightarrow\CC
$$
with that kernel.

Conversely, let $\mathfrak m$ be maximal and put $L=A/\mathfrak m$. Then $L$ is a field generated as a $\CC$-algebra by the residue classes of $X,Y,Z$. To see directly that these generators are algebraic, suppose some maximal subfamily $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ is algebraically independent over $\CC$. The remaining generators are algebraic over
$$
F=\CC(u_1,\ldots,u_r),
$$
so after clearing the finitely many denominators in their monic equations, $L$ is integral over a localization
$$
R=\CC[u_1,\ldots,u_r,1/s]
$$
for some nonzero polynomial $s$. Since $L$ is a field integral over $R$, $R$ must itself be a field: for $0\ne r\in R$, the element $r^{-1}\in L$ is integral over $R$, and multiplying its monic equation by a suitable power of $r$ expresses $r^{-1}$ as an element of $R$.

If $r>0$, however, $R$ is not a field. Because $\CC$ is infinite, choose $c\in\CC$ such that $u_1-c$ does not divide $s$. Then $u_1-c$ is a nonunit of $R$, a contradiction. Hence $r=0$, so all generators are algebraic over $\CC$. Being finitely many algebraic generators, they generate a finite algebraic extension of $\CC$, which must equal $\CC$ because $\CC$ is algebraically closed.

Let the residue classes of $X,Y,Z$ be $x_0,y_0,z_0\in\CC$. Then
$$
X-x_0,\qquad Y-y_0,\qquad Z-z_0
$$
all lie in $\mathfrak m$, so
$$
(X-x_0,Y-y_0,Z-z_0)\subseteq\mathfrak m.
$$
The left-hand ideal is maximal by the first paragraph, hence equality holds.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The prime ideal $(X,Y)$ is neither minimal nonzero nor maximal.


::: pf-proof

The quotient
$$
A/(X,Y)\cong\CC[Z]
$$
is a domain, so $(X,Y)$ is prime. It properly contains the nonzero prime $(X)$, so it is not minimal nonzero. The quotient $\CC[Z]$ is not a field, so it is not maximal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For
$$
\varphi:A\to\CC[T],
\qquad
\varphi(X)=T^3,\quad
\varphi(Y)=T^4,\quad
\varphi(Z)=T^5,
$$
the three polynomials
$$
F_1=Y^2-XZ,\qquad
F_2=X^3-YZ,\qquad
F_3=Z^2-X^2Y
$$
belong to $\ker\varphi$.


::: pf-proof

Direct substitution gives
$$
T^8-T^8=0,\qquad
T^9-T^9=0,\qquad
T^{10}-T^{10}=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If
$$
I=(F_1,F_2,F_3),
$$
then every class in $A/I$ has a representative of the form
$$
a(X)+b(X)Y+c(X)Z,
\qquad
a,b,c\in\CC[X].
$$


::: pf-proof

The relations in $A/I$ are
$$
Y^2=XZ,\qquad
YZ=X^3,\qquad
Z^2=X^2Y.
$$
Starting with a monomial $X^aY^bZ^c$, repeatedly replace $Y^2$, $YZ$, or $Z^2$ whenever one occurs. Each replacement lowers the total exponent $b+c$ of $Y$ and $Z$. Thus the process terminates with a linear combination of monomials having $b+c\le1$, namely terms of the form
$$
X^n,\qquad X^nY,\qquad X^nZ.
$$
Summing over monomials gives the stated form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The induced map
$$
\bar\varphi:A/I\longrightarrow\CC[T^3,T^4,T^5]
$$
is an isomorphism. Hence
$$
\boxed{\ker\varphi=I}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Surjectivity is immediate from the definitions of the images of $X,Y,Z$.

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, suppose an element of the kernel has representative
$$
a(X)+b(X)Y+c(X)Z.
$$
Its image is
$$
a(T^3)+b(T^3)T^4+c(T^3)T^5.
$$
The exponents occurring in these three summands are respectively congruent to
$$
0,\qquad1,\qquad2\pmod3.
$$
Therefore no cancellation can occur between distinct summands. If the sum is zero, each summand is zero, hence $a=b=c=0$. Thus $\bar\varphi$ is injective.

Since $I\subseteq\ker\varphi$ by step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and the induced quotient map is injective, equality follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The prime ideal $P=\ker\varphi$ cannot be generated by two elements.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
P=(F_1,F_2,F_3).
$$
Let
$$
\mathfrak m=(X,Y,Z).
$$
Because $P\subseteq\mathfrak m^2$, one has
$$
\mathfrak mP\subseteq\mathfrak m^3.
$$
Consider the images of $F_1,F_2,F_3$ in the vector space
$$
P/\mathfrak mP
$$
over $A/\mathfrak m\cong\CC$.

Their quadratic parts are
$$
Y^2-XZ,\qquad -YZ,\qquad Z^2.
$$
These three quadratic forms are linearly independent over $\CC$. Hence no nontrivial $\CC$-linear combination of $F_1,F_2,F_3$ can lie in $\mathfrak mP\subseteq\mathfrak m^3$. Their classes in $P/\mathfrak mP$ are therefore linearly independent, so
$$
\dim_\CC P/\mathfrak mP\ge3.
$$

If $P$ were generated by two elements, their images would span $P/\mathfrak mP$, giving dimension at most $2$, a contradiction.

:::
