::: {.problem}
(2) Suppose$K / F$is a finite Galois extension. Let$G = \operatorname { G a l } ( K / F )$

(a) Suppose G acts transitively on a set I. Show that there exists a family$( \alpha _ { i } ) _ { i \in I }$of elements of K such that$\sigma ( \alpha _ { i } ) = \alpha _ { \sigma i }$for all$\sigma \in G$

(b) Let n be an integer$\geq 0$and suppose$h : G \hookrightarrow { \mathcal { S } } _ { n }$is an injective group homomorphism. Show that if F has at least n elements, then there is a polynomial$f ( X ) \in F [ X ]$with distinct roots such that K is a splitting field for f over F and such that$G _ { f } = h ( G ) \subset S _ { n }$
:::

::: {.solution}

By the Normal Basis Theorem, choose $\theta\in K$ such that
$$
\{\sigma(\theta):\sigma\in G\}
$$
is an $F$-basis of $K$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For part (a), there is an equivariant family $(\alpha_i)_{i\in I}$ whose elements are in fact pairwise distinct.


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Fix $i_0\in I$, let
$$
H=\operatorname{Stab}_G(i_0),
\qquad
\beta_H=\sum_{\tau\in H}\tau(\theta),
$$
and then $\operatorname{Stab}_G(\beta_H)=H$.


::: pf-proof

Every element of $H$ permutes the summands of $\beta_H$, so $H$ fixes $\beta_H$. Conversely, if $\gamma(\beta_H)=\beta_H$, then
$$
\sum_{\tau\in H}(\gamma\tau)(\theta)
=
\sum_{\tau\in H}\tau(\theta).
$$
The normal-basis elements $\sigma(\theta)$ are linearly independent, so the two sums have the same support in $G$. Hence $\gamma H=H$, which is equivalent to $\gamma\in H$. Thus the stabilizer is exactly $H$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Define
$$
\alpha_{\sigma i_0}=\sigma(\beta_H)
\qquad(\sigma\in G).
$$
This is well defined, the $\alpha_i$ are pairwise distinct, and
$$
\rho(\alpha_i)=\alpha_{\rho i}
\qquad
(\rho\in G,\ i\in I).
$$


::: pf-proof

If $\sigma i_0=\sigma' i_0$, then $\sigma^{-1}\sigma'\in H$. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that $H$ fixes $\beta_H$, so
$$
\sigma'(\beta_H)=\sigma(\beta_H).
$$
Thus the definition is independent of the chosen representative.

If $\alpha_{\sigma i_0}=\alpha_{\sigma' i_0}$, then $\sigma^{-1}\sigma'$ fixes $\beta_H$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $\sigma^{-1}\sigma'\in H$, so $\sigma i_0=\sigma' i_0$. Hence the family is pairwise distinct.

Finally,
$$
\rho(\alpha_{\sigma i_0})
=\rho\sigma(\beta_H)
=\alpha_{\rho\sigma i_0},
$$
which is the required equivariance.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} constructs the family required in part (a), with the stronger property that its entries are distinct.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For part (b), there exists a polynomial $f(X)\in F[X]$ with $n$ distinct roots, splitting field $K$, and root-permutation group $G_f=h(G)\subseteq S_n$.


::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

The case $n=0$ is immediate under the usual convention for the empty root set; hence suppose $n\ge1$.


::: pf-proof

If $n=0$, then $S_0$ is trivial, so injectivity of $h$ forces $G=1$ and therefore $K=F$. The constant polynomial $f=1$ has empty root set, splitting field $F$, and trivial permutation group. Thus it remains to treat $n\ge1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-2}

Let $I=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with $G$-action
$$
\sigma\cdot i=h(\sigma)(i).
$$
Write its orbits as $I_1,\ldots,I_r$, choose $i_j\in I_j$, and put
$$
H_j=\operatorname{Stab}_G(i_j).
$$
There exist scalars $c_1,\ldots,c_r\in F$ such that they are pairwise distinct, every $c_j$ is nonzero except possibly one, and $c_j=0$ only when $H_j=G$.


::: pf-proof

If the action has a fixed point, assign $c_j=0$ to one fixed-point orbit. There remain at most $r-1\le n-1$ orbits, and $|F|\ge n$ gives at least $n-1$ nonzero field elements from which to choose distinct scalars.

If there is no fixed point, every orbit has at least two elements, so $r\le n/2\le n-1$. Again $F$ has at least $n-1$ nonzero elements, so all $c_j$ can be chosen distinct and nonzero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-3}

For each orbit define
$$
\beta_j=c_j\sum_{\tau\in H_j}\tau(\theta),
\qquad
\alpha_{\sigma i_j}=\sigma(\beta_j).
$$
Then $(\alpha_i)_{i\in I}$ is an equivariant family of $n$ pairwise distinct elements of $K$, and
$$
\operatorname{Stab}_G(\alpha_i)=\operatorname{Stab}_G(i)
\qquad(i\in I).
$$


::: pf-proof

For every orbit with $c_j\ne0$, multiplication by the nonzero scalar $c_j\in F$ does not change a stabilizer. The construction proved in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, applied to $H_j$, therefore shows that $\operatorname{Stab}_G(\beta_j)=H_j$ and that the assignment $\alpha_{\sigma i_j}=\sigma(\beta_j)$ is well defined, equivariant, and injective on $I_j$. If $c_j=0$, then $H_j=G$, so $I_j$ is a singleton and the same conclusions hold for its sole value $\beta_j=0$.

It remains to compare different orbits. Suppose
$$
\sigma(\beta_j)=\sigma'(\beta_k)
\qquad(j\ne k).
$$
If one of $c_j,c_k$ is zero, exactly one side is zero and the other is a nonzero linear combination of normal-basis elements, a contradiction. If both are nonzero, then in the normal basis the left side has coefficient $c_j$ precisely on the support $\sigma H_j$, while the right side has coefficient $c_k$ precisely on $\sigma'H_k$. Equality forces both the supports and the nonzero coefficients to agree, hence $c_j=c_k$, contradicting step [](#s2-2){.pf-ref}. Thus all $n$ elements are pairwise distinct.

If $i=\sigma i_j$, then
$$
\operatorname{Stab}_G(\alpha_i)
=\sigma\operatorname{Stab}_G(\beta_j)\sigma^{-1}
=\sigma H_j\sigma^{-1}
=\operatorname{Stab}_G(i),
$$
which proves the stabilizer equality.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-4}

Define
$$
f(X)=\prod_{i=1}^n (X-\alpha_i).
$$
Then $f\in F[X]$, its roots are distinct, and its splitting field over $F$ is $K$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2-3){.pf-ref} gives distinct roots. Since $G$ permutes the set $\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\}$, every coefficient of $f$ is fixed by $G$. Because $K^G=F$, the coefficients lie in $F$.

Let
$$
M=F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)\subseteq K.
$$
An element $\sigma\in G$ fixes $M$ pointwise if and only if it fixes every $\alpha_i$. By step [](#s2-3){.pf-ref}, this is equivalent to fixing every $i\in I$. Hence
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/M)
=\bigcap_{i\in I}\operatorname{Stab}_G(\alpha_i)
=\ker h
=\{1\}.
$$
The Galois correspondence therefore gives $M=K$. Thus $K$ is the splitting field of $f$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-5}

Under the labeling $i\leftrightarrow\alpha_i$, the permutation group of the roots is exactly $h(G)$.


::: pf-proof

For every $\sigma\in G$ and $i\in I$, step [](#s2-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\sigma(\alpha_i)=\alpha_{h(\sigma)(i)}.
$$
Since step [](#s2-4){.pf-ref} identifies $K$ as the splitting field of $f$, its Galois group acts on the roots through precisely these permutations. Therefore
$$
G_f=h(G)\subseteq S_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s2-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s2-5){.pf-ref} establish every assertion in part (b).

:::

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s2){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
