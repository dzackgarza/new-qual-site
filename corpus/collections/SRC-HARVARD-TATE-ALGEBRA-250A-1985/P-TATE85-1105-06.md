::: {.problem}
(6)

(a) Prove that $\mathbb R$ has no non-trivial automorphism.
(Hint: show that an automorphism of $\mathbb R$ is order-preserving automatically.)

(b) Show that the only automorphisms of $\mathbb C$ which commute with complex conjugation are the identity and complex conjugation.
:::

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Every field automorphism \(\sigma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) is order-preserving.


::: pf-proof

Every field automorphism fixes the prime field, so \(\sigma(q)=q\) for all \(q\in\mathbb Q\).

If \(x>0\), then \(x=y^2\) for some nonzero \(y\in\mathbb R\). Hence
\[
\sigma(x)=\sigma(y)^2>0,
\]
because \(\sigma(y)\ne0\). Thus \(\sigma\) preserves positivity. If \(x<y\), then \(y-x>0\), so
\[
\sigma(y)-\sigma(x)=\sigma(y-x)>0.
\]
Therefore \(\sigma(x)<\sigma(y)\).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The only field automorphism of \(\mathbb R\) is the identity.


::: pf-proof

Let \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb R)\) and \(x\in\mathbb R\). Suppose first that \(x<\sigma(x)\). By density of \(\mathbb Q\) in \(\mathbb R\), choose \(q\in\mathbb Q\) with
\[
x<q<\sigma(x).
\]
Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and \(\sigma(q)=q\) give
\[
\sigma(x)<\sigma(q)=q,
\]
contradicting \(q<\sigma(x)\).

If instead \(\sigma(x)<x\), choose \(q\in\mathbb Q\) with
\[
\sigma(x)<q<x.
\]
Applying the order-preserving map \(\sigma\) to \(q<x\) gives
\[
q=\sigma(q)<\sigma(x),
\]
again a contradiction. Hence \(\sigma(x)=x\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R\), so
\[
\sigma=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb R}.
\]
This proves part (a).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If \(\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)\) commutes with complex conjugation, then \(\tau(\mathbb R)=\mathbb R\).


::: pf-proof

For \(x\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\overline{\tau(x)}
=\tau(\overline x)
=\tau(x),
\]
so \(\tau(x)\in\mathbb R\). Thus \(\tau(\mathbb R)\subseteq\mathbb R\).

Conversely, let \(y\in\mathbb R\). Since \(\tau\) is surjective, choose \(z\in\mathbb C\) with \(\tau(z)=y\). Then
\[
\tau(\overline z)
=\overline{\tau(z)}
=\overline y
=y
=\tau(z).
\]
Injectivity of \(\tau\) gives \(\overline z=z\), hence \(z\in\mathbb R\). Therefore \(y\in\tau(\mathbb R)\), proving
\[
\tau(\mathbb R)=\mathbb R.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The only automorphisms of \(\mathbb C\) commuting with conjugation are
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb C}\quad\text{and}\quad z\mapsto\overline z}.
\]


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the restriction \(\tau|_{\mathbb R}\) is an automorphism of \(\mathbb R\). Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} therefore gives
\[
\tau(x)=x
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).
\]
Since
\[
\tau(i)^2=\tau(i^2)=\tau(-1)=-1,
\]
one has \(\tau(i)=i\) or \(\tau(i)=-i\).

Every \(z\in\mathbb C\) has the form \(z=a+bi\) with \(a,b\in\mathbb R\). If \(\tau(i)=i\), then
\[
\tau(a+bi)=a+bi,
\]
so \(\tau=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb C}\). If \(\tau(i)=-i\), then
\[
\tau(a+bi)=a-bi=\overline{a+bi},
\]
so \(\tau\) is complex conjugation. Both maps commute with complex conjugation, so these are exactly the possibilities.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves part (b).
