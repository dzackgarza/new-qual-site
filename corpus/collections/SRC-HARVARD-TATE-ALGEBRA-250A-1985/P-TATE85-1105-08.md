::: {.problem}
(8) Suppose $[F:\mathbb Q]$ is odd.
Prove that $-1$ is not a sum of squares of elements of $F$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The number field $F$ has at least one real embedding $\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\RR$.

::: {.proof}
Let $r_1$ be the number of real embeddings of $F$ and let $r_2$ be the number of conjugate pairs of nonreal complex embeddings. Then
$$
[F:\QQ]=r_1+2r_2.
$$
The left side is odd, while $2r_2$ is even. Hence $r_1$ is odd, and in particular $r_1\geq1$.
:::

<1>2. $-1$ cannot be a sum of squares in $F$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose, to the contrary, that
$$
-1=x_1^2+\cdots+x_m^2
$$
for some $x_1,\ldots,x_m\in F$. Apply the real embedding $\sigma$ from step <1>1. Then
$$
-1
=\sigma(-1)
=\sigma(x_1)^2+\cdots+\sigma(x_m)^2.
$$
Every term on the right is a nonnegative real number, so the right side is nonnegative, contradicting the left side.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>2 proves the required impossibility.
:::
:::
