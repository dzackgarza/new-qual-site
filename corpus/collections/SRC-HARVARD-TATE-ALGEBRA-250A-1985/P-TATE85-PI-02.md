::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be a p.i.d. (principal ideal domain, e.g., $A = \ZZ$ or $A = K[X]$, $K$ a field). Show that the ideals of the form $\pi A$, with $\pi$ a prime element of $A$, are maximal, and are the only non-zero prime ideals of $A$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

If $\pi$ is a prime element of $A$, then $\pi A$ is a prime ideal.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
xy\in\pi A,
$$
then $\pi\mid xy$. Since $\pi$ is prime, $\pi\mid x$ or $\pi\mid y$, so
$$
x\in\pi A
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
y\in\pi A.
$$
Also $\pi A\ne A$ because $\pi$ is not a unit. Hence $\pi A$ is prime.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every ideal $\pi A$ generated by a prime element is maximal.


::: pf-proof

Let $I$ be an ideal satisfying
$$
\pi A\subseteq I\subseteq A.
$$
Because $A$ is a PID, write
$$
I=aA
$$
for some $a\in A$. The inclusion $\pi A\subseteq aA$ implies
$$
\pi=ab
$$
for some $b\in A$. Since $\pi$ is prime, hence irreducible, either $a$ or $b$ is a unit.

If $a$ is a unit, then $I=A$. If $b$ is a unit, then $a$ and $\pi$ are associates, so
$$
I=aA=\pi A.
$$
Thus no proper ideal lies strictly between $\pi A$ and $A$, and $\pi A$ is maximal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Every nonzero prime ideal of $A$ is of the form $\pi A$ for a prime element $\pi$.


::: pf-proof

Let $P$ be a nonzero prime ideal. Since $A$ is a PID,
$$
P=aA
$$
for some $0\ne a\in A$. Because $P$ is proper, $a$ is not a unit. If $a\mid xy$, then
$$
xy\in aA=P.
$$
Primality of $P$ gives $x\in P$ or $y\in P$, hence $a\mid x$ or $a\mid y$. Therefore $a$ is a prime element. Taking $\pi=a$ gives
$$
P=\pi A.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves that every ideal generated by a prime element is maximal, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves that these are exactly the nonzero prime ideals of $A$.

:::
