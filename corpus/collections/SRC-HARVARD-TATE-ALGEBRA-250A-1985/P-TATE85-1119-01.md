::: {.problem}
(1) Let $k$ be a field, $\Mat_{2}(k)$ the ring of $2 \times 2$ matrices $\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ with $a, b, c, d \in k$, and let $A$ be the subring of all such matrices with $c = 0$. The maps $\varphi_{1} \colon \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \mapsto a$ and $\varphi_{2} \colon \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \mapsto d$ are homomorphisms of $A$ onto $k$. Let $P_{j} = \ker \varphi_{j}$ for $j = 1, 2$. Since $\dim_{k} A = 3 < \infty$, $A$ is of finite length as a left $A$-module.

(a) Show that $A/P_{1}$ and $A/P_{2}$ are the only simple $A$-modules (up to isomorphism).

(b) Compute $P_{1}^{2}, P_{1} P_{2}, P_{2} P_{1}, P_{2}^{2}$ and $P_{1} \cap P_{2}$. Are these the only two sided ideals of $A$ (besides $(0)$ and $A$)? What are the left ideals?

(c) What are the multiplicities of $A/P_{1}$ and $A/P_{2}$ in the left $A$-module $A$?

(d) Show that $A$ is not isomorphic to the direct product of two non-zero rings.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
e=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
n=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
f=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $A=ke\oplus kn\oplus kf$, and
$$
P_1=kn\oplus kf,
\qquad
P_2=ke\oplus kn.
$$
Put
$$
J=kn=P_1\cap P_2.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

$J^2=0$ and
$$
A/J\cong k\times k.
$$


::: pf-proof

The matrix-unit multiplication rules give $n^2=0$, so $J^2=0$. The map
$$
A\longrightarrow k\times k,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix}
\longmapsto(a,d)
$$
is a surjective ring homomorphism with kernel $kn=J$. The first isomorphism theorem gives the displayed quotient.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Every simple left $A$-module is annihilated by $J$.


::: pf-proof

Let $M$ be a simple left $A$-module. The subspace $JM$ is an $A$-submodule, so either $JM=0$ or $JM=M$. If $JM=M$, then
$$
J^2M=J(JM)=JM=M,
$$
contradicting $J^2=0$. Hence $JM=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Up to isomorphism, the only simple left $A$-modules are
$$
\boxed{A/P_1\quad\text{and}\quad A/P_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, every simple $A$-module is naturally a simple module over
$$
A/J\cong k\times k.
$$
Let $M$ be a simple $k\times k$-module. The two central idempotents $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ give
$$
M=(1,0)M\oplus(0,1)M.
$$
Simplicity forces exactly one summand to be nonzero. Thus the action factors through exactly one of the two coordinate projections $k\times k\to k$, and a simple module over the field $k$ is one-dimensional. These two modules are precisely the quotients defined by $\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_2$, namely $A/P_1$ and $A/P_2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The requested products and intersection are
$$
\boxed{
P_1^2=P_1,
\qquad
P_1P_2=0,
\qquad
P_2P_1=J,
\qquad
P_2^2=P_2,
\qquad
P_1\cap P_2=J.}
$$


::: pf-proof

For
$$
x=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix},
\quad
x'=\begin{pmatrix}0&b'\\0&d'\end{pmatrix}
\in P_1,
$$
we have
$$
xx'=\begin{pmatrix}0&bd'\\0&dd'\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus $P_1^2\subseteq P_1$, while multiplying by $f\in P_1$ on the right shows every element of $P_1$ lies in $P_1^2$. Hence $P_1^2=P_1$.

Similarly, for
$$
y=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
y'=\begin{pmatrix}a'&b'\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\in P_2,
$$
we have
$$
yy'=\begin{pmatrix}aa'&ab'\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
and taking $y=e$ shows $P_2^2=P_2$.

Direct multiplication gives $P_1P_2=0$. It also gives
$$
P_2P_1\subseteq kn,
$$
and $en=n$ shows $kn\subseteq P_2P_1$, so $P_2P_1=J$. Finally, the simultaneous conditions $a=0$ and $d=0$ defining $P_1\cap P_2$ leave exactly the matrices $bn$, hence $P_1\cap P_2=J$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The complete list of two-sided ideals of $A$ is
$$
\boxed{0,\ J,\ P_1,\ P_2,\ A}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $I$ be a nonzero two-sided ideal and choose
$$
x=ae+bn+df\in I,
\qquad
x\ne0.
$$
If $b\ne0$, then
$$
exf=bn\in I.
$$
If $a\ne0$, then $xn=an\in I$. If $d\ne0$, then $nx=dn\in I$. Since at least one of $a,b,d$ is nonzero, every nonzero two-sided ideal contains $J=kn$.

Therefore nonzero two-sided ideals of $A$ correspond to ideals of
$$
A/J\cong k\times k.
$$
The latter has exactly four ideals,
$$
0,\quad k\times0,\quad0\times k,\quad k\times k.
$$
Their inverse images are respectively $J,P_2,P_1,A$. Together with the zero ideal, this gives exactly the displayed list. In particular, $P_1$ and $P_2$ are not the only nonzero proper two-sided ideals: $J$ is the third.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The left ideals of $A$ are precisely
$$
\boxed{
\text{all $k$-subspaces of }P_2=ke\oplus kn,
\quad P_1,
\quad A.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
x=ae+bn+df
$$
and
$$
m=\alpha e+\beta n+\delta f\in A.
$$
Then
$$
mx=\alpha a e+(\alpha b+\beta d)n+\delta d f.
$$
If $d=0$, this becomes
$$
mx=\alpha(ae+bn)=\alpha x.
$$
Hence every $k$-subspace of $P_2=ke\oplus kn$ is stable under left multiplication by $A$, so every such subspace is a left ideal.

Now let $I$ be a left ideal containing an element $x=ae+bn+df$ with $d\ne0$. Since
$$
fx=df,
\qquad
nx=dn,
$$
we get $f,n\in I$, hence $P_1\subseteq I$. If $a=0$ for every element of $I$, then $I=P_1$. If some element of $I$ has $a\ne0$, subtracting its $kn\oplus kf$ component shows $e\in I$, and therefore $I=A$. This proves the classification.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

In the left regular module $A$, the multiplicities are
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{mult}_A(A/P_1)=2,
\qquad
\operatorname{mult}_A(A/P_2)=1},
$$
where $\operatorname{mult}_A$ denotes composition-factor multiplicity.


::: pf-proof

The chain
$$
0\subset J\subset P_1\subset A
$$
is a composition series. Indeed, multiplication on $n$ gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix}n=an,
$$
so $J\cong A/P_1$. Modulo $J$,
$$
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix}f
=bn+df
\equiv df\pmod J,
$$
so $P_1/J\cong A/P_2$. Finally, the top quotient is $A/P_1$. Thus the three composition factors are
$$
A/P_1,
\qquad
A/P_2,
\qquad
A/P_1,
$$
which gives the stated multiplicities.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The ring $A$ is not isomorphic to a direct product of two nonzero rings.


::: pf-proof

First compute the center. If
$$
x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix}
$$
commutes with $e$, then comparing $xe$ and $ex$ gives $b=0$. With $b=0$, comparing $xn$ and $nx$ gives $a=d$. Hence
$$
Z(A)=k\cdot1.
$$
The only idempotents in the field $k$ are $0$ and $1$, so the only central idempotents of $A$ are $0$ and $1$.

If $A\cong R\times S$ with both $R$ and $S$ nonzero, the inverse image of $(1,0)$ would be a central idempotent different from both $0$ and $1$, a contradiction.

:::
