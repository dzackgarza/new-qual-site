::: {.problem}
(2) Consider the polynomials
\[
\begin{aligned}
A(X)&=X^5-X^3-2X^2-2X-1,\\
B(X)&=X^5-X+3,\\
C(X)&=X^5+X^4-4X^3-3X^2+3X+1,\\
D(X)&=X^5-5,\\
E(X)&=X^5+10X^3-10X^2+35X-18.
\end{aligned}
\]
Each of these five is irreducible.
Their discriminants are
\[
d_A=47^2,\qquad d_B=252869\ \text{(prime)},\qquad d_C=11^4,\qquad d_D=5^9,\qquad d_E=2^6 5^8 11^{12}.
\]
The following table, produced in about 25 hours of running time by Tate's Macintosh, gives for each polynomial the number of primes in $\mathcal P_i$ (cf.
Problem 1) among the first 360 primes.
Thus, the sum of each row is 360.

|  | 0 roots | 1 root | 2 roots | 3 roots | 5 roots |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $A$ | 147 | 180 | 0 | 1 ($p=47$) | 32 |
| $B$ | 143 | 131 | 58 | 27 | 1 |
| $C$ | 288 | 1 ($p=11$) | 0 | 0 | 71 |
| $D$ | 78 | 272 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| $E$ | 142 | 88 | 128 | 0 | 2 |

The primes among the first 360 which are in $\mathcal P_5$, i.e. for which $f(X)$ splits in $\mathbb F_p$, are in each case as follows:

- $A$: $p=83,191,197,269,439,487,523,619,761,823,907,947,977,1193,1277,1319,1447,1481,1499,1579,1693,1709,1741,1811,1861,1867,2053,2213,2221,2273,2339,2351$.

- $B$: just $p=1609$.

- $C$: all primes $p\equiv\pm1\pmod{11}$, i.e. $p=23,43,67,89,\ldots,2287,2309,2311,2333,2377,2399$.

- $D$: $p=31,191,251,271,601,641,761,1091,1861,2381$.

- $E$: just $p=2063$ and $2213$.

Armed with all this information (you do not really need much of it), and using the simple form of $D(X)$, determine the groups $G_B$, $G_E$, and $G_D$.
What are the only possibilities for $G_A$ and $G_C$?
Which of these possibilities do you guess is the correct one?
