::: {.problem}
(1) Suppose$f ( X )$is irreducible and$G _ { f }$is abelian. Prove that the order of$G _ { f }$is the degree of f .
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume the intended convention that $f\in F[X]$ is separable. Let $L$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $F$, let
\[
G=G_f=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F),
\]
let $\Omega$ be the set of roots of $f$ in $L$, and write $n=\deg f$.

<1>1. The action of $G$ on $\Omega$ is transitive, and $|\Omega|=n$.

::: {.proof}
Because $f$ is separable of degree $n$, it has exactly $n$ distinct roots in $L$, so $|\Omega|=n$.

Let $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega$. Since $f$ is irreducible, it is the minimal polynomial over $F$ of both $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Hence there is an $F$-isomorphism
\[
F(\alpha)\longrightarrow F(\beta)
\]
sending $\alpha$ to $\beta$. Since $L/F$ is a finite normal extension, this embedding extends to an $F$-automorphism of $L$. Thus some element of $G$ sends $\alpha$ to $\beta$, proving transitivity.
:::

<1>2. For every $\alpha\in\Omega$, the stabilizer $G_\alpha$ is trivial.

::: {.proof}
Fix $\alpha\in\Omega$ and let $\sigma\in G_\alpha$. For any $\beta\in\Omega$, step <1>1 gives $\tau\in G$ with $\tau(\alpha)=\beta$. Since $G$ is abelian,
\[
\sigma(\beta)
=\sigma\tau(\alpha)
=\tau\sigma(\alpha)
=\tau(\alpha)
=\beta.
\]
Thus $\sigma$ fixes every root of $f$. The splitting field $L$ is generated over $F$ by those roots, so $\sigma$ fixes all of $L$. Hence $\sigma=1$, and therefore $G_\alpha=\{1\}$.
:::

<1>3. The order of $G_f$ is
\[
\boxed{|G_f|=\deg f}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Fix $\alpha\in\Omega$. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem and steps <1>1 and <1>2,
\[
|G_f|
=|G\cdot\alpha|\,|G_\alpha|
=n\cdot1
=\deg f.
\]
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the required equality under the intended separability convention.
:::
:::

::: {.remark}
The printed statement omits separability. If $G_f$ is defined for an arbitrary irreducible polynomial as the automorphism group of its splitting field, the claim is false. For example, let
\[
F=\mathbb F_p(t),
\qquad
f(X)=X^p-t.
\]
Writing $t=u^p$, one has $F=\mathbb F_p(u^p)$. The elements $1,u,\ldots,u^{p-1}$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb F_p(u^p)$: after clearing denominators, any relation becomes a polynomial relation in $u$ whose exponents lie in distinct congruence classes modulo $p$. Hence
\[
[\mathbb F_p(u):\mathbb F_p(u^p)]=p,
\]
so $f$ is irreducible of degree $p$. In characteristic $p$,
\[
X^p-t=(X-u)^p,
\]
so $u$ is its unique root. Every $F$-automorphism of the splitting field $F(u)$ therefore fixes $u$, and the automorphism group is trivial. Thus $|G_f|=1\ne p=\deg f$.
:::
