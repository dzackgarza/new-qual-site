::: {.problem}
(1) Suppose $f(X)$ is irreducible and $G_{f}$ is abelian. Prove that the order of $G_{f}$ is the degree of $f$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume the intended convention that $f\in F[X]$ is separable. Let $L$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $F$, let
$$
G=G_f=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F),
$$
let $\Omega$ be the set of roots of $f$ in $L$, and write $n=\deg f$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The action of $G$ on $\Omega$ is transitive, and $\cardinality{\Omega}=n$.


::: pf-proof

Because $f$ is separable of degree $n$, it has exactly $n$ distinct roots in $L$, so $\cardinality{\Omega}=n$.

Let $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega$. Since $f$ is irreducible, it is the minimal polynomial over $F$ of both $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Hence there is an $F$-isomorphism
$$
F(\alpha)\longrightarrow F(\beta)
$$
sending $\alpha$ to $\beta$. Since $L/F$ is a finite normal extension, this embedding extends to an $F$-automorphism of $L$. Thus some element of $G$ sends $\alpha$ to $\beta$, proving transitivity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every $\alpha\in\Omega$, the stabilizer $G_\alpha$ is trivial.


::: pf-proof

Fix $\alpha\in\Omega$ and let $\sigma\in G_\alpha$. For any $\beta\in\Omega$, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives $\tau\in G$ with $\tau(\alpha)=\beta$. Since $G$ is abelian,
$$
\sigma(\beta)
=\sigma\tau(\alpha)
=\tau\sigma(\alpha)
=\tau(\alpha)
=\beta.
$$
Thus $\sigma$ fixes every root of $f$. The splitting field $L$ is generated over $F$ by those roots, so $\sigma$ fixes all of $L$. Hence $\sigma=1$, and therefore $G_\alpha=\{1\}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The order of $G_f$ is
$$
\boxed{\cardinality{G_f}=\deg f}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Fix $\alpha\in\Omega$. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem and steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\cardinality{G_f}
=\cardinality{G\cdot\alpha}\,\cardinality{G_\alpha}
=n\cdot1
=\deg f.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the required equality under the intended separability convention.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark}
The printed statement omits separability. If $G_f$ is defined for an arbitrary irreducible polynomial as the automorphism group of its splitting field, the claim is false. For example, let
$$
F=\FF_p(t),
\qquad
f(X)=X^p-t.
$$
Writing $t=u^p$, one has $F=\FF_p(u^p)$. The elements $1,u,\ldots,u^{p-1}$ are linearly independent over $\FF_p(u^p)$: after clearing denominators, any relation becomes a polynomial relation in $u$ whose exponents lie in distinct congruence classes modulo $p$. Hence
$$
[\FF_p(u):\FF_p(u^p)]=p,
$$
so $f$ is irreducible of degree $p$. In characteristic $p$,
$$
X^p-t=(X-u)^p,
$$
so $u$ is its unique root. Every $F$-automorphism of the splitting field $F(u)$ therefore fixes $u$, and the automorphism group is trivial. Thus $\cardinality{G_f}=1\ne p=\deg f$.
:::
