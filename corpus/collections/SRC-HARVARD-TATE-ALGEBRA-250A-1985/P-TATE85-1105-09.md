::: {.problem}
(9) Suppose F is a field of characteristic$p > 0$. The map$x \mapsto x ^ { p } - x$is a homomorphism of the additive group of F into itself with kernel$\FF _ { p } .$Suppose$a \in F$is not in the image, i.e., suppose the polynomial$f ( X ) = X ^ { p } - \dot { X } - a$has no root in F . Show that the splitting field of$f ( X )$is cyclic of degree p over$F$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
f(X)=X^p-X-a
$$
and let $\alpha$ be a root of $f$ in an algebraic closure of $F$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The roots of $f$ are exactly
$$
\alpha+c,
\qquad
c\in\FF_p,
$$
and they are pairwise distinct.


::: pf-proof

For $c\in\FF_p$, the characteristic-$p$ binomial identity and $c^p=c$ give
$$
f(\alpha+c)
=(\alpha+c)^p-(\alpha+c)-a
=\alpha^p-\alpha-a+c^p-c
=0.
$$
Thus the $p$ elements $\alpha+c$ are roots. They are distinct because the elements of $\FF_p$ are distinct. Since $f$ has degree $p$, these are all of its roots. Equivalently, $f'(X)=-1$, so $f$ is separable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The polynomial $f$ is irreducible over $F$.


::: pf-proof

Let $g$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$, and put
$$
d=\deg g=[F(\alpha):F].
$$
Since $g$ divides the separable polynomial $f$, its roots are distinct members of the set from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Hence for some distinct $c_1,\ldots,c_d\in\FF_p$, the roots of $g$ are
$$
\alpha+c_1,\ldots,\alpha+c_d.
$$
Their sum belongs to $F$, because it is the negative of the coefficient of $X^{d-1}$ in the monic polynomial $g$. Therefore
$$
d\alpha+(c_1+\cdots+c_d)\in F.
$$
If $d<p$, then the image of the integer $d$ in the characteristic-$p$ field $F$ is nonzero and hence invertible. Since each $c_i\in\FF_p\subseteq F$, the displayed relation would imply $\alpha\in F$. That would make $a=\alpha^p-\alpha$ lie in the image of $x\mapsto x^p-x$, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus $d$ cannot be less than $p$.

Because $d\leq\deg f=p$, we obtain $d=p$. Hence $g=f$, so $f$ is irreducible.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The splitting field of $f$ is $L=F(\alpha)$ and $[L:F]=p$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, every root of $f$ has the form $\alpha+c$ with $c\in\FF_p\subseteq F$. Therefore every root already lies in $F(\alpha)$, so $F(\alpha)$ is the splitting field. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
[F(\alpha):F]=p.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every $c\in\FF_p$ there is an $F$-automorphism $\sigma_c$ of $L$ satisfying
$$
\sigma_c(\alpha)=\alpha+c,
$$
and every $F$-automorphism of $L$ is of this form.


::: pf-proof

The extension $L/F$ is the splitting field of the separable polynomial $f$, hence is Galois. Since $f$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$, an $F$-automorphism must send $\alpha$ to another root of $f$, so by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} it has the form $\alpha\mapsto\alpha+c$ for some $c\in\FF_p$.

Conversely, each root $\alpha+c$ of the irreducible polynomial $f$ determines an $F$-embedding $F(\alpha)\hookrightarrow L$ sending $\alpha$ to $\alpha+c$. Because $L/F$ is normal, this embedding is an automorphism. Thus the automorphisms are precisely the $\sigma_c$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The Galois group is cyclic of order $p$:
$$
\boxed{\Gal(L/F)\cong(\FF_p,+)\cong\ZZ/p\ZZ}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $c,d\in\FF_p$,
$$
(\sigma_c\sigma_d)(\alpha)
=\sigma_c(\alpha+d)
=\alpha+c+d,
$$
so
$$
\sigma_c\sigma_d=\sigma_{c+d}.
$$
Therefore $c\mapsto\sigma_c$ is a group isomorphism from the additive group of $\FF_p$ onto $\Gal(L/F)$. The additive group of $\FF_p$ has order $p$ and is cyclic.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} identify the splitting field as a cyclic extension of degree $p$ over $F$.
