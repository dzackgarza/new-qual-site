::: {.problem}
Let $C(X) = X^5 + X^4 - 4X^3 - 3X^2 + 3X + 1$, an irreducible polynomial over $\QQ$ with discriminant $d_C = 11^4$, and let $G_C$ be its Galois group.
Among the first 360 primes, the congruence $C(X) \equiv 0 \bmod p$ has no solution for 288 primes, exactly one solution only for $p = 11$, and five incongruent solutions exactly for the primes $p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{11}$, i.e., $p = 23, 43, 67, 89, \ldots, 2287, 2309, 2311, 2333, 2377, 2399$.

Guess what the splitting field of $G_C$ is. Try to prove your guess by guessing the element $\alpha$ in that field whose minimal polynomial is $C(X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\zeta=\zeta_{11}=e^{2\pi i/11}$, let
$$
\alpha=\zeta+\zeta^{-1},
\qquad
K=\QQ(\alpha).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The element $\alpha$ is a root of $C(X)$.


::: pf-proof

For $n\geq 0$, put $s_n=\zeta^n+\zeta^{-n}$. Then $s_0=2$, $s_1=\alpha$, and
$$
s_{n+1}=\alpha s_n-s_{n-1}.
$$
Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_2&=\alpha^2-2,\\
s_3&=\alpha^3-3\alpha,\\
s_4&=\alpha^4-4\alpha^2+2,\\
s_5&=\alpha^5-5\alpha^3+5\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $\zeta$ is a primitive eleventh root of unity,
$$
0=\frac{1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{10}}{\zeta^5}
  =1+s_1+s_2+s_3+s_4+s_5.
$$
Substituting the displayed expressions for the $s_n$ gives
$$
\alpha^5+\alpha^4-4\alpha^3-3\alpha^2+3\alpha+1=0,
$$
which is $C(\alpha)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The polynomial $C(X)$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$, and $[K:\QQ]=5$.


::: pf-proof

The element $\zeta$ satisfies
$$
T^2-\alpha T+1=0
$$
over $K$, so
$$
[\QQ(\zeta):K]\leq 2.
$$
Because $[\QQ(\zeta):\QQ]=\varphi(11)=10$, the tower law gives $[K:\QQ]\geq5$. On the other hand, step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} exhibits $\alpha$ as a root of the degree-$5$ polynomial $C$, so $[K:\QQ]\leq5$. Hence $[K:\QQ]=5$, and the degree-$5$ polynomial $C$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The extension $K/\QQ$ is Galois and contains all five roots of $C$.


::: pf-proof

The cyclotomic extension $\QQ(\zeta)/\QQ$ is Galois with
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta)/\QQ)
\cong(\ZZ/11\ZZ)^\times.
$$
Complex conjugation corresponds to $-1$, and it fixes $\alpha$. Its fixed field therefore contains $K$. The fixed field of the order-$2$ subgroup $\{\pm1\}$ has degree $10/2=5$ over $\QQ$, so step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that this fixed field is exactly $K$. Since the cyclotomic Galois group is abelian, $\{\pm1\}$ is normal, and hence $K/\QQ$ is Galois.

For $a\in(\ZZ/11\ZZ)^\times$, the automorphism $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^a$ sends $\alpha$ to
$$
\zeta^a+\zeta^{-a}.
$$
The values for $a$ modulo the identification $a\sim-a$ give five conjugates, all lying in $K$. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $C$ is the degree-$5$ minimal polynomial of $\alpha$, so these are exactly its five roots.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The splitting field of $C(X)$ over $\QQ$ is
$$
\boxed{\QQ(\zeta_{11}+\zeta_{11}^{-1})}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $C$ splits in $K=\QQ(\zeta_{11}+\zeta_{11}^{-1})$. Conversely, any splitting field contains the root $\alpha$, hence contains $K=\QQ(\alpha)$. Thus $K$ is precisely the splitting field. In particular,
$$
G_C\cong (\ZZ/11\ZZ)^\times/\{\pm1\}\cong C_5,
$$
which agrees with the prime-splitting pattern stated in the problem.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} identifies and proves the claimed splitting field.

:::
