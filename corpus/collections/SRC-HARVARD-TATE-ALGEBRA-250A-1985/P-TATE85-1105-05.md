::: {.problem}
Find three integers $a, b, c$ such that $\QQ(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{4}}) = \QQ(\sqrt{a}, \sqrt{b}, \sqrt{c})$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The field on the left is $\QQ(i)$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
e^{2\pi i/4}=e^{\pi i/2}=i,
$$
we have
$$
\QQ\left(e^{2\pi i/4}\right)=\QQ(i).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

One valid choice is
$$
\boxed{a=b=c=-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For this choice, each of $\sqrt a,\sqrt b,\sqrt c$ is a square root of $-1$, hence equals either $i$ or $-i$. Therefore
$$
\QQ(\sqrt a,\sqrt b,\sqrt c)=\QQ(i).
$$
Together with step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} this gives the required equality of fields.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} supplies three integers satisfying the required equality.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark}
The source prints the exponent as $\frac{2\pi i}{4}$, so the left side is $\QQ(i)$ and the problem is trivially solved by $a = b = c = -1$.
The phrase "three integers" suggests a field of degree $8$ such as $\QQ(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{24}}) = \QQ(\sqrt{-1}, \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$.
:::
