::: {.problem}
(2) Consider the cubic polynomials
$$
f_1(X)=X^3+X^2+7X-8,
\qquad
f_2(X)=X^3-8X+15,
$$
$$
f_3(X)=X^3+X^2-7X+12,
\qquad
f_4(X)=X^3+10X+1.
$$
They satisfy
$$
f_1(X)\equiv (X-6)(X+5)(X+2)\pmod{13},
$$
and $f_1$ is irreducible modulo $17$, $19$, and $29$;
$$
f_2(X)\equiv (X+4)(X+6)(X+7)\pmod{17},
$$
and $f_2$ is irreducible modulo $13$, $29$, and $29$;
$$
f_3(X)\equiv (X-8)(X+8)(X+1)\pmod{19},
$$
and $f_3$ is irreducible modulo $13$, $17$, and $29$; and
$$
f_4(X)\equiv (X-2)(X-3)(X-5)\pmod{29},
$$
and $f_4$ is irreducible modulo $13$, $17$, and $19$.

Each of the four polynomials has discriminant $-4027$, a prime.
Nevertheless, the fields $\QQ(\alpha_i)$, where $\alpha_i$ is a root of $f_i(X)$, are pairwise non-isomorphic.
Why?
:::

::: {.solution}

For $1\le i\le4$, put
$$
K_i=\QQ(\alpha_i),
$$
where $f_i(\alpha_i)=0$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For every $i$,
$$
\mathcal O_{K_i}=\ZZ[\alpha_i]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{disc}(K_i)=-4027.
$$


::: pf-proof

Each $f_i$ is irreducible over $\QQ$, because the problem gives a prime modulo which $f_i$ is irreducible. Hence $K_i/\QQ$ has degree $3$.

Each $f_i$ is monic, so $\alpha_i$ is an algebraic integer. The discriminant of the power basis
$$
1,\alpha_i,\alpha_i^2
$$
is $\operatorname{disc}(f_i)=-4027$. For every order $\ZZ[\alpha_i]\subseteq\mathcal O_{K_i}$,
$$
\operatorname{disc}(\ZZ[\alpha_i])
=
[\mathcal O_{K_i}:\ZZ[\alpha_i]]^2
\operatorname{disc}(K_i).
$$
Since $4027$ is prime, the square
$$
[\mathcal O_{K_i}:\ZZ[\alpha_i]]^2
$$
dividing $4027$ must be $1$. Hence the index is $1$, giving both assertions.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every prime $p\ne4027$, the factorization of $f_i$ modulo $p$ is the splitting type of $p$ in $K_i$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the order $\ZZ[\alpha_i]$ is already the full ring of integers, so its index in $\mathcal O_{K_i}$ is $1$. Dedekind's factorization theorem therefore applies without an index exception: if
$$
\overline{f_i}
=
\prod_j \varphi_j
$$
is the factorization into distinct monic irreducibles over $\FF_p$, then
$$
p\mathcal O_{K_i}
=
\prod_j\mathfrak p_j
$$
with residue degrees $\deg\varphi_j$.

All primes used in the problem are different from $4027$, hence are unramified. Thus a factorization into three distinct linear factors means that $p$ splits completely, whereas irreducibility of the cubic means that $p$ is inert.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The four fields have different splitting behavior:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{field} & \text{prime splitting completely} & \text{comparison fields in which it is inert}\\
\hline
K_1 & 13 & K_2,K_3,K_4\\
K_2 & 17 & K_1,K_3,K_4\\
K_3 & 19 & K_1,K_4\\
K_4 & 29 & K_1,K_2,K_3
\end{array}
$$


::: pf-proof

The displayed congruence for $f_1$ has three distinct linear factors modulo $13$, while $f_2,f_3,f_4$ are irreducible modulo $13$. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $13$ splits completely in $K_1$ and is inert in $K_2,K_3,K_4$.

Likewise, the displayed congruence for $f_2$ shows that $17$ splits completely in $K_2$, whereas $f_1,f_3,f_4$ are irreducible modulo $17$.

The displayed congruence for $f_3$ shows that $19$ splits completely in $K_3$, while the stated irreducibility of $f_1$ and $f_4$ modulo $19$ is enough to distinguish $K_3$ from $K_1$ and $K_4$. The remaining pair $K_2,K_3$ was already distinguished at $17$.

Finally, the displayed congruence for $f_4$ shows that $29$ splits completely in $K_4$, while $f_1$ and $f_3$ are irreducible modulo $29$; the pairs involving $K_2$ were already distinguished at $13$ or $17$. Thus every pair of fields differs in the splitting type of at least one rational prime.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The fields $K_1,K_2,K_3,K_4$ are pairwise non-isomorphic over $\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

A $\QQ$-isomorphism
$$
\psi:K_i\longrightarrow K_j
$$
sends elements integral over $\ZZ$ to elements integral over $\ZZ$, so it restricts to an isomorphism
$$
\mathcal O_{K_i}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O_{K_j}.
$$
For every rational prime $p$, it therefore carries the ideal $p\mathcal O_{K_i}$ to $p\mathcal O_{K_j}$ and preserves the residue degrees and ramification indices of its prime factors.

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} exhibits, for every pair $i\ne j$, a rational prime whose splitting type in $K_i$ differs from its splitting type in $K_j$. Hence no such $\QQ$-isomorphism can exist. Therefore
$$
\boxed{K_1,K_2,K_3,K_4\text{ are pairwise non-isomorphic}.}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves the assertion.
