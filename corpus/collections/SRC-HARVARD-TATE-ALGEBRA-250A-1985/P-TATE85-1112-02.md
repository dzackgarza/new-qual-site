::: {.problem}
(2) Suppose A is integrally closed in its fraction field F . Prove that the same is true for A[X] (polynomial ring). (Suggestion :$F [ X ]$is integrally closed, being a PID).
:::

::: {.solution}

Write \(F=\operatorname{Frac}(A)\).

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If
\[
q(X)=c_0+c_1X+\cdots+c_dX^d\in F[X]
\]
is integral over \(A[X]\), then every coefficient \(c_i\) is integral over \(A\).


::: pf-proof

Recall that an element \(y\) of the fraction field of a domain \(R\) is *almost integral* over \(R\) if there is a nonzero \(a\in R\) such that
\[
ay^n\in R
\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Three elementary facts will be used.

First, every integral element \(y\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)\) is almost integral. Indeed, if \(y\) satisfies a monic equation of degree \(n\), then \(R[y]\) is generated as an \(R\)-module by
\[
1,y,\ldots,y^{n-1}.
\]
Clearing the finitely many denominators of these generators gives a nonzero \(a\in R\) with
\[
aR[y]\subset R,
\]
and hence \(ay^m\in R\) for every \(m\ge0\).

Second, the almost integral elements form a subring of \(\operatorname{Frac}(R)\). If \(ax^n\in R\) and \(by^n\in R\) for all \(n\), then
\[
ab(xy)^n=(ax^n)(by^n)\in R,
\]
while
\[
ab(x+y)^n
=
\sum_{k=0}^n
\binom nk
(ax^k)(by^{n-k})
\in R.
\]
Closure under additive inverses is immediate.

Third, if \(R\) is Noetherian, then every almost integral element \(y\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)\) is integral. Choose \(0\ne a\in R\) with \(ay^n\in R\) for all \(n\), and consider the ascending chain of ideals
\[
(a)\subset(a,ay)\subset(a,ay,ay^2)\subset\cdots.
\]
It stabilizes, so for some \(N\),
\[
ay^{N+1}
\in
(a,ay,\ldots,ay^N).
\]
Thus
\[
ay^{N+1}
=
r_0a+r_1ay+\cdots+r_Nay^N
\]
with \(r_i\in R\). Dividing by the nonzero element \(a\) in the fraction field gives a monic equation for \(y\) over \(R\).

Now suppose more generally that
\[
q(X)=c_0+\cdots+c_dX^d\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)[X]
\]
is almost integral over \(R[X]\). Choose a nonzero polynomial
\[
h(X)=b_0+\cdots+b_sX^s\in R[X],
\qquad b_s\ne0,
\]
such that
\[
h(X)q(X)^n\in R[X]
\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
The leading coefficient of this product is
\[
b_sc_d^n,
\]
so \(c_d\) is almost integral over \(R\). Consequently \(c_dX^d\) is almost integral over \(R[X]\). Since almost integral elements form a subring,
\[
q-c_dX^d
\]
is almost integral over \(R[X]\). Induction on \(d\) therefore shows that every coefficient \(c_i\) is almost integral over \(R\).

Return now to the original domain \(A\), where \(q\) is assumed integral over \(A[X]\). Write each
\[
c_i=\frac{a_i}{b_i}
\qquad(a_i,b_i\in A,\ b_i\ne0),
\]
and choose a monic integral equation
\[
q^n+f_{n-1}(X)q^{n-1}+\cdots+f_0(X)=0,
\qquad
f_j(X)\in A[X].
\]
Let \(A_0\subset A\) be the subring generated by the finitely many elements \(a_i,b_i\) and all coefficients of the \(f_j\). Then \(A_0\) is a finitely generated \(\ZZ\)-algebra, hence Noetherian, and
\[
q\in\operatorname{Frac}(A_0)[X]
\]
is integral over \(A_0[X]\).

By the first fact, \(q\) is almost integral over \(A_0[X]\). The coefficient argument above shows that every \(c_i\) is almost integral over \(A_0\). Since \(A_0\) is Noetherian, the third fact shows that every \(c_i\) is integral over \(A_0\), hence integral over \(A\). This proves the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The polynomial ring \(A[X]\) is integrally closed in its fraction field.


::: pf-proof

Let
\[
z\in\operatorname{Frac}(A[X])=F(X)
\]
be integral over \(A[X]\). The same monic equation also has coefficients in \(F[X]\), so \(z\) is integral over \(F[X]\). Since \(F[X]\) is a PID, it is integrally closed in its fraction field \(F(X)\). Therefore
\[
z\in F[X].
\]

Write
\[
z=c_0+c_1X+\cdots+c_dX^d,
\qquad c_i\in F.
\]
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, every coefficient \(c_i\) is integral over \(A\). Since \(A\) is integrally closed in \(F\),
\[
c_i\in A
\qquad(0\le i\le d).
\]
Hence \(z\in A[X]\). Thus every element of \(F(X)\) integral over \(A[X]\) already lies in \(A[X]\), which is exactly that \(A[X]\) is integrally closed.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves the assertion.
