::: {.problem}
(3) Let $F = \QQ(i)$ and $K = F(2^{\frac{1}{4}}, i^{\frac{1}{4}})$, where $2^{\frac{1}{4}}$ is the positive fourth root of $2$ and $i^{\frac{1}{4}} = e^{\frac{2\pi i}{16}}$. Determine $\Gal(K/F)$. Is $K/\QQ$ Galois, and if so, what is its Galois group?
:::

::: {.solution}

Put
$$
a=2^{1/4},
\qquad
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/16}=\zeta_{16}.
$$
Then $F=\QQ(i)$, $K=F(a,\zeta)$, and
$$
a^2\zeta^2=1+i.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Both $F(a)/F$ and $F(\zeta)/F$ are cyclic extensions of degree $4$.


::: pf-proof

The field $\QQ(a)$ has degree $4$ over $\QQ$ because $X^4-2$ is Eisenstein at $2$. Since $\QQ(a)\subset\RR$ and $\QQ(i)\cap\RR=\QQ$, one has
$$
\QQ(a)\cap F=\QQ.
$$
Hence
$$
[F(a):F]=[\QQ(a):\QQ]=4.
$$
All four roots $a,ia,-a,-ia$ of $X^4-2$ lie in $F(a)$, and the map $a\mapsto ia$ generates $\Gal(F(a)/F)$, so this Galois group is cyclic of order $4$.

Also $F(\zeta)=\QQ(\zeta_{16})$. Since $[\QQ(\zeta_{16}):\QQ]=\varphi(16)=8$ and $F=\QQ(\zeta_{16}^4)$ has degree $2$ over $\QQ$,
$$
[F(\zeta):F]=4.
$$
The automorphisms fixing $i=\zeta^4$ are $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^u$ with $u\equiv1\pmod4$, namely $u=1,5,9,13$. They form the cyclic subgroup generated by $5$ modulo $16$, so $\Gal(F(\zeta)/F)\cong C_4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The intersection is
$$
F(a)\cap F(\zeta)=F(\sqrt2),
$$
and consequently $[K:F]=8$.


::: pf-proof

The unique quadratic subfield of the cyclic quartic extension $F(a)/F$ is $F(a^2)=F(\sqrt2)$. On the other hand,
$$
\zeta^2=e^{\pi i/4}=\frac{1+i}{\sqrt2},
$$
so $F(\zeta^2)=F(\sqrt2)$; this is likewise the unique quadratic subfield of $F(\zeta)/F$. Hence
$$
F(\sqrt2)\subseteq F(a)\cap F(\zeta).
$$

If the intersection were larger, then, because each extension in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} is cyclic of degree $4$, the two quartic fields over $F$ would be equal. This is impossible: $F(\zeta)=\QQ(\zeta_{16})$ is abelian over $\QQ$, whereas $F(a)=\QQ(i,a)$ is the splitting field of $X^4-2$ and is nonabelian over $\QQ$. Indeed, if $s(a)=ia$, $s(i)=i$, and $c$ is complex conjugation, then $csc=s^{-1}\neq s$.

Thus the intersection is exactly $F(\sqrt2)$. The compositum degree formula gives
$$
[K:F]
=\frac{[F(a):F][F(\zeta):F]}{[F(\sqrt2):F]}
=\frac{4\cdot4}{2}
=8.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The first requested Galois group is
$$
\boxed{\Gal(K/F)\cong C_4\times C_2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Every $F$-automorphism of $K$ has the form
$$
a\longmapsto i^r a,
\qquad
\zeta\longmapsto i^s\zeta,
\qquad
r,s\in\ZZ/4\ZZ.
$$
Since $a^2\zeta^2=1+i\in F$, such a pair must satisfy
$$
(-1)^{r+s}=1,
$$
equivalently $r\equiv s\pmod2$. Conversely, step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that this is exactly the compatibility condition on the common quadratic subfield, so every such pair gives an $F$-automorphism. Therefore
$$
\Gal(K/F)
\cong
\{(r,s)\in(\ZZ/4\ZZ)^2:r\equiv s\pmod2\}.
$$
The elements
$$
\sigma=(1,1),
\qquad
\tau=(0,2)
$$
have orders $4$ and $2$, commute, and generate all eight pairs. Hence the group is $C_4\times C_2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The extension $K/\QQ$ is Galois of degree $16$.


::: pf-proof

The field $\QQ(i,a)=F(a)$ is the splitting field of $X^4-2$ over $\QQ$, while $\QQ(\zeta)$ is the splitting field of $X^8+1=\Phi_{16}(X)$. Hence their compositum
$$
K=\QQ(a,\zeta)
$$
is the splitting field of
$$
(X^4-2)(X^8+1)
$$
over $\QQ$. Therefore $K/\QQ$ is Galois. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
[K:\QQ]=[K:F][F:\QQ]=8\cdot2=16.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If $c$ denotes complex conjugation, then
$$
\boxed{
\Gal(K/\QQ)
\cong
(C_4\times C_2)\rtimes_{\varphi}C_2,
}
$$
where, for the generators $\sigma,\tau$ of step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\varphi(\sigma)=\sigma^{-1}\tau,
\qquad
\varphi(\tau)=\tau.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\Gal(K/\QQ)
\cong
\left\langle
\sigma,\tau,c
\ \presentationbar\
\sigma^4=\tau^2=c^2=1,
[\sigma,\tau]=[\tau,c]=1,
c\sigma c=\sigma^{-1}\tau
\right\rangle.
$$


::: pf-proof

Restriction to $F$ gives an exact sequence
$$
1\longrightarrow\Gal(K/F)
\longrightarrow\Gal(K/\QQ)
\longrightarrow\Gal(F/\QQ)
\longrightarrow1.
$$
Complex conjugation $c$ lies in $\Gal(K/\QQ)$ and maps to the nontrivial element of $\Gal(F/\QQ)$, so the sequence splits.

The generators from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} act by
$$
\sigma(a)=ia,
\qquad
\sigma(\zeta)=i\zeta,
$$
and
$$
\tau(a)=a,
\qquad
\tau(\zeta)=-\zeta.
$$
Since $c(a)=a$, $c(i)=-i$, and $c(\zeta)=\zeta^{-1}$, direct calculation gives
$$
c\sigma c=\sigma^{-1}\tau,
\qquad
c\tau c=\tau.
$$
Thus conjugation by $c$ acts on $C_4\times C_2$ by the stated involution $\varphi$, which proves the semidirect-product description.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} determines $\Gal(K/F)$, and steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} show that $K/\QQ$ is Galois and determine its Galois group.
