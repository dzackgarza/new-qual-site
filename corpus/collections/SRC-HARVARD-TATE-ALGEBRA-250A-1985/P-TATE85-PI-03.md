::: {.problem}
(3) Let $B$ be an integral domain with field of fractions $K$. Let $A = B[X]$ and let $P$ be a prime ideal of $A$; then $P \cap B$ is a prime ideal in $B$.

(a) If $P \cap B = (0)$, show:

(i) $PK = P(K[X])$ is a prime ideal in $AK = K[X]$.

(ii) $P = PK \cap A$.

(iii) If $B$ is a u.f.d., then either $P = (0)$, or $P = f(X) A$, where $f(X)$ is a polynomial with coefficients in $B$, these coefficients having “no” common divisor (i.e., none except units in $B$), and $f(X)$ being irreducible in $K[X]$. Moreover $f$ is determined by $P$ up to a unit (invertible element) of $B$.

(b) If $P \cap B = M$, a maximal ideal of $B$, then, making the identification $A/MA = B[X]/MB[X] \approx (B/M)[X] = k[X]$, where $k = B/M$, we see that $P/MA$ is a prime ideal in $k[X]$. Hence show: either $P = MA$, or $P = MA + g(X) A$, where $g(X)$ is a polynomial with coefficients in $B$ such that the polynomial $\overline{g}(X)$ which we obtain by reducing the coefficients of $g \pmod{M}$ is an irreducible polynomial in $k[X]$. Moreover $g$ is determined by $P$ up to multiplication by an element of $B$ not in $M$ and addition of a polynomial whose coefficients are in $M$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $S=B\setminus\{0\}$. Then $S^{-1}A=K[X]$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The contraction $P\cap B$ is prime in $B$.


::: pf-proof

If $ab\in P\cap B$ with $a,b\in B$, then $ab\in P$. Since $P$ is prime in $A$, either $a\in P$ or $b\in P$, hence either $a\in P\cap B$ or $b\in P\cap B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $P\cap B=(0)$, then $PK=S^{-1}P$ is prime in $K[X]$.


::: pf-proof

The hypothesis says $P\cap S=\varnothing$. Localization of a prime ideal disjoint from the multiplicative set is prime, and $S^{-1}A=K[X]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $P\cap B=(0)$, then $\boxed{P=PK\cap A}$.


::: pf-proof

Certainly $P\subseteq PK\cap A$. Conversely, if $h\in PK\cap A$, then for some $s\in S$ one has $sh\in P$. Since $s\notin P$ and $P$ is prime, $h\in P$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Suppose $B$ is a UFD, $P\cap B=(0)$, and $P\ne(0)$. Then $\boxed{P=fA}$ for a primitive $f\in B[X]$ irreducible in $K[X]$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $PK$ is a nonzero prime ideal of the PID $K[X]$, so $PK=hK[X]$ for some irreducible $h\in K[X]$. Clearing denominators and dividing by the gcd of the coefficients gives a primitive $f\in B[X]$ with $hK[X]=fK[X]$. By Gauss's lemma, $f$ is irreducible in $K[X]$.

For primitive $f$, Gauss's lemma also gives
$$
fK[X]\cap B[X]=fB[X].
$$
Indeed, if $g=fq\in B[X]$ with $q\in K[X]$, clear denominators in $q$. Comparing contents in the resulting identity shows that every denominator prime cancels, hence $q\in B[X]$.

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} now yields $P=PK\cap A=fA$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The primitive generator $f$ in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is determined by $P$ up to a unit of $B$.


::: pf-proof

If primitive $f,f'\in B[X]$ both generate $P$, then over $K[X]$ they differ by a scalar:
$$
f'=cf,\qquad c\in K^\times.
$$
Write $c=r/s$ in lowest terms in the UFD $B$. From $sf'=rf$ and primitivity of both polynomials, no nonunit prime can divide either $r$ or $s$. Thus $r$ and $s$ are units, so $c\in B^\times$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Suppose $P\cap B=M$ is maximal. Then $MA\subseteq P$, and $P/MA$ is a prime ideal of
$$
A/MA\cong(B/M)[X]=k[X].
$$


::: pf-proof

Because every element of $M=P\cap B$ lies in $P$, one has $MA\subseteq P$. The correspondence theorem identifies $P/MA$ with a prime ideal of $A/MA$, and reduction of coefficients gives the displayed isomorphism. Since $M$ is maximal, $k=B/M$ is a field.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

In step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, either $\boxed{P=MA}$ or $\boxed{P=MA+gA}$, where $g\in B[X]$ and $\bar g\in k[X]$ is irreducible.


::: pf-proof

The PID $k[X]$ has prime ideals $(0)$ and ideals generated by irreducible polynomials. If $P/MA=(0)$, then $P=MA$. Otherwise write $P/MA=(\bar g)$ with $\bar g$ irreducible, and lift $\bar g$ to $g\in B[X]$. The inverse image of $(\bar g)$ in $A$ is exactly $MA+gA$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

In the second case of step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, $g$ is determined by $P$ up to multiplication by an element of $B\setminus M$ and addition of an element of $M[X]$.


::: pf-proof

If $g'$ is another such lift, then $(\bar g')=(\bar g)$ in $k[X]$. Hence $\bar g'=\bar u\,\bar g$ for some $\bar u\in k^\times$. Choose a lift $u\in B$ of $\bar u$. Then $u\notin M$ and $g'-ug\in M[X]$.

Conversely, replacing $g$ by $ug+m$ with $u\notin M$ and $m\in M[X]$ multiplies $\bar g$ by a nonzero scalar in $k$ and therefore leaves $(\bar g)$, and hence its inverse image $P$, unchanged.

:::

:::

:::
