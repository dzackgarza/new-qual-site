::: {.problem}
(7) Let $\alpha = (2 + \sqrt{2})(3 + \sqrt{3}) = -\sqrt{6}(1 + \sqrt{2})(1 + \sqrt{3})$ and let $\theta = \sqrt{-\alpha} = i\sqrt{\alpha}$. Show $\QQ(\theta)/\QQ$ is Galois of degree $8$. Determine the structure of $G = \Gal(\QQ(\theta)/\QQ)$, and explain why $\QQ(\theta)$ is not the splitting field of any polynomial of degree $< 8$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The source has a sign error in its second expression for $\alpha$.
Indeed,
$$
2+\sqrt2=\sqrt2(1+\sqrt2),
\qquad
3+\sqrt3=\sqrt3(1+\sqrt3),
$$
so
$$
(2+\sqrt2)(3+\sqrt3)=+\sqrt6(1+\sqrt2)(1+\sqrt3).
$$
The solution uses the first displayed definition $\alpha=(2+\sqrt2)(3+\sqrt3)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Put
$$
F=\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3),
\qquad
r=\sqrt2,
\qquad
s=\sqrt3,
\qquad
K=\QQ(\theta).
$$
Thus
$$
\alpha=(2+r)(3+s),
\qquad
\theta^2=-\alpha.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

$F=\QQ(\alpha)$ and $[F:\QQ]=4$.


::: pf-proof

The fields $\QQ(r)$ and $\QQ(s)$ are distinct quadratic fields, hence their intersection is $\QQ$ and $[F:\QQ]=4$. The four $\QQ$-conjugates of $\alpha$ in $F$ are
$$
\alpha_{\varepsilon,\delta}=(2+\varepsilon r)(3+\delta s),
\qquad
\varepsilon,\delta\in\{\pm1\}.
$$
All four are positive. The choice $(+,+)$ is strictly largest and $(-,-)$ is strictly smallest. For the remaining two,
$$
(2+r)(3-s)-(2-r)(3+s)
=6r-4s
=2(3r-2s)>0,
$$
because $(3r)^2=18>12=(2s)^2$. Hence the four conjugates are distinct, so the orbit of $\alpha$ has size $4$. Therefore $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4$, and since $\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq F$, equality follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

$[K:F]=2$, hence $[K:\QQ]=8$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $F=\QQ(\alpha)=\QQ(\theta^2)$, so $F\subseteq K$. The field $F$ is totally real, while under its identity embedding
$$
\alpha=(2+\sqrt2)(3+\sqrt3)>0.
$$
Thus $-\alpha<0$ under that real embedding. A square of an element of $F$ is nonnegative under every real embedding, so $-\alpha$ is not a square in $F$. Consequently $X^2+\alpha$ is irreducible over $F$, and
$$
[K:F]=2.
$$
Together with step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} this gives $[K:\QQ]=8$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The two sign changes of $F/\QQ$ extend to automorphisms $x,y\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\QQ}(K)$ given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
x(r)&=-r,&x(s)&=s,&x(\theta)&=(r-1)\theta,\\
y(r)&=r,&y(s)&=-s,&y(\theta)&=\frac{s-1}{r}\theta.
\end{aligned}
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\sigma_r$ be the automorphism of $F$ sending $r\mapsto-r$ and fixing $s$. Then
$$
\frac{\sigma_r(\alpha)}{\alpha}
=\frac{2-r}{2+r}
=(r-1)^2.
$$
Hence
$$
((r-1)\theta)^2
=-(r-1)^2\alpha
=-\sigma_r(\alpha)
=\sigma_r(\theta^2),
$$
so $\sigma_r$ extends to the stated automorphism $x$ of $K$.

Similarly, if $\sigma_s$ fixes $r$ and sends $s\mapsto-s$, then
$$
\frac{\sigma_s(\alpha)}{\alpha}
=\frac{3-s}{3+s}
=2-s
=\left(\frac{s-1}{r}\right)^2.
$$
Thus
$$
\left(\frac{s-1}{r}\theta\right)^2
=-\sigma_s(\alpha),
$$
so $\sigma_s$ extends to the stated automorphism $y$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Let $z$ be the nontrivial element of $\Gal(K/F)$, so $z(\theta)=-\theta$. Then
$$
x^2=y^2=z,
\qquad
xy=z\,yx.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both $x^2$ and $y^2$ fix $F$. Moreover,
$$
x^2(\theta)
=x(r-1)(r-1)\theta
=(-r-1)(r-1)\theta
=-\theta,
$$
so $x^2=z$. Likewise,
$$
y^2(\theta)
=y\left(\frac{s-1}{r}\right)\frac{s-1}{r}\theta
=\frac{-s-1}{r}\frac{s-1}{r}\theta
=-\theta,
$$
so $y^2=z$.

Finally,
$$
x\left(\frac{s-1}{r}\right)=-\frac{s-1}{r},
\qquad
y(r-1)=r-1.
$$
Therefore
$$
(xy)(\theta)=-(r-1)\frac{s-1}{r}\theta,
\qquad
(yx)(\theta)=(r-1)\frac{s-1}{r}\theta.
$$
The two compositions agree on $F$, so $xy=z\,yx$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

$K/\QQ$ is Galois and
$$
\boxed{\Gal(K/\QQ)\cong Q_8}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The images of $x$ and $y$ in $\Gal(F/\QQ)$ are the two independent sign changes, so they generate $\Gal(F/\QQ)\cong(\ZZ/2)^2$. Their squares are the nontrivial element $z$ of the kernel $\Gal(K/F)$. Hence the subgroup generated by $x$ and $y$ contains the eight distinct elements
$$
1,\ z,\ x,\ zx,\ y,\ zy,\ xy,\ zxy.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $[K:\QQ]=8$, so these are all $\QQ$-automorphisms of $K$. Thus $K/\QQ$ is Galois.

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
z^2=1,
\qquad
x^2=y^2=z,
\qquad
xy=z\,yx.
$$
In particular,
$$
(xy)^2=x(yx)y=x(zxy)y=z x^2y^2=z.
$$
Thus $x$, $y$, and $xy$ all have order $4$ with common square $z$; these are the quaternion relations. Identifying $x,y,z$ with $i,j,-1$ gives the group $Q_8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Every faithful permutation action of $Q_8$ has at least $8$ points.


::: pf-proof

Let $z$ denote the unique element of order $2$ in $Q_8$. Every nontrivial subgroup of $Q_8$ contains $z$: the subgroup of order $2$ is $\{1,z\}$, and every subgroup of order $4$ is cyclic and contains its unique element of order $2$, namely $z$.

Suppose $Q_8$ acts on a set with fewer than $8$ points. No orbit can have size $8$, so every point stabilizer is nontrivial. Hence every point stabilizer contains $z$, which means that $z$ fixes every point. The action is therefore not faithful.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

$K$ is not the splitting field over $\QQ$ of any polynomial of degree less than $8$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose a polynomial $f\in\QQ[X]$ of degree $n<8$ had splitting field $K$. The Galois group $\Gal(K/\QQ)$ acts on the set of distinct roots of $f$. This action is faithful: an automorphism fixing every root fixes the field generated by all the roots, namely $K$, and is therefore the identity. The root set has at most $n<8$ elements, contradicting step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} and the identification $\Gal(K/\QQ)\cong Q_8$ from step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} prove that $K/\QQ$ is Galois of degree $8$ with quaternion Galois group, and step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} proves the final assertion.
