::: {.problem}
Use a cellular decomposition and intersection theory
to compute the integral cohomology ring of $S^2\times S^4$.
Show that this space is not homotopy equivalent to $\CP^3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The product CW decomposition has one cell in each of
dimensions $0,2,4,6$ and no odd-dimensional cells.
All cellular differentials therefore vanish. Write
$a$ and $b$ for the pullbacks of the orientation
classes of $S^2$ and $S^4$, respectively. They
generate $H^2$ and $H^4$, while $ab$ generates $H^6$.
The Poincaré duals of $a$ and $b$ are the submanifolds
$\{x\}\times S^4$ and $S^2\times\{y\}$.
They intersect once with positive sign. Two copies
of $\{x\}\times S^4$ can be chosen disjoint, so
$a^2=0$. Thus
$$
H^*(S^2\times S^4;\ZZ)
\cong\ZZ[a,b]/(a^2,b^2),
\qquad \deg a=2,\quad \deg b=4.
$$

In $H^*(\CP^3;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ[x]/(x^4)$, the square
of a generator $x\in H^2$ is nonzero. A graded
ring isomorphism would send $a$ to $\pm x$, which
cannot preserve $a^2=0$. Hence the spaces are not
homotopy equivalent.
:::
