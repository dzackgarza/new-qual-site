::: {.problem}
Use a cellular decomposition and intersection theory
to compute the integral cohomology ring of $S^2\times S^4$.
Show that this space is not homotopy equivalent to $\CP^3$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $S^2\times S^4$ with the product CW structure, and denote by $a\in H^2$
and $b\in H^4$ the pullbacks of the orientation classes of the two factors.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cells}

The product cell structure has one cell in each of the dimensions $0,2,4,6$
and none in odd dimensions, so all cellular differentials vanish and
$H^k(S^2\times S^4;\ZZ)$ is $\ZZ$ for $k=0,2,4,6$ and $0$ otherwise, generated
by $1,a,b,ab$.

::: pf-proof

$S^2$ has cells in dimensions $0,2$ and $S^4$ in dimensions $0,4$, and a
product cell $e^i\times e^j$ has dimension $i+j$, so the cells are those four.
A cellular differential lowers dimension by one, and there are no cells in an
odd dimension for it to hit, so every differential is zero and the cellular
chain complex is a sum of copies of $\ZZ$ with zero maps; the Künneth formula
gives the same answer over a field or over $\ZZ$ here, since all homology of
both factors is free, and it identifies the product classes as generators of
the products.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #duals}

The Poincaré dual of $a$ is the submanifold $\{x\}\times S^4$ and the Poincaré
dual of $b$ is $S^2\times\{y\}$; they meet transversely in the single point
$(x,y)$, positively, and two copies of $\{x\}\times S^4$ can be chosen
disjoint.

::: pf-proof

A point $\{x\}\times S^4$ is a closed oriented $4$-manifold in $S^2\times S^4$
whose class is a generator of $H_4(S^2\times S^4;\ZZ)$, and it pairs to $1$
with $a$ and to $0$ with $b$ by the Künneth description of the pairing, so it
is the dual of $a$; likewise for $S^2\times\{y\}$ and $b$. The two meet
exactly in $(x,y)$, and the orientation of the product makes the intersection
number $+1$. For $x\ne x'$ the submanifolds $\{x\}\times S^4$ and
$\{x'\}\times S^4$ are disjoint, and disjoint submanifolds have intersection
number $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ring}

$H^*(S^2\times S^4;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ[a,b]/(a^2,b^2)$ with $\deg a=2$ and $\deg
b=4$.

::: pf-proof

Intersection theory computes the cup product: for classes $\alpha,\beta$ of
complementary degrees, $\langle\alpha\smile\beta,[S^2\times S^4]\rangle$
equals the intersection number of transverse representatives of their Poincaré
duals. By step [](#duals){.pf-ref} two disjoint representatives of the dual of
$a$ show $\langle a\smile a,[S^2\times S^4]\rangle=0$, and the pairing is
nondegenerate, so $a^2=0$; besides, $a$ is a pullback from $S^2$, where $a^2$
would land in $H^4(S^2;\ZZ)=0$. For degree reasons $b^2\in H^8=0$ and $ab=ba$
generates $H^6$ because it pairs to $1$ with the point $(x,y)$, the duals of
$a$ and of $b$ meeting once. So the ring is the free abelian group on
$1,a,b,ab$ with those products.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #distinct}

$S^2\times S^4$ is not homotopy equivalent to $\CP^3$.

::: pf-proof

$H^*(\CP^3;\ZZ)=\ZZ[x]/(x^4)$ with $\deg x=2$, so $x^2\ne0$. A homotopy
equivalence would induce an isomorphism of graded rings, and a graded ring
isomorphism must send a generator of $H^2$ to a generator of $H^2$:
$H^2(S^2\times S^4;\ZZ)=\ZZ a$ and $H^2(\CP^3;\ZZ)=\ZZ x$, so $a\mapsto\pm x$.
Then $a^2=0$ would force $(\pm x)^2=x^2=0$, contradicting $x^2\ne0$. Hence no
homotopy equivalence exists.

:::
