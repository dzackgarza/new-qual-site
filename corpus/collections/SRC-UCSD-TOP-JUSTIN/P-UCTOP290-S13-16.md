::: {.problem}
Let $\Sigma$ be a connected closed oriented surface
and $\gamma\subset\Sigma$ a simple closed curve.
Show that $\Sigma\setminus\gamma$ is disconnected
if and only if $[\gamma]=0$ in $H_1(\Sigma;\ZZ)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

If $\gamma$ separates, the closure of either
component of $\Sigma\setminus\gamma$ is an oriented
surface with boundary $\gamma$. Its fundamental
chain shows that $[\gamma]=0$ in $H_1(\Sigma;\ZZ)$.

Conversely, suppose $\gamma$ does not separate.
Cut $\Sigma$ along $\gamma$. The resulting surface
is connected and has two boundary circles. Choose
an embedded arc joining the two boundary circles,
with its interior in the cut surface. After gluing
the circles back, close the arc across a small
neighborhood of $\gamma$ to obtain a simple closed
curve $\delta$ transverse to $\gamma$ at one point.
Their algebraic intersection is $\pm1$. Since
intersection number depends only on homology
classes, $[\gamma]\ne0$.
:::
