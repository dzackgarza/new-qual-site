::: {.problem}
Let $\Sigma$ be a connected closed oriented surface
and $\gamma\subset\Sigma$ a simple closed curve.
Show that $\Sigma\setminus\gamma$ is disconnected
if and only if $[\gamma]=0$ in $H_1(\Sigma;\ZZ)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\Sigma$ be a connected closed oriented surface and $\gamma\subset\Sigma$
a simple closed curve, that is a subspace homeomorphic to $S^1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #separates}

If $\gamma$ separates $\Sigma$, then $[\gamma]=0$ in $H_1(\Sigma;\ZZ)$.

::: pf-proof

Separating means $\Sigma\setminus\gamma$ is disconnected, with components
$U_1,U_2$, and each closure $S_j=\overline{U_j}$ is a compact surface whose
boundary is exactly $\gamma$: every point of $\gamma$ has a disc neighbourhood
in which $\gamma$ is a diameter, so a small neighbourhood of $\gamma$ in $S_j$
is a half-disc. Hence $\gamma$ is the boundary of the oriented surface $S_1$,
and the boundary of a fundamental $2$-chain is a $1$-boundary, so
$[\gamma]=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cuts}

If $\gamma$ does not separate, then cutting $\Sigma$ along $\gamma$ produces a
connected compact surface $S$ with two boundary circles $\gamma^+,\gamma^-$
coming from the two sides of $\gamma$.

::: pf-proof

$\Sigma\setminus\gamma$ is connected. Its metric completion, or equivalently
the surface with $\gamma$ replaced by the two circles of the unit normal
bundle of $\gamma$, is a compact surface $S$ with boundary, and $\Sigma$ is
recovered from $S$ by identifying $\gamma^+$ with $\gamma^-$ by the natural
homeomorphism; $S$ is connected because $\Sigma\setminus\gamma$ is.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #transverse}

There is a simple closed curve $\delta\subset\Sigma$ meeting $\gamma$
transversely in exactly one point, so the algebraic intersection number
$\gamma\cdot\delta=\pm1$.

::: pf-proof

In the cut surface $S$ choose an embedded arc $\alpha$ with endpoints on
$\gamma^+$ and $\gamma^-$ and interior in the interior of $S$, possible
because $S$ is connected and has those two boundary circles. Regluing
$\gamma^+$ to $\gamma^-$ turns $\alpha$ into a path from a point of $\gamma$
to a neighbouring point of $\gamma$ on the other side; closing it up by a
small arc in a tubular neighbourhood of $\gamma$ through those two points
gives a closed curve $\delta$ transverse to $\gamma$ and meeting it once.
Taking the arc $\alpha$ in general position with respect to itself and small,
$\delta$ is a simple closed curve. Its intersection number with $\gamma$ is
one, equal to $\pm1$ once orientations are fixed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #converse}

If $\gamma$ does not separate, then $[\gamma]\ne0$ in $H_1(\Sigma;\ZZ)$.

::: pf-proof

The algebraic intersection number $H_1(\Sigma;\ZZ)\times
H_1(\Sigma;\ZZ)\to\ZZ$ is well defined on homology classes, being the
evaluation of a cohomology class on a homology class via Poincaré duality:
$\gamma\cdot\delta=\langle\mathrm{PD}(\gamma),[\delta]\rangle$. If
$[\gamma]=0$ then the intersection number with every class would vanish,
contradicting step [](#transverse){.pf-ref}, which exhibits $\delta$ with
intersection number $\pm1$. So $[\gamma]\ne0$.

:::
