::: {.problem}
Recall the cube quotient from
[[P-UCTOP290-S3-5|sheet 3, Problem 5]]: opposite front and back
faces and opposite top and bottom faces are identified by
translations, while the left and right faces are identified by
a quarter turn. Describe the universal cover and deduce that the
quotient is a $3$-manifold.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $C=I^3$ for the cube with coordinates $(x,y,z)$, where $x$ runs from the
left face to the right face, and let $\rho(y,z)=(1-z,y)$ be the quarter turn
of the square $I^2$ about its centre.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #group}

Let $\Gamma$ be the group of homeomorphisms of $\RR^3$ generated by
$$
u(x,y,z)=(x,y+1,z),\qquad v(x,y,z)=(x,y,z+1),\qquad t(x,y,z)=(x+1,\rho(y,z)).
$$
Then $t\langle u,v\rangle t^{-1}=\langle u,v\rangle$, the subgroup $\langle
u,v\rangle$ is normal in $\Gamma$, and every element of $\Gamma$ is $u^m v^n
t^k$ for integers $m,n,k$.

::: pf-proof

From $t^{-1}(x,y,z)=(x-1,\rho^{-1}(y,z))$ and $\rho^{-1}(y,z)=(z,1-y)$ one
computes $tut^{-1}(x,y,z)=(x,y,z+1)=v$ and $tvt^{-1}(x,y,z)=(x,y-1,z)=u^{-1}$;
the maps $u$ and $v$ commute, being translations in independent coordinates.
So conjugation by $t$ preserves $\langle u,v\rangle$, which is therefore
normal, and $\Gamma=\langle u,v\rangle\rtimes\langle t\rangle$; expanding an
arbitrary word in the generators by moving each $t$ to the right through that
normal subgroup writes it as $u^mv^nt^k$. Also $\rho^{2}(y,z)=(1-y,1-z)$ and
$\rho^{4}=\mathrm{id}$, so $t$ has infinite order and this normal form is the
shape of every element, not a further relation among them.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #free}

The action of $\Gamma$ on $\RR^3$ is free.

::: pf-proof

An element in normal form $u^mv^nt^k$ acts by $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x+k,\
\rho^k(y,z)+(m,n))$, using that $u$ and $v$ commute with $\rho$ as
translations of the square. If $k\ne0$ the $x$-coordinate changes by $k$, so
there is no fixed point. If $k=0$ the element is the translation of the
$(y,z)$-plane by $(m,n)$, which is fixed-point free unless $(m,n)=(0,0)$, in
which case the element is the identity. Hence only the identity fixes a point.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #proper}

The action of $\Gamma$ on $\RR^3$ is properly discontinuous, and hence
$\RR^3\to\RR^3/\Gamma$ is a covering map with total space simply connected.

::: pf-proof

Let $K\subset\RR^3$ be compact, say $K\subset[-N,N]^3$. If $u^mv^nt^kK$ meets
$K$ then some point of $K$ and some point of its image differ by the action,
so $x+k\in[-N,N]$ for some $x\in[-N,N]$, giving $\abs k\le2N$; for each of the
finitely many such $k$, the $(y,z)$-coordinates of the image lie in a
translate of $[-N,N]^2$ by $\rho^k$ and by $(m,n)$, so $\abs m,\abs n\le2N$ as
well. Only finitely many elements of $\Gamma$ therefore move $K$ to meet
itself, which is proper discontinuity; together with freeness of step
[](#free){.pf-ref} this makes the quotient map a covering. $\RR^3$ is
contractible, so it is simply connected.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #quotient}

The quotient $\RR^3/\Gamma$ is the space $X$ of the source: the cube is a
fundamental domain whose paired faces are identified exactly as the figure
prescribes.

::: pf-proof

Every orbit meets $C=I^3$, since the $x$-coordinate can be put in $[0,1]$ by a
power of $t$ and then $y$ and $z$ by powers of $u$ and $v$; the only ambiguity
is on the boundary, where the faces $x=0$ and $x=1$ are matched by $t$
together with the quarter turn $\rho$, the faces $y=0$ and $y=1$ by $u$, and
the faces $z=0$ and $z=1$ by $v$. Interior points of the cube are equivalent
only to themselves, because a nontrivial element moves some coordinate by a
nonzero integer. So the quotient of $C$ by the four face pairings is
$\RR^3/\Gamma$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #manifold}

$\RR^3$ with this action is the universal cover of $X$, and $X$ is a compact
$3$-manifold without boundary.

::: pf-proof

The quotient map is a covering with simply connected total space by steps
[](#proper){.pf-ref} and [](#quotient){.pf-ref}, so $\RR^3$ is the universal
cover. Each point of $X$ then has an evenly covered neighbourhood whose sheets
are open subsets of $\RR^3$ and hence homeomorphic to open $3$-balls, which is
the local model of a $3$-manifold. The cube is compact and surjects onto $X$,
so $X$ is compact, and every face of the cube is paired with another, so no
boundary point survives; the quotient is closed.

:::
