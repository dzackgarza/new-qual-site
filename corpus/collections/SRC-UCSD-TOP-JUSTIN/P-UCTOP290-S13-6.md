::: {.problem}
Let $0<k<n$ and
$X=\CP^n/\CP^k$. Compute the integral cohomology ring of $X$.
For which $n,k$ is $X$ homotopy equivalent to a closed manifold?
What changes if noncompact manifolds are allowed?
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
H^*(\CP^n;\ZZ)=\ZZ[x]/(x^{n+1}),
\qquad \deg x=2.
$$
The quotient map $q:\CP^n\to X$ identifies reduced
cohomology of $X$ with $H^*(\CP^n,\CP^k;\ZZ)$.
The long exact sequence of the pair and the cellular
cohomology of projective space show that $q^*$ is
injective in positive degrees and that its image is the
ideal generated by $x^{k+1}$. Thus, as a unital graded
ring,
$$
H^*(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ\oplus
\bigoplus_{j=k+1}^{n}\ZZ u_j,
\qquad \deg u_j=2j,
$$
where
$$
u_i u_j=
\begin{cases}
u_{i+j},&i+j\le n,\\
0,&i+j>n.
\end{cases}
$$
Here $q^*(u_j)=x^j$.

If $n=k+1$, the quotient collapses all lower cells of
$\CP^n$ and leaves its top $2n$-cell. Hence
$X\cong S^{2n}$, a closed manifold.

If $n\ge k+2$, then over $\FF_2$ the groups
$H^2(X;\FF_2)$ and $H^{2n-2}(X;\FF_2)$ are $0$ and
$\FF_2$, respectively. The top nonzero mod-two
cohomology of $X$ is in degree $2n$. Any connected
closed manifold homotopy equivalent to $X$ would
therefore have dimension $2n$, but mod-two Poincaré
duality would make the dimensions of $H^2$ and
$H^{2n-2}$ equal. This is impossible. Thus the
closed-manifold answer is exactly $n=k+1$.

If noncompact manifolds are allowed, every pair
$0<k<n$ works. The finite CW complex $X$ embeds in
some Euclidean space and has a manifold regular
neighborhood. The interior of that neighborhood has
the homotopy type of $X$.

:::
