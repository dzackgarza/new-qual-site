::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be path-connected, locally path-connected and
semilocally simply connected, with $\pi_1(X)\cong S_3$.
Up to the appropriate equivalence, how many coverings does
$X$ have of each kind?

(a) Based path-connected coverings.

(b) Unbased path-connected coverings.

(c) Three-sheeted coverings that need not be path-connected.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $X$ be path-connected, locally path-connected and semilocally simply
connected with $\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong S_3$; identify the two groups and write
$G=S_3$. A based cover of $(X,x_0)$ corresponds to a subgroup of $G$ and an
unbased connected cover to a conjugacy class of subgroups, while a general
cover corresponds to the disjoint union of the connected covers of its
components.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #based}

There are $\boxed{6}$ based path-connected coverings of $(X,x_0)$ up to
equivalence over $X$ fixing the basepoint.

::: pf-proof

The classification of covering spaces gives a bijection between based
connected covers of $(X,x_0)$ and subgroups of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$, since $X$ is
path-connected, locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected. The
subgroups of $S_3$ are the trivial one, the three subgroups
$\langle(12)\rangle$, $\langle(13)\rangle$, $\langle(23)\rangle$ of order two,
the alternating group $A_3$ of order three, and $S_3$ itself: a subgroup of
order two is generated by an element of order two and $S_3$ has exactly three
of them, and a subgroup of order three is contained in $A_3$, which has a
unique subgroup of each of the orders $1$ and $3$. That is $6$ subgroups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #unbased}

There are $\boxed{4}$ unbased path-connected coverings of $X$ up to
equivalence.

::: pf-proof

Two subgroups give equivalent unbased covers exactly when they are conjugate,
so the count is the number of conjugacy classes of subgroups. The trivial
subgroup and $S_3$ are each alone in their class, being normal and not
conjugate to anything else; $A_3$ is normal, so it is alone as well; and the
three subgroups of order two are conjugate, since all elements of order two
are conjugate in $S_3$ and conjugation carries a cyclic subgroup to a cyclic
subgroup generated by the image. That is $1+1+1+1=4$ classes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #three}

There are $\boxed{3}$ equivalence classes of three-sheeted coverings of $X$
that need not be path-connected.

::: pf-proof

An unbased $k$-sheeted cover corresponds to an action of $G$ on a $k$-element
set up to isomorphism, its fibre, and a connected cover to a transitive such
set, the orbit $G/H$ for $H$ the subgroup of the cover. For $k=3$ a transitive
orbit has size $1$, $2$ or $3$: size $1$ is the one-point set with the trivial
action, size $2$ is $G/A_3$ since $A_3$ has index $2$, and size $3$ is $G/C$
for a subgroup $C$ of order $2$, which is the same isomorphism type for all
three choices of $C$ because they are conjugate. Hence the three-element
$G$-sets are the three singleton orbits, the union of the two-point orbit with
a singleton, and the three-point orbit, since the partitions of $3$ into parts
of the available sizes are $3$, $2+1$ and $1+1+1$. Each is one isomorphism
class, so there are $3$ covers, with $3$, $2$ and $1$ connected components
respectively.

:::
