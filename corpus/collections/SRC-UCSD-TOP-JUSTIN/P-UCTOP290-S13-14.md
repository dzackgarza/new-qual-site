::: {.problem}
Describe submanifolds representing the generators of
$H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)$ and use their intersections to
compute $H^*(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)$ as a ring.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $\ZZ_2=\ZZ/2\ZZ$, and for $0\le k\le n$ let $P^k\cong\RP^k$ be a linear
projective subspace of $\RP^n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cells}

$H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)\cong\ZZ_2$ for $0\le k\le n$ and $0$ otherwise.

::: pf-proof

$\RP^n$ has a CW structure with exactly one cell $e^k$ in each dimension $0\le
k\le n$, and the cellular boundary is multiplication by $1+(-1)^k$ on $\ZZ$,
which is $0$ or $2$ and hence is $0$ modulo $2$. So all cellular differentials
vanish over $\ZZ_2$ and each chain group $\ZZ_2$ is its own homology.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #submanifolds}

$P^k$ represents the nonzero class in $H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)$, and its Poincaré
dual is $a^{n-k}$.

::: pf-proof

$P^k$ is a closed $k$-manifold, so it carries a fundamental class in
$H_k(P^k;\ZZ_2)$ which is nonzero, and it is the $k$-skeleton's top cell
closure; the inclusion $P^k\hookrightarrow\RP^n$ sends the generator of
$H_k(P^k;\ZZ_2)=\ZZ_2$ to the nonzero class of $H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)$, since $P^k$
is a subcomplex carrying the $k$-cell. Poincaré duality over $\ZZ_2$ for the
closed $n$-manifold $\RP^n$ identifies $H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)$ with
$H^{n-k}(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)$, and the dual of $[P^k]$ is the class Poincaré dual to
$P^k$, which for a hyperplane's top power is $a^{n-k}$; the definition of $a$
as the dual of $P^{n-1}$ and the multiplicativity of duality under transverse
intersection give this inductively.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #generation}

$a^j\ne0$ for $0\le j\le n$, and $a^{n+1}=0$; hence
$H^*(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)=\ZZ_2[a]/(a^{n+1})$ with $\deg a=1$.

::: pf-proof

$a^j$ is dual to the intersection of $j$ hyperplanes in general position,
which is a linear $P^{n-j}$; so $a^j$ pairs to $1$ with the class of a
complementary linear $P^j$, the two meeting in exactly one point and
intersection numbers being counted modulo $2$. A class pairing to $1$ with
something is nonzero, so $a^j\ne0$. For $j>n$ the class $a^j$ lies in
$H^j(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)=0$ by step [](#cells){.pf-ref}, so $a^{n+1}=0$. The classes
$1,a,\dots,a^n$ are therefore a basis of the $\ZZ_2$-vector space
$H^*(\RP^n;\ZZ_2)$, whose total dimension is $n+1$, and the ring is the
truncated polynomial ring.

:::
