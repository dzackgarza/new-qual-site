::: {.problem}
Give simplicial or $\Delta$-complex models, integral
homology groups, and generators for the figure eight,
$S^2$, $\RP^2$, a sphere with an equatorial disk
glued to it, and a torus with a disk attached inside it.
:::

::: {.solution}

The figure eight is a $\Delta$-complex with one
vertex and two loop edges. Its $H_0$ is $\ZZ$,
and its $H_1$ is $\ZZ^2$, generated by the two
loops; all higher groups vanish.

Use the boundary of a tetrahedron for $S^2$.
Its $H_0$ and $H_2$ are $\ZZ$, generated by a
point and the oriented sum of faces.

For $\RP^2$, triangulate a disk whose boundary
edges carry the standard projective-plane
identification, then subdivide the quotient.
Its cellular chain complex has one cell in each
degree $0,1,2$, with $\partial_2$ multiplication
by $2$. Thus $H_0=\ZZ$ and $H_1=\ZZ/2$,
generated by the projective line; $H_2=0$.

Triangulate $S^2$ with its equator as a
subcomplex, then attach a triangulated disk along
that equator. The equator bounds both a hemisphere
and the attached disk. The difference of these
two disks is a second two-cycle independent of
the original sphere. Thus $H_0=\ZZ$ and
$H_2=\ZZ^2$, generated by the original sphere
and this second cycle; $H_1=0$.

For the torus clause, triangulate $T^2$ so that
the attaching circle $\gamma$ is a subcomplex,
then attach a triangulated disk. If
$[\gamma]=(p,q)\in H_1(T^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ^2$,
the cellular boundary of the new two-cell is
$(p,q)$. Consequently
$$
H_1\cong\ZZ^2/\langle(p,q)\rangle,\qquad
H_2\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ^2,&(p,q)=(0,0),\\
\ZZ,&(p,q)\ne(0,0),
\end{cases}
$$
and $H_0=\ZZ$. In degree two, the torus
fundamental class always survives; when
$(p,q)=0$, a bounding two-chain for $\gamma$
combined with the new disk gives the second
generator.

The source does not specify the attaching circle
in the torus. If “inside” means the boundary of
an embedded disk in the torus, then $(p,q)=0$
and the displayed groups are $\ZZ,\ZZ^2,\ZZ^2$
in degrees $0,1,2$.
:::
