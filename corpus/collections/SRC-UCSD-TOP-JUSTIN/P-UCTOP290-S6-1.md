::: {.problem}
Give simplicial or $\Delta$-complex models, integral
homology groups, and generators for the figure eight,
$S^2$, and $\RP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #eight}

The figure eight $S^1\vee S^1$ has a $\Delta$-complex structure with one
vertex $v$ and two $1$-simplices $a,b$ attached at $v$; its integral homology
is $\boxed{H_0=\ZZ}$, $\boxed{H_1=\ZZ^2}$, $H_i=0$ for $i\ge2$, generated by
$[v]$ and by the two loops.

::: pf-proof

Both ends of each of $a$ and $b$ are glued to $v$, so
$\partial_1a=\partial_1b=0$ and there are no simplices above dimension one.
Hence $H_0=C_0=\ZZ[v]$ and $H_1=C_1=\ZZ a\oplus\ZZ b$, generated by the
classes of the two edges, which are the two circles of the wedge with either
orientation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sphere}

The boundary of a tetrahedron is a simplicial complex homeomorphic to $S^2$
with $4$ vertices, $6$ edges and $4$ faces, and it has $\boxed{H_0=\ZZ}$,
$H_1=0$, $\boxed{H_2=\ZZ}$, generated by $[v]$ and by the sum of the four
faces oriented outwards.

::: pf-proof

The boundary of a tetrahedron is homeomorphic to $S^2$, being a polyhedral
model of it. Every edge lies in exactly two faces, and with the outward
orientations the two contributions to $\partial_2$ of the sum of the faces
cancel; so the sum is a cycle and defines a class in $H_2=\ker\partial_2$,
since there are no $3$-simplices. Its coefficient on any face is $1$, which is
primitive, so it generates the infinite cyclic group $\ker\partial_2$: a cycle
is determined by its coefficient on one face because the dual graph of the
tetrahedron is connected, and an integer determined by one coefficient $c$ is
either $0$ or a multiple of the generator. For $H_1$, the $1$-cycles are
generated by the differences of edges across faces, and each such difference
is exactly the boundary of a face, so
$\ker\partial_1=\operatorname{im}\partial_2$ and $H_1=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #projective}

$\RP^2$ has a cell structure with one cell in each dimension $0,1,2$, in which
the $2$-cell is attached to the $1$-skeleton by $z\mapsto z^2$; its homology
is $\boxed{H_0=\ZZ}$, $\boxed{H_1=\ZZ/2}$, $H_2=0$, the generator of $H_1$
being the projective line. Triangulating the attaching disc gives a simplicial
model with the same homology.

::: pf-proof

$\RP^2$ is the quotient of $S^2$ by the antipodal map, which preserves the
equatorial $S^1$ and acts on it antipodally, so $\RP^2$ is the disc attached
to a circle by the degree-two map $z\mapsto z^2$; that is the stated cell
structure. Its cellular chain complex is $\ZZ\xrightarrow{\ \partial_2\
}\ZZ\xrightarrow{\ \partial_1\ }\ZZ$ with $\partial_1=0$, because the $1$-cell
is a loop at the single $0$-cell, and $\partial_2$ multiplication by the
degree of the attaching map, which is $2$. Hence $H_0=\ZZ$, $H_1=\ZZ/2\ZZ$
generated by the $1$-cell, and $H_2=\ker\partial_2=0$; there are no higher
cells. Subdividing the boundary circle so that $z\mapsto z^2$ is simplicial on
the subdivision and coning the result gives a simplicial complex whose
simplicial chain complex has the same homology by the comparison of cellular
and simplicial homology.

:::
