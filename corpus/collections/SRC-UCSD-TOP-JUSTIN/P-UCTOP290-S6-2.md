::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be a connected simplicial complex with
base vertex $a_0$, and let $X$ be its geometric
realization. Form the edge-loop group
$E(K,a_0)$ by deleting repetitions and by
replacing two sides of a simplex with the third.
Prove
$$
E(K,a_0)\cong\pi_1(X,a_0),
\qquad
E(K,a_0)/[E(K,a_0),E(K,a_0)]\cong H_1(K;\ZZ).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Choose a maximal tree $T$ in the one-skeleton.
Every vertex can be reached from $a_0$ through
$T$, and the tree has a unique path between any
two vertices. Collapsing $T$ gives one loop
generator for each edge outside $T$. The
boundary of each two-simplex gives exactly
the edge-homotopy relation among these generators.
By van Kampen, attaching the two-simplices
imposes precisely these relations on
$\pi_1(X^{(1)},a_0)$; higher simplices do not
change $\pi_1$. Thus the map sending each edge
loop to its geometric path is an isomorphism
$E(K,a_0)\cong\pi_1(X,a_0)$.

Send an edge loop to the sum of its oriented
edges. The two edge-homotopy moves change this
cycle by zero or by the boundary of a
two-simplex, so the map to $H_1(K;\ZZ)$ is
well defined. The cellular chain complex after
collapsing $T$ has one vertex; its first cycles
are freely generated by the non-tree edges,
and its two-boundaries are the abelianized
two-simplex relations. Hence this map induces
$E(K,a_0)/[E(K,a_0),E(K,a_0)]\cong H_1(K;\ZZ)$.
Its kernel is the commutator subgroup.
:::

::: {.remark}
The source does not assume $K$ connected.
For a disconnected $K$, edge loops at $a_0$
see only its component, while $H_1(K)$ also
contains the first homology of other components.
The surjectivity claim therefore needs the
connectedness hypothesis.
:::
