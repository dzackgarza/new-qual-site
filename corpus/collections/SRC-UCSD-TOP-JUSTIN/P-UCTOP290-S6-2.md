::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be a connected simplicial complex with
base vertex $a_0$, and let $X$ be its geometric
realization. Form the edge-loop group
$E(K,a_0)$ by deleting repetitions and by
replacing two sides of a simplex with the third.
Prove
$$
E(K,a_0)\cong\pi_1(X,a_0),
\qquad
E(K,a_0)/[E(K,a_0),E(K,a_0)]\cong H_1(K;\ZZ).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $K$ be a connected simplicial complex with base vertex $a_0$, let $X=|K|$,
and let $E(K,a_0)$ be the set of edge loops at $a_0$, that is strings
$a_0a_1\cdots a_n$ of vertices with $a_n=a_0$ and each $\{a_i,a_{i+1}\}$ a
simplex, modulo the two edge-homotopy relations: deleting a repeated vertex
$a_i=a_{i+1}$, and replacing $a_{i-1}a_ia_{i+1}$ by $a_{i-1}a_{i+1}$ when the
three vertices span a simplex of dimension at most $2$. Composition is
juxtaposition of strings.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #generators}

Let $T$ be a maximal tree in the $1$-skeleton $K^{(1)}$. Then $E(K,a_0)$ is
generated by the classes of the edges of $K^{(1)}$ outside $T$.

::: pf-proof

Write $t(u)$ for the string of tree edges along the unique path in $T$ from
$a_0$ to a vertex $u$. An edge loop $a_0a_1\cdots a_n$ with $n\ge2$ is
edge-homotopic to $a_0a_1\cdots\widehat{a_i}\cdots a_n$ whenever three
consecutive vertices span a simplex, and to the concatenation of its two
shorter loops at each vertex; applying these moves to split the loop into
single-edge pieces and then to replace each tree-edge piece $a_{i-1}a_i$ by
$t(a_{i-1})^{-1}t(a_i)$ rewrites the loop as a word in the edges outside $T$
conjugated by tree paths. Since $T$ is a tree, each such conjugate is itself a
product of edges outside $T$ and their inverses.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #relations}

The relations among these generators are exactly the ones read from the
$2$-simplices: if a $2$-simplex has boundary edges $e_0,e_1,e_2$ in cyclic
order, then its three edge paths are edge-homotopic to each other, so
$e_2=e_0e_1$ up to conjugation by tree paths.

::: pf-proof

The first relation only deletes repeated vertices and changes nothing in the
group, and the second applies exactly when three consecutive vertices span a
simplex; for a $2$-simplex with vertices $u,v,w$ the loops $uvw$ and $uw$ are
related, and after replacing each edge by its tree-conjugated representative
the relation becomes the stated one. Every application of the second relation
is of this form.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pi1}

$E(K,a_0)\cong\pi_1(X,a_0)$.

::: pf-proof

The $1$-skeleton retracts onto the tree $T$ and $X^{(1)}/T$ is a wedge of one
circle per edge outside $T$, so by van Kampen $\pi_1(X^{(1)},a_0)$ is free on
those edges, exactly as $E(K,a_0)$ is by step [](#generators){.pf-ref}.
Attaching the $2$-simplices imposes, by van Kampen applied to $X^{(1)}$ with
the cone on its boundary circle adjoined, precisely the relation that the
boundary word of the attached $2$-cell is trivial, which is the relation of
step [](#relations){.pf-ref}. Cells of dimension at least $3$ do not change
$\pi_1$, since they are attached along simply connected skeleta; so
$\pi_1(X,a_0)$ has the presentation of $E(K,a_0)$ and the two are isomorphic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #abelian}

The abelianisation $E(K,a_0)/[E(K,a_0),E(K,a_0)]$ is isomorphic to
$H_1(K;\ZZ)$, and the quotient map is the surjection the source asks for.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#pi1){.pf-ref} the abelianisation is $\pi_1(X,a_0)/[\pi_1,\pi_1]$,
which is $H_1(X;\ZZ)$ by the Hurewicz theorem in degree one for path-connected
spaces. The cellular chain complex of $K$ computes $H_1(K;\ZZ)$: with the
$1$-cells collapsed along $T$ there is one $0$-cell, the $1$-cells are the
edges outside $T$, and $\partial_2$ is given by the abelianised boundary words
of step [](#relations){.pf-ref}; so $H_1(K;\ZZ)$ is the abelianisation of the
presentation of $E(K,a_0)$, with the commutator subgroup as kernel.

:::

:::

::: {.remark}

The source does not assume $K$ connected. For a disconnected $K$, edge loops
at $a_0$ see only its component, while $H_1(K)$ also contains the first
homology of other components. The surjectivity claim therefore needs the
connectedness hypothesis, and with it the two statements proved here hold.

:::
