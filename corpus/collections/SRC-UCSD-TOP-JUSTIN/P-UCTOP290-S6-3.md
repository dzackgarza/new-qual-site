::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be an $n$-dimensional simplicial complex,
$n\ge1$. Suppose every $(n-1)$-simplex belongs
to exactly two $n$-simplices, and any two
$n$-simplices can be joined by a sequence in
which consecutive simplices share an
$(n-1)$-face. Show that $H_n(K;\ZZ)$ is either
$0$ or $\ZZ$. In the second case describe a
generator.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $K$ be an $n$-dimensional simplicial complex, $n\ge1$, in which every
$(n-1)$-simplex is a face of exactly two $n$-simplices and any two
$n$-simplices are joined by a sequence of $n$-simplices consecutive ones
sharing an $(n-1)$-face. Orient every $n$-simplex arbitrarily and write them
$\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_m$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #kernel}

$H_n(K;\ZZ)=\ker\partial_n$, and it is the set of integer $(n-1)$-cochains'
worth of coefficient vectors that satisfy one linear condition per
$(n-1)$-simplex.

::: pf-proof

There are no $(n+1)$-simplices, since $K$ has dimension $n$, so $C_{n+1}=0$
and $H_n=\ker\partial_n$. A chain $z=\sum_jc_j\sigma_j$ has, in $\partial_nz$,
the coefficient $\varepsilon c_j+\varepsilon'c_{j'}$ on a shared $(n-1)$-face,
where $\varepsilon,\varepsilon'\in\{\pm1\}$ are the incidence signs of that
face in the two $n$-simplices containing it. So $\partial_nz=0$ is the system
of equations $\varepsilon c_j+\varepsilon'c_{j'}=0$, one for each
$(n-1)$-simplex.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #propagate}

$\ker\partial_n$ is the set of vectors whose entries have a common absolute
value $c\ge0$ and whose signs are determined by $\sigma_1$; so
$\ker\partial_n$ is infinite cyclic if some such vector is nonzero and is $0$
otherwise.

::: pf-proof

Each equation says $c_{j'}=\pm c_j$, so sharing an $(n-1)$-face makes two
coefficients equal up to sign. The second hypothesis makes the graph on the
$n$-simplices with an edge for each shared $(n-1)$-face connected, so by
following a path every $c_j$ is $\pm c_1$. Fixing $c_1$ therefore determines
$z$ completely, and the map $\ker\partial_n\to\ZZ$, $z\mapsto c_1$, is an
injective homomorphism onto a subgroup of $\ZZ$; a subgroup of $\ZZ$ is $0$ or
infinite cyclic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #generator}

If $\ker\partial_n\ne0$ then $H_n(K;\ZZ)=\ZZ$, generated by the sum of all
$n$-simplices with orientations chosen consistently across shared faces, when
$K$ is finite; for an infinite $K$ the group is $0$.

::: pf-proof

Let $d>0$ generate the image of the coefficient map, so a generator $z$ has
$c_1=d$ and all $\abs{c_j}=d$. Dividing by $d$ gives a cycle whose every
coefficient is $\pm1$: reorienting $\sigma_j$ by the sign of its coefficient
turns $z$ into the sum of all $n$-simplices with the new orientations, and
reorienting does not disturb the equations, only their signs. That sum is the
stated generator. If $K$ has infinitely many $n$-simplices, a cycle must have
finite support and all its coefficients of equal absolute value, so only the
zero cycle occurs, and $H_n=0$; the conclusion that $H_n$ is $0$ or $\ZZ$
therefore holds in both cases.

:::
