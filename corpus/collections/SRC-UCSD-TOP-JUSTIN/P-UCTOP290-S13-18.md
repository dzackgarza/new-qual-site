::: {.problem}
Let $V=S^1\times D^2$ be a solid torus and
$T=\partial V=S^1\times S^1$. Compute and describe
generators for $H_*(V;\ZZ)$, $H_*(V,T;\ZZ)$,
$H^*(V;\ZZ)$, and $H^*(V,T;\ZZ)$. Describe the
intersection pairings
$H_i(V;\ZZ)\times H_{3-i}(V,T;\ZZ)\to\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $V=S^1\times D^2$ be the closed solid torus, $T=\partial V=S^1\times S^1$,
$c=S^1\times\{0\}$ its core, $m=\{x\}\times\partial D^2$ a meridian and
$d=\{x\}\times D^2$ a meridional disc.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #absolute}

$H_0(V)=H_1(V)=\ZZ$, generated by a point and by $[c]$, and $H_k(V)=0$ for
$k\ge2$.

::: pf-proof

$V$ deformation retracts onto its core circle $c$, so its homology is that of
$S^1$: $\ZZ$ in degrees $0$ and $1$ and $0$ above.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #relative}

$H_2(V,T;\ZZ)=H_3(V,T;\ZZ)=\ZZ$ and $H_k(V,T;\ZZ)=0$ otherwise; $[d]$
generates degree two and the orientation class $[V,T]$ degree three.

::: pf-proof

In $H_1(T;\ZZ)=\ZZ^2$ the meridian $m$ bounds the disc $d$ inside $V$, so it
maps to $0$ in $H_1(V)$, while the longitude $S^1\times\{y\}$ maps to a
generator. The long exact sequence of the pair $(V,T)$ is
$$
0\to H_3(V,T)\to H_2(T)\xrightarrow{i_*}H_2(V)\to H_2(V,T)\to H_1(T)\xrightarrow{i_*}H_1(V)\to H_1(V,T)\to H_0(T)\to H_0(V)\to0,
$$
together with the segment above degree three where all terms vanish because
$V$ has dimension three. The first line gives $H_3(V,T)\cong H_2(T)=\ZZ$,
since $H_2(V)=0$; the map $H_1(T)=\ZZ^2\to H_1(V)=\ZZ$ is the projection
killing the meridian, so $H_2(V,T)=\ker i_*=\ZZ$ generated by the meridional
disc, $H_1(V,T)=\operatorname{coker}i_*=0$, and $H_0(V,T)=0$ because
$H_0(T)\to H_0(V)$ is an isomorphism. The relative fundamental class generates
the top relative group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #cohomology}

$H^*(V;\ZZ)$ is $\ZZ$ in degrees $0,1$ and $H^*(V,T;\ZZ)$ is $\ZZ$ in degrees
$2,3$, all other groups zero; the degree-one class evaluates to $1$ on $[c]$,
the degree-two relative class to $1$ on $[d]$, and the degree-three relative
class to $1$ on $[V,T]$.

::: pf-proof

All the absolute and relative homology groups computed in steps
[](#absolute){.pf-ref} and [](#relative){.pf-ref} are free, so the universal
coefficient theorems give $H^i=\Hom(H_i,\ZZ)$ and
$H^i(V,T)=\Hom(H_i(V,T),\ZZ)$, with no Ext terms. Each $\ZZ$ summand therefore
contributes a dual generator evaluating to $1$ on it.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pairings}

The intersection pairings $H_i(V)\times H_{3-i}(V,T)\to\ZZ$ are nonzero only
for $i=0$ and $i=1$, where both are the standard pairing $\ZZ\times\ZZ\to\ZZ$,
$(r,s)\mapsto rs$: a point meets $[V,T]$ in one point, and the core $c$ meets
the meridional disc $d$ transversely in one point, with sign $+1$ for
compatible orientations.

::: pf-proof

For $i=2$ and $i=3$ the absolute groups vanish by step [](#absolute){.pf-ref},
so those pairings are trivial. For $i=0$ a point in the interior of $V$ and
the relative fundamental class $[V,T]$ have intersection number $1$, by the
definition of the relative fundamental class as the dual of the orientation of
$V$ away from the boundary. For $i=1$, let $c$ be the core pushed slightly
away from its endpoints and $d$ the meridional disc slightly perturbed; they
meet transversely in the single point $(x,0)$, and the orientation of $V$ as a
product orients $c$ and $d$ so that the intersection number is $+1$.
Nondegeneracy of each pairing in its two variables follows since both groups
are $\ZZ$ and the pairing of generators is $\pm1$.

:::
