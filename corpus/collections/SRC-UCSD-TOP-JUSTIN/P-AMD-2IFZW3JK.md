::: {.problem}
Show that the Mayer-Vietoris sequence is natural, i.e. If $X\mapsvia{f} Y$ where $f(A) \subset C$ and $f(B) \subset D$, then this commutes:
$$
\begin{CD}
H_n(X) @>>> H_n(A\intersect B) @>>> H_n(A) \oplus H_n(B) @>>> H_{n-1}(X)\\
@VVf_*V @VVf_*V  @VVf_*V @VVf_*V\\
H_n(Y) @>>> H_n(C\intersect D) @>>> H_n(C) \oplus H_n(D) @>>> H_{n-1}(Y)\\
\end{CD}
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

The diagram printed in the retained source has the Mayer--Vietoris groups in the wrong order.

::: pf-proof

The source displays
$$
H_n(X)\longrightarrow H_n(A\cap B)\longrightarrow H_n(A)\oplus H_n(B)\longrightarrow H_{n-1}(X),
$$
which is not a segment of the Mayer--Vietoris long exact sequence. The correct segment is
$$
H_n(A\cap B)\xrightarrow{(i_*,-j_*)}H_n(A)\oplus H_n(B)
\xrightarrow{k_*+\ell_*}H_n(X)\xrightarrow{\partial}H_{n-1}(A\cap B).
$$
Thus the intended assertion is the naturality of this correct sequence.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let $S_\bullet(A+B)\subset S_\bullet(X)$ be the subcomplex generated by singular simplices whose images lie in $A$ or in $B$. There is a short exact sequence
$$
0\to S_\bullet(A\cap B)\xrightarrow{(i_\sharp,-j_\sharp)}
S_\bullet(A)\oplus S_\bullet(B)
\xrightarrow{k_\sharp+\ell_\sharp}S_\bullet(A+B)\to0.
$$

::: pf-proof

The first map is injective. A pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ lies in the kernel of $k_\sharp+\ell_\sharp$ exactly when $\alpha=-\beta$ as a chain of $X$; such a chain is supported in both $A$ and $B$, hence comes uniquely from a chain $\gamma\in S_\bullet(A\cap B)$ as $(\gamma,-\gamma)$. Surjectivity holds because every generator of $S_\bullet(A+B)$ lies wholly in $A$ or wholly in $B$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The analogous sequence for $Y=C\cup D$ fits into a commutative diagram of short exact sequences under $f$.

::: pf-proof

Because $f(A)\subset C$ and $f(B)\subset D$, also $f(A\cap B)\subset C\cap D$, and the induced singular-chain maps preserve all three subcomplexes. Naturality of inclusions gives
$$
(f_\sharp\oplus f_\sharp)(i_\sharp,-j_\sharp)
=(i'_\sharp,-j'_\sharp)f_\sharp,
$$
and
$$
f_\sharp(k_\sharp+\ell_\sharp)
=(k'_\sharp+\ell'_\sharp)(f_\sharp\oplus f_\sharp).
$$
Hence the chain-level diagram commutes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Passing to homology gives a commutative ladder of long exact sequences, including the connecting homomorphisms.

::: pf-proof

The long exact homology sequence of a short exact sequence of chain complexes is natural: a morphism of short exact sequences induces a morphism of the associated long exact sequences. Applying this to step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives commutativity at every term, including
$$
\begin{CD}
H_n(S_\bullet(A+B)) @>{\partial}>> H_{n-1}(A\cap B)\\
@V{f_*}VV @VV{f_*}V\\
H_n(S_\bullet(C+D)) @>{\partial'}>> H_{n-1}(C\cap D).
\end{CD}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

The inclusions $S_\bullet(A+B)\hookrightarrow S_\bullet(X)$ and $S_\bullet(C+D)\hookrightarrow S_\bullet(Y)$ induce isomorphisms on homology, naturally with respect to $f$.

::: pf-proof

This is the small-simplices theorem for excisive covers. Barycentric subdivision makes every singular simplex homologous, naturally, to a sum of simplices lying in one member of the cover. Therefore the inclusions are chain-homotopy equivalences onto homology and commute with the map induced by $f$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Under the identifications in step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, the ladder in step [](#s4){.pf-ref} is exactly the Mayer--Vietoris naturality ladder. Thus the intended Mayer--Vietoris sequence is natural.

::: pf-proof

Substitute $H_n(S_\bullet(A+B))\cong H_n(X)$ and $H_n(S_\bullet(C+D))\cong H_n(Y)$ into step [](#s4){.pf-ref}. The maps at the other terms are the homology maps induced by the restrictions of $f$, so every square in the corrected Mayer--Vietoris diagram commutes.

:::
