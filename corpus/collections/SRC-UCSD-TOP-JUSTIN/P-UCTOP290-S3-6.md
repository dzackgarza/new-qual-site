::: {.problem}
Compute the fundamental group of the space obtained by identifying the faces of the solid prism shown in Problem 6 of the source sheet.
In the figure, the three rectangular side faces each carry the letter $F$, upright and readable from outside, and the two triangular faces each carry the letter $Q$, turned relative to each other.
:::

::: {.remark}
The face-identification diagram appears on page 1 of the source sheet.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #section}

Write the prism as $\Delta\times I$ with $\Delta$ a triangle. The three side faces carry the letter $F$ upright and readable from outside, so they are identified with each other by maps of the form $e_i\times I\to e_j\times I$ that preserve the vertical coordinate and carry each edge $e_i$ of $\Delta$ to the next one in the same cyclic direction. Hence the quotient by the side pairings is $P\times I$, where $P$ is the triangle with all three edges identified to one edge $a$ by the boundary word $a^3$.

::: pf-proof

Read from outside the solid, the two visible side faces show $F$, and the dotted glyph on the back face is $F$ reflected, which is $F$ read from outside. Reading each face from left to right runs around the triangle in the same cyclic direction for all three faces, so the three edges are glued as $a,a,a$ and not as in the dunce cap $a,a,a^{-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #pi1P}

$\pi_1(P)\cong\ZZ/3$, generated by $a$, and every rotation of $\Delta$ through a multiple of $120^\circ$ induces the identity on $\pi_1(P,c)$, where $c$ is the centre of $\Delta$.

::: pf-proof

Join $c$ to the corners by segments $\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3$, which cut $\Delta$ into three triangles with boundaries $\gamma_i a\gamma_{i+1}^{-1}$ (indices mod $3$). The quotient $P$ is a CW complex with $0$-cells $c,v$, $1$-cells $\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3,a$ and these three $2$-cells. Contracting the tree $\gamma_1$ gives the presentation $\langle \gamma_2,\gamma_3,a\mid a\gamma_2^{-1},\ \gamma_2a\gamma_3^{-1},\ \gamma_3a\rangle$, so $\gamma_2=a$, $\gamma_3=a^2$ and $a^3=1$. The rotation through $120^\circ$ fixes $c$, sends $a\mapsto a$ and $\gamma_i\mapsto\gamma_{i+1}$, so it sends the generator $\gamma_1a\gamma_1^{-1}$ to $\gamma_2a\gamma_2^{-1}=a\,a\,a^{-1}=a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #torus}

$X$ is the mapping torus of a homeomorphism $h:P\to P$ induced by a rotation of $\Delta$ through a multiple of $120^\circ$.

::: pf-proof

The top and bottom faces carry the letter $Q$ in different positions, so they are glued by a rotation of $\Delta$ about its centre; a rotation that maps $\Delta$ to itself is through a multiple of $120^\circ$. It permutes the edges of $\Delta$ cyclically and so commutes with the side identifications of Step [](#section){.pf-ref}, which gives a homeomorphism $h$ of $P$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

The fundamental group of a mapping torus of a homeomorphism fixing the base point is $\pi_1(P)\rtimes_{h_*}\ZZ$. By Steps [](#pi1P){.pf-ref} and [](#torus){.pf-ref}, $h_*$ is the identity, so
$$\boxed{\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ/3\times\ZZ.}$$

:::
