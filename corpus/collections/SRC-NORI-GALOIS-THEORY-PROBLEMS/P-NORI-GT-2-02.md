::: {.problem}
Char.F is $\neq 2$ Let $a _ { 1 } , a _ { 2 } , . . . , a _ { r }$ be nonzero elements of F and let G be the subgroup generated by their images in ${ F ^ { \times } } / ( { F ^ { \times } } ) ^ { 2 }$ . Let $E = F ( { \sqrt { a _ { 1 } } } , { \sqrt { a _ { 2 } } } , . . . , { \sqrt { a _ { r } } } )$ Prove that $\deg ( E / F )$ is the order of G.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
V=F^\times/(F^\times)^2.
$$
Since every element of $V$ has order at most $2$, the subgroup $G$ is a finite-dimensional $\FF_2$-vector space.

::: pf

::: pf-step

Choose indices $i_1,\ldots,i_s$ such that
$$
[a_{i_1}],\ldots,[a_{i_s}]
$$
form an $\FF_2$-basis of $G$.


::: pf-proof

The finitely generated elementary abelian $2$-group $G$ is a finite-dimensional vector space over $\FF_2$, so a basis can be selected from the given generating set.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Then
$$
\cardinality{G}=2^s.
$$


::: pf-proof

An $s$-dimensional vector space over $\FF_2$ has exactly $2^s$ elements.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
E=F(\sqrt{a_{i_1}},\ldots,\sqrt{a_{i_s}}).
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
L=F(\sqrt{a_{i_1}},\ldots,\sqrt{a_{i_s}}).
$$
Certainly $L\subseteq E$. Conversely, because the chosen square classes span $G$, for each $j$ there exist $\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_s\in\{0,1\}$ and $c\in F^\times$ such that
$$
a_j=c^2\prod_{m=1}^s a_{i_m}^{\varepsilon_m}.
$$
Therefore
$$
c\prod_{m=1}^s(\sqrt{a_{i_m}})^{\varepsilon_m}
$$
is a square root of $a_j$ lying in $L$. Thus every generator $\sqrt{a_j}$ of $E$ lies in $L$, so $E=L$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For $0\leq j\leq s$, set
$$
E_j=F(\sqrt{a_{i_1}},\ldots,\sqrt{a_{i_j}}),
\qquad E_0=F.
$$
Then
$$
[E_j:E_{j-1}]\leq2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The field $E_j$ is obtained from $E_{j-1}$ by adjoining a root of
$$
T^2-a_{i_j},
$$
so its degree over $E_{j-1}$ is either $1$ or $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

In fact
$$
a_{i_j}\notin(E_{j-1}^\times)^2
$$
for every $1\leq j\leq s$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose instead that $a_{i_j}$ were a square in $E_{j-1}$. Apply the quadratic-extension kernel computation successively along
$$
F=E_0\subset E_1\subset\cdots\subset E_{j-1}.
$$
At each stage $E_m/E_{m-1}=F(\sqrt{a_{i_m}})/E_{m-1}$, a square class from the preceding field can become trivial only after multiplying by the class of the newly adjoined radicand. Inductively, the kernel of
$$
F^\times/(F^\times)^2\longrightarrow E_{j-1}^\times/(E_{j-1}^\times)^2
$$
is contained in the subgroup generated by
$$
[a_{i_1}],\ldots,[a_{i_{j-1}}].
$$
Thus $[a_{i_j}]$ would lie in the span of the preceding basis vectors, contradicting their linear independence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Consequently
$$
[E_j:E_{j-1}]=2
$$
for every $j=1,\ldots,s$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, the polynomial $T^2-a_{i_j}$ has no root in $E_{j-1}$. A quadratic polynomial over a field is reducible exactly when it has a root, so it is irreducible. Hence adjoining its root gives degree $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The tower law gives
$$
[E:F]=2^s.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $E=E_s$. Hence
$$
[E:F]
=\prod_{j=1}^s[E_j:E_{j-1}]
=\prod_{j=1}^s2
=2^s
$$
by step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{[E:F]=\cardinality{G}.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives $\cardinality{G}=2^s$, while step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives $[E:F]=2^s$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the required equality.

:::
