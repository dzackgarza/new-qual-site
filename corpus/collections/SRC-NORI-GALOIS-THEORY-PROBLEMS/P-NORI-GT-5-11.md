::: {.problem}
Let F be a field which contains a primitive p-th root of unity ⇣. Show that a cyclic extension $K / F$ of degree p is contained in a cyclic extension of degree $p ^ { 2 }$ if and only if there exists $u \in K$ such that $\mathrm { N o r m } _ { F } ^ { K } ( u ) = \zeta$

Hint: For $p = 2$ , this was proved in class using Hilbert’s Thm.90. That proof can be mimicked, relying on 5.9.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\zeta\in F$ be the given primitive $p$-th root of unity.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

Suppose $K$ is contained in a cyclic extension $L/F$ of degree $p^2$. Then there exists $u\in K^\times$ with
$$
N_{K/F}(u)=\zeta.
$$


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let $\tau$ generate $\Gal(L/F)$, and let
$$
\sigma=\tau\restrict_K.
$$
Then $\sigma$ generates $\Gal(K/F)$ and $\Gal(L/K)=\langle\tau^p\rangle$.


::: pf-proof

Since $[L:F]=p^2$ and $[K:F]=p$, one has $[L:K]=p$. The cyclic group $\Gal(L/F)=\langle\tau\rangle$ has a unique subgroup of order $p$, namely $\langle\tau^p\rangle$, whose fixed field is therefore the unique intermediate extension of degree $p$ over $F$, namely $K$. Thus
$$
\Gal(L/K)=\langle\tau^p\rangle.
$$
The quotient $\Gal(L/F)/\Gal(L/K)$ is generated by the image of $\tau$, so $\sigma=\tau\restrict_K$ generates $\Gal(K/F)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There exists $\beta\in L^\times$ such that
$$
\tau^p(\beta)=\zeta\beta.
$$


::: pf-proof

The automorphism $h=\tau^p$ has order $p$ and fixes $K$. By Artin's linear independence of distinct field automorphisms, the $K$-linear operator
$$
T=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\zeta^{-j}h^j
$$
is not the zero map on $L$. Choose $x\in L$ with $\beta=T(x)\neq0$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
h(\beta)
&=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\zeta^{-j}h^{j+1}(x)\\
&=\zeta\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\zeta^{-(j+1)}h^{j+1}(x)\\
&=\zeta\beta,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the indices are read modulo $p$ and $h^p=1$. Hence $\tau^p(\beta)=\zeta\beta$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
u=\frac{\tau(\beta)}{\beta}.
$$
Then $u\in K^\times$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and the fact that $\tau$ fixes $\zeta\in F$,
$$
\tau^p(u)
=\frac{\tau^{p+1}(\beta)}{\tau^p(\beta)}
=\frac{\tau(\zeta\beta)}{\zeta\beta}
=\frac{\zeta\tau(\beta)}{\zeta\beta}
=u.
$$
Thus $u$ is fixed by $\Gal(L/K)=\langle\tau^p\rangle$, so $u\in K^\times$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
N_{K/F}(u)=\zeta.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $\sigma=\tau\restrict_K$ generates $\Gal(K/F)$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{K/F}(u)
&=\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}\sigma^i(u)\\
&=\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}\tau^i\left(\frac{\tau(\beta)}{\beta}\right)\\
&=\frac{\tau^p(\beta)}{\beta}\\
&=\zeta
\end{aligned}
$$
by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the required element $u\in K^\times$.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Conversely, suppose there exists $u\in K^\times$ such that
$$
N_{K/F}(u)=\zeta.
$$
Then $K$ is contained in a cyclic extension of $F$ of degree $p^2$.


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s2-1}

Let $\sigma$ generate $\Gal(K/F)$. Then
$$
N_{K/F}(u^p)=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

By multiplicativity of the norm,
$$
N_{K/F}(u^p)=N_{K/F}(u)^p=\zeta^p=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-2}

There exists $v\in K^\times$ such that
$$
\frac{\sigma(v)}{v}=u^p.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2-1){.pf-ref}, the element $u^p$ has norm $1$. Hilbert's Theorem 90 for the cyclic extension $K/F$ therefore gives $v\in K^\times$ satisfying
$$
u^p=\frac{\sigma(v)}{v}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-3}

Let $\beta$ satisfy $\beta^p=v$ and set
$$
L=K(\beta).
$$
Then the rule
$$
\tau\restrict_K=\sigma,
\qquad
\tau(\beta)=u\beta
$$
defines an $F$-automorphism $\tau$ of $L$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2-2){.pf-ref},
$$
(u\beta)^p=u^p v=\sigma(v).
$$
Hence the assignment sends the defining relation $\beta^p=v$ to the corresponding relation
$$
\tau(\beta)^p=\sigma(v),
$$
so it extends $\sigma$ to an $F$-embedding of $L$ into an algebraic closure. Its image contains $\sigma(K)=K$ and also $u\beta$, hence contains
$$
\beta=u^{-1}\tau(\beta).
$$
Therefore its image is all of $L$, and $\tau$ is an $F$-automorphism of $L$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-4}

The automorphism $\tau$ satisfies
$$
\tau^p(\beta)=\zeta\beta.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using $\tau\restrict_K=\sigma$ and step [](#s2-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau^p(\beta)
&=\tau^{p-1}(u)\tau^{p-2}(u)\cdots\tau(u)u\,\beta\\
&=\sigma^{p-1}(u)\sigma^{p-2}(u)\cdots\sigma(u)u\,\beta\\
&=N_{K/F}(u)\beta\\
&=\zeta\beta.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2-5}

The extension $L/F$ is cyclic of degree $p^2$.


::: pf-proof

Since $L=K(\beta)$ with $\beta^p\in K$, one has
$$
[L:F]\leq p[K:F]=p^2.
$$
By step [](#s2-4){.pf-ref}, $\tau^p$ is nonidentity because $\zeta\neq1$. On the other hand, $\tau\restrict_K=\sigma$ has order $p$, so $\tau^{p^2}$ fixes $K$, and step [](#s2-4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\tau^{p^2}(\beta)=\zeta^p\beta=\beta.
$$
Thus $\tau$ has order exactly $p^2$.

Therefore $\Aut_F(L)$ contains a cyclic subgroup of order $p^2$, so
$$
p^2\leq\abs{\Aut_F(L)}\leq[L:F]\leq p^2.
$$
All inequalities are equalities. Hence $[L:F]=p^2$, the extension is Galois, and
$$
\Gal(L/F)=\langle\tau\rangle
$$
is cyclic of order $p^2$. Since $K\subseteq L$, this is the required cyclic extension.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s2-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s2-5){.pf-ref} construct the required cyclic degree-$p^2$ extension containing $K$.

:::
