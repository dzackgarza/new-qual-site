::: {.problem}
Let $f : A \to B$ and $g : B \to C$ be ring homomorphisms.
Let $\beta _ { 1 } , . . . , \beta _ { m } \in B$ and let $\gamma _ { 1 } , . . . , \gamma _ { n } \in C$

(1) Assume that B, when regarded as a left A-module, is generated by $\beta _ { 1 } , . . . , \beta _ { m }$ and also that C, when regarded as a left B-module, is generated by $\gamma _ { 1 } , . . . , \gamma _ { n } \in$ C .

Show that C, when regarded as a A-module, is generated by $\gamma _ { i } . g ( \beta _ { j } )$ for all $1 \leq i \leq n , 1 \leq j \leq m$

(2) Assume now that B is a free A-module with the $\beta _ { i }$ as basis, and C is a free Bmodule with the $\gamma _ { j }$ as basis.
Show that C is A-free with the given generators in part (1) as basis.
:::

::: {.solution}

Regard $B$ as a left $A$-module through $f$, and regard $C$ as a left $B$-module through $g$ and hence as a left $A$-module through $g\circ f$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The assertion in part (1), with the products ordered as $\gamma_i g(\beta_j)$, is false for arbitrary rings.


::: pf-proof

Let $k$ be a field, let
$$
A=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&b\end{pmatrix}:a,b\in k\right\}
\subseteq
B=M_2(k),
$$
take $f$ to be the inclusion, and set $C=B$ with $g=\operatorname{id}_B$. Put
$$
\beta_1=I,
\qquad
\beta_2=P\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Every matrix in $B$ has a unique expression
$$
\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\z&w\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\0&w\end{pmatrix}\beta_1
+
\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\0&z\end{pmatrix}\beta_2,
$$
so $B$ is free as a left $A$-module with basis $\beta_1,\beta_2$.

Now let
$$
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Since $\gamma$ is invertible, $C=B$ is free as a left $B$-module with basis $\gamma$. The two generators printed in the source are
$$
\gamma\beta_1=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\gamma\beta_2=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
An arbitrary element of their left $A$-span has the form
$$
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&b\end{pmatrix}\gamma\beta_1
+
\begin{pmatrix}c&0\\0&d\end{pmatrix}\gamma\beta_2
=
\begin{pmatrix}a+c&a+c\\d&b\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its two entries in the first row are equal. Hence, for example,
$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
$$
does not belong to this span. Thus the printed generators need not generate $C$ as a left $A$-module.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

With the multiplication order corrected to
$$
g(\beta_j)\gamma_i,
$$
the conclusion of part (1) is true.


::: pf-proof

Let $c\in C$. Since $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n$ generate $C$ as a left $B$-module, there are $b_1,\ldots,b_n\in B$ such that
$$
c=\sum_{i=1}^n g(b_i)\gamma_i.
$$
Since $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m$ generate $B$ as a left $A$-module, write
$$
b_i=\sum_{j=1}^m f(a_{ij})\beta_j
$$
with $a_{ij}\in A$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
c
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m
g\bigl(f(a_{ij})\beta_j\bigr)\gamma_i\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m
g(f(a_{ij}))\,g(\beta_j)\gamma_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is an $A$-linear combination of the elements $g(\beta_j)\gamma_i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Under the freeness hypotheses in part (2), the corrected generators
$$
g(\beta_j)\gamma_i
$$
are $A$-linearly independent.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m
g(f(a_{ij}))\,g(\beta_j)\gamma_i=0
$$
for some $a_{ij}\in A$. Regrouping by $i$ gives
$$
\sum_{i=1}^n
g\left(\sum_{j=1}^m f(a_{ij})\beta_j\right)\gamma_i=0.
$$
Because $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n$ form a left $B$-basis of $C$, for each $i$ one has
$$
\sum_{j=1}^m f(a_{ij})\beta_j=0.
$$
Because $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m$ form a left $A$-basis of $B$, it follows that
$$
a_{ij}=0
$$
for every $i,j$. Thus the corrected generators are $A$-linearly independent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Under the hypotheses of part (2), the corrected family
$$
\boxed{g(\beta_j)\gamma_i\ :\ 1\leq i\leq n,\ 1\leq j\leq m}
$$
is an $A$-basis of $C$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves that the family generates $C$ as a left $A$-module, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves that it is $A$-linearly independent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

If every $g(\beta_j)$ commutes with every $\gamma_i$—in particular, if $C$ is commutative—then the source's displayed family $\gamma_i g(\beta_j)$ also has the conclusions claimed in parts (1) and (2).


::: pf-proof

Under this additional hypothesis,
$$
\gamma_i g(\beta_j)=g(\beta_j)\gamma_i
$$
for every $i,j$. The claim therefore follows from steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that the printed statement is false without an additional commutativity hypothesis. Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} prove the left-module statement with the multiplication order corrected, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} identifies a sufficient hypothesis under which the printed order is valid.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark}
The retained source page prints the generators as $\gamma_i g(\beta_j)$ while explicitly using left $A$- and left $B$-module structures. For left modules over arbitrary rings, the order forced by the scalar actions is $g(\beta_j)\gamma_i$. The two orders agree when the relevant elements commute, for example when $C$ is commutative.
:::
