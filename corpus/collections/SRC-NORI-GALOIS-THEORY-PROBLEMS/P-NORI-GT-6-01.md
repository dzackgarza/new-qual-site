::: {.problem}
Let $f \colon A \to B$ and $g \colon B \to C$ be ring homomorphisms. Let $\beta_{1}, \ldots, \beta_{m} \in B$ and let $\gamma_{1}, \ldots, \gamma_{n} \in C$.

(a) Assume that $B$, when regarded as a left $A$-module, is generated by $\beta_{1}, \ldots, \beta_{m}$, and also that $C$, when regarded as a left $B$-module, is generated by $\gamma_{1}, \ldots, \gamma_{n} \in C$.

Show that $C$, when regarded as an $A$-module, is generated by $\gamma_{i} \cdot g(\beta_{j})$ for all $1 \leq i \leq n$, $1 \leq j \leq m$.

(b) Assume now that $B$ is a free $A$-module with the $\beta_{i}$ as basis, and $C$ is a free $B$-module with the $\gamma_{j}$ as basis. Show that $C$ is $A$-free with the given generators in part (a) as basis.
:::

::: {.solution}

Regard $B$ as a left $A$-module through $f$, and regard $C$ as a left $B$-module through $g$ and hence as a left $A$-module through $g\circ f$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The elements $g(\beta_j)\gamma_i$ generate $C$ as a left $A$-module.


::: pf-proof

Let $c\in C$. Since $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n$ generate $C$ as a left $B$-module, there are $b_1,\ldots,b_n\in B$ such that
$$
c=\sum_{i=1}^n g(b_i)\gamma_i.
$$
Since $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m$ generate $B$ as a left $A$-module, write
$$
b_i=\sum_{j=1}^m f(a_{ij})\beta_j
$$
with $a_{ij}\in A$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
c
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m
g\bigl(f(a_{ij})\beta_j\bigr)\gamma_i\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m
g(f(a_{ij}))\,g(\beta_j)\gamma_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is an $A$-linear combination of the elements $g(\beta_j)\gamma_i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Under the freeness hypotheses in part (b), the elements
$$
g(\beta_j)\gamma_i
$$
are $A$-linearly independent.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m
g(f(a_{ij}))\,g(\beta_j)\gamma_i=0
$$
for some $a_{ij}\in A$. Regrouping by $i$ gives
$$
\sum_{i=1}^n
g\left(\sum_{j=1}^m f(a_{ij})\beta_j\right)\gamma_i=0.
$$
Because $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n$ form a left $B$-basis of $C$, for each $i$ one has
$$
\sum_{j=1}^m f(a_{ij})\beta_j=0.
$$
Because $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m$ form a left $A$-basis of $B$, it follows that
$$
a_{ij}=0
$$
for every $i,j$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Under the hypotheses of part (b), the family
$$
\boxed{g(\beta_j)\gamma_i\ :\ 1\leq i\leq n,\ 1\leq j\leq m}
$$
is an $A$-basis of $C$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves that the family generates $C$ as a left $A$-module, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} proves that it is $A$-linearly independent.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} proves part (a), and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (b).

:::
