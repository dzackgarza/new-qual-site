::: {.problem}
Let $K/F$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G$.
Assume that $K$ contains a primitive $p$-th root of unity.
Let $a \in K^\times$, and let $L$ be the field obtained from $K$ by adjoining $p$-th roots of $\sigma(a)$ for all $\sigma \in G$.

If (i) $\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$ is Abelian, and (ii) $F$ contains a primitive $p$-th root of unity, show that $a^{-1}\sigma(a) \in (K^\times)^p$ for every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Choose a primitive $p$-th root of unity $\zeta\in F$ and choose $b\in L$ with $b^p=a$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The extension $L/F$ is finite Galois, and every $\sigma\in G$ extends to some
$$
\widetilde\sigma\in\Gal(L/F).
$$


::: pf-proof

The extension $L/K$ is generated by finitely many roots of the polynomials
$$
X^p-\tau(a),\qquad \tau\in G,
$$
so $L/F$ is finite. Since $K$ contains all $p$-th roots of unity, adjoining one $p$-th root of each $\tau(a)$ adjoins every root of each of these polynomials.

Let $\iota:L\to\overline F$ be an $F$-embedding. Because $K/F$ is normal, $\iota(K)=K$, so $\iota\restrict_K=\rho$ for some $\rho\in G$. If $u^p=\tau(a)$, then
$$
\iota(u)^p
=\iota(\tau(a))
=\rho\tau(a).
$$
Thus $\iota(u)$ is a $p$-th root of another element of the form $\gamma(a)$ with $\gamma\in G$, and therefore belongs to $L$. Hence $\iota(L)\subseteq L$. Since $L/F$ is finite, $\iota(L)=L$, so $L/F$ is normal. The existence of a primitive $p$-th root of unity implies $\operatorname{char}F\neq p$, and the generators are separable over $K$; together with the separability of $K/F$, this shows that $L/F$ is separable. Hence $L/F$ is Galois.

The restriction map
$$
\Gal(L/F)\longrightarrow\Gal(K/F)=G
$$
is surjective for a finite Galois extension, so every $\sigma\in G$ has an extension $\widetilde\sigma\in\Gal(L/F)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Fix $\sigma\in G$ and an extension $\widetilde\sigma\in\Gal(L/F)$. Then
$$
c\coloneqq\frac{\widetilde\sigma(b)}{b}
$$
is fixed by every element of $\Gal(L/K)$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\tau\in\Gal(L/K)$. Since $b^p=a\in K$ and $K$ contains all $p$-th roots of unity, there is an integer $j$ such that
$$
\tau(b)=\zeta^j b.
$$
By hypothesis, $\Gal(L/F)$ is Abelian, so $\tau\widetilde\sigma=\widetilde\sigma\tau$. Moreover, $\widetilde\sigma$ fixes $\zeta\in F$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau(c)
&=\frac{\tau\widetilde\sigma(b)}{\tau(b)}\\
&=\frac{\widetilde\sigma\tau(b)}{\zeta^j b}\\
&=\frac{\widetilde\sigma(\zeta^j b)}{\zeta^j b}\\
&=\frac{\zeta^j\widetilde\sigma(b)}{\zeta^j b}\\
&=c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every element of $\Gal(L/K)$ fixes $c$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The element $c$ lies in $K^\times$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $L/K$ is Galois because $L/F$ is Galois and $K$ is an intermediate field fixed by the subgroup $\Gal(L/K)$. By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory,
$$
L^{\Gal(L/K)}=K.
$$
Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} shows that $c$ belongs to this fixed field. Since $b$ and $\widetilde\sigma(b)$ are nonzero, $c\in K^\times$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

One has
$$
\boxed{a^{-1}\sigma(a)=c^p\in(K^\times)^p}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using $b^p=a$ and the fact that $\widetilde\sigma\restrict_K=\sigma$,
$$
c^p
=\frac{\widetilde\sigma(b)^p}{b^p}
=\frac{\widetilde\sigma(b^p)}{a}
=\frac{\sigma(a)}{a}
=a^{-1}\sigma(a).
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $c\in K^\times$, so this quotient is a $p$-th power in $K^\times$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

The element $\sigma\in G$ was arbitrary, so step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} proves the assertion for every $\sigma\in\Gal(K/F)$.

:::
