::: {.problem}
With the assumptions on $p$ and $F$ as in the previous problem, let $a_{1}, \ldots, a_{r} \in F^{\times}$.

(i) Show that $\deg\bigl(F(a_{1}^{1/p}, a_{2}^{1/p}, \ldots, a_{r}^{1/p})/F\bigr) = p^{s}$ where $0 \leq s \leq r$.

(ii) Show that the order of the subgroup of $F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{p}$ generated by the images of $a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{r}$ is $p^{s}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

For $0\leq i\leq r$, set
$$
E_i=F(a_1^{1/p},\ldots,a_i^{1/p}),
$$
with $E_0=F$, and let
$$
V_i=\left\langle [a_1],\ldots,[a_i]\right\rangle
\subseteq F^\times/(F^\times)^p,
$$
with $V_0=\{1\}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every $i$, the kernel of
$$
F^\times/(F^\times)^p\longrightarrow E_i^\times/(E_i^\times)^p
$$
is exactly $V_i$.


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The claim holds for $i=0$.


::: pf-proof

For $i=0$, the map is the identity on $F^\times/(F^\times)^p$, so its kernel is trivial, which is $V_0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Assume the claim holds for $i-1$. Then the claim holds for $i$.


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
E_i=E_{i-1}(a_i^{1/p}).
$$
The field $E_{i-1}$ contains the $p$ distinct $p$-th roots of unity because $F$ does. By Problem 5.6, the kernel of
$$
E_{i-1}^\times/(E_{i-1}^\times)^p
\longrightarrow
E_i^\times/(E_i^\times)^p
$$
is the cyclic subgroup generated by the image of $a_i$.

Let $x\in F^\times$. Its class becomes trivial in $E_i^\times/(E_i^\times)^p$ if and only if its image in $E_{i-1}^\times/(E_{i-1}^\times)^p$ is a power of the image of $a_i$. Equivalently, for some integer $j$,
$$
[x a_i^{-j}]
$$
maps trivially to $E_{i-1}^\times/(E_{i-1}^\times)^p$. By the induction hypothesis, this is equivalent to
$$
[x a_i^{-j}]\in V_{i-1},
$$
which is equivalent to
$$
[x]\in\left\langle V_{i-1},[a_i]\right\rangle=V_i.
$$
Thus the kernel is $V_i$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} prove the claim for all $0\leq i\leq r$ by induction.

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For each $1\leq i\leq r$,
$$
[E_i:E_{i-1}]\in\{1,p\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The field $E_i$ is obtained from $E_{i-1}$ by adjoining a root of
$$
X^p-a_i.
$$
Because $E_{i-1}$ contains all $p$-th roots of unity, if $a_i$ is a $p$-th power in $E_{i-1}$ then every root already lies in $E_{i-1}$ and the degree is $1$.

Otherwise, exactly as in Problem 5.6, $E_i/E_{i-1}$ is a nontrivial Galois extension whose Galois group injects into the group $\mu_p$ of order $p$. Hence the degree is $p$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

One has
$$
[E_i:E_{i-1}]=1
$$
if and only if
$$
[a_i]\in V_{i-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the degree is $1$ exactly when $a_i$ becomes a $p$-th power in $E_{i-1}$. By step [](#s1){.pf-ref} applied to $i-1$, this happens exactly when the class $[a_i]$ belongs to the kernel
$$
F^\times/(F^\times)^p\longrightarrow E_{i-1}^\times/(E_{i-1}^\times)^p,
$$
which is $V_{i-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Let $s$ be the number of indices $i$ for which $[a_i]\notin V_{i-1}$. Then
$$
\boxed{[E_r:F]=p^s},
\qquad 0\leq s\leq r.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s3){.pf-ref} and step [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the factor $[E_i:E_{i-1}]$ equals $p$ precisely for those indices counted by $s$, and equals $1$ for all other indices. Therefore the tower law gives
$$
[E_r:F]
=\prod_{i=1}^r [E_i:E_{i-1}]
=p^s.
$$
Since $s$ counts a subset of the $r$ indices, $0\leq s\leq r$. This proves part (i).

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

For each $i$, the order of $V_i$ is multiplied by $p$ exactly when $[a_i]\notin V_{i-1}$.


::: pf-proof

Every element of $F^\times/(F^\times)^p$ has order dividing $p$, because
$$
[x]^p=[x^p]=1.
$$
Thus if $[a_i]\in V_{i-1}$, then $V_i=V_{i-1}$ and the order does not change.

If $[a_i]\notin V_{i-1}$, then the cosets
$$
V_{i-1},\quad [a_i]V_{i-1},\quad\ldots,\quad [a_i]^{p-1}V_{i-1}
$$
are distinct. Indeed, equality of two such cosets would imply $[a_i]^m\in V_{i-1}$ for some $1\leq m<p$; since multiplication by $m$ is invertible modulo $p$, this would force $[a_i]\in V_{i-1}$, a contradiction. Therefore
$$
\abs{V_i}=p\abs{V_{i-1}}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

The subgroup generated by the images of $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ has order
$$
\boxed{\abs{V_r}=p^s}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Starting from $\abs{V_0}=1$, step [](#s5){.pf-ref} shows that the order is multiplied by $p$ exactly for the same $s$ indices counted in step [](#s4){.pf-ref}. Hence
$$
\abs{V_r}=p^s.
$$
This proves part (ii).

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s6){.pf-ref} prove parts (i) and (ii), respectively.
