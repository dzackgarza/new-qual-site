::: {.problem}
Assume that $F$ contains $p$ distinct $p$-th roots of unity, where $p$ is a prime.

Let $a \in F^{\times}$ and let $E = F(a^{1/p})$. Show that the kernel of the natural homomorphism $F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{p} \to E^{\times}/(E^{\times})^{p}$ is the cyclic subgroup generated by the image of $a$ in $F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{p}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Choose $b\in E$ with $b^p=a$, and let
$$
\varphi:F^\times/(F^\times)^p\longrightarrow E^\times/(E^\times)^p
$$
be the map induced by the inclusion $F^\times\subseteq E^\times$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The cyclic subgroup generated by the class of $a$ is contained in $\ker\varphi$.


::: pf-proof

In $E$ one has $a=b^p$, so the image of $a$ in $E^\times/(E^\times)^p$ is trivial. Therefore every power of the class of $a$ lies in $\ker\varphi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $a\in(F^\times)^p$, then $E=F$ and the theorem holds.


::: pf-proof

If $a=d^p$ for some $d\in F^\times$, then every chosen $p$-th root $b$ differs from $d$ by a $p$-th root of unity. All $p$-th roots of unity lie in $F$ by hypothesis, so $b\in F$ and hence $E=F$. Thus $\varphi$ is the identity map and its kernel is trivial. The class of $a$ is also trivial in $F^\times/(F^\times)^p$, so the subgroup it generates is trivial as well.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Assume $a\notin(F^\times)^p$. Then $E/F$ is cyclic Galois of degree $p$, and for a primitive $p$-th root of unity $\zeta\in F$ there is a generator
$$
\sigma\in\Gal(E/F)
$$
with
$$
\sigma(b)=\zeta b.
$$


::: pf-proof

The roots of $X^p-a$ are $\zeta^j b$ for $0\leq j<p$, and they all lie in $E$ because the $p$-th roots of unity lie in $F$. The hypothesis that there are $p$ distinct $p$-th roots of unity implies $\operatorname{char}F\neq p$, so $X^p-a$ is separable. Hence $E/F$ is Galois.

Every $F$-automorphism of $E$ sends $b$ to $\zeta^j b$, giving an injection
$$
\Gal(E/F)\hookrightarrow\mu_p.
$$
Because $a$ is not a $p$-th power in $F$, one has $b\notin F$, so $E\neq F$. Thus $\Gal(E/F)$ is a nontrivial subgroup of the group $\mu_p$ of prime order $p$. Consequently $[E:F]=p$, and the injection is an isomorphism. In particular there is an automorphism $\sigma$ satisfying $\sigma(b)=\zeta b$, and it generates $\Gal(E/F)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Suppose $x\in F^\times$ represents a class in $\ker\varphi$. Then there is an integer $j$ with $0\leq j<p$ and an element $f\in F^\times$ such that
$$
x=f^p a^j.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since the class of $x$ lies in $\ker\varphi$, there exists $y\in E^\times$ such that
$$
y^p=x.
$$
Because $x\in F$, step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\left(\frac{\sigma(y)}{y}\right)^p
=\frac{\sigma(y^p)}{y^p}
=\frac{\sigma(x)}{x}
=1.
$$
Hence
$$
\frac{\sigma(y)}{y}=\zeta^j
$$
for a unique integer $j$ with $0\leq j<p$. Since $\sigma(b^j)=\zeta^j b^j$, one obtains
$$
\sigma\left(\frac{y}{b^j}\right)=\frac{y}{b^j}.
$$
By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, the fixed field of the generator $\sigma$ is $F$. Therefore
$$
f\coloneqq\frac{y}{b^j}\in F^\times.
$$
Thus $y=fb^j$, and raising to the $p$-th power gives
$$
x=y^p=f^p b^{jp}=f^p a^j.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Every element of $\ker\varphi$ lies in the cyclic subgroup generated by the class of $a$.


::: pf-proof

If $a\in(F^\times)^p$, this is step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. Otherwise, let the kernel element be represented by $x\in F^\times$. By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
x=f^p a^j
$$
for some $f\in F^\times$ and $0\leq j<p$. Hence the class of $x$ in $F^\times/(F^\times)^p$ equals the $j$-th power of the class of $a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

One has
$$
\boxed{\ker\varphi=\left\langle [a]\right\rangle}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives the inclusion $\left\langle[a]\right\rangle\subseteq\ker\varphi$, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives the reverse inclusion.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is exactly the required description of the kernel.

:::
