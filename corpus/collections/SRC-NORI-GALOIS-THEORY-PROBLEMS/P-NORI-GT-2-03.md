::: {.problem}
Here $(a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}) = (2, 3, 5)$. Let $E = \QQ(\sqrt{a_{1}}, \sqrt{a_{2}}, \sqrt{a_{3}})$. Let $(b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}) \in \{\pm 1\}^{3}$. Prove

(1) There exists a field automorphism $\sigma$ of $E$ such that $\sigma\sqrt{a_{i}} = b_{i}\sqrt{a_{i}}$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$.

Hint: Use the previous problem to do this when $(b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3})$ equals $(1, 1, -1)$, $(1, -1, 1)$, $(-1, 1, 1)$, and then consider the group generated by these three automorphisms.

(2) Deduce that $\QQ(\sqrt{a_{1}} + \sqrt{a_{2}} + \sqrt{a_{3}}) = E$.

Hint: First deduce from part (1) that $\pm\sqrt{a_{1}} \pm \sqrt{a_{2}} \pm \sqrt{a_{3}}$ are all conjugates of each other.
:::

::: {.solution}

Put $E=\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt5)$ and $\alpha=\sqrt2+\sqrt3+\sqrt5$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The classes $[2],[3],[5]$ are independent in $\QQ^\times/(\QQ^\times)^2$, so $[E:\QQ]=8$.


::: pf-proof

If $2^{e_1}3^{e_2}5^{e_3}$ with $e_i\in\{0,1\}$ were a rational square, unique factorization would force every $e_i=0$. The preceding multiquadratic-extension result therefore gives degree $2^3=8$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The eight monomials
$$
(\sqrt2)^{e_1}(\sqrt3)^{e_2}(\sqrt5)^{e_3},
\qquad e_i\in\{0,1\},
$$
form a $\QQ$-basis of $E$.


::: pf-proof

They span because powers of each square root reduce using $\sqrt2^2=2$, $\sqrt3^2=3$, and $\sqrt5^2=5$. There are eight spanning elements, and step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} says the dimension is eight, so they form a basis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For every $(b_1,b_2,b_3)\in\{\pm1\}^3$, there is a $\QQ$-automorphism $\sigma_b$ of $E$ with
$$
\sigma_b(\sqrt2)=b_1\sqrt2,
\quad
\sigma_b(\sqrt3)=b_2\sqrt3,
\quad
\sigma_b(\sqrt5)=b_3\sqrt5.
$$


::: pf-proof

On the basis in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} define
$$
\sigma_b\!\left((\sqrt2)^{e_1}(\sqrt3)^{e_2}(\sqrt5)^{e_3}\right)
=b_1^{e_1}b_2^{e_2}b_3^{e_3}
(\sqrt2)^{e_1}(\sqrt3)^{e_2}(\sqrt5)^{e_3},
$$
and extend $\QQ$-linearly. This respects multiplication because it preserves the three defining square relations. Moreover $\sigma_b^2=\mathrm{id}$, so it is an automorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The eight images of $\alpha$ under these automorphisms are
$$
b_1\sqrt2+b_2\sqrt3+b_3\sqrt5,
\qquad (b_1,b_2,b_3)\in\{\pm1\}^3,
$$
and they are pairwise distinct.


::: pf-proof

The displayed formula follows from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}. If two such sums were equal, subtraction would give a nontrivial $\QQ$-linear relation among $\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt5$. These are three distinct members of the basis in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, so no such relation exists.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Let $m_\alpha(T)\in\QQ[T]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$. Every number in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} is a root of $m_\alpha$.


::: pf-proof

Since $m_\alpha(\alpha)=0$, applying any $\QQ$-automorphism $\sigma_b$ gives
$$
0=\sigma_b(m_\alpha(\alpha))
=m_\alpha(\sigma_b(\alpha)).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Hence
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=\deg m_\alpha\ge8.
$$


::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, the polynomial $m_\alpha$ has at least eight distinct roots, so its degree is at least eight.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Since $\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq E$ and $[E:\QQ]=8$, one also has
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]\le8.
$$


::: pf-proof

This follows from the tower law applied to
$$
\QQ\subseteq\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq E.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=8=[E:\QQ],
$$
so
$$
\boxed{\QQ(\sqrt2+\sqrt3+\sqrt5)=E.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} force equality of the degrees. The tower law then gives
$$
[E:\QQ(\alpha)]=1,
$$
hence the two fields are equal.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves part (1), and step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves part (2).

:::
