::: {.problem}
Let $q = p^{k}$ where $p$ is a prime. Let $E$ be a finite field extension of $\FF_{q}$ of degree $n$. Prove that $E$ is a Galois extension of $\FF_{q}$. Define $\sigma(x) = x^{q}$ for all $x \in E$. Prove that $\sigma$ belongs to $\Gal(E/\FF_{q})$, and in fact generates this group.
:::

::: {.solution}

Since
$$
[E:\FF_q]=n,
$$
the finite field $E$ has $q^n$ elements.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Every $x\in E$ satisfies
$$
x^{q^n}=x.
$$


::: pf-proof

For $x=0$ this is immediate. If $x\ne0$, then $x$ lies in the multiplicative group $E^*$ of order $q^n-1$. Lagrange's theorem gives
$$
x^{q^n-1}=1,
$$
so $x^{q^n}=x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The polynomial
$$
X^{q^n}-X\in\FF_q[X]
$$
has exactly the elements of $E$ as its roots and is separable.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives $q^n$ distinct roots in $E$, exactly the degree of the polynomial. Its derivative is
$$
q^nX^{q^n-1}-1=-1
$$
in characteristic $p$, so no root is repeated.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore $E/\FF_q$ is a finite Galois extension.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $E$ is the splitting field over $\FF_q$ of the separable polynomial $X^{q^n}-X$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map
$$
\sigma:E\to E,
\qquad
\sigma(x)=x^q,
$$
is a field automorphism.


::: pf-proof

Since $q=p^k$, Frobenius gives
$$
(x+y)^q=x^q+y^q,
\qquad
(xy)^q=x^qy^q.
$$
Thus $\sigma$ is a field homomorphism. It is injective, hence surjective because $E$ is finite.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The automorphism $\sigma$ fixes $\FF_q$ pointwise.


::: pf-proof

Every $a\in\FF_q$ satisfies $a^q=a$. Hence
$$
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/\FF_q).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

One has
$$
\sigma^n=\operatorname{id}_E.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $x\in E$,
$$
\sigma^n(x)=x^{q^n}=x
$$
by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The order of $\sigma$ is exactly $n$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\sigma^r=\operatorname{id}_E
$$
for some $0<r<n$. Then every $x\in E$ would satisfy
$$
x^{q^r}=x.
$$
Thus the polynomial
$$
X^{q^r}-X
$$
of degree $q^r$ would have all $q^n$ elements of $E$ as roots. Since $q^r<q^n$, this is impossible. Together with step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}, the order is $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The group $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\FF_q)$ has order $n$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, $E/\FF_q$ is finite Galois. Hence the order of its Galois group equals its degree:
$$
\cardinality{\operatorname{Gal}(E/\FF_q)}=[E:\FF_q]=n.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(E/\FF_q)=\langle\sigma\rangle\cong\ZZ/n\ZZ.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The subgroup generated by $\sigma$ has $n$ elements by step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}, while the full Galois group has $n$ elements by step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}. Hence they are equal.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} proves that the extension is Galois, step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} shows that Frobenius lies in the Galois group, and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} proves that it generates the group.

:::
