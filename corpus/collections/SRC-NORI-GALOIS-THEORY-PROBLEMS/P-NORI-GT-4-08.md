::: {.problem}
Let $q = p ^ { k }$ where p is a prime.
Let E be a finite field extension of $\mathbb { F } _ { q }$ of degree n. Prove that E is a Galois extension of $\mathbb { F } _ { q }$ . Define $\sigma ( x ) = x ^ { q }$ for all $x \in E$ . Prove that $\sigma$ belongs to $\operatorname { G a l } ( E / \mathbb { F } _ { q } )$ , and in fact generates this group.
:::

::: {.solution}
Since
$$
[E:\FF_q]=n,
$$
the finite field $E$ has $q^n$ elements.

<1>1. Every $x\in E$ satisfies
$$
x^{q^n}=x.
$$

::: {.proof}
For $x=0$ this is immediate. If $x\ne0$, then $x$ lies in the multiplicative group $E^*$ of order $q^n-1$. Lagrange's theorem gives
$$
x^{q^n-1}=1,
$$
so $x^{q^n}=x$.
:::

<1>2. The polynomial
$$
X^{q^n}-X\in\FF_q[X]
$$
has exactly the elements of $E$ as its roots and is separable.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives $q^n$ distinct roots in $E$, exactly the degree of the polynomial. Its derivative is
$$
q^nX^{q^n-1}-1=-1
$$
in characteristic $p$, so no root is repeated.
:::

<1>3. Therefore $E/\FF_q$ is a finite Galois extension.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $E$ is the splitting field over $\FF_q$ of the separable polynomial $X^{q^n}-X$.
:::

<1>4. The map
$$
\sigma:E\to E,
\qquad
\sigma(x)=x^q,
$$
is a field automorphism.

::: {.proof}
Since $q=p^k$, Frobenius gives
$$
(x+y)^q=x^q+y^q,
\qquad
(xy)^q=x^qy^q.
$$
Thus $\sigma$ is a field homomorphism. It is injective, hence surjective because $E$ is finite.
:::

<1>5. The automorphism $\sigma$ fixes $\FF_q$ pointwise.

::: {.proof}
Every $a\in\FF_q$ satisfies $a^q=a$. Hence
$$
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/\FF_q).
$$
:::

<1>6. One has
$$
\sigma^n=\operatorname{id}_E.
$$

::: {.proof}
For every $x\in E$,
$$
\sigma^n(x)=x^{q^n}=x
$$
by step <1>1.
:::

<1>7. The order of $\sigma$ is exactly $n$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose
$$
\sigma^r=\operatorname{id}_E
$$
for some $0<r<n$. Then every $x\in E$ would satisfy
$$
x^{q^r}=x.
$$
Thus the polynomial
$$
X^{q^r}-X
$$
of degree $q^r$ would have all $q^n$ elements of $E$ as roots. Since $q^r<q^n$, this is impossible. Together with step <1>6, the order is $n$.
:::

<1>8. The group $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\FF_q)$ has order $n$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, $E/\FF_q$ is finite Galois. Hence the order of its Galois group equals its degree:
$$
|\operatorname{Gal}(E/\FF_q)|=[E:\FF_q]=n.
$$
:::

<1>9. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Gal}(E/\FF_q)=\langle\sigma\rangle\cong\ZZ/n\ZZ.
}
$$

::: {.proof}
The subgroup generated by $\sigma$ has $n$ elements by step <1>7, while the full Galois group has $n$ elements by step <1>8. Hence they are equal.
:::

<1>10. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves that the extension is Galois, step <1>5 shows that Frobenius lies in the Galois group, and step <1>9 proves that it generates the group.
:::
