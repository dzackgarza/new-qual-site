::: {.problem}
Calculate $H^{*}(K(\ZZ, 3))$.
:::

::: {.solution}

All cohomology in this problem is with integral coefficients. Write
$$
X=K(\ZZ,3).
$$
The complete integral answer is the Cartan--Serre calculation; an explicit
modern presentation is given in X. Gu, *On the cohomology of the classifying
spaces of projective unitary groups*, Section 2
(arXiv:1612.00506).

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

There is a distinguished free class
$$
x_1\in H^3(X;\ZZ)
$$
whose rational image generates
$$
H^*(X;\QQ)
\cong
\Lambda_{\QQ}(x_1).
$$


::: pf-proof

By definition,
$$
\pi_i(X)
=
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&i=3,\\
0,&i\ne3.
\end{cases}
$$
The fundamental integral class
$$
x_1\in H^3(K(\ZZ,3);\ZZ)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_3(X),\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ
$$
is the class corresponding to the identity of $\ZZ$.

After rationalization,
$$
K(\ZZ,3)_\QQ
\simeq
K(\QQ,3).
$$
The latter has Sullivan model
$$
(\Lambda(x_1),0),
\qquad \gradeddegree{x_1}=3.
$$
Since the generator has odd degree, the free graded-commutative algebra is
exterior. Thus
$$
H^*(X;\QQ)
\cong
\Lambda_{\QQ}(x_1).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For each prime $p$ and each integer $k\ge0$, introduce symbols
$$
x_{p,k},
\qquad
y_{p,k}
$$
of degrees
$$
\gradeddegree{x_{p,k}}
=
2p^{k+1}+1,
\qquad
\gradeddegree{y_{p,k}}
=
2p^{k+1}+2.
$$
For $p=2$, impose
$$
y_{2,0}=x_1^2,
\qquad
y_{2,k}=x_{2,k-1}^2
\quad(k\ge1).
$$


::: pf-proof

These are the standard Cartan--Serre generators in the integral cochain model
for $K(\ZZ,3)$. The degree formulas are those obtained from the
successive mod-$p$ reduced-power operations and their integral Bocksteins.
For $p=2$, the Bockstein classes agree with the indicated squares; in
particular the first $2$-torsion class is
$$
y_{2,0}=x_1^2\in H^6(X;\ZZ).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let $C(3)$ be the graded-commutative differential graded
$\ZZ$-algebra generated by
$$
x_1,
\quad
x_{p,k},
\quad
y_{p,k}
$$
for all primes $p$ and $k\ge0$, subject to the relations in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, with
differential
$$
d x_1=0,
\qquad
d y_{p,k}=0,
\qquad
d x_{p,k}=p y_{p,k},
$$
extended by the graded Leibniz rule. Then
$$
H^*(X;\ZZ)
\cong
H^*(C(3),d).
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the Cartan--Serre integral cochain calculation of the
Eilenberg--Mac Lane space $K(\ZZ,3)$. The explicit DGA above is the
model recalled explicitly in Gu, Section 2, which records the resulting integral
cohomology ring.

Thus the calculation of $H^*(X;\ZZ)$ reduces exactly to taking the
cohomology of the displayed DGA.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Let
$$
I=(i_m<\cdots<i_1)
$$
be a nonempty strictly ordered finite tuple of nonnegative integers. Define
$$
y_{p,I}
\coloneqq
\frac1p
d\bigl(
x_{p,i_m}\cdots x_{p,i_1}
\bigr).
$$
Equivalently,
$$
y_{p,I}
=
\sum_{j=1}^m
(-1)^{\mu_j}
x_{p,i_m}\cdots
y_{p,i_j}
\cdots x_{p,i_1},
$$
with the Koszul sign $(-1)^{\mu_j}$ determined by moving the differential
past the preceding factors. Then $y_{p,I}$ is a cocycle of order dividing
$p$, of degree
$$
\gradeddegree{y_{p,I}}
=
1+
\sum_{j=1}^m
\bigl(2p^{i_j+1}+1\bigr).
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
d x_{p,k}=p y_{p,k},
$$
the Leibniz rule shows that
$$
d(x_{p,i_m}\cdots x_{p,i_1})
$$
is divisible by $p$, giving the displayed integral element $y_{p,I}$.

Since $d^2=0$,
$$
d y_{p,I}=0.
$$
Moreover,
$$
p y_{p,I}
=
d(x_{p,i_m}\cdots x_{p,i_1}),
$$
so its cohomology class is killed by $p$.

The differential raises degree by one, hence
$$
\gradeddegree{y_{p,I}}
=
1+
\sum_j\gradeddegree{x_{p,i_j}},
$$
which is the stated degree formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For each prime $p$, let $R_p$ denote the nonunital graded
$\FF_p$-subalgebra of $H^*(C(3),d)$ generated by all classes
$$
y_{p,I},
$$
and let
$$
\widehat R_p
\coloneqq
\ZZ\oplus R_p
$$
be its unitalization. Then the full integral cohomology ring is
$$
\boxed{
H^*(K(\ZZ,3);\ZZ)
\cong
\frac{
\left(
\bigotimes_{p\ {\rm prime}}
\widehat R_p
\right)
\otimes_{\ZZ}
\ZZ[x_1]
}
{(x_1^2-y_{2,0})}.
}
$$
Here the multiplication in each $R_p$ is the one inherited from the DGA in
step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, so it includes the relations among the multi-index classes
$y_{p,I}$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, every torsion class arises from the cohomology of the
Cartan--Serre DGA. Gu's explicit form of the Cartan--Serre computation
identifies its $p$-primary part with the algebra generated by the cocycles
$y_{p,I}$ from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

Distinct prime-primary torsion factors multiply to zero: if
$$
p\alpha=0,
\qquad
q\beta=0,
\qquad
p\ne q,
$$
then
$$
p(\alpha\beta)=q(\alpha\beta)=0,
$$
and Bézout gives
$$
\alpha\beta=0.
$$
Thus the total torsion algebra is the tensor product of the unitalized
$p$-primary pieces.

The only free positive-degree generator is $x_1$ in degree $3$, and its
square is precisely the first $2$-torsion class
$$
x_1^2=y_{2,0}.
$$
Combining the free generator with all prime-primary pieces gives the boxed
ring.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The first prime-primary classes occur in degrees
$$
\gradeddegree{y_{p,0}}=2p+2,
$$
and $y_{p,0}$ has order $p$. In particular,
$$
x_1^2=y_{2,0}\in H^6(X;\ZZ)
$$
has order $2$, while
$$
y_{3,0}\in H^8(X;\ZZ)
$$
has order $3$.


::: pf-proof

Take $I=(0)$ in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}. Then
$$
\gradeddegree{y_{p,0}}
=
1+(2p+1)
=
2p+2.
$$
The Cartan--Serre calculation identifies $y_{p,0}$ as a nonzero
$p$-torsion class. The special relation
$$
y_{2,0}=x_1^2
$$
comes from step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Rationally, all the torsion disappears and the answer reduces to
$$
\boxed{
H^*(K(\ZZ,3);\QQ)
\cong
\Lambda_{\QQ}(x_1),
\qquad \gradeddegree{x_1}=3.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Tensor the integral calculation in step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} with $\QQ$. Every
$R_p$ vanishes after tensoring with $\QQ$, while
$$
x_1^2=y_{2,0}
$$
becomes zero. Thus only the degree-$3$ generator remains, with square zero,
giving the exterior algebra. This agrees with step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} is the complete integral cohomology-ring calculation; step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}
records its rational reduction.
