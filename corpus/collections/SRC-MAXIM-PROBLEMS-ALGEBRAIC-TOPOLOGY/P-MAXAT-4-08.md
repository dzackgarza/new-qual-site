::: {.problem}
Calculate $\pi _ { 4 } ( S ^ { 3 } ) , \pi _ { 5 } ( S ^ { 3 } )$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
\eta_2:S^3\to S^2
\]
be the Hopf map and let
\[
\eta_3=E\eta_2:S^4\to S^3
\]
be its suspension.

<1>1. The Hopf fibration
\[
S^1\longrightarrow S^3\xrightarrow{\eta_2}S^2
\]
gives isomorphisms
\[
\pi_k(S^3)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_k(S^2)
\qquad(k\geq3).
\]

::: {.proof}
The long exact homotopy sequence contains
\[
\pi_k(S^1)\to\pi_k(S^3)\to\pi_k(S^2)
\to\pi_{k-1}(S^1).
\]
For $k\geq3$, both homotopy groups of $S^1$ appearing here vanish, so the middle map is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>2. The classical Hopf calculation gives
\[
\pi_4(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\]
generated by
\[
\eta_2\circ\eta_3:S^4\to S^2.
\]

::: {.proof}
Freudenthal suspension gives a surjection
\[
E:\pi_3(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z\twoheadrightarrow\pi_4(S^3)
\]
at the edge of the stable range. The suspension of the Hopf generator has order two; equivalently, the kernel is $2\mathbb Z$, as follows from the Hopf invariant calculation for $[\iota_2,\iota_2]=2\eta_2$ modulo the Whitehead-product kernel of suspension. Thus $\eta_3$ generates a group $\mathbb Z/2$. Composing with the Hopf fibration and using step <1>1 identifies its image in $\pi_4(S^2)$ as $\eta_2\eta_3$.
:::

<1>3. Hence
\[
\boxed{\pi_4(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z/2,}
\]
generated by $\eta_3$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 identifies $\pi_4(S^3)$ with $\pi_4(S^2)$, and step <1>2 computes the latter as $\mathbb Z/2$. Under the isomorphism induced by $\eta_2$, the class $\eta_3$ maps to $\eta_2\eta_3$.
:::

<1>4. Freudenthal suspension is an isomorphism
\[
E:\pi_4(S^3)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_5(S^4).
\]

::: {.proof}
The sphere $S^3$ is $2$-connected, so in the notation of Freudenthal one has $r=3$. The suspension map is an isomorphism for
\[
i<2r-1=5.
\]
Taking $i=4$ gives the claimed isomorphism.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\pi_5(S^4)\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\]
generated by
\[
\eta_4=E\eta_3:S^5\to S^4.
\]

::: {.proof}
Apply the isomorphism in step <1>4 to the generator $\eta_3$ from step <1>3.
:::

<1>6. The composite
\[
\eta_3\circ\eta_4:S^5\to S^3
\]
is nonzero and has order two; it generates $\pi_5(S^3)$.

::: {.proof}
This is the next low-dimensional Hopf calculation. The EHP exact sequence in these degrees identifies the kernel and cokernel around
\[
\pi_5(S^3)\xrightarrow{E}\pi_6(S^4)
\]
and shows that the composite of the two suspended Hopf maps survives as the unique nonzero element of $\pi_5(S^3)$, with twice that class zero. Equivalently, the standard low-dimensional EHP computation gives
\[
\pi_5(S^3)=\langle\eta_3\eta_4\mid2\eta_3\eta_4=0\rangle.
\]
:::

<1>7. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\pi_5(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z/2.}
\]

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 exhibits a nonzero generator of order two and states that it generates the entire group.
:::

<1>8. Thus
\[
\boxed{
\pi_4(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\qquad
\pi_5(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z/2.}
\]

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>3 and <1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 gives both requested homotopy groups.
:::
