::: {.problem}
Calculate $\pi _ { 4 } ( S ^ { 3 } ) , \pi _ { 5 } ( S ^ { 3 } )$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
\eta_2:S^3\to S^2
$$
be the Hopf map and let
$$
\eta_3=E\eta_2:S^4\to S^3
$$
be its suspension.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Hopf fibration
$$
S^1\longrightarrow S^3\xrightarrow{\eta_2}S^2
$$
gives isomorphisms
$$
\pi_k(S^3)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_k(S^2)
\qquad(k\geq3).
$$


::: pf-proof

The long exact homotopy sequence contains
$$
\pi_k(S^1)\to\pi_k(S^3)\to\pi_k(S^2)
\to\pi_{k-1}(S^1).
$$
For $k\geq3$, both homotopy groups of $S^1$ appearing here vanish, so the middle map is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The classical Hopf calculation gives
$$
\pi_4(S^2)\cong\ZZ/2,
$$
generated by
$$
\eta_2\circ\eta_3:S^4\to S^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Freudenthal suspension gives a surjection
$$
E:\pi_3(S^2)\cong\ZZ\twoheadrightarrow\pi_4(S^3)
$$
at the edge of the stable range. The suspension of the Hopf generator has order two; equivalently, the kernel is $2\ZZ$, as follows from the Hopf invariant calculation for $[\iota_2,\iota_2]=2\eta_2$ modulo the Whitehead-product kernel of suspension. Thus $\eta_3$ generates a group $\ZZ/2$. Composing with the Hopf fibration and using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} identifies its image in $\pi_4(S^2)$ as $\eta_2\eta_3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Hence
$$
\boxed{\pi_4(S^3)\cong\ZZ/2,}
$$
generated by $\eta_3$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} identifies $\pi_4(S^3)$ with $\pi_4(S^2)$, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} computes the latter as $\ZZ/2$. Under the isomorphism induced by $\eta_2$, the class $\eta_3$ maps to $\eta_2\eta_3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Freudenthal suspension is an isomorphism
$$
E:\pi_4(S^3)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_5(S^4).
$$


::: pf-proof

The sphere $S^3$ is $2$-connected, so in the notation of Freudenthal one has $r=3$. The suspension map is an isomorphism for
$$
i<2r-1=5.
$$
Taking $i=4$ gives the claimed isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\pi_5(S^4)\cong\ZZ/2,
$$
generated by
$$
\eta_4=E\eta_3:S^5\to S^4.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the isomorphism in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} to the generator $\eta_3$ from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The composite
$$
\eta_3\circ\eta_4:S^5\to S^3
$$
is nonzero and has order two; it generates $\pi_5(S^3)$.


::: pf-proof

This is the next low-dimensional Hopf calculation. The EHP exact sequence in these degrees identifies the kernel and cokernel around
$$
\pi_5(S^3)\xrightarrow{E}\pi_6(S^4)
$$
and shows that the composite of the two suspended Hopf maps survives as the unique nonzero element of $\pi_5(S^3)$, with twice that class zero. Equivalently, the standard low-dimensional EHP computation gives
$$
\pi_5(S^3)=\langle\eta_3\eta_4\mid2\eta_3\eta_4=0\rangle.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Consequently
$$
\boxed{\pi_5(S^3)\cong\ZZ/2.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} exhibits a nonzero generator of order two and states that it generates the entire group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Thus
$$
\boxed{
\pi_4(S^3)\cong\ZZ/2,
\qquad
\pi_5(S^3)\cong\ZZ/2.}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives both requested homotopy groups.

:::
