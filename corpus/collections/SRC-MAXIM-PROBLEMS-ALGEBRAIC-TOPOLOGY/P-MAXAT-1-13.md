::: {.problem}
Show that $\mathbb { R P } ^ { 2 n } , \mathbb { C P } ^ { 2 n }$ are fixed-point free spaces.
:::

::: {.solution}
The source phrase is interpreted as saying that these spaces admit no fixed-point-free self-map, i.e. they have the fixed-point property. This is proved by the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem.

<1>1. Let
\[
f:\mathbb{RP}^{2n}\to\mathbb{RP}^{2n}
\]
be any continuous map. With rational coefficients,
\[
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^{2n};\mathbb Q)=0
\quad(k>0),
\qquad
H_0(\mathbb{RP}^{2n};\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q.
\]

::: {.proof}
The integral homology of $\mathbb{RP}^{2n}$ has only $2$-torsion in positive degrees below the top, and the top integral homology vanishes because $\mathbb{RP}^{2n}$ is nonorientable. Tensoring with $\mathbb Q$ kills all positive-degree homology. Connectedness gives $H_0\cong\mathbb Q$.
:::

<1>2. Hence
\[
L(f)=1.
\]

::: {.proof}
The Lefschetz number is
\[
L(f)=\sum_k(-1)^k\operatorname{tr}
\bigl(f_*:H_k(\mathbb{RP}^{2n};\mathbb Q)\to
H_k(\mathbb{RP}^{2n};\mathbb Q)\bigr).
\]
By step <1>1 only degree $0$ contributes, and every self-map of a connected space induces the identity on $H_0$.
:::

<1>3. Therefore every self-map of $\mathbb{RP}^{2n}$ has a fixed point.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, $L(f)=1\ne0$. The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem therefore implies that $f$ has a fixed point.
:::

<1>4. Let
\[
g:\mathbb{CP}^{2n}\to\mathbb{CP}^{2n}
\]
be any continuous map, and let $u\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^{2n};\mathbb Z)$ be the standard generator. There is an integer $d$ such that
\[
g^*u=du.
\]

::: {.proof}
The group $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^{2n};\mathbb Z)$ is infinite cyclic generated by $u$, so every endomorphism is multiplication by a unique integer $d$.
:::

<1>5. On $H^{2k}(\mathbb{CP}^{2n};\mathbb Q)$, the map $g^*$ is multiplication by $d^k$.

::: {.proof}
The cohomology ring is
\[
H^*(\mathbb{CP}^{2n};\mathbb Z)
\cong\mathbb Z[u]/(u^{2n+1}),
\qquad |u|=2.
\]
Thus $u^k$ generates $H^{2k}$ and
\[
g^*(u^k)=(g^*u)^k=(du)^k=d^ku^k.
\]
:::

<1>6. Consequently
\[
L(g)=1+d+d^2+\cdots+d^{2n}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Complex projective space has one-dimensional rational cohomology in even degrees $0,2,\ldots,4n$ and zero cohomology in odd degrees. By step <1>5 the traces in those degrees are $1,d,\ldots,d^{2n}$, and every sign in the Lefschetz sum is positive.
:::

<1>7. For every integer $d$,
\[
1+d+d^2+\cdots+d^{2n}>0.
\]

::: {.proof}
If $d\geq0$, this is immediate. If $d<0$, write $d=-a$ with $a\geq1$. Then
\[
1-a+a^2-a^3+\cdots-a^{2n-1}+a^{2n}
=1+(a^2-a)+(a^4-a^3)+\cdots+(a^{2n}-a^{2n-1}),
\]
and every parenthesized term is $a^{2j-1}(a-1)\geq0$. Thus the sum is at least $1$.
:::

<1>8. Therefore every self-map of $\mathbb{CP}^{2n}$ has a fixed point.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>6--<1>7 give $L(g)\ne0$, so the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem applies.
:::

<1>9. Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb{RP}^{2n}\text{ and }\mathbb{CP}^{2n}
\text{ admit no fixed-point-free self-map}.}
\]

::: {.proof}
This combines steps <1>3 and <1>8.
:::

<1>10. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>9 is the asserted fixed-point property.
:::
