::: {.problem}
Show that $\mathbb { C P } ^ { 2 } \# \mathbb { C P } ^ { 2 }$ cannot be the boundary of an orientable 5-manifold.
However, it is the boundary of a non-orientable manifold.
:::

::: {.solution}

Put
\[
M=\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2
\]
with the connected-sum orientation.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The intersection form of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the one-dimensional form $(1)$, so
\[
\operatorname{sign}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=1.
\]


::: pf-proof

The group $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\ZZ)$ is generated by the hyperplane class $h$, and
\[
\langle h^2,[\mathbb{CP}^2]\rangle=1.
\]
Thus the middle-dimensional intersection matrix in the basis $h$ is $(1)$, which has one positive and no negative eigenvalues.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Signature is additive under oriented connected sum, hence
\[
\operatorname{sign}(M)=2.
\]


::: pf-proof

For closed oriented $4$-manifolds $X,Y$, the middle homology of $X\#Y$ is the direct sum of those of $X$ and $Y$, and its intersection form is the orthogonal direct sum
\[
Q_{X\#Y}\cong Q_X\oplus Q_Y.
\]
Therefore signatures add. Apply step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} to the two summands.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The manifold $M$ cannot be the boundary of a compact orientable $5$-manifold.


::: pf-proof

The preceding problem proves that every closed oriented $4n$-manifold which bounds a compact oriented $(4n+1)$-manifold has signature zero. Here $n=1$, while step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives signature $2\ne0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For every closed manifold $X$, the disjoint union $X\sqcup X$ is an unoriented boundary.


::: pf-proof

The product $X\times[0,1]$ is a compact manifold with
\[
\partial(X\times[0,1])
=X\times\{0\}\sqcup X\times\{1\}.
\]
After forgetting orientations, both boundary components are copies of $X$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The disjoint union $X\sqcup Y$ is unoriented cobordant to the connected sum $X\#Y$.


::: pf-proof

Start with $(X\sqcup Y)\times[0,1]$. Attach a $1$-handle $D^1\times D^{d}$ to the top boundary, with its two attaching $d$-balls lying respectively in $X\times\{1\}$ and $Y\times\{1\}$, where $d=\dim X=\dim Y$. The new top boundary is obtained by deleting one open $d$-ball from each of $X$ and $Y$ and joining the resulting boundary spheres by $S^{d-1}\times[0,1]$; this is precisely $X\#Y$. The bottom boundary remains $X\sqcup Y$. Thus the resulting $(d+1)$-manifold is a cobordism between them.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Consequently $\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ is an unoriented boundary.


::: pf-proof

Take $X=\mathbb{CP}^2$. By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $X\sqcup X$ bounds unorientedly. By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} there is an unoriented cobordism from $X\sqcup X$ to $X\#X$. Glue this cobordism to $X\times[0,1]$ along the two copies of $X$. The resulting compact $5$-manifold has sole boundary $X\#X=M$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Every compact $5$-manifold whose boundary is $M$ is non-orientable.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} supplies a compact bounding $5$-manifold. If that manifold were orientable, its boundary orientation would make $M$ an oriented boundary, contradicting step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}. Hence the bounding manifold constructed in step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} is necessarily non-orientable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2
\text{ bounds non-orientably but not orientably}.}
\]


::: pf-proof

The nonexistence of an orientable bounding manifold is step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}, and existence of a non-orientable one is steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} proves both assertions.

:::
