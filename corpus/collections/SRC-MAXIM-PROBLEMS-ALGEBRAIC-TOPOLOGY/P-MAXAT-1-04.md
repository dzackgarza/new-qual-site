::: {.problem}
Show that $\mathbb { C P } ^ { 2 } \# \mathbb { C P } ^ { 2 }$ cannot be the boundary of an orientable 5-manifold.
However, it is the boundary of a non-orientable manifold.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put
\[
M=\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2
\]
with the connected-sum orientation.

<1>1. The intersection form of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the one-dimensional form $(1)$, so
\[
\operatorname{sign}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=1.
\]

::: {.proof}
The group $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb Z)$ is generated by the hyperplane class $h$, and
\[
\langle h^2,[\mathbb{CP}^2]\rangle=1.
\]
Thus the middle-dimensional intersection matrix in the basis $h$ is $(1)$, which has one positive and no negative eigenvalues.
:::

<1>2. Signature is additive under oriented connected sum, hence
\[
\operatorname{sign}(M)=2.
\]

::: {.proof}
For closed oriented $4$-manifolds $X,Y$, the middle homology of $X\#Y$ is the direct sum of those of $X$ and $Y$, and its intersection form is the orthogonal direct sum
\[
Q_{X\#Y}\cong Q_X\oplus Q_Y.
\]
Therefore signatures add. Apply step <1>1 to the two summands.
:::

<1>3. The manifold $M$ cannot be the boundary of a compact orientable $5$-manifold.

::: {.proof}
The preceding problem proves that every closed oriented $4n$-manifold which bounds a compact oriented $(4n+1)$-manifold has signature zero. Here $n=1$, while step <1>2 gives signature $2\ne0$.
:::

<1>4. For every closed manifold $X$, the disjoint union $X\sqcup X$ is an unoriented boundary.

::: {.proof}
The product $X\times[0,1]$ is a compact manifold with
\[
\partial(X\times[0,1])
=X\times\{0\}\sqcup X\times\{1\}.
\]
After forgetting orientations, both boundary components are copies of $X$.
:::

<1>5. The disjoint union $X\sqcup Y$ is unoriented cobordant to the connected sum $X\#Y$.

::: {.proof}
Start with $(X\sqcup Y)\times[0,1]$. Attach a $1$-handle $D^1\times D^{d}$ to the top boundary, with its two attaching $d$-balls lying respectively in $X\times\{1\}$ and $Y\times\{1\}$, where $d=\dim X=\dim Y$. The new top boundary is obtained by deleting one open $d$-ball from each of $X$ and $Y$ and joining the resulting boundary spheres by $S^{d-1}\times[0,1]$; this is precisely $X\#Y$. The bottom boundary remains $X\sqcup Y$. Thus the resulting $(d+1)$-manifold is a cobordism between them.
:::

<1>6. Consequently $\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ is an unoriented boundary.

::: {.proof}
Take $X=\mathbb{CP}^2$. By step <1>4, $X\sqcup X$ bounds unorientedly. By step <1>5 there is an unoriented cobordism from $X\sqcup X$ to $X\#X$. Glue this cobordism to $X\times[0,1]$ along the two copies of $X$. The resulting compact $5$-manifold has sole boundary $X\#X=M$.
:::

<1>7. Every compact $5$-manifold whose boundary is $M$ is non-orientable.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>6 supplies a compact bounding $5$-manifold. If that manifold were orientable, its boundary orientation would make $M$ an oriented boundary, contradicting step <1>3. Hence the bounding manifold constructed in step <1>6 is necessarily non-orientable.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2
\text{ bounds non-orientably but not orientably}.}
\]

::: {.proof}
The nonexistence of an orientable bounding manifold is step <1>3, and existence of a non-orientable one is steps <1>6--<1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 proves both assertions.
:::
