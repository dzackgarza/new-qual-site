::: {.problem}
a. Carefully state the definition of **Noetherian** for a commutative ring $R$.

b. Let $R$ be a subset of $\ZZ[x]$ consisting of all polynomials
\[
f(x) = a_ 0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + \cdots + a_nx^n
\]
such that $a_k$ is even for $1\leq k \leq n$.
Show that $R$ is a subring of $\ZZ[x]$.

c. Show that $R$ is not Noetherian.

> *Hint: consider the ideal generated by \( \ts{ 2x^k \st 1\leq k \in \ZZ } \).*
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A commutative ring is Noetherian if every ascending chain of ideals eventually stabilizes.

::: {.proof}
This is the ascending-chain definition of a Noetherian ring; equivalently, every ideal is finitely generated.
:::

<1>2. The set $R=\mathbb Z+2x\mathbb Z[x]$ is a unital subring of $\mathbb Z[x]$.

::: {.proof}
Every element is uniquely written as $a+2xh(x)$ with $a\in\mathbb Z$ and $h\in\mathbb Z[x]$. This set contains $0$ and $1$ and is closed under differences. The product of $a+2xh$ and $b+2xk$ equals
\[
ab+2x(ak+bh+2xhk)\in R.
\]
Thus it is a subring.
:::

<1>3. The ideal $I=(2x,2x^2,2x^3,\ldots)_R$ is not finitely generated.

::: {.proof}
Let $I=2x\mathbb Z[x]$. It is an ideal of $R$, since multiplication by any member of $R$ preserves the property of having zero constant term and even remaining coefficients. Each $2x^k$ belongs to $I$, and every element of $I$ is a finite integer linear combination of these monomials, so the displayed generators give the entire ideal.

Suppose $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ generated $I$. Write each $g_i=2xh_i(x)$ with $h_i\in\mathbb Z[x]$, and let $D$ exceed every $\deg g_i$. Reduce coefficients of positive powers of $x$ modulo $4$. Any product
\[
(a+2xk(x))g_i=ag_i+4x^2k(x)h_i(x)
\]
has, modulo $4$, no nonzero coefficient in degrees greater than $D$. The same is therefore true of every element of the ideal generated by the $g_i$. But $2x^{D+1}\in I$ has nonzero coefficient modulo $4$ in degree $D+1$, a contradiction.
:::

<1>4. The ring $R$ is not Noetherian.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 provides an ideal that is not finitely generated, contradicting the equivalent Noetherian condition in step <1>1.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1, <1>2, and <1>4 establish parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.
:::
:::
