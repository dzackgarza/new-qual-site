::: {.problem}
Let $(X_a)_{a\in A}$ be an arbitrary family of spaces.

1. Define the topology on the Tychonoff product
   $$
   \prod_{a\in A}X_a.
   $$
2. Assume the $X_a$ are pairwise disjoint. Define the topological sum
   $$
   \sum_{a\in A}X_a
   $$
   by declaring $G\subseteq\bigcup_aX_a$ open exactly when $G\cap X_a$ is open in $X_a$ for every $a$. Verify that this is a topology.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Tychonoff product topology on
$$
\prod_{a\in A}X_a
$$
is the topology generated by the subbasic sets
$$
\pi_a^{-1}(U),
$$
where $a\in A$, $U\subseteq X_a$ is open, and
$$
\pi_a:\prod_{b\in A}X_b\to X_a
$$
is the coordinate projection.


::: pf-proof

Equivalently, a basis is given by the sets
$$
\prod_{a\in A}U_a,
$$
where each $U_a$ is open in $X_a$ and
$$
U_a=X_a
$$
for all but finitely many $a$. Indeed, a finite intersection of subbasic inverse images has exactly this form, and these finite intersections form the standard basis generated by a subbasis.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For pairwise disjoint $X_a$, define
$$
\mathcal T
=
\left\{
G\subseteq\bigcup_{a\in A}X_a:
G\cap X_a\text{ is open in }X_a\text{ for every }a\in A
\right\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is the proposed topology on the topological sum
$$
\sum_{a\in A}X_a.
$$
It remains to verify the topology axioms.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

One has
$$
\varnothing\in\mathcal T
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\bigcup_{a\in A}X_a\in\mathcal T.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $a$,
$$
\varnothing\cap X_a=\varnothing,
$$
which is open in $X_a$, and
$$
\left(\bigcup_{b\in A}X_b\right)\cap X_a=X_a,
$$
which is also open in $X_a$. Thus both sets belong to $\mathcal T$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The collection $\mathcal T$ is closed under arbitrary unions.


::: pf-proof

Let $(G_i)_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal T$. For every $a\in A$,
$$
\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}G_i\right)\cap X_a
=
\bigcup_{i\in I}(G_i\cap X_a).
$$
Each $G_i\cap X_a$ is open in $X_a$, so the union is open in $X_a$. Hence
$$
\bigcup_{i\in I}G_i\in\mathcal T.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The collection $\mathcal T$ is closed under finite intersections.


::: pf-proof

If $G,H\in\mathcal T$, then for every $a\in A$,
$$
(G\cap H)\cap X_a
=(G\cap X_a)\cap(H\cap X_a),
$$
which is open in $X_a$. Hence $G\cap H\in\mathcal T$. By induction, the same holds for every finite intersection.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Therefore $\mathcal T$ is a topology, and
$$
\boxed{\sum_{a\in A}X_a}
$$
denotes $\bigcup_{a\in A}X_a$ equipped with this topology.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} verify the three topology axioms.

:::
