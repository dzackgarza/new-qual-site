::: {.problem}
Compute, with proof, the fundamental group of each space:

1. two copies of the cylinder $S^1\times I$ identified at one point;
2. the disk $B^2$ with two points of its boundary identified.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Two copies of $S^1\times I$ identified at one point deformation retract onto a wedge of two circles.


::: pf-proof

Let the identified point in the first cylinder be $(z_1,t_1)$ and in the second be $(z_2,t_2)$. For $i=1,2$, the homotopy
$$
H_i((z,t),s)=\bigl(z,(1-s)t+st_i\bigr)
$$
strongly deformation retracts the $i$-th cylinder onto the circle $S^1\times\{t_i\}$. It fixes $(z_i,t_i)$ throughout. Therefore the two homotopies descend through the one-point identification and give a deformation retraction of the quotient onto
$$
(S^1\times\{t_1\})\vee(S^1\times\{t_2\})\cong S^1\vee S^1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The fundamental group of the first space is
$$
\boxed{\pi_1\cong\ZZ*\ZZ\cong F_2.}
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the space is homotopy equivalent to $S^1\vee S^1$. Applying the Seifert--van Kampen theorem to the wedge of two circles gives
$$
\pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)\cong
\pi_1(S^1)*\pi_1(S^1)
\cong
\ZZ*\ZZ.
$$
Homotopy equivalence preserves the fundamental group.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let $Q$ be the quotient of $B^2$ obtained by identifying two distinct boundary points $p$ and $q$. Then $Q$ has a CW structure with one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell attached along the word $ab$.


::: pf-proof

The points $p$ and $q$ divide $\partial B^2$ into two closed arcs. Orient the first arc from $p$ to $q$ and the second from $q$ back to $p$. After identifying $p\sim q$, their common endpoints become one $0$-cell, and the two arcs become loops $a$ and $b$, hence two $1$-cells. The interior of $B^2$ is one $2$-cell. Traversing its boundary once in the chosen orientation first follows $a$ and then $b$, so its attaching map represents the word $ab$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The fundamental group of $Q$ has presentation
$$
\pi_1(Q)\cong\langle a,b\mid ab=1\rangle.
$$


::: pf-proof

The $1$-skeleton from step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is $S^1\vee S^1$, with fundamental group freely generated by $a$ and $b$. Attaching a $2$-cell along the loop represented by $ab$ quotients this free group by the normal closure of $ab$. Hence
$$
\pi_1(Q)\cong F(a,b)/\langle\!\langle ab\rangle\!\rangle
\cong\langle a,b\mid ab=1\rangle.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The fundamental group of the second space is
$$
\boxed{\pi_1(Q)\cong\ZZ.}
$$


::: pf-proof

In the presentation from step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, the relation $ab=1$ gives $b=a^{-1}$. Eliminating $b$ leaves a presentation with one generator and no relations:
$$
\langle a\rangle\cong\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} computes the first fundamental group, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} computes the second.

:::
