::: problem
For $f\in L^1([0,1])$, set $g(x)=\int_x^1f(t)/t\,dt$.
Prove that $g\in L^1([0,1])$ and that $\int_0^1g=\int_0^1f$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Consider first the nonnegative function
\[
H(x,t)=\mathbf 1_{\{0\le x\le t\le1\}}\frac{|f(t)|}{t}.
\]
By Tonelli's theorem,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\int_x^1\frac{|f(t)|}{t}\,dt\,dx
&=\int_0^1\int_0^t\frac{|f(t)|}{t}\,dx\,dt\\
&=\int_0^1|f(t)|\,dt<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore the defining integral for $g(x)$ converges absolutely for almost every $x$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1|g(x)|\,dx
&\le
\int_0^1\int_x^1\frac{|f(t)|}{t}\,dt\,dx\\
&=\|f\|_1<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence $g\in L^1([0,1])$.

The signed integrand
\[
\mathbf 1_{\{0\le x\le t\le1\}}\frac{f(t)}{t}
\]
is absolutely integrable by the calculation above, so Fubini's theorem gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1g(x)\,dx
&=\int_0^1\int_x^1\frac{f(t)}{t}\,dt\,dx\\
&=\int_0^1\int_0^t\frac{f(t)}{t}\,dx\,dt\\
&=\int_0^1f(t)\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
:::
