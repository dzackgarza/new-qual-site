::: problem
Let $F\subseteq\mathbb R$ be closed and suppose $m(\mathbb R\setminus F)<\infty$.
Set $\delta(x)=d(x,F)$ and $I_F(x)=\int\delta(y)/|x-y|^2\,dy$.

1. Prove that $\delta$ is Lipschitz.

2. Prove that $I_F(x)=\infty$ for $x\notin F$.

3. Prove that $I_F(x)<\infty$ for almost every $x\in F$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. The distance function is $1$-Lipschitz.
::: proof
For any $x,y\in\mathbb R$ and any $z\in F$,
\[
\delta(x)\le |x-z|\le |x-y|+|y-z|.
\]
Taking the infimum over $z\in F$ gives
\[
\delta(x)\le |x-y|+\delta(y).
\]
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ yields
\[
|\delta(x)-\delta(y)|\le |x-y|.
\]
Thus $\delta$ is $1$-Lipschitz, hence continuous.
:::

<1>2. If $x\notin F$, then $I_F(x)=\infty$.
::: proof
Fix $x\notin F$ and put $d=\delta(x)>0$. If $|y-x|<d/2$, then by the Lipschitz estimate,
\[
\delta(y)\ge \delta(x)-|x-y|>\frac d2.
\]
Hence
\[
I_F(x)
\ge \int_{|y-x|<d/2}\frac{\delta(y)}{|x-y|^2}\,dy
\ge \frac d2\int_{|y-x|<d/2}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^2}
=\infty.
\]
:::

<1>3. The integral is finite for almost every point of $F$.
::: proof
Since $\delta(y)=0$ for $y\in F$, Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_F I_F(x)\,dx
&=\int_F\int_{\mathbb R\setminus F}
\frac{\delta(y)}{|x-y|^2}\,dy\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb R\setminus F}\delta(y)
\left(\int_F\frac{dx}{|x-y|^2}\right)dy.
\end{aligned}
\]
Fix $y\notin F$ and write $d=\delta(y)>0$. By definition of distance,
\[
F\subseteq(-\infty,y-d]\cup[y+d,\infty).
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_F\frac{dx}{|x-y|^2}
\le \int_{|x-y|\ge d}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^2}
=\frac2d.
\]
Multiplying by $\delta(y)=d$ gives
\[
\delta(y)\int_F\frac{dx}{|x-y|^2}\le2.
\]
Consequently
\[
\int_F I_F(x)\,dx
\le 2m(\mathbb R\setminus F)<\infty.
\]
Since $I_F\ge0$, a finite integral over $F$ implies
\[
I_F(x)<\infty
\]
for almost every $x\in F$.
:::
