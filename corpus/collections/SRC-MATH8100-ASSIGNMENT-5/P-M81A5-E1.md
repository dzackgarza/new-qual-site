::: problem
Prove that $\int_0^\infty|\sin x/x|\,dx=\infty$.
Use the specified repeated integral with $xe^{-xy}(1-\cos y)$ to prove that $\lim_{A\to\infty}\int_0^A\sin x/x\,dx=\pi/2$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The sine integral is not absolutely convergent.
::: {.proof}
For every integer $k\ge0$, on the interval
\[
I_k=\left[k\pi+\frac\pi6,\;k\pi+\frac{5\pi}6\right]
\]
we have
\[
|\sin x|\ge\frac12.
\]
Also, for $x\in I_k$,
\[
x\le(k+1)\pi.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{I_k}\frac{|\sin x|}{x}\,dx
&\ge
\frac12\cdot\frac{|I_k|}{(k+1)\pi}\\
&=
\frac12\cdot\frac{2\pi/3}{(k+1)\pi}\\
&=\frac1{3(k+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing over $k$ gives a divergent harmonic series. Hence
\[
\int_0^\infty\left|\frac{\sin x}{x}\right|dx=\infty.
\]
:::

<1>2. The improper sine integral converges to $\pi/2$.
::: {.proof}
Consider the nonnegative function
\[
H(x,y)=x e^{-xy}(1-\cos y),
\qquad x,y>0.
\]
Tonelli's theorem allows either order of integration.

For fixed $x>0$,
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-xy}\,dy=\frac1x
\]
and
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-xy}\cos y\,dy=\frac{x}{x^2+1}.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty H(x,y)\,dy
&=x\left(\frac1x-\frac{x}{x^2+1}\right)\\
&=\frac1{x^2+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty H(x,y)\,dy\,dx
=\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{1+x^2}
=\frac\pi2.
\]

On the other hand, for fixed $y>0$,
\[
\int_0^\infty x e^{-xy}\,dx=\frac1{y^2}.
\]
Thus Tonelli also gives
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{1-\cos y}{y^2}\,dy=\frac\pi2.
\]

Now integrate by parts on $[\varepsilon,A]$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_\varepsilon^A\frac{1-\cos y}{y^2}\,dy
&=
\left[-\frac{1-\cos y}{y}\right]_\varepsilon^A
+\int_\varepsilon^A\frac{\sin y}{y}\,dy.
\end{aligned}
\]
As $\varepsilon\to0^+$,
\[
\frac{1-\cos\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\to0,
\]
and as $A\to\infty$,
\[
\frac{1-\cos A}{A}\to0.
\]
Since the nonnegative integral on the left converges to $\pi/2$, it follows that
\[
\lim_{A\to\infty}\int_0^A\frac{\sin y}{y}\,dy
=\frac\pi2.
\]
