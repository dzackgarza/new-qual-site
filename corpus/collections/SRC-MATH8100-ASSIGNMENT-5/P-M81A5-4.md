::: problem
Let $A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be bounded measurable sets of positive measure.
Set $g(t)=m(A\cap(t-B))$.

1. Prove that $g$ is continuous and $\int g=m(A)m(B)$.

2. Deduce that $A+B$ contains a nonempty open set.
:::

::: {.solution}
For every $t\in\mathbb R^n$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
g(t)
&=m(A\cap(t-B))\\
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\mathbf1_A(x)\mathbf1_{t-B}(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\mathbf1_A(x)\mathbf1_B(t-x)\,dx\\
&=(\mathbf1_A*\mathbf1_B)(t).
\end{aligned}
\]

<1>1. The function $g$ is continuous and has integral $m(A)m(B)$.
::: {.proof}
Since $A$ and $B$ are bounded and measurable, they have finite measure, so
\[
\mathbf1_A,\mathbf1_B\in L^1(\mathbb R^n).
\]
For $h\in\mathbb R^n$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|g(t+h)-g(t)|
&\le
\int \mathbf1_A(x)
\left|\mathbf1_B(t+h-x)-\mathbf1_B(t-x)\right|\,dx\\
&\le
\|\mathbf1_B(\cdot+h)-\mathbf1_B\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand side is independent of $t$ and tends to $0$ as $h\to0$ by continuity of translations in $L^1$. Thus $g$ is uniformly continuous.

Since the integrand defining $g$ is nonnegative, Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^n}g(t)\,dt
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
\mathbf1_A(x)\mathbf1_B(t-x)\,dx\,dt\\
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\mathbf1_A(x)
\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}\mathbf1_B(t-x)\,dt\right)dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\mathbf1_A(x)m(B)\,dx\\
&=m(A)m(B).
\end{aligned}
\]
:::

<1>2. The sumset has nonempty interior.
::: {.proof}
Because $m(A)m(B)>0$ and $g\ge0$,
\[
\int g>0,
\]
so $g(t_0)>0$ for some $t_0\in\mathbb R^n$. By continuity, there exists $r>0$ such that
\[
g(t)>0
\qquad\text{for every }t\in B_r(t_0).
\]

If $g(t)>0$, then
\[
m(A\cap(t-B))>0,
\]
so the intersection is nonempty. Choose
\[
a\in A\cap(t-B).
\]
Then $a=t-b$ for some $b\in B$, hence
\[
t=a+b\in A+B.
\]
Therefore
\[
B_r(t_0)\subseteq A+B,
\]
so $A+B$ contains a nonempty open set.
:::
:::
