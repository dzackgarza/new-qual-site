::: problem
For a double sequence $a_{jk}\geq0$, prove that the iterated sum equals the supremum of all finite subsums.
Deduce that
$$
\sum_j\sum_k a_{jk}=\sum_k\sum_j a_{jk}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
S=\sup\left\{\sum_{(j,k)\in B}a_{jk}: B\subseteq\mathbb N\times\mathbb N\text{ finite}\right\}
\in[0,\infty].
\]

For each $j$, set
\[
b_j=\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_{jk}.
\]
We prove
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j=S.
\]

First let $B\subseteq\mathbb N\times\mathbb N$ be finite, and let $J$ be the finite set of first coordinates appearing in $B$. Then
\[
\sum_{(j,k)\in B}a_{jk}
\le
\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_{jk}
\le
\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j.
\]
Taking the supremum over finite $B$ gives
\[
S\le\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j.
\]

Conversely, fix a finite set $J\subseteq\mathbb N$. For every $j\in J$,
\[
b_j
=\sup_{K\subseteq\mathbb N\text{ finite}}\sum_{k\in K}a_{jk}.
\]
Since $J$ is finite, for every $\varepsilon>0$ we may choose finite sets $K_j$ so that
\[
\sum_{k\in K_j}a_{jk}>b_j-\frac\varepsilon{|J|}
\qquad(j\in J),
\]
with the evident interpretation if some $b_j=\infty$. Then
\[
B=\bigcup_{j\in J}\bigl(\{j\}\times K_j\bigr)
\]
is finite and
\[
S\ge\sum_{(j,k)\in B}a_{jk}
>\sum_{j\in J}b_j-\varepsilon.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{j\in J}b_j\le S.
\]
Taking the supremum over finite $J$ yields
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j\le S.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_{jk}=S.
\]

Repeating the same argument with $j$ and $k$ interchanged gives
\[
\sum_{k=1}^\infty\sum_{j=1}^\infty a_{jk}=S.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{\sum_j\sum_k a_{jk}=\sum_k\sum_j a_{jk}.}
\]
:::
