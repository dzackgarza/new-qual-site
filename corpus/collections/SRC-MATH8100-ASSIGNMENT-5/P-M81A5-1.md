::: {.problem}
For a double sequence $a_{jk}\geq0$, prove that the iterated sum equals the supremum of all finite subsums.
Deduce that
$$
\sum_j\sum_k a_{jk}=\sum_k\sum_j a_{jk}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the assignment sheet, so no retained solution set covers the problem and the argument is the card's own; it is the standard comparison of an iterated sum of non-negative terms with the supremum of finite subsums.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #definition}
Let $S=\sup\bigl\{\sum_{(j,k)\in B}a_{jk}:B\subseteq\NN\times\NN\text{ finite}\bigr\}\in[0,\infty]$ and $b_j=\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_{jk}\in[0,\infty]$ for each $j$.

::: pf-proof
Each finite sum is a non-negative real number, so the set of them is a nonempty subset of $[0,\infty)$ and has a supremum in $[0,\infty]$; each $b_j$ is the supremum of the partial sums of a series of non-negative terms, hence lies in $[0,\infty]$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #one-inequality}
$S\le\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j$.

::: pf-proof
Let $B$ be finite and let $J$ be the finite set of first coordinates occurring in $B$. Then
$$
\sum_{(j,k)\in B}a_{jk}\le\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_{jk}\le\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j,
$$
the first inequality because $B$ contains at most all the pairs with first coordinate in $J$ and the terms are non-negative, the second because each $b_j\ge0$ and $J$ is finite. The right-hand side does not depend on $B$, so it bounds the supremum $S$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #other-inequality}
$\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j\le S$.

::: pf-proof
Let $J\subseteq\NN$ be finite and let $\varepsilon>0$. For each $j\in J$, $b_j$ is the supremum of the finite subsums $\sum_{k\in K}a_{jk}$, so there is a finite $K_j\subseteq\NN$ with $\sum_{k\in K_j}a_{jk}>b_j-\varepsilon/\abs J$; if $b_j=\infty$, choose $K_j$ with $\sum_{k\in K_j}a_{jk}>\abs J$ and replace $\varepsilon/\abs J$ by $\abs J$ below. Then $B=\bigcup_{j\in J}\{j\}\times K_j$ is finite and
$$
S\ge\sum_{(j,k)\in B}a_{jk}=\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k\in K_j}a_{jk}>\sum_{j\in J}b_j-\varepsilon,
$$
so $\sum_{j\in J}b_j\le S+\varepsilon$; $\varepsilon>0$ being arbitrary, $\sum_{j\in J}b_j\le S$. If some $b_j=\infty$ the same choice with arbitrarily large bounds gives $S=\infty$, and the inequality holds as well. Taking the supremum over finite $J$ gives $\sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j\le S$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\boxed{\sum_j\sum_k a_{jk}=\sum_k\sum_j a_{jk}=\sup\{\text{finite subsums}\}}$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#one-inequality){.pf-ref} and [](#other-inequality){.pf-ref} give $\sum_j\sum_k a_{jk}=\sum_j b_j=S$; applying the same two steps with the roles of $j$ and $k$ interchanged, which is legitimate because the hypothesis is symmetric in the two indices and the sums are of non-negative terms, gives $\sum_k\sum_j a_{jk}=S$. Hence the two iterated sums are equal, and both equal the supremum of the finite subsums.
:::
