::: problem
(i) State what it means for a covering space to be normal (sometimes this condition is instead called regular).

(ii) Let $\Theta$ be the topological space formed as the union of a circle and a diameter of the circle (so this space looks exactly like the letter $\Theta$). Give an example of a covering space of $\Theta$ which is not normal.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A connected covering map
\[
p:\widetilde X\to X
\]
is **normal** (or **regular**) if its deck transformation group acts transitively on a fiber: for one, equivalently every, \(x\in X\), and for every
\[
\widetilde x_1,\widetilde x_2\in p^{-1}(x),
\]
there is a deck transformation \(h:\widetilde X\to\widetilde X\) such that
\[
h(\widetilde x_1)=\widetilde x_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the deck-transformation definition of a normal covering.
Equivalently, after choosing basepoints and under the usual hypotheses for covering classification,
\[
p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x)
\trianglelefteq
\pi_1(X,x).
\]
:::

<1>2. Regard \(\Theta\) as a graph with two vertices \(u,v\) and three edges
\[
e_0,e_1,e_2
\]
joining \(u\) to \(v\). Define a graph \(\widetilde\Theta\) with vertices
\[
u_1,u_2,u_3,
\qquad
v_1,v_2,v_3,
\]
and with three families of edges as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&1&2&3\\ \hline
e_0&u_1v_1&u_2v_2&u_3v_3\\
e_1&u_1v_2&u_2v_1&u_3v_3\\
e_2&u_1v_1&u_2v_3&u_3v_2.
\end{array}
\]
Map every \(u_i\) to \(u\), every \(v_i\) to \(v\), and every edge in the \(e_j\)-row homeomorphically onto \(e_j\). This defines a three-sheeted covering
\[
p:\widetilde\Theta\to\Theta.
\]
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
\sigma_0=1,
\qquad
\sigma_1=(12),
\qquad
\sigma_2=(23).
\]
The edge over \(e_j\) leaving \(u_i\) ends at \(v_{\sigma_j(i)}\). Thus at each \(u_i\) there is exactly one incident edge lying over each of \(e_0,e_1,e_2\). Since every \(\sigma_j\) is a permutation, the same is true at each \(v_i\). Consequently the star of every vertex maps bijectively to the star of its image, and the interiors of edges map homeomorphically.
This is precisely the local covering condition for graphs.
Each fiber over a vertex has three points, so the covering is three-sheeted.
:::

<1>3. The covering graph \(\widetilde\Theta\) is connected.
::: {.proof}
Starting at \(u_1\), travel along the lift of \(e_0\) to \(v_1\), then backward along the lift of \(e_1\) joining \(u_2\) to \(v_1\). This reaches \(u_2\). Next travel from \(u_2\) along its \(e_2\)-edge to \(v_3\), then backward along the \(e_0\)-edge to \(u_3\). Thus all three vertices \(u_1,u_2,u_3\) lie in one component.
Each \(v_i\) is joined to \(u_i\) by an \(e_0\)-edge, so every vertex lies in that component.
Hence the graph is connected.
:::

<1>4. Every deck transformation of \(p\) is the identity.
::: {.proof}
Let \(h\) be a deck transformation.
Since \(p\circ h=p\), it permutes the three points over \(u\); write
\[
h(u_i)=u_{\tau(i)}
\]
for some \(\tau\in S_3\). The \(e_0\)-edge from \(u_i\) ends at \(v_i\), and a deck transformation must send an edge over \(e_0\) to an edge over \(e_0\). Therefore
\[
h(v_i)=v_{\tau(i)}.
\]

Now consider the \(e_j\)-edge from \(u_i\) to \(v_{\sigma_j(i)}\). Its image under \(h\) has endpoints
\[
u_{\tau(i)}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
v_{\tau\sigma_j(i)}.
\]
But the unique \(e_j\)-edge starting at \(u_{\tau(i)}\) ends at
\[
v_{\sigma_j\tau(i)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\tau\sigma_j=\sigma_j\tau
\qquad(j=1,2).
\]
Thus \(\tau\) commutes with both \((12)\) and \((23)\). These transpositions generate \(S_3\), so \(\tau\) lies in the center of \(S_3\), which is trivial.
Therefore \(\tau=1\). It follows that \(h\) fixes every vertex.
Each lifted edge is the unique edge over its base edge with its prescribed initial vertex, so \(h\) fixes every edge as well.
Hence \(h=\operatorname{id}\).
:::

<1>5. The covering in <1>2 is not normal.
::: {.proof}
By <1>4,
\[
\operatorname{Deck}(p)=\{1\}.
\]
But a fiber, for example
\[
p^{-1}(u)=\{u_1,u_2,u_3\},
\]
has three points.
The trivial deck group cannot act transitively on this fiber.
By the definition in <1>1, \(p\) is not normal.
:::
:::
