::: problem
Let $X$ be a topological space.

(i) State what it means for $X$ to be Hausdorff.

(ii) Prove that every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A topological space \(X\) is **Hausdorff** if for every pair of distinct points \(x,y\in X\), there exist disjoint open sets \(U,V\subseteq X\) such that
\[
x\in U,
\qquad
y\in V.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the Hausdorff, or \(T_2\), separation axiom.
:::

<1>2. Let \(K\subseteq X\) be compact and let \(x\in X\setminus K\). Then there are disjoint open sets \(U,V\subseteq X\) satisfying
\[
K\subseteq U,
\qquad
x\in V.
\]
::: {.proof}
If \(K=\varnothing\), take \(U=\varnothing\) and \(V=X\). Assume \(K\ne\varnothing\).

For each \(y\in K\), the points \(x\) and \(y\) are distinct.
By Hausdorffness, choose disjoint open sets
\[
U_y\ni y,
\qquad
V_y\ni x.
\]
The family \(\{U_y:y\in K\}\) is an open cover of \(K\). Compactness gives points
\[
y_1,\dots,y_m\in K
\]
such that
\[
K\subseteq U_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{y_m}.
\]
Set
\[
U=U_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{y_m},
\qquad
V=V_{y_1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{y_m}.
\]
Then \(U\) and \(V\) are open, \(K\subseteq U\), and \(x\in V\). If \(z\in U\), then \(z\in U_{y_i}\) for some \(i\). Since
\[
V\subseteq V_{y_i}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
U_{y_i}\cap V_{y_i}=\varnothing,
\]
one has \(z\notin V\). Hence
\[
U\cap V=\varnothing.
\]
:::

<1>3. Every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed.
::: {.proof}
Let \(K\subseteq X\) be compact.
For every
\[
x\in X\setminus K,
\]
<1>2 gives an open neighborhood \(V_x\) of \(x\) disjoint from an open set containing \(K\). In particular,
\[
V_x\subseteq X\setminus K.
\]
Thus every point of \(X\setminus K\) has an open neighborhood contained in \(X\setminus K\), so \(X\setminus K\) is open.
Therefore \(K\) is closed.
:::
:::
