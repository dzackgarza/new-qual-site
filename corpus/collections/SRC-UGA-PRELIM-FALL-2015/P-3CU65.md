::: {.problem}
Find the Maclaurin series of $f(x)=\arctan x$.
Use an appropriate Maclaurin polynomial to approximate $\arctan(0.01)$ with error less than $10^{-5}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #series}

For $\abs{x} < 1$,
$$
\boxed{\arctan x = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1} x^{2k+1} = x - \frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{x^5}{5} - \cdots,}
$$
and this is the Maclaurin series of $\arctan$.

::: pf-proof

For $\abs{t} < 1$ the geometric series gives
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\arctan t = \frac{1}{1+t^2} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^k t^{2k}.
$$
For $\abs{x} < 1$ this series converges uniformly on the interval between $0$ and $x$, so it can be integrated term by term from $0$ to $x$. Since $\arctan 0 = 0$, this gives the stated series.
A convergent power series is the Taylor series of its sum at the center, so it is the Maclaurin series of $\arctan$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Maclaurin polynomial $x - \frac{x^3}{3}$ gives
$$
\boxed{\arctan(0.01) \approx 0.01 - \frac{(0.01)^3}{3} = 0.00999966\ldots}
$$
with error at most $\frac{(0.01)^5}{5} = 2 \times 10^{-11} < 10^{-5}$.

::: pf-proof

At $x = 0.01$ the series of step [](#series){.pf-ref} alternates, and its terms decrease to $0$ in absolute value.
By the alternating series estimate, the error after the term $-\frac{x^3}{3}$ is at most the next term, $\frac{x^5}{5}$.
The degree-one polynomial $x$ alone already suffices, with error at most $\frac{(0.01)^3}{3} < 4 \times 10^{-7}$.

:::
