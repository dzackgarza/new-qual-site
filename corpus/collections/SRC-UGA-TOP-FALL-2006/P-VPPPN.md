::: problem
a. State the **Mayer-Vietoris theorem**.

b. Use it to compute the homology of the space $X$ obtained by gluing two solid tori along their boundary as follows.
Let $\DD^2$ be the unit disk and let $S^1$ be the unit circle in the complex plane $\CC$.
Let $A = S^1 \times \DD^2$ and $B = S^1 \times \DD^2$.

Then $X$ is the quotient space of the disjoint union $A \disjoint B$ obtained by identifying $(z, w) \in A$ with $(zw^3 , w) \in B$ for all $(z, w) \in S^1 \times S^1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The Mayer--Vietoris theorem gives the long exact sequence
\[
\cdots
\longrightarrow H_k(U\cap V)
\xrightarrow{(i_*,-j_*)}
H_k(U)\oplus H_k(V)
\longrightarrow H_k(X)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
H_{k-1}(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow\cdots
\]
whenever $X=U\cup V$ is an open cover.
::: {.proof}
Here $i:U\cap V\hookrightarrow U$ and $j:U\cap V\hookrightarrow V$ are the inclusions.
This is the homological Mayer--Vietoris theorem requested in part (a).
:::

<1>2. For the space in part (b), Mayer--Vietoris may be computed using
\[
U\simeq A,
\qquad
V\simeq B,
\qquad
U\cap V\simeq T^2=S^1\times S^1,
\]
where the second boundary inclusion is read through the gluing map
\[
\phi(z,w)=(zw^3,w).
\]
::: {.proof}
The images of $A$ and $B$ in the quotient are closed rather than open, so take open collar neighborhoods of the two solid tori in $X$.
Each collar neighborhood deformation retracts onto the corresponding solid torus, while their intersection deformation retracts onto the common boundary torus.
Under these deformation retractions, the two maps from the intersection into the pieces are the boundary inclusion into $A$ and the boundary inclusion into $B$ after applying $\phi$.
Thus the Mayer--Vietoris sequence in <1>1 has the displayed homotopy data.
:::

<1>3. The homology groups of the pieces and their intersection are
\[
H_k(A)\cong H_k(B)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
H_k(T^2)\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,2,\\
\ZZ^2,&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Each solid torus $S^1\times D^2$ deformation retracts onto its core circle $S^1\times\{0\}$.
The homology of $T^2$ is the standard homology of the product $S^1\times S^1$.
:::

<1>4. Let $a$ and $b$ denote the generators of $H_1(A)$ and $H_1(B)$ coming from their core circles.
On the common boundary torus let
\[
\alpha(t)=(e^{2\pi i t},1),
\qquad
\beta(t)=(1,e^{2\pi i t}).
\]
Then $[\alpha],[\beta]$ form a basis of $H_1(T^2)$ and the Mayer--Vietoris map
\[
\Psi:H_1(T^2)\longrightarrow H_1(A)\oplus H_1(B)
\]
satisfies
\[
\Psi([\alpha])=(a,-b),
\qquad
\Psi([\beta])=(0,-3b).
\]
::: {.proof}
For the inclusion into $A$, the loop $\alpha$ is a longitude and represents $a$, while $\beta$ is a meridian and bounds the disk $\{1\}\times D^2$.
Hence
\[
(i_A)_*[\alpha]=a,
\qquad
(i_A)_*[\beta]=0.
\]

For the inclusion into $B$, first apply the gluing map.
Along $\alpha$ one has
\[
\phi(e^{2\pi i t},1)=(e^{2\pi i t},1),
\]
so its image represents the longitude $b$.
Along $\beta$ one has
\[
\phi(1,e^{2\pi i t})=(e^{6\pi i t},e^{2\pi i t}).
\]
The first coordinate winds three times around the core direction of $B$, while the second coordinate is a meridian and dies in $H_1(B)$.
Thus
\[
(i_B\circ\phi)_*[\alpha]=b,
\qquad
(i_B\circ\phi)_*[\beta]=3b.
\]
The first Mayer--Vietoris map is $(i_A)_*\oplus-(i_B\circ\phi)_*$, giving the claimed formulas.
:::

<1>5. Relative to the bases $([\alpha],[\beta])$ and $(a,b)$, the map $\Psi$ has matrix
\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
-1&-3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is injective and its cokernel is $\ZZ/3\ZZ$.
::: {.proof}
The determinant is
\[
\det M=-3\ne0,
\]
so $M:\ZZ^2\to\ZZ^2$ is injective.
Moreover its image is generated by
\[
(1,-1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(0,-3).
\]
Modulo the first generator every class has a representative of the form $(0,m)$, and the second generator imposes the relation $3(0,1)=0$.
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{coker}M\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ.
\]
:::

<1>6. One has
\[
H_3(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_2(X;\ZZ)=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
The degree-$3$ and degree-$2$ part of Mayer--Vietoris is
\[
0
\longrightarrow H_3(X)
\longrightarrow H_2(T^2)
\longrightarrow H_2(A)\oplus H_2(B)
\longrightarrow H_2(X)
\longrightarrow H_1(T^2)
\xrightarrow{\Psi}
H_1(A)\oplus H_1(B).
\]
By <1>3 this becomes
\[
0
\longrightarrow H_3(X)
\longrightarrow\ZZ
\longrightarrow0
\longrightarrow H_2(X)
\longrightarrow\ZZ^2
\xrightarrow{\Psi}\ZZ^2.
\]
Hence $H_3(X)\cong\ZZ$.
By <1>5, $\ker\Psi=0$, so exactness gives $H_2(X)=0$.
:::

<1>7. One has
\[
H_1(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ,
\qquad
H_0(X;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
\]
::: {.proof}
The next part of Mayer--Vietoris is
\[
H_1(T^2)
\xrightarrow{\Psi}
H_1(A)\oplus H_1(B)
\longrightarrow H_1(X)
\longrightarrow H_0(T^2)
\longrightarrow H_0(A)\oplus H_0(B).
\]
All three spaces $T^2,A,B$ are connected, so the last map is, up to the Mayer--Vietoris sign convention,
\[
\ZZ\longrightarrow\ZZ\oplus\ZZ,
\qquad
1\longmapsto(1,-1),
\]
which is injective.
Exactness therefore gives
\[
H_1(X)\cong\operatorname{coker}\Psi\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ
\]
by <1>5.
The same exact sequence in degree zero says that $H_0(X)$ is the cokernel of the diagonal-with-sign map above, which is $\ZZ$.
:::

<1>8. Therefore
\[
H_k(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,3,\\
\ZZ/3\ZZ,&k=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>6 and <1>7 give the groups in degrees $0$ through $3$.
For $k>3$, all adjacent homology groups of $A$, $B$, and $T^2$ in the Mayer--Vietoris sequence vanish by <1>3, so $H_k(X)=0$.
:::
