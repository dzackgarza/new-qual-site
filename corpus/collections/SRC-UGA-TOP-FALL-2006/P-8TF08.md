::: problem
a. What is the degree of the antipodal map $a_n:S^n\to S^n$?
(Do not prove your answer.)

b. Define the structure of a CW complex on real projective $n$-space $\RP^n$.

c. Use parts (a) and (b) to compute the cellular homology of $\RP^n$.
Show your work.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The antipodal map
\[
a_n:S^n\longrightarrow S^n,
\qquad
a_n(x)=-x,
\]
has degree
\[
\deg(a_n)=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the answer requested in part (a), for which the problem explicitly says no proof is required.
:::

<1>2. The standard CW structure on $\RP^n$ has exactly one cell $e^k$ in each dimension $0\le k\le n$.
::: {.proof}
For each $k$, regard $\RP^k$ as the quotient of a closed $k$-disk $D^k$ in which antipodal points of the boundary $S^{k-1}$ are identified.
The interior of $D^k$ maps homeomorphically onto
\[
\RP^k\setminus\RP^{k-1},
\]
so it is the open $k$-cell $e^k$.
Its attaching map is the quotient
\[
\phi_k:S^{k-1}\longrightarrow\RP^{k-1},
\qquad
x\longmapsto[x].
\]
Thus
\[
\RP^0\subset\RP^1\subset\cdots\subset\RP^n
\]
is the skeletal filtration and there is one cell in every dimension from $0$ through $n$.
:::

<1>3. With the generators given by these cells, the cellular boundary
\[
d_k:C_k(\RP^n)\longrightarrow C_{k-1}(\RP^n)
\]
is multiplication by
\[
1+(-1)^k.
\]
::: {.proof}
For $1\le k\le n$, both chain groups are $\ZZ$.
The coefficient of the unique $(k-1)$-cell in $d_k(e^k)$ is the degree of
\[
S^{k-1}
\xrightarrow{\phi_k}
\RP^{k-1}
\xrightarrow{q}
\RP^{k-1}/\RP^{k-2}
\cong S^{k-1}.
\]
Away from the collapsed $(k-2)$-skeleton, the domain has two hemispherical pieces mapping homeomorphically onto the open $(k-1)$-cell.
The two restrictions differ by precomposition with the antipodal map on $S^{k-1}$.
Their local-degree contributions are therefore
\[
1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\deg(a_{k-1})=(-1)^k
\]
by <1>1.
Adding the two contributions gives
\[
d_k=1+(-1)^k.
\]
:::

<1>4. Consequently
\[
d_k=
\begin{cases}
0,&k\text{ odd},\\
2,&k\text{ even},
\end{cases}
\]
and the cellular chain complex has one copy of $\ZZ$ in every degree $0\le k\le n$, with differentials alternating between $0$ and multiplication by $2$.
::: {.proof}
This is immediate from <1>3 because
\[
1+(-1)^k=
\begin{cases}
0,&k\text{ odd},\\
2,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
There are no cells outside the dimensions $0,\ldots,n$ by <1>2.
:::

<1>5. In degree zero,
\[
H_0(\RP^n;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
\]
For $0<k<n$,
\[
H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ/2\ZZ,&k\text{ odd},\\
0,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $d_1=0$, one has
\[
H_0=C_0/\operatorname{im}d_1\cong\ZZ.
\]
If $0<k<n$ is odd, then $d_k=0$ and $d_{k+1}=2$, so
\[
H_k
=\ker d_k/\operatorname{im}d_{k+1}
=\ZZ/2\ZZ.
\]
If $0<k<n$ is even, then $d_k=2$ is injective, hence
\[
\ker d_k=0
\]
and therefore $H_k=0$.
:::

<1>6. In the top dimension,
\[
H_n(\RP^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&n\text{ odd},\\
0,&n\text{ even and }n>0.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
There is no incoming differential from degree $n+1$.
Thus
\[
H_n=\ker d_n.
\]
If $n$ is odd, then $d_n=0$, so $H_n\cong\ZZ$.
If $n>0$ is even, then $d_n=2$ is injective, so $H_n=0$.
:::

<1>7. Altogether,
\[
H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
\ZZ/2\ZZ,&0<k<n\text{ and }k\text{ odd},\\
\ZZ,&k=n\text{ and }n\text{ odd},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>5 and <1>6 compute every degree in the range $0\le k\le n$, and the cellular chain groups vanish for $k>n$.
:::
