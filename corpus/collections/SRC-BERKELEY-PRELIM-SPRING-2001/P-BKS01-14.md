::: {.problem}
Consider the delay equation
\[
y'(t)=-y(t-t_0),
\]
where $t_0>0$ and $y$ may be complex-valued.
Prove that if
\[
0<t_0<\frac\pi2,
\]
then every solution of the form $y(t)=e^{\lambda t}$ tends to zero as $t\to+\infty$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
\lambda=a+ib,
$$
with $a,b\in\RR$.

<1>1. The parameter $\lambda$ satisfies
$$
\lambda=-e^{-\lambda t_0}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Substituting $y(t)=e^{\lambda t}$ into the differential-delay equation
gives
$$
\lambda e^{\lambda t}
=
-e^{\lambda(t-t_0)}
=
-e^{\lambda t}e^{-\lambda t_0}.
$$
Since $e^{\lambda t}\neq0$, cancellation gives the claimed equation.
:::

<1>2. One has $a<0$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose instead that $a\geq0$. Taking absolute values in step <1>1 gives
$$
|\lambda|
=
e^{-a t_0}
\leq1.
$$
Hence $|b|\leq|\lambda|\leq1$. Since
$$
0<t_0<\frac{\pi}{2},
$$
it follows that
$$
|bt_0|<\frac{\pi}{2},
$$
and therefore
$$
\cos(bt_0)>0.
$$

On the other hand, taking real parts in step <1>1 yields
$$
a
=
-e^{-a t_0}\cos(bt_0)
<0,
$$
contradicting $a\geq0$. Thus $a<0$.
:::

<1>3. The solution satisfies
$$
\lim_{t\to+\infty}y(t)=0.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2,
$$
|y(t)|
=
|e^{(a+ib)t}|
=
e^{at}
\longrightarrow0
$$
as $t\to+\infty$. Hence $y(t)\to0$.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is the required conclusion for every exponential solution.
:::
:::
