::: {.problem}
Let $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R$ and let $\Gamma\le(\mathbb R,+)$ be the subgroup generated by $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
Assume $\Gamma$ is closed.
Prove that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are linearly dependent over $\mathbb Q$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
\Gamma
=
\ZZ\alpha+\ZZ\beta.
$$

<1>1. Let $H\leq(\RR,+)$ be a nonzero closed subgroup, and set
$$
d
=
\inf\bigl(H\cap(0,\infty)\bigr).
$$
If $d=0$, then
$$
H=\RR.
$$

::: {.proof}
Assume $d=0$. Given $x\in\RR$ and $\varepsilon>0$, choose
$$
0<h\in H
$$
with $h<2\varepsilon$. Choose an integer $m$ nearest to $x/h$. Then
$$
\abs{x-mh}
\leq
\frac h2
<
\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $H$ is dense in $\RR$. Since $H$ is closed, it follows that
$H=\RR$.
:::

<1>2. In the setting of step <1>1, if $d>0$, then
$$
H=d\ZZ.
$$

::: {.proof}
Choose a sequence
$$
h_n\in H\cap(0,\infty)
$$
with $h_n\to d$. Closedness of $H$ gives $d\in H$.

Now let $h\in H$. Choose $q\in\ZZ$ such that
$$
0\leq h-qd<d.
$$
The remainder
$$
r=h-qd
$$
belongs to $H$. If $r>0$, then
$$
r\in H\cap(0,\infty)
$$
and $r<d$, contradicting the definition of $d$. Hence $r=0$, so
$h\in d\ZZ$. The reverse inclusion follows from $d\in H$.
:::

<1>3. Every closed subgroup of $\RR$ is one of
$$
\{0\},
\qquad
d\ZZ\quad(d>0),
\qquad
\RR.
$$

::: {.proof}
The zero subgroup is one case. For a nonzero closed subgroup, steps
<1>1 and <1>2 give the remaining two possibilities.
:::

<1>4. The subgroup $\Gamma$ cannot equal $\RR$.

::: {.proof}
Every element of $\Gamma$ has the form
$$
m\alpha+n\beta,
\qquad
(m,n)\in\ZZ^2.
$$
Thus $\Gamma$ is countable, whereas $\RR$ is uncountable.
:::

<1>5. The numbers $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are linearly dependent over
$\QQ$.

::: {.proof}
If $\Gamma=\{0\}$, then $\alpha=\beta=0$, and the conclusion is
immediate.

Otherwise, steps <1>3 and <1>4 give
$$
\Gamma=d\ZZ
$$
for some $d>0$. Hence there exist $m,n\in\ZZ$ such that
$$
\alpha=md,
\qquad
\beta=nd.
$$
Since $\Gamma\ne0$, the pair $(m,n)$ is not $(0,0)$. Therefore
$$
n\alpha-m\beta=0
$$
is a nontrivial rational linear relation.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 is the required conclusion.
:::
