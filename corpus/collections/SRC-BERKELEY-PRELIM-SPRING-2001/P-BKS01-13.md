    sums of the series of remainders.

::: {.problem}
Let
\[
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nz^n
\]
have positive radius of convergence $R$, and let
\[
s_k(z)=\sum_{n=0}^k c_nz^n.
\]
Prove that for every $|z|<R$,
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|f(z)-s_k(z)|<\infty.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $z$ with $|z|<R$. Choose $\rho$ such that
$$
|z|<\rho<R,
$$
and set
$$
q\coloneqq \frac{|z|}{\rho}.
$$
Then $0\leq q<1$.

<1>1. The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n|c_n||z|^n
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
Since $\rho<R$, the power series converges absolutely at $\rho$, so
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|c_n|\rho^n<\infty.
$$
In particular, its terms are bounded. Thus there is $M>0$ such that
$$
|c_n|\rho^n\leq M
$$
for every $n\geq0$. Hence, for $n\geq1$,
$$
n|c_n||z|^n
=n|c_n|\rho^n q^n
\leq Mnq^n.
$$
Because $0\leq q<1$, the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nq^n$
converges. The comparison test therefore gives the claim.
:::

<1>2. For every $N\geq0$,
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{N}|f(z)-s_k(z)|
\leq
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|c_n||z|^n.
$$

::: {.proof}
For each $k\geq0$,
$$
f(z)-s_k(z)
=
\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}c_nz^n,
$$
so the triangle inequality gives
$$
|f(z)-s_k(z)|
\leq
\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}|c_n||z|^n.
$$
Therefore, since the outer sum is finite,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=0}^{N}|f(z)-s_k(z)|
&\leq
\sum_{k=0}^{N}\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}|c_n||z|^n\\
&=
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\min\{n,N+1\}|c_n||z|^n\\
&\leq
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|c_n||z|^n.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>3. The series
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|f(z)-s_k(z)|
$$
converges.

::: {.proof}
Its partial sums are nondecreasing because all terms are nonnegative.
By step <1>2 they are bounded above by the finite quantity from
step <1>1. Hence the partial sums converge, proving that the displayed
series is finite.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 proves the required assertion for the arbitrary point
$z$ with $|z|<R$.
:::
:::
