::: {.problem}
Let $U$ be a nonempty proper open subset of $\mathbb R^n$.
Construct a function $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ that is discontinuous at every point of $U$ and continuous at every point of $\mathbb R^n\setminus U$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
C=\RR^n\sm U
$$
and define
$$
d(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,C).
$$
Since $U$ is proper, $C$ is nonempty. Let
$$
D=U\cap\QQ^n
$$
and define
$$
f(x)
=
\begin{cases}
d(x),&x\in D,\\
0,&x\notin D.
\end{cases}
$$

<1>1. The function $d$ is $1$-Lipschitz, vanishes on $C$, and is
strictly positive on $U$.

::: {.proof}
For any $x,y\in\RR^n$ and any $c\in C$,
$$
d(x)
\leq
\norm{x-c}
\leq
\norm{x-y}+\norm{y-c}.
$$
Taking the infimum over $c\in C$ gives
$$
d(x)-d(y)\leq\norm{x-y}.
$$
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ yields
$$
\abs{d(x)-d(y)}
\leq
\norm{x-y}.
$$
Thus $d$ is $1$-Lipschitz.

If $x\in C$, then $d(x)=0$. If $x\in U$, openness gives $r>0$ with
$$
B(x,r)\subseteq U.
$$
Every point of $C$ is therefore at distance at least $r$ from $x$, so
$$
d(x)\geq r>0.
$$
:::

<1>2. The sets $D$ and $U\sm D$ are both dense in $U$.

::: {.proof}
The set $\QQ^n$ is dense in $\RR^n$, so
$$
D=U\cap\QQ^n
$$
is dense in the open set $U$.

Also, every nonempty open ball in $\RR^n$ contains uncountably many
points, whereas $\QQ^n$ is countable. Hence every nonempty open ball
contained in $U$ contains a point outside $\QQ^n$. Thus $U\sm D$ is
dense in $U$.
:::

<1>3. The function $f$ is discontinuous at every point of $U$.

::: {.proof}
Fix $x\in U$. By step <1>1,
$$
d(x)>0.
$$
By step <1>2, choose sequences
$$
x_k\in D,
\qquad
y_k\in U\sm D
$$
with
$$
x_k\to x,
\qquad
y_k\to x.
$$
Then
$$
f(y_k)=0
$$
for every $k$, while continuity of $d$ gives
$$
f(x_k)
=
d(x_k)
\longrightarrow
d(x)>0.
$$
Thus $f$ has two different sequential limiting values at $x$, so it is
not continuous there.
:::

<1>4. The function $f$ is continuous at every point of $C$.

::: {.proof}
Fix $x\in C$. Then
$$
f(x)=0.
$$
For every $y\in\RR^n$,
$$
\abs{f(y)}
\leq
d(y).
$$
Since $x\in C$,
$$
d(y)
\leq
\norm{y-x}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}
\leq
\norm{y-x}
\longrightarrow0
$$
as $y\to x$. Hence $f$ is continuous at $x$.
:::

<1>5. The discontinuity set of $f$ is exactly $U$.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 gives discontinuity at every point of $U$, while step <1>4
gives continuity at every point of $\RR^n\sm U$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 proves the required construction.
