::: {.problem}
Let $T_0$ be the interior of a triangle with vertices $A,B,C$. Let $T_1$ be the interior of the triangle whose vertices are the midpoints of the sides of $T_0$, and define $T_2,T_3,\ldots$ recursively in the same way.
Describe
\[
\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}T_n.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
G\coloneqq\frac{A+B+C}{3}
$$
be the centroid of the original triangle, and define
$$
H(x)\coloneqq G-\frac12(x-G).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The map $H$ sends $T_n$ onto $T_{n+1}$ for every $n\geq0$.

::: pf-proof

The assertion and the fact that $G$ is the centroid of $T_n$ are
proved simultaneously by induction. For $T_0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(A)&=\frac{B+C}{2},\\
H(B)&=\frac{C+A}{2},\\
H(C)&=\frac{A+B}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $H$ sends the three vertices of the original triangle to the
midpoints of the opposite sides. Since $H$ is affine, it sends the
interior $T_0$ onto the interior of the medial triangle, namely $T_1$.
Moreover, affine maps preserve centroids, so the centroid of $T_1$ is
$H(G)=G$.

Now suppose the centroid of $T_n$ is $G$, and let $V_1,V_2,V_3$ be its
vertices. Since
$$
G=\frac{V_1+V_2+V_3}{3},
$$
one has
$$
H(V_1)
=
G-\frac12(V_1-G)
=
\frac{V_2+V_3}{2},
$$
and similarly for $V_2,V_3$. Thus $H(T_n)$ is precisely the medial
triangle $T_{n+1}$, whose centroid is again $H(G)=G$. Therefore, by
induction,
$$
H(T_n)=T_{n+1}
$$
for every $n\geq0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

For every $n\geq0$,
$$
T_n=H^n(T_0),
$$
and $G\in T_n$.

::: pf-proof

The first statement follows from step [](#s1){.pf-ref} by induction. Since the
centroid lies in the interior of a triangle, $G\in T_0$. Also
$H(G)=G$, so
$$
G=H^n(G)\in H^n(T_0)=T_n
$$
for every $n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

If $x\in T_n$, then
$$
\norm{x-G}\leq 2^{-n}D
$$
for some constant $D$ independent of $n$.

::: pf-proof

Because $T_0$ is bounded, choose $D>0$ such that
$$
\norm{u-G}\leq D
$$
for every $u\in T_0$. If $x\in T_n$, then by step [](#s2){.pf-ref} there is
$u\in T_0$ with $x=H^n(u)$. Since
$$
H(u)-G=-\frac12(u-G),
$$
iteration gives
$$
H^n(u)-G
=
\left(-\frac12\right)^n(u-G).
$$
Taking norms proves the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One has
$$
\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}T_n
=
\boxed{\{G\}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Step [](#s2){.pf-ref} shows that $G$ belongs to every $T_n$. Conversely, let
$$
x\in\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}T_n.
$$
Then step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\norm{x-G}\leq2^{-n}D
$$
for every $n$. Letting $n\to\infty$ yields $\norm{x-G}=0$, hence
$x=G$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives the complete description of the intersection.
