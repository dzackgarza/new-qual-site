::: {.problem}
Let $S_9$ be the symmetric group and $A_9$ its subgroup of even permutations.

1. Determine the smallest positive integer $m$ such that $\sigma^m=1$ for every $\sigma\in S_9$.
2. Determine the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $\sigma^n=1$ for every $\sigma\in A_9$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every element of $S_9$ has order dividing
$$
L
=
\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,9).
$$

::: {.proof}
Write a permutation as a product of disjoint cycles. Its order is the
least common multiple of its cycle lengths, and every cycle length is
at most $9$. Hence its order divides $L$.
:::

<1>2. Any positive integer $m$ satisfying
$$
\sigma^m=1
$$
for every $\sigma\in S_9$ must be divisible by every integer
$1,2,\ldots,9$.

::: {.proof}
For each $1\leq k\leq9$, the group $S_9$ contains a $k$-cycle, whose
order is $k$. If its $m$-th power is the identity, then $k\mid m$.
:::

<1>3. The answer for $S_9$ is
$$
\boxed{m=2520}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 show that the least possible value is
$$
\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,9).
$$
Its prime-power factorization is
$$
2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7,
$$
so
$$
L=8\cdot9\cdot5\cdot7=2520.
$$
:::

<1>4. No element of $A_9$ has order divisible by $8$.

::: {.proof}
If a permutation in $S_9$ has order divisible by $8$, then one of its
disjoint cycle lengths must be divisible by $8$. Since all cycle
lengths are at most $9$, it must contain an $8$-cycle.

An $8$-cycle uses eight of the nine letters, leaving only one fixed
point. Its sign is
$$
(-1)^{8-1}=-1,
$$
so the whole permutation is odd. Hence no such permutation belongs to
$A_9$.
:::

<1>5. Every element of $A_9$ has order dividing
$$
1260
=
2^2\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7.
$$

::: {.proof}
Every element of $A_9$ lies in $S_9$, so by step <1>3 its order divides
$$
2520
=
2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7.
$$
Step <1>4 rules out the factor $2^3$. Therefore its order divides
$$
2^2\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7=1260.
$$
:::

<1>6. The group $A_9$ contains elements of orders
$$
4,
\qquad
9,
\qquad
5,
\qquad
7.
$$

::: {.proof}
The permutation
$$
(1\ 2\ 3\ 4)(5\ 6)
$$
is even and has order $4$. A $9$-cycle is even because its sign is
$(-1)^8=1$, so $A_9$ contains an element of order $9$. Likewise,
$5$-cycles and $7$-cycles are even and give elements of orders $5$ and
$7$.
:::

<1>7. Any positive integer $n$ satisfying
$$
\sigma^n=1
$$
for every $\sigma\in A_9$ is divisible by $1260$.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>6, such an $n$ must be divisible by
$$
\operatorname{lcm}(4,9,5,7)
=
2^2\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7
=
1260.
$$
:::

<1>8. The answer for $A_9$ is
$$
\boxed{n=1260}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Step <1>5 shows that $1260$ works, and step <1>7 shows that no smaller
positive integer can work.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>3 and <1>8 give the two requested integers.
:::
