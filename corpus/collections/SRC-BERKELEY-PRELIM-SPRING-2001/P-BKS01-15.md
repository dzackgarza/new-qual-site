::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ matrix over a field $K$.
Prove that
\[
\operatorname{rank}A^2-\operatorname{rank}A^3
\le
\operatorname{rank}A-\operatorname{rank}A^2.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
For $j\geq1$, set
$$
W_j\coloneqq\im A^j.
$$

<1>1. For $j=1,2$,
$$
\operatorname{rank}A^j-\operatorname{rank}A^{j+1}
=
\dim(W_j\cap\ker A).
$$

::: {.proof}
The restriction
$$
A|_{W_j}:W_j\longrightarrow W_{j+1}
$$
is surjective because
$$
A(W_j)
=
A(\im A^j)
=
\im A^{j+1}
=
W_{j+1}.
$$
Its kernel is
$$
\ker(A|_{W_j})
=
W_j\cap\ker A.
$$
Hence rank-nullity gives
$$
\dim W_j-\dim W_{j+1}
=
\dim(W_j\cap\ker A).
$$
Since $\dim W_j=\operatorname{rank}A^j$, this is the claimed identity.
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
\dim(W_2\cap\ker A)
\leq
\dim(W_1\cap\ker A).
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
W_2
=
\im A^2
\subseteq
\im A
=
W_1,
$$
one has
$$
W_2\cap\ker A
\subseteq
W_1\cap\ker A.
$$
Both are finite-dimensional vector spaces, so the inclusion gives the
stated inequality of dimensions.
:::

<1>3. The required inequality holds:
$$
\operatorname{rank}A^2-\operatorname{rank}A^3
\leq
\operatorname{rank}A-\operatorname{rank}A^2.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>1, the left-hand side is
$$
\dim(W_2\cap\ker A),
$$
while the right-hand side is
$$
\dim(W_1\cap\ker A).
$$
Step <1>2 compares these two dimensions.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 is exactly the desired conclusion.
:::
:::
