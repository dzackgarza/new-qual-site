::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be entire and suppose that for some positive integer $k$ and constant $A>0$,
\[
\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta\le Ar^{2k}
\qquad(r>0).
\]
Prove that $f$ is a constant multiple of $z^k$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write the entire function as
$$
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^n.
$$

<1>1. For every $r>0$,
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta
=
2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_n|^2r^{2n}.
$$

::: {.proof}
For fixed $r>0$, the power series for $f$ converges absolutely and
uniformly on the circle $|z|=r$. Hence
$$
f(re^{i\theta})
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nr^ne^{in\theta},
$$
and the product with its complex conjugate may be integrated term by
term. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta
&=
\sum_{n,m\geq0}
a_n\overline{a_m}r^{n+m}
\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i(n-m)\theta}\,d\theta\\
&=
2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_n|^2r^{2n},
\end{aligned}
$$
because the integral is $2\pi$ when $n=m$ and $0$ otherwise.
:::

<1>2. One has $a_n=0$ for every $0\leq n<k$.

::: {.proof}
Fix $n<k$. By step <1>1 and the assumed bound,
$$
2\pi|a_n|^2r^{2n}
\leq
Ar^{2k}
$$
for every $r>0$. Therefore
$$
|a_n|^2
\leq
\frac{A}{2\pi}r^{2(k-n)}.
$$
Letting $r\to0^+$ gives $a_n=0$.
:::

<1>3. One has $a_n=0$ for every $n>k$.

::: {.proof}
Fix $n>k$. The same estimate from step <1>1 gives
$$
|a_n|^2
\leq
\frac{A}{2\pi}r^{-2(n-k)}
$$
for every $r>0$. Letting $r\to\infty$ gives $a_n=0$.
:::

<1>4. There is a constant $c\in\CC$ such that
$$
f(z)=\boxed{cz^k}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2 and <1>3 show that every Taylor coefficient except possibly
$a_k$ vanishes. Taking $c=a_k$ gives the stated form.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 is exactly the required conclusion.
:::
:::
