::: {.problem}
In $\SL(2, \ZZ)$, the multiplicative group of all $2$ by $2$ matrices with integer entries whose determinant is $1$, what is the order of the cyclic subgroup generated by the following element?

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

(A) $2$
(B) $3$
(C) $4$
(D) $6$
(E) $\infty$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The square of $A$ is
$$
A^2=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&-1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Direct multiplication gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
:::

<1>2. One has
$$
A^3=I.
$$

::: {.proof}
Multiplying the matrix in step <1>1 by $A$ gives
$$
A^3=
\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
:::

<1>3. Neither $A$ nor $A^2$ is the identity matrix.

::: {.proof}
The displayed matrix $A$ has upper-left entry $0$, while $I$ has upper-left
entry $1$. The matrix $A^2$ from step <1>1 has upper-left entry $-1$.
Therefore neither power is $I$.
:::

<1>4. Hence the cyclic subgroup generated by $A$ has order
$$
\boxed{3},
$$
so the answer is (B).

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2, the order of $A$ divides $3$. Step <1>3 excludes orders $1$
and $2$, so the least positive exponent with $A^n=I$ is $3$.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>4 gives the requested order.
:::
:::
