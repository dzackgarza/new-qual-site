::: problem
a. Let $S_1$ and $S_2$ be disjoint surfaces.
Give the definition of their connected sum $S_1 \# S_2$.

b. Compute the fundamental group of the connected sum of the projective plane and the two-torus.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. To form the connected sum \(S_1\#S_2\), choose embedded closed disks
\[
D_i\subset S_i
\qquad(i=1,2),
\]
remove their interiors, and identify the two resulting boundary circles by a homeomorphism:
\[
S_1\#S_2
=
\frac{(S_1\setminus\operatorname{int}D_1)\sqcup
(S_2\setminus\operatorname{int}D_2)}
{\partial D_1\sim\partial D_2}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Each punctured surface has one boundary component homeomorphic to \(S^1\). Gluing these boundary circles produces a surface.
For connected surfaces, the homeomorphism type of the connected sum is independent of the choices of disks and boundary identification, up to the usual orientation convention when orientations are specified.
:::

<1>2. The punctured projective plane
\[
\RP^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2
\]
is a Möbius band.
Its fundamental group is infinite cyclic,
\[
\pi_1(\RP^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2)
\cong
\langle a\rangle,
\]
and its boundary circle represents \(a^2\).
::: {.proof}
A projective plane can be obtained by attaching a disk to the boundary of a Möbius band, so removing the interior of that disk leaves the Möbius band.
The Möbius band deformation retracts onto its core circle, giving the generator \(a\). Its boundary circle winds twice around the core, hence represents \(a^2\) in the fundamental group.
:::

<1>3. The punctured torus
\[
T^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2
\]
deformation retracts onto a wedge of two circles.
Hence
\[
\pi_1(T^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2)
\cong
F(b,c),
\]
and, after choosing the usual orientation, its boundary loop represents the commutator
\[
[b,c]=bcb^{-1}c^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Use the standard square model of the torus with one open disk removed from the interior.
Collapsing the punctured square onto its two generating edge loops gives a deformation retraction to \(S^1\vee S^1\), so the fundamental group is free on \(b,c\). Traversing the boundary of the original square gives the word
\[
bcb^{-1}c^{-1}=[b,c].
\]
The boundary of the removed disk is homotopic, with the induced boundary orientation, to the inverse of this word; reversing the chosen boundary orientation replaces it by \([b,c]\). Thus, up to the orientation convention used in the gluing, the peripheral subgroup is generated by \([b,c]\).
:::

<1>4. Seifert--van Kampen gives
\[
\pi_1(\RP^2\#T^2)
\cong
\left\langle a,b,c\ \middle|\ a^2[b,c]=1\right\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
M=\RP^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2,
\qquad
N=T^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2.
\]
After gluing their boundary circles, enlarge \(M\) and \(N\) slightly across a collar of the common circle.
These enlarged sets form an open cover of \(\RP^2\#T^2\), and their intersection deformation retracts onto the common boundary circle.

By <1>2, the boundary generator maps to \(a^2\) in \(\pi_1(M)\). By <1>3, choose the boundary orientation on \(N\) so that its image is \([b,c]\). The boundary-identifying homeomorphism for the connected sum reverses the two boundary orientations, so van Kampen identifies
\[
a^2=[b,c]^{-1}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
a^2[b,c]=1.
\]
Since
\[
\pi_1(M)=\langle a\rangle,
\qquad
\pi_1(N)=\langle b,c\rangle,
\]
this is the complete amalgamated-product presentation.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(\RP^2\#T^2)
\cong
\left\langle a,b,c\ \middle|\ a^2[b,c]=1\right\rangle.}
\]
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the presentation obtained in <1>4. In particular, the connected-sum group is not the free product \((\mathbb Z/2)*\mathbb Z^2\); the common boundary circle contributes the displayed relation.
:::
