::: {.problem}
(i) State the classification theorem for compact surfaces.

(ii) What surface is represented by the $6$-gon with the edges identified in pairs according to the symbol
\[
xyzxy^{-1}z^{-1}?
\]
:::

::: {.solution}

**Part (i).**

::: pf

::: pf-step
Every compact connected surface without boundary is homeomorphic to exactly one of the following:

- the sphere $S^2$;

- a connected sum of $g \ge 1$ tori (the orientable surface of genus $g$);

- a connected sum of $g \ge 1$ projective planes (the nonorientable surface of genus $g$).

::: pf-proof
statement of the classification theorem.
:::

:::

:::

**Part (ii).**

::: pf

::: pf-step
In the word $xyzxy^{-1}z^{-1}$, the letter $x$ appears twice with the same sign, while $y$ and $z$ each appear once with each sign.

::: pf-proof
This is immediate from the boundary word.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Hence the surface is nonorientable.

::: pf-proof
For an orientable polygonal presentation, every paired edge occurs once with each sign in the boundary word.
Since both occurrences of $x$ have the same sign, this presentation is nonorientable.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-3}
The number of edges after identification is $E = 3$ and the number of faces is $F = 1$.

::: pf-proof
three pairs of identified edges, one polygon.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-4}
The number of vertices after identification is $V = 1$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Label the vertices $v_0, \ldots, v_5$ in order.

::: pf-proof
Let the six directed boundary edges be
\[
v_0v_1=x,
\quad
v_1v_2=y,
\quad
v_2v_3=z,
\quad
v_3v_4=x,
\quad
v_4v_5=y^{-1},
\quad
v_5v_0=z^{-1}.
\]
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-4-2}
The identifications force all six vertices to be identified.

::: pf-proof
Matching the two $x$-edges according to their indicated directions gives
\[
v_0\sim v_3,
\qquad
v_1\sim v_4.
\]
Matching $y$ with $y^{-1}$ gives
\[
v_1\sim v_5,
\qquad
v_2\sim v_4,
\]
and matching $z$ with $z^{-1}$ gives
\[
v_2\sim v_0,
\qquad
v_3\sim v_5.
\]
Thus
\[
v_0\sim v_2\sim v_4\sim v_1\sim v_5\sim v_3\sim v_0,
\]
so all six vertices lie in one equivalence class.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Hence $V = 1$.

::: pf-proof
step [](#p2-4-2){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-5}
The Euler characteristic is $\chi = V - E + F = 1 - 3 + 1 = -1$.

::: pf-proof
step [](#p2-3){.pf-ref} and step [](#p2-4){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-6}
For a nonorientable surface of genus $g$, $\chi = 2 - g$, so $-1 = 2 - g$ gives $g = 3$.

::: pf-proof
solve for $g$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #p2-7}
Hence the surface is the nonorientable surface of genus $3$, i.e. the connected sum of three projective planes $\RP^2 \# \RP^2 \# \RP^2$.

::: pf-proof
step [](#p2-5){.pf-ref} and step [](#p2-6){.pf-ref}.
:::
