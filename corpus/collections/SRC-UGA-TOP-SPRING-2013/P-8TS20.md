::: problem
(i) Let $X$ be a topological space.
Give the definition of the statement "$X$ is compact."

(ii) Prove that every compact subspace of a Hausdorff space is closed.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A topological space \(X\) is compact if every open cover of \(X\) has a finite subcover.
::: {.proof}
Explicitly, if
\[
X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in I}U_\alpha
\]
with every \(U_\alpha\) open, then there exist
\[
\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_m\in I
\]
such that
\[
X=U_{\alpha_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{\alpha_m}.
\]
:::

<1>2. Let \(K\) be a compact subspace of a Hausdorff space \(X\), and let \(x\in X\setminus K\). Then \(x\) and \(K\) have disjoint open neighborhoods.
::: {.proof}
For each \(y\in K\), Hausdorffness gives disjoint open sets
\[
U_y\ni y,
\qquad
V_y\ni x.
\]
The sets \(U_y\cap K\) form an open cover of the compact subspace \(K\). Choose
\[
y_1,\dots,y_m\in K
\]
with
\[
K\subseteq U_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{y_m}.
\]
Set
\[
U=U_{y_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{y_m},
\qquad
V=V_{y_1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{y_m}.
\]
Then \(U,V\) are open,
\[
K\subseteq U,
\qquad
x\in V.
\]
If \(z\in U\), then \(z\in U_{y_i}\) for some \(i\), while
\[
V\subseteq V_{y_i}
\]
and
\[
U_{y_i}\cap V_{y_i}=\varnothing.
\]
Hence \(z\notin V\), so
\[
U\cap V=\varnothing.
\]
:::

<1>3. Every compact subspace of a Hausdorff space is closed.
::: {.proof}
Let \(K\subseteq X\) be compact.
For every \(x\in X\setminus K\), <1>2 gives an open neighborhood \(V_x\) with
\[
x\in V_x\subseteq X\setminus K.
\]
Therefore
\[
X\setminus K=\bigcup_{x\in X\setminus K}V_x
\]
is open.
Thus \(K\) is closed.
:::
:::
