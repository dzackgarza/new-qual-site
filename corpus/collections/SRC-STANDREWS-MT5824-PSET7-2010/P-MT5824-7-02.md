::: {.problem}
Show that $Z(S_3)=1$.
Hence calculate the upper central series of $S_3$ and deduce that $S_3$ is not nilpotent.

Show that $\gamma_i(S_3)=A_3$ for every $i\ge2$.
Finally, find a normal subgroup $N\triangleleft S_3$ such that both $S_3/N$ and $N$ are nilpotent.
:::

::: {.solution}
Conjugacy classes in $S_3$ show that no nonidentity element is central, so $Z(S_3)=1$.
Inductively, if $Z_i(S_3)=1$, then
\[
Z_{i+1}(S_3)/Z_i(S_3)=Z(S_3/Z_i(S_3))=Z(S_3)=1,
\]
so every term of the upper central series is trivial.
Thus $S_3$ is not nilpotent.

Also $\gamma_2(S_3)=S_3'=A_3$.
Since $A_3$ has prime order, every later lower-central term is either $A_3$ or $1$; the latter would make $S_3$ nilpotent, so $\gamma_i(S_3)=A_3$ for all $i\ge2$.

Take $N=A_3$.
Then $N\cong C_3$ and $S_3/N\cong C_2$, so both are abelian and hence nilpotent.
:::
