::: {.problem}
Show that
$$
Z(G\times H)=Z(G)\times Z(H).
$$
Show by induction that
$$
Z_i(G\times H)=Z_i(G)\times Z_i(H)
$$
for every $i$.
Deduce that a finite direct product of nilpotent groups is nilpotent.
:::

::: {.pf-step #centers}
$Z(G\times H)=Z(G)\times Z(H)$.

::: pf-proof
An element $(x,y)$ commutes with every $(g,h)\in G\times H$ if and only if $(x,y)(g,h)=(xg,yh)$ equals $(gx,hy)=(g,h)(x,y)$ for all $g,h$, that is if and only if $xg=gx$ for all $g$ and $yh=hy$ for all $h$. So the centre of the product is $Z(G)\times Z(H)$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #base}
With $Z_{0}(K)=\{1\}$ and $Z_{i+1}(K)/Z_{i}(K)=Z(K/Z_{i}(K))$, the formula $Z_{i}(G\times H)=Z_{i}(G)\times Z_{i}(H)$ holds for $i=0$.

::: pf-proof
$Z_{0}(G\times H)=\{1\}=\{1\}\times\{1\}=Z_{0}(G)\times Z_{0}(H)$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #step}
If $Z_{i}(G\times H)=Z_{i}(G)\times Z_{i}(H)$ then $Z_{i+1}(G\times H)=Z_{i+1}(G)\times Z_{i+1}(H)$.

::: pf-proof
By the induction hypothesis the quotients are isomorphic:
$$\frac{G\times H}{Z_{i}(G\times H)}\cong\frac{G}{Z_{i}(G)}\times\frac{H}{Z_{i}(H)},$$
the isomorphism being induced by the projection, which is a homomorphism with kernel $Z_{i}(G)\times Z_{i}(H)$. The centre of the left side is $Z_{i+1}(G\times H)/Z_{i}(G\times H)$ by definition, and by step [](#centers){.pf-ref} the centre of the right side is $Z\!\left(\frac{G}{Z_{i}(G)}\right)\times Z\!\left(\frac{H}{Z_{i}(H)}\right)=\frac{Z_{i+1}(G)}{Z_{i}(G)}\times\frac{Z_{i+1}(H)}{Z_{i}(H)}$. Pulling the second subgroup back along the isomorphism gives
$$Z_{i+1}(G\times H)=Z_{i+1}(G)\times Z_{i+1}(H),$$
since the preimage of a product of subgroups under the projection is the product of the preimages.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #induction}
$Z_{i}(G\times H)=Z_{i}(G)\times Z_{i}(H)$ for every $i\geq0$.

::: pf-proof
Induction on $i$: the case $i=0$ is step [](#base){.pf-ref} and the passage from $i$ to $i+1$ is step [](#step){.pf-ref}.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #nilpotent}
A finite direct product of nilpotent groups is nilpotent.

::: pf-proof
Let $G_{1},\dots,G_{n}$ be nilpotent of classes at most $c$, so that $Z_{c}(G_{j})=G_{j}$ for every $j$; such a $c$ exists by taking the maximum of the nilpotency classes. Repeated application of step [](#induction){.pf-ref} to two factors at a time, the formula being associative in the evident sense, gives
$$Z_{c}(G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{n})=Z_{c}(G_{1})\times\cdots\times Z_{c}(G_{n})=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{n},$$
so the product is nilpotent of class at most $c$.
:::
