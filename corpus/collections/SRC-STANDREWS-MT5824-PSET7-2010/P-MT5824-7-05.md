::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite $p$-group.

(a) If $M$ is a maximal subgroup of $G$, show that $[G:M]=p$.
Deduce that $G^pG'\le\Phi(G)$.

(b) Use the preceding exercise to show that $\Phi(G)=G^pG'$.

(c) Show that $G$ can be generated by precisely $d$ elements if and only if $G/\Phi(G)$ is a direct product of $d$ copies of $C_p$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A maximal subgroup of a finite $p$-group is normal of index $p$, so $G^pG'\le\Phi(G)$; the reverse inclusion comes from the same computation applied to $G/G^pG'$, which is elementary abelian, so that its Frattini subgroup is trivial, together with the correspondence theorem. Part (c) reads the minimal number of generators off the elementary abelian quotient, with the Frattini property $H\Phi(G)=G\Rightarrow H=G$ proved rather than quoted. The argument is this card's own: the checkout retains no solution set for this problem set.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #maximal}
(a) If $M<G$ is maximal then $M\triangleleft G$ and $[G:M]=p$.

::: pf-proof
A nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite $p$-group meets the centre: $G$ acts on $N$ by conjugation, the orbits have size a power of $p$, and the fixed points are $N\cap Z(G)$, so $\abs{N\cap Z(G)}\equiv\abs N\equiv0\pmod p$ and $N\cap Z(G)\ne1$. Apply this to $N=G$: $Z(G)\ne1$, and $M\cap Z(G)$ is normal in $G$, so by maximality either $M\cap Z(G)=M$ or $M\cap Z(G)=G$; the second is impossible as $M<G$. Hence $Z(G)\subseteq M$. Now $M\triangleleft G$: $Z(G)$ is normal and $G/Z(G)$ is a $p$-group of smaller order, so by induction on $\abs G$ the image of $M$ in $G/Z(G)$ is normal there, whence $M$ is normal in $G$ (the base case $\abs G=p$ being abelian). The quotient $G/M$ has no subgroup other than $1$ and itself, by maximality of $M$, so it is cyclic of prime order; its order divides $\abs G$, a power of $p$, so $[G:M]=p$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #power}
(a) $G^pG'\le\Phi(G)$, where $\Phi(G)$ is the intersection of the maximal subgroups of $G$.

::: pf-proof
Let $M$ be maximal. By step [](#maximal){.pf-ref}, $G/M\cong\ZZ_p$, so for every $x\in G$ the class of $x$ satisfies $\bar x^p=1$, i.e. $x^p\in M$; hence $G^p=\langle x^p:x\in G\rangle\le M$. Since $G/M$ is abelian, $G'\le M$ as well. So $G^pG'\le M$ for every maximal $M$, and intersecting over the nonempty family of maximal subgroups (nonempty because $G$ is finite and nontrivial) gives $G^pG'\le\Phi(G)$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #quotient}
(a) $G/G^pG'$ is elementary abelian, and $\Phi(G/G^pG')=1$.

::: pf-proof
$G^p$ and $G'$ are characteristic in $G$, hence normal, so $N=G^pG'$ is normal and the quotient is a group. In it every element satisfies $\bar x^p=1$ by construction and the quotient is abelian because $G'\le N$; an abelian group of exponent $p$ is a vector space over $\ZZ_p$. A nonzero vector space is a direct sum of copies of $\ZZ_p$, and each summand is contained in a maximal subgroup (a hyperplane) that omits the others, so the intersection of all maximal subgroups is $0$; in dimension $0$ the group is trivial and its Frattini subgroup is itself, again trivial. So $\Phi(G/N)=1$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #reverse}
(b) $\Phi(G)=G^pG'$.

::: pf-proof
Let $N=G^pG'$, so $N\le\Phi(G)$ by step [](#power){.pf-ref}. The correspondence theorem puts the subgroups of $G/N$ in bijection with the subgroups of $G$ containing $N$, preserving normality and maximality; hence the maximal subgroups of $G/N$ are the images $M/N$ of the maximal subgroups $M$ of $G$ that contain $N$, and $\Phi(G/N)=\Phi(G)/N$ (every maximal subgroup contains $N$ because $N\le\Phi(G)$). By step [](#quotient){.pf-ref} $\Phi(G/N)=1$, so $\Phi(G)/N=N/N$ and $\Phi(G)=N$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #lift}
If $H\le G$ satisfies $H\Phi(G)=G$ then $H=G$.

::: pf-proof
Suppose $H<G$. As $G$ is finite, $H$ is contained in a maximal subgroup $M$ (enlarge $H$ to a maximal element of the set of proper subgroups containing it). Then $H\Phi(G)\le M$, since $\Phi(G)\le M$ by definition of $\Phi$ as the intersection of all maximal subgroups; so $H\Phi(G)\ne G$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #c-one}
(c) If $G$ is generated by $d$ elements then $G/\Phi(G)$ is a direct product of at most $d$ copies of $C_p$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#reverse){.pf-ref} $G/\Phi(G)=G/G^pG'$ is elementary abelian, hence a direct product of $r$ copies of $C_p$ for some $r\ge0$. If $G=\langle x_1,\dots,x_d\rangle$ then the classes of the $x_i$ generate the quotient, so the quotient has dimension $r\le d$.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #c-two}
(c) If $G/\Phi(G)$ is a direct product of $d$ copies of $C_p$ then $G$ is generated by $d$ elements.

::: pf-proof
Choose $x_1,\dots,x_d\in G$ whose classes form a basis of $G/\Phi(G)$ over $\ZZ_p$ and put $H=\langle x_1,\dots,x_d\rangle$. Every class in $G/\Phi(G)$ is a product of powers of the classes of the $x_i$, so for $g\in G$ the element $g(x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_d^{a_d})^{-1}$ lies in $\Phi(G)$; hence $H\Phi(G)=G$. By step [](#lift){.pf-ref} $H=G$, so $G$ is generated by $d$ elements.
:::

:::


::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
Parts (a), (b) and (c) hold as stated, "precisely $d$" meaning that $d$ is the minimal number of generators.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#maximal){.pf-ref} and [](#power){.pf-ref} give (a); steps [](#quotient){.pf-ref} and [](#reverse){.pf-ref} give (b); steps [](#c-one){.pf-ref} and [](#c-two){.pf-ref} give (c) for $d$ the number of copies of $C_p$. Reading "precisely $d$" as the minimal number: if $G$ needs exactly $d$ generators, then by step [](#c-one){.pf-ref} the quotient is a product of $r\le d$ copies of $C_p$, and by step [](#c-two){.pf-ref} the quotient being a product of $r$ copies would give $r$ generators, so minimality forces $r=d$.
:::
