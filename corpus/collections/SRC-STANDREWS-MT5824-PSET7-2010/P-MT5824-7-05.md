::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a finite $p$-group.

1. If $M$ is a maximal subgroup of $G$, show that $[G:M]=p$.
   Deduce that $G^pG'\le\Phi(G)$.

2. Use the preceding exercise to show that $\Phi(G)=G^pG'$.

3. Show that $G$ can be generated by precisely $d$ elements if and only if $G/\Phi(G)$ is a direct product of $d$ copies of $C_p$.
:::

::: {.solution}
A finite $p$-group is nilpotent, so a maximal subgroup $M$ is normal.
The quotient $G/M$ has no nontrivial proper subgroups, hence is cyclic of prime order; because its order is a power of $p$, it has order $p$.
Thus $x^p\in M$ for every $x\in G$, and $G'\le M$ because $G/M$ is abelian.
Intersecting over all maximal subgroups gives
\[
G^pG'\le\Phi(G).
\]

Set $N=G^pG'$.
Then $G/N$ is elementary abelian.
By the preceding exercise, $\Phi(G/N)=1$.
The correspondence theorem therefore gives maximal subgroups of $G$ whose intersection is $N$, so $\Phi(G)\le N$.
Hence
\[
\Phi(G)=G^pG'.
\]

Finally, $G/\Phi(G)$ is an elementary abelian $p$-group, say $C_p^r$.
Choose lifts in $G$ of a basis of this quotient.
Their subgroup together with $\Phi(G)$ is all of $G$; if the lifts generated a proper subgroup, it would lie in a maximal subgroup containing $\Phi(G)$, contradicting surjectivity to $G/\Phi(G)$.
Hence $G$ is generated by $r$ elements.
Conversely, every quotient of a $d$-generated group is $d$-generated, while $C_p^r$ requires $r$ generators.
Therefore the minimal number of generators of $G$ equals $r$.
:::
