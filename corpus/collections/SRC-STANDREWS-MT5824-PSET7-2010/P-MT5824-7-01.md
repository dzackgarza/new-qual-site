::: {.problem}
Show that $\gamma_2(G)=G'$.
Deduce that abelian groups are nilpotent.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set is present in this checkout, so the argument is the card's own. Recall the lower central series of a group $G$: $\gamma_1(G)=G$ and $\gamma_{n+1}(G)=[\gamma_n(G),G]$, where $[A,B]$ is the subgroup generated by the commutators $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ with $x\in A$, $y\in B$; the derived subgroup is $G'=[G,G]$. A group is nilpotent of class at most $c$ when $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #second-term}
$\gamma_2(G)=G'$.

::: pf-proof
By the recursion for the lower central series with $n=1$, $\gamma_2(G)=[\gamma_1(G),G]=[G,G]$; and $[G,G]$, the subgroup generated by all commutators of pairs of elements of $G$, is by definition the derived subgroup $G'$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #abelian}
If $G$ is abelian, then $\gamma_2(G)=1$.

::: pf-proof
For $x,y\in G$ one has $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}=xx^{-1}yy^{-1}=1$, the middle step by commutativity, so every commutator is trivial and the subgroup it generates is trivial; by step [](#second-term){.pf-ref} that subgroup is $\gamma_2(G)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Every abelian group is nilpotent, of class at most $1$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#abelian){.pf-ref} gives $\gamma_2(G)=1$, which is the defining condition $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=1$ for nilpotency with $c=1$; so $G$ is nilpotent of class at most $1$. The bound is attained exactly when $G\ne1$, since then $\gamma_1(G)=G\ne1$ and the class is $1$; for the trivial group the class is $0$.
:::
