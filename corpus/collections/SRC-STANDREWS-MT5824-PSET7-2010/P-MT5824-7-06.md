::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be nilpotent with lower central series
$$
G=\gamma_1(G)>\gamma_2(G)>\cdots>\gamma_c(G)>\gamma_{c+1}(G)=1.
$$
Suppose $N\triangleleft G$ is nontrivial.
Choose $i$ maximal such that $N\cap\gamma_i(G)\ne1$.
Show that
$$
[N\cap\gamma_i(G),G]=1,
$$
and deduce that $N\cap Z(G)\ne1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: $[N\cap\gamma_{i}(G),G]$ sits inside both $N$ and $\gamma_{i+1}(G)$ by normality and by the lower central series, so it is trivial.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #choice}

With $i$ maximal such that $N\cap\gamma_{i}(G)\ne1$, one has $N\cap\gamma_{i+1}(G)=1$.

::: pf-proof

By maximality, $N\cap\gamma_{j}(G)=1$ for every $j>i$, and in particular at $j=i+1$; the lower central series is descending, so this is the strongest statement available. Note $i\ge1$ exists because $N\cap\gamma_{1}(G)=N\ne1$ by hypothesis.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inside}

$[N\cap\gamma_{i}(G),G]\le N\cap\gamma_{i+1}(G)$.

::: pf-proof

$N$ is normal in $G$, so $[N,G]\le N$, hence $[N\cap\gamma_{i}(G),G]\le[N,G]\le N$. By the definition of the lower central series, $\gamma_{i+1}(G)=[\gamma_{i}(G),G]$, so $[N\cap\gamma_{i}(G),G]\le[\gamma_{i}(G),G]=\gamma_{i+1}(G)$. Being contained in both, the subgroup is contained in the intersection.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #central}

$[N\cap\gamma_{i}(G),G]=1$, hence $N\cap\gamma_{i}(G)\le Z(G)$.

::: pf-proof

Steps [](#choice){.pf-ref} and [](#inside){.pf-ref} give $[N\cap\gamma_{i}(G),G]\le N\cap\gamma_{i+1}(G)=1$. A subgroup $H$ with $[H,G]=1$ consists of elements commuting with every element of $G$, i.e. $H\le Z(G)$; take $H=N\cap\gamma_{i}(G)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}

$N\cap Z(G)\ne1$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#choice){.pf-ref}'s choice of $i$, $N\cap\gamma_{i}(G)\ne1$, and by step [](#central){.pf-ref} it lies in $Z(G)$. Hence $N\cap Z(G)$ contains the nontrivial subgroup $N\cap\gamma_{i}(G)$:
$$
\boxed{[N\cap\gamma_{i}(G),G]=1,\qquad N\cap Z(G)\ne1.}
$$

:::
