::: {.problem}
(a) Let $\{f_n\}$ be a sequence of analytic functions on a region $\Omega$ in $\CC$ and assume that for some function $g$ on $\Omega$, $f_n\to g$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega$.
Show that $f_n'\to g'$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega$ and that $g$ is analytic on $\Omega$.

(b) Explain what part (a) has to do with differentiation of power series.
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #gp}
$g$ is continuous on $\Omega$, and $\int_{\partial T}g=0$ for every triangle $T$ whose closed triangle lies in $\Omega$.

::: pf-proof
$g$ is a locally uniform limit of the continuous functions $f_n$, hence continuous. On $\partial T$, a compact subset of $\Omega$, the convergence $f_n\to g$ is uniform, so $\int_{\partial T}g=\lim_n\int_{\partial T}f_n=0$, each $f_n$ being holomorphic on a neighbourhood of $T$ so that $\int_{\partial T}f_n=0$ by Cauchy's theorem.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #gholo}
$g$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$.

::: pf-proof
$g$ is continuous and its integral over the boundary of every triangle in $\Omega$ vanishes (step [](#gp){.pf-ref}), which is the hypothesis of Morera's theorem.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #derivs}
$f_n'\to g'$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega$.

::: pf-proof
Let $K\Subset\Omega$ and choose $r>0$ with the closed $r$-neighbourhood $K_r$ of $K$ compactly contained in $\Omega$. For $z\in K$ the circle $\abs{\zeta-z}=r$ lies in $K_r$, and Cauchy's formula for derivatives applied to the holomorphic $f_n-g$ (step [](#gholo){.pf-ref}) gives
$$
f_n'(z)-g'(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\abs{\zeta-z}=r}\frac{f_n(\zeta)-g(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^2}d\zeta,
$$
so
$$
\abs{f_n'(z)-g'(z)}\le\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot2\pi r\cdot\frac{\sup_{\zeta\in K_r}\abs{f_n(\zeta)-g(\zeta)}}{r^2}=\frac1r\sup_{K_r}\abs{f_n-g},
$$
which is independent of $z\in K$ and tends to $0$ because $K_r$ is compact.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #powerseries}
For a power series $\sum a_nz^n$ with radius of convergence $R>0$ and sum $S$, one has $S'=\sum_{n\ge1}na_nz^{n-1}$ on $\abs z<R$.

::: pf-proof
Let $K$ be a compact subset of the disc $\abs z<R$ and choose $\rho<R$ with $K\subseteq\{\abs z\le\rho\}$. The partial sums $S_N$ are holomorphic, and $\sum a_nz^n$ converges uniformly on $\abs z\le\rho$ by the Weierstrass $M$-test with $M_n=\abs{a_n}\rho^n$ (summable because $\rho<R$). So $S_N\to S$ uniformly on $K$, and step [](#derivs){.pf-ref} gives $S_N'\to S'$ uniformly on $K$; but $S_N'=\sum_{n=1}^Nna_nz^{n-1}$, which is the differentiated series. Hence that series converges locally uniformly to $S'$, which is exactly term-by-term differentiation. (Its radius of convergence is $R$ as well, by the root test applied to $\sqrt[n]{n\abs{a_n}}$.)
:::

Parts (a) and (b).

::: pf-proof
