::: {.problem}
Evaluate the integral
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos x}{1+x^4}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Consider
\[
F(z)=\frac{e^{iz}}{1+z^4}
\]
and integrate over the upper semicircle. The arc contribution tends to zero
by Jordan's lemma. The poles in the upper half-plane are
\[
z_1=e^{i\pi/4},\qquad z_2=e^{3i\pi/4},
\]
and both are simple. Since $(1+z^4)'=4z^3$,
\[
\operatorname{Res}(F,z_k)=\frac{e^{iz_k}}{4z_k^3}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{1+x^4}\,dx
=2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^2\frac{e^{iz_k}}{4z_k^3}.
\]
Writing $a=1/\sqrt2$ and simplifying the two residues gives
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{1+x^4}\,dx
=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt2}e^{-a}(\cos a+\sin a).
\]
The imaginary part vanishes by oddness, and the cosine integrand is even.
Hence
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos x}{1+x^4}\,dx
=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt2}e^{-1/\sqrt2}
\left(\cos\frac1{\sqrt2}+\sin\frac1{\sqrt2}\right).}
\]
:::
