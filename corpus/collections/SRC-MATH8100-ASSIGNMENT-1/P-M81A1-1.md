::: problem
Let $C$ be the ternary Cantor set.

1. Find a point of $C$ that is not an endpoint of a removed interval.

2. Prove that $C$ is nowhere dense and has measure zero.

3. Prove that $C$ is uncountable by mapping its ternary digits $a_k\in\{0,2\}$ to binary digits $b_k=a_k/2$ and showing that the resulting function maps $C$ onto $[0,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A point of $C$ which is not a construction endpoint.
::: {.proof}
Take
\[
x=0.020202\ldots_3
=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2\,3^{-2j}
=\frac{2/9}{1-1/9}
=\frac14.
\]
Its ternary expansion uses only the digits $0$ and $2$, so $x\in C$.

Endpoints of the intervals occurring in the Cantor construction have a ternary expansion which is eventually constant in the allowed digits: after some stage the expansion has only $0$'s or only $2$'s. The expansion
\[
0.020202\ldots_3
\]
is not eventually constant, so $1/4$ is not such an endpoint.
:::

<1>2. $C$ is nowhere dense and has measure zero.
::: {.proof}
Let $C_n$ be the union of the $2^n$ closed intervals remaining after $n$ stages of the construction. Each such interval has length $3^{-n}$, hence
\[
m(C_n)=2^n3^{-n}=\left(\frac23\right)^n.
\]
Since
\[
C\subset C_n
\qquad\text{for every }n,
\]
we obtain
\[
0\le m(C)\le \left(\frac23\right)^n
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Letting $n\to\infty$ gives
\[
m(C)=0.
\]

Also,
\[
C=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}C_n,
\]
an intersection of closed sets, so $C$ is closed. A subset of $\mathbb R$ of measure zero cannot contain a nonempty open interval, hence $C$ has empty interior. Therefore
\[
\overline C=C
\]
has empty interior, which is exactly to say that $C$ is nowhere dense.
:::

<1>3. The digit map sends $C$ onto $[0,1]$.
::: {.proof}
Every $x\in C$ has a ternary expansion
\[
x=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_k3^{-k},
\qquad a_k\in\{0,2\}.
\]
Among expansions using only $0$ and $2$, this expansion is unique: the usual ambiguity of positional expansions replaces one digit by a digit smaller by $1$ and changes the tail from all $0$'s to all maximal digits; such a replacement would introduce the ternary digit $1$.

Define
\[
F(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_k2^{-k},
\qquad b_k=\frac{a_k}{2}\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Thus $F(x)\in[0,1]$.

Conversely, let $y\in[0,1]$. Choose a binary expansion
\[
y=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_k2^{-k},
\qquad b_k\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Set $a_k=2b_k$ and
\[
x=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_k3^{-k}.
\]
Then every $a_k$ is $0$ or $2$, so $x\in C$, and by construction
\[
F(x)=y.
\]
Hence $F:C\to[0,1]$ is surjective. Since $[0,1]$ is uncountable, $C$ must be uncountable as well.
:::
