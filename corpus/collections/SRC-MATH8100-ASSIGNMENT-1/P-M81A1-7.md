::: problem
Let $h_n(x)=x/(1+x)^{n+1}$ for $x\geq0$.

1. Prove that $h_n\to0$ uniformly on $[0,\infty)$.

2. Compute $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_n(x)$ and decide whether the series converges uniformly on $[0,\infty)$.

3. Prove that the series converges uniformly on $[a,\infty)$ for every $a>0$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $h_n\to0$ uniformly on $[0,\infty)$.
::: {.proof}
For $n\ge1$,
\[
h_n(x)=\frac{x}{(1+x)^{n+1}}.
\]
Differentiating,
\[
h_n'(x)
=\frac{1-nx}{(1+x)^{n+2}}.
\]
Thus the maximum occurs at $x=1/n$, and
\[
\|h_n\|_\infty
=h_n(1/n)
=\frac{1/n}{(1+1/n)^{n+1}}
=\frac{n^n}{(n+1)^{n+1}}
\le \frac1{n+1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|h_n\|_\infty\longrightarrow0,
\]
which is exactly uniform convergence of $h_n$ to $0$.
:::

<1>2. The series sums to a discontinuous function and is not uniformly convergent on $[0,\infty)$.
::: {.proof}
If $x>0$, then
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_n(x)
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x}{(1+x)^{n+1}}
\]
is geometric with first term $x/(1+x)$ and ratio $1/(1+x)$. Hence
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_n(x)
=\frac{x/(1+x)}{1-1/(1+x)}
=1.
\]
At $x=0$, every term vanishes, so the sum is $0$. Therefore the pointwise sum is
\[
S(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&x=0,\\
1,&x>0.
\end{cases}
\]
Each partial sum is continuous on $[0,\infty)$, whereas $S$ is discontinuous at $0$. A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, so the series cannot converge uniformly on $[0,\infty)$.
:::

<1>3. The convergence is uniform on every $[a,\infty)$ with $a>0$.
::: {.proof}
For $x>0$, the tail after the $N$th term is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}h_n(x)
&=
\frac{x}{(1+x)^{N+2}}
\frac{1}{1-1/(1+x)}\\
&=\frac{1}{(1+x)^{N+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence for $x\ge a>0$,
\[
0\le
\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}h_n(x)
\le \frac{1}{(1+a)^{N+1}}.
\]
The right-hand side tends to $0$ independently of $x\in[a,\infty)$, so the series converges uniformly there.
:::
:::
