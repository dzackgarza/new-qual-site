::: {.problem}
Let $(x_n)$ enumerate a countable set $X\subseteq\RR$.
Set $f_n(x)=1$ for $x>x_n$ and $f_n(x)=0$ for $x\leq x_n$.
Prove that
$$
f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f_n(x)}{n^2}
$$
is increasing on $\RR$ and continuous on $\RR\setminus X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise: the collection's provenance is the homework sheet alone, so the argument is the card's own. Let $(x_n)_{n\ge1}$ enumerate a countable set $X\subseteq\RR$, let $f_n$ be the indicator of $(x_n,\infty)$, that is $f_n(x)=1$ for $x>x_n$ and $f_n(x)=0$ for $x\le x_n$, and let
$$
f(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{f_n(x)}{n^2}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #converges}
The series defining $f$ converges for every $x$, indeed uniformly on $\RR$.

::: pf-proof
Each $f_n$ takes only the values $0$ and $1$, so $0\le f_n(x)/n^2\le1/n^2$. The $M$-test with $M_n=1/n^2$ applies because $\sum_nn^{-2}$ converges, and it gives uniform convergence of the series on $\RR$; in particular $f(x)$ is a well-defined real number.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #increasing}
$f$ is increasing.

::: pf-proof
Fix $n$. Each $f_n$ is the indicator of the interval $(x_n,\infty)$, so $x\le y$ implies $f_n(x)\le f_n(y)$. Multiplying by $1/n^2>0$ and summing, the partial sums satisfy $\sum_{n\le N}f_n(x)/n^2\le\sum_{n\le N}f_n(y)/n^2$, and passing to the limit in $N$ preserves the inequality because both sides converge by step [](#converges){.pf-ref}. Hence $f(x)\le f(y)$ whenever $x\le y$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #continuous}
$f$ is continuous at every $x\notin X$.

::: pf-proof
Let $x\notin X$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $N$ with $\sum_{n>N}n^{-2}<\varepsilon/2$, possible since the series converges. Since $x\ne x_n$ for every $n$, and this for each of the finitely many $n\le N$, there is $\delta>0$ such that $f_n$ is constant on $(x-\delta,x+\delta)$ for all $n\le N$: take $\delta_n=\abs{x-x_n}$ for those $n$ and let $\delta$ be their minimum, which is positive because the set is finite. Then for $\abs{y-x}<\delta$,
$$
\abs{f(y)-f(x)}\le\sum_{n\le N}\frac{\abs{f_n(y)-f_n(x)}}{n^2}+\sum_{n>N}\frac{\abs{f_n(y)-f_n(x)}}{n^2}\le0+2\sum_{n>N}\frac1{n^2}<\varepsilon,
$$
where the first sum vanishes by the choice of $\delta$ and the second is bounded by $2\sum_{n>N}n^{-2}$ because $\abs{f_n(y)-f_n(x)}\le1$ always. So $f$ is continuous at $x$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$f$ is increasing on $\RR$ and continuous on $\RR\setminus X$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#increasing){.pf-ref} gives monotonicity and step [](#continuous){.pf-ref} continuity at each point outside $X$, which are the two assertions of the problem. The hypothesis $x\notin X$ entered only in step [](#continuous){.pf-ref}, where it made the finitely many functions $f_n$ with $n\le N$ constant near $x$; at a point $x=x_m$ the series jumps by $1/m^2$, so $f$ is not continuous there.
:::
