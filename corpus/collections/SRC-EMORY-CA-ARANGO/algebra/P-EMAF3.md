::: problem
Fix a field $F$, a separable polynomial $f \in F[x]$ of degree $n \geq 3$, and a splitting field $L$ for $f$.
Prove that if $[L:F] = n!$ then:

(a) $f$ is irreducible.

(b) For each root $r$ of $f$, $r$ is the unique root of $f$ in $F(r)$.

(c) For every root $r$ of $f$, there are no proper intermediate fields $F \subset \mathfrak{L} \subset F(r)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $L$ is a splitting field of $f\in F[x]$ over $F$, so $L/F$ is normal.
::: {.proof}
Splitting fields are normal extensions [@DF04].
:::

<1>2. Since $f$ is separable and $L$ is generated by the roots of $f$, each generator is separable over $F$; hence $L/F$ is separable.
::: {.proof}
A splitting field of a separable polynomial is separable:
it is generated by finitely many separable elements
[@DF04].
:::

<1>3. Hence $L/F$ is Galois (normal and separable), so $|\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)| = [L:F] = n!$.
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1 and <1>2 show that the extension is Galois.
The order of a finite Galois group is its extension
degree [@DF04].
:::

<1>4. $\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$ embeds into $S_n$ (it permutes the $n$ roots), and $|S_n| = n!$.
::: {.proof}
An automorphism fixing $F$ permutes the roots.
If it fixes every root, it fixes the field they
generate over $F$, which is $L$, so it is the
identity. This proves faithfulness of the action.
:::

<1>5. Hence $\operatorname{Gal}(L/F) \cong S_n$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and <1>4 (a subgroup of $S_n$ of order $n!$ is all of $S_n$).
:::

**(a).**

<1>1. $S_n$ acts transitively on the $n$ roots.
::: {.proof}
$S_n$ is transitive.
:::

<1>2. Hence $f$ is irreducible.
::: {.proof}
Choose a root $r$ and let $m_r$ be its monic minimal
polynomial over $F$. It divides $f$. Every image
$\sigma(r)$ under the Galois group is a root of
$m_r$, since $\sigma$ fixes its coefficients.
Transitivity therefore makes all $n$ roots of
$f$ roots of $m_r$. Hence $n\leq\deg m_r\leq n$,
so $f$ is a constant multiple of $m_r$ and is irreducible.
:::

**(b).**

<1>1. Let $r$ be a root of $f$, and let $H = \operatorname{Gal}(L/F(r))$ be its stabilizer.
::: {.proof}
the stabilizer of $r$ under the $S_n$-action.
:::

<1>2. $H \cong S_{n-1}$ (the stabilizer of one point in $S_n$).
::: {.proof}
Restriction to the $n-1$ roots other than $r$
identifies its stabilizer with $S_{n-1}$: every
permutation of those roots extends uniquely by
fixing $r$.
:::

<1>3. The roots of $f$ lying in $F(r)$ correspond to the fixed points of $H$ acting on the $n$ roots.
::: {.proof}
The Galois correspondence gives $L^H=F(r)$,
so membership is equivalent to being fixed by
every element of $H$ [@DF04].
:::

<1>4. $H = S_{n-1}$ fixes exactly one point (the point $r$).
::: {.proof}
It fixes $r$. For any other root $s$, choose a
third root $t$, using $n\geq3$. The transposition
$(s\ t)$ fixes $r$ and belongs to $H$, but moves
$s$. Thus no root other than $r$ is fixed by all of $H$.
:::

<1>5. Hence $r$ is the unique root of $f$ in $F(r)$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3 and <1>4.
:::

**(c).**

<1>1. An intermediate field $F \subset \mathfrak{L} \subset F(r)$ corresponds to a subgroup $K$ with $H \le K \le S_n$ (where $H = S_{n-1}$).
::: {.proof}
This is the inclusion-reversing Galois correspondence
for the finite Galois extension $L/F$ [@DF04].
:::

<1>2. $S_{n-1}$ is a maximal subgroup of $S_n$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $H\subsetneq K\leq S_n$ and choose
$g\in K\setminus H$. Then $g(r)\ne r$.
Since $H$ permutes the other $n-1$ roots arbitrarily,
the $K$-orbit of $r$ includes every root.
Its stabilizer in $K$ is exactly $H$, since $H$
is the full stabilizer in $S_n$ and is contained
in $K$. Orbit-stabilizer gives
$|K|=n|H|=n(n-1)!=n!$, so $K=S_n$.
There is no proper overgroup of $H$ other than $S_n$.
:::

<1>3. Hence there is no subgroup strictly between $H = S_{n-1}$ and $S_n$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2.
:::

<1>4. Therefore there are no proper intermediate fields $F \subsetneq \mathfrak{L} \subsetneq F(r)$.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 (a), <1>5 (b), <1>4 (c).
:::
:::
