::: problem
Let $K$ and $L$ be finite fields with $K \subseteq L$.
Prove that $L$ is Galois over $K$ and that $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is cyclic.
:::

::: solution
Let $q=|K|=p^r$, where $p$ is the characteristic,
and put $n=[L:K]$. The degree is finite because $L$
is finite, and counting vectors in a basis gives
$|L|=q^n$.

<1>1. The field $L$ is the splitting field over $K$
of the separable polynomial $T^{q^n}-T$.

::: proof
For $a\ne0$ in $L$, Lagrange's theorem in the group
$L^\times$ of order $q^n-1$ gives $a^{q^n-1}=1$
[@DF04]. Thus $a^{q^n}=a$ for every $a\in L$,
including zero. These $q^n$ distinct elements are
all the roots of the degree-$q^n$ polynomial, so
$$
T^{q^n}-T=\prod_{a\in L}(T-a).
$$
The roots generate $L$ because they comprise all
its elements. The derivative is $-1$, since
$p\mid q^n$, so the polynomial is separable.
Consequently $L/K$ is Galois [@DF04].
:::

<1>2. The relative Frobenius $\sigma(a)=a^q$ generates
the Galois group.

::: proof
Iterating $(a+b)^p=a^p+b^p$ in characteristic $p$
shows that $a\mapsto a^q$ preserves addition.
It also preserves multiplication and one. Its
kernel is zero, and hence it is bijective on
the finite field $L$. For $a\in K$, Lagrange's
theorem in $K^\times$ gives $a^q=a$, also for
zero. Thus $\sigma$ is a $K$-automorphism.

Step <1>1 implies $\sigma^n=1$. If $0<j<n$ and
$\sigma^j=1$, every element of $L$ would be a root
of $T^{q^j}-T$. A nonzero polynomial of degree
$q^j<q^n$ cannot have $q^n$ distinct roots.
Hence $\sigma$ has order exactly $n$.
The finite Galois group has order $[L:K]=n$
[@DF04], so its elements are precisely
$1,\sigma,\ldots,\sigma^{n-1}$, proving cyclicity.
When $n=1$, this is the trivial cyclic group.
:::
:::
