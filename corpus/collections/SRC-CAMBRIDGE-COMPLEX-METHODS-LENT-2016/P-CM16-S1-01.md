::: {.problem}

(i) Where, if anywhere, in the complex plane are the following functions differentiable, and where are they analytic?

$\Im z$; $\abs{z}^{2}$; $\operatorname{sech} z$.

(ii) Let $f(z) = z^{5}/\abs{z}^{4}$ for $z \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$. Show that the real and imaginary parts of $f$ satisfy the Cauchy–Riemann equations at $z = 0$, but that $f$ is not differentiable at $z = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

For a complex-valued function $g$, write $D(g)$ for its set of points of complex differentiability and $A(g)$ for the set of points at which it is analytic. Write $z=x+iy$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The function $g(z)=\operatorname{Im}z$ is nowhere complex differentiable.


::: pf-proof

Here $g=u+iv$ with $u(x,y)=y$ and $v(x,y)=0$. Thus
$$
u_x=0,
\qquad
u_y=1,
\qquad
v_x=v_y=0.
$$
The Cauchy--Riemann equation $u_y=-v_x$ would require $1=0$. Hence the Cauchy--Riemann equations fail at every point, so $D(g)=\varnothing$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The function $g(z)=\abs{z}^2$ is complex differentiable exactly at $z=0$, with derivative $0$ there, and is nowhere analytic.


::: pf-proof

Here $u(x,y)=x^2+y^2$ and $v(x,y)=0$. The Cauchy--Riemann equations are
$$
2x=0,
\qquad
2y=0,
$$
so they can hold only at $(0,0)$. At the origin,
$$
\frac{g(h)-g(0)}{h}
=\frac{\abs{h}^2}{h}
=\overline h\longrightarrow0,
$$
so $g'(0)=0$. Thus $D(g)=\{0\}$. Since analyticity at a point requires complex differentiability on a neighborhood of that point, an isolated differentiability point cannot be an analytic point; hence $A(g)=\varnothing$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The function $g(z)=\operatorname{sech}z$ is analytic precisely away from the zeros of $\cosh z$.


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\operatorname{sech}z=\frac1{\cosh z},
$$
it is holomorphic wherever $\cosh z\ne0$. Moreover,
$$
\cosh z=0
\iff e^z+e^{-z}=0
\iff e^{2z}=-1
\iff z=\left(n+\frac12\right)\pi i
$$
for some $n\in\ZZ$. At those points $\operatorname{sech}z$ is not defined. Therefore its differentiability and analyticity sets are both the complement of this discrete set.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The answer to part (i) is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D(\operatorname{Im}z)&=A(\operatorname{Im}z)=\varnothing,\\
D(\abs{z}^2)&=\{0\},\qquad A(\abs{z}^2)=\varnothing,\\
D(\operatorname{sech}z)&=A(\operatorname{sech}z)
=\CC\setminus\left\{\left(n+\frac12\right)\pi i:n\in\ZZ\right\}.
\end{aligned}}
$$


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the combination of steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For the function in part (ii), the real and imaginary parts satisfy the Cauchy--Riemann equations at the origin.


::: pf-proof

Write $f=u+iv$. Since $f(0)=0$, one has $u(0,0)=v(0,0)=0$. Along the real axis,
$$
f(h)=\frac{h^5}{\abs{h}^4}=h
$$
for real $h\ne0$, while along the imaginary axis,
$$
f(ik)=\frac{(ik)^5}{\abs{k}^4}=ik
$$
for real $k\ne0$. Therefore
$$
u_x(0,0)=1,
\qquad
v_x(0,0)=0,
\qquad
u_y(0,0)=0,
\qquad
v_y(0,0)=1.
$$
Hence
$$
u_x(0,0)=v_y(0,0),
\qquad
u_y(0,0)=-v_x(0,0),
$$
which are the Cauchy--Riemann equations at $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The function $f$ is nevertheless not complex differentiable at $0$.


::: pf-proof

For $z\ne0$,
$$
\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z}
=\frac{z^4}{\abs{z}^4}.
$$
Along the positive real axis, $z=t$ with $t>0$, this quotient equals $1$. Along the ray $z=te^{i\pi/4}$ with $t>0$, it equals
$$
e^{4i(\pi/4)}=-1.
$$
The difference quotient therefore has no limit as $z\to0$, so $f'(0)$ does not exist.

:::
