::: {.problem}
By considering the integral of $f(z) = \cot z/(z^{2} + \pi^{2}a^{2})$ around a suitable large contour, prove that, provided $ia$ is not an integer,
$$
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{2} + a^{2}} = \frac{\pi}{a}\coth(\pi a).
$$

By considering a similar integral prove also that, if $a$ is not an integer,
$$
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n + a)^{2}} = \frac{\pi^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\pi a)}.
$$

Find an expression for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{2} + a^{2}}$ and take the limit as $a \to 0$ to deduce the value of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{2}}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let $C_N$ be the positively oriented rectangle with vertices
$$
\pm\pi\left(N+\frac12\right)
\pm i\pi\left(N+\frac12\right).
$$
For fixed $a$ with $ia\notin\ZZ$,
$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\oint_{C_N}
\frac{\cot z}{z^2+\pi^2a^2}\,dz
=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The identity
$$
\cot(x+iy)
=\frac{\sin 2x-i\sinh 2y}{\cosh 2y-\cos 2x}
$$
shows that $\abs{\cot z}\leq1$ on the vertical sides
$$
x=\pm\pi\left(N+\frac12\right),
$$
because there $\sin2x=0$ and $\cos2x=-1$. On the horizontal sides,
$$
\abs{\cot(x+iy)}^2
=\frac{\cos^2x+\sinh^2y}{\sin^2x+\sinh^2y}
\leq\coth^2\abs{y},
$$
so $\abs{\cot z}$ is bounded there uniformly in $N$.

Every point of $C_N$ has
$$
\abs{z}\geq\pi\left(N+\frac12\right).
$$
For sufficiently large $N$,
$$
\abs{z^2+\pi^2a^2}
\geq \abs{z}^2-\pi^2\abs{a}^2
\geq\frac12\abs{z}^2.
$$
The perimeter of $C_N$ is $O(N)$, while the integrand is therefore $O(N^{-2})$ uniformly on $C_N$. The contour integral is consequently $O(N^{-1})$ and tends to $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The residues of
$$
f(z)=\frac{\cot z}{z^2+\pi^2a^2}
$$
inside $C_N$, for all sufficiently large $N$, are
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=n\pi}f
=\frac1{\pi^2(n^2+a^2)}
\qquad (-N\leq n\leq N),
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=i\pi a}f
=\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i\pi a}f
=-\frac1{2\pi a}\coth(\pi a).
$$


::: pf-proof

The function $\cot z$ has a simple pole of residue $1$ at every $n\pi$. The condition $ia\notin\ZZ$ says precisely that neither zero $\pm i\pi a$ of $z^2+\pi^2a^2$ coincides with such a pole. Thus
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=n\pi}f
=\frac1{(n\pi)^2+\pi^2a^2}
=\frac1{\pi^2(n^2+a^2)}.
$$
At $z=i\pi a$,
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=i\pi a}f
=\frac{\cot(i\pi a)}{2i\pi a}.
$$
Since
$$
\cot(iw)=-i\coth w,
$$
this equals $-\coth(\pi a)/(2\pi a)$. The same computation at $-i\pi a$ gives the same residue.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Provided $ia\notin\ZZ$,
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac1{n^2+a^2}
=\frac\pi a\coth(\pi a)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the residue theorem and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\oint_{C_N}f(z)\,dz
=2\pi i
\left[
\frac1{\pi^2}
\sum_{n=-N}^{N}\frac1{n^2+a^2}
-\frac1{\pi a}\coth(\pi a)
\right]
$$
for all sufficiently large $N$. Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} shows that the left-hand side tends to $0$. The series on the right converges absolutely, so taking $N\to\infty$ and multiplying by $\pi^2$ gives the displayed identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Let $D_N$ be the positively oriented rectangle with vertices
$$
\pm\left(N+\frac12\right)
\pm i\left(N+\frac12\right).
$$
For $a\notin\ZZ$,
$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\oint_{D_N}
\frac{\pi\cot(\pi z)}{(z+a)^2}\,dz
=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The same estimates as in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, now applied to $\cot(\pi z)$, give a uniform bound on the numerator along $D_N$. For sufficiently large $N$, every point of $D_N$ also satisfies
$$
\abs{z+a}\geq cN
$$
for some constant $c>0$ independent of $N$. Hence the integrand is $O(N^{-2})$ on a contour of length $O(N)$, and the integral tends to $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The residues of
$$
g(z)=\frac{\pi\cot(\pi z)}{(z+a)^2}
$$
inside $D_N$, for all sufficiently large $N$, are
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=n}g
=\frac1{(n+a)^2}
\qquad (-N\leq n\leq N),
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=-a}g
=-\pi^2\csc^2(\pi a).
$$


::: pf-proof

The function $\pi\cot(\pi z)$ has a simple pole of residue $1$ at every integer $n$, giving the first formula. Because $a\notin\ZZ$, $z=-a$ is not one of those poles. It is a double pole of $g$, and its residue is the derivative at $-a$ of the holomorphic factor $\pi\cot(\pi z)$:
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=-a}g
=\evaluated{
\frac d{dz}\bigl(\pi\cot(\pi z)\bigr)
}_{z=-a}
=-\pi^2\csc^2(\pi a).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If $a\notin\ZZ$, then
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac1{(n+a)^2}
=\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2(\pi a)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the residue theorem and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
\oint_{D_N}g(z)\,dz
=2\pi i
\left[
\sum_{n=-N}^{N}\frac1{(n+a)^2}
-\pi^2\csc^2(\pi a)
\right].
$$
Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} makes the left-hand side tend to $0$. The series is absolutely convergent, so passage to the limit gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For $a\neq0$ with $ia\notin\ZZ$,
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2+a^2}
=\frac{\pi a\coth(\pi a)-1}{2a^2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The summand in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is even in $n$. Thus
$$
\frac1{a^2}
+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2+a^2}
=\frac\pi a\coth(\pi a).
$$
Solving for the one-sided sum gives the displayed expression.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The Basel sum is
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}
=\frac{\pi^2}{6}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For real $a>0$, the summands in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} increase to $1/n^2$ as $a\downarrow0$, so monotone convergence gives
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}
=
\lim_{a\downarrow0}
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2+a^2}.
$$
The Taylor expansions
$$
\sinh x=x+\frac{x^3}{6}+O(x^5),
\qquad
\cosh x=1+\frac{x^2}{2}+O(x^4)
$$
give
$$
\coth x
=\frac1x+\frac x3+O(x^3).
$$
Substituting $x=\pi a$ into step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} yields
$$
\frac{\pi a\coth(\pi a)-1}{2a^2}
=\frac{\pi^2}{6}+O(a^2),
$$
and the limit follows.

:::

::: {.remark title="Variations"}
For $a>0$, the one-sided sum is
$$
\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k^2 + a^2} = \frac{\pi \coth(\pi a)}{2a} - \frac{1}{2a^2}.
$$
The summand is even in $k$, so the two-sided sum of the first part equals $\frac1{a^2}+2\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k^2+a^2}$; solving for the one-sided sum gives the formula.
:::
