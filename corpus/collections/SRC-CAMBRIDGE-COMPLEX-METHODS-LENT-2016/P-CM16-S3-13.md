::: {.problem}
Using the equality $\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x^{2}} \, \mathrm{d}x = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi}$, find the Laplace transform of $f(t) = t^{-1/2}$. By integrating around a Bromwich keyhole contour, verify the inversion formula for $f(t)$. What is the Laplace transform of $t^{1/2}$?
:::

::: {.solution}

Use the principal branch of $p^{1/2}$, with
$-\pi<\arg p<\pi$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Laplace transform of $t^{-1/2}$ is
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L\{t^{-1/2}\}(p)
=
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt p}},
\qquad \operatorname{Re}p>0.
$$


::: pf-proof

First take $p>0$. With $x=\sqrt{pt}$,
$$
t=\frac{x^2}{p},
\qquad
dt=\frac{2x}{p}\,dx,
$$
so
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty e^{-pt}t^{-1/2}\,dt
&=
\frac2{\sqrt p}
\int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\,dx \\
&=
\frac2{\sqrt p}\cdot\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}
=
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt p}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both sides are analytic for $\operatorname{Re}p>0$, so the identity
extends throughout that half-plane.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The Bromwich inversion integral of the transform in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} is
$t^{-1/2}$ for every $t>0$.


::: pf-proof

Fix $\gamma>0$. The Bromwich integral is
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}
\int_{\gamma-i\infty}^{\gamma+i\infty}
e^{pt}\sqrt\pi\,p^{-1/2}\,dp.
$$
Close a truncated Bromwich contour to the left and keyhole the branch
cut $(-\infty,0]$. There are no poles inside the slit contour. The small
circle about $0$ contributes $O(\varepsilon^{1/2})$, and the large
closing arc tends to $0$ by the usual Jordan estimate for $e^{pt}$ on
a left semicircle, since $p^{-1/2}=O(R^{-1/2})$.

On the upper bank of the cut,
$$
p=-r+i0=re^{i\pi},
\qquad
p^{-1/2}=-ir^{-1/2},
$$
whereas on the lower bank,
$$
p=-r-i0=re^{-i\pi},
\qquad
p^{-1/2}=ir^{-1/2}.
$$
With the orientations induced by the positively oriented slit contour,
the sum of the two bank integrals tends to
$$
-2i\sqrt\pi
\int_0^\infty e^{-rt}r^{-1/2}\,dr.
$$
Since the total contour integral is $0$, the Bromwich-line integral is
therefore
$$
2i\sqrt\pi
\int_0^\infty e^{-rt}r^{-1/2}\,dr.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, with the Laplace parameter equal to $t$,
$$
\int_0^\infty e^{-rt}r^{-1/2}\,dr
=
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt t}.
$$
Hence
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}
\int_{\gamma-i\infty}^{\gamma+i\infty}
e^{pt}\sqrt\pi\,p^{-1/2}\,dp
=
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\pi}
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt t}
=
t^{-1/2},
$$
which verifies Bromwich inversion.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Laplace transform of $t^{1/2}$ is
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L\{t^{1/2}\}(p)
=
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2p^{3/2}}},
\qquad \operatorname{Re}p>0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
t^{1/2}=t\,t^{-1/2},
$$
the rule
$$
\mathcal L\{th(t)\}(p)
=
-\frac d{dp}\mathcal L\{h(t)\}(p)
$$
and step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} give
$$
\mathcal L\{t^{1/2}\}(p)
=
-\frac d{dp}
\left(\sqrt\pi\,p^{-1/2}\right)
=
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2p^{3/2}}.
$$

:::
