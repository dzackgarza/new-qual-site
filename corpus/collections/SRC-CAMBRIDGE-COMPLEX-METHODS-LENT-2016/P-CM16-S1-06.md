::: {.problem}
How many branch points does $f(z) = [z(z+1)]^{1/3}$ have? Draw some possible branch cuts in the complex plane and on the Riemann sphere.

Repeat for $f(z) = (z^{2} + 1)^{1/2}$.

$\ast$ Repeat also for $f(z) = [z(z+1)(z+2)]^{1/3}$ and $f(z) = [z(z+1)(z+2)(z+3)]^{1/2}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Regard the functions as multivalued algebraic functions on the Riemann sphere $\widehat{\CC}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For $P(z)^{1/n}$, a zero of $P$ of multiplicity $m$ is a branch point exactly when $n\nmid m$.


::: pf-proof

Near a zero $a$ of multiplicity $m$, write
$$
P(z)=(z-a)^m h(z),
\qquad h(a)\ne0.
$$
On a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of $a$, a local branch of $h(z)^{1/n}$ exists. Analytic continuation once around $a$ changes $(z-a)^{m/n}$ by the factor
$$
e^{2\pi i m/n}.
$$
The continuation returns to its original value exactly when this factor is $1$, equivalently when $n\mid m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $P$ has degree $d$, then infinity is a branch point of $P(z)^{1/n}$ exactly when $n\nmid d$.


::: pf-proof

Use the local coordinate $t=1/z$ at infinity. Then
$$
P(1/t)=t^{-d}H(t),
\qquad H(0)\ne0.
$$
Thus locally
$$
P(1/t)^{1/n}=t^{-d/n}H(t)^{1/n}.
$$
Continuation once around $t=0$ multiplies this by $e^{-2\pi i d/n}$, which is nontrivial exactly when $n\nmid d$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The branch-point sets on $\widehat{\CC}$ are
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
[z(z+1)]^{1/3}&:\ \{-1,0,\infty\},\\
(z^2+1)^{1/2}&:\ \{-i,i\},\\
[z(z+1)(z+2)]^{1/3}&:\ \{-2,-1,0\},\\
[z(z+1)(z+2)(z+3)]^{1/2}&:\ \{-3,-2,-1,0\}.
\end{aligned}}
$$


::: pf-proof

Every displayed finite zero is simple, so step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} makes each one a branch point. The respective polynomial degrees are $2,2,3,4$. Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} therefore makes infinity a branch point only in the first case, because $3\nmid2$, while $2\mid2$, $3\mid3$, and $2\mid4$ in the remaining cases.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For $[z(z+1)]^{1/3}$, a valid plane cut is the ray $(-\infty,0]$; on the sphere this is an arc joining $\infty$ to $-1$ to $0$.


::: pf-proof

The ray contains all three branch points when its endpoint at infinity is included. Its complement in the plane is simply connected and contains no zero of $z(z+1)$. Hence $z(z+1)$ has a holomorphic logarithm there, and
$$
\exp\left(\frac13\log(z(z+1))\right)
$$
defines a single-valued branch. A cut only along the finite segment $[-1,0]$ would not suffice: a loop surrounding that segment winds around two simple zeros, producing the nontrivial cube-root monodromy $e^{4\pi i/3}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For $(z^2+1)^{1/2}$, one may cut along the segment from $-i$ to $i$; equivalently, one may send each branch point to infinity along the two imaginary-axis rays.


::: pf-proof

The two finite branch points have square-root monodromy $-1$ individually. A loop surrounding both has total monodromy $(-1)^2=1$, so joining them by one cut, for example
$$
\{iy:-1\le y\le1\},
$$
gives a single-valued branch on the complement. On the Riemann sphere the same cut is an arc joining $-i$ to $i$. Another valid spherical arc can pass through infinity, which in the plane appears as the two rays $iy$ with $\abs{y}\ge1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For $[z(z+1)(z+2)]^{1/3}$, the segment $[-2,0]$ is a valid cut; on the sphere it is an arc joining the three finite branch points in order.


::: pf-proof

Any loop in the complement that winds nontrivially around this connected cut has the same winding number about all three points $-2,-1,0$. The resulting cube-root monodromy is therefore
$$
e^{2\pi i(3k)/3}=1
$$
for some $k\in\ZZ$. Thus analytic continuation is single-valued on the complement. Equivalently, a nonminimal but simpler cut is the ray $(-\infty,0]$, which also connects the three branch points to infinity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For $[z(z+1)(z+2)(z+3)]^{1/2}$, one may use the two cuts $[-3,-2]$ and $[-1,0]$; on the sphere these are two arcs pairing the four branch points.


::: pf-proof

Each cut joins two simple square-root branch points. A loop surrounding either cut has total monodromy $(-1)^2=1$, while the cuts prevent loops from circling one endpoint alone. Hence the square root is single-valued on the complement. Other pairings of the four branch points give equally valid cuts, and the nonminimal ray $(-\infty,0]$ is also valid.

:::
