::: {.problem}
Find the first three terms of the Laurent expansion of $f(z) = \csc^{2} z$ valid for $0 < \abs{z} < \pi$.

$\ast$ Show that the function $h(z) = f(z) - z^{-2} - (z + \pi)^{-2} - (z - \pi)^{-2}$ has only removable singularities in $\abs{z} < 2\pi$. Explain how to remove them to obtain a function $H(z)$ analytic in that region. Find a Taylor series for $H(z)$ about the origin and explain why it must be convergent in $\abs{z} < 2\pi$. Hence, or otherwise, find the three non-zero central terms of the Laurent expansion of $f(z)$ valid for $\pi < \abs{z} < 2\pi$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $B_n$ denote the Bernoulli numbers, defined by
$$
\frac{t}{e^t-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_n\frac{t^n}{n!},
$$
and for $m\geq0$ put
$$
c_m=
\frac{(-1)^m2^{2m+2}(2m+1)B_{2m+2}}{(2m+2)!}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For $0<\abs{z}<\pi$,
$$
\boxed{
\csc^2z
=\frac1{z^2}+\frac13+\frac1{15}z^2+O(z^4)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\frac{\sin z}{z}
=1-\frac{z^2}{6}+\frac{z^4}{120}+O(z^6),
$$
the binomial expansion gives
$$
\left(\frac{\sin z}{z}\right)^{-2}
=1+\frac{z^2}{3}+\frac{z^4}{15}+O(z^6).
$$
Multiplication by $z^{-2}$ gives the displayed terms. The only zeros of $\sin z$ in $\abs{z}<\pi$ occur at $z=0$, so this Laurent expansion is valid throughout the punctured disk $0<\abs{z}<\pi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The singularities of
$$
h(z)=\csc^2z-z^{-2}-(z+\pi)^{-2}-(z-\pi)^{-2}
$$
in $\abs{z}<2\pi$ are removable; after filling them in, the resulting function $H$ is holomorphic on $\abs{z}<2\pi$.


::: pf-proof

For $k\in\ZZ$ and $w=z-k\pi$,
$$
\sin z=(-1)^k\sin w,
$$
so step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} applied at $w=0$ gives
$$
\csc^2z
=\frac1{(z-k\pi)^2}+\frac13+O\bigl((z-k\pi)^2\bigr).
$$
Thus the principal part at every pole $k\pi$ is exactly $(z-k\pi)^{-2}$. The only poles of $\csc^2z$ in $\abs{z}<2\pi$ are $0$ and $\pm\pi$, and the three subtracted terms cancel precisely those principal parts. Hence $h$ has removable singularities at those points and no others in the disk. Defining $H$ there by the corresponding limits gives a holomorphic function on $\abs{z}<2\pi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The Taylor series of $H$ at the origin is
$$
\boxed{
H(z)=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\left(
c_m-\frac{2(2m+1)}{\pi^{2m+2}}
\right)z^{2m}},
$$
and it converges for $\abs{z}<2\pi$. In particular,
$$
H(z)
=\left(\frac13-\frac2{\pi^2}\right)
+\left(\frac1{15}-\frac6{\pi^4}\right)z^2
+\left(\frac2{189}-\frac{10}{\pi^6}\right)z^4
+\cdots .
$$


::: pf-proof

From
$$
\cot z=i\frac{e^{2iz}+1}{e^{2iz}-1}
$$
and the defining generating function for the Bernoulli numbers,
$$
\cot z
=\frac1z+
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^n2^{2n}B_{2n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n-1}
\qquad (\abs{z}<\pi).
$$
Differentiating and using $-(\cot z)'=\csc^2z$ gives
$$
\csc^2z
=\frac1{z^2}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_mz^{2m}
\qquad (0<\abs{z}<\pi).
$$
Also, for $\abs{z}<\pi$,
$$
\frac1{(z-\pi)^2}+\frac1{(z+\pi)^2}
=\frac1{\pi^2}
\left[
\frac1{(1-z/\pi)^2}
+\frac1{(1+z/\pi)^2}
\right]
=2\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{2m+1}{\pi^{2m+2}}z^{2m}.
$$
Subtracting these series gives the displayed Taylor coefficients of $H$. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, $H$ is holomorphic on $\abs{z}<2\pi$, so its Taylor series at $0$ converges throughout that disk. The stated first coefficients use $c_0=1/3$, $c_1=1/15$, and $c_2=2/189$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For $\pi<\abs{z}<2\pi$, the Laurent expansion is
$$
\csc^2z
=H(z)+\frac1{z^2}
+2\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(2m+1)\pi^{2m}z^{-2m-2},
$$
and its three nonzero central terms are
$$
\boxed{
\frac3{z^2}
+\left(\frac13-\frac2{\pi^2}\right)
+\left(\frac1{15}-\frac6{\pi^4}\right)z^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

When $\abs{z}>\pi$,
$$
\frac1{(z-\pi)^2}
=\frac1{z^2}\frac1{(1-\pi/z)^2},
\qquad
\frac1{(z+\pi)^2}
=\frac1{z^2}\frac1{(1+\pi/z)^2},
$$
so
$$
\frac1{(z-\pi)^2}+\frac1{(z+\pi)^2}
=2\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(2m+1)\pi^{2m}z^{-2m-2}.
$$
Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\csc^2z
=H(z)+z^{-2}+(z-\pi)^{-2}+(z+\pi)^{-2}.
$$
Combining the preceding series with the Taylor series in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is valid exactly on their common region $\pi<\abs{z}<2\pi$. The Laurent series is even, so the nonzero exponents nearest $0$ are $-2,0,2$. Their coefficients are respectively $1+2=3$, $1/3-2/\pi^2$, and $1/15-6/\pi^4$.

:::
