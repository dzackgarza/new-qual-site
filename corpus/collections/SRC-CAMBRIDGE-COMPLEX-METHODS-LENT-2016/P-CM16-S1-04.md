::: {.problem}
Verify that the function $\phi ( x , y ) = e ^ { x } ( x \cos y - y \sin y )$ is harmonic.
Find its harmonic conjugate and, by considering $\nabla\phi$ or otherwise, determine the family of curves orthogonal to $\phi ( x , y ) = c$ for a given constant c.

Find an analytic function f(z) such that $\operatorname { R e } f = \phi$ . Can the expression $f ( z ) = \phi ( z , 0 )$ be used to determine f(z) in general?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function $\phi$ is harmonic on $\RR^2$.

::: {.proof}
Differentiating gives
$$
\phi_x=e^x\bigl((x+1)\cos y-y\sin y\bigr),
$$
and hence
$$
\phi_{xx}=e^x\bigl((x+2)\cos y-y\sin y\bigr).
$$
Also,
$$
\phi_y=e^x\bigl(-(x+1)\sin y-y\cos y\bigr),
$$
so
$$
\phi_{yy}=e^x\bigl(-(x+2)\cos y+y\sin y\bigr).
$$
Therefore $\phi_{xx}+\phi_{yy}=0$ everywhere.
:::

<1>2. A harmonic conjugate of $\phi$ is
$$
\boxed{\psi(x,y)=e^x(x\sin y+y\cos y)+C},
\qquad C\in\RR.
$$

::: {.proof}
The Cauchy--Riemann equations require
$$
\psi_x=-\phi_y
=e^x\bigl((x+1)\sin y+y\cos y\bigr).
$$
Integrating with respect to $x$ gives
$$
\psi=e^x(x\sin y+y\cos y)+g(y).
$$
Differentiating this expression with respect to $y$ yields
$$
\psi_y=e^x\bigl((x+1)\cos y-y\sin y\bigr)+g'(y).
$$
Since this must equal $\phi_x$, one has $g'(y)=0$, so $g=C$.
:::

<1>3. At every regular point of a level curve $\phi=c$, its orthogonal trajectory belongs to the family
$$
\boxed{e^x(x\sin y+y\cos y)=k},
\qquad k\in\RR.
$$

::: {.proof}
By step <1>2 and the Cauchy--Riemann equations,
$$
\psi_x=-\phi_y,
\qquad
\psi_y=\phi_x.
$$
Consequently
$$
\nabla\phi\mathbin{\cdot}\nabla\psi
=\phi_x\psi_x+\phi_y\psi_y
=-\phi_x\phi_y+\phi_y\phi_x
=0.
$$
Thus the level curves of $\psi$ are orthogonal to the level curves of $\phi$ wherever the gradients are nonzero. Absorbing the additive constant in $\psi$ into the level value gives the displayed family.
:::

<1>4. An analytic function with real part $\phi$ is
$$
\boxed{f(z)=ze^z+iC},
\qquad C\in\RR.
$$

::: {.proof}
Using $z=x+iy$ and $e^z=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)$,
$$
ze^z
=e^x\bigl(x\cos y-y\sin y\bigr)
+i e^x\bigl(x\sin y+y\cos y\bigr).
$$
Its real part is $\phi$, and its imaginary part agrees with step <1>2 up to the arbitrary constant $C$. Since $ze^z$ is entire, the displayed $f$ is analytic on $\CC$.
:::

<1>5. For this particular $\phi$, the rule $f(z)=\phi(z,0)$ recovers the normalized choice $f(z)=ze^z$, but this rule does not work in general.

::: {.proof}
Here
$$
\phi(x,0)=xe^x,
$$
whose entire continuation obtained by replacing $x$ with $z$ is $ze^z$, the function from step <1>4 with $C=0$.

In general the values of the real part on the real axis do not determine the imaginary part there. For example, the harmonic function $\phi(x,y)=-y$ is the real part of the entire function $f(z)=iz$, but $\phi(x,0)=0$. The prescription $f(z)=\phi(z,0)$ would therefore give the zero function instead of $iz$.
:::

<1>6. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves harmonicity; steps <1>2--<1>4 give the harmonic conjugate, orthogonal trajectories, and analytic function; step <1>5 answers the final question.
:::
