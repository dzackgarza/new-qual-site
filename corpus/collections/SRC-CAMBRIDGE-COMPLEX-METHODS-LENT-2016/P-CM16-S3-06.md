::: {.problem}
Starting from the Laplace transform of $1$ (namely $p^{-1}$), and using only standard properties of the Laplace transform (shifting, etc.), find the Laplace transforms of the following functions: (i) $e^{-2t}$ (ii) $t^{3}e^{-3t}$ (iii) $e^{3t}\sin 4t$ (iv) $e^{-4t}\cosh 2t$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
\mathcal L\{h\}(p)=\int_0^\infty e^{-pt}h(t)\,dt.
$$
Starting from $\mathcal L\{1\}(p)=p^{-1}$, use the standard rules
$$
\mathcal L\{e^{ct}h(t)\}(p)=H(p-c),
\qquad
\mathcal L\{t^n h(t)\}(p)=(-1)^n H^{(n)}(p),
$$
where $H(p)=\mathcal L\{h\}(p)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For part (i),
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L\{e^{-2t}\}(p)=\frac1{p+2}},
\qquad \operatorname{Re}p>-2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply the exponential-shift rule to
$\mathcal L\{1\}(p)=1/p$ with $c=-2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For part (ii),
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L\{t^3e^{-3t}\}(p)=\frac6{(p+3)^4}},
\qquad \operatorname{Re}p>-3.
$$


::: pf-proof

Three differentiations of $p^{-1}$ give
$$
\mathcal L\{t^3\}(p)
=
(-1)^3\frac{d^3}{dp^3}\left(\frac1p\right)
=
\frac6{p^4}.
$$
The exponential-shift rule with $c=-3$ then replaces $p$ by $p+3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For part (iii),
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L\{e^{3t}\sin4t\}(p)
=
\frac4{(p-3)^2+16}},
\qquad \operatorname{Re}p>3.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using
$$
e^{3t}\sin4t
=
\frac{e^{(3+4i)t}-e^{(3-4i)t}}{2i},
$$
the shift rule applied to $1$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L\{e^{3t}\sin4t\}(p)
&=
\frac1{2i}
\left(
\frac1{p-3-4i}
-
\frac1{p-3+4i}
\right) \\
&=
\frac4{(p-3)^2+16}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For part (iv),
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L\{e^{-4t}\cosh2t\}(p)
=
\frac12\left(\frac1{p+2}+\frac1{p+6}\right)
=
\frac{p+4}{(p+2)(p+6)}},
\qquad \operatorname{Re}p>-2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
e^{-4t}\cosh2t
=
\frac12\left(e^{-2t}+e^{-6t}\right),
$$
linearity and step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, together with the same shift rule for
$e^{-6t}$, give the displayed transform.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} give the four requested Laplace transforms using only
the transform of $1$, exponential shifting, differentiation in $p$,
linearity, and the elementary exponential expressions for sine and
hyperbolic cosine.

:::
