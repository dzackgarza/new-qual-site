::: {.problem}
By integrating around a keyhole contour, show that

$$
\int _ { 0 } ^ { \infty } { \frac { x ^ { a - 1 } \mathrm { d } x } { 1 + x } } = { \frac { \pi } { \sin ( \pi a ) } } \qquad ( 0 < a < 1 ) .
$$

Explain why the given restrictions on the value of a are necessary.
:::

::: {.solution}

Use the branch
$$
z^{a-1}=\exp\bigl((a-1)\Log z\bigr),
\qquad 0<\arg z<2\pi,
$$
so the branch cut is the positive real axis. Let $C_{R,\varepsilon}$ be the positively oriented keyhole contour about that axis, with outer radius $R$ and inner radius $\varepsilon$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For
$$
F(z)=\frac{z^{a-1}}{1+z},
$$
the two straight sides of $C_{R,\varepsilon}$ contribute
$$
\left(1-e^{2\pi ia}\right)
\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x}\,dx.
$$


::: pf-proof

On the upper side of the cut, $z=x$ with $\arg z=0$, so
$$
z^{a-1}=x^{a-1},
$$
and the contribution is
$$
\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x}\,dx.
$$
On the lower side, $\arg z=2\pi$, so
$$
z^{a-1}=e^{2\pi i(a-1)}x^{a-1}
=e^{2\pi ia}x^{a-1}.
$$
That side is traversed from $R$ to $\varepsilon$, giving
$$
-e^{2\pi ia}
\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x}\,dx.
$$
Adding the two contributions proves the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $0<a<1$, the contributions from both circular arcs tend to $0$ as $R\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\to0$.


::: pf-proof

On the outer circle $\abs{z}=R>1$,
$$
\abs{F(z)}
\leq\frac{R^{a-1}}{R-1}.
$$
Its length is at most $2\pi R$, so the magnitude of the outer-arc integral is at most
$$
\frac{2\pi R^a}{R-1},
$$
which tends to $0$ because $a<1$.

On the inner circle $\abs{z}=\varepsilon<1$,
$$
\abs{F(z)}
\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{a-1}}{1-\varepsilon}.
$$
Its length is at most $2\pi\varepsilon$, so the inner-arc integral has magnitude at most
$$
\frac{2\pi\varepsilon^a}{1-\varepsilon},
$$
which tends to $0$ because $a>0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The only pole of $F$ inside the keyhole contour is $z=-1$, and
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=-1}F(z)
=e^{i\pi(a-1)}
=-e^{i\pi a}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The branch chosen before step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} has argument $\pi$ at $-1$. Since $1+z$ has a simple zero there,
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=-1}F(z)
=(-1)^{a-1}
=e^{i\pi(a-1)}.
$$
The last equality in the claim follows from $e^{-i\pi}=-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For $0<a<1$,
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^{\infty}\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x}\,dx
=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi a)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the residue theorem and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\oint_{C_{R,\varepsilon}}F(z)\,dz
\longrightarrow
-2\pi i e^{i\pi a}.
$$
By steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, the same limit is
$$
\left(1-e^{2\pi ia}\right)
\int_0^{\infty}\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x}\,dx.
$$
Since
$$
1-e^{2\pi ia}
=-2ie^{i\pi a}\sin(\pi a),
$$
division gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The restrictions
$$
\boxed{0<a<1}
$$
are exactly the conditions for convergence of the improper integral.


::: pf-proof

As $x\downarrow0$,
$$
\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x}\sim x^{a-1},
$$
whose integral near $0$ converges exactly when $a>0$. As $x\to\infty$,
$$
\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x}\sim x^{a-2},
$$
whose integral near infinity converges exactly when $a<1$. At $a=0$ or $a=1$ the corresponding divergence is logarithmic. These are also precisely the inequalities used in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} to make the keyhole arcs vanish.

:::
