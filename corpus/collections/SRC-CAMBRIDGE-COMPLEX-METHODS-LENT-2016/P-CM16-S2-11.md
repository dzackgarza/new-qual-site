::: {.problem}
*Starred problem.*

By integrating around a contour involving the real axis and the line $z = re^{2\pi i/n}$, evaluate
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{1 + x^{n}} \qquad (n \geqslant 2).
$$

Check (by change of variable) that your answer agrees with that of the previous question.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $n\geq2$ be an integer and let $C_R$ be the positively oriented boundary of the sector
$$
0\leq\arg z\leq\frac{2\pi}{n},
\qquad \abs{z}\leq R.
$$
Set
$$
F(z)=\frac1{1+z^n}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The two radial sides of $C_R$ contribute
$$
\left(1-e^{2\pi i/n}\right)
\int_0^R\frac{dx}{1+x^n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

On the positive real axis, the contribution is
$$
\int_0^R\frac{dx}{1+x^n}.
$$
On the ray $z=re^{2\pi i/n}$, the positive orientation runs from $r=R$ back to $r=0$. Since
$$
z^n=r^ne^{2\pi i}=r^n,
\qquad
dz=e^{2\pi i/n}\,dr,
$$
that side contributes
$$
-e^{2\pi i/n}
\int_0^R\frac{dr}{1+r^n}.
$$
Adding the two radial contributions proves the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The integral over the circular arc of $C_R$ tends to $0$ as $R\to\infty$.


::: pf-proof

For $R>1$ and $\abs{z}=R$,
$$
\abs{1+z^n}
\geq R^n-1,
$$
so
$$
\abs{F(z)}
\leq\frac1{R^n-1}.
$$
The arc has length $2\pi R/n$. Hence its integral has magnitude at most
$$
\frac{2\pi R}{n(R^n-1)},
$$
which tends to $0$ because $n\geq2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The sector contains exactly one pole of $F$, namely
$$
z_0=e^{i\pi/n},
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}F(z)
=-\frac{e^{i\pi/n}}{n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The poles satisfy
$$
z^n=-1,
$$
so they are
$$
e^{(2k+1)\pi i/n},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,n-1.
$$
Exactly the root with argument $\pi/n$ lies in the open sector $0<\arg z<2\pi/n$. It is simple, and therefore
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}F(z)
=\frac1{n z_0^{n-1}}.
$$
Since $z_0^n=-1$, one has $z_0^{n-1}=-z_0^{-1}$, giving the stated residue.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The integral is
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{1+x^n}
=\frac{\pi}{n\sin(\pi/n)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the residue theorem and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\oint_{C_R}F(z)\,dz
\longrightarrow
-\frac{2\pi i}{n}e^{i\pi/n}.
$$
Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} give
$$
\left(1-e^{2\pi i/n}\right)
\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{1+x^n}
=-\frac{2\pi i}{n}e^{i\pi/n}.
$$
Using
$$
1-e^{2\pi i/n}
=-2ie^{i\pi/n}\sin(\pi/n)
$$
and dividing yields the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The formula agrees with the previous card under the substitution $t=x^n$ and $a=1/n$.


::: pf-proof

The change of variable $t=x^n$ gives
$$
dx=\frac1n t^{1/n-1}\,dt,
$$
and hence
$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{1+x^n}
=\frac1n
\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{1/n-1}}{1+t}\,dt.
$$
Because $n\geq2$, one has $0<1/n<1$. The preceding card's keyhole formula with $a=1/n$ gives
$$
\frac1n\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi/n)},
$$
which is exactly the value in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

:::
