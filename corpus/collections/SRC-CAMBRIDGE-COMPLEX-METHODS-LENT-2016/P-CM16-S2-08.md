::: {.problem}
By integrating the function $z^n (z-a)^{-1} (z-a^{-1})^{-1}$ around the unit circle in the $z$-plane (where $a$ is real, $a > 1$, and $n$ is a non-negative integer), evaluate

$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\cos n\theta}{1 - 2a\cos\theta + a^2}\,d\theta .
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
D(\theta)=1-2a\cos\theta+a^2
$$
and let
$$
J_n=
\oint_{\abs{z}=1}
\frac{z^n}{(z-a)(z-a^{-1})}\,dz,
$$
with the unit circle positively oriented.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The contour integral satisfies
$$
J_n
=-ia\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{e^{in\theta}}{D(\theta)}\,d\theta.
$$


::: pf-proof

On $\abs{z}=1$, write $z=e^{i\theta}$, so $z^{-1}=e^{-i\theta}$ and $dz=iz\,d\theta$. Moreover,
$$
D(\theta)
=1-a(z+z^{-1})+a^2
=-\frac a z (z-a)(z-a^{-1}).
$$
Hence
$$
\frac{z^n}{(z-a)(z-a^{-1})}\,dz
=-ia\frac{z^n}{D(\theta)}\,d\theta,
$$
which gives the stated identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Since $a>1$,
$$
J_n
=-\frac{2\pi i\,a^{1-n}}{a^2-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The poles of the integrand defining $J_n$ are $a$ and $a^{-1}$. Because $a>1$, only $a^{-1}$ lies inside the unit circle. Its residue is
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=a^{-1}}
\frac{z^n}{(z-a)(z-a^{-1})}
=\frac{a^{-n}}{a^{-1}-a}
=-\frac{a^{1-n}}{a^2-1}.
$$
The residue theorem therefore yields the displayed value of $J_n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The required integral is
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{\cos(n\theta)}
{1-2a\cos\theta+a^2}\,d\theta
=\frac{2\pi}{a^n(a^2-1)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Combining steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{e^{in\theta}}{D(\theta)}\,d\theta
=\frac{i}{a}J_n
=\frac{2\pi}{a^n(a^2-1)}.
$$
The right-hand side is real. Taking real parts of the left-hand side therefore gives exactly the integral with numerator $\cos(n\theta)$.

:::
