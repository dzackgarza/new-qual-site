::: {.problem}
By considering $w(z) = (i + z)/(i - z)$, show that $\phi(x, y) = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{2x}{x^{2} + y^{2} - 1}$ is harmonic.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
w(z)=\frac{i+z}{i-z}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For $z=x+iy$ with $z\ne i$,
$$
w(z)=\frac{1-x^2-y^2-2ix}{x^2+(1-y)^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Writing $i+z=x+i(y+1)$ and $i-z=-x+i(1-y)$ and multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate of $i-z$ gives
$$
w(z)
=\frac{(x+i(y+1))(-x-i(1-y))}{x^2+(1-y)^2}
=\frac{1-x^2-y^2-2ix}{x^2+(1-y)^2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The map $w$ sends the unit disk into the right half-plane and the exterior of the closed unit disk into the left half-plane.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\operatorname{Re}w(z)
=\frac{1-\abs{z}^2}{\abs{i-z}^2}.
$$
Thus $\operatorname{Re}w(z)>0$ when $\abs{z}<1$, and $\operatorname{Re}w(z)<0$ when $\abs{z}>1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

On the unit disk,
$$
\phi(x,y)=\operatorname{Im}L_R(w(z)),
$$
where $L_R$ is the logarithm branch on the right half-plane with argument in $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\theta_R=\operatorname{Im}L_R(w(z))$. By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\tan\theta_R
=\frac{\operatorname{Im}w(z)}{\operatorname{Re}w(z)}
=\frac{-2x}{1-x^2-y^2}
=\frac{2x}{x^2+y^2-1}.
$$
Since $\theta_R\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, the principal arctangent in the definition of $\phi$ returns exactly $\theta_R$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

On the exterior domain $\abs{z}>1$,
$$
\phi(x,y)=\operatorname{Im}L_L(w(z))-\pi,
$$
where $L_L$ is the logarithm branch on the left half-plane with argument in $(\pi/2,3\pi/2)$.


::: pf-proof

Let $\theta_L=\operatorname{Im}L_L(w(z))$. The same quotient computation as in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\tan\theta_L=\frac{2x}{x^2+y^2-1}.
$$
Because $\theta_L\in(\pi/2,3\pi/2)$, one has $\theta_L-\pi\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ and
$$
\tan^{-1}(\tan\theta_L)=\theta_L-\pi.
$$
Hence the displayed formula for $\phi$ holds on $\abs{z}>1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The function $\phi$ is harmonic on each of the domains $\abs{z}<1$ and $\abs{z}>1$.


::: pf-proof

The Möbius map $w$ is holomorphic on both domains. By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} its image lies in a half-plane on which the corresponding logarithm branch is holomorphic. Therefore $L_R\circ w$ is holomorphic on $\abs{z}<1$ and $L_L\circ w$ is holomorphic on $\abs{z}>1$. The imaginary part of a holomorphic function is harmonic, and subtracting the constant $\pi$ does not affect harmonicity. Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} therefore prove the claim.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} establishes harmonicity on every connected component of the natural domain $x^2+y^2\ne1$.

:::
