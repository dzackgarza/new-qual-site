::: {.problem}
Show how the principal branch of log z can be used to define a branch of $z ^ { i }$ which is singlevalued on the domain $\mathcal { D } = \CC \backslash \RR ^ { - }$.
Evaluate $i ^ { i }$ for this branch.

Show, using polar coordinates, that the branch of $z ^ { i }$ defined above maps $\mathcal { D }$ onto an annulus which is covered infinitely often.

How would your answers change, if at all, for a different branch?
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
D=\CC\setminus(-\infty,0],
$$
and let $L_0(z)=\log\abs{z}+i\operatorname{Arg}z$ denote the principal logarithm, with $-\pi<\operatorname{Arg}z<\pi$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The principal-logarithm branch of $z^i$ is the single-valued holomorphic function
$$
Z_i(z)=\exp(iL_0(z)),
\qquad z\in D.
$$


::: pf-proof

The principal logarithm is single-valued and holomorphic on $D$, and the exponential is entire. Therefore their composition $Z_i$ is single-valued and holomorphic on $D$. By definition it is a branch of the multivalued power $z^i$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $z=re^{i\theta}$ with $r>0$ and $-\pi<\theta<\pi$, then
$$
Z_i(z)=e^{-\theta}e^{i\log r}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For the principal logarithm,
$$
L_0(z)=\log r+i\theta.
$$
Hence
$$
iL_0(z)=i\log r-\theta,
$$
and exponentiating gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For this branch,
$$
\boxed{i^i=e^{-\pi/2}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $\operatorname{Arg}i=\pi/2$, one has $L_0(i)=i\pi/2$. Therefore
$$
Z_i(i)=\exp\left(i\cdot\frac{i\pi}{2}\right)=e^{-\pi/2}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The image of $D$ is the annulus
$$
\boxed{\left\{w\in\CC:e^{-\pi}<\abs{w}<e^{\pi}\right\}},
$$
and every point of this annulus has infinitely many preimages.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{Z_i(re^{i\theta})}=e^{-\theta}.
$$
As $-\pi<\theta<\pi$, the modulus ranges exactly over $(e^{-\pi},e^{\pi})$.

Conversely, let $w=\rho e^{i\varphi}$ satisfy $e^{-\pi}<\rho<e^{\pi}$. Put
$$
\theta=-\log\rho,
$$
so $-\pi<\theta<\pi$. For every $n\in\ZZ$, define
$$
r_n=\exp(\varphi+2\pi n),
\qquad
z_n=r_ne^{i\theta}.
$$
Then $z_n\in D$, the points $z_n$ are pairwise distinct, and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives
$$
Z_i(z_n)
=e^{-\theta}e^{i\log r_n}
=\rho e^{i(\varphi+2\pi n)}
=w.
$$
Thus the annulus is covered infinitely often.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Every other logarithm branch on the same domain $D$ is
$$
L_k(z)=L_0(z)+2\pi i k,
\qquad k\in\ZZ,
$$
and the corresponding branch of $z^i$ is $e^{-2\pi k}Z_i(z)$.


::: pf-proof

Any two holomorphic logarithms on the connected domain $D$ differ by a constant in $2\pi i\ZZ$. Hence
$$
\exp(iL_k(z))
=\exp(iL_0(z)-2\pi k)
=e^{-2\pi k}Z_i(z).
$$
Consequently that branch gives
$$
i^i=e^{-\pi/2-2\pi k}
$$
and maps $D$ onto the radially rescaled annulus
$$
e^{-\pi-2\pi k}<\abs{w}<e^{\pi-2\pi k}.
$$
Multiplication by the nonzero constant $e^{-2\pi k}$ does not change the infinite covering multiplicity.

:::
