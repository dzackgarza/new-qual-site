::: {.problem}
Solve the integral equation $f(t) + 4\int_{0}^{t} (t - \tau)f(\tau) \, \mathrm{d}\tau = t$ for the unknown function $f$. Verify your solution.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
F(p)=\mathcal L\{f\}(p).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Laplace transform of the integral term is
$$
\mathcal L
\left\{
\int_0^t(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau
\right\}(p)
=
\frac{F(p)}{p^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The integral is the convolution of $f$ with the function $t$:
$$
\int_0^t(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau=(t*f)(t).
$$
Since
$$
\mathcal L\{t\}(p)=\frac1{p^2},
$$
the convolution theorem gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The transformed equation gives
$$
F(p)=\frac1{p^2+4}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Taking Laplace transforms in the integral equation and using step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
F(p)+\frac4{p^2}F(p)=\frac1{p^2}.
$$
Multiplying by $p^2$ gives
$$
(p^2+4)F(p)=1,
$$
hence the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The solution is
$$
\boxed{f(t)=\frac12\sin2t}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\mathcal L\{\sin2t\}(p)=\frac2{p^2+4},
$$
step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} inverts to the displayed function.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The function in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} satisfies the integral equation.


::: pf-proof

For this function, the convolution integral has transform
$$
\frac1{p^2(p^2+4)}
=
\frac14
\left(
\frac1{p^2}-\frac1{p^2+4}
\right).
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_0^t(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau
=
\frac14
\left(
t-\frac12\sin2t
\right).
$$
It follows that
$$
f(t)+4\int_0^t(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau
=
\frac12\sin2t
+
t
-
\frac12\sin2t
=
t,
$$
as required.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} solve the equation by Laplace transforms, and step
[](#s1-4){.pf-ref} verifies the resulting function directly.

:::
