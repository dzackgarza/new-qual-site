::: {.problem}
Solve the integral equation $f ( t ) + 4 \int _ { 0 } ^ { t } ( t - \tau ) f ( \tau ) \mathrm { d } \tau = t$ for the unknown function $f .$ Verify your solution.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
$$
F(p)=\mathcal L\{f\}(p).
$$

<1>1. The Laplace transform of the integral term is
$$
\mathcal L
\left\{
\int_0^t(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau
\right\}(p)
=
\frac{F(p)}{p^2}.
$$

::: {.proof}
The integral is the convolution of $f$ with the function $t$:
$$
\int_0^t(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau=(t*f)(t).
$$
Since
$$
\mathcal L\{t\}(p)=\frac1{p^2},
$$
the convolution theorem gives the displayed formula.
:::

<1>2. The transformed equation gives
$$
F(p)=\frac1{p^2+4}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Taking Laplace transforms in the integral equation and using step <1>1,
$$
F(p)+\frac4{p^2}F(p)=\frac1{p^2}.
$$
Multiplying by $p^2$ gives
$$
(p^2+4)F(p)=1,
$$
hence the claim.
:::

<1>3. The solution is
$$
\boxed{f(t)=\frac12\sin2t}.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since
$$
\mathcal L\{\sin2t\}(p)=\frac2{p^2+4},
$$
step <1>2 inverts to the displayed function.
:::

<1>4. The function in step <1>3 satisfies the integral equation.

::: {.proof}
For this function, the convolution integral has transform
$$
\frac1{p^2(p^2+4)}
=
\frac14
\left(
\frac1{p^2}-\frac1{p^2+4}
\right).
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_0^t(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau
=
\frac14
\left(
t-\frac12\sin2t
\right).
$$
It follows that
$$
f(t)+4\int_0^t(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau
=
\frac12\sin2t
+
t
-
\frac12\sin2t
=
t,
$$
as required.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>1--<1>3 solve the equation by Laplace transforms, and step
<1>4 verifies the resulting function directly.
:::
:::
