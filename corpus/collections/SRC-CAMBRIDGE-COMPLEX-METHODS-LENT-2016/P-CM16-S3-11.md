::: {.problem}
*Starred problem.*

The zeroth order Bessel function $J _ { 0 } ( t )$ satisfies the differential equation

$$
t J _ { 0 } ^ { \prime \prime } + J _ { 0 } ^ { \prime } + t J _ { 0 } = 0
$$

with $J _ { 0 } ( 0 ) = 1$ (and $J _ { 0 } ^ { \prime } ( 0 ) = 0$ from the equation).
Find the Laplace transform of $J _ { 0 }$ and deduce that $\begin{array} { r } { \int _ { 0 } ^ { \infty } J _ { 0 } ( t ) \mathrm { d } t = 1 } \end{array}$ . Find the convolution of $J _ { 0 }$ with itself.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
F(p)=\mathcal L\{J_0\}(p).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Bessel equation transforms to
$$
(p^2+1)F'(p)+pF(p)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Using $J_0(0)=1$ and $J_0'(0)=0$,
$$
\mathcal L\{J_0'\}=pF-1
$$
and
$$
\mathcal L\{J_0''\}=p^2F-p.
$$
For any transform $H(p)=\mathcal L\{h\}(p)$,
$$
\mathcal L\{th(t)\}(p)=-H'(p).
$$
Hence
$$
\mathcal L\{tJ_0''\}
=
-\frac d{dp}(p^2F-p)
=
-2pF-p^2F'+1,
$$
while
$$
\mathcal L\{tJ_0\}=-F'.
$$
Transforming
$tJ_0''+J_0'+tJ_0=0$ therefore gives
$$
-2pF-p^2F'+1+pF-1-F'=0,
$$
which is the claimed equation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The Laplace transform of $J_0$ is
$$
\boxed{
F(p)=\frac1{\sqrt{p^2+1}}},
\qquad p>0,
$$
with the square root positive for positive real $p$.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\frac{F'(p)}{F(p)}
=
-\frac{p}{p^2+1}.
$$
Integration yields
$$
F(p)=\frac{C}{\sqrt{p^2+1}}.
$$
For $t>0$, the differential equation gives
$$
J_0''+\frac1tJ_0'+J_0=0.
$$
Hence, with
$$
E(t)=J_0(t)^2+J_0'(t)^2,
$$
one has
$$
E'(t)
=
2J_0'(t)\bigl(J_0(t)+J_0''(t)\bigr)
=
-\frac2tJ_0'(t)^2
\leq0.
$$
Since $E(t)\to1$ as $t\downarrow0$, it follows that
$\abs{J_0(t)}\leq1$ for $t\geq0$. Therefore, after the change of variables
$u=pt$,
$$
pF(p)
=
\int_0^\infty e^{-u}J_0(u/p)\,du
\longrightarrow
\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\,du
=
1
$$
as $p\to\infty$, by dominated convergence. On the other hand,
$C/\sqrt{p^2+1}$ has
$$
\lim_{p\to\infty}pF(p)=C.
$$
Thus $C=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The integral of $J_0$ over the positive half-line is
$$
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty J_0(t)\,dt=1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

First prove convergence. Put
$$
v(t)=\sqrt t\,J_0(t),
\qquad t\geq1.
$$
A direct substitution into the Bessel equation gives
$$
v''+\left(1+\frac1{4t^2}\right)v=0.
$$
Set
$$
E_v(t)
=
v'(t)^2+\left(1+\frac1{4t^2}\right)v(t)^2.
$$
Then
$$
E_v'(t)
=
-\frac1{2t^3}v(t)^2
\leq0.
$$
Thus $v$ and $v'$ are bounded on $[1,\infty)$, and consequently
$$
J_0(t)=O(t^{-1/2}),
\qquad
J_0'(t)=O(t^{-1/2}).
$$
The Bessel equation also gives
$$
J_0=-J_0''-\frac1tJ_0'.
$$
Therefore, for $R>1$,
$$
\int_1^R J_0(t)\,dt
=
-J_0'(R)+J_0'(1)
-\int_1^R\frac{J_0'(t)}t\,dt.
$$
The first term tends to $0$, while the last integral converges
absolutely because $J_0'(t)/t=O(t^{-3/2})$. Hence
$\int_0^\infty J_0(t)\,dt$ converges.

Let
$$
S(T)=\int_0^T J_0(t)\,dt,
\qquad
I=\lim_{T\to\infty}S(T).
$$
Integration by parts and the change of variables $u=pt$ give
$$
F(p)
=
p\int_0^\infty e^{-pt}S(t)\,dt
=
\int_0^\infty e^{-u}S(u/p)\,du.
$$
Since $S$ is bounded and $S(u/p)\to I$ for every $u>0$ as
$p\downarrow0$, dominated convergence yields
$$
\lim_{p\downarrow0}F(p)=I.
$$
By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\lim_{p\downarrow0}F(p)=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The self-convolution of $J_0$ is
$$
\boxed{
(J_0*J_0)(t)
=
\int_0^tJ_0(\tau)J_0(t-\tau)\,d\tau
=
\sin t}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By the convolution theorem and step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\mathcal L\{J_0*J_0\}(p)
=
F(p)^2
=
\frac1{p^2+1}.
$$
Since
$$
\mathcal L\{\sin t\}(p)=\frac1{p^2+1},
$$
uniqueness of the Laplace transform gives the claim.

:::
