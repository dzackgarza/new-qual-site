::: {.problem}
*Starred problem.*

The gamma and beta functions are defined for $z , w \in \CC$ by

$$
\Gamma ( z ) = \int _ { 0 } ^ { \infty } t ^ { z - 1 } e ^ { - t } \mathrm { d } t \quad \mathrm { a n d } \quad B ( z , w ) = \int _ { 0 } ^ { 1 } t ^ { z - 1 } ( 1 - t ) ^ { w - 1 } \mathrm { d } t
$$

when Re z, Re w > 0 (and by analytic continuation elsewhere).
Show that $\Gamma ( z + 1 ) = z \Gamma ( z )$ and hence that $\Gamma ( n + 1 ) = n !$ if n is a positive integer.
Using the previous question, write down the value of $\Gamma ( \frac 1 2 )$

For a fixed value of $z ,$ find the Laplace transform of $f ( t ) = t ^ { z - 1 }$ in terms of $\Gamma ( z )$ . Find the Laplace transform of the convolution $t ^ { z - 1 } * t ^ { w - 1 }$ . Hence establish that

$$
B ( z , w ) = { \frac { \Gamma ( z ) \Gamma ( w ) } { \Gamma ( z + w ) } } .
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

For positive real $t$ and complex $z$, write
$t^z=e^{z\log t}$ with the real logarithm.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For $\operatorname{Re}z>0$,
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Integration by parts gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(z+1)
&=
\int_0^\infty t^ze^{-t}\,dt \\
&=
\left[-t^ze^{-t}\right]_0^\infty
+z\int_0^\infty t^{z-1}e^{-t}\,dt.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary term vanishes. Indeed,
$\abs{t^z}=t^{\operatorname{Re}z}\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$, while
$t^{\operatorname{Re}z}e^{-t}\to0$ as $t\to\infty$.
The remaining integral is $z\Gamma(z)$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For every positive integer $n$,
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(n+1)=n!}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Directly,
$$
\Gamma(1)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\,dt=1.
$$
Repeated application of step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\Gamma(n+1)
=
n\Gamma(n)
=
n(n-1)\Gamma(n-1)
=
\cdots
=
n!\Gamma(1)
=
n!.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The half-integer value is
$$
\boxed{\Gamma\left(\frac12\right)=\sqrt\pi}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The preceding card gives
$$
\mathcal L\{t^{-1/2}\}(p)
=
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt p}.
$$
Setting $p=1$ in the defining Laplace integral gives
$$
\Gamma\left(\frac12\right)
=
\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}e^{-t}\,dt
=
\sqrt\pi.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For $\operatorname{Re}z>0$ and $p>0$,
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L\{t^{z-1}\}(p)
=
\frac{\Gamma(z)}{p^z}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

With $u=pt$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L\{t^{z-1}\}(p)
&=
\int_0^\infty e^{-pt}t^{z-1}\,dt \\
&=
p^{-z}
\int_0^\infty e^{-u}u^{z-1}\,du \\
&=
p^{-z}\Gamma(z).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For $\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Re}w>0$, the convolution
of $t^{z-1}$ and $t^{w-1}$ is
$$
(t^{z-1}*t^{w-1})(t)
=
B(z,w)t^{z+w-1},
$$
and its Laplace transform is
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L\{t^{z-1}*t^{w-1}\}(p)
=
\frac{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(w)}{p^{z+w}}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The convolution theorem and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} give
$$
\mathcal L\{t^{z-1}*t^{w-1}\}(p)
=
\frac{\Gamma(z)}{p^z}
\cdot
\frac{\Gamma(w)}{p^w}
=
\frac{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(w)}{p^{z+w}}.
$$
On the other hand, substituting $\tau=tu$ in the convolution integral
gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
(t^{z-1}*t^{w-1})(t)
&=
\int_0^t
\tau^{z-1}(t-\tau)^{w-1}\,d\tau \\
&=
t^{z+w-1}
\int_0^1u^{z-1}(1-u)^{w-1}\,du \\
&=
B(z,w)t^{z+w-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

For $\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Re}w>0$,
$$
\boxed{
B(z,w)
=
\frac{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(w)}{\Gamma(z+w)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

First take $z,w>0$ real. By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} and then step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} applied with
parameter $z+w$,
$$
\frac{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(w)}{p^{z+w}}
=
\mathcal L\{B(z,w)t^{z+w-1}\}(p)
=
B(z,w)\frac{\Gamma(z+w)}{p^{z+w}}.
$$
Since $\Gamma(z+w)>0$ for positive real $z,w$, multiplication by
$p^{z+w}$ and division by $\Gamma(z+w)$ give the claimed identity on
the positive real quadrant.

The defining integrals make $B(z,w)$ and $\Gamma(z)$ holomorphic for
$\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Re}w>0$. Thus the equivalent identity
$$
B(z,w)\Gamma(z+w)=\Gamma(z)\Gamma(w)
$$
extends from positive real $z,w$ to the whole product of right
half-planes by applying the one-variable identity theorem successively
in $z$ and $w$. The displayed quotient identity is the corresponding
form of this analytic identity, and then continues wherever the gamma
functions are continued.

:::
