::: {.problem}
*Starred problem.*

Let $P(z)$ be a non-constant polynomial. Consider the contour integral
$$
I = \oint_{\gamma} \frac{P'(z)}{P(z)} \, \mathrm{d}z.
$$

Show that, if $\gamma$ is a contour that encloses no zeros of $P$, then $I = 0$.

Evaluate the limit of $I$ as $R \to \infty$, where $\gamma$ is the circle $\abs{z} = R$, and deduce that $P$ has at least one zero in the complex plane.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
P(z)=a_nz^n+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0,
\qquad a_n\neq0,
$$
where $n\geq1$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If a closed contour $\gamma$ and its interior contain no zero of $P$, then
$$
\boxed{
\oint_\gamma\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)}\,dz=0}.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $P$ has no zero on or inside $\gamma$, then $1/P$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the region bounded by $\gamma$. Hence
$$
\frac{P'}P
$$
is holomorphic there. Cauchy's theorem therefore gives the stated integral.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

As $\abs{z}\to\infty$,
$$
\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)}
=\frac nz+O(\abs{z}^{-2}).
$$


::: pf-proof

Factoring out the leading powers gives
$$
P(z)
=a_nz^n
\left(
1+\frac{a_{n-1}}{a_nz}+O(\abs{z}^{-2})
\right)
$$
and
$$
P'(z)
=na_nz^{n-1}
\left(
1+O(\abs{z}^{-1})
\right).
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\frac{zP'(z)}{P(z)}
=
\frac{
n+(n-1)(a_{n-1}/a_n)z^{-1}+O(\abs{z}^{-2})
}{
1+(a_{n-1}/a_n)z^{-1}+O(\abs{z}^{-2})
}
=n+O(\abs{z}^{-1}),
$$
uniformly in the argument of $z$. Division by $z$ yields the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $\gamma_R$ is the positively oriented circle $\abs{z}=R$, then
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\oint_{\gamma_R}
\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)}\,dz
=2\pi i n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref},
$$
\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)}
=\frac nz+E(z),
\qquad
\abs{E(z)}\leq\frac C{R^2}
$$
on $\abs{z}=R$ for all sufficiently large $R$. Therefore
$$
\oint_{\gamma_R}\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)}\,dz
=n\oint_{\gamma_R}\frac{dz}{z}
+\oint_{\gamma_R}E(z)\,dz.
$$
The first integral is $2\pi i n$, while the second has magnitude at most
$$
2\pi R\frac C{R^2}
=\frac{2\pi C}{R},
$$
which tends to $0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The polynomial $P$ has at least one zero in $\CC$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that $P$ has no zero in $\CC$. Then every circle $\gamma_R$ encloses no zero of $P$, so step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\oint_{\gamma_R}\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)}\,dz=0
$$
for every $R>0$. Its limit would therefore be $0$. Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} says that the same limit is $2\pi i n$, which is nonzero because $n\geq1$. This contradiction proves that $P$ has a complex zero.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} evaluate the contour integral in the two required regimes, and step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} deduces the existence of a zero.

:::
