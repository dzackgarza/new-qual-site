::: {.problem}
Using partial fractions and expressions for the Laplace transforms of elementary functions, find the inverse Laplace transform of $\widehat { f } ( p ) = ( p + 3 ) / \{ ( p - 2 ) ( p ^ { 2 } + 1 ) \}$ . Verify this result using the Bromwich inversion formula.
:::

::: {.solution}

Write
$$
\widehat f(p)=\frac{p+3}{(p-2)(p^2+1)}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The partial-fraction decomposition is
$$
\widehat f(p)
=
\frac1{p-2}
-\frac{p}{p^2+1}
-\frac1{p^2+1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
\frac{p+3}{(p-2)(p^2+1)}
=
\frac{A}{p-2}+\frac{Bp+C}{p^2+1}.
$$
Clearing denominators gives
$$
p+3=(A+B)p^2+(C-2B)p+(A-2C).
$$
Hence
$$
A+B=0,
\qquad
C-2B=1,
\qquad
A-2C=3,
$$
whose solution is $A=1$, $B=-1$, $C=-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The inverse Laplace transform is
$$
\boxed{f(t)=e^{2t}-\cos t-\sin t},
\qquad t\geq0.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} and the elementary transforms
$$
\mathcal L\{e^{2t}\}(p)=\frac1{p-2},
\qquad
\mathcal L\{\cos t\}(p)=\frac{p}{p^2+1},
\qquad
\mathcal L\{\sin t\}(p)=\frac1{p^2+1},
$$
linearity gives the displayed inverse transform.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Bromwich inversion gives the same function for $t>0$.


::: pf-proof

Choose $\gamma>2$. The Bromwich formula is
$$
f(t)=\frac1{2\pi i}
\int_{\gamma-i\infty}^{\gamma+i\infty}
e^{pt}\widehat f(p)\,dp.
$$
For $t>0$, close the truncated vertical segment by a semicircle in the
left half-plane. Since
$$
\widehat f(p)=O(\abs{p}^{-2}),
$$
the closing-arc integral is $O(R^{-1})$: on that semicircle
$\abs{e^{pt}}\leq e^{\gamma t}$, while the arc has length $\pi R$.
Thus the closing contribution tends to $0$.

For sufficiently large $R$, the contour encloses the three poles
$2$, $i$, and $-i$. Their residues in
$e^{pt}\widehat f(p)$ are
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{p=2}
e^{pt}\widehat f(p)=e^{2t},
$$
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{p=i}
e^{pt}\widehat f(p)
=
-\frac{1+i}{2i}e^{it},
\qquad
\operatorname{Res}_{p=-i}
e^{pt}\widehat f(p)
=
\frac{1-i}{2i}e^{-it}.
$$
The last two residues sum to
$$
-\cos t-\sin t.
$$
Therefore the residue theorem gives
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}
\int_{\gamma-i\infty}^{\gamma+i\infty}
e^{pt}\widehat f(p)\,dp
=
e^{2t}-\cos t-\sin t,
$$
which agrees with step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}. The value at $t=0$ follows by continuity.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} gives the requested inverse Laplace transform, and step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref}
verifies it directly from the Bromwich inversion formula.
