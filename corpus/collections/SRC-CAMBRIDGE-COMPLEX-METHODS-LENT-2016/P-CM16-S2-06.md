::: {.problem}
(i) Show that if $f(z)$ is analytic, then the residue of $f(z)/(z - z_{0})$ at $z = z_{0}$ is $f(z_{0})$.

(ii) Show that if $1/f(z)$ has a simple pole at $z = z_{0}$, then its residue at $z = z_{0}$ is $1/f'(z_{0})$.

(iii) Show that if $h(z)$ has a simple zero at $z = z_{0}$ and $g(z)$ is analytic and non-zero, the residue of $g(z)/h(z)$ at $z = z_{0}$ is $g(z_{0})/h'(z_{0})$.

(iv) Prove the formula for the residue of a function $f(z)$ that has a pole of order $N$ at $z = z_{0}$:
$$
\lim_{z \to z_{0}} \left\{ \frac{1}{(N-1)!} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{N-1}}{\mathrm{d}z^{N-1}} \bigl((z - z_{0})^{N} f(z)\bigr) \right\}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If $f$ is holomorphic near $z_0$, then
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}
=f(z_0)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The Taylor expansion of $f$ at $z_0$ is
$$
f(z)=f(z_0)+f'(z_0)(z-z_0)+\cdots .
$$
Hence
$$
\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}
=\frac{f(z_0)}{z-z_0}+f'(z_0)+\cdots .
$$
The coefficient of $(z-z_0)^{-1}$ is therefore $f(z_0)$, which is the residue.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If $f$ is holomorphic near $z_0$ and $1/f$ has a simple pole there, then
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\frac1{f(z)}
=\frac1{f'(z_0)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $1/f$ has a simple pole at $z_0$, $f$ has a simple zero there. Thus
$$
f(z)=(z-z_0)u(z)
$$
for a holomorphic function $u$ with $u(z_0)\neq0$. Differentiating the factorization and evaluating at $z_0$ gives
$$
u(z_0)=f'(z_0).
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac1{f(z)}
=\frac{1/u(z)}{z-z_0}.
$$
Applying step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} to the holomorphic function $1/u$ gives residue
$$
\frac1{u(z_0)}=\frac1{f'(z_0)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If $h$ has a simple zero at $z_0$ and $g$ is holomorphic there, then
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\frac{g(z)}{h(z)}
=\frac{g(z_0)}{h'(z_0)}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
h(z)=(z-z_0)v(z),
$$
where $v$ is holomorphic and
$$
v(z_0)=h'(z_0)\neq0.
$$
Then $g/v$ is holomorphic near $z_0$ and
$$
\frac{g(z)}{h(z)}
=\frac{g(z)/v(z)}{z-z_0}.
$$
Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} yields
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\frac{g(z)}{h(z)}
=\frac{g(z_0)}{v(z_0)}
=\frac{g(z_0)}{h'(z_0)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

If $f$ has a pole of order $N$ at $z_0$, then
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}f(z)
=
\lim_{z\to z_0}
\frac1{(N-1)!}
\frac{d^{N-1}}{dz^{N-1}}
\left((z-z_0)^Nf(z)\right)}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write the Laurent expansion
$$
f(z)
=\sum_{n=-N}^{\infty}a_n(z-z_0)^n,
\qquad a_{-N}\neq0.
$$
Then
$$
\phi(z):=(z-z_0)^Nf(z)
=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m-N}(z-z_0)^m
$$
is holomorphic near $z_0$. The residue of $f$ is $a_{-1}$, and this is the coefficient of $(z-z_0)^{N-1}$ in the Taylor series of $\phi$. Therefore
$$
a_{-1}
=\frac{\phi^{(N-1)}(z_0)}{(N-1)!}.
$$
Since $\phi^{(N-1)}$ is holomorphic at $z_0$, its value there equals its limit as $z\to z_0$, giving exactly the displayed formula.

:::
