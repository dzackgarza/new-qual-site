::: {.problem}
Use Laplace transforms to solve the heat equation $\partial T/\partial t = \partial^{2}T/\partial x^{2}$ with boundary conditions $T(x, 0) = 3\sin 2\pi x$ $(0 < x < 1)$, $T(0, t) = T(1, t) = 0$ $(t > 0)$.
:::

::: {.solution}

For fixed $x\in[0,1]$, let
$$
\widehat T(x,p)
=
\mathcal L_t\{T(x,t)\}(p).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The Laplace transform in $t$ satisfies
$$
\frac{\partial^2\widehat T}{\partial x^2}
-p\widehat T
=
-3\sin2\pi x,
$$
with
$$
\widehat T(0,p)=\widehat T(1,p)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Taking the Laplace transform of
$T_t=T_{xx}$ in the time variable gives
$$
p\widehat T(x,p)-T(x,0)
=
\frac{\partial^2\widehat T}{\partial x^2}(x,p).
$$
Since $T(x,0)=3\sin2\pi x$, rearrangement gives the differential
equation. The two transformed boundary conditions follow from
$T(0,t)=T(1,t)=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For $\operatorname{Re}p>0$,
$$
\widehat T(x,p)
=
\frac{3\sin2\pi x}{p+4\pi^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

A particular solution of the equation in step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} is
$$
\widehat T_p(x,p)
=
\frac{3\sin2\pi x}{p+4\pi^2},
$$
because
$$
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}\sin2\pi x
=
-4\pi^2\sin2\pi x.
$$
The general homogeneous solution is
$$
A(p)\sinh(\sqrt p\,x)+B(p)\cosh(\sqrt p\,x).
$$
The boundary condition at $x=0$ gives $B(p)=0$. Since the particular
solution also vanishes at $x=1$, the second boundary condition gives
$$
A(p)\sinh\sqrt p=0.
$$
For $\operatorname{Re}p>0$, $\sqrt p$ is not an integral multiple of
$\pi i$, so $\sinh\sqrt p\neq0$. Hence $A(p)=0$, proving the claim.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The solution is
$$
\boxed{
T(x,t)
=
3e^{-4\pi^2t}\sin2\pi x}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} has the form
$$
\widehat T(x,p)
=
3\sin2\pi x\,
\frac1{p+4\pi^2}.
$$
Since
$$
\mathcal L_t^{-1}
\left\{
\frac1{p+4\pi^2}
\right\}
=
e^{-4\pi^2t},
$$
Laplace inversion gives the displayed function.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The function in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} satisfies the PDE, the initial
condition, and both boundary conditions.


::: pf-proof

Differentiation gives
$$
T_t
=
-12\pi^2e^{-4\pi^2t}\sin2\pi x
$$
and
$$
T_{xx}
=
-12\pi^2e^{-4\pi^2t}\sin2\pi x,
$$
so $T_t=T_{xx}$. At $t=0$,
$$
T(x,0)=3\sin2\pi x.
$$
Finally, $\sin0=\sin2\pi=0$, so
$$
T(0,t)=T(1,t)=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} solve the transformed boundary-value problem and
invert its Laplace transform, while step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} verifies all conditions.

:::
