::: {.problem}
Let $a , b$ be complex constants with $0 < | a | < | b |$ . Use partial fractions to find the Laurent expansions of $1 { \big / } \{ ( z - a ) ( z - b ) \}$ about $z = 0$ in each of the regions $| z | < | a | , | a | < | z | < | b |$ and $| z | > | b |$
:::

Let
$$
F(z)=\frac1{(z-a)(z-b)}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The partial-fraction decomposition is
$$
F(z)=\frac1{a-b}\left(\frac1{z-a}-\frac1{z-b}\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

The right-hand side equals
$$
\frac1{a-b}\frac{(z-b)-(z-a)}{(z-a)(z-b)}
=\frac1{(z-a)(z-b)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

On $|z|<|a|$,
$$
\boxed{
F(z)=\frac1{a-b}
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
\left(\frac1{b^{n+1}}-\frac1{a^{n+1}}\right)z^n}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $|z/a|<1$ and $|z/b|<1$,
$$
\frac1{z-a}
=-\frac1a\frac1{1-z/a}
=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{a^{n+1}},
$$
and similarly
$$
\frac1{z-b}
=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{b^{n+1}}.
$$
Substitution into step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} gives the displayed series. Both geometric series converge throughout $|z|<|a|$, because $|a|<|b|$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

On $|a|<|z|<|b|$,
$$
\boxed{
F(z)=\frac1{a-b}\left[
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^n z^{-n-1}
+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{b^{n+1}}
\right]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The inequality $|a|<|z|$ gives
$$
\frac1{z-a}
=\frac1z\frac1{1-a/z}
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^n z^{-n-1},
$$
while $|z|<|b|$ gives
$$
\frac1{z-b}
=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{b^{n+1}}.
$$
Substituting these two expansions into step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} yields the stated Laurent series, and the two geometric-series conditions are exactly $|a|<|z|<|b|$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

On $|z|>|b|$,
$$
\boxed{
F(z)=\frac1{a-b}
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(a^n-b^n)z^{-n-1}}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Now both $|a/z|<1$ and $|b/z|<1$, so
$$
\frac1{z-a}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^n z^{-n-1},
\qquad
\frac1{z-b}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^n z^{-n-1}.
$$
Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} then gives the displayed series. Its $n=0$ coefficient vanishes, as expected from the decay $F(z)=O(z^{-2})$ at infinity. Both geometric expansions converge precisely when $|z|>|b|$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} give the Laurent expansion about $0$ in each of the three annuli determined by the singularities at $a$ and $b$.

:::
