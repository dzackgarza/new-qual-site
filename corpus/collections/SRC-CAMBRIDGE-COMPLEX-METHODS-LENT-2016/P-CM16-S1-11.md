::: {.problem}
Using conformal mapping(s), find a solution to Laplace's equation in the upper half-plane $\{z \st \Im z > 0\}$ with boundary conditions
$$
\phi(x, 0) = \begin{cases} 1 & x \in [-1, 1], \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}
$$

[Find a map $f$ of the upper half-plane onto itself that makes the boundary conditions easier to deal with.]
:::

::: {.solution}

Let $\halfplane=\{z:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$ and define
$$
M(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The map $M$ is a conformal bijection from $\halfplane$ onto itself.


::: pf-proof

For $z=x+iy\in\halfplane$,
$$
M(z)
=\frac{x^2+y^2-1+2iy}{(x+1)^2+y^2},
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{Im}M(z)
=\frac{2y}{(x+1)^2+y^2}>0.
$$
The inverse Möbius transformation is
$$
M^{-1}(w)=\frac{1+w}{1-w},
$$
and the same calculation, or the positivity of the determinant of the real Möbius matrix, shows that it also maps $\halfplane$ into itself. Hence $M$ is bijective. Moreover
$$
M'(z)=\frac{2}{(z+1)^2}\ne0
$$
on $\halfplane$, so the map is conformal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

On the real boundary, $M$ sends $(-1,1)$ to the negative real axis and $(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,\infty)$ to the positive real axis.


::: pf-proof

For real $x\ne-1$,
$$
M(x)=\frac{x-1}{x+1}\in\RR.
$$
If $-1<x<1$, the numerator is negative and the denominator positive, so $M(x)<0$. If $x>1$, both are positive, while if $x<-1$, both are negative; hence $M(x)>0$ outside $[-1,1]$. The endpoints satisfy $M(1)=0$ and $M(-1)=\infty$ on the Riemann sphere.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The function
$$
\boxed{
\phi(z)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{Arg}M(z)
=\frac1\pi\operatorname{Arg}\!\left(\frac{z-1}{z+1}\right)},
\qquad 0<\operatorname{Arg}<\pi,
$$
is harmonic on $\halfplane$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, $M(z)\in\halfplane$ for every $z\in\halfplane$. Let
$$
L(re^{i\theta})=\log r+i\theta,
\qquad 0<\theta<\pi,
$$
be the holomorphic logarithm branch on $\halfplane$. Then
$$
\phi(z)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{Im}(L(M(z))).
$$
The composition $L\circ M$ is holomorphic, so its imaginary part is harmonic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The limiting boundary values of $\phi$ are $1$ on $(-1,1)$ and $0$ on $(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,\infty)$.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, when $z$ approaches a point of $(-1,1)$ from $\halfplane$, the value $M(z)$ approaches the negative real axis from above. Hence
$$
\operatorname{Arg}M(z)\longrightarrow\pi,
$$
so $\phi(z)\to1$. When $z$ approaches a real point outside $[-1,1]$, $M(z)$ approaches the positive real axis from above, so its argument tends to $0$ and $\phi(z)\to0$. These are the prescribed boundary values away from the jump points $x=\pm1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

In real coordinates, the same solution is
$$
\boxed{
\phi(x,y)=\frac1\pi\left[
\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1-x}{y}\right)
+\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1+x}{y}\right)
\right]},
\qquad y>0,
$$
where $\tan^{-1}$ takes values in $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$.


::: pf-proof

For $z=x+iy\in\halfplane$,
$$
\operatorname{Arg}M(z)
=\operatorname{Arg}(z-1)-\operatorname{Arg}(z+1).
$$
Since $y>0$,
$$
\operatorname{Arg}(z-1)
=\frac\pi2+\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1-x}{y}\right),
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Arg}(z+1)
=\frac\pi2-\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1+x}{y}\right).
$$
Subtracting these identities and applying step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} give a harmonic solution with the required boundary data, and step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives it explicitly as a function of $x$ and $y$.

:::
