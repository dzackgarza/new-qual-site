::: {.problem}
Let $f$ be a function mapping $\RR$ to $\RR$.
Give the definitions of each of the following concepts in words, without using logical symbols or the word “not”.

(a) $f$ is surjective

(b) $f$ is not injective

(c) $f$ is uniformly continuous

(d) $f$ is not uniformly continuous
:::

::: {.solution}
The collection's provenance is the examination paper alone, so no retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own.
Each part is answered in words, without logical symbols and without the word "not", as the problem requires; each justification says which symbolic statement the words render.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step}
(a) Every real number is the value of $f$ at some real number.

::: pf-proof
That is the statement $\forall y\in\RR\ \exists x\in\RR$ with $f(x)=y$, which is surjectivity of $f:\RR\to\RR$; the sentence uses no logical symbol and no negation.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(b) There are two distinct real numbers at which $f$ takes the same value.

::: pf-proof
Injectivity says that equal values force equal arguments; its failure is the existence of $x_1\ne x_2$ with $f(x_1)=f(x_2)$, which the sentence asserts affirmatively, naming the two numbers and the equality rather than negating injectivity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #uniform}
(c) For every positive $\varepsilon$ there is a positive $\delta$ such that whenever two real numbers differ by at most $\delta$, their images under $f$ differ by at most $\varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof
This renders $\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall x,y\in\RR:\ \abs{x-y}\le\delta\Rightarrow\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le\varepsilon$, the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition of uniform continuity; in words, and with no symbol.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
(d) There is a positive $\varepsilon$ such that for every positive $\delta$ there are two real numbers differing by at most $\delta$ whose images under $f$ differ by more than $\varepsilon$.

::: pf-proof
Negating the statement of step [](#uniform){.pf-ref} reverses each quantifier and negates the conclusion, giving the existence of $\varepsilon>0$ such that for every $\delta>0$ some pair with $\abs{x-y}\le\delta$ has $\abs{f(x)-f(y)}>\varepsilon$; the sentence states exactly that, and the two parts are therefore complementary in the form chosen, with $\le$ in the hypothesis of both.
:::
