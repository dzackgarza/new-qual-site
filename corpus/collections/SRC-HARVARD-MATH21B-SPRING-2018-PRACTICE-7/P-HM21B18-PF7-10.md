::: {.problem}
Find the general solutions of

(a) $f''(t)=t^2+3$;

(b) $f''(t)+f(t)=t^2+3$;

(c) $f''(t)+6f'(t)+9f(t)=2e^{-3t}$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The general solution of part (a) is
$$
\boxed{
f(t)=\frac{t^4}{12}+\frac32t^2+C_1t+C_2
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Integrating
$$
f''(t)=t^2+3
$$
once gives
$$
f'(t)=\frac{t^3}{3}+3t+C_1,
$$
and integrating again gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The general solution of part (b) is
$$
\boxed{
f(t)=t^2+1+C_1\cos t+C_2\sin t
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The homogeneous equation $f''+f=0$ has basis $\cos t,\sin t$. For a particular solution, take
$$
f_p(t)=t^2+1.
$$
Then
$$
f_p''+f_p=2+t^2+1=t^2+3.
$$
Adding the general homogeneous solution gives the stated formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The general solution of part (c) is
$$
\boxed{
f(t)=e^{-3t}\bigl(t^2+C_1t+C_2\bigr)
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s3-1}

If $f=e^{-3t}g$, then
$$
(D+3)f=e^{-3t}g'
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(D+3)^2f=e^{-3t}g''.
$$


::: pf-proof

The product rule gives
$$
D(e^{-3t}g)=e^{-3t}(g'-3g),
$$
so adding $3e^{-3t}g$ leaves $e^{-3t}g'$. Applying the same identity once more gives the second formula.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The transformed equation is $g''=2$.


::: pf-proof

The left-hand side of part (c) is
$$
f''+6f'+9f=(D+3)^2f.
$$
By step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref}, the equation becomes
$$
e^{-3t}g''=2e^{-3t},
$$
hence $g''=2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Integrating $g''=2$ twice gives
$$
g=t^2+C_1t+C_2.
$$
Substitution into $f=e^{-3t}g$ gives the formula in step [](#s3){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, and [](#s3){.pf-ref} give the general solutions of parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively.

:::
