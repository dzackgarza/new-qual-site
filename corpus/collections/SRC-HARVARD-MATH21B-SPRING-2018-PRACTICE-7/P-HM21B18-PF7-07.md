::: {.problem}
Fit
$$
z=axy+bx^2
$$
by least squares to the data
$$
(x,y,z)=(1,1,4),(1,2,6),(1,0,8).
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The least-squares coefficients and fitted function are
$$
\boxed{
(a,b)=(-1,7),
\qquad
z=-xy+7x^2
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

The data give the overdetermined linear system
$$
M\binom ab\approx c,
\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
2&1\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
c=\begin{pmatrix}4\\6\\8\end{pmatrix}.
$$


::: pf-proof

At the three data points, the model $z=axy+bx^2$ becomes respectively
$$
a+b=4,
\qquad
2a+b=6,
\qquad
b=8.
$$
These equations have coefficient matrix $M$ and right-hand side $c$ as displayed.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The least-squares coefficients satisfy
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
5&3\\
3&3
\end{pmatrix}
\binom ab
=
\binom{16}{18}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The normal equations are
$$
M^TM\binom ab=M^Tc.
$$
Direct multiplication gives
$$
M^TM=\begin{pmatrix}5&3\\3&3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M^Tc=\begin{pmatrix}16\\18\end{pmatrix}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The unique solution of the normal equations is $a=-1$, $b=7$.


::: pf-proof

The two equations are
$$
5a+3b=16,
\qquad
3a+3b=18.
$$
Subtracting the second from the first gives $2a=-2$, hence $a=-1$.
Substitution into the second gives $b=7$.
Since
$$
\det(M^TM)=15-9=6\ne0,
$$
this solution is unique.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} determines the unique least-squares coefficients, and substitution into $z=axy+bx^2$ gives the fitted function stated in step [](#s1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives the requested least-squares fit.
