::: {.problem}
For each of
$$
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\2&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_3=\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
$$
$$
A_4=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\-2&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_5=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&4\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix},
$$
choose the corresponding phase portrait A--F for the continuous system $\dot x=Ax$, decide whether the continuous system is asymptotically stable, decide whether the discrete system $x(t+1)=Ax(t)$ is asymptotically stable, and decide whether $A$ is diagonalizable over $\CC$.

The source phase portraits are image-only in the retained extraction.
:::

::: {.solution}

For each matrix, the ordered answer below is
$$
(\text{phase portrait},\ \text{continuous stable},\ \text{discrete stable},\ \text{diagonalizable}).
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

For $A_1$ the answer is
$$
\boxed{(\mathrm B,\ \mathrm{no},\ \mathrm{no},\ \mathrm{yes})}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The characteristic polynomial is
$$
\det(\lambda I-A_1)=\lambda(\lambda-3),
$$
so the eigenvalues are $0$ and $3$. They are distinct, hence $A_1$ is diagonalizable. The $3$-eigenmode grows like $e^{3t}$ in continuous time and like $3^n$ in discrete time, so neither system is asymptotically stable. The zero eigenvalue gives a line of equilibria, while the other trajectories move along parallel affine lines away from that equilibrium line; this is source portrait B.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For $A_2$ the answer is
$$
\boxed{(\mathrm C,\ \mathrm{yes},\ \mathrm{no},\ \mathrm{no})}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
A_2=-I+N,
\qquad
N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad N^2=0.
$$
The only eigenvalue is $-1$ with algebraic multiplicity $2$, while $\ker(A_2+I)=\ker N$ is one-dimensional, so $A_2$ is not diagonalizable. Since
$$
e^{tA_2}=e^{-t}(I+tN),
$$
every continuous trajectory tends to $0$, so the continuous system is asymptotically stable. In discrete time, any nonzero vector in $\ker N$ satisfies $A_2^n v=(-1)^n v$ and does not tend to $0$, so the discrete system is not asymptotically stable. The resulting attracting improper node is source portrait C.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For $A_3$ the answer is
$$
\boxed{(\mathrm D,\ \mathrm{no},\ \mathrm{no},\ \mathrm{yes})}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because $A_3$ is triangular, its eigenvalues are $2$ and $-1$. They are distinct, so $A_3$ is diagonalizable. The continuous system has one exponentially growing mode $e^{2t}$ and one decaying mode $e^{-t}$, hence a saddle and no asymptotic stability. The discrete system has a mode growing like $2^n$ and therefore is also not asymptotically stable. The saddle is source portrait D.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

For $A_4$ the answer is
$$
\boxed{(\mathrm F,\ \mathrm{yes},\ \mathrm{no},\ \mathrm{yes})}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The characteristic polynomial is
$$
\lambda^2+\lambda+4,
$$
with distinct eigenvalues
$$
\lambda_\pm=\frac{-1\pm i\sqrt{15}}2.
$$
Thus $A_4$ is diagonalizable over $\CC$. Both continuous modes have magnitude proportional to $e^{-t/2}$, so every continuous trajectory tends to $0$. The nonreal part produces rotation, giving the spiral sink in source portrait F. On the other hand,
$$
\abs{\lambda_\pm}=2,
$$
so discrete iterates have modes of magnitude $2^n$ and the discrete system is not asymptotically stable.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For $A_5$ the answer is
$$
\boxed{(\mathrm A,\ \mathrm{no},\ \mathrm{yes},\ \mathrm{yes})}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The triangular matrix $A_5$ has distinct eigenvalues $3/4$ and $1/2$, so it is diagonalizable. Both eigenvalues are positive, so the corresponding continuous modes $e^{3t/4}$ and $e^{t/2}$ grow rather than decay; the origin is a repelling node, shown in source portrait A. In discrete time both eigenmodes tend to zero because
$$
\abs{\frac34}<1,
\qquad
\abs{\frac12}<1.
$$
Since the eigenvectors form a basis, every discrete trajectory tends to $0$, so the discrete system is asymptotically stable.

:::
