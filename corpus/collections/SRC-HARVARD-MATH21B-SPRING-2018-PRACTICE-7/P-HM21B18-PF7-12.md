::: {.problem}
(a) Find the Fourier series on $[-\pi,\pi]$ of
\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&|x|<\pi/4,\\
0,&|x|\ge\pi/4.
\end{cases}
\]

(b) Given
\[
x^3-\pi^2x
=
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{12(-1)^n}{n^3}\sin(nx),
\]
compute
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n^6}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
Use the standard Fourier normalization
\[
f(x)\sim \frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\bigl(a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\bigr),
\]
with
\[
a_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\cos(nx)\,dx,
\qquad
b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx.
\]

<1>1. The Fourier series in part (a) is
\[
\boxed{
f(x)\sim \frac14+
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{2}{n\pi}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{4}\right)\cos(nx)
}.
\]

<2>1. All sine coefficients vanish.

::: {.proof}
The function $f$ is even, whereas $\sin(nx)$ is odd. Hence
\[
b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx=0
\]
for every $n\ge1$.
:::

<2>2. The constant coefficient is $a_0=1/2$.

::: {.proof}
The function equals $1$ on an interval of length $\pi/2$ and vanishes elsewhere, so
\[
a_0=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\,dx
=\frac1\pi\cdot\frac\pi2
=\frac12.
\]
Therefore its constant Fourier term is $a_0/2=1/4$.
:::

<2>3. For every $n\ge1$,
\[
a_n=\frac{2}{n\pi}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{4}\right).
\]

::: {.proof}
By evenness and the support of $f$,
\[
a_n
=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/4}\cos(nx)\,dx
=\frac{2}{n\pi}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{4}\right).
\]
:::

<2>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Substituting steps <2>1--<2>3 into the Fourier expansion gives the series in step <1>1. At $x=\pm\pi/4$ the series converges to the midpoint value $1/2$; at every other point it converges to the periodic extension of $f$.
:::

<1>2. The value in part (b) is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^6}=\frac{\pi^6}{945}
}.
\]

<2>1. Parseval's identity for
\[
g(x)=x^3-\pi^2x
\]
gives
\[
144\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^6}
=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}g(x)^2\,dx.
\]

::: {.proof}
The given Fourier series has only sine terms, with
\[
b_n=\frac{12(-1)^n}{n^3}.
\]
Parseval's identity in the normalization above is
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|g(x)|^2\,dx
=\frac{a_0^2}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2+b_n^2).
\]
Here $a_0=a_n=0$, so
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}g(x)^2\,dx
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{144}{n^6}.
\]
:::

<2>2. The normalized squared norm is
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(x^3-\pi^2x)^2\,dx
=\frac{16\pi^6}{105}.
\]

::: {.proof}
The integrand is even, so
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(x^3-\pi^2x)^2\,dx
=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi
\left(x^6-2\pi^2x^4+\pi^4x^2\right)\,dx.
\]
The integral on $[0,\pi]$ equals
\[
\pi^7\left(\frac17-\frac25+\frac13\right)
=\frac{8\pi^7}{105},
\]
because
\[
\frac17-\frac25+\frac13
=\frac{15-42+35}{105}
=\frac8{105}.
\]
Multiplication by $2/\pi$ gives $16\pi^6/105$.
:::

<2>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Combining steps <2>1 and <2>2 gives
\[
144\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^6}
=\frac{16\pi^6}{105}.
\]
Dividing by $144$ yields
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^6}
=\frac{16\pi^6}{105\cdot144}
=\frac{\pi^6}{945}.
\]
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 gives the requested Fourier series, and step <1>2 computes the requested value of $\zeta(6)$.
:::
