::: {.problem}
Match five of the graphs A--F to
$$
f''+f=\sin t,\quad f(0)=1,\ f'(0)=0,
$$
$$
f'=-f,\quad f(0)=1,
$$
$$
f''=-1,\quad f(0)=0,\ f'(0)=1,
$$
$$
f''=-\sin t,\quad f(0)=0,\ f'(0)=1,
$$
$$
f'+f=t,\quad f(0)=1.
$$
:::

::: {.remark}
The graphs A--F are printed in the source exam.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The solution of
$$
f''+f=\sin t,\qquad f(0)=1,\quad f'(0)=0
$$
matches graph $\boxed{\mathrm C}$.


::: pf-proof

The homogeneous solutions are $\cos t$ and $\sin t$. Because the forcing $\sin t$ is resonant, a particular solution is
$$
f_p(t)=-\frac12t\cos t,
$$
for which $f_p''+f_p=\sin t$. Thus
$$
f(t)=C\cos t+D\sin t-\frac12t\cos t.
$$
The initial conditions give $C=1$ and $D=1/2$, so
$$
f(t)=\cos t+\frac12\sin t-\frac12t\cos t.
$$
Its oscillation has linearly increasing amplitude, which is exactly the behavior shown in source graph C.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The solution of
$$
f'=-f,\qquad f(0)=1
$$
matches graph $\boxed{\mathrm F}$.


::: pf-proof

The initial-value problem has solution
$$
f(t)=e^{-t}.
$$
It begins at $1$, stays positive, and decreases monotonically to $0$, which is source graph F.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

The solution of
$$
f''=-1,\qquad f(0)=0,\quad f'(0)=1
$$
matches graph $\boxed{\mathrm E}$.


::: pf-proof

Integrating twice and applying the initial conditions gives
$$
f'(t)=1-t,
\qquad
f(t)=t-\frac12t^2.
$$
This is a concave-down parabola through the origin, with a maximum at $t=1$ and another zero at $t=2$, as in source graph E.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The solution of
$$
f''=-\sin t,\qquad f(0)=0,\quad f'(0)=1
$$
matches graph $\boxed{\mathrm A}$.


::: pf-proof

Integrating once gives $f'(t)=\cos t+C$. Since $f'(0)=1$, one has $C=0$. Integrating again gives $f(t)=\sin t+D$, and $f(0)=0$ gives $D=0$. Hence
$$
f(t)=\sin t,
$$
the constant-amplitude sinusoid through the origin shown in source graph A.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The solution of
$$
f'+f=t,\qquad f(0)=1
$$
matches graph $\boxed{\mathrm D}$.


::: pf-proof

A particular solution is $t-1$, so
$$
f(t)=t-1+Ce^{-t}.
$$
The initial condition gives $C=2$, hence
$$
f(t)=t-1+2e^{-t}.
$$
Moreover,
$$
f'(t)=1-2e^{-t},
\qquad
f''(t)=2e^{-t}>0.
$$
Thus the graph initially decreases, reaches one minimum, and then rises while approaching the line $t-1$, which is source graph D.

:::

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} match the five initial-value problems to C, F, E, A, and D, respectively.
