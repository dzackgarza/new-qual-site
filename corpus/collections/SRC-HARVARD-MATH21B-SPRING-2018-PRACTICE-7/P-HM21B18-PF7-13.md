::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
u_t=u_{xx}+bu=(D^2+b)u.
$$

(a) Show that $\sin(nx)$ is an eigenfunction of $D^2+b$ and find its eigenvalue.

(b) Solve the equation for
$$
u(x,0)=\sin(3x)+2\sin(5x)
$$
when $b=1$.

(c) Solve it for general $b$ with the same initial condition.

(d) Find the threshold $b_0$ such that for $b>b_0$ the solution grows indefinitely.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

For every positive integer $n$, the function $\sin(nx)$ is an eigenfunction of $D^2+b$ with eigenvalue
$$
\boxed{b-n^2}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
D^2\sin(nx)=-n^2\sin(nx),
$$
one has
$$
(D^2+b)\sin(nx)
=(-n^2+b)\sin(nx)
=(b-n^2)\sin(nx).
$$
Thus $\sin(nx)$ is an eigenfunction with the stated eigenvalue.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

When $b=1$, the solution with
$$
u(x,0)=\sin(3x)+2\sin(5x)
$$
is
$$
\boxed{
u(x,t)=e^{-8t}\sin(3x)+2e^{-24t}\sin(5x)
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, the two initial modes are eigenfunctions with eigenvalues
$$
1-3^2=-8,
\qquad
1-5^2=-24.
$$
For an eigenfunction $\phi$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$, the function
$$
e^{\lambda t}\phi(x)
$$
satisfies $u_t=(D^2+b)u$. Linearity therefore gives the displayed sum. At $t=0$ it equals the prescribed initial condition.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For general $b$, the solution is
$$
\boxed{
u(x,t)=e^{(b-9)t}\sin(3x)+2e^{(b-25)t}\sin(5x)
}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1){.pf-ref} gives eigenvalues $b-9$ and $b-25$ for $\sin(3x)$ and $\sin(5x)$, respectively. Evolving each initial eigenmode by its exponential factor and adding them gives the displayed solution, which has the required value at $t=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The instability threshold for this initial condition is
$$
\boxed{b_0=9}.
$$


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s4-1}

If $b<9$, then the solution tends to zero as $t\to\infty$.


::: pf-proof

For $b<9$ one has
$$
b-9<0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b-25<0.
$$
Hence both exponential coefficients in step [](#s3){.pf-ref} tend to zero. Since the sine factors are bounded, $u(\cdot,t)$ tends uniformly to zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4-2}

If $b=9$, then the solution remains bounded and does not grow indefinitely.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} becomes
$$
u(x,t)=\sin(3x)+2e^{-16t}\sin(5x),
$$
which converges uniformly to $\sin(3x)$ and is therefore bounded for all $t\ge0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4-3}

If $b>9$, then the solution grows without bound in norm.


::: pf-proof

On $[0,\pi]$, the functions $\sin(3x)$ and $\sin(5x)$ are orthogonal and both have squared $L^2$ norm $\pi/2$. Consequently step [](#s3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^2(0,\pi)}^2
=\frac\pi2 e^{2(b-9)t}
+2\pi e^{2(b-25)t}.
$$
When $b>9$, the first term tends to $+\infty$, so the norm of the solution tends to $+\infty$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s4-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s4-3){.pf-ref} show decay below $9$, bounded behavior at $9$, and unbounded growth above $9$. Thus the exact threshold is $b_0=9$.

:::
