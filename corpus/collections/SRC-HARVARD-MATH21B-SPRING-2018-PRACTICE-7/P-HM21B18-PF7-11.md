::: {.problem}
Consider
$$
\dot x=y,
\qquad
\dot y=-x-xy-2x^3.
$$

(a) Find the nullclines and all equilibrium points.

(b) Analyze the stability of all equilibrium points.

(c) Choose which phase portrait A--C belongs to the system.
:::

::: {.remark}
Part (c) refers to three candidate phase portraits A--C that are printed in the source exam.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The nullclines are
$$
\boxed{
y=0,
\qquad
x=0,
\qquad
y=-1-2x^2
},
$$
and the unique equilibrium point is $\boxed{(0,0)}$.


::: pf-proof

The $x$-nullcline is determined by $\dot x=y=0$. The $y$-nullcline satisfies
$$
-x-xy-2x^3=-x(1+y+2x^2)=0,
$$
so it is the union of $x=0$ and $y=-1-2x^2$. An equilibrium must also have $y=0$. Intersecting $y=0$ with $x=0$ gives $(0,0)$, while intersecting it with $y=-1-2x^2$ would require $x^2=-1/2$, which has no real solution.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The linearization at the origin has eigenvalues $i$ and $-i$.


::: pf-proof

The Jacobian is
$$
J(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-1-y-6x^2&-x
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus
$$
J(0,0)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
whose characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^2+1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The origin is a Lyapunov-stable center and is not asymptotically stable.


::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s3-1}

The system is reversible under
$$
R(x,y)=(-x,y).
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
X(x,y)=\bigl(y,-x-xy-2x^3\bigr)
$$
be the vector field. Since $DR=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$,
$$
X(R(x,y))
=\bigl(y,x+xy+2x^3\bigr)
=-DR\,X(x,y).
$$
Therefore its local flow $\phi_t$ satisfies
$$
R\phi_t=\phi_{-t}R.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-2}

Every sufficiently small point $p_a=(0,a)$ with $a\ne0$ returns to the line $x=0$ at a time $\tau(a)$ near $\pi$.


::: pf-proof

Let $(x_a(t),y_a(t))=\phi_t(0,a)$. Smooth dependence on the initial condition makes $x_a(t)$ smooth in $(a,t)$ and $x_0(t)=0$. Hence $x_a(t)/a$ for $a\ne0$ extends smoothly to $a=0$, with limiting value given by the variational equation at the origin. By step [](#s2){.pf-ref} that variational equation is the harmonic oscillator with initial data $(0,1)$, so
$$
\left.\frac{x_a(t)}a\right|_{a=0}=\sin t.
$$
At $t=\pi$ this vanishes and its $t$-derivative is $\cos\pi=-1\ne0$. The implicit-function theorem therefore gives, for all sufficiently small $a$, a time $\tau(a)$ near $\pi$ such that
$$
x_a(\tau(a))=0.
$$
Thus $q_a:=\phi_{\tau(a)}(p_a)$ lies again in the fixed line of $R$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-3}

Each trajectory through such a point $p_a$ is periodic.


::: pf-proof

Both $p_a$ and $q_a$ are fixed by $R$. By reversibility from step [](#s3-1){.pf-ref},
$$
\phi_{-\tau(a)}(p_a)
=R\phi_{\tau(a)}(p_a)
=Rq_a
=q_a
=\phi_{\tau(a)}(p_a).
$$
Applying $\phi_{\tau(a)}$ to the two ends gives
$$
p_a=\phi_{2\tau(a)}(p_a).
$$
Hence the orbit is periodic.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3-4}

These periodic orbits imply Lyapunov stability but exclude asymptotic stability.


::: pf-proof

As $a\to0$, smooth dependence of the flow shows that after division by $a$ the periodic orbit through $p_a$ converges in $C^1$ to the unit circle traced by the linearized oscillator. Hence, for sufficiently small nonzero $a$, the unscaled orbit is a simple closed curve surrounding the origin, and such curves occur arbitrarily close to the origin. Uniqueness of solutions prevents trajectories from crossing one of these invariant closed curves, so every sufficiently small neighborhood contains an invariant closed curve enclosing a smaller neighborhood of the origin. This proves Lyapunov stability. A nonconstant periodic trajectory does not converge to the origin, so the origin is not asymptotically stable.

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s3-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s3-4){.pf-ref} prove that the nonlinear equilibrium is a center, rather than merely relying on the inconclusive purely imaginary linearization.

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

The matching source phase portrait is $\boxed{\mathrm A}$.


::: pf-proof

Direct inspection of the retained source PDF shows closed trajectories around the origin in portrait A, while portraits B and C spiral toward or away from the origin. Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves that nearby trajectories of the given system are periodic, so only portrait A has the correct local phase type.
