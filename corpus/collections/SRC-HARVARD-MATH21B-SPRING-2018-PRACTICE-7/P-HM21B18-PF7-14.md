::: {.problem}
Let a symmetric matrix $A$ have QR decomposition $A=QR$, and set $B=RQ$.

(a) Verify that $A$ and $B$ have the same eigenvalues.

(b) Is the map $T:A\mapsto B$ linear?

(c) What happens when $T$ is applied to a diagonal matrix?

(d) Iterating $T$ produces matrices converging to a diagonal matrix; take this convergence for granted. Show that the iteration also produces an orthonormal set of eigenvectors of $A$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix the QR convention used by the iteration. For the explicit invertible
matrices below, use the standard convention in which the diagonal entries of
$R$ are positive.

<1>1. The matrices $A$ and $B=RQ$ are orthogonally similar, so they have the same eigenvalues.

::: {.proof}
Since $A=QR$ and $Q$ is orthogonal,
\[
Q^TAQ
=Q^T(QR)Q
=RQ
=B.
\]
Thus $B=Q^{-1}AQ$, so $A$ and $B$ are similar and therefore have the same characteristic polynomial and eigenvalues.
:::

<1>2. The QR-step map $T$ is not linear.

<2>1. For
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
\]
one has
\[
T(C)=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}14&3\\3&6\end{pmatrix}.
\]

::: {.proof}
A positive-diagonal QR factorization is
\[
C=Q_CR_C,
\qquad
Q_C=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
R_C=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}5&4\\0&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication verifies $Q_CR_C=C$ and $Q_C^TQ_C=I$. Hence
\[
T(C)=R_CQ_C
=\frac15
\begin{pmatrix}5&4\\0&3\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}
=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}14&3\\3&6\end{pmatrix}.
\]
:::

<2>2. For $I+C$ one has
\[
T(I+C)=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}18&4\\4&12\end{pmatrix}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since
\[
I+C=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix},
\]
a positive-diagonal QR factorization is
\[
I+C=Q_DR_D,
\qquad
Q_D=\frac1{\sqrt{10}}\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\1&3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
R_D=\frac1{\sqrt{10}}\begin{pmatrix}10&6\\0&8\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Therefore
\[
T(I+C)=R_DQ_D
=\frac1{10}\begin{pmatrix}36&8\\8&24\end{pmatrix}
=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}18&4\\4&12\end{pmatrix}.
\]
:::

<2>3. Additivity fails.

::: {.proof}
The identity matrix is fixed by its QR step, so $T(I)=I$. By step <2>1,
\[
T(I)+T(C)
=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}19&3\\3&11\end{pmatrix},
\]
whereas step <2>2 gives
\[
T(I+C)=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}18&4\\4&12\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These matrices are unequal, so $T$ is not additive and hence not linear.
:::

<2>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <2>3 gives an explicit failure of the linearity axiom $T(X+Y)=T(X)+T(Y)$.
:::

<1>3. Every diagonal matrix is fixed by a QR step:
\[
\boxed{T(D)=D}.
\]

::: {.proof}
For a diagonal matrix
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n),
\]
the standard QR factorization can be taken as
\[
Q=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n),
\qquad
R=\operatorname{diag}(|d_1|,\ldots,|d_n|),
\]
where $\varepsilon_i=\operatorname{sgn}(d_i)$ for $d_i\ne0$ and $\varepsilon_i=1$ for $d_i=0$. Then $D=QR$, and since $Q$ and $R$ are diagonal they commute. Hence
\[
T(D)=RQ=QR=D.
\]
:::

<1>4. The accumulated orthogonal factors produce an orthonormal eigenbasis of the original symmetric matrix $A$.

<2>1. Set $A_0=A$, and for each $k\ge1$ write
\[
A_{k-1}=Q_kR_k,
\qquad
A_k=R_kQ_k,
\qquad
S_k=Q_1Q_2\cdots Q_k.
\]
Then
\[
A_k=S_k^TAS_k.
\]

::: {.proof}
For one step, step <1>1 gives
\[
A_k=Q_k^TA_{k-1}Q_k.
\]
Iterating this identity yields
\[
A_k
=Q_k^T\cdots Q_1^TAQ_1\cdots Q_k
=S_k^TAS_k.
\]
:::

<2>2. Every $S_k$ is orthogonal, and therefore the sequence $(S_k)$ has a convergent subsequence $S_{k_j}\to S$ with $S$ orthogonal.

::: {.proof}
Each $Q_i$ is orthogonal, so their product $S_k$ is orthogonal. The orthogonal group $O(n)$ is compact as a closed and bounded subset of the finite-dimensional space of $n\times n$ real matrices. Hence $(S_k)$ has a convergent subsequence. Since $O(n)$ is closed, its limit $S$ is again orthogonal.
:::

<2>3. If the QR iterates satisfy the granted convergence $A_k\to D$ with $D$ diagonal, then
\[
D=S^TAS.
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <2>1,
\[
A_{k_j}=S_{k_j}^TAS_{k_j}.
\]
The left-hand side tends to $D$ by the assumed convergence of the full QR iteration, while the right-hand side tends to $S^TAS$ by step <2>2 and continuity of matrix multiplication. Therefore $D=S^TAS$.
:::

<2>4. The columns of $S$ form an orthonormal eigenbasis of $A$.

::: {.proof}
Write
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)
\]
and let $s_i$ be the $i$-th column of $S$. From step <2>3,
\[
AS=SD.
\]
Taking the $i$-th column gives
\[
As_i=\lambda_i s_i,
\]
so every $s_i$ is an eigenvector of $A$. Since $S$ is orthogonal by step <2>2, its columns are an orthonormal basis.
:::

<2>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Steps <2>1--<2>4 show that an accumulation point of the accumulated QR factors has columns forming an orthonormal eigenbasis of $A$. If the full sequence $S_k$ converges under additional hypotheses, its limit is this diagonalizing orthogonal matrix.
:::

<1>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 answers part (a), step <1>2 answers part (b), step <1>3 answers part (c), and step <1>4 answers part (d).
:::
:::
