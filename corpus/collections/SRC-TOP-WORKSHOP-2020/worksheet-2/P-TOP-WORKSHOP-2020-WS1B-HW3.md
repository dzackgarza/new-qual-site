::: {.problem}
Describe how to make a topology from a subbasis, and what (if any) requirements there are on the subbasis.
:::

::: {.solution}
This is a worksheet question of the 2020 topology workshop (`SRC-TOP-WORKSHOP-2020`, worksheet 1B), whose collection `provenance` lists the worksheets as `assets/attachments/Topology_Workshop_Worksheet_*.pdf`. No solution set is retained for it and the worksheet itself was not re-read in this pass; the argument is the card's own.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #generated}
For any $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)$ there is a smallest topology $\tau_{\mathcal{S}}$ on $X$ containing $\mathcal{S}$, namely the intersection of all topologies containing $\mathcal{S}$.

::: pf-proof
The discrete topology contains $\mathcal{S}$, so the family of topologies on $X$ containing $\mathcal{S}$ is nonempty, and the intersection of any family of topologies on $X$ is again a topology: it contains $\varnothing$ and $X$ and is closed under finite intersections and arbitrary unions because each member of the family is. That intersection contains $\mathcal{S}$ and is contained in every topology containing $\mathcal{S}$, so it is the smallest one.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #basis}
Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the family of all finite intersections of members of $\mathcal{S}$, the empty intersection being $X$. Then $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis, and $\tau_{\mathcal{S}}$ is the family of all unions of members of $\mathcal{B}$.

::: pf-proof
$\mathcal{B}$ covers $X$, the empty intersection being $X$; and $\mathcal{B}$ is closed under finite intersections, the intersection of two finite intersections being a finite intersection. A family with those two properties is a basis for a topology, whose open sets are the unions of its members, and that topology contains $\mathcal{S}$ since each member of $\mathcal{S}$ is a one-fold intersection. Conversely every topology containing $\mathcal{S}$ contains every finite intersection of members of $\mathcal{S}$, hence every union of them, so this topology is the smallest one, that is $\tau_{\mathcal{S}}$.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #none}
No condition on $\mathcal{S}$ is needed: every collection of subsets of $X$ generates a topology.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#generated){.pf-ref} and [](#basis){.pf-ref} apply to an arbitrary $\mathcal{S}$, including $\mathcal{S}=\varnothing$, for which $\mathcal{B}=\{X\}$ and $\tau_{\mathcal{S}}$ is the indiscrete topology.
:::
:::

::: {.pf-step #convention}
If a subbasis is required to cover $X$ and only nonempty finite intersections are admitted, the requirement is $\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S=X$, and the two conventions give the same topology after adjoining $X$ to $\mathcal{S}$.

::: pf-proof
With nonempty intersections only, the resulting family $\mathcal{B}'$ covers $X$ exactly when $\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S=X$: a point lies in some member of $\mathcal{B}'$ exactly when it lies in some member of $\mathcal{S}$. Closedness under finite intersections holds regardless. If $\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S\neq X$, replacing $\mathcal{S}$ by $\mathcal{S}\cup\{X\}$ makes it cover $X$, adds $X$ among the admissible intersections and leaves $\mathcal{B}'$ unchanged otherwise, hence leaves the generated topology unchanged.
:::
