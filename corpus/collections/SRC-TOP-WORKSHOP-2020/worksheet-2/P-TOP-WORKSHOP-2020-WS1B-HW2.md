::: {.problem}
Describe how to make a topology from a basis, and what (if any) requirements there are on the basis.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A basis $\mathcal B$ for a topology on $X$ is a collection of subsets of $X$ such that: (i) every $x \in X$ lies in some $B \in \mathcal B$; (ii) if $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ with $B_1, B_2 \in \mathcal B$, then there is $B_3 \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$.
::: {.proof}
the two requirements on a basis.
:::

<1>2. The topology generated by $\mathcal B$ is: $U \subseteq X$ is open iff for every $x \in U$ there is $B \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B \subseteq U$.
::: {.proof}
definition of the topology generated by a basis.
:::

<1>3. Equivalently, the open sets are exactly the unions of elements of $\mathcal B$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1–<1>3.
