::: {.problem}
Describe how to make a topology from a basis, and what (if any) requirements there are on the basis.
:::

::: {.solution}
A collection $\mathcal B$ of subsets of $X$ is a basis if (i) every $x \in X$ lies in some $B \in \mathcal B$, and (ii) whenever $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ with $B_1, B_2 \in \mathcal B$, there is $B_3 \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$. Declare $U \subseteq X$ open if for every $x \in U$ there is $B \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B \subseteq U$, and let $\mathcal T$ be the collection of open sets.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

$\mathcal T$ is a topology on $X$.

::: pf-proof

$\varnothing \in \mathcal T$ vacuously, and $X \in \mathcal T$ by (i). A union of members of $\mathcal T$ is in $\mathcal T$, since a basic set around a point of one member lies in the union. If $U_1, U_2 \in \mathcal T$ and $x \in U_1 \cap U_2$, choose $B_i \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B_i \subseteq U_i$; by (ii) there is $B_3 \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2 \subseteq U_1 \cap U_2$. So $\mathcal T$ is closed under finite intersections.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The members of $\mathcal T$ are exactly the unions of members of $\mathcal B$.

::: pf-proof

Each $B \in \mathcal B$ is in $\mathcal T$ (take $B$ itself around each of its points), so unions of members of $\mathcal B$ are in $\mathcal T$ by step [](#s1){.pf-ref}. Conversely, if $U \in \mathcal T$, choose for each $x \in U$ some $B_x \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B_x \subseteq U$; then $U = \bigcup_{x \in U} B_x$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

The topology generated by $\mathcal B$ is $\mathcal T$, the collection of unions of members of $\mathcal B$ (step [](#s2){.pf-ref}); conditions (i) and (ii) are the requirements on $\mathcal B$, and step [](#s1){.pf-ref} shows they make $\mathcal T$ a topology.

:::
