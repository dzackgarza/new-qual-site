::: {.problem}
Let $f(x,y)$ on $[-1,1]^2$ be defined by
$$
f(x,y) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{xy}{(x^2+y^2)^2} & (x,y) \neq (0,0) \\ 0 & (x,y) = (0,0). \end{cases}
$$
Determine if $f$ is integrable.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

$f$ is $\boxed{\text{not integrable}}$ on $[-1,1]^2$.

::: pf-proof

Away from the origin $f$ is continuous. In polar coordinates,
$$
\abs{\frac{xy}{(x^2+y^2)^2}} = \frac{\abs{\sin\theta\cos\theta}}{r^2}.
$$
On the disk of radius $\delta \leq 1$ about the origin, which lies in $[-1,1]^2$,
$$
\iint \abs{f}\,dx\,dy = \int_0^{2\pi}\abs{\sin\theta\cos\theta}\,d\theta\int_0^\delta \frac{dr}{r} = \infty.
$$
Hence $f\notin L^1([-1,1]^2)$.

:::
