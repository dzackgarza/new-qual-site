::: {.problem}
Let
$$
\Omega=\{z\in\CC:0<|z|<1\}.
$$
Prove that for every bounded harmonic function $u:\Omega\to\RR$ there is a harmonic function $v:\Omega\to\RR$ obeying
$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial y},
\qquad
\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}=-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Define the conjugate differential
$$
\alpha
\coloneqq
-u_y\,dx+u_x\,dy.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The one-form $\alpha$ is closed on $\Omega$.


::: pf-proof

Since $u$ is harmonic,
$$
u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
d\alpha
=
(u_{xx}+u_{yy})\,dx\wedge dy
=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Let
$$
m(r)
\coloneqq
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}
u(re^{i\theta})\,d\theta
\qquad
(0<r<1).
$$
Then
$$
m''(r)+\frac1r m'(r)=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

In polar coordinates,
$$
\Delta u
=
u_{rr}
+\frac1r u_r
+\frac1{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}
=0.
$$
Integrating this identity over $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$ and differentiating under the integral sign gives
$$
2\pi m''(r)
+\frac{2\pi}{r}m'(r)
+\frac1{r^2}
\int_0^{2\pi}u_{\theta\theta}(r,\theta)\,d\theta
=0.
$$
The last integral vanishes by periodicity, proving the differential equation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

There are constants $A,B\in\RR$ such that
$$
m(r)=A\log r+B.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} is equivalent to
$$
(r m'(r))'=0.
$$
Hence $r m'(r)=A$ for some constant $A$, so
$$
m'(r)=\frac Ar.
$$
Integrating gives the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

In fact,
$$
A=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $u$ is bounded on $\Omega$, there is $M<\infty$ with
$$
|u(z)|\le M.
$$
Therefore
$$
|m(r)|
\le
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}|u(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
\le M
$$
for every $0<r<1$. If $A\ne0$, then $A\log r+B$ is unbounded as $r\downarrow0$, contradicting this estimate. Thus $A=0$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For every $0<r<1$,
$$
\int_{|z|=r}\alpha=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

Parameterize the positively oriented circle by
$$
z(\theta)=re^{i\theta}.
$$
Then
$$
dx=-r\sin\theta\,d\theta,
\qquad
dy=r\cos\theta\,d\theta,
$$
so
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha
&=
-u_y\,dx+u_x\,dy\\
&=
r\bigl(u_x\cos\theta+u_y\sin\theta\bigr)\,d\theta\\
&=
r u_r(re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\int_{|z|=r}\alpha
=
r\int_0^{2\pi}u_r(re^{i\theta})\,d\theta
=
2\pi r m'(r).
$$
By steps [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}, $m'(r)=0$, so the period vanishes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The closed one-form $\alpha$ is exact on $\Omega$.


::: pf-proof

The punctured disk has first homology group generated by any positively oriented circle $|z|=r$. Since $\alpha$ is closed by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, its integral depends only on the homology class of a closed curve. Step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} shows that the period on the generator is zero. Therefore
$$
\int_\gamma\alpha=0
$$
for every closed piecewise smooth curve $\gamma\subset\Omega$.

Fix $z_0\in\Omega$ and define
$$
v(z)
\coloneqq
\int_{z_0}^{z}\alpha,
$$
where the integral is taken along any piecewise smooth path in $\Omega$. Vanishing of all periods makes this definition path-independent, and
$$
dv=\alpha.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The function $v$ satisfies
$$
\boxed{
u_x=v_y,
\qquad
u_y=-v_x
}
$$
and is harmonic on $\Omega$.


::: pf-proof

From step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref},
$$
dv
=
-u_y\,dx+u_x\,dy.
$$
Comparing coefficients gives
$$
v_x=-u_y,
\qquad
v_y=u_x,
$$
which are the required Cauchy--Riemann equations. Consequently
$$
u+iv
$$
is holomorphic on $\Omega$. The imaginary part of a holomorphic function is harmonic, so $v$ is harmonic.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} gives the required harmonic conjugate.
