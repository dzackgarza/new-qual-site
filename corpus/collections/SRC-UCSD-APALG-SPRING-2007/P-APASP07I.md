::: problem
Consider the ideal $I$ generated by the polynomials $x^2 - y^2 + 1$, $x^3 + y^2 + z^2 - 1$, and $y^2 + y + 1$.

(a) Calculate a Gröbner basis for $I$ with respect to the lexicographic order $x > y > z$.

(b) Calculate all the solutions given by the common zeros of the generating polynomials, i.e.\ calculate the variety $V(I)$ of $I$.
:::

::: solution
Write
\[
f_1=x^2-y^2+1,
\qquad
f_2=x^3+y^2+z^2-1,
\qquad
f_3=y^2+y+1.
\]
All computations are over a characteristic-zero field, and the variety below is taken in $\mathbb C^3$.

For lexicographic order $x>y>z$, an exact Buchberger computation gives the reduced Gröbner basis
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
g_1&=z^8-6z^6+15z^4-36z^2+63,\\
g_2&=33y+2z^6-3z^4-6,\\
g_3&=33x-3z^6+10z^4-33z^2+75.
\end{aligned}}
\]
For reproducibility, the computation is:

```singular
ring r=0,(x,y,z),lp;
ideal I=x2-y2+1,x3+y2+z2-1,y2+y+1;
option(redSB);
ideal G=std(I);
G;
```

which returns

```text
G[1]=z8-6z6+15z4-36z2+63
G[2]=33y+2z6-3z4-6
G[3]=33x-3z6+10z4-33z2+75
```

Thus part (a) is settled by an exact Gröbner-basis computation. Notice also that the leading monomials are respectively $z^8$, $y$, and $x$, which are pairwise relatively prime; hence Buchberger's criterion is consistent with the reduced output.

For part (b), it is simpler to solve the original equations directly. From
\[
f_3=0
\]
and
\[
f_1+f_3=x^2+y+2,
\]
we obtain
\[
y=-x^2-2.
\]
Also
\[
f_2-f_3=x^3-y+z^2-2,
\]
so substituting the preceding expression for $y$ gives
\[
x^3+x^2+z^2=0,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
z^2=-x^2(x+1).
\]
Finally, substituting $y=-x^2-2$ into $f_3=0$ gives
\[
(-x^2-2)^2+(-x^2-2)+1=0,
\]
i.e.
\[
x^4+3x^2+3=0.
\]
Conversely, these three equations imply $f_1=f_2=f_3=0$, so they describe exactly the same variety.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
V(I)=
\left\{
\left(
\xi,
-\xi^2-2,
\varepsilon\sqrt{-\xi^2(\xi+1)}
\right)
:\
\xi^4+3\xi^2+3=0,
\ \varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}
\right\}.
}
\]
The quartic $x^4+3x^2+3$ has four distinct roots: its derivative is
\[
4x^3+6x=2x(2x^2+3),
\]
and neither $x=0$ nor $x^2=-3/2$ is a root of the quartic. Moreover, no root is $0$ or $-1$, since the quartic takes the values $3$ and $7$ there. Thus $-\xi^2(\xi+1)\ne0$, so each of the four choices of $\xi$ yields two distinct values of $z$.

Hence $V(I)$ consists of exactly
\[
\boxed{8}
\]
points over $\mathbb C$.
:::
