::: {.problem}
Consider the ideal $I$ generated by the polynomials $x^3 - y^2 + 1$, $x^3 + y^2 + z^2 - 1$, and $y^2 + yz - 1$.

(a) Calculate a Gröbner basis for $I$ with respect to the lexicographic order $x > y > z$.

(b) Calculate all the solutions given by the common zeros of the generating polynomials, i.e.\ calculate the variety $V(I)$ of $I$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write
$$
f_1=x^3-y^2+1,\qquad f_2=x^3+y^2+z^2-1,\qquad f_3=y^2+yz-1.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #gens}
The ideal $I$ is generated by
$$
g_1=x^3+\tfrac12z^2,\qquad g_2=y^2+\tfrac12z^2-1,\qquad g_3=yz-\tfrac12z^2,\qquad g_4=z^3-\tfrac43z.
$$

::: pf-proof
First, $g_1=\frac12(f_1+f_2)$, $g_2=\frac12(f_2-f_1)$ and $g_3=f_3-g_2$, so $g_1,g_2,g_3\in I$. Next,
$$
zg_2-yg_3=\tfrac12z^3-z+\tfrac12yz^2,
\qquad
\tfrac12yz^2=\tfrac12z\,g_3+\tfrac14z^3,
$$
so $\frac34z^3-z=zg_2-yg_3-\frac12zg_3\in I$ and $g_4\in I$. Conversely,
$$
f_1=g_1-g_2,\qquad f_2=g_1+g_2,\qquad f_3=g_2+g_3,
$$
so the $g_i$ generate $I$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #gb}
(a) $\{g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4\}$ is the reduced Gröbner basis of $I$ for lex order with $x>y>z$.

::: pf-proof
The leading terms are $x^3$, $y^2$, $yz$ and $z^3$. By Buchberger's criterion it suffices that every $S$-polynomial reduces to $0$. The pairs $(g_1,g_j)$ and $(g_2,g_4)$ have coprime leading terms, so their $S$-polynomials reduce to $0$. For the other two pairs,
$$
S(g_2,g_3)=zg_2-yg_3=\tfrac12yz^2+\tfrac12z^3-z
\;\longrightarrow\;\tfrac34z^3-z=\tfrac34g_4\;\longrightarrow\;0,
$$
using $yz^2=z\,g_3+\frac12z^3$, and
$$
S(g_3,g_4)=z^2g_3-yg_4=-\tfrac12z^4+\tfrac43yz
\;\longrightarrow\;-\tfrac12z^4+\tfrac23z^2
\;\longrightarrow\;-\tfrac23z^2+\tfrac23z^2=0,
$$
using $yz\to\frac12z^2$ and $z^4\to\frac43z^2$. The basis is reduced: each $g_i$ is monic, and no term of $g_i$ is divisible by the leading term of another $g_j$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #var}
(b) $V(I)$ consists of eight points:
$$
(0,1,0),\quad(0,-1,0),\quad
\Bigl(\omega\sqrt[3]{-\tfrac23},\ \tfrac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt3},\ \tfrac{2\varepsilon}{\sqrt3}\Bigr),
$$
where $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$ and $\omega$ runs over the three cube roots of unity.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#gb){.pf-ref}, $V(I)=V(g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4)$. The equation $g_4=0$ gives $z=0$ or $z^2=\frac43$.

If $z=0$, then $g_2=0$ gives $y=\pm1$, $g_3=0$ holds, and $g_1=0$ gives $x=0$.

If $z^2=\frac43$, then $z\ne0$ and $g_3=0$ gives $y=\frac z2$. Then $g_2=\frac{z^2}4+\frac{z^2}2-1=0$ holds. Write $z=\frac{2\varepsilon}{\sqrt3}$, so $y=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt3}$. Finally $g_1=0$ gives $x^3=-\frac23$, which has three complex solutions.
:::
