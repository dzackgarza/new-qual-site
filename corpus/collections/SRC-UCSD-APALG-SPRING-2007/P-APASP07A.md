::: {.problem}
(a) Let $H$ be generated by $a$ and $b$ with the relations $a^3 = b^3 = 1$ and $ab = ba$ (i.e.\ $H \cong \ZZ/3 \times \ZZ/3$). Write down all irreducible representations of $H$.

Let now $G$ be the group generated by $a$, $b$, $c$ with the relations as in (a) as well as $c^3 = 1$ and $c a^i b^j c^{-1} = a^i b^{i+j}$.
You can assume that $G$ has order 27.

(b) Calculate the matrices of the generators $a$, $b$, and $c$ in the representation $W$ induced by a given irreducible representation $V$ of $H$.
Determine for which $V$ all elements of $H$ act via multiples of the identity matrix on $W$.
Show directly that in this case the induced representation is reducible.

(c) Determine all irreducible representations of $G$.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own: the characters of $H$, the induced module computed on the coset basis, and the count of degrees. No retained set solves this exercise, and the sets searched are recorded in the audit trail.

Let $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, $H=\langle a,b\rangle\cong C_{3}\times C_{3}$, and $G=\langle a,b,c\rangle$ with $a^{3}=b^{3}=c^{3}=1$, $ab=ba$ and $c\,a^{i}b^{j}c^{-1}=a^{i}b^{i+j}$, of order $27$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #h-characters}
(a) The irreducible complex representations of $H$ are the nine characters $\chi_{r,s}$ with $\chi_{r,s}(a)=\omega^{r}$ and $\chi_{r,s}(b)=\omega^{s}$, for $(r,s)\in(\ZZ/3)^{2}$.

::: pf-proof
$H$ is abelian of order $9$, so every irreducible complex representation is one-dimensional (a commuting family of operators is simultaneously triangularizable, and a one-dimensional invariant subspace is a subrepresentation), that is a homomorphism $H\to\CC^{\times}$. Such a homomorphism is determined by its values on the generators, which must satisfy $x^{3}=y^{3}=1$, so the values lie in $\{1,\omega,\omega^{2}\}$; conversely any choice gives a homomorphism because $H$ is the direct product of the cyclic groups generated by $a$ and $b$. That is the nine characters displayed, and they are pairwise distinct since they differ on $a$ or on $b$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #induced-basis}
(b) For $V=\CC v$ affording $\chi_{r,s}$, the induced module $W=\CC[G]\otimes_{\CC[H]}V$ has the basis $e_{j}=c^{j}\otimes v$, $j=0,1,2$.

::: pf-proof
$H$ has index $3$ in $G$, and $G/H$ has the representatives $1,c,c^{2}$; a set of coset representatives is a basis of $\CC[G]$ as a right $\CC[H]$-module, so the elements $g\otimes v$ for $g$ in that set form a basis of the induced module.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrices}
(b) With $e_{j}=c^{j}\otimes v$ and $\varphi(h)=chc^{-1}$, one has $\varphi(a)=ab$, $\varphi(b)=b$, $\varphi^{-1}(a)=ab^{-1}$ and $\varphi^{-2}(a)=ab$, and
$$
\rho_{W}(a)=\mathrm{diag}\big(\omega^{r},\omega^{r-s},\omega^{r+s}\big),\qquad \rho_{W}(b)=\omega^{s}I_{3},\qquad \rho_{W}(c)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The relation gives $\varphi(a)=cac^{-1}=ab$ and $\varphi(b)=cbc^{-1}=b$ (the case $i=0$). Then $\varphi^{-1}(a)=ab^{-1}$, since $\varphi(ab^{-1})=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)^{-1}=ab\,b^{-1}=a$, and $\varphi^{-2}(a)=\varphi^{-1}(ab^{-1})=ab^{-1}b^{-1}=ab^{-2}=ab$ because $b^{3}=1$. For $h\in H$,
$$
h\,(c^{j}\otimes v)=c^{j}\otimes(c^{-j}hc^{j})v=\chi_{r,s}\big(c^{-j}hc^{j}\big)\,e_{j},
$$
so each $e_{j}$ is an eigenvector of $\rho_{W}(a)$ and of $\rho_{W}(b)$. Now $c^{-j}ac^{j}=\varphi^{-j}(a)$ equals $a$, $ab^{-1}$, $ab$ for $j=0,1,2$, giving the eigenvalues $\omega^{r},\omega^{r-s},\omega^{r+s}$; and $c^{-j}bc^{j}=b$ for every $j$, giving $\omega^{s}$ three times. Finally $c\,e_{j}=c^{j+1}\otimes v=e_{j+1}$ with indices mod $3$, so the columns of $\rho_{W}(c)$ are the images of the basis vectors, which is the matrix displayed.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #scalar-case}
(b) Every element of $H$ acts on $W$ by a scalar exactly when $s=0$, and in that case $W$ is reducible.

::: pf-proof
$b$ acts by the scalar $\omega^{s}$ always, so it suffices that $a$ act by a scalar; that happens exactly when the three diagonal entries of $\rho_{W}(a)$ are equal, that is $r\equiv r-s\equiv r+s\pmod 3$, which holds exactly when $s\equiv0\pmod 3$. If $s=0$ then $\rho_{W}(c)$ is the permutation matrix of a $3$-cycle, hence is diagonalizable with the three distinct eigenvalues $1,\omega,\omega^{2}$, and each of its eigenspaces is one-dimensional and stable under $\rho_{W}(a)$ and $\rho_{W}(b)$ because those are scalars; so each eigenspace is a $G$-submodule and $W$ is a direct sum of three one-dimensional submodules.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #one-dim}
(c) $G$ has exactly nine one-dimensional complex representations, the characters $\psi_{r,t}$ with $\psi(a)=\omega^{r}$, $\psi(b)=1$, $\psi(c)=\omega^{t}$.

::: pf-proof
In a one-dimensional representation the relation $cac^{-1}=ab$ becomes $\psi(a)=\psi(a)\psi(b)$, so $\psi(b)=1$; hence $\psi$ is determined by the values $\omega^{r}$ on $a$ and $\omega^{t}$ on $c$. Conversely, for any $r,t$ the displayed assignment respects the relations: $a^{3},b^{3},c^{3}\mapsto1$, $ab=ba$ is trivial, and $cac^{-1}=ab$ reads $\omega^{r}=1\cdot\omega^{r}$. So each $\psi_{r,t}$ is a representation, and the nine are pairwise inequivalent since they take different values on $a$ or on $c$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #three-dim}
(c) For $s=1,2$ the induced module $W_{s}=\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\chi_{0,s}$ is an irreducible three-dimensional representation.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#matrices){.pf-ref} with $r=0$ the matrix of $a$ in $W_{s}$ is $\mathrm{diag}(1,\omega^{-s},\omega^{s})$, whose three eigenvalues $1,\omega^{-s},\omega^{s}$ are distinct for $s=1,2$. A $G$-stable subspace $U$ is in particular $a$-stable, and for a diagonalizable operator with distinct eigenvalues every invariant subspace is spanned by the eigenvectors it contains — the spectral projection onto an eigenspace is a polynomial in the operator, so it carries $U$ into $U$; hence $U$ is spanned by some subset of $\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\}$. But $\rho_{W}(c)$ permutes the three basis vectors cyclically, so a nonzero $c$-stable subspace among the spans of subsets must contain all three, that is $U=W_{s}$. So $W_{s}$ has no nonzero proper submodule.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #complete}
(c) The nine characters and $W_{1},W_{2}$ are pairwise inequivalent, and they are all the irreducible representations of $G$.

::: pf-proof
The nine characters are one-dimensional and $W_{1},W_{2}$ are three-dimensional by step [](#three-dim){.pf-ref}, so no character is equivalent to either $W_{s}$. The two three-dimensional ones are inequivalent because the central element $b$ acts on $W_{s}$ by the scalar $\omega^{s}$, which differs for $s=1$ and $s=2$. Finally
$$
9\cdot1^{2}+2\cdot3^{2}=9+18=27=\cardinality{G},
$$
and for a finite group the sum of the squares of the dimensions of pairwise inequivalent irreducible complex representations equals the order of the group, so no further irreducible exists. Hence the complete list is
$$
\boxed{9\text{ one-dimensional representations and }2\text{ three-dimensional representations}.}
$$
:::
