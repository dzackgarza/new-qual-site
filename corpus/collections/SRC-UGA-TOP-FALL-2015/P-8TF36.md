::: problem
Let $\tau$ be the map that in cylindrical coordinates takes $(r,\theta,z)$ to $(r,\theta+\pi,z)$, thus $\tau$ maps the torus $T$ to itself by a half-turn about its vertical axis, as shown on the exam.

a. Find a cell structure on $T$ such that $\tau$ maps cells to cells.

b. Let $Q$ be the quotient of $T$ given by identifying $x$ and $\tau(x)$ for all $x\in T$.
What is $\chi(Q)$?

c. Is $Q$ a surface?
:::

::: {.solution}
Identify the torus with
\[
T=S^1\times S^1,
\]
where the first factor records the cylindrical angle $\theta$ and the second records the meridional angle.
Then
\[
\tau(z,w)=(-z,w).
\]

<1>1. Give the first factor $S^1$ a CW structure with two $0$-cells
\[
p=1,
\qquad
q=-1,
\]
and two open $1$-cells $e_+$ and $e_-$, the two components of $S^1\setminus\{p,q\}$.
Give the second factor a CW structure with one $0$-cell $v$ and one open $1$-cell $f$.
The product CW structure on $T$ is preserved by $\tau$.
::: {.proof}
Multiplication by $-1$ on the first circle satisfies
\[
p\longleftrightarrow q,
\qquad
e_+\longleftrightarrow e_-.
\]
The second coordinate is fixed by $\tau$.
Thus the product cells are
\[
p\times v,\ q\times v
\]
in dimension $0$,
\[
e_+\times v,\ e_-\times v,\ p\times f,\ q\times f
\]
in dimension $1$, and
\[
e_+\times f,\ e_-\times f
\]
in dimension $2$.
The map $\tau$ exchanges the members of each displayed pair, so it maps cells to cells.
:::

<1>2. The quotient $Q=T/(x\sim\tau(x))$ inherits a CW structure with one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells, and one $2$-cell.
::: {.proof}
No cell in <1>1 is fixed by $\tau$; the cells occur in the four two-element orbits
\[
\{p\times v,q\times v\},
\quad
\{e_+\times v,e_-\times v\},
\quad
\{p\times f,q\times f\},
\quad
\{e_+\times f,e_-\times f\}.
\]
Passing to the quotient therefore gives one cell from the first orbit, two from the middle two orbits, and one from the last orbit.
:::

<1>3. The Euler characteristic of the quotient is
\[
\boxed{\chi(Q)=0}.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2,
\[
\chi(Q)=1-2+1=0.
\]
:::

<1>4. The quotient $Q$ is a surface; in fact, it is homeomorphic to the torus.
::: {.proof}
Define
\[
F:T\longrightarrow S^1\times S^1,
\qquad
F(z,w)=(z^2,w).
\]
Since
\[
F(-z,w)=F(z,w),
\]
the map is constant on the $\tau$-orbits and hence factors through a continuous map
\[
\overline F:Q\longrightarrow S^1\times S^1,
\qquad
\overline F([z,w])=(z^2,w).
\]

The map $\overline F$ is surjective because every point of $S^1$ has a square root.
It is injective because
\[
z_1^2=z_2^2
\]
for $z_1,z_2\in S^1$ implies $z_2=z_1$ or $z_2=-z_1$, exactly the two points identified by the quotient; equality of the second coordinates is also forced.

The torus $T$ is compact, so its quotient $Q$ is compact.
The target $S^1\times S^1$ is Hausdorff.
Therefore the continuous bijection $\overline F$ is a homeomorphism.
Hence
\[
Q\cong S^1\times S^1,
\]
so $Q$ is a surface.
:::
