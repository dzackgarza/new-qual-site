::: problem
Given two points $a$ and $b$ of a space $S$, a collection of sets $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ in $S$ is called a simple chain from $a$ to $b$ if $a\in A_1$, $b\in A_n$, and $A_i\cap A_j\neq\varnothing$ if and only if $|i-j|\leq 1$.

Prove that if $\{U_\alpha\}$ is a collection of open sets covering $S$ and $S$ is a connected space, then there is a simple chain of elements in $\{U_\alpha\}$ joining $a$ and $b$ for any $a$ and $b$.
Hint: Consider the set $C_a$ of all $b$ so that there is a simple chain of elements joining $a$ and $b$.
Prove that $C_a$ is open and closed.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $a\in S$ and let $C_a$ be the set of all points $b\in S$ for which there is a simple chain of members of the cover from $a$ to $b$.

<1>1. The set $C_a$ is nonempty.
::: {.proof}
Because the family $\{U_\alpha\}$ covers $S$, choose a member $U$ containing $a$.
The one-term chain
\[
U
\]
is a simple chain from $a$ to $a$.
Thus $a\in C_a$.
:::

<1>2. The set $C_a$ is open.
::: {.proof}
Let $b\in C_a$ and choose a simple chain
\[
A_1,\ldots,A_n
\]
from $a$ to $b$.
Since $b\in A_n$ and $A_n$ is a member of the open cover, $A_n$ is an open neighborhood of $b$.

For every $c\in A_n$, the same sequence $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ is a simple chain from $a$ to $c$: the intersection conditions among the sets are unchanged, while $c\in A_n$.
Hence
\[
A_n\subseteq C_a.
\]
Therefore $C_a$ is open.
:::

<1>3. If a member $U$ of the cover meets $C_a$, then every point of $U$ belongs to $C_a$.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
c\in U\cap C_a.
\]
Choose a simple chain
\[
A_1,\ldots,A_n
\]
from $a$ to $c$.
Then $U\cap A_n\ne\varnothing$.

Let $j$ be the least index such that
\[
U\cap A_j\ne\varnothing.
\]
If $U=A_j$, then
\[
A_1,\ldots,A_j
\]
is already a simple chain from $a$ to every point of $U$.

Suppose instead that $U\ne A_j$.
By minimality of $j$,
\[
U\cap A_i=\varnothing
\qquad (i<j),
\]
while $U\cap A_j\ne\varnothing$.
Consequently
\[
A_1,\ldots,A_j,U
\]
is a simple chain: the original prefix is simple, $U$ meets its immediate predecessor $A_j$, and $U$ is disjoint from every earlier member of the prefix.
Thus this chain joins $a$ to every point of $U$.

In either case,
\[
U\subseteq C_a.
\]
:::

<1>4. The complement $S\setminus C_a$ is open; equivalently, $C_a$ is closed.
::: {.proof}
Let $b\in S\setminus C_a$.
Choose a member $U$ of the cover with $b\in U$.
If $U$ met $C_a$, then <1>3 would give
\[
U\subseteq C_a,
\]
contradicting $b\notin C_a$.
Hence
\[
U\cap C_a=\varnothing,
\]
so $U$ is an open neighborhood of $b$ contained in $S\setminus C_a$.
Thus the complement is open.
:::

<1>5. One has $C_a=S$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $C_a$ is nonempty.
By <1>2 and <1>4, it is both open and closed in the connected space $S$.
The only nonempty clopen subset of a connected space is the whole space, so
\[
C_a=S.
\]
:::

<1>6. Any two points $a,b\in S$ can therefore be joined by a simple chain of members of the given open cover.
::: {.proof}
Fix $a\in S$.
By <1>5 every $b\in S$ lies in $C_a$, which is exactly the asserted conclusion.
:::
:::
