::: problem
Evaluate
\[
\int \ln\sqrt{x^2+1}\,dx
\]
and
\[
\int \frac{2\arctan(e^x)}{e^{2x}}\,dx.
\]
:::

::: solution
Since $\ln\sqrt{x^2+1}=\tfrac12\ln(x^2+1)$, integration by parts gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int \ln\sqrt{x^2+1}\,dx
&=\frac x2\ln(x^2+1)-\int\frac{x^2}{x^2+1}\,dx\\
&=\boxed{\frac x2\ln(x^2+1)-x+\arctan x+C.}
\end{aligned}
\]

For the second integral, integrate by parts with
\[
u=\arctan(e^x),\qquad dv=2e^{-2x}\,dx,
\]
so $du=e^x(1+e^{2x})^{-1}dx$ and $v=-e^{-2x}$. Thus
\[
I=-e^{-2x}\arctan(e^x)+\int\frac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{2x}}\,dx.
\]
Using
\[
\frac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{2x}}=e^{-x}-\frac{e^x}{1+e^{2x}},
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{I=-e^{-2x}\arctan(e^x)-e^{-x}-\arctan(e^x)+C.}
\]
The stored displayed answer had $e^{2x}$ in the first term, contrary to its own integration-by-parts computation.
:::
