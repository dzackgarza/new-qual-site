::: {.problem}
Evaluate
$$
\int \ln\sqrt{x^2+1}\,dx
$$
and
$$
\int \frac{2\arctan(e^x)}{e^{2x}}\,dx.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Both integrands are continuous on $\RR$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #logarithm}

$\displaystyle\int\ln\sqrt{x^2+1}\,dx=\frac x2\ln(x^2+1)-x+\arctan x+C$.

::: pf-proof

Since $\ln\sqrt{x^2+1}=\frac12\ln(x^2+1)$, integration by parts with
$u=\frac12\ln(x^2+1)$ and $dv=dx$ gives $\frac x2\ln(x^2+1)-\frac12\int
x\cdot\frac{2x}{x^2+1}dx=\frac x2\ln(x^2+1)-\int\frac{x^2}{x^2+1}dx$. Writing
$\frac{x^2}{x^2+1}=1-\frac{1}{x^2+1}$ gives the last integral as $x-\arctan
x$, so the primitive is the stated one; differentiating it gives
$\frac12\ln(x^2+1)+\frac{x^2}{x^2+1}-1+\frac{1}{x^2+1}=\frac12\ln(x^2+1)$,
which checks it.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #arctangent}

$\displaystyle\int\frac{2\arctan(e^x)}{e^{2x}}\,dx=-e^{-2x}\arctan(e^x)-e^{-x}-\arctan(e^x)+C$.

::: pf-proof

Integrate by parts with $u=\arctan(e^x)$ and $dv=2e^{-2x}dx$, so
$du=\frac{e^x}{1+e^{2x}}dx$ and $v=-e^{-2x}$: the integral is
$-e^{-2x}\arctan(e^x)+\int\frac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{2x}}dx$. Next,
$\frac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{2x}}=e^{-x}-\frac{e^x}{1+e^{2x}}$, because
$e^{-x}(1+e^{2x})-e^x=e^{-x}$, so
$\int\frac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{2x}}dx=-e^{-x}-\arctan(e^x)$. Substituting gives the
stated primitive; differentiating it,
$2e^{-2x}\arctan(e^x)-\frac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{2x}}+e^{-x}-\frac{e^x}{1+e^{2x}}=2e^{-2x}\arctan(e^x)$
since
$e^{-x}-\frac{e^{-x}+e^x}{1+e^{2x}}=e^{-x}-\frac{e^{-x}(1+e^{2x})}{1+e^{2x}}=0$.

:::
