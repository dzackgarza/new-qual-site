::: {.problem}
Evaluate the Gaussian integrals
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(x-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)}\,dx,
\qquad \sigma\ne0,
$$
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}\,dx,
\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2/2}\,dx,
$$
and
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2+6x-3}\,dx.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Write $G=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-u^2}\,du$ for the standard Gaussian
integral, and let $\sigma\ne0$ and $\mu$ be real.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #gauss}

$G=\sqrt\pi$.

::: pf-proof

$G^2=\int_{\RR}\int_{\RR}e^{-(u^2+v^2)}\,du\,dv$, which in polar coordinates
$(r,\theta)$ is $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}r\,dr\,d\theta$ by Fubini
and the change of variables theorem, the integrand being positive so that the
double integral exists. The inner integral is
$\left[-\frac12e^{-r^2}\right]_0^\infty=\frac12$, so
$G^2=2\pi\cdot\frac12=\pi$; $G>0$, so $G=\sqrt\pi$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #scaled}

$\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(x-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)}\,dx=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\abs{\sigma}$.

::: pf-proof

Put $x=\mu+\sqrt2\,\abs\sigma\,u$, so that $\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}=u^2$
because $\sigma^2=\abs\sigma^2$, and $dx=\sqrt2\,\abs\sigma\,du$. As $x$ runs
over $\RR$ so does $u$, so the integral is
$\sqrt2\,\abs\sigma\,G=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\abs\sigma$ by step [](#gauss){.pf-ref}.
Taking $\mu=0$ and $2\sigma^2=1$, that is $\sigma=\pm1/\sqrt2$, gives
$\sqrt{2\pi}/\sqrt2=\sqrt\pi$, recovering $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #remaining}

$\displaystyle\int_{\RR}e^{-x^2}\,dx=\sqrt\pi$,
$\displaystyle\int_{\RR}e^{-x^2/2}\,dx=\sqrt{2\pi}$, and
$\displaystyle\int_{\RR}e^{-x^2+6x-3}\,dx=e^6\sqrt\pi$.

::: pf-proof

The first two are step [](#scaled){.pf-ref} with
$(\mu,\sigma)=(0,\pm1/\sqrt2)$ and $(\mu,\sigma)=(0,\pm1)$ respectively:
$2\sigma^2=1$ and $2\sigma^2=2$. For the third, completing the square gives
$-x^2+6x-3=-(x-3)^2+6$, so the integral is $e^6\int_{\RR}e^{-(x-3)^2}\,dx$,
and the substitution $u=x-3$ together with the first of these values gives
$e^6\sqrt\pi$.

:::
