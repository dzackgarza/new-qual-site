::: {.problem}
Evaluate
$$
\int\frac{x^3}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\,dx.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Take $\abs{x} < 5$, so that $25-x^2>0$ and the integrand is defined.

::: pf

::: pf-step
$\int \frac{x^3}{\sqrt{25-x^2}} \, dx = \frac{1}{3}(25-x^2)^{3/2} - 25\sqrt{25-x^2} + C = \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{25-x^2}(-x^2 - 50) + C$.

::: pf-proof
The subclaims carry out the algebraic substitution $u=\sqrt{25-x^2}$, integrate in $u$, and substitute back.

::: pf-step
Make the algebraic substitution $u = \sqrt{25 - x^2}$.
Then $u^2 = 25 - x^2 \implies x^2 = 25 - u^2$, and $2u \, du = -2x \, dx \implies x \, dx = -u \, du$.

::: pf-proof
Differentiating $u^2=25-x^2$ gives $2u\,du=-2x\,dx$, and dividing by $2$ gives the displayed relation; on $\abs x<5$ the quantity $u$ is positive and differentiable.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Rewrite the integrand in terms of $u$: $$\int \frac{x^3}{\sqrt{25-x^2}} \, dx = \int \frac{x^2 \cdot x \, dx}{\sqrt{25-x^2}} = \int \frac{25 - u^2}{u} (-u \, du) = \int (u^2 - 25) \, du.$$

::: pf-proof
Write $x^3=x^2\cdot x$, replace $x^2$ by $25-u^2$, $\sqrt{25-x^2}$ by $u$ and $x\,dx$ by $-u\,du$; the factor $u$ cancels and the sign is absorbed.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Integrate polynomials in $u$: $$\int (u^2 - 25) \, du = \frac{1}{3} u^3 - 25u + C.$$

::: pf-proof
Integrate $u^2$ and the constant term $-25$ separately: $\int u^2du=\frac13u^3$ and $\int 25\,du=25u$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Substitute back $u = \sqrt{25-x^2}$: $$\frac{1}{3} (\sqrt{25-x^2})^3 - 25\sqrt{25-x^2} + C = \frac{1}{3}(25-x^2)^{3/2} - 25(25-x^2)^{1/2} + C.$$

::: pf-proof
Replace $u$ by $\sqrt{25-x^2}$ in each term. Factoring $\sqrt{25-x^2}$ out of the two terms gives $\sqrt{25-x^2}\left(\frac{25-x^2}{3}-25\right)=\sqrt{25-x^2}\cdot\frac{25-x^2-75}{3}=\frac13\sqrt{25-x^2}(-x^2-50)$, which is the factored form stated in the claim.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
The trigonometric substitution $x = 5\sin(\theta)$ gives the same antiderivative.

::: pf-proof
The subclaims transform the integral with $x=5\sin\theta$, integrate the cube of the sine, and substitute back.

::: pf-step
Substitute $x = 5\sin(\theta)$ with $\theta \in (-\pi/2, \pi/2)$.
Then $dx = 5\cos(\theta)\, d\theta$ and $\sqrt{25-x^2} = 5\cos(\theta)$.

::: pf-proof
Differentiating gives $dx=5\cos\theta\,d\theta$, and $\sqrt{25-x^2}=\sqrt{25\cos^2\theta}=5\abs{\cos\theta}=5\cos\theta$ because the cosine is positive on $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The integral transforms into: $$\int \frac{125\sin^3(\theta)}{5\cos(\theta)} \cdot 5\cos(\theta) \, d\theta = 125 \int \sin^3(\theta) \, d\theta = 125 \int (1 - \cos^2(\theta)) \sin(\theta) \, d\theta.$$

::: pf-proof
Substituting $x^3=125\sin^3\theta$, $dx=5\cos\theta\,d\theta$ and $\sqrt{25-x^2}=5\cos\theta$ cancels the common cosine, and $\sin^3\theta=\sin\theta(1-\cos^2\theta)$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Substituting $w = \cos(\theta) \implies dw = -\sin(\theta)\, d\theta$: $$125 \int (w^2 - 1) \, dw = 125 \left(\frac{w^3}{3} - w\right) + C.$$

::: pf-proof
With $dw=-\sin\theta\,d\theta$ the integral becomes $-125\int(1-w^2)\,dw=125\int(w^2-1)\,dw$, and the polynomial integrates term by term.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since $w = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\sqrt{25-x^2}}{5}$: $$125 \left(\frac{(\sqrt{25-x^2})^3}{3 \cdot 125} - \frac{\sqrt{25-x^2}}{5}\right) + C = \frac{1}{3}(\sqrt{25-x^2})^3 - 25\sqrt{25-x^2} + C.$$

::: pf-proof
$\cos\theta=\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta}=\sqrt{25-x^2}/5$ on the chosen range; substituting into $125\left(\frac{w^3}{3}-w\right)$ gives $\frac{125}{3}\cdot\frac{(25-x^2)^{3/2}}{125}=\frac13(25-x^2)^{3/2}$ and $125\cdot\frac{\sqrt{25-x^2}}{5}=25\sqrt{25-x^2}$.
:::
