::: {.problem}
Let $\pi:E\to M$ be a rank-two vector bundle, and let $\Phi$ be its Thom class.
Wedge product with $\Phi$ gives an isomorphism
$$
H^*(M)\longrightarrow H_{cv}^{*+2}(E).
$$
Thus every class $\omega\in H_{cv}^{*+2}(E)$ can be written as
$$
\omega=\Phi\wedge\pi^*u
$$
for some $u\in H^*(M)$.

Give a natural description of the class $u$ for which
$$
\Phi\wedge\pi^*u=\Phi\wedge\Phi.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume the bundle is oriented over the coefficient ring being used, so that
the Thom class exists.
Let
$$
s:M\to E
$$
be the zero section.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

The natural class is the Euler class
$$
\boxed{
u=e(E)
\coloneqq
s^*\Phi
\in
H^2(M).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For an oriented rank-two bundle, the Euler class is defined as the pullback
of the Thom class along the zero section:
$$
e(E)=s^*\Phi.
$$
The remaining steps verify that this class satisfies the required identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Under the Thom isomorphism, the Gysin map of the zero section is
$$
s_!:
H^*(M)
\longrightarrow
H_{cv}^{*+2}(E),
\qquad
s_!(a)
=
\Phi\wedge\pi^*a.
$$


::: pf-proof

The normal bundle of the zero section
$$
s(M)\subset E
$$
is canonically the original vector bundle
$$
E\to M.
$$
Therefore the Gysin map for the zero section is precisely the Thom
isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

In particular,
$$
\Phi
=
s_!(1).
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} to the unit class
$$
1\in H^0(M):
$$
$$
s_!(1)
=
\Phi\wedge\pi^*(1)
=
\Phi.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The Gysin map satisfies the projection formula
$$
s_!(a)\wedge\eta
=
s_!
\bigl(
a\wedge s^*\eta
\bigr)
$$
for
$$
a\in H^*(M),
\qquad
\eta\in H^*(E)
$$
whenever the products are defined.


::: pf-proof

This is the standard projection formula for the Gysin map.
For the zero-section Thom model it follows directly from
$$
s_!(a)
=
\Phi\wedge\pi^*a
$$
and
$$
\pi\circ s
=
\operatorname{id}_M.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Apply the projection formula with
$$
a=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\eta=\Phi.
$$
Then
$$
\Phi\wedge\Phi
=
s_!(s^*\Phi).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref},
$$
\Phi=s_!(1).
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi\wedge\Phi
&=
s_!(1)\wedge\Phi\\
&=
s_!
\bigl(
1\wedge s^*\Phi
\bigr)\\
&=
s_!(s^*\Phi).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Since
$$
s^*\Phi=e(E),
$$
step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} becomes
$$
\Phi\wedge\Phi
=
s_!(e(E)).
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute the definition of the Euler class from step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Using the Thom formula for $s_!$,
$$
s_!(e(E))
=
\Phi\wedge\pi^*e(E).
$$


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} with
$$
a=e(E).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\Phi\wedge\Phi
=
\Phi\wedge\pi^*e(E).
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

By uniqueness in the Thom isomorphism, the class $u$ satisfying
$$
\Phi\wedge\pi^*u
=
\Phi\wedge\Phi
$$
is exactly
$$
\boxed{
u=e(E)=s^*\Phi.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
H^*(M)
\xrightarrow{\cong}
H_{cv}^{*+2}(E),
\qquad
u\mapsto
\Phi\wedge\pi^*u
$$
is an isomorphism by hypothesis.
Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} shows that
$$
e(E)
$$
maps to
$$
\Phi\wedge\Phi.
$$
Hence it is the unique preimage.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Equivalently,
$$
u
=
\pi_*
\bigl(
\Phi\wedge\Phi
\bigr),
$$
where
$$
\pi_*
$$
is fiber integration.


::: pf-proof

Fiber integration is the inverse of the Thom isomorphism in this
normalization:
$$
\pi_*
\bigl(
\Phi\wedge\pi^*a
\bigr)
=
a.
$$
Apply it to the identity in step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives the requested natural description of $u$.

:::
