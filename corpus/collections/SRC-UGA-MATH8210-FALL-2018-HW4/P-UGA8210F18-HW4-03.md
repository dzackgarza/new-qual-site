::: {.problem}
Let $L^n$ denote the tautological complex line bundle over $\CP^n$,
$$
L^n=\{(\ell,z)\in \CP^n\times\CC^{n+1}:z\in\ell\}.
$$
Compute the Euler class of $L^1$ by writing down transition functions for $L^1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Identify
$$
\CP^1
\cong
S^2.
$$
Let
$$
\eta
\in
H^2(\CP^1;\ZZ)
\cong
\ZZ
$$
be the positive generator normalized by
$$
\langle\eta,[\CP^1]\rangle=1.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

Use the standard affine cover
$$
U_0
\coloneqq
\{[z_0:z_1]:z_0\ne0\},
$$
$$
U_1
\coloneqq
\{[z_0:z_1]:z_1\ne0\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

These two open sets cover
$$
\CP^1,
$$
since a projective point cannot have both homogeneous coordinates zero.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

On
$$
U_0,
$$
use the affine coordinate
$$
z
\coloneqq
\frac{z_1}{z_0}.
$$
Define a nonvanishing local section of the tautological bundle by
$$
s_0([1:z])
\coloneqq
(1,z)
\in
\CC^2.
$$


::: pf-proof

The vector
$$
(1,z)
$$
spans the line represented by
$$
[1:z],
$$
so it is a vector in the corresponding fiber of the tautological line
bundle.
It never vanishes.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

On
$$
U_1,
$$
use
$$
w
\coloneqq
\frac{z_0}{z_1}.
$$
Define
$$
s_1([w:1])
\coloneqq
(w,1).
$$


::: pf-proof

Exactly as in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}, this is a nowhere-zero local frame for the
tautological line bundle on
$$
U_1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

On the overlap
$$
U_0\cap U_1,
$$
one has
$$
w=z^{-1}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
s_1
=
z^{-1}s_0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Indeed,
$$
s_1
=
(w,1)
=
\left(
\frac1z,1
\right)
=
\frac1z(1,z)
=
z^{-1}s_0.
$$
Thus the transition function is
$$
g_{01}(z)
=
z^{-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Retract the overlap
$$
U_0\cap U_1
\cong
\CC^*
$$
onto the unit circle
$$
|z|=1.
$$
The transition function restricts to
$$
g_{01}(e^{i\theta})
=
e^{-i\theta}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Substitute
$$
z=e^{i\theta}
$$
into
$$
g_{01}(z)=z^{-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The clutching map
$$
S^1\to GL_1(\CC)
\simeq
S^1
$$
has degree
$$
-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

The map
$$
e^{i\theta}
\longmapsto
e^{-i\theta}
$$
traverses the target circle once in the negative direction.
Its winding number, hence degree, is
$$
-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For a complex line bundle over
$$
S^2,
$$
the first Chern number equals the degree of its clutching function.
Hence
$$
\boxed{
c_1(L^1)
=
-\eta.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Complex line bundles over
$$
S^2
$$
are classified by
$$
\pi_1(GL_1(\CC))
\cong
\pi_1(S^1)
\cong
\ZZ.
$$
Under this classification, the integer is the first Chern number
$$
\langle c_1(L),[S^2]\rangle.
$$
Step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref} gives the integer
$$
-1.
$$
Therefore
$$
c_1(L^1)
=
-\eta.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Regard the complex line bundle
$$
L^1
$$
as an oriented real rank-two bundle using its complex orientation.
Then
$$
e(L^1)
=
c_1(L^1).
$$


::: pf-proof

For every complex line bundle, the Euler class of the underlying oriented
real two-plane bundle equals its first Chern class.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
e(L^1)
=
-\eta
\in
H^2(\CP^1;\ZZ).
}
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\boxed{
\langle e(L^1),[\CP^1]\rangle
=
-1.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Combine steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives the transition function and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} gives the Euler class
determined by its winding number.
