::: {.problem}
Let $M$ be a manifold.
Show that two vector bundles on $M$ are isomorphic if and only if their cocycles are equivalent, relative to some open cover.
:::

::: {.solution}

Fix rank
$$
r.
$$
Let
$$
E\to M
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
F\to M
$$
be rank-$r$ vector bundles.

Choose an open cover
$$
\mathcal U=\{U_i\}_{i\in I}
$$
on which both bundles are trivial, with trivializations
$$
\phi_i:
E\restrict_{U_i}
\xrightarrow{\cong}
U_i\times\RR^r
$$
and
$$
\psi_i:
F\restrict_{U_i}
\xrightarrow{\cong}
U_i\times\RR^r.
$$

Use the convention that the transition functions are defined by
$$
\phi_i\phi_j^{-1}(x,v)
=
(x,g_{ij}(x)v)
$$
and
$$
\psi_i\psi_j^{-1}(x,v)
=
(x,h_{ij}(x)v)
$$
on
$$
U_i\cap U_j.
$$

Two cocycles
$$
\{g_{ij}\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\{h_{ij}\}
$$
are called equivalent on this cover if there exist continuous maps
$$
a_i:
U_i\to GL_r(\RR)
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{
h_{ij}
=
a_i g_{ij}a_j^{-1}
}
$$
on every overlap.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Suppose first that
$$
\Phi:E\xrightarrow{\cong}F
$$
is a vector-bundle isomorphism over
$$
M.
$$
For every $i$, the local expression of $\Phi$ has the form
$$
\psi_i\Phi\phi_i^{-1}(x,v)
=
(x,a_i(x)v)
$$
for a continuous map
$$
a_i:
U_i\to GL_r(\RR).
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
\Phi
$$
covers the identity on $M$, the first coordinate remains $x$.
Because $\Phi$ is fiberwise linear, the second coordinate is multiplication
by a linear map
$$
a_i(x):\RR^r\to\RR^r.
$$
Because $\Phi$ is a bundle isomorphism, every $a_i(x)$ is invertible.
Continuity of the local representative gives continuity of
$$
a_i.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

On an overlap
$$
U_i\cap U_j,
$$
the local expressions satisfy
$$
a_i g_{ij}
=
h_{ij}a_j.
$$


::: pf-proof

Take
$$
(x,v)
\in
(U_i\cap U_j)\times\RR^r.
$$
Compute the image of
$$
\phi_j^{-1}(x,v)
\in
E_x
$$
in the $i$th trivialization of $F$ in two ways.

First convert in $E$ from chart $j$ to chart $i$:
$$
\phi_i\phi_j^{-1}(x,v)
=
(x,g_{ij}(x)v).
$$
Then apply the $i$th local formula for $\Phi$:
$$
(x,a_i(x)g_{ij}(x)v).
$$

Alternatively first apply the $j$th local formula:
$$
(x,a_j(x)v)
$$
in the $j$th trivialization of $F$, then convert to the $i$th trivialization:
$$
(x,h_{ij}(x)a_j(x)v).
$$

The two expressions represent the same vector, so
$$
a_i(x)g_{ij}(x)
=
h_{ij}(x)a_j(x)
$$
for every $x$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
h_{ij}
=
a_i g_{ij}a_j^{-1}.
}
$$
Thus the two cocycles are equivalent.


::: pf-proof

Right-multiply the identity in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} by
$$
a_j^{-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Conversely, suppose the two cocycles are equivalent:
there are continuous
$$
a_i:
U_i\to GL_r(\RR)
$$
such that
$$
h_{ij}
=
a_i g_{ij}a_j^{-1}.
$$
Define local maps
$$
\Phi_i:
E\restrict_{U_i}\to F\restrict_{U_i}
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
\Phi_i
\coloneqq
\psi_i^{-1}
\circ
\bigl(
(x,v)\mapsto(x,a_i(x)v)
\bigr)
\circ
\phi_i.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Each displayed middle map is continuous, fiber-preserving, linear on every
fiber, and invertible with inverse given by
$$
(x,v)
\mapsto
(x,a_i(x)^{-1}v).
$$
Thus each
$$
\Phi_i
$$
is a local vector-bundle isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

On every overlap,
$$
\Phi_i
=
\Phi_j.
$$


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
e\in E_x
$$
with
$$
x\in U_i\cap U_j.
$$
Write
$$
\phi_j(e)
=
(x,v).
$$
Then
$$
\phi_i(e)
=
(x,g_{ij}(x)v).
$$

The $i$th local map gives, in the $i$th trivialization of $F$,
$$
(x,a_i g_{ij}v).
$$
The $j$th local map gives
$$
(x,a_jv)
$$
in the $j$th trivialization.
Converting this to the $i$th trivialization gives
$$
(x,h_{ij}a_jv).
$$

By the assumed cocycle equivalence,
$$
h_{ij}a_j
=
a_i g_{ij}.
$$
Hence the two local maps have identical coordinates.
Therefore
$$
\Phi_i(e)=\Phi_j(e).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The local maps glue to a global continuous vector-bundle map
$$
\Phi:E\to F.
$$


::: pf-proof

The subsets
$$
E\restrict_{U_i}
$$
form an open cover of $E$.
By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, the continuous maps
$$
\Phi_i
$$
agree on all pairwise overlaps.
The pasting lemma therefore produces a unique continuous global map
$$
\Phi
$$
whose restriction to
$$
E\restrict_{U_i}
$$
is $\Phi_i$.
It is fiber-preserving and fiberwise linear because these properties hold
locally.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

The map
$$
\Phi
$$
is a vector-bundle isomorphism.


::: pf-proof

On each
$$
U_i,
$$
the local inverse is given in coordinates by
$$
(x,v)
\mapsto
(x,a_i(x)^{-1}v).
$$

The relation
$$
h_{ij}
=
a_i g_{ij}a_j^{-1}
$$
is equivalent to
$$
g_{ij}
=
a_i^{-1}h_{ij}a_j,
$$
so the local inverses satisfy the same overlap compatibility and glue to a
global inverse
$$
\Phi^{-1}:F\to E.
$$
Thus
$$
\Phi
$$
is a bundle isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
E\cong F
\iff
\text{their transition cocycles are equivalent on some common open cover}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} prove that an isomorphism produces equivalent cocycles.
Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} prove that equivalent cocycles produce an isomorphism.

If the original trivializing covers differ, replace them by their common
refinement
$$
\{U_i\cap V_j\},
$$
on which both bundles are trivial.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} is the desired equivalence.
