::: {.problem}
Suppose a continuous multiplication on $\RR^n$ makes it a real division algebra.
Write $e_i$ for the $i$th standard basis vector.

1. For $p\in S^{n-1}\subset\RR^n$, show that there is a unique $a\in\RR^n$ such that
   $$
   p=ae_1.
   $$

2. If $b\in\RR^n$ is nonzero, show that
   $$
   be_1,\ldots,be_n
   $$
   are linearly independent.

3. For $p=ae_1$ as above, orthogonally project $ae_2,\ldots,ae_n$ to $T_pS^{n-1}$.
   Describe these projections and show that they remain linearly independent.

4. Deduce that $TS^{n-1}$ is trivial.
   Conclude that $\RR^3$ cannot admit a real division-algebra structure.
:::

::: {.solution}

Let
$$
A=\RR^n
$$
with the assumed real division-algebra structure.
Thus multiplication is bilinear, and multiplication by any nonzero element
on either side is an invertible linear map.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Right multiplication by
$$
e_1
$$
defines a linear map
$$
R_{e_1}:A\to A,
\qquad
R_{e_1}(a)=ae_1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Because
$$
e_1\ne0
$$
and $A$ is a division algebra, the equation
$$
ae_1=0
$$
forces
$$
a=0.
$$
Thus
$$
R_{e_1}
$$
is injective.
Since $A$ is finite-dimensional, an injective linear endomorphism is
surjective.
Hence $R_{e_1}$ is a linear isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

Therefore, for every
$$
p\in S^{n-1},
$$
there exists a unique
$$
a\in A
$$
such that
$$
\boxed{
p=ae_1.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Existence and uniqueness are exactly bijectivity of
$$
R_{e_1}.
$$
Explicitly,
$$
a=R_{e_1}^{-1}(p).
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Let
$$
b\in A
$$
be nonzero.
Left multiplication
$$
L_b:A\to A,
\qquad
L_b(x)=bx,
$$
is a linear isomorphism.


::: pf-proof

If
$$
bx=0,
$$
then, because $b\ne0$ and $A$ has no zero divisors,
$$
x=0.
$$
Thus $L_b$ is injective.
Finite-dimensionality again implies surjectivity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
be_1,\dots,be_n
}
$$
are linearly independent.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
c_1be_1+cdots+c_nbe_n=0.
$$
By bilinearity,
$$
b(c_1e_1+cdots+c_ne_n)=0.
$$
Injectivity of $L_b$ gives
$$
c_1e_1+cdots+c_ne_n=0.
$$
Since
$$
e_1,dots,e_n
$$
is a basis,
$$
c_1=cdots=c_n=0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Fix
$$
p\in S^{n-1}
$$
and write
$$
p=ae_1
$$
as in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.
Then
$$
a\ne0.
$$


::: pf-proof

If
$$
a=0,
$$
then
$$
p=ae_1=0,
$$
contradicting
$$
p\in S^{n-1}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

The tangent space of the sphere at $p$ is
$$
T_pS^{n-1}
=
p^\perp
=
\{
v\in\RR^n:
\langle v,p\rangle=0
\}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The sphere is the level set
$$
\norm{x}^2=1.
$$
Differentiating gives
$$
2\langle v,p\rangle=0
$$
for tangent vectors $v$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

For
$$
j=2,\dots,n,
$$
define the orthogonal projection of $ae_j$ to
$$
T_pS^{n-1}
$$
by
$$
\boxed{
v_j(p)
=
ae_j
-
\langle ae_j,p\rangle p.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\norm{p}=1,
$$
the orthogonal projection of a vector $w$ onto
$$
p^\perp
$$
is
$$
w-\langle w,p\rangle p.
$$
Apply this to
$$
w=ae_j.
$$
Indeed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle v_j(p),p\rangle
&=
\langle ae_j,p\rangle
-
\langle ae_j,p\rangle
\langle p,p\rangle\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

The vectors
$$
v_2(p),\dots,v_n(p)
$$
are linearly independent in
$$
T_pS^{n-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
\sum_{j=2}^n c_jv_j(p)=0.
$$
Substitute step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref}:
$$
\sum_{j=2}^n c_jae_j
-
\left(
\sum_{j=2}^n
c_j
\langle ae_j,p\rangle
\right)p
=
0.
$$
Set
$$
\lambda
\coloneqq
\sum_{j=2}^n
c_j
\langle ae_j,p\rangle.
$$
Since
$$
p=ae_1,
$$
the relation becomes
$$
a
\left(
\sum_{j=2}^n c_je_j
-
\lambda e_1
\right)
=
0.
$$

By step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref},
$$
a\ne0,
$$
so left multiplication by $a$ is injective.
Therefore
$$
\sum_{j=2}^n c_je_j
-
\lambda e_1
=
0.
$$
Linear independence of
$$
e_1,dots,e_n
$$
gives
$$
c_2=cdots=c_n=0
$$
and
$$
\lambda=0.
$$
Thus the projected vectors are independent.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The vectors
$$
v_2(p),\dots,v_n(p)
$$
form a basis of
$$
T_pS^{n-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The tangent space has dimension
$$
n-1.
$$
Step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} gives exactly
$$
n-1
$$
linearly independent tangent vectors.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

For each
$$
j=2,\dots,n,
$$
the assignment
$$
p\longmapsto v_j(p)
$$
is a continuous global tangent vector field on
$$
S^{n-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

The element
$$
a
=
R_{e_1}^{-1}(p)
$$
depends linearly, hence continuously, on $p$.

Multiplication
$$
(a,e_j)\mapsto ae_j
$$
is continuous by hypothesis.
The Euclidean inner product and the projection formula in step [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} are
continuous.
Hence
$$
v_j
$$
is a continuous tangent section.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Define
$$
\Theta:
S^{n-1}\times\RR^{n-1}
\to
TS^{n-1}
$$
by
$$
\Theta
\left(
p,
(c_2,\dots,c_n)
\right)
=
\left(
p,
\sum_{j=2}^n c_jv_j(p)
\right).
$$
Then $\Theta$ is a vector-bundle isomorphism.


::: pf-proof

The map is continuous and fiberwise linear by step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

For each fixed $p$, step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} says
$$
v_2(p),\dots,v_n(p)
$$
is a basis of
$$
T_pS^{n-1}.
$$
Thus the fiber map
$$
\RR^{n-1}
\to
T_pS^{n-1}
$$
is a linear isomorphism.

The inverse coefficients depend continuously on $p$ because the matrix
whose columns are the $v_j(p)$ has continuously varying full rank.
Hence $\Theta$ is a bundle isomorphism.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-12}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
TS^{n-1}
\cong
S^{n-1}\times\RR^{n-1}.
}
$$
Thus
$$
S^{n-1}
$$
is parallelizable.


::: pf-proof

This is exactly the conclusion of step [](#s1-11){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-13}

If
$$
\RR^3
$$
admitted a real division-algebra structure, then
$$
S^2
$$
would be parallelizable.


::: pf-proof

Apply step [](#s1-12){.pf-ref} with
$$
n=3.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-14}

But
$$
S^2
$$
is not parallelizable.


::: pf-proof

A trivial tangent bundle would admit a nowhere-zero tangent vector field.
The Hairy Ball Theorem says that every continuous tangent vector field on
$$
S^2
$$
vanishes somewhere.
Therefore
$$
TS^2
$$
cannot be trivial.

Equivalently, its Euler class is nonzero:
$$
\langle e(TS^2),[S^2]\rangle
=
\chi(S^2)
=
2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-15}

Hence
$$
\boxed{
\RR^3
\text{ admits no real division-algebra structure}.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-13){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-14){.pf-ref} give a contradiction to the existence of such a
structure.

:::

steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} prove part (3), and steps [](#s1-10){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-15){.pf-ref} prove part (4).

::: remark
The assignment sheet defines a division algebra by the condition that $a=bx$ with $b\neq0$ implies $a=yb$ for some $y$, which does not give the uniqueness in part 1. The statement here uses the standard definition: the multiplication is bilinear, and multiplication on either side by a nonzero element is invertible.
:::
