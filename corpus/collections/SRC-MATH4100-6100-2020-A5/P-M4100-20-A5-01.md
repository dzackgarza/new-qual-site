::: {.problem}
(a) Prove that every bounded sequence in $\RR^n$ has a convergent subsequence, using the one-dimensional result.

(b) Deduce that every closed and bounded subset of $\RR^n$ is sequentially compact.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let
$$
x_m=(x_m^{(1)},\ldots,x_m^{(n)})
$$
be a bounded sequence in $\RR^n$. Then each coordinate sequence
$$
(x_m^{(j)})_{m\geq1}
$$
is bounded in $\RR$.


::: pf-proof

Choose $M>0$ with
$$
\|x_m\|_2\leq M
$$
for every $m$. For each coordinate,
$$
|x_m^{(j)}|
\leq
\|x_m\|_2
\leq M.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There is a subsequence of $(x_m)$ whose first coordinate converges.


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}, the real sequence $(x_m^{(1)})$ is bounded. The
one-dimensional Bolzano--Weierstrass theorem therefore gives a subsequence
$$
(x_{m_1(k)})_{k\geq1}
$$
for which
$$
x_{m_1(k)}^{(1)}
$$
converges.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

For each $j=2,\ldots,n$, after a subsequence has been chosen whose first
$j-1$ coordinates converge, one can choose a further subsequence whose first
$j$ coordinates all converge.


::: pf-proof

Suppose
$$
(x_{m_{j-1}(k)})_{k\geq1}
$$
has convergent coordinate sequences $1,\ldots,j-1$. Its $j$th coordinate
sequence remains bounded by step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. Apply the one-dimensional
Bolzano--Weierstrass theorem to
$$
(x_{m_{j-1}(k)}^{(j)})_{k\geq1}
$$
and select a further subsequence
$$
(x_{m_j(k)})_{k\geq1}
$$
on which the $j$th coordinate converges. Since a subsequence of a convergent
sequence converges to the same limit, the first $j-1$ coordinates still
converge.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

There is a subsequence
$$
x_{m_k}
$$
and real numbers $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ such that
$$
x_{m_k}^{(j)}\longrightarrow a_j
$$
for every $j=1,\ldots,n$.


::: pf-proof

Start with step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and apply step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} successively for
$$
j=2,3,\ldots,n.
$$
The final subsequence has convergence in every coordinate.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The subsequence in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} converges in $\RR^n$ to
$$
a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n).
$$


::: pf-proof

Let $\varepsilon>0$. For each $j$, coordinate convergence gives an index
$N_j$ such that
$$
|x_{m_k}^{(j)}-a_j|
<
\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt n}
$$
whenever $k\geq N_j$. Let
$$
N=\max\{N_1,\ldots,N_n\}.
$$
Then for $k\geq N$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|x_{m_k}-a\|_2^2
&=
\sum_{j=1}^n
|x_{m_k}^{(j)}-a_j|^2\\
&<
n\frac{\varepsilon^2}{n}
=
\varepsilon^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\|x_{m_k}-a\|_2<\varepsilon,
$$
so $x_{m_k}\to a$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

Every bounded sequence in $\RR^n$ has a convergent subsequence.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} construct such a subsequence.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

Part (a) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-6){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

Let $K\subseteq\RR^n$ be closed and bounded, and let $(x_m)$ be a
sequence in $K$. Then $(x_m)$ has a subsequence converging in $\RR^n$.


::: pf-proof

Because $K$ is bounded, the sequence $(x_m)$ is bounded. Part (a) therefore
gives a subsequence
$$
x_{m_k}\longrightarrow x
$$
for some $x\in\RR^n$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-9}

The limit $x$ from step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} belongs to $K$.


::: pf-proof

Suppose $x\notin K$. Since $K$ is closed,
$$
\RR^n\setminus K
$$
is open. Hence there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$
B(x,\varepsilon)
\subseteq
\RR^n\setminus K.
$$
But $x_{m_k}\to x$, so for all sufficiently large $k$,
$$
x_{m_k}\in B(x,\varepsilon),
$$
contradicting $x_{m_k}\in K$. Thus $x\in K$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-10}

Every closed and bounded subset of $\RR^n$ is sequentially compact.


::: pf-proof

Given any sequence in such a set, step [](#s1-8){.pf-ref} produces a convergent subsequence,
and step [](#s1-9){.pf-ref} shows that its limit lies in the set.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-11}

Part (b) follows.


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-10){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-7){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-11){.pf-ref} establish parts (a) and (b), respectively.

:::
