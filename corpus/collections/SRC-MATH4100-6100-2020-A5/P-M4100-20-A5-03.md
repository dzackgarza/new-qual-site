::: {.problem}
Determine which of the following functions are metrics on $\mathbb R$:
\[
d_1(x,y)=(x-y)^2,
\qquad
d_2(x,y)=\sqrt{|x-y|},
\]
\[
d_3(x,y)=|x^2-y^2|,
\qquad
d_4(x,y)=|x-2y|,
\]
and
\[
d_5(x,y)=\frac{|x-y|}{1+|x-y|}.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function
$$
d_1(x,y)=(x-y)^2
$$
is not a metric.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
x=0,\qquad y=1,\qquad z=2.
$$
Then
$$
d_1(0,2)=4,
$$
while
$$
d_1(0,1)+d_1(1,2)=1+1=2.
$$
Thus the triangle inequality fails.
:::

<1>2. The function
$$
d_2(x,y)=\sqrt{|x-y|}
$$
satisfies nonnegativity, symmetry, and separation.

::: {.proof}
These properties follow directly from the corresponding properties of
$|x-y|$ and the fact that the square-root function is nonnegative and vanishes
only at $0$.
:::

<1>3. The function $d_2$ satisfies the triangle inequality.

::: {.proof}
For $x,y,z\in\RR$, the ordinary triangle inequality gives
$$
|x-z|
\leq
|x-y|+|y-z|.
$$
Since the square-root function is increasing on $[0,\infty)$,
$$
\sqrt{|x-z|}
\leq
\sqrt{|x-y|+|y-z|}.
$$
For $a,b\geq0$,
$$
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt a+\sqrt b,
$$
because
$$
(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2
=
a+b+2\sqrt{ab}
\geq
a+b.
$$
Applying this with
$$
a=|x-y|,
\qquad
b=|y-z|
$$
gives
$$
d_2(x,z)
\leq
d_2(x,y)+d_2(y,z).
$$
:::

<1>4. Therefore $d_2$ is a metric.

::: {.proof}
Steps <1>2 and <1>3 verify all metric axioms.
:::

<1>5. The function
$$
d_3(x,y)=|x^2-y^2|
$$
is not a metric.

::: {.proof}
Take
$$
x=1,
\qquad
y=-1.
$$
Then $x\neq y$, but
$$
d_3(1,-1)
=
|1-1|
=0.
$$
Thus the separation axiom fails.
:::

<1>6. The function
$$
d_4(x,y)=|x-2y|
$$
is not a metric.

::: {.proof}
For example,
$$
d_4(1,1)=|1-2|=1\neq0.
$$
Thus a point need not have zero distance from itself. In addition,
$$
d_4(0,1)=2
\qquad\text{but}\qquad
d_4(1,0)=1,
$$
so symmetry also fails.
:::

<1>7. Let
$$
\phi(t)=\frac{t}{1+t},
\qquad t\geq0.
$$
Then $\phi$ is increasing.

::: {.proof}
If $0\leq s\leq t$, then
$$
\frac{s}{1+s}\leq\frac{t}{1+t}
$$
because cross-multiplication by the positive denominators gives
$$
s(1+t)\leq t(1+s)
\iff
s\leq t.
$$
:::

<1>8. The function $\phi$ is subadditive:
$$
\phi(a+b)\leq\phi(a)+\phi(b)
$$
for all $a,b\geq0$.

::: {.proof}
Direct calculation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi(a)+\phi(b)-\phi(a+b)
&=
\frac{a}{1+a}
+
\frac{b}{1+b}
-
\frac{a+b}{1+a+b}\\
&=
\frac{ab(2+a+b)}
{(1+a)(1+b)(1+a+b)}
\geq0.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

<1>9. The function
$$
d_5(x,y)=\frac{|x-y|}{1+|x-y|}
$$
satisfies the triangle inequality.

::: {.proof}
Set
$$
a=|x-y|,
\qquad
b=|y-z|.
$$
The ordinary triangle inequality gives
$$
|x-z|\leq a+b.
$$
By step <1>7,
$$
d_5(x,z)
=
\phi(|x-z|)
\leq
\phi(a+b).
$$
By step <1>8,
$$
\phi(a+b)
\leq
\phi(a)+\phi(b)
=
d_5(x,y)+d_5(y,z).
$$
:::

<1>10. The function $d_5$ is a metric.

::: {.proof}
Nonnegativity and symmetry are immediate from the absolute value. Also,
$$
d_5(x,y)=0
\iff
|x-y|=0
\iff
x=y.
$$
The triangle inequality is step <1>9.
:::

<1>11. The complete classification is
$$
\boxed{
d_2\text{ and }d_5\text{ are metrics; }
d_1,d_3,d_4\text{ are not.}
}
$$

::: {.proof}
Combine steps <1>1, <1>4, <1>5, <1>6, and <1>10.
:::

<1>12. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>11 answers all five cases.
:::
