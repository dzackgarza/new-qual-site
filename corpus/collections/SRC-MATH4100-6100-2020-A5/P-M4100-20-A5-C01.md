::: {.problem}
Construct an explicit bijection from $\mathbb R$ onto the proper subset
\[
\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q.
\]
:::

Fix an enumeration
$$
\QQ=\{q_1,q_2,q_3,\ldots\}.
$$
For definiteness, one may enumerate reduced fractions by increasing value of
$|p|+q$ for $p/q$ with $q>0$, breaking ties lexicographically. Also set
$$
s_n=\sqrt2+n,
\qquad n\in\NN.
$$
Then every $s_n$ is irrational and the $s_n$ are pairwise distinct.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The sets
$$
\QQ
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
S=\{s_n:n\in\NN\}
$$
are disjoint.


::: pf-proof

Every element of $\QQ$ is rational, while
$$
s_n=\sqrt2+n
$$
is irrational because the sum of the irrational number $\sqrt2$ and the
rational number $n$ is irrational.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Define
$$
h:\RR\longrightarrow\RR\setminus\QQ
$$
by
$$
h(x)=
\begin{cases}
s_{2n},&x=q_n,\\
s_{2n-1},&x=s_n,\\
x,&x\notin\QQ\cup S.
\end{cases}
$$
Then $h$ is well-defined and takes only irrational values.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>1, the first two cases are disjoint, and the third is disjoint from
both by definition. Thus exactly one case applies to every real number.

In the first two cases, the image is some $s_k$, hence irrational. In the
third case, $x\notin\QQ$, so $x$ itself is irrational. Therefore
$$
h(x)\in\RR\setminus\QQ
$$
for every $x\in\RR$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $h$ is injective.


::: pf-proof

The first branch maps the rationals bijectively onto the even-indexed set
$$
\{s_2,s_4,s_6,\ldots\},
$$
while the second branch maps $S$ bijectively onto the odd-indexed set
$$
\{s_1,s_3,s_5,\ldots\}.
$$
These two image sets are disjoint.

The third branch is the identity on
$$
\RR\setminus(\QQ\cup S),
$$
whose image is disjoint from $S$. Hence images from distinct branches cannot
coincide, and each branch is injective. Therefore $h$ is injective.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The map $h$ is surjective onto $\RR\setminus\QQ$.


::: pf-proof

Let
$$
y\in\RR\setminus\QQ.
$$

If $y\notin S$, then
$$
h(y)=y
$$
by the third branch.

If
$$
y=s_k
$$
for some $k$, there are two cases. If $k=2n$ is even, then
$$
h(q_n)=s_{2n}=y.
$$
If $k=2n-1$ is odd, then
$$
h(s_n)=s_{2n-1}=y.
$$
Thus every irrational number has a preimage.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
h:\RR\xrightarrow{\sim}\RR\setminus\QQ
}
$$
is a bijection.


::: pf-proof

Step <1>3 proves injectivity and step <1>4 proves surjectivity.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>5 gives the requested explicit bijection.

:::

:::
