::: {.problem}
Prove that $\mathbb R$ cannot be written as the disjoint union of two nonempty closed sets.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
\RR=A\sqcup B
$$
with $A$ and $B$ nonempty and closed.

::: {.proof}
Choose
$$
a\in A,
\qquad
b\in B.
$$
Since $A$ and $B$ are disjoint, $a\neq b$. Interchanging the names of the
sets if necessary, assume
$$
a<b.
$$
:::

<1>2. The set
$$
S=A\cap[a,b]
$$
is nonempty and bounded above, so
$$
c=\sup S
$$
exists and satisfies
$$
a\leq c\leq b.
$$

::: {.proof}
The point $a$ belongs to $S$, so $S$ is nonempty. Every element of $S$ is at
most $b$, so $S$ is bounded above. Completeness of $\RR$ gives its supremum
$c$.
:::

<1>3. The point $c$ belongs to $A$.

::: {.proof}
For every $n\in\NN$, the number $c-1/n$ cannot be an upper bound for $S$.
Hence choose
$$
s_n\in S
$$
with
$$
c-\frac1n<s_n\leq c.
$$
Then
$$
s_n\longrightarrow c.
$$
Since every $s_n\in A$ and $A$ is closed, the limit $c$ belongs to $A$.
:::

<1>4. The point $c$ does not belong to $B$.

::: {.proof}
The sets $A$ and $B$ are disjoint, and step <1>3 gives $c\in A$.
:::

<1>5. There exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$
(c-\varepsilon,c+\varepsilon)\cap B=\varnothing.
$$

::: {.proof}
Since $B$ is closed, its complement $\RR\setminus B$ is open. By step <1>4,
$c\in\RR\setminus B$, so some open interval around $c$ lies in that
complement.
:::

<1>6. One must have $c=b$.

::: {.proof}
Suppose $c<b$. Choose
$$
0<\delta<\min\{\varepsilon,b-c\}.
$$
Then
$$
c<c+\delta<b
$$
and
$$
c+\delta\notin B
$$
by step <1>5. Since
$$
\RR=A\sqcup B,
$$
it follows that
$$
c+\delta\in A.
$$
Thus
$$
c+\delta\in A\cap[a,b]=S,
$$
contradicting the fact that $c$ is an upper bound for $S$. Hence $c=b$.
:::

<1>7. The equality $c=b$ is impossible.

::: {.proof}
By step <1>3, $c\in A$. By step <1>6, $c=b$, while the original choice gave
$b\in B$. Hence
$$
b\in A\cap B,
$$
contradicting disjointness.
:::

<1>8. Therefore $\RR$ cannot be written as the disjoint union of two
nonempty closed sets.

::: {.proof}
The assumed decomposition led to the contradiction in step <1>7.
:::

<1>9. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>8 is the required conclusion.
:::
:::
