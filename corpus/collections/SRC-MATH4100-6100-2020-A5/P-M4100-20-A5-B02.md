::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal C$ be the middle-thirds Cantor set. Prove
\[
\mathcal C+\mathcal C=[0,2],
\]
where
\[
\mathcal C+\mathcal C=\{x+y:x,y\in\mathcal C\}.
\]

Hint: consider the intersection of $\mathcal C\times\mathcal C$ with the lines $x+y=c$, $c\in[0,2]$, and use nested compact sets.
:::

Let
$$
C_0=[0,1],
$$
and let $C_n$ denote the $n$th finite-stage Cantor approximation, so that
$$
C_{n+1}
=
\frac13 C_n
\cup
\left(
\frac23+\frac13 C_n
\right),
$$
and
$$
\mathcal C
=
\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}C_n.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

One has
$$
C_0+C_0=[0,2].
$$


::: pf-proof

Since $C_0=[0,1]$,
$$
C_0+C_0
=
[0,1]+[0,1]
=
[0,2].
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If
$$
C_n+C_n=[0,2],
$$
then
$$
C_{n+1}+C_{n+1}=[0,2].
$$


::: pf-proof

Using the recursive description of $C_{n+1}$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{n+1}+C_{n+1}
&=
\frac13(C_n+C_n)\\
&\qquad\cup
\left(
\frac23+\frac13(C_n+C_n)
\right)\\
&\qquad\cup
\left(
\frac43+\frac13(C_n+C_n)
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
By the induction hypothesis this becomes
$$
[0,2/3]
\cup
[2/3,4/3]
\cup
[4/3,2]
=[0,2].
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

For every $n\geq0$,
$$
\boxed{C_n+C_n=[0,2].}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>1 is the base case and step <1>2 is the induction step.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Fix $c\in[0,2]$ and define
$$
K_n
=
\{(x,y)\in C_n\times C_n:x+y=c\}.
$$
Then each $K_n$ is nonempty.


::: pf-proof

By step <1>3,
$$
c\in C_n+C_n.
$$
Hence there exist $x,y\in C_n$ with
$$
x+y=c,
$$
so $(x,y)\in K_n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Each $K_n$ is compact.


::: pf-proof

The set $C_n$ is a finite union of closed bounded intervals, hence compact.
Therefore
$$
C_n\times C_n
$$
is compact in $\RR^2$.

The line
$$
L_c=\{(x,y)\in\RR^2:x+y=c\}
$$
is closed, since it is the inverse image of the closed singleton $\{c\}$
under the continuous map
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto x+y.
$$
Thus
$$
K_n=(C_n\times C_n)\cap L_c
$$
is closed in a compact set and therefore compact.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The compact sets $K_n$ are nested:
$$
K_0\supseteq K_1\supseteq K_2\supseteq\cdots.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
C_{n+1}\subseteq C_n,
$$
one has
$$
C_{n+1}\times C_{n+1}
\subseteq
C_n\times C_n.
$$
Intersecting both sides with the fixed line $L_c$ gives
$$
K_{n+1}\subseteq K_n.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The intersection
$$
\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}K_n
$$
is nonempty.


::: pf-proof

By steps <1>4--<1>6, $(K_n)$ is a nested sequence of nonempty compact subsets
of the compact set $K_0$. The nested compact-set theorem gives
$$
\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}K_n\neq\varnothing.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If
$$
(x,y)\in\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}K_n,
$$
then
$$
x,y\in\mathcal C
$$
and
$$
x+y=c.
$$


::: pf-proof

Membership in every $K_n$ gives
$$
x,y\in C_n
$$
for every $n$, hence
$$
x,y\in\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}C_n=\mathcal C.
$$
The defining equation of each $K_n$ gives $x+y=c$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Every $c\in[0,2]$ belongs to
$$
\mathcal C+\mathcal C.
$$


::: pf-proof

Fix $c\in[0,2]$. By step <1>7, choose
$$
(x,y)\in\bigcap_nK_n.
$$
Step <1>8 gives
$$
x,y\in\mathcal C
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x+y=c.
$$
Thus
$$
c\in\mathcal C+\mathcal C.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

One has
$$
\mathcal C+\mathcal C\subseteq[0,2].
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\mathcal C\subseteq[0,1],
$$
for $x,y\in\mathcal C$ one has
$$
0\leq x+y\leq2.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathcal C+\mathcal C=[0,2].}
$$


::: pf-proof

Step <1>9 gives
$$
[0,2]\subseteq\mathcal C+\mathcal C,
$$
and step <1>10 gives the reverse inclusion.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step <1>11 is the required conclusion.

:::

:::
