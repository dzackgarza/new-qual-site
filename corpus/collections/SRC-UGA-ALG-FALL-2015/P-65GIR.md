::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain (PID), and let $(a_1) \subseteq (a_2) \subseteq (a_3) \subseteq \cdots$ be an ascending chain of ideals in $R$.

Prove that there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $(a_j) = (a_n)$ for all $j \ge n$ (that is, $R$ satisfies the ascending chain condition on ideals).
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Prove that every PID is Noetherian by considering the union ideal $I = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty (a_k)$ and applying the principal ideal property.

::: pf

::: pf-step
The union $I = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty (a_k)$ is an ideal of $R$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Non-empty: $0 \in (a_1) \subseteq I$.
:::

::: pf-step
Closure under subtraction:

- Let $x, y \in I$.
- There exist indices $i, j \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $x \in (a_i)$ and $y \in (a_j)$.
- Let $k = \max(i, j)$. Since the chain is ascending, $(a_i) \subseteq (a_k)$ and $(a_j) \subseteq (a_k)$, so $x, y \in (a_k)$.
- Since $(a_k)$ is an ideal, $x - y \in (a_k) \subseteq I$.
:::

::: pf-step
Closure under multiplication by elements of $R$:

- Let $r \in R$ and $x \in I$.
- There exists $i \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $x \in (a_i)$.
- Since $(a_i)$ is an ideal, $r x \in (a_i) \subseteq I$.
:::

::: pf-step
Thus $I$ is an ideal of $R$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Existence of a finite stabilization index $n$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
Since $R$ is a PID, every ideal of $R$ is principal.
:::

::: pf-step
Thus there exists an element $b \in R$ such that $I = (b)$.
:::

::: pf-step
Since $b \in (b) = I = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty (a_k)$, there exists an index $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $b \in (a_n)$.
:::

::: pf-step
Since $b \in (a_n)$, the principal ideal generated by $b$ satisfies $(b) \subseteq (a_n)$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Chain stabilization for all $j \ge n$:

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
For any integer $j \ge n$, the chain condition gives $(a_n) \subseteq (a_j)$.
:::

::: pf-step
By definition of $I$, $(a_j) \subseteq I = (b)$.
:::

::: pf-step
Combining the inclusions:
$$(b) \subseteq (a_n) \subseteq (a_j) \subseteq I = (b).$$
:::

::: pf-step
Therefore $(a_j) = (a_n) = (b)$ for all $j \ge n$.
:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step
Conclusion:

::: pf-proof
The ascending chain $(a_1) \subseteq (a_2) \subseteq \cdots$ stabilizes at index $n$.
:::
