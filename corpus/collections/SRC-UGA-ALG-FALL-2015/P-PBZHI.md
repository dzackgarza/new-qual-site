::: problem
a.
Let $G$ be a finite group.
Show that there exists a field extension $K/F$ with $\gal(K/F) = G$.

  > You may assume that for any natural number $n$ there is a field extension with Galois group $S_n$.

b.
Let $K$ be a Galois extension of $F$ with $\abs{\gal(K/F)} = 12$.
Prove that there exists an intermediate field $E$ of $K/F$ with $[E: F] = 3$.

c.
With $K/F$ as in (b), does an intermediate field $L$ necessarily exist satisfying $[L: F] = 2$?
Give a proof or counterexample.
:::

::: solution
(a) Let $|G|=n$. By Cayley's theorem, $G$ embeds as a subgroup of $S_n$.
By the allowed assumption, choose a finite Galois extension $K/F$ with
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\cong S_n.
\]
Identify $G$ with its image in this Galois group and let
\[
E=K^G
\]
be its fixed field. By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, $K/E$ is
Galois and
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)=G}.
\]
Thus every finite group occurs as the Galois group of some finite Galois
extension.

(b) Put $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$, so $|\Gamma|=12$. A Sylow
$2$-subgroup $H\le\Gamma$ has order $4$. Its fixed field
\[
E=K^H
\]
satisfies
\[
[E:F]=[\Gamma:H]=\frac{12}{4}=3.
\]
Hence an intermediate field of degree $3$ always exists.

(c) No. A degree-$2$ intermediate field would correspond to a subgroup of
$\Gamma$ of index $2$, hence of order $6$. Take $\Gamma=A_4$, which occurs as
a Galois group by part (a). The group $A_4$ has no subgroup of order $6$.
Indeed, any index-$2$ subgroup would be normal and would give a nontrivial
quotient homomorphism
\[
A_4\longrightarrow C_2.
\]
But $A_4$ is generated by $3$-cycles, and every $3$-cycle must map to the
identity in $C_2$ because its image has order dividing both $3$ and $2$.
Thus every homomorphism $A_4\to C_2$ is trivial, a contradiction.

Therefore a Galois extension of degree $12$ need not have an intermediate
field of degree $2$.
:::
