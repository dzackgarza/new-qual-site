::: {.problem}
A subset $A$ of a topological space $(X,\mathcal T)$ is *countably compact* if every countable open cover $\{U_i\}_{i=1}^{\infty}$ of $A$ has a finite subcover.

(a) Show that the image of a countably compact set under a continuous map is countably compact.

(b) Show that a closed subset of a countably compact space is countably compact.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #part-a}
(a) If $f\colon X\to Y$ is continuous and $A\subseteq X$ is countably compact, then $f(A)$ is countably compact.

::: pf-proof
Let $\{V_i\}_{i\ge1}$ be a countable open cover of $f(A)$.
Then $\{f^{-1}(V_i)\}_{i\ge1}$ is a countable family of open sets covering $A$: if $a\in A$, then $f(a)\in V_i$ for some $i$.
By countable compactness, $A\subseteq f^{-1}(V_{i_1})\cup\cdots\cup f^{-1}(V_{i_m})$ for some indices $i_1,\ldots,i_m$.
If $y=f(a)$ with $a\in A$, then $a\in f^{-1}(V_{i_j})$ for some $j$, so $y\in V_{i_j}$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #part-b}
(b) If $X$ is countably compact and $F\subseteq X$ is closed, then $F$ is countably compact.

::: pf-proof
Let $\{U_i\}_{i\ge1}$ be a countable cover of $F$ by open subsets of $X$.
Since $F$ is closed, $\{U_i\}_{i\ge1}\cup\{X\setminus F\}$ is a countable open cover of $X$, so finitely many of its members cover $X$.
$X\setminus F$ contains no point of $F$, so the finitely many $U_i$ among them cover $F$.
:::
