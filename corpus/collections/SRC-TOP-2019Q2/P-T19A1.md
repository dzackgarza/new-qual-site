::: {.problem}
A subset $A$ of a topological space $(X,\mathcal T)$ is *countably compact* if every countable open cover $\{U_i\}_{i=1}^{\infty}$ of $A$ has a finite subcover.

(a) Show that the image of a countably compact set under a continuous map is countably compact.

(b) Show that a closed subset of a countably compact space is countably compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $f:X\to Y$ be continuous and let $A\subseteq X$ be countably compact.
::: {.proof}
We prove first that $f(A)$ is countably compact.
:::

<1>2. Let $\{V_i\}_{i=1}^\infty$ be a countable open cover of $f(A)$ in $Y$.
::: {.proof}
Then $\{f^{-1}(V_i)\}_{i=1}^\infty$ is a countable family of open subsets of $X$.
:::

<1>3. The family $\{f^{-1}(V_i)\}$ covers $A$.
::: {.proof}
If $a\in A$, then $f(a)\in f(A)$, so $f(a)\in V_i$ for some $i$. Hence $a\in f^{-1}(V_i)$.
:::

<1>4. There are indices $i_1,\dots,i_m$ such that
$$
A\subseteq f^{-1}(V_{i_1})\cup\cdots\cup f^{-1}(V_{i_m}).
$$
::: {.proof}
Apply countable compactness of $A$ to the cover from <1>3.
:::

<1>5. The sets $V_{i_1},\dots,V_{i_m}$ cover $f(A)$.
::: {.proof}
If $y=f(a)\in f(A)$, then <1>4 gives $a\in f^{-1}(V_{i_j})$ for some $j$, hence $y\in V_{i_j}$.
:::

<1>6. Therefore continuous images of countably compact sets are countably compact.
::: {.proof}
Every countable open cover of $f(A)$ has a finite subcover by <1>2--<1>5.
:::

<1>7. Now let $X$ be countably compact and let $F\subseteq X$ be closed.
::: {.proof}
We prove that $F$ is countably compact.
:::

<1>8. Let $\{U_i\}_{i=1}^\infty$ be a countable open cover of $F$ by open subsets of $X$.
::: {.proof}
Then
$$
\{U_i\}_{i=1}^\infty\cup\{X\setminus F\}
$$
is a countable open cover of $X$, because $F$ is closed.
:::

<1>9. A finite subfamily of this enlarged cover covers $X$.
::: {.proof}
This follows from countable compactness of $X$.
:::

<1>10. Discarding $X\setminus F$ if it occurs leaves finitely many $U_i$ that cover $F$.
::: {.proof}
The set $X\setminus F$ contains no point of $F$, so the remaining selected $U_i$ still cover every point of $F$.
:::

<1>11. Hence every closed subset of a countably compact space is countably compact.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the definition applied in <1>8--<1>10.
:::
:::
