::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the free group generated by two elements, $G=\langle x,y\rangle$.
Use covering space theory to prove that the commutator subgroup $[G,G]$ of $G$ (the normal subgroup generated by $xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$) is not finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $R=S^1\vee S^1$ be the rose with oriented loops labeled $x$ and $y$, so $\pi_1(R)\cong G=F(x,y)$.
::: {.proof}
We use the covering corresponding to the commutator subgroup.
:::

<1>2. The subgroup $[G,G]$ is the kernel of the abelianization map
$$
\operatorname{ab}:F(x,y)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad x\mapsto(1,0),\quad y\mapsto(0,1).
$$
::: {.proof}
By definition, abelianization quotients a group by its commutator subgroup.
:::

<1>3. The connected covering graph corresponding to $[G,G]$ is the integer grid.
::: {.proof}
Its vertices are the cosets $G/[G,G]\cong\mathbb Z^2$. From a vertex $(m,n)$ there is an $x$-edge to $(m+1,n)$ and a $y$-edge to $(m,n+1)$. The label-preserving map to the rose is a covering, and the subgroup represented by loops at $(0,0)$ is exactly the set of words with zero abelianization, namely $[G,G]$.
:::

<1>4. The grid graph has fundamental group a free group of infinite rank.
::: {.proof}
Choose the spanning tree $T$ consisting of every vertical edge together with all horizontal edges on the row $n=0$. This graph is connected and contains no cycle: each vertex first moves vertically to the row $0$ and then horizontally along that row, giving a unique simple path to $(0,0)$. Every horizontal edge in a row $n\ne0$ lies outside $T$, and there are infinitely many such edges. For a connected graph, adjoining each edge outside a spanning tree contributes one free generator to the fundamental group. Hence the grid has free fundamental group on an infinite set.
:::

<1>5. Therefore $[G,G]$ is not finitely generated.
::: {.proof}
Covering-space theory identifies $[G,G]$ with the fundamental group of the grid cover. By <1>4 this group is free of infinite rank, hence cannot be generated by finitely many elements.
:::
:::
