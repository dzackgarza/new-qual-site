::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be the free group generated by two elements, $G=\langle x,y\rangle$.
Use covering space theory to prove that the commutator subgroup $[G,G]$ of $G$ (the normal subgroup generated by $xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$) is not finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $R=S^1\vee S^1$ with oriented loops labeled $x$ and $y$, so that $\pi_1(R)\cong G$.
$[G,G]$ is the kernel of the abelianization $\operatorname{ab}\colon G\to\ZZ^2$, $x\mapsto(1,0)$, $y\mapsto(0,1)$.

<1>1. The covering of $R$ corresponding to $[G,G]$ is the grid graph $\Gamma$ with vertex set $\ZZ^2$, an $x$-edge from $(m,n)$ to $(m+1,n)$, and a $y$-edge from $(m,n)$ to $(m,n+1)$.

::: {.proof}
Each vertex of $\Gamma$ has one incoming and one outgoing edge of each label, so the label-preserving map $\Gamma\to R$ is a covering.
A word lifts to a loop at $(0,0)$ exactly when its image under $\operatorname{ab}$ is $0$, so the subgroup of this based covering is $[G,G]$.
:::

<1>2. $\pi_1(\Gamma)$ is free of infinite rank.

::: {.proof}
Let $T$ consist of all vertical edges and the horizontal edges in the row $n=0$.
$T$ is a spanning tree: each vertex $(m,n)$ has a unique reduced path in $T$ to $(0,0)$, vertically to $(m,0)$ and then horizontally.
For a connected graph, $\pi_1$ is free on the edges outside a spanning tree, and the horizontal edges in rows $n\neq0$ are infinitely many.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
By steps <1>1 and <1>2, $[G,G]\cong\pi_1(\Gamma)$ is free of infinite rank.
Its abelianization is free abelian of infinite rank, so it is not finitely generated, and neither is $[G,G]$.
:::
