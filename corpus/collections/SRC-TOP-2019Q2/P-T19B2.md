::: {.problem}
Let $X$ be locally path-connected, and let $p:\widetilde X\to X$ and $q:\widetilde Y\to Y$ be covering maps such that both $\widetilde X$ and $\widetilde Y$ are connected and simply-connected.
Show that for every map $f:X\to Y$ there is a map $\widetilde f:\widetilde X\to\widetilde Y$ such that $$f\circ p=q\circ\widetilde f.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Since $X$ is locally path-connected and $p$ is a local homeomorphism, $\widetilde X$ is locally path-connected.
Let $g=f\circ p\colon\widetilde X\to Y$, choose $\widetilde x_0\in\widetilde X$, and, since the covering map $q$ is surjective, choose $\widetilde y_0\in\widetilde Y$ with $q(\widetilde y_0)=g(\widetilde x_0)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #image-in-subgroup}
$g_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\subseteq q_*\pi_1(\widetilde Y,\widetilde y_0)$.

::: pf-proof
$\widetilde X$ is simply connected, so the left side is trivial.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
$\widetilde X$ is path-connected and locally path-connected, so by step [](#image-in-subgroup){.pf-ref} and the lifting criterion for covering spaces there is a map $\widetilde f\colon\widetilde X\to\widetilde Y$ with $\widetilde f(\widetilde x_0)=\widetilde y_0$ and $q\circ\widetilde f=g=f\circ p$.
:::
