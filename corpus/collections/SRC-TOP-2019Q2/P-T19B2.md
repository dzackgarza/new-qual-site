::: {.problem}
Let $p:\widetilde X\to X$ and $q:\widetilde Y\to Y$ be covering maps such that both $\widetilde X$ and $\widetilde Y$ are connected and simply-connected.
Show that for every map $f:X\to Y$ there is a map $\widetilde f:\widetilde X\to\widetilde Y$ such that $$f\circ p=q\circ\widetilde f.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
The printed statement uses the standard covering-space lifting theorem without stating its usual local path-connectedness hypothesis on the domain. Under the standard exam convention that the spaces used in covering-space theory are locally path connected, the proof is immediate. Without such a hypothesis, triviality of $\pi_1$ alone is not the general lifting criterion.

Assume therefore that $\widetilde X$ is path connected and locally path connected (as is automatic, for example, when $X$ is locally path connected).

<1>1. Let
$$
g=f\circ p:\widetilde X\longrightarrow Y.
$$
Choose $\widetilde x_0\in\widetilde X$ and choose $\widetilde y_0\in\widetilde Y$ with
$$
q(\widetilde y_0)=g(\widetilde x_0).
$$
::: {.proof}
The covering map $q$ is surjective onto the connected component of $Y$ containing $g(\widetilde x_0)$; in the usual connected-covering setup one may therefore choose such a point $\widetilde y_0$.
:::

<1>2. The subgroup condition in the lifting criterion is satisfied:
$$
g_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)=0
\subseteq
q_*\pi_1(\widetilde Y,\widetilde y_0)=0.
$$
::: {.proof}
Both $\widetilde X$ and $\widetilde Y$ are simply connected by hypothesis, so both fundamental groups displayed above are trivial.
:::

<1>3. Hence there exists a lift
$$
\widetilde f:\widetilde X\longrightarrow\widetilde Y
$$
of $g$ with $\widetilde f(\widetilde x_0)=\widetilde y_0$.
::: {.proof}
Apply the covering-space lifting criterion to $g=f\circ p$ and the covering $q:\widetilde Y\to Y$, using the subgroup inclusion from <1>2.
:::

<1>4. By definition of a lift,
$$
q\circ\widetilde f=g=f\circ p.
$$
::: {.proof}
This is the defining equality for the lift obtained in <1>3.
:::

Thus the claimed lift exists under the standard local path-connectedness hypothesis required by the lifting theorem.
:::
 {.proof}
compose $f$ with the covering $p$.
:::

<1>2. Since $\widetilde X$ is simply connected, $f \circ p$ lifts to a map $\widetilde f : \widetilde X \to \widetilde Y$ through the covering $q : \widetilde Y \to Y$.
::: {.proof}
the lifting criterion (a map from a simply connected space lifts through a covering map).
:::

<1>3. The lift satisfies $q \circ \widetilde f = f \circ p$.
::: {.proof}
<1>2 (definition of a lift).
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>3.
:::
