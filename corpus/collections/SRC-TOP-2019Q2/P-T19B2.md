::: {.problem}
Let $p:\widetilde X\to X$ and $q:\widetilde Y\to Y$ be covering maps such that both $\widetilde X$ and $\widetilde Y$ are connected and simply-connected.
Show that for every map $f:X\to Y$ there is a map $\widetilde f:\widetilde X\to\widetilde Y$ such that $$f\circ p=q\circ\widetilde f.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Assume that $\widetilde X$ is locally path-connected, which holds when $X$ is locally path-connected.
Let $g=f\circ p\colon\widetilde X\to Y$, choose $\widetilde x_0\in\widetilde X$, and, since the covering map $q$ is surjective, choose $\widetilde y_0\in\widetilde Y$ with $q(\widetilde y_0)=g(\widetilde x_0)$.

<1>1. $g_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde x_0)\subseteq q_*\pi_1(\widetilde Y,\widetilde y_0)$.

::: {.proof}
$\widetilde X$ is simply connected, so the left side is trivial.
:::

<1>2. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
$\widetilde X$ is path-connected and locally path-connected, so by step <1>1 and the lifting criterion for covering spaces there is a map $\widetilde f\colon\widetilde X\to\widetilde Y$ with $\widetilde f(\widetilde x_0)=\widetilde y_0$ and $q\circ\widetilde f=g=f\circ p$.
:::
:::

::: {.remark}
The statement is false without local path-connectedness of $\widetilde X$.
Let $X$ be the quasi-circle, a compact, path-connected, simply connected subspace of $\RR^2$, with $\widetilde X=X$ and $p=\id_X$; let $Y=S^1$ with universal covering $q\colon\RR\to S^1$; and let $f\colon X\to S^1$ be the quotient map collapsing the limit segment of $X$ to a point.
Then $f$ does not lift to $\RR$ [@Hat02, §1.3, Exercise 7].
:::
