::: {.problem}
For a topological space $X$, the cone on $X$, $cX$, is the quotient space of $X\times[0,1]$ under the equivalence relation $(x,s)\sim(y,t)$ iff either $(x,s)=(y,t)$ or $s=t=1$.
The suspension of $X$, $\Sigma X$, is the union of two copies of $cX$ along $X\times\{0\}$, realized as the quotient space of $X\times[0,1]$ under the equivalence relation $(x,s)\sim(y,t)$ iff either $(x,s)=(y,t)$ or $s=t=1$ or $s=t=0$.

(a) Show that the cone on $X$ is contractible.

(b) Use a Mayer–Vietoris sequence to show that for every $n\ge 1$ we have $$\widetilde H_n(\Sigma X)\cong\widetilde H_{n-1}(X).$$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. (a) $cX$ is contractible.

::: {.proof}
Let $v$ be the cone point, the image of $X\times\{1\}$.
The map $X\times[0,1]\times[0,1]\to cX$, $(x,s,t)\mapsto[x,(1-t)s+t]$, is continuous and sends $(x,1,t)$ to $v$ for all $x$, so it induces a map $H\colon cX\times[0,1]\to cX$, $H([x,s],t)=[x,(1-t)s+t]$, continuous because $X\times[0,1]\times[0,1]\to cX\times[0,1]$ is a quotient map ($[0,1]$ is locally compact Hausdorff).
$H(\cdot,0)=\id_{cX}$ and $H(\cdot,1)$ is the constant map at $v$.
:::

<1>2. Let $X\neq\varnothing$, and let $U,V\subseteq\Sigma X$ be the images of $X\times[0,\tfrac23)$ and $X\times(\tfrac13,1]$. Then $U$ and $V$ are open and contractible, and $U\cap V$ deformation retracts onto a copy of $X$.

::: {.proof}
$U$ and $V$ are images of saturated open sets, hence open.
Each is homeomorphic to an open cone $X\times[0,1)/X\times\{0\}$, which contracts to its cone point by the formula of step <1>1.
$U\cap V$ is homeomorphic to $X\times(\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$, which deformation retracts onto $X\times\{\tfrac12\}$.
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves (a).
For (b), when $X=\varnothing$ both sides are $0$.
Otherwise, with $U,V$ from step <1>2, the reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence contains the exact segment
$$\widetilde H_n(U)\oplus\widetilde H_n(V)\to\widetilde H_n(\Sigma X)\xrightarrow{\partial}\widetilde H_{n-1}(U\cap V)\to\widetilde H_{n-1}(U)\oplus\widetilde H_{n-1}(V).$$
The outer terms vanish because $U$ and $V$ are contractible, so $\partial$ is an isomorphism, and $\widetilde H_{n-1}(U\cap V)\cong\widetilde H_{n-1}(X)$.
:::
:::
