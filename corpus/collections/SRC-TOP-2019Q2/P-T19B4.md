::: {.problem}
For a topological space $X$, the cone on $X$, $cX$, is the quotient space of $X\times[0,1]$ under the equivalence relation $(x,s)\sim(y,t)$ iff either $(x,s)=(y,t)$ or $s=t=1$.
The suspension of $X$, $\Sigma X$, is the union of two copies of $cX$ along $X\times\{0\}$, realized as the quotient space of $X\times[0,1]$ under the equivalence relation $(x,s)\sim(y,t)$ iff either $(x,s)=(y,t)$ or $s=t=1$ or $s=t=0$.

(a) Show that the cone on $X$ is contractible.

(b) Use a Mayer–Vietoris sequence to show that for every $n\ge 1$ we have $$\widetilde H_n(\Sigma X)\cong\widetilde H_{n-1}(X).$$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $v$ denote the cone point of $cX$.
::: {.proof}
The cone is the quotient of $X\times[0,1]$ obtained by collapsing $X\times\{1\}$ to $v$.
:::

<1>2. The formula
$$
H([x,s],t)=[x,(1-t)s+t]
$$
defines a homotopy from $\operatorname{id}_{cX}$ to the constant map with value $v$.
::: {.proof}
If $s=1$, then $[x,1]=v$ for all $x$, and the formula remains $v$ for every $t$, so the map is well-defined on the quotient. At $t=0$ it is the identity, while at $t=1$ every point maps to $[x,1]=v$. Continuity follows from the quotient universal property applied to the continuous map $(x,s,t)\mapsto(x,(1-t)s+t)$ on $X\times[0,1]\times[0,1]$.
:::

<1>3. Hence $cX$ is contractible.
::: {.proof}
This is exactly the homotopy constructed in <1>2.
:::

<1>4. Cover $\Sigma X$ by open sets $U$ and $V$ obtained by slightly enlarging the interiors of the two cone halves across the equatorial copy of $X$.
::: {.proof}
Each of $U$ and $V$ deformation-retracts onto one cone $cX$, hence is contractible by <1>3, while $U\cap V$ deformation-retracts onto the equatorial copy of $X$.
:::

<1>5. The reduced Mayer--Vietoris sequence contains
$$
\widetilde H_n(U)\oplus\widetilde H_n(V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_n(\Sigma X)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
\widetilde H_{n-1}(U\cap V)
\longrightarrow
\widetilde H_{n-1}(U)\oplus\widetilde H_{n-1}(V).
$$
::: {.proof}
This is the reduced Mayer--Vietoris exact sequence for the open cover $\Sigma X=U\cup V$.
:::

<1>6. For every $n\ge1$, the connecting map gives an isomorphism
$$
\widetilde H_n(\Sigma X)\cong\widetilde H_{n-1}(X).
$$
::: {.proof}
By <1>3--<1>4, $U$ and $V$ are contractible, so all of their reduced homology groups vanish. Exactness in <1>5 therefore makes $\partial$ an isomorphism, and the deformation retraction $U\cap V\simeq X$ identifies its target with $\widetilde H_{n-1}(X)$.
:::
:::
