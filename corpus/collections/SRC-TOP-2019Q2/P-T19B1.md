::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be the annulus $\{re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb C:1\le r\le 2\}$, and let $B$ be the quotient space obtained from $A$ by identifying each point $e^{i\theta}$ on the circle $r=1$ with the point $2e^{i(\theta+\pi)}$ on the circle $r=2$.
Determine $\pi_1(B)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Identify the annulus with $S^1\times[0,1]$ by $(z,t)\mapsto(1+t)z$.
::: {.proof}
Under this identification the boundary relation becomes
$$
(z,0)\sim(-z,1).
$$
Thus $B$ is the mapping torus of the rotation $r(z)=-z$ of $S^1$.
:::

<1>2. The map
$$
\Phi:S^1\times[0,1]\longrightarrow S^1\times S^1,
\qquad
\Phi(z,t)=(e^{\pi i t}z,e^{2\pi i t})
$$
is constant on the equivalence classes defining $B$.
::: {.proof}
At the identified endpoints,
$$
\Phi(z,0)=(z,1),
$$
while
$$
\Phi(-z,1)=(e^{\pi i}(-z),1)=(z,1).
$$
Hence $\Phi$ descends to a continuous map $\bar\Phi:B\to S^1\times S^1$.
:::

<1>3. The descended map $\bar\Phi$ is a homeomorphism.
::: {.proof}
Given $(w,e^{2\pi i t})\in S^1\times S^1$, choose $t\in[0,1]$ and send it to the class of $(e^{-\pi i t}w,t)$. Changing the representative $t=0$ to $t=1$ changes $e^{-\pi i t}w$ by a minus sign, exactly the boundary identification. Thus this gives a well-defined continuous inverse. Equivalently, $B$ is the mapping torus of a rotation isotopic to the identity, hence a torus.
:::

<1>4. Therefore
$$
\pi_1(B)\cong\pi_1(S^1\times S^1)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
$$
::: {.proof}
The fundamental group of a product is the product of the fundamental groups, and $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z$.
:::
:::
