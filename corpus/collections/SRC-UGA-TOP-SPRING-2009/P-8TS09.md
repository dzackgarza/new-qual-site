::: problem
Let $X$ be the one-point union of $S^1\times S^1$ and $S^1$.

(a) Compute the fundamental group of $X$.

(b) Compute the homology groups of $X$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Give the torus its standard CW structure with one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $a,b$, and one $2$-cell attached by the word
\[
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]
Wedging on the additional circle adds one more $1$-cell $c$ at the same $0$-cell.

<1>1. The fundamental group has presentation
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b,c\mid [a,b]=1\rangle.
\]
::: {.proof}
For a connected CW complex with one $0$-cell, the $1$-skeleton contributes one free generator for each $1$-cell, and each $2$-cell contributes its attaching word as a relator.

Here the $1$-skeleton is
\[
S^1_a\vee S^1_b\vee S^1_c,
\]
so its fundamental group is free on $a,b,c$.
The only $2$-cell is the torus $2$-cell, whose attaching map represents
\[
[a,b].
\]
Van Kampen therefore gives
\[
\pi_1(X)
\cong
\langle a,b,c\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\pi_1(X)\cong\ZZ^2*\ZZ.
\]
:::

<1>2. The cellular chain complex of $X$ is
\[
0
\longrightarrow
\ZZ
\xrightarrow{\partial_2}
\ZZ^3
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}
\ZZ
\longrightarrow
0.
\]
::: {.proof}
There is one $0$-cell, three $1$-cells, one $2$-cell, and no cells in higher dimensions.
Thus
\[
C_0(X)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
C_1(X)\cong\ZZ^3,
\qquad
C_2(X)\cong\ZZ,
\]
and $C_n(X)=0$ for $n\ge3$.
:::

<1>3. Both cellular boundary maps vanish:
\[
\partial_1=0,
\qquad
\partial_2=0.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since every $1$-cell begins and ends at the unique $0$-cell, the cellular boundary
\[
\partial_1:C_1(X)\longrightarrow C_0(X)
\]
is zero.

For $\partial_2$, the coefficient of a $1$-cell in the cellular boundary of the unique $2$-cell is the degree with which its attaching map runs around that $1$-cell after the other $1$-cells are collapsed.
The attaching word is
\[
aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]
Its total exponent of $a$ is $1-1=0$, its total exponent of $b$ is $1-1=0$, and it does not use $c$.
Hence
\[
\partial_2(1)=(0,0,0),
\]
so $\partial_2=0$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore the integral homology groups are
\[
H_n(X;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ, & n=0,\\
\ZZ^3, & n=1,\\
\ZZ, & n=2,\\
0, & n\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>3, the cellular chain complex has zero differentials.
Therefore each homology group is the corresponding cellular chain group:
\[
H_0(X)\cong\ZZ,
\qquad
H_1(X)\cong\ZZ^3,
\qquad
H_2(X)\cong\ZZ,
\]
and all higher groups vanish.
:::

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\pi_1(X)\cong\langle a,b,c\mid[a,b]=1\rangle\cong\ZZ^2*\ZZ,
\qquad
H_*(X;\ZZ)=(\ZZ,\ZZ^3,\ZZ,0,\ldots).
}
\]
:::
