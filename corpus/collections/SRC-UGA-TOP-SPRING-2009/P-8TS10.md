::: problem
(a) What is the degree of the antipodal map on the $n$-sphere?
(no justification required)

(b) Define a CW-complex homeomorphic to real projective $n$-space $\mathbb{RP}^n$.

(c) Use parts (a) and (b) to compute the integral homology groups of $\mathbb{RP}^n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The antipodal map
\[
a_n:S^n\longrightarrow S^n,
\qquad
a_n(x)=-x,
\]
has degree
\[
\deg(a_n)=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the answer to part (a), for which the problem explicitly requires no justification.
:::

<1>2. The standard CW structure on $\RP^n$ has one cell $e^k$ in every dimension $0\le k\le n$.
::: {.proof}
For each $k\ge1$, view $\RP^k$ as the quotient of the closed disk $D^k$ obtained by identifying antipodal points of its boundary $S^{k-1}$.
The interior of $D^k$ maps homeomorphically onto
\[
\RP^k\setminus\RP^{k-1},
\]
and hence is the open $k$-cell $e^k$.
The attaching map is
\[
\phi_k:S^{k-1}\longrightarrow\RP^{k-1},
\qquad
x\longmapsto[x].
\]
Together with the single point $\RP^0$, this gives the skeletal filtration
\[
\RP^0\subset\RP^1\subset\cdots\subset\RP^n
\]
with exactly one cell in each dimension.
:::

<1>3. In the cellular chain complex, the boundary
\[
d_k:C_k(\RP^n;\ZZ)\longrightarrow C_{k-1}(\RP^n;\ZZ)
\]
is multiplication by
\[
1+(-1)^k.
\]
::: {.proof}
For $1\le k\le n$, both chain groups are isomorphic to $\ZZ$.
The coefficient of the unique $(k-1)$-cell in $d_k(e^k)$ is the degree of the composite
\[
S^{k-1}
\xrightarrow{\phi_k}
\RP^{k-1}
\longrightarrow
\RP^{k-1}/\RP^{k-2}
\cong S^{k-1}.
\]

Away from the collapsed $(k-2)$-skeleton, the domain has two hemispherical pieces mapping homeomorphically onto the open $(k-1)$-cell.
Choose the orientation so that one piece contributes local degree $1$.
The other piece differs by precomposition with the antipodal map on $S^{k-1}$, whose degree is
\[
\deg(a_{k-1})=(-1)^k
\]
by <1>1. Thus the total degree, and hence the cellular boundary coefficient, is
\[
1+(-1)^k.
\]
:::

<1>4. Therefore
\[
d_k=
\begin{cases}
0,&k\text{ odd},\\
2,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
This follows immediately from <1>3. Thus the cellular chain complex has one copy of $\ZZ$ in each degree $0,1,\ldots,n$, with differentials alternating between $0$ and multiplication by $2$.
:::

<1>5. For $0<k<n$,
\[
H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ/2\ZZ,&k\text{ odd},\\
0,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Also
\[
H_0(\RP^n;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ.
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $d_1=0$,
\[
H_0=C_0/\operatorname{im}d_1\cong\ZZ.
\]

If $0<k<n$ is odd, then $d_k=0$ and $d_{k+1}=2$, so
\[
H_k
=
\ker d_k/\operatorname{im}d_{k+1}
\cong
\ZZ/2\ZZ.
\]
If $0<k<n$ is even, then $d_k=2$ is injective, so $\ker d_k=0$ and hence $H_k=0$.
:::

<1>6. In the top dimension,
\[
H_n(\RP^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&n\text{ odd},\\
0,&n\text{ even and }n>0.
\end{cases}
\]
::: {.proof}
There is no incoming differential from degree $n+1$, so
\[
H_n=\ker d_n.
\]
If $n$ is odd then $d_n=0$, giving $H_n\cong\ZZ$.
If $n>0$ is even then $d_n=2$ is injective, giving $H_n=0$.
:::

<1>7. Altogether,
\[
\boxed{
H_k(\RP^n;\ZZ)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ,&k=0,\\
\ZZ/2\ZZ,&0<k<n\text{ and }k\text{ odd},\\
\ZZ,&k=n\text{ and }n\text{ odd},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]
::: {.proof}
Steps <1>5 and <1>6 compute every degree $0\le k\le n$, and there are no cells in dimensions greater than $n$.
:::
