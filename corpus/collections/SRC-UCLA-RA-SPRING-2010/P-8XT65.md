::: {.problem}
Let $f(z)$ be a continuous function on the closed unit disk $\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\le 1\}$ such that $f(z)$ is analytic on the open disk $\{|z|<1\}$ and $f(0)\ne 0$.

(a) Prove that if $0<r<1$ and if $\inf_{|z|=r}|f(z)|>0$, then $$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta\ge\log|f(0)|.$$

(b) Use (a) to prove that $|\{\theta\in[0,2\pi]:f(e^{i\theta})=0\}|=0$, where $|E|$ is the Lebesgue measure of $E$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $0<r<1$ and assume
\[
\inf_{|z|=r}|f(z)|>0.
\]
Then $f$ has no zero on the circle $|z|=r$.

<1>1. The function $f$ has only finitely many zeros in $|z|<r$.
::: {.proof}
Choose $R$ with
\[
r<R<1.
\]
The zeros of the nonzero holomorphic function $f$ are discrete in the open unit disk.
Hence the compact disk $|z|\le r$ contains only finitely many zeros.
Because $f(0)\ne0$, none of them is $0$.
List them with multiplicity as
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_N.
\]
:::

<1>2. If
\[
g(z)=\frac{f(z)}{\prod_{k=1}^N(z-a_k)},
\]
then $g$ is holomorphic and nonvanishing on a neighborhood of the closed disk $|z|\le r$, and
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|g(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
=\log|g(0)|.
\]
::: {.proof}
Removing each zero with its multiplicity makes the apparent singularities of the quotient removable.
After filling them in, $g$ is holomorphic on $|z|<R$.
By construction it has no zero in $|z|\le r$.

Since the disk $|z|<R$ is simply connected and $g$ is nonvanishing there after shrinking $R$ if necessary to avoid any further zeros, $g$ has a holomorphic logarithm $G$ on a neighborhood of $|z|\le r$ with
\[
e^{G}=g.
\]
Therefore
\[
\log|g|=\operatorname{Re}G
\]
is harmonic there.
The mean-value property at the origin gives
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|g(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
=\log|g(0)|.
\]
:::

<1>3. For every $a\in\mathbb C$ with $|a|<r$,
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|re^{i\theta}-a|\,d\theta
=\log r.
\]
::: {.proof}
Put
\[
w=\frac ar,
\qquad |w|<1.
\]
Then
\[
\log|re^{i\theta}-a|
=\log r+\log|1-we^{-i\theta}|.
\]
The function
\[
z\longmapsto 1-wz
\]
is holomorphic and nonvanishing on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk.
Hence $\log|1-wz|$ is harmonic there.
By the mean-value property at $0$,
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|1-we^{i\theta}|\,d\theta
=\log|1|=0.
\]
Replacing $\theta$ by $-\theta$ does not change the integral, so the displayed identity follows.
:::

<1>4. One has Jensen's identity
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
=
\log|f(0)|+
\sum_{k=1}^N\log\frac r{|a_k|}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Using the factorization from <1>2 and then <1>2--<1>3,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
&=
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|g(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
+
\sum_{k=1}^N
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|re^{i\theta}-a_k|\,d\theta\\
&=
\log|g(0)|+N\log r.
\end{aligned}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
f(0)=g(0)\prod_{k=1}^N(-a_k),
\]
so
\[
\log|f(0)|
=\log|g(0)|+\sum_{k=1}^N\log|a_k|.
\]
Subtracting gives the claimed identity.
:::

<1>5. The inequality in part (a) follows.
::: {.proof}
Every zero $a_k$ satisfies $|a_k|<r$, so
\[
\log\frac r{|a_k|}>0.
\]
Thus <1>4 gives
\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
\ge\log|f(0)|.
\]
:::

For part (b), let
\[
E=\{\theta\in[0,2\pi]:f(e^{i\theta})=0\}.
\]

<1>6. There is a sequence $r_n\uparrow1$ such that $f$ has no zero on any circle $|z|=r_n$.
::: {.proof}
For each $0<\rho<1$, the compact disk $|z|\le\rho$ contains only finitely many zeros of $f$.
Therefore the set
\[
\{|a|:f(a)=0,\ |a|<1\}
\]
is countable, being a countable union of finite sets.
Every interval $(1-1/n,1)$ contains radii outside this countable set.
Choose such an $r_n$ with
\[
1-\frac1n<r_n<1
\]
and, passing to an increasing subsequence if necessary, arrange $r_n\uparrow1$.
On each compact circle $|z|=r_n$, the continuous nonvanishing function $|f|$ has a positive minimum, so part (a) applies.
:::

<1>7. If $|E|>0$, then the circle averages in part (a) become arbitrarily negative along the radii $r_n$.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
M=\max_{|z|\le1}|f(z)|,
\qquad
C=\max\{M,1\}.
\]
Since $f$ is uniformly continuous on the closed unit disk and $f(e^{i\theta})=0$ for $\theta\in E$,
\[
\sup_{\theta\in E}|f(r_ne^{i\theta})|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence for every $0<\varepsilon<1$, all sufficiently large $n$ satisfy
\[
|f(r_ne^{i\theta})|<\varepsilon
\qquad(\theta\in E).
\]
For such $n$, because $f$ has no zero on $|z|=r_n$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(r_ne^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
&=
\int_E\log|f(r_ne^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
+
\int_{E^c}\log|f(r_ne^{i\theta})|\,d\theta\\
&\le
|E|\log\varepsilon+(2\pi-|E|)\log C.
\end{aligned}
\]
If $|E|>0$, the right-hand side tends to $-\infty$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
Thus the left-hand side can be made arbitrarily negative along the sequence $r_n$.
:::

<1>8. The boundary zero set $E$ has measure zero.
::: {.proof}
Part (a), applied to every $r_n$ from <1>6, gives the fixed lower bound
\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(r_ne^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
\ge 2\pi\log|f(0)|.
\]
This contradicts <1>7 if $|E|>0$.
Therefore
\[
|E|=0.
\]
:::
:::
