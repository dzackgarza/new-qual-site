::: {.problem}
Let $f(z)$ be a continuous function on the closed unit disk $\{z\in\CC:\abs{z}\le 1\}$ such that $f(z)$ is analytic on the open disk $\{\abs{z}<1\}$ and $f(0)\ne 0$.

(a) Prove that if $0<r<1$ and if $\inf_{\abs{z}=r}\abs{f(z)}>0$, then $$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{f(re^{i\theta})}\,d\theta\ge\log\abs{f(0)}.$$

(b) Use (a) to prove that $\setmeasure{\{\theta\in[0,2\pi]:f(e^{i\theta})=0\}}=0$, where $\setmeasure{E}$ is the Lebesgue measure of $E$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $0<r<1$ and assume
$$
\inf_{\abs{z}=r}\abs{f(z)}>0.
$$
Then $f$ has no zero on the circle $\abs{z}=r$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The function $f$ has only finitely many zeros in $\abs{z}<r$.

::: pf-proof

Choose $R$ with
$$
r<R<1.
$$
The zeros of the nonzero holomorphic function $f$ are discrete in the open unit disk.
Hence the compact disk $\abs{z}\le r$ contains only finitely many zeros.
Because $f(0)\ne0$, none of them is $0$.
List them with multiplicity as
$$
a_1,\ldots,a_N.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If
$$
g(z)=\frac{f(z)}{\prod_{k=1}^N(z-a_k)},
$$
then $g$ is holomorphic and nonvanishing on a neighborhood of the closed disk $\abs{z}\le r$, and
$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{g(re^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
=\log\abs{g(0)}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Removing each zero with its multiplicity makes the apparent singularities of the quotient removable.
After filling them in, $g$ is holomorphic on $\abs{z}<R$.
By construction it has no zero in $\abs{z}\le r$.

Since the disk $\abs{z}<R$ is simply connected and $g$ is nonvanishing there after shrinking $R$ if necessary to avoid any further zeros, $g$ has a holomorphic logarithm $G$ on a neighborhood of $\abs{z}\le r$ with
$$
e^{G}=g.
$$
Therefore
$$
\log\abs{g}=\operatorname{Re}G
$$
is harmonic there.
The mean-value property at the origin gives
$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{g(re^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
=\log\abs{g(0)}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

For every $a\in\CC$ with $\abs{a}<r$,
$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{re^{i\theta}-a}\,d\theta
=\log r.
$$

::: pf-proof

Put
$$
w=\frac ar,
\qquad \abs{w}<1.
$$
Then
$$
\log\abs{re^{i\theta}-a}
=\log r+\log\abs{1-we^{-i\theta}}.
$$
The function
$$
z\longmapsto 1-wz
$$
is holomorphic and nonvanishing on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk.
Hence $\log\abs{1-wz}$ is harmonic there.
By the mean-value property at $0$,
$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{1-we^{i\theta}}\,d\theta
=\log\abs{1}=0.
$$
Replacing $\theta$ by $-\theta$ does not change the integral, so the displayed identity follows.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

One has Jensen's identity
$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{f(re^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
=
\log\abs{f(0)}+
\sum_{k=1}^N\log\frac r{\abs{a_k}}.
$$

::: pf-proof

Using the factorization from step [](#s2){.pf-ref} and then steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref},
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{f(re^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
&=
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{g(re^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
+
\sum_{k=1}^N
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{re^{i\theta}-a_k}\,d\theta\\
&=
\log\abs{g(0)}+N\log r.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the other hand,
$$
f(0)=g(0)\prod_{k=1}^N(-a_k),
$$
so
$$
\log\abs{f(0)}
=\log\abs{g(0)}+\sum_{k=1}^N\log\abs{a_k}.
$$
Subtracting gives the claimed identity.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The inequality in part (a) follows.

::: pf-proof

Every zero $a_k$ satisfies $\abs{a_k}<r$, so
$$
\log\frac r{\abs{a_k}}>0.
$$
Thus step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{f(re^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
\ge\log\abs{f(0)}.
$$

For part (b), let
$$
E=\{\theta\in[0,2\pi]:f(e^{i\theta})=0\}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s6}

There is a sequence $r_n\uparrow1$ such that $f$ has no zero on any circle $\abs{z}=r_n$.

::: pf-proof

For each $0<\rho<1$, the compact disk $\abs{z}\le\rho$ contains only finitely many zeros of $f$.
Therefore the set
$$
\{\abs{a}:f(a)=0,\ \abs{a}<1\}
$$
is countable, being a countable union of finite sets.
Every interval $(1-1/n,1)$ contains radii outside this countable set.
Choose such an $r_n$ with
$$
1-\frac1n<r_n<1
$$
and, passing to an increasing subsequence if necessary, arrange $r_n\uparrow1$.
On each compact circle $\abs{z}=r_n$, the continuous nonvanishing function $\abs{f}$ has a positive minimum, so part (a) applies.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s7}

If $\setmeasure{E}>0$, then the circle averages in part (a) become arbitrarily negative along the radii $r_n$.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
M=\max_{\abs{z}\le1}\abs{f(z)},
\qquad
C=\max\{M,1\}.
$$
Since $f$ is uniformly continuous on the closed unit disk and $f(e^{i\theta})=0$ for $\theta\in E$,
$$
\sup_{\theta\in E}\abs{f(r_ne^{i\theta})}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Hence for every $0<\varepsilon<1$, all sufficiently large $n$ satisfy
$$
\abs{f(r_ne^{i\theta})}<\varepsilon
\qquad(\theta\in E).
$$
For such $n$, because $f$ has no zero on $\abs{z}=r_n$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{f(r_ne^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
&=
\int_E\log\abs{f(r_ne^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
+
\int_{E^c}\log\abs{f(r_ne^{i\theta})}\,d\theta\\
&\le
\setmeasure{E}\log\varepsilon+(2\pi-\setmeasure{E})\log C.
\end{aligned}
$$
If $\setmeasure{E}>0$, the right-hand side tends to $-\infty$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
Thus the left-hand side can be made arbitrarily negative along the sequence $r_n$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The boundary zero set $E$ has measure zero.

::: pf-proof

Part (a), applied to every $r_n$ from step [](#s6){.pf-ref}, gives the fixed lower bound
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\log\abs{f(r_ne^{i\theta})}\,d\theta
\ge 2\pi\log\abs{f(0)}.
$$
This contradicts step [](#s7){.pf-ref} if $\setmeasure{E}>0$.
Therefore
$$
\setmeasure{E}=0.
$$

:::
