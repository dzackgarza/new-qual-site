::: {.problem}
Let $F$ be a function from the open unit disk $\mathbb{D}=\{|z|<1\}$ to $\mathbb{D}$ such that whenever $z_1,z_2$ and $z_3$ are distinct points of $\mathbb{D}$ there exists an analytic function $f_{z_1,z_2,z_3}$ from $\mathbb{D}$ into $\mathbb{D}$ such that $$F(z_j)=f_{z_1,z_2,z_3}(z_j),\qquad j=1,2,3.$$ Prove that $F$ is analytic at every point of $\mathbb{D}$.

Hint: Fix $z\in\mathbb{D}$ and let $\mathbb{D}\ni z_n\to z$, $z_n\ne z$.
Show that the sequence $$\frac{F(z_n)-F(z)}{z_n-z}$$ is bounded and then prove that every two of its convergent subsequences have the same limit.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix $z\in\mathbb D$.
Choose $R>0$ such that
\[
\overline{D(z,R)}\subset\mathbb D.
\]
If $h:\mathbb D\to\mathbb D$ is holomorphic, then $|h|\le1$.
Hence Cauchy's estimates, applied on disks of radius $R/2$ centered at points of $D(z,R/4)$, give the uniform bounds
\[
|h'(w)|\le\frac2R,
\qquad
|h''(w)|\le\frac8{R^2}
\qquad
(w\in D(z,R/4)).
\]
These constants are independent of the particular self-map $h$.

Let $z_n\to z$ with $z_n\ne z$, and define
\[
q_n=\frac{F(z_n)-F(z)}{z_n-z}.
\]

<1>1. The sequence $\{q_n\}$ is bounded.
::: {.proof}
Choose once and for all a point $b\in\mathbb D$ with $b\ne z$.
For all sufficiently large $n$,
\[
z_n\in D(z,R/4)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
z_n\ne b.
\]
For such $n$, the hypothesis supplies a holomorphic map
\[
h_n:\mathbb D\to\mathbb D
\]
interpolating $F$ at the three distinct points $z,z_n,b$.
Thus
\[
q_n=rac{h_n(z_n)-h_n(z)}{z_n-z}.
\]
Along the line segment from $z$ to $z_n$,
\[
h_n(z_n)-h_n(z)
=(z_n-z)
\int_0^1 h_n'\bigl(z+t(z_n-z)\bigr)\,dt.
\]
The segment lies in $D(z,R/4)$, so the Cauchy bound gives
\[
|q_n|
\le\int_0^1\frac2R\,dt
=\frac2R.
\]
Only finitely many initial terms were excluded, hence the whole sequence is bounded.
:::

<1>2. Let $a,c\in D(z,R/4)\setminus\{z\}$ be distinct, and let $h:\mathbb D\to\mathbb D$ interpolate $F$ at $z,a,c$.
Then
\[
\left|
\frac{F(a)-F(z)}{a-z}-h'(z)
\right|
\le\frac4{R^2}|a-z|,
\]
and the analogous estimate holds with $c$ in place of $a$.
::: {.proof}
Because $h(a)=F(a)$ and $h(z)=F(z)$,
\[
\frac{F(a)-F(z)}{a-z}
=
\int_0^1 h'\bigl(z+t(a-z)\bigr)\,dt.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\frac{F(a)-F(z)}{a-z}-h'(z)
\right|
&\le
\int_0^1
\left|h'\bigl(z+t(a-z)\bigr)-h'(z)\right|\,dt\\
&\le
\int_0^1
\frac8{R^2}t|a-z|\,dt\\
&=
\frac4{R^2}|a-z|.
\end{aligned}
\]
The same calculation applies to $c$.
:::

<1>3. Any two convergent subsequences of $\{q_n\}$ have the same limit.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
q_{n_k}\longrightarrow\alpha,
\qquad
q_{m_k}\longrightarrow\beta.
\]
If $z_{n_k}=z_{m_k}$ along an infinite subsequence, then the corresponding quotients are equal there, so $\alpha=\beta$.
Otherwise, after passing to a further subsequence, assume
\[
z_{n_k}\ne z_{m_k}
\]
for every $k$.
For large $k$, both points lie in $D(z,R/4)$.
Use the hypothesis to choose one holomorphic disk self-map $h_k$ interpolating $F$ at the three distinct points
\[
z,\ z_{n_k},\ z_{m_k}.
\]
By <1>2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|q_{n_k}-q_{m_k}|
&\le
|q_{n_k}-h_k'(z)|+|q_{m_k}-h_k'(z)|\\
&\le
\frac4{R^2}
\left(|z_{n_k}-z|+|z_{m_k}-z|\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero.
Taking limits gives
\[
|\alpha-\beta|=0,
\]
so $\alpha=\beta$.
:::

<1>4. For every sequence $z_n\to z$, $z_n\ne z$, the quotient sequence $q_n$ converges.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $\{q_n\}$ is bounded in $\mathbb C$, hence has a convergent subsequence.
By <1>3, all convergent subsequences have the same limit, say $L$.
If the whole sequence did not converge to $L$, there would be an $\varepsilon>0$ and a subsequence satisfying
\[
|q_{n_k}-L|\ge\varepsilon
\]
for every $k$.
That subsequence is bounded, so it has a convergent further subsequence.
By <1>3 its limit must be $L$, contradicting the displayed inequality.
Thus $q_n\to L$.
:::

<1>5. The limit in <1>4 is independent of the chosen sequence approaching $z$.
::: {.proof}
Let $a_n\to z$ and $c_n\to z$, with all terms different from $z$.
By <1>4, the corresponding quotient sequences converge, say to $L_a$ and $L_c$.
Interleave the two point sequences:
\[
w_{2n}=a_n,
\qquad
w_{2n+1}=c_n.
\]
Then $w_n\to z$ and $w_n\ne z$.
By <1>4, its quotient sequence converges to one limit.
Its even and odd subsequences therefore have the same limit, so
\[
L_a=L_c.
\]
:::

<1>6. The function $F$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb D$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>5, there is a number $F'(z)$ such that
\[
\lim_{w\to z,\ w\ne z}
\frac{F(w)-F(z)}{w-z}
=F'(z).
\]
Thus $F$ is complex differentiable at the arbitrary point $z\in\mathbb D$.
Since $z$ was arbitrary, $F$ is complex differentiable everywhere on the open disk, hence holomorphic there.
:::
