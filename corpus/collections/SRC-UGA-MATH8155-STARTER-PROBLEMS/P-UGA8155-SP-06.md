::: {.problem}
Use the $n$-th roots of unity, i.e. the solutions of $z^n-1=0$, to show that
$$
\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}+\cos\frac{4\pi}{n}+\cos\frac{6\pi}{n}+\cdots+
\cos\frac{2(n-1)\pi}{n}=-1
$$
and
$$
\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}+\sin\frac{4\pi}{n}+\sin\frac{6\pi}{n}+\cdots+
\sin\frac{2(n-1)\pi}{n}=0.
$$

Hint: If
$$
z^n+c_1z^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{n-1}z+c_n=0
$$
has roots $z_1,\ldots,z_n$, then
$$
z_1+\cdots+z_n=-c_1,
\qquad
z_1\cdots z_n=(-1)^n c_n
$$
(the second identity is not used).
:::

::: {.solution}

Assume $n\ge2$ and set
$$
\zeta
\coloneqq
e^{2\pi i/n}.
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step

The $n$ distinct roots of
$$
z^n-1=0
$$
are
$$
1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}.
$$


::: pf-proof

For every integer $k$,
$$
(\zeta^k)^n
=
e^{2\pi i k}
=
1,
$$
so each listed number is an $n$-th root of unity.

If
$$
0\le j<k\le n-1
$$
and
$$
\zeta^j=\zeta^k,
$$
then
$$
\zeta^{k-j}=1.
$$
This would mean $n$ divides $k-j$, impossible because
$$
0<k-j<n.
$$
Thus the $n$ listed roots are distinct, and a degree-$n$ polynomial has no
others.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

The sum of all $n$-th roots of unity is zero:
$$
1+\zeta+\zeta^2+\cdots+\zeta^{n-1}=0.
$$


::: pf-proof

The polynomial
$$
z^n-1
$$
has coefficient $0$ on $z^{n-1}$. By Vieta's formula, the sum of its roots
is the negative of that coefficient, hence is $0$.

Equivalently, one may use the geometric-series identity
$$
1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{n-1}
=
\frac{1-\zeta^n}{1-\zeta}
=
0.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\zeta+\zeta^2+\cdots+\zeta^{n-1}
=
-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Subtract $1$ from both sides of the identity in step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Taking real parts in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\boxed{
\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}
+
\cos\frac{4\pi}{n}
+
\cdots
+
\cos\frac{2(n-1)\pi}{n}
=
-1.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

By de Moivre's theorem,
$$
\zeta^k
=
e^{2\pi i k/n}
=
\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n}
+
i\sin\frac{2\pi k}{n}.
$$
Hence
$$
\operatorname{Re}
\left(
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\zeta^k
\right)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}
\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n}.
$$
The right-hand side of step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} is the real number $-1$, so its real part is
$-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Taking imaginary parts in step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives
$$
\boxed{
\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}
+
\sin\frac{4\pi}{n}
+
\cdots
+
\sin\frac{2(n-1)\pi}{n}
=
0.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Using the same de Moivre expansion,
$$
\operatorname{Im}
\left(
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\zeta^k
\right)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}
\sin\frac{2\pi k}{n}.
$$
The number $-1$ has imaginary part zero, so step [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} gives the result.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} are exactly the two required trigonometric identities.

:::
