::: {.problem}
1. Characterize the positive integers $n$ such that
\[
(1+i)^n=(1-i)^n.
\]

2. Let $n$ be a natural number.
   Show that
   \[
   \left(\frac12(-1+\sqrt3\,i)\right)^n
   +
   \left(\frac12(-1-\sqrt3\,i)\right)^n
   \]
   is equal to $2$ if $n$ is a multiple of $3$, and is equal to $-1$ otherwise.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. One has
\[
\frac{1+i}{1-i}=i.
\]

::: {.proof}
Multiply numerator and denominator by $1+i$:
\[
\frac{1+i}{1-i}
=
\frac{(1+i)^2}{(1-i)(1+i)}
=
\frac{1+2i+i^2}{2}
=
\frac{2i}{2}
=
i.
\]
:::

<1>2. For a positive integer $n$,
\[
(1+i)^n=(1-i)^n
\]
if and only if
\[
i^n=1.
\]

::: {.proof}
Since
\[
1-i\ne0,
\]
divide the equality by $(1-i)^n$:
\[
\left(
\frac{1+i}{1-i}
\right)^n
=
1.
\]
Apply step <1>1.
:::

<1>3. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
(1+i)^n=(1-i)^n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
4\mid n.
}
\]

::: {.proof}
The powers of $i$ repeat with period $4$:
\[
i, -1, -i, 1.
\]
Hence
\[
i^n=1
\]
exactly when $n$ is divisible by $4$. Combine this with step <1>2.
:::

<1>4. The two numbers in part 2 are
\[
\omega
\coloneqq
\frac{-1+\sqrt3,i}{2}
=
e^{2\pi i/3}
\]
and
\[
\overline\omega
=
\frac{-1-\sqrt3,i}{2}
=
e^{-2\pi i/3}.
\]

::: {.proof}
Both numbers have modulus $1$. Their real part is
\[
-\frac12
\]
and their imaginary parts are
\[
\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
These are exactly the cosine and sine values at angles
\[
\pm\frac{2\pi}{3}.
\]
:::

<1>5. For every natural number $n$,
\[
\omega^n+\overline\omega^{,n}
=
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{3}\right).
\]

::: {.proof}
By step <1>4 and de Moivre's theorem,
\[
\omega^n
=
e^{2\pi i n/3},
\qquad
\overline\omega^{,n}
=
e^{-2\pi i n/3}.
\]
Adding and using
\[
e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}=2\cos\theta
\]
gives the formula.
:::

<1>6. If
\[
3\mid n,
\]
then
\[
\omega^n+\overline\omega^{,n}=2.
\]

::: {.proof}
Write
\[
n=3m.
\]
Then step <1>5 gives
\[
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi(3m)}3\right)
=
2\cos(2\pi m)
=
2.
\]
:::

<1>7. If
\[
3\nmid n,
\]
then
\[
\omega^n+\overline\omega^{,n}=-1.
\]

::: {.proof}
If $3$ does not divide $n$, then
\[
n\equiv1
\quad\text{or}\quad
n\equiv2
\pmod3.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{2\pi n}{3}
\equiv
\frac{2\pi}{3}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac{4\pi}{3}
\pmod{2\pi}.
\]
In either case,
\[
\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{3}\right)
=
-\frac12.
\]
Apply step <1>5:
\[
\omega^n+\overline\omega^{,n}
=
2\left(-\frac12\right)
=
-1.
\]
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>3 answers part 1. Steps <1>6--<1>7 prove the two cases in part 2.
:::
:::
