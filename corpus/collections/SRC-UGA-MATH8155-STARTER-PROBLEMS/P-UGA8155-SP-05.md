::: {.problem}
1. Characterize the positive integers $n$ such that
$$
(1+i)^n=(1-i)^n.
$$

2. Let $n$ be a natural number.
   Show that
   $$
   \left(\frac12(-1+\sqrt3\,i)\right)^n
   +
   \left(\frac12(-1-\sqrt3\,i)\right)^n
   $$
   is equal to $2$ if $n$ is a multiple of $3$, and is equal to $-1$ otherwise.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

One has
$$
\frac{1+i}{1-i}=i.
$$


::: pf-proof

Multiply numerator and denominator by $1+i$:
$$
\frac{1+i}{1-i}
=
\frac{(1+i)^2}{(1-i)(1+i)}
=
\frac{1+2i+i^2}{2}
=
\frac{2i}{2}
=
i.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

For a positive integer $n$,
$$
(1+i)^n=(1-i)^n
$$
if and only if
$$
i^n=1.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
1-i\ne0,
$$
divide the equality by $(1-i)^n$:
$$
\left(
\frac{1+i}{1-i}
\right)^n
=
1.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Therefore
$$
\boxed{
(1+i)^n=(1-i)^n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
4\mid n.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

The powers of $i$ repeat with period $4$:
$$
i, -1, -i, 1.
$$
Hence
$$
i^n=1
$$
exactly when $n$ is divisible by $4$. Combine this with step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

The two numbers in part 2 are
$$
\omega
\coloneqq
\frac{-1+\sqrt3,i}{2}
=
e^{2\pi i/3}
$$
and
$$
\overline\omega
=
\frac{-1-\sqrt3,i}{2}
=
e^{-2\pi i/3}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Both numbers have modulus $1$. Their real part is
$$
-\frac12
$$
and their imaginary parts are
$$
\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
$$
These are exactly the cosine and sine values at angles
$$
\pm\frac{2\pi}{3}.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

For every natural number $n$,
$$
\omega^n+\overline\omega^{,n}
=
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{3}\right).
$$


::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} and de Moivre's theorem,
$$
\omega^n
=
e^{2\pi i n/3},
\qquad
\overline\omega^{,n}
=
e^{-2\pi i n/3}.
$$
Adding and using
$$
e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}=2\cos\theta
$$
gives the formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If
$$
3\mid n,
$$
then
$$
\omega^n+\overline\omega^{,n}=2.
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
n=3m.
$$
Then step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} gives
$$
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi(3m)}3\right)
=
2\cos(2\pi m)
=
2.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If
$$
3\nmid n,
$$
then
$$
\omega^n+\overline\omega^{,n}=-1.
$$


::: pf-proof

If $3$ does not divide $n$, then
$$
n\equiv1
\quad\text{or}\quad
n\equiv2
\pmod3.
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{2\pi n}{3}
\equiv
\frac{2\pi}{3}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac{4\pi}{3}
\pmod{2\pi}.
$$
In either case,
$$
\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{3}\right)
=
-\frac12.
$$
Apply step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}:
$$
\omega^n+\overline\omega^{,n}
=
2\left(-\frac12\right)
=
-1.
$$

:::

:::
