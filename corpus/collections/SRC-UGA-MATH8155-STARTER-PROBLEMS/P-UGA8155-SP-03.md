::: {.problem}
Let
$$
f(z)=z+\frac1z.
$$
Describe, in terms of well-known curves, the images under $f$ of both the circle $|z|=r$ of radius $r$ ($r\ne0$) and the ray $\arg z=\theta_0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Let
$$
z=re^{i\theta},
\qquad
r>0.
$$
Then
$$
f(z)
=
\left(r+\frac1r\right)\cos\theta
+
i
\left(r-\frac1r\right)\sin\theta.
$$


::: pf-proof

Since
$$
\frac1z
=
r^{-1}e^{-i\theta},
$$
one has
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(z)
&=
re^{i\theta}+r^{-1}e^{-i\theta}\\
&=
r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)
+
r^{-1}(\cos\theta-i\sin\theta),
\end{aligned}
$$
which simplifies to the displayed formula.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

If
$$
r\ne1,
$$
the image of the circle $|z|=r$ is the ellipse
$$
\boxed{
\frac{x^2}{(r+r^{-1})^2}
+
\frac{y^2}{(r-r^{-1})^2}
=
1.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Write
$$
f(z)=x+iy.
$$
By step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref},
$$
x
=
\left(r+r^{-1}\right)\cos\theta,
\qquad
y
=
\left(r-r^{-1}\right)\sin\theta.
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{x^2}{(r+r^{-1})^2}
+
\frac{y^2}{(r-r^{-1})^2}
=
\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta
=
1.
$$
As $\theta$ runs through $[0,2\pi]$, the whole ellipse is traced.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

If
$$
r=1,
$$
the image of the unit circle is the line segment
$$
\boxed{[-2,2]}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} becomes
$$
f(e^{i\theta})
=
2\cos\theta.
$$
This is real and takes every value in $[-2,2]$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Now let
$$
z=\rho e^{i\theta_0},
\qquad
\rho>0.
$$
Then
$$
x
=
\left(\rho+\frac1\rho\right)\cos\theta_0,
\qquad
y
=
\left(\rho-\frac1\rho\right)\sin\theta_0.
$$


::: pf-proof

This is step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} with the angular coordinate fixed at
$$
\theta=\theta_0
$$
and the radius renamed $\rho$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

Suppose
$$
\sin\theta_0\ne0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\cos\theta_0\ne0.
$$
Then the image of the ray lies on the hyperbola
$$
\boxed{
\frac{x^2}{\cos^2\theta_0}
-
\frac{y^2}{\sin^2\theta_0}
=
4.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

Set
$$
u
\coloneqq
\rho+\rho^{-1},
\qquad
v
\coloneqq
\rho-\rho^{-1}.
$$
Then
$$
u^2-v^2
=
(\rho+\rho^{-1})^2
-
(\rho-\rho^{-1})^2
=
4.
$$
By step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref},
$$
u
=
\frac{x}{\cos\theta_0},
\qquad
v
=
\frac{y}{\sin\theta_0}.
$$
Substitution gives the displayed equation.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

In the situation of step [](#s1-5){.pf-ref}, the ray traces exactly one branch of
that hyperbola, namely the branch satisfying
$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{\cos\theta_0}\ge2.
}
$$


::: pf-proof

For every $\rho>0$,
$$
\rho+\rho^{-1}\ge2
$$
by the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality. Hence
$$
\frac{x}{\cos\theta_0}
=
\rho+\rho^{-1}
\ge2.
$$

Conversely,
$$
v
=
\rho-\rho^{-1}
$$
runs through all real numbers as $\rho$ runs through $(0,\infty)$, while
$$
u
=
\sqrt{v^2+4}
\ge2.
$$
Thus every point on that branch occurs.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-7}

If
$$
\sin\theta_0=0,
$$
then the ray maps to a real half-line:
$$
\boxed{
[2,\infty)
}
$$
when
$$
\theta_0\equiv0\pmod{2\pi},
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
(-\infty,-2]
}
$$
when
$$
\theta_0\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}.
$$


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives $y=0$. Also
$$
x
=
(\rho+\rho^{-1})\cos\theta_0.
$$
Since
$$
\rho+\rho^{-1}\in[2,\infty),
$$
the stated two half-lines follow according to whether
$$
\cos\theta_0=1
$$
or
$$
\cos\theta_0=-1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-8}

If
$$
\cos\theta_0=0,
$$
the ray maps onto the entire imaginary axis.


::: pf-proof

Step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} gives $x=0$ and
$$
y
=
(\rho-\rho^{-1})\sin\theta_0.
$$
The function
$$
\rho\longmapsto\rho-\rho^{-1}
$$
is continuous and strictly increasing from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$ on
$(0,\infty)$. Multiplication by
$$
\sin\theta_0=\pm1
$$
does not change its range. Hence every point of the imaginary axis occurs.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Steps [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} describe the images of circles, and steps [](#s1-5){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-8){.pf-ref}
describe the images of rays.
