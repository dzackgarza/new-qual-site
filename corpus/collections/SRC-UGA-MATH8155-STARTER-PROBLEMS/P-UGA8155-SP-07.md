::: {.problem}
Describe each set in the $z$-plane below, where $\alpha$ is a complex number and $k$ is a positive number such that $2|\alpha|<k$:

1. $|z-\alpha|+|z+\alpha|=k$;

2. $|z-\alpha|+|z+\alpha|\le k$.
:::

::: {.solution}

Set
$$
a\coloneqq\frac{k}{2},
\qquad
c\coloneqq|\alpha|.
$$
The hypothesis
$$
2|\alpha|<k
$$
is exactly
$$
c<a.
$$
Define
$$
b\coloneqq\sqrt{a^2-c^2}
=
\frac12\sqrt{k^2-4|\alpha|^2}.
$$

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

If
$$
\alpha\ne0,
$$
write
$$
\alpha
=
c e^{i\phi}
$$
and rotate coordinates by setting
$$
w
\coloneqq
e^{-i\phi}z
=
x+iy.
$$
Then
$$
|z-\alpha|
=
|w-c|,
\qquad
|z+\alpha|
=
|w+c|.
$$


::: pf-proof

Multiplication by
$$
e^{-i\phi}
$$
is a rotation and therefore preserves Euclidean distance. Hence
$$
|z-\alpha|
=
|e^{-i\phi}z-e^{-i\phi}\alpha|
=
|w-c|,
$$
and similarly
$$
|z+\alpha|
=
|w+c|.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

In the rotated coordinates, let
$$
r_+
\coloneqq
\sqrt{(x-c)^2+y^2},
\qquad
r_-
\coloneqq
\sqrt{(x+c)^2+y^2}.
$$
If
$$
r_++r_-=2a,
$$
then
$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2}
+
\frac{y^2}{b^2}
=
1.
$$


::: pf-proof

The difference of squares is
$$
r_+^2-r_-^2
=
(x-c)^2-(x+c)^2
=
-4cx.
$$
Since
$$
r_++r_-=2a,
$$
one gets
$$
r_+-r_-
=
\frac{-4cx}{2a}
=
-\frac{2cx}{a}.
$$
Therefore
$$
r_+
=
a-\frac{cx}{a}.
$$
Squaring this and using
$$
r_+^2=(x-c)^2+y^2
$$
gives
$$
(x-c)^2+y^2
=
\left(
a-\frac{cx}{a}
\right)^2.
$$
Expanding both sides and cancelling the common term $-2cx$ yields
$$
x^2+c^2+y^2
=
a^2
+
\frac{c^2x^2}{a^2}.
$$
Rearrange:
$$
\left(
1-\frac{c^2}{a^2}
\right)x^2+y^2
=
a^2-c^2.
$$
Since
$$
b^2=a^2-c^2,
$$
this becomes
$$
\frac{b^2}{a^2}x^2+y^2=b^2,
$$
and division by $b^2>0$ gives
$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2}
+
\frac{y^2}{b^2}
=
1.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-3}

Conversely, if
$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2}
+
\frac{y^2}{b^2}
=
1,
$$
then
$$
r_++r_-=2a.
$$


::: pf-proof

From the ellipse equation,
$$
y^2
=
b^2
\left(
1-\frac{x^2}{a^2}
\right).
$$
Using
$$
b^2=a^2-c^2,
$$
one computes
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_+^2
&=
(x-c)^2+y^2\\
&=
x^2-2cx+c^2
+
(a^2-c^2)
\left(
1-\frac{x^2}{a^2}
\right)\\
&=
\left(
a-\frac{cx}{a}
\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Likewise,
$$
r_-^2
=
\left(
a+\frac{cx}{a}
\right)^2.
$$
On the ellipse,
$$
|x|\le a.
$$
Since $c<a$,
$$
a\pm\frac{cx}{a}
>
0.
$$
Therefore
$$
r_+
=
a-\frac{cx}{a},
\qquad
r_-
=
a+\frac{cx}{a},
$$
and hence
$$
r_++r_-=2a.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-4}

Thus, if
$$
\alpha\ne0,
$$
the set
$$
|z-\alpha|+|z+\alpha|=k
$$
is the ellipse centered at the origin with foci
$$
\pm\alpha,
$$
semi-major axis
$$
\boxed{a=\frac{k}{2}},
$$
and semi-minor axis
$$
\boxed{
b
=
\frac12\sqrt{k^2-4|\alpha|^2}.
}
$$
Its major axis lies on the line through $0$ and $\alpha$.


::: pf-proof

Steps [](#s1-1){.pf-ref} through [](#s1-3){.pf-ref} identify the equality set, after rotation, with
$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2}
+
\frac{y^2}{b^2}
=
1.
$$
Rotating back preserves lengths and sends the real-axis foci
$$
\pm c
$$
to
$$
\pm\alpha.
$$

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-5}

The inequality
$$
|z-\alpha|+|z+\alpha|
\le
k
$$
describes the closed filled ellipse bounded by the ellipse in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.


::: pf-proof

Define
$$
F(z)
\coloneqq
|z-\alpha|+|z+\alpha|.
$$
This is continuous. Its level set
$$
F(z)=k
$$
is the ellipse of step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref}.

The origin satisfies
$$
F(0)
=
2|\alpha|
<
k,
$$
so the sublevel set
$$
F(z)\le k
$$
contains the center of the ellipse.

Also,
$$
F(z)
\ge
2|z|-2|\alpha|
\longrightarrow
\infty
\qquad
(|z|\to\infty),
$$
so the sublevel set is bounded.

The function $F$ is convex because both maps
$$
z\longmapsto |z-\alpha|
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
z\longmapsto |z+\alpha|
$$
are convex. It is also even:
$$
F(-z)=F(z).
$$

Fix a unit complex number $u$ and set
$$
g(t)=F(tu),
\qquad
t\in\RR.
$$
Then $g$ is an even convex function. Hence $g$ is nondecreasing for
$t\ge0$: if
$$
0\le s<t,
$$
convexity and evenness give
$$
g(s)
\le
\frac{t+s}{2t}g(t)
+
\frac{t-s}{2t}g(-t)
=
g(t).
$$

The ellipse in step [](#s1-4){.pf-ref} meets every ray from the origin in exactly one
point. Let that point be
$$
t_0u,
\qquad
t_0>0.
$$
Then
$$
g(t_0)=k.
$$
For
$$
0\le t\le t_0,
$$
monotonicity gives
$$
g(t)\le k.
$$
For $t>t_0$, convexity gives
$$
\frac{g(t)-g(t_0)}{t-t_0}
\ge
\frac{g(t_0)-g(0)}{t_0}
>
0,
$$
because
$$
g(0)=2|\alpha|<k=g(t_0).
$$
Thus
$$
g(t)>k
\qquad
(t>t_0).
$$
Therefore the sublevel set consists exactly of the radial segments from the
origin to the equality ellipse, i.e. the closed filled ellipse.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-6}

If
$$
\alpha=0,
$$
the ellipse degenerates to a circle:
$$
\boxed{
|z|=\frac{k}{2}
}
$$
in part 1 and the closed disk
$$
\boxed{
|z|\le\frac{k}{2}
}
$$
in part 2.


::: pf-proof

When $\alpha=0$,
$$
|z-\alpha|+|z+\alpha|
=
2|z|.
$$
Thus equality with $k$ gives
$$
|z|=k/2,
$$
and the inequality gives
$$
|z|\le k/2.
$$

:::
