::: {.problem}
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a Hilbert space and $\{\varphi_k\}_{k=1}^\infty$ be a subset of $\mathcal{H}$ with the property that for every $f\in\mathcal{H}$
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty\abs{\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle}^2=\norm{f}^2,
$$
where $\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle$ denotes the inner product of $f$ and $\varphi_k$ in $\mathcal{H}$ and $\norm{f}$ denotes the norm of $f$ on $\mathcal{H}$.

(a) Show that for all $f,g$ in $\mathcal{H}$,
$$
\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle\overline{\langle g,\varphi_k\rangle}.
$$
[Hint: Consider $\norm{f+g}^2$ and $\norm{f+ig}^2$.]

(b) Show that for all $f$ in $\mathcal{H}$,
$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}\norm{f-\sum_{k=1}^N\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle\varphi_k}=0.
$$
[Hint: Use the fact that $\norm{h}=\sup_{\norm{g}\le 1}\abs{\langle h,g\rangle}$ for all $h$ in $\mathcal{H}$.]
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Recover the inner product from the Parseval identity.

::: pf-proof

For $f,g\in\mathcal H$, write
$$
a_k:=\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle,
\qquad
b_k:=\langle g,\varphi_k\rangle.
$$
By hypothesis,
$$
\sum_k\abs{a_k}^2=\norm{f}^2,
\qquad
\sum_k\abs{b_k}^2=\norm{g}^2.
$$
Applying the same identity to $f+g$ gives
$$
\norm{f+g}^2
=\sum_k\abs{a_k+b_k}^2.
$$
Subtracting the identities for $f$ and $g$ yields
$$
\operatorname{Re}\langle f,g\rangle
=\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_k\overline{b_k}.
$$
Similarly, applying the identity to $f+ig$ yields equality of the imaginary parts. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\langle f,g\rangle
=\sum_{k=1}^\infty
\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle
\overline{\langle g,\varphi_k\rangle}.}
$$
The series is absolutely convergent by Cauchy--Schwarz in $\ell^2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Estimate the reconstruction error against an arbitrary test vector.

::: pf-proof

Let
$$
S_Nf:=\sum_{k=1}^N
\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle\varphi_k.
$$
For $g\in\mathcal H$, part (a) gives
$$
\langle f,g\rangle
=\sum_{k=1}^\infty
\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle
\overline{\langle g,\varphi_k\rangle}.
$$
Hence
$$
\langle f-S_Nf,g\rangle
=\sum_{k>N}
\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle
\overline{\langle g,\varphi_k\rangle}.
$$
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\abs{\langle f-S_Nf,g\rangle}
&\le
\left(\sum_{k>N}\abs{\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle}^2\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{k>N}\abs{\langle g,\varphi_k\rangle}^2\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le
\left(\sum_{k>N}\abs{\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle}^2\right)^{1/2}\norm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Pass from the dual estimate to norm convergence.

::: pf-proof

Using
$$
\norm{h}=\sup_{\norm{g}\le1}\abs{\langle h,g\rangle},
$$
Step 2 gives
$$
\norm{f-S_Nf}
\le
\left(\sum_{k>N}\abs{\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle}^2\right)^{1/2}.
$$
The full series of squared coefficients converges to $\norm{f}^2$, so its tails tend to $0$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\norm{f-\sum_{k=1}^N
\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle\varphi_k}=0.}
$$

:::
