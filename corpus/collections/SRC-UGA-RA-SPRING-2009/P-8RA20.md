::: problem
(a) Show that if $f\in L^4([0,1])$, then $f\in L^2([0,1])$ and $\|f\|_2\le\|f\|_4$.

(b) Does there exist a constant $C$ so that for all $f\in L^4([0,1])$, $\|f\|_4\le C\|f\|_2$?
Justify your answer.

(c) For a fixed nonzero function $g\in L^4([0,1])$, let $A$ denote the ratio $\|g\|_4/\|g\|_2$.
Find a constant $B$, depending only on $A$, such that $\|g\|_2\le B\|g\|_1$.
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Prove $L^4([0,1])\subseteq L^2([0,1])$ with norm one.
::: proof
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\int_0^1|f|^2
\le
\left(\int_0^1|f|^4\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_0^1 1^2\right)^{1/2}
=\|f\|_4^2.
\]
Taking square roots gives
\[
\boxed{\|f\|_2\le\|f\|_4.}
\]
:::

<1>2. Show that no uniform reverse inequality exists.
::: proof
For $n\ge1$, let
\[
f_n:=\mathbf1_{[0,1/n]}.
\]
Then
\[
\|f_n\|_4=n^{-1/4},
\qquad
\|f_n\|_2=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{\|f_n\|_4}{\|f_n\|_2}=n^{1/4}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Therefore there is no constant $C$ such that
\[
\|f\|_4\le C\|f\|_2
\]
for all $f\in L^4([0,1])$.
:::

<1>3. Derive the $L^1$ lower control for a fixed $g$.
::: proof
Hölder's inequality with exponents $3/2$ and $3$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|g\|_2^2
&=\int_0^1 |g|^{2/3}|g|^{4/3}\\
&\le
\left(\int_0^1|g|\right)^{2/3}
\left(\int_0^1|g|^4\right)^{1/3}\\
&=\|g\|_1^{2/3}\|g\|_4^{4/3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking square roots,
\[
\|g\|_2
\le \|g\|_1^{1/3}\|g\|_4^{2/3}.
\]
Since
\[
\|g\|_4=A\|g\|_2,
\]
we obtain
\[
\|g\|_2
\le A^{2/3}\|g\|_1^{1/3}\|g\|_2^{2/3}.
\]
Because $g\neq0$, divide by $\|g\|_2^{2/3}$ and cube:
\[
\boxed{\|g\|_2\le A^2\|g\|_1.}
\]
Thus one may take
\[
\boxed{B=A^2.}
\]
:::
:::
