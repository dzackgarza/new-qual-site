::: {.problem}
(a) Show that if $f\in L^4([0,1])$, then $f\in L^2([0,1])$ and $\norm{f}_2\le\norm{f}_4$.

(b) Does there exist a constant $C$ so that for all $f\in L^4([0,1])$, $\norm{f}_4\le C\norm{f}_2$?
Justify your answer.

(c) For a fixed nonzero function $g\in L^4([0,1])$, let $A$ denote the ratio $\norm{g}_4/\norm{g}_2$.
Find a constant $B$, depending only on $A$, such that $\norm{g}_2\le B\norm{g}_1$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Prove $L^4([0,1])\subseteq L^2([0,1])$ with norm one.

::: pf-proof

By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$$
\int_0^1\abs{f}^2
\le
\left(\int_0^1\abs{f}^4\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_0^1 1^2\right)^{1/2}
=\norm{f}_4^2.
$$
Taking square roots gives
$$
\boxed{\norm{f}_2\le\norm{f}_4.}
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Show that no uniform reverse inequality exists.

::: pf-proof

For $n\ge1$, let
$$
f_n:=\mathbf1_{[0,1/n]}.
$$
Then
$$
\norm{f_n}_4=n^{-1/4},
\qquad
\norm{f_n}_2=n^{-1/2}.
$$
Hence
$$
\frac{\norm{f_n}_4}{\norm{f_n}_2}=n^{1/4}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$
Therefore there is no constant $C$ such that
$$
\norm{f}_4\le C\norm{f}_2
$$
for all $f\in L^4([0,1])$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Derive the $L^1$ lower control for a fixed $g$.

::: pf-proof

Hölder's inequality with exponents $3/2$ and $3$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\norm{g}_2^2
&=\int_0^1 \abs{g}^{2/3}\abs{g}^{4/3}\\
&\le
\left(\int_0^1\abs{g}\right)^{2/3}
\left(\int_0^1\abs{g}^4\right)^{1/3}\\
&=\norm{g}_1^{2/3}\norm{g}_4^{4/3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking square roots,
$$
\norm{g}_2
\le \norm{g}_1^{1/3}\norm{g}_4^{2/3}.
$$
Since
$$
\norm{g}_4=A\norm{g}_2,
$$
we obtain
$$
\norm{g}_2
\le A^{2/3}\norm{g}_1^{1/3}\norm{g}_2^{2/3}.
$$
Because $g\neq0$, divide by $\norm{g}_2^{2/3}$ and cube:
$$
\boxed{\norm{g}_2\le A^2\norm{g}_1.}
$$
Thus one may take
$$
\boxed{B=A^2.}
$$

:::
