::: problem
Let $f$ be integrable on $\mathbb R^n$.

1. Prove that $\{x:|f(x)|=\infty\}$ has measure zero.

2. For every $\varepsilon>0$, find a measurable set $E$ of finite measure with $\int_E|f|>\int|f|-\varepsilon$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
M=\int_{\mathbb R^n}|f(x)|\,dx<\infty.
\]

<1>1. The set on which $|f|=\infty$ is null.
::: {.proof}
For every $N\ge1$,
\[
\{|f|=\infty\}\subseteq\{|f|>N\}.
\]
Hence Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
m(\{|f|=\infty\})
\le m(\{|f|>N\})
\le \frac1N\int|f|
=\frac MN.
\]
Letting $N\to\infty$ yields
\[
m(\{|f|=\infty\})=0.
\]
:::

<1>2. Almost all of the integral is captured on a finite-measure set.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
B_R=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:|x|\le R\}.
\]
Then $B_R\uparrow\mathbb R^n$ as $R\to\infty$. Since $|f|\ge0$, the monotone convergence theorem gives
\[
\int_{B_R}|f|\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb R^n}|f|=M.
\]
Therefore for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $R$ such that
\[
\int_{B_R}|f|>M-\varepsilon.
\]
Taking
\[
E=B_R
\]
gives a measurable set of finite measure with the required property.
:::
:::
