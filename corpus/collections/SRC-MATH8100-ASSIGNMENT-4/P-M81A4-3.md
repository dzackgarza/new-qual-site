::: problem
Let $f\geq0$ be measurable and finite almost everywhere.
Set $F_k=\{2^k<f\leq2^{k+1}\}$ and $E_{2^k}=\{f>2^k\}$.
Prove that
$$
\int f<\infty\iff\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^km(F_k)<\infty\iff\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^km(E_{2^k})<\infty.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Because $f$ is finite almost everywhere, the sets
\[
F_k=\{2^k<f\le2^{k+1}\},
\qquad k\in\mathbb Z,
\]
are pairwise disjoint and their union is $\{f>0\}$ up to a null set.

On $F_k$ we have
\[
2^k<f\le2^{k+1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
2^k m(F_k)
\le \int_{F_k} f
\le 2^{k+1}m(F_k).
\]
Summing over $k$ and using monotone convergence/Tonelli for nonnegative terms gives
\[
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^km(F_k)
\le \int_{\mathbb R^n}f
\le 2\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^km(F_k).
\]
Hence
\[
\int f<\infty
\iff
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^km(F_k)<\infty.
\]

Now observe that
\[
E_{2^k}=\{f>2^k\}
=\bigsqcup_{j\ge k}F_j.
\]
Thus
\[
m(E_{2^k})=\sum_{j\ge k}m(F_j).
\]
Using Tonelli again,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^k m(E_{2^k})
&=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^k\sum_{j\ge k}m(F_j)\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}m(F_j)\sum_{k\le j}2^k\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}m(F_j)\,2^{j+1}\\
&=2\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}2^j m(F_j).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^km(F_k)<\infty
\iff
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^km(E_{2^k})<\infty.
\]

Combining the two equivalences proves the result.
:::
