::: {.problem}
Assume Fatou's theorem and deduce the monotone convergence theorem.
:::

::: {.solution}
The assignment sheet sets the problem and carries no solutions, so no retained solution set covers it; the argument is the card's own. Fatou's theorem is taken in the form: for nonnegative measurable functions $g_{n}$ on a measure space, $\int\liminf_{n}g_{n}\le\liminf_{n}\int g_{n}$, with values in $[0,\infty]$. Let $f_{n}$ be nonnegative measurable with $f_{n}\uparrow f$ pointwise.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #upper}
$\limsup_{n}\int f_{n}\le\int f$.

::: pf-proof
Since $f_{n}\le f_{n+1}$ and $f_{n}\to f$ pointwise, $f_{n}\le f$ for every $n$. Monotonicity of the integral gives $\int f_{n}\le\int f$ for every $n$, so $\int f$, which may be $+\infty$, is an upper bound for the sequence of numbers $\int f_{n}$, and therefore also for its limit superior.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #lower}
$\int f\le\liminf_{n}\int f_{n}$.

::: pf-proof
The functions $f_{n}$ are nonnegative and measurable, so Fatou's theorem applies and gives $\int\liminf_{n}f_{n}\le\liminf_{n}\int f_{n}$. Since $f_{n}\to f$ pointwise, $\liminf_{n}f_{n}=f$ everywhere, so the left side is $\int f$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #squeeze}
$\lim_{n}\int f_{n}=\int f$, the limit existing in $[0,\infty]$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#upper){.pf-ref} and [](#lower){.pf-ref} give $\int f\le\liminf_{n}\int f_{n}\le\limsup_{n}\int f_{n}\le\int f$, so the two are equal and the limit exists and equals $\int f$. The value $+\infty$ is permitted: if $\int f=+\infty$ the inequalities give $\liminf=\limsup=+\infty$, and if $\int f<\infty$ all terms are finite real numbers.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_{n}=\int f}$ for every sequence $0\le f_{1}\le f_{2}\le\cdots$ of measurable functions with $f_{n}\uparrow f$ pointwise; this is the monotone convergence theorem, deduced from Fatou's theorem alone.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#squeeze){.pf-ref} is exactly the assertion of the monotone convergence theorem for such a sequence, and its only ingredient beyond the definition of the integral is Fatou's theorem as stated at the head of the solution. No other convergence theorem is used.
:::
