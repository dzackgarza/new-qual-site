::: problem
Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ have finite measure.
Let $(f_k)$ be uniformly integrable in $L^1(\Omega)$ and suppose $f_k\to f$ almost everywhere.

1. If $f\in L^1(\Omega)$, prove that $f_k\to f$ in $L^1$.

2. Decide whether the assumption $f\in L^1(\Omega)$ is necessary.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $f\in L^1(\Omega)$, then $f_k\to f$ in $L^1$.
::: {.proof}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$.

By uniform integrability, there exists $\delta_1>0$ such that for every measurable $E\subseteq\Omega$ with
\[
m(E)<\delta_1,
\]
we have
\[
\int_E|f_k|<\frac\varepsilon4
\qquad\text{for every }k.
\]

Since $f\in L^1(\Omega)$, absolute continuity of the Lebesgue integral gives $\delta_2>0$ such that
\[
m(E)<\delta_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int_E|f|<\frac\varepsilon4.
\]

By Egorov's theorem, because $m(\Omega)<\infty$, there exists a measurable set $A\subseteq\Omega$ with
\[
m(A)<\min(\delta_1,\delta_2)
\]
such that $f_k\to f$ uniformly on $\Omega\setminus A$.

For all sufficiently large $k$,
\[
\sup_{\Omega\setminus A}|f_k-f|
<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\max(1,m(\Omega))}.
\]
Hence
\[
\int_{\Omega\setminus A}|f_k-f|
\le
m(\Omega)\sup_{\Omega\setminus A}|f_k-f|
<\frac\varepsilon2.
\]
On $A$,
\[
\int_A|f_k-f|
\le \int_A|f_k|+\int_A|f|
<\frac\varepsilon2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|f_k-f\|_1<\varepsilon
\]
for all sufficiently large $k$. Thus
\[
f_k\to f\quad\text{in }L^1(\Omega).
\]
:::

<1>2. The assumption $f\in L^1(\Omega)$ is not necessary.
::: {.proof}
We show that it follows automatically.

Apply uniform integrability with $\varepsilon=1$. Choose $\delta>0$ such that
\[
m(E)<\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int_E|f_k|<1
\qquad\text{for every }k.
\]

Because $m(\Omega)<\infty$, choose $R$ so large that
\[
m(\Omega\setminus[-R,R]^n)<\delta.
\]
Partition the bounded cube $[-R,R]^n$ into finitely many cubes $Q_1,\dots,Q_N$, each of measure less than $\delta$. Then
\[
\Omega
=
\bigl(\Omega\setminus[-R,R]^n\bigr)
\cup
\bigcup_{j=1}^N(\Omega\cap Q_j).
\]
Every set on the right has measure less than $\delta$. Hence, for every $k$,
\[
\|f_k\|_1
\le (N+1).
\]
Thus
\[
\sup_k\|f_k\|_1<\infty.
\]

Since $f_k\to f$ almost everywhere, Fatou's theorem gives
\[
\int_\Omega|f|
\le
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_\Omega|f_k|
<\infty.
\]
Therefore
\[
f\in L^1(\Omega).
\]
Part <1>1 now applies, so $f_k\to f$ in $L^1$ without assuming $f\in L^1$ in advance.

Hence the hypothesis in part 1 is redundant.
