::: {.problem}
1. Prove that $L^1$ convergence implies convergence in measure, and give a counterexample to the converse.

2. Suppose $|f_k|\leq g\in L^1$ and $f_k\to f$ in measure.
   Prove that $f\in L^1$ and $f_k\to f$ in $L^1$.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal.** (1) $L^1$ convergence implies convergence in measure, with a counterexample to the converse. (2) Dominated convergence in measure implies $L^1$ convergence.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #L1-implies-convergence-in-measure}
(1) $L^1$ convergence implies convergence in measure.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
For $\eps > 0$, $\mu\theset{|f_k - f| \ge \eps} \le \frac{1}{\eps}\int |f_k - f|\,d\mu$.

::: pf-proof
Chebyshev's (Markov's) inequality.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Since $\int |f_k - f| \to 0$, the right side tends to $0$.

::: pf-proof
$L^1$ convergence means $\|f_k - f\|_1 \to 0$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Hence $f_k \to f$ in measure.

::: pf-proof
$\mu\theset{|f_k - f| \ge \eps} \to 0$ for every $\eps > 0$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #converse-counterexample}
(1) Counterexample to the converse.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
On $[0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure, let
\[
f_k=k\mathbf1_{[0,1/k]}.
\]

::: pf-proof
This sequence concentrates unit mass on intervals whose lengths tend to zero.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$f_k \to 0$ in measure but not in $L^1$.

::: pf-proof
For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and all $k>\varepsilon$,
\[
\mu\{|f_k|\ge\varepsilon\}=\frac1k\longrightarrow0,
\]
so $f_k\to0$ in measure. But
\[
\|f_k\|_1=k\cdot\frac1k=1
\]
for every $k$, so there is no convergence to $0$ in $L^1$.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #dominated-convergence-in-measure}
(2) Dominated convergence in measure.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step
$f \in L^1$.

::: pf-proof
Convergence in measure has an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence. Thus some $f_{k_j}\to f$ a.e. Since $|f_{k_j}|\le g$, passage to the limit gives $|f|\le g$ a.e. Hence $f\in L^1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$f_k \to f$ in $L^1$.

::: pf-proof
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $\|f_k-f\|_1\not\to0$. Then some subsequence satisfies
\[
\|f_{k_j}-f\|_1\ge\eta>0
\qquad\text{for all }j.
\]
That subsequence still converges to $f$ in measure, so it has a further subsequence $f_{k_{j_\ell}}\to f$ almost everywhere. By Step 1,
\[
|f_{k_{j_\ell}}-f|\le2g\in L^1.
\]
Dominated convergence gives
\[
\|f_{k_{j_\ell}}-f\|_1\longrightarrow0,
\]
contradicting the lower bound by $\eta$. Therefore $f_k\to f$ in $L^1$.
:::

:::
