  note: >-
    In a cyclic group G=<g>, the squares form the subgroup <g^2>, whose
    index is at most 2. If nonsquares exist, they comprise its unique
    nontrivial coset, so the product of two nonsquares is a square. In
    C_2 x C_2 every square is the identity, so two distinct nonidentity
    elements give a counterexample.

::: {.problem}
1. Let $G$ be a cyclic group and let $a,b\in G$ be elements that are not squares. Prove that $ab$ is a square in $G$.
2. Give an example showing that the conclusion can fail when $G$ is not cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

If $G=\langle g\rangle$ is cyclic, then the set of squares in
$G$ is the subgroup
$$
G^2
\coloneqq
\{x^2:x\in G\}
=
\langle g^2\rangle.
$$

::: pf-proof

Every element of $G$ has the form $g^m$, and
$$
(g^m)^2=g^{2m}.
$$
Thus every square lies in $\langle g^2\rangle$. Conversely, every
element $g^{2m}$ of $\langle g^2\rangle$ is the square of $g^m$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The quotient $G/G^2$ has at most two elements.

::: pf-proof

The quotient is generated by the coset $gG^2$, and
$$
(gG^2)^2=g^2G^2=G^2.
$$
Hence the generator has order at most $2$, so the quotient has order
at most $2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Part 1: if $a$ and $b$ are nonsquares, then $ab$ is a square.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s1){.pf-ref}, being a nonsquare means lying outside $G^2$. Since
nonsquares exist, step [](#s2){.pf-ref} implies that $G/G^2$ has exactly two
elements. Therefore $aG^2$ and $bG^2$ are both its unique
nonidentity element. Their product is the identity coset:
$$
abG^2
=
(aG^2)(bG^2)
=
G^2.
$$
Thus $ab\in G^2$, so step [](#s1){.pf-ref} shows that $ab$ is a square.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Part 2: the conclusion fails for
$$
G=C_2\times C_2.
$$

::: pf-proof

Write
$$
G=\{1,a,b,ab\},
$$
where
$$
a^2=b^2=1
$$
and $a\neq b$. Every element of $G$ has square equal to $1$, so the
only square in $G$ is $1$. Hence $a$ and $b$ are nonsquares, while
their product $ab$ is also a nonidentity element and therefore also a
nonsquare. Thus the conclusion of part 1 fails in this noncyclic
group.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Step [](#s3){.pf-ref} proves the cyclic case, and step [](#s4){.pf-ref} supplies the requested
noncyclic counterexample.

:::
