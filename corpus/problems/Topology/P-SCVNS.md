::: {.problem}
- Describe all connected covering spaces of $\RP^2 \vee \RP^2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let
$$X=\mathbb{RP}^2\vee\mathbb{RP}^2.$$
Then
$$\pi_1(X)\cong C_2*C_2\cong D_\infty.$$

::: pf-proof

Van Kampen gives the free product of the two fundamental groups $C_2$. Writing
$$D_\infty=\langle r,s\mid s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle,$$
one may take $s=a$ and $r=ab$ if $a,b$ are the two order-$2$ generators.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Connected covering spaces of $X$ correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups of $D_\infty$.

::: pf-proof

The space $X$ is connected, locally path connected, and semilocally simply connected, so the standard subgroup-covering correspondence applies. Unbased connected covers correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every subgroup $H\le D_\infty$ is one of the following:
$$\{1\},\qquad \langle r^n\rangle\ (n\ge1),\qquad \langle r^k s\rangle,\qquad \langle r^n,r^k s\rangle\ (n\ge1).$$

::: pf-proof

The subgroup $\langle r\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$ has index $2$ in $D_\infty$. If $H\subseteq\langle r\rangle$, then $H$ is trivial or $\langle r^n\rangle$. Otherwise $H$ contains a reflection $r^k s$. Its intersection with $\langle r\rangle$ is either trivial, in which case $H=\langle r^k s\rangle\cong C_2$, or equals $\langle r^n\rangle$ for some $n\ge1$, in which case $H=\langle r^n,r^k s\rangle\cong D_\infty$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Up to conjugacy, the cyclic rotation subgroups $\langle r^n\rangle$ are all distinct; the reflection subgroups have exactly two conjugacy classes, represented by $\langle s\rangle$ and $\langle rs\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

Each $\langle r^n\rangle$ is normal and determined by its intersection with $\langle r\rangle$. Conjugation by $r^m$ sends $r^k s$ to $r^{k+2m}s$, while conjugation by $s$ sends $k$ to $-k$, so parity of $k$ is the only invariant for reflection subgroups.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

For $H_{n,k}=\langle r^n,r^k s\rangle$, the parameter $k$ is taken modulo $n$. If $n$ is odd, all $H_{n,k}$ are conjugate; if $n$ is even, there are exactly two conjugacy classes, represented by $k=0$ and $k=1$.

::: pf-proof

Replacing the reflection generator by $r^{n\ell}(r^k s)$ changes $k$ by a multiple of $n$. Ambient conjugation changes $k$ by $2m$ or by a sign. Thus the conjugacy orbits on $\mathbb Z/n$ are generated by $k\mapsto k+2m$ and $k\mapsto-k$. When $n$ is odd, $2$ is invertible modulo $n$, giving one orbit; when $n$ is even, parity is preserved and yields exactly two orbits.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore the connected covers of $X$ are exactly the covers corresponding to the subgroup classes listed in steps [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref}, together with the universal cover corresponding to the trivial subgroup.

::: pf-proof

This is the subgroup-covering correspondence from step [](#s2){.pf-ref} combined with the complete subgroup classification in steps [](#s3){.pf-ref}, [](#s4){.pf-ref} and [](#s5){.pf-ref}.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Their sheet numbers are: infinite for the trivial and reflection subgroups, $2n$ for $\langle r^n\rangle$, and $n$ for $\langle r^n,r^k s\rangle$.

::: pf-proof

The number of sheets is the subgroup index. Since $\langle r\rangle$ has index $2$, one has $[D_\infty:\langle r^n\rangle]=2n$. The dihedral subgroup $\langle r^n,r^k s\rangle$ contains one reflection coset and has index $n$. Reflection subgroups are finite while $D_\infty$ is infinite, hence have infinite index.

:::
