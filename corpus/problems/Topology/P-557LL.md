::: {.problem}
Describe the CW complex structure of the real projective plane $\RP^2$, compute its cellular boundary maps, and compute its simplicial/cellular homology and cohomology groups with coefficients in $\ZZ$ and $\ZZ_2$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #cw}

$\RP^2$ has a CW structure with one cell $e^k$ in each dimension $k=0,1,2$, whose $1$-skeleton is $\RP^1\cong S^1$ and whose $2$-cell is attached by the degree-$2$ map $S^1\to S^1$, $z\mapsto z^2$.

::: pf-proof

$\RP^2$ is the quotient of $D^2$ by the antipodal identification on $\del D^2=S^1$. The quotient $S^1/(z\sim-z)$ is $\RP^1\cong S^1$, the composite $S^1\to\RP^1\cong S^1$ is $z\mapsto z^2$, and $\RP^1$ is a $0$-cell with one $1$-cell attached.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #chains}

The integral cellular chain complex is $0\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{\,2\,}\ZZ\xrightarrow{\,0\,}\ZZ\to0$, in degrees $2,1,0$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#cw){.pf-ref}, the cellular boundary $d_2$ is multiplication by the degree $2$ of the attaching map, and $d_1=0$ because the $1$-cell is attached to a single $0$-cell.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$H_0(\RP^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ$, $H_1(\RP^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/2$, and $H_k(\RP^2;\ZZ)=0$ for $k\ge2$.

::: pf-proof

From step [](#chains){.pf-ref}: $H_2=\ker(2)=0$, $H_1=\ZZ/2\ZZ$, $H_0=\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$H_k(\RP^2;\ZZ_2)\cong\ZZ_2$ for $k=0,1,2$, and $0$ for $k>2$.

::: pf-proof

Tensoring step [](#chains){.pf-ref} with $\ZZ_2$ turns multiplication by $2$ into $0$, so every boundary map vanishes.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$H^0(\RP^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ$, $H^2(\RP^2;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ/2$, and $H^k(\RP^2;\ZZ)=0$ otherwise.

::: pf-proof

Dualizing step [](#chains){.pf-ref} gives the cochain complex $0\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{\,0\,}\ZZ\xrightarrow{\,2\,}\ZZ\to0$ in degrees $0,1,2$. So $H^0=\ZZ$, $H^1=\ker(2)=0$, and $H^2=\ZZ/2\ZZ$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$H^k(\RP^2;\ZZ_2)\cong\ZZ_2$ for $k=0,1,2$, and $0$ for $k>2$.

::: pf-proof

Dualizing with $\ZZ_2$ coefficients, every coboundary is $0$ or multiplication by $2$, hence zero.

:::

:::

:::

Cellular homology and cohomology agree with singular, and with simplicial for any triangulation of $\RP^2$.

:::

::: {.solution title="By Mayer-Vietoris"}

Decompose $\RP^2$ as the union of a Möbius band $M$ and a disk $D^2$ glued along the boundary circle $\del M \cong S^1$ of the Möbius band, and apply the Mayer-Vietoris sequence.

::: pf

::: pf-step

$H_1(\del M)\to H_1(M)\oplus H_1(D^2)$ is injective, so $H_2(\RP^2)=0$.

::: pf-proof

The Möbius band deformation-retracts onto its core circle, so $H_1(M)\cong\ZZ$, and $H_1(D^2)=0$. The inclusion $\del M\into M$ wraps the boundary circle twice around the core: on $H_1$ it is $n\mapsto 2n$. Mayer-Vietoris gives
$$
0=H_2(M)\oplus H_2(D^2)\to H_2(\RP^2)\to H_1(\del M)\xrightarrow{\,(2,0)\,}H_1(M)\oplus H_1(D^2),
$$
and the map $n\mapsto(2n,0)$ is injective, so $H_2(\RP^2)$, which is the kernel of that map, vanishes.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$H_1(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

In the same Mayer-Vietoris sequence, $\del M$, $M$, and $D^2$ are path-connected, so the reduced $H_0$ terms vanish and
$$
H_1(\RP^2)\cong \coker\bigl(H_1(\del M)\xrightarrow{\,n\mapsto(2n,0)\,}H_1(M)\oplus H_1(D^2)\bigr)
= \ZZ/2\ZZ.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$H_0(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ$, and the cohomology and $\ZZ_2$ groups are as in the cellular solution.

::: pf-proof

$\RP^2$ is path-connected, so $H_0(\RP^2)\cong\ZZ$. The universal coefficient theorem applied to these integral homology groups gives $H^k(\RP^2;\ZZ)$ and the groups with $\ZZ_2$ coefficients.

:::
