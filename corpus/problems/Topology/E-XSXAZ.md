::: exercise
Construct 6 distinct topologies on the set $\mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \ge 1$), and order them by coarseness/fineness where applicable.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Define six distinct topologies on $\mathbb{R}^d$ and verify that they are topologies and distinct.

<1>1. Six Topologies on $\mathbb{R}^d$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **1. The Indiscrete (Trivial) Topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}}$:**
        - $\mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}} = \{\varnothing, \mathbb{R}^d\}$.
        - The coarsest possible topology on $\mathbb{R}^d$.
    <2>2. **2. The Standard (Euclidean / Metric) Topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}$:**
        - Generated by open metric balls $B_r(x) = \{y \in \mathbb{R}^d \mid \|x - y\|_2 < r\}$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}^d, r > 0$.
        - Hausdoff, connected, separable, locally compact, and second-countable.
    <2>3. **3. The Discrete Topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}$:**
        - $\mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}} = \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ (all subsets are open).
        - The finest possible topology on $\mathbb{R}^d$.
    <2>4. **4. The Cofinite (Finite Complement) Topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}}$:**
        - $\mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}} = \{U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d \mid \mathbb{R}^d \setminus U \text{ is finite}\} \cup \{\varnothing\}$.
        - $T_1$ but not $T_2$ (not Hausdorff), compact, and strictly coarser than $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}$.
    <2>5. **5. The Cocountable (Countable Complement) Topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}}$:**
        - $\mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}} = \{U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d \mid \mathbb{R}^d \setminus U \text{ is at most countable}\} \cup \{\varnothing\}$.
        - Strictly finer than $\mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}}$, strictly incomparable with $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}$.
    <2>6. **6. The Lower Limit (Sorgenfrey product) Topology $\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^d$ (or Zariski topology):**
        - Generated by half-open rectangles $\prod_{i=1}^d [a_i, b_i)$ with $a_i < b_i$.
        - Strictly finer than the Euclidean topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}$ (since $(a_i, b_i) = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty [a_i + 1/n, b_i)$), completely normal, Hausdorff, but not second-countable.

<1>2. Verification of Distinctness:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. A singleton set $\{0\}$:
        - Open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}$.
        - Closed but not open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}}, \mathcal{T}_{\ell}^d$.
        - Neither open nor closed in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}}$.
    <2>2. An open interval/box $\prod (0, 1)$:
        - Open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}, \mathcal{T}_{\ell}^d, \mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}$.
        - Not open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}}$ (since complement is uncountable and non-empty).
    <2>3. A half-open box $\prod [0, 1)$:
        - Open in $\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^d, \mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}$.
        - Not open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}}$.
    <2>4. The complement of a countable dense set $\mathbb{R}^d \setminus \mathbb{Q}^d$:
        - Open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}$.
        - Not open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}$ (has empty interior in Euclidean topology).
    <2>5. The complement of a single point $\mathbb{R}^d \setminus \{0\}$:
        - Open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}, \mathcal{T}_{\ell}^d, \mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}$.
        - Not open in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}}$.

<1>3. Comparison by Inclusions:
    $$\mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}} \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}} \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}} \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}$$
    $$\mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}} \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}} \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_{\text{std}} \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_{\ell}^d \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}.$$

<1>4. Conclusion:
    The 6 topologies are $\mathcal{T}_{\text{ind}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{cof}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{coc}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}, \mathcal{T}_{\ell}^d, \mathcal{T}_{\text{disc}}$. Q.E.D.
:::
