::: {.exercise}
- What does it mean for a family of sets to form a **basis** for a topology?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

A family $\mathcal B$ of subsets of $X$ is a basis if every point of $X$ lies in some $B\in\mathcal B$ and whenever $x\in B_1\cap B_2$ with $B_1,B_2\in\mathcal B$, there is $B_3\in\mathcal B$ with $x\in B_3\subseteq B_1\cap B_2$.

::: pf-proof

These two conditions are precisely the basis axioms.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The topology generated by $\mathcal B$ consists of all unions of elements of $\mathcal B$.

::: pf-proof

The basis axioms ensure these unions contain $\varnothing,X$ and are closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.

:::
