::: {.exercise}
Show that $[0, 1]$ is compact without using the Heine-Borel theorem.
:::

::: {.hint}
Bisect repeatedly and use the nested interval property of $\RR$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\mathcal{U}$ be an open cover of $[0,1]$, and suppose, for a contradiction, that no finite subcollection of $\mathcal{U}$ covers $[0,1]$. Call a closed interval $I\subseteq[0,1]$ *uncoverable* if no finite subcollection of $\mathcal{U}$ covers $I$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nested}

There are uncoverable closed intervals $[0,1]=I_0\supseteq I_1\supseteq I_2\supseteq\cdots$ with $I_k$ of length $2^{-k}$.

::: pf-proof

$I_0$ is uncoverable by assumption. Given an uncoverable $I_k=[a_k,b_k]$ of length $2^{-k}$, split it at its midpoint into two closed halves of length $2^{-k-1}$. If finite subcollections $\mathcal{U}_1$ and $\mathcal{U}_2$ covered the two halves, then $\mathcal{U}_1\cup\mathcal{U}_2$ would be a finite subcollection covering $I_k$. So one half is uncoverable; let $I_{k+1}$ be such a half.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #point}

There is $x_0\in\bigcap_{k\ge0}I_k$.

::: pf-proof

The left endpoints $a_k$ increase and are bounded above by $1$, so $x_0=\sup_k a_k$ exists by the least upper bound property of $\RR$. For every $k$ and $j\ge k$ we have $a_j\le b_j\le b_k$, so $a_k\le x_0\le b_k$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

Choose $U\in\mathcal{U}$ with $x_0\in U$, and $\varepsilon>0$ with $(x_0-\varepsilon,x_0+\varepsilon)\cap[0,1]\subseteq U$. Choose $k$ with $2^{-k}<\varepsilon$. By step [](#point){.pf-ref}, every $y\in I_k$ satisfies $\abs{y-x_0}\le 2^{-k}<\varepsilon$, so $I_k\subseteq U$. Then the single set $U$ covers $I_k$, which contradicts step [](#nested){.pf-ref}. Hence $[0,1]$ is compact.

:::
