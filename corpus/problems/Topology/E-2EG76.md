::: {.exercise}

Show that a finite product of compact spaces is compact, and that a finite union of compact subspaces of a topological space is compact.

:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

If $K_1, \ldots, K_n$ are compact subspaces of a space, then $X = \bigcup_{i=1}^n K_i$ is compact.

::: pf-proof

Let $\mathcal{U}$ be a cover of $X$ by sets open in $X$. Each $K_i$ is covered by the traces of $\mathcal{U}$ on $K_i$, so compactness of $K_i$ gives a finite subcollection $\mathcal{U}_i \subseteq \mathcal{U}$ covering $K_i$. Then $\bigcup_{i=1}^n \mathcal{U}_i$ is a finite subcollection of $\mathcal{U}$ covering $X$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $X$ and $Y$ are compact, then $X \times Y$ is compact.

::: pf-proof

Let $\mathcal{U}$ be an open cover of $X \times Y$.

::: {.pf-step #tube}

Each $x \in X$ has an open neighborhood $V_x$ such that $V_x \times Y$ is covered by finitely many members of $\mathcal{U}$.

::: pf-proof

The slice $\{x\} \times Y$ is homeomorphic to $Y$, hence compact, so finitely many members $U_1, \ldots, U_k$ of $\mathcal{U}$ cover it. By the tube lemma, there is an open neighborhood $V_x$ of $x$ with $V_x \times Y \subseteq U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_k$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$X \times Y$ is covered by finitely many members of $\mathcal{U}$.

::: pf-proof

The sets $V_x$ of step [](#tube){.pf-ref} cover $X$, so compactness of $X$ gives $x_1, \ldots, x_m$ with $X = \bigcup_{i=1}^m V_{x_i}$. Then $X \times Y = \bigcup_{i=1}^m (V_{x_i} \times Y)$, and each of these finitely many tubes is covered by finitely many members of $\mathcal{U}$.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-step

A finite product $\prod_{i=1}^n X_i$ of compact spaces is compact.

::: pf-proof

By induction on $n$, using $\prod_{i=1}^{n} X_i \cong \big(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1} X_i\big) \times X_n$ and the two-factor case.

:::
