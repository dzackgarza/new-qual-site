::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f(x)=x^3 - 5$ over $\QQ$.

(a) What is the dimension of $K$ over $\QQ$?

(b) What is the Galois group of $K$ over $\QQ$?
(Identify the isomorphism type of the Galois group, and also indicate how its elements act as permutations of the roots of $f$.)

(c) What are all the subfields of $K$?
Be sure to explain how you know you have included all of them.

(d) Which of these subfields are Galois over $\QQ$?
:::


::: {.solution}
Let
$$
\alpha=\sqrt[3]{5},\qquad \zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}.
$$
Then the roots of $x^3-5$ are
$$
\alpha,\quad \zeta\alpha,\quad \zeta^2\alpha,
$$
so
$$
K=\QQ(\alpha,\zeta).
$$

::: pf

::: pf-step
The degree is
$$
\boxed{[K:\QQ]=6}.
$$

::: pf-proof
The polynomial $x^3-5$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ by Eisenstein's criterion at $5$, so $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=3$. Since $\alpha$ is real, $\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq\RR$, while $\zeta\notin\RR$. Hence $\zeta\notin\QQ(\alpha)$. The element $\zeta$ satisfies $x^2+x+1=0$, so
$$
[K:\QQ(\alpha)]=2.
$$
Therefore the tower law gives $[K:\QQ]=2\cdot3=6$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The Galois group is
$$
\boxed{\Gal(K/\QQ)\cong S_3}.
$$

::: pf-proof
Because $K$ is the splitting field over characteristic $0$ of the separable polynomial $x^3-5$, the extension $K/\QQ$ is Galois. Thus
$$
\abs{\Gal(K/\QQ)}=[K:\QQ]=6.
$$
The Galois group acts faithfully on the three roots, so it embeds in $S_3$. Since both groups have order $6$, the embedding is an isomorphism.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Define automorphisms $\sigma,\tau\in\Gal(K/\QQ)$ by
$$
\sigma(\alpha)=\zeta\alpha,
\qquad
\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta,
$$
$$
\tau(\alpha)=\alpha,
\qquad
\tau(\zeta)=\zeta^2.
$$
Then, on the ordered root set $(\alpha,\zeta\alpha,\zeta^2\alpha)$,
$$
\sigma=(123),
\qquad
\tau=(23).
$$

::: pf-proof
The map $\sigma$ cyclically permutes the three cube roots of $5$, while $\tau$ is complex conjugation: it fixes the real root $\alpha$ and exchanges the two nonreal roots. These permutations generate $S_3$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The complete list of subfields of $K$ is
$$
\QQ,
\quad
K,
\quad
\QQ(\zeta),
\quad
\QQ(\alpha),
\quad
\QQ(\zeta\alpha),
\quad
\QQ(\zeta^2\alpha).
$$

::: pf-proof
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, intermediate fields correspond bijectively to subgroups of $S_3$. The subgroups of $S_3$ are exactly
$$
S_3,\quad \{e\},\quad A_3,\quad
\langle(12)\rangle,\quad
\langle(13)\rangle,\quad
\langle(23)\rangle.
$$
Thus there are exactly six intermediate fields. The fixed field of $A_3=\langle\sigma\rangle$ has degree $2$ and is $\QQ(\zeta)$, since $\sigma$ fixes $\zeta$. Each order-$2$ subgroup is the stabilizer of one of the three roots, so its fixed field is respectively one of the three cubic fields generated by that root.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The subfields Galois over $\QQ$ are exactly
$$
\boxed{\QQ,\quad \QQ(\zeta),\quad K.}
$$

::: pf-proof
Under the Galois correspondence, an intermediate field is Galois over $\QQ$ exactly when its corresponding subgroup is normal in $S_3$. The normal subgroups of $S_3$ are
$$
\{e\},\quad A_3,\quad S_3.
$$
They correspond respectively to $K$, $\QQ(\zeta)$, and $\QQ$. The three order-$2$ subgroups are not normal, so the three cubic subfields are not Galois over $\QQ$.
:::
