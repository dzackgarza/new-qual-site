::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $x^3 - 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

a. What is the dimension of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$?

b. What is the Galois group of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$?
(Identify the isomorphism type of the Galois group, and also indicate how its elements act as permutations of the roots of $f$.)

c. What are all the subfields of $K$?
Be sure to explain how you know you have included all of them.

d. Which of these subfields are Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$?
:::


::: solution
<1>1. Let
\[
\alpha=\sqrt[3]{5},\qquad \zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\]
Then the roots of $x^3-5$ are
\[
\alpha,\quad \zeta\alpha,\quad \zeta^2\alpha,
\]
so
\[
K=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta).
\]
:::

<1>2. The degree is
\[
\boxed{[K:\mathbb Q]=6}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The polynomial $x^3-5$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by Eisenstein's criterion at $5$, so $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=3$. Since $\alpha$ is real, $\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subseteq\mathbb R$, while $\zeta\notin\mathbb R$. Hence $\zeta\notin\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. The element $\zeta$ satisfies $x^2+x+1=0$, so
\[
[K:\mathbb Q(\alpha)]=2.
\]
Therefore the tower law gives $[K:\mathbb Q]=2\cdot3=6$.
:::

<1>3. The Galois group is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Because $K$ is the splitting field over characteristic $0$ of the separable polynomial $x^3-5$, the extension $K/\mathbb Q$ is Galois. Thus
\[
|\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)|=[K:\mathbb Q]=6.
\]
The Galois group acts faithfully on the three roots, so it embeds in $S_3$. Since both groups have order $6$, the embedding is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>4. Define automorphisms $\sigma,\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)$ by
\[
\sigma(\alpha)=\zeta\alpha,
\qquad
\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta,
\]
\[
\tau(\alpha)=\alpha,
\qquad
\tau(\zeta)=\zeta^2.
\]
Then, on the ordered root set $(\alpha,\zeta\alpha,\zeta^2\alpha)$,
\[
\sigma=(123),
\qquad
\tau=(23).
\]
::: {.proof}
The map $\sigma$ cyclically permutes the three cube roots of $5$, while $\tau$ is complex conjugation: it fixes the real root $\alpha$ and exchanges the two nonreal roots. These permutations generate $S_3$.
:::

<1>5. The complete list of subfields of $K$ is
\[
\mathbb Q,
\quad
K,
\quad
\mathbb Q(\zeta),
\quad
\mathbb Q(\alpha),
\quad
\mathbb Q(\zeta\alpha),
\quad
\mathbb Q(\zeta^2\alpha).
\]
::: {.proof}
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, intermediate fields correspond bijectively to subgroups of $S_3$. The subgroups of $S_3$ are exactly
\[
S_3,\quad \{e\},\quad A_3,\quad
\langle(12)\rangle,\quad
\langle(13)\rangle,\quad
\langle(23)\rangle.
\]
Thus there are exactly six intermediate fields. The fixed field of $A_3=\langle\sigma\rangle$ has degree $2$ and is $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$, since $\sigma$ fixes $\zeta$. Each order-$2$ subgroup is the stabilizer of one of the three roots, so its fixed field is respectively one of the three cubic fields generated by that root.
:::

<1>6. The subfields Galois over $\mathbb Q$ are exactly
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Q,\quad \mathbb Q(\zeta),\quad K.}
\]
::: {.proof}
Under the Galois correspondence, an intermediate field is Galois over $\mathbb Q$ exactly when its corresponding subgroup is normal in $S_3$. The normal subgroups of $S_3$ are
\[
\{e\},\quad A_3,\quad S_3.
\]
They correspond respectively to $K$, $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$, and $\mathbb Q$. The three order-$2$ subgroups are not normal, so the three cubic subfields are not Galois over $\mathbb Q$.
:::
