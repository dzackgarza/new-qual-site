::: problem
Let $p$ be a prime and $n \ge 1$ a positive integer.
Prove that the finite field $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is the splitting field of the polynomial $f(x) = x^{p^n} - x$ over the prime field $\mathbb{F}_p$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is the splitting field of $x^{p^n} - x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

<1>1. Roots of $f(x) = x^{p^n} - x$ and Separability:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Consider the polynomial $f(x) = x^{p^n} - x \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ of degree $q = p^n$.
    <2>2. We compute its formal derivative in characteristic $p$:
        $$f'(x) = p^n x^{p^n - 1} - 1 = 0 - 1 = -1 \ne 0.$$
    <2>3. Since $\gcd(f(x), f'(x)) = \gcd(x^{p^n} - x, -1) = 1$, the polynomial $f(x)$ has no repeated roots in any field extension.
    <2>4. Therefore, $f(x)$ has exactly $q = p^n$ **distinct roots** in its splitting field $L$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

<1>2. The Set of Roots Forms a Subfield:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $S = \{\alpha \in L \mid \alpha^{p^n} = \alpha\}$ be the set of all roots of $f(x)$ in $L$.
    <2>2. We verify that $S$ is closed under the field operations:
        - **Closure under subtraction:**
          Since $\operatorname{char}(L) = p$, the Frobenius endomorphism $\sigma_{p^n}(x) = x^{p^n}$ is an automorphism of $L$.
          For any $\alpha, \beta \in S$:
          $$(\alpha - \beta)^{p^n} = \alpha^{p^n} - \beta^{p^n} = \alpha - \beta \implies \alpha - \beta \in S.$$
        - **Closure under multiplication and inverses:**
          $$(\alpha \beta)^{p^n} = \alpha^{p^n} \beta^{p^n} = \alpha \beta \implies \alpha \beta \in S.$$
          For $\beta \ne 0$, $(\beta^{-1})^{p^n} = (\beta^{p^n})^{-1} = \beta^{-1} \implies \beta^{-1} \in S$.
        - **Contains $\mathbb{F}_p$:**
          For any $c \in \mathbb{F}_p$, by Fermat's Little Theorem, $c^p = c \implies c^{p^n} = c$, so $\mathbb{F}_p \subseteq S$.
    <2>3. Thus $S$ is a subfield of $L$.

<1>3. Splitting Field Identification:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By definition of the splitting field $L$, $L = \mathbb{F}_p(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{p^n})$ is generated by the roots $S$.
    <2>2. Since $S$ is already a field containing $\mathbb{F}_p$, we have:
        $$L = S.$$
    <2>3. By Step <1>1, $|S| = p^n$.
    <2>4. Any finite field of order $p^n$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.
    <2>5. Conversely, in any field $K$ with $|K| = p^n$, the multiplicative group $K^\times$ has order $p^n - 1$, so every non-zero element satisfies $\alpha^{p^n - 1} = 1 \implies \alpha^{p^n} = \alpha$ (and $0^{p^n} = 0$).
    <2>6. Thus every element of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is a root of $x^{p^n} - x$, meaning $x^{p^n} - x$ splits completely in $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is the splitting field of $x^{p^n} - x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Q.E.D.
:::
