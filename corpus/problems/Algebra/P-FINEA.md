::: problem
Show that $A_n$ is generated by $3$-cycles.
:::


::: {.solution}
Every element of $A_n$ is a product of an even number of transpositions. Pair those transpositions.

<1>1. A product of two transpositions that share a point is a $3$-cycle (or the identity).
::: {.proof}
For distinct $a,b,c$,
\[
(a\ b)(b\ c)=(a\ b\ c),
\]
up to the convention for composition order. If the two transpositions are equal, their product is the identity.
:::

<1>2. A product of two disjoint transpositions is a product of two $3$-cycles.
::: {.proof}
For distinct $a,b,c,d$,
\[
(a\ b)(c\ d)=(a\ c\ b)(a\ c\ d).
\]
A direct check on the four moved points verifies the equality.
:::

<1>3. Therefore every even permutation is a product of $3$-cycles.
::: {.proof}
Write an element of $A_n$ as a product of an even number of transpositions and group them into pairs. Apply <1>1 or <1>2 to each pair. Hence the $3$-cycles generate $A_n$.
:::
:::
