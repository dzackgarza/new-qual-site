::: {.problem}
Show that $A_n$ is generated by $3$-cycles.
:::

::: {.solution}
Every element of $A_n$ is a product of an even number of transpositions. Pair those transpositions.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

A product of two transpositions that share a point is a $3$-cycle (or the identity).

::: pf-proof

For distinct $a,b,c$,
\[
(a\ b)(b\ c)=(a\ b\ c),
\]
up to the convention for composition order. If the two transpositions are equal, their product is the identity.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

A product of two disjoint transpositions is a product of two $3$-cycles.

::: pf-proof

For distinct $a,b,c,d$,
\[
(a\ b)(c\ d)=(a\ c\ b)(a\ c\ d).
\]
A direct check on the four moved points verifies the equality.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore every even permutation is a product of $3$-cycles.

::: pf-proof

Write an element of $A_n$ as a product of an even number of transpositions and group them into pairs. Apply step [](#s1){.pf-ref} or step [](#s2){.pf-ref} to each pair. Hence the $3$-cycles generate $A_n$.

:::
