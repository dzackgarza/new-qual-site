::: problem
Let $f(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree $n \ge 2$, and let $K/\mathbb{Q}$ be its splitting field with $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_n$.
What algebraic, geometric, and field-theoretic properties must the roots $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n \in \mathbb{C}$ of $f(x)$ satisfy?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Characterize the algebraic independence, conjugate symmetries, degree towers, and non-real pairs of roots for a polynomial with full symmetric Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_n$.

<1>1. Distinctness and Irreducibility (Separability):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{Q}) = 0$, $f(x)$ is separable, so its $n$ roots $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n \in \mathbb{C}$ are **mutually distinct**.
    <2>2. Since $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_n$ acts $n$-transitively on the roots:
        - $f(x)$ is **irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$** (1-transitive).
        - For any root $\alpha_i$, the intermediate extension has degree $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_i) : \mathbb{Q}] = n$.

<1>2. Complete Permutability (No Non-Trivial Algebraic Relations):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By the fundamental definition of the Galois group, an algebraic relation $P(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n) = 0$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Q}$ holds if and only if:
        $$P(\alpha_{\sigma(1)}, \dots, \alpha_{\sigma(n)}) = 0 \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) = S_n.$$
    <2>2. Since $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ contains **every permutation** $\sigma \in S_n$, there are **no algebraic relations among the roots other than symmetric ones** (the elementary symmetric polynomials evaluating to the coefficients of $f$).
    <2>3. In particular, no root $\alpha_j$ ($j \ne i$) lies in the subfield generated by the other roots $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{i-1}, \alpha_{i+1}, \dots, \alpha_n)$ unless all $n$ roots are included.
    <2>4. The stem extensions form a maximal tower:
        $$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_k) : \mathbb{Q}] = n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1) = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!}.$$

<1>3. Splitting Field Degree and Minimality:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The splitting field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)$ has maximal possible degree:
        $$[K : \mathbb{Q}] = |S_n| = n!$$
    <2>2. The Discriminant $\Delta = \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} (\alpha_i - \alpha_j)^2$ is **not a square** in $\mathbb{Q}$ (since $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) = S_n \not\subseteq A_n$).
    <2>3. The unique quadratic intermediate field in $K$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta})$.

<1>4. Complex Conjugation and Real Roots:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Restricting complex conjugation $\tau(z) = \bar{z}$ to the splitting field $K \subset \mathbb{C}$ defines an automorphism $\tau \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$.
    <2>2. Since $\tau^2 = \operatorname{id}$, $\tau$ is a product of disjoint 2-cycles swapping pairs of complex conjugate non-real roots.
    <2>3. If $f(x)$ has $r_1$ real roots and $2 r_2$ non-real roots ($n = r_1 + 2 r_2$):
        - $\tau$ is a product of $r_2$ disjoint transpositions.
        - In particular, if $f(x)$ is an irreducible polynomial of prime degree $p$ with exactly $p - 2$ real roots and 2 non-real complex conjugate roots, $\tau$ is a single transposition $(a \, b)$. Together with the $p$-cycle generated by irreducibility, this forces $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_p$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    The roots of $f(x)$ are distinct, admit every permutation as a field symmetry, satisfy no asymmetric algebraic relations, and generate a maximal degree $n!$ splitting field. Q.E.D.
:::
