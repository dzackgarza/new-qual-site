::: {.problem}
Let $f(x) \in \ZZ[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree $n \ge 2$, and let $K/\QQ$ be its splitting field with $\Gal(K/\QQ) \cong S_n$.
What algebraic, geometric, and field-theoretic properties must the roots $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n \in \CC$ of $f(x)$ satisfy?
:::

::: {.solution}
Identify $G=\Gal(K/\QQ)$ with the full permutation group $S_n$ of the roots $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$.

::: pf

::: pf-step
The roots are distinct, and $[\QQ(\alpha_i):\QQ]=n$ for every $i$.

::: pf-proof
An irreducible polynomial over a field of characteristic $0$ is separable. Each $\alpha_i$ has minimal polynomial a scalar multiple of $f$, which has degree $n$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$[\QQ(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k):\QQ]=\dfrac{n!}{(n-k)!}$ for $0\le k\le n$; in particular $[K:\QQ]=n!$ and $K=\QQ(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})$.

::: pf-proof
By the Galois correspondence, $\QQ(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)$ corresponds to the pointwise stabilizer of $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k$ in $S_n$, which is $S_{n-k}$ for $k\le n$, with $S_0=S_1=1$. Its index is $n!/(n-k)!$. For $k=n-1$ this is already $n!=[K:\QQ]$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The ideal of polynomials $P\in\QQ[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with $P(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)=0$ is stable under every permutation of the variables.

::: pf-proof
If $P(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)=0$ and $\sigma\in S_n=G$, applying the field automorphism $\sigma$ gives $P(\alpha_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\alpha_{\sigma(n)})=0$, because $P$ has rational coefficients.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The discriminant $\Delta(f)$ is not a square in $\QQ$. For $n\ge3$, $\QQ(\sqrt{\Delta(f)})$ is the unique quadratic subfield of $K$; for $n=2$, $K=\QQ(\sqrt{\Delta(f)})$.

::: pf-proof
An element $\sigma\in G$ maps $\sqrt{\Delta(f)}=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)$ to $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\sqrt{\Delta(f)}$, so $\sqrt{\Delta(f)}\in\QQ$ exactly when $G\subseteq A_n$, which fails for $G=S_n$. Quadratic subfields correspond to index-two subgroups, and for $n\ge3$ the only one is $A_n$, whose fixed field is $\QQ(\sqrt{\Delta(f)})$. For $n=2$, $[K:\QQ]=2$ and $\sqrt{\Delta(f)}=\alpha_1-\alpha_2\in K\setminus\QQ$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Complex conjugation restricts to an element of $G$ that fixes the real roots and swaps the nonreal roots in conjugate pairs; its cycle type in $S_n$ records the number of real and nonreal roots.

::: pf-proof
$K$ is generated by the roots of a real polynomial, so complex conjugation maps $K$ onto itself and restricts to a field automorphism of $K$ fixing $\QQ$.
:::
