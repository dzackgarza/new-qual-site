::: problem
1. State the fundamental theorem of Galois theory for a finite Galois extension $L/K$.
2. Describe $\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_{43})/\QQ)$.
3. How many proper subfields does $\QQ(\zeta_{43})$ have?
:::

::: solution
<1>1. Let $L/K$ be finite Galois with group
\[
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K).
\]
The assignments
\[
E\longmapsto \operatorname{Gal}(L/E),
\qquad
H\longmapsto L^H
\]
give inclusion-reversing mutually inverse bijections between intermediate fields
\[
K\subseteq E\subseteq L
\]
and subgroups $H\le G$. Moreover,
\[
[E:K]=[G:H],
\]
and $E/K$ is Galois exactly when $H\normal G$, in which case
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\cong G/H.
\]

<1>2. Since $43$ is prime,
\[
[\QQ(\zeta_{43}):\QQ]=\varphi(43)=42.
\]
Every automorphism is determined by
\[
\zeta_{43}\longmapsto\zeta_{43}^a,
\qquad a\in(\ZZ/43\ZZ)^\times.
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_{43})/\QQ)
\cong (\ZZ/43\ZZ)^\times.
\]
The multiplicative group of a finite field is cyclic, so this group is cyclic of order $42$.

<1>3. A cyclic group has exactly one subgroup of order $d$ for every divisor $d$ of its order. Since
\[
42=2\cdot3\cdot7,
\]
it has
\[
\tau(42)=(1+1)^3=8
\]
subgroups. Therefore there are $8$ intermediate fields including the two endpoints $\QQ$ and $\QQ(\zeta_{43})$.

Hence there are $6$ nontrivial proper intermediate fields. If “proper subfields” means simply subfields strictly smaller than $\QQ(\zeta_{43})$, counting $\QQ$ as well, then there are $7$.
:::
