::: {.problem}
Let $A$ be an $R$-module (or abelian group), and let $p \in R$ be a ring element (e.g. a prime integer $p \in \ZZ$).
Define the multiplication-by-$p$ map:
$$\phi_p: A \longrightarrow A, \qquad x \longmapsto p x.$$

(1) Prove that $\phi_p$ is an $R$-module homomorphism when $p \in Z(R)$.

(2) Define the image submodule $p A \coloneqq \im(\phi_p)$ and the kernel submodule $A[p] \coloneqq \ker(\phi_p)$ ($p$-torsion submodule).

(3) Compute $p A$, $A/pA$, and $A[p]$ for $A = \ZZ$, $A = \ZZ/n\ZZ$, and $A = \QQ/\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

(1) If $p\in Z(R)$, then $\phi_p$ is $R$-linear.

::: pf-proof

Additivity is $p(x+y)=px+py$. For $r\in R$ and $x\in A$,
$$
\phi_p(rx)=p(rx)=(pr)x=(rp)x=r(px)=r\phi_p(x).
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(2) $pA=\{px:x\in A\}$ and $A[p]=\{x\in A:px=0\}$ are submodules, the sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow A[p]\longrightarrow A\xrightarrow{\,p\,}A
\longrightarrow A/pA\longrightarrow0
$$
is exact, and $A/A[p]\cong pA$.

::: pf-proof

The image and kernel of a module homomorphism are submodules. Exactness at each term is the definition of $A[p]$ as the kernel and of $pA$ as the image, and the isomorphism is the first isomorphism theorem.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(3) For a prime integer $p$ and abelian groups:

- for $A=\ZZ$: $pA=p\ZZ$, $A/pA\cong\ZZ/p\ZZ$, and $A[p]=0$;

- for $A=\ZZ/n\ZZ$ with $d\coloneqq\gcd(p,n)\in\{1,p\}$: $pA=\langle p\rangle$ has order $n/d$, $A/pA\cong\ZZ/d\ZZ$, and $A[p]=\langle n/d\rangle\cong\ZZ/d\ZZ$;

- for $A=\QQ/\ZZ$: $pA=A$, $A/pA=0$, and $A[p]=\left\{\frac{k}{p}+\ZZ:0\le k<p\right\}\cong\ZZ/p\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof

In $\ZZ$, $px=0$ forces $x=0$, and $\ZZ/p\ZZ$ is the quotient by definition.

In $\ZZ/n\ZZ$, the class of $p$ has order $n/\gcd(p,n)=n/d$, so $\abs{pA}=n/d$ and $\abs{A/pA}=d$; a quotient of a cyclic group is cyclic, so $A/pA\cong\ZZ/d\ZZ$. A class $[x]$ satisfies $p[x]=0$ exactly when $n\mid px$, that is, $n/d\mid x$, so $A[p]=\langle n/d\rangle$, of order $d$. Since $p$ is prime, $d\in\{1,p\}$.

In $\QQ/\ZZ$, the class of $a/b$ equals $p$ times the class of $a/(pb)$, so multiplication by $p$ is surjective. A class $x+\ZZ$ satisfies $px\in\ZZ$ exactly when $x\in\frac1p\ZZ$, which gives the $p$ classes $k/p+\ZZ$, a cyclic group generated by $1/p+\ZZ$.

:::
