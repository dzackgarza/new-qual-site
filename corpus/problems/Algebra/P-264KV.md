::: {.exercise}
How would you start classifying groups of order $56$? Is there a nontrivial action of $C_7$ on a group of order $8$?
:::
::: {.solution}
Let $\abs{G}=56=8\cdot7$.

::: pf

::: pf-step
Every group $G$ of order $56$ has a normal Sylow $7$-subgroup or a normal Sylow $2$-subgroup, so $G$ is a semidirect product of a group of order $8$ and $C_7$.

::: pf-proof
By Sylow's theorems,
$$
n_7\equiv1\pmod7,\qquad n_7\mid8,
$$
so $n_7=1$ or $8$.
If $n_7=1$, the Sylow $7$-subgroup is normal.
If $n_7=8$, the eight Sylow $7$-subgroups intersect pairwise trivially and contribute
$$
8(7-1)=48
$$
elements of order $7$. Exactly eight elements remain, and every Sylow $2$-subgroup has eight elements, none of order $7$, so there is a unique Sylow $2$-subgroup, which is therefore normal.
In either case the normal Sylow subgroup and a complementary Sylow subgroup intersect trivially and generate $G$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
A nontrivial action of $C_7$ on a group $P$ of order $8$ exists only for $P\cong C_2^3$, and then $C_2^3\rtimes C_7$ is the unique nontrivial semidirect product of this form.

::: pf-proof
A nontrivial action of $C_7$ on $P$ exists exactly when $\Aut(P)$ contains an element of order $7$. For the five groups of order $8$:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
P&\abs{\Aut(P)}\\ \hline
C_8&4\\
C_4\times C_2&8\\
D_4&8\\
Q_8&24\\
C_2^3&\abs{\GL_3(\FF_2)}=168
\end{array}
$$
Only $C_2^3$ admits such an action. Since the Sylow $7$-subgroups of $\GL_3(\FF_2)$ are conjugate, all nontrivial actions are equivalent up to automorphisms of $C_2^3$ and $C_7$, so they give isomorphic semidirect products.
:::
