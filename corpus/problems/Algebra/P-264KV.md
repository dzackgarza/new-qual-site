::: problem
How would you start classifying groups of order 56?
Is there a way for $\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$ to act non-trivially on a group of order 8?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Classify groups of order $56 = 2^3 \cdot 7$, prove that either the Sylow 7-subgroup or Sylow 2-subgroup is normal, and describe all actions of $\mathbb{Z}_7$ on groups of order 8.

<1>1. Sylow Analysis of $|G| = 56 = 8 \cdot 7$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By Sylow's Theorems, the number $n_7$ of Sylow 7-subgroups satisfies $n_7 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$ and $n_7 \mid 8$.
    <2>2. Thus $n_7 \in \{1, 8\}$.
    <2>3. **If $n_7 = 8$:**
        - Each Sylow 7-subgroup is cyclic of order 7, intersecting trivially with other Sylow 7-subgroups at $\{e\}$.
        - The 8 Sylow 7-subgroups contain $8 \times (7 - 1) = 48$ elements of order 7.
        - The remaining elements in $G$ number $56 - 48 = 8$.
        - Any Sylow 2-subgroup $P_2$ has order 8.
        - Because $|P_2| = 8$ and there are only 8 elements left in $G$, $P_2$ must consist of exactly these 8 elements.
        - Therefore, $P_2$ is unique, so $n_2 = 1$, which implies $P_2 \trianglelefteq G$.
    <2>4. **Conclusion on Sylow normality:** In any group of order 56, at least one of $P_7 \trianglelefteq G$ (if $n_7 = 1$) or $P_2 \trianglelefteq G$ (if $n_7 = 8$) is a normal subgroup.
    <2>5. In particular, every group of order 56 is solvable and is a semidirect product:
        - Either $G \cong P_2 \rtimes P_7$ (if $P_2 \trianglelefteq G$),
        - Or $G \cong P_7 \rtimes P_2$ (if $P_7 \trianglelefteq G$).

<1>2. Actions of $\mathbb{Z}_7$ on Groups of Order 8:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. An action of $\mathbb{Z}_7$ on a group $P$ of order 8 corresponds to a homomorphism $\theta: \mathbb{Z}_7 \to \operatorname{Aut}(P)$.
    <2>2. The image $\operatorname{im}\theta$ is a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(P)$, so $|\operatorname{im}\theta|$ divides $|\mathbb{Z}_7| = 7$.
    <2>3. A non-trivial action exists if and only if $7$ divides $|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|$.
    <2>4. We test all 5 groups of order 8:
        - $P = \mathbb{Z}_8$: $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_8) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, order 4. ($7 \nmid 4$, only trivial action).
        - $P = \mathbb{Z}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$: $|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_2)| = 8$. ($7 \nmid 8$, only trivial action).
        - $P = D_4$: $\operatorname{Aut}(D_4) \cong D_4$, order 8. ($7 \nmid 8$, only trivial action).
        - $P = Q_8$: $\operatorname{Aut}(Q_8) \cong S_4$, order 24. ($7 \nmid 24$, only trivial action).
        - $P = \mathbb{Z}_2^3$ (Elementary abelian): $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_2^3) \cong \operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)$.
    <2>5. **Computation of $|\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)|$:**
        $$|\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)| = (2^3 - 1)(2^3 - 2)(2^3 - 4) = 7 \cdot 6 \cdot 4 = 168.$$
    <2>6. Since $7 \mid 168$, $\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)$ contains Sylow 7-subgroups of order 7 (e.g. companion matrix of $x^3 + x + 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$).
    <2>7. All subgroups of order 7 in $\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)$ are conjugate (by Sylow theory), yielding a **unique non-abelian semidirect product** $\mathbb{Z}_2^3 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_7$ of order 56 with $n_7 = 8$ (the Frobenius group $F_{56}$).

<1>3. Conclusion:
    $n_7 \in \{1, 8\}$, forcing $P_7 \trianglelefteq G$ or $P_2 \trianglelefteq G$. The only group of order 8 admitting a non-trivial action by $\mathbb{Z}_7$ is $\mathbb{Z}_2^3$, since $|\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)| = 168 = 7 \times 24$. Q.E.D.
:::
