::: {.exercise}
How would you start classifying groups of order $56$? Is there a nontrivial action of $C_7$ on a group of order $8$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $|G|=56=8\cdot7$. Sylow gives
\[
n_7\equiv1\pmod7,\qquad n_7\mid8,
\]
so $n_7=1$ or $8$.

If $n_7=1$, the Sylow $7$-subgroup is normal. If $n_7=8$, the eight Sylow $7$-subgroups contribute
\[
8(7-1)=48
\]
nonidentity elements. Exactly eight elements remain, and every Sylow $2$-subgroup has eight elements, so there is a unique Sylow $2$-subgroup. Thus in every group of order $56$, at least one Sylow subgroup is normal, giving the starting semidirect-product reduction.

Now let $P$ be a group of order $8$. A nontrivial action of $C_7$ on $P$ exists exactly when $\operatorname{Aut}(P)$ contains an element of order $7$. For the five groups of order $8$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
P&|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|\\ \hline
C_8&4\\
C_4\times C_2&8\\
D_4&8\\
Q_8&24\\
C_2^3&|\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)|=168
\end{array}
\]
Only $C_2^3$ admits such an action. Since Sylow $7$-subgroups of $\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)$ are conjugate, all faithful actions are equivalent up to automorphisms of $C_2^3$ and $C_7$. Hence there is a unique nontrivial semidirect product
\[
C_2^3\rtimes C_7
\]
of this form.

This does not by itself finish the full classification of groups of order $56$, but it gives the main Sylow/semidirect-product starting point requested.
:::
