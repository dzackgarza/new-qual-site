::: {.exercise}
Prove that an irreducible polynomial $\pi(x)\in \FF_p[x]$ divides $x^{p^n}-x \iff \deg \pi(x)$ divides $n$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\pi(x) \in \FF_p[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree $d = \deg \pi(x)$.
Let $\alpha$ be a root of $\pi(x)$ in an algebraic closure $\overline{\FF_p}$.

1. **Characterization of the Splitting Field and Degree of $\alpha$:** Since $\pi(x)$ is irreducible over $\FF_p$ and $\pi(\alpha) = 0$, the simple field extension $\FF_p(\alpha)$ is a finite field with degree:
   $$
   [\FF_p(\alpha) : \FF_p] = \deg \pi(x) = d.
   $$
   By the uniqueness of finite fields of given order, $\FF_p(\alpha) \cong \FF_{p^d}$.

2. **Roots of $x^{p^n} - x$:** The polynomial $x^{p^n} - x \in \FF_p[x]$ has roots precisely the elements of the finite field $\FF_{p^n}$:
   $$
   \beta \in \overline{\FF_p} \text{ satisfies } \beta^{p^n} = \beta \iff \beta \in \FF_{p^n}.
   $$

3. **Subfield Criterion for Finite Fields:** A finite field $\FF_{p^d}$ is a subfield of $\FF_{p^n}$ if and only if $d$ divides $n$:
   $$
   \FF_{p^d} \subseteq \FF_{p^n} \iff d \mid n.
   $$

4. **Connecting Divisibility of Polynomials to Subfields:**

   - **$(\Longrightarrow)$ Suppose $\pi(x) \mid (x^{p^n} - x)$:** Then every root of $\pi(x)$ is a root of $x^{p^n} - x$.
     In particular, $\alpha^{p^n} - \alpha = 0$, so $\alpha \in \FF_{p^n}$.
     Therefore, the subfield generated by $\alpha$ satisfies:
     $$
     \FF_{p^d} = \FF_p(\alpha) \subseteq \FF_{p^n}.
     $$
     By the tower law $[\FF_{p^n} : \FF_p] = [\FF_{p^n} : \FF_{p^d}] \cdot [\FF_{p^d} : \FF_p]$, we have $n = [\FF_{p^n} : \FF_{p^d}] \cdot d$, which means $d \mid n$.

   - **$(\Longleftarrow)$ Suppose $d \mid n$:** Then $\FF_{p^d} \subseteq \FF_{p^n}$.
     Since $\alpha \in \FF_{p^d} \subseteq \FF_{p^n}$, we have $\alpha^{p^n} - \alpha = 0$.
     The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\FF_p$ is the monic scalar multiple of $\pi(x)$ (since $\pi(x)$ is irreducible and monic up to unit).
     Since $\alpha$ is a root of $x^{p^n} - x$, the minimal polynomial $\pi(x)$ must divide $x^{p^n} - x$ in $\FF_p[x]$.

Hence, $\pi(x) \mid (x^{p^n} - x) \iff \deg \pi(x) \mid n$.

*(As a consequence, over $\FF_p$, $x^{p^n} - x = \prod_{d \mid n} \prod_{\substack{\pi \text{ monic irred} \\ \deg \pi = d}} \pi(x)$.
)*
:::
