::: problem
If $M$ is free abelian, how can I put quotients of M in some standard form?
What was crucial about the integers here (abelian groups being modules over $\ZZ$)? How does the procedure simplify if the ring is a Euclidean domain, not just a PID?
:::


::: {.solution}
Let $M\cong\ZZ^n$ and let $N\le M$ be generated by the columns of an integer matrix
\[
A\in M_{n\times r}(\ZZ).
\]
Then
\[
M/N\cong\operatorname{coker}(A).
\]

<1>1. Smith normal form gives invertible integer matrices $U,V$ such that
\[
UAV=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_s,0,\dots,0),
\qquad
d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_s,
\]
with each $d_i>0$.
::: {.proof}
The ring $\ZZ$ is a PID, so every matrix admits Smith normal form. Multiplication by unimodular matrices corresponds to changing bases in the codomain and domain and therefore preserves the cokernel up to isomorphism.
:::

<1>2. Therefore
\[
M/N
\cong
\ZZ^{\,n-s}
\oplus
\ZZ/d_1\ZZ
\oplus\cdots\oplus
\ZZ/d_s\ZZ.
\]
::: {.proof}
This is the cokernel of the diagonal Smith matrix.
:::

<1>3. What is crucial is not specifically the integers but the PID property.
::: {.proof}
Over any PID $R$, a matrix has Smith normal form and the same argument gives
\[
R^n/N
\cong
R^{n-s}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^s R/(d_i).
\]
This is the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID.
:::

<1>4. Over a Euclidean domain, the existence theorem is the same but the computation is more algorithmic.
::: {.proof}
The Euclidean algorithm supplies explicit gcd computations and Bézout coefficients. Repeated elementary row and column operations can use Euclidean division to replace entries by smaller remainders until a gcd appears in a pivot position, then clear its row and column. Repeating produces Smith normal form constructively. Over a general PID the same normal form exists, but one does not necessarily have a Euclidean norm giving this direct reduction algorithm.
:::
:::
