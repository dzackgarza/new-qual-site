::: problem
- Show that the transitive subgroups of $S_4$ are $S_4, A_4, D_4, \ZZ_2^2, \ZZ_4$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. A transitive subgroup $G \le S_4$ has order divisible by $4$ (by the orbit–stabilizer theorem, $|G| = 4 \cdot |\operatorname{Stab}(1)|$).
::: {.proof}
the orbit of $1$ has size $4$, so $|G| = 4 \cdot |\operatorname{Stab}(1)|$.
:::

<1>2. Hence $|G| \in \{4, 8, 12, 24\}$ (the divisors of $24$ divisible by $4$).
::: {.proof}
$|G|$ divides $24$ and is divisible by $4$.
:::

<1>3. $|G| = 24$: $G = S_4$.
::: {.proof}
the only subgroup of $S_4$ of order $24$ is $S_4$ itself.
:::

<1>4. $|G| = 12$: $G = A_4$.
::: {.proof}
the only subgroup of $S_4$ of order $12$ is $A_4$ (a subgroup of index $2$ is normal, and $A_4$ is the unique subgroup of index $2$).
:::

<1>5. $|G| = 8$: $G \cong D_4$ (the dihedral group of order $8$).
::: {.proof}
the Sylow $2$-subgroups of $S_4$ have order $8$ and are all conjugate to $D_4$ (the stabilizer of a partition of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ into two pairs, or equivalently the symmetry group of a square); a transitive subgroup of order $8$ is a Sylow $2$-subgroup, hence $D_4$.
:::

<1>6. $|G| = 4$: $G \cong \ZZ_4$ or $G \cong \ZZ_2 \times \ZZ_2$.
<2>1. A transitive subgroup of order $4$ acts regularly on the $4$ points.
::: {.proof}
$|G| = 4$ and the orbit has size $4$, so the stabilizer is trivial, i.e. the action is regular.
:::
<2>2. A regular subgroup of $S_4$ of order $4$ is either cyclic ($\ZZ_4$, generated by a $4$-cycle) or $\ZZ_2 \times \ZZ_2$ (the Klein four-group $V_4 = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$).
::: {.proof}
the groups of order $4$ are $\ZZ_4$ and $\ZZ_2 \times \ZZ_2$; both embed regularly in $S_4$ ($\ZZ_4$ as $\langle (1234) \rangle$, and $\ZZ_2 \times \ZZ_2$ as $V_4$).
:::

<1>7. Hence the transitive subgroups of $S_4$ are exactly $S_4$, $A_4$, $D_4$, $\ZZ_4$, and $\ZZ_2 \times \ZZ_2$.
::: {.proof}
<1>3–<1>6.
:::

<1>8. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>7.
:::
