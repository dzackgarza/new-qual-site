::: {.problem}
Show that the transitive subgroups of $S_4$ are $S_4, A_4, D_4, \ZZ_2^2, \ZZ_4$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Here $D_4$ is the dihedral group of order $8$, and the list is up to conjugacy in $S_4$. Let $G\le S_4$ be transitive.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #orders}

$\abs{G}\in\{4,8,12,24\}$.

::: pf-proof

By the orbit-stabilizer theorem $4\mid\abs{G}$, and by Lagrange's theorem $\abs{G}\mid24$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $\abs{G}=24$ then $G=S_4$, and if $\abs{G}=12$ then $G=A_4$.

::: pf-proof

The first statement is clear. A subgroup of index $2$ is normal and contains the square of every element, because $S_4/G$ has order $2$. Every $3$-cycle $c$ is a square, $c=(c^2)^2$, and the $3$-cycles generate $A_4$, so $A_4\le G$ and $G=A_4$ by order.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $\abs{G}=8$ then $G$ is conjugate to $D_4=\langle(1\,2\,3\,4),(1\,3)\rangle$, the symmetry group of a square with vertices labelled $1,2,3,4$ in cyclic order.

::: pf-proof

$G$ is a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_4$, the subgroup $\langle(1\,2\,3\,4),(1\,3)\rangle$ has order $8$, and Sylow $2$-subgroups are conjugate. That subgroup is transitive because it contains a $4$-cycle.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $\abs{G}=4$ then $G$ is $\langle(1\,2\,3\,4)\rangle\cong\ZZ_4$ up to conjugacy, or $G=\{e,(1\,2)(3\,4),(1\,3)(2\,4),(1\,4)(2\,3)\}\cong\ZZ_2^2$.

::: pf-proof

By the orbit-stabilizer theorem every point stabilizer in $G$ is trivial, so no element $g\neq e$ of $G$ has a fixed point. The elements of $S_4$ without fixed points are the $4$-cycles and the double transpositions. If $G$ is cyclic, it is generated by a $4$-cycle, and all $4$-cycles are conjugate. Otherwise every element of $G$ has order at most $2$, so $G$ consists of $e$ and three double transpositions, which are all the double transpositions in $S_4$.

Both groups are transitive: the first contains a $4$-cycle, and the orbit of $1$ under the second is $\{1,2,3,4\}$.

:::
