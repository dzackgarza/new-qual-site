::: {.exercise}
6. If $F$ is over $K$, and $E$ is an intermediate extension of $F$ over $K$, is $F$ necessarily over $E ?$ Answer this question for each of the words "algebraic," "normal," and "separable" in the blanks.
:::

::: {.solution}
The answer is yes in all three cases.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #algebraic-passes-to-e}
If $F/K$ is algebraic, then $F/E$ is algebraic.

::: pf-proof
Let $\alpha\in F$.
Since $F/K$ is algebraic, there is a nonzero polynomial $f(x)\in K[x]$ with $f(\alpha)=0$.
Because $K\subseteq E$, the same polynomial belongs to $E[x]$.
Thus $\alpha$ is algebraic over $E$.
Since $\alpha$ was arbitrary, $F/E$ is algebraic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #separable-passes-to-e}
If $F/K$ is separable, then $F/E$ is separable.

::: pf-proof
Let $\alpha\in F$.
Since $F/K$ is separable, the minimal polynomial $m_{\alpha,K}(x)$ has distinct roots in a splitting field.
The minimal polynomial $m_{\alpha,E}(x)$ divides $m_{\alpha,K}(x)$ in $E[x]$: indeed, $m_{\alpha,K}(\alpha)=0$ and $m_{\alpha,E}$ is the monic polynomial of least positive degree in $E[x]$ annihilating $\alpha$.
A divisor of a separable polynomial is separable, so $m_{\alpha,E}$ is separable.
Hence every element of $F$ is separable over $E$, and therefore $F/E$ is separable.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #normal-passes-to-e}
If $F/K$ is normal, then $F/E$ is normal.

::: pf-proof
Because $F/K$ is normal and algebraic, there is a family $\mathcal S\subseteq K[x]$ such that $F$ is the splitting field of $\mathcal S$ over $K$.
Since $K\subseteq E\subseteq F$, we may also regard $\mathcal S$ as a family in $E[x]$.
All of its roots already lie in $F$, and $F$ is generated over $K$ by those roots; hence it is also generated over the larger base field $E$ by the same roots.
Thus $F$ is the splitting field of $\mathcal S$ over $E$, so $F/E$ is normal.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore algebraicity, separability, and normality each pass from $F/K$ to $F/E$ for every intermediate field $K\subseteq E\subseteq F$.
