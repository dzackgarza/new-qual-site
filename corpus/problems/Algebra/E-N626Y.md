::: exercise
6. Let $K\subseteq E\subseteq F$. If $F/K$ is algebraic, normal, or separable, respectively, must $F/E$ have the same property? Answer this question in each of the three cases.
:::

::: {.solution}
All three answers are yes.

<1>1. If $F/K$ is algebraic, then $F/E$ is algebraic.
::: {.proof}
Let $\alpha\in F$. Since $F/K$ is algebraic, there is a nonzero polynomial $f(x)\in K[x]$ with $f(\alpha)=0$. Since $K\subseteq E$, the same polynomial belongs to $E[x]$. Hence $\alpha$ is algebraic over $E$. Since $\alpha$ was arbitrary, $F/E$ is algebraic.
:::

<1>2. If $F/K$ is separable, then $F/E$ is separable.
::: {.proof}
Let $\alpha\in F$. Let $p_K(x)\in K[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K$, and $p_E(x)\in E[x]$ its minimal polynomial over $E$. Since $p_K(\alpha)=0$ and $p_E$ is minimal over $E$,
\[
p_E\mid p_K
\]
in $E[x]$. Because $F/K$ is separable, $p_K$ has no repeated root in an algebraic closure. Every divisor of a separable polynomial is separable, so $p_E$ is separable. Thus every $\alpha\in F$ is separable over $E$, and $F/E$ is separable.
:::

<1>3. If $F/K$ is normal, then $F/E$ is normal.
::: {.proof}
Let $\alpha\in F$, and use the same notation $p_K$ and $p_E$ as in <1>2. Again $p_E\mid p_K$ in $E[x]$. Since $F/K$ is normal, $p_K$ splits completely over $F$. Therefore its factor $p_E$ also splits completely over $F$. Hence the minimal polynomial over $E$ of every element of $F$ splits over $F$, so $F/E$ is normal.
:::

<1>4. Therefore algebraicity, separability, and normality all pass from $F/K$ to $F/E$ for every intermediate field $E$.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>1--<1>3.
:::
:::
