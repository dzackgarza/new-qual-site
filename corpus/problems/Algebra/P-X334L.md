::: {.problem}
Let $\FF_q$ be a finite field, where $q=p^n$. Determine $\Gal(\FF_q/\FF_p)$ and a generator. How many elements does $\FF_q$ have, and what is the structure of $\FF_q^\times$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $q=p^n$ with $p$ prime and $n\geq1$, and let $\varphi\colon\FF_q\to\FF_q$, $\varphi(x)=x^p$, be the Frobenius map.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #size}
$\cardinality{\FF_q}=q$, every $x\in\FF_q$ satisfies $x^q=x$, and $\FF_q$ is the splitting field over $\FF_p$ of $x^q-x$; hence $\FF_q/\FF_p$ is Galois of degree $n$.

::: pf-proof
$\FF_q$ is a finite field with $q$ elements by hypothesis. Its multiplicative group $\FF_q^\times$ has $q-1$ elements, so Lagrange's theorem gives $x^{q-1}=1$ for $x\neq0$, and hence $x^q=x$ for every $x\in\FF_q$, the case $x=0$ being clear. So $\FF_q$ consists of roots of $x^q-x$, and since that polynomial has degree $q$ and $\FF_q$ has $q$ elements, its roots are exactly the elements of $\FF_q$; those roots generate $\FF_q$ over $\FF_p$, so $\FF_q$ is the splitting field. The polynomial is separable because its derivative is $qx^{q-1}-1=-1\neq0$ in characteristic $p$, so the extension is separable as well as normal, hence Galois, and $[\FF_q:\FF_p]=n$ because a finite field with $p^n$ elements has dimension $n$ over its prime field.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #frobenius}
$\varphi$ is an automorphism of $\FF_q$ fixing $\FF_p$ pointwise.

::: pf-proof
$\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism by the binomial theorem in characteristic $p$, and it is injective because $x^p=0$ implies $x=0$; an injective map from the finite set $\FF_q$ to itself is bijective, so $\varphi$ is an automorphism. For $a\in\FF_p$ we have $a^p=a$, because $a=0$ or $a\in\FF_p^\times$, a group of order $p-1$ in which every element satisfies $a^{p-1}=1$; so $\varphi$ fixes $\FF_p$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order}
$\varphi$ has order $n$.

::: pf-proof
$\varphi^n(x)=x^{p^n}=x^q=x$ for every $x\in\FF_q$ by step [](#size){.pf-ref}, so $\varphi^n=\mathrm{id}$. If $0<r<n$, the fixed points of $\varphi^r$ satisfy $x^{p^r}=x$, a polynomial equation of degree $p^r$, which has at most $p^r<p^n=q$ solutions, while $\mathrm{id}$ fixes all $q$ elements; so $\varphi^r\neq\mathrm{id}$. Hence the least $r>0$ with $\varphi^r=\mathrm{id}$ is $n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #galois-group}
$\Gal(\FF_q/\FF_p)=\generators{\varphi}\cong\ZZ/n\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
$\FF_q/\FF_p$ is Galois of degree $n$ by step [](#size){.pf-ref}, so $|\Gal(\FF_q/\FF_p)|=[\FF_q:\FF_p]=n$; a finite extension of degree $n$ has at most $n$ automorphisms over the base field, and here that bound is attained. The elements $\mathrm{id},\varphi,\ldots,\varphi^{n-1}$ are $n$ distinct $\FF_p$-automorphisms by steps [](#frobenius){.pf-ref} and [](#order){.pf-ref}, so they exhaust the group, which is the cyclic group of order $n$ generated by $\varphi$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #units}
$\FF_q^\times$ has $q-1$ elements and is cyclic.

::: pf-proof
It has $q-1$ elements because $\FF_q$ has $q$ and $0$ is the only nonunit. Let $m$ be the exponent of the finite abelian group $\FF_q^\times$, that is, the least positive integer with $x^m=1$ for all $x\in\FF_q^\times$. Every nonzero element is a root of $x^m-1$, so $q-1\leq m$, the polynomial having at most $m$ roots; and $m\leq q-1$ because some element has order $m$ dividing the group order. Hence $m=q-1$. A finite abelian group contains an element whose order is its exponent, so $\FF_q^\times$ has an element of order $q-1$, that is, it is cyclic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\FF_q$ has $q=p^n$ elements, $\FF_q^\times$ is cyclic of order $q-1$, and $\Gal(\FF_q/\FF_p)=\generators{\varphi}\cong\ZZ/n\ZZ$ with $\varphi(x)=x^p$.

::: pf-proof
The three assertions are steps [](#size){.pf-ref}, [](#units){.pf-ref} and [](#galois-group){.pf-ref}; the generator named is the Frobenius automorphism, which is the $p$-power map.
:::
