::: {.problem}
Let $\FF_q$ be a finite field, where $q=p^n$. Determine $\Gal(\FF_q/\FF_p)$ and a generator. How many elements does $\FF_q$ have, and what is the structure of $\FF_q^\times$?
:::

::: {.solution}
By definition $\cardinality{\FF_q}=q=p^n$. Every element satisfies
$$
x^q=x,
$$
so $\FF_q$ is the splitting field over $\FF_p$ of $x^q-x$.

The Frobenius map
$$
\varphi:\FF_q\to\FF_q,
\qquad
x\mapsto x^p,
$$
is an $\FF_p$-automorphism. Its $n$th power is the identity because $x^{p^n}=x$. If $0<r<n$, then the fixed field of $\varphi^r$ has at most $p^r<q$ elements, so $\varphi^r\ne1$. Hence $\varphi$ has order $n$.

Since
$$
[\FF_q:\FF_p]=n,
$$
and an extension of degree $n$ has at most $n$ automorphisms over the base field,
$$
\Gal(\FF_q/\FF_p)
=\langle\varphi\rangle
\cong C_n.
$$

The multiplicative group $\FF_q^\times$ has order $q-1$ and is cyclic. To prove cyclicity, let $m$ be the exponent of the finite abelian group $\FF_q^\times$. Every nonzero element is a root of $x^m-1$, so
$$
q-1\le m.
$$
But always $m\le q-1$, hence $m=q-1$. A finite abelian group contains an element whose order equals its exponent, so $\FF_q^\times$ contains an element of order $q-1$ and is cyclic.
:::
