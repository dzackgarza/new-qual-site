::: problem
(1) Describe the irreducible complex representations and character table of the dihedral group $D_4$ (order 8).
(2) Explicitly construct the 2-dimensional irreducible representation.
(3) Provide its geometric interpretation as symmetries of the square.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Conjugacy classes and representation dimensions of $D_4$:
<2>1. $D_4 = \langle r, s \mid r^4 = s^2 = 1, \, s r s = r^{-1} \rangle$ has order $|D_4| = 8$, with elements $\{e, r, r^2, r^3, s, s r, s r^2, s r^3\}$.
::: {.proof}
presentation of $D_4$.
:::
<2>2. The 5 conjugacy classes are:
- $C_1 = \{e\}$ (identity, size 1),
- $C_2 = \{r^2\}$ (central rotation by $\pi$, size 1),
- $C_3 = \{r, r^3\}$ (rotations by $\pm \pi/2$, size 2),
- $C_4 = \{s, s r^2\}$ (reflections across coordinate axes, size 2),
- $C_5 = \{s r, s r^3\}$ (reflections across diagonals, size 2).
::: {.proof}
conjugation relations in $D_4$.
:::
<2>3. Since there are 5 conjugacy classes, there are exactly 5 irreducible complex representations.
Their dimensions $d_i$ satisfy $\sum_{i=1}^5 d_i^2 = 8$, whose unique positive integer solution is $1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 = 8$.
Thus there are four 1-dimensional representations and one 2-dimensional representation.
::: {.proof}
Artin–Wedderburn / character orthogonality relation $\sum d_i^2 = |G|$.
:::

<1>2. The four 1-dimensional representations:
<2>1. The commutator subgroup is $[D_4, D_4] = \langle r^2 \rangle$.
The abelianization is $D_4 / [D_4, D_4] \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, generated by the images of $r$ and $s$.
::: {.proof}
$[s, r] = s r s^{-1} r^{-1} = r^{-2} = r^2$.
:::
<2>2. The 1D representations correspond to homomorphisms to $\{\pm 1\}$:
- **$\chi_1$ (Trivial):** $\chi_1(r) = 1, \, \chi_1(s) = 1$.
- **$\chi_2$ (Sign on rotations):** $\chi_2(r) = -1, \, \chi_2(s) = 1$.
- **$\chi_3$ (Sign on reflections):** $\chi_3(r) = 1, \, \chi_3(s) = -1$.
- **$\chi_4$ (Sign on both):** $\chi_4(r) = -1, \, \chi_4(s) = -1$.
::: {.proof}
4 characters of $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$.
:::

<1>3. The 2-dimensional irreducible representation:
<2>1. Define $\rho: D_4 \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ on generators by:
\[
\rho(r) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \rho(s) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
::: {.proof}
definition of standard representation.
:::
<2>2. Verify the relations:
- $\rho(r)^4 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}^2 = I_2$,
- $\rho(s)^2 = I_2$,
- $\rho(s)\rho(r)\rho(s) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \rho(r)^{-1}$.
::: {.proof}
matrix multiplication.
:::
<2>3. Compute the character $\chi_5 = \operatorname{tr}(\rho)$:
\[
\chi_5(e) = 2, \quad \chi_5(r^2) = -2, \quad \chi_5(r) = 0, \quad \chi_5(s) = 0, \quad \chi_5(sr) = 0.
\]
::: {.proof}
matrix traces.
:::
<2>4. Check irreducibility via the inner product:
\[
\langle \chi_5, \chi_5 \rangle = \frac{1}{8}\big(1 \cdot 2^2 + 1 \cdot (-2)^2 + 2 \cdot 0^2 + 2 \cdot 0^2 + 2 \cdot 0^2\big) = \frac{4 + 4}{8} = 1.
\]
Thus $\chi_5$ is irreducible.
::: {.proof}
Schur's character orthogonality criterion.
:::

<1>4. The Character Table of $D_4$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{Class} & [e] & [r^2] & [r] & [s] & [s r] \\
\text{Size} & 1 & 1 & 2 & 2 & 2 \\
\hline
\chi_1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
\chi_2 & 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 & -1 \\
\chi_3 & 1 & 1 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\
\chi_4 & 1 & 1 & -1 & -1 & 1 \\
\chi_5 & 2 & -2 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}
\]
::: {.proof}
<1>2 and <1>3.
:::

<1>5. Geometric Interpretation:
<2>1. The 2-dimensional representation $\rho$ realizes $D_4$ as the isometry group of a square in $\mathbb{R}^2$ centered at the origin:
- $\rho(r)$ is counterclockwise rotation by $\pi/2$,
- $\rho(s)$ is reflection across the $x$-axis,
- $\rho(sr)$ is reflection across the line $y = -x$.
::: {.proof}
geometry of the square in $\mathbb{R}^2$.
:::

<1>6. Conclusion:
$D_4$ has four 1D irreducible representations $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_4$, one 2D irreducible representation $\rho$, and the character table above. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>5.
:::
:::
