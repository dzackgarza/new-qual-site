::: problem
(1) Describe the irreducible complex representations and character table of the dihedral group $D_4$ (order 8).
(2) Explicitly construct the 2-dimensional irreducible representation.
(3) Provide its geometric interpretation as symmetries of the square.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Construct the 5 irreducible representations of $D_4 = \langle r, s \mid r^4 = s^2 = 1, s r s = r^{-1} \rangle$, write down its character table, and explain the geometric 2D representation.

<1>1. Conjugacy Classes of $D_4$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $D_4$ has order $|D_4| = 8$, with elements $\{e, r, r^2, r^3, s, s r, s r^2, s r^3\}$.
    <2>2. The 5 conjugacy classes are:
        - $C_1 = \{e\}$ (identity, size 1).
        - $C_2 = \{r^2\}$ (central rotation by $\pi$, size 1).
        - $C_3 = \{r, r^3\}$ (rotations by $\pm \pi/2$, size 2).
        - $C_4 = \{s, s r^2\}$ (reflections across coordinate axes, size 2).
        - $C_5 = \{s r, s r^3\}$ (reflections across diagonals, size 2).
    <2>3. Since there are 5 conjugacy classes, there are exactly **5 irreducible complex representations**.
    <2>4. The dimensions $d_i$ satisfy $\sum_{i=1}^5 d_i^2 = 8$, whose unique integer solution is $1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 = 8$ (four 1D representations and one 2D representation).

<1>2. The Four 1-Dimensional Representations:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The abelianization is $D_4/[D_4, D_4] = D_4/\langle r^2 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, generated by the images of $r$ and $s$.
    <2>2. The 1D representations correspond to choices of signs for $\chi(r), \chi(s) \in \{\pm 1\}$:
        - **$\chi_1$ (Trivial):** $\chi_1(r) = 1, \chi_1(s) = 1$.
        - **$\chi_2$ (Sign on rotations):** $\chi_2(r) = -1, \chi_2(s) = 1$.
        - **$\chi_3$ (Sign on reflections):** $\chi_3(r) = 1, \chi_3(s) = -1$.
        - **$\chi_4$ (Sign on both):** $\chi_4(r) = -1, \chi_4(s) = -1$.

<1>3. The 2-Dimensional Irreducible Representation $\rho_{\text{std}}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define $\rho: D_4 \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ on generators:
        $$\rho(r) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \rho(s) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
    <2>2. We check the relations:
        - $\rho(r)^4 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}^2 = I_2$.
        - $\rho(s)^2 = I_2$.
        - $\rho(s) \rho(r) \rho(s) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \rho(r)^{-1}$.
    <2>3. Traces (character values $\chi_5 = \operatorname{Tr} \rho$):
        - $\chi_5(e) = \operatorname{Tr}(I_2) = 2$.
        - $\chi_5(r^2) = \operatorname{Tr}(-I_2) = -2$.
        - $\chi_5(r) = \operatorname{Tr}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = 0$.
        - $\chi_5(s) = \operatorname{Tr}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = 0$.
        - $\chi_5(s r) = \operatorname{Tr}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = 0$.
    <2>4. Irreducibility check: $\langle \chi_5, \chi_5 \rangle = \frac{1}{8}(1 \cdot 2^2 + 1 \cdot (-2)^2 + 2 \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot 0) = \frac{4 + 4}{8} = 1$.

<1>4. The Character Table of $D_4$:
    $$\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
    \text{Class} & [e] & [r^2] & [r] & [s] & [s r] \\
    \text{Size} & 1 & 1 & 2 & 2 & 2 \\
    \hline
    \chi_1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
    \chi_2 & 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 & -1 \\
    \chi_3 & 1 & 1 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\
    \chi_4 & 1 & 1 & -1 & -1 & 1 \\
    \chi_5 & 2 & -2 & 0 & 0 & 0
    \end{array}$$

<1>5. Geometric Interpretation:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $\rho_{\text{std}}$ is the standard geometric action of $D_4$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ as the symmetry group of the square with vertices $(\pm 1, \pm 1)$:
        - $r$ rotates the square counterclockwise by $\pi/2$.
        - $s$ reflects across the horizontal $x$-axis.
        - $s r$ reflects across the main diagonal $y = -x$.

<1>6. Conclusion:
    $D_4$ has 4 1D characters and 1 standard 2D geometric rotation/reflection representation. Q.E.D.
:::
