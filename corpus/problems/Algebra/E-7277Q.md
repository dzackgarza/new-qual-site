::: {.problem}
Characterize the splitting field and all intermediate fields of the polynomial $x^{4}-3$ over $\QQ$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\alpha=\sqrt[4]{3}\in\RR_{>0}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The splitting field of $x^4-3$ over $\QQ$ is $K=\QQ(\alpha,i)$, of degree $8$, and $G=\Gal(K/\QQ)\cong D_4$.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

The roots of $x^4-3$ are $\alpha, i\alpha, -\alpha, -i\alpha$, so the splitting field is $K=\QQ(\alpha,i)$.

::: pf-proof

These are the four fourth roots of $3$ in $\CC$; they generate the same field as $\alpha$ and $i=(i\alpha)/\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4$.

::: pf-proof

The polynomial $x^4-3$ is irreducible over $\QQ$ by Eisenstein's criterion at $3$, and it is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$[K:\QQ]=8$.

::: pf-proof

Since $\QQ(\alpha)\subset\RR$ and $i\notin\RR$, the element $i$ has minimal polynomial $x^2+1$ over $\QQ(\alpha)$, so $[K:\QQ(\alpha)]=2$. The tower law gives $[K:\QQ]=2\cdot4=8$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$G$ is generated by
$$
\sigma: \begin{cases} \alpha \mapsto i\alpha \\ i \mapsto i \end{cases}, \qquad
\tau: \begin{cases} \alpha \mapsto \alpha \\ i \mapsto -i \end{cases},
$$
of orders $4$ and $2$, with $\tau\sigma\tau=\sigma^{-1}$; hence $G\cong D_4$, the dihedral group of order $8$.

::: pf-proof

An automorphism of $K$ sends $\alpha$ to a root of $x^4-3$ and $i$ to $\pm i$, so there are at most $8$ automorphisms, and there are exactly $[K:\QQ]=8$; hence every such assignment extends, and $\sigma,\tau$ exist.
Evaluating on $\alpha$ and $i$ gives $\sigma^4=\tau^2=1$ and $\tau\sigma\tau(\alpha)=\tau\sigma(\alpha)=\tau(i\alpha)=-i\alpha=\sigma^{-1}(\alpha)$, while both sides fix $i$.
The subgroup $\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle$ has order $8$, so it is $G$.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The intermediate fields correspond, under the Galois correspondence, to the subgroups of $D_4$ as follows.

::: pf-proof

::: pf-step

The fields of degree $2$ over $\QQ$, fixed by the three subgroups of order $4$, are $K^{\langle\sigma\rangle}=\QQ(i)$, $K^{\langle\sigma^2,\tau\rangle}=\QQ(\alpha^2)=\QQ(\sqrt3)$ and $K^{\langle\sigma^2,\sigma\tau\rangle}=\QQ(i\alpha^2)=\QQ(i\sqrt3)$.

::: pf-proof

Each listed generator is fixed by the generators of its subgroup, and generates a field of degree $2$, which is the index of the subgroup.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The fields of degree $4$ over $\QQ$, fixed by the five subgroups of order $2$, are $K^{\langle\tau\rangle}=\QQ(\alpha)$, $K^{\langle\sigma^2\tau\rangle}=\QQ(i\alpha)$, $K^{\langle\sigma\tau\rangle}=\QQ((1+i)\alpha)$, $K^{\langle\sigma^3\tau\rangle}=\QQ((1-i)\alpha)$ and $K^{\langle\sigma^2\rangle}=\QQ(\sqrt3,i)$.
Only the last is normal over $\QQ$, since $\langle\sigma^2\rangle=Z(D_4)$ is the only normal subgroup of order $2$.

::: pf-proof

Each listed field is fixed by the generator of its subgroup, so it lies in the fixed field, which has degree $4$ over $\QQ$, the index of the subgroup. Each listed field also has degree $4$: $\alpha$ and $i\alpha$ are roots of the irreducible $x^4-3$, $(1\pm i)\alpha$ are roots of $x^4+12$, irreducible by Eisenstein at $3$, and $[\QQ(\sqrt3,i):\QQ]=4$. Hence the listed field is the fixed field.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The trivial subgroup fixes $K$ and $G$ fixes $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

This is the Galois correspondence for the Galois extension $K/\QQ$.

:::

:::

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

By steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}, the splitting field is $\QQ(\sqrt[4]{3}, i)$ with Galois group $D_4$, and there are exactly $10$ intermediate fields: $\QQ$; the three quadratic fields $\QQ(i), \QQ(\sqrt{3}), \QQ(i\sqrt{3})$; the five quartic fields $\QQ(\sqrt[4]{3}), \QQ(i\sqrt[4]{3}), \QQ((1\pm i)\sqrt[4]{3}), \QQ(\sqrt{3}, i)$; and $\QQ(\sqrt[4]{3}, i)$.

:::
