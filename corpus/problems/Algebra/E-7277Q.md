::: problem
14. Characterize the splitting field and all intermediate fields of the polynomial $x^{4}-3$ over $Q$
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Splitting field and Galois group of $f(x) = x^4 - 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$:
<2>1. The four complex roots of $x^4 - 3$ are $\{\alpha, i\alpha, -\alpha, -i\alpha\}$ where $\alpha = \sqrt[4]{3} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$.
Thus the splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, i)$.
Proof: roots of $x^4 - 3 = 0$.
<2>2. The polynomial $x^4 - 3 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ is irreducible by Eisenstein’s criterion at $p = 3$.
Hence $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$.
Proof: Eisenstein's criterion.
<2>3. Since $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subset \mathbb{R}$ and $i \notin \mathbb{R}$, $[K : \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)] = 2$, so $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = 8$.
Proof: degree tower $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = [K : \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)][\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2 \times 4 = 8$.
<2>4. The Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ is generated by:
\[
\sigma: \begin{cases} \alpha \mapsto i\alpha \\ i \mapsto i \end{cases} \quad (\text{order } 4), \qquad
\tau: \begin{cases} \alpha \mapsto \alpha \\ i \mapsto -i \end{cases} \quad (\text{order } 2).
\]
They satisfy $\tau \sigma \tau = \sigma^{-1}$, so $G \cong D_4$, the dihedral group of order 8.
Proof: action on generators $\alpha$ and $i$.

<1>2. Classification of all intermediate fields via the Galois correspondence:
<2>1. **Quadratic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$** (fixed fields of subgroups of order 4 / index 2):
- $H_1 = \langle \sigma \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_4 \implies K^{H_1} = \mathbb{Q}(i)$.
- $H_2 = \langle \sigma^2, \tau \rangle \cong V_4 \implies K^{H_2} = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha^2) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$.
- $H_3 = \langle \sigma^2, \sigma\tau \rangle \cong V_4 \implies K^{H_3} = \mathbb{Q}(i\alpha^2) = \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{3})$.
Proof: fixed fields of the three index-2 normal subgroups of $D_4$.
<2>2. **Quartic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$** (fixed fields of subgroups of order 2 / index 4):
- Non-normal extensions:
  - $J_1 = \langle \tau \rangle \implies K^{J_1} = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{3})$.
  - $J_2 = \langle \sigma^2\tau \rangle \implies K^{J_2} = \mathbb{Q}(i\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt[4]{3})$.
  - $J_3 = \langle \sigma\tau \rangle \implies K^{J_3} = \mathbb{Q}((1+i)\alpha)$.
  - $J_4 = \langle \sigma^3\tau \rangle \implies K^{J_4} = \mathbb{Q}((1-i)\alpha)$.
- Normal extension:
  - $J_5 = \langle \sigma^2 \rangle = Z(D_4) \implies K^{J_5} = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha^2, i) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}, i)$.
Proof: fixed fields of the five order-2 subgroups of $D_4$.
<2>3. **Extreme fields:**
- Fixed field of $\{1\}$ is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{3}, i)$ (degree 8).
- Fixed field of $D_4$ is $\mathbb{Q}$ (degree 1).
Proof: Galois correspondence.

<1>3. Conclusion:
The splitting field is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{3}, i)$ with Galois group $D_4$, and there are exactly 10 intermediate fields: $\mathbb{Q}$, three quadratic fields $\mathbb{Q}(i), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}), \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{3})$, five quartic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{3}), \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt[4]{3}), \mathbb{Q}((1\pm i)\sqrt[4]{3}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}, i)$, and the splitting field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{3}, i)$. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1 and <1>2.
