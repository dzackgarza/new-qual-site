::: {.problem}
Why is every finite extension algebraic?
Why is the degree of an extension given by joining algebraic elements always finite?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $F/K$ be a field extension.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #finite-alg}

If $[F:K]<\infty$, then every $\alpha\in F$ is algebraic over $K$.

::: pf-proof

If $[F:K]=n$, the $n+1$ elements $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^n$ are linearly dependent over $K$. A dependence relation is a nonzero polynomial with coefficients in $K$ that vanishes at $\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #simple}

If $\beta$ is algebraic over a field $L$, then $L(\beta)=L[\beta]$ and $[L(\beta):L]$ is the degree of the minimal polynomial of $\beta$ over $L$.

::: pf-proof

The evaluation map $L[x]\to L[\beta]$ has kernel generated by the irreducible minimal polynomial $f$ of $\beta$, so $L[\beta]\cong L[x]/(f)$ is a field, hence equals $L(\beta)$, and it has basis $1,\beta,\ldots,\beta^{\deg f-1}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r\in F$ are algebraic over $K$, then $K(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)=K[\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r]$ is finite over $K$, and every element of it is algebraic over $K$.

::: pf-proof

Induct on $r$; the case $r=1$ is step [](#simple){.pf-ref}. Let $L=K(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{r-1})=K[\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{r-1}]$, finite over $K$ by induction. Since $\alpha_r$ is algebraic over $K$, it is algebraic over $L$, so by step [](#simple){.pf-ref}
$$
L[\alpha_r]=L(\alpha_r)=K(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)
$$
is finite over $L$. By the tower law $[K(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r):K]=[L(\alpha_r):L][L:K]<\infty$. Step [](#finite-alg){.pf-ref} shows that every element is algebraic over $K$.

:::
