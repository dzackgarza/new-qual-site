::: problem
Classify finite fields, their subfields, and their field extensions.
What are the automorphisms of a finite field?
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every finite field has cardinality $p^n$ for a unique prime $p$ and integer $n\ge1$.
::: {.proof}
If $K$ is finite, its characteristic is a prime $p$. The prime field $\FF_p$ is therefore a subfield of $K$, so $K$ is a finite-dimensional vector space over $\FF_p$. If
\[
[K:\FF_p]=n,
\]
then
\[
|K|=p^n.
\]
:::

<1>2. For every prime power $q=p^n$, there is, up to isomorphism, a unique field $\FF_q$ with $q$ elements.
::: {.proof}
In an algebraic closure of $\FF_p$, the roots of
\[
x^{p^n}-x
\]
form a field: Frobenius identities show closure under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and inversion. The derivative is $-1$, so the polynomial has exactly $p^n$ distinct roots. Thus its root field has $p^n$ elements.

Conversely, if $K$ has $p^n$ elements, then every $a\in K$ satisfies
\[
a^{p^n}=a
\]
(by Lagrange's theorem on $K^\times$, together with $0$). Hence $K$ is a splitting field of $x^{p^n}-x$ over $\FF_p$. Splitting fields are unique up to $\FF_p$-isomorphism.
:::

<1>3. The subfields of $\FF_{p^n}$ are exactly
\[
\FF_{p^d}
\qquad(d\mid n),
\]
and for each divisor $d\mid n$ there is exactly one such subfield.
::: {.proof}
If $d\mid n$, then
\[
p^d-1\mid p^n-1,
\]
so every root of $x^{p^d}-x$ is also a root of $x^{p^n}-x$. Hence the unique field of $p^d$ elements sits inside $\FF_{p^n}$.

Conversely, let $K\subseteq\FF_{p^n}$ be a subfield. Then $|K|=p^d$ for some $d$, and
\[
[\FF_{p^n}:\FF_p]
=
[\FF_{p^n}:K]\,[K:\FF_p]
\]
shows that $d\mid n$.

Uniqueness follows because any subfield with $p^d$ elements is exactly the root set of $x^{p^d}-x$ inside $\FF_{p^n}$.
:::

<1>4. Every finite extension of $\FF_q$ of degree $m$ is isomorphic to $\FF_{q^m}$.
::: {.proof}
Write $q=p^d$. If $L/\FF_q$ has degree $m$, then
\[
[L:\FF_p]=md,
\]
so
\[
|L|=p^{md}=q^m.
\]
By <1>2, there is only one field of that cardinality up to isomorphism.
:::

<1>5. The extension
\[
\FF_{q^m}/\FF_q
\]
is Galois and cyclic of degree $m$, generated by the $q$-Frobenius
\[
\operatorname{Fr}_q:x\mapsto x^q.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every element of $\FF_{q^m}$ is a root of
\[
x^{q^m}-x,
\]
whose derivative is $-1$. Hence $\FF_{q^m}$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial over $\FF_q$, so the extension is Galois.

The map $\operatorname{Fr}_q$ fixes $\FF_q$ and satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Fr}_q^m(x)=x^{q^m}=x.
\]
Its fixed field is exactly $\FF_q$, because the fixed points are the roots of $x^q-x$, of which there are exactly $q$. Therefore its order is $m$. Since the Galois group also has order $m$, it is generated by $\operatorname{Fr}_q$.
:::

<1>6. In particular,
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\FF_{p^n})
=\operatorname{Gal}(\FF_{p^n}/\FF_p)
\cong C_n,
\]
generated by
\[
x\mapsto x^p.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every field automorphism fixes the prime field $\FF_p$. Apply <1>5 with $q=p$ and $m=n$.
:::
