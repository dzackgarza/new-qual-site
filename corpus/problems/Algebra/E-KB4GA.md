::: {.exercise}
Let $R$ be a PID.

1. Prove that every nonzero prime ideal is maximal.
2. Prove that $R$ is Noetherian.
3. Prove that every nonzero nonunit factors into finitely many irreducibles.
4. Prove that every nonzero maximal ideal is generated by an irreducible element.
5. Prove that $\mathbb Z$ is Noetherian but not Artinian.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Every nonzero prime ideal is maximal.
::: {.proof}
Let $(p)$ be a nonzero prime ideal and suppose
\[
(p)\subseteq(a)\subseteq R.
\]
Then $p=ra$. Since $p=ra\in(p)$ and $(p)$ is prime, either $a\in(p)$ or $r\in(p)$. In the first case $(a)=(p)$. In the second, $r=sp$, so
\[
p=spa.
\]
Since $R$ is a domain and $p\ne0$, $sa=1$, hence $(a)=R$. Thus $(p)$ is maximal.
:::

<1>2. Every PID is Noetherian.
::: {.proof}
Every ideal is principal, hence finitely generated. A ring in which every ideal is finitely generated is Noetherian.
:::

<1>3. Every nonzero nonunit factors into finitely many irreducibles.
::: {.proof}
Suppose not. Choose a nonzero nonunit $a_0$ with no factorization into irreducibles. Then $a_0$ is reducible, say
\[
a_0=b_1a_1
\]
with both factors nonunits. At least one factor, say $a_1$, also has no factorization into irreducibles. Then
\[
(a_0)\subsetneq(a_1).
\]
Repeating produces an infinite strictly ascending chain of ideals, contradicting Noetherianity. Hence the process terminates and gives a finite irreducible factorization.
:::

<1>4. Every nonzero maximal ideal is generated by an irreducible.
::: {.proof}
Write $\mathfrak m=(m)\ne(0)$. Since $\mathfrak m$ is proper, $m$ is not a unit. If $m=ab$, then
\[
(m)\subseteq(a)\subseteq R.
\]
Maximality gives $(a)=(m)$ or $(a)=R$, so respectively $b$ or $a$ is a unit. Thus $m$ is irreducible.
:::

<1>5. $\mathbb Z$ is Noetherian but not Artinian.
::: {.proof}
It is a PID, hence Noetherian. But
\[
(2)\supsetneq(4)\supsetneq(8)\supsetneq\cdots
\]
is an infinite strictly descending chain of ideals, so $\mathbb Z$ is not Artinian.
:::
:::
