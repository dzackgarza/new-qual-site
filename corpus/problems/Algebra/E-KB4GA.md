::: {.exercise}
Let $R$ be a Principal Ideal Domain (PID).
(1) Prove that every non-zero prime ideal in $R$ is maximal (so $\operatorname{Spec}(R) \setminus \{(0)\} = \operatorname{mSpec}(R)$).
(2) Prove that $R$ is Noetherian.
(3) Prove that every non-zero non-unit in $R$ factors into a finite product of irreducibles.
(4) Prove that every maximal ideal in $R$ is generated by an irreducible element.
(5) Prove that $\mathbb{Z}$ is Noetherian but not Artinian.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove the standard ideal-theoretic, factorization, and chain-condition properties of PIDs.

<1>1. Non-Zero Prime Ideals are Maximal:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\mathfrak{p} = (p) \subset R$ be a non-zero prime ideal ($p \ne 0$, $p \notin R^\times$).
    <2>2. Suppose there exists an ideal $I = (m) \subseteq R$ such that $(p) \subseteq (m) \subseteq R$.
    <2>3. Since $(p) \subseteq (m)$, $p \in (m)$, so $p = r m$ for some $r \in R$.
    <2>4. Since $\mathfrak{p} = (p)$ is a **prime ideal**, $r m = p \in \mathfrak{p} \implies r \in \mathfrak{p}$ or $m \in \mathfrak{p}$.
    <2>5. **Case 1 ($m \in \mathfrak{p}$):** Then $(m) \subseteq (p)$, so $(m) = (p)$.
    <2>6. **Case 2 ($r \in \mathfrak{p}$):** Then $r = s p$ for some $s \in R$.
        Substituting into $p = r m$: $p = s p m \implies p(1 - s m) = 0$.
        Since $R$ is an integral domain and $p \ne 0$, $1 - s m = 0 \implies s m = 1$, so $m \in R^\times$.
        Thus $(m) = R$.
    <2>7. Therefore, there are no proper ideals strictly containing $(p)$, so $(p)$ is a **maximal ideal**.

<1>2. PIDs are Noetherian:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. In a PID $R$, every ideal $I \subseteq R$ is generated by a single element ($I = (a)$).
    <2>2. Since every ideal is 1-generated, every ideal is **finitely generated** as an $R$-module.
    <2>3. A ring in which every ideal is finitely generated satisfies the Ascending Chain Condition (ACC) on ideals, so $R$ is **Noetherian**.

<1>3. Factorization into Irreducibles:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $a \in R \setminus (R^\times \cup \{0\})$.
    <2>2. By the ACC on principal ideals (since $R$ is Noetherian), the set of proper principal ideals containing $(a)$ has a maximal element $(p)$, where $p$ is irreducible. Thus $a$ has an irreducible divisor $p_1$.
    <2>3. Write $a = p_1 a_1$. If $a_1 \in R^\times$, we are done.
    <2>4. Otherwise, continue inductively to write $a = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_k a_k$, producing a strictly ascending chain of principal ideals:
        $$(a) \subsetneq (a_1) \subsetneq (a_2) \subsetneq \cdots.$$
    <2>5. By ACC, this chain must terminate at some finite step $n$, forcing $a_n \in R^\times$.
    <2>6. Absorbing the unit $a_n$ into $p_n$, we obtain a finite factorization $a = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_n$ into irreducibles.

<1>4. Maximal Ideals are Generated by Irreducibles:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\mathfrak{m} = (m)$ be a maximal ideal in $R$.
    <2>2. If $m = a b$, then $(m) \subseteq (a)$.
    <2>3. By maximality of $(m)$, either $(a) = (m)$ (so $b \in R^\times$) or $(a) = R$ (so $a \in R^\times$).
    <2>4. Thus $m$ cannot be factored into two non-units, so $m$ is **irreducible**.

<1>5. $\mathbb{Z}$ is Noetherian but Not Artinian:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $\mathbb{Z}$ is a PID, hence **Noetherian** by Step <1>2.
    <2>2. Consider the infinite descending chain of ideals for any integer $n \ge 2$ (e.g. $n = 2$):
        $$(2) \supsetneq (4) \supsetneq (8) \supsetneq (16) \supsetneq \cdots \supsetneq (2^k) \supsetneq \cdots$$
    <2>3. Since $2^k \nmid 2^{k-1}$, $(2^{k-1}) \ne (2^k)$ for all $k \ge 1$.
    <2>4. This descending chain of ideals never stabilizes, violating the Descending Chain Condition (DCC).
    <2>5. Therefore, $\mathbb{Z}$ is **not Artinian**.

<1>6. Conclusion:
    In a PID, non-zero primes are maximal and generated by irreducibles, ACC holds (Noetherian) yielding finite factorization, but DCC fails for $\mathbb{Z}$ (not Artinian). Q.E.D.
:::
