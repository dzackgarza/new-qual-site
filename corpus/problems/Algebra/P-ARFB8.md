::: {.problem}
Let $R=\mathbb C[x]$ and $M=\mathbb C^2$, with $R$-module structure defined by
\[
p(x)\cdot v=p(A)v,
\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Show that $M$ is a cyclic $R$-module generated by $e_1=(1,0)^t$, and show that $M$ is a torsion $R$-module. Determine its annihilator.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let \(e_1=(1,0)^t\). Since
\[
Ae_1=(1,2)^t,
\]
the vectors \(e_1,Ae_1\) are linearly independent. Hence they form a basis of \(\mathbb C^2\), so every vector has the form
\[
(s+tA)e_1
\]
for suitable \(s,t\in\mathbb C\). Thus \(M\) is cyclic as a \(\mathbb C[x]\)-module, generated by \(e_1\).

The characteristic polynomial of \(A\) is
\[
\chi_A(x)=x^2-2x-3=(x-3)(x+1).
\]
Because \(A\) has two distinct eigenvalues, its minimal polynomial is also \(x^2-2x-3\). Therefore
\[
(x^2-2x-3)(A)=0,
\]
so every element of \(M\) is annihilated by this nonzero polynomial. Hence \(M\) is torsion, and since \(e_1\) is cyclic,
\[
\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathbb C[x]}(M)=(x^2-2x-3).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
M\cong \mathbb C[x]/(x^2-2x-3).
\]
:::
