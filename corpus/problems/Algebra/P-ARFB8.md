::: {.problem}
Let $R=\CC[x]$ and $M=\CC^2$, with $R$-module structure defined by
$$
p(x)\cdot v=p(A)v,
\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Show that $M$ is a cyclic $R$-module generated by $e_1=(1,0)^t$, and show that $M$ is a torsion $R$-module. Determine its annihilator.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

$M$ is generated by $e_1$ as an $R$-module.

::: pf-proof

Since $Ae_1=(1,2)^t$, the vectors $e_1$ and $Ae_1$ are linearly independent, so they form a basis of $\CC^2$. Hence every $v\in M$ equals $(s+tA)e_1=(s+tx)\cdot e_1$ for some $s,t\in\CC$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #annihilated}

The polynomial $x^2-2x-3=(x-3)(x+1)$ annihilates $M$.

::: pf-proof

The characteristic polynomial of $A$ is $\chi_A(x)=(x-1)^2-4=x^2-2x-3$. By the Cayley--Hamilton theorem, $\chi_A(A)=0$, so $\chi_A\cdot v=\chi_A(A)v=0$ for every $v\in M$. Thus $M$ is torsion.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)=\boxed{(x^2-2x-3)}$, and $M\cong R/(x^2-2x-3)$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#annihilated){.pf-ref}, $(x^2-2x-3)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$. If $f\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ is nonzero of degree at most $1$, say $f=s+tx$, then $f\cdot e_1=se_1+tAe_1=0$ forces $s=t=0$ by the independence of $e_1$ and $Ae_1$. Since $R$ is a principal ideal domain, $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ is generated by a monic polynomial of degree at least $2$ dividing $x^2-2x-3$, which is $x^2-2x-3$ itself. The map $R\to M$, $f\mapsto f\cdot e_1$, is surjective with kernel $\operatorname{Ann}_R(e_1)=\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$, because $e_1$ generates $M$.

:::
