::: {.problem}
Classify all groups of order $99$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be a group of order $99=3^2\cdot11$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The Sylow $11$-subgroup $N$ is unique and normal.

::: pf-proof

Sylow gives $n_{11}\mid9$ and $n_{11}\equiv1\pmod{11}$. Among $1,3,9$, only $1$ satisfies the congruence. Thus $n_{11}=1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

If $P$ is a Sylow $3$-subgroup, then $G=NP$ and $N\cap P=1$.

::: pf-proof

The orders of $N$ and $P$ are $11$ and $9$, so their intersection is trivial. Since $N\trianglelefteq G$,
\[
|NP|=\frac{|N||P|}{|N\cap P|}=99=|G|.
\]
Hence $G=NP$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

The conjugation action of $P$ on $N$ is trivial.

::: pf-proof

Because $N\cong C_{11}$,
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(N)\cong C_{10}.
\]
Conjugation gives a homomorphism $P\to C_{10}$. Its image has order dividing both $9$ and $10$, hence has order $1$. Thus $P$ centralizes $N$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s4}

Therefore $G\cong N\times P$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s2){.pf-ref} the factors generate $G$ and intersect trivially, and by step [](#s3){.pf-ref} they commute elementwise.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

There are exactly two isomorphism types:
\[
C_{99}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
C_{11}\times C_3\times C_3.
\]

::: pf-proof

A group of order $9$ is isomorphic either to $C_9$ or to $C_3\times C_3$. Combining this with step [](#s4){.pf-ref} gives
\[
C_{11}\times C_9\cong C_{99}
\]
or
\[
C_{11}\times C_3\times C_3.
\]
These are not isomorphic because only the first has an element of order $9$.

:::
