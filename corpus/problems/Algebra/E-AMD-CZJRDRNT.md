::: {.exercise}
Show that $\QQ(2^{1\over 3})$ and $\QQ(\zeta_3 2^{1\over 3})$ are isomorphic fields, but not identical.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Let $\alpha = \sqrt[3]{2} \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\beta = \zeta_3 \sqrt[3]{2} \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{R}$, where $\zeta_3 = e^{2\pi i / 3}$.
Prove that the fields $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ and $K_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$ are isomorphic as fields over $\mathbb{Q}$ ($K_1 \cong K_2$), but are not equal ($K_1 \neq K_2$).

<1>1. Determination of minimal polynomials over $\mathbb{Q}$: <2>1. $\alpha = \sqrt[3]{2}$ satisfies $\alpha^3 - 2 = 0$.
::: {.proof}
By definition of the real cube root of 2. <2>2. $\beta = \zeta_3 \sqrt[3]{2}$ satisfies $\beta^3 - 2 = (\zeta_3 \sqrt[3]{2})^3 - 2 = \zeta_3^3 (\sqrt[3]{2})^3 - 2 = 1 \cdot 2 - 2 = 0$.
:::
::: {.proof}
Since $\zeta_3^3 = 1$.
:::
<2>3. The polynomial $f(x) = x^3 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's Criterion at $p = 2$.
::: {.proof}
$2$ divides the constant term $-2$, $2$ does not divide the leading coefficient $1$, and $4$ does not divide $-2$.
:::
<2>4. Therefore, $f(x) = x^3 - 2$ is the minimal polynomial of both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
::: {.proof}
$f(x)$ is a monic irreducible polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ having both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ as roots.
:::

<1>2. Proof that $K_1 \cong K_2$ (Field Isomorphism): <2>1. By the Fundamental Theorem of Simple Algebraic Extensions, for any root $\gamma$ of a monic irreducible polynomial $m(x) \in F[x]$, the evaluation map induces a field isomorphism $F[x]/\langle m(x) \rangle \cong F(\gamma)$.
::: {.proof}
Standard field theory result (kernel of evaluation $x \mapsto \gamma$ is the maximal ideal generated by the minimal polynomial).
:::
<2>2. For $\gamma = \alpha$, there is a $\mathbb{Q}$-isomorphism $\phi_1: \mathbb{Q}[x]/\langle x^3 - 2 \rangle \xrightarrow{\cong} \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ defined by $\phi_1(g(x) \bmod (x^3-2)) = g(\alpha)$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2.<2>1 and <1>1.<2>4. <2>3. For $\gamma = \beta$, there is a $\mathbb{Q}$-isomorphism $\phi_2: \mathbb{Q}[x]/\langle x^3 - 2 \rangle \xrightarrow{\cong} \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$ defined by $\phi_2(g(x) \bmod (x^3-2)) = g(\beta)$.
:::
::: {.proof}
By <1>2.<2>1 and <1>1.<2>4. <2>4. The composition $\psi = \phi_2 \circ \phi_1^{-1}: \mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \to \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$ is a field isomorphism.
:::
::: {.proof}
The composition of two field isomorphisms is a field isomorphism.
:::
<2>5. Under $\psi$, $\psi(\alpha) = \beta$ and $\psi|_F = \operatorname{id}_\mathbb{Q}$.
::: {.proof}
$\phi_1^{-1}(\alpha) = x \bmod (x^3-2)$, and $\phi_2(x \bmod (x^3-2)) = \beta$.
:::
<2>6. Therefore, $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$.
::: {.proof}
Follows from <2>4.
:::

<1>3. Proof that $K_1 \neq K_2$ (Distinct Subfields of $\mathbb{C}$): <2>1. Since $\alpha = \sqrt[3]{2} \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R}$, every element of $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha) = \{c_0 + c_1 \alpha + c_2 \alpha^2 \mid c_i \in \mathbb{Q}\}$ is a real number, so $K_1 \subset \mathbb{R}$.
::: {.proof}
Real numbers are closed under addition and multiplication.
:::
<2>2. The element $\beta = \zeta_3 \sqrt[3]{2} = \sqrt[3]{2} \left(-\frac{1}{2} + i \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = -\frac{\sqrt[3]{2}}{2} + i \frac{\sqrt[3]{2}\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
::: {.proof}
Definition of $\zeta_3 = e^{2\pi i / 3} = -\frac{1}{2} + i\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
:::
<2>3. The imaginary part $\operatorname{Im}(\beta) = \frac{\sqrt[3]{2}\sqrt{3}}{2} > 0 \neq 0$, so $\beta \notin \mathbb{R}$.
::: {.proof}
Product of positive real numbers is strictly positive.
:::
<2>4. Since $\beta \in K_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$ and $\beta \notin \mathbb{R}$, $K_2 \not\subseteq \mathbb{R}$.
::: {.proof}
$K_2$ contains a non-real element.
:::
<2>5. Since $K_1 \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ and $K_2 \not\subseteq \mathbb{R}$, the sets cannot be equal: $K_1 \neq K_2$.
::: {.proof}
$\beta \in K_2 \setminus K_1$.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3 \sqrt[3]{2})$ are isomorphic fields, but are distinct as subfields of $\mathbb{C}$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>3.
:::
:::
