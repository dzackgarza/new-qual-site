::: problem
Let $G$ be a group, and let $H, K \le G$ be subgroups of $G$.
Prove that the set product $H K = \{h k \mid h \in H, \; k \in K\}$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $H K = K H$.
:::

::: solution
For subgroups \(H,K\le G\),
\[
(HK)^{-1}=K^{-1}H^{-1}=KH.
\]
Hence if \(HK\) is a subgroup, then it is closed under inverses and therefore
\[
HK=(HK)^{-1}=KH.
\]

Conversely, suppose \(HK=KH\). Then \(e\in HK\), and
\[
(HK)(HK)=H(KH)K=H(HK)K=(HH)(KK)=HK,
\]
so \(HK\) is closed under multiplication. Also
\[
(HK)^{-1}=KH=HK,
\]
so it is closed under inverses. Therefore \(HK\le G\).
:::
