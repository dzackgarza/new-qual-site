::: problem
Let $G$ be a group, and let $H, K \le G$ be subgroups of $G$.
Prove that the set product $H K = \{h k \mid h \in H, \; k \in K\}$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $H K = K H$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove the equivalence: $H K \le G \iff H K = K H$.

<1>1. Subgroup Inverse Criterion:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For any subsets $A, B \subseteq G$, define the inverse set $A^{-1} = \{a^{-1} \mid a \in A\}$.
    <2>2. For any subgroup $S \le G$, $S^{-1} = S$ and $S S = S$.
    <2>3. In particular, for the subgroups $H$ and $K$:
        $$H^{-1} = H, \qquad K^{-1} = K.$$
    <2>4. The inverse of the product set $H K$ is:
        $$(H K)^{-1} = K^{-1} H^{-1} = K H.$$

<1>2. Direction $(\implies)$: $H K \le G \implies H K = K H$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose $H K$ is a subgroup of $G$.
    <2>2. Since $H K$ is a subgroup, it is closed under taking inverses:
        $$(H K)^{-1} = H K.$$
    <2>3. By Step <1>1, $(H K)^{-1} = K H$.
    <2>4. Therefore:
        $$K H = H K.$$

<1>3. Direction $(\impliedby)$: $H K = K H \implies H K \le G$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose $H K = K H$. We verify the two-step subgroup criterion for $H K$:
    <2>2. **Non-empty:** Since $e \in H$ and $e \in K$, $e = e \cdot e \in H K$.
    <2>3. **Closure under multiplication:**
        $$(H K)(H K) = H (K H) K = H (H K) K = (H H)(K K) = H K.$$
        Thus $H K$ is closed under multiplication.
    <2>4. **Closure under inverses:**
        $$(H K)^{-1} = K^{-1} H^{-1} = K H = H K.$$
        Thus $H K$ is closed under inverses.
    <2>5. Therefore, $H K$ is a subgroup of $G$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $H K$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $H K = K H$. Q.E.D.
:::
