::: {.problem}
Let $G$ be a group, and let $H, K \le G$ be subgroups of $G$.
Prove that the set product $H K = \{h k \mid h \in H, \; k \in K\}$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $H K = K H$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #inverse-set}
$(HK)^{-1}=KH$.

::: pf-proof
$(hk)^{-1}=k^{-1}h^{-1}$, and $H^{-1}=H$, $K^{-1}=K$ because $H$ and $K$ are subgroups.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $HK\le G$, then $HK=KH$.

::: pf-proof
A subgroup is closed under inverses, so $HK=(HK)^{-1}=KH$ by step [](#inverse-set){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $HK=KH$, then $HK\le G$.

::: pf-proof
$e=ee\in HK$. The set $HK$ is closed under multiplication because
$$
(HK)(HK)=H(KH)K=H(HK)K=(HH)(KK)=HK,
$$
and closed under inverses because $(HK)^{-1}=KH=HK$ by step [](#inverse-set){.pf-ref}.
:::
