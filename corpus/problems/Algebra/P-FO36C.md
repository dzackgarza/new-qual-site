::: {.problem}
Define lower central series, upper central series, nilpotent and solvable groups.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be a group.

::: pf

::: pf-step
Recall that the [[D-D7L4X|lower central series]] of $G$ is $\gamma_1(G)=G$, $\gamma_{i+1}(G)=[\gamma_i(G),G]$, and the [[D-XEDSI|upper central series]] is $Z_0(G)=1$, $Z_{i+1}(G)/Z_i(G)=Z\bigl(G/Z_i(G)\bigr)$.

::: pf-proof
Each $\gamma_{i+1}(G)$ is normal in $G$ because it is generated by commutators of a normal subgroup with $G$, and each $Z_{i+1}(G)$ is the preimage of a characteristic subgroup of $G/Z_i(G)$, so both series are well defined.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$G$ is [[D-53JVH|nilpotent]] if $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=1$ for some $c\ge0$; equivalently, $Z_c(G)=G$ for some $c$.
The least such $c$ is the nilpotency class.

::: pf-proof
The equivalence is the standard comparison of the two series: $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=1$ if and only if $Z_c(G)=G$, for each $c$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Recall that the [[D-W2QAA|derived series]] is $G^{(0)}=G$, $G^{(i+1)}=[G^{(i)},G^{(i)}]$, and $G$ is [[D-DFIDP|solvable]] if $G^{(r)}=1$ for some $r$.

::: pf-proof
This is the definition; each $G^{(i+1)}$ is normal in $G^{(i)}$ with abelian quotient.
:::
