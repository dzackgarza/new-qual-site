::: problem
necessarily over $E ?$ Answer this question for each of the words "algebraic," "normal," and "separable" in the blanks.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Algebraic: If $K/F$ is algebraic, then $K/E$ is algebraic:
<2>1. Let $\alpha \in K$. Since $K/F$ is algebraic, there exists a non-zero polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$ such that $p(\alpha) = 0$.
::: {.proof}
definition of algebraic extension.
:::
<2>2. Since $F \subseteq E$, we have $F[x] \subseteq E[x]$, so $p(x)$ is also a non-zero polynomial in $E[x]$ satisfying $p(\alpha) = 0$.
Thus $\alpha$ is algebraic over $E$.
Since this holds for every $\alpha \in K$, $K/E$ is algebraic.
::: {.proof}
definition of algebraic element over $E$.
:::

<1>2. Normal: If $K/F$ is normal, then $K/E$ is normal:
<2>1. Let $K/F$ be a normal algebraic extension. Then $K$ is the splitting field of a family of polynomials $\mathcal{F} \subseteq F[x]$ over $F$.
::: {.proof}
characterization of normal algebraic extensions as splitting fields.
:::
<2>2. Because $F[x] \subseteq E[x]$, the family $\mathcal{F}$ is also a family of polynomials with coefficients in $E$.
Since $K$ is generated over $F$ by the roots of $\mathcal{F}$ and $F \subseteq E$, $K$ is generated over $E$ by the roots of $\mathcal{F}$.
Thus $K$ is the splitting field of $\mathcal{F}$ over $E$, which implies $K/E$ is a normal extension.
::: {.proof}
splitting field over a base field is normal.
:::

<1>3. Separable: If $K/F$ is separable, then $K/E$ is separable:
<2>1. Let $\alpha \in K$. Let $m_{\alpha, F}(x) \in F[x]$ and $m_{\alpha, E}(x) \in E[x]$ be the minimal polynomials of $\alpha$ over $F$ and $E$, respectively.
::: {.proof}
existence of minimal polynomials.
:::
<2>2. Because $m_{\alpha, F}(x) \in E[x]$ and $m_{\alpha, F}(\alpha) = 0$, by the definition of minimal polynomials $m_{\alpha, E}(x)$ divides $m_{\alpha, F}(x)$ in $E[x]$.
::: {.proof}
divisibility property of minimal polynomials.
:::
<2>3. Since $K/F$ is separable, $m_{\alpha, F}(x)$ has only simple roots in an algebraic closure $\overline{F}$.
Since $m_{\alpha, E}(x) \mid m_{\alpha, F}(x)$, every root of $m_{\alpha, E}(x)$ is a root of $m_{\alpha, F}(x)$, so $m_{\alpha, E}(x)$ has only simple roots.
Thus $\alpha$ is separable over $E$.
Since this holds for all $\alpha \in K$, $K/E$ is separable.
::: {.proof}
separability criterion via distinct roots of minimal polynomials.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
All three properties ("algebraic", "normal", and "separable") are preserved: if $K/F$ is algebraic, normal, or separable, then $K/E$ is necessarily algebraic, normal, or separable, respectively. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>3.
:::
