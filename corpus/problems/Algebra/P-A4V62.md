::: {.problem}
necessarily over $E ?$ Answer this question for each of the words "algebraic," "normal," and "separable" in the blanks.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $F\subseteq E\subseteq K$ be fields. Each of the three properties passes from $K/F$ to $K/E$.

<1>1. If $K/F$ is algebraic, then $K/E$ is algebraic.

::: {.proof}
For $\alpha\in K$, a nonzero $p\in F[x]$ with $p(\alpha)=0$ also lies in $E[x]$.
:::

<1>2. If $K/F$ is normal, then $K/E$ is normal.

::: {.proof}
$K$ is the splitting field over $F$ of a family $\mathcal F\subseteq F[x]$.
Then $\mathcal F\subseteq E[x]$, its roots lie in $K$, and they generate $K$ over $F$, hence over $E\supseteq F$; so $K$ is the splitting field of $\mathcal F$ over $E$.
:::

<1>3. If $K/F$ is separable, then $K/E$ is separable.

::: {.proof}
For $\alpha\in K$, the minimal polynomial $m_{\alpha,E}$ divides $m_{\alpha,F}$ in $E[x]$, since $m_{\alpha,F}\in E[x]$ kills $\alpha$.
As $m_{\alpha,F}$ has distinct roots, so does its divisor $m_{\alpha,E}$.
:::
:::
