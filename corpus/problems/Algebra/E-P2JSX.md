::: problem
13. Characterize the splitting field and all intermediate fields of the polynomial $\left(x^{2}-2\right)\left(x^{2}-3\right)\left(x^{2}-5\right)$ over $\QQ$.
    Using this characterization, find a primitive element of the splitting field.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
f(x)=(x^2-2)(x^2-3)(x^2-5).
\]

<1>1. The splitting field is
\[
L=\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt5).
\]
::: {.proof}
The roots of $f$ are
\[
\pm\sqrt2,\qquad \pm\sqrt3,\qquad \pm\sqrt5.
\]
Thus every splitting field contains $\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt5$, while the field generated by those three elements contains all six roots. Hence it is exactly the splitting field.
:::

<1>2. One has
\[
[L:\QQ]=8
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Gal(L/\QQ)\cong (C_2)^3.
\]
::: {.proof}
First $\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\QQ$ has degree $4$: $\sqrt3\notin\QQ(\sqrt2)$, since an equality $(a+b\sqrt2)^2=3$ with $a,b\in\QQ$ forces $2ab=0$ and then gives either $a^2=3$ or $2b^2=3$, both impossible in $\QQ$.

Set $E=\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. The extension $E/\QQ$ is biquadratic with Galois group $(C_2)^2$, so its only quadratic subfields are
\[
\QQ(\sqrt2),\qquad \QQ(\sqrt3),\qquad \QQ(\sqrt6).
\]
The field $\QQ(\sqrt5)$ is distinct from all three, so $\sqrt5\notin E$. Hence
\[
[L:\QQ]=[L:E][E:\QQ]=2\cdot4=8.
\]

Each independent choice of signs on $\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt5$ defines a $\QQ$-automorphism of $L$, giving eight automorphisms. Thus
\[
\Gal(L/\QQ)\cong (C_2)^3.
\]
:::

<1>3. The seven quadratic intermediate fields are
\[
\QQ(\sqrt2),\ \QQ(\sqrt3),\ \QQ(\sqrt5),\ \QQ(\sqrt6),\ \QQ(\sqrt{10}),\ \QQ(\sqrt{15}),\ \QQ(\sqrt{30}).
\]
::: {.proof}
The index-$2$ subgroups of $(C_2)^3$ correspond to the nonzero linear functionals on the $3$-dimensional $\FF_2$-vector space, hence there are seven. Equivalently, every nontrivial squareclass generated by $2,3,5$ gives one quadratic field. The seven nonzero products are
\[
2,3,5,6,10,15,30,
\]
which gives exactly the displayed list.
:::

<1>4. The seven quartic intermediate fields are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3),\qquad
\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt5),\qquad
\QQ(\sqrt3,\sqrt5),\\
&\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt{15}),\qquad
\QQ(\sqrt3,\sqrt{10}),\qquad
\QQ(\sqrt5,\sqrt6),\\
&\QQ(\sqrt6,\sqrt{10}).
\end{aligned}
\]
::: {.proof}
Quartic intermediate fields correspond to subgroups of order $2$ in $G=(C_2)^3$. For a nonempty subset $S\subseteq\{2,3,5\}$, let $\sigma_S$ change the sign of $\sqrt d$ exactly for $d\in S$. The seven nontrivial order-$2$ subgroups are $\langle\sigma_S\rangle$, and their fixed fields are respectively
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
S & L^{\langle\sigma_S\rangle}\\ \hline
\{2\} & \QQ(\sqrt3,\sqrt5)\\
\{3\} & \QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt5)\\
\{5\} & \QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\\
\{2,3\} & \QQ(\sqrt6,\sqrt5)\\
\{2,5\} & \QQ(\sqrt{10},\sqrt3)\\
\{3,5\} & \QQ(\sqrt{15},\sqrt2)\\
\{2,3,5\} & \QQ(\sqrt6,\sqrt{10}).
\end{array}
\]
Each displayed field has degree $4$ over $\QQ$, so these are exactly the fixed fields.
:::

<1>5. Together with $\QQ$ and $L$, the fields in <1>3 and <1>4 are all intermediate fields.
::: {.proof}
The group $(C_2)^3$ has
\[
1+7+7+1=16
\]
subgroups: one of dimension $0$, seven of dimension $1$, seven of dimension $2$, and one of dimension $3$ over $\FF_2$. By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, these correspond bijectively to intermediate fields. The fields already listed account for all sixteen.
:::

<1>6. A primitive element is
\[
\alpha=\sqrt2+\sqrt3+\sqrt5.
\]
::: {.proof}
Every $\sigma\in G$ changes signs independently. If $\sigma\neq1$, it changes at least one sign, and therefore
\[
\alpha-\sigma(\alpha)
=2\sum_{d\in S}\sqrt d
\]
for some nonempty $S\subseteq\{2,3,5\}$. Under the standard real embedding every term in this sum is positive, so the difference is nonzero. Hence the stabilizer of $\alpha$ in $G$ is trivial.

By Galois theory,
\[
[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=|G|=8.
\]
Since $\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq L$ and $[L:\QQ]=8$, one gets
\[
L=\QQ(\alpha).
\]
Thus $\alpha$ is a primitive element of the splitting field.
:::
