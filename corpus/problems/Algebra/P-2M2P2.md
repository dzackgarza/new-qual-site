::: {.problem}
- Let $P\in \Syl_p(G)$ and let $H\normal G$. Show that $P\intersect H \in \Syl_p(H)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
|P|=p^a,
\qquad
|H|=p^b m,
\qquad p\nmid m.
\]
Since $P$ is Sylow in $G$, $p^a$ is the largest power of $p$ dividing $|G|$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

The product $PH$ is a subgroup of $G$.

::: pf-proof

Because $H\trianglelefteq G$, the product of the subgroup $P$ with the normal subgroup $H$ is a subgroup.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

Write $|P\cap H|=p^c$. Then $c\ge b$.

::: pf-proof

By the product formula,
\[
|PH|=\frac{|P||H|}{|P\cap H|}
=p^{a+b-c}m.
\]
Since $PH\le G$, the $p$-part of $|PH|$ cannot exceed the $p$-part $p^a$ of $|G|$. Therefore
\[
a+b-c\le a,
\]
so $c\ge b$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

One also has $c\le b$.

::: pf-proof

The intersection $P\cap H$ is a $p$-subgroup of $H$. Since $p^b$ is the largest power of $p$ dividing $|H|$, Lagrange's theorem gives
\[
|P\cap H|=p^c\le p^b.
\]
Thus $c\le b$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $P\cap H$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$.

::: pf-proof

By steps [](#s2){.pf-ref} and [](#s3){.pf-ref}, $c=b$. Therefore
\[
|P\cap H|=p^b,
\]
which is the full $p$-part of $|H|$. Hence $P\cap H\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(H)$.

:::
