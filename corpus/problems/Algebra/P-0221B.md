::: problem
Let $g\in F[x]$ be a separable polynomial, and let $E/F$ be its splitting field. Prove that $E/F$ is Galois.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. The extension $E/F$ is finite and normal.
::: {.proof}
The field $E$ is generated over $F$ by the finitely many roots of $g$, each of which is algebraic over $F$, so $E/F$ is finite. Since $E$ is the splitting field of a polynomial over $F$, it is normal over $F$.
:::

<1>2. Every root of $g$ is separable over $F$.
::: {.proof}
Because $g$ is separable, it has no repeated roots in an algebraic closure. If $\alpha$ is a root and $m_\alpha\in F[x]$ is its minimal polynomial, then $m_\alpha$ divides $g$. Hence $m_\alpha$ also has no repeated roots, so $\alpha$ is separable over $F$.
:::

<1>3. The extension $E/F$ is separable.
::: {.proof}
The splitting field $E$ is generated by the roots of $g$, and by <1>2 each of those generators is separable over $F$. A finite extension generated by separable elements is separable.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $E/F$ is Galois.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $E/F$ is finite and normal, and by <1>3 it is separable. Hence it is Galois.
:::
:::
