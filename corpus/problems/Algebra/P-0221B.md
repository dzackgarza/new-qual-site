::: {.problem}
Let $g\in F[x]$ be a separable polynomial, and let $E/F$ be its splitting field. Prove that $E/F$ is Galois.
:::


::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #e-f-finite-normal}
The extension $E/F$ is finite and normal.

::: pf-proof
The field $E$ is generated over $F$ by the finitely many roots of $g$, each of which is algebraic over $F$, so $E/F$ is finite. Since $E$ is the splitting field of a polynomial over $F$, it is normal over $F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #g-roots-separable}
Every root of $g$ is separable over $F$.

::: pf-proof
Because $g$ is separable, it has no repeated roots in an algebraic closure. If $\alpha$ is a root and $m_\alpha\in F[x]$ is its minimal polynomial, then $m_\alpha$ divides $g$. Hence $m_\alpha$ also has no repeated roots, so $\alpha$ is separable over $F$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #e-f-separable}
The extension $E/F$ is separable.

::: pf-proof
The splitting field $E$ is generated by the roots of $g$, and by step [](#g-roots-separable){.pf-ref} each of those generators is separable over $F$. A finite extension generated by separable elements is separable.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
Therefore $E/F$ is Galois.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#e-f-finite-normal){.pf-ref}, $E/F$ is finite and normal, and by step [](#e-f-separable){.pf-ref} it is separable. Hence it is Galois.
:::
