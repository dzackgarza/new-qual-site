::: problem
Do polynomial rings in several variables $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ over a field $k$ form a Principal Ideal Domain (PID)?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ is a PID if and only if $n \le 1$ (for $n \ge 2$, it is never a PID).

<1>1. Case $n = 0$ and $n = 1$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For $n = 0$, $k$ is a field, which has only two ideals: $(0)$ and $(1) = k$. Both are principal, so $k$ is a PID (and a field).
    <2>2. For $n = 1$, $k[x_1]$ is a Euclidean Domain with Euclidean degree function $d(f) = \deg(f)$.
    <2>3. Because every Euclidean Domain is a PID, $k[x_1]$ is a PID.

<1>2. Case $n \ge 2$: $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ is NOT a PID:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $n \ge 2$. Consider the ideal generated by two distinct variables:
        $$I = (x_1, x_2) = \{f(x_1, \dots, x_n) x_1 + g(x_1, \dots, x_n) x_2 \mid f, g \in k[x_1, \dots, x_n]\}.$$
    <2>2. Geometrically/algebraically, $I$ consists of all polynomials in $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ with constant term zero: $I = \{P \in k[x_1, \dots, x_n] \mid P(0, 0, \dots, 0) = 0\}$.
    <2>3. Suppose, for contradiction, that $I$ is a principal ideal: $I = (d)$ for some polynomial $d \in k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$.
    <2>4. **$d$ divides the generators:**
        - Since $x_1 \in I = (d)$, $d$ must divide $x_1$. Since $x_1$ is irreducible in $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, $d$ must be an associate of $x_1$ or a non-zero constant in $k^\times$.
        - Since $x_2 \in I = (d)$, $d$ must divide $x_2$. Since $x_2$ is irreducible and not associate to $x_1$, $d$ cannot be an associate of $x_1$.
        - Therefore, $d$ must be a unit (constant $c \in k^\times$).
    <2>5. **Contradiction with properness:**
        - If $d \in k^\times$, then $I = (d) = (1) = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$.
        - But $1 \in I \implies 1 = f(x_1, \dots) x_1 + g(x_1, \dots) x_2$.
        - Evaluating at $x_1 = x_2 = \cdots = x_n = 0$ gives $1 = 0$, a contradiction.
    <2>6. Thus $(x_1, x_2)$ cannot be generated by a single element.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    Polynomials in several variables ($n \ge 2$) do not form a PID: $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ is a PID if and only if $n \le 1$. Q.E.D.
:::
