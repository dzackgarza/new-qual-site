::: problem
- Show that the order of $1+p$ in $\qty{\ZZ/p^2\ZZ}\units$ is equal to $p$.
  Use this to construct a non-abelian group of order $p^3$.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. The class of $1+p$ in $(\ZZ/p^2\ZZ)^\times$ has order $p$.
::: {.proof}
For $1\le j<p$, the binomial theorem gives
\[
(1+p)^j=1+jp+\binom j2p^2+\cdots\equiv1+jp\pmod{p^2}.
\]
Since $p\nmid j$, the residue $jp$ is not divisible by $p^2$, so $(1+p)^j\not\equiv1\pmod{p^2}$. On the other hand,
\[
(1+p)^p\equiv1+p^2\equiv1\pmod{p^2};
\]
for $p=2$ this is the same computation, $(1+2)^2\equiv1\pmod4$. Hence the order is exactly $p$.
:::

<1>2. Multiplication by $1+p$ defines an automorphism $\varphi$ of the cyclic group $C_{p^2}$ of order $p$.
::: {.proof}
Identify $C_{p^2}$ with the additive group $\ZZ/p^2\ZZ$. Multiplication by the unit $1+p$ is an automorphism. Its order as an automorphism is the multiplicative order of $1+p$ in $(\ZZ/p^2\ZZ)^\times$, which is $p$ by <1>1.
:::

<1>3. Therefore the semidirect product
\[
G=C_{p^2}\rtimes_{\varphi} C_p
\]
has order $p^3$ and is nonabelian.
::: {.proof}
The order is
\[
|G|=p^2p=p^3.
\]
Let $a$ generate $C_{p^2}$ and $b$ generate $C_p$. The defining action gives
\[
bab^{-1}=a^{1+p}.
\]
Since $a^p\ne1$ in $C_{p^2}$, one has $a^{1+p}\ne a$. Thus $a$ and $b$ do not commute, so $G$ is nonabelian.
:::
:::
