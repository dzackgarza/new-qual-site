::: problem
What is the structure of the multiplicative group $(\mathbb{F}_9)^\times$?
Construct $\mathbb{F}_9$ as a quotient ring and find an explicit primitive generator of $(\mathbb{F}_9)^\times$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Determine the structure of $(\mathbb{F}_9)^\times$ and find an explicit generator.

<1>1. General Theorem on Multiplicative Groups of Finite Fields:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Theorem:** The multiplicative group of non-zero elements in any finite field $\mathbb{F}_q^\times$ is cyclic:
        $$\mathbb{F}_q^\times \cong \mathbb{Z}_{q-1}.$$
    <2>2. For $q = 9 = 3^2$, the group has order $|\mathbb{F}_9^\times| = 9 - 1 = 8$.
    <2>3. Therefore, as an abstract group:
        $$(\mathbb{F}_9)^\times \cong \mathbb{Z}_8.$$

<1>2. Field Construction:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The polynomial $p(x) = x^2 + 1 \in \mathbb{F}_3[x]$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_3$ since $p(0) = 1 \ne 0$, $p(1) = 2 \ne 0$, and $p(2) = 5 \equiv 2 \ne 0$.
    <2>2. We construct the finite field of 9 elements as:
        $$\mathbb{F}_9 \cong \mathbb{F}_3[x]/(x^2 + 1) = \{a + bi \mid a, b \in \mathbb{F}_3, \; i^2 = -1 = 2\}.$$

<1>3. Finding an Explicit Generator (Primitive Element):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The elements of $(\mathbb{F}_9)^\times$ have orders dividing 8 (i.e. $1, 2, 4, 8$).
    <2>2. Consider $\alpha = 1 + i \in \mathbb{F}_9$:
        $$\begin{aligned}
        \alpha^1 &= 1 + i, \\
        \alpha^2 &= (1 + i)^2 = 1 + 2i + i^2 = 1 + 2i + 2 = 3 + 2i \equiv 2i = -i, \\
        \alpha^3 &= \alpha^2 \cdot \alpha = (2i)(1 + i) = 2i + 2i^2 = 2i + 4 \equiv 1 + 2i, \\
        \alpha^4 &= (\alpha^2)^2 = (2i)^2 = 4i^2 = 4(2) = 8 \equiv 2 = -1, \\
        \alpha^5 &= -\alpha = 2 + 2i, \\
        \alpha^6 &= -\alpha^2 = i, \\
        \alpha^7 &= -\alpha^3 = 2 + i, \\
        \alpha^8 &= (-\alpha^4) = (-1)^2 = 1.
        \end{aligned}$$
    <2>3. Since $\alpha^4 = 2 \ne 1$ and $\alpha^8 = 1$, the order of $\alpha = 1 + i$ is exactly 8.
    <2>4. Thus $\alpha = 1 + i$ is a primitive root generating $(\mathbb{F}_9)^\times = \langle 1 + i \rangle$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $(\mathbb{F}_9)^\times$ is a cyclic group of order 8 ($\mathbb{Z}_8$), generated by $1 + i$. Q.E.D.
:::
