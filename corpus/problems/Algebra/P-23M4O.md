::: problem
Let $R=\ZZ[x]$ and let $J=(2,x)\trianglelefteq R$. Prove that the $R$-module $J$ is not a direct sum of cyclic $R$-modules.
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. The ideal $J=(2,x)$ is not principal.
::: {.proof}
Suppose $J=(f)$ for some $f\in\ZZ[x]$. Since $2\in(f)$, the polynomial $f$ divides the constant polynomial $2$. Hence $f$ is a nonzero constant integer. Since also $x\in(f)$, this integer divides the polynomial $x$, so it must be a unit $\pm1$. Thus $(f)=R$.

But $J$ is proper: the quotient map
\[
\ZZ[x]\longrightarrow\FF_2,
\qquad
h(x)\longmapsto h(0)\bmod2
\]
has kernel $(2,x)$. Hence $J\ne R$, a contradiction. Therefore $J$ is not principal.
:::

<1>2. Any two nonzero cyclic $R$-submodules of $J$ have nonzero intersection.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
M=R\alpha,
\qquad
N=R\beta
\]
with $0\ne\alpha,\beta\in J$. Since $R=\ZZ[x]$ is an integral domain,
\[
\alpha\beta\ne0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\alpha\beta\in R\alpha=M
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\alpha\beta\in R\beta=N.
\]
Thus
\[
0\ne\alpha\beta\in M\cap N.
\]
:::

<1>3. In any internal direct-sum decomposition of $J$ into cyclic submodules, at most one summand can be nonzero.
::: {.proof}
Suppose
\[
J=\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i
\]
with each $M_i$ cyclic. Distinct summands in an internal direct sum have zero intersection. By <1>2, two distinct nonzero cyclic summands would have nonzero intersection. Hence at most one $M_i$ is nonzero.
:::

<1>4. No such direct-sum decomposition exists.
::: {.proof}
The module $J$ is nonzero, so in a decomposition as in <1>3 exactly one summand would be nonzero. Then $J$ itself would be cyclic as an $R$-module. Since $J$ is an ideal of $R$, a cyclic $R$-module structure would mean
\[
J=R\alpha=(\alpha)
\]
for some $\alpha\in R$, making $J$ principal. This contradicts <1>1.
:::
:::
