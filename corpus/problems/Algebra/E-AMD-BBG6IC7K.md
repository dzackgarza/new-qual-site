::: {.exercise}
Show that if $R$ is Noetherian then every ideal is finitely generated.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Let $R$ be a ring satisfying the Ascending Chain Condition (ACC) on ideals (i.e., every strictly ascending chain of ideals terminates).
Prove that every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated.

<1>1. Definitions: <2>1. A ring $R$ is defined to be Noetherian if it satisfies the Ascending Chain Condition (ACC) on ideals: whenever $I_1 \subseteq I_2 \subseteq I_3 \subseteq \dots$ is an ascending chain of ideals of $R$, there exists an integer $N \ge 1$ such that $I_n = I_N$ for all $n \ge N$.
::: {.proof}
Standard definition of ACC. <2>2. An ideal $I \subseteq R$ is finitely generated if there exists a finite subset $\{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k\} \subseteq I$ such that $I = \langle x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k \rangle$.
:::
::: {.proof}
Standard definition of finitely generated ideal.
:::

<1>2. Proof by contrapositive / contradiction: <2>1. Let $I$ be an ideal of $R$.
Suppose for contradiction that $I$ is not finitely generated.
::: {.proof}
Setting an arbitrary ideal and assuming it is not finitely generated.
:::
<2>2. Inductive construction of a strictly ascending chain of ideals: <3>1. Since $I$ is not finitely generated, $I \neq (0)$ (as $(0) = \langle 0 \rangle$ is generated by the empty set/zero).
Thus, choose an element $x_1 \in I$.
::: {.proof}
$I$ contains at least one non-zero element.
:::
<3>2. Define $I_1 = \langle x_1 \rangle$.
::: {.proof}
Principal ideal generated by $x_1$.
:::
<3>3. $I_1 \subsetneq I$.
::: {.proof}
If $I_1 = I$, then $I = \langle x_1 \rangle$ is finitely generated by $1$ element, contradicting that $I$ is not finitely generated.
:::
<3>4. Inductive step: Suppose we have chosen $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k \in I$ such that the ideal $I_k = \langle x_1, \dots, x_k \rangle \subsetneq I$.
Then there exists an element $x_{k+1} \in I \setminus I_k$.
Define $I_{k+1} = \langle x_1, \dots, x_k, x_{k+1} \rangle$.
Then $I_k \subsetneq I_{k+1} \subseteq I$, and $I_{k+1} \subsetneq I$ because if $I_{k+1} = I$, then $I$ would be finitely generated by $\{x_1, \dots, x_{k+1}\}$.
::: {.proof}
By the Principle of Mathematical Induction / Axiom of Dependent Choice.
:::
<2>3. The sequence of ideals $I_1 \subsetneq I_2 \subsetneq I_3 \subsetneq \dots$ is an infinite strictly ascending chain of ideals of $R$.
::: {.proof}
For every $k \ge 1$, $I_k \subsetneq I_{k+1}$ because $x_{k+1} \in I_{k+1} \setminus I_k$.
:::
<2>4. Contradiction: $R$ satisfies the Ascending Chain Condition, so no infinite strictly ascending chain of ideals can exist.
::: {.proof}
Contradicts <1>1.<2>1. <2>5. Therefore, the assumption was false, so $I$ must be finitely generated.
:::
::: {.proof}
By contradiction from <2>1 through <2>4.
:::

<1>3. Conclusion: In any Noetherian ring $R$, every ideal is finitely generated.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2.
:::
:::
