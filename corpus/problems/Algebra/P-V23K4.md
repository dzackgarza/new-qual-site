::: problem
Show that if $E/k$ and $F/k$ are finite Galois extensions inside a common field with $E \cap F = k$, then the compositum $EF/k$ is Galois and:
$$\operatorname{Gal}(EF/k) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(E/k) \times \operatorname{Gal}(F/k).$$
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that $EF/k$ is Galois with $\operatorname{Gal}(EF/k) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(E/k) \times \operatorname{Gal}(F/k)$ when $E/k, F/k$ are Galois and $E \cap F = k$.

<1>1. $EF/k$ is a Galois extension:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $E/k$ and $F/k$ are finite Galois extensions, $E$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial $f(x) \in k[x]$ and $F$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial $g(x) \in k[x]$.
    <2>2. The compositum $EF$ is generated over $k$ by the roots of $f(x)$ and the roots of $g(x)$.
    <2>3. Thus $EF$ is the splitting field of $f(x)g(x)$ over $k$.
    <2>4. Since $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are separable over $k$, the splitting field $EF/k$ is normal and separable, hence Galois.

<1>2. Construction of the restriction homomorphism:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Define the map $\Phi: \operatorname{Gal}(EF/k) \to \operatorname{Gal}(E/k) \times \operatorname{Gal}(F/k)$ by:
        $$\Phi(\sigma) = (\sigma|_E, \, \sigma|_F).$$
    <2>2. **Well-defined:** Since $E/k$ and $F/k$ are normal extensions, for every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(EF/k)$, we have $\sigma(E) = E$ and $\sigma(F) = F$. Thus $\sigma|_E \in \operatorname{Gal}(E/k)$ and $\sigma|_F \in \operatorname{Gal}(F/k)$.
    <2>3. **Homomorphism:** For $\sigma, \tau \in \operatorname{Gal}(EF/k)$:
        $$\Phi(\sigma \tau) = ((\sigma \tau)|_E, (\sigma \tau)|_F) = (\sigma|_E \circ \tau|_E, \sigma|_F \circ \tau|_F) = \Phi(\sigma) \Phi(\tau).$$

<1>3. Computation of the kernel ($\Phi$ is injective):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $\sigma \in \ker\Phi \iff \sigma|_E = \operatorname{id}_E$ and $\sigma|_F = \operatorname{id}_F$.
    <2>2. This means $\sigma(x) = x$ for all $x \in E$ and $\sigma(y) = y$ for all $y \in F$.
    <2>3. Since $EF$ is generated as a ring/field by $E$ and $F$ (every element of $EF$ is a linear combination/rational function of elements from $E$ and $F$), $\sigma$ fixes every generator of $EF$.
    <2>4. Thus $\sigma = \operatorname{id}_{EF}$, so $\ker\Phi = \{\operatorname{id}_{EF}\}$, which proves $\Phi$ is injective.

<1>4. Surjectivity via degree counting and the intersection hypothesis:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory applied to $E \subseteq EF$:
        $$\operatorname{Gal}(EF/E) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(F / (E \cap F)).$$
    <2>2. Since $E \cap F = k$, we have $\operatorname{Gal}(EF/E) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(F/k)$, so $[EF : E] = [F : k]$.
    <2>3. By the tower law:
        $$[EF : k] = [EF : E] \cdot [E : k] = [F : k] \cdot [E : k].$$
    <2>4. Therefore:
        $$|\operatorname{Gal}(EF/k)| = [EF : k] = [E : k][F : k] = |\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)| \cdot |\operatorname{Gal}(F/k)| = |\operatorname{Gal}(E/k) \times \operatorname{Gal}(F/k)|.$$
    <2>5. Since $\Phi$ is an injective homomorphism between two finite groups of the same order, $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $\operatorname{Gal}(EF/k) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(E/k) \times \operatorname{Gal}(F/k)$. Q.E.D.
:::
