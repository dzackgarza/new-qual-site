::: {.problem}
Show that if $E/k$ and $F/k$ are finite Galois extensions inside a common field with $E \cap F = k$, then the compositum $EF/k$ is Galois and:
$$\operatorname{Gal}(EF/k) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(E/k) \times \operatorname{Gal}(F/k).$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Since $E/k$ and $F/k$ are finite Galois, choose separable polynomials over $k$ whose splitting fields are $E$ and $F$. Their product has splitting field $EF$, so
\[
EF/k
\]
is finite Galois.

Restriction gives an injective homomorphism
\[
\Phi:\operatorname{Gal}(EF/k)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)\times\operatorname{Gal}(F/k),
\qquad
\sigma\mapsto(\sigma|_E,\sigma|_F).
\]
It is injective because $E$ and $F$ generate $EF$.

Because $F/k$ is Galois, restriction induces
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(EF/E)
\cong
\operatorname{Gal}(F/E\cap F).
\]
The hypothesis $E\cap F=k$ therefore gives
\[
[EF:E]=[F:k].
\]
Hence
\[
[EF:k]=[E:k][F:k].
\]
Since all three extensions are Galois,
\[
|\operatorname{Gal}(EF/k)|
=|\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)|\,|\operatorname{Gal}(F/k)|.
\]
Thus the injective map $\Phi$ is between finite groups of the same order, so it is an isomorphism:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(EF/k)
\cong\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)\times\operatorname{Gal}(F/k)}.
\]
:::
