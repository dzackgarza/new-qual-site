::: problem
State and prove the **Hilbert Basis Theorem**: if $R$ is a Noetherian ring, then the polynomial ring $R[x]$ is also Noetherian.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that if $R$ is a Noetherian ring, then every ideal in $R[x]$ is finitely generated.

<1>1. Setting and the Leading Coefficient Ideal:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, and let $I \subseteq R[x]$ be an arbitrary ideal of the polynomial ring $R[x]$.
    <2>2. For each integer $d \ge 0$, define the set of leading coefficients of polynomials of degree $d$ in $I$:
        $$L_d = \{a \in R \mid \exists f(x) \in I \text{ with } f(x) = a x^d + \text{lower degree terms}\} \cup \{0\}.$$
    <2>3. Each $L_d$ is an ideal of $R$:
        - If $a, b \in L_d$, there exist $f(x) = a x^d + \dots \in I$ and $g(x) = b x^d + \dots \in I$, so $f + g = (a+b)x^d + \dots \in I \implies a+b \in L_d$.
        - For any $r \in R$, $r f(x) = (ra)x^d + \dots \in I \implies ra \in L_d$.
    <2>4. Furthermore, if $a \in L_d$, then $x f(x) = a x^{d+1} + \dots \in I$, so $a \in L_{d+1}$.
    <2>5. Thus we have an ascending chain of ideals in $R$:
        $$L_0 \subseteq L_1 \subseteq L_2 \subseteq L_3 \subseteq \cdots.$$

<1>2. Finite Generation of the Ascending Chains:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $L = \bigcup_{d=0}^\infty L_d$. $L$ is an ideal of $R$.
    <2>2. Since $R$ is **Noetherian**, the ideal $L$ is finitely generated: $L = (a_1, a_2, \dots, a_m)$.
    <2>3. Moreover, the ascending chain $L_0 \subseteq L_1 \subseteq L_2 \subseteq \cdots$ must stabilize at some degree $N$:
        $$L_N = L_{N+1} = L_{N+2} = \cdots = L.$$
    <2>4. For each $d \in \{0, 1, \dots, N\}$, since $R$ is Noetherian, the ideal $L_d$ is finitely generated:
        $$L_d = (a_{d, 1}, a_{d, 2}, \dots, a_{d, k_d}).$$
    <2>5. For each generator $a_{d, i} \in L_d$, choose a polynomial $f_{d, i}(x) \in I$ of degree $d$ with leading coefficient $a_{d, i}$:
        $$f_{d, i}(x) = a_{d, i} x^d + \text{lower degree terms}.$$
    <2>6. Let $\mathcal{F} = \{f_{d, i}(x) \mid 0 \le d \le N, \; 1 \le i \le k_d\}$ be the finite collection of all such polynomials, and let $J = (\mathcal{F}) \subseteq I$ be the ideal in $R[x]$ generated by $\mathcal{F}$.

<1>3. Proof that $I = J$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. We have $J \subseteq I$: $J$ is generated by the polynomials $f_{d,i}$, each of which lies in $I$ (by construction in <1>2), and $I$ is an ideal, so it contains every finite $R[x]$-linear combination of the generators, i.e. all of $J$. We prove $I \subseteq J$ by induction on the degree of polynomials in $I$.
    <2>2. Let $g(x) \in I$ with $\deg(g) = n$ and leading coefficient $c \in L_n$.
    <2>3. **Case 1: $n \ge N$:**
        - Since $n \ge N$, $c \in L_n = L_N = (a_{N, 1}, \dots, a_{N, k_N})$.
        - Thus $c = \sum_{i=1}^{k_N} r_i a_{N, i}$ for some $r_i \in R$.
        - Consider the polynomial $h(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{k_N} r_i x^{n - N} f_{N, i}(x) \in J$.
        - The leading term of $h(x)$ is $\sum r_i a_{N, i} x^n = c x^n$.
        - Thus $g(x) - h(x) \in I$ has degree strictly less than $n$.
    <2>4. **Case 2: $n < N$:**
        - $c \in L_n = (a_{n, 1}, \dots, a_{n, k_n})$, so $c = \sum_{i=1}^{k_n} r_i a_{n, i}$.
        - The polynomial $h(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{k_n} r_i f_{n, i}(x) \in J$ has leading term $c x^n$.
        - Thus $g(x) - h(x) \in I$ has degree strictly less than $n$.
    <2>5. In both cases, subtracting an element of $J$ strictly reduces the degree.
    <2>6. By induction, we can reduce the degree until the polynomial is 0.
    <2>7. Therefore, $g(x) \in J$, proving $I = J = (\mathcal{F})$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    Every ideal $I \subseteq R[x]$ is finitely generated, so $R[x]$ is Noetherian. Q.E.D.
:::
