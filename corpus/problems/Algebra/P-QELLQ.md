::: {.problem}
State and prove the **Hilbert Basis Theorem**: if $R$ is a Noetherian ring, then the polynomial ring $R[x]$ is also Noetherian.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $R$ be Noetherian and let $I\subseteq R[x]$ be an ideal. For $d\ge0$, let $L_d\subseteq R$ be the ideal consisting of $0$ together with the leading coefficients of degree-$d$ elements of $I$. Then
\[
L_0\subseteq L_1\subseteq\cdots
\]
because multiplying by $x$ preserves the leading coefficient. Since $R$ is Noetherian, this chain stabilizes: choose $N$ with $L_d=L_N$ for all $d\ge N$.

For each $0\le d\le N$, choose generators
\[
L_d=(a_{d1},\dots,a_{dr_d})
\]
and choose $f_{di}\in I$ of degree $d$ with leading coefficient $a_{di}$. Let $J$ be the ideal of $R[x]$ generated by these finitely many $f_{di}$.

We prove $I=J$ by degree reduction. Let $g\in I$ have degree $m$ and leading coefficient $c$. If $m<N$, write $c$ as an $R$-linear combination of the $a_{mi}$ and subtract the corresponding combination of the $f_{mi}$. If $m\ge N$, write $c$ as a combination of the $a_{Ni}$ and subtract the same combination of $x^{m-N}f_{Ni}$. In either case the degree strictly decreases. Induction gives $g\in J$.

Thus every ideal of $R[x]$ is finitely generated, so $R[x]$ is Noetherian.
:::
