::: {.exercise}
Show that the splitting field of $f(x) = x^3-2$ over $\QQ$ is $\QQ(\sqrt[3]{2}, \zeta_3)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this exercise; the argument is the card's own. Write $\alpha=\sqrt[3]{2}$ for the real cube root of $2$ and $\zeta_3=e^{2\pi i/3}$ for a primitive cube root of unity, so that $\zeta_3^2+\zeta_3+1=0$ and the three cube roots of unity are $1,\zeta_3,\zeta_3^2$. Let $L\subseteq\CC$ be the splitting field of $f(x)=x^3-2$ over $\QQ$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #roots}
The roots of $x^3-2$ in $\CC$ are $\alpha$, $\alpha\zeta_3$ and $\alpha\zeta_3^2$.

::: pf-proof
The roots of $x^3-2$ are the cube roots of $2$, and $\alpha\ne0$ is one of them, so they are $\alpha u$ with $u^3=1$; in $\CC$ the equation $u^3=1$ has the three solutions $1,\zeta_3,\zeta_3^2$, since $u^3-1=(u-1)(u^2+u+1)$ and the roots of $u^2+u+1$ are the two non-real cube roots of unity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #inside}
$L\subseteq\QQ(\alpha,\zeta_3)$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#roots){.pf-ref} every root of $f$ lies in $\QQ(\alpha,\zeta_3)$, since $\alpha,\zeta_3\in\QQ(\alpha,\zeta_3)$ and that field is closed under products. A splitting field is generated over $\QQ$ by the roots of $f$, so it is contained in any field containing them.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #zeta}
$\zeta_3\in L$, hence $\QQ(\alpha,\zeta_3)\subseteq L$.

::: pf-proof
$L$ contains every root of $f$, so $\alpha\in L$ and, by step [](#roots){.pf-ref}, $\alpha\zeta_3\in L$. As $\alpha\ne0$ and $L$ is a field, $\zeta_3=(\alpha\zeta_3)/\alpha\in L$; with $\alpha\in L$ this gives $\QQ(\alpha,\zeta_3)\subseteq L$, the left-hand side being the smallest field containing $\QQ$, $\alpha$ and $\zeta_3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$L=\QQ(\sqrt[3]{2},\zeta_3)$, and $[L:\QQ]=6$.

::: pf-proof
The two inclusions are steps [](#inside){.pf-ref} and [](#zeta){.pf-ref}. For the degree: $x^3-2$ is Eisenstein at the prime $2$, so it is irreducible over $\QQ$ and $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=\deg f=3$; and $x^2+x+1$ is the minimal polynomial of $\zeta_3$ over $\QQ$, so $[\QQ(\alpha,\zeta_3):\QQ(\alpha)]\le2$. It is not $1$, because $\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq\RR$ while $\zeta_3\notin\RR$; hence that degree is $2$ and the tower formula gives $[L:\QQ]=3\cdot2=6$.
:::
