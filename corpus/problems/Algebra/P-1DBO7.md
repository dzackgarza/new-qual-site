::: problem
If $E \geq F$ is a finite extension, then it is algebraic and $E = F[\alpha_1, \cdots, \alpha_n]$.
So we can let $g(x) = \prod_{i=1}^n (x-\alpha_i)$.
By construction, each $\alpha_i$ is a root, and so $E$ is a splitting field for $g$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The first assertion is correct: every finite extension $E/F$ is algebraic and is generated by finitely many algebraic elements.
::: {.proof}
If $[E:F]<\infty$ and $\alpha\in E$, then the $[E:F]+1$ elements
\[
1,\alpha,\alpha^2,\ldots,\alpha^{[E:F]}
\]
are linearly dependent over $F$, so $\alpha$ satisfies a nonzero polynomial in $F[x]$.
Thus $E/F$ is algebraic.

Choosing an $F$-basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ of $E$, one has
\[
E=F[e_1,\ldots,e_r],
\]
so $E$ is finitely generated as an $F$-algebra.
:::

<1>2. The displayed argument does not prove that $E/F$ is a splitting field.
::: {.proof}
If
\[
E=F[\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n],
\]
then
\[
g(x)=\prod_{i=1}^n(x-\alpha_i)
\]
lies in $E[x]$, but in general it need not lie in $F[x]$.
A splitting field over $F$ must be the splitting field of a polynomial with coefficients in $F$.
Thus the argument misses the essential coefficient condition.
:::

<1>3. In fact, the claimed conclusion is false: a finite extension need not be a splitting field over its base field.
::: {.proof}
Take
\[
F=\mathbb Q,
\qquad
E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2})\subseteq\mathbb R.
\]
Then $[E:\mathbb Q]=3$, so $E/\mathbb Q$ is finite.
The element $\alpha=\sqrt[3]{2}$ has minimal polynomial
\[
x^3-2,
\]
whose other roots are $\zeta_3\alpha$ and $\zeta_3^2\alpha$ for a primitive cube root of unity $\zeta_3$.
Those roots are nonreal, hence do not lie in $E\subseteq\mathbb R$.
Therefore $E/\mathbb Q$ is not normal.

Every splitting field over a base field is normal over that base field, so $E$ cannot be the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of any polynomial in $\mathbb Q[x]$.
:::

<1>4. The correct replacement is: if a finite extension $E/F$ is normal, then $E$ is a splitting field over $F$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
E=F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)
\]
and let $m_i(x)\in F[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha_i$ over $F$.
Set
\[
h(x)=\prod_{i=1}^n m_i(x)\in F[x].
\]
Because $E/F$ is normal, every $m_i$ splits completely over $E$.
Hence $h$ splits over $E$.
Conversely, any splitting field of $h$ contains the chosen roots $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$, and therefore contains
\[
F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)=E.
\]
Thus $E$ is exactly the splitting field of $h$ over $F$.
:::
:::
