::: problem
- Show that $ff(R[t]) = ff(R)(t)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let $K = \operatorname{Frac}(R)$ be the field of fractions of the domain $R$.
Proof: definition.

<1>2. $R[t] \subseteq K(t)$, so $\operatorname{Frac}(R[t]) \subseteq K(t)$.
Proof: $R[t] \subseteq K[t] \subseteq K(t)$, and $K(t)$ is a field, so the field of fractions of $R[t]$ embeds into $K(t)$.

<1>3. Conversely, $K(t) \subseteq \operatorname{Frac}(R[t])$.
<2>1. Every element of $K$ is of the form $a/b$ with $a, b \in R$, $b \neq 0$, so $K \subseteq \operatorname{Frac}(R[t])$.
Proof: $a/b$ is a ratio of two elements of $R \subseteq R[t]$.
<2>2. $t \in \operatorname{Frac}(R[t])$.
Proof: $t \in R[t]$.
<2>3. Hence $K(t) \subseteq \operatorname{Frac}(R[t])$.
Proof: $\operatorname{Frac}(R[t])$ is a field containing $K$ and $t$, so it contains the field $K(t)$ generated by them.

<1>4. Therefore $\operatorname{Frac}(R[t]) = K(t) = \operatorname{Frac}(R)(t)$.
Proof: <1>2 and <1>3.

<1>5. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>4.
:::
