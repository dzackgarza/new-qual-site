::: problem
- Show that $ff(R[t]) = ff(R)(t)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Since
\[
R[t]\subseteq K[t]\subseteq K(t)
\]
and $K(t)$ is a field, the universal property of the fraction field gives
\[
\operatorname{Frac}(R[t])\subseteq K(t).
\]

Conversely, every element $a/b\in K$ with $a,b\in R$ and $b\ne0$ is already a quotient of two elements of $R[t]$, so
\[
K\subseteq \operatorname{Frac}(R[t]).
\]
Also $t\in R[t]\subseteq \operatorname{Frac}(R[t])$. Hence the field $\operatorname{Frac}(R[t])$ contains the field generated by $K$ and $t$, namely $K(t)$. Thus
\[
K(t)\subseteq \operatorname{Frac}(R[t]).
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Frac}(R[t])=K(t)=\operatorname{Frac}(R)(t).
\]
:::
