::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be an integral domain. Show that $\ff(R[t]) = \ff(R)(t)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K=\Frac(R)$. Since
$$
R[t]\subseteq K[t]\subseteq K(t)
$$
and $K(t)$ is a field, the universal property of the fraction field gives
$$
\Frac(R[t])\subseteq K(t).
$$

Conversely, every element $a/b\in K$ with $a,b\in R$ and $b\ne0$ is already a quotient of two elements of $R[t]$, so
$$
K\subseteq \Frac(R[t]).
$$
Also $t\in R[t]\subseteq \Frac(R[t])$. Hence the field $\Frac(R[t])$ contains the field generated by $K$ and $t$, namely $K(t)$. Thus
$$
K(t)\subseteq \Frac(R[t]).
$$
Therefore
$$
\Frac(R[t])=K(t)=\Frac(R)(t).
$$
:::
