::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be an integral domain. Show that $\ff(R[t]) = \ff(R)(t)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K=\Frac(R)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #sub}
$\Frac(R[t])\subseteq K(t)$.

::: pf-proof
We have $R[t]\subseteq K[t]\subseteq K(t)$, and $K(t)$ is a field.
The universal property of the fraction field gives an embedding of $\Frac(R[t])$ into $K(t)$ over $R[t]$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sup}
$K(t)\subseteq\Frac(R[t])$.

::: pf-proof
Every element $a/b\in K$ with $a,b\in R$ and $b\ne0$ is a quotient of two elements of $R[t]$, so $K\subseteq\Frac(R[t])$.
Also $t\in R[t]\subseteq\Frac(R[t])$.
Hence the field $\Frac(R[t])$ contains the field generated by $K$ and $t$, which is $K(t)$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
The inclusions of steps [](#sub){.pf-ref} and [](#sup){.pf-ref} are inclusions of subfields of $K(t)$, so $\Frac(R[t])=K(t)=\Frac(R)(t)$.
:::
