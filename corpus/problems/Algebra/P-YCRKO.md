::: {.problem}
Define the commutator and the derived series. State and prove two nontrivial theorems about derived series.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $x,y$ in a group $G$, the commutator is $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$, and for subgroups $A,B\le G$, $[A,B]$ is the subgroup generated by the $[a,b]$ with $a\in A$, $b\in B$.
The derived series is $G^{(0)}=G$, $G^{(n+1)}=[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}]$, and $G$ is solvable when $G^{(r)}=1$ for some $r$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #image}
If $\phi:G\to H$ is a homomorphism, then $\phi(G^{(n)})=\phi(G)^{(n)}$ for every $n\ge0$; in particular each $G^{(n)}$ is characteristic in $G$.

::: pf-proof
Since $\phi([x,y])=[\phi(x),\phi(y)]$ and the image of the subgroup generated by a set is the subgroup generated by its image, $\phi([A,A])=[\phi(A),\phi(A)]$ for every $A\le G$.
Induction on $n$ gives the formula.
For an automorphism $\phi$ of $G$ it reads $\phi(G^{(n)})=G^{(n)}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $N\trianglelefteq G$ and both $N$ and $G/N$ are solvable, then $G$ is solvable.

::: pf-proof
Say $(G/N)^{(r)}=1$ and $N^{(s)}=1$.
Applying step [](#image){.pf-ref} to the quotient map gives $G^{(r)}N/N=(G/N)^{(r)}=1$, so $G^{(r)}\le N$.
Since $A\le B$ implies $[A,A]\le[B,B]$, induction on $j$ gives $G^{(r+j)}\le N^{(j)}$, so $G^{(r+s)}\le N^{(s)}=1$.
:::
