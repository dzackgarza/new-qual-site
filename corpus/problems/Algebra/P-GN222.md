::: problem
How many irreducible polynomials of degree six are there over \( \FF_2 \)?
:::


::: {.solution}
Let $N_q(n)$ denote the number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree $n$ over $\FF_q$.

<1>1. One has
\[
q^n=\sum_{d\mid n} d\,N_q(d).
\]
::: {.proof}
The polynomial $x^{q^n}-x$ has as its roots exactly the elements of $\FF_{q^n}$ and is squarefree. Its irreducible factors over $\FF_q$ are exactly the monic irreducibles whose degrees divide $n$. Each degree-$d$ irreducible contributes $d$ roots, so comparison of degrees gives the formula.
:::

<1>2. Möbius inversion gives
\[
N_q(n)=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)q^{n/d}.
\]
::: {.proof}
Apply ordinary Möbius inversion to the identity in <1>1.
:::

<1>3. For $q=2$ and $n=6$,
\[
N_2(6)=9.
\]
::: {.proof}
The divisors of $6$ are $1,2,3,6$, with Möbius values $1,-1,-1,1$. Hence
\[
N_2(6)
=
\frac16\left(2^6-2^3-2^2+2\right)
=
\frac{64-8-4+2}{6}
=9.
\]
:::

Thus there are exactly $9$ monic irreducible degree-$6$ polynomials over $\FF_2$.
:::
