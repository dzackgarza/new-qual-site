::: {.problem}
Does a group $G$ of order $12$ with a normal subgroup of order $4$ have to be isomorphic to $A_4$? Give a counterexample.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G=C_{12}=\langle g\rangle$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #normal}

$H=\langle g^3\rangle\cong C_4$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ of order $4$.

::: pf-proof

The element $g^3$ has order $12/3=4$, and every subgroup of the abelian group $G$ is normal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not-a4}

$C_{12}\not\cong A_4$.

::: pf-proof

$C_{12}$ is abelian, whereas $A_4$ is not: $(123)(12)(34)=(134)$ but $(12)(34)(123)=(243)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The answer is $\boxed{\text{no}}$: $C_{12}$ has order $12$ and a normal subgroup of order $4$ but is not isomorphic to $A_4$.

::: pf-proof

This follows from steps [](#normal){.pf-ref} and [](#not-a4){.pf-ref}.

:::
