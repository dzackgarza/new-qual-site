::: problem
Does a group $G$ of order $12$ with a normal subgroup of order $4$ have to be isomorphic to $A_4$? Give a counterexample.
:::


::: {.solution}
No. Take
\[
G=C_{12}.
\]
A cyclic group has a unique subgroup of order $d$ for every divisor $d$ of its order. Hence $C_{12}$ has a unique subgroup
\[
H=\langle g^3\rangle\cong C_4
\]
of order $4$, and $H$ is normal because $C_{12}$ is abelian.

But $C_{12}\not\cong A_4$: the first group is abelian and contains an element of order $12$, whereas $A_4$ is nonabelian and has element orders only $1,2,3$.

Thus the existence of a normal subgroup of order $4$ does not characterize $A_4$ among groups of order $12$.
:::
