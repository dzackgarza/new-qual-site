::: {.problem}
- Show that if $K/F$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial then it is Galois.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $K/F$ be the splitting field of a separable polynomial $f\in F[x]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #s1}

The extension $K/F$ is finite and normal.

::: pf-proof

A splitting field of a polynomial of finite degree is finite over the base. By definition, $K$ is generated by all roots of $f$, and every $F$-embedding of $K$ into an algebraic closure permutes those roots. Hence every such embedding sends $K$ onto itself, so $K/F$ is normal.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s2}

The extension $K/F$ is separable.

::: pf-proof

Because $f$ is separable, each of its roots is separable over $F$. The field $K$ is generated over $F$ by those separable elements, so $K/F$ is separable.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Therefore $K/F$ is Galois.

::: pf-proof

A finite extension is Galois iff it is both normal and separable. Apply steps [](#s1){.pf-ref} and [](#s2){.pf-ref}.

:::
