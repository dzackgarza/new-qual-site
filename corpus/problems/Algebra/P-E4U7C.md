::: problem
Describe the Galois theory of cyclotomic extensions $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}$ (irreducibility of cyclotomic polynomials, Galois group isomorphism, and subfield structure).
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Characterize the cyclotomic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}$, its Galois group, and its subfields.

<1>1. Cyclotomic Polynomials $\Phi_n(x)$ and Irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i / n} \in \mathbb{C}$ be a primitive $n$-th root of unity.
    <2>2. The $n$-th **cyclotomic polynomial** is defined by:
        $$\Phi_n(x) = \prod_{\substack{1 \le k \le n \\ \gcd(k, n) = 1}} (x - \zeta_n^k).$$
    <2>3. $\deg(\Phi_n) = \varphi(n)$, where $\varphi$ is Euler's totient function.
    <2>4. In $\mathbb{C}[x]$, we have the factorization $x^n - 1 = \prod_{d \mid n} \Phi_d(x)$, which shows by induction that $\Phi_n(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ is monic.
    <2>5. **Gauss's Theorem:** $\Phi_n(x)$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ for all $n \ge 1$.
        *(Proof via Dedekind/Landau: if $\Phi_n = f g$, any root $\zeta$ of $f$ and prime $p \nmid n$ satisfies that $\zeta^p$ is also a root of $f$, establishing that all primitive roots are roots of $f$)*.

<1>2. Galois Group Structure:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The extension $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ is the splitting field of $x^n - 1$ (and $\Phi_n(x)$) over $\mathbb{Q}$.
    <2>2. Since $\Phi_n(x)$ is separable and irreducible of degree $\varphi(n)$, $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is an abelian Galois extension of degree $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = \varphi(n)$.
    <2>3. Any automorphism $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ maps $\zeta_n$ to another primitive root $\zeta_n^a$ for some $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$.
    <2>4. The map $\Psi: \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \to (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ defined by $\sigma_a \mapsto a \pmod n$ is an injective group homomorphism between groups of order $\varphi(n)$.
    <2>5. Therefore:
        $$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times.$$

<1>3. Subfields and the Kronecker–Weber Theorem:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, subfields of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ correspond bijectively to subgroups of $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$.
    <2>2. **Maximal Real Subfield:** The complex conjugation automorphism corresponds to $-1 \pmod n \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Its fixed field is the maximal real subfield:
        $$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n + \zeta_n^{-1}) = \mathbb{Q}(\cos(2\pi/n)), \qquad [\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) : \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)^+] = 2.$$
    <2>3. **Quadratic Subfields:** For any odd prime $p$, $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ contains a unique quadratic subfield:
        $$\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{(-1)^{(p-1)/2} p}\right) = \begin{cases} \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p}) & \text{if } p \equiv 1 \pmod 4, \\ \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}) & \text{if } p \equiv 3 \pmod 4. \end{cases}$$
        (generated by the quadratic Gauss sum $g = \sum_{a=1}^{p-1} \left(\frac{a}{p}\right) \zeta_p^a$).
    <2>4. **Kronecker–Weber Theorem:** Every finite abelian extension $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is contained in a cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ for some $n \ge 1$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois of degree $\varphi(n)$ with abelian Galois group $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$, forming the building blocks of all abelian extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$. Q.E.D.
:::
