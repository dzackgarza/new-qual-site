::: {.exercise}
Let $F/K$ be a field extension, and let $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\in F$ be algebraic over $K$.
Show that
\[
K[\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n]=K(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n).
\]
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that if $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n \in F$ are algebraic over a base field $K$, then the ring extension $K[\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n]$ is a field, and hence equals $K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)$.

<1>1. Trivial inclusion:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $K[\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n]$ is the subring generated by $K$ and $\{\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n\}$.
    <2>2. $K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)$ is the subfield generated by $K$ and $\{\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n\}$.
    <2>3. Because any subfield is in particular a subring, $K[\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n] \subseteq K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)$.

<1>2. Finite dimensionality of $R = K[\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n]$ over $K$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $d_i = [K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{i-1})(\alpha_i) : K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{i-1})]$.
    <2>2. Since each $\alpha_i$ is algebraic over $K$, $d_i < \infty$ for all $i = 1, \dots, n$.
    <2>3. The set of monomials $\{\alpha_1^{e_1} \cdots \alpha_n^{e_n} \mid 0 \le e_i < d_i\}$ spans $R$ as a $K$-vector space.
    <2>4. Thus $\dim_K(R) \le \prod_{i=1}^n d_i < \infty$.

<1>3. Every finite-dimensional integral domain over a field is a field:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Because $R$ is a subring of the field $F$, $R$ is an integral domain.
    <2>2. Let $x \in R$ with $x \neq 0$.
    <2>3. Define the $K$-linear multiplication operator $\mu_x: R \to R$ by $\mu_x(y) = x y$.
    <2>4. Since $R$ has no zero divisors, $x y = 0 \implies y = 0$, so $\ker(\mu_x) = \{0\}$; thus $\mu_x$ is injective.
    <2>5. By the Rank-Nullity theorem for finite-dimensional vector spaces, an injective linear endomorphism is surjective.
    <2>6. Since $1 \in R$, there exists $y \in R$ such that $\mu_x(y) = x y = 1$.
    <2>7. Thus every non-zero element of $R$ has a multiplicative inverse in $R$, proving that $R$ is a field.

<1>4. Reverse inclusion and equality:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $R = K[\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n]$ is a field containing $K$ and each $\alpha_i$, by the minimality of $K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)$ we have $K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n) \subseteq K[\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n]$.
    <2>2. Combining with <1>1 gives $K[\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n] = K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $K[\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n] = K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)$. Q.E.D.
:::
