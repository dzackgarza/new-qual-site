::: problem
- Show that $\QQ$ is not finitely generated as a group.
:::


::: {.solution}
Suppose for contradiction that the additive group $(\QQ,+)$ is generated by finitely many rationals
\[
q_1,\ldots,q_r.
\]
Write each
\[
q_i=\frac{a_i}{b_i}
\]
with $a_i\in\ZZ$ and $b_i\ge1$, and let
\[
B=\operatorname{lcm}(b_1,\ldots,b_r).
\]
Then every integer linear combination of the $q_i$ lies in
\[
\frac1B\ZZ.
\]
Hence the subgroup they generate is contained in $B^{-1}\ZZ$.

But
\[
\frac{1}{2B}\in\QQ
\]
does not lie in $B^{-1}\ZZ$, since $1/(2B)=m/B$ would force $m=1/2\notin\ZZ$. This contradicts generation of all of $\QQ$. Therefore $(\QQ,+)$ is not finitely generated.
:::
