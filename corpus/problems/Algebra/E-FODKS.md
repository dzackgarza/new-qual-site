::: exercise
Let $R$ be a Principal Ideal Domain (PID), and let $I = (b)$ be a proper ideal of $R$.
Prove that if $a \in I$ is an irreducible element, then $(a) = (b) = I$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that an irreducible element $a$ in a PID $R$ generates any proper principal ideal containing it (i.e. $(a)$ is a maximal ideal).

<1>1. Ideal Containment and Divisibility:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $R$ be a PID, and let $I = (b)$ with $b \in R$.
    <2>2. Since $a \in I = (b)$, the principal ideal generated by $a$ is contained in $(b)$:
        $$(a) \subseteq (b).$$
    <2>3. This containment is equivalent to the divisibility condition:
        $$a = r b \quad \text{for some } r \in R.$$

<1>2. Irreducibility of $a$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By definition of an **irreducible element** in an integral domain:
        - $a \ne 0$,
        - $a$ is not a unit ($a \notin R^\times$), and
        - Whenever $a = r b$, either $r$ is a unit or $b$ is a unit.
    <2>2. **Case 1: $b$ is a unit ($b \in R^\times$):**
        - If $b \in R^\times$, then $(b) = R$.
        - But $I = (b)$ is a proper ideal ($I \ne R$), so $b$ cannot be a unit.
        - (Even if $I = R$, $(a) \subseteq R$).
    <2>3. **Case 2: $r$ is a unit ($r \in R^\times$):**
        - Since $r$ is a unit, $r^{-1} \in R$ exists.
        - From $a = r b$, multiplying both sides by $r^{-1}$ gives:
            $$b = r^{-1} a \in (a).$$
        - Therefore, every element of $(b)$ is a multiple of $a$:
            $$(b) \subseteq (a).$$

<1>3. Equality of Ideals:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Combining the inclusions $(a) \subseteq (b)$ and $(b) \subseteq (a)$ yields:
        $$(a) = (b) = I.$$
    <2>2. In particular, this proves that in a PID, every non-zero irreducible element generates a **maximal ideal** $(a)$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $(a) = (b) = I$. Q.E.D.
:::
