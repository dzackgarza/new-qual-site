::: {.problem}
What is the Galois group of $x^2+9$ over $\QQ$?
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this question, so the argument is the card's own. Write $i$ for a square root of $-1$ in $\CC$ and $\Gal(E/F)$ for the group of $F$-automorphisms of a splitting field $E$ of a polynomial over $F$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #roots}
The zeros of $x^{2}+9$ in $\CC$ are $3i$ and $-3i$, so its splitting field over $\QQ$ is $\QQ(3i)$.

::: pf-proof
$x^{2}+9=0$ gives $x^{2}=-9$, whose solutions in $\CC$ are $\pm3i$. A splitting field is generated over $\QQ$ by the zeros, so it is $\QQ(3i,-3i)=\QQ(3i)$ because $-3i=-(3i)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #same-field}
$\QQ(3i)=\QQ(i)$.

::: pf-proof
$3i=3\cdot i$ lies in $\QQ(i)$ because $i\in\QQ(i)$ and $\QQ(i)$ is closed under multiplication by rationals, so $\QQ(3i)\subset\QQ(i)$. Conversely $i=\tfrac13(3i)$ lies in $\QQ(3i)$ by the same reason, so $\QQ(i)\subset\QQ(3i)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #minimal-polynomial}
$x^{2}+9$ is the minimal polynomial of $3i$ over $\QQ$, so $[\QQ(3i):\QQ]=2$.

::: pf-proof
$3i$ is a zero of $x^{2}+9\in\QQ[x]$, which is monic. It has no rational zero, since $x^{2}+9\ge9>0$ for real $x$, so being of degree $2$ it is irreducible over $\QQ$. An irreducible monic polynomial over $\QQ$ with $3i$ as a zero is the minimal polynomial of $3i$, and the degree of an algebraic element equals the degree of its minimal polynomial.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #automorphisms}
Every $\QQ$-automorphism of $\QQ(3i)$ sends $3i$ to $3i$ or to $-3i$, and both occur, so $\cardinality{\Gal(\QQ(3i)/\QQ)}=2$.

::: pf-proof
Let $\sigma$ be a $\QQ$-automorphism of $\QQ(3i)$. It fixes $\QQ$, so $\sigma(3i)^{2}=\sigma((3i)^{2})=\sigma(-9)=-9$, hence $\sigma(3i)$ is a zero of $x^{2}+9$ in $\QQ(3i)$, that is $3i$ or $-3i$ by step [](#roots){.pf-ref}. Since $\QQ(3i)$ is generated over $\QQ$ by $3i$, $\sigma$ is determined by this value, so there are at most two automorphisms. The number of automorphisms of a splitting field equals its degree over the base field, which is $2$ by step [](#minimal-polynomial){.pf-ref}, so both choices occur; concretely $3i\mapsto-3i$ extends to complex conjugation, which fixes $\QQ$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #answer}
$\boxed{\Gal(\QQ(3i)/\QQ)\cong\ZZ_{2}}$, generated by complex conjugation.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#same-field){.pf-ref} the splitting field of $x^{2}+9$ over $\QQ$ is $\QQ(i)$, and by step [](#automorphisms){.pf-ref} its Galois group has two elements. A group of two elements is cyclic, generated by its nonidentity element, which is the automorphism $i\mapsto-i$ of complex conjugation; so the Galois group is $\ZZ_{2}$.
:::
