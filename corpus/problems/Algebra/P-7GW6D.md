::: problem
What's a Galois group that's not $S_n$ or $A_n$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The Galois group of $x^4 - 2$ over $\QQ$ is the dihedral group $D_4$ of order $8$.
Proof: the splitting field is $\QQ(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$, and its Galois group is generated by $\sigma: \sqrt[4]{2} \mapsto i\sqrt[4]{2}$ (order $4$) and $\tau: i \mapsto -i$ (order $2$), with $\tau\sigma\tau = \sigma^{-1}$; this is $D_4$.

<1>2. $D_4$ is neither $S_n$ nor $A_n$ for any $n$.
Proof: $D_4$ has order $8$, while $S_n$ has order $n!$ and $A_n$ has order $n!/2$; the only $n$ with $n! = 8$ or $n!/2 = 8$ is none (since $4! = 24$ and $3! = 6$), so $D_4$ is not isomorphic to any symmetric or alternating group.

<1>3. Another example: the Galois group of $x^3 - 2$ over $\QQ$ is $S_3$, but the Galois group of a cyclic extension $\QQ(\zeta_n)/\QQ$ is $(\ZZ/n\ZZ)^\times$, which is abelian and generally not $S_n$ or $A_n$.
Proof: e.g. $\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_7)/\QQ) \cong (\ZZ/7\ZZ)^\times \cong \ZZ/6$, a cyclic group of order $6$, not isomorphic to any $S_n$ or $A_n$.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>1–<1>3 give explicit examples.
:::
