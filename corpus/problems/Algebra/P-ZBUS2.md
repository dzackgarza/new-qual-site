::: {.problem}
Do you know any representation theory?
What about representations of $A_4$?

Give a nontrivial one.
What else is there?
How many irreducible representations do we have?
What are their degrees?
Write the character table of $A_4$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $V_4=\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}\trianglelefteq A_4$ and $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$. All representations are complex.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #classes}

$A_4$ has four conjugacy classes, with representatives $1$, $(12)(34)$, $(123)$, $(132)$ and sizes $1,3,4,4$, so it has four irreducible representations.

::: pf-proof

The three double transpositions are conjugate under $(123)$. The centralizer of $(123)$ in $A_4$ is $\langle(123)\rangle$, so its class has $12/3=4$ elements; it does not contain $(132)$, which is the image of $(123)$ under the odd permutation $(23)$, so the remaining four $3$-cycles form the fourth class. The number of irreducible representations equals the number of conjugacy classes.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #linear}

$A_4$ has three one-dimensional representations: the trivial one and two nontrivial ones $\chi_2,\chi_3$, trivial on $V_4$, with $\chi_2(123)=\omega$, $\chi_2(132)=\omega^2$, $\chi_3=\overline{\chi_2}$.

::: pf-proof

$A_4/V_4\cong C_3$, generated by the image of $(123)$, so the three characters of $C_3$ pull back to $A_4$. There are no others, since $V_4$ is the commutator subgroup of $A_4$: the commutator subgroup lies in $V_4$ because $A_4/V_4$ is abelian, and it is a normal subgroup containing the nontrivial element $[(123),(12)(34)]$ of $V_4$, whose conjugates under $(123)$ are the three nontrivial elements of $V_4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #standard}

The fourth irreducible representation has degree $3$; it is the standard representation, the permutation representation on $\CC^4$ minus its trivial summand, with character $(3,-1,0,0)$ on the classes of step [](#classes){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof

The degrees satisfy $12=1^2+1^2+1^2+d^2$, so $d=3$. The permutation character counts fixed points, $(4,0,1,1)$, and removing the trivial character gives $\chi_4=(3,-1,0,0)$. It is irreducible because
$$
\inner{\chi_4}{\chi_4}=\tfrac1{12}\bigl(9+3\cdot1+0+0\bigr)=1.
$$

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The character table of $A_4$ is
$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&1&(12)(34)&(123)&(132)\\\hline
\chi_1&1&1&1&1\\
\chi_2&1&1&\omega&\omega^2\\
\chi_3&1&1&\omega^2&\omega\\
\chi_4&3&-1&0&0
\end{array}}
$$

::: pf-proof

The rows are the characters of steps [](#linear){.pf-ref} and [](#standard){.pf-ref}.

:::
