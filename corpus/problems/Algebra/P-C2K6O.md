::: problem
Prove that for $p$ prime, $S_p$ is generated by a $p$-cycle and a transposition.
:::


::: {.solution}
Let $\sigma$ be a $p$-cycle and $\tau$ a transposition in $S_p$. We show
\[
\langle \sigma,\tau\rangle=S_p.
\]
After relabeling the underlying set as $\ZZ/p\ZZ$, we may write
\[
\sigma(i)=i+1
\]
and
\[
\tau=(0\ d)
\]
for some nonzero $d\in\ZZ/p\ZZ$.

<1>1. The subgroup $H=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle$ contains every transposition
\[
(k\ k+d),\qquad k\in\ZZ/p\ZZ.
\]
::: {.proof}
Conjugating $\tau$ by powers of $\sigma$ gives
\[
\sigma^k\tau\sigma^{-k}=(k\ k+d).
\]
Since $\sigma,\tau\in H$, all these conjugates lie in $H$.
:::

<1>2. The graph on $\ZZ/p\ZZ$ with edges $\{k,k+d\}$ is connected.
::: {.proof}
Starting at $0$, repeated addition of $d$ reaches
\[
0,d,2d,\ldots,(p-1)d.
\]
Because $p$ is prime and $d\ne0$, multiplication by $d$ permutes $\ZZ/p\ZZ$. Hence these are all $p$ vertices.
:::

<1>3. Transpositions corresponding to the edges of a connected graph generate the full symmetric group on its vertices.
::: {.proof}
Fix a root vertex $v_0$. For any vertex $v$, choose a path
\[
v_0=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_r=v.
\]
Then the transposition $(v_0\ v)$ is a product of edge transpositions along the path:
\[
(v_0\ v_1)(v_1\ v_2)\cdots(v_{r-1}\ v_r)(v_{r-2}\ v_{r-1})\cdots(v_0\ v_1).
\]
Thus all transpositions $(v_0\ v)$ lie in the generated subgroup. These star transpositions generate the full symmetric group.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $H=S_p$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1 and <1>2, $H$ contains the edge transpositions of a connected graph. By <1>3 these generate all of $S_p$.
:::
:::
