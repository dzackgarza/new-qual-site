::: {.problem}
What is Galois theory?
State the main theorem.
What is the splitting field of $x^5 - 2$ over $\QQ$?
What are the intermediate extensions?
Which extensions are normal, which are not, and why?
What are the Galois groups (over $\QQ$) of all intermediate extensions?
:::

::: {.solution}
Galois theory studies a field extension through its group of automorphisms. Let $\alpha=2^{1/5}\in\RR$ and $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ftgt}

(Fundamental theorem) If $L/F$ is a finite Galois extension with group $G$, then $E\mapsto\Gal(L/E)$ and $H\mapsto L^H$ are inverse inclusion-reversing bijections between intermediate fields $F\subseteq E\subseteq L$ and subgroups $H\le G$, with $[L:E]=\abs{H}$. Moreover $E/F$ is Galois exactly when $H\normal G$, in which case $\Gal(E/F)\cong G/H$; in general $\Aut_F(E)\cong N_G(H)/H$.

:::

::: {.pf-step #degree}

The splitting field of $x^5-2$ over $\QQ$ is $L=\QQ(\alpha,\zeta)$, and $[L:\QQ]=20$.

::: pf-proof

The roots are $\zeta^k\alpha$ for $0\le k\le 4$, so $L=\QQ(\alpha,\zeta)$. By Eisenstein at $2$, $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=5$, and $[\QQ(\zeta):\QQ]=4$. Both divide $[L:\QQ]$, which is at most $5\cdot4$, so $[L:\QQ]=20$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #group}

$G\coloneqq\Gal(L/\QQ)=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\cong C_5\rtimes C_4$, where $\sigma(\alpha)=\zeta\alpha$, $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta$, $\tau(\alpha)=\alpha$, $\tau(\zeta)=\zeta^2$, with $\sigma^5=\tau^4=1$ and $\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^2$.

::: pf-proof

An automorphism is determined by $\alpha\mapsto\zeta^a\alpha$ and $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^b$ with $a\in\ZZ/5$ and $b\in(\ZZ/5)^\times$, giving at most $20$ choices; by step [](#degree){.pf-ref} all $20$ occur. The relations follow by evaluating on $\alpha$ and $\zeta$; for instance $\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}(\alpha)=\tau(\zeta\alpha)=\zeta^2\alpha=\sigma^2(\alpha)$. The element $\sigma$ has order $5$ and $\tau$ has order $4$, so they generate $G$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #subgroups}

The proper nontrivial subgroups of $G$ are $H_5=\langle\sigma\rangle$, $H_{10}=\langle\sigma,\tau^2\rangle\cong D_{10}$, the five conjugates $H_{4,j}=\sigma^j\langle\tau\rangle\sigma^{-j}$, and the five conjugates $H_{2,j}=\sigma^j\langle\tau^2\rangle\sigma^{-j}$, for $0\le j<5$. Exactly $H_5$ and $H_{10}$ are normal; $N_G(H_{4,j})=H_{4,j}$ and $\abs{N_G(H_{2,j})}=4$.

::: pf-proof

$H_5$ is the unique Sylow $5$-subgroup, so it is normal and lies in every subgroup of order $10$. Such a subgroup is $H_5$ together with an element of order $2$, and the elements of order $2$ are the conjugates $\sigma^j\tau^2\sigma^{-j}$, all in $\langle\sigma,\tau^2\rangle$; so $H_{10}$ is the unique subgroup of order $10$, normal of index $2$. The Sylow $2$-subgroups are the conjugates of $\langle\tau\rangle$; since $\tau$ does not commute with $\sigma$, $n_2\ne1$, so $n_2=5$ and each is self-normalizing. The subgroups of order $2$ are generated by the five elements of order $2$; $\langle\tau^2\rangle$ is not normal, so it has five conjugates and its normalizer has order $20/5=4$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The intermediate fields and their groups over $\QQ$ are
$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
H & L^H & [L^H:\QQ] & \text{group over }\QQ\\\hline
G & \QQ & 1 & 1\\
H_{10} & \QQ(\sqrt5) & 2 & \Gal\cong C_2\\
H_5 & \QQ(\zeta) & 4 & \Gal\cong C_4\\
H_{4,j} & \QQ(\zeta^j\alpha) & 5 & \Aut=1,\ \text{not normal}\\
H_{2,j} & \QQ(\zeta^j\alpha,\zeta+\zeta^{-1}) & 10 & \Aut\cong C_2,\ \text{not normal}\\
1 & L & 20 & \Gal\cong C_5\rtimes C_4
\end{array}}
$$
The normal intermediate extensions are exactly $\QQ$, $\QQ(\sqrt5)$, $\QQ(\zeta)$, and $L$.

::: pf-proof

$H_5$ fixes $\QQ(\zeta)$, which has degree $4=[G:H_5]$. $H_{10}\supseteq H_5$ fixes a quadratic subfield of $\QQ(\zeta)$, and the unique one is $\QQ(\sqrt5)$, since $\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=\tfrac{-1+\sqrt5}{2}$. $\langle\tau\rangle$ fixes $\alpha$ and $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=5=[G:\langle\tau\rangle]$, so its fixed field is $\QQ(\alpha)$, and $\sigma^j$ carries it to $\QQ(\zeta^j\alpha)$. $\tau^2$ sends $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}$ and fixes $\alpha$, so $\langle\tau^2\rangle$ fixes $\QQ(\alpha,\zeta+\zeta^{-1})$, which has degree $10$ because it contains fields of degrees $5$ and $2$; conjugating by $\sigma^j$ gives the rest. The normality and automorphism groups follow from step [](#subgroups){.pf-ref} and step [](#ftgt){.pf-ref}: $N_G(H_{4,j})/H_{4,j}=1$ and $N_G(H_{2,j})/H_{2,j}\cong C_2$. Concretely, $\QQ(\alpha)\subset\RR$ does not contain the nonreal roots $\zeta^k\alpha$ of $x^5-2$, which is why it is not normal.

:::
