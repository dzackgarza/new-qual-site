::: {.problem}
Let $f(x) = x^3 - 7$.

1. Let $K$ be the splitting field for $f$ over $\mathbb Q$.
   Describe the Galois group of $K / \mathbb Q$ and the intermediate fields between $\mathbb Q$ and $K$.
   Which intermediate fields are not Galois over $\mathbb Q$?

2. Let $L$ be the splitting field for $f$ over $\mathbb R$.
   What is the Galois group $L/ \mathbb R$?

3. Let $M$ be the splitting field for $f$ over $\mathbb F_{13}$, the field with 13 elements.
   What is the Galois group of $M / \mathbb F_{13}$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Part 1: Splitting field $K/\mathbb{Q}$, Galois group, and intermediate fields: <2>1. The roots of $f(x) = x^3 - 7$ in $\mathbb{C}$ are $\alpha_1 = \sqrt[3]{7}$, $\alpha_2 = \omega \sqrt[3]{7}$, and $\alpha_3 = \omega^2 \sqrt[3]{7}$, where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3} = \frac{-1 + i\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
::: {.proof}
roots of $x^3 - 7 = 0$.
:::
<2>2. $f(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein’s criterion with $p = 7$.
::: {.proof}
$7 \mid 7$, $7^2 \nmid 7$, and $7 \nmid 1$.
:::
<2>3. The splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7}, \omega)$.
Since $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3$ and $\omega \notin \mathbb{R} \supset \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7})$, $[\mathbb{Q}(\omega) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2$, so $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = 6$.
::: {.proof}
tower law $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7}, \omega) : \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7})] \cdot [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2 \cdot 3 = 6$.
:::
<2>4. The discriminant of $f(x) = x^3 - 7$ is $D = -27(-7)^2 = -3 \cdot 21^2$, which is not a square in $\mathbb{Q}$.
Thus $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$, generated by $\sigma = (\alpha_1 \, \alpha_2 \, \alpha_3)$ of order 3 and complex conjugation $\tau = (\alpha_2 \, \alpha_3)$ of order 2.
::: {.proof}
transitive subgroup of $S_3$ of order 6. <2>5. By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, intermediate fields correspond to subgroups of $S_3$:
:::

- Subgroup $A_3 = \langle \sigma \rangle \trianglelefteq S_3$ of order 3 corresponds to the quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\omega) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, which is **Galois** over $\mathbb{Q}$.

- Subgroups of order 2 ($\langle \tau \rangle$, $\langle \sigma\tau \rangle$, $\langle \sigma^2\tau \rangle$) correspond to the three cubic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\omega\sqrt[3]{7})$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\omega^2\sqrt[3]{7})$.
  ::: {.proof}
  Galois correspondence.
  :::
  <2>6. The intermediate fields that are **not Galois** over $\mathbb{Q}$ are precisely the three non-normal extensions of degree 3:
\[
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7}), \quad \mathbb{Q}(\omega\sqrt[3]{7}), \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbb{Q}(\omega^2\sqrt[3]{7}).
\]
::: {.proof}
their corresponding subgroups of order 2 are not normal in $S_3$.
:::

<1>2. Part 2: Splitting field $L/\mathbb{R}$ and its Galois group: <2>1. The real root $\sqrt[3]{7} \in \mathbb{R}$, while the roots $\omega\sqrt[3]{7}, \omega^2\sqrt[3]{7} \notin \mathbb{R}$.
::: {.proof}
non-zero imaginary parts.
:::
<2>2. Thus adjoining the roots of $f(x)$ over $\mathbb{R}$ gives $L = \mathbb{R}(\omega) = \mathbb{R}(i\sqrt{3}) = \mathbb{C}$.
::: {.proof}
$[\mathbb{C} : \mathbb{R}] = 2$.
:::
<2>3. Therefore $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{R}) = \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, generated by complex conjugation.
::: {.proof}
automorphism group of $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}$.
:::

<1>3. Part 3: Splitting field $M/\mathbb{F}_{13}$ and its Galois group: <2>1. Check for roots of $x^3 - 7$ in $\mathbb{F}_{13}$: Since $13 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, an element $a \in \mathbb{F}_{13}^\times$ is a cube if and only if $a^{(13-1)/3} = a^4 \equiv 1 \pmod{13}$.
For $a = 7$: $7^2 = 49 \equiv 10 \equiv -3 \pmod{13}$, so $7^4 \equiv (-3)^2 = 9 \not\equiv 1 \pmod{13}$.
Thus $7$ is not a cube in $\mathbb{F}_{13}$, so $x^3 - 7$ has no roots in $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ and is irreducible of degree 3 over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$.
::: {.proof}
Euler's criterion for cubic residues in $\mathbb{F}_{13}$.
:::
<2>2. The field $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ already contains the primitive 3rd roots of unity: $3^3 = 27 \equiv 1 \pmod{13}$, so $\omega = 3 \in \mathbb{F}_{13}$.
::: {.proof}
$3 \in \mathbb{F}_{13}^\times$ has order 3. <2>3. If $\alpha$ is one root of $x^3 - 7$ in an extension, then all three roots are $\{\alpha, 3\alpha, 9\alpha\}$, all of which lie in $\mathbb{F}_{13}(\alpha)$.
:::
::: {.proof}
$\omega = 3 \in \mathbb{F}_{13}$.
:::
<2>4. Thus the splitting field is $M = \mathbb{F}_{13}(\alpha) \cong \mathbb{F}_{13^3}$, which is an extension of degree 3 over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$.
::: {.proof}
degree of minimal polynomial $x^3 - 7$.
:::
<2>5. The Galois group of any degree $d$ extension of finite fields is cyclic of order $d$, generated by the Frobenius map:
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(M / \mathbb{F}_{13}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
Galois theory of finite fields.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:

1. $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$; non-Galois subfields are $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{7})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\omega\sqrt[3]{7})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\omega^2\sqrt[3]{7})$.

2. $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$.

3. $\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb{F}_{13}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$.
   ::: {.proof}
   <1>1, <1>2, and <1>3.
   :::
   Q.E.D.
:::
