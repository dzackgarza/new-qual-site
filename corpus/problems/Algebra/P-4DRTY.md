::: problem
Is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21})$ normal?
What is its splitting field?
What is its Galois group?
Describe the lattice of subfields.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Non-normality of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21})$: <2>1. The minimal polynomial of $\alpha = \sqrt[3]{21}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $f(x) = x^3 - 21$.
It is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein’s Criterion at the prime $p = 3$ (or $p = 7$).
::: {.proof}
Eisenstein's Criterion.
:::
<2>2. The roots of $f(x)$ in $\mathbb{C}$ are:
\[
\alpha_1 = \sqrt[3]{21}, \qquad \alpha_2 = \sqrt[3]{21} \, \omega, \qquad \alpha_3 = \sqrt[3]{21} \, \omega^2,
\]
where $\omega = e^{2\pi i / 3} = \frac{-1 + i\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is a primitive cube root of unity.
::: {.proof}
solving $x^3 = 21$ in $\mathbb{C}$.
:::
<2>3. $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}) \subset \mathbb{R}$ contains only the real root $\alpha_1$.
The non-real roots $\alpha_2, \alpha_3 \notin \mathbb{R}$, so $\alpha_2, \alpha_3 \notin \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21})$.
Thus $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21})$ is **not normal** over $\mathbb{Q}$.
::: {.proof}
normal extensions contain all conjugates of their elements.
:::

<1>2. The Splitting Field: <2>1. The splitting field of $x^3 - 21$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}, \omega) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}, i\sqrt{3})$.
::: {.proof}
generated by all three roots.
:::
<2>2. $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3$ and $[\mathbb{Q}(\omega) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2$.
Since $\gcd(3, 2) = 1$, the degree is $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = 3 \cdot 2 = 6$.
::: {.proof}
multiplicativity of degrees in field towers.
:::

<1>3. The Galois Group: <2>1. The discriminant of $x^3 - 21$ is $\Delta = -27(21)^2 = -11907 < 0$, which is not a square in $\mathbb{Q}$.
Thus the Galois group is the full symmetric group $S_3$:
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3 = \langle \sigma, \tau \mid \sigma^3 = 1, \, \tau^2 = 1, \, \tau \sigma \tau = \sigma^{-1} \rangle,
\]
where $\sigma(\sqrt[3]{21}) = \sqrt[3]{21}\omega, \, \sigma(\omega) = \omega$ (order 3) and $\tau(\sqrt[3]{21}) = \sqrt[3]{21}, \, \tau(\omega) = \omega^2$ (complex conjugation, order 2).
::: {.proof}
Galois group of irreducible cubic with non-square discriminant.
:::

<1>4. Lattice of Subfields: <2>1. By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, subfields of $K/\mathbb{Q}$ correspond bijectively (inclusion-reversing) to subgroups of $S_3$:

- $\{e\}$ (order 1) $\longleftrightarrow K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}, i\sqrt{3})$ (degree 6),

- $\langle \tau \rangle$ (order 2) $\longleftrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21})$ (degree 3),

- $\langle \sigma\tau \rangle$ (order 2) $\longleftrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}\omega^2)$ (degree 3),

- $\langle \sigma^2\tau \rangle$ (order 2) $\longleftrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}\omega)$ (degree 3),

- $A_3 = \langle \sigma \rangle$ (order 3, normal subgroup) $\longleftrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(\omega) = \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{3})$ (unique quadratic subfield, Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$),

- $S_3$ (order 6) $\longleftrightarrow \mathbb{Q}$ (degree 1).
::: {.proof}
Galois correspondence.
:::

<1>5. Conclusion: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21})$ is not normal, the splitting field is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}, i\sqrt{3})$ with Galois group $S_3$, and the intermediate subfields are the three non-normal cubic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{21}\omega^j)$ and the quadratic normal field $\mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{3})$.
Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>1 through <1>4.
