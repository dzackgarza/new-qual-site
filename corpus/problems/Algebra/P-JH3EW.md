::: {.problem}
Let $n_1,\ldots,n_m$ be nonzero integers, and let
$$
L=\QQ(\sqrt{n_1},\ldots,\sqrt{n_m}),
\qquad
\alpha=\sqrt{n_1}+\cdots+\sqrt{n_m}.
$$
Describe
$$
\Gal\bigl(L/\QQ(\alpha)\bigr).
$$
What happens when the square classes of the $n_i$ are linearly independent in $\QQ^\times/(\QQ^\times)^2$?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $d_1,\ldots,d_s$ be the distinct squarefree integers whose square classes contain some $n_i$. Each $n_i=d_jt_i^2$ for one $j$ and one rational $t_i>0$, and then $\sqrt{n_i}=t_i\sqrt{d_j}$. Grouping terms,
$$
\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^sC_j\sqrt{d_j},
\qquad
C_j=\sum_{i:\,n_i\in d_j(\QQ^\times)^2}t_i>0,
$$
and $L=\QQ(\sqrt{d_1},\ldots,\sqrt{d_s})$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #independent}

$\sqrt{d_1},\ldots,\sqrt{d_s}$ are linearly independent over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

Choose integers $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ whose square classes form an $\FF_2$-basis of the subgroup of $\QQ^\times/(\QQ^\times)^2$ generated by the classes of the $d_j$. By Kummer theory, $[\QQ(\sqrt{b_1},\ldots,\sqrt{b_r}):\QQ]=2^r$, so the $2^r$ products $\beta_S=\prod_{k\in S}\sqrt{b_k}$ for $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}$, which span this field, form a $\QQ$-basis of it. Each $d_j=c_j^2\prod_{k\in S_j}b_k$ for one subset $S_j$ and some $c_j\in\QQ^\times$, so $\sqrt{d_j}=\pm c_j\beta_{S_j}$. Distinct squarefree integers have distinct square classes, so the subsets $S_j$ are distinct, and the $\sqrt{d_j}$ are nonzero multiples of distinct basis vectors.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

$\Gal\bigl(L/\QQ(\alpha)\bigr)=\boxed{1}$, so $\QQ(\alpha)=L$, whether or not the square classes of the $n_i$ are independent.

::: pf-proof

Let $\sigma\in\Gal(L/\QQ)$ fix $\alpha$. Since $\sigma(\sqrt{d_j})^2=d_j$, we have $\sigma(\sqrt{d_j})=\varepsilon_j\sqrt{d_j}$ with $\varepsilon_j=\pm1$, so
$$
0=\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^sC_j(\varepsilon_j-1)\sqrt{d_j}.
$$
By step [](#independent){.pf-ref}, $C_j(\varepsilon_j-1)=0$ for all $j$, and $C_j>0$ gives $\varepsilon_j=1$. So $\sigma$ fixes every generator of $L$, and $\sigma=1$. By the Galois correspondence, $\QQ(\alpha)=L$. When the square classes of the $n_i$ are independent, $s=m$ and $[L:\QQ]=2^m$.

:::
