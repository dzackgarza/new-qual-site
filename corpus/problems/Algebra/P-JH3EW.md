::: {.problem}
Let
\[
L=\QQ(\sqrt{n_1},\ldots,\sqrt{n_m}),
\qquad
\alpha=\sqrt{n_1}+\cdots+\sqrt{n_m}.
\]
Describe
\[
\Gal\bigl(L/\QQ(\alpha)\bigr).
\]
What happens when the square classes of the $n_i$ are linearly independent in $\QQ^\times/(\QQ^\times)^2$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The extension $L/\QQ$ is multiquadratic. Let $r$ be the dimension over $\FF_2$ of the subgroup of $\QQ^\times/(\QQ^\times)^2$ generated by the square classes of $n_1,\ldots,n_m$. Then
\[
\Gal(L/\QQ)\cong (C_2)^r.
\]
Every automorphism is determined by compatible sign changes of the square roots.

By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory,
\[
\Gal(L/\QQ(\alpha))
=
\{\sigma\in\Gal(L/\QQ):\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha\}.
\]
Thus the answer in complete generality is the stabilizer of the sum
\[
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^m\sqrt{n_i}
\]
under the sign-change action.

Suppose now that the square classes of $n_1,\ldots,n_m$ are linearly independent in
\[
\QQ^\times/(\QQ^\times)^2.
\]
Then $r=m$, and
\[
1,\sqrt{n_1},\ldots,\sqrt{n_m}
\]
are linearly independent over $\QQ$ as part of the standard multiquadratic basis. If
\[
\sigma(\sqrt{n_i})=\varepsilon_i\sqrt{n_i},
\qquad \varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\},
\]
and $\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha$, then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m(\varepsilon_i-1)\sqrt{n_i}=0.
\]
Linear independence forces every $\varepsilon_i=1$. Hence the stabilizer is trivial:
\[
\Gal(L/\QQ(\alpha))=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\QQ(\alpha)=L
\]
in the square-class-independent case.

When square-class relations are present, the stabilizer can be nontrivial; the displayed stabilizer formula is the exact general answer.
:::
