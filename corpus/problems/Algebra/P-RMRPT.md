::: {.problem}
What are the group characters of the multiplicative group of a finite field $\FF_q^\times$?
:::

::: {.solution}
The group $\FF_q^\times$ is cyclic of order $q-1$. Fix a generator $g$ and let $\zeta\coloneqq e^{2\pi i/(q-1)}$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #defined}
For each $k\in\ZZ/(q-1)\ZZ$, $\chi_k(g^m)\coloneqq\zeta^{km}$ defines a character $\chi_k:\FF_q^\times\to\CC^\times$, and $\chi_k\ne\chi_l$ for $k\ne l$.

::: pf-proof
If $g^m=g^{m'}$, then $(q-1)\mid m-m'$, so $\zeta^{km}=\zeta^{km'}$. Multiplicativity is $\zeta^{k(m+m')}=\zeta^{km}\zeta^{km'}$. The values $\chi_k(g)=\zeta^k$ are distinct for distinct $k$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #exhaust}
Every character $\chi:\FF_q^\times\to\CC^\times$ is some $\chi_k$.

::: pf-proof
$\chi$ is determined by $\chi(g)$, and $\chi(g)^{q-1}=\chi(g^{q-1})=1$, so $\chi(g)=\zeta^k$ for some $k$, and then $\chi=\chi_k$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
The characters of $\FF_q^\times$ are exactly $\boxed{\chi_0,\ldots,\chi_{q-2}}$, and $\Hom(\FF_q^\times,\CC^\times)\cong C_{q-1}$, generated by $\chi_1$. For odd $q$, $\chi_{(q-1)/2}$ is the unique character of order $2$, the quadratic character $x\mapsto\pm1$ according as $x$ is a square or not.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#defined){.pf-ref} and [](#exhaust){.pf-ref} give the list, and $\chi_k\chi_l=\chi_{k+l}$ gives the group structure. The elements of order $2$ in $\ZZ/(q-1)\ZZ$ are $k=(q-1)/2$ only, and $\chi_{(q-1)/2}(g^m)=(-1)^m$, which is $1$ exactly on the squares $g^{2j}$.
:::
