::: problem
(1) Find the Galois group of $f(x) = x^3 + 2x + 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
(2) Let $\alpha$ be a root of $f(x)$. What is the degree $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}]$?
(3) Can you say something about the roots of $g(x) = x^3 + 3x + 1$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Compute $\operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q})$ for $f(x) = x^3 + 2x + 1$ and analyze $g(x) = x^3 + 3x + 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$.

<1>1. Irreducibility and Discriminant of $f(x) = x^3 + 2x + 1$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By the Rational Root Theorem, any rational root of $f(x)$ must be in $\{+1, -1\}$.
        - $f(1) = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4 \ne 0$.
        - $f(-1) = -1 - 2 + 1 = -2 \ne 0$.
        Since $f(x)$ has degree 3 and no rational roots, $f(x)$ is **irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$**.
    <2>2. The discriminant of a cubic $x^3 + px + q$ is $\Delta = -4p^3 - 27q^2$.
        For $p = 2, q = 1$:
        $$\Delta(f) = -4(2)^3 - 27(1)^2 = -32 - 27 = -59.$$
    <2>3. Because $-59 < 0$, $\Delta(f)$ is not a square in $\mathbb{Q}$ (and $f$ has 1 real root and 2 complex conjugate non-real roots).
    <2>4. Therefore, the Galois group over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the full symmetric group:
        $$\operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3.$$

<1>2. Degree of $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $f(x)$ is monic and irreducible of degree 3 over $\mathbb{Q}$, $f(x)$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$.
    <2>2. Thus $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3$.

<1>3. Analysis of $g(x) = x^3 + 3x + 1$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Test $g(x)$ for rational roots: $g(1) = 5 \ne 0$, $g(-1) = -3 \ne 0$. Thus $g(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.
    <2>2. The discriminant of $g(x)$ (with $p = 3, q = 1$) is:
        $$\Delta(g) = -4(3)^3 - 27(1)^2 = -108 - 27 = -135 = -3^3 \cdot 5 = -15 \cdot 9.$$
    <2>3. If $g(x)$ had a root $\beta \in \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, then since $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3$ and $[\mathbb{Q}(\beta) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3$, we would have $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$.
    <2>4. If two cubic extensions coincide, their Galois closures must coincide, so the quadratic subfields generated by the square roots of their discriminants must coincide:
        $$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta(f)}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta(g)}) \implies \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-59}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-135}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15}).$$
    <2>5. But $\sqrt{-59 / -15} = \sqrt{59/15} \notin \mathbb{Q}$, so $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-59}) \ne \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$.
    <2>6. Thus the Galois closures of $f$ and $g$ are distinct, which forces $g(x)$ to have **no roots in $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$**.
    <2>7. Therefore, $g(x)$ remains irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $\operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$, $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3$, and $g(x) = x^3 + 3x + 1$ has no roots in $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ (remains irreducible). Q.E.D.
:::
