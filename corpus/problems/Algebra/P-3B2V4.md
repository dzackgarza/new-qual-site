::: {.exercise}
1. Find the Galois group over $\mathbb Q$ of $f(x)=x^3+2x+1$.
2. If $\alpha$ is a root, compute $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]$.
3. Determine whether $g(x)=x^3+3x+1$ has a root in $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The polynomial
\[
f(x)=x^3+2x+1
\]
has no rational root, hence is irreducible. Its discriminant is
\[
\Delta_f=-4(2)^3-27=-59,
\]
which is not a square in $\mathbb Q$. Therefore the Galois group of its splitting field is
\[
S_3.
\]
Since $f$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$,
\[
[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=3.
\]

Now
\[
g(x)=x^3+3x+1
\]
also has no rational root, so it is irreducible of degree $3$, and
\[
\Delta_g=-4(3)^3-27=-135=-9\cdot15.
\]
Suppose $g$ had a root $\beta\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Then
\[
[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]=3=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q],
\]
so $\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Their normal closures over $\mathbb Q$ would therefore coincide. For an irreducible cubic with Galois group $S_3$, the unique quadratic subfield of its normal closure is generated by the square root of the discriminant. Hence we would have
\[
\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-59})=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-15}),
\]
which is false because $59/15$ is not a rational square. Thus $g$ has no root in $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$; since it is cubic, it remains irreducible over $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$.
:::
