::: {.problem}
(a) Find the Galois group over $\QQ$ of $f(x)=x^3+2x+1$.

(b) If $\alpha$ is a root, compute $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]$.

(c) Determine whether $g(x)=x^3+3x+1$ has a root in $\QQ(\alpha)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
For a cubic $x^3+px+q$, the discriminant is $-4p^3-27q^2$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #irreducible}

$f$ and $g$ are irreducible over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof

A cubic over $\QQ$ is reducible only if it has a rational root, and by the rational root theorem the only candidates are $\pm1$. Now $f(1)=4$, $f(-1)=-2$, $g(1)=5$, $g(-1)=-3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #galois}

(a) The Galois group of $f$ over $\QQ$ is $\boxed{S_3}$, and the unique quadratic subfield of the splitting field of $f$ is $\QQ(\sqrt{-59})$.

::: pf-proof

$\Delta_f=-4\cdot2^3-27=-59$, which is not a square in $\QQ$. By step [](#irreducible){.pf-ref} the Galois group is a transitive subgroup of $S_3$, hence $A_3$ or $S_3$; it is not contained in $A_3$ because $\Delta_f$ is not a square. The fixed field of $A_3$ is $\QQ(\sqrt{\Delta_f})$, and it is the only quadratic subfield since $A_3$ is the only subgroup of index $2$ in $S_3$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=\boxed{3}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#irreducible){.pf-ref}, $f$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #galois-g}

The Galois group of $g$ is $S_3$, and the unique quadratic subfield of the splitting field of $g$ is $\QQ(\sqrt{-15})$.

::: pf-proof

$\Delta_g=-4\cdot3^3-27=-135=-9\cdot15$ is not a square in $\QQ$, and $\QQ(\sqrt{-135})=\QQ(\sqrt{-15})$. The argument of step [](#galois){.pf-ref} applies.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(c) $g$ has $\boxed{\text{no}}$ root in $\QQ(\alpha)$.

::: pf-proof

Suppose $\beta\in\QQ(\alpha)$ is a root of $g$. By step [](#irreducible){.pf-ref}, $[\QQ(\beta):\QQ]=3=[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]$, so $\QQ(\beta)=\QQ(\alpha)$. The splitting field of $f$ (respectively $g$) is the normal closure of $\QQ(\alpha)$ (respectively $\QQ(\beta)$) over $\QQ$, so the two splitting fields coincide. By steps [](#galois){.pf-ref} and [](#galois-g){.pf-ref}, their unique quadratic subfields would then agree:
$$
\QQ(\sqrt{-59})=\QQ(\sqrt{-15}).
$$
This is false, because $(-59)(-15)=885$ is not a rational square.

:::
