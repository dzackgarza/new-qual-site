::: {.problem}
Compute all intermediate fields of $\QQ(2^{1/4},\zeta_8)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Put $\alpha=2^{1/4}$ and $L=\QQ(\alpha,\zeta_8)$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #galois}

$L=\QQ(\alpha,i)$ is the splitting field of $x^4-2$ over $\QQ$, of degree $8$, and $G=\Gal(L/\QQ)\cong D_8$ is generated by
$$
r:\alpha\mapsto i\alpha,\ i\mapsto i,
\qquad
s:\alpha\mapsto\alpha,\ i\mapsto -i.
$$

::: pf-proof

Since $\zeta_8=(1+i)/\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt2=\alpha^2$, $\QQ(\alpha,\zeta_8)=\QQ(\alpha,i)$, and the roots of $x^4-2$ are $\pm\alpha,\pm i\alpha$.
The polynomial $x^4-2$ is Eisenstein at $2$, so $[\QQ(\alpha):\QQ]=4$, and $i\notin\QQ(\alpha)\subset\RR$, so $[L:\QQ]=8$.
An automorphism is determined by $\alpha\mapsto i^k\alpha$ and $i\mapsto\pm i$, and all $8$ choices occur; $r$ has order $4$, $s$ has order $2$, and $srs=r^{-1}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #quadratic}

The quadratic subfields are $\QQ(i)$, $\QQ(\sqrt2)$ and $\QQ(\sqrt{-2})$.

::: pf-proof

They correspond to the three subgroups of order $4$: $\langle r\rangle$ fixes $i$, $\langle r^2,s\rangle$ fixes $\alpha^2=\sqrt2$, and $\langle r^2,rs\rangle$ fixes $i\alpha^2=\sqrt{-2}$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #quartic}

The quartic subfields are $\QQ(i,\sqrt2)=\QQ(\zeta_8)$, $\QQ(\alpha)$, $\QQ(i\alpha)$, $\QQ((1+i)\alpha)$ and $\QQ((1-i)\alpha)$.

::: pf-proof

They correspond to the five subgroups of order $2$.
The center $\langle r^2\rangle$ fixes $i$ and $\alpha^2$, hence $\QQ(i,\sqrt2)$.
The reflections $s$, $r^2s$, $rs$ and $r^3s$ fix $\alpha$, $i\alpha$, $(1+i)\alpha$ and $(1-i)\alpha$ respectively, and each of these elements has four distinct images under $G$, so it has degree $4$ over $\QQ$ and generates the whole fixed field.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The intermediate fields of $L/\QQ$ are $\QQ$, $L$, and the eight fields of steps [](#quadratic){.pf-ref} and [](#quartic){.pf-ref}.

::: pf-proof

By the Galois correspondence they are the fixed fields of the ten subgroups of $D_8$ found in step [](#galois){.pf-ref}: the trivial group, five of order $2$, three of order $4$, and $G$.

:::
