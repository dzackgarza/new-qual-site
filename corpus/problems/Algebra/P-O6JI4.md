::: {.problem}
(a) What is the symmetry group of an icosahedron?

(b) What is the stabiliser of an edge?

(c) How many edges are there?

(d) How do you know the symmetry group of the icosahedron is the same as the symmetry group of the dodecahedron?

(e) State the classification of regular convex polytopes in dimension at least $4$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $I$ be the group of rotations of $\RR^3$ preserving a regular icosahedron $X$ centred at the origin, and $I_h$ the group of all isometries preserving $X$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #edges}

(c) The icosahedron has $\boxed{30}$ edges.

::: pf-proof

It has $20$ triangular faces, and each edge lies on exactly two faces, so $2E=3\cdot20$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #group}

(a) $I\cong A_5$, of order $60$, and $I_h=I\times\{\pm I_3\}\cong A_5\times\ZZ/2$, of order $120$.

::: pf-proof

$I$ acts transitively on the $12$ vertices, and the stabilizer of a vertex is the cyclic group of order $5$ of rotations about the axis through it, so $\abs{I}=60$ by the orbit-stabilizer theorem. The $30$ edges form $15$ pairs of opposite edges, and these pairs fall into $5$ triples whose midpoint axes are mutually orthogonal; $I$ permutes the $5$ triples, and the only rotation fixing every triple is the identity, so $I$ embeds in $S_5$. A subgroup of order $60$ of $S_5$ is $A_5$. The central inversion $-I_3$ preserves $X$, is not a rotation, and commutes with every element of $I$; since $[I_h:I]=2$, we get $I_h=I\times\{\pm I_3\}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) The stabilizer of an edge $e$ in $I$ is cyclic of order $2$, generated by the half-turn about the axis through the midpoint of $e$; in $I_h$ it is a Klein four-group.

::: pf-proof

$I$ and $I_h$ act transitively on the edges, so by step [](#edges){.pf-ref}, step [](#group){.pf-ref} and the orbit-stabilizer theorem the stabilizers have orders $60/30=2$ and $120/30=4$. The half-turn about the midpoint axis of $e$ fixes $e$, so it generates the stabilizer in $I$. In $I_h$ the stabilizer contains the half-turn, the reflection in the plane through $e$ and the centre, and the reflection in the perpendicular bisector plane of $e$; these are commuting involutions, so the stabilizer is $\ZZ/2\times\ZZ/2$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(d) The icosahedron and the dodecahedron have the same symmetry group.

::: pf-proof

The centres of the $20$ faces of $X$ are the vertices of a regular dodecahedron $Y$, the dual of $X$. An isometry fixing the origin and preserving $X$ permutes its faces, hence its face centres, hence preserves $Y$; dually, the face centres of $Y$ are the vertices of a scaled copy of $X$, so every isometry preserving $Y$ preserves $X$. Thus the two symmetry groups are equal as subgroups of $O(3)$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(e) In dimension $4$ there are exactly six regular convex polytopes: the $5$-cell (simplex), the $8$-cell (tesseract), the $16$-cell (cross-polytope), the $24$-cell, the $120$-cell and the $600$-cell. In each dimension $d\ge5$ there are exactly three: the $d$-simplex, the $d$-cube and the $d$-dimensional cross-polytope.

::: pf-proof

This is Schläfli's classification; the symmetry groups are the finite Coxeter groups $A_d$, $B_d$, $F_4$ and $H_4$.

:::
