::: {.problem}
18. Prove that the multiplicative group of units of a finite field must be cyclic, and so is generated by a single element.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

Let $F$ be a finite field and put $G=F^\times$.
Then $G$ is a finite abelian group.

::: pf-proof

Multiplication in a field is commutative, and every nonzero element is a unit.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Let
\[
m=\operatorname{lcm}\{\operatorname{ord}(g):g\in G\},
\]
the exponent of $G$.
There exists an element $h\in G$ of order exactly $m$.

::: pf-proof

Write
\[
m=\prod_{\ell}\ell^{a_\ell}
\]
over the finitely many primes dividing $m$.
For each such prime $\ell$, by the definition of the least common multiple there is some $g_\ell\in G$ whose order has $\ell$-adic valuation $a_\ell$.
If $\operatorname{ord}(g_\ell)=\ell^{a_\ell}r_\ell$ with $\gcd(\ell,r_\ell)=1$, then
\[
h_\ell=g_\ell^{r_\ell}
\]
has order $\ell^{a_\ell}$.
Because $G$ is abelian and the orders of the $h_\ell$ are pairwise coprime, the product
\[
h=\prod_\ell h_\ell
\]
has order
\[
\prod_\ell \ell^{a_\ell}=m.
\]

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s3}

Every element of $G$ is a root of the polynomial $x^m-1\in F[x]$.

::: pf-proof

For $g\in G$, its order divides $m$ by the definition of $m$, so $g^m=1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Hence $|G|\le m$.

::: pf-proof

A nonzero polynomial of degree $m$ over a field has at most $m$ roots.
By step [](#s3){.pf-ref}, all $|G|$ elements of $G$ are roots of $x^m-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s5}

Since $h\in G$ has order $m$, one also has $m\le |G|$.
Therefore $m=|G|$.

::: pf-proof

The cyclic subgroup $\langle h\rangle$ has exactly $m$ elements and is contained in $G$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

Thus $\langle h\rangle=G$, so the multiplicative group of a finite field is cyclic.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#s5){.pf-ref}, the subgroup $\langle h\rangle$ and the whole group $G$ have the same finite cardinality.

:::
