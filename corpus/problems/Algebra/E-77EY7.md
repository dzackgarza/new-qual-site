::: problem
18. Prove that the multiplicative group of units of a finite field must be cyclic, and so is generated by a single element.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Let \(F\) be a finite field and let \(G=F^\times\). Then \(G\) is a finite abelian group.
::: {.proof}
Multiplication in a field is commutative, and every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse.
:::

<1>2. Let \(m\) be the exponent of \(G\), i.e. the least common multiple of the orders of all elements of \(G\). Then every element of \(G\) is a root of \(x^m-1\in F[x]\).
::: {.proof}
For \(g\in G\), the order of \(g\) divides \(m\), so \(g^m=1\).
:::

<1>3. One has \(|G|\le m\).
::: {.proof}
By <1>2, all \(|G|\) elements of \(G\) are roots of the nonzero polynomial \(x^m-1\), which has degree \(m\). A nonzero polynomial over a field has at most as many roots as its degree.
:::

<1>4. One also has \(m\le |G|\).
::: {.proof}
By the structure theorem for finite abelian groups, \(G\cong C_{n_1}\times\cdots\times C_{n_r}\) with \(n_1\mid\cdots\mid n_r\). The exponent is \(m=n_r\), while
\[
|G|=n_1\cdots n_r,
\]
so \(m\le |G|\).
:::

<1>5. Therefore \(m=|G|\), and \(G\) is cyclic.
::: {.proof}
From <1>3 and <1>4, \(m=|G|\). In the invariant-factor decomposition from <1>4, the element whose last coordinate generates \(C_{n_r}\) and whose other coordinates are chosen compatibly has order equal to the exponent \(m\); equivalently, finite abelian groups contain an element whose order equals their exponent. Thus \(G\) contains an element of order \(|G|\), which generates all of \(G\).
:::
:::
