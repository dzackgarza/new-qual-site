::: problem
Give an example of a Principal Ideal Domain (PID) with a unique non-zero prime ideal.
Prove that it is a PID and classify all of its ideals.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Exhibit a local PID (a Discrete Valuation Ring, DVR) having a unique non-zero prime ideal, and classify its ideal lattice.

<1>1. Candidate Ring: Localization of $\mathbb{Z}$ at $(p)$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Fix a prime integer $p$ (e.g. $p = 2$).
    <2>2. Let $R = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ be the localization of the integers $\mathbb{Z}$ at the prime ideal $(p)$:
        $$R = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \left\{ \frac{a}{b} \in \mathbb{Q} \;\middle|\; a, b \in \mathbb{Z}, \; p \nmid b \right\}.$$
    <2>3. *(Alternative candidate: The formal power series ring $k[[t]]$ over a field $k$, or the $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p$).*

<1>2. Proof that $R = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ is a Principal Ideal Domain:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $R$ is a subring of the field $\mathbb{Q}$, so $R$ is an integral domain.
    <2>2. Any non-zero element $x \in R$ can be uniquely written as:
        $$x = p^k \frac{u}{v}$$
        where $k \ge 0$ is a non-negative integer (the $p$-adic valuation $v_p(x) = k$), and $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $p \nmid u$ and $p \nmid v$.
    <2>3. Since $p \nmid u$, $\frac{u}{v}$ is invertible in $R$, so $\frac{u}{v} \in R^\times$.
    <2>4. Thus, every non-zero element of $R$ has the form $x = p^k u$ for a unit $u \in R^\times$.
    <2>5. Let $I \subseteq R$ be any non-zero ideal of $R$.
    <2>6. Consider the non-empty set of valuations $S = \{v_p(x) \mid x \in I \setminus \{0\}\} \subset \mathbb{N}_0$.
    <2>7. By the Well-Ordering Principle of $\mathbb{N}_0$, $S$ has a minimum element $n = \min(S) \ge 0$.
    <2>8. There exists an element $y = p^n u_0 \in I$ with $u_0 \in R^\times$.
    <2>9. Since $u_0$ is a unit, $p^n = y u_0^{-1} \in I$, so $(p^n) \subseteq I$.
    <2>10. Conversely, for any $x \in I \setminus \{0\}$, $x = p^k u$ with $k \ge n$.
        Then $x = p^n (p^{k-n} u) \in (p^n)$.
    <2>11. Therefore:
        $$I = (p^n) = p^n R.$$
    <2>12. Since every ideal is generated by a single element $p^n$, $R$ is a **Principal Ideal Domain**.

<1>3. Classification of Prime Ideals of $R$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The complete list of ideals of $R$ is the strictly descending chain:
        $$R = (1) \supset (p) \supset (p^2) \supset (p^3) \supset \cdots \supset (0).$$
    <2>2. **Prime Ideals:**
        - $(0)$ is prime because $R$ is an integral domain.
        - $(p)$ is prime (and maximal) because $R/(p) \cong \mathbb{F}_p$ is a field.
        - For $n \ge 2$, $(p^n)$ is not prime because $p \cdot p^{n-1} \in (p^n)$ but $p \notin (p^n)$ and $p^{n-1} \notin (p^n)$.
    <2>3. Therefore, $(p) = p \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ is the **unique non-zero prime ideal** (and unique maximal ideal) of $R$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $R = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ (or $k[[t]]$) is a PID whose only non-zero prime ideal is $(p)$ (or $(t)$). Q.E.D.
:::
