::: {.problem}
Give an example of a Principal Ideal Domain (PID) with a unique non-zero prime ideal.
Prove that it is a PID and classify all of its ideals.
:::

::: {.solution}
Fix a prime $p$ and let $R=\ZZ_{(p)}=\theset{a/b\in\QQ: p\nmid b}$, with $v_p$ the exponent of $p$ in a nonzero rational and $R^\times$ its group of units.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #ring}
$R$ is a subring of $\QQ$, hence a domain, and $R^\times=\theset{a/b\in R: p\nmid a}$.

::: pf-proof
$R$ is closed under subtraction and multiplication: sums and products of fractions with denominators prime to $p$ again have denominators prime to $p$, since a product of integers prime to $p$ is prime to $p$; and $1=1/1\in R$. So $R$ is a subring of the field $\QQ$, hence a domain. If $a/b\in R$ has $p\nmid a$ then $b/a\in R$ and $(a/b)(b/a)=1$, so it is a unit; conversely if $a/b$ is a unit with inverse $c/d\in R$ then $ac=bd$, and $p\nmid b$, $p\nmid d$ force $p\nmid a$ and $p\nmid c$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #valuation}
Every nonzero $x\in R$ has a unique expression $x=p^nu$ with $n\geq0$ and $u\in R^\times$.

::: pf-proof
Write $x=a/b$ with $p\nmid b$. If $a=0$ then $x=0$, which is excluded, so $a\neq0$ and $n\coloneqq v_p(a)\geq0$ is defined; write $a=p^na'$ with $p\nmid a'$. Then $x=p^n\,(a'/b)$, and $a'/b\in R^\times$ by step [](#ring){.pf-ref}. Uniqueness: if $p^nu=p^mv$ with $u,v$ units and $n>m$, then $p^{n-m}=vu^{-1}$ is a unit, which is impossible since a unit $c/d\in R$ with $p\nmid c$ cannot equal a positive power of $p$; so $n=m$ and $u=v$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #ideals}
Every ideal of $R$ is $(0)$ or $(p^n)$ for some $n\geq0$; in particular $R$ is a PID.

::: pf-proof
Let $I\trianglelefteq R$ be nonzero. The set $S=\theset{v_p(x):0\neq x\in I}$ is a nonempty subset of $\NN$, so it has a least element $n$.

::: {.pf-step #lower}
$(p^n)\subseteq I$.

::: pf-proof
Choose $x\in I$ with $x=p^nu$ and $u\in R^\times$, which step [](#valuation){.pf-ref} permits. Then $p^n=xu^{-1}\in I$ because $I$ is an ideal and $u^{-1}\in R$, so the ideal generated by $p^n$ lies in $I$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #upper}
$I\subseteq(p^n)$.

::: pf-proof
Let $0\neq y\in I$ and write $y=p^mv$ with $v\in R^\times$; by definition of $n$ as the minimum of $S$ we have $m\geq n$, so $y=p^n\,p^{m-n}v\in(p^n)$. And $0\in(p^n)$. Hence $I\subseteq(p^n)$.
:::

:::

Steps [](#lower){.pf-ref} and [](#upper){.pf-ref} give $I=(p^n)$, and every nonzero ideal is principal; with $(0)$ also principal, $R$ is a PID. The value $n=0$ gives $(p^0)=(1)=R$, so the complete list of ideals is $(0)$ and $(p^n)$ for $n\geq0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #primes}
$(0)$ is prime, $(p)$ is maximal, and no $(p^n)$ with $n\geq2$ is prime.

::: pf-proof
$(0)$ is prime because $R$ is a domain by step [](#ring){.pf-ref}. For $(p)$: the map $\varphi\colon R\to\FF_p$, $a/b\mapsto(a\bmod p)(b\bmod p)^{-1}$, is a well-defined ring homomorphism — $b\bmod p\neq0$ since $p\nmid b$ — it is surjective because $a/1\mapsto a\bmod p$, and its kernel is exactly $(p)$: $a/b\in\ker\varphi$ iff $p\mid a$ iff $a/b=p\,(a/p)/b\in(p)$. A surjective ring homomorphism with kernel $I$ induces $R/I\cong\FF_p$, and since $\FF_p$ is a field $(p)$ is maximal, hence prime.

For $n\geq2$ the product $p\cdot p^{n-1}=p^n$ lies in $(p^n)$; but $p\notin(p^n)$ and $p^{n-1}\notin(p^n)$, because an element of $(p^n)$ has $p$-valuation at least $n$ by step [](#valuation){.pf-ref}, whereas $v_p(p)=1<n$ and $v_p(p^{n-1})=n-1<n$. So $(p^n)$ is not prime.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$R=\ZZ_{(p)}$ is a PID with exactly one nonzero prime ideal, namely $(p)$.

::: pf-proof
$R$ is a PID by step [](#ideals){.pf-ref}, and its ideals are $(0)$ and $(p^n)$ for $n\geq0$ by the same step. By step [](#primes){.pf-ref}, $(0)$ is prime, $(p)$ is prime, and $(p^n)$ is not prime for $n\geq2$; the whole ring $R=(p^0)$ is not prime because a prime ideal is proper and $R$ has a nonzero element. So $(p)$ is the only nonzero prime ideal.
:::
