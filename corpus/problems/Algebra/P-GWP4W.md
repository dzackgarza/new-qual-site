::: problem
- Prove that every simple abelian group is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
**Goal:** Let $G$ be a simple abelian group.
Prove that $G$ is cyclic (and isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ for some prime $p$).

<1>1. Definitions: <2>1. A group $G$ is simple if $G \neq \{e\}$ and its only normal subgroups are $\{e\}$ and $G$.
Proof: Standard definition of a simple group.
<2>2. In an abelian group $G$, every subgroup $H \le G$ is normal in $G$.
Proof: For all $g \in G$ and $h \in H$, $g h g^{-1} = g g^{-1} h = h \in H$, so $g H g^{-1} = H$.
<2>3. Consequently, if $G$ is a simple abelian group, its only subgroups (not just normal subgroups) are $\{e\}$ and $G$.
Proof: Since every subgroup is normal in $G$.

<1>2. Proof that $G$ is cyclic: <2>1. Since $G$ is simple, $G \neq \{e\}$, so there exists an element $x \in G$ such that $x \neq e$.
Proof: By definition of simple group.
<2>2. Consider the cyclic subgroup $\langle x \rangle = \{x^k \mid k \in \mathbb{Z}\} \le G$.
Proof: Standard definition of the subgroup generated by an element.
<2>3. $\langle x \rangle \neq \{e\}$.
Proof: $x \in \langle x \rangle$ and $x \neq e$.
<2>4. Since the only subgroups of $G$ are $\{e\}$ and $G$ (<1>1.<2>3), we must have $\langle x \rangle = G$.
Proof: $\langle x \rangle$ is a non-trivial subgroup of $G$.
<2>5. Therefore, $G$ is cyclic, generated by $x$.
Proof: By definition of cyclic group.

<1>3. Characterization of the order of $G$: <2>1. If $|G| = \infty$, then $G \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
But $\mathbb{Z}$ has proper non-trivial subgroups such as $2\mathbb{Z} = \langle 2 \rangle \lneq \mathbb{Z}$, contradicting simplicity.
Proof: $2\mathbb{Z} \neq \{0\}$ and $2\mathbb{Z} \neq \mathbb{Z}$.
<2>2. Thus $G$ must be finite, with order $n = |G| \ge 2$.
Proof: $G \neq \{e\}$ is not infinite cyclic.
<2>3. If $n$ is composite, say $n = a b$ with $1 < a < n$, then the element $x^a$ generates a proper non-trivial subgroup $\langle x^a \rangle \le G$ of order $b$, where $1 < b < n$, contradicting simplicity.
Proof: Subgroups of finite cyclic groups correspond to divisors of $n$.
<2>4. Therefore, $|G| = p$ for some prime $p$, so $G \cong \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.
Proof: The only integers $n \ge 2$ with no proper non-trivial divisors are primes.

<1>4. Conclusion: Every simple abelian group is cyclic (of prime order).
Proof: By <1>2 and <1>3.
:::
