::: problem
What is rational canonical form?
:::


::: {.solution}
Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $F$.

<1>1. Regard $V$ as an $F[x]$-module by letting
\[
x\cdot v=T(v).
\]
::: {.proof}
For a polynomial $f(x)=\sum_i a_ix^i$, define
\[
f(x)\cdot v=f(T)v=\sum_i a_iT^i(v).
\]
This gives an $F[x]$-module structure. Since $V$ is finite-dimensional over $F$, it is finitely generated as an $F[x]$-module. It is torsion because the minimal polynomial $m_T$ satisfies $m_T(T)=0$.
:::

<1>2. By the structure theorem for finitely generated torsion modules over the PID $F[x]$,
\[
V\cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^r F[x]/(f_i(x)),
\qquad
f_1\mid f_2\mid\cdots\mid f_r,
\]
for uniquely determined monic invariant factors $f_i$.
::: {.proof}
The ring $F[x]$ is a PID. Applying the invariant-factor form of the structure theorem gives the decomposition, with uniqueness of the monic factors subject to the divisibility chain.
:::

<1>3. On a cyclic summand $F[x]/(f)$, multiplication by $x$ is represented in the basis
\[
1,x,\ldots,x^{d-1},\qquad d=\deg f,
\]
by the companion matrix $C(f)$.
::: {.proof}
Write
\[
f(x)=x^d+a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0.
\]
Multiplication by $x$ sends $x^i$ to $x^{i+1}$ for $i<d-1$, while in the quotient
\[
x^d=-a_0-a_1x-\cdots-a_{d-1}x^{d-1}.
\]
These are exactly the columns of the companion matrix $C(f)$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore the matrix of $T$ is similar to
\[
C(f_1)\oplus C(f_2)\oplus\cdots\oplus C(f_r).
\]
This block diagonal matrix is the **rational canonical form** of $T$.
::: {.proof}
Choose on each cyclic summand the basis from <1>3 and concatenate these bases. Under the module isomorphism from <1>2, multiplication by $x$ corresponds to $T$, so its matrix is precisely the displayed block diagonal matrix.
:::

<1>5. The rational canonical form is unique up to the order convention on the blocks, and it exists over every field.
::: {.proof}
The invariant factors $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ are uniquely determined by the $F[x]$-module $V_T$, so the corresponding companion blocks are uniquely determined. No splitting of the polynomials is required, unlike Jordan form, so rational canonical form exists over an arbitrary field.
:::

<1>6. The largest invariant factor is the minimal polynomial, and the product of the invariant factors is the characteristic polynomial:
\[
m_T=f_r,
\qquad
\chi_T=\prod_{i=1}^r f_i.
\]
::: {.proof}
The annihilator of the direct sum is the least common multiple of the annihilators $(f_i)$, which is $(f_r)$ because of the divisibility chain; hence $m_T=f_r$. Also the characteristic polynomial of a companion matrix $C(f_i)$ is $f_i$, and characteristic polynomials multiply across block diagonal sums.
:::
:::
