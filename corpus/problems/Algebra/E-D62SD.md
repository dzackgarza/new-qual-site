:::{.exercise}
Consider the Vandermonde matrix:
\[
A \da 
\left(\begin{array}{ccc}
1 & \cdots & 1 \\
\lambda_{1} & \cdots & \lambda_{k} \\
\vdots & & \vdots \\
\lambda_{1}^{k-1} & \cdots & \lambda_{k}^{k-1}
\end{array}\right)
.\]

Show that
\[
\det A = \prod_{i < j} (\lambda_j - \lambda_i)
.\]
:::


::: {.solution}
Regard $D(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)=\det A$ as a polynomial in the $\lambda_i$.

<1>1. For every $i<j$, the polynomial $\lambda_j-\lambda_i$ divides $D$.
::: {.proof}
Interchanging $\lambda_i$ and $\lambda_j$ interchanges columns $i$ and $j$ of $A$, so it changes the sign of $D$. In particular, after setting $\lambda_i=\lambda_j$, the two columns coincide and $D=0$. Viewing $D$ as a polynomial in $\lambda_j$ over the coefficient ring generated by the other variables, the factor theorem gives
\[
\lambda_j-\lambda_i\mid D.
\]
:::

<1>2. Hence
\[
\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)\mid D.
\]
::: {.proof}
The factors $\lambda_j-\lambda_i$ are pairwise nonassociate irreducible polynomials in the UFD generated by the $\lambda_i$, hence are pairwise relatively prime. By <1>1 each divides $D$, so their product divides $D$.
:::

<1>3. Both $D$ and the product in <1>2 have total degree $k(k-1)/2$.
::: {.proof}
Expanding the determinant,
\[
D=\sum_{\sigma\in S_k}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
   \prod_{r=1}^k \lambda_{\sigma(r)}^{r-1}.
\]
Every monomial in this sum has total degree
\[
0+1+\cdots+(k-1)=\frac{k(k-1)}2.
\]
The product $\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$ has one linear factor for each of the $\binom{k}{2}$ pairs, so it has the same total degree.
:::

<1>4. The quotient in <1>2 is the constant $1$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>2 and <1>3, there is a constant $c$ such that
\[
D=c\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i).
\]
Consider the monomial
\[
\lambda_2\lambda_3^2\cdots\lambda_k^{k-1}.
\]
In the determinant expansion of <1>3, this monomial occurs only for the identity permutation and has coefficient $1$. In the product, it is obtained uniquely by choosing the term $\lambda_j$ from every factor with second index $j$, and again has coefficient $1$. Hence $c=1$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\det A=\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i).
\]
::: {.proof}
This is <1>4 with $c=1$.
:::
:::
