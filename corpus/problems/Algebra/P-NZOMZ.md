::: problem
Let $p$ be a prime integer, and let $G$ be a group of order $|G| = p$.
Prove that:
(1) $G$ is cyclic ($G \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$).
(2) $G$ is simple (has no non-trivial proper normal subgroups).
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that any group of prime order $p$ is cyclic ($G = \langle g \rangle$) and simple.

<1>1. Proof that $G$ is Cyclic:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $|G| = p \ge 2$, there exists at least one non-identity element $g \in G \setminus \{e\}$.
    <2>2. Consider the cyclic subgroup $\langle g \rangle \le G$ generated by $g$.
    <2>3. Since $g \ne e$, the order of this subgroup satisfies $|\langle g \rangle| > 1$.
    <2>4. By **Lagrange's Theorem**, the order $|\langle g \rangle|$ must divide the group order $|G| = p$.
    <2>5. Since $p$ is a prime number, its only positive divisors are $1$ and $p$.
    <2>6. Because $|\langle g \rangle| > 1$, we must have:
        $$|\langle g \rangle| = p = |G|.$$
    <2>7. Since $\langle g \rangle$ is a subgroup of $G$ with the same finite cardinality as $G$, we conclude:
        $$\langle g \rangle = G.$$
    <2>8. Thus $G$ is cyclic, generated by any non-identity element, and $G \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$.

<1>2. Proof that $G$ is Simple:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $H \le G$ be any subgroup of $G$.
    <2>2. By Lagrange's Theorem, $|H|$ must divide $|G| = p$.
    <2>3. Thus $|H| \in \{1, p\}$.
    <2>4. If $|H| = 1$, then $H = \{e\}$ (the trivial subgroup).
    <2>5. If $|H| = p$, then $H = G$ (the whole group).
    <2>6. Therefore, $G$ has **no proper non-trivial subgroups** at all (normal or otherwise).
    <2>7. In particular, $G$ has no non-trivial proper normal subgroups, which is the definition of a **simple group**.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    Every group of prime order $p$ is cyclic ($G \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$) and simple. Q.E.D.
:::
