::: {.problem}
Are there separable polynomials of any degree $n \ge 1$ over any field $F$?
What about irreducible separable polynomials?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #exists}
$\boxed{\text{Yes}}$: for every field $F$ and every $n\ge1$ there is a separable polynomial of degree $n$ over $F$.

::: pf-proof
If $F$ is infinite, choose distinct $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in F$; then $\prod_{i=1}^n(x-a_i)$ has $n$ distinct roots. If $F=\FF_q$ is finite, the field $\FF_{q^n}$ is a degree-$n$ extension of $\FF_q$ generated by one element, whose minimal polynomial is irreducible of degree $n$; it is separable because finite fields are perfect.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\boxed{\text{No}}$ for irreducible separable polynomials: over some fields there are no irreducible polynomials of some degrees $n\ge2$, although over every finite field there are irreducible separable polynomials of every degree.

::: pf-proof
An algebraically closed field such as $\CC$ has only linear irreducible polynomials, and $\RR$ has irreducible polynomials only in degrees $1$ and $2$, since every real polynomial factors into real linear and quadratic factors. Over a finite field, the polynomial constructed in step [](#exists){.pf-ref} is irreducible and separable.
:::
