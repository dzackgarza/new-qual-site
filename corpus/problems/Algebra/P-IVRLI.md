::: problem
Prove that $SL_2(\ZZ)$ is not solvable.
:::

::: {.solution}
Reduce matrices modulo $5$.

<1>1. The reduction map
\[
\rho:SL_2(\ZZ)\longrightarrow SL_2(\FF_5)
\]
is surjective.
::: {.proof}
The group $SL_2(\FF_5)$ is generated by the elementary matrices
\[
E_{12}(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E_{21}(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\a&1\end{pmatrix}
\qquad(a\in\FF_5).
\]
Indeed, elementary row operations reduce any matrix of determinant $1$ to the identity; equivalently, these elementary matrices generate $SL_2$ over a field.

Each $E_{ij}(a)$ is the reduction modulo $5$ of an integral elementary matrix of determinant $1$. Hence every generator of $SL_2(\FF_5)$ lies in the image of $\rho$, so $\rho$ is surjective.
:::

<1>2. The group $SL_2(\FF_5)$ has a nonsolvable quotient.
::: {.proof}
Its center is
\[
Z=\{\pm I\}.
\]
The quotient
\[
PSL_2(\FF_5)=SL_2(\FF_5)/Z
\]
is the classical group of order $60$ isomorphic to $A_5$. Since $A_5$ is nonabelian simple, it is not solvable.
:::

<1>3. Therefore $SL_2(\ZZ)$ is not solvable.
::: {.proof}
A quotient of a solvable group is solvable. If $SL_2(\ZZ)$ were solvable, then its quotient $SL_2(\FF_5)$ would be solvable by <1>1, and then the further quotient
\[
PSL_2(\FF_5)\cong A_5
\]
would be solvable by <1>2. This is impossible.
:::
:::
