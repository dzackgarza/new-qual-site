::: {.exercise}
Show that every abelian group is nilpotent.
:::

::: {.solution}

The argument is the card's own; the card is not an exercise from a retained textbook in `tmp/sources`, and no solution set covers it.

Let $G$ be a group with identity $1$. For $x,y\in G$ the commutator is $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$, and for subgroups $H,K$ the subgroup $[H,K]$ is the one generated by all $[h,k]$ with $h\in H$, $k\in K$. The lower central series is $\gamma_{1}(G)=G$ and $\gamma_{k+1}(G)=[\gamma_{k}(G),G]$, and $G$ is nilpotent when $\gamma_{n+1}(G)=1$ for some $n\geq0$, the least such $n$ being the nilpotency class.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #commutator}
If $G$ is abelian then $[x,y]=1$ for all $x,y\in G$.

::: pf-proof
$xy=yx$ gives $xyx^{-1}y^{-1}=(yx)x^{-1}y^{-1}=y(xx^{-1})y^{-1}=yy^{-1}=1$, using associativity and $xx^{-1}=yy^{-1}=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #derived}
If $G$ is abelian then $[G,G]=1$.

::: pf-proof
$[G,G]$ is the subgroup generated by the elements $[x,y]$, and by step [](#commutator){.pf-ref} every generator is $1$. The subgroup generated by $\{1\}$ is the trivial subgroup, so $[G,G]=1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #series}
If $G$ is abelian then $\gamma_{2}(G)=1$.

::: pf-proof
$\gamma_{2}(G)=[\gamma_{1}(G),G]=[G,G]$, which is trivial by step [](#derived){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
Every abelian group is nilpotent, of class at most $1$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#series){.pf-ref} gives $\gamma_{1+1}(G)=1$, so the defining condition of nilpotency holds with $n=1$; hence $G$ is nilpotent and its class, the least such $n$, is $0$ or $1$.
:::
