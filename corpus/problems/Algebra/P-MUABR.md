::: problem
Let $F \subseteq K \subseteq L$ be a tower of field extensions. Prove that if $K/F$ is algebraic and $L/K$ is algebraic, then $L/F$ is algebraic.
:::

::: solution
Let $\alpha\in L$. Since $L/K$ is algebraic, $\alpha$ satisfies a nonzero polynomial
\[
p(x)=c_0+c_1x+\cdots+c_nx^n\in K[x].
\]
Let
\[
E=F(c_0,\dots,c_n)\subseteq K.
\]
Because $K/F$ is algebraic, each $c_i$ is algebraic over $F$. A field generated by finitely many algebraic elements is finite over the base field, so
\[
[E:F]<\infty.
\]
Since $p\in E[x]$ and $p(\alpha)=0$, the extension $E(\alpha)/E$ is finite. Hence by the tower law,
\[
[E(\alpha):F]
=[E(\alpha):E][E:F]<\infty.
\]
Every finite extension is algebraic, so $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$. Since $\alpha\in L$ was arbitrary, $L/F$ is algebraic.
:::
