::: problem
Draw the subgroup lattice for $S_3$.
:::


::: {.solution}
The subgroups of $S_3$ are
\[
\{e\},
\qquad
\langle(12)\rangle,
\qquad
\langle(13)\rangle,
\qquad
\langle(23)\rangle,
\qquad
A_3=\langle(123)\rangle,
\qquad
S_3.
\]

<1>1. These are all the subgroups of $S_3$.
::: {.proof}
By Lagrange's theorem, a subgroup has order dividing $6$, hence order $1,2,3$, or $6$. There is one subgroup of order $1$ and one of order $6$. A subgroup of order $2$ is generated by an element of order $2$; the three transpositions give exactly the three displayed subgroups. A subgroup of order $3$ is generated by a $3$-cycle, and the two $3$-cycles generate the same subgroup $A_3$. Thus the list is exhaustive.
:::

<1>2. The subgroup lattice is
\[
\begin{array}{c}
S_3\\[6pt]
/\quad|\quad|\quad\\\\[-6pt]
A_3\quad \langle(12)\rangle\quad \langle(13)\rangle\quad \langle(23)\rangle\\[6pt]
\backslash\quad|\quad|\quad/\\\\[-6pt]
\{e\}.
\end{array}
\]
::: {.proof}
Each order-$2$ or order-$3$ subgroup is maximal because its index is prime. Distinct subgroups of prime order intersect trivially, and none of the order-$2$ subgroups lies in $A_3$. Thus the only strict inclusions are from $\{e\}$ to the four proper nontrivial subgroups and from each of those to $S_3$.
:::

<1>3. Among the proper nontrivial subgroups, only $A_3$ is normal.
::: {.proof}
The subgroup $A_3$ has index $2$, hence is normal. The three transposition subgroups are conjugate to one another and therefore none is normal.
:::
:::
