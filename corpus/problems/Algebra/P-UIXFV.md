::: problem
How would you work out the orders of the irreducible representations of the dihedral group $D_n$?

Why is the sum of squares of dimensions equal to the order of the group?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. $D_n$ has order $2n$, with presentation $\langle r, s \mid r^n = s^2 = 1, srs = r^{-1} \rangle$.
Proof: standard presentation of the dihedral group.

<1>2. The commutator subgroup $[D_n, D_n] = \langle r^2 \rangle$, so the abelianization $D_n^{\mathrm{ab}}$ has order $2$ if $n$ is odd and order $4$ if $n$ is even.
Proof: $srs^{-1} = r^{-1}$, so $r$ and $r^{-1}$ are identified in the abelianization, giving $r^2 = 1$; the abelianization is generated by the images of $r$ and $s$.

<1>3. Hence the number of $1$-dimensional (linear) representations is $2$ if $n$ is odd and $4$ if $n$ is even.
Proof: the number of linear characters equals the order of the abelianization.

<1>4. The remaining irreducible representations are $2$-dimensional, induced from the $1$-dimensional representations of the cyclic subgroup $\langle r \rangle \cong \ZZ/n$.
Proof: the $2$-dimensional irreducibles of $D_n$ are the induced representations $\operatorname{Ind}_{\langle r \rangle}^{D_n}(\chi_k)$ for the nontrivial characters $\chi_k : r \mapsto e^{2\pi i k/n}$.

<1>5. If $n$ is odd, there are $(n-1)/2$ such $2$-dimensional irreducibles; if $n$ is even, there are $(n-2)/2$.
Proof: the characters $\chi_k$ and $\chi_{n-k}$ induce isomorphic representations, and $\chi_0$ (trivial) and $\chi_{n/2}$ (when $n$ even) induce reducible representations, leaving $(n-1)/2$ (odd) or $(n-2)/2$ (even) distinct $2$-dimensional irreducibles.

<1>6. The sum of squares of dimensions is:
- $n$ odd: $2 \cdot 1^2 + \frac{n-1}{2} \cdot 2^2 = 2 + 2(n-1) = 2n$;
- $n$ even: $4 \cdot 1^2 + \frac{n-2}{2} \cdot 2^2 = 4 + 2(n-2) = 2n$.
Proof: <1>3 and <1>5.

<1>7. Hence $\sum_i (\dim V_i)^2 = 2n = |D_n|$.
Proof: <1>6 and <1>1.

<1>8. The general reason: for any finite group $G$, the regular representation $\mathbb{C}[G]$ decomposes as $\bigoplus_i V_i^{\oplus \dim V_i}$, so $|G| = \dim \mathbb{C}[G] = \sum_i (\dim V_i)^2$.
Proof: the regular representation contains each irreducible $V_i$ with multiplicity $\dim V_i$.

<1>9. Q.E.D.
Proof: <1>7 and <1>8.
:::
