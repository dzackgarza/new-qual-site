::: {.problem}
How do you construct finite fields with $p^n$ elements?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $p$ be a prime, $n$ a positive integer, and $f(x)=x^{p^n}-x\in\FF_p[x]$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #construction}
A splitting field $K$ of $f$ over $\FF_p$ has exactly $p^n$ elements, and they are the roots of $f$.

::: pf-proof
Since $f'=-1$ is coprime to $f$, $f$ has $p^n$ distinct roots in $K$.
The Frobenius map $x\mapsto x^p$ is a ring homomorphism of $K$, so its $n$th iterate $x\mapsto x^{p^n}$ is too, and its fixed points, the roots of $f$, form a subfield of $K$ containing $\FF_p$.
That subfield contains all roots of $f$, so it equals the splitting field $K$; hence $K$ is exactly the set of $p^n$ roots of $f$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #uniqueness}
Every field $L$ with $p^n$ elements is isomorphic to $K$.

::: pf-proof
$L$ has characteristic $p$ and contains $\FF_p$.
The group $L^\times$ has order $p^n-1$, so $x^{p^n-1}=1$ for $x\in L^\times$, and every element of $L$ is a root of $f$.
Since $f$ has at most $p^n$ roots, $L$ is generated by them and $f$ splits in $L$, so $L$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $\FF_p$.
Any two splitting fields of one polynomial are isomorphic.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
A field with $p^n$ elements is constructed as the splitting field of $x^{p^n}-x$ over $\FF_p$, and it is unique up to isomorphism.

::: pf-proof
This is step [](#construction){.pf-ref} together with step [](#uniqueness){.pf-ref}.
Concretely, if $g\in\FF_p[x]$ is irreducible of degree $n$, then $\FF_p[x]/(g)$ is a field with $p^n$ elements, so it is this field.
:::
