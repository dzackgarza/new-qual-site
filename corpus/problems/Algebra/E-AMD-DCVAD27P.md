::: {.exercise}
Show that $\bigcup_{\mathfrak{m} \in \operatorname{MaxSpec}(R)} \mathfrak{m} = R \setminus R^\times$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that for any commutative ring with identity $R$, the union of all maximal ideals coincides with the set of non-units $R \setminus R^\times$.

<1>1. Forward inclusion ($\subseteq$):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $x \in \bigcup_{\mathfrak{m} \in \operatorname{MaxSpec}(R)} \mathfrak{m}$.
    <2>2. There exists a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} \in \operatorname{MaxSpec}(R)$ such that $x \in \mathfrak{m}$.
    <2>3. Since $\mathfrak{m}$ is a maximal ideal, it is proper ($\mathfrak{m} \neq R$), so $1 \notin \mathfrak{m}$.
    <2>4. If $x \in R^\times$, then $1 = x^{-1} x \in \mathfrak{m}$, which implies $\mathfrak{m} = R$, a contradiction.
    <2>5. Thus $x \notin R^\times$, which means $x \in R \setminus R^\times$.

<1>2. Reverse inclusion ($\supseteq$):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $x \in R \setminus R^\times$.
    <2>2. The principal ideal generated by $x$ is $(x) = \{rx \mid r \in R\}$.
    <2>3. Because $x$ is not a unit, $1 \notin (x)$, so $(x)$ is a proper ideal of $R$.
    <2>4. By Krull's Theorem (Zorn's Lemma), every proper ideal in a commutative ring with identity is contained in some maximal ideal.
    <2>5. Thus there exists $\mathfrak{m}_0 \in \operatorname{MaxSpec}(R)$ such that $(x) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_0$.
    <2>6. Since $x \in (x)$, we have $x \in \mathfrak{m}_0 \subseteq \bigcup_{\mathfrak{m} \in \operatorname{MaxSpec}(R)} \mathfrak{m}$.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    Combining <1>1 and <1>2 yields $\bigcup_{\mathfrak{m} \in \operatorname{MaxSpec}(R)} \mathfrak{m} = R \setminus R^\times$. Q.E.D.
:::
