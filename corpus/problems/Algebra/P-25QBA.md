::: {.exercise}
Let $M\subseteq L^1(\mathbb R)$ be a closed translation-invariant subspace.

1. Prove that $M$ is a closed ideal in the convolution Banach algebra $L^1(\mathbb R)$.
2. Explain the connection with Wiener's Tauberian theorem and the Fourier transform.
3. Explain the relation with $L^2(\mathbb R)$ as an $L^1$-convolution module and with the Plancherel transform.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $\tau_yf(x)=f(x-y)$.

<1>1. $M$ is a closed convolution ideal.
::: {.proof}
Fix $f\in M$ and $g\in L^1(\mathbb R)$. The map
\[
y\longmapsto g(y)\tau_yf
\]
is Bochner integrable as an $L^1$-valued function, since
\[
\|g(y)\tau_yf\|_1=|g(y)|\|f\|_1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
g*f=\int_{\mathbb R}g(y)\tau_yf\,dy
\]
in $L^1$. Simple-function approximations to this Bochner integral are finite linear combinations of translates of $f$, hence lie in $M$. Since $M$ is closed, $g*f\in M$. Convolution on $\mathbb R$ is commutative, so $M$ is a closed two-sided ideal.
:::

<1>2. Fourier transform and Wiener's theorem.
::: {.proof}
For $f,g\in L^1$,
\[
\widehat{f*g}=\hat f\,\hat g.
\]
For a closed ideal $M$, its hull is
\[
Z(M)=\{\xi\in\mathbb R:\hat f(\xi)=0\text{ for every }f\in M\}.
\]
Wiener's Tauberian theorem says, equivalently, that
\[
Z(M)=\varnothing\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M=L^1(\mathbb R).
\]
For the principal closed ideal generated by $f$, this is the familiar statement that the translates of $f$ span a dense subspace of $L^1$ exactly when $\hat f$ is nowhere zero.

The hull $Z(M)$ is an invariant of a closed ideal, but one should not identify arbitrary closed ideals solely from their hulls: that stronger assertion is spectral synthesis and is false in general for nondiscrete locally compact abelian groups.
:::

<1>3. Relation with $L^2$.
::: {.proof}
Young's inequality gives
\[
\|f*g\|_2\le\|f\|_1\|g\|_2,
\qquad f\in L^1,\ g\in L^2.
\]
Thus $L^2(\mathbb R)$ is a Banach module over the convolution algebra $L^1(\mathbb R)$; it is not itself, in general, a convolution algebra. Under the Fourier-Plancherel unitary,
\[
\mathcal F(f*g)=\hat f\,\mathcal Fg,
\]
so convolution by $f\in L^1$ becomes multiplication by the bounded function $\hat f$ on the Fourier side.

A closed subspace of $L^2$ invariant under all translations becomes, under Plancherel, a closed subspace invariant under all multiplication operators $e^{-iy\xi}$. By the spectral theorem for this multiplication representation, such subspaces are exactly $L^2(E)$ for measurable $E\subseteq\mathbb R$. Hence translation-invariant $L^2$ subspaces are spectral support subspaces, while an $L^1$ ideal acts on them by Fourier multipliers.
:::
