::: {.exercise}
Show that a Dedekind domain $R$ is a PID iff $R$ is a UFD.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #pid-implies-ufd}
If $R$ is a PID, then $R$ is a UFD.

::: pf-proof
In a PID every nonzero nonunit factors into irreducibles, because the ascending chain condition on principal ideals holds, and every irreducible element is prime, because it generates a maximal ideal. Hence factorization is unique up to order and associates.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #prime-ideals-principal}
Suppose now that $R$ is a Dedekind domain and a UFD. Then every nonzero prime ideal of $R$ is principal.

::: pf-proof
A Dedekind domain is Noetherian and every nonzero prime ideal has height $1$. In a Noetherian UFD, every height-$1$ prime ideal is principal. Thus for every nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak p\subset R$ there is a prime element $\pi_{\mathfrak p}\in R$ with
$$
\mathfrak p=(\pi_{\mathfrak p}).
$$
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
Every ideal of the Dedekind UFD $R$ is principal, so $R$ is a PID; with step [](#pid-implies-ufd){.pf-ref} this proves the equivalence.
The zero ideal is $(0)$. Let $0\neq I\subseteq R$. Since $R$ is Dedekind, $I$ has a factorization
$$
I=\mathfrak p_1^{e_1}\cdots \mathfrak p_r^{e_r}
$$
into nonzero prime ideals. By step [](#prime-ideals-principal){.pf-ref}, $\mathfrak p_i=(\pi_i)$, so
$$
I=(\pi_1)^{e_1}\cdots(\pi_r)^{e_r}=\bigl(\pi_1^{e_1}\cdots\pi_r^{e_r}\bigr).
$$
:::
