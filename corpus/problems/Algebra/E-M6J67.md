::: {.exercise}
Show that a Dedekind domain $R$ is a PID iff $R$ is a UFD.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. If $R$ is a PID, then $R$ is a UFD.
::: {.proof}
This is the standard PID factorization theorem: every nonzero nonunit factors into irreducibles, and in a PID every irreducible element is prime. Hence factorization is unique up to order and associates.
:::

<1>2. Suppose now that $R$ is a Dedekind domain and a UFD. Then every nonzero prime ideal of $R$ is principal.
::: {.proof}
A Dedekind domain is Noetherian and every nonzero prime ideal has height $1$. In a Noetherian UFD, every height-$1$ prime ideal is principal. Thus for every nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak p\subset R$ there is a prime element $\pi_{\mathfrak p}\in R$ with
\[
\mathfrak p=(\pi_{\mathfrak p}).
\]
:::

<1>3. Every nonzero ideal of $R$ is principal.
::: {.proof}
Let $0\neq I\subseteq R$. Since $R$ is Dedekind, $I$ has a factorization
\[
I=\mathfrak p_1^{e_1}\cdots \mathfrak p_r^{e_r}
\]
into nonzero prime ideals. By <1>2, write
\[
\mathfrak p_i=(\pi_i).
\]
Then
\[
I=(\pi_1)^{e_1}\cdots(\pi_r)^{e_r}
 =\bigl(\pi_1^{e_1}\cdots\pi_r^{e_r}\bigr),
\]
so $I$ is principal. The zero ideal is $(0)$, hence is principal as well.
:::

<1>4. Therefore a Dedekind domain is a PID if and only if it is a UFD.
::: {.proof}
The forward implication is <1>1. For the reverse implication, <1>3 shows that every ideal of $R$ is principal, so $R$ is a PID.
:::
:::
