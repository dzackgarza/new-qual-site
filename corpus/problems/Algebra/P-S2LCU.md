::: problem
Let $L/K$ be a finite separable field extension. Prove that $L/K$ is normal if and only if $L$ is the splitting field of some separable polynomial $p(x) \in K[x]$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove the fundamental equivalence: for a finite separable extension $L/K$, normality is equivalent to being the splitting field of a polynomial over $K$.

<1>1. Direction $(\implies)$: $L/K$ normal $\implies L$ is a splitting field of a polynomial over $K$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $L/K$ is finite and separable, by the **Primitive Element Theorem**, $L$ is a simple extension:
        $$L = K(\theta) \quad \text{for some } \theta \in L.$$
    <2>2. Let $p(x) = \operatorname{irr}(\theta, K) \in K[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\theta$ over $K$.
    <2>3. Since $L/K$ is separable, $p(x)$ is a separable polynomial.
    <2>4. The polynomial $p(x) \in K[x]$ has a root $\theta \in L$.
    <2>5. By definition of a **normal extension**, every irreducible polynomial in $K[x]$ having at least one root in $L$ splits completely into linear factors over $L$:
        $$p(x) = (x - \theta_1)(x - \theta_2)\cdots(x - \theta_n) \in L[x] \quad (\text{with } \theta_1 = \theta).$$
    <2>6. Thus all roots $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n$ of $p(x)$ lie in $L$.
    <2>7. The subfield generated by the roots of $p(x)$ is $K(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n) \subseteq L$.
    <2>8. But since $\theta_1 = \theta$ already generates $L$, we have $L = K(\theta) \subseteq K(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n) \subseteq L$.
    <2>9. Therefore, $L = K(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n)$ is the splitting field of the separable polynomial $p(x)$ over $K$.

<1>2. Direction $(\impliedby)$: $L$ is a splitting field over $K \implies L/K$ is normal:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose $L$ is the splitting field of a polynomial $p(x) \in K[x]$ over $K$.
    <2>2. Let $\Omega$ be an algebraic closure of $K$ containing $L$.
    <2>3. Let $\sigma: L \hookrightarrow \Omega$ be any $K$-embedding of $L$ into $\Omega$ (so $\sigma|_K = \operatorname{id}_K$).
    <2>4. If $\alpha \in L$ is any root of $p(x)$, then $p(\sigma(\alpha)) = \sigma(p(\alpha)) = \sigma(0) = 0$.
    <2>5. Thus $\sigma$ permutes the set of roots $\{\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n\}$ of $p(x)$.
    <2>6. Since $L = K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)$ is generated by these roots, we have:
        $$\sigma(L) = \sigma(K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n)) = K(\sigma(\alpha_1), \dots, \sigma(\alpha_n)) = K(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n) = L.$$
    <2>7. Therefore, every $K$-embedding of $L$ into $\Omega$ satisfies $\sigma(L) = L$.
    <2>8. This is the definition of a normal extension: $L/K$ is normal.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    A finite separable extension $L/K$ is normal if and only if it is the splitting field of a polynomial over $K$. Q.E.D.
:::
