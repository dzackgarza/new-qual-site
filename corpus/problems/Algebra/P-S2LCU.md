::: problem
Let $L/K$ be a finite separable field extension.
Prove that $L/K$ is normal if and only if $L$ is the splitting field of some separable polynomial $p(x) \in K[x]$.
:::

::: solution
Suppose first that $L/K$ is normal. Since the extension is finite and separable, the primitive element theorem gives
\[
L=K(\alpha)
\]
for some $\alpha\in L$. Let $m_\alpha\in K[x]$ be its minimal polynomial. Separability of $L/K$ makes $m_\alpha$ separable, and normality implies that $m_\alpha$ splits completely in $L$. Since one of its roots, namely $\alpha$, already generates $L$, the field generated by all its roots is exactly $L$. Thus $L$ is the splitting field of the separable polynomial $m_\alpha$.

Conversely, suppose $L$ is the splitting field over $K$ of a separable polynomial $p\in K[x]$. Let $\Omega$ be an algebraic closure of $K$, and let
\[
\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\Omega
\]
be a $K$-embedding. Every root $\alpha$ of $p$ in $L$ satisfies
\[
p(\sigma(\alpha))=\sigma(p(\alpha))=0,
\]
so $\sigma$ permutes the roots of $p$. Since those roots generate $L$,
\[
\sigma(L)=L.
\]
Hence every $K$-embedding of $L$ into an algebraic closure has image $L$, so $L/K$ is normal.
:::
