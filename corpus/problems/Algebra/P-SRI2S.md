::: problem
What's the Galois group of $x^2 + 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$? What's the integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Determine $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q})$ and the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(i)}$.

<1>1. Galois group of $x^2 + 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The polynomial $f(x) = x^2 + 1 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ has roots $\pm i \notin \mathbb{Q}$.
    <2>2. Since $\deg(f) = 2$ and $f$ has no rational roots, $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.
    <2>3. The splitting field of $f$ is $K = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, with degree $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = 2$.
    <2>4. Since $\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q}$ is a degree 2 extension in characteristic 0, it is normal and separable, hence Galois.
    <2>5. The Galois group has order $|\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q})| = [K : \mathbb{Q}] = 2$.
    <2>6. Thus $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by complex conjugation $\sigma: a + bi \mapsto a - bi$.

<1>2. Integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\alpha = a + bi \in \mathbb{Q}(i)$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}$.
    <2>2. If $b = 0$, $\alpha = a \in \mathbb{Q}$, which is integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ if and only if $a \in \mathbb{Z}$.
    <2>3. If $b \ne 0$, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is:
        $$(x - (a+bi))(x - (a-bi)) = x^2 - 2ax + (a^2 + b^2).$$
    <2>4. The element $\alpha$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ if and only if the coefficients of its minimal polynomial are in $\mathbb{Z}$:
        $$2a \in \mathbb{Z} \quad \text{and} \quad a^2 + b^2 \in \mathbb{Z}.$$
    <2>5. Write $2a = u \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $2b = v \in \mathbb{Q}$. Then $a^2 + b^2 = \frac{u^2 + v^2}{4} \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $u^2 + v^2 \equiv 0 \pmod 4$.
    <2>6. Since squares modulo 4 are $0$ or $1$, the only way $u^2 + v^2 \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ is $u \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ and $v \equiv 0 \pmod 2$.
    <2>7. Thus $u = 2a \in 2\mathbb{Z} \implies a \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $v = 2b \in 2\mathbb{Z} \implies b \in \mathbb{Z}$.
    <2>8. Therefore $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(i)} = \{a + bi \mid a, b \in \mathbb{Z}\} = \mathbb{Z}[i]$, the ring of Gaussian integers.

<1>3. Conclusion:
    The Galois group is $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and the integral closure is $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Q.E.D.
:::
