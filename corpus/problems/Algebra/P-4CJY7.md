::: {.problem}
What are the characteristic and minimal polynomial of the Frobenius automorphism $x\mapsto x^p$ of $\FF_{p^n}$, regarded as an $\FF_p$-linear map?
:::

::: {.solution}
The statement is the one printed on this card; no retained solution set covers it — it is not a textbook exercise with a solution sheet in `tmp/sources`, and the card carries no solution citation — so the argument below is the card's own. It uses the normal basis theorem for finite Galois extensions, which is the standard input for this question.

Throughout $F=\FF_{p^n}$, $\Phi:F\to F$ is $\Phi(x)=x^p$ regarded as an $\FF_p$-linear endomorphism, and $\chi_\Phi$, $m_\Phi$ are its characteristic and minimal polynomials.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #basis}
There is $\alpha\in F$ such that $\alpha,\Phi(\alpha),\dots,\Phi^{n-1}(\alpha)$ is an $\FF_p$-basis of $F$.

::: pf-proof
$F/\FF_p$ is a finite Galois extension with Galois group cyclic of order $n$ generated by $\Phi$: $F$ is the splitting field of $X^{p^n}-X$, which is separable, and its automorphism group is generated by the Frobenius, which has order $n$ since $\Phi^n=\mathrm{id}$ and no lower power fixes $\FF_{p^n}$. The normal basis theorem for a finite Galois extension asserts that such a basis exists, namely the Galois conjugates of a suitable element.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #matrix}
In that basis the matrix of $\Phi$ is the permutation matrix of an $n$-cycle, so $\chi_\Phi(X)=X^n-1$.

::: pf-proof
$\Phi^n=\mathrm{id}$ because $x^{p^n}=x$ for all $x\in F$, so $\Phi$ permutes the basis cyclically: $\Phi\bigl(\Phi^{k}(\alpha)\bigr)=\Phi^{k+1}(\alpha)$ for $k<n-1$ and $\Phi\bigl(\Phi^{n-1}(\alpha)\bigr)=\alpha$. A permutation matrix's characteristic polynomial is $\prod_{\text{cycles}}(X^{\text{length}}-1)$, and there is one cycle of length $n$, so $\chi_\Phi(X)=X^n-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #minimal}
$m_\Phi(X)=X^n-1$.

::: pf-proof
$\alpha$ is cyclic for $\Phi$: its orbit $\alpha,\Phi(\alpha),\dots,\Phi^{n-1}(\alpha)$ is a basis by step [](#basis){.pf-ref}, so a polynomial $P$ of degree $<n$ with $P(\Phi)=0$ would satisfy $0=P(\Phi)(\alpha)$, a nontrivial $\FF_p$-linear combination of basis vectors with coefficients the coefficients of $P$; hence $P=0$. So $m_\Phi$ has degree at least $n$. By step [](#matrix){.pf-ref} and the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, $m_\Phi$ divides $\chi_\Phi=X^n-1$, which has degree $n$; a divisor of degree exactly $n$ of a monic polynomial of degree $n$ is that polynomial. So $m_\Phi=X^n-1$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
The characteristic and minimal polynomials of the Frobenius automorphism of $\FF_{p^n}$ are both $X^n-1$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#matrix){.pf-ref} and [](#minimal){.pf-ref} give $\chi_\Phi=m_\Phi=X^n-1$, hence the eigenvalues of $\Phi$ are the $n$-th roots of unity $1,\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{n-1}$ for a primitive $n$-th root $\zeta$, counted with multiplicity, and their sum, the trace of $\Phi$, is $0$ unless $n=1$.

:::
