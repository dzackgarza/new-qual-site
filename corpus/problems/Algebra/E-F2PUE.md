::: problem
5. If $F$ is over $E$, and $E$ is $\quad$ over $K$ is $F$ necessarily over $K$ ? Answer
   this question for each of the words "algebraic," "normal," and "separable" in the
   blanks.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Algebraicity is transitive: if \(F/E\) and \(E/K\) are algebraic, then \(F/K\) is
algebraic.
::: {.proof}
Fix \(\alpha\in F\). Since \(\alpha\) is algebraic over \(E\), there is a relation
\[
a_0+a_1\alpha+\cdots+a_d\alpha^d=0
\]
with \(a_i\in E\), not all zero. Put \(E_0=K(a_0,\ldots,a_d)\). Because \(E/K\) is
algebraic and only finitely many coefficients are adjoined, \(E_0/K\) is finite. The
displayed relation shows that \(\alpha\) is algebraic over \(E_0\), hence
\(E_0(\alpha)/E_0\) is finite. Therefore
\[
[K(\alpha):K]\le [E_0(\alpha):K]=[E_0(\alpha):E_0][E_0:K]<\infty.
\]
Thus \(\alpha\) is algebraic over \(K\). Since \(\alpha\) was arbitrary, \(F/K\) is
algebraic.
:::

<1>2. Separability is transitive: if \(F/E\) and \(E/K\) are separable algebraic
extensions, then \(F/K\) is separable.
::: {.proof}
Fix \(\alpha\in F\), and let \(m_{\alpha,E}(x)\in E[x]\) be its minimal polynomial over
\(E\). Let \(E_0/K\) be the finite extension generated by the finitely many coefficients
of \(m_{\alpha,E}\). Since \(E/K\) is separable, \(E_0/K\) is finite separable.

Because \(F/E\) is separable, \(m_{\alpha,E}\) has no repeated root in an algebraic
closure. The same polynomial lies in \(E_0[x]\), and squarefreeness is unchanged after
extending scalars. The minimal polynomial \(m_{\alpha,E_0}\) divides \(m_{\alpha,E}\) in
\(E_0[x]\), so it too has no repeated root. Hence \(\alpha\) is separable over \(E_0\),
and \(E_0(\alpha)/E_0\) is finite separable.

A tower of finite separable extensions is separable: if \(L/M\) and \(M/K\) are finite
separable, then the number of \(K\)-embeddings of \(L\) into an algebraic closure equals
\[
[L:M][M:K]=[L:K],
\]
because each of the \([M:K]\) embeddings of \(M\) extends to exactly \([L:M]\)
embeddings of \(L\). Hence \(L/K\) is separable. Applying this to
\[
K\subseteq E_0\subseteq E_0(\alpha)
\]
shows that \(\alpha\) is separable over \(K\). Therefore \(F/K\) is separable.
:::

<1>3. Normality is not transitive.
::: {.proof}
Take
\[
K=\mathbb Q,\qquad E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2),\qquad F=\mathbb Q(2^{1/4}).
\]
The extension \(E/K\) is quadratic, hence normal. Also \(F/E\) is quadratic, because
\(2^{1/4}\) satisfies
\[
x^2-\sqrt2\in E[x],
\]
and this polynomial splits in \(F\) as
\[
(x-2^{1/4})(x+2^{1/4});
\]
therefore \(F/E\) is normal.

However \(F/K\) is not normal. The minimal polynomial of \(2^{1/4}\) over \(\mathbb Q\)
is \(x^4-2\), irreducible by Eisenstein at \(2\). Its roots are
\[
\pm 2^{1/4},\qquad \pm i2^{1/4}.
\]
The field \(F\subset\mathbb R\) contains the first two roots but not the nonreal roots.
Hence \(x^4-2\) does not split over \(F\), so \(F/\mathbb Q\) is not normal.
:::

<1>4. Therefore the answers are: algebraic — yes; normal — no; separable — yes.
::: {.proof}
Combine <1>1, <1>2, and <1>3.
:::
:::
