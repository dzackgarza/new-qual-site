::: {.problem}
Suppose $D$ is an integral domain and the fundamental theorem holds for finitely-generated modules over $D$ (i.e. they are all direct sums of finitely many cyclic modules).

Does $D$ have to be a PID?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #bezout}
Every finitely generated ideal of $D$ is principal.

::: pf-proof
Let $I\ne0$ be a finitely generated ideal.
By hypothesis $I\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^k D/J_i$ with each $D/J_i\ne0$.
Since $D$ is a domain, $I$ is torsion-free; if some $J_i\ne0$, a nonzero $r\in J_i$ kills the nonzero element $1+J_i$ of that summand.
So every $J_i=0$ and $I\cong D^k$.
If $k\ge2$, choose nonzero $x$ in the first summand and $y$ in the second; as elements of the ideal $I\subseteq D$, the product $xy=yx$ lies in both summands, so $xy=0$, which contradicts that $D$ is a domain.
Hence $k=1$, and $I\cong D$ is generated by the image of $1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #example}
Let $k$ be a field, $K=k((t^{\QQ}))$ the field of Hahn series $\sum_{q} a_q t^q$ with well-ordered support in $\QQ$, $v(f)=\min\operatorname{supp} f$ its valuation, and $V=\{0\}\cup\{f\in K: v(f)\ge0\}$ its valuation ring.
Every finitely generated $V$-module is a direct sum of finitely many cyclic modules, and $V$ is not a PID.

::: pf-proof
The Hahn series field $K$ is maximally complete, so $V$ is a maximal valuation ring, and by Kaplansky's theorem every finitely generated module over a maximal valuation ring is a direct sum of cyclic modules.
The maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=\{0\}\cup\{f\in V: v(f)>0\}$ is not principal: if $\mathfrak m=(g)$, then $v(g)>0$, and $t^{v(g)/2}\in\mathfrak m$ has valuation smaller than $v(g)$, so it is not a multiple of $g$ in $V$.
:::

:::

::: pf-qed
$\boxed{\text{No}}$. By step [](#bezout){.pf-ref} the hypothesis forces $D$ to be a Bézout domain, and step [](#example){.pf-ref} gives a Bézout domain that satisfies the hypothesis and is not a PID.
:::
