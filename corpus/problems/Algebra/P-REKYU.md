::: {.problem}
- Show that if $N\normal D_n$ is a normal subgroup of a dihedral group, then $D_n/N$ is again a dihedral group.
:::

::: {.solution}
The literal claim fails for $N=D_n$, whose quotient is trivial.
Every other quotient is dihedral, where $D_1\cong C_2$ and $D_2\cong C_2\times C_2$ are the dihedral groups of orders $2$ and $4$.

Write
\[
D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=1,\ s^2=1,\ srs^{-1}=r^{-1}\rangle,
\]
let $Q=D_n/N$, let $\bar r=rN$ and $\bar s=sN$, and let $k$ be the order of $\bar r$, so $k\mid n$.

<1>1. $Q$ is generated by $\bar r$ and $\bar s$, and
\[
\bar r^k=1,
\qquad
\bar s^2=1,
\qquad
\bar s\bar r\bar s^{-1}=\bar r^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The quotient map is a surjective homomorphism, so it carries the generators $r,s$ to generators of $Q$ and the defining relations of $D_n$ to relations in $Q$; $\bar r^k=1$ is the definition of $k$.
:::

<1>2. Every element of $Q$ has the form $\bar r^i$ or $\bar s\bar r^i$ with $0\le i<k$.
::: {.proof}
The relation $\bar r\bar s=\bar s\bar r^{-1}$ moves every occurrence of $\bar s$ in a word to the left, $\bar s^2=1$ leaves at most one occurrence, and powers of $\bar r$ reduce modulo $k$.
:::

<1>3. If $\bar s\notin\langle\bar r\rangle$, then $Q\cong D_k$.
::: {.proof}
The elements $\bar r^i$ with $0\le i<k$ are distinct because $\bar r$ has order $k$, and so are the elements $\bar s\bar r^i$.
If $\bar s\bar r^i=\bar r^j$, then $\bar s=\bar r^{j-i}\in\langle\bar r\rangle$, contrary to hypothesis.
So the $2k$ elements of <1>2 are distinct and $|Q|=2k$.
By <1>1, $r\mapsto\bar r$, $s\mapsto\bar s$ defines a surjective homomorphism $D_k\to Q$, which is an isomorphism because both groups have order $2k$.
:::

<1>4. If $\bar s\in\langle\bar r\rangle$, then $Q$ is cyclic of order $1$ or $2$.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, $Q=\langle\bar r\rangle$, so $Q$ is abelian and
\[
\bar r=\bar s\bar r\bar s^{-1}=\bar r^{-1}.
\]
Hence $\bar r^2=1$ and $|Q|=k\le2$.
:::

<1>5. $Q$ is dihedral unless $N=D_n$.
::: {.proof}
If $\bar s\notin\langle\bar r\rangle$, apply <1>3.
Otherwise <1>4 gives $|Q|\le2$; if $|Q|=2$ then $Q\cong C_2\cong D_1$, and $|Q|=1$ means $N=D_n$.
:::
:::
