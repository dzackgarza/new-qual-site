::: problem
- Show that if $N\normal D_n$ is a normal subgroup of a dihedral group, then $D_n/N$ is again a dihedral group.
:::


::: {.solution}
Write
\[
D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=1,\ s^2=1,\ srs^{-1}=r^{-1}\rangle,
\]
and let \(\bar r=rN\), \(\bar s=sN\) be the images in \(Q=D_n/N\). Put
\(k=|\bar r|\), so \(k\mid n\).

<1>1. The quotient \(Q\) is generated by \(\bar r,\bar s\), with
\[
\bar r^k=1,
\qquad
\bar s^2=1,
\qquad
\bar s\bar r\bar s^{-1}=\bar r^{-1}.
\]
::: {.proof}
The quotient map sends the defining generators and relations of \(D_n\) to these generators and relations in \(Q\).
:::

<1>2. Every element of \(Q\) has the form \(\bar r^i\) or \(\bar s\bar r^i\), with \(0\le i<k\).
::: {.proof}
The relation \(\bar s\bar r=\bar r^{-1}\bar s\) moves every occurrence of \(\bar s\) to the left, and \(\bar s^2=1\) reduces the number of occurrences of \(\bar s\) to at most one. Powers of \(\bar r\) reduce modulo \(k\).
:::

<1>3. If \(\bar s\notin\langle\bar r\rangle\), then the \(2k\) normal forms in <1>2 are distinct. Hence \(|Q|=2k\) and \(Q\cong D_k\).
::: {.proof}
The elements \(\bar r^i\) are distinct because \(|\bar r|=k\), and the elements \(\bar s\bar r^i\) are distinct for the same reason. If \(\bar s\bar r^i=\bar r^j\), then \(\bar s=\bar r^{j-i}\in\langle\bar r\rangle\), contrary to hypothesis. Thus the presentation in <1>1 has exactly the usual \(2k\) elements of the dihedral group \(D_k\).
:::

<1>4. If \(\bar s\in\langle\bar r\rangle\), then \(Q=\langle\bar r\rangle\) is cyclic and \(k\le2\).
::: {.proof}
Because \(Q\) is generated by \(\bar r\) and \(\bar s\), the assumption gives \(Q=\langle\bar r\rangle\). Hence \(\bar r\) commutes with \(\bar s\). Combining commutativity with the relation from <1>1 gives
\[
\bar r=\bar s\bar r\bar s^{-1}=\bar r^{-1},
\]
so \(\bar r^2=1\). Therefore its order \(k\) is \(1\) or \(2\).
:::

<1>5. Thus every quotient \(D_n/N\) is dihedral, allowing the standard small cases
\[
D_0=\{1\},\qquad D_1\cong C_2,\qquad D_2\cong C_2\times C_2.
\]
::: {.proof}
If \(\bar s\notin\langle\bar r\rangle\), apply <1>3. If \(\bar s\in\langle\bar r\rangle\), then <1>4 shows that \(Q\) is either trivial or cyclic of order \(2\), namely \(D_0\) or \(D_1\) under the displayed conventions.
:::
:::
