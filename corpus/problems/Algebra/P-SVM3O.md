::: problem
Let $G$ be a finite group and let $g \in G$. Prove that the order of $g$, denoted $\operatorname{ord}(g)$, divides the order of the group $|G|$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that for any element $g$ in a finite group $G$, $\operatorname{ord}(g) \mid |G|$, and consequently $g^{|G|} = e$ (Euler-Fermat Theorem for groups).

<1>1. The Cyclic Subgroup Generated by $g$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $g \in G$, and consider the cyclic subgroup generated by $g$:
        $$H = \langle g \rangle = \{g^k \mid k \in \mathbb{Z}\} \le G.$$
    <2>2. Let $n = \operatorname{ord}(g)$ be the smallest positive integer such that $g^n = e$.
    <2>3. The distinct elements of $H$ are precisely $\{e, g, g^2, \dots, g^{n-1}\}$.
        *(Proof: If $g^i = g^j$ with $0 \le i < j < n$, then $g^{j-i} = e$ with $0 < j-i < n$, contradicting the minimality of $n$).*
    <2>4. Therefore, the order (cardinality) of the subgroup $H$ is:
        $$|H| = |\langle g \rangle| = \operatorname{ord}(g) = n.$$

<1>2. Application of Lagrange's Theorem:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Lagrange's Theorem:** If $G$ is a finite group and $H \le G$ is a subgroup, then the order of $H$ divides the order of $G$:
        $$|G| = [G : H] \cdot |H|$$
        where $[G : H]$ is the index of $H$ in $G$ (the number of left cosets of $H$).
    <2>2. Since $H = \langle g \rangle$ is a subgroup of $G$, $|H| = \operatorname{ord}(g)$ must divide $|G|$.
    <2>3. Thus $\operatorname{ord}(g) \mid |G|$.

<1>3. Corollary ($g^{|G|} = e$):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $\operatorname{ord}(g) \mid |G|$, there exists an integer $k \ge 1$ such that $|G| = k \cdot \operatorname{ord}(g)$.
    <2>2. Then:
        $$g^{|G|} = g^{k \cdot \operatorname{ord}(g)} = (g^{\operatorname{ord}(g)})^k = e^k = e.$$

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $|\langle g \rangle| = \operatorname{ord}(g)$ divides $|G|$ by Lagrange's Theorem. Q.E.D.
:::
