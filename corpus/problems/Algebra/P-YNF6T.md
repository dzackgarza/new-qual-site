::: problem
Suppose $|G| = pq$ with $p, q \geq 2$ prime, and let $H \le G$ be a proper subgroup.
Prove that $H$ must be cyclic.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that every proper subgroup of a group $G$ of order $pq$ ($p, q$ primes) is cyclic.

<1>1. Order of a proper subgroup:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By Lagrange's Theorem, $|H|$ divides $|G| = pq$.
    <2>2. The positive divisors of $pq$ are $1, p, q, pq$.
    <2>3. Since $H$ is a proper subgroup ($H \ne G$), $|H| < pq$.
    <2>4. Thus $|H| \in \{1, p, q\}$.

<1>2. Case $|H| = 1$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. If $|H| = 1$, then $H = \{e\} = \langle e \rangle$, which is the trivial cyclic group.

<1>3. Case $|H| = p$ (or $q$):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Lemma: Every group of prime order is cyclic.
        *Proof of lemma:* Let $K$ be a group of prime order $r$. Since $r \ge 2$, choose $x \in K$ with $x \ne e$. By Lagrange's Theorem, the order of the cyclic subgroup $\langle x \rangle$ divides $|K| = r$. Since $r$ is prime and $|\langle x \rangle| > 1$, we must have $|\langle x \rangle| = r = |K|$. Thus $K = \langle x \rangle$, so $K$ is cyclic and generated by any non-identity element.
    <2>2. Since $|H| = p$ (or $q$) is prime, the lemma applies directly, so $H$ is cyclic.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    In every case, $|H| \in \{1, p, q\}$, all of which are 1 or prime, so $H$ is cyclic. Q.E.D.
:::
