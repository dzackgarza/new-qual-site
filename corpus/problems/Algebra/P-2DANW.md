::: problem
Let $G$ be a group of order $|G| = 240 = 2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$.
(1) What are the possible values for the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups $n_p$ for $p \in \{2, 3, 5\}$?
(2) Show that if $G$ has a subgroup $H$ of order 15, then $G$ has an element of order 15 (so $H \cong \mathbb{Z}_{15}$ is cyclic).
(3) Show that if $G$ does not have a subgroup of order 15, then the number of Sylow 3-subgroups $n_3$ must be either 10 or 40.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Analyze Sylow numbers for $|G| = 240$, prove that any subgroup of order 15 is cyclic, and show $n_3 \in \{10, 40\}$ in the absence of order 15 subgroups.

<1>1. Possible Sylow numbers $n_p$ by Sylow's Theorems:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **For $p = 5$:** $n_5 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ and $n_5 \mid 48$.
        - Divisors of $48 = 2^4 \cdot 3$: $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48$.
        - Condition $n_5 \equiv 1 \pmod 5 \implies n_5 \in \{1, 6, 16\}$.
    <2>2. **For $p = 3$:** $n_3 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $n_3 \mid 80$.
        - Divisors of $80 = 2^4 \cdot 5$: $1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 16, 20, 40, 80$.
        - Condition $n_3 \equiv 1 \pmod 3 \implies n_3 \in \{1, 4, 10, 40\}$.
    <2>3. **For $p = 2$:** $n_2 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ (odd) and $n_2 \mid 15$.
        - Odd divisors of $15 \implies n_2 \in \{1, 3, 5, 15\}$.

<1>2. Part (2): Any subgroup of order 15 is cyclic (has an element of order 15):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $H \le G$ with $|H| = 15 = 3 \cdot 5$.
    <2>2. In $H$, the number of Sylow 5-subgroups $n_5(H)$ divides 3 and $n_5(H) \equiv 1 \pmod 5 \implies n_5(H) = 1$.
    <2>3. The number of Sylow 3-subgroups $n_3(H)$ divides 5 and $n_3(H) \equiv 1 \pmod 3 \implies n_3(H) = 1$.
    <2>4. Thus both the Sylow 3-subgroup $P_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$ and Sylow 5-subgroup $P_5 \cong \mathbb{Z}_5$ are normal in $H$.
    <2>5. Since $P_3 \cap P_5 = \{e\}$, $H \cong P_3 \times P_5 \cong \mathbb{Z}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_5 \cong \mathbb{Z}_{15}$.
    <2>6. Therefore, $H$ is cyclic and generated by an element of order 15.

<1>3. Part (3): If $G$ has no subgroup of order 15, then $n_3 \in \{10, 40\}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose $G$ has no subgroup of order 15.
    <2>2. Let $P_5 \le G$ be a Sylow 5-subgroup ($|P_5| = 5$).
    <2>3. The normalizer $N_G(P_5)$ has order $|N_G(P_5)| = \frac{|G|}{n_5} = \frac{240}{n_5}$.
    <2>4. If $3$ divides $|N_G(P_5)|$, then by Cauchy's Theorem, $N_G(P_5)$ contains an element of order 3, say $x$.
    <2>5. Then $P_5 \trianglelefteq \langle P_5, x \rangle$, so $\langle P_5, x \rangle$ is a subgroup of order $5 \cdot 3 = 15$.
    <2>6. Since $G$ has no subgroup of order 15, $3$ cannot divide $|N_G(P_5)|$.
    <2>7. Thus $3 \nmid \frac{240}{n_5}$, which forces $3 \mid n_5$. From $n_5 \in \{1, 6, 16\}$, this forces:
        $$n_5 = 6.$$
    <2>8. Now let $P_3 \le G$ be a Sylow 3-subgroup ($|P_3| = 3$).
    <2>9. The normalizer $N_G(P_3)$ has order $|N_G(P_3)| = \frac{240}{n_3}$.
    <2>10. If $5$ divides $|N_G(P_3)|$, Cauchy's Theorem gives an element of order 5 in $N_G(P_3)$, creating a subgroup $\langle P_3, y \rangle$ of order 15, a contradiction.
    <2>11. Therefore, $5$ cannot divide $|N_G(P_3)| = \frac{240}{n_3} = \frac{2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5}{n_3}$.
    <2>12. This requires $5 \mid n_3$.
    <2>13. From the list of possible values $n_3 \in \{1, 4, 10, 40\}$, the only multiples of 5 are:
        $$n_3 \in \{10, 40\}.$$

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $n_p$ are computed via Sylow's theorem, any group of order 15 is cyclic $\mathbb{Z}_{15}$, and absence of order 15 subgroups forces $5 \mid n_3 \implies n_3 \in \{10, 40\}$. Q.E.D.
:::
