::: problem
Let $n \ge 2$, and let $G$ be a finite group of odd order $|G| = m$.
Let $\phi: S_n \to G$ be a group homomorphism.
(1) Prove that if $\tau \in S_n$ is a transposition, then $\tau \in \ker\phi$.
(2) Prove that $\phi$ is the trivial homomorphism ($\ker\phi = S_n$).
(3) Does this result depend on whether $n$ is even or odd?
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that any group homomorphism $\phi: S_n \to G$ to an odd-order group $G$ is trivial.

<1>1. Transpositions belong to $\ker\phi$:
::: {.proof}
<2>1. Let $\tau = (i \, j) \in S_n$ be any transposition.
<2>2. Since $\tau^2 = e$, the order of $\tau$ in $S_n$ is $\operatorname{ord}(\tau) = 2$.
<2>3. Because $\phi$ is a group homomorphism, the order of the image $\phi(\tau) \in G$ must divide $\operatorname{ord}(\tau) = 2$:
$$\operatorname{ord}(\phi(\tau)) \mid 2 \implies \operatorname{ord}(\phi(\tau)) \in \{1, 2\}.$$
<2>4. By Lagrange's Theorem in the target group $G$, $\operatorname{ord}(\phi(\tau))$ must also divide $|G| = m$.
<2>5. Since $m = |G|$ is **odd**, $m$ has no even divisors, so $\gcd(2, m) = 1$.
<2>6. Therefore, $\operatorname{ord}(\phi(\tau)) = 1$, which means:
$$\phi(\tau) = e_G.$$
<2>7. Thus $\tau \in \ker\phi$ for every transposition $\tau \in S_n$.
:::

<1>2. Proof that $\phi$ is the trivial homomorphism:
::: {.proof}
<2>1. A fundamental property of the symmetric group $S_n$ is that **$S_n$ is generated by the set of all transpositions**:
$$S_n = \langle (i \, j) \mid 1 \le i < j \le n \rangle.$$
<2>2. By Step <1>1, every transposition $\tau$ is contained in the subgroup $\ker\phi$.
<2>3. Since $\ker\phi$ contains a generating set of $S_n$, we must have:
$$\ker\phi = S_n.$$
<2>4. Therefore, $\phi(\sigma) = e_G$ for every $\sigma \in S_n$, so $\phi$ is the **trivial homomorphism**.
:::

<1>3. Independence of the Parity of $n$:
::: {.proof}
<2>1. The argument in Steps <1>1 and <1>2 relies only on two facts:
- Transpositions have order 2 (true for all $n \ge 2$).
- Transpositions generate $S_n$ (true for all $n \ge 2$).
- $|G|$ has no element of order 2 (true whenever $|G|$ is odd).
<2>2. The parity of $n$ plays no role whatsoever.
<2>3. Therefore, the result holds for **all $n \ge 2$**, whether $n$ is even or odd.
:::

<1>4. Conclusion:
::: {.proof}
Every homomorphism $\phi: S_n \to G$ to an odd-order group is trivial.
:::
:::
