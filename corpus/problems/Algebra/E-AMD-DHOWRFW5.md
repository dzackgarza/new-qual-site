::: {.exercise}
Show that $S_n$ for $n \ge 5$ has exactly one non-trivial proper normal subgroup: $A_n$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that for $n \ge 5$, the alternating group $A_n$ is the unique non-trivial proper normal subgroup of the symmetric group $S_n$.

<1>1. Simplicity of $A_n$ for $n \ge 5$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For $n \ge 5$, the alternating group $A_n$ is simple (it is generated by 3-cycles, and all 3-cycles belong to a single conjugacy class in $A_n$).

<1>2. Analysis of the intersection $N \cap A_n$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $N \trianglelefteq S_n$ be a non-trivial normal subgroup ($N \neq \{e\}$).
    <2>2. The intersection $N \cap A_n$ is a normal subgroup of $A_n$.
    <2>3. By the simplicity of $A_n$ (<1>1), either $N \cap A_n = A_n$ or $N \cap A_n = \{e\}$.

<1>3. Case 1: $N \cap A_n = A_n$.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $N \cap A_n = A_n \implies A_n \le N \le S_n$.
    <2>2. Since $[S_n : A_n] = 2$, Lagrange's Theorem implies that either $N = A_n$ or $N = S_n$.
    <2>3. Thus if $N$ is a proper subgroup, $N = A_n$.

<1>4. Case 2: $N \cap A_n = \{e\}$ is impossible.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose $N \cap A_n = \{e\}$.
    <2>2. By the Second Isomorphism Theorem:
        $$N \cong N / (N \cap A_n) \cong N A_n / A_n \le S_n / A_n \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$
    <2>3. Since $N \neq \{e\}$, we must have $|N| = 2$, so $N = \{e, \sigma\}$ for some involution $\sigma \in S_n$.
    <2>4. For every $g \in S_n$, $g \sigma g^{-1} \in N \setminus \{e\}$, which forces $g \sigma g^{-1} = \sigma$.
    <2>5. Thus $\sigma \in Z(S_n)$, the center of $S_n$.
    <2>6. For $n \ge 3$, the center of $S_n$ is trivial ($Z(S_n) = \{e\}$): if $\sigma \neq e$ with $\sigma(1) = 2$, choosing $\tau = (2 \, 3)$ gives $(\tau \sigma)(1) = 3 \neq 2 = (\sigma \tau)(1)$, so $\sigma \tau \neq \tau \sigma$.
    <2>7. Hence no such central involution $\sigma$ exists, ruling out Case 2.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $A_n$ is the unique non-trivial proper normal subgroup of $S_n$ for $n \ge 5$. Q.E.D.
:::
