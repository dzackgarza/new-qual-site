::: problem
- Show that $\Aut(S_3) = \Inn(S_3) \cong S_3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
The center of $S_3$ is trivial, so conjugation gives
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(S_3)\cong S_3/Z(S_3)\cong S_3,
\]
and hence $|\operatorname{Inn}(S_3)|=6$.

Any automorphism of $S_3$ preserves element orders, so it permutes the three transpositions. Since two distinct transpositions generate $S_3$, an automorphism is determined by the images of two fixed distinct transpositions. There are at most $3\cdot2=6$ such choices. Therefore
\[
6=|\operatorname{Inn}(S_3)|\le |\operatorname{Aut}(S_3)|\le6.
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(S_3)=\operatorname{Inn}(S_3)\cong S_3.
\]
:::
