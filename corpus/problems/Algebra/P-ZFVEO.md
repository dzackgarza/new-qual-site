::: {.problem}
Show that $S_n$ is generated by transpositions.
:::

::: {.solution}
Every permutation can be written as a product of disjoint cycles, so it suffices to express a cycle as a product of transpositions.

For a $k$-cycle,
\[
(a_1a_2\cdots a_k)
=(a_1a_k)(a_1a_{k-1})\cdots(a_1a_2).
\]
Thus every cycle is a product of transpositions, hence every permutation is a product of transpositions.

Therefore
\[
S_n=\langle\text{transpositions}\rangle.
\]
In fact the adjacent transpositions already suffice:
\[
S_n=\langle(12),(23),\ldots,(n-1\ n)\rangle,
\]
because any transposition $(ij)$ can be obtained from adjacent ones.
:::
