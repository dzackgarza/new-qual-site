::: {.problem}
Show that if $G$ is finite and solvable, then every composition factor of $G$ has prime order.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let
\[
1=G_0\triangleleft G_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft G_r=G
\]
be a composition series. Each factor
\[
G_i/G_{i-1}
\]
is simple by definition of a composition series.

Because $G$ is solvable, every subgroup and every quotient of $G$ is solvable. Hence each composition factor is both simple and solvable.

A nontrivial simple solvable group must be abelian: if $H$ is solvable and nonabelian, then its derived subgroup $H'=[H,H]$ is a proper nontrivial normal subgroup, contradicting simplicity. Thus every composition factor is a finite simple abelian group.

A finite simple abelian group is cyclic of prime order. Indeed, if $A$ is finite abelian and $1\ne a\in A$, then $\langle a\rangle\normal A$; simplicity forces $A=\langle a\rangle$, so $A$ is cyclic. A finite cyclic group is simple exactly when its order is prime.

Therefore every composition factor of $G$ has prime order.
:::
