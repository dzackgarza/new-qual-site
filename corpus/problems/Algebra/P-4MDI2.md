::: problem
7. If $F$ is some (not necessarily Galois) field extension over $K$ such that $[F: K]=6$ and \operatorname{Aut}_K F \simeq S_{3}$, then $F$ is the splitting field of an irreducible cubic over $K[x]$.
8. Recall the definition of the join $H_1\vee H_2=\langle H_1,H_2\rangle$ of two subgroups. For $F$ a finite dimensional Galois extension over $K$ and let $A$ and $B$ be intermediate extensions. Prove that
a. $\operatorname{Aut}_{A B} F=\mathrm{Aut}_{A} F \cap \mathrm{Aut}_{B} F$
b. \operatorname{Aut}_{A\cap B}F=\mathrm{Aut}_{A} F \vee \mathrm{Aut}_{B} F$
:::


::: {.solution}
<1>1. In part 7, the extension $F/K$ is Galois with Galois group $S_3$.
::: {.proof}
For every finite extension,
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}_K(F)|\le [F:K],
\]
with equality exactly for a Galois extension. Here
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}_K(F)|=|S_3|=6=[F:K].
\]
Hence $F/K$ is Galois and $\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)\cong S_3$.
:::

<1>2. Choose a subgroup $H\le\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)$ of order $2$ and let $E=F^H$. Then $[E:K]=3$.
::: {.proof}
By Galois correspondence,
\[
[E:K]=[\operatorname{Gal}(F/K):H]=6/2=3.
\]
Because $F/K$ is finite Galois, $E/K$ is finite separable.
:::

<1>3. There is $\alpha\in E$ such that $E=K(\alpha)$ and the minimal polynomial $f$ of $\alpha$ over $K$ is irreducible of degree $3$.
::: {.proof}
The primitive element theorem applies to the finite separable extension $E/K$. Thus $E=K(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha$, and
\[
\deg f=[K(\alpha):K]=3.
\]
:::

<1>4. The splitting field of $f$ is all of $F$.
::: {.proof}
Since $F/K$ is normal, all $K$-conjugates of $\alpha$ lie in $F$, so $f$ splits in $F$. Let $L$ be its splitting field inside $F$. The pointwise stabilizer of all conjugates of $\alpha$ is the core
\[
\bigcap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}
\]
of $H$ in $G=S_3$. A subgroup of order $2$ in $S_3$ is generated by a transposition; its three conjugates intersect trivially. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/L)=1,
\]
so $L=F$. Therefore $F$ is the splitting field of the irreducible cubic $f$.
:::

Now let $F/K$ be finite Galois for part 8, set
\[
G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K),
\qquad
H_A=\operatorname{Gal}(F/A),
\qquad
H_B=\operatorname{Gal}(F/B).
\]

<1>5. One has
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/AB)=H_A\cap H_B.
\]
::: {.proof}
An automorphism $\sigma\in G$ fixes the compositum $AB$ pointwise if and only if it fixes both $A$ and $B$ pointwise. Thus
\[
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F/AB)
\Longleftrightarrow
\sigma\in H_A\cap H_B.
\]
:::

<1>6. The fixed field of the join $H_A\vee H_B=\langle H_A,H_B\rangle$ is $A\cap B$.
::: {.proof}
An element $x\in F$ is fixed by every element of the subgroup generated by $H_A$ and $H_B$ exactly when it is fixed by every element of $H_A$ and every element of $H_B$. Therefore
\[
F^{H_A\vee H_B}=F^{H_A}\cap F^{H_B}=A\cap B,
\]
using Galois correspondence for $F^{H_A}=A$ and $F^{H_B}=B$.
:::

<1>7. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/A\cap B)=H_A\vee H_B.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>6, the subgroup $H_A\vee H_B$ has fixed field $A\cap B$. The finite Galois correspondence is a bijection between subgroups of $G$ and intermediate fields, so the unique subgroup with this fixed field is
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/A\cap B).
\]
Thus the two groups are equal.
:::
