::: problem
(1) State the definition of a **Noetherian ring** (give all three equivalent characterizations).
(2) Give standard examples and non-examples of Noetherian rings.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Define Noetherian rings, prove equivalence of the three standard conditions (ACC, Finite Generation, Maximal Elements), and provide examples.

<1>1. Three Equivalent Definitions of a Noetherian Ring:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $R$ be a ring (with 1). $R$ is called **(left) Noetherian** if it satisfies any of the following equivalent conditions:
        1. **Ascending Chain Condition (ACC):** Every ascending chain of (left) ideals:
           $$I_1 \subseteq I_2 \subseteq I_3 \subseteq \cdots$$
           eventually stabilizes (i.e. there exists $N \ge 1$ such that $I_n = I_N$ for all $n \ge N$).
        2. **Finite Generation:** Every (left) ideal $I \subseteq R$ is **finitely generated** as an $R$-module:
           $$I = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k) = R x_1 + \dots + R x_k.$$
        3. **Maximal Condition:** Every non-empty family $\mathcal{S}$ of (left) ideals of $R$ has a **maximal element** with respect to inclusion.

<1>2. Proof of Equivalence ($1 \iff 2 \iff 3$):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **(1 $\implies$ 2):** Suppose ACC holds. Let $I \subseteq R$.
        If $I = (0)$, it is generated by $0$. Otherwise, pick $x_1 \in I \setminus \{0\}$.
        If $(x_1) \subsetneq I$, pick $x_2 \in I \setminus (x_1)$.
        If $(x_1, x_2) \subsetneq I$, pick $x_3 \in I \setminus (x_1, x_2)$.
        This produces a strictly ascending chain $(x_1) \subsetneq (x_1, x_2) \subsetneq (x_1, x_2, x_3) \subsetneq \cdots$.
        By ACC, this chain must terminate at some finite step $k$, meaning $I = (x_1, \dots, x_k)$.
    <2>2. **(2 $\implies$ 3):** Suppose all ideals are finitely generated. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a non-empty family of ideals.
        Every chain in $\mathcal{S}$ has an upper bound (its union $U = \bigcup I_\alpha$, which is an ideal, hence finitely generated, so $U \in \mathcal{S}$).
        By Zorn's Lemma, $\mathcal{S}$ has a maximal element.
    <2>3. **(3 $\implies$ 1):** Given a chain $I_1 \subseteq I_2 \subseteq \cdots$, the family $\{I_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ has a maximal element $I_N$, which forces $I_n = I_N$ for all $n \ge N$.

<1>3. Examples of Noetherian Rings:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Fields:** Any field $k$ (only ideals are $(0)$ and $k$).
    <2>2. **PIDs:** The integers $\mathbb{Z}$, and any Principal Ideal Domain (since every ideal is generated by 1 element).
    <2>3. **Polynomial Rings (Hilbert Basis Theorem):** $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ and $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ over any Noetherian ring.
    <2>4. **Quotients:** Any quotient $R/I$ of a Noetherian ring $R$.
    <2>5. **Localizations / Power Series:** Formal power series ring $k[[x]]$ and localizations $S^{-1} R$.

<1>4. Non-Examples:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The polynomial ring in infinitely many variables $k[x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots]$, with the infinite non-terminating chain $(x_1) \subsetneq (x_1, x_2) \subsetneq (x_1, x_2, x_3) \subsetneq \cdots$.
    <2>2. The ring of continuous functions $C([0, 1], \mathbb{R})$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    A Noetherian ring satisfies the ACC, finite generation of ideals, and maximal element property. Q.E.D.
:::
