::: {.problem}
(a) State the definition of a Noetherian ring (give all three equivalent characterizations).

(b) Give standard examples and non-examples of Noetherian rings.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring with $1$. Consider the conditions:

(i) every ascending chain of ideals $I_1\subseteq I_2\subseteq\cdots$ of $R$ stabilizes;

(ii) every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated;

(iii) every nonempty set of ideals of $R$ has a maximal element under inclusion.

The ring $R$ is Noetherian if it satisfies these equivalent conditions.

::: pf

::: pf-step

(a) Conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) are equivalent.

::: pf-proof

(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): if an ideal $I$ were not finitely generated, choose successively $x_i\in I\setminus(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1})$ to obtain a strictly ascending chain $(x_1)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2)\subsetneq\cdots$.

(ii)$\Rightarrow$(i): if $I_1\subseteq I_2\subseteq\cdots$, then $I=\bigcup_n I_n$ is an ideal. Its finitely many generators all lie in some $I_N$, so $I=I_N$ and the chain stabilizes.

(i)$\Rightarrow$(iii): if a nonempty set $\mathcal S$ of ideals had no maximal element, then starting from any $I_1\in\mathcal S$ we could choose $I_{n+1}\in\mathcal S$ with $I_n\subsetneq I_{n+1}$, giving a chain that does not stabilize.

(iii)$\Rightarrow$(i): a maximal element $I_N$ of the set $\{I_n\}$ of members of a chain satisfies $I_n=I_N$ for all $n\ge N$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) Fields, $\ZZ$ and every PID, $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ and $R[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]$ for Noetherian $R$, and every quotient and localization of a Noetherian ring are Noetherian. The polynomial ring $k[x_1,x_2,\ldots]$ in infinitely many variables over a field $k$ is not Noetherian.

::: pf-proof

In a PID every ideal is generated by one element. The polynomial and power series rings are Noetherian by Hilbert's basis theorem and its power series form. Ideals of $R/I$ are the images of ideals of $R$ containing $I$, and ideals of $S^{-1}R$ are extended from ideals of $R$, so finite generation passes to both. In $k[x_1,x_2,\ldots]$ the chain
$$
(x_1)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2)\subsetneq\cdots
$$
does not stabilize, because $x_{n+1}\notin(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$: setting $x_1=\cdots=x_n=0$ kills $(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ but not $x_{n+1}$.

:::
