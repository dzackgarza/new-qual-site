::: {.exercise}
Show that $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ is not finitely generated as an abelian group.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that the additive group of rational numbers $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ is not finitely generated.

<1>1. Strategy: Proof by contradiction:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose, for contradiction, that $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ is generated by a finite set of rational numbers:
        $$\mathbb{Q} = \left\langle \frac{a_1}{b_1}, \frac{a_2}{b_2}, \dots, \frac{a_n}{b_n} \right\rangle$$
        where $a_i \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $b_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ for each $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$.

<1>2. Common denominator construction:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $M = b_1 b_2 \cdots b_n$ (or $M = \operatorname{lcm}(b_1, \dots, b_n)$) be the product of all denominators of the generators.
    <2>2. Any element $x \in \langle \frac{a_1}{b_1}, \dots, \frac{a_n}{b_n} \rangle$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of the generators:
        $$x = k_1 \frac{a_1}{b_1} + k_2 \frac{a_2}{b_2} + \cdots + k_n \frac{a_n}{b_n} \quad (k_i \in \mathbb{Z}).$$
    <2>3. Putting all terms over the common denominator $M$:
        $$x = \frac{k_1 a_1 (M/b_1) + k_2 a_2 (M/b_2) + \cdots + k_n a_n (M/b_n)}{M} = \frac{N}{M}$$
        where $N \in \mathbb{Z}$ is an integer.
    <2>4. Thus, every element generated by this set is of the form $N/M$ for some integer $N \in \mathbb{Z}$, which means:
        $$\left\langle \frac{a_1}{b_1}, \dots, \frac{a_n}{b_n} \right\rangle \subseteq \frac{1}{M}\mathbb{Z} = \left\{ \frac{N}{M} \;\middle|\; N \in \mathbb{Z} \right\}.$$

<1>3. Constructing an element outside the generated subgroup:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Choose a prime $p$ that does not divide $M$ (such a prime exists because there are infinitely many primes).
    <2>2. Consider the rational number $y = \frac{1}{pM} \in \mathbb{Q}$.
    <2>3. If $y \in \frac{1}{M}\mathbb{Z}$, then there exists an integer $N \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that:
        $$\frac{1}{pM} = \frac{N}{M} \implies 1 = pN.$$
    <2>4. But $p \ge 2$ and $N \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $pN \ge 2 > 1$ (or $pN \le -2 < 1$), which is a contradiction.
    <2>5. Thus $\frac{1}{pM} \notin \langle \frac{a_1}{b_1}, \dots, \frac{a_n}{b_n} \rangle$.

<1>4. Alternative proof via divisibility:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ is a **divisible group**: for every $x \in \mathbb{Q}$ and every non-zero integer $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, there exists $y \in \mathbb{Q}$ such that $my = x$ (namely $y = x/m$).
    <2>2. A non-trivial finitely generated abelian group $G \cong \mathbb{Z}^r \oplus T$ has non-trivial homomorphisms to $\mathbb{Z}$ (if $r > 0$) or is finite (if $r = 0$).
    <2>3. But $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ is infinite and has no non-trivial finite quotients (any quotient of a divisible group is divisible, but no non-trivial finite group is divisible since $m = |H|$ annihilates $H$).
    <2>4. Hence $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ cannot be finitely generated.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ is not finitely generated. Q.E.D.
:::
