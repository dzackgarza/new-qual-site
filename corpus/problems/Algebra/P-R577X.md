::: {.problem}
For which positive integers $n$ is a regular $n$-gon constructible by ruler and compass?
:::

::: {.solution}
By the Gauss--Wantzel theorem, a regular $n$-gon is constructible if and only if
\[
n=2^k p_1p_2\cdots p_r,
\]
where $k\ge0$ and the $p_i$ are distinct Fermat primes,
\[
p_i=2^{2^{m_i}}+1.
\]

Indeed, a regular $n$-gon is constructible exactly when the primitive $n$th root of unity $\zeta_n$ is constructible over $\QQ$. This is equivalent to the degree
\[
[\QQ(\zeta_n):\QQ]=\varphi(n)
\]
being a power of $2$ and the cyclotomic extension having a tower of quadratic subextensions. The condition that $\varphi(n)$ be a power of $2$ is equivalent to the displayed factorization of $n$.

Thus powers of $2$ are always allowed, and every odd prime factor must be a distinct Fermat prime.
:::
