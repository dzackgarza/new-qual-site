::: {.exercise}
Compute all intermediate field extensions of $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/4}, \zeta_8)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Compute the Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ of $K = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/4}, \zeta_8)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and determine all intermediate fields via the Galois correspondence.

<1>1. Field identification and Galois group $G \cong D_4$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $\alpha = 2^{1/4}$. Then $\alpha^2 = \sqrt{2}$.
    <2>2. Since $\zeta_8 = \frac{\sqrt{2} + i\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{1+i}{\sqrt{2}}$, we have $i = \alpha^2 \zeta_8 - 1 \in K$ and $\zeta_8 = \frac{1+i}{\alpha^2} \in \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, i)$.
    <2>3. Thus $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, i)$, which is the splitting field of the irreducible polynomial $x^4 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
    <2>4. The degree is $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha, i) : \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)][\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2 \cdot 4 = 8$.
    <2>5. The Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4$ is generated by:
        - $\sigma$: $\sigma(\alpha) = i\alpha$, $\sigma(i) = i$ (order $4$),
        - $\tau$: $\tau(\alpha) = \alpha$, $\tau(i) = -i$ (complex conjugation, order $2$),
        satisfying $\tau \sigma \tau = \sigma^{-1}$.

<1>2. Intermediate fields of degree $2$ (subgroups of order $4$):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $D_4$ has three subgroups of index $2$:
        - $H_1 = \langle \sigma \rangle = \{e, \sigma, \sigma^2, \sigma^3\} \implies K^{H_1} = \mathbb{Q}(i)$.
        - $H_2 = \langle \sigma^2, \tau \rangle = \{e, \sigma^2, \tau, \sigma^2\tau\} \implies K^{H_2} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$.
        - $H_3 = \langle \sigma^2, \sigma\tau \rangle = \{e, \sigma^2, \sigma\tau, \sigma^3\tau\} \implies K^{H_3} = \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{2})$.

<1>3. Intermediate fields of degree $4$ (subgroups of order $2$):
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. $D_4$ has five subgroups of order $2$:
        - $J_1 = \langle \sigma^2 \rangle = \{e, \sigma^2\} = Z(D_4) \implies K^{J_1} = \mathbb{Q}(i, \sqrt{2}) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)$ (the unique Galois quartic intermediate field).
        - $J_2 = \langle \tau \rangle = \{e, \tau\} \implies K^{J_2} = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/4})$.
        - $J_3 = \langle \sigma^2\tau \rangle = \{e, \sigma^2\tau\} \implies K^{J_3} = \mathbb{Q}(i\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}(i 2^{1/4})$.
        - $J_4 = \langle \sigma\tau \rangle = \{e, \sigma\tau\} \implies K^{J_4} = \mathbb{Q}((1+i)\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}((1+i)2^{1/4})$.
        - $J_5 = \langle \sigma^3\tau \rangle = \{e, \sigma^3\tau\} \implies K^{J_5} = \mathbb{Q}((1-i)\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}((1-i)2^{1/4})$.

<1>4. Complete list of intermediate fields:
    - **Degree 1:** $\mathbb{Q}$.
    - **Degree 2:** $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, $\mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{2})$.
    - **Degree 4:** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)$, $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/4})$, $\mathbb{Q}(i 2^{1/4})$, $\mathbb{Q}((1+i)2^{1/4})$, $\mathbb{Q}((1-i)2^{1/4})$.
    - **Degree 8:** $K = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/4}, \zeta_8)$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    There are exactly $10$ intermediate fields between $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/4}, \zeta_8)$, corresponding bijectively to the $10$ subgroups of $D_4$. Q.E.D.
:::
