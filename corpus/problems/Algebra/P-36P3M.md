::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a Principal Ideal Domain (PID). Let $n > 0$ and $A \in M_n(R)$ be a square $n \times n$ matrix with coefficients in $R$.
Consider the finitely generated $R$-module $M \coloneqq R^n / \operatorname{im}(A)$.

(1) Give a necessary and sufficient condition for $M$ to be a **torsion module** (i.e. every element $m \in M$ is a torsion element).
Justify your answer.
(2) Let $F$ be a field and let $R \coloneqq F[x]$.
Give an explicit example of an integer $n > 0$ and a square matrix $A \in M_n(R)$ such that $M \coloneqq R^n / \operatorname{im}(A)$ is isomorphic as an $R$-module to $R \times F$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Apply the Smith Normal Form over PIDs to characterize torsion cokernels, and construct an explicit matrix with cokernel $R \times F$.

<1>1. Part 1: Condition for $M = R^n/\operatorname{im}(A)$ to be Torsion: *Proof:* <2>1. **Claim:** $M$ is a torsion $R$-module if and only if $\det(A) \ne 0$.
<2>2. **Proof via Smith Normal Form:** - Since $R$ is a PID, there exist invertible matrices $P, Q \in \operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ such that: $$P A Q = D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, d_2, \dots, d_n)$$ where $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_n \in R$.

- The change of basis by $P$ and $Q$ induces an $R$-module isomorphism: $$M = R^n / \operatorname{im}(A) \cong R^n / \operatorname{im}(D) \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^n R / (d_i).$$ - Notice that $\det(A) = \det(P^{-1}) \det(D) \det(Q^{-1}) = u \cdot \prod_{i=1}^n d_i$, where $u = (\det P \det Q)^{-1} \in R^\times$.
  <2>3. **Direction $(\implies)$:** - If $M$ is torsion, then every direct summand $R/(d_i)$ is a torsion $R$-module.

- For a PID $R$, $R/(d_i)$ is torsion $\iff d_i \ne 0$ (since if $d_i = 0$, $R/(0) \cong R$ is free of rank 1, which has no non-zero torsion elements).

- Thus all $d_i \ne 0$, so $\det(D) = \prod_{i=1}^n d_i \ne 0$, which implies $\det(A) \ne 0$.
  <2>4. **Direction $(\impliedby)$:** - If $\det(A) \ne 0$, then $\det(D) \ne 0$, so $d_i \ne 0$ for all $i = 1, \dots, n$.

- Let $d = \det(A) \in R \setminus \{0\}$.

- By Cramer's Rule, $A \cdot \operatorname{adj}(A) = \det(A) I_n = d I_n$.

- For any $v \in R^n$, $d v = A(\operatorname{adj}(A) v) \in \operatorname{im}(A)$.

- Thus $d \cdot (v + \operatorname{im}(A)) = 0$ in $M$, meaning every element in $M$ is annihilated by $d \ne 0$.

- Therefore, $M$ is a torsion module.

<1>2. Part 2: Example with $R = F[x]$ and $M \cong R \times F$: *Proof:* <2>1. Note that as an $R$-module, $F \cong F[x]/(x)$ (or $F[x]/(x - c)$ for any $c \in F$). <2>2. Thus we want: $$M \cong R \times F \cong F[x] \times (F[x]/(x)) \cong (F[x]/(0)) \times (F[x]/(x)).$$ <2>3. Choose $n = 2$ and define the diagonal matrix: $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & x \end{pmatrix} \in M_2(F[x]).$$ <2>4. The image of $A$ in $R^2 = F[x]^2$ is: $$\operatorname{im}(A) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ x g(x) \end{pmatrix} \;\middle|\; g(x) \in F[x] \right\} = \{0\} \times x F[x].$$ <2>5. The quotient module is: $$M = \frac{F[x] \times F[x]}{\{0\} \times x F[x]} \cong \frac{F[x]}{\{0\}} \times \frac{F[x]}{(x)} \cong F[x] \times F = R \times F.$$ <2>6. This provides an explicit $2 \times 2$ matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & x \end{pmatrix}$ over $F[x]$.

<1>3. Conclusion: $R^n/\operatorname{im}(A)$ is torsion $\iff \det(A) \ne 0$; and $A = \operatorname{diag}(0, x) \in M_2(F[x])$ gives $M \cong R \times F$.
Q.E.D.
