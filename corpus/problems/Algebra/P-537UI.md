::: {.problem}
Is $\QQ(2^{1/3})/\QQ$ normal?
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this exercise: the card's own argument follows, with the minimal polynomial, its roots and the failure of splitting as separate steps.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #minimal}
The minimal polynomial of $\alpha=2^{1/3}$ over $\QQ$ is $x^{3}-2$, which is irreducible.

::: pf-proof
$\alpha$ is a root of $x^{3}-2$, and that polynomial is irreducible over $\QQ$ by Eisenstein's criterion at the prime $2$: the leading coefficient is $1$, not divisible by $2$, while the constant term $-2$ and the other coefficients $0,0$ are divisible by $2$, and $-2$ is not divisible by $4$. So $x^{3}-2$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ and $\deg_{\QQ}\alpha=3$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #roots}
The roots of $x^{3}-2$ are $\alpha$, $\omega\alpha$ and $\omega^{2}\alpha$, where $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ is a primitive cube root of unity; the latter two are non-real.

::: pf-proof
$x^{3}-2=(x-\alpha)(x^{2}+\alpha x+\alpha^{2})$ and the quadratic factor has roots $\alpha\cdot(-\tfrac12\pm i\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2})$, that is $\omega\alpha$ and $\omega^{2}\alpha$; these are non-real since $\operatorname{Im}(\omega\alpha)=\alpha\sqrt3/2\ne0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not-split}
$x^{3}-2$ does not split over $\QQ(\alpha)$.

::: pf-proof
$\QQ(\alpha)\subseteq\RR$, because $\alpha$ is real and $\QQ\subseteq\RR$; a subfield of $\RR$ contains no non-real number. So the two non-real roots of step [](#roots){.pf-ref} are not in $\QQ(\alpha)$, and the polynomial has a root in $\QQ(\alpha)$ without splitting there.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
By steps [](#minimal){.pf-ref} and [](#not-split){.pf-ref} the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\QQ$ has a root in $\QQ(\alpha)$ and does not split over it, so $\QQ(2^{1/3})/\QQ$ is not normal.
:::
