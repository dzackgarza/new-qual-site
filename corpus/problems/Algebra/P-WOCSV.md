::: {.problem}
Classify the groups of order $4$. Which of them occur as Galois groups over $\QQ$?
:::

::: {.solution}
No retained solution set covers this problem, and the card names no source; the argument below is the card's own and follows the card's previous prose, with the abelianness of a group of order $p^2$ and the two Galois computations made into steps.

Throughout $G$ is a group with $\cardinality G=4$, $C_n$ is the cyclic group of order $n$, and "occurs as a Galois group over $\QQ$" means: some finite Galois extension of $\QQ$ has that group as its Galois group.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #abelian}
Every group of order $p^2$, for $p$ prime, is abelian; in particular $G$ is abelian.

::: pf-proof
The centre $Z(G)$ has order divisible by $p$: the class equation $\cardinality G=\cardinality{Z(G)}+\sum_i[\text{index of a nontrivial class}]$ has all the summands divisible by $p$, so $p$ divides $\cardinality{Z(G)}$. Hence $Z(G)$ has order $p$ or $p^2$; in the second case $G$ is abelian, and in the first, $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic of order $p$, which forces $G$ to be abelian (if $G/Z(G)$ is generated by the class of $a$, every element is $a^ig$ with $g\in Z(G)$, and such elements commute). Either way $G$ is abelian.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #classification}
Up to isomorphism there are exactly two groups of order $4$: $C_4$ and $C_2\times C_2$.

::: pf-proof
$G$ is abelian by step [](#abelian){.pf-ref}, so by the classification of finite abelian groups $G$ is a product of cyclic groups of prime power order, here $C_4$ or $C_2\times C_2$. Both occur, and they are not isomorphic: $C_4$ has an element of order $4$ and $C_2\times C_2$ has none.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #c4-galois}
$C_4$ occurs as a Galois group over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
The cyclotomic extension $\QQ(\zeta_5)/\QQ$ is Galois, being the splitting field of the separable $x^5-1$, and its Galois group is $(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^\times$, acting by $\sigma_a(\zeta_5)=\zeta_5^a$: every automorphism is determined by the image of $\zeta_5$, which must be another primitive fifth root of unity, and any such choice gives an automorphism. Now $(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^\times$ is cyclic of order $4$, and a cyclic group of order $4$ is $C_4$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #v4-galois}
$C_2\times C_2$ occurs as a Galois group over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
$L=\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ is the splitting field of $(x^2-2)(x^2-3)$ over $\QQ$, hence Galois. It has degree $4$ over $\QQ$: $[\QQ(\sqrt2):\QQ]=2$ and $\sqrt3\notin\QQ(\sqrt2)$ — if $\sqrt3=a+b\sqrt2$ with $a,b\in\QQ$, then $3=a^2+2b^2+2ab\sqrt2$ forces $ab=0$, giving $\sqrt3\in\QQ$ or $\sqrt3/\sqrt2\in\QQ$, both false. An automorphism is determined by the signs it assigns to $\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt3$, each choice $\pm$ being realized (the identity and complex conjugation on each quadratic subfield extend), so $\Gal(L/\QQ)\cong C_2\times C_2$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #conclusion}
The groups of order $4$ are $C_4$ and $C_2\times C_2$, and both occur as Galois groups over $\QQ$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#classification){.pf-ref} is the classification, and steps [](#c4-galois){.pf-ref} and [](#v4-galois){.pf-ref} give an extension realizing each type. Since the two types exhaust the groups of order $4$, both are realized.
:::
