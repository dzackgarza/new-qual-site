::: {.problem}
Show that the transitive subgroups of the symmetric group $S_3$ (acting naturally on $\{1, 2, 3\}$) are precisely $S_3$ and $A_3$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Write $H_1$ for the stabilizer of $1$ in a subgroup $H\le S_3$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #order}

If $H\le S_3$ is transitive, then $\abs{H}$ is $3$ or $6$.

::: pf-proof

By the orbit-stabilizer theorem, $\abs{H}=3\,\abs{H_1}$, so $3\mid\abs{H}$. Since $\abs{H}\mid6$ by Lagrange's theorem, $\abs{H}$ is $3$ or $6$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

The transitive subgroups of $S_3$ are exactly $S_3$ and $A_3=\{1,(123),(132)\}$.

::: pf-proof

By step [](#order){.pf-ref}, a transitive $H$ has order $6$, so $H=S_3$, or order $3$, so $H$ is generated by a $3$-cycle; both $3$-cycles generate $A_3$.
Conversely, $A_3$ is transitive because $(123)$ sends $1\mapsto2\mapsto3$, and hence so is $S_3\supseteq A_3$.

:::
