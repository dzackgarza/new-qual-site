::: problem
Show that the transitive subgroups of the symmetric group $S_3$ (acting naturally on $\{1, 2, 3\}$) are precisely $S_3$ and $A_3$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Classify all transitive subgroups of $S_3$ acting on $X = \{1, 2, 3\}$.

<1>1. Orbit-Stabilizer divisibility condition:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $H \le S_3$ be a transitive subgroup acting on $X = \{1, 2, 3\}$.
    <2>2. By the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem, for any element $x \in X$:
        $$|H| = |\operatorname{Orb}_H(x)| \cdot |\operatorname{Stab}_H(x)|.$$
    <2>3. Because the action of $H$ is transitive, the orbit is the entire set: $\operatorname{Orb}_H(x) = X$, so $|\operatorname{Orb}_H(x)| = |X| = 3$.
    <2>4. Thus:
        $$|H| = 3 \cdot |\operatorname{Stab}_H(x)| \implies 3 \mid |H|.$$
    <2>5. By Lagrange's Theorem, $|H|$ divides $|S_3| = 6$.
    <2>6. The only positive integers dividing 6 that are multiples of 3 are $|H| = 3$ and $|H| = 6$.

<1>2. Subgroups of order 6:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. If $|H| = 6$, then $H = S_3$.
    <2>2. The full symmetric group $S_3$ is transitive on $\{1, 2, 3\}$ (e.g. $(1 \ 2 \ 3)$ takes $1 \mapsto 2 \mapsto 3$).

<1>3. Subgroups of order 3:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. If $|H| = 3$, since 3 is prime, $H$ is a cyclic group generated by an element of order 3.
    <2>2. The only elements of order 3 in $S_3$ are the 3-cycles $(1 \ 2 \ 3)$ and $(1 \ 3 \ 2)$.
    <2>3. These generate the unique subgroup of order 3 in $S_3$, which is the alternating group:
        $$A_3 = \{e, (1 \ 2 \ 3), (1 \ 3 \ 2)\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_3.$$
    <2>4. The action of $A_3$ on $\{1, 2, 3\}$ is transitive because $\operatorname{Orb}_{A_3}(1) = \{1, (1\,2\,3)(1), (1\,3\,2)(1)\} = \{1, 2, 3\}$.

<1>4. Other subgroups of $S_3$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The remaining subgroups of $S_3$ have orders $|H| \in \{1, 2\}$.
    <2>2. - Order 1: $\{e\}$ (orbit of 1 has size 1, not transitive).
    <2>3. - Order 2: $\langle (1 \ 2) \rangle, \langle (1 \ 3) \rangle, \langle (2 \ 3) \rangle$ (the orbit of 3 under $\langle (1 \ 2) \rangle$ is $\{3\}$, size 1, not transitive).
    <2>4. Thus no subgroup of order 1 or 2 is transitive.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    The only transitive subgroups of $S_3$ are $A_3$ and $S_3$. Q.E.D.
:::
