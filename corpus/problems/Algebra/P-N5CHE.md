::: {.problem}
Show that
$$
[S_n,S_n]=A_n.
$$
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: {.pf-step #sub}
$[S_n,S_n]\subseteq A_n$.

::: pf-proof
The sign homomorphism $\operatorname{sgn}\colon S_n\to\{\pm1\}$ has abelian target, so every commutator lies in its kernel $A_n$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #sup}
$A_n\subseteq[S_n,S_n]$.

::: pf-proof
For $n\le2$, $A_n$ is trivial.
For $n\ge3$, $A_n$ is generated by $3$-cycles.
The $3$-cycle $(132)$ is the commutator $(12)(23)(12)^{-1}(23)^{-1}=(12)(23)(12)(23)$.
Every $3$-cycle is conjugate in $S_n$ to $(132)$, and a conjugate of a commutator is a commutator, so every $3$-cycle lies in $[S_n,S_n]$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$[S_n,S_n]=A_n$.

::: pf-proof
Steps [](#sub){.pf-ref} and [](#sup){.pf-ref}.
:::
