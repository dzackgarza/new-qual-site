::: problem
Show that
\[
[S_n,S_n]=A_n.
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
The sign homomorphism
\[
\operatorname{sgn}:S_n\to\{\pm1\}
\]
has abelian target, so every commutator lies in its kernel. Hence
\[
[S_n,S_n]\subseteq A_n.
\]

For the reverse inclusion, recall that $A_n$ is generated by $3$-cycles. Every $3$-cycle is a commutator of transpositions. For example,
\[
[(12),(23)]
=(12)(23)(12)(23)
=(132).
\]
Conjugating gives every $3$-cycle, so all generators of $A_n$ lie in $[S_n,S_n]$. Therefore
\[
A_n\subseteq[S_n,S_n].
\]

Thus
\[
[S_n,S_n]=A_n.
\]
For $n=1,2$ both sides are trivial, so the statement remains valid.
:::
