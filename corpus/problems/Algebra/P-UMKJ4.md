::: {.problem}
Let $R$ be a PID and $A\in M_{m\times n}(R)$. Prove that there exist invertible matrices $P\in GL_m(R)$ and $Q\in GL_n(R)$ such that
\[
PAQ=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r,0,\ldots,0),
\qquad d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_r.
\]
Explain uniqueness up to multiplication of the $d_i$ by units.
:::

::: {.solution}
We prove existence by induction on $m+n$.

If $A=0$, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise use row and column swaps to put a nonzero entry in position $(1,1)$; call it $d$.

<1>1. Improve the pivot until it divides every entry.
Suppose first that $d$ does not divide some entry in the first column, say $a$. Since $R$ is a PID,
\[
(d,a)=(g)
\]
for a gcd $g$, and Bézout gives
\[
g=ud+va.
\]
An invertible $2\times2$ row operation on rows $1$ and the row containing $a$ replaces the first-column pair $(d,a)^t$ by $(g,0)^t$. If $d\nmid a$, then
\[
(d)\subsetneq(g).
\]
The same argument with column operations applies when $d$ fails to divide an entry in the first row.

After clearing the rest of the first row and column, suppose some entry $b$ in the lower-right block is not divisible by $d$. Add the row containing $b$ to the first row. Then $b$ appears in the first row, and the same Bézout column operation replaces $d$ by $\gcd(d,b)$, again strictly enlarging the principal ideal generated by the pivot.

Thus every time the current pivot fails to divide some entry of the matrix, elementary operations replace it by a new pivot whose principal ideal strictly contains the old one. A PID is Noetherian, so there is no infinite strictly ascending chain of ideals. Hence after finitely many steps we reach a pivot $d_1$ dividing every entry of the current matrix.

Using elementary row and column operations, clear the rest of the first column and first row. We obtain
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
d_1&0\\
0&B
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $d_1$ divides every entry of $B$.

<1>2. Induct on the remaining block.
Apply the induction hypothesis to $B$ using operations only on its rows and columns. This gives
\[
B\sim\operatorname{diag}(d_2,\ldots,d_r,0,\ldots,0)
\]
with
\[
d_2\mid d_3\mid\cdots\mid d_r.
\]
Because every entry of $B$ was divisible by $d_1$, and elementary operations form $R$-linear combinations of entries, every resulting diagonal entry is still divisible by $d_1$. Hence
\[
d_1\mid d_2.
\]
This proves existence of Smith normal form.

<1>3. Uniqueness.
For $k\ge1$, let $\Delta_k(A)$ be the ideal generated by the $k\times k$ minors of $A$, with $\Delta_0(A)=R$. Multiplication on the left or right by an invertible matrix does not change these determinantal ideals.

For a Smith form,
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r,0,\ldots),
\qquad d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_r,
\]
one has
\[
\Delta_k(D)=(d_1d_2\cdots d_k)
\qquad(1\le k\le r).
\]
Therefore the ideals $(d_1\cdots d_k)$ are determined by $A$. Successively taking quotients of principal ideals determines each $(d_k)$, hence each $d_k$ up to multiplication by a unit.
:::
