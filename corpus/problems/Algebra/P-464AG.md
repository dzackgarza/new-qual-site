::: problem
Talk about the representation theory of compact Lie groups.
How do you know you have a finite-dimensional representation?
:::


::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be a compact Lie group. We work with continuous complex representations.

<1>1. Every finite-dimensional representation of $G$ is equivalent to a unitary representation.
::: {.proof}
Let $\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)$ be finite-dimensional and choose any positive-definite Hermitian form $\langle-,-\rangle_0$ on $V$. Normalize Haar measure $dg$ on $G$ so that $\int_G dg=1$, and define
\[
\langle v,w\rangle_G
=\int_G \langle\rho(g)v,\rho(g)w\rangle_0\,dg.
\]
This is again positive definite. By left-invariance of Haar measure, for $h\in G$,
\[
\langle\rho(h)v,\rho(h)w\rangle_G
=\int_G\langle\rho(gh)v,\rho(gh)w\rangle_0\,dg
=\langle v,w\rangle_G.
\]
Thus $\rho$ is unitary for this averaged inner product.
:::

<1>2. Every finite-dimensional representation of $G$ is completely reducible.
::: {.proof}
After <1>1, suppose $W\subseteq V$ is $G$-invariant. Its orthogonal complement $W^\perp$ is also invariant: if $v\in W^\perp$, $w\in W$, and $g\in G$, then
\[
\langle\rho(g)v,w\rangle_G
=\langle v,\rho(g^{-1})w\rangle_G=0
\]
because $W$ is invariant. Hence
\[
V=W\oplus W^\perp.
\]
Induction on $\dim V$ decomposes $V$ as a direct sum of irreducibles.
:::

<1>3. Every irreducible continuous unitary representation of a compact group is finite-dimensional.
::: {.proof}
This is a fundamental consequence of the Peter--Weyl theorem. Peter--Weyl decomposes the regular representation on $L^2(G)$ into finite-dimensional irreducible unitary representations and, equivalently, implies that every irreducible continuous unitary representation of a compact group is finite-dimensional.
:::

<1>4. Thus the irreducible unitary dual of a compact Lie group consists of finite-dimensional representations, and arbitrary unitary representations decompose into Hilbert direct sums of these irreducibles.
::: {.proof}
Finite-dimensionality is <1>3. The unitary decomposition theorem for compact groups, again part of Peter--Weyl theory, expresses a continuous unitary representation as an orthogonal direct sum of isotypic pieces built from finite-dimensional irreducibles.
:::

<1>5. For connected compact Lie groups, the finite-dimensional irreducibles are classified by highest weights.
::: {.proof}
Choose a maximal torus $T\subseteq G$ and a positive system of roots. The highest-weight theorem identifies isomorphism classes of irreducible finite-dimensional complex representations with the dominant integral weights compatible with $G$. This is the compact form of the highest-weight classification.
:::

<1>6. The answer to ``how do you know you have a finite-dimensional representation?'' depends on the setting.
::: {.proof}
If one starts with a representation $G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)$ where $V$ is explicitly finite-dimensional, finite-dimensionality is part of the definition. If one starts instead with an irreducible continuous unitary representation on an arbitrary Hilbert space, compactness plus Peter--Weyl implies finite-dimensionality by <1>3. General reducible unitary representations of compact groups may themselves be infinite-dimensional; they are built as Hilbert direct sums of finite-dimensional irreducibles.
:::
