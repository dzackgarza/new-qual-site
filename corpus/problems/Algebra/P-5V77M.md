::: {.problem}
Give an example of a ring where unique factorization fails, and prove the failure.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $R=\ZZ[\sqrt{-5}]$ with the multiplicative norm $N(a+b\sqrt{-5})=a^2+5b^2$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #units}
The units of $R$ are $\pm1$, and associates have equal norm.

::: pf-proof
A unit $u$ satisfies $N(u)N(u^{-1})=1$, so $N(u)=1$, which forces $b=0$ and $a=\pm1$. Associates differ by a unit factor of norm $1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #no-norm-2-3}
No element of $R$ has norm $2$ or $3$.

::: pf-proof
If $b\ne0$, then $a^2+5b^2\ge5$. If $b=0$, the norm is a square.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #irreducibles}
$2$, $3$, $1+\sqrt{-5}$ and $1-\sqrt{-5}$ are irreducible.

::: pf-proof
Their norms are $4$, $9$, $6$ and $6$. A factorization into two nonunits would split the norm into two factors greater than $1$, and each of $4$, $9$, $6$ has such a splitting only with a factor $2$ or $3$, which step [](#no-norm-2-3){.pf-ref} excludes.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\boxed{\ZZ[\sqrt{-5}]}$ is not a UFD: $6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})$ are two inequivalent factorizations into irreducibles.

::: pf-proof
Both products equal $6$, and their factors are irreducible by step [](#irreducibles){.pf-ref}. The factor $2$ has norm $4$, while $1\pm\sqrt{-5}$ have norm $6$, so by step [](#units){.pf-ref} the factor $2$ is associate to neither factor of the second factorization.
:::
