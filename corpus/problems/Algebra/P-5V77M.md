::: {.problem}
Give an example of a ring where unique factorization fails, and prove the failure.
:::

::: {.solution}
Take
\[
R=\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}].
\]
Its norm
\[
N(a+b\sqrt{-5})=a^2+5b^2
\]
is multiplicative. The only units are $\pm1$, since $N(u)=1$ for a unit $u$.

In $R$,
\[
6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5}).
\]
There is no element of norm $2$ or $3$: if $b\ne0$, then $a^2+5b^2\ge5$, while for $b=0$ the norm is a square. Hence:

- $2$ is irreducible, because a nontrivial factorization would force norms $2$ and $2$;
- $3$ is irreducible, because a nontrivial factorization would force a factor of norm $3$;
- $1\pm\sqrt{-5}$ are irreducible, because a nontrivial factorization of an element of norm $6$ would require a factor of norm $2$ or $3$.

The two factorizations are not associate: the factors $2,3$ have norms $4,9$, whereas $1\pm\sqrt{-5}$ have norm $6$, and associates have equal norm. Thus $6$ has two inequivalent factorizations into irreducibles, so $R$ is not a UFD.
:::
