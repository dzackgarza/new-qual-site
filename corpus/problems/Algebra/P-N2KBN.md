::: {.problem}
I give you two matrices over a field.
How would you tell if they are conjugate or not?
What theorem are you using?
State it.
How does it apply to this situation?
Why is $k[x]$ a PID? If two matrices are conjugate over the algebraic closure of a field, does that mean that they are conjugate over the base field too?
:::

::: {.solution}
For $A\in M_n(k)$, write $V_A$ for $k^n$ made into a $k[x]$-module by $x\cdot v=Av$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #pid}
$k[x]$ is a PID.

::: pf-proof
The degree is a Euclidean function on $k[x]$, and a Euclidean domain is a PID: a nonzero ideal is generated by any element of least degree in it.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #test}
$A,B\in M_n(k)$ are conjugate over $k$ if and only if they have the same invariant factors, equivalently the same rational canonical form.

::: pf-proof
A matrix $S\in\GL_n(k)$ satisfies $SAS^{-1}=B$ exactly when $S$ is a $k[x]$-module isomorphism $V_A\to V_B$.
By step [](#pid){.pf-ref}, the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID applies: $V_A\cong\bigoplus_i k[x]/(d_i)$ for unique monic invariant factors $d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots$, and two such modules are isomorphic exactly when their invariant factors agree.
The rational canonical form is the block-diagonal matrix of companion matrices of the $d_i$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
If $A,B\in M_n(k)$ are conjugate over $\bar k$, or over any extension field $K$ of $k$, then they are conjugate over $k$.

::: pf-proof
For every irreducible $f\in k[x]$ and every $r\ge1$, conjugacy over $K$ gives $\operatorname{rank}_K f(A)^r=\operatorname{rank}_K f(B)^r$.
The rank of a matrix with entries in $k$ does not change under extension of scalars, so $\operatorname{rank}_k f(A)^r=\operatorname{rank}_k f(B)^r$.
These ranks determine, for each $f$ and $m$, the number of elementary divisors $f^m$ of $V_A$ and of $V_B$, so $A$ and $B$ have the same elementary divisors over $k$, hence the same invariant factors, and step [](#test){.pf-ref} applies.
:::
