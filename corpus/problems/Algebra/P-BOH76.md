::: {.problem}
(a) What are the groups of order $p^2$ for $p$ prime?

(b) What are the groups of order $pq$ for distinct primes $p < q$?

(c) What if $q \equiv 1 \pmod p$?
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

(a) A group of order $p^2$ is isomorphic to $\boxed{C_{p^2}}$ or $\boxed{C_p\times C_p}$.

::: pf-proof

The center of the $p$-group $G$ is nontrivial. If $\abs{Z(G)}=p$, then $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, which forces $G$ to be abelian, so $Z(G)=G$, a contradiction. Hence $G$ is abelian, and the structure theorem for finite abelian groups gives the two groups listed.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #semidirect}

If $\abs{G}=pq$ with primes $p<q$, then $G\cong C_q\rtimes_\theta C_p$ for some homomorphism $\theta:C_p\to\Aut(C_q)\cong C_{q-1}$.

::: pf-proof

By the Sylow theorems, $n_q\equiv1\pmod q$ and $n_q\mid p$. Since $p<q$, $n_q=1$, so the Sylow $q$-subgroup $Q\cong C_q$ is normal. A Sylow $p$-subgroup $P\cong C_p$ meets $Q$ trivially, and $\abs{QP}=pq$, so $G=Q\rtimes P$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(b) If $p\nmid q-1$, the only group of order $pq$ is $\boxed{C_{pq}}$.

::: pf-proof

A homomorphism $C_p\to C_{q-1}$ has image of order dividing $\gcd(p,q-1)=1$, so $\theta$ in step [](#semidirect){.pf-ref} is trivial and $G\cong C_q\times C_p\cong C_{pq}$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

(c) If $q\equiv1\pmod p$, there are exactly two groups of order $pq$: $C_{pq}$ and the nonabelian group $\boxed{\langle x,y\mid x^q=y^p=1,\ yxy^{-1}=x^r\rangle}$, where $r\in(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$ has order $p$.

::: pf-proof

The trivial $\theta$ gives $C_{pq}$. A nontrivial $\theta$ is injective, and its image is the unique subgroup of order $p$ of the cyclic group $C_{q-1}$, generated by $x\mapsto x^r$. Two injective homomorphisms with the same image differ by an automorphism of $C_p$, that is, by a change of generator $y\mapsto y^j$, so they give isomorphic semidirect products. This group is nonabelian because $\theta$ is nontrivial, so it is not isomorphic to $C_{pq}$.

:::
