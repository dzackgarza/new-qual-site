::: {.exercise}
Show that if $E/k, F/k$ are Galois with $E\intersect F = k$, then $EF/k$ is Galois and $G(EF/k) \cong G(E/k)\times G(F/k)$.
:::


::: {.solution}
Regard $E$ and $F$ as subfields of a common algebraic closure of $k$.

<1>1. The extensions $E/k$ and $F/k$ are linearly disjoint over $k$.
::: {.proof}
Let $E_0\subseteq E$ and $F_0\subseteq F$ be finite Galois subextensions of $k$. Then
\[
E_0\cap F_0\subseteq E\cap F=k,
\]
so $E_0\cap F_0=k$. For finite Galois extensions, the standard compositum degree formula gives
\[
[E_0F_0:k]=[E_0:k][F_0:k].
\]
Equivalently, the multiplication map
\[
E_0\otimes_k F_0\longrightarrow E_0F_0
\]
is injective. Every finite subset of $E$ and of $F$ is contained in such finite Galois subextensions. Hence, passing to the filtered union, the multiplication map
\[
E\otimes_k F\longrightarrow EF
\]
is injective. Thus $E$ and $F$ are linearly disjoint over $k$.
:::

<1>2. The compositum $EF/k$ is Galois.
::: {.proof}
Both $E/k$ and $F/k$ are algebraic and separable, so every element of the field generated by $E\cup F$ is separable over $k$; hence $EF/k$ is separable. If $\iota:EF\hookrightarrow\overline{k}$ is a $k$-embedding, normality of $E/k$ and $F/k$ gives
\[
\iota(E)=E,
\qquad
\iota(F)=F.
\]
Therefore
\[
\iota(EF)=\iota(E)\iota(F)=EF,
\]
so $EF/k$ is normal. Hence it is Galois.
:::

<1>3. Restriction defines an injective homomorphism
\[
\rho:\Gal(EF/k)\longrightarrow\Gal(E/k)\times\Gal(F/k),
\qquad
\rho(\gamma)=(\gamma|_E,\gamma|_F).
\]
::: {.proof}
Normality of $E/k$ and $F/k$ makes both restrictions well-defined. If $\rho(\gamma)$ is trivial, then $\gamma$ fixes both $E$ and $F$, hence fixes the field they generate, namely $EF$. Thus $\gamma=1$, so $\rho$ is injective.
:::

<1>4. The restriction map $\rho$ is surjective.
::: {.proof}
Let
\[
(\sigma,\tau)\in\Gal(E/k)\times\Gal(F/k).
\]
The map
\[
\sigma\otimes\tau:E\otimes_kF\longrightarrow E\otimes_kF,
\qquad
x\otimes y\longmapsto\sigma(x)\otimes\tau(y),
\]
is a $k$-algebra automorphism. By <1>1, $E\otimes_kF$ embeds in $EF$ by multiplication. Its image is a domain whose fraction field is $EF$, because it contains the images of both $E$ and $F$. Therefore $\sigma\otimes\tau$ extends uniquely to a $k$-automorphism $\gamma$ of $EF$. By construction,
\[
\gamma|_E=\sigma,
\qquad
\gamma|_F=\tau.
\]
Thus $\rho(\gamma)=(\sigma,\tau)$.
:::

<1>5. Therefore
\[
\Gal(EF/k)\cong\Gal(E/k)\times\Gal(F/k).
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>3 and <1>4, the restriction homomorphism $\rho$ is bijective.
:::
