::: {.problem}
Show that a matrix $A \in M_n(K)$ is similar to a diagonal matrix over $K$ if and only if all the elementary divisors of $A$ are linear (degree 1 polynomials over $K$).
:::

::: {.solution}
View $V=K^n$ as a $K[x]$-module by $x\cdot v=Av$. Its elementary-divisor decomposition is
$$
V\cong\bigoplus_j K[x]/(p_j^{e_j}),
$$
where each $p_j$ is monic irreducible and $e_j\ge 1$; the elementary divisors $p_j^{e_j}$ are determined by $A$.

::: pf

::: pf-step

If $A$ is diagonalizable over $K$, then every elementary divisor of $A$ has degree $1$.

::: pf-proof

Choose an eigenbasis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$. Each line $Kv_i$ is a submodule isomorphic to $K[x]/(x-\lambda_i)$, so
$$
V\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^n K[x]/(x-\lambda_i).
$$
By uniqueness of the elementary divisors, they are the polynomials $x-\lambda_i$, each of degree $1$.

:::

:::

::: pf-step

If every elementary divisor of $A$ has degree $1$, then $A$ is diagonalizable over $K$.

::: pf-proof

A monic polynomial $p_j^{e_j}$ of degree $1$ has $e_j=1$ and $p_j=x-\lambda_j$ for some $\lambda_j\in K$. Hence
$$
V\cong\bigoplus_j K[x]/(x-\lambda_j),
$$
a direct sum of one-dimensional submodules on each of which $x$ acts as $\lambda_j$. A generator of each summand is an eigenvector, and together these generators form a basis of $V$, so $A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix.

:::
