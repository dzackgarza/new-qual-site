::: {.problem}
Show that a matrix $A \in M_n(K)$ is similar to a diagonal matrix over $K$ if and only if all the elementary divisors of $A$ are linear (degree 1 polynomials over $K$).
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that $A \in M_n(K)$ is diagonalizable over $K$ if and only if its elementary divisors in $K[x]$ are all of degree 1 (i.e. of the form $x - \lambda$ for $\lambda \in K$).

<1>1. Algebraic Framework via $K[x]$-Modules:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $V = K^n$ be equipped with the $K[x]$-module structure where $x \cdot v = A v$ for $v \in V$.
    <2>2. Since $K[x]$ is a Principal Ideal Domain and $V$ is a finitely generated torsion $K[x]$-module, the Primary Decomposition Theorem yields an isomorphism of $K[x]$-modules:
        $$V \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^m K[x] / (p_i(x)^{e_i})$$
        where each $p_i(x) \in K[x]$ is a monic irreducible polynomial, $e_i \ge 1$, and the prime powers $p_i(x)^{e_i}$ are precisely the **elementary divisors** of $A$.

<1>2. Direction $(\implies)$: $A$ is diagonalizable $\implies$ elementary divisors are linear:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose $A$ is diagonalizable over $K$.
    <2>2. Then there exists a basis $\{v_1, \dots, v_n\}$ of $V$ consisting of eigenvectors of $A$, with corresponding eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n \in K$.
    <2>3. For each $j \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, $(x - \lambda_j) v_j = A v_j - \lambda_j v_j = 0$, so the 1-dimensional subspace $K v_j$ is a cyclic $K[x]$-submodule isomorphic to $K[x] / (x - \lambda_j)$.
    <2>4. Therefore:
        $$V = K v_1 \oplus K v_2 \oplus \cdots \oplus K v_n \cong \bigoplus_{j=1}^n K[x] / (x - \lambda_j).$$
    <2>5. By uniqueness of the primary decomposition (elementary divisors), the elementary divisors of $A$ are precisely the linear polynomials $x - \lambda_1, x - \lambda_2, \dots, x - \lambda_n$.
    <2>6. All elementary divisors are linear (degree 1 with roots in $K$).

<1>3. Direction $(\impliedby)$: All elementary divisors are linear $\implies A$ is diagonalizable:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose all elementary divisors of $A$ are linear polynomials over $K$, say $x - \lambda_1, \dots, x - \lambda_m$ with $\lambda_j \in K$.
    <2>2. The sum of the degrees of the elementary divisors is $\sum \deg(x - \lambda_j) = m = \dim_K(V) = n$.
    <2>3. The primary decomposition gives:
        $$V \cong \bigoplus_{j=1}^n K[x] / (x - \lambda_j).$$
    <2>4. Each summand $W_j \cong K[x] / (x - \lambda_j)$ is 1-dimensional over $K$.
    <2>5. Choose a generator $w_j \in W_j$ (the image of $1$). Since $(x - \lambda_j) \cdot w_j = 0$, we have $A w_j = \lambda_j w_j$.
    <2>6. Thus $\{w_1, \dots, w_n\}$ is a basis of $V$ consisting of eigenvectors of $A$.
    <2>7. Relative to this basis, the matrix of the transformation is $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$, so $A$ is diagonalizable.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    $A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix over $K$ if and only if all its elementary divisors are of the form $x - \lambda$ with $\lambda \in K$. Q.E.D.
:::
