::: {.problem}
Let $K$ be a field, $x$ an indeterminate, and $\alpha$ an element of a field extension of $K$.
What are the following objects?

- $K(x)$

- $K[x]$

- $K(\alpha)$

- $K[\alpha]$
:::

::: {.solution}

No retained solution set covers this question, which asks for definitions and their relation: each object is characterised, and the two cases (transcendental, algebraic) are separated with the reason the two rings agree in the second.

Let $K$ be a field, $x$ an indeterminate over $K$, $K\subseteq F$ a field extension and $\alpha\in F$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #polynomials}
$K[x]$ is the polynomial ring in $x$ over $K$, and $K(x)=\operatorname{Frac}(K[x])$ is the field of rational functions $p/q$ with $p,q\in K[x]$, $q\ne0$.

::: pf-proof
$K[x]$ is by definition the $K$-algebra of finite formal sums $\sum a_{i}x^{i}$ with $a_{i}\in K$, a domain; $K(x)$ is by definition its fraction field, i.e. the set of quotients of pairs $(p,q)$ with $q\ne0$ modulo the usual equivalence, with the field operations of fractional arithmetic.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #generated}
$K[\alpha]$ is the smallest subring of $F$ containing $K$ and $\alpha$, equal to the image of the evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}_{\alpha}:K[x]\to F$, $p\mapsto p(\alpha)$; $K(\alpha)$ is the smallest subfield of $F$ containing $K$ and $\alpha$, equal to $\operatorname{Frac}(K[\alpha])$.

::: pf-proof
A subring of $F$ containing $K$ and $\alpha$ contains every $p(\alpha)$, so it contains $\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{ev}_{\alpha})$, which is itself a subring containing $K$ and $\alpha$; hence $K[\alpha]=\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{ev}_{\alpha})$, and $\mathrm{ev}_{\alpha}$ is the unique $K$-algebra map sending $x$ to $\alpha$ (a $K$-algebra map is determined by the image of $x$). The smallest subfield is the fraction field of the smallest subring, since $K[\alpha]$ is a domain and $\operatorname{Frac}(K[\alpha])\subseteq F$ is a subfield.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #transcendental}
If $\alpha$ is transcendental over $K$, then $\mathrm{ev}_{\alpha}$ is injective, $K[\alpha]\cong K[x]$ and $K(\alpha)\cong K(x)$.

::: pf-proof
Transcendence means no nonzero polynomial over $K$ vanishes at $\alpha$, that is $\ker\mathrm{ev}_{\alpha}=0$; an injective map of $K$-algebras with image $K[\alpha]$ is an isomorphism $K[x]\to K[\alpha]$, and taking fraction fields gives $K(x)\cong K(\alpha)$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #algebraic}
If $\alpha$ is algebraic over $K$ with minimal polynomial $m$, then $\ker\mathrm{ev}_{\alpha}=(m)$, $K[\alpha]\cong K[x]/(m)$, and $K[\alpha]=K(\alpha)$.

::: pf-proof
$\ker\mathrm{ev}_{\alpha}$ is the set of polynomials vanishing at $\alpha$, which is the principal ideal generated by the monic polynomial of least degree with $\alpha$ as a root, namely $m$. The minimal polynomial is irreducible (a factorisation would give a lower-degree polynomial vanishing at $\alpha$), so $(m)$ is a maximal ideal of $K[x]$ and $K[x]/(m)$ is a field; hence $K[\alpha]$, its isomorphic image, is a field, and the smallest subfield containing $K$ and $\alpha$ is therefore $K[\alpha]$ itself.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-qed}
$K(x)$ and $K[x]$ are the rational functions and the polynomials in the indeterminate $x$ (steps [](#polynomials){.pf-ref}); $K[\alpha]$ and $K(\alpha)$ are the ring and the field generated by $\alpha$ over $K$ (step [](#generated){.pf-ref}), agreeing with the polynomial and rational-function descriptions exactly when $\alpha$ is transcendental (step [](#transcendental){.pf-ref}); when $\alpha$ is algebraic the ring is already a field, $K[\alpha]=K(\alpha)\cong K[x]/(m)$ (step [](#algebraic){.pf-ref}).
:::
