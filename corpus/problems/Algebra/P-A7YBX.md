::: problem
We want to show that every simple $R\dash$module $M$ is cyclic, i.e. if the only ideals of $M$ are $(0)$ and $M$ itself, that $M = \generators{m}$ for some element $m\in M$.

Towards a contradiction, let $M$ be a simple $R\dash$module and suppose $M$ is not cyclic, so $M\neq \generators{m}$ for any $m\in M$.
But then let $a\in M$ be an arbitrary nontrivial element; then $(a)$ is a non-empty ideal (since it contains $a$), so $(a) \neq 0$.
Since $M$ is simple, we must have $(a) = M$, a contradiction.
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Definition of simple $R$-module:
<2>1. An $R$-module $M$ is **simple** (or irreducible) if $M \neq \{0\}$ and the only $R$-submodules of $M$ are $\{0\}$ and $M$.

<1>2. Proof that every simple module is generated by any non-zero element:
<2>1. Since $M \neq \{0\}$, there exists a non-zero element $m \in M \setminus \{0\}$.
<2>2. Consider the cyclic submodule generated by $m$:
\[
R m = \{ r m \mid r \in R \}.
\]
Because $R$ has identity $1$, $m = 1 \cdot m \in R m$, so $R m \neq \{0\}$.
<2>3. Because $R m$ is an $R$-submodule of $M$ and $M$ is simple, the only submodules of $M$ are $\{0\}$ and $M$.
Since $R m \neq \{0\}$, we must have:
\[
R m = M.
\]
Thus $M$ is a cyclic $R$-module, generated by $m$.

<1>3. Characterization as a quotient $R/\mathfrak{m}$:
<2>1. The evaluation map $\varphi: R \to M$ given by $\varphi(r) = r m$ is a surjective $R$-module homomorphism with kernel $\operatorname{ann}_R(m) = \{ r \in R \mid r m = 0 \}$.
By the First Isomorphism Theorem for modules:
\[
M \cong R / \operatorname{ann}_R(m).
\]
<2>2. By the Correspondence Theorem for modules, submodules of $R/\operatorname{ann}_R(m)$ correspond bijectively to left ideals of $R$ containing $\operatorname{ann}_R(m)$.
Since $M$ is simple, $\operatorname{ann}_R(m)$ is a maximal left ideal of $R$.

<1>4. Conclusion:
Every simple $R$-module is cyclic, generated by any of its non-zero elements. Q.E.D.
:::
