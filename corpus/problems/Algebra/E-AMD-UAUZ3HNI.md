::: {.exercise}
Classify the five groups of order 12.
:::


::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be a group of order $12=2^2\cdot3$.

<1>1. At least one Sylow subgroup of $G$ is normal.
::: {.proof}
Sylow's theorems give
\[
n_3\in\{1,4\},\qquad n_2\in\{1,3\}.
\]
If $n_3=4$, the four Sylow $3$-subgroups contribute $4(3-1)=8$ nonidentity elements. The remaining four elements contain every Sylow $2$-subgroup, so there is only one Sylow $2$-subgroup and $n_2=1$. Thus either $n_3=1$ or $n_2=1$.
:::

<1>2. Hence $G$ is a semidirect product of a group of order $3$ and a group of order $4$.
::: {.proof}
Let $P_3\in\operatorname{Syl}_3(G)$ and $P_2\in\operatorname{Syl}_2(G)$. Their intersection is trivial and
\[
|P_3P_2|=\frac{|P_3||P_2|}{|P_3\cap P_2|}=12,
\]
so $G=P_3P_2$. By <1>1 one of the two factors is normal. Also $P_3\cong C_3$, while $P_2\cong C_4$ or $V_4$.
:::

<1>3. If the Sylow $2$-subgroup is normal, the possibilities are $C_{12}$, $C_3\times V_4$, and $A_4$.
::: {.proof}
If $P_2\cong C_4$, an action $C_3\to\operatorname{Aut}(C_4)\cong C_2$ must be trivial, so
\[
G\cong C_4\times C_3\cong C_{12}.
\]
If $P_2\cong V_4$, then $\operatorname{Aut}(V_4)\cong S_3$. The action of $C_3$ is either trivial, giving $C_3\times V_4$, or injective onto a subgroup generated by a $3$-cycle on the three nonidentity elements of $V_4$. All such subgroups are conjugate in $S_3$, and the resulting semidirect product is $A_4$.
:::

<1>4. If the Sylow $3$-subgroup is normal, the new possibilities are $C_3\rtimes C_4$ with inversion action and $C_2\times S_3$.
::: {.proof}
Now the action is a homomorphism
\[
P_2\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(C_3)\cong C_2.
\]
For $P_2\cong C_4$, the trivial action again gives $C_{12}$, while the unique nontrivial action sends a generator of $C_4$ to inversion on $C_3$. This gives the nontrivial semidirect product
\[
C_3\rtimes C_4,
\]
the dicyclic group of order $12$ (often denoted $\operatorname{Dic}_3$ or $Q_{12}$).

For $P_2\cong V_4$, the trivial action again gives $C_3\times V_4$. Every nontrivial homomorphism $V_4\to C_2$ has kernel of order $2$, and the three such kernels are carried to one another by automorphisms of $V_4$. Thus there is one nontrivial semidirect-product type. Writing $V_4=\langle a\rangle\times\langle b\rangle$ with $a$ acting by inversion and $b$ acting trivially gives
\[
G\cong (C_3\rtimes\langle a\rangle)\times\langle b\rangle
\cong S_3\times C_2,
\]
which is the dihedral group $D_6$ of order $12$.
:::

<1>5. These five groups are pairwise nonisomorphic.
::: {.proof}
The two groups $C_{12}$ and $C_3\times V_4\cong C_6\times C_2$ are abelian and are distinguished by the existence of an element of order $12$. The other three are nonabelian. Among them, $A_4$ has no element of order $6$, $D_6$ has an element of order $6$ but none of order $4$, and $\operatorname{Dic}_3$ has an element of order $4$. Hence they are pairwise nonisomorphic.
:::

<1>6. Therefore the groups of order $12$, up to isomorphism, are
\[
C_{12},\qquad C_6\times C_2,\qquad A_4,\qquad D_6,\qquad \operatorname{Dic}_3.
\]
::: {.proof}
By <1>2--<1>4 every group of order $12$ is one of these, and by <1>5 no two on the list are isomorphic.
:::
