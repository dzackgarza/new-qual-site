::: {.exercise}
Let $p$ be a prime. Show that every group of order $p$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $G$ be a finite group with $\abs{G}=p$ and identity $e$, and write $\operatorname{ord}(g)$ for the order of an element $g\in G$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #nontrivial}
There is $g\in G$ with $g\neq e$.

::: pf-proof
$p$ is a prime, so $p\geq2$ and $\abs{G}\geq2$; a group with at least two elements has an element other than its identity.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #divides}
$\operatorname{ord}(g)\mid p$.

::: pf-proof
By Lagrange's theorem the order of the subgroup $\generators{g}$ divides $\abs{G}=p$, and $\operatorname{ord}(g)=\abs{\generators{g}}$ by the definition of the order of an element.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #not-one}
$\operatorname{ord}(g)\neq1$.

::: pf-proof
If $\operatorname{ord}(g)=1$ then $\generators{g}=\{e\}$, so $g=e$, contrary to step [](#nontrivial){.pf-ref}.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #order-p}
$\operatorname{ord}(g)=p$.

::: pf-proof
Step [](#divides){.pf-ref} makes $\operatorname{ord}(g)$ a positive divisor of the prime $p$, so it is $1$ or $p$, and step [](#not-one){.pf-ref} excludes $1$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step}
$\generators{g}=G$ and $G$ is cyclic; in particular $G\cong\ZZ/p\ZZ$.

::: pf-proof
The subgroup $\generators{g}$ has $\operatorname{ord}(g)=p$ elements by step [](#order-p){.pf-ref}, and it is contained in $G$, which has $p$ elements; a subset with as many elements as the finite set containing it is the whole set, so $\generators{g}=G$ and $G$ is generated by one element. A cyclic group of order $p$ is isomorphic to the additive group of integers modulo its order, $\ZZ/p\ZZ$.
:::
