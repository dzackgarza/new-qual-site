::: problem
- Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring and $A,B$ algebras over $R$.
  Suppose $A$ is finite type over $R$ and finite over B. Then $B$ is finite type over $R$.
:::


::: {.solution}
Assume $B\subseteq A$ as $R$-subalgebras. Choose elements
\[
x_1,\dots,x_n\in A
\]
generating $A$ as an $R$-algebra, and elements
\[
y_1,\dots,y_m\in A
\]
generating $A$ as a $B$-module. Enlarge the module generating set if necessary so that $1=y_1$.

<1>1. There are coefficients $a_{ij},b_{ijk}\in B$ such that
\[
x_i=\sum_j a_{ij}y_j,
\qquad
y_i y_j=\sum_k b_{ijk}y_k.
\]
::: {.proof}
Both assertions follow because the $y_j$ generate $A$ as a $B$-module.
:::

<1>2. Let $B_0\subseteq B$ be the $R$-subalgebra generated by all $a_{ij}$ and $b_{ijk}$. Then $B_0$ is Noetherian.
::: {.proof}
It is finitely generated as an $R$-algebra, and $R$ is Noetherian. Hilbert's basis theorem implies that $B_0$ is Noetherian.
:::

<1>3. The $B_0$-submodule
\[
M=B_0y_1+\cdots+B_0y_m
\]
is a subring of $A$ containing $R$ and all $x_i$.
::: {.proof}
It contains $1=y_1$. The multiplication formulas in <1>1 show that products of the generators $y_i$ remain in $M$, hence $M$ is closed under multiplication. The first formulas in <1>1 show that every $x_i\in M$.
:::

<1>4. Therefore $A=M$, so $A$ is finite as a $B_0$-module.
::: {.proof}
Since $A=R[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ and $M$ is a subring containing $R$ and every $x_i$, one has $A\subseteq M$. The reverse inclusion is tautological.
:::

<1>5. The subalgebra $B$ is finite as a $B_0$-module.
::: {.proof}
Since $B_0\subseteq B\subseteq A$, the ring $B$ is a $B_0$-submodule of the finite $B_0$-module $A$. By <1>2, $B_0$ is Noetherian, so every submodule of a finite module is finite.
:::

<1>6. Hence $B$ is of finite type over $R$.
::: {.proof}
The algebra $B_0$ is finite type over $R$, and $B$ is finite as a $B_0$-module by <1>5. A finite module algebra over a finite-type algebra is finite type over the base.
:::
:::
