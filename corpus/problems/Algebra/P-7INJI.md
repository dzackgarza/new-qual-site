::: problem
Discuss representations of $\ZZ$, the infinite cyclic group.
What is the group algebra of $\ZZ$?
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. Fix a field $k$. A $k$-linear representation
\[
\rho:\mathbb Z\longrightarrow \operatorname{GL}(V)
\]
is completely determined by the single invertible operator
\[
T=\rho(1)\in\operatorname{GL}(V).
\]
::: {.proof}
Since $\mathbb Z$ is generated by $1$, every $n\in\mathbb Z$ satisfies
\[
\rho(n)=\rho(1)^n=T^n.
\]
Conversely, any invertible linear map $T:V\to V$ defines a representation by $\rho(n)=T^n$.
:::

<1>2. Thus the category of $k$-linear representations of $\mathbb Z$ is the category of $k$-vector spaces equipped with an invertible linear operator.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, objects are precisely pairs $(V,T)$ with $T\in\operatorname{GL}(V)$.
A linear map $f:V\to W$ intertwines the corresponding representations exactly when
\[
fT=Uf,
\]
where $U$ is the operator defining the representation on $W$.
:::

<1>3. The group algebra of $\mathbb Z$ over $k$ is the Laurent polynomial ring
\[
k[\mathbb Z]\cong k[t,t^{-1}].
\]
::: {.proof}
Let $e_n$ denote the basis element of the group algebra corresponding to $n\in\mathbb Z$.
Multiplication is
\[
e_m e_n=e_{m+n}.
\]
The assignment
\[
e_n\longmapsto t^n
\]
extends $k$-linearly to an algebra homomorphism
\[
k[\mathbb Z]\longrightarrow k[t,t^{-1}].
\]
Both sides have $k$-basis indexed by $\mathbb Z$, namely $\{e_n\}_{n\in\mathbb Z}$ and $\{t^n\}_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, so this homomorphism is an isomorphism.
:::

<1>4. Equivalently, representations of $\mathbb Z$ are exactly modules over $k[t,t^{-1}]$.
::: {.proof}
Given a representation determined by $T$, define
\[
t\cdot v=T(v),
\qquad
t^{-1}\cdot v=T^{-1}(v).
\]
This extends uniquely to a $k[t,t^{-1}]$-module structure on $V$.
Conversely, on any $k[t,t^{-1}]$-module, multiplication by $t$ is invertible with inverse multiplication by $t^{-1}$, and therefore defines a representation of $\mathbb Z$.
These constructions are inverse to each other.
:::

<1>5. If $V$ is finite-dimensional and $k$ is algebraically closed, the finite-dimensional representations are classified up to isomorphism by Jordan normal forms with nonzero eigenvalues.
::: {.proof}
By <1>1, isomorphism classes are similarity classes of invertible matrices.
Over an algebraically closed field every matrix has Jordan normal form, and invertibility is equivalent to the absence of the eigenvalue $0$.
:::
:::
