::: {.problem}
Consider a commutative ring, $R$, and a maximal ideal $I$, what can you say about the structure of $R/I$?
What if $I$ were prime?
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $R$ be commutative with $1$.

If $I$ is maximal, then the ideals of $R/I$ correspond to ideals of $R$ containing $I$. Maximality leaves only
\[
(0)\quad\text{and}\quad R/I.
\]
Thus every nonzero class in $R/I$ generates the whole ring, hence is invertible. Therefore $R/I$ is a field.

If $I$ is prime, then
\[
(a+I)(b+I)=0
\]
means $ab\in I$. Primality gives $a\in I$ or $b\in I$, so
\[
a+I=0\quad\text{or}\quad b+I=0.
\]
Hence $R/I$ has no zero divisors and is an integral domain.

Since every field is an integral domain, every maximal ideal is prime.
:::
