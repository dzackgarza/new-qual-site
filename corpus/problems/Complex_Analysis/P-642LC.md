::: problem
Find the Laurent series of $f(z)=\frac{1}{(z-1)(z-2)}$ valid in the annulus $1<|z|<2$.

::: solution
Partial fractions give
\[
\frac1{(z-1)(z-2)}
=-\frac1{z-1}+\frac1{z-2}.
\]
On the annulus $1<|z|<2$, expand the first term in powers of $z^{-1}$ and
the second in powers of $z$:
\[
-\frac1{z-1}
=-\frac1z\frac1{1-z^{-1}}
=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n},
\]
because $|z^{-1}|<1$, while
\[
\frac1{z-2}
=-\frac12\frac1{1-z/2}
=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+1}},
\]
because $|z/2|<1$. Hence the Laurent series valid precisely on
$1<|z|<2$ is
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{(z-1)(z-2)}
=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n}
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+1}}.}
\]
:::
