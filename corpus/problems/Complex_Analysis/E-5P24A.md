::: {.exercise}
Let $f$ be entire, and for every $a\in \CC$ write the Taylor expansion about $a$ as $f(z) = \sum_{k\geq 0} c_{k, a} (z-a)^k$.
Suppose that for every $a$, there exists some $k$ such that $c_{k, a} = 0$.
Show that $f$ is a polynomial.
:::

::: {.solution}
For $n\ge0$ let $Z_n \coloneqq \theset{a\in \CC \st c_{n, a} = 0}$. Since $c_{n,a}=f^{(n)}(a)/n!$, $f^{(n)}$ vanishes on $Z_n$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #uncountable}
Some $Z_n$ is uncountable.

::: pf-proof
By hypothesis $\bigcup_n Z_n = \CC$, which is uncountable, and a countable union of countable sets is countable.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #vanish}
$f^{(n)}\equiv0$ on $\CC$ for that $n$.

::: pf-proof
$Z_n=\bigcup_{m\ge1}(Z_n\intersect\overline{D_m(0)})$, so by step [](#uncountable){.pf-ref} some $Z_n\intersect\overline{D_m(0)}$ is infinite.
An infinite subset of the compact disk $\overline{D_m(0)}$ has a limit point in $\CC$, and $f^{(n)}$ vanishes on it, so the identity theorem gives $f^{(n)}\equiv0$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$f$ is a polynomial of degree less than $n$.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#vanish){.pf-ref}, the Taylor coefficients $f^{(k)}(0)/k!$ of $f$ at $0$ vanish for $k\ge n$, and the Taylor series of the entire function $f$ converges to $f$ on $\CC$.
:::
