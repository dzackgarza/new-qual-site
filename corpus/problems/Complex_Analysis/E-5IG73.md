::: {.problem}
Let $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n z^n$ be analytic and one-to-one in $\abs{z} < 1$.
For $0<r<1$, let $D_r$ be the disk $\abs{z}<r$.
Show that the area of $f(D_r)$ is finite and is given by
$$
S = \pi \sum_{n=1}^\infty n \abs{c_n}^2 r^{2n}.
$$

:::

::: {.solution}
Write $f=u+iv$ and use polar coordinates $z=\rho e^{it}$ on $D_r$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #jacobian}
The area of $f(D_r)$ is $\int_{D_r}\abs{f'(z)}^2\,dA$, and it is finite.

::: pf-proof
By the Cauchy--Riemann equations the Jacobian determinant of $f$ is $u_xv_y-u_yv_x=u_x^2+v_x^2=\abs{f'}^2$.
Since $f$ is injective on $D_r$, the change of variables formula gives the integral.
It is finite because $\abs{f'}^2$ is bounded on the compact set $\overline{D_r}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\int_{D_r}\abs{f'(z)}^2\,dA=\pi \sum_{n\geq 1} n \abs{c_n}^2 r^{2n}$.

::: pf-proof
The double series below converges uniformly on $\overline{D_r}$, which justifies integrating term by term, and only the terms $j=k$ survive the $t$-integration:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{D_r} \abs{f'(z)}^2\,dA
&= \int_0^r\int_0^{2\pi} \sum_{j, k\geq 1}
jk c_j \bar{c_k} \rho^{j+k-1} e^{i(j-k)t} \,dt\,d\rho \\
&= \int_0^r 2\pi\sum_{k\geq 1} k^2 \abs{c_k}^2 \rho^{2k-1} \,d\rho
= \pi \sum_{k\geq 1} k \abs{c_k}^2 r^{2k}.
\end{aligned}
$$
With step [](#jacobian){.pf-ref} this is the area.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.remark}
The area of $f(D_1)$ can be infinite: for the Koebe function $z/(1-z)^2=\sum_n nz^n$, the image of the unit disk is $\CC$ minus a ray.
:::
