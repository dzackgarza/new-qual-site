::: {.problem}
Find the Laurent series expansions centered at $z_0 = 0$ for the rational function:
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{1 - z^2} + \frac{1}{3 - z}.$$

(1) How many distinct Laurent series expansions centered at $0$ exist for $f(z)$?

(2) State the annular domain of convergence for each expansion and write the explicit series.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step
(1) There are exactly $\boxed{3}$ Laurent expansions of $f$ centred at $0$, on $\abs{z}<1$, $1<\abs{z}<3$ and $\abs{z}>3$.

::: pf-proof
The poles of $f$ are $\pm1$ and $3$, at distances $1$ and $3$ from $0$.
So the maximal annuli centred at $0$ on which $f$ is holomorphic are the three listed, and $f$ has one Laurent expansion on each.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
(2) On $\abs{z}<1$,
$$
\boxed{f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{2n} + \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^n}{3^{n+1}}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For $\abs{z}<1$, $\frac{1}{1-z^2}=\sum_{n\ge0} z^{2n}$, and $\frac{1}{3-z}=\frac13\cdot\frac{1}{1-z/3}=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{3^{n+1}}$ since $\abs{z/3}<1$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
On $1<\abs{z}<3$,
$$
\boxed{f(z)=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-2n} + \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^n}{3^{n+1}}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For $\abs{z}>1$, $\frac{1}{1-z^2}=-\frac{1}{z^2}\cdot\frac{1}{1-z^{-2}}=-\sum_{n\ge1} z^{-2n}$; the expansion of $\frac{1}{3-z}$ holds for $\abs{z}<3$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
On $\abs{z}>3$,
$$
\boxed{f(z)=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-2n} - \sum_{n=1}^\infty 3^{n-1}z^{-n}}.
$$

::: pf-proof
For $\abs{z}>3$, $\frac{1}{3-z}=-\frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1-3/z}=-\sum_{n\ge1}3^{n-1}z^{-n}$; the expansion of $\frac{1}{1-z^2}$ holds for $\abs{z}>1$.
:::
