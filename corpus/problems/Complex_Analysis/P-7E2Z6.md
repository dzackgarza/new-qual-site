::: problem
Find the Laurent series expansions centered at $z_0 = 0$ for the rational function:
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{1 - z^2} + \frac{1}{3 - z}.$$
(1) How many distinct Laurent series expansions centered at $0$ exist for $f(z)$?
(2) State the annular domain of convergence for each expansion and write the explicit series.
:::

::: solution
The poles are at $z=\pm1$ and $z=3$. Their distances from the center $0$ are $1$ and $3$, so there are three maximal annuli of holomorphy centered at $0$:
\[
|z|<1,
\qquad
1<|z|<3,
\qquad
|z|>3.
\]
Thus there are exactly three Laurent expansions centered at $0$.

<1>1. For $|z|<1$,
\[
\frac1{1-z^2}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{2n},
\qquad
\frac1{3-z}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^n}{3^{n+1}}.
\]
Hence
\[
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{2n}
+\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^n}{3^{n+1}}.
\]

<1>2. For $1<|z|<3$,
\[
\frac1{1-z^2}
=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-2n},
\qquad
\frac1{3-z}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^n}{3^{n+1}},
\]
so
\[
f(z)=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-2n}
+\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^n}{3^{n+1}}.
\]

<1>3. For $|z|>3$,
\[
\frac1{1-z^2}=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-2n},
\qquad
\frac1{3-z}=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty3^{n-1}z^{-n}.
\]
Therefore
\[
f(z)=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-2n}
-\sum_{n=1}^\infty3^{n-1}z^{-n}.
\]
:::
