::: problem
Find the Laurent series expansions centered at $z_0 = 0$ for the rational function:
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{1 - z^2} + \frac{1}{3 - z}.$$
(1) How many distinct Laurent series expansions centered at $0$ exist for $f(z)$?
(2) State the annular domain of convergence for each expansion and write the explicit series.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Determine all Laurent series expansions of $f(z)$ centered at $z = 0$ and their annular domains of convergence.

<1>1. Singularities and Annular Regions:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The singularities of $f(z) = \frac{1}{1 - z^2} + \frac{1}{3 - z}$ occur at $z = \pm 1$ and $z = 3$.
    <2>2. The distances from the center $z_0 = 0$ to the singularities are $|z| = 1$ and $|z| = 3$.
    <2>3. These divide the complex plane into **three concentric annular regions**:
        1. **Region I (Disk):** $D_1 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid |z| < 1\}$.
        2. **Region II (Annulus):** $A_{1, 3} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid 1 < |z| < 3\}$.
        3. **Region III (Exterior):** $E_3 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid |z| > 3\}$.
    <2>4. Thus, there are exactly **3 distinct Laurent series expansions** centered at $0$.

<1>2. Component Geometric Series:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For the term $\frac{1}{1 - z^2}$:
        - For $|z| < 1$: $\frac{1}{1 - z^2} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (z^2)^n = \sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{2n}$.
        - For $|z| > 1$: $\frac{1}{1 - z^2} = -\frac{1}{z^2} \frac{1}{1 - 1/z^2} = -\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{z^{2n+2}} = -\sum_{k=1}^\infty z^{-2k}$.
    <2>2. For the term $\frac{1}{3 - z} = \frac{1}{3} \frac{1}{1 - z/3}$:
        - For $|z| < 3$: $\frac{1}{3 - z} = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{n=0}^\infty \left(\frac{z}{3}\right)^n = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{3^{n+1}} z^n$.
        - For $|z| > 3$: $\frac{1}{3 - z} = -\frac{1}{z} \frac{1}{1 - 3/z} = -\frac{1}{z} \sum_{n=0}^\infty \left(\frac{3}{z}\right)^n = -\sum_{n=0}^\infty 3^n z^{-(n+1)} = -\sum_{k=1}^\infty 3^{k-1} z^{-k}$.

<1>3. Explicit Expansions in Each Region:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. **Region I ($|z| < 1$):**
        Both $|z| < 1$ and $|z| < 3$ hold, so both components expand as Taylor series:
        $$f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{2n} + \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{3^{n+1}} z^n = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n z^n$$
        where $a_{2k} = 1 + \frac{1}{3^{2k+1}}$ and $a_{2k+1} = \frac{1}{3^{2k+2}}$.
    <2>2. **Region II ($1 < |z| < 3$):**
        Here $|z| > 1$ and $|z| < 3$:
        $$f(z) = -\sum_{k=1}^\infty z^{-2k} + \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{3^{n+1}} z^n = \cdots - \frac{1}{z^4} - \frac{1}{z^2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{z}{9} + \frac{z^2}{27} + \cdots$$
    <2>3. **Region III ($|z| > 3$):**
        Here both $|z| > 1$ and $|z| > 3$ hold:
        $$f(z) = -\sum_{k=1}^\infty z^{-2k} - \sum_{k=1}^\infty 3^{k-1} z^{-k} = -\sum_{k=1}^\infty \left( [k \text{ is even}] + 3^{k-1} \right) z^{-k}.$$

<1>4. Conclusion:
    There are 3 expansions corresponding to $|z| < 1$, $1 < |z| < 3$, and $|z| > 3$. Q.E.D.
:::
