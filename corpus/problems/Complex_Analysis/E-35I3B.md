::: problem
Assume $f(z)$ is analytic on the unit disk $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid |z| < 1\}$, maps $\mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{D}$, satisfies $f(0) = 0$, and is not a rotation (i.e. $f(z) \ne e^{i\theta} z$).
Show that the series of iterates:
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty f^n(z)$$
converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb{D}$ to an analytic function, where $f^1(z) = f(z)$ and $f^{n+1}(z) = f(f^n(z))$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove locally uniform convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^\infty f^n(z)$ using the strict contraction estimate from the Schwarz Lemma.

<1>1. Strict Contraction from the Schwarz Lemma:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By the **Schwarz Lemma** applied to the holomorphic self-map $f: \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{D}$ with $f(0) = 0$:
        - $|f(z)| \le |z|$ for all $z \in \mathbb{D}$, and
        - $|f'(0)| \le 1$.
    <2>2. Furthermore, if $|f(z_0)| = |z_0|$ for some $z_0 \ne 0$ or if $|f'(0)| = 1$, then $f(z) = e^{i\theta} z$ is a rotation.
    <2>3. Since $f$ is given to **not be a rotation**, the strict inequalities hold:
        $$|f'(0)| < 1 \quad \text{and} \quad |f(z)| < |z| \text{ for all } z \in \mathbb{D} \setminus \{0\}.$$

<1>2. Uniform Contraction Constant on Compact Subsets:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Let $K \subset \mathbb{D}$ be a compact subset.
    <2>2. Since $K$ is compact and contained in the open disk $\mathbb{D}$, there exists $r \in (0, 1)$ such that $K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{D}}_r = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid |z| \le r\}$.
    <2>3. Consider the auxiliary function $g(z) = \frac{f(z)}{z}$ for $z \ne 0$ and $g(0) = f'(0)$.
    <2>4. Since $f(0) = 0$, $g(z)$ is analytic on $\mathbb{D}$.
    <2>5. By the Maximum Modulus Principle applied to $g$ on the disk $\overline{\mathbb{D}}_r \subset \mathbb{D}$:
        - $g(\mathbb{D}) \subset \mathbb{D}$ (since $f$ is not a rotation, $|g(z)| < 1$ for all $z \in \mathbb{D}$).
        - Because $\overline{\mathbb{D}}_r$ is compact, the continuous function $|g(z)|$ attains its maximum:
            $$c \coloneqq \max_{|z| \le r} |g(z)| = \max_{|z| \le r} \frac{|f(z)|}{|z|} < 1.$$
    <2>6. Thus, for all $z \in \overline{\mathbb{D}}_r$:
        $$|f(z)| \le c |z| \quad \text{where } 0 \le c < 1.$$

<1>3. Exponential Decay of Iterates $f^n(z)$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Since $c < 1$, for any $z \in \overline{\mathbb{D}}_r$:
        $$|f(z)| \le c |z| \le c r < r.$$
        Thus $f(z) \in \overline{\mathbb{D}}_{c r} \subset \overline{\mathbb{D}}_r$.
    <2>2. By induction on $n \ge 1$:
        $$|f^n(z)| \le c^n |z| \le r c^n \quad \text{for all } z \in K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{D}}_r.$$

<1>4. Weierstrass M-Test and Analyticity of Sum:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. For each $n \ge 1$, let $M_n = r c^n$.
    <2>2. The series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty M_n = r \sum_{n=1}^\infty c^n = \frac{r c}{1 - c} < \infty$ is a convergent geometric series since $0 \le c < 1$.
    <2>3. Since $|f^n(z)| \le M_n$ for all $z \in K$, by the **Weierstrass M-Test**, the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty f^n(z)$ converges absolutely and uniformly on $K$.
    <2>4. Because each term $f^n(z)$ is analytic on $\mathbb{D}$ and the convergence is uniform on every compact subset $K \subset \mathbb{D}$, by **Weierstrass's Theorem on Analytic Series**, the limit function:
        $$F(z) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty f^n(z)$$
        is analytic on $\mathbb{D}$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $\sum_{n=1}^\infty f^n(z)$ converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb{D}$ to an analytic function. Q.E.D.
:::
