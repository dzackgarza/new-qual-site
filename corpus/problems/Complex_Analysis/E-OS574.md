::: problem
(1) Show that $\displaystyle f(z) = \frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z}$ and $\displaystyle g(z) = \sum_{n = - \infty}^{ \infty} \frac{1}{(z-n)^2}$ have the same principal part at each integer point $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

(2) Show that $\displaystyle h(z) = \frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z} - g(z)$ is bounded on $\mathbb{C}$ and conclude that $\displaystyle \frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2 \pi z} = \sum_{n = - \infty}^{ \infty} \frac{1}{(z-n)^2}$.
:::

::: solution
Write
\[
g(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{(z-n)^2}.
\]
The series converges locally uniformly on $\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z$, since on a compact set away from the integers its tails are dominated by a constant multiple of $\sum n^{-2}$. Hence $g$ is meromorphic there and is $1$-periodic.

Near an integer $k$,
\[
g(z)=\frac1{(z-k)^2}+\text{holomorphic terms}.
\]
Also
\[
\sin(\pi z)=(-1)^k\pi(z-k)+O((z-k)^3),
\]
so
\[
\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2(\pi z)}
=rac1{(z-k)^2}+O(1).
\]
Thus the two meromorphic functions have the same principal part at every integer.

Therefore
\[
h(z)=\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2(\pi z)}-g(z)
\]
extends to an entire $1$-periodic function.

It remains to bound $h$ on one vertical fundamental strip. For $z=x+iy$ with $0\le x\le1$,
\[
|\sin(\pi z)|^2=\sin^2(\pi x)+\sinh^2(\pi y),
\]
so the first term tends uniformly to $0$ as $|y|\to\infty$. Moreover
\[
|g(x+iy)|
\le
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{(x-n)^2+y^2}
\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly in $x\in[0,1]$ as $|y|\to\infty$; for example, compare the sum with $|y|^{-2}+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(t^2+y^2)^{-1}dt$.
Hence $h$ is bounded for large $|y|$. On the remaining compact part of the strip it is bounded by continuity. Periodicity then makes $h$ bounded on all of $\mathbb C$.

By Liouville's theorem $h$ is constant, and the limit along $iy$ as $y\to\infty$ is $0$. Thus $h\equiv0$, i.e.
\[
\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2(\pi z)}
=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{(z-n)^2}.
\]
:::
