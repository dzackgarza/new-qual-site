::: {.exercise}
Show that
$$
\int_{S^1}z^k \dz =
\begin{cases}
2\pi i & k=-1,
\\
0 & \text{else},
\end{cases}
$$
and thus, for a series $\sum_{k\geq -M} c_k z^k$ that converges on an annulus containing $S^1$,
$$
\int_{S^1} \sum_{k\geq -M} c_k z^k \dz = \sum_{k\geq -M} \int_{S^1} c_k z^k \dz = 2\pi i c_{-1},
$$
i.e. the integral picks out the $c_{-1}$ coefficient in a Laurent series expansion.
:::

::: {.solution}
Orient $S^1$ positively and parametrize it by $z = e^{i\theta}$, $0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #monomial}
$\int_{S^1} z^k\dz$ is $2\pi i$ for $k=-1$ and $0$ for every other integer $k$.

::: pf-proof
With $\dz = ie^{i\theta}\dtheta$,
$$
\int_{S^1} z^k \dz = i\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i(k+1)\theta} \dtheta.
$$
For $k=-1$ the integrand is $1$ and the integral is $2\pi i$. For $k\neq -1$ the function $e^{i(k+1)\theta}/(i(k+1))$ is an antiderivative with equal values at $0$ and $2\pi$, so the integral is $0$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$\int_{S^1} \sum_{k\geq -M} c_k z^k \dz = 2\pi i c_{-1}$.

::: pf-proof
A Laurent series converges uniformly on compact subsets of its annulus of convergence, hence uniformly on $S^1$, so the integral of the sum is the sum of the integrals. By step [](#monomial){.pf-ref} only the term $k=-1$ contributes, and it contributes $2\pi i c_{-1}$.
:::
