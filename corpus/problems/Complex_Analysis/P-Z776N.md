::: {.problem}
Let $U,V\subseteq\CC$ be open. Prove that if $f:U\to V$ is [[D-E7A5W|holomorphic]] and injective then $f'(z)\neq 0$ on $U$.
:::

::: {.solution}
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $f'(z_0)=0$ at some $z_0\in U$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #order}
$f(z)-f(z_0)=(z-z_0)^mg(z)$ near $z_0$ with $m\ge2$, $g$ holomorphic and $g(z_0)\ne0$.

::: pf-proof
An injective holomorphic function is nonconstant, so some derivative of $f$ at $z_0$ is nonzero; let $m$ be the least $k\ge1$ with $f^{(k)}(z_0)\ne0$. Then $m\ge2$ since $f'(z_0)=0$.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #disk}
There is $r>0$ such that $\overline{D(z_0,r)}\subseteq U$, $g$ has no zero on it, and $z_0$ is the only zero of $f'$ on it.

::: pf-proof
$g(z_0)\ne0$ and $g$ is continuous; $f'$ is holomorphic and not identically zero near $z_0$, since $f$ is nonconstant, so its zeros are isolated.
:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #count}
For $w$ with $0<\abs{w-f(z_0)}<M\coloneqq\min_{\abs{z-z_0}=r}\abs{f(z)-f(z_0)}$, the equation $f(z)=w$ has exactly $m$ solutions in $D(z_0,r)$, counted with multiplicity.

::: pf-proof
By steps [](#order){.pf-ref} and [](#disk){.pf-ref}, $M>0$. On $\abs{z-z_0}=r$, $\abs{(f(z)-w)-(f(z)-f(z_0))}=\abs{w-f(z_0)}<M\le\abs{f(z)-f(z_0)}$, so by Rouché's theorem $f-w$ has as many zeros in $D(z_0,r)$ as $f-f(z_0)$, which has exactly $m$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$f$ is not injective, a contradiction; hence $f'(z)\ne0$ on $U$.

::: pf-proof
No solution in step [](#count){.pf-ref} is $z_0$, because $w\ne f(z_0)$, and by step [](#disk){.pf-ref} $f'\ne0$ at each solution, so every solution is simple. Thus $f$ takes the value $w$ at $m\ge2$ distinct points of $D(z_0,r)$.
:::

::: {.remark title="Alternative proof by an $m$-th root"}
With $f(z)-f(z_0)=(z-z_0)^mg(z)$ as in step [](#order){.pf-ref}, shrink to a disc about $z_0$ on which $g$ has no zero, so $g$ has a holomorphic $m$-th root $h$ there.
Then $f-f(z_0)=\varphi^m$ with $\varphi(z)\coloneqq(z-z_0)h(z)$ and $\varphi'(z_0)=h(z_0)\neq0$, so by the inverse function theorem $\varphi$ maps a neighborhood of $z_0$ biholomorphically onto a disc $\abs w<\delta$.
For $0<\varepsilon<\delta$ and $\zeta\coloneqq e^{2\pi i/m}\neq1$, the points $\varepsilon$ and $\zeta\varepsilon$ have the same $m$-th power, so their distinct preimages under $\varphi$ have the same image under $f$.
:::

::: {.remark title="Variations"}
The conclusion extends: $f(U)$ is open and $f^{-1}\colon f(U)\to U$ is holomorphic, so $f$ is a [[D-TM4TE|biholomorphism]] onto its image [@SS03, Chapter 8, Proposition 1.1].
By the [[C-FRF33|open mapping theorem]] on each component of $U$, $f$ maps open sets to open sets, so $f(U)$ is open and $g\coloneqq f^{-1}$ is continuous.
For $w\neq w_0$ in $f(U)$ put $z\coloneqq g(w)$ and $z_0\coloneqq g(w_0)$; then $z\neq z_0$, $z\to z_0$ as $w\to w_0$, and
$$
\frac{g(w)-g(w_0)}{w-w_0}=\qty{\frac{f(z)-f(z_0)}{z-z_0}}^{-1}\to\frac{1}{f'(z_0)},
$$
which exists because $f'(z_0)\neq0$.
:::

::: {.example}
A nowhere-vanishing derivative does not imply injectivity: $f(z)=e^z$ satisfies $f'(z)=e^z\neq0$ on $\CC$, and $f(0)=f(2\pi i)$.
Over $\RR$ the result fails: $x\mapsto x^3$ is an injective smooth map with derivative $0$ at $0$, and its inverse $x^{1/3}$ is not differentiable at $0$.
:::
