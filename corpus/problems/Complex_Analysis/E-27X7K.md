:::{.exercise}
Let $f$ be entire and define a function
\[
m: [0, \infty) &\to \mathbb{R} \\
r &\mapsto \min_{\abs{z} = r} \abs{f(z)}
.\]
Suppose $\tilde m := \lim_{r\to \infty}m(r)$ exists and is a finite positive real number, and show that $f$ is constant.

> Hint: consider $g(z) := f(1/z)$.

:::

::: solution
Let
\[
m(r)=\min_{|z|=r}|f(z)|,
\qquad
m(r)\longrightarrow \widetilde m\in(0,\infty).
\]

<1>1. Since $m(r)\to\widetilde m>0$, there is $R>0$ such that
\[
|f(z)|\ge \widetilde m/2
\qquad(|z|\ge R).
\]
Thus
\[
g(w):=f(1/w)
\]
is holomorphic on $0<|w|<1/R$ and bounded away from $0$ there.

<1>2. The singularity of $g$ at $0$ is not essential. Indeed, Casorati--Weierstrass would force values of $g$ arbitrarily close to $0$, contradicting the lower bound.

<1>3. The singularity is not a pole either. If $g$ had a pole of order $k\ge1$, then
\[
w^k g(w)\longrightarrow a\ne0
\qquad(w\to0).
\]
Hence uniformly on $|w|=1/r$,
\[
|g(w)|\sim |a|r^k,
\]
so
\[
m(r)=\min_{|w|=1/r}|g(w)|\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
contrary to the assumed finite limit.

<1>4. Therefore $0$ is removable for $g$. After extending $g$ holomorphically across $0$, it is bounded on a small closed disk about $0$. Translating back, $f$ is bounded for all sufficiently large $|z|$. It is also bounded on the remaining compact disk, so $f$ is bounded on $\mathbb C$.

By Liouville's theorem, $f$ is constant.
:::
