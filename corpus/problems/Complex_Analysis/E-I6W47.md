::: {.exercise}
Show that if $u$ is harmonic on $\RR^2$ and bounded, then $u$ is constant.
:::

::: {.solution title="Using Liouville"}

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #entire}
There is an entire function $f$ with $\Re f=u$, and $e^f$ is a bounded entire function.

::: pf-proof
Since $\RR^2$ is simply connected, $u$ has a harmonic conjugate $v$, and $f\coloneqq u+iv$ is entire.
Then $\abs{e^f}=e^u$, which is bounded because $u$ is bounded above.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$u$ is constant.

::: pf-proof
By step [](#entire){.pf-ref} and Liouville's theorem, $e^f$ is constant, so $0=(e^f)'=f'e^f$.
Since $e^f$ has no zeros, $f'\equiv0$, so $f$ is constant and $u=\Re f$ is constant.
:::

:::

:::

:::

::: {.solution title="Using the mean value property"}
Let $\abs u\le M$ on $\RR^2$, and write $B_R(p)$ for the open disk of radius $R$ about $p$.

::: pf

::: {.pf-step #average}
For $p\in\RR^2$ and $R>0$, $u(p)={1\over\pi R^2}\int_{B_R(p)}u\,dA$.

::: pf-proof
By the mean value property, $u(p)$ is the average of $u$ over each circle about $p$; integrating over the radii $0<\rho<R$ against $2\pi\rho\,d\rho$ gives the average over the disk.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$u(p)=u(q)$ for all $p,q\in\RR^2$.

::: pf-proof
Let $d\coloneqq\abs{p-q}$. By step [](#average){.pf-ref},
$$
\abs{u(p)-u(q)}\le{1\over\pi R^2}\int_{B_R(p)\,\triangle\,B_R(q)}\abs u\,dA\le{M\cdot\operatorname{area}\qty{B_R(p)\triangle B_R(q)}\over\pi R^2}
.$$
The symmetric difference lies in the annulus $B_{R+d}(p)\setminus B_{R-d}(p)$ for $R>d$, whose area is $4\pi Rd$, so the right side is at most $4Md/R$, which tends to $0$ as $R\to\infty$.
:::
