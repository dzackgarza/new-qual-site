::: problem
Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{C}$ be an open and bounded domain and $\phi: \Omega \to \Omega$ a holomorphic map.
Prove that if there exists a point $z_0 \in \Omega$ such that $\phi(z_0) = z_0$ and $\phi'(z_0) = 1$, then $\phi(z) = z$ for all $z \in \Omega$.
:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove Cartan's Uniqueness Theorem / Fixed Point Theorem: a holomorphic self-map of a bounded domain with $\phi(z_0) = z_0$ and $\phi'(z_0) = 1$ is the identity map $\phi(z) = z$.

<1>1. Translation to $z_0 = 0$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By translating the domain and map via $\tau(z) = z - z_0$, we define $\Omega_0 = \Omega - z_0$ and $f(z) = \phi(z + z_0) - z_0$.
    <2>2. Then $\Omega_0$ is a bounded open set containing $0$, and $f: \Omega_0 \to \Omega_0$ is a holomorphic self-map with $f(0) = 0$ and $f'(0) = \phi'(z_0) = 1$.
    <2>3. Proving $f(z) = z$ on $\Omega_0$ is equivalent to proving $\phi(z) = z$ on $\Omega$.

<1>2. Expansion near $0$ and power series of iterates:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Suppose, for contradiction, that $f(z) \not\equiv z$.
    <2>2. Since $f(0) = 0$ and $f'(0) = 1$, the Taylor series of $f$ near $0$ begins as:
        $$f(z) = z + a_n z^n + O(z^{n+1})$$
        where $n \ge 2$ is the smallest integer such that the coefficient $a_n \ne 0$.
    <2>3. For each $k \ge 1$, let $f_k = \underbrace{f \circ f \circ \cdots \circ f}_{k \text{ times}}$ be the $k$-th iterate of $f$.
    <2>4. **Induction claim:** For every $k \ge 1$, the Taylor series of $f_k$ near 0 satisfies:
        $$f_k(z) = z + k a_n z^n + O(z^{n+1}).$$
    <2>5. *Base case $k = 1$:* $f_1(z) = z + a_n z^n + O(z^{n+1})$, which holds.
    <2>6. *Inductive step:* Assume $f_k(z) = z + k a_n z^n + O(z^{n+1})$. Then:
        $$\begin{aligned}
        f_{k+1}(z) &= f(f_k(z)) = f_k(z) + a_n (f_k(z))^n + O((f_k(z))^{n+1}) \\
        &= (z + k a_n z^n + O(z^{n+1})) + a_n (z + O(z^n))^n + O(z^{n+1}) \\
        &= z + k a_n z^n + a_n z^n + O(z^{n+1}) \\
        &= z + (k+1) a_n z^n + O(z^{n+1}).
        \end{aligned}$$
    <2>7. Thus the $n$-th derivative of the $k$-th iterate at 0 is:
        $$f_k^{(n)}(0) = n! \cdot k a_n.$$

<1>3. Boundedness and Cauchy Estimates on Iterates:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Because $\Omega_0$ is bounded, there exists $M > 0$ such that $|w| \le M$ for all $w \in \Omega_0$.
    <2>2. Since $f: \Omega_0 \to \Omega_0$, every iterate satisfies $f_k(\Omega_0) \subseteq \Omega_0$, which means:
        $$|f_k(z)| \le M \quad \text{for all } z \in \Omega_0 \text{ and all } k \ge 1.$$
    <2>3. Choose $r > 0$ such that the closed disk $\overline{D}_r(0) \subset \Omega_0$.
    <2>4. By Cauchy's Estimates applied to $f_k$ on $D_r(0)$:
        $$\left| f_k^{(n)}(0) \right| \le \frac{n! M}{r^n} \quad \text{for all } k \ge 1.$$

<1>4. Deriving the Contradiction:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. Substituting $f_k^{(n)}(0) = n! k a_n$ into the Cauchy estimate:
        $$n! k |a_n| \le \frac{n! M}{r^n} \implies k |a_n| \le \frac{M}{r^n} \quad \text{for every integer } k \ge 1.$$
    <2>2. Taking the limit as $k \to \infty$, the right side $\frac{M}{r^n}$ is constant, while the left side $k |a_n| \to \infty$ if $|a_n| > 0$.
    <2>3. This forces $|a_n| = 0$, so $a_n = 0$.
    <2>4. This contradicts the assumption that there is any non-zero coefficient $a_n \ne 0$ for $n \ge 2$.
    <2>5. By the Identity Theorem for holomorphic functions, $f(z) = z$ on all of $\Omega_0$.

<1>5. Conclusion:
    $\phi(z) = z$ for all $z \in \Omega$. Q.E.D.
:::
