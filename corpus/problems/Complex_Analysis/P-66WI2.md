::: problem
Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{C}$ be an open and bounded domain and $\phi: \Omega \to \Omega$ a holomorphic map.
Prove that if there exists a point $z_0 \in \Omega$ such that $\phi(z_0) = z_0$ and $\phi'(z_0) = 1$, then $\phi(z) = z$ for all $z \in \Omega$.
:::

::: solution
Translate so that $z_0=0$. Thus $0\in\Omega$, $\phi(0)=0$, and $\phi'(0)=1$. Suppose $\phi$ is not the identity. Since $\phi(z)-z$ is holomorphic and not identically zero, there is a least $m\ge2$ such that
$$
\phi(z)=z+az^m+O(z^{m+1}),\qquad a\ne0.
$$

<1>1. Let $\phi^{\circ n}$ denote the $n$-fold iterate. Induction on $n$ gives
$$
\phi^{\circ n}(z)=z+na z^m+O(z^{m+1}).
$$
Indeed, composing $z+na z^m+O(z^{m+1})$ with $z+az^m+O(z^{m+1})$ changes the coefficient of $z^m$ from $na$ to $(n+1)a$.
Hence
$$
(\phi^{\circ n})^{(m)}(0)=m!na.
$$

<1>2. Because $\Omega$ is bounded, choose $M$ with $|w|\le M$ for every $w\in\Omega$. Also choose $r>0$ with $\overline{D_r(0)}\subset\Omega$. Every iterate maps $\Omega$ into itself, so on $D_r(0)$,
$$
|\phi^{\circ n}(z)|\le M.
$$
Cauchy's estimate gives
$$
|(\phi^{\circ n})^{(m)}(0)|\le\frac{m!M}{r^m}.
$$
Thus
$$
n|a|\le\frac{M}{r^m}
$$
for every $n$, impossible because $a\ne0$. Therefore $\phi(z)=z$ near $0$, and the identity theorem gives $\phi(z)=z$ on the connected domain $\Omega$.
:::
