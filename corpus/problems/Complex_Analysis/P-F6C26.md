::: problem
Suppose $A, B \subseteq \RR^n$ are disjoint and compact.
Prove that there exist $a\in A, b\in B$ such that
\[  
\norm{a - b} = \inf\theset{\norm{x-y} \suchthat x\in A,\, y\in B}
.\]
:::

::: {.solution}
<1>1. The function $d: A \times B \to \RR$, $d(x, y) = \|x - y\|$, is continuous.
::: {.proof}
the norm and subtraction are continuous.
:::

<1>2. $A \times B$ is compact.
::: {.proof}
the product of two compact sets is compact (Tychonoff).
:::

<1>3. Hence $d$ attains its minimum on $A \times B$: there exist $a \in A$, $b \in B$ with $d(a, b) = \inf_{x \in A, y \in B} d(x, y)$.
::: {.proof}
a continuous real-valued function on a compact set attains its infimum.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.
::: {.proof}
<1>3.
:::
:::
