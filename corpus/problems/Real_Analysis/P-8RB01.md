::: problem
(a) Use the definition of the derivative to prove that if $f$ and $g$ are differentiable at $x$, then $fg$ is differentiable at $x$.

(b) Use the definition of the Riemann integral to prove that if $f$ is bounded on $[a,b]$ and is continuous everywhere except for finitely many points in $(a,b)$, then $f\in\mathscr{R}$ on $[a,b]$.
:::


::: solution
<1>1. Prove the product rule from the definition of derivative.
::: proof
Assume that $f$ and $g$ are differentiable at $x$. For $h\ne0$,
\[
\frac{f(x+h)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x)}{h}
=
f(x+h)\frac{g(x+h)-g(x)}{h}
+
g(x)\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}.
\]
Differentiability of $f$ at $x$ implies continuity there, so
\[
f(x+h)\to f(x).
\]
Also,
\[
\frac{g(x+h)-g(x)}{h}\to g'(x),
\qquad
\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\to f'(x).
\]
Therefore
\[
\lim_{h\to0}
\frac{f(x+h)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x)}{h}
=
f(x)g'(x)+g(x)f'(x).
\]
Thus $fg$ is differentiable at $x$, with
\[
\boxed{(fg)'(x)=f'(x)g(x)+f(x)g'(x).}
\]
:::

<1>2. Reduce Riemann integrability to a Darboux-sum estimate.
::: proof
Let the discontinuity points of $f$ in $(a,b)$ be
\[
c_1,\dots,c_N.
\]
Because $f$ is bounded, choose $M>0$ such that
\[
|f(x)|\le M
\qquad(x\in[a,b]).
\]
Fix $\varepsilon>0$.

Choose pairwise disjoint open intervals $I_j$ around $c_j$ whose total length is less than
\[
\frac{\varepsilon}{4M}
\]
(if $M=0$, the conclusion is immediate). The contribution to any upper-minus-lower Darboux sum from subintervals contained in these neighborhoods is at most
\[
2M\sum_{j=1}^N |I_j|<\frac\varepsilon2.
\]
:::

<1>3. Control the oscillation away from the discontinuities.
::: proof
Remove the chosen neighborhoods from $[a,b]$. The remaining set is a finite union of compact intervals on each of which $f$ is continuous, hence uniformly continuous.

Therefore there exists $\delta>0$ such that whenever $x,y$ lie in the same remaining compact component and $|x-y|<\delta$, one has
\[
|f(x)-f(y)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2(b-a)}.
\]
Choose a partition $P$ containing all endpoints of the intervals $I_j$ and subdividing every complementary compact interval into pieces of length less than $\delta$.

On each complementary partition interval $J$, the oscillation of $f$ is less than $\varepsilon/(2(b-a))$. Hence the total contribution of all such intervals to
\[
U(f,P)-L(f,P)
\]
is at most
\[
\frac{\varepsilon}{2(b-a)}(b-a)=\frac\varepsilon2.
\]
Combining this with Step 2 gives
\[
U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon.
\]
Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, the Darboux criterion for Riemann integrability implies
\[
\boxed{f\in\mathscr R([a,b]).}
\]
:::
