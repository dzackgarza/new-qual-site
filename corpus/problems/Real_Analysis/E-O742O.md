::: {.exercise}
- Prove that $\hat f = \hat g$ implies that $f=g$ a.e.

- Show that if $f, g \in L^1$ then $$\int \hat f g = \int f\hat g.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
Use $\hat f(\xi) = \int f(x) e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx$ on $\RR^n$.

<1>1. If $f, g \in L^1$ and $\hat f = \hat g$, then $f = g$ a.e.

<2>1. Let $h = f - g$. Then $h \in L^1$ and $\hat h = 0$.

::: {.proof}
The Fourier transform is linear.
:::

<2>2. Let $\phi \geq 0$ be continuous with compact support and $\int\phi = 1$, and put $\phi_\eps(x) = \eps^{-n}\phi(x/\eps)$ and $u_\eps = h \ast \phi_\eps$. Then $u_\eps \to h$ in $L^1$ as $\eps \to 0$.

::: {.proof}
This is the $L^1$ convergence of approximations to the identity.
:::

<2>3. $\widehat{u_\eps}(\xi) = \hat h(\xi)\hat\phi(\eps\xi) = 0$ for every $\xi$.

::: {.proof}
By Fubini's theorem the Fourier transform of a convolution of $L^1$ functions is the product of the transforms, and $\hat h = 0$ by step <2>1.
:::

<2>4. $u_\eps = 0$ identically.

::: {.proof}
$u_\eps$ is continuous, as the convolution of an $L^1$ function with a bounded uniformly continuous function. It lies in $L^1 \cap L^\infty \subseteq L^2$. By Plancherel's theorem and step <2>3, $\|u_\eps\|_2 = (2\pi)^{-n/2}\|\widehat{u_\eps}\|_2 = 0$, so $u_\eps = 0$ a.e., and a continuous function that vanishes a.e. vanishes everywhere.
:::

<2>5. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
By steps <2>2 and <2>4, $h$ is the $L^1$ limit of the zero functions $u_\eps$, so $h = 0$ a.e.
:::

<1>2. If $f, g \in L^1$, then $\int \hat f\,g = \int f\,\hat g$.

::: {.proof}
$\iint |f(x)g(\xi)|\,dx\,d\xi = \|f\|_1\|g\|_1 < \infty$, so Fubini's theorem gives
$$
\int \hat f(\xi)g(\xi)\,d\xi = \int_\xi \int_x f(x)g(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx\,d\xi = \int_x \int_\xi f(x)g(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi\,dx = \int f(x)\hat g(x)\,dx.
$$
:::

<1>3. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Step <1>1 proves the first part and step <1>2 the second.
:::
:::
