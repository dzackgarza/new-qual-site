::: {.exercise}
- What does it mean for a metric space to be **complete**?

- Give two or more equivalently definitions for **compactness** in a complete metric space.

- What is an interior point?
  An isolated point?
  A limit point?

- What does it mean for a set to be open?
  Closed?

- What is the **closure** of a subspace $E\subseteq X$?

- What does it mean for $E\subseteq X$ to be a **dense** subspace?

- What does it mean for a family of sets to form a **basis** for a topology?

  - What is a basis for the standard topology on $\RR^d$?

- Let $X$ be a subset of $\RR^d$.
  Prove the Heine-Borel theorem:

  - Show that $X$ compact $\implies X$ is closed

  - Show that $X$ compact $\implies X$ is bounded

  - Show that a closed subset of a compact set must be bounded.

  - Show that if $X$ closed and bounded $\implies X$ is compact.

- Find an example of a metric space with a closed and bounded subspace that is not compact.

  - How can this be modified to obtain a necessary and sufficient condition?

- Determine if the following subsets of $\RR$ are opened, closed, both, or neither:

  - $\QQ$

  - $\ZZ$

  - $\ts{1}$

  - $\ts{p \in \ZZ^{\geq 0} \st p\text{ is prime}}$

  - $\ts{ {1\over n} \st n\in \ZZ^{\geq 0}}$

  - $\ts{ {1\over n} \st n\in \ZZ^{\geq 0}} \union \ts{0}$
:::

::: {.solution}
Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space and $E \subseteq X$.

- $(X,d)$ is \dfn{complete} if every Cauchy sequence in $X$ converges to a point of $X$.

- For a metric space $K$ the following are equivalent: every open cover of $K$ has a finite subcover (the definition of compactness); every sequence in $K$ has a subsequence converging in $K$; $K$ is complete and totally bounded, that is, for every $\eps > 0$ finitely many $\eps$-balls cover $K$.

- $x$ is an \dfn{interior point} of $E$ if $B(x,r) \subseteq E$ for some $r > 0$; an \dfn{isolated point} of $E$ if $x \in E$ and $B(x,r) \cap E = \theset{x}$ for some $r > 0$; a \dfn{limit point} of $E$ if $(B(x,r)\setminus\theset{x}) \cap E \neq \emptyset$ for every $r > 0$.

- $E$ is \dfn{open} if every point of $E$ is an interior point, and \dfn{closed} if $X \setminus E$ is open, equivalently if $E$ contains all its limit points.

- The \dfn{closure} $\overline E$ is the smallest closed set containing $E$, equivalently $E$ together with its limit points.

- $E$ is \dfn{dense} if $\overline E = X$, equivalently every nonempty open subset of $X$ meets $E$.

- A family $\mathcal B$ of subsets of a set $X$ is a \dfn{basis} for a topology if every point lies in some $B \in \mathcal B$, and for $B_1, B_2 \in \mathcal B$ and $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ there is $B_3 \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$; the topology it generates consists of the unions of members of $\mathcal B$. The open balls $B(x,r)$, $x \in \RR^d$, $r > 0$, form a basis for the standard topology on $\RR^d$, as do the open boxes.

::: pf

::: pf-step

If $K \subseteq \RR^d$ is compact, then $K$ is closed.

::: pf-proof

::: {.pf-step #s1-1}

Fix $x \in \RR^d \setminus K$. For $y \in K$ the balls $U_y = B(y, \abs{x-y}/3)$ and $V_y = B(x, \abs{x-y}/3)$ are disjoint.

::: pf-proof

$\abs{x-y} > 0$, and a common point $z$ would give $\abs{x-y} \le \abs{x-z} + \abs{z-y} < 2\abs{x-y}/3$.

:::

:::

::: {.pf-step #s1-2}

There are $y_1, \ldots, y_k \in K$ with $K \subseteq U_{y_1} \cup \cdots \cup U_{y_k}$.

::: pf-proof

The balls $U_y$, $y \in K$, form an open cover of the compact set $K$.

:::

:::

::: pf-qed

$V = \bigcap_{i=1}^k V_{y_i}$ is an open ball about $x$. A point $z \in V \cap K$ would lie in some $U_{y_i}$ by step [](#s1-2){.pf-ref} and in $V_{y_i}$, contradicting step [](#s1-1){.pf-ref}. So every point of $\RR^d \setminus K$ has a neighborhood disjoint from $K$, and $\RR^d \setminus K$ is open.

:::
