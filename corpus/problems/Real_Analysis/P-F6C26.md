::: {.problem}
Suppose $A, B \subseteq \RR^n$ are nonempty, disjoint and compact.
Prove that there exist $a\in A, b\in B$ such that
$$
\norm{a - b} = \inf\theset{\norm{x-y} \suchthat x\in A,\, y\in B}
.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
::: pf

::: pf-step
The function $d: A \times B \to \RR$, $d(x, y) = \norm{x - y}$, is continuous.

::: pf-proof
It is the composition of the continuous maps $(x,y)\mapsto x-y$ and $v\mapsto\norm{v}$.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
$A \times B$ is a nonempty compact set.

::: pf-proof
A finite product of compact sets is compact, and $A\times B$ is nonempty because $A$ and $B$ are.
:::

:::

::: pf-step
There exist $a \in A$ and $b \in B$ with $\norm{a-b} = \inf_{x \in A,\, y \in B} \norm{x-y}$.

::: pf-proof
A continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact set attains its infimum; apply this to $d$ on $A\times B$.
:::
