::: {.problem}
Prove: if $f \in C[0,1]$ and $\int_0^1 f(x)e^{-nx}\,dx = 0$ for all $n \in \NN_0$, then $f = 0$.
:::

::: {.solution}

::: pf

::: pf-step

If $f \in C[0,1]$ and $\int_0^1 f(x)e^{-nx}\,dx = 0$ for every $n \geq 0$, then $f \equiv 0$.

::: pf-proof

Substitute $t = e^{-x}$, so $x = -\log t$ and $dx = -dt/t$, and $x \in [0,1]$ corresponds to $t \in [e^{-1}, 1]$. Then
$$
0 = \int_0^1 f(x)e^{-nx}\,dx = \int_{e^{-1}}^1 f(-\log t)\,t^{n-1}\,dt = \int_{e^{-1}}^1 g(t)\,t^n\,dt,
\qquad g(t) \coloneqq \frac{f(-\log t)}{t},
$$
for every $n \geq 0$, and $g$ is continuous on $[e^{-1},1]$. So $\int_{e^{-1}}^1 gp = 0$ for every polynomial $p$. By the Weierstrass approximation theorem, choose polynomials $p_k \to g$ uniformly on $[e^{-1},1]$. Then
$$
\int_{e^{-1}}^1 g(t)^2\,dt = \lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{e^{-1}}^1 g(t)p_k(t)\,dt = 0,
$$
so the continuous function $g$ vanishes on $[e^{-1},1]$. Since $t \mapsto -\log t$ maps $[e^{-1},1]$ onto $[0,1]$, $f \equiv 0$.

:::
