::: {.exercise title="Munkres §36.5"}


The Hausdorff condition is an essential part of the definition of a manifold; it is not implied by the other parts of the definition. Consider the following space. Let $X$ be the union of the set $\mathbb{R} - \ts{0}$ and the two-point set $\ts{p, q}$. Topologize $X$ by taking as basis the collection of all open intervals in $\mathbb{R}$ that do not contain 0, along with all sets of the form $(-a, 0) \cup \ts{p} \cup (0, a)$ and all sets of the form $(-a, 0) \cup \ts{q} \cup (0, a)$, for $a > 0$. The space $X$ is called the line with two origins.

(a) Check that this is a basis for a topology.

(b) Show that each of the spaces $X - \ts{p}$ and $X - \ts{q}$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$.

(c) Show that $X$ satisfies the $T_1$ axiom, but is not Hausdorff.

(d) Show that $X$ satisfies all the conditions for a 1-manifold except for the Hausdorff condition.
:::
