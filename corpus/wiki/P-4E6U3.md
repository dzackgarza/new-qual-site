::: problem
Show that the infinite series $\sum_{n=0}^\infty x^n2^{-nx}$ converges uniformly on $[0,B]$ for any $B > 0$.
Does this series converge uniformly on $[0,\infty)$?
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

<1>1. The terms are $x^n2^{-nx} = (x2^{-x})^n$.
    Proof: $2^{-nx} = (2^{-x})^n$.

<1>2. $\sup_{x \ge 0} x2^{-x} = \frac{1}{e\ln 2} < 1$.
    <2>1. The function $h(x) = x2^{-x} = xe^{-x\ln 2}$ has its global maximum on $[0,\infty)$ at $x = 1/\ln 2$.
        Proof: $h'(x) = e^{-x\ln 2}(1 - x\ln 2)$ vanishes only at $x = 1/\ln 2$, where $h$ changes from increasing to decreasing; $h(0) = 0$ and $h \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$.
    <2>2. $h(1/\ln 2) = \frac{1}{e\ln 2} \approx 0.531 < 1$.
        Proof: $h(1/\ln 2) = \frac{1}{\ln 2}e^{-1} = \frac{1}{e\ln 2}$, and $e\ln 2 > 1$.

<1>3. The series converges uniformly on $[0, B]$ for every $B > 0$.
    Proof: on $[0,B]$, $x2^{-x} \le \max(h(B), h(1/\ln 2)) < 1$ by <1>2, so the Weierstrass M-test applies: $\sum (x2^{-x})^n \le \sum r^n$ with $r < 1$.

<1>4. The series converges uniformly on $[0, \infty)$ as well.
    Proof: <1>2 gives a uniform ratio bound $x2^{-x} \le \frac{1}{e\ln 2} < 1$ on all of $[0,\infty)$, so the M-test applies globally: $\left|\sum_{n>N}(x2^{-x})^n\right| \le \frac{r^{N+1}}{1-r}$ with $r = \frac{1}{e\ln 2}$, which $\to 0$ as $N \to \infty$ independent of $x$.
:::
