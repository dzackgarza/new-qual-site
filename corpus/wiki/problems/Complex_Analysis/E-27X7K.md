:::{.exercise}
Let $f$ be entire and define a function
\[
m: [0, \infty) &\to \mathbb{R} \\
r &\mapsto \min_{\abs{z} = r} \abs{f(z)}
.\]
Suppose $\tilde m := \lim_{r\to \infty}m(r)$ exists and is a finite positive real number, and show that $f$ is constant.

> Hint: consider $g(z) := f(1/z)$.

:::

::: solution
**Goal:** Prove that if an entire function $f$ satisfies $\lim_{r \to \infty} \min_{|z|=r} |f(z)| = \tilde{m} \in (0, \infty)$, then $f$ is constant.

<1>1. Non-vanishing of $f$ near $\infty$:
    There exists $R_0 > 0$ such that $|f(z)| \ge \frac{\tilde{m}}{2} > 0$ for all $|z| \ge R_0$.
    *Proof:* Since $\lim_{r \to \infty} m(r) = \tilde{m} > 0$, taking $\varepsilon = \frac{\tilde{m}}{2} > 0$ yields an $R_0 > 0$ such that $m(r) > \frac{\tilde{m}}{2}$ for all $r \ge R_0$. For any $z$ with $|z| = r \ge R_0$, $|f(z)| \ge m(r) > \frac{\tilde{m}}{2}$.

<1>2. Classification of the singularity of $g(z) = f(1/z)$ at $z = 0$:
    The isolated singularity of $g(z)$ at $z = 0$ is a removable singularity.
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. The function $g$ is holomorphic on the punctured disk $\mathbb{D}^* = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : 0 < |z| < \frac{1}{R_0}\}$.
    <2>2. For all $z \in \mathbb{D}^*$, $|g(z)| = |f(1/z)| \ge \frac{\tilde{m}}{2} > 0$ by <1>1.
    <2>3. Not an essential singularity: If $z = 0$ were an essential singularity, the Casorati-Weierstrass Theorem would imply that $g(\mathbb{D}^*)$ is dense in $\mathbb{C}$. In particular, there would exist $z_k \to 0$ with $g(z_k) \to 0$, contradicting $|g(z)| \ge \frac{\tilde{m}}{2} > 0$.
    <2>4. Not a pole: If $z = 0$ were a pole of order $k \ge 1$, then $\lim_{z \to 0} |g(z)| = \infty$. This would imply $\lim_{r \to \infty} m(r) = \infty$, contradicting the hypothesis that $\tilde{m}$ is finite.
    <2>5. Therefore $z = 0$ is a removable singularity of $g$.

<1>3. $f$ is bounded on $\mathbb{C}$:
    *Proof:*
    <2>1. By Riemann's Removable Singularity Theorem, $g$ extends to a holomorphic function on the whole disk $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < \frac{1}{R_0}\}$.
    <2>2. Since $g$ is continuous on the compact closure $\overline{B}(0, \frac{1}{2R_0})$, $g$ is bounded: there exists $M_1 > 0$ such that $|g(z)| \le M_1$ for all $|z| \le \frac{1}{2R_0}$.
    <2>3. Thus $|f(w)| \le M_1$ for all $|w| \ge 2R_0$.
    <2>4. On the compact disk $\overline{B}(0, 2R_0)$, the continuous function $|f(z)|$ is bounded by some $M_2 > 0$.
    <2>5. Hence $|f(z)| \le \max\{M_1, M_2\}$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$, so $f$ is bounded on the entire complex plane.

<1>4. Conclusion:
    By Liouville's Theorem, every bounded entire function is constant. Therefore $f$ is constant. Q.E.D.
:::
