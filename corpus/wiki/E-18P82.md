A collection $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $X$ is said to be locally discrete if each point of $X$ has a neighborhood that intersects at most one element of $\mathcal{A}$. A collection $\mathcal{B}$ is countably locally discrete (or "$\sigma$-locally discrete") if it equals a countable union of locally discrete collections. Prove the following.

Theorem (Bing metrization theorem). A space $X$ is metrizable if and only if it is regular and has a basis that is countably locally discrete.

::: {.remark}
Munkres, *Topology*, §40 Exercise 5.
:::
