::: {.exercise title="Munkres §30.16"}


(a) Show that the product space $\mathbb{R}^I$, where $I = [0, 1]$, has a countable dense subset.

(b) Show that if $J$ has cardinality greater than $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}_+)$, then the product space $\mathbb{R}^J$ does not have a countable dense subset. [Hint: If $D$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}^J$, define $f: J \to \mathcal{P}(D)$ by the equation $f(\alpha) = D \cap \pi_\alpha^{-1}((a, b))$, where $(a, b)$ is a fixed interval in $\mathbb{R}$.]
:::
