::: problem
Let $f: \RR \to \RR$ and suppose
$$
\forall x\in \RR,\quad f(x) \geq \limsup _{y \rightarrow x} f(y)
$$
Prove that $f$ is Borel measurable.
:::
::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

<1>1. It suffices to show that $\{f > a\}$ is Borel for every real $a$.
Proof: the Borel $\sigma$-algebra is generated by the rays $(a, \infty)$, so a function is Borel iff all its superlevel sets $\{f > a\}$ are Borel.

<1>2. The hypothesis says $f(x) \ge \limsup_{y \to x} f(y)$ for every $x$, i.e. $f$ is upper semicontinuous (u.s.c.). Proof: by definition, $\limsup_{y \to x} f(y) \le f(x)$ is exactly the u.s.c. condition.

<1>3. $f$ is u.s.c. iff $\{f < a\}$ is open for every $a \in \RR$.
<2>1. ($\Rightarrow$) If $f(x) < a$, then $\limsup_{y \to x} f(y) \le f(x) < a$, so there is $\delta > 0$ with $f(y) < a$ for all $y$ with $|y - x| < \delta$.
Proof: if every neighborhood of $x$ contained a point with $f(y) \ge a$, the limsup would be $\ge a$, contradiction.
<2>2. ($\Leftarrow$) If every $\{f < a\}$ is open, then for $x$ with $f(x) < a$, a whole neighborhood satisfies $f < a$, so $\limsup_{y \to x} f(y) \le a$; letting $a \downarrow f(x)$... more carefully: for any $a > f(x)$, a neighborhood has $f < a$, so $\limsup_{y \to x} f(y) \le a$, and taking $a \to f(x)^+$ gives $\limsup_{y \to x} f(y) \le f(x)$.
Proof: the limsup is the infimum over neighborhoods of the supremum, and <2>2's open sublevel sets make those suprema $\le a$.

<1>4. Hence $\{f \ge a\} = \RR \setminus \{f < a\}$ is closed, and $\{f > a\} = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \{f \ge a + 1/n\}$ is a countable union of closed sets, i.e. $F_\sigma$.
Proof: <1>3 gives $\{f < a\}$ open; the superlevel-set identity is elementary.

<1>5. Q.E.D. Proof: <1>4 shows every superlevel set $\{f > a\}$ is Borel, so by <1>1 the function $f$ is Borel measurable.
(No regularity of $f$ beyond the limsup hypothesis is needed; u.s.c. functions are Borel.)
