::: problem
Let $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuous with $f(0)\neq f(1)$ and define $f_n(x)=f(x^n)$.
Prove that $f_n$ does not converge uniformly on $[0,1]$.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

<1>1. $f_n \to g$ pointwise, where $g(x) = f(0)$ for $x \in [0,1)$ and $g(1) = f(1)$.
Proof: $x^n \to 0$ for $0 \le x < 1$ and $1^n = 1$; $f$ is continuous, so $f(x^n) \to f(0)$ for $x < 1$ and $f(1^n) = f(1)$.

<1>2. $g$ is discontinuous at $1$.
Proof: $g(1) = f(1) \ne f(0) = \lim_{x \to 1^-}g(x)$ by hypothesis $f(0) \ne f(1)$.

<1>3. If $f_n$ converged uniformly, its limit would be continuous.
Proof: the uniform limit theorem — a uniform limit of continuous functions ($f_n$ continuous since $x \mapsto x^n$ and $f$ are continuous) is continuous.

<1>4. Q.E.D. Proof: the pointwise limit $g$ of $f_n$ is unique, and it is discontinuous by <1>2, contradicting <1>3; hence the convergence is not uniform.
:::
