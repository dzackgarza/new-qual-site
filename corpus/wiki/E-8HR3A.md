Show that if $X$ has a countable basis $\ts{B_n}$, then every basis $\mathcal{C}$ for $X$ contains a countable basis for $X$. [Hint: For every pair of indices $n, m$ for which it is possible, choose $C_{n,m} \in \mathcal{C}$ such that $B_n \subset C_{n,m} \subset B_m$.]

::: {.remark}
Munkres, *Topology*, §30 Exercise 2.
:::
