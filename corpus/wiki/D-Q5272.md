::: {.definition title="Closed Maps"}
A map $f: X\to Y$ is a **closed map** iff $f(A)$ is closed in $Y$ for every closed $A \subseteq X$.
Dually, $f$ is an **open map** iff $f(U)$ is open in $Y$ for every open $U\subseteq X$.

Neither condition implies or is implied by continuity, and neither implies the other: the projection $\pi_1: \RR\cross\RR \to \RR$ is open and not closed, since the hyperbola $\ts{xy = 1}$ is closed and its image $\RR\smz$ is not.

Two uses:

- A surjective continuous map that is open or closed is a quotient map; the converse fails.

- If $X$ is compact and $Y$ is Hausdorff, every continuous $f: X\to Y$ is closed. Hence a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism, which is how most explicit homeomorphisms out of quotients are proved.
:::

::: {.concept}
See Munkres, *Topology*, §22 for the definitions, the quotient-map criterion and the projection example; §26 Exercise 6 for the compact-to-Hausdorff statement; Theorem 26.6 for the homeomorphism corollary.
:::
