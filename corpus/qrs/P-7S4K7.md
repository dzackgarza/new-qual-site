::: problem
- Show that any power series is continuous on its domain of convergence.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

**Goal:** Show that a power series $\sum a_n z^n$ is continuous on its domain of convergence (the disk $\abs z < R$, where $R$ is its radius of convergence).

<1>1. Fix $z_0$ with $\abs{z_0} < R$, and choose $\rho$ with $\abs{z_0} < \rho < R$.
Proof: The interval $(\abs{z_0}, R)$ is nonempty, so such $\rho$ exists.

<1>2. The series converges absolutely and uniformly on the closed disk $\abs z \leq \rho$.
<2>1. $\sum_{n} \abs{a_n} \rho^n < \infty$.
Proof: A power series converges absolutely inside its radius of convergence; since $\rho < R$, the series at $\rho$ converges absolutely.
<2>2. $\abs{a_n z^n} \leq \abs{a_n} \rho^n$ for all $\abs z \leq \rho$.
Proof: $\abs z^n \leq \rho^n$.
<2>3. Weierstrass $M$-test.
Proof: <2>1 and <2>2 give uniform convergence on $\abs z \leq \rho$.

<1>3. The partial sums $S_N(z) := \sum_{n=0}^{N} a_n z^n$ are continuous on $\abs z \leq \rho$.
Proof: Polynomials are continuous.

<1>4. The uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous.
Proof: Standard epsilon/3 argument using uniform convergence (<1>2.3).

<1>5. Hence the power series is continuous at $z_0$.
Proof: $z_0$ lies in the interior of $\abs z \leq \rho$ (<1>1), and the series is continuous on that closed disk (<1>2--<1>4).

<1>6. Q.E.D. Proof: $z_0$ was arbitrary with $\abs{z_0} < R$, so the series is continuous on its whole domain of convergence.
