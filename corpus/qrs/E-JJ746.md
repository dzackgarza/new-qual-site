::: exercise
- $\star$: Show that cylinder functions are measurable, i.e. if $f$ is measurable on $\RR^s$, then $F(x, y) \definedas f(x)$ is measurable on $\RR^s\cross \RR^t$ for any $t$.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

**Goal:** If $f$ is measurable on $\RR^s$, then the cylinder function $F(x,y) := f(x)$ is measurable on $\RR^s \cross \RR^t$.

<1>1. The claim holds when $f = \chi_E$ is an indicator of a measurable set $E \subseteq \RR^s$.
<2>1. $F = \chi_{E \cross \RR^t}$.
Proof: $F(x,y) = 1$ iff $x \in E$, i.e. iff $(x,y) \in E \cross \RR^t$.
<2>2. $E \cross \RR^t$ is measurable in the product $\sigma$-algebra.
Proof: $E \in \mathcal{B}(\RR^s)$, and $E \cross \RR^t = \pi_s^{-1}(E)$ where $\pi_s: \RR^s \cross \RR^t \to \RR^s$ is the (continuous, hence measurable) projection; the product $\sigma$-algebra on $\RR^{s+t}$ is the one generated by such cylinder sets.
<2>3. Q.E.D. Proof: <2>1 and <2>2 show $F$ is a measurable indicator.
<1>2. The claim holds for simple functions $s = \sum_{i=1}^k c_i \chi_{E_i}$.
Proof: the lift $S(x,y) = s(x) = \sum_i c_i \chi_{E_i}(x)$ is a finite linear combination of measurable indicators by <1>1, hence measurable.
<1>3. The claim holds for arbitrary measurable $f$.
<2>1. Choose simple $s_n \nearrow f$ pointwise (for $f \geq 0$; for signed $f$ split $f = f^+ - f^-$). Proof: standard approximation of measurable functions by simple functions (monotone convergence construction).
<2>2. $F_n(x,y) := s_n(x)$ is measurable for each $n$ and $F_n \nearrow F$ pointwise.
Proof: <1>2 gives measurability; $F_n(x,y) = s_n(x) \nearrow f(x) = F(x,y)$ for each fixed $(x,y)$.
<2>3. $F$ is measurable.
Proof: pointwise limits of measurable functions are measurable.
<1>4. Q.E.D. Proof: <1>1–<1>3 lift measurability from indicators to simple functions to all measurable $f$.
:::
