::: problem
Show that if $f$ is entire and $\lim_{z\to\infty}f(z) = \infty$, then
$f$ is a polynomial.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

**Goal:** Show that if $f$ is entire and $\lim_{z \to \infty} f(z) = \infty$, then $f$ is a polynomial.

<1>1. The singularity of $f$ at $\infty$ is not removable.
    Proof: if $\infty$ were a removable singularity, $f$ would be bounded near $\infty$, contradicting $f(z) \to \infty$.

<1>2. The singularity at $\infty$ is not essential.
    Proof: if $\infty$ were an essential singularity, the Casorati--Weierstrass theorem would imply $f$ takes values arbitrarily close to any prescribed value (in particular $f$ does not tend to $\infty$) in every neighborhood of $\infty$; contradiction.

<1>3. Hence $\infty$ is a pole of $f$.
    Proof: by <1>1 and <1>2, the isolated singularity at $\infty$ is a pole (of some order $m \ge 1$).

<1>4. A function holomorphic on $\CC$ with a pole of order $m$ at $\infty$ is a polynomial of degree $m$.
<2>1. Write $g(w) = f(1/w)$; then $g$ has a pole of order $m$ at $w = 0$, so $g(w) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \frac{b_j}{w^j} + h(w)$ with $h$ holomorphic near $0$.
    Proof: definition of a pole of order $m$.
<2>2. $f(z) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} b_j z^j + h(1/z)$ on $\CC \setminus \{0\}$.
    Proof: substitute $w = 1/z$ in <2>1.
<2>3. $h$ is constant, so $f$ is a polynomial.
    Proof: $f$ is entire, hence holomorphic at $z = 0$; but $h(1/z)$ is holomorphic at $0$ only if $h$ is constant (a nonconstant holomorphic $h(w) = \sum_{k \ge 0} c_k w^k$ would make $h(1/z) = \sum c_k z^{-k}$ singular at $0$ unless $c_k = 0$ for $k \ge 1$). Thus $f(z) = \sum_{j=1}^m b_j z^j + c_0$ is a polynomial of degree $m$.

<1>5. Q.E.D.
    Proof: <1>3 and <1>4 show $f$ is a polynomial.
