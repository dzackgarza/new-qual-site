::: exercise
- Prove that $\hat f = \hat g$ implies that $f=g$ a.e.

- Show that if $f, g \in L^1$ then $$\int \hat f g = \int f\hat g.$$
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

<1>1. Uniqueness: if $f, g \in L^1$ and $\hat f = \hat g$, then $f = g$ a.e. <2>1. It suffices to show that $\hat h = 0$ forces $h = 0$ a.e., applied to $h = f - g$.
Proof: $\widehat{f - g} = \hat f - \hat g = 0$ by linearity of the transform.
<2>2. Let $\phi_\eps$ be an approximation to the identity and set $u_\eps = h \ast \phi_\eps$; then $u_\eps \to h$ in $L^1$ as $\eps \to 0$.
Proof: standard property of approximations to the identity ($\phi_\eps(x) = \eps^{-n}\phi(x/\eps)$ with $\phi \ge 0$, $\int\phi = 1$, $\supp \phi$ compact).
<2>3. $\hat{u_\eps}(\xi) = \hat h(\xi)\hat\phi(\eps\xi) = 0$ for every $\xi$.
Proof: the Fourier transform of a convolution is the product of the transforms (justified by Fubini), and $\hat h = 0$ by hypothesis.
<2>4. $u_\eps = 0$ identically.
Proof: $u_\eps$ is continuous (convolution of an $L^1$ function with a compactly supported $L^1$ function) and lies in $L^2$ (Young's inequality), so Plancherel gives $\|u_\eps\|_2 = \|\hat{u_\eps}\|_2 = 0$ by <2>3; a continuous function that is zero a.e. is identically zero.
<2>5. $h = 0$ a.e. Proof: $h = \lim_{\eps \to 0} u_\eps$ in $L^1$ by <2>2, and each $u_\eps = 0$ by <2>4. <2>6. Q.E.D. Proof: <2>1–<2>5.

<1>2. The identity $\int \hat f\,g = \int f\,\hat g$ holds for $f, g \in L^1$.
<2>1. Both sides are iterated integrals of the same double integral.
Proof: $\int \hat f(\xi)g(\xi)\,d\xi = \int_\xi \int_x f(x)g(\xi)e^{-ix\xi}\,dx\,d\xi$, while $\int f(x)\hat g(x)\,dx = \int_x \int_\xi f(x)g(\xi)e^{-ix\xi}\,d\xi\,dx$.
<2>2. Fubini's theorem applies.
Proof: $\iint |f(x)g(\xi)|\,dx\,d\xi = \|f\|_1\|g\|_1 < \infty$.
<2>3. Q.E.D. Proof: <2>1 and <2>2.

<1>3. Q.E.D. Proof: <1>1 proves the first bullet, <1>2 the second.
