::: exercise
- $\star$: Show that for $E\subseteq \RR^n$, TFAE:

  1. $E$ is measurable

  2. $E = H\union Z$ here $H$ is $F_\sigma$ and $Z$ is null

  3. $E = V\setminus Z'$ where $V\in G_\delta$ and $Z'$ is null.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

**Goal:** For $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, show the three statements are equivalent: (1) $E$ is measurable; (2) $E = H \cup Z$ with $H$ an $F_\sigma$ set and $Z$ null; (3) $E = V \setminus Z'$ with $V$ a $G_\delta$ set and $Z'$ null.

<1>1. (1) $\Rightarrow$ (2).
    <2>1. For each $k$, inner regularity gives a closed $F_k \subseteq E$ with $m(E \setminus F_k) < 1/k$.
        Proof: Lebesgue measure is inner regular: $m(E) = \sup\theset{m(F) : F \subseteq E, F \text{ closed}}$ (approximation by closed sets holds for all measurable $E$, with the finite/infinite cases handled by truncating to bounded balls).
    <2>2. $H := \bigcup_k F_k$ is $F_\sigma$, $Z := E \setminus H$ is null, and $E = H \cup Z$ (disjoint).
        Proof: $H$ is a countable union of closed sets, hence $F_\sigma$. Since $Z \subseteq E \setminus F_k$ for every $k$, $m(Z) \leq m(E \setminus F_k) < 1/k$ for all $k$, so $m(Z) = 0$. $E = H \cup Z$ by construction.
<1>2. (1) $\Rightarrow$ (3).
    <2>1. For each $k$, outer regularity gives an open $G_k \supseteq E$ with $m(G_k \setminus E) < 1/k$.
        Proof: Lebesgue measure is outer regular.
    <2>2. $V := \bigcap_k G_k$ is $G_\delta$, $Z' := V \setminus E$ is null, and $E = V \setminus Z'$.
        Proof: $V$ is a countable intersection of open sets, hence $G_\delta$. Since $Z' \subseteq G_k \setminus E$ for every $k$, $m(Z') \leq m(G_k \setminus E) < 1/k$ for all $k$, so $m(Z') = 0$. And $V \setminus Z' = V \setminus (V \setminus E) = E$ since $E \subseteq V$.
<1>3. (2) $\Rightarrow$ (1) and (3) $\Rightarrow$ (1).
    <2>1. If $E = H \cup Z$ with $H$ $F_\sigma$ and $Z$ null, then $E$ is measurable.
        Proof: $H$ is a countable union of closed (hence measurable) sets, so $H$ is measurable; $Z$ is null, hence measurable; a union of measurable sets is measurable.
    <2>2. If $E = V \setminus Z'$ with $V$ $G_\delta$ and $Z'$ null, then $E$ is measurable.
        Proof: $V$ is a countable intersection of open (hence measurable) sets, so $V$ is measurable; $Z'$ is null, hence measurable; $E = V \cap (Z')^c$ is measurable.
<1>4. Q.E.D.
    Proof: <1>1 and <1>2 give (1) $\Rightarrow$ (2) and (1) $\Rightarrow$ (3); <1>3 gives both converses, so all three are equivalent.
