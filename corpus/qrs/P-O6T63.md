::: problem
Compute the following limit:
\[
\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \int_{1}^{n} \frac{n e^{-x}}{1+n x^{2}} \, \sin \left(\frac x n\right) \, dx
\]
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

<1>1. Extend each integrand to all of $[1, \infty)$: $g_n(x) = \frac{n e^{-x}}{1 + nx^2}\sin(x/n)\,\chi_{[1,n]}(x)$.
Proof: the integral in the problem equals $\int_1^\infty g_n(x)\,dx$ since $g_n = 0$ on $(n, \infty)$.

<1>2. $|g_n(x)| \le \frac{e^{-x}}{x^2}$ for every $n$ and every $x \ge 1$.
Proof: $|\sin(x/n)| \le 1$; and $\frac{n}{1 + nx^2} \le \frac{n}{nx^2} = \frac{1}{x^2}$.

<1>3. The dominating function $x \mapsto \frac{e^{-x}}{x^2}$ is integrable on $[1, \infty)$.
Proof: $\int_1^\infty \frac{e^{-x}}{x^2}\,dx \le \int_1^\infty e^{-x}\,dx = e^{-1} < \infty$.

<1>4. $g_n(x) \to 0$ pointwise for every $x \ge 1$.
Proof: for fixed $x$: $\frac{n}{1 + nx^2} \to \frac{1}{x^2}$ and $\sin(x/n) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

<1>5. Q.E.D. Proof: dominated convergence (<1>2, <1>3, <1>4) gives $\lim_n \int_1^n \frac{n e^{-x}}{1+nx^2}\sin(x/n)\,dx = \int_1^\infty 0\,dx = 0$.
:::
