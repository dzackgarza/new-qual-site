::: exercise
Show that a finite product or union compact spaces is again compact.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution** (Gemini 3.7 Flash)

**(1) Finite Union of Compact Spaces:**
Let $X = \bigcup_{i=1}^n K_i$, where each $K_i$ is a compact topological space (or compact subspace of a larger space).
Let $\mathcal{U} = \{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be an open cover of $X$.
For each $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, $\mathcal{U}$ is an open cover of $K_i$.
Since $K_i$ is compact, there exists a finite subcover $\mathcal{U}_i = \{U_{i, 1}, \ldots, U_{i, m_i}\} \subseteq \mathcal{U}$ such that:
$$
K_i \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^{m_i} U_{i, j}.
$$
Then the union of these finite collections:
$$
\mathcal{U}' = \bigcup_{i=1}^n \mathcal{U}_i = \bigcup_{i=1}^n \{U_{i, 1}, \ldots, U_{i, m_i}\}
$$
is a finite subcollection of $\mathcal{U}$.
Moreover:
$$
X = \bigcup_{i=1}^n K_i \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^n \bigcup_{j=1}^{m_i} U_{i, j}.
$$
Thus $\mathcal{U}'$ is a finite subcover of $X$, so $X$ is compact.

**(2) Finite Product of Compact Spaces:**
It suffices to prove that the product of two compact spaces $X$ and $Y$ is compact (by induction, any finite product $\prod_{i=1}^n X_i$ is compact).

Let $\mathcal{U} = \{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be an open cover of $X \times Y$.
1. **Tube Lemma:** For each fixed $x \in X$, the slice $\{x\} \times Y \cong Y$ is compact.
   The collection $\mathcal{U}$ covers $\{x\} \times Y$.
   By compactness of $Y$, there is a finite subcollection $\{U_{\alpha_1}, \ldots, U_{\alpha_k}\} \subseteq \mathcal{U}$ such that:
   $$
   \{x\} \times Y \subseteq U_x := \bigcup_{j=1}^k U_{\alpha_j}.
   $$
   By the Tube Lemma, there exists an open neighborhood $V_x$ of $x$ in $X$ such that:
   $$
   V_x \times Y \subseteq U_x = \bigcup_{j=1}^k U_{\alpha_j}.
   $$

2. **Covering $X$:**
   The collection $\{V_x\}_{x \in X}$ forms an open cover of $X$.
   Since $X$ is compact, there exists a finite subcover $\{V_{x_1}, V_{x_2}, \ldots, V_{x_m}\}$ of $X$, so $X = \bigcup_{i=1}^m V_{x_i}$.

3. **Finite Subcover of $X \times Y$:**
   For each $i \in \{1, \ldots, m\}$, $V_{x_i} \times Y$ is covered by a finite subcollection $\mathcal{U}_{x_i} \subseteq \mathcal{U}$.
   Then:
   $$
   X \times Y = \left( \bigcup_{i=1}^m V_{x_i} \right) \times Y = \bigcup_{i=1}^m (V_{x_i} \times Y) \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^m \bigcup_{U \in \mathcal{U}_{x_i}} U.
   $$
   The union $\bigcup_{i=1}^m \mathcal{U}_{x_i}$ is a finite subcollection of $\mathcal{U}$ covering $X \times Y$.
   Hence $X \times Y$ is compact.
:::
