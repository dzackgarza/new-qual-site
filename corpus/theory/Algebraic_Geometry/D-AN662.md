::: {.definition title="Ringed spaces"}
A \dfn{ringed space} is a pair $(X, \OO_X)$ of a topological space $X$ and a sheaf of rings $\OO_X$ on $X$, its \dfn{structure sheaf}.
An \dfn{isomorphism} of ringed spaces $(X, \OO_X) \to (Y, \OO_Y)$ is a homeomorphism $f: X \to Y$ together with an isomorphism of sheaves of rings $\OO_Y \to f_* \OO_X$.
A ringed space is a \dfn{locally ringed space} if every stalk $\OO_{X,p}$ is a local ring.
:::

::: {.definition title="Scheme"}
A \dfn{scheme} is a locally ringed space $(X, \OO_X)$ admitting an open cover by spaces isomorphic, as locally ringed spaces, to $\Spec A$ for various rings $A$.
A \dfn{morphism of schemes} is a morphism of locally ringed spaces: a continuous $f: X \to Y$ together with $f^\sharp : \OO_Y \to f_* \OO_X$ whose induced maps on stalks are local homomorphisms.
:::

::: {.definition title="Scheme over a ring"}
Let $A$ be a ring.
A \dfn{scheme over $A$} is a scheme $X$ with a morphism $X \to \Spec A$.
Morphisms $X \to \Spec A$ correspond to ring homomorphisms $A \to \Gamma(X, \OO_X)$, so this is the same as making every $\OO_X(U)$ an $A$-algebra with $A$-algebra restriction maps.
[@Har10a, Exercise II.2.4]
:::

::: {.example title="A morphism of ringed spaces that is not a morphism of schemes"}
Let $R$ be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$, closed point $\mfm$ and generic point $\eta$.
Send the one point of $\Spec K$ to $\mfm$, and let $f^\sharp$ be the inclusion $R \injects K$ on sections over $\Spec R$ and zero over the open set $\theset{\eta}$, whose preimage is empty.
This is a morphism of ringed spaces.
Its map on stalks at the point is the inclusion $R = \OO_{\Spec R, \mfm} \injects K$, which sends a uniformizer to a unit and so is not local.
It is not induced by a ring homomorphism: the only candidate $R \to K$ is the inclusion, and $\Spec$ of the inclusion sends the point to $\eta$.
:::

::: {.proposition title="Morphisms to affine space"}
For an $S$-scheme $X$, an $S$-morphism $X \to \AA^n_S$ is the same as an $n$-tuple of global sections $h_1, \ldots, h_n \in \Gamma(X, \OO_X)$, the pullbacks of the coordinates.
[@Har10a]
:::

::: {.proposition title="The scheme of a variety"}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed.
There is a fully faithful functor $t$ from varieties over $k$ to schemes over $k$.
The points of $t(V)$ are the nonempty irreducible closed subsets of $V$, with closed sets the sets $t(Y)$ for $Y \subseteq V$ closed.
The closed points of $t(V)$ form a subspace homeomorphic to $V$, and restricting $\OO_{t(V)}$ to it recovers the sheaf of regular functions on $V$.
[@Har10a]
:::

::: {.remark}
For rings $A$ and $B$, every morphism of locally ringed spaces $\Spec B\to\Spec A$ is induced by a unique ring homomorphism $A\to B$ [@Har10a, Proposition II.2.3], so $\Spec$ is an anti-equivalence from the category of rings to the category of affine schemes.
The morphism $\Spec K\to\Spec R$ in the example of a morphism of ringed spaces that is not a morphism of schemes has a non-local map on stalks, and it is induced by no ring homomorphism.

A scheme is glued from affine schemes along open subschemes, as a manifold is glued from open subsets of $\RR^n$ [@Har10a, Exercise II.2.12].
:::
