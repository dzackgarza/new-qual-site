::: {.definition title="Scheme"}
A **scheme** is a locally ringed space $(X, \OO_X)$ admitting an open cover by spaces isomorphic, as locally ringed spaces, to $\Spec A$ for various rings $A$.
A **morphism of schemes** is a morphism of locally ringed spaces: a continuous $f: X \to Y$ together with $f^\sharp : \OO_Y \to f_* \OO_X$ whose induced maps on stalks are local homomorphisms.
:::

::: {.remark}
The definition is one line and the examiner is using it to get to the next question, so the content to volunteer is what each clause buys.

*Locally ringed* is what makes $\Spec$ an anti-equivalence onto affine schemes, and hence what makes the category of schemes an enlargement of the category of rings rather than something else.
*Locally isomorphic to a spectrum* is the only globalisation: a scheme is a space glued from rings, and every local question about it is a question about a ring.

The manifold analogy is exact and is worth stating: charts are affines, transition data is the gluing of structure sheaves, and the properties that matter are the ones stable under passing to a smaller chart.
:::
