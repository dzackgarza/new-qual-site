::: {.example title="The fan"}
For positive weights $q_0,\ldots,q_n$ with $\gcd(q_0,\ldots,q_n)=1$, put
$$
N
\coloneqq
\ZZ^{n+1}/\ZZ(q_0,\ldots,q_n)
$$
and let $\bar e_i\in N$ be the image of the $i$th standard basis vector.
The fan of $\PP(q_0,\ldots,q_n)$ has rays $\RR_{\ge0}\bar e_i$ and maximal cones generated by every $n$ of these rays.
The relation
$$
q_0\bar e_0+\cdots+q_n\bar e_n=0
$$
is built into the quotient lattice.
If an image $\bar e_i$ is not primitive, replace it by the primitive generator of the same ray. The resulting primitive ray generators give the fan associated with the well-formed reduction of the weights.
For $q_i=1$, identifying $N\cong\ZZ^n$ gives rays $e_1,\ldots,e_n,-\sum_i e_i$, the fan of $\PP^n$.
:::

::: {.example title="$\PP(1,1,2)$"}
In $N=\ZZ^2$, set
$$
v_2 = (1,0), \quad v_1 = (0,1), \quad v_0 = (-2,-1)
$$
so $v_0+v_1+2v_2=0$, and $v_1,v_2$ form a basis of $N$.
The three maximal cones have determinants
$$
\det(v_1, v_2) = -1, \qquad \det(v_2, v_0) = -1, \qquad \det(v_0, v_1) = -2 ,
$$
so exactly one torus-fixed point is singular, an $A_1$ point of index $2$.
Its cone is $\Cone\big( (-2,-1), (0,1) \big)$, which is $\Cone\big( (0,1), (2,-1) \big)$ after a change of basis: the quadric cone $V(xy - z^2)$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The principal-divisor map gives
$$
\Cl\bigl(\PP(1,1,2)\bigr)=\operatorname{coker}(M\to\ZZ^3)\cong\ZZ.
$$
If $H$ denotes its positive generator, then $[D_0]=[D_1]=H$ and $[D_2]=2H$. The rank-one reflexive sheaf associated with $H$ is $\OO(1)$; it is not invertible at the singular point. The Cartier subgroup is $2\ZZ H$, so
$$
\Pic\bigl(\PP(1,1,2)\bigr)\cong\ZZ
$$
with generator $\OO(2)$.
:::

::: {.example title="$\PP(1,1,2)$ from a polygon"}
Let $P = \operatorname{Conv}(0, 2e_1, e_2) \subseteq M_\RR = \RR^2$, with lattice points $0, e_1, 2e_1, e_2$.
The characters of these points give a map $(\CC^\times)^2 \to \PP^3$, $(s,t) \mapsto [1 : s : s^2 : t]$, and $X_P$ is the closure of the image,
$$
X_P = V(y_0 y_2 - y_1^2) \subseteq \PP^3 ,
$$
the projective cone over a conic.
The inward facet normals of $P$ are $(0,1)$, $(1,0)$ and $(-1,-2)$, with the relation $1 \cdot (1,0) + 2 \cdot (0,1) + 1 \cdot (-1,-2) = 0$, so the normal fan is the fan of $\PP(1,1,2)$ above.
:::
