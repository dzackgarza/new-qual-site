::: {.theorem}
Every presheaf $\mcf$ admits a morphism $\theta : \mcf \to \mcf^+$ to a sheaf, universal among morphisms from $\mcf$ to sheaves.
The map $\theta$ is an isomorphism on every stalk.
:::

::: {.proof}
Use the construction of [@Har77, Proposition-Definition II.1.2]: for an open $U$, $\mcf^+(U)$ is the set of functions
$$
s\colon U\longrightarrow\bigcup_{p\in U}\mcf_p,\qquad s(p)\in\mcf_p,
$$
such that each $p\in U$ has a neighbourhood $V\subseteq U$ and an element $t\in\mcf(V)$ with $t_q=s(q)$ for all $q\in V$.
Restriction of functions makes this a presheaf, and $\theta(U)\colon\mcf(U)\to\mcf^+(U)$ sends $t$ to $q\mapsto t_q$.

**$\mcf^+$ is a sheaf.**
Locality holds because a function is determined by its values and the representing condition is local.
For gluing, let $s_i\in\mcf^+(U_i)$ agree on the overlaps and define $s$ on $U=\bigcup_iU_i$ by $s(p)=s_i(p)$ for $p\in U_i$; this is well defined, at each $p$ a section representing $s_i$ on a neighbourhood inside $U_i$ represents $s$, and $s\restrict_{U_i}=s_i$.

**Universal property.**
Let $\varphi\colon\mcf\to\mcg$ with $\mcg$ a sheaf.
For $s\in\mcf^+(U)$ and $p\in U$ choose $V\ni p$ and $t\in\mcf(V)$ representing $s$ on $V$, and put $\psi(s)\restrict_V\coloneqq\varphi(V)(t)$.
If $t'\in\mcf(V')$ also represents $s$ on $V'$, then $t$ and $t'$ have equal germs at every point of $V\cap V'$, hence so do $\varphi(t)$ and $\varphi(t')$, and $\mcg$ separated gives $\varphi(t)\restrict_{V\cap V'}=\varphi(t')\restrict_{V\cap V'}$; so $\psi(s)\in\mcg(U)$ is well defined by gluing, $\psi$ is compatible with restrictions, and $\psi\circ\theta=\varphi$.
If $\psi'\circ\theta=\varphi$, then $\psi'=\psi$ on every section that is locally of the form $\theta(t)$, and every section of $\mcf^+$ is locally of that form; so $\psi'=\psi$.

**Stalks.**
The map $\theta_p\colon\mcf_p\to\mcf^+_p$ is onto, since an element of $\mcf^+_p$ is represented by some $s\in\mcf^+(V)$ with $p\in V$, and $s$ is represented near $p$ by a section $t\in\mcf(W)$, so the element is $\theta_p(t_p)$.
It is injective, since $\theta_p(t_p)=0$ means $\theta(t)$ vanishes on a neighbourhood of $p$, that is $t_q=0$ for $q$ near $p$, hence $t_p=0$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The stalk statement follows from the construction: $\mcf^+(U)$ is the set of functions $s: U \to \coprod_{p \in U} \mcf_p$ with $s(p) \in \mcf_p$ that are locally represented by sections of $\mcf$.
Nothing is added to a stalk, because a stalk is already a local object; what is added is the ability to assemble, and what is removed are the sections that were locally zero.

Sheafification is left adjoint to the inclusion of sheaves into presheaves, so it preserves colimits and the inclusion preserves limits.
That is the abstract reason kernels of sheaf morphisms need no sheafification and cokernels do.
:::
