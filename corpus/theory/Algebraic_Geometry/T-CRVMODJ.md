$J$ is the analytic moduli coordinate: two points of $\HH$ give the same elliptic curve exactly when they lie in one $\SL_2(\ZZ)$-orbit.

::: {.definition title="The modular function"}
For $\tau\in\HH$, put
$$
\Lambda_\tau\coloneqq\generators{1,\tau}_\ZZ,
\qquad
J(\tau)\coloneqq\frac{g_2(\Lambda_\tau)^3}{\Delta(\Lambda_\tau)},
$$
where $\Delta=g_2^3-27g_3^2$ as in [[D-CRVWEIER]]. With the normalization of [[T-CRVJINV]],
$$
j(E_{\Lambda_\tau})=1728\,J(\tau).
$$
:::

::: {.theorem title="Modular classification"}
One has $J(\tau)=J(\tau')$ if and only if
$$
\tau' = \frac{a\tau + b}{c\tau + d}
\qquad\text{for some}\qquad
\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \in \SL_2(\ZZ) ,
$$
and every $\SL_2(\ZZ)$-orbit has exactly one representative in the fundamental region
$$
F = \theset{ \tau \in \HH \st -\tfrac{1}{2} \leq \Re \tau < \tfrac{1}{2}, \ \abs{\tau} \geq 1, \text{ and } \abs\tau > 1 \text{ when } \Re\tau > 0 } .
$$
So $J \colon \HH/\SL_2(\ZZ) \to \CC$ is a bijection.
:::

::: {.remark title="Normalisation, stated once"}
With the normalization on this card,
$$
J=g_2^3/\Delta,\qquad j=1728J.
$$
In particular,
$$
J(i) = 1, \quad j(i) = 1728 ,
\qquad
J(\rho) = 0, \quad j(\rho) = 0 ,
\qquad \rho \coloneqq e^{2\pi i/3} .
$$
The first holds because multiplication by $i$ preserves $\generators{1,i}$, so $g_3 = 140 \sum_{0 \neq \omega \in \Lambda} \omega^{-6}$ satisfies $g_3 = i^{-6} g_3 = -g_3$, which forces $g_3 = 0$ and $\Delta = g_2^3$.
The second holds because multiplication by $\rho$ preserves $\generators{1,\rho}$, so $g_2 = \rho^{-4} g_2$ forces $g_2 = 0$.
Many sources write $j=1728g_2^3/\Delta$ without introducing $J$.
This is the same normalization used in [[T-CRVJINV]].
:::

::: {.remark}
$J$ is a function of $\tau$ rather than of $\Lambda$ because numerator and denominator have the same weight: $\Lambda \mapsto \alpha\Lambda$ sends $g_2 \mapsto \alpha^{-4}g_2$ and $g_3 \mapsto \alpha^{-6}g_3$, so both $g_2^3$ and $\Delta$ scale by $\alpha^{-12}$ and the ratio does not move.
Every lattice in $\CC$ is homothetic to some $\Lambda_\tau$ with $\tau\in\HH$.

The $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ action comes from change of lattice basis: $\Lambda_\tau$ and $\Lambda_{\tau'}$ are homothetic exactly when $(1,\tau)$ and $(1,\tau')$ are related by an integral matrix of determinant $\pm 1$, and requiring both $\tau$ and $\tau'$ to lie in the upper half plane forces determinant $+1$, since $\Im\qty{\tfrac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}} = \tfrac{(ad-bc)\Im\tau}{\abs{c\tau+d}^2}$.
Hence the acting group is $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ rather than $\GL_2(\ZZ)$.

$\SL_2(\ZZ)$ is generated by $\tau \mapsto \tau+1$ and $\tau \mapsto -1/\tau$, which identify the two vertical edges of $F$ and fold the bottom arc onto itself; the half-open description above picks one point from each identified pair.
Combined with surjectivity of $J$ this gives the analytic proof that $\AA^1$ is a coarse moduli space, matching the algebraic proof by Legendre normal form.
The two fixed points of the folding are the two curves with extra automorphisms: $\tau = i$ fixed by $\tau \mapsto -1/\tau$, and $\tau = \rho$ fixed by an order-three element --- exactly the $j = 1728$ and $j = 0$ cases.
:::
