::: {.theorem title="The modular function"}
For $\tau$ in the upper half plane $\HH$ write $\Lambda_\tau \da \gens{1, \tau}_\ZZ$ and define
\[
J(\tau) \da \frac{g_2(\Lambda_\tau)^3}{\Delta(\Lambda_\tau)},
\qquad
\Delta = g_2^3 - 27 g_3^2,
\qquad
j \da 1728 \, J .
\]
This $j$ is the $j$-invariant of $E_{\Lambda_\tau}$.
Then $J(\tau) = J(\tau')$ if and only if
\[
\tau' = \frac{a\tau + b}{c\tau + d}
\qquad\text{for some}\qquad
\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \in \SL_2(\ZZ) ,
\]
and every $\SL_2(\ZZ)$-orbit has exactly one representative in the fundamental region
\[
F = \ts{ \tau \in \HH \st -\tfrac{1}{2} \leq \Re \tau < \tfrac{1}{2}, \ \abs{\tau} \geq 1, \text{ and } \abs\tau > 1 \text{ when } \Re\tau > 0 } .
\]
So $J \colon \HH/\SL_2(\ZZ) \to \CC$ is a bijection.
:::

::: {.remark title="Normalisation, stated once"}
Conventions differ between sources and the difference is the factor $1728 = 2^6 \cdot 3^3$, so fix it before computing: here $J = g_2^3/\Delta$ and $j = 1728 J$.
The two checkable consequences, which pin the convention without appeal to any book, are
\[
J(i) = 1, \quad j(i) = 1728 ,
\qquad
J(\rho) = 0, \quad j(\rho) = 0 ,
\qquad \rho \da e^{2\pi i/3} .
\]
The first holds because multiplication by $i$ preserves $\gens{1,i}$, so $g_3 = \sum \omega^{-6} \cdot 140 = i^{-6} g_3 = -g_3$ forces $g_3 = 0$ and $\Delta = g_2^3$.
The second holds because multiplication by $\rho$ preserves $\gens{1,\rho}$, so $g_2 = \rho^{-4} g_2$ forces $g_2 = 0$.
Many sources instead write $j = 1728 g_2^3/\Delta$ with no intermediate $J$, and analytic number theory rescales $\Delta$ by $(2\pi)^{12}$ to make $q$-expansions integral; none of that moves $j(i)$ or $j(\rho)$.
:::

::: {.remark}
$J$ is a function of $\tau$ rather than of $\Lambda$ because numerator and denominator have the same weight: $\Lambda \mapsto \alpha\Lambda$ sends $g_2 \mapsto \alpha^{-4}g_2$ and $g_3 \mapsto \alpha^{-6}g_3$, so both $g_2^3$ and $\Delta$ scale by $\alpha^{-12}$ and the ratio does not move.
Every lattice is homothetic to some $\Lambda_\tau$, by dividing through by one generator, so nothing is lost.

The $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ statement is then just a change of basis: $\Lambda_\tau$ and $\Lambda_{\tau'}$ are homothetic exactly when $(1,\tau)$ and $(1,\tau')$ are related by an integral matrix of determinant $\pm 1$, and demanding that both $\tau$ and $\tau'$ lie in the upper half plane forces determinant $+1$, since $\Im\qty{\tfrac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}} = \tfrac{(ad-bc)\Im\tau}{\abs{c\tau+d}^2}$.
That identity is the whole reason the group is $\SL_2$ and not $\GL_2$, and it is the standard trap.

The uniqueness clause is what makes this a moduli statement rather than an invariance statement.
$\SL_2(\ZZ)$ is generated by $\tau \mapsto \tau+1$ and $\tau \mapsto -1/\tau$, which identify the two vertical edges of $F$ and fold the bottom arc onto itself; the half-open description above picks one point from each identified pair.
Combined with surjectivity of $J$ this gives the analytic proof that $\AA^1$ is a coarse moduli space, matching the algebraic proof by Legendre normal form.
The two fixed points of the folding are the two curves with extra automorphisms: $\tau = i$ fixed by $\tau \mapsto -1/\tau$, and $\tau = \rho$ fixed by an order-three element --- exactly the $j = 1728$ and $j = 0$ cases.
:::
