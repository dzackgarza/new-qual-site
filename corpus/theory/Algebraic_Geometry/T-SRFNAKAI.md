::: {.theorem}
A divisor $D$ on a smooth projective surface $X$ is ample if and only if
$$
D^2 > 0 \quad\text{and}\quad D \cdot C > 0 \text{ for every irreducible curve } C \subseteq X .
$$
:::

::: {.proof}
**Necessity.**
If $D$ is ample then $mD$ is very ample for some $m>0$, so $mD$ defines a projective embedding in which $m^2D^2$ is the degree of $X$ and $mD\cdot C$ is the degree of the curve $C$; both are positive [@Har77, Remark V.1.6.2].

**Sufficiency, step 1: a positive multiple is effective.**
Assume $D^2>0$ and $D\cdot C>0$ for every irreducible curve $C$, and let $H$ be a very ample divisor on $X$; it is represented by an irreducible curve, so $D\cdot H>0$. Then for $n\gg0$ we have $nD\cdot H>K\cdot H$, so $H^2(X,\mathcal{L}(nD))=0$ by [@Har77, Lemma V.1.7], and Riemann–Roch [@Har77, Theorem V.1.6] gives
$$
l(nD)\geq\tfrac12n^2D^2-\tfrac12n\,D\cdot K+1+p_a\longrightarrow\infty,
$$
because $D^2>0$; hence $nD$ is effective for $n\gg0$ [@Har77, Corollary V.1.8]. Replacing $D$ by such an $nD$ we may assume $D$ effective, possibly singular and reducible.

**Step 2: $\mathcal{L}(D)$ is ample on $D$.**
By the reduction of [@Har77, Exercise III.5.7] it suffices that $\mathcal{L}\otimes\OO_{D_{\mathrm{red}}}$ be ample on $D_{\mathrm{red}}$; this is the step the book carries out in the passage immediately following the reference, and it uses $D\cdot C>0$ for the irreducible components $C$ of $D$.

**Step 3: $\mathcal{L}^n$ is generated by global sections for $n\gg0$.**
Tensor $0\to\mathcal{L}^{-1}\to\OO_X\to\OO_D\to0$ with $\mathcal{L}^n$ and take cohomology. Since $\mathcal{L}\otimes\OO_D$ is ample on $D$, [@Har77, III.5.3] gives $H^1(D,\mathcal{L}^n\otimes\OO_D)=0$ for $n\gg0$, and the exact sequence shows
$$
\dim H^1(X,\mathcal{L}^n)\leq\dim H^1(X,\mathcal{L}^{n-1})
$$
for all large $n$; the dimensions being finite and non-increasing, they become constant, so the maps $H^0(X,\mathcal{L}^n)\to H^0(D,\mathcal{L}^n\otimes\OO_D)$ are surjective for $n\gg0$. The sheaf $\mathcal{L}^n\otimes\OO_D$ is generated by its global sections for $n\gg0$, again because it is ample [@Har77, III.5.3], and those sections lift, so $\mathcal{L}^n$ is generated by global sections by Nakayama's lemma.

**Step 4: a finite morphism to projective space.**
Fix such an $n$ and let $\varphi\colon X\to\PP^N$ be the morphism defined by $\mathcal{L}^n$ [@Har77, Theorem II.7.1]. Its fibres are finite: a positive-dimensional fibre would contain an irreducible curve $C$ contracted by $\varphi$, so $\mathcal{L}^n\cdot C=0$ and hence $D\cdot C=0$, contradicting the hypothesis. By the Stein factorization [@Har77, Theorem III.11.5] and [@Har77, Exercise III.11.2] the morphism $\varphi$ is then finite, so $\mathcal{L}^n=\varphi^*\OO(1)$ is ample [@Har77, Exercise III.5.7], and therefore $D$ is ample.
:::

::: {.remark}
This is the surface analogue of "ample means positive degree" on a curve, and the two conditions are exactly the two dimensions of subvariety: positivity against every curve, and positivity against the surface itself.

Both are needed.
On the blowup of $\PP^2$ at a point, $D = \pi^*H$ has $D^2 = 1 > 0$ but $D \cdot E = 0$ on the exceptional curve, so it is not ample — it is only the pullback of an ample class, and the morphism $\pi$ it defines contracts $E$.
Conversely a class can meet every curve positively on a surface while failing $D^2 > 0$, which is why the first condition is not implied by the second.

The criterion is purely numerical, so ampleness on a surface depends only on the class in $\NS(X)$.
That is what lets one draw the ample cone inside $\NS(X) \otimes \RR$ and use the Hodge index theorem to describe it.
:::
