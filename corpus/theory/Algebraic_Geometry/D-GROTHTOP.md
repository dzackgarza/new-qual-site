::: {.definition title="Site"}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a category.
A \dfn{sieve} $R$ on an object $U$ is a collection of morphisms with codomain $U$ that is closed under precomposition: if $V\to U$ lies in $R$ and $W\to V$ is any morphism, then the composite $W\to U$ lies in $R$.
For $f\colon V\to U$, the pullback sieve $f^*R$ consists of the morphisms $W\to V$ whose composite $W\to U$ lies in $R$.

A \dfn{Grothendieck topology} $J$ on $\mathcal C$ assigns to every object $U$ a collection $J(U)$ of \dfn{covering sieves} such that:

1. the maximal sieve, consisting of every morphism into $U$, belongs to $J(U)$;

2. if $R\in J(U)$ and $f\colon V\to U$, then $f^*R\in J(V)$;

3. if $R\in J(U)$ and $S$ is a sieve on $U$ such that $f^*S\in J(V)$ for every $f\colon V\to U$ in $R$, then $S\in J(U)$.

A \dfn{site} is a category equipped with a Grothendieck topology.
:::

::: {.definition title="Pretopology"}
Suppose now that $\mathcal C$ has fibre products.
A \dfn{Grothendieck pretopology} assigns to every object $U$ a collection of families $\{U_i\to U\}_{i\in I}$, called \dfn{covering families}, such that:

1. every isomorphism $V\to U$, as a one-element family, is a covering family;

2. if $\{U_i\to U\}_i$ is a covering family and $V\to U$ is any morphism, then $\{U_i\times_UV\to V\}_i$ is a covering family;

3. if $\{U_i\to U\}_i$ is a covering family and $\{U_{ij}\to U_i\}_j$ is a covering family for each $i$, then $\{U_{ij}\to U\}_{i,j}$ is a covering family.

It generates a Grothendieck topology by declaring a sieve on $U$ to be covering exactly when it contains the sieve generated by some covering family.
:::

::: {.definition title="Sheaf on a site"}
A \dfn{presheaf} of sets on a site $(\mathcal C,J)$ is a functor $F \colon \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathsf{Set}$.
Viewing a sieve $R$ on $U$ as a subfunctor of the representable presheaf $h_U$, the presheaf $F$ is a \dfn{sheaf} if for every covering sieve $R\in J(U)$ the restriction map
$$
F(U)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Nat}(R,F)
$$
is a bijection.
If the topology is generated by a pretopology, this is equivalent to requiring for every covering family $\{U_i\to U\}_i$ that
$$
F(U) \to \prod_i F(U_i) \rightrightarrows \prod_{i,j} F(U_i\times_UU_j)
$$
be an equalizer, where the two parallel maps are induced by the projections $U_i\times_UU_j\to U_i$ and $U_i\times_UU_j\to U_j$.
Sheaves of abelian groups and rings are defined by the same descent condition with values in those categories.
If $\mathcal O$ is a sheaf of rings on $(\mathcal C,J)$, an $\mathcal O$-module sheaf is a sheaf $\mathcal F$ of abelian groups together with compatible $\mathcal O(U)$-module structures on every $\mathcal F(U)$.
The sheaves of sets form a full subcategory $\Sh(\mathcal{C}) \subseteq \operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathsf{Set})$.
:::

::: {.definition title="Grothendieck topos"}
A \dfn{Grothendieck topos} is a category equivalent to $\Sh(\mathcal{C},J)$ for some site $(\mathcal{C},J)$.
:::

::: {.example}
For a topological space $X$, let $\operatorname{Op}(X)$ be the category of open subsets with inclusions, where $V \times_U W = V \cap W$, and let the coverings of $U$ be the families $\{U_i \subseteq U\}$ with $\bigcup_i U_i = U$.
The sheaves on this site are the sheaves on $X$ in the [[D-RCCFY|usual sense]], and $\Sh(X)$ is a topos.
The étale and fppf topologies on a category of schemes are the [[D-ETFPPF|étale and fppf sites]].
:::
