::: {.definition}
For $D$ a divisor on $X$, the sheaf $\OO_X(D)$ is the subsheaf of the constant sheaf $\mck$ given on an open $U$ by
\[
\OO_X(D)(U) = \ts{ f \in \mck(U)\units \st \div(f) + D \geq 0 } \union \ts{0} .
\]
Written from Cartier data $\ts{(U_i, f_i)}$ it is the $\OO_X$-submodule of $\mck$ generated by $f_i\inv$ on $U_i$, hence invertible.
:::

::: {.proposition}
$\OO_X(D_1 + D_2) \cong \OO_X(D_1) \tensor \OO_X(D_2)$ and $\OO_X(-D) \cong \OO_X(D)\dual$, and $\OO_X(D_1) \cong \OO_X(D_2)$ exactly when $D_1 \sim D_2$.
A divisor $D$ is effective if and only if $\OO_X(D)$ has a global section $s$ with $\div(s) = D$.
For $D$ effective the subscheme it cuts out has ideal sheaf $\mci_D \cong \OO_X(-D)$, giving
\[
0 \to \OO_X(-D) \to \OO_X \to \OO_D \to 0 .
\]
:::

::: {.remark}
This is the sheaf that converts divisor questions into cohomology questions, and the defining inequality is the thing to say out loud: a section of $\OO(D)$ is a rational function whose poles are no worse than $D$ allows.
Effective divisors linearly equivalent to $D$ are therefore the same data as nonzero global sections of $\OO(D)$ up to scaling, which is why $\abs{D} = \PP H^0(X, \OO(D))$.

The last exact sequence is the workhorse: twisting it by $\OO(nH)$ and taking cohomology computes the Hilbert polynomial of a hypersurface, and on a curve it is where $\deg$ and $\chi$ get compared.
:::
