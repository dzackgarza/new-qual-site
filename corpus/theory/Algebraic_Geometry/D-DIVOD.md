::: {.definition}
For $D$ a divisor on $X$, the sheaf $\OO_X(D)$ is the subsheaf of the constant sheaf $\mck$ given on an open $U$ by
$$
\OO_X(D)(U) = \ts{ f \in \unitsof{\mck(U)} \st \div(f) + D \geq 0 } \union \ts{0} .
$$
Written from Cartier data $\ts{(U_i, f_i)}$ it is the $\OO_X$-submodule of $\mck$ generated by $\inverseof{f_i}$ on $U_i$, hence invertible.
:::

::: {.definition title="Divisor of a section and the Riemann--Roch space"}
A nonzero global section $s$ of $\OO_X(D)$ is a rational function $f$ with $\div(f) + D \geq 0$, and its \dfn{divisor} is the effective divisor $\div(s) = \div(f) + D$, linearly equivalent to $D$.
For a line bundle $\mcl$ with a nonzero rational section $s$, $\div(s)$ is defined by the orders of $s$ in local trivializations, and $\mcl \cong \OO_X(\div(s))$.
The \dfn{Riemann--Roch space} of $D$ is $L(D) = H^0(X, \OO_X(D))$, and the complete linear system associated to a section $s \neq 0$ of $\mcl$ is $\abs{\div(s)} = \PP H^0(X, \mcl)$.
:::

::: {.proposition}
$\OO_X(D_1 + D_2) \cong \OO_X(D_1) \tensor \OO_X(D_2)$ and $\OO_X(-D) \cong \dualof{\OO_X(D)}$, and $\OO_X(D_1) \cong \OO_X(D_2)$ exactly when $D_1 \sim D_2$.
A divisor $D$ is effective if and only if $\OO_X(D)$ has a global section $s$ with $\div(s) = D$.
For $D$ effective the subscheme it cuts out has ideal sheaf $\mci_D \cong \OO_X(-D)$, giving
$$
0 \to \OO_X(-D) \to \OO_X \to \OO_D \to 0 .
$$
:::

::: {.remark}
A nonzero section of $\OO(D)$ is a rational function $f$ with $\div(f)\ge-D$.
The map $f\mapsto\div(f)+D$ is a bijection from nonzero global sections of $\OO(D)$ modulo nonzero scalars to effective divisors linearly equivalent to $D$, which gives $\abs{D} = \PP H^0(X, \OO(D))$.

For a hypersurface $D\subseteq\PP^n$ of degree $d$, twisting the exact sequence by $\OO(m)$ gives $\chi(\OO_D(m))=\binom{m+n}{n}-\binom{m-d+n}{n}$, the Hilbert polynomial of $D$.
On a nonsingular projective curve $X$ over an algebraically closed field and a closed point $p$, the sequence $0\to\OO_X(D-p)\to\OO_X(D)\to k(p)\to0$ gives $\chi(\OO_X(D))=\chi(\OO_X(D-p))+1$, hence $\chi(\OO_X(D))=\deg D+\chi(\OO_X)$.
:::
