::: {.definition title="regular sequence"}
Let $M$ be an $A$-module and $x_1,\ldots,x_r \in A$.
This is a \dfn{regular sequence for $M$} if

(i) for each $i$, $x_i$ is not a zero divisor on $M/(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1})M$, and in particular $x_1$ is not a zero divisor on $M$;

(ii) the inclusion $(x_1,\ldots,x_r)M \subsetneq M$ is proper.
:::

::: {.remark}
Order matters in general: in $k[x,y,z]$ the sequence $x,\ y(1-x),\ z(1-x)$ is regular but its reordering is not.
Over a Noetherian local ring with the $x_i$ in the maximal ideal, order does not matter, and the maximal length of a regular sequence in an ideal $I$ on $M$ is the **depth** of $M$ along $I$.

Depth is always at most the dimension, and the modules where the two agree are the **Cohen-Macaulay** ones --- which is the hypothesis under which Serre duality takes its clean form.

Operationally, a regular sequence is what makes the Koszul complex a free resolution of $A/(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$, which is how regular sequences enter cohomology computations and how a regular embedding of codimension $r$ is recognised: the ideal of $X$ in $\OO_{Y,p}$ is generated by a regular sequence of length $r$.
:::
