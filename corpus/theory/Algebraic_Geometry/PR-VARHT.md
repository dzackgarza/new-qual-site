::: {.theorem title="Hauptidealsatz"}
Let $A$ be Noetherian and $f \in A$ neither a unit nor a zero divisor.
Every minimal prime over $(f)$ has height one.
[@Har10a, Theorem I.1.11A]
:::

::: {.proposition title="Consequences used on varieties"}
For $B$ a finitely generated domain over a field and $\mfp \in \Spec B$,
\[
\height \mfp + \krulldim (B/\mfp) = \krulldim B .
\]
[@Har10a, Theorem I.1.8A]
A closed irreducible $Y \subseteq \AA^n$ has $\codim Y = 1$ exactly when $Y = V(f)$ for a single irreducible nonconstant $f \in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$.
[@Har10a, Proposition I.1.13]
:::

::: {.proposition title="Unique factorization via height-one primes"}
A Noetherian domain $A$ is a UFD if and only if every prime ideal of $A$ of height one is principal.
[@Har10a, Proposition I.1.12A]
:::

::: {.remark}
The Hauptidealsatz is the statement that one equation cuts exactly one dimension, and the codimension-one corollary is the geometric form an examiner will ask for: divisors are hypersurfaces because height-one primes are principal in $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, which is a UFD.

The additivity formula fails without finite generation over a field.
Codimension does not bound the number of equations: the ideal of the image of $t \mapsto (t^3,t^4,t^5)$ in $\AA^3$ is a prime of height two that cannot be generated by two elements ([[P-AGHNOTLCI]]).
:::
