::: {.definition title="Module of finite type"}
Let $A$ be a commutative ring.
An $A$-module $M$ is \dfn{of finite type}, or \dfn{finitely generated}, if there exist $k \geq 0$ and $b_1, \ldots, b_k \in M$ such that every $b \in M$ can be written as $b = \sum_{i=1}^{k} a_i b_i$ for some $a_1, \ldots, a_k \in A$; equivalently, if there is a surjective $A$-linear map $A^{k} \to M$.
:::

::: {.example}
By the Hilbert basis theorem ([[T-YYLPH]]), every ideal $I \subseteq \OO(\AA^n_k) = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is an $\OO(\AA^n_k)$-module of finite type: $I = (b_1, \ldots, b_k)$ for some $b_1, \ldots, b_k \in I$.
The polynomial ring $k[x]$ is a $k$-algebra generated by one element but is not a $k$-module of finite type, since $1, x, x^2, \ldots$ are linearly independent; a morphism $\Spec B \to \Spec A$ is finite ([[D-MORFIN]]) when $B$ is an $A$-module of finite type.
:::
