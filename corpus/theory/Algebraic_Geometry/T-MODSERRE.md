::: {.theorem title="Serre A and B"}
Let $X$ be projective over a noetherian ring $A$, with $\OO_X(1)$ very ample, and let $\mcf$ be [[D-QNTZY|coherent]].
Then there is $N_0$ such that for all $n \geq N_0$:

(A) $\mcf(n)$ is generated by finitely many global sections;

(B) $H^i(X, \mcf(n)) = 0$ for all $i > 0$.

[@Har10a, Theorems II.5.17 and III.5.2]
:::

::: {.remark title="Dimension shifting on projective space"}
On $\PP_A^r$, part (A) gives a surjection from a finite sum of twists to any [[D-QNTZY|coherent]] sheaf $\mathcal K_0$.
Iterating on the kernels gives exact sequences
$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal K_{j+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal E_j\longrightarrow\mathcal K_j\longrightarrow0,
$$
where $\mathcal E_j$ is a finite sum of twists and $\mathcal K_j$ is [[D-QNTZY|coherent]].
Take the first $r$ such sequences.
For sufficiently large $n$, the positive-degree cohomology of every $\mathcal E_j(n)$ in this finite list vanishes by the cohomology calculation for $\OO(d)$ [@Har10a, Theorem III.5.1].
The long exact sequences then give, for $i>0$,
$$
H^i(\PP_A^r,\mathcal K_0(n))\cong H^{i+r}(\PP_A^r,\mathcal K_r(n))=0.
$$
The last vanishing follows from the standard cover by $r+1$ affine opens: its intersections are affine, so its Cech complex computes quasi-coherent cohomology and has no terms in degrees greater than $r$ [@Har10a, Theorem III.4.5].
For $r=0$, affineness gives the vanishing directly.
Thus a finite initial part of the resolution suffices for this dimension-shifting proof, even when the resolution does not terminate.
:::

::: {.example title="A coherent sheaf with no finite resolution by twists"}
Let $k$ be a field, $A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, and $X=\PP_A^0\cong\Spec A$.
Every twist of $\OO_X$ is trivial.
The finite $A$-module $k=A/(\varepsilon)$ has the free resolution
$$
\cdots\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}A\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}A\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}A\longrightarrow k\longrightarrow0.
$$
It is exact because the kernel and image of multiplication by $\varepsilon$ are both $(\varepsilon)$.
Tensoring with $k$ gives zero differentials, so $\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(k,k)\cong k$ for every $i\ge0$.
A finite free resolution would make these groups zero in sufficiently high degrees.
Therefore the associated [[D-QNTZY|coherent]] sheaf has no finite resolution by finite sums of twists.
:::

::: {.example title="An explicit twist bound"}
Let $k$ be a field, let $m\ge0$ be an integer, and take $\mcf=\OO_{\PP_k^1}(-m)$.
For every $n\ge m$, the sheaf $\mcf(n)=\OO(n-m)$ is generated by its degree-$(n-m)$ monomials, and its positive-degree cohomology vanishes.
Indeed, $H^1(\PP_k^1,\OO(q))=0$ for $q\ge-1$, and all higher groups vanish [@Har10a, Theorem III.5.1].
Thus $N_0=m$ is a numerical bound for both (A) and (B).
There is no common vanishing bound for all these sheaves on $\PP_k^1$: for any fixed $n$, choosing $m\ge\max(0,n+2)$ gives $\dim_k H^1(\PP_k^1,\mcf(n))=m-n-1>0$.
:::

::: {.remark title="Finiteness over the base"}
By [[T-COHFIN|finiteness of coherent cohomology]], each $H^i(X,\mcf)$ is a finite $A$-module.
When $A=k$ is a field, the dimensions $h^i(X,\mcf)=\dim_k H^i(X,\mcf)$ are finite and the Euler characteristic $\chi(X,\mcf)=\sum_i(-1)^i h^i(X,\mcf)$ is defined.
For all sufficiently large $n$, part (B) gives $\chi(X,\mcf(n))=h^0(X,\mcf(n))$.
:::
