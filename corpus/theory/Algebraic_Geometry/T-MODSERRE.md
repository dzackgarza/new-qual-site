::: {.theorem title="Serre A and B"}
Let $X$ be projective over a Noetherian ring $A$, with $\OO_X(1)$ very ample, and let $\mcf$ be coherent.
Then there is $N_0$ such that for all $n \geq N_0$:

(A) $\mcf(n)$ is generated by finitely many global sections;

(B) $H^i(X, \mcf(n)) = 0$ for all $i > 0$.
:::

::: {.remark}
Both say the same thing in different currencies: twisting enough makes a coherent sheaf behave like a sheaf on an affine.
(A) says it becomes a quotient of a free sheaf, so every coherent sheaf on $\PP^n$ sits in $\bigoplus \OO(-d_i) \surjects \mcf$ after untwisting, which is the presentation the whole theory of coherent sheaves on projective space rests on.
(B) says its higher cohomology dies, so any coherent sheaf's cohomology is computed by a finite resolution by twists and descending induction — the technique behind the computation of $H^*(\PP^n, \OO(d))$ being enough for everything.

The bound $N_0$ depends on $\mcf$ and is not effective in general, so the theorems are always used in the form "for $n \gg 0$" and never with a number.
Finiteness of $\dim_A H^i(X,\mcf)$ for projective $X$ is the companion statement, and it is what makes the Euler characteristic and the Hilbert polynomial defined at all.
:::
