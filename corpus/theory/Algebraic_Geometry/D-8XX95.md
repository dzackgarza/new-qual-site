::: {.definition title="Proper"}
A morphism is \dfn{proper} if it is separated, of finite type, and universally closed [@Har77, §II.4].
:::

::: {.theorem title="Valuative criteria for valuation rings"}
Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a quasiseparated morphism.
For every valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$ and every commutative square $\Spec K \to X$, $\Spec R \to Y$ over $f$, consider the lifts $\Spec R \to X$ making both triangles commute.

- $f$ is separated if and only if every such square has at most one lift [Stacks, Lemma 26.22.1, tag 01KZ, and Lemma 26.22.2, tag 01L0; the second is the converse and uses that $f$ is quasi-separated].

- If $f$ is of finite type, $f$ is proper if and only if every such square has exactly one lift [Stacks, Lemma 29.43.1, tag 0BX5, whose printed hypotheses are that $f$ be of finite type and quasi-separated].
:::

::: {.theorem title="Valuative criteria for discrete valuation rings"}
Let $Y$ be locally Noetherian and let $f \colon X \to Y$ be of finite type.
For every discrete valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$ and every commutative square
$$
\Spec K \to X, \qquad \Spec R \to Y ,
$$
consider the lifts $\Spec R \to X$ making both triangles commute.

- $f$ is separated if and only if every such square has at most one lift [Stacks, Lemma 32.15.2, tag 0207].

- $f$ is proper if and only if every such square has exactly one lift [Stacks, Lemma 32.15.3, tag 0208].

For Noetherian schemes this is the form of the criteria in which only discrete valuation rings are tested [@Har77, Exercise II.4.11], under that exercise's standing hypothesis that all schemes involved are Noetherian; the hypothesis above is the one the printed lemmas carry, and it constrains the base rather than the source.
:::

::: {.remark}
For a discrete valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$, $\Spec R$ has one closed point and one generic point, like the germ of a smooth curve at a point, and $\Spec K$ is the punctured germ.
A lift $\Spec R\to X$ extends a map of the punctured germ across the puncture.
Uniqueness of extensions is the analogue of the Hausdorff property (limits of sequences are unique), and existence is the analogue of compactness (limits exist).
:::

::: {.example title="A closed morphism that is not universally closed"}
$\AA^1_k \to \Spec k$ is separated, of finite type, and closed, but not proper: after base change along $\AA^1_k\to\Spec k$, the projection $\AA^1 \times \AA^1 \to \AA^1$ sends the closed hyperbola $V(xy-1)$ onto $\AA^1 \sm \theset{0}$, which is not closed [@Har77, Example II.4.6.1].
:::
