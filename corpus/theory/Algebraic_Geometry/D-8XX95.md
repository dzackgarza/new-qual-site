::: {.definition title="Proper"}
A morphism is **proper** if it is separated, of finite type, and universally closed.
:::

::: {.theorem title="Valuative criteria"}
Let $f: X \to Y$ be of finite type with $X$ Noetherian.
For every discrete valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$ and every commutative square
\[
\Spec K \to X, \qquad \Spec R \to Y ,
\]
consider the lifts $\Spec R \to X$ making both triangles commute.

- $f$ is separated exactly when there is **at most one** lift, always.

- $f$ is proper exactly when there is **exactly one**.
:::

::: {.remark}
The criteria are the reason the two conditions belong together, and the slogan is worth carrying: a discrete valuation ring is an algebraic germ of a punctured disc together with its centre, so a lift is a way of filling in a limit.
Uniqueness of limits is separatedness — the Hausdorff half — and existence of limits is properness, the compactness half.

$\AA^1_k \to \Spec k$ is separated and not proper, and the witness is universal closedness rather than closedness: $\AA^1$ is closed in itself, but after base change the projection $\AA^1 \times \AA^1 \to \AA^1$ sends the closed hyperbola $V(xy-1)$ onto $\AA^1 \sm \ts{0}$, which is not closed.
This is why the definition says *universally* closed, and producing this example is the usual follow-up to the definition.
:::
