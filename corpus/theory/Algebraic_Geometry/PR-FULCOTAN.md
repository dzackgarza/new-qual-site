At a toric fixed point, Hilbert-basis monomials form a basis of the cotangent space; smoothness forces exactly $n$ of them.

::: {.definition title="Hilbert basis"}
Let $k$ be a field, let $N$ be a lattice of rank $n$, put $M\coloneqq\Hom(N,\ZZ)$, and let $\sigma\subseteq N_\RR$ be a full-dimensional strongly convex rational polyhedral cone.
Then $\sigma^\perp=0$, and the distinguished orbit of $U_\sigma$ is a torus-fixed $k$-point $x_\sigma$.
The \dfn{Hilbert basis} of the pointed affine semigroup $S_\sigma=\sigma^\vee\cap M$ is
$$
\operatorname{Hilb}(S_\sigma)
\coloneqq
\theset{u\in S_\sigma\smz\st u\ne u_1+u_2\text{ for all }u_1,u_2\in S_\sigma\smz}.
$$
It is the unique minimal generating set of $S_\sigma$ as a commutative monoid.
:::

::: {.proposition title="The cotangent space"}
The maximal ideal of $k[S_\sigma]$ at $x_\sigma$ and its square are
$$
\mfm = \generators{ \chi^u \st u \in S_\sigma \smz } , \qquad \mfm^2 = \generators{ \chi^u \st u \in (S_\sigma \smz) + (S_\sigma \smz) } ,
$$
so
$$
T^\vee_{x_\sigma}U_\sigma=\mfm/\mfm^2
=\bigoplus_{u\in\operatorname{Hilb}(S_\sigma)}k\cdot\overline{\chi^u},
$$
and hence
$$
\dim T_{x_\sigma} U_\sigma = \size\operatorname{Hilb}(S_\sigma) .
$$
:::

::: {.proposition title="Smoothness criterion"}
$U_\sigma$ is smooth at $x_\sigma$ exactly when the Hilbert basis has $n = \dim N_\RR$ elements, and then $\sigma$ is generated by a $\ZZ$-basis of $N$.
:::

::: {.proof}
Each extremal ray of $\dualof{\sigma}$ contributes its primitive generator as an irreducible element, and distinct rays contribute distinct ones.
So if $U_\sigma$ is smooth at $x_\sigma$ then
$$
\size\sigma^\vee(1) \leq \size\operatorname{Hilb}(S_\sigma) = \dim T_{x_\sigma} U_\sigma = n .
$$
A pointed full-dimensional cone in $\RR^n$ has at least $n$ rays, so $\dualof{\sigma}$ has exactly $n$ of them, and the Hilbert basis is exactly those $n$ primitive generators.
They generate $S_\sigma$, hence generate $M$ as a group, and $n$ generators of a rank-$n$ lattice form a $\ZZ$-basis.
Dualising, $\sigma$ is generated by the dual basis, which is a $\ZZ$-basis of $N$.

Conversely, if the Hilbert basis has $n$ elements, the first proposition gives
$$
\dim_k\mfm/\mfm^2=n=\dim\OO_{U_\sigma,x_\sigma}.
$$
Hence the local ring at $x_\sigma$ is regular, so $U_\sigma$ is smooth at the torus-fixed point.
:::

::: {.remark title="The quadric cone"}
For $\sigma = \Cone\big( (0,1), (2,-1) \big)$ the Hilbert basis of $S_\sigma$ is $(1,0), (1,1), (1,2)$, three elements in dimension two, so $U_\sigma$ embeds in $\AA^3$ and not in $\AA^2$: it is the quadric cone, singular at the fixed point and smooth everywhere else.
:::
