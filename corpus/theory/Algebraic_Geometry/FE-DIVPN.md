::: {.example}
$\Cl(\PP^n) \cong \ZZ$, generated by a hyperplane $H$, with the isomorphism given by degree.
The computation: if $D = V(f)$ is a hypersurface of degree $d$, then $D - dH = \div(g)$ for $g = f/x_0^d$, so $D \sim dH$.
$\Cl(\AA^n) = 0$, because $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ is a unique factorization domain.
:::

::: {.example}
For $Y \subseteq \PP^2$ an irreducible curve of degree $d$ and $X = \PP^2 \sm Y$, the excision sequence reads
\[
\ZZ \xrightarrow{\times d} \Cl(\PP^2) \cong \ZZ \to \Cl(X) \to 0 ,
\]
so $\Cl(X) \cong \ZZ/d$.
:::

::: {.remark}
These two are the entire computational toolkit for class groups of open sets.
Excision says removing a codimension-one subvariety kills its class and nothing else, and removing something of codimension at least two changes nothing; the generator of the kernel maps in with multiplicity equal to the degree of what was removed.

The second example is the standard answer to "is the class group always free": no, and a conic complement in $\PP^2$ gives $\ZZ/2$ explicitly.
The criterion behind $\Cl(\AA^n) = 0$ is worth stating in general: for $A$ a Noetherian normal domain, $A$ is a unique factorization domain exactly when $\Cl(\Spec A) = 0$, because unique factorization is the statement that every height-one prime is principal.
:::
