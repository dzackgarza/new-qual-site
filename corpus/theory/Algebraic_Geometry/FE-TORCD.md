::: {.example title="The cone"}
Take $\sigma = \Cone(e_2,\ d e_1 - e_2) \subseteq N_\RR = \RR^2$ for $d \geq 2$.
Rotating as usual, $\sigma\dual = \Cone\big( (1,0),\ (1,d) \big)$, whose lattice points are generated by
\[
(1,0), (1,1), \ldots, (1,d) \rightsquigarrow x, xy, \ldots, xy^d ,
\]
so $X_\sigma = \hat C_d$ is the affine cone over the rational normal curve of degree $d$, cut out by the $2 \times 2$ minors of
\[
\begin{bmatrix} z_0 & z_1 & \cdots & z_{d-1} \\ z_1 & z_2 & \cdots & z_d \end{bmatrix} .
\]
For $d = 2$ this is the quadric cone $V(z_0 z_2 - z_1^2)$.
:::

::: {.example title="The class group"}
The two rays give $D_1, D_2$, and the sequence
\[
M \to \Div_T(U_\sigma) = \ZZ D_1 \oplus \ZZ D_2 \to \Cl(U_\sigma) \to 0
\]
sends $m \mapsto \sum_\rho \inp{m}{u_\rho} D_\rho$.
On the basis $e_1, e_2$ of $M$ the matrix against the ray generators $u_1 = e_2$, $u_2 = d e_1 - e_2$ is
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 0 & d \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} ,
\]
that is $\operatorname{div}(\chi^{e_1}) = d D_2$ and $\operatorname{div}(\chi^{e_2}) = D_1 - D_2$.
The second relation kills $D_1$ in favour of $D_2$, and the first says $d D_2 = 0$:
\[
\Cl(\hat C_d) = \ZZ/d\ZZ .
\]
:::

::: {.remark}
$U_\sigma$ is affine, so $\Pic(U_\sigma) = 0$ while $\Cl(U_\sigma) = \ZZ/d$.
Therefore $D_2$ is a Weil divisor that is not Cartier — no case analysis needed, the two groups simply differ.
This is the standard counterexample to "Weil implies Cartier" and, at $d = 2$, the standard example of a normal surface singularity, $V(xy - z^2)$ with its two rulings.
:::
