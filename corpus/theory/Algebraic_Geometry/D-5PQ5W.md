::: {.definition title="Weil"}
Let $X$ be a noetherian integral separated scheme regular in codimension one, and let $K(X)$ be its function field.
A \dfn{prime divisor} is a codimension-one integral closed subscheme of $X$.
A \dfn{Weil divisor} is a finite formal $\ZZ$-linear combination of prime divisors; these form the group $\operatorname{Div}(X)$.
For a prime divisor $Y$ with generic point $\eta_Y$, let $v_Y$ be the normalized valuation of the DVR $\OO_{X,\eta_Y}$.
For $f\in K(X)^\times$, the \dfn{principal Weil divisor} is
$$
\operatorname{div}_X(f)\coloneqq\sum_{\operatorname{codim}(Y,X)=1}v_Y(f)Y.
$$
Only finitely many coefficients are nonzero [@Har10a, Lemma II.6.1].
The \dfn{divisor class group} $\Cl(X)$ is $\operatorname{Div}(X)$ modulo principal Weil divisors.
:::

::: {.definition title="Cartier"}
Let $X$ be a scheme, and let $\mck$ be its sheaf of total quotient rings.
Explicitly, for an open set $U$, let $S(U)\subseteq\Gamma(U,\OO_X)$ consist of sections whose germ at every point of $U$ is a non-zero-divisor; $\mck$ is the sheafification of $U\mapsto S(U)^{-1}\Gamma(U,\OO_X)$.
A \dfn{Cartier divisor} is a global section of $\mck^\times/\OO_X^\times$.
It is represented by an open cover $(U_i)$ and elements $f_i\in\mck^\times(U_i)$ such that $f_i/f_j$ is a unit on each overlap.
For integral $X$, the sheaf $\mck$ is the constant sheaf of its function field and the $f_i$ are nonzero rational functions [@Har10a, Chapter II, §6].
:::

::: {.definition title="Principal, effective, support"}
A Cartier divisor $D$ is \dfn{principal} if it is represented by a single element of $\Gamma(X,\mck^\times)$.
The \dfn{Cartier divisor class group} $\CaCl(X)$ is the group of Cartier divisors modulo principal Cartier divisors.
The divisor $D$ is \dfn{effective} if it has local equations $f_i\in\OO_X(U_i)$, necessarily non-zero-divisors in their stalks.
Its \dfn{support} is the complement of the largest open subset on which all its local equations are units [@Har10a, Chapter II, §6].
:::

::: {.proposition}
For a Cartier divisor $D$ on a scheme $X$, the sheaf $\OO_X(D)\subseteq\mck$ is locally generated by $f_i^{-1}$ when $f_i$ is a local equation of $D$.
The rule $[D]\mapsto[\OO_X(D)]$ gives an injective homomorphism $\CaCl(X)\to\Pic(X)$.
It is an isomorphism when $X$ is integral [@Har10a, Corollary II.6.14 and Proposition II.6.15].
If $D$ is effective, $\OO_X(-D)$ is the ideal sheaf locally generated by the $f_i$ [@Har10a, Proposition II.6.18].
:::

::: {.proposition title="Cartier and Weil comparison"}
Let $X$ be a noetherian integral separated [[D-QJ5M9|normal]] scheme.
Sending a Cartier divisor with local equations $f_i$ to the Weil divisor with coefficient $v_Y(f_i)$ at a prime divisor $Y$ meeting $U_i$ identifies Cartier divisors with locally principal Weil divisors.
It induces an injection $\CaCl(X)\to\Cl(X)$.
For such $X$, the support of a Cartier divisor is the union of the prime divisors with nonzero coefficient in its associated Weil divisor [@Har10a, Remark II.6.11.2 and the proof of Proposition II.6.11].
If $X$ is locally factorial, the comparison is an isomorphism, and $\Cl(X)\cong\Pic(X)$ [@Har10a, Proposition II.6.11 and Corollary II.6.16].
In particular, these conclusions apply to regular integral separated noetherian schemes, since regular local rings are factorial [@Har10a, Remark II.6.11.1A].
:::

::: {.example title="A Weil divisor that is not Cartier"}
Let $k$ be a field, put $A=k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^2)$, and let $X=\Spec A$.
This is a normal domain: the map $x\mapsto s^2$, $y\mapsto t^2$, $z\mapsto st$ identifies $A$ with the second Veronese subring of $k[s,t]$, which is normal by the graded-domain argument in [[P-AGH2514PROJNORM]].
The identification is injective because $A=k[x,y]\oplus z k[x,y]$, whose two summands map to distinct even-even and odd-odd monomials in $s,t$.
The ruling $D=V(x,z)$ is a prime divisor, and its class generates $\Cl(X)\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ$ [@Har10a, Example II.6.5.2].

At the vertex with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)$, the ideal of $D$ is $(x,z)A_{\mathfrak m}$.
Its image in $\mathfrak m A_{\mathfrak m}/\mathfrak m^2A_{\mathfrak m}$ has dimension two: the defining relation is quadratic, so the classes of $x,y,z$ are linearly independent.
A principal ideal contained in $\mathfrak m A_{\mathfrak m}$ has image of dimension at most one there.
Thus the ideal of $D$ is not principal at the vertex, and $D$ is not Cartier.
Since every nonzero divisor class is represented by $D$, the Cartier-to-Weil injection has zero image, so $\Pic(X)=0$.
:::

::: {.remark}
On a smooth integral curve over a field, the local ring at every closed point is a DVR and the generic local ring is its function field.
The curve is regular and locally factorial, so its Weil and Cartier divisor classes both identify with its Picard group.
:::
