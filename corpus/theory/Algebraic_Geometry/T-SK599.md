::: {.theorem}
$A \mapsto \Spec A$ is an anti-equivalence from rings to affine schemes.
More generally, for any scheme $X$ and any ring $A$,
$$
\Hom_{\mathsf{Sch}}(X, \Spec A) \cong \Hom_{\mathsf{Ring}}(A, \OO_X(X)) .
$$
:::

::: {.corollary title="Affine varieties"}
Over an algebraically closed field $k$, taking an affine variety $X$ to its coordinate ring $A(X)$ is an anti-equivalence from affine varieties over $k$ to finitely generated $k$-algebras that are domains [@Har10a, Corollary I.3.8].
:::

::: {.corollary}
A scheme $X$ is affine exactly when the canonical morphism $X \to \Spec \OO_X(X)$ is an isomorphism.
:::

::: {.remark}
The adjunction supplies the canonical comparison morphism
$$
X\longrightarrow\Spec\Gamma(X,\OO_X).
$$
Affineness is exactly the assertion that this morphism is an isomorphism.

For $\PP^1_k$, one has $\Gamma(\PP^1_k,\OO)=k$, so the comparison morphism lands in the point $\Spec k$ and is not an isomorphism.
For $\AA^2_k\sm\theset{0}$, normality of $k[x,y]$ and the codimension-two complement give
$$
\Gamma(\AA^2_k\sm\theset{0},\OO)=k[x,y].
$$
The comparison morphism is the open immersion $\AA^2_k\sm\theset{0}\hookrightarrow\AA^2_k$, again not an isomorphism.

Under the Noetherian or qcqs hypotheses stated in [[T-5IOUR]], Serre's criterion gives a cohomological characterization of affineness by vanishing of higher cohomology of quasicoherent sheaves.
:::
