::: {.theorem}
$A \mapsto \Spec A$ is an anti-equivalence from rings to affine schemes.
More generally, for any scheme $X$ and any ring $A$,
\[
\Hom_{\mathsf{Sch}}(X, \Spec A) \cong \Hom_{\mathsf{Ring}}(A, \OO_X(X)) .
\]
:::

::: {.corollary title="Affine varieties"}
Over an algebraically closed field $k$, $X \mapsto A(X)$ is an anti-equivalence from affine varieties over $k$ to finitely generated $k$-algebras that are domains.
[@Har10a, Corollary I.3.8]
:::

::: {.corollary}
A scheme $X$ is affine exactly when the canonical morphism $X \to \Spec \OO_X(X)$ is an isomorphism.
:::

::: {.remark}
The adjunction is the practical test, and it is why the question "how can you tell if a scheme is affine" has a first answer that needs no cohomology: compute the global sections, and compare $X$ with the spectrum of that ring.

Doing this on $\PP^1_k$ gives $\OO(\PP^1) = k$ and $\Spec k$ a point, so the comparison map collapses the line and $\PP^1$ is not affine.
Doing it on $\AA^2 \sm \ts{0}$ gives $\OO(\AA^2 \sm 0) = k[x,y]$ by Hartogs, and the comparison map is the inclusion into $\AA^2$, which is not an isomorphism: the standard example of a non-affine scheme whose ring of functions is nevertheless that of an affine.

The cohomological criterion — $H^1$ vanishing against all quasicoherent sheaves — is the version that is a *criterion*, in that it can be checked without already knowing the answer.
:::
