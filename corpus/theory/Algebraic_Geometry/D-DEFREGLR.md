::: {.definition title="regular local ring"}
A Noetherian local ring $(A,\mm)$ is a \dfn{regular local ring} if
$$
\dim A = \dim_{A/\mm} \mm/\mm^2 .
$$
A Noetherian ring $A$ is a \dfn{regular ring} if $A_\mfp$ is a regular local ring for every prime $\mfp$.
:::

::: {.definition title="valuation"}
Let $K$ be a field and $G$ a totally ordered abelian group.
A \dfn{valuation} of $K$ with values in $G$ is a group homomorphism $v \colon K^\times \to G$ such that $v(x+y) \geq \min(v(x), v(y))$ for all $x, y \in K^\times$ with $x + y \neq 0$.
Its \dfn{valuation ring} is $R = \theset{x \in K^\times \st v(x) \geq 0} \cup \theset{0}$.
A domain $R$ is a valuation ring if it is the valuation ring of some valuation of $\operatorname{Frac} R$, and the valuation is \dfn{discrete} if $G = \ZZ$.
[@Har10a, §I.6]
:::

::: {.remark}
By Krull's height theorem, $\dim A \leq \dim_{A/\mm}\mm/\mm^2$ for every Noetherian local ring, and $\dim_{A/\mm}\mm/\mm^2$ is the minimal number of generators of $\mm$.
If $A$ is regular, then $\mm$ is generated by a regular sequence, and $A$ is Cohen--Macaulay.

A regular local ring is a domain, is a UFD by the Auslander--Buchsbaum theorem, and is therefore integrally closed.
By Serre's theorem, a Noetherian local ring is regular if and only if its residue field has finite projective dimension, if and only if it has finite global dimension; consequently every localization of a regular local ring at a prime is regular.

For a Noetherian local domain of dimension one, the following are equivalent: it is regular; it is a DVR; it is integrally closed; $\mm$ is principal.
Hence on a Noetherian integral scheme regular in codimension one, the local ring at the generic point of each prime divisor $Y$ is a DVR, and its valuation $v_Y$ gives the coefficients of the principal Weil divisor $\operatorname{div}(f)$ ([[D-5PQ5W]]).
:::
