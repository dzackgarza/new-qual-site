::: {.definition title="regular local ring"}
A Noetherian local ring $(A,\mm)$ is a \dfn{regular local ring} if
\[
\dim A = \dim_{A/\mm} \mm/\mm^2 .
\]
A Noetherian ring $A$ is a **regular ring** if $A_\mfp$ is a regular local ring for every prime $\mfp$.
:::

::: {.definition title="valuation"}
Let $K$ be a field and $G$ a totally ordered abelian group.
A \dfn{valuation} of $K$ with values in $G$ is a group homomorphism $v \colon K^\times \to G$ such that $v(x+y) \geq \min(v(x), v(y))$ for all $x, y \in K^\times$ with $x + y \neq 0$.
Its **valuation ring** is $R = \ts{x \in K^\times \st v(x) \geq 0} \cup \ts{0}$.
A domain $R$ is a **valuation ring** if it is the valuation ring of some valuation of $\operatorname{Frac} R$, and the valuation is **discrete** if $G = \ZZ$.
[@Har10a, §I.6]
:::

::: {.remark}
Krull's height theorem gives $\dim A \leq \dim_{A/\mm}\mm/\mm^2$ always, so regularity is the statement that the minimal number of generators of $\mm$ is as small as the dimension allows: $\mm$ is generated by a regular sequence, and $A$ is Cohen-Macaulay.

A regular local ring is a domain, is a UFD by the Auslander-Buchsbaum theorem, and is therefore integrally closed.
By Serre's criterion, regularity is equivalent to the residue field having finite projective dimension, which is what makes it a homological condition rather than merely a numerical one.

In dimension one everything collapses to a single class: a Noetherian local domain of dimension one is regular iff it is a DVR iff it is integrally closed iff $\mm$ is principal.
This equivalence is what the "regular in codimension one" hypothesis is for --- it is exactly what makes the order of vanishing of a rational function along a prime divisor a well-defined valuation, and hence what makes Weil divisors work.
:::
