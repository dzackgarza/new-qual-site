::: {.definition title="Picard group"}
$\Pic(X)$ is the set of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves on $X$, a group under $\tensor_{\OO_X}$ with identity $\OO_X$ and inverse $\mcl\dual = \sheafhom(\mcl, \OO_X)$.
:::

::: {.proposition}
$\mcl \tensor \mcl\dual \cong \OO_X$ for $\mcl$ invertible, and $\Pic(X) \cong H^1(X, \OO_X^\times)$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The name explains the structure: an invertible sheaf is exactly an invertible object of $(\mods{\OO_X}, \tensor)$, and the dual is its inverse because the evaluation map $\mcl \tensor \mcl\dual \to \OO_X$ is an isomorphism on each trivialising open, hence an isomorphism.
Rank $1$ is what makes this work; a locally free sheaf of rank $\geq 2$ has no tensor inverse.

The cohomological description is the transition-function picture read as a Čech class, and it is how $\Pic$ is actually computed.
$\Pic(\Spec A) = 0$ for $A$ a PID or local ring, so the group measures a global obstruction and is invisible affine-locally.
$\Pic(\PP^n) = \ZZ$ generated by $\OO(1)$, and on a smooth variety $\Pic(X) \cong \Cl(X)$ by $D \mapsto \OO_X(D)$, which is the bridge into the divisor theory of [divisors](wiki/algebraic-geometry/divisors/index.html).
:::
