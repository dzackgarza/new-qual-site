::: {.definition title="Absolute Frobenius"}
Let $X$ be a scheme all of whose local rings contain $\FF_p$.
The \dfn{absolute Frobenius} $F_X : X \to X$ is the identity on the underlying topological space, with
$$
F_X^\sharp : \OO_X \to \OO_X, \qquad f \mapsto f^p .
$$
This is a ring homomorphism in characteristic $p$.
:::

::: {.definition title="Frobenius twist and relative Frobenius"}
Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p>0$, let $F_k\colon\Spec k\to\Spec k$ be absolute Frobenius, and let $X$ be a $k$-scheme. The \dfn{Frobenius twist} of $X$ is
$$
X^{(p)}\coloneqq X\times_{\Spec k,F_k}\Spec k.
$$
The \dfn{relative Frobenius} is the unique $k$-morphism
$$
F_{X/k}\colon X\longrightarrow X^{(p)}
$$
whose composite with the projection $X^{(p)}\to X$ is $F_X$.

If $X=\Spec A$, then $X^{(p)}=\Spec(A\tensor_{k,F_k}k)$ and
$$
F_{X/k}^\sharp\colon A\tensor_{k,F_k}k\longrightarrow A,
\qquad
a\tensor\lambda\longmapsto\lambda a^p.
$$
:::

::: {.proposition title="Degree on a smooth curve"}
Let $X$ be a smooth curve over a perfect field $k$ of characteristic $p>0$. Then $F_{X/k}$ is finite of degree $p$. If $K=k(X)$, its map on function fields identifies
$$
k(X^{(p)})\cong K^p\subseteq K.
$$
More generally, the $n$-fold relative Frobenius
$$
F_{X/k}^{(n)}\colon X\longrightarrow X^{(p^n)}
$$
is finite of degree $p^n$ and induces $K^{p^n}\subseteq K$.
:::

::: {.proof}
On an affine open $\Spec A\subseteq X$ with $A$ finitely generated over the perfect field $k$, the image of relative Frobenius contains $A^p$. If $A$ is generated by $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ as a $k$-algebra, then the monomials
$$
x_1^{e_1}\cdots x_r^{e_r},\qquad 0\leq e_i<p,
$$
generate $A$ as an $A^p$-module, so relative Frobenius is finite.
At the generic point, choose a separating transcendence element $t\in K$, so $K/k(t)$ is finite separable. Frobenius gives an isomorphism of field extensions
$$
K/k(t)\xrightarrow{\sim}K^p/k(t^p),
$$
and therefore $[K^p:k(t^p)]=[K:k(t)]$. Since $[k(t):k(t^p)]=p$,
$$
[K:k(t^p)]=p[K:k(t)].
$$
Dividing the two tower degrees gives $[K:K^p]=p$. Therefore $F_{X/k}$ has degree $p$; iteration gives degree $p^n$ and the inclusion $K^{p^n}\subseteq K$.
:::

::: {.remark title="Absolute and relative Frobenius"}
For a $k$-scheme with structure morphism $\pi$, one has
$$
\pi\circ F_X=F_k\circ\pi.
$$
Thus absolute Frobenius is not generally a morphism over $k$: its structural triangle commutes with $F_k\circ\pi$ rather than with $\pi$. Over $k=\FF_p$, one has $F_k=\operatorname{id}$, so absolute Frobenius is a $k$-morphism. The relative Frobenius is a $k$-morphism for every $k$ of characteristic $p$ by construction.

If $k$ is perfect, then $F_k$ is an automorphism, so the projection $X^{(p)}\to X$ is an isomorphism of abstract schemes. It need not be an isomorphism of $k$-schemes. For an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field, $j(E^{(p)})=j(E)^p$, so $E^{(p)}\cong E$ over $k$ exactly when $j(E)^p=j(E)$.

The absolute Frobenius example in [[FE-MORFROB]] and the relative Frobenius used in [[PR-IV2INSEP]] have the same local differential calculation: $d(a^p)=0$.
:::
