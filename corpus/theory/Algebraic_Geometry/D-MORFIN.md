::: {.definition title="Finite"}
$f : X \to Y$ is \dfn{finite} if $Y$ has an affine cover by $\Spec B_i$ with $f^{-1}(\Spec B_i) = \Spec A_i$ affine and $A_i$ a $B_i$-module of finite type ([[D-DEFFGMOD]]).
:::

::: {.proposition title="Maps of proper curves"}
Let $C$ and $C'$ be irreducible curves proper over a field $k$.
Every $k$-morphism $f: C \to C'$ is proper, so $f(C)$ is an irreducible closed subset of $C'$: $f$ is either constant with value a closed point or surjective.
If $C$ is nonsingular, $C'$ is integral and $f$ is surjective, then $f$ is finite and $k(C')\subseteq k(C)$ is a finite extension.
[@Har10a, Corollary II.4.8, Proposition II.6.8]
:::

::: {.remark}
A module generated by $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ is generated as an algebra by the same elements, so finite morphisms are of finite type.
The converse fails: $k[x]$ has one algebra generator over $k$ and infinite dimension as a $k$-vector space, so $\AA^1_k \to \Spec k$ is of finite type and not finite.

Finite morphisms are affine and proper, and their fibres are finite sets.
Closedness of a finite morphism follows from lying over and going up for the integral extension $B_i/\ker\to A_i$.
If $f$ is finite and flat and $Y$ is Noetherian, then $f_*\OO_X$ is locally free, and its rank at $y$ is $\dim_{\kappa(y)}\Gamma(X_y,\OO_{X_y})$.
A nonconstant morphism of nonsingular projective curves over a field is finite, and its degree is the degree of the extension of function fields.
:::
