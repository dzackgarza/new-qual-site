::: {.definition title="graded ring"}
A $\ZZ$-\dfn{graded ring} is a ring $S_\bullet = \bigoplus_{n\in\ZZ} S_n$ whose multiplication restricts to maps $S_m \times S_n \to S_{m+n}$.
Often the grading is taken to be non-negative, i.e. $S_n = 0$ for $n<0$.
Elements of $S_n$ are the **homogeneous elements of degree $n$**. $S_0$ is the **base ring**: $S_\bullet$ is an $S_0$-algebra and each $S_n$ is an $S_0$-module.
The ideal $S_+ = \bigoplus_{n\geq 1} S_n$ is the **irrelevant ideal**.

$S_\bullet$ is a **finitely generated graded ring over $S_0$** if the irrelevant ideal is finitely generated, and is **generated in degree $1$** if $S_\bullet$ is generated by $S_1$ as an $S_0$-algebra.
:::

::: {.definition title="homogeneous"}
A homogeneous element of $S_\bullet$ is one contained in some single $S_n$.
An ideal $I \subseteq S_\bullet$ is \dfn{homogeneous} if it is generated by homogeneous elements, equivalently if $I = \bigoplus_n (I \cap S_n)$.
:::

::: {.proposition title="Operations on homogeneous ideals"}
Sums, products, intersections and radicals of homogeneous ideals are homogeneous.
A homogeneous ideal $\mfa \neq S_\bullet$ is prime if and only if for all homogeneous $f, g \in S_\bullet$, $fg \in \mfa$ implies $f \in \mfa$ or $g \in \mfa$.
[@Har10a, §I.2]
:::

::: {.example title="Monomials of a fixed degree"}
In $S = k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ the degree-$d$ part $S_d$ has the monomials of total degree $d$ as a $k$-basis, and
\[
\dim_k S_d = \binom{d+n}{n} .
\]
The monomial $x_0^{e_0} \cdots x_n^{e_n}$ corresponds to the string of $e_0$ stars, a bar, $e_1$ stars, a bar, $\ldots$, a bar, $e_n$ stars: $d$ stars and $n$ bars in some order.
For three variables ($n = 2$), written $x_1, x_2, x_3$, and $d = 3$, the $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ monomials correspond to the strings:

| monomial | string | monomial | string |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x_1^3$ | $\star\star\star\mid\mid$ | $x_1x_3^2$ | $\star\mid\mid\star\star$ |
| $x_1^2x_2$ | $\star\star\mid\star\mid$ | $x_2^3$ | $\mid\star\star\star\mid$ |
| $x_1^2x_3$ | $\star\star\mid\mid\star$ | $x_2^2x_3$ | $\mid\star\star\mid\star$ |
| $x_1x_2^2$ | $\star\mid\star\star\mid$ | $x_2x_3^2$ | $\mid\star\mid\star\star$ |
| $x_1x_2x_3$ | $\star\mid\star\mid\star$ | $x_3^3$ | $\mid\mid\star\star\star$ |

With $n$ variables $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ instead, the count is $\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The motivating example is $S = k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ with its usual grading, generated in degree $1$ over $S_0 = k$.
Homogeneity is what makes the vanishing of $f$ at a point of $\PP^n$ well defined, since $f(\lambda x) = \lambda^{\deg f} f(x)$; this is the entire reason $\Proj$ takes homogeneous primes as input.

Generation in degree $1$ is not a technicality one may drop: it is what gives $\Proj S$ a closed embedding into $\PP^n_{S_0}$, and it is the hypothesis under which $\OO(1)$ is invertible and $\widetilde{\Gamma_*(\mcf)} \cong \mcf$.
The irrelevant ideal is excluded because $V(S_+)$ is the origin, which has no image in projective space, and it is also the source of the failure of $M \mapsto \tilde M$ to be injective on graded modules.
:::
