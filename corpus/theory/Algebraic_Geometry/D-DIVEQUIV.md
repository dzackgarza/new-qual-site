::: {.definition}
Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field, with intersection pairing $D \cdot C = \deg \restrictionof{\OO_X(D)}{C}$ for $C$ an irreducible curve.
Let $D_1,D_2$ be divisors on $X$.

- $D_1$ and $D_2$ are \dfn{linearly equivalent}, $D_1 \sim D_2$, when $D_1 - D_2 = \div(f)$ for a rational function $f$.

- $D_1$ and $D_2$ are \dfn{algebraically equivalent}, $D_1 \sim_{\mathrm{alg}} D_2$, when they are related by the equivalence relation generated by the following: there are a nonsingular connected curve $T$, points $t_1,t_2\in T$, and a divisor $\mathcal D$ on $X\times T$, flat over $T$, whose fibres over $t_1$ and $t_2$ are $D_1$ and $D_2$.

- $D_1$ and $D_2$ are \dfn{numerically equivalent}, $D_1 \equiv D_2$, when $(D_1 - D_2) \cdot C = 0$ for every irreducible curve $C \subseteq X$.
:::

::: {.definition}
The \dfn{Néron--Severi group} is $\NS(X) = \Div(X)/{\sim_{\mathrm{alg}}} = \Pic(X)/\Pic^0(X)$, a finitely generated abelian group by the theorem of the base; its rank is the \dfn{Picard number} $\rho(X)$.
A divisor $D$ is \dfn{nef} when $D \cdot C \geq 0$ for every irreducible curve $C$, and \dfn{big} when $\limsup_{m\to\infty} h^0(\OO(mD))/m^{\dim X}>0$.
:::

::: {.remark}
Linear equivalence implies algebraic equivalence, which implies numerical equivalence.
The group $\Pic(X)$ of linear equivalence classes can have a positive-dimensional component $\Pic^0(X)$; the quotient $\NS(X)$ is finitely generated, and the group of numerical classes is $\NS(X)$ modulo its torsion subgroup.

Ampleness and nefness depend only on the numerical class: by the Nakai--Moishezon criterion, $D$ is ample if and only if $D^{\dim V}\cdot V>0$ for every closed subvariety $V$ of positive dimension.
In $\NS(X)\otimes\RR$, the nef classes form the closure of the cone of ample classes (Kleiman), and a divisor is big if and only if some positive multiple is linearly equivalent to an ample divisor plus an effective divisor (Kodaira's lemma).

On a smooth projective curve, algebraic and numerical equivalence both mean equality of degree, so $\NS(X)=\ZZ$ by degree; linear equivalence is finer when the genus is positive, and nef means $\deg\ge0$.
:::
