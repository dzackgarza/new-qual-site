::: {.definition}
Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety, with intersection pairing $D \cdot C = \deg \restrictionof{\OO_X(D)}{C}$ for $C$ an irreducible curve.

- $D_1 \sim D_2$ (linear) when $D_1 - D_2 = \div(f)$;

- $D_1 \sim_{\mathrm{alg}} D_2$ (algebraic) when the two sit in one connected family of divisors on $X$;

- $D_1 \equiv D_2$ (numerical) when $(D_1 - D_2) \cdot C = 0$ for every curve $C \subseteq X$.
:::

::: {.definition}
$\NS(X) = \Div(X)/{\sim_{\mathrm{alg}}} = \Pic(X)/\Pic^0(X)$, a finitely generated abelian group; its rank is the Picard number $\rho(X)$.
$D$ is nef when $D \cdot C \geq 0$ for every curve $C$.
If $n=\dim X$, then $D$ is big when
$$
\limsup_{m\to\infty}
\frac{h^0(X,\OO_X(mD))}{m^n/n!}>0.
$$
:::

::: {.remark}
The implications go one way, $\sim \implies \sim_{\mathrm{alg}} \implies \equiv$; passing from $\Pic(X)$ to $\NS(X)$ quotients by $\Pic^0(X)$, and passing to numerical classes further kills torsion.
Linear equivalence classes form $\Pic$, which can be positive-dimensional; quotienting by the connected component $\Pic^0$ leaves the discrete group $\NS$, finitely generated by the theorem of the base.
Quotienting further by numerical equivalence kills only torsion.

The point of the coarser relations is that ampleness and nefness are numerical, so they are questions about the finitely generated lattice $\NS(X)$ with its intersection form.
On a smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field, algebraic and numerical equivalence are both equality of degree, so $\NS(C)\cong\ZZ$ by the degree map.
Linear equivalence is finer: two divisors of the same degree differ by a class in $\Pic^0(C)$, which need not be trivial.
For a divisor on such a curve, nef means $\deg D\geq0$, while ample and big both mean $\deg D>0$.
:::
