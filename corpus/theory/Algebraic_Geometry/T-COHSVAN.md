::: {.theorem}
Let $A$ be Noetherian, $X$ projective over $A$ with $\OO_X(1)$ very ample, and $\mcf \in \Coh(X)$.
Then there is $n_0$ such that
\[
H^i(X, \mcf(n)) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i > 0 \text{ and all } n \geq n_0 .
\]
:::

<1>1. It suffices to take $X = \PP^N_A$.

::: {.proof}
For the closed immersion $i \colon X \to \PP^N_A$ with $\OO_X(1) = i^* \OO(1)$, $i_*$ preserves coherence, $i_*(\mcf(n)) \cong (i_* \mcf)(n)$ by the projection formula, and $H^j(X, \mcf(n)) \cong H^j(\PP^N_A, (i_* \mcf)(n))$ because $i$ is affine ([[D-COHRIF]]).
:::

<1>2. For every coherent $\mcf$ on $\PP^N_A$ there are $m \in \ZZ$, $r \geq 0$ and an exact sequence $0 \to \mcg \to \OO(-m)^{\oplus r} \to \mcf \to 0$ with $\mcg$ coherent.

::: {.proof}
For $m \gg 0$, $\mcf(m)$ is generated by finitely many global sections, giving a surjection $\OO^{\oplus r} \to \mcf(m)$; twist by $\OO(-m)$. The kernel of a map of coherent sheaves on a Noetherian scheme is coherent.
:::

<1>3. For $i_0 \geq 1$ let $P(i_0)$ be the statement: for every coherent $\mcf$ there is $n_{\mcf}$ with $H^i(\PP^N_A, \mcf(n)) = 0$ for all $i \geq i_0$ and $n \geq n_{\mcf}$. Then $P(N+1)$ holds, and $P(i_0 + 1)$ implies $P(i_0)$.

::: {.proof}
$P(N+1)$ holds with every $n_{\mcf}$, because $\PP^N_A$ is covered by $N+1$ affine opens with affine intersections, so Čech cohomology of quasicoherent sheaves vanishes above degree $N$.
Assume $P(i_0 + 1)$ and take the sequence of step <1>2 for $\mcf$. Twisting by $\OO(n)$ and taking cohomology gives the exact segment
$$H^{i}(\OO(n-m))^{\oplus r} \to H^{i}(\mcf(n)) \to H^{i+1}(\mcg(n)) .$$
For $i \geq i_0 \geq 1$ and $n - m \geq -N$, the left term vanishes by [[T-IJW1K]]; for $n \geq n_{\mcg}$ the right term vanishes by $P(i_0 + 1)$. So $n_{\mcf} = \max(m - N, n_{\mcg})$ works.
:::

<1>4. Q.E.D.

::: {.proof}
Descending induction with step <1>3 gives $P(1)$, which is the theorem for $X = \PP^N_A$, and step <1>1 transfers it to $X$.
:::

::: {.remark}
Twisting enough kills everything above degree zero, and $\mcf(n)$ becomes globally generated as well.
The threshold $n_0$ depends on $\mcf$, not only on $X$: this is the hypothesis examiners probe, since no single $n$ works for all coherent sheaves at once.

Two uses are constant.
The Hilbert polynomial $\chi(\mcf(n))$ equals $h^0(\mcf(n))$ for $n \gg 0$, which is what identifies the Euler characteristic with the classical Hilbert function of a graded module.
And the converse direction is a criterion: an invertible $\mcl$ on a proper $A\dash$scheme is ample exactly when every coherent $\mcf$ has $H^{i>0}(\mcf \tensor \mcl^{\tensor n}) = 0$ for $n \gg 0$.
That is the statement to quote when asked for a cohomological characterisation of ampleness, and it is the one that makes ampleness checkable without producing an embedding.
:::
