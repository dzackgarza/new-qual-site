::: {.definition title="Star"}
Let $\Sigma$ be a fan in $N_\RR$, where $N$ is a lattice of rank $n$, and let $\tau \in \Sigma$ be a cone.
Put $N_\tau \coloneqq \ZZ\generators{\tau \intersect N}$, the sublattice generated by the lattice points of $\tau$, and
$$
N(\tau)_\RR \coloneqq N_\RR / (N_\tau)_\RR .
$$
For $\sigma \geq \tau$ write $\bar\sigma$ for the image of $\sigma$ under the quotient map.
The \dfn{star} of $\tau$ is
$$
\Star(\tau) \coloneqq \theset{ \bar\sigma \subseteq N(\tau)_\RR \st \tau \leq \sigma \in \Sigma } .
$$
:::

::: {.proposition}
$\Star(\tau)$ is a fan in $N(\tau)_\RR$, and the orbit closure of $\tau$ is its toric variety:
$$
V(\tau) \coloneqq \cl \Orb(\tau) = X_{\Star(\tau)} , \qquad \dim V(\tau) = n - \dim \tau .
$$
:::

::: {.remark title="Orbit closures"}
Every irreducible torus-invariant closed subvariety of $X_\Sigma$ is some $V(\tau)$, hence again a toric variety, one dimension lower for each dimension of $\tau$, and its own fan is obtained by quotienting out the directions inside $\tau$ and keeping only the cones above $\tau$.
An arbitrary torus-invariant closed subset is a union of orbit closures.

Basic cases:

- A ray $\rho$ gives $V(\rho) = D_\rho$, the boundary divisor, and $\Star(\rho)$ is the fan of the cones containing $\rho$, projected along $\rho$; in particular $D_\rho \cong \PP^1$ for a smooth complete surface.

- A star subdivision of a smooth $n$-dimensional cone at the ray generated by the sum of its primitive ray generators is the blowup of the corresponding smooth torus fixed point; in this case its exceptional divisor is $\PP^{n-1}$.

- A maximal cone gives a point.

The boundary $X_\Sigma \setminus T$ is the union of the divisors $D_\rho$.
For rays $\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_k$, the intersection $D_{\rho_1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{\rho_k}$ is $V(\tau)$ if $\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_k$ span a cone $\tau\in\Sigma$, and is empty otherwise; when $\Sigma$ is smooth, the boundary is a simple normal crossings divisor.
:::
