::: {.example title="The invariant ring"}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field whose characteristic does not divide $m$.
Take $\sigma = \Cone\big( m e_1 - e_2,\ e_2 \big) \subseteq N_\RR = \RR^2$ with $m \geq 2$.
Solving $\inner{u}{m e_1 - e_2} \geq 0$ and $\inner{u}{e_2} \geq 0$ gives $\dualof{\sigma} = \Cone\big( (1,0), (1,m) \big)$, whose lattice points have Hilbert basis $(1,0), (1,1), \ldots, (1,m)$, so with $x = \chi^{e_1}$ and $y = \chi^{e_2}$,
$$
A_\sigma = k[S_\sigma] = k\big[ x,\ xy,\ \ldots,\ xy^m \big] .
$$
Substituting $x = u^m$ and $y = v/u$ turns the generators into the degree-$m$ monomials:
$$
A_\sigma = k\big[ u^m,\ u^{m-1} v,\ \ldots,\ u v^{m-1},\ v^m \big] .
$$
:::

::: {.example title="The group"}
Let $\mu_m$ act diagonally on $\AA^2$ by
$$
\zeta \cdot (u,v) = (\zeta u,\ \zeta v), \qquad \zeta^m = 1 .
$$
A monomial $u^i v^j$ is invariant exactly when $i + j \equiv 0 \pmod m$, so the invariant ring is generated by the degree-$m$ monomials, and
$$
A_\sigma = k[u,v]^{\mu_m} , \qquad U_\sigma \cong \AA^2 / \mu_m .
$$
In the standard notation this is the cyclic quotient singularity $\tfrac{1}{m}(1,1)$.
:::

::: {.remark title="The lattice reading of the same fact"}
The two generators of $\sigma$ span a sublattice of index
$$
\abs{\det \begin{bmatrix} m & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}} = m
$$
inside $N$, and that index is the order of the group.
More generally, if a full-dimensional simplicial cone has primitive ray generators spanning a sublattice $N'\subseteq N$ of index $d$, then its affine toric variety is a quotient of $\AA^n$ by the finite diagonalizable group
$$
G=\Hom(N/N',\GG_m),
$$
whose order is $d$ when the characteristic does not divide $d$.
Thus the same determinant that detects non-smoothness gives the order of the finite quotient group.

For $m = 2$ this is the quadric cone $V(z_0 z_2 - z_1^2) = \AA^2/\pm 1$, the $A_1$ surface singularity, and the same variety appears as the singular chart of $\PP(1,1,2)$.
The quotient description extends to the cyclic quotient singularities $\tfrac{1}{n}(1,q)$ and to higher dimension, whose coordinate rings are in general not cut out by the $2 \times 2$ minors of one matrix.
:::
