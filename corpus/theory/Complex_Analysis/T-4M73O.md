::: {.theorem}
Let $E\subseteq\CC$ be open and let $f,g\in C^1(E)$ be real-valued.
Let $\Omega$ be a closed subset of $E$ with positively oriented boundary $\bd\Omega$ in the sense of [@Rud76, sec. 10.31]: $\Omega$ is a finite union of sets $E_1,\ldots,E_r$ with pairwise disjoint interiors, each the image of the standard simplex under a one-to-one $C^2$ map whose Jacobian is positive at least in the interior, and $\bd\Omega=\bd E_1+\cdots+\bd E_r$ is the sum of their boundaries, each oriented positively.
Then
$$
\int_{\bd\Omega}f\dx+g\dy=\iint_\Omega\Big(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\Big)\dA.
$$
In vector form, for the vector field $F=(f,g)$ with scalar curl $\curl F=g_x-f_y$,
$$
\int_{\bd\Omega}F\cdot\dr=\iint_\Omega\curl F\dA.
$$

This is [@Rud76, Theorem 10.45].
:::

::: {.remark}
The classical statement for a bounded open set whose boundary is a finite union of piecewise smooth closed curves, oriented with the set on the left, is obtained by decomposing the set into finitely many pieces $E_i$ of the kind above and applying the theorem to each; the hypotheses on $f$ and $g$ are then that they extend to $C^1$ functions on a neighborhood of the closure.
For a triangle $T$ the identity is [@SS03, ch. 2, Exercise 5], the case used in the proof of Goursat's theorem there.
:::

::: {.example}
Taking $(f,g)=(0,x)$, $(-y,0)$, and $\big(-\frac y2,\frac x2\big)$, each with $g_x-f_y=1$, gives the area of $\Omega$:
$$
\mu(\Omega)=\oint_{\bd\Omega}x\dy=-\oint_{\bd\Omega}y\dx=\frac12\oint_{\bd\Omega}(x\dy-y\dx).
$$
:::

::: {.remark}
For $f$ [[D-E7A5W|holomorphic]] and injective on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega$, the real Jacobian determinant of $f$ is $\abs{f'(z)}^2$, so the area of the image is $\mu(f(\Omega))=\iint_\Omega\abs{f'(z)}^2\dA$.
:::
