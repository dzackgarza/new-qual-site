::: {.lemma}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be connected Riemann surfaces with $X$ compact, and let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a nonconstant holomorphic map.
Then

- $f$ is surjective,

- $Y$ is compact,

- $\inverseof{f}(q)$ is finite for every $q\in Y$, and

- the set of points at which $f$ is not locally injective, and its image in $Y$, are finite.

Surjectivity is the statement, in the proper-maps paragraph of §2 of C. McMullen, *Advanced Complex Analysis*, that a holomorphic map from a compact Riemann surface is proper and a nonconstant proper map is surjective; the same paragraph gives the degree as the constant number of preimages with multiplicity. The compactness of $Y$ and the finiteness of the fibres are compactness of the image and discreteness of a fibre in the compact $X$; in a local coordinate the points at which $f$ is not locally injective are the zeros of the derivative, which are isolated, hence finite in the compact $X$, and their image is finite.
:::
