::: {.fact}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring.
The [[D-2IO6Q|Jacobson radical]] of $R$ is the intersection of the annihilators of the [[D-4KM4P|simple]] $R$-modules:
$$
J(R)=\bigcap_{\mfm\in\mspec R}\mfm=\bigcap_{M\text{ simple}}\Ann_R(M).
$$
If $R$ is a finitely generated algebra over a field $k$, then $J(R)$ equals the [[D-C2IM4|nilradical]] $\nilrad{R}$.
:::

::: {.proof}
A simple module $M$ is generated by any nonzero $m\in M$, so $M\cong R/\Ann_R(m)$, and simplicity makes $\Ann_R(m)$ a maximal ideal $\mfm$; then $\Ann_R(M)=\mfm$.
Conversely $R/\mfm$ is simple with annihilator $\mfm$ for every maximal ideal $\mfm$.
So the two intersections run over the same ideals.

For a finitely generated $k$-algebra, every prime ideal is an intersection of maximal ideals by the Nullstellensatz, so the intersection of all maximal ideals equals the intersection of all prime ideals, which is $\nilrad{R}$.
:::

::: {.remark}
Write $R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I$.
Then $J(R)=\nilrad{R}=\sqrt I/I$, so $J(R)=0$ exactly when $I$ is a radical ideal; for $k$ algebraically closed, these are the ideals $I(Z)$ of affine algebraic sets $Z\subseteq\AA^n_k$, whose coordinate rings $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I(Z)$ are the rings of regular functions on $Z$.
:::
