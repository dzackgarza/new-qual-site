::: {.theorem}
Let $\phi\colon G\to H$ be a group homomorphism.
Then $\ker \phi$ is a [[D-EKE4Q|normal subgroup]] of $G$, $\im \phi$ is a subgroup of $H$, and
$$
G/\ker \phi \to \im \phi,\qquad g\ker\phi\mapsto\phi(g),
$$
is a well-defined group isomorphism.
:::

::: {.proof}
For $x\in\ker\phi$ and $g\in G$, $\phi(gxg^{-1})=\phi(g)\phi(g)^{-1}=e$, so $\ker\phi\normal G$; $\im\phi$ is the image of a group under a homomorphism, hence a subgroup of $H$.
The map is well defined and injective because $\phi(g)=\phi(g')$ if and only if $g^{-1}g'\in\ker\phi$, it is surjective by construction, and it is a homomorphism because $\phi$ is.
:::
