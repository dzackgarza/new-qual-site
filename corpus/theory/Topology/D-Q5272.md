::: {.definition}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces and $f\colon X\to Y$ a map.
The map $f$ is a \dfn{closed map} if $f(A)$ is closed in $Y$ for every closed $A\subseteq X$, and an \dfn{open map} if $f(U)$ is open in $Y$ for every open $U\subseteq X$ [@Mun00, sec. 22].
:::

::: {.remark}
Neither condition implies or is implied by [[D-AEAAD|continuity]], and neither implies the other.
:::

::: {.example}
The projection $\pi_1\colon\RR\times\RR\to\RR$, $(x,y)\mapsto x$, is continuous and open but not closed: the hyperbola $\ts{(x,y)\st xy=1}$ is closed in $\RR\times\RR$, and its image $\RR\setminus\ts{0}$ is not closed in $\RR$ [@Mun00, sec. 22].
:::

::: {.proposition}
Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a continuous map of topological spaces.

(a) If $f$ is surjective and open or closed, then $f$ is a [[D-ITBUT|quotient map]]; a quotient map need not be open or closed [@Mun00, sec. 22].

(b) If $X$ is [[D-EILKJ|compact]] and $Y$ is [[D-6FMP3|Hausdorff]], then $f$ is closed [@Mun00, sec. 26, Exercise 6].

(c) If $X$ is compact, $Y$ is Hausdorff, and $f$ is bijective, then $f$ is a [[D-9KQZT|homeomorphism]] [@Mun00, Theorem 26.6].
:::
