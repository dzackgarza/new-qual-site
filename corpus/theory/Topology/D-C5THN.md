::: {.definition}
Let $(X_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of topological spaces, let $X \coloneqq \prod_{i\in I} X_i$, and for $j\in I$ let $p_j\colon X\to X_j$ be the projection.
The \dfn{box topology} on $X$ is the topology [[D-WKURJ|generated]] by the basis
$$
\mathcal B_{\Box} \coloneqq \ts{ \prod_{i\in I} U_i \st U_i \text{ open in } X_i \text{ for every } i }.
$$
The \dfn{product topology} on $X$ is the topology generated by the basis
$$
\mathcal B_{\prod} \coloneqq \ts{ \prod_{i\in I} U_i \st U_i \text{ open in } X_i \text{ for every } i, \text{ and } U_i = X_i \text{ for all but finitely many } i }.
$$
:::

::: {.proposition}
The product topology on $X$ is the [[D-KW52R|coarsest]] topology on $X$ for which every projection $p_j$ is [[D-AEAAD|continuous]].
It is the topology generated by the subbasis $\ts{ p_j\inv(U) \st j\in I,\ U \text{ open in } X_j }$.
:::

::: {.proof}
For $U$ open in $X_j$, the set $p_j\inv(U)$ is the product with $j$th factor $U$ and every other factor $X_i$, which lies in $\mathcal B_{\prod}$; so each $p_j$ is continuous for the product topology.
Conversely, a topology on $X$ for which every $p_j$ is continuous contains every set $p_j\inv(U)$ and hence every finite intersection $p_{j_1}\inv(U_1)\cap\cdots\cap p_{j_k}\inv(U_k)$.
These finite intersections are exactly the elements of $\mathcal B_{\prod}$, so such a topology contains the product topology.
:::

::: {.proposition}
Give $X$ the product topology.
For every topological space $Y$ and every family of continuous maps $f_i\colon Y\to X_i$, there is a unique continuous map $f\colon Y\to X$ with $p_i\circ f = f_i$ for every $i\in I$.
Hence $(X, (p_i)_{i\in I})$ is the product of the $X_i$ in $\Top$:

\begin{tikzcd}
	& Y \\
	\\
	& {\prod_{k\in I} X_k} \\
	{X_i} && {X_j}
	\arrow["{\exists ! f}"{description}, dashed, from=1-2, to=3-2]
	\arrow["{f_i}"', curve={height=18pt}, from=1-2, to=4-1]
	\arrow["{f_j}", curve={height=-18pt}, from=1-2, to=4-3]
	\arrow["{p_i}", from=3-2, to=4-1]
	\arrow["{p_j}"', from=3-2, to=4-3]
\end{tikzcd}
:::

::: {.proof}
The conditions $p_i\circ f = f_i$ force $f(y) = (f_i(y))_{i\in I}$, so $f$ is unique as a function.
For $U$ open in $X_j$, $f\inv(p_j\inv(U)) = f_j\inv(U)$ is open in $Y$.
Preimages commute with unions and finite intersections, so $f\inv$ of every open set of the product topology is open, and $f$ is continuous.
:::

::: {.remark}
Every element of $\mathcal B_{\prod}$ lies in $\mathcal B_{\Box}$, so the box topology is [[D-KW52R|finer]] than the product topology.
The two topologies coincide when $I$ is finite.
:::

::: {.example}
For the box topology, the universal property of the product fails.
Let $X = \prod_{n\geq 1}\RR$ and let $\Delta\colon\RR\to X$, $\Delta(t) = (t,t,\ldots)$, whose components are all $\id_\RR$.
Then $\Delta$ is continuous for the product topology, but not for the box topology: $\prod_{n\geq1}(-1/n, 1/n)$ is open in the box topology and $\Delta\inv\qty{\prod_{n\geq1}(-1/n,1/n)} = \ts{0}$ is not open in $\RR$.
:::
