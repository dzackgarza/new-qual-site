::: {.definition}
A topological space $X$, not necessarily Hausdorff, is \dfn{quasicompact} if every open [[D-AOJG3|cover]] of $X$ has a finite subcover.
A quasicompact space is \dfn{compact} if it is also [[D-6FMP3|Hausdorff]].
:::

::: {.remark}
Requiring compact spaces to be Hausdorff is the convention of Bourbaki and of algebraic geometry, where the Zariski topology on the [[D-CXXCG|spectrum]] $\Spec A$ of a commutative ring $A$ is always quasicompact but is Hausdorff only in degenerate cases.
Many topology texts call a space compact under the open-cover condition alone, as in [[D-EILKJ]].
:::
