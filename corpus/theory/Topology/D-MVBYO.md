::: {.definition}
Let $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$ be topologies on a set $X$.
The topology $\tau_1$ is \dfn{finer} (also \dfn{stronger} or \dfn{larger}) than $\tau_2$ if $\tau_2\subseteq\tau_1$, and \dfn{coarser} (also \dfn{weaker} or \dfn{smaller}) than $\tau_2$ if $\tau_1\subseteq\tau_2$.
The topologies $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$ are \dfn{comparable} if $\tau_1\subseteq\tau_2$ or $\tau_2\subseteq\tau_1$.
:::

::: {.proposition}
Let $X$ be a set and let $\mathrm{Top}(X)$ be the set of topologies on $X$, partially ordered by inclusion.
Then $\mathrm{Top}(X)$ is a complete lattice [@Mun00]:

- the infimum of a nonempty family $(\tau_i)_{i\in I}$ is the intersection $\bigcap_{i\in I}\tau_i$;

- the supremum of $(\tau_i)_{i\in I}$ is the topology generated by $\bigcup_{i\in I}\tau_i$, the intersection of all topologies containing every $\tau_i$;

- the greatest element is the discrete topology $\mathcal P(X)$ and the least element is the indiscrete topology $\ts{\emptyset,X}$.
:::

::: {.remark}
The union of two topologies need not be a topology.
On $X=\ts{a,b,c}$, the union of $\ts{\emptyset,\ts{a},X}$ and $\ts{\emptyset,\ts{b},X}$ contains $\ts{a}$ and $\ts{b}$ but not $\ts{a,b}$, so the supremum is in general strictly larger than the union.
:::

::: {.proposition}
Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a map of sets, let $\tau_1\subseteq\tau_2$ be topologies on $X$, and let $\sigma_1\subseteq\sigma_2$ be topologies on $Y$.

(a) If $f\colon(X,\tau_1)\to(Y,\sigma_2)$ is [[D-AEAAD|continuous]], then $f\colon(X,\tau_2)\to(Y,\sigma_1)$ is continuous.

(b) If $f\colon(X,\tau_2)\to(Y,\sigma_1)$ is an [[D-CTGON|open map]], then $f\colon(X,\tau_1)\to(Y,\sigma_2)$ is an open map.

(c) If a sequence $(x_k)$ converges to $x$ in $(X,\tau_2)$, then it converges to $x$ in $(X,\tau_1)$.
:::

::: {.example}
For $n\geq1$, the Zariski topology $\tau_{\zar}$ on $\CC^n$, whose closed sets are the common zero sets of families of polynomials, is strictly coarser than the Euclidean topology $\tau_{\an}$.
Zero sets of polynomials are closed in $\tau_{\an}$ because polynomials are continuous, so $\tau_{\zar}\subseteq\tau_{\an}$.
The open unit ball is in $\tau_{\an}$ but not in $\tau_{\zar}$: every nonempty Zariski-open subset of $\CC^n$ contains the complement of the zero set of a nonzero polynomial, which is dense in $\tau_{\an}$ and hence not contained in the unit ball.
:::
