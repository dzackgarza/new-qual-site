::: problem
Let
$$
M = \{(w, x, y, z) \in \ZZ^4 \suchthat w + x + y + z \in 2\ZZ\}
,$$

and

$$
N = \{(w, x, y, z) \in \ZZ^4 \suchthat 4\divides (w - x),~ 4\divides (x - y),~ 4\divides ( y - z)\}
.$$

(a) Show that $N$ is a $\ZZ\dash$submodule of $M$ .

(b) Find vectors $u_1 , u_2 , u_3 , u_4 \in \ZZ^4$ and integers $d_1 , d_2 , d_3 , d_4$ such that
    $$
    \{u_1 , u_2 , u_3 , u_4 \}
    $$
    is a free basis for $M$, and
    $$
    \{d_1 u_1,~ d_2 u_2,~ d_3 u_3,~ d_4 u_4 \}
    $$
    is a free basis for $N$ .

(c) Use the previous part to describe $M/N$ as a direct sum of cyclic $\ZZ\dash$modules.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution** (Gemini 3.7 Flash)

**(a) $N$ is a $\ZZ$-submodule of $M$:**
- Both $M$ and $N$ are subgroups of the free abelian group $\ZZ^4$.
- $M$ is the kernel of the group homomorphism $\phi: \ZZ^4 \to \ZZ_2$ given by $(w,x,y,z) \mapsto (w+x+y+z) \bmod 2$.
- $N$ consists of vectors where $w \equiv x \equiv y \equiv z \pmod 4$.
  If $(w,x,y,z) \in N$, then $w = x + 4a, y = x + 4b, z = x + 4c$ for some $a, b, c \in \ZZ$.
  Then $w + x + y + z = 4x + 4(a+b+c) = 4(x+a+b+c) \in 4\ZZ \subseteq 2\ZZ$.
  Thus $(w, x, y, z) \in M$, which shows $N \subseteq M$.
- Since $N$ is the kernel of the homomorphism $\psi: \ZZ^4 \to (\ZZ_4)^3$ defined by $(w-x, x-y, y-z) \bmod 4$, $N$ is a subgroup (i.e. $\ZZ$-submodule) of $\ZZ^4$, and hence of $M$.

**(b) Smith Normal Form basis:**

1. **Basis for $M$:**
   $M$ is generated by vectors with even coordinate sum. A convenient basis for $M$ is:
   - $u_1 = (1, 1, 1, 1)$ (sum = 4)
   - $u_2 = (1, -1, 0, 0)$ (sum = 0)
   - $u_3 = (0, 1, -1, 0)$ (sum = 0)
   - $u_4 = (0, 0, 1, -1)$ (sum = 0)
   
   To verify $\{u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4\}$ is a basis of $M$:
   Any $(w,x,y,z) \in M$ has $w+x+y+z = 2k$.
   Then $(w,x,y,z) = w(1,0,0,-1) + x(0,1,0,-1) + y(0,0,1,-1) + (w+x+y+z)(0,0,0,1)$, and since $w+x+y+z=2k$, this is spanned over $\ZZ$ by differences of standard basis elements together with $(1,1,1,1)$ and $(2,0,0,0)$.
   The determinant of the matrix with columns $u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4$ is $1 \cdot 1 - (-1) \cdot (-1) \cdot (-1) = 4$, which equals the index $[\ZZ^4 : M] = 2$ times 2.

2. **Expressing $N$ in this basis:**
   Any $v \in N$ has $w \equiv x \equiv y \equiv z \pmod 4$.
   Let $w = c + 4a_1, x = c + 4a_2, y = c + 4a_3, z = c + 4a_4$.
   Then:
   $$
   v = c(1,1,1,1) + 4a_1 e_1 + 4a_2 e_2 + 4a_3 e_3 + 4a_4 e_4.
   $$
   Since $4e_1 = (1,1,1,1) + 3u_2 + 2u_3 + u_4 - (0,0,0,0)$, in terms of $u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4$:
   - $N$ contains $u_1 = (1,1,1,1)$ (since all coordinates are equal, differences are $0 \equiv 0 \pmod 4$).
   - For differences, $v \in N$ has $w-x \in 4\ZZ, x-y \in 4\ZZ, y-z \in 4\ZZ$.
   - Thus the generators along $u_2, u_3, u_4$ are $4u_2, 4u_3, 4u_4$.

   Therefore, a free basis for $N$ aligned with $\{u_1, u_2, u_3, u_4\}$ is:
   $$
   \{1 \cdot u_1, \ 4 \cdot u_2, \ 4 \cdot u_3, \ 4 \cdot u_4\}.
   $$
   Thus $d_1 = 1, \ d_2 = 4, \ d_3 = 4, \ d_4 = 4$.

**(c) Quotient module $M/N$:**
From the aligned bases:
$$
M / N \cong \frac{\ZZ u_1 \oplus \ZZ u_2 \oplus \ZZ u_3 \oplus \ZZ u_4}{\ZZ (1 u_1) \oplus \ZZ (4 u_2) \oplus \ZZ (4 u_3) \oplus \ZZ (4 u_4)}
$$
$$
\cong (\ZZ / 1\ZZ) \oplus (\ZZ / 4\ZZ) \oplus (\ZZ / 4\ZZ) \oplus (\ZZ / 4\ZZ) \cong \ZZ_4 \oplus \ZZ_4 \oplus \ZZ_4.
$$
