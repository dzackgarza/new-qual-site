::: problem
Let $R=(R,+,\cdot)$ be an integral domain.
Let $C$ be the additive group of $R$ generated by the identity, that is, let $C$ be the smallest subgroup of $R$ such that $C$ contains $0$ and $1$ and $C$ is closed under $+$.

(a) Show that $C=\{n\cdot 1 : n\in\mathbb{Z}\}$ and hence is a subring of $R$.
Here if $n\ge 0$, then we can define $n\cdot 1$ by induction as $0\cdot 1=0$, $1\cdot 1=1$ and $(n+1)\cdot 1=1+(n\cdot 1)$, and if $n<0$, we define $n\cdot 1=-(|n|\cdot 1)$.

(b) Show that $\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}\to C$ defined by $\phi(n)=n\cdot 1$ is a surjective ring homomorphism.

(c) Prove that either $C$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$ or $C$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$ for some prime $p$.
:::
