::: problem
(a) Give an example of two finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-modules $M$ and $N$ such that $M$, $N$ are not isomorphic (as $\mathbb{Z}$-modules) but $\mathbb{Q} \otimes_\mathbb{Z} M \cong \mathbb{Q} \otimes_\mathbb{Z} N$ (as $\mathbb{Q}$-modules).

(b) Let $M$ be a finitely generated $\mathbb{R}$-module, described using the classification of finitely generated modules over a PID. Give a similar description of $\mathbb{C}[x_1, \ldots, x_p] \otimes_\mathbb{R} M$ as a $\mathbb{C}[x_1, \ldots, x_p]$-module.

(c) Show that if $M$ and $N$ are two finitely generated $\mathbb{R}$-modules, and $\mathbb{C}[x_1, \ldots, x_n] \otimes_\mathbb{R} M \cong \mathbb{C}[x_1, \ldots, x_n] \otimes_\mathbb{R} N$ (as $\mathbb{C}[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$-modules), then $M \cong N$ (as $\mathbb{R}$-modules).
:::
