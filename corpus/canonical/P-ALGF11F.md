::: problem
(a) Suppose that $K \subseteq L \subseteq M$ are fields such that $L/K$ and $M/L$ are Galois, and that every automorphism of $L/K$ extends to an automorphism of $M$. Prove that $M/K$ is Galois.

(b) Give an example of fields $K \subseteq L \subseteq M$ such that $L/K$ and $M/L$ are Galois, but $M/K$ is not Galois.
:::
