::: {.problem}
Define $M$ as the group ring $R = \ZZ[\ZZ_2]$ with the action $(\cdot) \times -1$.
Construct a free resolution of $M$ and compute $\tor_R^*(M, M)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution** (Gemini 3.7 Flash)

**Goal:** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}_2 = \{1, \sigma\}$ with $\sigma^2 = 1$, and let $R = \mathbb{Z}[G] \cong \mathbb{Z}[\sigma] / (\sigma^2 - 1)$ be the group ring.
Let $M$ be the rank-1 $\mathbb{Z}$-module $\mathbb{Z}$ on which $\sigma$ acts by $-1$ (the sign module, i.e., $R$-module with $\sigma \cdot m = -m$). Construct a free resolution of $M$ over $R$ and compute the Tor groups $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M, M)$ for all $n \ge 0$.

<1>1. Construct a free resolution of $M$ over $R$.
<2>1. Elements in $R$ are of the form $a + b\sigma$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$.
<2>2. The augmentation-type map $\epsilon_- \colon R \to M \cong \mathbb{Z}$ given by $\epsilon_-(a + b\sigma) = a - b$ is an $R$-module homomorphism onto $M$: $\epsilon_-(\sigma(a + b\sigma)) = \epsilon_-(b + a\sigma) = b - a = -(a - b) = \sigma \cdot \epsilon_-(a + b\sigma)$.
<2>3. The kernel of $\epsilon_-$ is generated by $\sigma + 1$: $a - b = 0 \iff a = b \iff a + b\sigma = a(1 + \sigma)$.
<2>4. The annihilator / kernel of multiplication by $\sigma + 1$ on $R$: $(a + b\sigma)(1 + \sigma) = (a+b) + (a+b)\sigma = 0 \iff a + b = 0 \iff a + b\sigma = a(1 - \sigma)$.
<2>5. The kernel of multiplication by $\sigma - 1$ on $R$: $(a + b\sigma)(\sigma - 1) = (b-a) + (a-b)\sigma = 0 \iff a = b \iff a + b\sigma = a(1 + \sigma)$.
<2>6. Thus, a free resolution of $M$ over $R$ is given by the 2-periodic resolution: $$\cdots \xrightarrow{\sigma - 1} R \xrightarrow{\sigma + 1} R \xrightarrow{\sigma - 1} R \xrightarrow{\sigma + 1} R \xrightarrow{\epsilon_-} M \to 0.$$ Let $F_\bullet$ be the deleted free resolution: $$F_\bullet \colon \cdots \xrightarrow{d_3 = \sigma - 1} F_2 = R \xrightarrow{d_2 = \sigma + 1} F_1 = R \xrightarrow{d_1 = \sigma - 1} F_0 = R \to 0.$$ <2>7. Proof: By exactness at each step $n \ge 1$: $\ker(d_{2k}) = \operatorname{im}(d_{2k+1}) = (\sigma - 1)R$ and $\ker(d_{2k-1}) = \operatorname{im}(d_{2k}) = (\sigma + 1)R$.
Q.E.D.

<1>2. Tensor the resolution $F_\bullet$ with $M$ over $R$.
<2>1. For each $n \ge 0$, $F_n \otimes_R M = R \otimes_R M \cong M \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
<2>2. Under the isomorphism $R \otimes_R M \cong M$ sending $(a + b\sigma) \otimes m \mapsto (a - b)m$:

- The differential $d_{2k} = \sigma + 1$ induces: $$(d_{2k} \otimes \operatorname{id}_M)(1 \otimes m) = (\sigma + 1) \otimes m = 1 \otimes (\sigma \cdot m) + 1 \otimes m = 1 \otimes (-m + m) = 0.$$ Thus $d_{2k} \otimes \operatorname{id}_M \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is the zero map: $\times 0$.

- The differential $d_{2k-1} = \sigma - 1$ induces: $$(d_{2k-1} \otimes \operatorname{id}_M)(1 \otimes m) = (\sigma - 1) \otimes m = 1 \otimes (\sigma \cdot m) - 1 \otimes m = 1 \otimes (-2m) = -2m.$$ Thus $d_{2k-1} \otimes \operatorname{id}_M \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is multiplication by $-2$ (isomorphic to $\times 2$). <2>3. The complex $F_\bullet \otimes_R M$ is: $$\cdots \xrightarrow{-2} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{0} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{-2} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{0} \mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$ <2>4. Proof: Direct calculation of the induced differentials on the tensor product.
  Q.E.D.

<1>3. Compute homology of the complex $F_\bullet \otimes_R M$ to obtain $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M, M)$.
<2>1. For $n = 0$: $$\operatorname{Tor}_0^R(M, M) \cong \ker(0) / \operatorname{im}(d_1 \otimes \operatorname{id}) = \mathbb{Z} / (-2\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z} / 2\mathbb{Z}.$$ <2>2. For $n$ odd ($n = 2k - 1 \ge 1$): $$\operatorname{Tor}_{2k-1}^R(M, M) = \ker(d_{2k-1} \otimes \operatorname{id}) / \operatorname{im}(d_{2k} \otimes \operatorname{id}) = \ker(\times -2) / \operatorname{im}(\times 0) = 0 / 0 = 0.$$ <2>3. For $n$ even ($n = 2k \ge 2$): $$\operatorname{Tor}_{2k}^R(M, M) = \ker(d_{2k} \otimes \operatorname{id}) / \operatorname{im}(d_{2k+1} \otimes \operatorname{id}) = \ker(\times 0) / \operatorname{im}(\times -2) = \mathbb{Z} / 2\mathbb{Z}.$$ <2>4. Proof: By taking kernel mod image of the complex.
Q.E.D.

<1>4. Conclusion.
<2>1. The Tor groups are: $$\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M, M) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} & \text{if } n \text{ is even } (n \ge 0), \\ 0 & \text{if } n \text{ is odd } (n \ge 1). \end{cases}$$ <2>2. Proof: By <1>1–<1>3. Q.E.D.
