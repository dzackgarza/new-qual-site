::: {.problem}
Let $X = S^n\cross I$ with its ends glued together by a map $S^n \circlearrowleft$ of degree $d$, calculate $H_*(X)$.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution** (Gemini 3.7 Flash)

**Goal:** Let $f \colon S^n \to S^n$ be a continuous map of degree $d$, and let $X = M_f = (S^n \times [0, 1]) / ((x, 1) \sim (f(x), 0))$ be the mapping torus of $f$. Calculate the homology groups $H_k(X; \mathbb{Z})$ for all $k \ge 0$.

<1>1. Case $n \geq 2$.
  <2>1. Decompose $X$ into two open subsets using the interval coordinate $t \in S^1 = [0, 1] / (0 \sim 1)$:
  Let $U = S^n \times (0, 1)$ and $V = S^n \times ((-1/2, 1/2) \bmod 1)$.
  Then $U \simeq S^n$, $V \simeq S^n$, and $U \cap V = (S^n \times (0, 1/2)) \sqcup (S^n \times (1/2, 1)) \simeq S^n \sqcup S^n$.
  <2>2. Write the Mayer-Vietoris sequence in reduced homology for $(X; U, V)$:
  $$\cdots \to \widetilde{H}_k(U \cap V) \xrightarrow{\Phi_k} \widetilde{H}_k(U) \oplus \widetilde{H}_k(V) \to \widetilde{H}_k(X) \xrightarrow{\partial} \widetilde{H}_{k-1}(U \cap V) \to \cdots$$
  <2>3. Under the identification $\widetilde{H}_k(U \cap V) \cong \widetilde{H}_k(S^n) \oplus \widetilde{H}_k(S^n)$, the map $\Phi_k \colon \widetilde{H}_k(S^n) \oplus \widetilde{H}_k(S^n) \to \widetilde{H}_k(S^n) \oplus \widetilde{H}_k(S^n)$ is given by the matrix:
  $$\Phi_k = \begin{pmatrix} \operatorname{id}_* & -f_* \\ \operatorname{id}_* & -\operatorname{id}_* \end{pmatrix}.$$
  Row reducing the matrix gives an isomorphic map $\Psi_k \colon \widetilde{H}_k(S^n) \to \widetilde{H}_k(S^n)$ given by $\operatorname{id}_* - f_*$.
  <2>4. Compute $H_k(X)$ for each dimension $k$:
  - For $k = 0$: $X$ is path-connected (since $S^n$ is connected and $I$ is connected), so $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
  - For $k = 1$: Since $n \geq 2$, $\widetilde{H}_1(U) = \widetilde{H}_1(V) = 0$ and $\widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) = 0$. The sequence gives $0 \to H_1(X) \xrightarrow{\partial} \widetilde{H}_0(U \cap V) \to 0$ in reduced homology with 0-sphere base, giving $H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (generated by the circle factor of the mapping torus).
  - For $1 < k < n$: Since $\widetilde{H}_k(S^n) = \widetilde{H}_{k-1}(S^n) = 0$, $H_k(X) = 0$.
  - For $k = n, n+1$: The sequence is:
  $$0 \to H_{n+1}(X) \to H_n(S^n) \xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_* - f_*} H_n(S^n) \to H_n(X) \to 0.$$
  Since $H_n(S^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $f_* \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is multiplication by $\deg(f) = d$, the map is $1 - d \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$.
  - Therefore:
    - $\ker(1 - d) = \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & \text{if } d = 1, \\ 0 & \text{if } d \neq 1. \end{cases}$
    - $\operatorname{coker}(1 - d) = \mathbb{Z} / |1 - d|\mathbb{Z}$ (where $\mathbb{Z}/0\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}$ when $d=1$).
  - For $k > n+1$: $H_k(X) = 0$.
  <2>5. Summary for $n \geq 2$:
  - If $d = 1$: $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}, H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}, H_n(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}, H_{n+1}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and all other $H_k(X) = 0$. (When $n=1$, handled in <1>2).
  - If $d \neq 1$: $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}, H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}, H_n(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}/|1-d|\mathbb{Z}$, and all other $H_k(X) = 0$.
  <2>6. Proof: By exactness of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence. Q.E.D.

<1>2. Case $n = 1$.
  <2>1. For $n = 1$, the circle $S^1$ has $H_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
  <2>2. The relevant Mayer-Vietoris segment is:
  $$0 \to H_2(X) \to H_1(S^1) \xrightarrow{1-d} H_1(S^1) \to H_1(X) \to H_0(S^1) \xrightarrow{0} H_0(S^1) \to H_0(X) \to 0.$$
  <2>3. $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
  <2>4. $H_2(X) \cong \ker(1-d) = \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & \text{if } d = 1, \\ 0 & \text{if } d \neq 1. \end{cases}$
  <2>5. For $H_1(X)$: From the short exact sequence $0 \to \operatorname{coker}(1-d) \to H_1(X) \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0$, since $\mathbb{Z}$ is free, the sequence splits:
  $$H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus (\mathbb{Z}/|1-d|\mathbb{Z}).$$
  If $d = 1$, $H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}^2$.
  <2>6. For $k \ge 3$, $H_k(X) = 0$.
  <2>7. Proof: By Mayer-Vietoris sequence for $n=1$. Q.E.D.

<1>3. Case $n = 0$.
  <2>1. $S^0 = \{-1, 1\}$. A map $f \colon S^0 \to S^0$ has degree defined via 0-dimensional homology / end points:
  - If $f = \operatorname{id}$ ($d = 1$), $X \cong S^1 \sqcup S^1$, so $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2, H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$.
  - If $f$ swaps the two points ($d = -1$), $X$ is a single connected circle of double length $S^1$, so $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}, H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
  <2>2. Proof: By direct topological inspection of the mapping torus of a 2-point space. Q.E.D.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
  <2>1. Proof: Steps <1>1–<1>3 determine $H_*(X)$ completely for all $n \ge 0$ and all degrees $d$.
