::: {.problem}
Show that $\pi_1(K)$, the Klein bottle, is given by pairs $(m,n)$ where $(m,n)\star (p,q) = (m+(-1)^np, n+q)$

1. Show this is torsion-free

2. Show that $T$ is a double cover of $K$.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution** (Gemini 3.7 Flash)

**Goal:** Prove that the fundamental group of the Klein bottle $\pi_1(K)$ is isomorphic to the semidirect product $G = \mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}$ whose underlying set is $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ with group operation $(m, n) \star (p, q) = (m + (-1)^n p, n + q)$. Prove that:
1. $G$ is torsion-free.
2. The 2-torus $T^2$ is a 2-fold covering space of the Klein bottle $K$.

<1>1. Realize $\pi_1(K)$ as the group of deck transformations on $\mathbb{R}^2$.
  <2>1. The Klein bottle $K$ is the quotient of $\mathbb{R}^2$ by the group of affine isometries $G$ generated by the translations and glide reflections:
  $a(x, y) = (x + 1, y)$ and $b(x, y) = (-x, y + 1)$ (or $a(x, y) = (x, y+1)$ and $b(x, y) = (x+1, -y)$).
  <2>2. Using the standard presentation $K = \langle a, b \mid a b a b^{-1} = 1 \rangle \cong \langle a, b \mid b a b^{-1} = a^{-1} \rangle$:
  Every element of $G$ can be written uniquely in normal form as $a^m b^n$ for $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
  <2>3. Group multiplication in normal form:
  $$(a^m b^n)(a^p b^q) = a^m (b^n a^p b^{-n}) b^{n+q} = a^m (a^{(-1)^n p}) b^{n+q} = a^{m + (-1)^n p} b^{n+q}.$$
  <2>4. Under the bijection $(m, n) \leftrightarrow a^m b^n$, the group multiplication is:
  $$(m, n) \star (p, q) = (m + (-1)^n p, n + q).$$
  <2>5. The identity element is $(0, 0)$ and the inverse is $(m, n)^{-1} = (-(-1)^n m, -n)$.
  <2>6. Proof: By the semidirect product structure $\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_\theta \mathbb{Z}$ where $\theta(n)(m) = (-1)^n m$. Q.E.D.

<1>2. Show that $G = \pi_1(K)$ is torsion-free.
  <2>1. Let $(m, n) \in G$ and let $k \ge 1$ be an integer such that $(m, n)^k = (0, 0)$.
  <2>2. Projecting $G \to \mathbb{Z}$ onto the second coordinate via $(m, n) \mapsto n$ is a group homomorphism:
  The second coordinate of $(m, n)^k$ is $k \cdot n$.
  <2>3. Since $(m, n)^k = (0, 0)$, we must have $k \cdot n = 0$, which implies $n = 0$ (since $\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion-free).
  <2>4. For $n = 0$, $(m, 0)^k = (k \cdot m, 0) = (0, 0)$, which implies $k \cdot m = 0$, hence $m = 0$.
  <2>5. Therefore, $(m, n) = (0, 0)$ is the only element of finite order in $G$.
  <2>6. Proof: By <2>1–<2>5, $G$ is torsion-free. Q.E.D.

<1>3. Show that the torus $T^2$ is a double cover of $K$.
  <2>1. Define the homomorphism $\phi \colon G \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ by $\phi(m, n) = n \bmod 2$.
  <2>2. $\phi$ is a surjective group homomorphism because $\phi((m, n) \star (p, q)) = (n + q) \bmod 2 = \phi(m, n) + \phi(p, q)$.
  <2>3. The kernel $H = \ker(\phi) = \{(m, n) \in G \mid n \equiv 0 \pmod 2\} = \{(m, 2k) \mid m, k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ is a normal subgroup of index 2 in $G$.
  <2>4. For elements in $H$, the multiplication rule simplifies because $(-1)^n = (-1)^{2k} = 1$:
  $$(m, 2k) \star (p, 2l) = (m + p, 2(k + l)).$$
  <2>5. Thus $H$ is isomorphic to the free abelian group $\mathbb{Z}^2$:
  $$H \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}^2 \cong \pi_1(T^2).$$
  <2>6. By covering space theory, the index 2 subgroup $H \le \pi_1(K)$ corresponds to a connected 2-sheeted (double) covering space $\widetilde{K} \to K$.
  <2>7. Since $\pi_1(\widetilde{K}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ and $\widetilde{K}$ is a closed orientable 2-manifold (the orientable double cover of the non-orientable surface $K$), $\widetilde{K}$ is homeomorphic to the 2-torus $T^2$.
  <2>8. Proof: By classification of closed surfaces and covering space Galois correspondence. Q.E.D.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
  <2>1. Proof: Steps <1>1–<1>3 complete the proof.
