::: {.problem}
Given a group $G = <A : R>$, show how to construct a CW-complex $X$ such that $\pi_1(X) = G$.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution** (Gemini 3.7 Flash)

**Goal:** Given a group presentation $G = \langle A \mid R \rangle$ with generator set $A$ and relator set $R$, construct a 2-dimensional CW complex $X$ such that $\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong G$.

<1>1. Construct the 0-skeleton $X^0$ and 1-skeleton $X^1$.
  <2>1. Set the 0-skeleton $X^0 = \{x_0\}$ to be a single vertex.
  <2>2. For each generator $a \in A$, attach a 1-cell $e_a^1 = [0, 1]$ to $X^0$ by identifying both endpoints $0, 1 \in \partial e_a^1$ to the vertex $x_0$.
  <2>3. The 1-skeleton is the wedge sum of circles indexed by $A$:
  $$X^1 = \bigvee_{a \in A} S_a^1.$$
  <2>4. By the Seifert-van Kampen theorem, the fundamental group of $X^1$ is the free group on $A$:
  $$\pi_1(X^1, x_0) \cong F(A) = \langle A \mid \emptyset \rangle.$$
  <2>5. Proof: Standard fundamental group of a wedge of circles. Q.E.D.

<1>2. Attach 2-cells to form the 2-skeleton $X = X^2$.
  <2>1. Each relator $r \in R$ is a reduced word in the free group $F(A)$, say $r = a_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1} a_{i_2}^{\epsilon_2} \cdots a_{i_k}^{\epsilon_k}$ with $a_{i_j} \in A$ and $\epsilon_j \in \{\pm 1\}$.
  <2>2. Define a continuous attaching map $\phi_r \colon \partial D^2 \cong S^1 \to X^1$ by parameterizing $S^1 = [0, 1] / (0 \sim 1)$ into $k$ equal subintervals $[(j-1)/k, j/k]$, and mapping the $j$-th subinterval to traverse the loop $e_{a_{i_j}}^1$ in the positive direction if $\epsilon_j = 1$ and in the reverse direction if $\epsilon_j = -1$.
  <2>3. Under this definition, the loop $\phi_r$ represents the homotopy class $[\phi_r] = r \in \pi_1(X^1, x_0)$.
  <2>4. Attach a 2-cell $e_r^2 \cong D^2$ along $\phi_r$ for each relator $r \in R$:
  $$X = X^2 = X^1 \cup_{\{\phi_r\}_{r \in R}} \left( \bigsqcup_{r \in R} D_r^2 \right).$$
  <2>5. Proof: By construction of cell attachments. Q.E.D.

<1>3. Apply the cellular Seifert-van Kampen theorem to compute $\pi_1(X, x_0)$.
  <2>1. By the standard theorem on fundamental groups of 2-dimensional CW complexes (e.g. Hatcher Proposition 1.26), attaching a family of 2-cells $\{e_r^2\}_{r \in R}$ along maps $\phi_r \colon \partial D^2 \to X^1$ induces an isomorphism:
  $$\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \pi_1(X^1, x_0) / N,$$
  where $N = \langle\langle [\phi_r] : r \in R \rangle\rangle$ is the normal subgroup of $\pi_1(X^1, x_0)$ generated by the homotopy classes of the attaching maps.
  <2>2. By <1>1, $\pi_1(X^1, x_0) = F(A)$.
  <2>3. By <1>2, $[\phi_r] = r \in F(A)$ for each $r \in R$, so $N = \langle\langle R \rangle\rangle$.
  <2>4. Thus, $\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong F(A) / \langle\langle R \rangle\rangle \cong \langle A \mid R \rangle = G$.
  <2>5. Proof: By the cellular Seifert-van Kampen theorem. Q.E.D.

<1>4. Q.E.D.
  <2>1. Proof: Steps <1>1–<1>3 explicitly construct the standard presentation complex $X = X(A; R)$ satisfying $\pi_1(X) \cong G$.
