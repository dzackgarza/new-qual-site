::: {.exercise}
Show that every group of order $p$ is cyclic.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution** (Gemini 3.7 Flash)

**Goal:** Let $p$ be a prime number, and let $G$ be a group of order $|G| = p$.
Prove that $G$ is cyclic (and hence $G \cong \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$).

<1>1. Lagrange's Theorem for finite groups: If $G$ is a finite group and $H \le G$ is a subgroup, then $|H|$ divides $|G|$.
In particular, the order of any element $x \in G$ divides $|G|$.
Proof: The cosets of $H$ form an equipotent partition of $G$, each of size $|H|$, so $|G| = [G : H] \cdot |H|$.
The order of an element $|x| = |\langle x \rangle|$ divides $|G|$.

<1>2. Selection of a non-identity element: <2>1. $|G| = p \ge 2$, so $G \neq \{e\}$.
Proof: The smallest prime is $2$.
<2>2. There exists an element $g \in G$ such that $g \neq e$.
Proof: Since $|G| \ge 2$, $G$ contains elements other than the identity.

<1>3. Determination of the order of $g$: <2>1. Let $d = |g| = |\langle g \rangle|$ be the order of $g$ in $G$.
Proof: Definition of element order.
<2>2. By Lagrange's Theorem (<1>1), $d \mid |G| = p$.
Proof: The order of an element divides the order of the group.
<2>3. The only positive divisors of the prime $p$ are $1$ and $p$.
Thus $d \in \{1, p\}$.
Proof: Definition of prime number.
<2>4. Since $g \neq e$, $d \neq 1$.
Proof: The identity $e$ is the unique element of order 1. <2>5. Therefore, $d = |g| = p$.
Proof: Ruling out $d = 1$ from $d \in \{1, p\}$.

<1>4. $G$ is cyclic generated by $g$: <2>1. The cyclic subgroup $\langle g \rangle = \{g^0, g^1, \dots, g^{p-1}\} \subseteq G$ has cardinality $|\langle g \rangle| = |g| = p$.
Proof: A cyclic group generated by an element of order $p$ has $p$ distinct elements.
<2>2. Since $\langle g \rangle \subseteq G$ and $|\langle g \rangle| = |G| = p$, we must have $\langle g \rangle = G$.
Proof: A subset of a finite set with the same cardinality as the whole set is the entire set.
<2>3. Therefore, $G$ is a cyclic group generated by $g$.
Proof: Definition of cyclic group.

<1>5. Conclusion: Every group of prime order $p$ is cyclic.
Proof: By <1>4.
