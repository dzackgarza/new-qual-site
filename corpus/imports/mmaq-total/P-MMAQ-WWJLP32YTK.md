::: problem
Prove or disprove each of the following statements.

(a) If $f : [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}$ is a measurable function, then given any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a compact set $K \subset [0, 1]$ such that $f$ is continuous on $K$ relative to $K$.

(b) If f is Borel measurable on $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$, then for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the function $g(y) = f(x, y)$ is also Borel measurable on $\mathbb{R}$.

(c) If $E \subset \mathbb{R}$, then $E$ is measurable if and only if given any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exist a closed set $F$ and an open set $G$ such that $F \subset E \subset G$ and the measure of $G-F$ is less than $\varepsilon$.
:::

::: {.solution}
> **AI-Generated Solution**

**Goal:** Decide each of (a), (b), (c): (a) Lusin-type approximation of a measurable function by a continuous one on a compact set; (b) Borel measurability of sections of a Borel function of two variables; (c) the closed/open inner–outer approximation characterization of measurability.

<1>1. (a) is true.
<2>1. Lusin's theorem applies: since $f : [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ is measurable (hence finite a.e.), for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a compact $K \subseteq [0,1]$ with $m([0,1] \setminus K) < \varepsilon$ and $f|_K$ continuous relative to $K$.
Proof: this is exactly the statement of Lusin's theorem, which holds for finite a.e. measurable functions on sets of finite measure.
<2>2. Q.E.D. Proof: <2>1 is precisely the assertion of (a).

<1>2. (b) is true.
<2>1. For every Borel set $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ and every fixed $x$, the section $B_x := \theset{y : (x, y) \in B}$ is Borel in $\mathbb{R}$.
Proof: let $\mathcal{F}$ be the family of sets $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ whose sections $B_x$ are Borel for every $x$.
$\mathcal{F}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra: sections of unions, intersections, and complements are the corresponding unions, intersections, and complements of sections.
Every open rectangle $U \times V$ lies in $\mathcal{F}$ (its section is $V$ or $\emptyset$). Hence $\mathcal{F}$ contains the $\sigma$-algebra generated by open rectangles, which is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of $\mathbb{R}^2$.
<2>2. $g^{-1}(U) = \left(f^{-1}(U)\right)_x$ for every open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$; since $f$ is Borel measurable, $f^{-1}(U)$ is Borel, so $g^{-1}(U)$ is Borel by <2>1. Proof: $y \in g^{-1}(U) \iff f(x, y) \in U \iff (x, y) \in f^{-1}(U)$.
<2>3. Q.E.D. Proof: by <2>2 the preimage of every open set is Borel, so $g$ is Borel measurable.

<1>3. (c) is true.
<2>1. ($\Rightarrow$) If $E$ is measurable, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exist closed $F$ and open $G$ with $F \subseteq E \subseteq G$ and $m(G \setminus F) < \varepsilon$.
Proof: by outer regularity pick open $G \supseteq E$ with $m(G \setminus E) < \varepsilon/2$, and by inner regularity (approximation by closed sets for Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$) pick closed $F \subseteq E$ with $m(E \setminus F) < \varepsilon/2$.
Since $G \setminus F \subseteq (G \setminus E) \cup (E \setminus F)$, subadditivity gives $m(G \setminus F) < \varepsilon$.
<2>2. ($\Leftarrow$) If for every $\varepsilon > 0$ such $F, G$ exist, then $E$ is measurable.
Proof: $E = F \cup (E \setminus F)$ with $F$ closed (measurable).
Since $E \setminus F \subseteq G \setminus F$, we get $m^*(E \setminus F) \leq m(G \setminus F) < \varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$, so $m^*(E \setminus F) = 0$; null sets are measurable, hence $E \setminus F$ is measurable and so is $E$.
<2>3. Q.E.D. Proof: <2>1 and <2>2 give both implications.
