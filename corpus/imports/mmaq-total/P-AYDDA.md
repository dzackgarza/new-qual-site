::: problem
Describe the process that extends a measure on an algebra
$\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $X$, to a complete measure defined on a
$\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}$ containing $\mathcal{A}$. State the
corresponding definitions and results (without proofs).
:::

> **AI-Generated Solution** (Gemini 3.7 Flash)

**Goal:** Describe the Carathéodory Extension process extending a pre-measure (measure) on an algebra $\mathcal A \subseteq \mathcal P(X)$ to a complete measure on a $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal B \supseteq \mathcal A$, stating the precise definitions and main results.

<1>1. **Fundamental Definitions.**
  <2>1. **Algebra:** A collection $\mathcal A \subseteq \mathcal P(X)$ of subsets of $X$ is an *algebra* on $X$ if:
    1. $\emptyset, X \in \mathcal A$;
    2. $A \in \mathcal A \implies A^c = X \setminus A \in \mathcal A$;
    3. $A, B \in \mathcal A \implies A \cup B \in \mathcal A$ (closed under finite unions).
  <2>2. **Pre-measure (Measure on an Algebra):** A function $\mu_0: \mathcal A \to [0, \infty]$ is a *pre-measure* on $\mathcal A$ if:
    1. $\mu_0(\emptyset) = 0$;
    2. Countable additivity on $\mathcal A$: if $\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal A$ is a pairwise disjoint sequence such that $\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \in \mathcal A$, then
    $$
    \mu_0\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu_0(A_n).
    $$
  <2>3. **Induced Outer Measure:** The *outer measure* $\mu^*: \mathcal P(X) \to [0, \infty]$ induced by $\mu_0$ is defined for any $E \subseteq X$ by:
    $$
    \mu^*(E) \definedas \inf \left\{ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu_0(A_n) : A_n \in \mathcal A \text{ for each } n \in \NN, \text{ and } E \subseteq \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \right\},
    $$
    with the convention $\inf \emptyset = \infty$.
  <2>4. **Carathéodory Measurability:** A subset $E \subseteq X$ is called *$\mu^*$-measurable* (or *Carathéodory-measurable*) if for every "test set" $T \subseteq X$,
    $$
    \mu^*(T) = \mu^*(T \cap E) + \mu^*(T \cap E^c).
    $$
    (Since $\mu^*(T) \leq \mu^*(T \cap E) + \mu^*(T \cap E^c)$ always holds by subadditivity, measurability is equivalent to $\mu^*(T) \geq \mu^*(T \cap E) + \mu^*(T \cap E^c)$ for all $T$ with $\mu^*(T) < \infty$.)
    We denote the collection of all $\mu^*$-measurable sets by $\mathcal M(\mu^*)$ (or $\mathcal B$).

<1>2. **Properties and Intermediate Results of the Construction.**
  <2>1. **Properties of $\mu^*$:**
    1. $\mu^*(\emptyset) = 0$;
    2. Monotonicity: $E_1 \subseteq E_2 \implies \mu^*(E_1) \leq \mu^*(E_2)$;
    3. Countable subadditivity: $\mu^*\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n\right) \leq \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu^*(E_n)$ for any sequence $\{E_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal P(X)$.
  <2>2. **Consistency on the Base Algebra:**
    For every $A \in \mathcal A$, $\mu^*(A) = \mu_0(A)$, so $\mu^*$ agrees with $\mu_0$ on $\mathcal A$.

<1>3. **The Extension Theorems.**
  <2>1. **Carathéodory's Theorem:**
    Let $\mu^*$ be an outer measure on $X$, and let $\mathcal M(\mu^*)$ be the collection of all $\mu^*$-measurable sets. Then:
    1. $\mathcal M(\mu^*)$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing all sets of $\mu^*$-outer measure zero (i.e. $\mu^*(N) = 0 \implies N \in \mathcal M(\mu^*)$);
    2. The restriction $\mu \definedas \mu^*|_{\mathcal M(\mu^*)}$ is a countably additive measure on $\mathcal M(\mu^*)$;
    3. The measure space $(X, \mathcal M(\mu^*), \mu)$ is *complete* (every subset of a measure-zero set belongs to $\mathcal M(\mu^*)$ and has measure zero).
  <2>2. **Inclusion of the Algebra:**
    If $\mu^*$ is induced by a pre-measure $\mu_0$ on an algebra $\mathcal A$, then $\mathcal A \subseteq \mathcal M(\mu^*)$. Consequently, $\sigma(\mathcal A) \subseteq \mathcal M(\mu^*)$, where $\sigma(\mathcal A)$ is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal A$.
  <2>3. **Hahn-Kolmogorov (Carathéodory) Extension and Uniqueness Theorem:**
    Let $\mu_0$ be a pre-measure on an algebra $\mathcal A$, and let $\mu^*$ and $\mathcal M(\mu^*)$ be constructed as above.
    1. **Existence:** $\mu = \mu^*|_{\mathcal M(\mu^*)}$ is a complete measure on $\mathcal M(\mu^*)$ extending $\mu_0$ (meaning $\mu(A) = \mu_0(A)$ for all $A \in \mathcal A$).
    2. **Uniqueness:** If $\mu_0$ is $\sigma$-finite on $\mathcal A$ (i.e., there exists a sequence $\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal A$ such that $X = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n$ and $\mu_0(A_n) < \infty$ for all $n$), then the extension $\mu|_{\sigma(\mathcal A)}$ to the generated $\sigma$-algebra $\sigma(\mathcal A)$ is *unique*. Furthermore, $\mathcal M(\mu^*)$ is the completion of $\sigma(\mathcal A)$ with respect to $\mu|_{\sigma(\mathcal A)}$.

<1>4. **Summary of the Process.**
  1. Start with $(X, \mathcal A, \mu_0)$.
  2. Construct the outer measure $\mu^*$ on $\mathcal P(X)$ via infima of countable covers by elements of $\mathcal A$.
  3. Identify the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal B = \mathcal M(\mu^*)$ of sets satisfying Carathéodory's criterion.
  4. Restrict $\mu^*$ to $\mathcal B$ to obtain the complete measure space $(X, \mathcal B, \mu)$, which contains $\sigma(\mathcal A)$ and extends $\mu_0$. Q.E.D.
