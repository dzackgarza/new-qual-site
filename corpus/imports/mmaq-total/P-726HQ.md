**Goal:** State the Riemann Mapping Theorem precisely, and provide a complete Lamport-style structured proof of the uniqueness assertion.

* * *

### Statement of the Riemann Mapping Theorem

**Theorem (Riemann Mapping Theorem):** Let $\Omega \subsetneq \mathbb{C}$ be a non-empty simply connected domain (open connected subset), and let $z_0 \in \Omega$ be an arbitrary fixed point.
Then there exists a unique biholomorphic (conformal bijection) mapping $f: \Omega \to \mathbb{D}$ (where $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ is the open unit disk) such that: $$f(z_0) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad f'(z_0) > 0.$$

* * *

### Proof of the Uniqueness Part

<1>1. **Set up the comparison between two such Riemann mappings.** <2>1. Suppose $f_1, f_2: \Omega \to \mathbb{D}$ are two biholomorphic mappings satisfying the normalization conditions: $$f_1(z_0) = 0, \quad f_1'(z_0) > 0, \qquad f_2(z_0) = 0, \quad f_2'(z_0) > 0.$$ *Proof:* Stating the uniqueness hypothesis.
<2>2. Define $g: \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{D}$ by $g = f_1 \circ f_2^{-1}$.
*Proof:* Well-defined because $f_2: \Omega \to \mathbb{D}$ is biholomorphic, so its inverse $f_2^{-1}: \mathbb{D} \to \Omega$ exists and is biholomorphic.
<2>3. Q.E.D.

<1>2. **$g$ is an automorphism of the unit disk fixing the origin.** <2>1. $g$ is a composition of two biholomorphisms ($f_1$ and $f_2^{-1}$), hence $g \in \text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})$.
*Proof:* The set of biholomorphic self-maps $\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})$ is a group under composition.
<2>2. $g(0) = f_1(f_2^{-1}(0)) = f_1(z_0) = 0$.
*Proof:* $f_2(z_0) = 0 \implies f_2^{-1}(0) = z_0$, and $f_1(z_0) = 0$.
<2>3. Q.E.D.

<1>3. **By the Schwarz Lemma, $g$ is a pure rotation.** <2>1. Any holomorphic map $g: \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{D}$ with $g(0) = 0$ that is bijective (an automorphism) is of the form $g(w) = e^{i\theta} w$ for some constant $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$.
*Proof:* By the Schwarz Lemma, $|g'(0)| \leq 1$ and $|(g^{-1})'(0)| \leq 1$.
By the chain rule, $g'(0) \cdot (g^{-1})'(0) = 1$, so $|g'(0)| = 1$.
The equality case of the Schwarz Lemma implies $g(w) = e^{i\theta} w$.
<2>2. Therefore, $g(w) = e^{i\theta} w$ for all $w \in \mathbb{D}$.
*Proof:* Follows from <2>1. <2>3. Q.E.D.

<1>4. **Determine the angle $\theta$ using the derivative normalization.** <2>1. Differentiating $g(w) = e^{i\theta} w$ gives $g'(0) = e^{i\theta}$.
*Proof:* Derivative of linear function.
<2>2. By the chain rule on $g(w) = f_1(f_2^{-1}(w))$ at $w=0$: $$g'(0) = f_1'(f_2^{-1}(0)) \cdot (f_2^{-1})'(0) = f_1'(z_0) \cdot \frac{1}{f_2'(z_0)} = \frac{f_1'(z_0)}{f_2'(z_0)}.$$ *Proof:* Inverse function theorem derivative rule $(f_2^{-1})'(0) = \frac{1}{f_2'(z_0)}$.
<2>3. Since $f_1'(z_0) > 0$ and $f_2'(z_0) > 0$, the ratio $\frac{f_1'(z_0)}{f_2'(z_0)}$ is a strictly positive real number: $$\frac{f_1'(z_0)}{f_2'(z_0)} > 0.$$ *Proof:* Quotient of two positive real numbers.
<2>4. From <2>1 and <2>2, $e^{i\theta} = \frac{f_1'(z_0)}{f_2'(z_0)} \in (0, \infty)$.
Since $|e^{i\theta}| = 1$, we must have $e^{i\theta} = 1$.
*Proof:* The only positive real number on the unit circle is $1$.
<2>5. Q.E.D.

<1>5. **Conclusion: $f_1 = f_2$.** <2>1. Since $e^{i\theta} = 1$, $g(w) = w$ for all $w \in \mathbb{D}$, which means $g = \text{id}_{\mathbb{D}}$.
*Proof:* Substitution of $e^{i\theta} = 1$ into $g(w) = e^{i\theta} w$.
<2>2. $f_1 \circ f_2^{-1} = \text{id}_{\mathbb{D}} \implies f_1(z) = f_2(z)$ for all $z \in \Omega$.
*Proof:* Composing both sides with $f_2$ on the right.
<2>3. Q.E.D.
