::: {.problem title="?"}
Suppose $\{f_n\}$ is a sequence of functions in $L^2([0,1])$ such that $\|f_n\|_{L^2}\le 1$.
If $f$ is measurable and $f_n\to f$ in measure, then

(a) $f\in L^2([0,1])$;

(b) $f_n\to f$ weakly in $L^2$;

(c) $f_n\to f$ with respect to norm in $L^p$ for $1\le p<2$.

Hint for part (b): Use Vitali convergence theorem, that is, if $g_n\to g$ pointwise a.e. on $[0,1]$ and $\{g_n\}$ is uniformly integrable, then $$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 g_n=\int_0^1 g.$$ Recall that $\{g_n\}$ being uniformly integrable means that for each $\varepsilon>0$, there exists a $\delta>0$ such that $\int_A|g_n|<\varepsilon$ for all $n$ and all measurable $A\subseteq[0,1]$ with $m(A)<\delta$.

Hint for part (c): use the fact that, if $g_n$ is a sequence in $L^p([0,1])$ that converges pointwise a.e. to $g\in L^p([0,1])$, then $g_n$ converges to $g$ in norm if and only if $\{|g_n|^p\}$ is uniformly integrable.
:::
