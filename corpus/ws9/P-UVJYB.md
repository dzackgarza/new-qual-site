::: {.problem title="?"}
For a positive integer $n$, let $I_n$ denote the $n \times n$ identity matrix.

a. Let $A$ be a $2\times 2$ real matrix with eigenvalues $\lambda_1=1$ and $\lambda_2=-1$.
Show: $A^2 = I_2$.
b. Find a $3\times 3$ real matrix whose only eigenvalues (in $\mathbb{C}$) are $1$ and $-1$ such that $A^2 \ne I_3$.
c. Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ real symmetric matrix whose only eigenvalues (in $\mathbb{C}$) are $1$ and $-1$.
Show that $A^2 = I_n$.
:::
